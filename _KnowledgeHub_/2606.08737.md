# Dream-Tac: A Unified Tactile World Action Model for Contact-Rich Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and collaborators introduce Dream-Tac, a unified tactile world action model that leverages visuo-tactile data for robot manipulation. This model, built on a Diffusion Transformer, achieved an 83.3% average success rate across six contact-rich tasks, outperforming vision-only baselines by 31.6%, while also accelerating training by up to 2.9x and inference by 1.8x.

## Problem

- Existing world action models rely heavily on visual observations, which are insufficient for capturing precise physical interaction cues in contact-rich and fine-grained robot manipulation tasks.
- Integrating sparse and transient tactile signals into world models is challenging, as indiscriminate inclusion can dilute their impact and diminish sensitivity to critical contact events.
- The high computational demands of generative world action models, such as Diffusion Transformers, hinder their real-time deployment for practical robotic control.

## Method

- **Dream-Tac**, a unified ==generative world action== model, was developed to jointly predict future visual observations, tactile observations, and robot actions using a pre-trained video Diffusion Transformer backbone.
- A Contact-Aware Self Attention (CASA) mechanism with a gated logit bias was introduced to adaptively amplify the influence of tactile information during salient contact events, addressing tactile sparsity.
- Dual-level acceleration strategies, including FlashBias for training (up to 2.9x speedup) and diffusion-step caching for inference (1.8x speedup), were implemented to enable real-time robotic deployment.

## Results

- Dream-Tac achieved an average success rate of **83.3%** across six real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, representing a **31.6%** improvement over the strongest baseline, Cosmos-Policy (**51.7%**).
- The model demonstrated enhanced generalization capabilities under various environmental variations, such as changes in table height, object appearance, and background, especially in scenarios affecting tactile cues.
- Training efficiency was improved by up to **2.9x** using FlashBias, and inference latency was reduced by **1.8x** through diffusion-step caching, allowing for real-time operation without sacrificing performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile sensing provides critical and unambiguous information for precise physical interaction that is often visually ambiguous, and can be effectively integrated into generative world action models.
> - Selectively emphasizing tactile information through a contact-aware attention mechanism is crucial for leveraging sparse tactile signals effectively, particularly during active interaction phases.
> - Optimizations like FlashBias and diffusion-step caching can significantly mitigate the computational burden of Diffusion Transformer-based world action models, making them practical for real-time robotic control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research builds upon the paradigm of world models and world action models in robotics. World models learn predictive representations of environmental dynamics, enabling agents to anticipate future observations. World action models extend this by integrating predictive knowledge into decision-making, guiding action generation using dynamics priors, often acquired from large-scale web data pretraining. These models have demonstrated capabilities in general manipulation tasks.

However, existing world action models predominantly rely on visual observations. This reliance presents limitations in contact-rich and fine-grained manipulation scenarios where vision alone may not adequately capture critical physical interaction cues such as precise contact states, local geometry, and subtle object properties. Tactile sensing offers a complementary modality by directly providing contact and local interaction signals that are often ambiguous or entirely unavailable in visual observations. Previous research has explored incorporating tactile feedback into robotic policies for tasks like grasping, insertion, in-hand manipulation, and tool use, and more recently, into end-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) models. These efforts have advanced tactile-aware control and representation learning.

This paper addresses a gap in the existing literature by integrating tactile sensing explicitly into a generative world action model. While tactile signals are inherently temporal and event-driven, making them suitable for world models, their sparsity and transient nature require a selective integration approach to prevent dilution of critical interaction cues. Prior work has not extensively explored the use of tactile signals for explicitly modeling future state evolution and contact dynamics within a unified world action model framework. Dream-Tac positions itself as an advancement in this area by jointly modeling future visual observations, robot actions, and future tactile observations, thereby extending world action modeling to leverage contact dynamics for enhanced precision in robot manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified tactile world action model, termed Dream-Tac, capable of performing contact-rich robot manipulation more effectively than existing vision-centric models. The work is motivated by several observations regarding the limitations of current robot manipulation systems:

1. **Insufficiency of Vision in Contact-Rich Tasks:** Traditional world action models, primarily relying on visual input, struggle with tasks requiring precise physical interaction. Visual observations often fail to provide clear, fine-grained cues about contact states, local geometry, and subtle material properties crucial for tasks like inserting, peeling, or cutting. For instance, RGB images may not clearly indicate contact onset, whereas tactile signals can reveal distinct physical interaction cues (as illustrated in Figure 1a of the paper).
2. **Necessity of Tactile Sensing for Physical Interaction:** Tactile sensing offers direct and unambiguous information about physical contact, local deformations, and interaction forces. This makes it a critical modality for achieving high precision and robustness in manipulation tasks where direct physical interaction is central to success.
3. **Challenges of Integrating Sparse Tactile Signals:** While tactile signals are valuable, they are often sparse and transient. During many manipulation phases, tactile input might remain static or exhibit only minor noise. However, critical events such as contact onset, slip, or release manifest as sharp, localized variations. Incorporating these signals symmetrically with other modalities without specific consideration for their temporal characteristics could dilute their impact and diminish the model's sensitivity to crucial contact events.
4. **Computational Demands of Generative Models for Real-Time Control:** World action models, particularly those built on Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), are computationally intensive due to quadratic self-attention complexity and iterative multi-step denoising processes. Adding multimodal sequences, especially tactile, further exacerbates this computational burden, making real-time deployment for robotic control challenging.

To address these motivations, Dream-Tac sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Unified Visuo-Tactile-Action Modeling:** To create a generative framework that jointly predicts future visual observations, future tactile observations, and robot actions. This aims to allow the robot to reason about both scene evolution and contact dynamics simultaneously.
- **Contact-Aware Multimodal Fusion:** To selectively integrate tactile signals into the world action model. This involves developing a mechanism that adaptively amplifies the influence of tactile information specifically during salient contact events, thereby mitigating the issue of tactile sparsity.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Deployment:** To design and implement acceleration strategies that make the computationally intensive world action model practical for real-time robotic operation. This includes optimizing both training and inference processes.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to enhance robot capabilities in contact-rich manipulation, leading to improved action accuracy and robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dream-Tac is formulated as a unified generative world action model that jointly predicts future visual observations ($v_{1:T}$), future tactile observations ($x_{1:T}$), and an action chunk ($a_{1:H}$), conditioned on current visual ($o$), tactile ($x$), and language instruction ($l$) inputs. This extends standard visual-only world action models $p(a_{1:H}, v_{1:T} | o, l)$ to a visuo-tactile formulation $p(a_{1:H}, v_{1:T}, x_{1:T} | o, x, l)$.

**Architecture:**
Dream-Tac is built upon a pre-trained video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, leveraging its existing T5 text encoder and video Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The core architectural elements are:
- **Unified Latent Space:** Visual observations, tactile observations, robot states, and action chunks are all mapped into a shared latent space. The pre-trained VAE, originally for video, is used to encode both visual and tactile observations into latent tokens. Robot states and actions are represented as latent-frame tokens and integrated into the video backbone. This design enables all modalities to be processed within a single diffusion transformer, facilitating bidirectional self-attention between action, visual, and tactile tokens.
- **Shared Backbone:** The DiT backbone processes this unified latent sequence for joint denoising, benefiting from the stability and priors of the pre-trained model.

**Contact-Aware Self Attention (CASA):**
To address the sparsity and transient nature of tactile signals, Dream-Tac introduces a gated attention mechanism to selectively emphasize tactile information.
- **Gated Logit Bias:** A scalar-gated additive bias is introduced into the attention logits. This bias is applied when a non-tactile query token attends to a tactile key token. The formulation is:
 $logit_{ij} = \frac{q_i^\top k_j}{\sqrt{d}} + \alpha g_t (1 - M_i) M_j$
 where $q_i, k_j$ are query/key vectors, $d$ is head dimension, $\alpha > 0$ controls bias magnitude, and $M_i, M_j \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate if token $i$ or $j$ is tactile. This asymmetric bias encourages non-tactile tokens (action, proprioceptive, visual) to attend more strongly to tactile information when relevant.
- **Gate Computation ($g_t$):** The gate $g_t$ quantifies the magnitude of tactile change between consecutive timesteps. It is computed from raw tactile RGB frames without a learned network.
 - For each left ($I_{L_t}$) and right ($I_{R_t}$) tactile image, the normalized mean absolute difference to the previous frame ($\delta_{L_t}$, $\delta_{R_t}$) is calculated.
 - The per-timestep event strength $\rho_t = \max(\delta_{L_t}, \delta_{R_t})$ is then derived. For the initial timestep, $\rho_0 = 0$.
 - $\rho_t$ is mapped to the final gate $g_t \in [g_{min}, g_{max}]$ using a fixed robust normalization and a sigmoid function. This mapping is designed to suppress benign sensor noise while amplifying salient contact events, making $g_t$ low during quiescent periods and high during active contact.

**Training Objective:**
Dream-Tac is trained using the latent denoising objective inherited from its pre-trained video model backbone.
- **Target Latent Variables:** The model aims to predict the latent tokens for future visual observations ($z_{v_{1:T}}$), future tactile observations ($z_{x_{1:T}}$), and action chunks ($z_{a_{1:H}}$), denoted collectively as $y$.
- **Denoising Process:** During training, a corrupted latent sequence $\tilde{y} = y + \sigma \epsilon$ is constructed by adding Gaussian noise $\epsilon$ at a sampled noise level $\sigma$. The diffusion transformer $f_\theta$ is trained to predict the noise $\epsilon$ from the corrupted sequence and the clean prefix context (current observations and language).
- **Loss Function:** The training loss is an L2 loss on the predicted noise:
 $\mathcal{L}_{\text{denoise}} = \mathbb{E}_{y, \epsilon, \sigma} \left[ \left\| f_\theta(\tilde{y}, \sigma, o, x, l) - \epsilon \right\|^2_2 \right]$
 This objective simultaneously supervises action generation, future visual prediction, and future tactile prediction, and can be decomposed into modality-specific terms $\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}} + \lambda_v \mathcal{L}_{\text{img}} + \lambda_t \mathcal{L}_{\text{tac}}$.

**Efficiency Design:**
To enable real-time robotic deployment, Dream-Tac incorporates a dual-level acceleration system:
- **Training Acceleration (FlashBias):** The contact-aware gated bias attention cannot be directly used with standard FlashAttention without breaking its optimized fused path. Following FlashBias, the structured logit bias is reformulated into a low-rank form and integrated into block-wise attention computation. This preserves the fused attention path, avoids explicit $O(S^2)$ bias materialization, and reduces memory access, achieving up to 2.94x training speedup.
- **Inference Acceleration (Diffusion-Step Caching):** Diffusion models require multiple denoising steps, which are computationally expensive. Dream-Tac implements a diffusion-step cache strategy, which reuses intermediate computation results from previous denoising steps for subsequent, similar steps. This reduces the number of effective inference steps to 2 while maintaining performance, yielding a 1.8x inference speedup. This is justified by the observed high cosine similarity (approx. 0.997) between neighboring diffusion steps, indicating redundancy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Dream-Tac involved experiments on six real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, assessing effectiveness, efficiency, generalization, and conducting ablation studies.

**Performance on Real-World Experiments:**
- Dream-Tac achieved an average success rate of 83.3% across the six tasks, outperforming all baselines: Cosmos-Policy (51.7%), ForceVLA (50.8%), $\pi_{0.5}$ (45.0%), and $\pi_0$ (30.8%). This represents a 31.6% improvement over Cosmos-Policy.
- The model obtained the highest success rate in five out of six tasks: Pick Baguette (100%), Insert USB (35%), Clean Whiteboard (90%), Play Mahjong (100%), and Cut Banana (90%). For Peel Cucumber, Dream-Tac achieved 85%, performing comparably to ForceVLA (90%).
- Dream-Tac demonstrated its most significant advantages in tasks requiring precise contact-rich interaction and fine-grained adjustment, such as Insert USB and Cut Banana.
- In the Play Mahjong task, where visual input was intentionally occluded, Dream-Tac achieved 100% success, attributed to its ability to leverage tactile feedback effectively, while ForceVLA, which can rely on visual observations and robot state priors, exhibited limitations in such visually deprived settings.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Tactile Fusion:** Incorporating tactile observations into the World Action Model (Visuo-tactile WAM) improved the average success rate from 51.7% (Visual WAM) to 74.2%. This indicates that tactile fusion is a primary contributor to performance gain.
- **Contact-Aware Attention Bias:** Further adding the proposed contact-aware attention bias (Visuo-tactile WAM + Bias) increased the average success rate to 83.3%. This suggests that the attention bias mechanism provides additional benefits by more effectively utilizing tactile-relevant interaction cues.

**Generalization Under Environment Variations:**
- Dream-Tac generally exhibited stronger generalization capabilities compared to Cosmos-Policy under various out-of-distribution environment changes, particularly those affecting tactile-relevant interaction cues.
 - **Table-Height Generalization (Peel Cucumber):** Dream-Tac achieved 85%, 90%, and 75% success under standard, +5 cm, and -5 cm settings, respectively, whereas Cosmos-Policy's performance dropped significantly (from 65% to 30% and 0%).
 - **Object Generalization (Play Mahjong):** Dream-Tac improved success from 35% to 100% on training-distribution tiles and from 15% to 85% on unseen tile appearances.
 - **Background Generalization (Cut Banana):** Dream-Tac achieved 90% and 70% under standard and altered backgrounds, outperforming Cosmos-Policy (40% and 25%).
 - **Spatial Generalization (Pick Baguette):** Both methods achieved comparable performance (100% in-distribution, 80% out-of-distribution).

**Training and Inference Efficiency:**
- **Training Efficiency:** Dream-Tac consistently improved training efficiency over Cosmos-Policy across all settings.
 - Without tactile input and attention bias, Dream-Tac achieved a 1.8x speedup (10.49 s vs. 19.02 s).
 - With tactile input but without attention bias, it showed a 1.5x speedup (15.97 s vs. 24.59 s).
 - The largest gain was observed in the full setting with both tactile input and attention bias, where Dream-Tac achieved a 2.9x speedup (27.48 s vs. 80.82 s). This highlights the effectiveness of the FlashBias-based reformulation in mitigating the computational overhead of the structured logit bias.
- **Inference Efficiency:** The diffusion-step caching strategy reduced inference latency from 1109 ms to 619 ms (1.8x speedup) while maintaining the same 85% success rate on the Peel Cucumber task. This indicates that Dream-Tac's real-time practicality is enhanced without sacrificing performance.

**Analysis:**
- **Latent Separability of Tactile Representations:** t-SNE visualizations (Figure 6) indicated that the pre-trained VAE encoder was sufficient to separate distinct tactile patterns in the shared latent space, demonstrating that projecting tactile observations alongside RGB images preserves discriminative tactile information.
- **Contact Gate Behavior:** Analysis of the contact gate $g_t$ showed that it effectively tracks the manipulation phase. $g_t$ remained low during approach phases (pre-contact), rose rapidly and sustained high values during active contact, and returned to lower levels after interaction subsided. This confirms its role in adaptively modulating tactile influence based on contact relevance.

**Additional Reconstruction Results (Appendix):**
- **Visual Reconstruction:** Dream-Tac demonstrated the ability to faithfully capture global scene layout, robot-object geometry, and coarse motion trends in future visual predictions, maintaining semantic consistency with ground truth.
- **Visuo-Tactile Reconstruction:** The model accurately predicted tactile image changes, distinguishing between pre-contact (undeformed patterns) and cutting phases (deformation and intensity redistribution). This indicates that Dream-Tac learns physically grounded, cross-modal dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Dream-Tac represents an advancement in robotic manipulation by addressing the limitations of vision-centric world action models in contact-rich environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Enhanced Performance in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** By jointly modeling visual observations, tactile feedback, and robot actions, Dream-Tac enables robots to achieve higher success rates in tasks requiring precise physical interaction. The demonstrated performance improvements over strong baselines, particularly in tasks sensitive to subtle contact dynamics, suggest a move towards more robust and capable robotic systems. This could facilitate the deployment of robots in complex real-world scenarios where fine-grained control and interaction are necessary.
- **Effective Multimodal Integration:** The introduction of contact-gated visuotactile fusion and a contact-aware attention bias offers a method for selectively leveraging sparse yet critical tactile information. This addresses a challenge in multimodal learning for robotics, ensuring that the most relevant sensory cues are emphasized during different phases of a manipulation task, without diluting the model's sensitivity.
- **Practicality for Real-Time Robotic Systems:** The dual-level acceleration strategy, incorporating FlashBias for training and diffusion-step caching for inference, significantly improves the computational efficiency of the framework. This makes diffusion-based world action models, which are typically resource-intensive, more viable for real-time operation on robotic hardware. Reducing latency and training time is crucial for iterative development and deployment in dynamic environments.
- **Improved Generalization:** The research indicates that Dream-Tac exhibits better generalization capabilities under various environmental variations that affect tactile-relevant interaction cues. This implies that learning grounded physical interaction models through tactile sensing can lead to more adaptable policies, potentially reducing the need for extensive retraining in slightly altered environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Dream-Tac establishes a unified framework for visuo-tactile world action modeling, pointing towards new directions for future research. This includes scaling to larger and more diverse datasets, exploring more sophisticated contact modeling mechanisms beyond simple frame-to-frame differences, and further optimizing computational efficiency. It also opens avenues for applying this approach to more complex manipulation challenges like dexterous multi-stage tasks and interaction with deformable objects.

The overall impact could be seen in the development of more intelligent and adaptable robots that can perceive, understand, and interact with their physical environment with greater precision and reliability, benefiting applications across manufacturing, healthcare, service robotics, and other domains requiring dexterous manipulation.
