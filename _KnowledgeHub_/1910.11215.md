# RoboNet: Large-Scale Multi-Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, Stanford University, and collaborators present RoboNet, a large-scale, multi-robot dataset for learning generalizable vision-based manipulation skills. They show that pre-training on RoboNet enables models to achieve improved zero-shot generalization to novel viewpoints and backgrounds, and facilitates few-shot adaptation to new robot hardware, outperforming methods trained with up to 20 times more robot-specific data.

## Problem

- Robot learning typically requires vast amounts of diverse data for generalizable skills, but most experiments are small-scale, single-domain, and single-robot.
- Current methods, such as simulators or data-efficient algorithms, struggle to capture the full complexity of real-world environments or are insufficient for intricate details.
- A lack of shared, large-scale real-world datasets limits transferability of learned skills across different robotic platforms and research institutions.

## Method

- Developed RoboNet, an open-source dataset comprising 162,000 real-world interaction trajectories (15 million video frames) from 7 diverse robot platforms across 4 institutions.
- Employed self-supervised learning algorithms, specifically Visual Foresight (a video prediction model with MPC) and a supervised inverse model, to train on the diverse RoboNet data.
- Designed experiments to evaluate generalization to novel objects, scenes, viewpoints, grippers, and entirely new robot platforms, as well as few-shot adaptation capabilities.

## Results

- Models trained on diverse viewpoints within RoboNet showed improved zero-shot generalization to novel viewpoints (16.2 ± 2.9 cm final distance) compared to single-view models (23.2 ± 2.6 cm).
- Pre-training on RoboNet and fine-tuning with 300-400 trajectories achieved significantly higher success rates for new robots (e.g., Kuka: 40% vs. 10% from scratch; Franka: 40% vs. 20% from scratch).
- Larger visual foresight models (500M parameters) still showed underfitting, achieving an L1 per-pixel error of 0.0847 ± 0.045 on a held-out test set, suggesting further model capacity improvements are needed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, diverse real-world datasets like RoboNet can enable broad generalization in robotic learning, allowing models to transfer skills across varied hardware and environments.
> - Pre-training on RoboNet significantly reduces the data required for adapting to new robot platforms, outperforming robot-specific training with 4x-20x more data in some cases.
> - Current visual prediction models, even with hundreds of millions of parameters, exhibit underfitting on RoboNet, indicating a need for higher capacity models or more intelligent data selection strategies.
