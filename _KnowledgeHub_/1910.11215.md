# RoboNet: Large-Scale Multi-Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, Stanford University, and collaborators present RoboNet, a large-scale, multi-robot dataset for learning generalizable vision-based manipulation skills. They show that pre-training on RoboNet enables models to achieve improved zero-shot generalization to novel viewpoints and backgrounds, and facilitates few-shot adaptation to new robot hardware, outperforming methods trained with up to 20 times more robot-specific data.

## Problem

- Robot learning typically requires vast amounts of diverse data for generalizable skills, but most experiments are small-scale, single-domain, and single-robot.
- Current methods, such as simulators or data-efficient algorithms, struggle to capture the full complexity of real-world environments or are insufficient for intricate details.
- A lack of shared, large-scale real-world datasets limits transferability of learned skills across different robotic platforms and research institutions.

## Method

- Developed RoboNet, an open-source dataset comprising 162,000 real-world interaction trajectories (15 million video frames) from 7 diverse robot platforms across 4 institutions.
- Employed self-supervised learning algorithms, specifically Visual Foresight (a video prediction model with MPC) and a supervised inverse model, to train on the diverse RoboNet data.
- Designed experiments to evaluate generalization to novel objects, scenes, viewpoints, grippers, and entirely new robot platforms, as well as few-shot adaptation capabilities.

## Results

- Models trained on diverse viewpoints within RoboNet showed improved zero-shot generalization to novel viewpoints (16.2 ± 2.9 cm final distance) compared to single-view models (23.2 ± 2.6 cm).
- Pre-training on RoboNet and fine-tuning with 300-400 trajectories achieved significantly higher success rates for new robots (e.g., Kuka: 40% vs. 10% from scratch; Franka: 40% vs. 20% from scratch).
- Larger visual foresight models (500M parameters) still showed underfitting, achieving an L1 per-pixel error of 0.0847 ± 0.045 on a held-out test set, suggesting further model capacity improvements are needed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, diverse real-world datasets like RoboNet can enable broad generalization in robotic learning, allowing models to transfer skills across varied hardware and environments.
> - Pre-training on RoboNet significantly reduces the data required for adapting to new robot platforms, outperforming robot-specific training with 4x-20x more data in some cases.
> - Current visual prediction models, even with hundreds of millions of parameters, exhibit underfitting on RoboNet, indicating a need for higher capacity models or more intelligent data selection strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning aims to develop robotic systems capable of operating in diverse, open-world environments by generalizing to new settings and tasks. A common challenge in this domain is the significant amount of data typically required for machine learning algorithms, coupled with the limited generalization capabilities of current methods to even minor environmental changes. Most existing robotic learning experiments are conducted on a small scale, within single domains, and using a single robot, which restricts the diversity and quantity of training data.

Previous attempts to address this data scarcity and generalization issue have included developing simulators for large-scale data collection or engineering more data-efficient robot learning algorithms. However, simulator development is a manual and resource-intensive process that has not yet scaled to capture the full breadth and complexity of real-world environments. Conversely, highly data-efficient algorithms often rely on simpler models, which may be insufficient for modeling intricate real-world details such as object geometry or appearance.

This paper positions itself as an alternative to these approaches by proposing the use of substantially larger and more varied datasets collected directly in the real world. It draws inspiration from the success of large-scale datasets in other machine learning fields, notably ImageNet in computer vision, which demonstrated that data diversity could drive significant advancements in generalization. While previous robotic manipulation datasets and methods have typically focused on single robot platforms, this work introduces a multi-robot, multi-domain dataset to facilitate broader generalization.

The work builds upon prior research in large-scale, self-supervised robot learning, including visual foresight and inverse models, but differentiates itself by explicitly focusing on enabling transfer across multiple robot platforms and environments. This focus aims to allow researchers to share data more effectively across institutions and reduce the need for each lab to collect data from scratch for every new experimental setup. It connects with concepts in multi-task learning, lifelong learning, and meta-learning, aiming to extract generalizable priors from a wide variety of domains, thereby allowing robotic systems to adapt rapidly to novel environments with minimal new data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a perceived tension in robot learning: the requirement for large quantities of diverse training data to enable generalizable robotic controllers, contrasting with the prevailing small-scale, single-domain, and single-robot nature of most experimental setups. The authors contend that the common practice of re-collecting data from scratch for every new environment or robot represents an inefficient re-learning of fundamental world knowledge.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Establish RoboNet as an Open Database:** The primary objective is to create and publicly release RoboNet, an open and extensible database for sharing robotic experience. This dataset is designed to provide a large, diverse, and real-world pool of interaction data to address the data scarcity problem in robotic learning.
2. **Investigate Multi-Robot, Multi-Domain Learning:** The research aims to study the viability of using such a large-scale, diverse dataset to learn generalizable models for vision-based robotic manipulation. This involves demonstrating that a single model can acquire skills that transfer across various robotic platforms and environmental conditions.
3. **Evaluate Generalization Capabilities:** A detailed objective is to experimentally evaluate how algorithms trained on RoboNet perform with respect to different forms of generalization. This includes assessing their ability to operate effectively with:
 - Novel objects
 - New tasks (specifically object relocation via pushing or grasping)
 - New scenes and backgrounds
 - New camera viewpoints
 - New end-effector grippers
 - Entirely new robot platforms not seen during pre-training.
4. **Assess Few-Shot Adaptation and Transfer Learning:** The authors aim to demonstrate that pre-training models on the diverse RoboNet dataset can enable rapid adaptation (few-shot learning) when introduced to new robots or environments, requiring significantly less target-domain data compared to training from scratch.
5. **Test Applicability Across Learning Algorithms:** The work seeks to show RoboNet's utility beyond a single algorithm by evaluating its effectiveness with two distinct self-supervised robot learning approaches: visual foresight (a model-based reinforcement learning method using video prediction) and supervised inverse models.

Ultimately, the motivation is to lay groundwork for a future where robotic learning systems can efficiently acquire broad manipulation capabilities by leveraging shared, real-world experience, moving beyond the limitations of isolated, robot-specific data collection efforts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers around the creation of the RoboNet dataset and its application to train and evaluate two different vision-based robotic manipulation algorithms under various generalization conditions.

#### 3.1. The RoboNet Dataset

RoboNet is conceived as an open database for sharing robotic experience, designed to be large-scale, diverse, and extensible.

- **Scale and Composition:** The initial release comprises approximately 162,000 trajectories, corresponding to nearly 15 million video frames. This data was collected from 7 distinct robot platforms: Sawyer, Franka, WidowX, Kuka, Baxter, Fetch (5k trajectories from), and a Google Robot (56k trajectories from). The collection spanned 4 different institutions, contributing to diversity in lab environments.
- **Diversity Factors:** The dataset incorporates a wide range of variations, including:
 - Different robot hardware (arms and grippers).
 - Varied arena setups, table inserts (10 types), and backgrounds.
 - Over a hundred camera configurations (113), leading to diverse viewpoints, lighting conditions, and color temperatures.
 - Hundreds of different objects for manipulation.
 - Multiple gripper configurations (10 types), some using 3D printed finger attachments.
- **Data Collection Process:**
 - **Self-Supervised and Autonomous:** Trajectories were collected autonomously with minimal human intervention. Operators programmed initial action distributions and periodically introduced new objects. No explicit reward signals or labels were used.
 - **Unified Action Space:** A consistent action space was adopted across most robots, defined as deltas in end-effector Cartesian position (XYZ), yaw rotation, and a binary gripper joint state (open/close). The robot's root link served as the frame of reference, obviating camera calibration. This end-effector space choice supports generalization across robots with varying degrees of freedom.
 - **Exploration Policy:** Actions were primarily sampled from simple hand-engineered diagonal Gaussian distributions. To increase instances of grasping, a primitive from prior work was incorporated, which biases gripper closing when the end-effector is at table height.
- **Data Management:** The dataset stores each trajectory as a separate entity with filterable attributes (e.g., robot type, gripper type, camera configuration). Data is stored in HDF5 format, with videos encoded as MP4. Code infrastructure for filtering and usage is provided, along with tools for contributing new data.

#### 3.2. Learning Algorithms

Two distinct algorithms were used to demonstrate RoboNet's utility:

- **Visual Foresight:** This is a deep model-based reinforcement learning approach that learns vision-based manipulation skills from random interaction.
 - **Video Prediction Model:** A core component is an action-conditioned video prediction model, a deterministic variant of the SAVP model, which predicts future image sequences (`Î_t+1:t+h`) given past images (`I_t`) and a sequence of actions (`a_t:t+h-1`). The model is a recurrent neural network with convolutional LSTMs, interleaving convolution layers and skip connections. It predicts future images and 2D flow fields (`F_t+1←t`) that transform pixels from one frame to the next.
 - **Planning via MPC:** The model enables image-based Model Predictive Control (MPC). Tasks are specified by designating pixels (e.g., on an object) and a goal position for them. The robot plans actions by optimizing over action sequences to minimize the expected distance between the predicted designated pixel position and the goal. The Model-Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) algorithm is used for sampling-based planning, with a prediction horizon of 15 steps. The first action of the optimal sequence is executed, and the process is repeated.
- **Inverse Models:** A simplified one-step inverse model, adapted from, was used to evaluate RoboNet's applicability to different control paradigms.
 - **Training:** The model is trained with supervised regression to predict the action (`a_t`) required to transition from a current image (`I_t`) to a subsequent image (`I_t+1`). Inputs include two context frame/action pairs, the current image, and a goal image.
 - **Control:** At test time, the model takes current and goal images (and context) to predict an action, operating in a closed-loop fashion for goal-image reaching tasks.

#### 3.3. Experimental Design

The research involved a series of experiments designed to address specific questions regarding generalization and data utility:

- **Evaluation Protocol:** Object relocation tasks (pushing or grasping to a specified location) were used as benchmarks. Performance was measured by the final distance between the achieved object position and the goal, and human-judged success rates.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:**
 - **Viewpoints:** Compared a model trained on 90 diverse viewpoints against a single-view model on object relocation tasks from both seen and novel camera viewpoints using a Sawyer robot.
 - **Backgrounds:** Evaluated the multi-view model on tasks in an environment with substantially different backgrounds from its training set.
- **Few-Shot Adaptation to New Hardware:**
 - **Robots:** Models were pre-trained on RoboNet (excluding data from the target robot), then fine-tuned with a small number of new trajectories (300-400) collected from unseen Kuka, Franka, and Baxter robots. Performance was compared against training from scratch on the small dataset and against training on larger robot-specific datasets.
 - **Grippers:** Evaluated adaptation to an unseen Robotiq 2-finger gripper on a Sawyer robot, comparing zero-shot generalization, training from scratch, pre-training on Sawyer-only data, and pre-training on all of RoboNet.
- **Model Capacity Analysis:** To investigate observed underfitting, two visual foresight models of different parameter sizes (200M and 500M) were trained on Sawyer data, and their L1 per-pixel error on a held-out test set was compared.
- **Inverse Model Evaluation:** A subset of RoboNet (Sawyer and Franka data) was used to train an inverse model, which was then evaluated on simple reaching tasks across both robots and novel viewpoints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several findings regarding the utility of RoboNet for multi-robot learning and generalization:

#### 4.1. RoboNet Dataset Characteristics

The paper successfully introduced RoboNet, an initial pool of 15 million video frames from 7 different robot platforms across 4 institutions. This dataset exhibited significant diversity in objects, viewpoints, environments, and robot hardware.

#### 4.2. Visual Foresight: Zero-Shot Generalization

- **Viewpoint Generalization:** Models trained on diverse camera viewpoints within RoboNet demonstrated improved generalization to novel viewpoints compared to models trained on a single viewpoint. The multi-view model achieved an average final distance to the goal of 9 ± 2.2 cm on seen views and 16.2 ± 2.9 cm on held-out views, whereas the single-view model performed worse (14.8 ± 3.8 cm on seen, 23.2 ± 2.6 cm on held-out).
- **Background Generalization:** The multi-view model also exhibited the ability to generalize to new backgrounds not seen during training, achieving an average final distance of 14.4 ± 2 cm on object relocation tasks. This performance was comparable to its generalization to novel viewpoints, suggesting robustness to environmental changes.

#### 4.3. Visual Foresight: Few-Shot Adaptation to New Robots and Grippers

Pre-training on RoboNet, followed by fine-tuning with a modest amount of target-domain data, consistently improved performance on unseen robot platforms.

- **Kuka Robot Adaptation:** Pre-training on RoboNet (excluding Kuka, R3, and Fetch data) and fine-tuning with only 400 trajectories from the Kuka robot resulted in a 40% success rate. This substantially outperformed training from scratch with 400 trajectories (10% success rate) and even training from scratch with a larger dataset of 1800 trajectories (30% success rate).
- **Franka Robot Adaptation:** Similar to the Kuka, pre-training on RoboNet (excluding Franka, R3, and Fetch data) and fine-tuning with 400 Franka trajectories achieved a 40% success rate. This was higher than training from scratch with 400 trajectories (20%) and also surpassed training from scratch with 8000 trajectories (35%). These results indicate that RoboNet pre-training can outperform robot-specific training with 4x-20x more data in certain conditions.
- **Baxter Robot Adaptation:** For the Baxter robot, pre-training on the *Sawyer-only* subset of RoboNet and fine-tuning with 300 Baxter trajectories achieved the highest success rate (83%). Pre-training on the *entire* RoboNet and fine-tuning yielded a lower success rate (58%), while training from scratch was 33%. This suggested that pre-training on a subset of RoboNet that is visually or dynamically more similar to the target robot can sometimes be more effective than training on the full, more diverse dataset.
- **Unseen Gripper Adaptation (Robotiq on Sawyer):** When adapting to a Robotiq gripper on a Sawyer arm, pre-training on only the Sawyer data from RoboNet and fine-tuning with 300 new trajectories resulted in the best performance (9.8 ± 2.1 cm average distance to goal). This was superior to pre-training on the entire RoboNet (14.7 ± 2.1 cm), training without pre-training (17 ± 1.8 cm), and zero-shot generalization (15.5 ± 2.6 cm). These results reinforced the observation from the Baxter experiment that in some cases, increased diversity during pre-training might negatively impact performance, potentially due to model underfitting.

#### 4.4. Visual Foresight: Model Capacity Experiments

The experiments revealed that the visual foresight models, even those with significant parameter counts, exhibited signs of underfitting on the RoboNet dataset.

- **Underfitting Evidence:** Both training and validation errors plateaued before achieving very high performance on training sequences, and inaccurate predictions during testing were a common cause of poor robot performance.
- **Impact of Model Size:** A larger visual foresight model with 500 million parameters achieved a lower `L1` per-pixel error (0.0847 ± 0.045) on a held-out Sawyer test set compared to a 200 million parameter model (0.104 ± 0.057). This confirmed that even larger models than those used in control experiments (5M-75M parameters) were underfitting, indicating a need for higher capacity models in future work.

#### 4.5. Inverse Model: Multi-Robot and Multi-Viewpoint Reaching

The inverse model, trained on a subset of RoboNet (Sawyer and Franka data), demonstrated its applicability.

- **Generalization:** It successfully performed simple visual reaching tasks on both the Sawyer and Franka robots, including from a novel viewpoint not seen during training (Sawyer: 4/5 success for front view, 5/5 for unseen view; Franka: 4/5 success for front view).
- **Limitations:** Due to its greedy action selection, the inverse model tended to perform poorly on more complex object manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboNet project represents a significant step towards enabling large-scale, data-driven robot learning and addresses fundamental challenges in robot generalization.

- **Enabling Broad Generalization:** By providing a diverse, large-scale dataset of real-world robotic interactions, RoboNet demonstrates that learning systems can acquire capabilities that generalize across a wide array of experimental conditions, including novel objects, scenes, viewpoints, grippers, and entirely different robot platforms. This moves beyond the limitations of single-robot, single-domain learning, offering a pathway for robots to operate in unstructured, open-world environments.
- **Reducing Data Requirements for New Deployments:** The finding that pre-training on RoboNet significantly improves few-shot adaptation to new robots is impactful. It suggests that pre-trained models can outperform robot-specific models trained with 4x-20x more data, substantially reducing the amount of data and time required to deploy and adapt new robotic hardware or skills in new environments. This efficiency gain could accelerate the adoption of learning-based robotics.
- **Fostering Collaborative Research and Data Sharing:** RoboNet's design as an open and extensible database, with infrastructure for contributions, aims to foster a collaborative research ecosystem. By enabling researchers to share and build upon a common pool of robotic experience, it could collectively accelerate progress in the field, much like ImageNet did for computer vision.
- **Identifying Critical Research Avenues:** The observed underfitting of current visual prediction models on RoboNet highlights a crucial area for future research. It indicates a need for:
 - **Higher Capacity Models:** Developing more flexible and powerful deep learning architectures capable of effectively leveraging the vast and diverse data available in RoboNet.
 - **Intelligent Data Selection:** Algorithms that can automatically identify and select the most relevant subsets of the dataset for specific pre-training or adaptation tasks, potentially optimizing performance where overall diversity currently presents a challenge.
- **Guidance for Future Data Collection:** The results suggest that prioritizing *variety* (small amounts of data from many different domains) might be more important than sheer *quantity* from limited domains for future data collection efforts. This offers strategic guidance for community-wide data gathering initiatives.
- **Advancing Robotic Capabilities:** While current tasks are relatively simple, the work lays a foundation for future robots to learn more complex and nuanced interactions, such as tool use, rope manipulation, and tasks requiring higher fidelity. Improving video prediction quality and incorporating more sophisticated data collection policies (e.g., demonstrations, active exploration) are identified as key directions for achieving these advanced capabilities.

In the long term, RoboNet is positioned as a catalyst for iteratively strengthening robotic datasets and algorithms, ultimately aiming for agents that achieve greater levels of generalization across tasks, environments, and hardware setups.
