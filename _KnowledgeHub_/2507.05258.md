# Spatio-Temporal LLM: Reasoning about Environments and Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign developed the Reasoning about Environments and Actions (REA) dataset and two Spatio-Temporal LLM (STLLM) baselines to enable large language models to jointly understand 3D environments and egocentric video actions. The STLLM models achieved up to 46.50% overall accuracy on REA, outperforming existing multimodal LLMs on complex spatio-temporal reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle with jointly understanding 3D environments (point clouds) and dynamic egocentric video actions.
- Existing MLLMs often simplify videos as sequences of independent frames or neglect deeper temporal dynamics, failing to integrate global spatial context with local actions.
- A comprehensive benchmark dataset specifically designed for evaluating this joint spatio-temporal reasoning capability was lacking.

## Method

- A novel dataset, ==REA (Reasoning about Environments and Actions)==, was created with 24,371 question-answer pairs requiring joint understanding of 3D point clouds and egocentric video clips, derived from EPIC-KITCHENS, VISOR, and EPIC-FIELDS.
- Two baseline Spatio-Temporal LLM (STLLM) architectures were proposed: ==STLLM-3D==, which directly concatenates 3D features with image and text embeddings, and ==STLLM-Aligner==, which uses a cross-modal alignment module to create compact spatial representations.
- The REA dataset includes five distinct tasks: Relative Direction, Relative Distance, Find My Item, Furniture Affordance Prediction, and Action Planning, to probe various aspects of spatio-temporal reasoning.

## Results

- Existing MLLMs achieved low overall LLM-Judge accuracies on the REA dataset, ranging from **23.85%** to **31.46%** (ChatGPT-4o judge) and **35.74%** to **39.50%** (Gemini 2.0 Flash judge).
- The proposed STLLM baselines significantly outperformed finetuned existing models on REA, with STLLM-Aligner achieving the highest overall accuracy of **41.89%** (ChatGPT-4o) and **46.50%** (Gemini 2.0 Flash).
- In zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on SQA3D, STLLM baselines and models finetuned on REA showed consistent improvements over vanilla counterparts, demonstrating generalization capabilities for 3D reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-the-shelf multimodal LLMs demonstrate limited capabilities in spatio-temporal reasoning, confirming that current models struggle to effectively integrate 3D environmental context with egocentric actions.
> - Specialized architectural designs that explicitly integrate 3D information are crucial for improving spatio-temporal understanding in MLLMs, as evidenced by the superior performance of STLLM baselines.
> - The REA dataset effectively serves as a benchmark for training and evaluating models on complex spatio-temporal prompts, with finetuning on REA showing transferability to other 3D reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, enabling models to process and reason across various data modalities, predominantly text and images. However, this paper identifies a significant limitation: the struggle of current MLLMs with "spatio-temporal" prompts that demand a holistic understanding of a 3D environment (represented by a point cloud) concurrently with dynamic actions occurring within that environment (captured by an egocentric video clip).

The authors contextualize their work by reviewing existing advancements:
- **Image LLMs:** These models (e.g., OpenAI's ChatGPT-4o, Meta's Llama 3.2, Anthropic's Claude 3.5 Sonnet) have shown prowess in image-text tasks, often employing image encoders, connector modules, and language decoders. Many are primarily trained on static image-text data.
- **Video LLMs:** Building on image LLM architectures, these models extend to video data by encoding video frames. Recent efforts focus on long-form videos, real-time efficiency, and memory for long-term grounding, but often treat videos as sequences of independent frames, neglecting deeper temporal dynamics.
- **3D LLMs:** These works inject 3D information into LLMs, typically by extracting 2D features and projecting them into 3D representations, or by using 3D positional embeddings. However, they are often limited by dataset availability and still struggle with the full complexity of 3D spatial reasoning.
- **Image+3D LLMs and Video+3D LLMs:** More advanced models integrate images (especially egocentric) or videos with 3D scene representations (like point clouds). Notable efforts by Man et al. (2024), Huang et al. (2024), and Ma et al. (2023) introduce 3D situational awareness. However, the paper points out that existing Video+3D LLMs often simplify videos as sets of multi-view images, largely ignoring temporal dynamics and requiring additional information like per-frame depth maps, which limits their applicability to real-world, dynamic scenarios.

The critical gap highlighted by this paper is the lack of models capable of *jointly* reasoning about an unfolding event from an egocentric (action-focused) perspective while simultaneously grounding it within an allocentric (global 3D environment) context. This holistic spatio-temporal understanding is vital for embodied AI agents, robots, and applications requiring advanced situational awareness, such as interpreting human actions in a kitchen setting and predicting next steps. The authors argue that current MLLMs see the world "one frame at a time" and are "blind to the surrounding space" in a dynamically integrated manner, which their proposed work aims to bridge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **equip MLLMs with robust joint spatial and temporal understanding capabilities** to address complex "spatio-temporal" prompts. This involves enabling models to:
1. **Understand global 3D environments:** Interpret the overarching layout and objects within a scene, typically encoded in a point cloud.
2. **Understand local temporal actions:** Comprehend dynamic, egocentric human actions within a specific part of that environment, represented by a short video clip.
3. **Integrate both streams of information:** Reason about how local actions relate to the global environment and predict future interactions or navigational paths based on this combined understanding.

The motivation stems from the identified limitations of current MLLMs, which struggle to effectively combine these two crucial modalities. Such holistic spatio-temporal reasoning is indispensable for **embodied AI agents** that need to operate in the real world. For instance, a robot must not only recognize an action (e.g., "opening a drawer") but also contextualize it within its surroundings (e.g., "the drawer to the left of the sink") and plan subsequent actions (e.g., "move to the oven which is now closer"). The lack of a comprehensive benchmark dataset for this specific challenge further motivates the creation of the REA dataset.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors address their objectives through a two-pronged approach: developing a large-scale dataset for spatio-temporal reasoning and designing baseline spatio-temporal LLM (STLLM) architectures.

#### 3.1. The Reasoning about Environments and Actions (REA) Dataset

To facilitate research into joint spatio-temporal reasoning, the authors developed a novel dataset called "Reasoning about Environments and Actions" (REA). This dataset comprises question-answer (QA) pairs that necessitate understanding both global scene context from a 3D point cloud and localized temporal cues from an egocentric video. REA is built upon existing benchmarks:
- **EPIC-KITCHENS (Damen et al., 2018):** Provides dense action annotations for egocentric videos.
- **VISOR (Darkhalil et al., 2022):** Offers object segmentation annotations.
- **EPIC-FIELDS (Tschernezki et 2023):** Supplies sparse point clouds for the environments.

##### 3.1.1. REA Task Breakdown

The REA dataset includes five distinct tasks, each probing different aspects of spatio-temporal reasoning:
1. **Relative Direction:** Assesses changes in an object's relative direction to the person across a series of actions (e.g., "Is the microwave to the left of the person when performing 'put down knives' and also to the left when performing 'pick up plate'?").
2. **Relative Distance:** Evaluates changes in a person's proximity to objects over time (e.g., "Does the person move closer to the hob between 'turn off rice cooker' and 'serve curry'?").
3. **Find My Item:** Requires localizing an object and inferring spatial steps to reach it, integrating scene understanding and movement planning (e.g., "Where is the tarragon, and how can the person get to it?").
4. **Furniture Affordance Prediction:** Predicts which static furniture object the person will interact with next based on video observations and spatial cues (e.g., "Which object will the person most likely interact with next: A. hob, B. oven, C. fridge?").
5. **Action Planning:** Anticipates the next action in a task sequence and provides navigation instructions to reach the location for that action (e.g., "What should I do next, and how can I get to the place where the next step takes place?").

##### 3.1.2. Data Collection Pipeline

The data collection pipeline involves six steps (illustrated in Fig. 3):
1. **Query Video Sampling:** Short egocentric video clips (20-40 seconds) are sampled from EPIC-KITCHENS. Actions within these clips are segmented into intervals (3-5 seconds), chosen based on task-specific criteria (e.g., non-consecutive actions for relative direction/distance, next action for affordance prediction).
2. **3D Position Estimation:** The 3D locations of the person (estimated via mean camera pose from EPIC-FIELDS) and query objects (projected 2D segmentation masks from VISOR onto COLMAP point clouds) are computed.
3. **Spatial Relationship Estimation:** Ground-truth spatial relationships (e.g., left/right/front/back for direction, distance change for proximity) between the person and stationary objects are calculated using their estimated 3D poses.
4. **Navigation Movement Estimation:** For action planning, displacement in world coordinates and relative direction to the destination are estimated, refined by a VideoLLM (Zhang et al., 2024) to account for complex navigation.
5. **Point Cloud Reconstruction:** Dense 3D models are created by recomputing point clouds from selected frames (filtered to exclude hands) using pose-free reconstruction methods (e.g., VGGT by Wang et al., 2025). This addresses the sparseness of EPIC-FIELDS.
6. **Frame to Point Cloud Registration:** Dense camera poses for each video frame are registered to the reconstructed point cloud using Reloc3r (Dong et al., 2025), enabling high-fidelity scene grounding.

The dataset contains 24,371 training samples and 1,757 validation samples, showcasing a rich diversity of kitchen activities and objects, with strong long-tail distributions. Manual quality control was performed to ensure reliability of both QA pairs and point cloud representations.

#### 3.2. Spatio-Temporal LLM (STLLM) Architectures

The authors propose two baseline STLLM architectures, both built upon LLaVA-Video-Qwen2 (Zhang et al., 2024), extending it to integrate 3D spatial information. They share a vision encoder (SigLip) and a point cloud encoder (masked transformer decoder T_pcd applied to downsampled point clouds).

##### 3.2.1. STLLM-3D

This baseline directly integrates 3D information. After extracting point-wise features from the point cloud, Farthest Point Sampling and grouping are used to form compact spatial features. These features are then projected into the language embedding space via an MLP and **concatenated** directly with the image and text embeddings, which are subsequently fed into the LLM decoder. STLLM-3D is straightforward and computationally lightweight for direct integration but struggles with scalability for large scenes due to increased token embeddings.

##### 3.2.2. STLLM-Aligner

STLLM-Aligner employs a **cross-modal alignment module** to create a compact spatial representation. Learnable queries attend to image and text features, and spatial context from the point cloud is incorporated through cross-attention. The resulting compact spatial queries, along with image and text embeddings, are then passed to the LLM decoder. This design prioritizes efficiency and a compact representation but trades off some interpretability of the token compression. The authors also explore a variant with high-frequency positional encoding in the alignment module to provide explicit geometric cues.

Both baselines aim to capture global scene context while grounding fine-grained temporal dynamics for spatio-temporal reasoning, showcasing different trade-offs in integrating 3D spatial information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that spatio-temporal reasoning remains a significant challenge for existing MLLMs and that the proposed STLLM baselines offer superior performance, validated on the REA dataset and through cross-dataset evaluation.

#### 4.1. REA Dataset Statistics and Evaluation Metrics

The REA dataset consists of 24,371 training samples and 1,757 validation samples, featuring 299 unique objects and 4,759 unique actions in training, derived from EPIC-KITCHENS.
Evaluation metrics include:
- **Standard QA Metrics:** Sentence Similarity (SenSim), CIDEr, BLEU-4, METEOR, and ROUGE-L.
- **LLM Judges:** ChatGPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash are used to assign discrete correctness labels ("Correct"/"Wrong") based on reasoning validity, rather than exact text matching. The LLM judges were refined to ensure high agreement with human judgments.

#### 4.2. Performance on REA Dataset

- **Existing MLLMs struggle:** Off-the-shelf MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2, LLaVA-OV-Qwen2-7B, Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, VideoLLaMa3) achieve low overall LLM-Judge accuracies (ranging from 23.85% to 31.46% with ChatGPT-4o judge and 35.74% to 39.50% with Gemini 2.0 Flash judge). This confirms the fundamental challenge of spatio-temporal prompts for current models.
- **Finetuning improves performance:** Finetuning existing models on the REA dataset yields noticeable gains (e.g., LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2 improves to 36.99%/42.92% C/G overall accuracy).
- **STLLM baselines outperform:** The proposed STLLM baselines significantly outperform finetuned existing models. STLLM-Aligner achieves 41.89%/46.50% overall accuracy (C/G), and STLLM-3D achieves 40.94%/46.39% (C/G). This highlights the effectiveness of specialized spatio-temporal LLM architectures.
- **Impact of Positional Encoding and Query Number:** While a larger number of spatial queries (1024 Nquery vs. 32 Nquery) generally improves performance, the additional positional encoding in STLLM-Aligner shows only limited benefits on REA, suggesting that REA's emphasis on QA might not fully leverage explicit 3D grounding cues. Further adaptation of LLM layers with LoRA significantly boosts performance, indicating the importance of trainable parameters.
- **Task Difficulty:** Tasks like "Find My Item" and "Action Planning" were generally more challenging, requiring open-ended answers and complex spatio-temporal reasoning. STLLM baselines showed well-balanced results across tasks.
- **Judge Consistency:** ChatGPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash judges exhibited consistent relative rankings of models, despite some differences in absolute scores for open-ended tasks, affirming the robustness of the evaluation.

#### 4.3. Cross-Dataset Evaluation on SQA3D

In zero-shot evaluation on SQA3D (Ma et al., 2023), a benchmark for situated QA in 3D scenes, STLLM baselines consistently outperformed existing models. Models finetuned on REA also showed clear improvements over their vanilla counterparts, demonstrating the **transferability and generalization capabilities** of the REA dataset for enhancing 3D reasoning tasks in MLLMs. For example, finetuned LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen improved its average accuracy from 43-45% to 52-53%.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Insights

Qualitative examples (Fig. 5, Appendix F) illustrate STLLM models' ability to correctly predict future interactions (e.g., "The person is preparing to interact with the oven, as they are moving closer to it"), demonstrating effective reasoning about temporal progression and spatial intention. In contrast, existing MLLMs often provided responses grounded only in the immediate visual context (e.g., "preparing to interact with the sink"), failing to exhibit forward-looking spatio-temporal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to achieve comprehensive spatio-temporal understanding, a capability crucial for bridging the gap between current AI and intelligent agents operating autonomously in complex, dynamic real-world environments.

The key contributions and potential impacts are:
- **Novel Benchmark Dataset (REA):** The creation of the "Reasoning about Environments and Actions" (REA) dataset is a major contribution. It provides a much-needed, large-scale, and challenging benchmark specifically designed to test the joint spatio-temporal reasoning abilities of MLLMs. Its foundation on existing, high-quality egocentric vision datasets (EPIC-KITCHENS, VISOR, EPIC-FIELDS) ensures realistic and diverse scenarios. This dataset will likely become a standard for evaluating progress in embodied AI and spatio-temporal understanding.
- **Architectural Innovations for STLLMs:** The development and evaluation of STLLM-3D and STLLM-Aligner baselines provide initial, effective architectures for integrating global 3D scene context with local egocentric video actions. These models demonstrate that explicit architectural designs are beneficial for this challenging task, outperforming general-purpose MLLMs. Their comparative study also highlights trade-offs between direct feature fusion and compact cross-modal alignment, guiding future architectural research.
- **Advancing Embodied AI:** The ability to reason about actions within a grounded 3D environment is fundamental for embodied AI. Robots and virtual agents require this capability for tasks such as navigation, manipulation, human-robot interaction, and complex task execution. This work directly contributes to developing agents that can "understand" their surroundings and ongoing activities, moving beyond mere perception to true situational awareness.
- **Enhanced Situational Awareness:** Beyond robotics, robust spatio-temporal reasoning can significantly improve situational awareness in various applications, from smart homes to augmented reality, by allowing systems to interpret human intent and anticipate future actions based on real-time observations and environmental context.
- **Improved Multimodal Understanding:** The findings push the boundaries of MLLMs, demonstrating the necessity of moving beyond 2D static images or simple video frame sequences to richer, dynamically integrated 3D representations.
