# Analytic Energy-Guided Policy Optimization for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Analytic Energy-guided Policy Optimization (AEPO) introduces a novel Offline RL algorithm that leverages an analytically derived intermediate energy function to guide diffusion models, providing accurate guidance signals. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance across various D4RL benchmark tasks, including Gym-MuJoCo, maze2d, and antmaze, outperforming numerous existing baselines.

## Problem

- Learning effective policies from static datasets in Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) is challenged by policy distribution shifts, extrapolation errors in value estimation, and limited data coverage.
- Existing diffusion-based Offline RL methods often rely on approximate or manually defined guidance mechanisms, which can lead to suboptimal policy optimization.
- The inherent intractability of computing intermediate energy functions at arbitrary diffusion steps prevents precise guidance in energy-based diffusion models for RL.

## Method

- **AEPO** introduces an analytically derived intermediate ==energy function== that provides a principled theoretical foundation for guiding diffusion models in Offline RL.
- The approach derives a closed-form solution for the intermediate energy function by leveraging properties of conditional Gaussian transformations within the diffusion process.
- The algorithm combines a diffusion model to learn action distributions, a Q-function for value estimation, and the analytically derived energy function to steer the diffusion process towards high-value actions.

## Results

- AEPO achieves superior performance across the D4RL Gym-MuJoCo benchmark, surpassing numerous representative baselines.
- The algorithm matches or surpasses state-of-the-art algorithms on maze2d and antmaze tasks.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that the analytic approximation provides more accurate guidance than previous methods and that guidance rescaling significantly improves performance, especially at higher guidance strengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An analytically derived intermediate energy function can effectively guide diffusion models in Offline RL, overcoming the intractability of direct log-expectation computation.
> - The derived energy function, which includes terms based on the Q-value and its gradient, provides a theoretically sound and accurate guidance signal for action generation.
> - Proper scaling of the guidance signal significantly improves performance and stability in energy-guided diffusion processes for Offline RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL) has emerged as a promising approach for learning policies from static datasets without further environment interaction. This capability is crucial for real-world applications where online exploration might be costly, dangerous, or impractical. However, effectively learning from fixed datasets presents unique challenges, particularly in terms of policy distribution shift and the need for accurate value estimation.

Recent advances in diffusion models have shown promising results in Offline RL by leveraging their ability to model complex, multi-modal action distributions. These approaches typically use some form of guidance mechanism to steer the diffusion process toward generating high-quality actions.

This paper introduces Analytic Energy-guided Policy Optimization (AEPO), a novel Offline RL algorithm that leverages an analytically derived intermediate energy function to guide diffusion models more effectively. Unlike previous approaches that rely on approximations or manually defined guidance signals, AEPO provides a principled theoretical foundation for energy-based guidance in diffusion models for RL.

### 2. Background

#### 2.1. Offline Reinforcement Learning

In the standard RL framework, an agent interacts with an environment, represented as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by states, actions, transition probabilities, rewards, and a discount factor. The objective is to learn a policy that maximizes the expected cumulative discounted reward.

Offline RL constrains the learning process to use only a static dataset of previously collected experiences, without further environment interaction. This constraint introduces challenges such as:

1. Distribution shift between the behavior policy that generated the dataset and the learned policy
2. Extrapolation errors in value function estimation
3. Limited coverage of the state-action space

#### 2.2. Diffusion Models in RL

Diffusion models have recently been applied to RL due to their ability to model complex distributions. These models work by gradually adding noise to data and then learning to reverse this process. In the context of RL, this can be formalized as:

- Forward process: $q(a_t|a_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(a_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}a_{t-1}, \beta_t\mathbf{I})$
- Reverse process: $p_\theta(a_{t-1}|a_t, s) = \mathcal{N}(a_{t-1}; \mu_\theta(a_t, t, s), \Sigma_\theta(a_t, t))$

where $a_t$ represents the action at diffusion step $t$, $\beta_t$ is the noise schedule, and $s$ is the state.

### 3. The AEPO Algorithm

AEPO addresses a key limitation in existing diffusion-based Offline RL methods: the guidance mechanism. Previous approaches typically use classifier-guided or classifier-free guidance, which often involve approximations or manually defined intermediate guidance signals.

The key innovation of AEPO is its analytically derived intermediate energy function that guides the diffusion process. This approach provides several advantages:

1. A theoretically sound basis for energy-based guidance
2. More accurate intermediate guidance signals
3. Improved policy optimization performance

At a high level, AEPO consists of three main components:

1. A diffusion model that learns the action distribution from the offline dataset
2. A Q-function that estimates the value of state-action pairs
3. An analytically derived intermediate energy function that guides the diffusion process toward high-value actions

### 4. Theoretical Framework

AEPO establishes a theoretical connection between energy-guided diffusion models and constrained RL problems. The energy function in diffusion models can be viewed as imposing constraints on the policy optimization process.

The energy function at the final step is defined as:

$$E(a_0, s) = -\omega Q(s, a_0)$$

where $\omega$ is a hyperparameter controlling the guidance strength.

However, the key challenge is computing the intermediate energy function $E(a_t, s)$ at arbitrary diffusion steps $t$. This function is defined implicitly as:

$$E(a_t, s) = -\log \mathbb{E}_{q(a_0|a_t, s)}[\exp(-\omega Q(s, a_0))]$$

This log-expectation form is generally intractable and cannot be computed directly. Previous methods resort to various approximations, potentially leading to suboptimal guidance.

### 5. Analytic Approximation of Intermediate Energy

AEPO's key contribution is an analytical approximation of the intermediate energy function. The authors derive a closed-form solution when the diffusion model obeys conditional Gaussian transformations.

Starting with the posterior distribution $q(a_0|a_t, s)$ under the diffusion process, they analyze its Gaussian properties and derive:

$$E(a_t, s) \approx -\log \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{N}(a_0; \mu_t, \sigma_t^2 \mathbf{I})}[\exp(-\omega Q(s, a_0))]$$

Using Taylor expansion to convert the implicit dependence on action within the exponential term to an explicit dependence:

$$\exp(-\omega Q(s, a_0)) \approx \exp(-\omega Q(s, \mu_t)) \cdot \exp(-\omega \nabla_{a_0} Q(s, \mu_t)^T (a_0 - \mu_t))$$

By leveraging the moment-generating function of the Gaussian distribution, they obtain a simplified expression:

$$E(a_t, s) \approx \omega Q(s, \mu_t) - \frac{\omega^2 \sigma_t^2}{2} \|\nabla_{a_0} Q(s, \mu_t)\|^2_2$$

This analytically derived energy function provides accurate guidance during the diffusion process without requiring additional approximations or manual definitions.

### 6. Neural Network Implementation

AEPO implements its algorithm using three neural networks:

1. **Diffusion Model**: A conditional denoising model that learns to predict the noise added during the forward process.

2. **Q-function**: A neural network that estimates the value of state-action pairs.

3. **Intermediate Energy Network**: A neural network trained to approximate the derived log-expectation formulation.

The training process involves:

1. Training the diffusion model on the offline dataset to learn the behavior policy.
2. Training the Q-function using standard Offline RL techniques.
3. Training the intermediate energy network to approximate the analytically derived energy function.

During inference, the diffusion process is guided by the energy function to generate actions from a high-return action distribution:

# Pseudocode for guided sampling in AEPO
def sample_action(state, omega):
    # Initialize from Gaussian noise
    a_T = sample_gaussian_noise()

    # Reverse diffusion process with guidance
    for t in range(T, 0, -1):
        # Predict noise
        noise = diffusion_model(a_t, t, state)

        # Energy guidance
        energy_grad = grad(energy_net(a_t, t, state), a_t)

        # Apply guidance
        a_t_minus_1 = apply_posterior_with_guidance(a_t, noise, energy_grad, omega, t)

    return a_0  # Final denoised action

### 7. Experimental Results

AEPO was evaluated on the D4RL benchmark suite, which includes various continuous control tasks with different dataset qualities (expert, medium, medium-replay). The algorithm demonstrates impressive performance across different environments and dataset types.

Key results include:

1. **Superior Performance**: AEPO surpasses numerous representative baselines in the Gym-MuJoCo benchmark and matches or surpasses state-of-the-art algorithms in maze2d and antmaze tasks.

2. **Effective Guidance**: The experiments demonstrate that the analytic approximation provides more accurate guidance than previous methods, leading to better policy optimization.

3. **Environment-Specific Results**:
 - In HalfCheetah, AEPO shows stable performance across different guidance strengths (Figure 3).
 - In Hopper, AEPO demonstrates robust performance even with strong guidance (Figure 4).
 - In Walker2d, AEPO maintains consistent performance across various guidance parameters (Figure 5).

### 8. Robustness Analysis

The authors conducted extensive ablation studies to analyze the robustness of AEPO:

1. **Guidance Rescaling**: The experiments show that rescaling the guidance signal significantly improves performance, especially at higher guidance strengths. This suggests that controlling the magnitude of the guidance is crucial for stable learning.

2. **Parameter Sensitivity**: AEPO's performance is evaluated across different hyperparameter configurations, demonstrating robustness to parameter choices.

3. **Posterior Approximations**: The authors investigated different posterior approximations, finding that their initial formulation (Posterior 1) outperformed alternative formulations (Posterior 2), likely due to differences in variance calculation.

### 9. Conclusion

AEPO represents a significant advancement in diffusion-based Offline RL by providing a principled, theoretically sound approach to energy-guided policy optimization. The key innovation lies in the analytic approximation of the intermediate energy function, which provides more accurate guidance during the diffusion process.

The empirical results demonstrate that AEPO achieves state-of-the-art performance across various D4RL benchmark tasks, outperforming previous methods in most environments. Furthermore, the algorithm shows robustness to hyperparameter choices and guidance strength, making it a practical choice for real-world applications.

Future directions for this research include extending AEPO to more complex environments, exploring alternative energy formulations, and investigating the theoretical connections between energy-guided diffusion and other RL paradigms. The developed techniques may also have broader applications beyond RL, potentially benefiting other areas where controllable generation with diffusion models is valuable.
