# Test-Time Gradient Guidance of Flow Policies in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and Physical Intelligence introduce QGF (Q-Guided Flow), a reinforcement learning algorithm that achieves policy optimization entirely at test time. This method guides a pre-trained flow policy using gradients from a learned Q-function, demonstrating performance competitive with or superior to state-of-the-art training-time RL methods on continuous control tasks, while improving computational efficiency compared to Best-of-N sampling.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) training, especially with expressive generative policies like flow models, suffers from instability, hyperparameter sensitivity, and high computational cost (e.g., backpropagation through time).
- Existing test-time policy improvement methods for RL, such as Best-of-N (BFN) sampling, are computationally prohibitive for high-dimensional actions, and naive critic gradient guidance struggles with unreliable gradients from out-of-distribution intermediate actions.
- Integrating expressive generative models into RL pipelines often requires specialized training objectives that undermine their scalability and stability benefits seen in supervised learning.

## Method

- **QGF** pre-trains a ==reference flow policy== using behavioral cloning (supervised learning) and a Q-function critic using Implicit Q-learning (IQL), decoupling policy acquisition from reward optimization.
- During test-time inference, QGF guides the flow policy's iterative denoising process by applying gradients from the Q-function, but crucially, it computes these gradients on a first-order approximation of the *fully denoised action*, avoiding out-of-distribution critic queries and expensive backpropagation.
- The method empirically omits the Jacobian term from the gradient estimation, which is shown to yield lower variance and better performance compared to its inclusion.

## Results

- QGF consistently outperforms existing gradient-based test-time guidance algorithms (e.g., QFQL, BPTT) and is competitive with, or superior to, state-of-the-art training-time RL algorithms (e.g., FQL, QAM) across diverse OGBench environments.
- QGF demonstrates significantly higher computational efficiency than Best-of-N (BFN) sampling, outperforming BFN with N=4 samples while using substantially less compute, and combined with BFN, achieves performance of BFN N=16 at reduced cost.
- The algorithm scales effectively to harder, long-horizon goal-conditioned tasks and benefits substantially from larger model architectures, showing nearly **4x** performance improvement when scaling from 800k to **3.2M** parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy optimization can be effectively and stably performed entirely at test time by leveraging a pre-trained generative policy and a critic, sidestepping the instabilities of traditional actor-critic training.
> - Accurate and stable gradient estimation for guiding generative models requires querying the critic on actions that are within its training distribution, which QGF achieves by approximating a fully denoised action rather than using noisy intermediate steps.
> - Simplified gradient estimators, specifically a first-order approximation for the denoised action and omitting the Jacobian, can lead to lower variance and improved performance in practice for generative policy guidance.
