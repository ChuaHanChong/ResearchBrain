# TwiFF (Think With Future Frames): A Large-Scale Dataset for Dynamic Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A collaborative research effort introduces TwiFF (Think With Future Frames), a large-scale dataset (TwiFF-2.7M) and a corresponding model for dynamic visual reasoning, enabling multimodal AI to understand and predict temporal progression in visual tasks. The framework jointly leverages video generation and image comprehension to produce coherent reasoning chains, showing substantial improvements in reasoning plausibility and answer accuracy on dynamic benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Visual Chain-of-Thought (VCoT) methods are confined to static scenarios, unable to reason about temporal progression, future events, or step-by-step instructions.
- There is a lack of large-scale, temporally grounded VCoT datasets necessary for training models in dynamic visual reasoning tasks.
- Current multimodal benchmarks for dynamic tasks often suffer from small sample sizes, utilize multiple-choice formats, or exclusively focus on final answer accuracy, neglecting the plausibility and coherence of intermediate reasoning steps.

## Method

- The researchers developed ==TwiFF-2.7M==, the first large-scale, temporally grounded VCoT dataset, constructed from millions of video clips through a three-stage pipeline (coarse filtering, event extraction, and VCoT generation).
- They introduced ==TwiFF-Bench==, a novel evaluation benchmark that uses GPT-5.1 as a judge to assess both the plausibility of reasoning trajectories and the correctness of final answers in open-ended dynamic settings.
- A unified ==TwiFF model== was proposed, which fine-tunes Bagel-7B to iteratively generate future action frames and accompanying textual reasoning, thereby synergistically combining video generation and image comprehension capabilities.

## Results

- The TwiFF model achieved a **28.8%** improvement in CoT scores (reasoning plausibility) and **41.6%** in answer scores on TwiFF-Bench compared to its base model, Bagel-7B.
- It demonstrated strong generalization, showing a **21.0%** improvement in answer scores on the out-of-distribution Seed-Bench-R1 dataset.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the interleaved vision-textual VCoT (TwiFF-Lite) consistently outperformed single-modality variants (TwiFF-Text, TwiFF-Image) in both reasoning plausibility and answer accuracy, especially for out-of-distribution generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic VCoT demonstrates superior performance over Textual CoT and static VCoT in dynamic reasoning tasks, providing a more effective paradigm for understanding temporal visual information; this holds because physically plausible and temporally accurate visual cues are paramount for enhancing reasoning performance, while static or misleading cues actively impede the process.
> - The synergistic interaction between textual and visual modalities is crucial for VCoT's effectiveness and generalization, as neither modality alone suffices to achieve full reasoning capability.
> - Dynamic VCoT exhibits a potential for information compression, where the first generated visual cue can effectively capture and retain critical contextual information from the input image.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal reasoning, particularly involving large language models (LLMs) and visual information, has seen rapid advancements. A key paradigm in this area is Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which aims to expose the intermediate steps of a model's thought process, leading to more robust and interpretable outputs. Initially, CoT was primarily text-based (TCoT), struggling with tasks requiring deep visual processing. This led to the emergence of Visual Chain-of-Thought (VCoT), where models actively perceive, process, and utilize visual information as intermediate reasoning steps.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation within existing VCoT approaches: their confinement to *static scenarios*. Current VCoT models, whether tool-augmented (e.g., DeepEyes, Refocus) or directly generative (e.g., Zebra-CoT, ThinkMorph), are predominantly trained on data where visual reasoning cues are derived from the content present in a single input image. This makes them effective for tasks like maze navigation, geometric reasoning, or visual search, but fundamentally inadequate for *dynamic scenarios*. Dynamic tasks, such as understanding step-by-step instructions, predicting future events, or planning camera motions, inherently require reasoning about temporal progression and anticipating future visual states that extend beyond a single, static observation.

The current work by Liu et al. directly addresses this significant gap. It posits that true multimodal reasoning in dynamic contexts necessitates the ability for a unified model to jointly leverage *pre-trained video generation* and *image comprehension capabilities*. The paper argues that reasoning should involve the iterative generation of future action frames, which are then enriched with textual descriptions to articulate the underlying reasoning process.

Furthermore, the existing landscape of multimodal benchmarks for dynamic tasks is limited. Many benchmarks focus solely on image content understanding, while those that do include predictive tasks (e.g., MMBench, Seed-Bench-R1) often suffer from small sample sizes, multiple-choice formats (reducing realism and difficulty), or an exclusive focus on final answer accuracy, neglecting the plausibility and coherence of the intermediate reasoning process.

TwiFF fundamentally reshapes this landscape by:
1. **Introducing the first large-scale, temporally grounded VCoT dataset (TwiFF-2.7M):** This dataset, derived from millions of video clips, explicitly supports dynamic visual reasoning across instructional, predictive, and camera domains, aligning reasoning steps with real-world physical laws.
2. **Proposing a novel evaluation benchmark (TwiFF-Bench):** This benchmark uniquely assesses both the plausibility of reasoning trajectories and the correctness of final answers in open-ended dynamic settings, overcoming limitations of prior benchmarks.
3. **Demonstrating a unified model (TwiFF model):** This model synergistically combines video generation and image comprehension to produce temporally coherent visual reasoning, showcasing a new paradigm for multimodal AI in dynamic environments.

In essence, TwiFF pushes the boundaries of VCoT from static analysis to dynamic prediction and planning, laying a new foundation for advancing multimodal models in real-world, temporally evolving scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the TwiFF project stems from the limitations of existing Visual Chain-of-Thought (VCoT) approaches in handling dynamic scenarios. The authors identify several key objectives:

1. **Extend VCoT beyond Static Analysis:** The primary objective is to enable VCoT models to reason effectively in dynamic contexts. Current VCoT methods, even those capable of generating visual cues, are fundamentally constrained to the visual information present in a static input image. This makes them inadequate for tasks like providing step-by-step instructions, predicting future events, or guiding camera movements, all of which require anticipating or understanding temporal changes.
2. **Develop a Large-Scale, Temporally Grounded VCoT Dataset:** To overcome the data scarcity for dynamic visual reasoning, a crucial objective is to construct TwiFF-2.7M. This dataset aims to provide step-by-step visual-textual rationales derived from millions of video clips, ensuring that the reasoning trajectories are grounded in real-world temporal dynamics and physical laws. The motivation is that such a dataset is essential to train unified models to perform temporally coherent visual reasoning, moving beyond tool-augmented or static VCoT pipelines.
3. **Create a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark for Dynamic Reasoning:** A significant objective is to introduce TwiFF-Bench, a high-quality benchmark specifically designed for dynamic scenarios. The motivation here is two-fold:
 - **Plausibility of Reasoning:** Existing benchmarks often focus solely on the accuracy of the final answer, neglecting the coherence and plausibility of the intermediate reasoning steps. TwiFF-Bench aims to jointly evaluate both the reasoning process and the correctness of the final answer.
 - **Open-Ended Responses:** Many existing benchmarks for prediction tasks utilize multiple-choice formats, which reduce the task's difficulty and realism. TwiFF-Bench focuses on open-ended questions, better reflecting real-world challenges.
4. **Propose a Unified Model for Dynamic VCoT:** The authors aim to introduce the TwiFF model, a novel architecture that synergistically leverages pre-trained video generation and image comprehension capabilities. The motivation is to demonstrate that by iteratively generating future action frames and accompanying textual reasoning, unified models can achieve superior performance in dynamic visual question answering, validating the effectiveness of their proposed dynamic VCoT paradigm.
5. **Uncover Key Insights into Dynamic VCoT:** Through extensive experimentation and ablation studies, the researchers intend to explore and validate fundamental properties of dynamic VCoT, such as the synergistic interaction between modalities, the crucial role of physically plausible visual cues, and the potential for information compression within dynamic visual reasoning.

In summary, the project is motivated by the recognized need for multimodal AI systems that can reason about and interact with dynamic, real-world visual information, and it addresses this need by providing foundational data, robust evaluation tools, and an effective model architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TwiFF project follows a comprehensive methodology encompassing large-scale data generation, benchmark creation, and model development and evaluation.

#### 3.1. Data Generation (TwiFF-2.7M)

The TwiFF-2.7M dataset, comprising 2,708,318 VCoT data instances, is constructed from video clips sourced from Panda-70M, a large dataset of YouTube videos. The data generation process is a sophisticated three-stage pipeline designed to ensure high quality and temporal relevance:

1. **Stage 1: Coarse Filter:**
 - **Purpose:** To filter out low-quality video clips lacking sufficient visual variation or semantic alignment.
 - **Criteria Applied:**
 - **Semantic Alignment:** Clips with a low Unmasked Teacher matching score (below 0.43) between the clip and its caption are discarded.
 - **Visual Quality:** Only clips labeled as "desirable" in Panda-70M are retained, excluding static foregrounds, screen-in-screen, or computer screen recordings.
 - **Duration:** Clips shorter than 2 seconds are removed to ensure they capture complete events.
 - **Dynamic Content:** Clips with insufficient motion are filtered by computing the mean optical flow magnitude between uniformly sampled frames. Clips with a maximum inter-frame optical flow magnitude below 4 are excluded.
 - **Source Diversity:** A maximum of four qualifying clips are retained per original video to prevent overrepresentation.
 - **Outcome:** 10,596,462 high-dynamics video clips are retained for the next stage.

2. **Stage 2: Event Extraction:**
 - **Purpose:** To extract salient events and key frames from the filtered video clips and classify them.
 - **Process:** A multimodal large language model (InternVL3.5-8B) is used to:
 - **Classify Clips:** Each clip is categorized into "Instructional" (step-by-step procedures), "Predictive" (causal or temporal reasoning), "Camera" (intentional camera movements), or "Undesirable" (lacking clear structure). Undesirable clips are discarded.
 - **Select Key Frames:** For desirable clips, the model selects at least two key frames that capture the cause, process, and outcome of the event, uniformly sampling 8 frames per clip for this purpose.
 - **Generate Descriptions:** Detailed natural-language textual descriptions are generated across the selected key frames, focusing on the process and a concise summary.
 - **Outcome:** 3,075,048 high-quality event instances, each with process-relevant key frames and narratives.

3. **Stage 3: VCoT Generation:**
 - **Purpose:** To construct the dynamic VCoT data using the extracted event information.
 - **Process:**
 - Key frames from each event are temporally ordered.
 - The earliest frame (frame 1) serves as the query image for a question.
 - Subsequent frames (frame 2 to frame N) are designated as visual cues for the reasoning process.
 - An MLLM generates a question conditioned on frame 1, the event category, and the textual description.
 - The model then constructs a reasoning chain where visual inputs (the subsequent frames) and textual reasoning steps alternate, simulating human-like inference.
 - The output follows a structured format: `⟨reasoning about frame_i-1⟩, ⟨frame_i⟩, ⟨reasoning about frame_i⟩..., ⟨final answer⟩`.
 - **Outcome:** The final TwiFF-2.7M dataset, featuring interleaved visual-textual reasoning trajectories grounded in dynamic scenarios across instructional, predictive, and camera domains.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Generation and Evaluation (TwiFF-Bench)

- **Construction:** TwiFF-Bench consists of 1,078 question-answer pairs derived from the Panda-70M test subset, ensuring no overlap with the training data. All generated samples are manually filtered to ensure validity, correct reasoning, and non-overly-open-ended answers.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Unlike traditional benchmarks, TwiFF-Bench uses GPT-5.1 as a judge to evaluate two critical aspects:
 - **Reasoning Chain Plausibility (CoT score):** Assesses the logical coherence, completeness, and relevance of the model's generated reasoning process (0-5 scale).
 - **Answer Correctness (Answer score):** Assesses how well the final answer matches the ground truth (0-5 scale).
- **Reference Inputs:** The judge model is provided with a reference VCoT and the ground-truth answer, both derived from actual future events in the video, ensuring factual grounding and temporal consistency.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Evaluation:** The TwiFF model is also evaluated on Seed-Bench-R1 (4,676 samples from EPIC-Kitchens-100 and Ego4D) to test generalization. For this OOD benchmark, only answer accuracy is evaluated due to the absence of reference reasoning chains.

#### 3.3. Model and Experiment Setup

- **TwiFF Model:** The TwiFF model is built by finetuning Bagel-7B, a unified multimodal model, on the TwiFF-2.7M dataset. It operates by iteratively generating future action frames and textual reasoning cues.
- **Ablation Studies:** To analyze the contribution of different modalities, the authors trained variants:
 - **TwiFF-Text:** Retains only textual components of VCoT.
 - **TwiFF-Image:** Retains only visual components of VCoT (with minimal text "frame i" to control image generation).
 - **TwiFF-Lite:** Trained on a smaller subset of TwiFF-2.7M (300K samples) to evaluate efficiency.
- **Comparison Baselines:** The TwiFF model is compared against a wide range of models categorized into:
 - Understanding-only multimodal models adopting TCoT (e.g., Qwen2.5VL, InternVL3.5, Qwen3VL).
 - Unified models adopting TCoT (e.g., Bagel, Janus-Pro).
 - Understanding-only multimodal models with tool-based VCoT (e.g., DeepEyes).
 - Unified models with VCoT (e.g., Zebra-CoT, ThinkMorph).
- **Inference Constraints:** To prevent infinite loops in image generation, explicit limits are imposed on tool usage (5 for DeepEyes) and image generation (8 for generative VCoT models).

This rigorous methodology ensures the generation of a high-quality, large-scale dataset, a comprehensive evaluation benchmark, and a capable model that can effectively perform dynamic visual reasoning, while providing insights into its underlying mechanisms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted reveal several significant findings regarding the effectiveness, mechanisms, and properties of dynamic VCoT:

1. **Superior Performance of Dynamic VCoT in Dynamic Scenarios:**
 - The TwiFF model, trained on TwiFF-2.7M, significantly outperforms both Textual Chain-of-Thought (TCoT) and static VCoT approaches on dynamic reasoning tasks.
 - On TwiFF-Bench, TwiFF achieves a 28.8% improvement in CoT scores (reasoning plausibility) and a 41.6% improvement in answer scores compared to its base model, Bagel-7B.
 - It also demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, showing a 21.0% improvement in answer scores on the out-of-distribution (OOD) Seed-Bench-R1 dataset.
 - TwiFF surpasses most competing TCoT and static VCoT methods, establishing itself as a leading approach for dynamic visual question answering. While Qwen3VL-8B (a TCoT model) achieved slightly higher overall scores on TwiFF-Bench, the authors attribute this residual gap to architectural advancements in Qwen3VL compared to the Qwen2.5 foundation of Bagel (TwiFF's base model), rather than a fundamental limitation of dynamic VCoT.
 - The TwiFF-Lite model, trained on a significantly smaller subset of data, still performs competitively, highlighting the efficiency and scalability of the TwiFF framework.

2. **Synergistic Interaction of Multimodal Cues is Crucial for VCoT:**
 - Ablation studies reveal that the effectiveness of VCoT stems from the synergistic interaction between its textual and visual modalities; neither modality alone suffices to achieve its full reasoning capability.
 - TwiFF-Lite (interleaved vision-textual VCoT) consistently outperforms TwiFF-Text (text-only VCoT) and TwiFF-Image (image-only VCoT) in both reasoning plausibility and answer accuracy on TwiFF-Bench.
 - More strikingly, TwiFF-Lite shows significantly more pronounced performance gains on the OOD Seed-Bench-R1 benchmark compared to its single-modality counterparts. This indicates that the generalization capability in dynamic scenes primarily arises from the interplay of visual and textual cues. Single-modality CoTs exhibit limited generalization to OOD data, even if they show improvements on in-distribution data.

3. **Critical Impact of Physically Plausible Visual Cues:**
 - The quality and temporal fidelity of visual cues in the reasoning process are paramount. Higher-quality VCoTs, which exhibit stronger alignment between the model's generated reasoning and actual future events, consistently correlate with higher final answer scores.
 - Experiments with "TwiFF-True" (replacing generated initial image with ground-truth future image) resulted in significant improvements (20.7% CoT, 19.8% answer score) over the standard TwiFF model.
 - Conversely, "TwiFF-False" (replacing with a duplicate static input image) led to degradations in both CoT and answer scores (1.0% CoT, 1.9% answer score). This underscores that static or misleading visual cues actively impede the reasoning process, while physically plausible and temporally accurate visual cues substantially enhance performance.

4. **Potential for Information Compression in Dynamic VCoT:**
 - Dynamic VCoT demonstrates a notable potential for information compression.
 - The "TwiFF-Comp" variant, which discards the original input question image after the model generates its first intermediate image, showed only minor performance declines (2.7% CoT, 4.6% answer score). This suggests that the model's first generated visual cue effectively captures and retains the critical contextual information initially present in the input image.
 - In contrast, "TwiFF-Drop" (discarding both the input image and the first generated image) suffered substantial performance degradation (24.4% CoT, 14.1% answer score), highlighting that *some* visual information is essential for accurate reasoning. These findings suggest that dynamic VCoT can enable efficient information compression while mitigating catastrophic forgetting during multi-step reasoning.

These findings collectively validate the TwiFF framework's effectiveness for dynamic visual reasoning and offer profound insights into the underlying mechanisms and requirements for building robust multimodal AI capable of understanding and interacting with the temporal complexities of the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "TwiFF (Think With Future Frames): A Large-Scale Dataset for Dynamic Visual Reasoning" carries substantial significance for the field of multimodal AI, particularly in pushing the boundaries of visual reasoning beyond static image analysis. Its potential impacts are multifaceted:

1. **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** TwiFF represents a crucial step forward in enabling multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to reason about dynamic, temporally evolving visual information. By moving beyond static Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigms, this research paves the way for AI systems that can better understand and interact with the real world, which is inherently dynamic.

2. **Creation of Foundational Resources:**
 - **TwiFF-2.7M Dataset:** This is the first large-scale, temporally grounded Visual Chain-of-Thought (VCoT) dataset. Its scale (2.7 million video clips) and explicit design for dynamic scenarios (instructional, predictive, camera) make it an invaluable resource. It will serve as a foundational benchmark for training and evaluating future generations of MLLMs capable of dynamic visual reasoning, similar to how ImageNet or COCO catalyzed advancements in static vision.
 - **TwiFF-Bench Benchmark:** This benchmark uniquely evaluates both the *plausibility of reasoning steps* and the *correctness of final answers* in open-ended, dynamic settings. This comprehensive evaluation approach addresses a critical limitation of prior benchmarks that focused solely on end-task accuracy or relied on less realistic multiple-choice formats. TwiFF-Bench will guide the development of models that are not only accurate but also transparent and logically coherent in their dynamic reasoning.

3. **New Paradigm for Unified Multimodal Models:** The proposed TwiFF model demonstrates the effectiveness of a unified approach that synergistically leverages pre-trained video generation and image comprehension. This iterative process of generating future action frames and textual reasoning cues offers a powerful paradigm for designing MLLMs capable of sophisticated temporal understanding and prediction. It highlights the potential for integrated generative capabilities to enhance reasoning, suggesting that future models will likely incorporate more explicit visual "thinking" steps.

4. **Enabling Real-World Applications:** The ability to perform robust dynamic visual reasoning has profound implications for various practical applications:
 - **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Improved understanding of instructional sequences, better prediction of physical outcomes, and enhanced navigation capabilities for autonomous agents. Robots could learn complex tasks by visually "thinking through" future steps and predicting outcomes.
 - **Instructional Systems and Guides:** More intelligent AI assistants that can provide precise, visually grounded, step-by-step instructions for complex procedures (e.g., cooking, repairs, assembly) by anticipating future states.
 - **Video Content Analysis and Generation:** Advanced tools for analyzing video content, predicting future events in surveillance or entertainment, and even aiding in the generation of dynamic visual content, such as suggesting optimal camera movements for cinematic effects.
 - **Medical Imaging and Procedures:** Potentially assisting in understanding dynamic medical processes or guiding surgical procedures where temporal understanding of visual cues is critical.

5. **Deeper Insights into AI Cognition:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the mechanisms of multimodal reasoning, particularly the critical role of synergistic interaction between modalities and the dependence on physically plausible visual cues. The finding on information compression highlights how dynamic VCoT can efficiently represent and retain task-critical information, which has implications for model efficiency and robustness. This research opens avenues for exploring reinforcement learning techniques guided by CoT plausibility, pushing the boundaries of how AI models "think" visually.

In conclusion, TwiFF is not merely an incremental improvement; it marks a significant conceptual shift in VCoT research. By providing a novel framework, a large-scale dataset, and a rigorous evaluation methodology, it establishes a new frontier for developing AI systems with genuinely dynamic visual reasoning capabilities, promising a wide array of impactful applications and further research directions.
