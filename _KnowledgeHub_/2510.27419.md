# DeepCompress: A Dual Reward Strategy for Dynamically Exploring and Compressing Reasoning Chains

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tencent AI Lab developed DeepCompress, a framework that dynamically adjusts Large Reasoning Model (LRM) chain-of-thought length based on problem difficulty to simultaneously enhance both accuracy and inference efficiency. The method achieved an average absolute accuracy improvement of +2.7 points on mathematical benchmarks for the 7B model while compressing response length by 16.6%.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) exhibit cognitive inefficiencies, such as 'overthinking' simple problems with excessive steps and 'underthinking' complex problems, leading to unstable reasoning.
- Existing methods for improving LRM efficiency (e.g., SFT on concise CoT or RL with token-length penalties) often reduce reasoning length at the cost of a slight degradation in accuracy.
- A fundamental challenge remains in achieving both superior reasoning accuracy and computational efficiency in LRMs, as current approaches often present a trade-off.

## Method

- **DeepCompress** extends the Zero RL training paradigm by introducing a ==Dual Length Reward== that can either penalize brevity or encourage exploration, dynamically adapting its direction.
- A Model-Aware Difficulty mechanism assesses question difficulty in real-time by comparing a question's group pass ratio (correct responses in a batch) to the overall batch pass ratio, classifying problems as 'Simple' or 'Hard'.
- Robustness is enhanced by applying the length reward only if the generated solution is correct, and by smoothing the batch pass ratio using an Exponential Moving Average to ensure stable training and gradual adaptation.

## Results

- DeepCompress-Zero-**7B** achieved an average absolute improvement of +2.7 points in accuracy over DeepMath-Zero-**7B** on mathematical benchmarks, with notable gains of +6.5 points on AIME 25.
- The framework significantly compressed response lengths, reducing token usage by an average of **57.9%** for the **3B** model and **16.6%** for the **7B** model across benchmarks, demonstrating improved inference efficiency.
- DeepCompress fostered more effective learning dynamics, exhibiting increased policy entropy for exploration and promoting more frequent, yet concise, reflection behaviors on challenging problems, leading to 'smarter thinking'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Consistently favoring shorter reasoning paths is not optimal; while short responses benefit single-attempt accuracy, longer responses can encompass more correct solutions for challenging problems, improving group accuracy.
> - Dynamically tailoring reasoning length to problem difficulty allows LRMs to compress simple problem solutions for efficiency while exploring longer chains for hard problems to maintain or enhance accuracy.
> - A combined reward structure, integrating outcome correctness with an adaptively scaled length reward, and conditioned on correct solutions, is crucial for balanced learning without reward hacking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in complex reasoning tasks, often leveraging techniques such as self-verification, reflection, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to improve accuracy. However, research has identified inefficiencies in LRM cognition, categorized as "overthinking" for simple problems (generating excessive intermediate steps) and "underthinking" for complex problems (exhibiting unstable thought shifts). These inefficiencies highlight a critical need for adaptive strategies that can enhance both the efficiency and accuracy of LRMs.

Existing research has pursued two primary strategies to improve LRM reasoning efficiency. The first involves Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on datasets of concise CoT exemplars, effectively training models to achieve correct answers with fewer intermediate steps. The second approach incorporates token-length reward functions into Reinforcement Learning (RL) frameworks, explicitly optimizing for shorter reasoning paths by penalizing verbosity. While these compression techniques generally yield efficiency gains, they frequently do so at the cost of a slight degradation in accuracy. Consequently, a fundamental challenge persists in simultaneously achieving both superior accuracy and computational efficiency in LRMs.

This paper addresses this gap by challenging the assumption that consistently favoring shorter reasoning paths is optimal. It proposes that for difficult problems, longer responses may contain a wider array of correct solutions. DeepCompress therefore seeks to develop a dynamic, adaptive strategy that tailors reasoning length to problem difficulty, thereby attempting to overcome the limitations of prior methods that either sacrifice accuracy for efficiency or lack real-time adaptability. The work integrates aspects of post-training efficiency improvement (RL) with an intelligent mechanism for dynamic adaptation, drawing parallels to research on manipulating reasoning length through prompt engineering but applying a more intrinsic model-aware approach during training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, DeepCompress, that can simultaneously enhance both the accuracy and efficiency of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing LRM Cognitive Inefficiencies:** Current LRMs exhibit significant cognitive inefficiencies, namely "overthinking" simple problems by generating unnecessarily long reasoning chains, and "underthinking" complex problems, leading to unstable or insufficient reasoning. These behaviors indicate a lack of adaptive reasoning allocation.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Efficiency Methods:** Prior approaches, such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on shortened Chain-of-Thought (CoT) exemplars or Reinforcement Learning (RL) with token-length penalties, primarily aim to reduce reasoning length. While these methods succeed in improving efficiency, they often incur a trade-off, resulting in a decline in model accuracy. The research is motivated by the need to bypass this accuracy-efficiency dilemma.
3. **Challenging the "Shorter is Always Better" Assumption:** The paper specifically challenges the prevailing notion that consistently favoring shorter reasoning paths is optimal for all problems. Through preliminary experiments, the authors found that while shorter responses might yield better `pass@1` scores (single attempt accuracy), longer responses, especially for challenging problems, tend to encompass a broader range of potentially correct solutions, leading to higher `pass@k` scores (accuracy over multiple attempts). This observation suggests that restricting reasoning length indiscriminately can constrain the model's problem-solving capacity and reasoning boundary, particularly for complex tasks requiring extensive exploration.
4. **Necessity for Adaptive Reasoning:** Given the varying nature of problem difficulty and the dual findings regarding response length and accuracy, the authors are motivated to design a mechanism that allows LRMs to autonomously adjust their CoT length. This mechanism should enable compression for well-mastered (simple) problems to improve efficiency, while promoting longer, more exploratory thought chains for challenging (hard) problems to maintain or enhance accuracy. The goal is to create a model-aware system that dynamically classifies problem difficulty and applies an appropriate length reward strategy in real-time.
5. **Practical Implications for Training and Inference:** While favoring longer responses for hard problems can enhance accuracy, it is impractical to encourage consistently longer responses due to training and inference efficiency considerations. Therefore, the motivation is to strike a balance through an adaptive strategy that allocates computational resources intelligently, leading to overall improved performance and reduced operational costs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepCompress introduces a novel framework designed to enhance both the accuracy and efficiency of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by dynamically adapting the length of reasoning chains based on problem difficulty. The methodology extends the Zero RL training paradigm by incorporating a Dual Length Reward and a Model-Aware Difficulty mechanism, along with robustness enhancements.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Experiments

Before introducing DeepCompress, the authors conducted preliminary experiments to analyze the relationship between response length and mathematical reasoning performance.
- **Experimental Setup:** The experiments utilized DeepMath-Zero-3B and DeepMath-Zero-7B models, fine-tuned Qwen models, on four mathematical benchmarks: MATH-500, Olympiad-Bench, Minerva Math, and AIME 2025. Responses were generated with a maximum length of 32,768 tokens using a sampling temperature of 0.6 and top-p of 0.95. A rule-based verifier was used to calculate `pass@k` scores, with `pass@1` (single sample accuracy) and `pass@32` (group accuracy) being reported. Response lengths were standardized and divided into 16 bins for analysis.
- **Results:** The results indicated a divergence: `pass@1` scores generally decreased with increasing response length, suggesting efficiency benefits from shorter responses for a single attempt. Conversely, `pass@32` scores generally increased with longer responses, particularly for more challenging benchmarks (e.g., AIME25 with k=64), implying that longer responses offered a wider coverage of potentially correct solutions within a group of generated samples. This finding motivated the development of an adaptive strategy that avoids uniformly penalizing longer reasoning paths.

#### 3.2. DeepCompress Framework

DeepCompress builds upon the Zero RL training recipe, specifically using the DAPO (Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization) algorithm. The core innovations are:

- **Zero RL Foundation:** The base RL algorithm optimizes a language model policy π_θ using an outcome reward R_o. This binary reward is +1 if the extracted final answer is correct and -1 otherwise, for a given question-answer pair (x_i, y_i) and a group of G generated outputs {ŷ_i^j}.

- **Dual Length Reward (R_l):**
 - **Standardized Length (z_i):** For a group of G responses to a question x_i, the mean (μ_i) and standard deviation (σ_i) of their token lengths are calculated. The standardized length z_i for each response ŷ_i is derived as (|ŷ_i| - μ_i) / (σ_i + ε), where ε is a small constant for numerical stability.
 - **Sigmoid Transformation:** The length reward R_z is computed using a sigmoid function: R_z(ŷ, β) = 1 / (1 + e^(βz_i)). The hyperparameter β dictates the steepness and direction of the reward.
 - **Adaptive Modes:**
 - If β > 0, the reward is higher for shorter responses, favoring brevity. This mode is intended for "Simple" questions.
 - If β < 0, the reward encourages longer responses, facilitating more extensive exploration. This mode is intended for "Hard" questions, as indicated by the preliminary experiments.
 - **Scaling Factor:** A hyperparameter α scales the magnitude of R_z(ŷ, β), resulting in the final length reward R_l = α * R_z(ŷ, β).

- **Model-Aware Difficulty:**
 - **Dynamic Classification:** Instead of relying on static dataset labels, DeepCompress dynamically assesses question difficulty based on the model's real-time performance.
 - **Group Pass Ratio (P_g(x_i)):** For each question x_i in a batch, P_g(x_i) is the proportion of correct responses among its G generated outputs.
 - **Batch Pass Ratio (P_b):** P_b is the average of P_g(x_i) across all questions in the current training batch, providing an indicator of the model's overall capability.
 - **Determining β_i:** The sign and magnitude of β are dynamically set as β_i = P_g(x_i) - P_b.
 - If P_g(x_i) > P_b (positive β_i), the question is considered "Simple" for the current model, encouraging shorter responses.
 - If P_g(x_i) < P_b (negative β_i), the question is considered "Hard," promoting longer, exploratory reasoning.
 - **Combined Reward:** The total reward for RL optimization integrates both outcome and length rewards: R = R_o + R_l.

- **Enhancing Robustness:**
 - **Correctness-Conditioned Length Reward:** To prevent reward hacking (where models might prioritize length optimization over accuracy), the length reward R_l is applied only if the generated solution ŷ_i is correct (i.e., R_o(ŷ_i, y_i) = 1). If the solution is incorrect, only R_o is used.
 - **Smoothed Batch Pass Ratio:** To improve training stability and prevent premature length constraints, the batch pass ratio P_b is smoothed using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA). P_b,t = λ * P_b,t-1 + (1 - λ) * P_true_b,t, where P_true_b,t is the true batch pass ratio and λ is the EMA parameter. The initial P_b,t is set to 1.0, ensuring a gradual adaptation from an optimistic state and encouraging exploration in early training stages where true P_b,t might be low.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate DeepCompress focused on its ability to improve both reasoning accuracy and token efficiency across challenging benchmarks, as well as analyzing its underlying mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities

- **Superior Performance on Mathematical Benchmarks:** DeepCompress consistently outperformed existing Zero RL baselines, including DeepMath-Zero, across seven challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, AMC 2023, OlympiadBench, Minerva Math, AIME 2024-2025, and PolyMath).
 - DeepCompress-Zero-3B achieved an average absolute improvement of +2.0 points over DeepMath-Zero-3B.
 - DeepCompress-Zero-7B achieved an average absolute improvement of +2.7 points over DeepMath-Zero-7B.
 - Notable gains were observed on highly challenging problems, with DeepCompress-Zero-7B surpassing DeepMath-Zero-7B by +4.1 points on AIME 24 and +6.5 points on AIME 25. These results indicate that DeepCompress effectively enhances LRM reasoning, particularly for complex tasks requiring extensive exploration.
- **Generalizable Reasoning:** DeepCompress demonstrated robust generalization beyond mathematical tasks, exhibiting consistent improvements on GPQA-Diamond (biology, physics, chemistry), MMLU-STEM (science and engineering), and Big-Bench Hard (complex multi-step reasoning). This suggests that the dynamic strategy for exploration and efficiency fostered by DeepCompress cultivates a reasoning capability applicable across diverse scientific domains.

#### 4.2. Improved Inference Efficiency

- **Significant Token Compression:** DeepCompress generated substantially more concise responses compared to DeepMath-Zero models across all evaluated benchmarks.
 - On average, DeepCompress compressed the response length by 57.9% for the 3B model and 16.6% for the 7B model.
 - For instance, on AIME 24, DeepCompress-Zero-3B used 37.6% fewer tokens while achieving a +5.2 absolute improvement in accuracy. DeepCompress-Zero-7B used 35.2% fewer tokens for a +4.1 improvement. These findings demonstrate that dynamically adjusting reasoning length can advance reasoning boundaries while simultaneously reducing inference costs.

#### 4.3. Impact of Dual Length Reward

- **Policy Entropy Dynamics:** Ablation studies comparing DeepCompress with fixed "Length Penalty" (β=1) and "Length Bonus" (β=-1) variants revealed distinct learning dynamics.
 - Models with a fixed "Length Bonus" exhibited higher policy entropy during training, consistent with broader exploration.
 - Models with "Length Penalty" consistently maintained low policy entropy, indicating a narrower exploration strategy.
 - DeepCompress showed an initial increase in policy entropy (for exploration) followed by gradual stabilization as the model converged, demonstrating its adaptive balancing act.
- **Response Length and Performance Trade-offs:** The response length of variants aligned with their reward design: "Length Bonus" models generated longer outputs, "Length Penalty" variants produced shorter responses. While "Length Bonus" initially showed higher performance, DeepCompress demonstrated continuous performance growth throughout its dynamic process, indicating a balanced approach to exploration and efficiency.

#### 4.4. Emergence of Reasoning Behaviors

- **Efficient Reflection:** An analysis on a set of challenging problems (where baselines failed) showed that DeepCompress models exhibited more frequent reflection behaviors compared to DeepMath-Zero models.
 - Despite higher reflection frequency, DeepCompress maintained shorter average response lengths, suggesting a more efficient reflection process leading to concise and targeted attempts.
 - This mechanism correlated with stronger `pass@1` scores, indicating that DeepCompress encourages "smarter thinking" where each reflective act contributes productively to the solution.

#### 4.5. Hyperparameter Analysis

- **Robustness to Reward Weight (α):** The method exhibited robustness to the reward weight α, with all tested configurations outperforming the DeepMath-Zero-3B baseline.
- **Importance of EMA Parameter (λ):** Empirical results indicated that a larger smoothing factor (λ = 0.99) for the batch pass ratio was crucial for optimal performance. This factor encourages longer reasoning trajectories and deeper exploration during early training, helping the model overcome performance plateaus.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DeepCompress framework represents a notable advancement in the development of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by simultaneously addressing the long-standing challenge of balancing reasoning accuracy with computational efficiency. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking the Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off:** DeepCompress directly tackles the prevalent issue where improving LRM efficiency (e.g., shorter reasoning chains) often comes at the cost of reduced accuracy, and vice-versa. By demonstrating superior accuracy while significantly improving token efficiency on challenging mathematical benchmarks and generalizing to other scientific domains, this work suggests a path toward models that are both more capable and less resource-intensive. This dual optimization is crucial for the practical deployment and scalability of advanced AI systems.

2. **Adaptive and Intelligent Reasoning Allocation:** The introduction of a model-aware difficulty mechanism combined with a dual length reward allows LRMs to dynamically allocate their reasoning efforts. This means models can "think less" for simple, well-mastered problems, leading to faster inference and lower computational costs, while "thinking more" (exploring longer reasoning paths) for genuinely challenging problems, which increases the probability of finding correct solutions. This intelligent allocation mimics human cognitive flexibility and represents a step towards more autonomous and context-aware reasoning.

3. **Enhanced Learning Dynamics and Exploration:** The analysis of policy entropy and reasoning behaviors indicates that DeepCompress fosters a more effective learning process. By encouraging high policy entropy, particularly for harder problems, the model is guided towards more comprehensive exploration of solution spaces. The observed increase in reflection frequency, coupled with shorter average response lengths on challenging problems, suggests that DeepCompress promotes a "smarter thinking" paradigm, where exploration leads to more targeted and efficient problem-solving strategies rather than mere verbosity. This could lead to more robust and generalized reasoning capabilities.

4. **Foundation for Future LRM Development:** DeepCompress provides a new paradigm for post-training optimization of LRMs using reinforcement learning. The insights gained from dynamically adjusting length rewards and the model-aware assessment of difficulty could inform the design of future RL algorithms and training objectives for various complex tasks beyond mathematical reasoning. The framework's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance while concurrently reducing token usage opens avenues for developing more powerful yet sustainable AI reasoners.

5. **Practical Implications for AI Applications:** Reduced inference costs due to token efficiency make advanced LRMs more accessible and economical for real-world applications requiring complex reasoning, such as scientific discovery, engineering design, and advanced analytics. Simultaneously, improved accuracy on difficult problems means these models can be trusted with more critical and intricate tasks. The generalizability across diverse scientific domains further broadens the potential impact across various industries.

A recognized limitation is the reliance on sufficient length variation within sampled responses for the method's effectiveness, and the maximum generation length cap, which might restrict exploration for extremely complex problems. However, the demonstrated ability to intelligently balance exploration and compression represents a promising trajectory for developing highly efficient and effective autonomous reasoning AI.
