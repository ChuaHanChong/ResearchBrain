# Thinker: A vision-language foundation model for embodied intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> UBTECH Robotics introduces Thinker, a 10-billion parameter vision-language foundation model engineered for embodied intelligence. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on robotic task planning benchmarks like Robovqa and Egoplan-bench2, demonstrating enhanced egocentric perspective, temporal reasoning, and spatial understanding through specialized data and a two-stage fine-tuning approach.

## Problem

- General-purpose vision-language models (VLMs) often confuse third-person and first-person perspectives, hindering their application in embodied AI.
- Existing VLMs struggle with temporal reasoning, frequently overlooking critical information towards the end of video sequences, essential for understanding action flows.
- Lack of tailored training data for robot-specific tasks results in deficiencies in spatial grounding from an egocentric viewpoint and effective language instruction understanding for physical actions.

## Method

- Developed **Thinker**, a 10-billion parameter VLM with a unified architecture for vision, language, and time, designed to process images, videos, and complex language instructions.
- Implemented a two-stage training strategy: first, building foundational embodied capabilities with diverse datasets, then fine-tuning for complex industrial planning tasks using the proprietary Industroplan-200K dataset.
- Constructed extensive, purpose-built datasets for visual grounding, ego-view reasoning, robotic manipulation planning, and industrial tasks, crucially incorporating joint video and last-frame inputs to enhance temporal understanding.

## Results

- Thinker-7B achieved state-of-the-art performance on the Robovqa benchmark, with an average BLEU score of 63.5, surpassing general-purpose VLMs like GPT-4V (26.8 average BLEU) by a substantial margin.
- The model secured the highest overall accuracy of **58.21%** on the Egoplan-bench2 benchmark, leading in three out of four specific domains including Daily life (**63.78%**), Work (**54.95%**), and Recreation (**61.20%**).
- These findings confirm Thinker-7B's superior ability to parse fine-grained spatiotemporal cues and effectively decompose complex, long-range planning tasks into coherent, actionable responses for robotic applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Specialized training with large-scale, robotics-tailored datasets and a multi-stage fine-tuning process is crucial for adapting general VLMs to the complexities of embodied intelligence.
> - Jointly incorporating key frames alongside full video inputs significantly improves temporal reasoning and the model's ability to focus on critical, task-relevant information.
> - Creating an egocentric coordinate system for spatial understanding, coupled with explicit training on task planning, enables VLMs to bridge the gap between abstract AI and practical robotic action.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on "Thinker" is situated at the critical intersection of large vision-language models (VLMs) and embodied AI, specifically addressing their application in robotics. In recent years, VLMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding and generating human language, and interpreting visual information across a wide range of tasks like visual question answering and image captioning. This success has naturally led to an exploration of their potential in robotics, where intelligent agents need to perceive their environment, understand instructions, and plan actions.

However, the authors correctly identify a significant gap: most existing VLMs, trained predominantly on third-person perspective data, struggle with the unique demands of embodied intelligence. Key limitations include:
1. **Perspective Mismatch:** VLMs often confuse third-person (observer's) and first-person (robot's ego-view) perspectives, which is crucial for a robot interacting with its own environment.
2. **Temporal Reasoning Deficiencies:** They tend to overlook critical information, particularly towards the end of videos, which is vital for understanding sequences of actions and predicting future states.
3. **Lack of Embodied Context:** General VLMs lack training data specifically tailored for robot-specific tasks, leading to difficulties in planning, understanding spatial relationships from an egocentric viewpoint, and grounding language instructions in physical actions.
4. **Spatial and Temporal Grounding:** The absence of robust temporal and spatial information, directly tied to a first-person perspective, fundamentally constrains their ability to enable effective robotic task planning.

"Thinker" directly addresses these challenges by proposing a VLM specifically designed for embodied intelligence. It positions itself as a foundation model that aims to provide core capabilities—Task Planning, Spatial Understanding (egocentric), Temporal Understanding, and Object Grounding—that are essential for robots. By constructing large-scale, robotics-tailored datasets and introducing novel training methodologies (like jointly incorporating key frames and video inputs), Thinker attempts to fill the void left by general-purpose VLMs.

In the broader landscape, this work contributes to the growing effort to develop specialized VLMs for robotics, alongside models like RoboBrain, ThinkAct, and RoboMamba. It differentiates itself through its emphasis on a comprehensive dataset construction strategy, including an industrial task planning dataset, and a refined training approach to enhance temporal and spatial reasoning from an ego-view. The ambition to create a "foundation model" for embodied intelligence signals a move towards creating generalizable AI brains for robots, much like large language models have become foundational for natural language processing. The eventual goal, as implied by the field, is to enable robots to understand complex human instructions, perceive their environment in an embodied manner, and execute multi-step tasks autonomously.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "Thinker" project is to develop a robust and comprehensive large vision-language foundation model specifically engineered for embodied intelligence. This overarching goal is driven by the critical limitations observed when applying general-purpose VLMs to robotic systems, which, despite their advancements in scene understanding, consistently fail in nuanced robotic planning and interaction tasks.

The key motivations stem from the identified "gap between existing VLMs and their application in robotics," primarily due to the absence of temporal and spatial information grounded in a first-person perspective. To bridge this gap, the authors set out to equip Thinker with four core capabilities, forming the specific objectives of their model:

1. **Task Planning Capability:** To enable VLMs to understand intricate user instructions, maintain a memory of past states, and accurately predict future states relevant to robotic actions. This is crucial for robots to execute multi-step tasks autonomously and intelligently.
2. **Spatial Understanding Capability:** To establish an egocentric coordinate system where the robot's camera serves as the origin. All states and spatial relationships must be defined relative to this frame of reference, allowing the robot to accurately perceive and interact with objects in its immediate physical space. This directly counters the third-person perspective bias of many existing VLMs.
3. **Temporal Understanding Capability:** To enable VLMs to effectively extract key information from past events within video sequences and integrate this historical context with current instructions to assess the present state. This addresses the challenge of VLMs overlooking information in video endings and struggling with sequential reasoning.
4. **Object Grounding Capability:** To empower VLMs to describe and localize objects precisely using formats like bounding boxes and points. This is fundamental for robots to accurately identify and interact with specific items in their environment.

The motivation for creating a large-scale dataset tailored to these robotic perception and reasoning challenges is equally significant. The authors recognized that existing datasets, optimized for general VQA or image captioning, are insufficient for fostering the specific spatial, temporal, and planning skills required by embodied agents. Therefore, a major objective was to construct a diverse, large-scale dataset encompassing ego-view videos, visual grounding, spatial understanding, and chain-of-thought data, including proprietary industrial task planning data, to properly train and evaluate Thinker. Ultimately, the project aims to create a VLM that can achieve state-of-the-art performance on task planning benchmarks, demonstrating its practical utility for complex robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing Thinker is characterized by a multi-faceted approach, combining a novel model architecture, a two-stage training strategy, and the construction of extensive, purpose-built datasets, all supported by a robust infrastructure.

**A. Model Architecture:**
Thinker is built upon a ten billion parameter large vision-language model, designed for a unified representation across vision, language, and time. Its architecture comprises four key modules:
- **Text Tokenizer:** For processing and encoding textual instructions and responses.
- **Visual Encoder:** To extract features from visual inputs, including both video sequences and individual frames.
- **Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) Adapter:** A crucial component that aligns the visual and language spaces, facilitating seamless communication between the visual encoder and the language model backbone.
- **Language Model Backbone:** The core generative model responsible for comprehending task instructions, conducting reasoning, and producing textual outputs (e.g., plans or answers).
This design allows Thinker to process diverse inputs—images, videos, and complex language instructions—and accurately capture visual details, comprehend tasks, and perform multi-scenario reasoning. A key architectural detail for video comprehension is the joint incorporation of the video sequence *and* its last frame as inputs, enhancing the model's ability to focus on critical temporal information.

**B. Training Strategy:**
Thinker employs a rigorous two-step training strategy to imbue it with robust task planning capabilities:
1. **Stage 1: Building Embodied Capabilities:** This foundational stage focuses on developing core perception and reasoning skills. Thinker is fine-tuned on a combination of general datasets, spatial understanding datasets, and large-scale planning datasets. This process equips the model with robust spatial perception and reasoning, laying a solid groundwork for downstream tasks. A crucial element here is the incorporation of the last frame of each video clip as an auxiliary input during video understanding training, which specifically targets improved temporal comprehension.
2. **Stage 2: Downstream Task Fine-Tuning:** The second stage aligns Thinker's reasoning capabilities with complex, real-world industrial planning tasks. Supervised fine-tuning is performed on the specialized Industroplan-200K dataset. This stage enables the model to adapt its inherited reasoning abilities to sequential dependencies, diverse object layouts, and corrective feedback, allowing it to generate executable plans in practical industrial scenarios.

**C. Training Data Construction:**
Recognizing the limitations of generic VLMs, the authors meticulously constructed four types of large-scale, diverse datasets tailored for embodied settings:
- **Visual Grounding Data (1.7M files):** Built from Lvis-520K (with GPT-4o generated QA for object functions), Sharerobot-affordance-6.5K (for graspable regions), Pixmopoint-570K, and Robopoint-667K (refined for point grounding). This data fosters precise spatial perception and instruction understanding.
- **Ego-View Reasoning Data (Egoplan-it-100K):** A refined version of Egoplan-it, including video clips and their last frames, with tasks in both open-ended and multiple-choice formats. This dataset is crucial for advancing temporal reasoning and egocentric task planning.
- **Robotic Manipulation Planning Data (Robovideo-1.8M):** An integration of Robovqa (800K QA pairs across embodiments) and Sharerobot (1M QA for fine-grained planning across diverse scenes and robot embodiments). This dataset enables complex reasoning in robotic task scenarios.
- **Industrial Task Planning Data (Industroplan-200K):** A proprietary dataset focusing on long-horizon task planning in industrial environments, involving multi-object manipulation and transportation. It includes video demonstrations, task goals, and chain-of-thought annotations, vital for training models for complex factory tasks.

**D. Infrastructures:**
The training and inference processes are supported by a robust infrastructure designed to handle large-scale multi-task, multi-modal training and deployment:
- **Large-Scale Multi-Task Training Infrastructure:** Addresses heterogeneity across data sources (video vs. single-image VQA), efficient initialization, and stable throughput. It uses a unified sampling schema, a dynamic sampler for balanced task mixing, sharded loading, and selective freezing.
- **Inference Infrastructure for Fine-Tuned Model:** Standardizes inputs and outputs for specific benchmarks (EgoPlan-Bench2, RoboVQA) to ensure seamless and repeatable assessment.
- **Fault Tolerance and Monitoring:** Continuous tracking of optimization signals, throughput, and resource utilization, with automated alerts and periodic checkpointing to ensure rapid recovery from failures during long training runs.

This comprehensive approach, from tailored data generation to a specialized training paradigm and architecture, directly targets the identified shortcomings of applying general VLMs to the complexities of embodied robotics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Thinker-7B demonstrates its state-of-the-art performance across two widely recognized benchmarks in robotic task planning, effectively showcasing its enhanced video perception and robot-task understanding capabilities. The model was rigorously compared against seven prominent VLM baselines, including both open-source and closed-source models such as Qwen2.5-VL-7B, GPT-4V, Cosmos-Reason1-7B, ThinkAct-7B, RoboBrain-7B, and RoboBrain2-7B/32B.

**A. Performance on Robovqa:**
On the Robovqa benchmark, which evaluates multimodal long-horizon reasoning for robotics through free-form textual answers, Thinker-7B achieved the best performance across all BLEU metrics:
- **BLEU-1:** 72.7
- **BLEU-2:** 65.7
- **BLEU-3:** 59.5
- **BLEU-4:** 56.0
- **Average BLEU Score:** 63.5

Notably, Thinker-7B surpassed the second-best model, RoboBrain-7B (with an average BLEU of 62.7), by a margin of 0.8. More significantly, it vastly outperformed general-purpose VLMs like GPT-4V (average BLEU of 26.8), highlighting the critical necessity of robotics-tailored training. These results underscore Thinker-7B's superior ability to parse fine-grained spatiotemporal cues within video sequences and to effectively decompose complex, long-range planning tasks into coherent, actionable textual responses. This demonstrates its proficiency in understanding nuanced robotic instructions and generating relevant action plans.

**B. Performance on Egoplan-bench2:**
For the Egoplan-bench2 benchmark, designed to assess multimodal large language model planning in real-world scenarios from an egocentric perspective, Thinker-7B achieved the highest overall accuracy:
- **Overall Accuracy:** 58.21

Thinker-7B outperformed all baselines comprehensively, including larger models like RoboBrain2-32B (57.23% overall accuracy). It also secured first place in three out of the four specific domains evaluated: Daily life (63.78%), Work (54.95%), and Recreation (61.20%). This consistent strong performance across diverse domains indicates Thinker-7B's remarkable adaptability to various egocentric contexts. It not only excels at common household or recreational tasks but also demonstrates competitive planning abilities in professional and work-related environments.

In summary, the main findings confirm that Thinker-7B consistently outperforms both general-purpose and specialized embodied VLMs across challenging robotic benchmarks. Its success is attributed to its specialized architecture, the targeted two-stage training strategy, and, critically, the construction and utilization of large-scale, robotics-specific datasets, particularly the joint input of video and key frames for enhanced temporal understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Thinker represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied intelligence and robotic AI, offering substantial significance and potential impact across several dimensions.

Firstly, **Thinker directly addresses fundamental limitations** that have hindered the effective deployment of general-purpose VLMs in robotics. By explicitly tackling the confusion between third-person and first-person perspectives, improving temporal reasoning to prevent oversight of crucial video information, and providing spatial understanding grounded in an egocentric coordinate system, Thinker moves beyond mere scene understanding to genuine embodied perception and planning. This targeted approach unlocks a new level of capability for robots to interact intelligently with their environment.

Secondly, the creation of **large-scale, purpose-built datasets** is a major contribution. The Visual Grounding, Ego-View Reasoning, Robotic Manipulation Planning, and particularly the proprietary Industroplan-200K datasets fill critical data gaps. These datasets, especially Industroplan with its focus on long-horizon, multi-object manipulation in industrial settings and chain-of-thought annotations, provide invaluable resources for training and evaluating future robotic AI models. Such domain-specific data is essential for bridging the sim-to-real gap and developing robust systems for practical applications.

Thirdly, the **methodological innovations**, specifically the two-stage training strategy and the simple yet effective approach of jointly incorporating key frames and video as inputs, offer valuable insights for the broader research community. This strategy demonstrates how foundational embodied capabilities can be built and then refined for specific, complex tasks, and how temporal reasoning in VLMs can be significantly enhanced.

Fourthly, Thinker's **state-of-the-art performance** on challenging benchmarks like Robovqa and Egoplan-bench2, consistently outperforming both general-purpose and other embodied VLMs, validates its design and training philosophy. Its ability to excel in diverse egocentric contexts, from daily life to industrial tasks, showcases its robustness and generalizability, which are paramount for real-world robotic applications. The clear margin over models like GPT-4V underscores that bespoke training with robot-specific data is crucial for achieving high performance in embodied tasks, rather than relying solely on the scaling of general-purpose models.

The potential impact of Thinker is far-reaching. For **UBTECH Robotics**, it provides a powerful AI "brain" for their humanoid and service robots, enabling them to understand more complex instructions, adapt to dynamic environments, and perform intricate manipulation tasks with greater autonomy. This could lead to more capable and versatile robots in various sectors, from logistics and manufacturing (as evidenced by Industroplan) to healthcare and consumer services. Beyond UBTECH, Thinker could serve as a **catalyst for future research** in embodied AI, inspiring further work on world models and video-language-action models built upon strong perception and planning foundations. The announced intent to open-source its architecture and weights, along with a full technical report, further amplifies its potential impact by making these advancements accessible to the wider research community, fostering collaboration and accelerating progress in the field. Ultimately, Thinker brings us closer to a future where robots can perform sophisticated tasks in human environments with greater intelligence and adaptability.
