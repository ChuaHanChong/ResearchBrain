# Agent0: Unleashing Self-Evolving Agents from Zero Data via Tool-Integrated Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Agent0, developed by researchers at UNC-Chapel Hill, Salesforce Research, and Stanford University, introduces a fully autonomous framework for evolving high-performing LLM agents from scratch, without human-curated data. The system achieves substantial improvements, such as an 18% increase in mathematical reasoning and a 24% boost in general reasoning for Qwen3-8B-Base models, through a co-evolutionary loop and tool-integrated learning.

## Problem

- Current LLM agents, especially those trained with Reinforcement Learning (RL), heavily depend on massive, high-quality human-curated datasets, creating scalability and cost bottlenecks.
- Existing self-evolution frameworks often suffer from "learning stagnation," as the tasks they generate rarely surpass the model's current capabilities, limiting continuous improvement.
- Many frameworks struggle to foster complex multi-step reasoning and effective external tool integration, often limited to single-round interactions.

## Method

- A fully autonomous, iterative ==co-evolutionary framework== where a ==Curriculum Agent== generates increasingly challenging tasks and an Executor Agent learns to solve them, both initialized from a base LLM.
- Seamless integration of an external code interpreter tool, where the Curriculum Agent is explicitly rewarded for generating tasks that require tool use, establishing a "virtuous cycle."
- ==Ambiguity-Dynamic Policy Optimization (ADPO)== for Executor training, which employs ambiguity-aware advantage scaling and dynamically modulated trust regions to handle noisy pseudo-labels from self-generated data.

## Results

- Agent0 achieved an **18%** average improvement in mathematical reasoning and a **24%** boost in general-domain reasoning on Qwen3-8B-Base models, consistently outperforming existing self-evolutionary and tool-integrated baselines.
- The co-evolutionary loop demonstrated stable, progressive performance improvement over multiple iterations, with the curriculum agent successfully generating more difficult, tool-reliant tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of Curriculum Agent training, the explicit tool reward, repetition penalty, ADPO, and multi-turn reasoning for the framework's overall effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A symbiotic co-evolutionary process between a task-generating Curriculum Agent and a task-solving Executor Agent can drive continuous, autonomous improvement from zero human data.
> - Deeply integrating external tools into the curriculum generation process itself creates a self-reinforcing cycle, leading to the discovery and mastery of increasingly complex, tool-aware reasoning skills.
> - Addressing ambiguity and noise in self-generated training data through mechanisms like ambiguity-aware advantage scaling and dynamic trust regions is crucial for stable and effective self-evolutionary learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM) Agents has rapidly advanced, showcasing remarkable abilities in tackling complex, long-horizon problems that necessitate extensive environmental interaction, from scientific research to agentic coding. Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), has emerged as a dominant paradigm for optimizing these multi-step interactions and enhancing reasoning capabilities.

However, the efficacy of these RL-based approaches is fundamentally tethered to the availability of massive, high-quality, human-curated datasets. This dependency presents a severe scalability bottleneck, as data annotation is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and costly. More critically, it limits the potential of AI to the boundaries of human knowledge and annotation speed, preventing models from autonomously discovering or mastering skills beyond human supervision.

In response to this limitation, self-evolution frameworks have gained traction as a promising alternative. These frameworks, including self-play and self-challenging approaches, aim to enable models to autonomously generate their own training data, offering a pathway to scalable improvement. Nevertheless, existing self-evolutionary methods face their own constraints:
1. **Capability Ceiling:** Their learning capabilities are often capped by the model's inherent knowledge and reasoning abilities. This results in generated tasks that rarely surpass the model’s current complexity, leading to "learning stagnation."
2. **Single-Round Interaction:** Many frameworks operate predominantly in single-round interactions, which fails to capture the dynamic, context-dependent nature of real-world problem-solving. This limitation particularly hinders the development of complex skills requiring tool use or multi-step reasoning.

Agent0 directly addresses these critical challenges, positioning itself as a significant advancement in the landscape of LLM agent development. It distinguishes itself by pioneering a fully autonomous framework that combines **multi-step co-evolution** with **seamless tool integration**. Unlike prior works that might use tools only for verification (e.g., Absolute Zero) or rely on external proprietary models for assistance (e.g., Socratic-Zero), Agent0 deeply integrates tool use into the *curriculum generation process itself*, creating a virtuous cycle where tool-augmented problem-solving drives the generation of increasingly complex, tool-aware tasks. This approach aims to break both the data dependency and the inherent capability ceiling, pushing the frontier of autonomous agent evolution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Agent0 is to introduce a novel, fully autonomous framework capable of evolving high-performing LLM agents entirely from scratch, **without any reliance on external human-curated data or annotations.**

This ambitious objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Overcoming Data Dependency and Scalability Bottlenecks:** Current state-of-the-art LLM agents, especially those trained with Reinforcement Learning (RL), heavily depend on massive, high-quality human-curated datasets. This reliance is a significant practical barrier, making training expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive. Agent0 seeks to eliminate this dependency, paving the way for truly scalable agent development.
- **Unleashing AI Beyond Human Knowledge:** By breaking free from human-curated data, Agent0 aims to untether the potential of AI from the limitations of human knowledge and annotation speed. This could enable agents to discover novel reasoning paths and master skills that are difficult or impossible for humans to explicitly teach or even conceive.
- **Addressing Learning Stagnation in Self-Evolution:** Existing self-evolution frameworks often suffer from "learning stagnation" because the tasks they generate rarely exceed the model's current capabilities. This limits continuous improvement. Agent0 is motivated to solve this by creating a dynamic, self-reinforcing curriculum that perpetually pushes the agent's frontier.
- **Enabling Complex Tool Use and Multi-Step Reasoning:** Many real-world problems require agents to engage in multi-step reasoning and integrate external tools (e.g., code interpreters). Traditional self-evolution frameworks, often limited to single-round interactions, struggle to cultivate these capabilities. Agent0 is motivated to build agents that can seamlessly perform tool-augmented, multi-turn interactions.
- **Boosting Core Reasoning Capabilities:** Ultimately, the framework is designed to substantially boost the reasoning capabilities of base LLMs across diverse domains, particularly in challenging areas like mathematical and general reasoning.

In essence, Agent0 is motivated by the vision of creating truly autonomous, self-improving AI agents that can continually expand their own intelligence and problem-solving prowess without explicit human intervention.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Agent0 introduces a novel, fully autonomous, and iterative co-evolutionary framework designed to enhance LLM agent capabilities from scratch, completely devoid of human-annotated data. The core of this approach lies in a symbiotic competition between two functionally distinct agents, both initialized from the same base LLM: a **Curriculum Agent (π_θ)** and an **Executor Agent (π_ϕ)**.

The framework operates through an iterative loop, where each iteration `t` consists of two main stages:

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

- **Initialization:** Both the Curriculum Agent (π_θ^(0)) and the Executor Agent (π_ϕ^(0)) are initialized from the same base LLM (π_base).
- **Co-evolutionary Loop:**
 1. **Curriculum Evolution:** The Curriculum Agent (π_θ) is trained using Reinforcement Learning (RL) to generate increasingly challenging "frontier tasks" for the current Executor Agent (π_ϕ^(t-1)).
 2. **Executor Evolution:** The frozen Curriculum Agent (π_θ^(t)) is then used to generate a pool of tasks. From this pool, a challenging subset is filtered, and the Executor Agent (π_ϕ) is trained via RL on this dataset to solve these problems, evolving into π_ϕ^(t).

This iterative process is critically enhanced by the seamless integration of an external code interpreter tool. The Executor Agent's problem-solving capacity is boosted by this tool, which, in turn, compels the Curriculum Agent to generate more complex, tool-based curricula. This establishes a "virtuous cycle," driving synchronous improvement in both agent capabilities and curriculum complexity. Furthermore, Agent0 extends this paradigm to support multi-turn interactions, allowing for the generation of context-rich, conversational tasks that better reflect real-world problem-solving.

#### 3.2. Curriculum Agent Training (π_θ)

The Curriculum Agent's objective is to generate prompts (`x`) that maximize a composite reward signal (`R_C`), which quantifies the challenge of task `x` for the current Executor Agent (π_ϕ). Training is performed using the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm, a PPO-style method that avoids training a critic by using intra-group relative rewards.

The composite reward `R_C` for a generated task `x_i` is calculated by having the Executor Agent (π_ϕ) sample `k` responses, `{y_j}^k_{j=1}`. `R_C` comprises three key components:

- **Uncertainty Reward (`R_unc`):** This reward incentivizes the Curriculum Agent to generate tasks that the Executor finds challenging or ambiguous. It is based on the Executor's self-consistency (`ˆp(x; π_ϕ)`), defined as the proportion of `k` responses that agree with the majority answer. `R_unc` is maximized when `ˆp = 0.5` (highest uncertainty) using the formula `1 - 2 |ˆp(x; π_ϕ) - 0.5|`. This penalizes tasks that are either too easy or too hard.
- **Tool Use Reward (`R_tool`):** To drive the virtuous cycle, `R_tool` explicitly rewards tasks that prompt the Executor to use its external tool (e.g., code interpreter). It is proportional to the number of tool invocations within a response (`N_tool(y)`), with a scaling hyperparameter `γ` and a cap `C` to prevent excessive tool calls: `γ ⋅ min(N_tool(y), C)`.
- **Repetition Penalty (`R_rep`):** To ensure curriculum diversity and prevent the Curriculum Agent from generating highly similar tasks, `R_rep` penalizes tasks based on their similarity to others within the same training batch, computed using BLEU score and cluster size. `λ_rep ⋅ |C_k| / B`.

The final composite reward `R_C(x_i)` combines these signals, subtracting the repetition penalty, and is gated by a format check `R_format`: `R_format(x_i) ⋅ max(0, (λ_unc R_unc + λ_tool R_tool) - R_rep(x_i))`.

#### 3.3. Executor Agent Training (π_ϕ)

The Executor Agent's objective is to maximize its success rate in solving tasks generated by the Curriculum Agent. This stage also leverages an RL-based approach, but with crucial modifications to account for the inherent ambiguity of self-generated data.

##### 3.3.1. Dataset Curation and Trajectory Generation

- **Challenging Dataset Construction:** After the Curriculum Agent (π_θ^(t)) is trained and frozen, it generates a large pool of candidate tasks (`X_pool`). For each task `x`, the *previous* Executor Agent (π_ϕ^(t-1)) samples `k` responses, and its self-consistency `ˆp(x)` is calculated (proportion of responses voting for the majority answer `˜y`). To create an efficient training curriculum, only tasks whose `ˆp(x)` falls within an "informative band" (e.g., between 0.3 and 0.8) are retained, ensuring they are appropriately challenging: `D^(t) = {x ∈ X_pool | |ˆp(x; π_ϕ^(t-1)) - 0.5| ≤ δ}`.
- **Multi-Turn Rollout with Tool Integration:** Instead of standard single-turn generation, Agent0 employs a multi-step, tool-integrated rollout. For each trajectory, the Executor policy generates natural language reasoning. Upon emitting a tool-call trigger (e.g., ````python...````), generation pauses, the code is executed in a sandbox, and the `output...` feedback is prepended back to the policy. This iterative process allows the agent to refine its reasoning and self-correct, producing a complete, hybrid reasoning trajectory. The majority answer (`˜y`) from the `k` trajectories serves as the pseudo-label, and a terminal reward `R_i = I(o_i = ˜y)` is assigned to each trajectory based on whether its final answer matches `˜y`.

##### 3.3.2. Ambiguity-Dynamic Policy Optimization (ADPO)

Standard GRPO treats all training samples equally, which can be problematic with noisy pseudo-labels from majority voting in self-evolution. ADPO addresses this with two key modifications:

- **Ambiguity-Aware Advantage Scaling:** For tasks with high ambiguity (low `ˆp(x)`), pseudo-labels are prone to errors. To prevent overfitting to potentially inaccurate labels, ADPO scales the normalized advantage (`ˆA_i`) by `s(x) = f(ˆp(x))`, where `f` is an increasing function of self-consistency. This down-weights the training signal from unreliable samples.
- **Ambiguity-Modulated Trust Regions:** Standard PPO-style clipping bounds can stifle exploration by clamping the growth of unlikely tokens, which is detrimental for ambiguous tasks where the correct reasoning might require significant updates. ADPO dynamically modulates the upper clipping bound (`ϵ_high(x)`) as a decreasing function of `ˆp(x)`. This relaxes constraints for ambiguous inputs, allowing larger gradient steps to uplift potential low-probability solutions, while maintaining tighter bounds on confident samples.

The Executor Agent is updated by minimizing the ADPO objective, which incorporates these modifications to the standard GRPO loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Agent0 consistently demonstrates substantial improvements across various reasoning benchmarks, significantly outperforming existing self-evolutionary and tool-integrated baselines.

#### 4.1. Comprehensive Performance Gains

- **Mathematical Reasoning (Table 1):** Agent0 substantially boosts mathematical reasoning capabilities. On the Qwen3-8B-Base model, it achieves an 18% improvement on average across a suite of benchmarks including AMC, Minerva, MATH, GSM8K, Olympiad-Bench, AIME25, and AIME24. For instance, on Qwen3-8B-Base, Agent0 reached an average score of 58.2, significantly surpassing the base model's 49.2.
- **General-Domain Reasoning (Table 2):** The framework also exhibits strong generalization capabilities. Agent0 improves general reasoning performance by 24% on benchmarks like SuperGPQA, MMLU-Pro, and BBEH. On Qwen3-8B-Base, Agent0 attained an overall average score of 42.1, compared to the base model's 34.5.

#### 4.2. Superiority Over Baselines

Agent0 consistently outperforms all compared state-of-the-art self-improvement methods across both Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base models.
- On Qwen3-8B-Base, Agent0 surpasses the powerful data-free method R-Zero by 6.4% on mathematical reasoning and outcompetes the self-play method Absolute Zero (which also uses a code executor for verification) by 10.6%.
- It even exceeds Socratic-Zero, a method relying on external OpenAI APIs for reasoning assistance, by 3.7%, demonstrating the efficacy of its fully autonomous approach.

#### 4.3. Validation of the Co-Evolutionary Loop and Tool Integration

- **Consistent Improvement (Figure 4, Table 10, 11):** The method exhibits stable and progressive performance improvement over multiple co-evolutionary iterations. For Qwen3-8B-Base, the average math score increased from 55.1 (Iteration 1) to 58.2 (Iteration 3), and general-domain tasks showed a similar upward trend, validating the continuous learning mechanism.
- **Evolution of Task Difficulty and Tool Use (Table 5, Figure 5):** Analysis of tasks generated by the curriculum agent across iterations shows a clear increase in difficulty. The pass rate of a fixed Executor Agent (from Iteration 1) progressively decreases when evaluated on tasks from later iterations, confirming the curriculum's adaptation. Crucially, the average number of tool calls per generated task steadily increases, directly demonstrating that the `R_tool` reward successfully guides the curriculum agent to generate more complex, tool-reliant problems, thereby driving the virtuous cycle.
- **Strategic Tool Integration Matters (Table 4):** Merely providing a tool (Base Model w/ tool) yields only a modest performance boost. Agent0's significant gains highlight that its strategic integration—where the curriculum agent explicitly learns to incentivize tool use through `R_tool` and the executor performs multi-step, tool-augmented reasoning—is far more effective than simply having access to a tool or relying on non-tool methods.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Confirm Component Effectiveness (Table 3, 9)

Ablation studies confirm the necessity and effectiveness of each proposed component:
- **Curriculum Agent Training:** Essential, as removing it leads to a 9.3% performance drop.
- **Tool Reward:** Critical for the virtuous cycle, its absence results in a 7.2% performance degradation.
- **Repetition Penalty:** Crucial for curriculum diversity, preventing learning stagnation.
- **ADPO (Ambiguity Scaling):** Improves performance by 1.9% by effectively handling noisy pseudo-labels and fostering exploration.
- **Multi-Turn Reasoning:** Significantly boosts performance (e.g., 3.4% overall for 4 turns vs. 1 turn), especially for complex mathematical reasoning, by enabling the generation of more context-dependent and multi-step tasks.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Insights (Figure 5)

Qualitative analysis reveals that the Curriculum Agent effectively progresses from generating basic geometry problems in early iterations to complex constraint satisfaction tasks in later ones. Concurrently, the Executor Agent demonstrates reliable problem-solving, effectively combining natural language reasoning with Python code execution for verification, showcasing its ability to handle hybrid reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Agent0 represents a significant leap forward in the development of autonomous and scalable AI agents, with profound potential implications across several dimensions:

- **Breaking the Data Dependency Barrier:** The most critical impact of Agent0 is its complete elimination of reliance on human-curated data for training. This addresses a fundamental bottleneck in the scalability and cost-effectiveness of developing advanced LLM agents. By allowing agents to evolve from "zero data," Agent0 opens up possibilities for AI systems to operate and improve in domains where human annotation is scarce, impossible, or prohibitively expensive.
- **Towards True Autonomous Learning and AGI:** By establishing a self-reinforcing co-evolutionary loop between a curriculum agent and an executor agent, Agent0 moves beyond the limitations of human-supervised learning. The continuous generation of increasingly challenging, tool-aware tasks, coupled with the executor's improving problem-solving capabilities, creates a virtuous cycle that prevents learning stagnation. This mechanism is a key step towards achieving more generalized and autonomously evolving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
- **Enhanced Reasoning and Generalization:** The substantial empirical improvements in both mathematical and general-domain reasoning benchmarks underscore Agent0's ability to cultivate sophisticated problem-solving skills. The demonstrated generalization across diverse tasks suggests that the learned reasoning patterns are robust and transferable, making the agents more versatile.
- **Innovative Methodology in RL for LLMs:** The novel components of Agent0, including the dual-agent co-evolution, the explicit reward for tool use in curriculum generation, and the Ambiguity-Dynamic Policy Optimization (ADPO) for handling noisy pseudo-labels and modulating exploration, contribute new techniques to the field of reinforcement learning for large language models. ADPO, in particular, offers a practical solution to the challenges of self-generated data.
- **Facilitating Complex Problem Solving:** The seamless integration of external tools, such as a code interpreter, within the learning loop, significantly enhances the agents' ability to tackle complex, multi-step problems that require external computation or verification. This mirrors human problem-solving, where external aids are often indispensable.
- **Reduced Development Costs and Increased Accessibility:** By automating the data generation and curriculum design process, Agent0 could drastically reduce the development costs and time associated with training highly capable LLM agents. This could make advanced AI agent technology more accessible to a wider range of researchers and practitioners, fostering innovation.

In summary, Agent0 provides a compelling blueprint for how LLM agents can achieve continuous self-improvement, overcome traditional data dependencies, and progressively master complex reasoning tasks by strategically integrating tools into a self-evolving curriculum. Its impact could reshape the trajectory of agent development, paving the way for more autonomous, capable, and general-purpose AI systems.
