# DivScene: Towards Open-Vocabulary Object Navigation with Large Vision Language Models in Diverse Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced DIVSCENE, a large-scale dataset featuring 81 diverse scene types and over 5,700 unique target object categories, to benchmark open-vocabulary object navigation. Their fine-tuned Idefics 2-based NATVLM, employing Chain-of-Thought explanations, achieved a 56.17% success rate, outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o by over 20 percentage points.

## Problem

- Existing object navigation datasets (e.g., Matterport-3D, ProcTHOR) suffered from limited diversity in scenes and object categories, restricting the training and evaluation of agents for real-world scenarios.
- The full potential of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for open-vocabulary object navigation in complex, diverse embodied environments remained largely unexplored due to the lack of suitable comprehensive datasets.
- Developing effective methodologies to enable LVLMs to translate their general visual and linguistic understanding into robust navigation policies in diverse, unseen environments posed a challenge.

## Method

- ==Created DIVSCENE==, a large-scale dataset comprising 4,614 houses across 81 distinct scene types, and DIVSCENE_ep, consisting of approximately 23,000 shortest-path episodes for open-vocabulary object navigation.
- Developed NATVLM (Navigational Chain-of-Thought VLM), a fine-tuned Idefics 2 (8B) model, using imitation learning on BFS-generated shortest-path demonstrations from DIVSCENE_ep.
- Integrated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanations into NATVLM's training responses, guiding the LVLM through structured reasoning steps for action prediction based on target object, agent position, and visual observations.

## Results

- NATVLM achieved an average Success Rate (SR) of **56.17%** on DIVSCENE_ep, outperforming the best off-the-shelf proprietary model (GPT-4o) by over 20 percentage points (SR **37.66%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that CoT explanations are critical, as fine-tuning without them resulted in a drastic performance drop (SR **28.09%**) for the Idefics 2-based model.
- NATVLM demonstrated strong few-shot learning capabilities and exhibited robust zero-shot transferability, outperforming all baselines on unseen datasets like iTHOR (SR **72.79%**) and ProcTHOR (SR **53.12%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing a significantly more diverse dataset, such as DIVSCENE, is essential for advancing research in open-vocabulary object navigation and evaluating the generalization capabilities of embodied AI agents.
> - Chain-of-Thought explanations are crucial for enhancing LVLMs' ability to reason about embodied navigation tasks, leading to substantial performance improvements over models without such structured guidance.
> - Fine-tuning LVLMs with imitation learning on computationally generated shortest paths offers an efficient and scalable method to develop robust navigation agents without relying on costly human demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI involves developing intelligent agents that can perceive, understand, and interact with physical environments. Object navigation, a core task within embodied AI, requires an agent to locate and navigate to a specified target object within a 3D environment. Historically, much of the research in object navigation has focused on "closed-vocabulary" settings, where agents are tasked with finding objects from a limited, predefined set of categories. Existing benchmarks, such as Matterport-3D (Chang et al., 2017) and ProcTHOR (Deitke et al., 2022), typically feature a small number of target object types (e.g., 21 in Matterport-3D, 16 in ProcTHOR) and a restricted diversity of indoor scenes (e.g., 90 private homes or four room types). While HM3D-OVON (Yokoyama et al., 2024b) attempted to expand this to 379 object categories across 181 scenes, a significant gap remained for truly "open-vocabulary" navigation in highly diverse environments.

In parallel, Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in various visual and language understanding tasks, including visual question answering and document comprehension. These foundation models are increasingly being explored for embodied tasks, with some methods employing them for perception, planning, or object grounding. However, their potential to effectively comprehend and navigate embodied environments with a wide, unrestricted range of objects and scene types has been largely underexplored due to the aforementioned limitations in available datasets.

This work addresses this gap by introducing the DIVSCENE dataset, which offers a substantially greater diversity of scenes and target objects than prior benchmarks. By providing a more comprehensive testbed, DIVSCENE enables a thorough evaluation of LVLMs and LLMs in open-vocabulary object navigation. Furthermore, the research explores fine-tuning strategies for LVLMs, integrating imitation learning with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanations, to enhance their navigational capabilities in these complex and diverse environments, thereby advancing the application of large foundation models in embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to study the challenge of open-vocabulary object navigation and evaluate the performance of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) in this context. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Limitations of Existing Navigation Benchmarks:** Current object navigation datasets are characterized by a limited diversity of scenes and target objects. For instance, Matterport-3D considers only 21 object types across 90 homes, and ProcTHOR features 16 object types in only four room categories (bedroom, living room, kitchen, bathroom). While some datasets have expanded object categories, the overall number of distinct scene types and the sheer variety of objects within those scenes remain constrained. This "closed-vocabulary" nature of existing benchmarks restricts the ability to comprehensively evaluate or train agents for real-world scenarios, where objects and environments are far more diverse and often encountered without prior explicit definition. The motivation here is to overcome this limitation by creating a dataset that reflects the complexity and diversity of real-world embodied environments.

2. **Exploring the Potential of Large Vision-Language Models in Embodied Environments:** LVLMs and LLMs have achieved significant advancements in various vision-language tasks, demonstrating sophisticated comprehension abilities. However, their capacity to interpret and navigate within embodied environments, especially those featuring a wide array of unseen or diverse objects and scene types, remains underexplored. The lack of suitable datasets has hindered a thorough investigation into how these powerful models can translate their general visual and linguistic understanding into effective embodied navigation policies. This research is motivated by the desire to assess and unlock the potential of LVLMs in understanding and acting within complex 3D environments.

3. **Developing Effective Strategies for Open-Vocabulary Object Navigation:** Beyond evaluating existing models, a core motivation is to develop and test methodologies that can enhance the navigation capabilities of LVLMs in open-vocabulary settings. Current models, when tested on diverse environments, often perform inadequately, highlighting a need for tailored training approaches. The research is motivated to investigate if fine-tuning LVLMs with structured guidance, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanations derived from efficient shortest-path demonstrations, can bridge the gap between their general language and vision understanding and the specific requirements of embodied navigation. The aim is to create a model that can robustly infer actions in novel situations and generalize across diverse scenes and object types.

In summary, the research is motivated by the recognized limitations in existing embodied navigation benchmarks and the desire to leverage and enhance the capabilities of advanced foundation models (LVLMs/LLMs) for more practical and generalizable open-vocabulary object navigation in diverse 3D environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research introduces a new task, dataset, and a fine-tuned LVLM-based agent for open-vocabulary object navigation.

#### 3.1. Task Definition

The open-vocabulary object navigation task requires an embodied agent to navigate to a target object of a given category within an environment, where object categories are not restricted to a predefined set. Episodes are defined by a house, an initial agent position and rotation, a target object category, and its location. At each time step, the agent receives an egocentric RGB image and its current status (position and rotation) and predicts the next action. The action space consists of `MOVEAHEAD` (0.25m), `ROTATERIGHT` (90 degrees clockwise), `ROTATELEFT` (90 degrees counter-clockwise), and `DONE`. An episode is successful if the agent takes the `DONE` action or reaches the maximum action limit (200 steps) and the distance to the target object is below 1.5 meters. Environments are discretized into 0.25 x 0.25-meter grid maps.

#### 3.2. Data Collection for DIVSCENE and DIVSCENE_ep

To address the lack of diversity in existing datasets, the authors created DIVSCENE, a large-scale scene dataset, and DIVSCENE_ep, a dataset of shortest-path episodes within these scenes.

- **Scene Collection for DIVSCENE:**
 - **Framework:** The Holodeck framework (Yang et al., 2024) was utilized to automatically build scenes from textual house descriptions, leveraging GPT-4 for layout, styles, and object selection.
 - **Scene Types:** 81 distinct scene types were compiled across five categories (Store, Home, Public spaces, Leisure, Working place), expanding upon the MIT Scenes Dataset (Quattoni and Torralba, 2009).
 - **House Descriptions:** GPT-4 was prompted using an in-context learning setup with five exemplars to compose diverse house descriptions. This involved randomly sampling 1-3 from 12 predefined house attributes (e.g., room style, lighting, objects in the room) and having GPT-4 assign values to them, then generating a description incorporating the scene type and attributes.
 - **Filtering:** Strict data filtering was applied, including a ROUGE-L similarity threshold of 0.8 to ensure description diversity, and checks for correct parsing of attribute values and presence of a house description in GPT-4's output.
 - **Result:** This process yielded 4,614 houses on the AI2THOR platform (Kolve et al., 2017).

- **Episode Collection for DIVSCENE_ep:**
 - **Sampling Strategy:** For each house, the environment was discretized into a 0.25 x 0.25-meter grid map. An initial agent position was randomly sampled from reachable grid points. A target object was randomly selected from all available objects, with a height constraint (0.3m-2.0m) and a mechanism to encourage object type diversity across episodes within the same house.
 - **Shortest Path Generation:** Breadth-First Search (BFS) was employed to find the shortest path from the agent's initial position to the grid point nearest the target object. The BFS planner was designed to minimize rotations, assigning higher costs to rotation changes.
 - **Action Sequence and Observations:** From the shortest path, a sequence of actions (`MOVEAHEAD`, `ROTATERIGHT`, `ROTATELEFT`) was derived. Observations (agent status and egocentric RGB images) were collected at each step by executing these actions in AI2THOR.
 - **Result:** DIVSCENE_ep comprises 22,962 shortest-path episodes, featuring 5,707 distinct target object types and 22,696 overall object types. The dataset is split into training (4,506 houses), validation (27 houses), and test (81 houses) sets, with 5 episodes per house for training and 4 for evaluation.

#### 3.3. NATVLM Model Development

The proposed NATVLM (Navigational Chain-of-Thought VLM) is a fine-tuned Idefics 2 (8B) model designed to predict the next action.

- **Base Model and Learning:** Idefics 2 (Laurençon et al., 2024) was selected as the base LVLM. It was fine-tuned using imitation learning (Behavior Cloning) to mimic the shortest-path demonstrations from DIVSCENE_ep. The objective was to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the expert actions given the environment observations.
- **Instruction Compilation:** For each time step, an instruction was manually constructed, comprising four parts:
 1. A brief introduction to the navigation task, including possible actions and step lengths.
 2. Episode-specific information: target object type, its position, agent's current position and rotation, and the visual observation.
 3. History of recent steps: Agent's positions and actions for the past 8 steps, along with visual observations for the past 4 steps.
 4. Prediction request: Instructing the model to predict the next action, considering current observation and history, in accordance with a CoT explanation.
- **Response with CoT Explanation:** To enhance the LVLM's understanding of navigation rationale, responses included manually crafted Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanation traces. These traces guided the model through three reasoning steps:
 1. Comparing current agent position to the target to determine if forward movement is needed.
 2. Checking for obstacles in the visual observation that might necessitate rotation.
 3. Deciding the final action based on the preceding analyses.
 Specific templates were developed for `MOVEAHEAD`, `DONE`, and three scenarios of `ROTATE` actions (distance change, obstacle avoidance, view adjustment). Position information was filled with coordinates.
- **Data Postprocessing:** To mitigate data imbalance (77.94% `MOVEAHEAD` actions in raw data), `MOVEAHEAD` actions were downsampled to 25%. Conflicting data points (identical input leading to different ground-truth actions, likely from overlapping paths to different targets) were also removed to prevent model confusion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding open-vocabulary object navigation, the DIVSCENE dataset, and the performance of various models, particularly NATVLM.

#### 4.1. Dataset Diversity and Characteristics

The DIVSCENE dataset comprises 4,614 houses across 81 distinct scene types, categorized into five groups (Store, Home, Public Spaces, Leisure, Working Place). The DIVSCENE_ep dataset contains approximately 23,000 shortest-path episodes, featuring 5,707 unique target object types from a total of 22,696 object types available in the scenes. This diversity significantly surpasses existing object navigation benchmarks like iTHOR and ProcTHOR, which have fewer scene types (4 for both) and substantially fewer object types (38-116). The average number of objects and object types per house in DIVSCENE is also higher, indicating more complex environments.

#### 4.2. Baseline Performance Evaluation

Various methods, including blind LLMs, LLMs with image captioning, open-source LVLMs, and proprietary LVLMs (API-based), were benchmarked on DIVSCENE_ep using Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), and Success weighted by Episode Length (SEL).
- **Blind LLMs:** These models (e.g., Llama 2, Llama 3.1, Mistral) performed slightly above a random baseline (8.03% SR), achieving SRs typically between 8-12%. This indicates their limited ability to navigate without visual information.
- **LLMs with Image Captions:** Adding image captions (generated by Llava 1.5) to LLM inputs marginally improved performance (SRs between 9-14%). This suggests that simple captioning may miss critical visual details necessary for navigation.
- **Open-Source LVLMs:** Models like Qwen-VL, Llava 1.5, Idefics 2, and InternVL3, which natively process images, showed better performance than LLM-based approaches. Idefics 2 (8B) achieved the highest among this group with an SR of 20.99%, indicating a foundational capacity for visual navigation.
- **API-based LVLMs:** Proprietary models like GPT-4v and GPT-4o demonstrated the best performance among off-the-shelf models, with GPT-4o achieving an SR of 37.66%. While superior to other baselines, this performance was noted as "far from adequate for real-world applications."

#### 4.3. NATVLM Performance

The fine-tuned NATVLM model, based on Idefics 2, significantly outperformed all baselines. It achieved an average SR of 56.17%, an SPL of 46.15%, and an SEL of 46.86% across the validation and test sets. This represents an approximately 20% increase in success rate compared to GPT-4o, the highest-performing off-the-shelf model. NATVLM also demonstrated better efficiency in reaching targets, as indicated by its higher SPL and SEL scores.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on CoT Explanation Traces

- **Efficacy of CoT:** Fine-tuning Idefics 2 without CoT explanation traces (`⋄ w/o ET`) resulted in a drastic performance drop (SR 28.09%), similar to the performance of off-the-shelf Idefics 2. This confirms that CoT explanations are crucial for helping LVLMs understand the underlying rationale of object navigation.
- **Impact of Gold Positional Labels:** When gold labels for positional differences were provided to the model *without* CoT (`⋄ w/o ET & w Gold`), performance only fluctuated slightly (SR 29.78%), indicating that CoT's benefit is beyond just providing accurate coordinate information. When gold labels were provided *with* CoT (`⋄ w Gold Label`), performance improved further (SR 61.11%), suggesting that the model's self-generated positional difference computations within CoT might sometimes contain errors.
- **Difference-Equation Prompt:** Using a difference-equation prompt format for positional comparison (`⋄ w Diff-EQ`) led to only minor variations in performance (SR 54.78%), indicating it did not significantly enhance reasoning beyond the default CoT structure.

#### 4.5. Design Investigations

- **Number of Recent Images:** Providing NATVLM with four recent images was found to be optimal. Using only two images led to a performance decline (SR 50%), while increasing to six or eight images did not yield further improvements.
- **Number of Recent Steps (History):** Providing information from eight recent steps (positions and actions) resulted in optimal performance. Reducing to four steps decreased performance (SR 46.14%), while increasing to 12 or 16 steps provided limited additional benefits.

#### 4.6. Few-Shot Learning Evaluation

NATVLM exhibited strong few-shot learning capabilities. With only 20% of the training data, its performance (SR 38.89%) was comparable to or slightly surpassed that of GPT-4o. Performance progressively improved as the training data volume increased, with a plateau observed around 80% of the full dataset, demonstrating its ability to generalize effectively even with reduced training data.

#### 4.7. Zero-Shot Transferring Evaluation

NATVLM demonstrated strong generalization abilities by successfully transferring to unseen datasets: iTHOR, ProcTHOR, and HM3D. It significantly outperformed all baseline models (open-source VLMs and ablated NATVLM versions) on these datasets, with an SR of 72.79% on iTHOR and 53.12% on ProcTHOR, indicating its robustness in diverse environments beyond its training distribution.

#### 4.8. Case Study (Limitations)

Error analysis revealed that NATVLM occasionally struggled with long navigation trajectories and extensive exploration, sometimes meandering in limited areas instead of heading directly to distant goals. This suggests limitations in handling long-term planning and exploration behaviors, potentially due to the finite context window for historical information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI and large model applications in several significant ways:

1. **Establishment of a Comprehensive Benchmark:** The introduction of DIVSCENE and DIVSCENE_ep addresses a critical gap in embodied AI research. By providing a large-scale dataset with 4,614 diverse houses, 81 scene types, and 5,707 distinct target object categories, this work offers an unprecedentedly comprehensive benchmark for open-vocabulary object navigation. This dataset will facilitate more realistic and challenging evaluations of embodied agents, pushing the boundaries beyond closed-vocabulary and limited-scene settings.

2. **Advancement in LVLM Embodied Navigation:** The study empirically demonstrates that while off-the-shelf LVLMs and LLMs show limited capabilities in open-vocabulary object navigation, fine-tuning an LVLM with imitation learning on BFS-generated shortest paths, combined with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanations, can substantially improve performance. The NATVLM model's significant lead (over 20% higher success rate than GPT-4o) establishes a new baseline and highlights the effectiveness of structured reasoning guidance for embodied tasks.

3. **Efficient and Scalable Training Paradigm:** The methodology of using BFS-generated shortest paths for imitation learning, without requiring human supervision or costly human demonstrations, offers a highly efficient and economical training paradigm. This approach reduces the resource burden typically associated with collecting expert demonstrations for embodied tasks, making it more scalable for developing robust navigation agents.

4. **Robust Generalization Capabilities:** NATVLM's strong performance in few-shot learning and its impressive zero-shot transferability to unseen datasets (iTHOR, ProcTHOR, HM3D) indicate its robustness and ability to generalize across novel environments and object instances. This generalization is crucial for real-world deployment, where agents must operate in diverse and unpredictable settings.

5. **Insights into LVLM Reasoning for Embodiment:** The ablation studies on CoT explanations provide valuable insights into how LVLMs can be guided to understand the rationale behind navigation actions. The significant performance drop when CoT is removed underscores its role in enabling deeper reasoning, rather than just superficial pattern matching, for embodied tasks. This suggests a promising direction for future research in enhancing the interpretability and decision-making processes of embodied AI.

6. **Identification of Future Research Directions:** The explicit discussion of NATVLM's limitations, particularly concerning long navigation horizons and complex exploration, provides clear avenues for future work. This includes improving LVLMs' memory, long-context understanding, and advanced exploration strategies to address complex, multi-room, or obstructed navigation challenges.

In summary, this research not only provides a critical dataset for the embodied AI community but also demonstrates an effective approach to leverage and enhance LVLMs for open-vocabulary navigation, pushing the field closer to agents capable of operating robustly in diverse, real-world environments.
