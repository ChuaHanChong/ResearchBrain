# G-MAPP: GPU-accelerated Multi-Agent Planning and Perception for Reactive Motion Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> G-MAPP is a GPU-accelerated framework coupling perception and reactive planning for robot manipulators. A CUDA perception pipeline fuses multi-camera point clouds into a sphere-world model, while a GPU-parallelized multi-agent vector-field planner rolls out heterogeneous heuristic agents in task space and selects the lowest-cost one. It delivers up to 5x speedup over CPU and real-time collision avoidance on a 7-DoF Franka arm.

## Problem

- Sampling-based planners give probabilistic guarantees but scale poorly in narrow-passage and geometry-constrained scenes, sacrificing the runtime needed for reactive control.
- Locally reactive potential and circular field methods run fast but get trapped in local minima and fail on non-convex geometries and dynamic obstacles.
- Reactive planners rely on slow, decoupled perception; dense distance queries force conservative obstacle assumptions or restricted task-space exploration under real-time budgets.

## Method

- GPU perception pipeline fuses multi-view depth clouds, filters the robot via forward-kinematics sphere sets, and voxelizes into a ==sphere-world== of spherical primitives resident in GPU DRAM.
- SIMT force kernel assigns one obstacle per thread, computing ==APF== or magnetic-field-inspired repulsion with shared-memory block reduction and atomic accumulation into a net repulsive vector.
- ==Multi-agent task-space planner== spawns heterogeneous agents with per-heuristic CUDA streams, integrating geometric-algebra pose attractors and selecting the best rollout by a four-term cost.

## Results

- GPU planner cut per-step computation at least **2x** versus CPU, reaching about **5x** in the Cluttered scene (**405 ms** versus 1599 ms at 200 planning steps), with near-zero variance.
- Faster cycles raised success rates: Trap improved from **40% to 70%** and Passage from **70% to 100%** under identical agent configurations and cost logic.
- Heterogeneous ensembles beat single-heuristic baselines, with **73.3%** versus 66.7% success on Opening2 and **93.3%** versus 80.0% on the dynamic Complex scene; APF alone failed everywhere.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Faster planning cycles alone improve success rates without algorithmic change, because more frequent cost evaluations let the planner switch strategies before constraints bind.
> - Sphere-world voxel abstraction yields uniform repulsive forces, avoiding the motion distortion that raw point clouds cause through uneven local point density.
> - Prefer per-heuristic specialized kernels over one bulk kernel: warp homogeneity and smaller code footprints preserve occupancy when agent behaviors are heterogeneous.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic systems operating in dynamic, unstructured, and collaborative environments require the ability to generate collision-free motions reactively. This capability is challenged by the computational demands of high-fidelity environmental representations and the need for tight integration between perception and planning modules.

Existing approaches to motion generation can be broadly categorized into two main types:
- **Sampling-based Motion Planners (SBMPs):** Methods such as RRT and PRM offer probabilistic completeness and can handle high-DOF systems. However, they typically struggle with real-time performance in narrow passages or complex, geometry-aware environments due to the computational cost of state-space exploration and collision checking. Recent work in SBMPs has focused on accelerating internal algorithmic primitives to improve performance.
- **Locally Reactive or Dynamical Methods:** Inspired by physical analogies (e.g., Artificial Potential Fields - APF, Circular Fields - CF), these methods prioritize real-time performance and safety by directly computing control laws with low computational overhead. This makes them suitable for instantaneous reactions to dynamic environments. A known limitation is the problem of local minima, where the robot can become trapped and fail to reach its goal. Recent advancements aim to extend these methods to achieve quasi-global trajectory planning, often through multi-agent strategies for operational space exploration.

A significant bottleneck across both categories, particularly for reactive methods, is the perception pipeline. Traditional perception systems are often slow and decoupled from planning, making it computationally expensive to compute distances to numerous obstacles or dense point clouds. This forces a trade-off: planners either restrict exploration, sacrifice real-time performance, or simplify the environment model with conservative assumptions. Moreover, effective integration of perception with planning is crucial, as planning methods validated in abstraction may fail in practical deployments without adequate sensor coupling. While some systems use implicit world models, others explicitly construct environment models. The design of an optimal perception module, characterized by low end-to-end latency, tight system integration, and sufficient representational fidelity, remains an active area of research.

The present work, G-MAPP, addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that specifically targets the computational performance bottleneck and the temporal integration gap between perception and planning. It aims to achieve reactive motion generation in high-fidelity dynamic environments without compromising on real-time performance or the richness of world representations. By accelerating both world modeling and vector-field-based planning using GPU technology, the authors seek to enable faster parallel state exploration for quasi-global trajectory planning and tighter coupling of the perception-action loop, distinguishing it from prior work that focused on GPU-accelerated collision avoidance but not the full perception-to-planning integration for multi-agent reactive control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the ongoing challenge of enabling robotic systems to generate reactive motions in unstructured and dynamic environments. The authors identify the core impediments as the high runtime performance demand of planning in high-fidelity environments and the insufficient temporal integration between perception and planning modules. Traditional "sense-plan-act" paradigms, while effective for static scenes, lack the reactivity required for human-centered or rapidly changing scenarios.

The overarching objective of this work is to develop a novel reactive planning architecture that effectively couples perception, planning, and action within a real-time framework. This involves leveraging GPU acceleration to manage computational demands, thereby allowing for the use of detailed world representations and complex planning strategies.

The paper articulates three distinct sub-problems that are considered essential for achieving real-world reactive motion generation:

- **Problem P1 (Perception Constraints):** This problem focuses on the requirements for a perception pipeline suitable for reactive motion planning. The pipeline must possess:
 - **Modularity:** Ability to integrate with various reactive planners and additional sensors.
 - **Occlusion-free environment sensing:** Comprehensive world understanding.
 - **High-fidelity representation:** Detailed and accurate environmental modeling.
 - **Low spatio-temporal artifacts and noise:** Clean data for robust planning.
 - **Low-latency processing time:** Essential for real-time reactivity.
 The motivation here is that existing perception systems often fail to meet all these criteria simultaneously, leading to compromises in planning robustness or performance.

- **Problem P2 (Control Law Time-costs):** This problem addresses the computational budget for reactive planners. The planner must be capable of computing virtual forces from high-fidelity environmental representations within a strict time budget, specifically targeting a frequency of 100 Hz for real-time robot control. The motivation is that increasing scene complexity or representational fidelity often leads to an exponential increase in computation time for force calculations, hindering real-time control.

- **Problem P3 (Parallel Task-space exploration):** This problem aims to overcome the local minima issue inherent in many reactive methods by actively exploring the task space. The framework should allow for parallel consideration of alternate trajectories, with the computational cost remaining largely unaffected by scene complexity or the number of parallel explorations. This is motivated by the limitations of single-agent reactive methods that can get trapped in local minima, failing to reach the goal in complex environments.

In summary, the authors aim to bridge the gap in existing literature by demonstrating a GPU-accelerated perception-planning framework that satisfies online real-world manipulation demands in dynamic unstructured environments. This framework seeks to achieve a balance between modularity and tight system integration, thereby enabling continuous, reactive motion planning in cluttered and fast-changing scenarios using off-the-shelf depth sensors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The G-MAPP framework integrates a GPU-accelerated perception pipeline with a GPU-parallelized multi-agent task-space planner. The methodology is structured to address the identified problems of perception latency, control law computation time, and parallel task-space exploration.

**A. Approach Overview**
The framework processes observations from multiple sensors (P_W, fused point clouds) and transforms them into a compact geometric abstraction (S_W) consisting of spherical primitives. This "sphere-world approximation" represents the occupied task-space and enables the use of analytic potential-based vector fields.
Robot motion is guided by a net wrench (f_i) composed of:
- **Attractive potential (f_a):** A C1-differentiable force pulling the robot's end-effector (EE) towards the goal (ξ_g). This force is calculated using stiffness (K) and damping (D) gains, with the task-space error (e) computed via the logarithmic map between the current EE pose (ξ_i^(k)) and the goal pose.
- **Repulsive potential (f_r):** A C1-differentiable force defined over S_W, calculated using specified heuristics.
The resulting net force f_i generates a task-space acceleration, which is then integrated to update the agent's task-space velocity (v_i^(k)) and pose (ξ_i^(k+1)) using geometric algebra (GA) for a pointmass agent. For a robotic arm, the joint velocities are derived using the pseudo-Jacobian, and new joint positions are computed, followed by forward kinematics to determine the new EE pose. The GA formulation allows for a unified treatment of translation and orientation.

**B. Multi-Agent Planning Scheme**
To address the local minima problem (Problem P3), a multi-agent planning scheme generates N_a candidate trajectories (T_i) in parallel. Each agent (A_i) can employ a different heuristic or parameter set. These trajectories are evaluated against a task-dependent cost function (C) which is a weighted sum of four components:
1. **Goal Distance Cost (C_goal):** Penalizes distance from the goal within a detection radius.
2. **Obstacle Distance Cost (C_obs):** Penalizes proximity to the closest obstacle within a detection radius.
3. **Path Length Cost (C_path):** Penalizes deviations from a direct path to the goal.
4. **Trajectory Smoothness Cost (C_smooth):** Penalizes aggregate jerks along the trajectory.
After N_k planning steps, the best agent (i*) is selected based on the minimum cost, with an additional scalar (α) to ensure a sufficiently lower cost than the current best.

**C. Vector Field Heuristics**
The framework employs two main repulsive heuristics:
1. **Artificial Potential Field (APF):** A classical heuristic where repulsive force is inversely proportional to the distance from obstacles, active within a threshold radius.
2. **Magnetic-Field Inspired (MFI):** A heuristic inspired by electromagnetic fields, modeling the robot as a charged particle. It computes a Lorentz-like force, with an artificial current (c) derived from the EE's current linear velocity projected onto the obstacle surface. MFI aims to overcome local minima and limit cycle issues of traditional APF by enabling rotation around obstacles. The framework instantiates multiple agents, each potentially using variations of these heuristics (e.g., different detection radii) to emulate broad-phase and narrow-phase collision avoidance.

**D. GPU-accelerated Perception**
To meet perception constraints (Problem P1), a low-latency GPU-accelerated pipeline is utilized.
- **Multi-view Fusion:** Fuses point clouds from N_S multi-view 3D sensors to minimize occlusions.
- **Robot Filtering:** Performs forward kinematics (FK) on a set of bounding spheres (created offline with Foam tool) to subtract robot geometry from the point cloud, focusing on the environment.
- **Voxel Grid Approximation:** The remaining environment point cloud is voxelized with a voxel size of 2r_O. Voxel-center points are extracted to form S_W, providing a structured, volumetrically consistent, and smooth force interaction model.
- **GPU Acceleration:** Open3D CUDA is used for rapid 3D data processing operations (downsampling, bounding box filtering, voxel grid construction, coordinate transforms). Point cloud data is kept resident in GPU DRAM to minimize CPU-GPU memory transfers.

**E. SIMT-optimized Force Computation**
To address control law time-costs (Problem P2), force calculations are massively parallelized on the GPU.
- **Parallel Obstacle Processing:** Each GPU thread is assigned a single obstacle from S_W.
- **Distance and Force Calculation:** The shortest Euclidean distance between the agent and the obstacle surface is computed. If within the detection radius, the repulsive force contribution is calculated based on the selected heuristic.
- **Reduction and Accumulation:** Partial results are stored in shared memory, followed by intra-block reductions and an atomic accumulation into global memory to produce the net repulsive force for each agent.
This approach makes each thread computationally lightweight, promoting high occupancy and overlapping memory access with computation to efficiently utilize Streaming Multiprocessors (SMs). The time complexity per query is estimated as O(N_O), considering the serial atomic accumulation step.

**F. GPU-enabled Multi-Agent Compute Concurrency**
To efficiently process forces for all N_a agents in parallel (Problem P3), the framework employs specialized CUDA kernels for each heuristic type. This strategy avoids warp divergence that would occur in a single "bulk" kernel attempting to handle heterogeneous agents. By organizing agents by heuristic and using parallel CUDA streams, the approach achieves efficient batching of force queries and allows for overlapped kernel execution and memory transfers, improving throughput for diverse agent behaviors.

The system is implemented in C++ using the `gafro` library for Geometric Algebra computations. It uses a dual-threaded architecture for the planner, with one thread controlling the robot and another performing multi-agent trajectory rollouts in parallel, communicating via ROS 2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations of the G-MAPP framework in both simulated and real-world environments.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
Simulations were conducted on a PC with an Intel i7-8565U processor and an NVIDIA RTX 3070 GPU using artificially generated, high-fidelity environments (Opening, Cluttered, Trap, Passage) designed to test goal convergence and collision avoidance in complex scenarios.

1. **CPU vs. GPU Computation Performance Comparison (Table I, Figure 6):**
 - The GPU-accelerated version demonstrated a significant reduction in computation time (T_C) compared to the CPU version, achieving at least a 2x speedup, with up to 5x in some cases (e.g., Cluttered scene, N_k=200).
 - GPU T_C was largely invariant to scene fidelity, maintaining an approximate T_C/N_k ratio of 2 ms per planning step.
 - The standard deviations for T_C on the GPU were consistently smaller, often near zero, indicating more predictable and stable performance.
 - The addition of multi-agents (N_a) incurred a penalty of approximately 0.4 ms per planning step on the GPU, whereas it nearly doubled the T_C for the CPU version.
 - The GPU's enhanced computational efficiency contributed to higher success rates in challenging scenarios, such as the "Trap" scene (70% for GPU at N_k=200 vs. 40% for CPU). This improvement is attributed to faster planning cycles and more frequent cost-function evaluations, allowing agents to adapt strategies more promptly.

2. **Quantitative Evaluation for Multi-Agents (Table II):**
 - The performance of multi-agent strategies (MULTI, 11 agents) and twin-agent MFI heuristics was compared against a twin-agent APF baseline on the GPU across various static and dynamic scenes.
 - **APF (baseline):** Consistently failed in complex scenarios (0% success rate across all evaluated scenes: Opening2, Mobile Trap, Bookshelf, Complex), despite having the lowest computation time. It often resulted in zero motion duration, indicating failure to progress.
 - **MFI (twin-agent):** Showed robust performance, achieving high success rates (66.67% in Opening2, 100% in Mobile Trap and Bookshelf, 80% in Complex). It maintained a reasonable computation time and minimum obstacle distance (L_min).
 - **MULTI (ensemble):** Demonstrated the best scalability and robustness in challenging settings. It achieved higher success rates than MFI in some cases (73.33% in Opening2 vs. 66.67% for MFI; 93.33% in Complex vs. 80% for MFI) and matched MFI's 100% success rate in others (Mobile Trap, Bookshelf).
 - **Agent Switches (N_sw):** The MULTI method generally exhibited a higher number of agent switches, indicating its ability to adapt and explore different strategies more frequently, which correlated with its higher success rates in constrained scenarios.
 - **Computation Time (T_C):** While MULTI had higher T_C than MFI or APF due to more agents, its T_C remained consistent with GPU performance observed in Table I and did not significantly impede real-time operation.
 - **Motion Duration (T_M) and Minimum Obstacle Distance (L_min):** MULTI often achieved comparable or shorter motion durations and maintained similar minimum obstacle distances to MFI, showcasing efficient and safe trajectory generation.

**B. Real-World Experiments**
Qualitative experiments were performed using a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robot with two Intel RealSense stereo depth cameras, streaming 848 × 480 depth frames at 60 FPS.
- **Concave Trap Scenario:** The robot successfully navigated a concave trap environment, which is known to challenge reactive planners due to the local minima problem.
- **Dynamic Human Disturbance:** The robot demonstrated successful obstacle avoidance and reactive behavior while two human actors actively perturbed its motion with pool noodles.
These experiments collectively demonstrated the framework's robustness and ability to adapt to dynamic conditions and environmental uncertainties in physical real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The G-MAPP framework represents a contribution to the field of reactive motion generation for robotics, particularly in dynamic and unstructured environments. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable approach to overcome long-standing computational bottlenecks and integration challenges that have limited the practicality of real-time reactive planning.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**
- **Real-time Reactivity with High-Fidelity Environments:** The GPU-accelerated perception and planning pipeline enables real-time motion generation even when using high-fidelity environmental representations (sphere-world approximation from fused point clouds). This addresses the trade-off typically faced by reactive planners between environmental detail and real-time performance.
- **Tight Perception-Planning Coupling:** By accelerating both world modeling and force computation on the GPU, the framework achieves a tighter coupling between the perception-action loop. This is critical for robust operation in dynamic scenarios where rapid updates and responses are necessary.
- **Enhanced Planning Robustness:** The multi-agent planning strategy, supported by GPU acceleration, improves robustness against local minima problems, as demonstrated by higher success rates in complex and challenging environments (e.g., concave traps, cluttered scenes). The ability for agents to rapidly switch strategies, facilitated by faster planning cycles, contributes to this robustness.
- **Scalability and Predictable Performance:** The SIMT-optimized force computation on the GPU offers superior scaling with increasing scene complexity and agent count compared to CPU-based approaches. This leads to more predictable and consistent computation times, crucial for real-time control (100 Hz target).
- **Practical Applicability:** The successful qualitative real-world experiments on a 7-DoF robot in scenarios involving dynamic human interaction and complex obstacles indicate the framework's potential for deployment in collaborative robotics and human-centered environments where safety and responsiveness are paramount.
- **Modular Architecture:** The design, particularly the GPU-accelerated perception module and the multi-agent planning scheme, aims for modularity, suggesting potential for integration with various reactive planners and sensor types. The use of Geometric Algebra for task-space planning provides a concise and coupled formulation for position and orientation control.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that indicate avenues for future research:
- **CPU and ROS-related Overhead:** The current implementation incurs non-trivial CPU and ROS overhead. Future work could investigate tensorized motion planning to mitigate this, potentially by offloading more coordination tasks to the GPU.
- **Higher-Dimensional Repulsive Terms:** The experimental evaluation primarily focused on demonstrating reactivity in challenging scenarios rather than providing a comprehensive treatment of higher-dimensional repulsive terms. Further exploration with specialized tooling and analysis is needed for this aspect.
- **Generalization and Adaptability:** Future work could investigate task-dependent approximation fidelity, the use of policy networks for automatic agent tuning, active inference for improved environment perception, and generalization across diverse robot morphologies, moving towards more autonomous and versatile reactive systems.

In conclusion, G-MAPP contributes a practical, performant, and robust framework for reactive motion generation, leveraging GPU acceleration to address critical challenges in perception-planning integration for dynamic robotic tasks. Its success in both simulated and real-world complex scenarios suggests its potential for advancing robotic autonomy and human-robot collaboration.
