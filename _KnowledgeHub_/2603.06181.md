# Towards Motion Turing Test: Evaluating Human-Likeness in Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the "Motion Turing Test" framework and the Human-Humanoid Motion (HHMotion) dataset to objectively evaluate the human-likeness of humanoid robot movements by disentangling motion from visual appearance using SMPL-X representations. It also develops PTR-Net, a specialized model that accurately predicts human-likeness scores, outperforming general-purpose Vision-Language Models.

## Problem

- A lack of a standardized, human-centered method to quantitatively evaluate the "human-likeness" of humanoid robot motions, beyond task-oriented metrics.
- The absence of a comprehensive dataset containing both human and diverse humanoid robot motions, specifically annotated for perceptual human-likeness.
- Current motion generation research prioritizes technical metrics (e.g., efficiency, accuracy) but often overlooks subjective human perception of naturalness and fluidity.

## Method

- Proposed the "==Motion Turing Test==" framework to evaluate human-likeness based solely on kinematic information (SMPL-X poses), removing visual appearance bias.
- Created the Human-Humanoid Motion (HHMotion) dataset, comprising 1,000 5-second SMPL-X motion clips (500 human, 500 humanoid) across 15 action categories, extensively annotated by 25 human evaluators with 0-5 Likert scale human-likeness scores.
- Developed PTR-Net, a Pose-Temporal Regression Network, as a specialized baseline model for automated human-likeness assessment, utilizing a temporal encoder and spatial-temporal graph convolution.

## Results

- PTR-Net achieved significantly lower MAE (**0.5813**) and RMSE (**0.7926**), and higher Spearman’s ρ (**0.6841**) compared to VLM-based and other motion-centric baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness in aligning with human judgments.
- Humanoid motions, particularly from real robots, consistently received lower human-likeness scores than human motions in the HHMotion dataset.
- The ablation study confirmed the critical contribution of PTR-Net's temporal encoder and attention pooling, with the full model achieving optimal performance and demonstrating generalization by accurately scoring an unseen robot's motion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human evaluators consistently identify a perceivable gap between human and humanoid robot motions, even when appearance cues are removed, with real robots typically performing less human-like than simulated ones.
> - Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Gemini 2.5 Pro and Qwen3-VL-Plus are largely ineffective for nuanced motion human-likeness assessment without domain-specific training.
> - The human-likeness of robot motions varies significantly across action categories; dynamic actions (e.g., jumping, boxing) show the largest discrepancies, while smoother actions (e.g., walking, dancing) exhibit closer alignment to human performance.
