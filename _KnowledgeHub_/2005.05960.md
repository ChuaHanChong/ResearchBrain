# Planning to Explore via Self-Supervised World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Plan2Explore is a self-supervised reinforcement learning agent that leverages learned world models and proactive planning to seek expected future novelty, enabling efficient exploration and rapid adaptation to new tasks directly from pixel observations. It demonstrated superior zero-shot performance over prior unsupervised methods and often matched or exceeded state-of-the-art supervised agents like Dreamer, learning generalizable world models for various downstream tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents often suffer from sample inefficiency, requiring millions of interactions to learn a single task.
- Traditional RL agents are highly task-specific, lacking the ability to easily transfer learned knowledge or adapt to new tasks.
- Ineffective exploration strategies in complex environments with sparse rewards and high-dimensional visual inputs hinder learning.

## Method

- Developed **Plan2Explore**, a two-phase learning framework: a self-supervised, task-agnostic exploration phase followed by rapid zero-shot or few-shot adaptation.
- Utilized a robust ==latent dynamics world model==, building on ==PlaNet== and ==Dreamer==, to learn compressed representations from high-dimensional pixel inputs.
- Introduced an intrinsic motivation for exploration based on '==latent disagreement==' (variance across an ==ensemble of predictors==) and planned an exploration policy in imagination to maximize expected future novelty.

## Results

- Achieved superior zero-shot performance compared to prior state-of-the-art unsupervised exploration strategies, often performing competitively with or surpassing the fully supervised Dreamer agent.
- Demonstrated rapid few-shot adaptation, quickly matching or outperforming fully supervised agents with a small number of task-specific episodes (e.g., **100-150 episodes** after **1 million exploration steps**).
- Learned highly generalizable world models, evidenced by successful zero-shot adaptation to multiple tasks (e.g., Cheetah Run Forward/Backward, Flip Forward/Backward) where task-specific supervised agents failed to transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling costly data collection from task-specific training through self-supervised exploration yields more generalizable agents, since self-supervised world models capture more global environmental dynamics than task-specific supervised models.
> - Proactive exploration, achieved by planning in imagination to seek expected future novelty, is more efficient than merely reacting to retrospective novelty.
> - Leveraging learned world models allows for efficient policy training entirely in imagination, enabling rapid adaptation to new tasks with minimal or no additional real-world data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved remarkable successes in solving complex tasks, yet it continues to grapple with two fundamental challenges: **sample inefficiency** and **task-specificity**. Traditional RL agents often require millions of interactions with the environment to learn a single task, and this learning is typically not transferable to new tasks without extensive re-training. "Planning to Explore via Self-Supervised World Models" directly addresses these limitations by situating itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving research areas:

- **Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning and Intrinsic Motivation:** A core paradigm in current RL research is to enable agents to learn useful behaviors and representations without explicit external rewards. This is often achieved through *intrinsic motivation*, where agents are driven by internal "rewards" such as curiosity (seeking novel or unpredictable states), empowerment (maximizing control over the environment), or maximizing information gain. This paper firmly positions itself within this paradigm, aiming for "task-agnostic" exploration. It critiques prior intrinsic motivation methods, particularly model-free approaches like Curiosity (Pathak et al., 2017), for their "retrospective novelty" – assessing novelty *after* visiting a state, which can be inefficient.

- **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and World Models:** MBRL focuses on learning a model of the environment's dynamics, which can then be used for planning, prediction, and policy optimization. World models, such as PlaNet (Hafner et al., 2019) and Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2020), have demonstrated impressive capabilities in learning compact latent dynamics from high-dimensional visual observations and then training policies entirely in imagination. Plan2Explore builds directly on these advancements, leveraging the data efficiency and planning capabilities of world models as its foundation. This allows it to move beyond model-free intrinsic motivation, integrating planning into the exploration process.

- **Efficient Exploration Strategies:** A significant challenge in deep RL, especially in environments with sparse rewards, is effective exploration. The paper contrasts its approach with count-based methods or those relying on pseudo-counts, which struggle with high-dimensional observations. It also distinguishes itself from ensemble-based approaches (e.g., Osband et al., 2018) that often focus on task-specific exploration with rewards. Plan2Explore proposes a novel exploration strategy that uses *planning to seek expected future novelty*, drawing inspiration from information-theoretic principles like expected information gain. This is a direct evolution from prior disagreement-based exploration methods (e.g., Pathak et al., 2019; Shyam et al., 2019) by extending them to high-dimensional visual inputs and incorporating multi-step planning.

- **Generalization and Transfer Learning:** A key goal of self-supervised RL is to learn generalizable representations and skills that can transfer to new tasks. The paper aims for "zero-shot" or "few-shot" adaptation, where an agent, after an initial task-agnostic exploration phase, can quickly solve new tasks with minimal or no additional interaction. This contrasts with traditional supervised RL, where each task typically requires learning from scratch. The ability to generalize is crucial for scalable real-world RL systems, where acquiring new task-specific data is often impractical.

In essence, Plan2Explore integrates the strengths of self-supervised learning (task-agnostic data collection), intrinsic motivation (disagreement as an exploration driver), and model-based RL (planning in a learned world model from pixels) to create an agent that can both efficiently explore complex visual environments and rapidly adapt to a wide array of unforeseen tasks. It represents a significant step towards more autonomous, general-purpose RL agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-supervised reinforcement learning agent, named **Plan2Explore**, that can efficiently explore complex environments and rapidly adapt to new, unforeseen tasks without the need for task-specific supervision during exploration. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcome Task-Specificity and Sample Inefficiency in RL:** Traditional reinforcement learning often trains agents for pre-specified tasks, requiring substantial, task-specific environment interaction. This process is highly inefficient when an agent needs to learn multiple tasks or adapt to new ones. The authors aim to break this cycle by enabling an agent to learn a comprehensive understanding of its environment *once*, in a task-agnostic manner, and then leverage this knowledge to solve various downstream tasks.

2. **Enable Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Adaptation to New Tasks:** A crucial motivation is to allow agents to generalize quickly to tasks they have never encountered during exploration. This means providing a task specification (a reward function) at test time and expecting the agent to perform well either immediately (zero-shot) or with a very limited amount of additional interaction (few-shot). This capability is vital for real-world applications where collecting extensive labeled data for every new task is impractical.

3. **Improve Exploration Efficiency through "Expected Future Novelty":** Prior self-supervised exploration methods, especially model-free ones, often compute "novelty" retrospectively – after the agent has already visited a state. This can lead to inefficient exploration, as the agent might only react to already visited novel states. The authors are motivated to develop an exploration strategy that proactively seeks out *expected future novelty* by planning ahead. This involves anticipating which future states would be most informative for improving the agent's world model.

4. **Learn from High-Dimensional Visual Inputs:** Many real-world applications of RL involve processing high-dimensional sensory data, such as images. The research is motivated to tackle complex control tasks directly from raw pixel observations, rather than relying on pre-engineered low-dimensional state representations. This adds a layer of complexity but also increases the applicability of the method to real-world scenarios.

5. **Leverage the Power of World Models:** The paper acknowledges the recent successes of latent dynamics models (world models) in learning accurate representations and enabling efficient planning. A key motivation is to integrate and extend these powerful model-based techniques into the self-supervised exploration framework, thereby combining the benefits of model-based planning with intrinsic motivation.

In summary, the authors are motivated to create a more general, autonomous, and data-efficient RL agent by decoupling the costly data collection phase from specific task objectives. They hypothesize that a sophisticated world model, coupled with a proactive, planning-based exploration strategy driven by expected novelty, can achieve this goal, leading to agents that learn faster and generalize better across tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Plan2Explore operates in two distinct phases: a self-supervised task-agnostic exploration phase, followed by a zero-shot or few-shot adaptation phase to specific downstream tasks. The core of its methodology revolves around learning and leveraging a "world model."

#### 3.1. World Model Learning

Plan2Explore builds upon the robust latent dynamics model used in PlaNet (Hafner et al., 2019) and Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2020). This world model is designed to learn a compressed, recurrent latent representation of the environment from high-dimensional visual observations (pixels). It consists of several key components:

- **Image Encoder `e_θ(o_t)`:** A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) that processes raw image observations `o_t` into compact feature vectors `h_t`.
- **Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM):** The heart of the dynamics model, which captures the temporal evolution of the environment in a latent space `s_t`. It comprises:
 - **Posterior Dynamics `q_θ(s_t | s_{t-1}, a_{t-1}, h_t)`:** Infers the current latent state `s_t` from the previous latent state `s_{t-1}`, previous action `a_{t-1}`, and the current image embedding `h_t`.
 - **Prior Dynamics `p_θ(s_t | s_{t-1}, a_{t-1})`:** Predicts the next latent state `s_t` purely from the previous latent state and action, without access to the current image. This enables imagination.
- **Reward Predictor `p_θ(r_t | s_t)`:** Predicts the scalar reward `r_t` from the latent state `s_t`. During exploration, this is effectively ignored or set to zero for task rewards, but a *novelty reward* is used. During adaptation, it learns to predict the task-specific reward.
- **Image Decoder `p_θ(o_t | s_t)`:** Reconstructs the original image `o_t` from the latent state `s_t`.

All these components are jointly trained to maximize an evidence lower bound (ELBO), similar to a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), ensuring that the latent states are rich enough to reconstruct observations and predict dynamics. The model operates efficiently by predicting thousands of future sequences in parallel in its compact latent space.

#### 3.2. Task-Agnostic Exploration Phase: Planning to Explore

During this phase, the agent collects a diverse dataset of experiences without any external rewards, driven solely by an intrinsic motivation to seek novelty.

- **Intrinsic Motivation: Latent Disagreement:** Instead of simple prediction error, Plan2Explore quantifies novelty as the model's *uncertainty* about its future predictions. This uncertainty is approximated using an **ensemble of one-step predictive models** `E = {w_k | k ∈ [1; K]}`. Each model `w_k` takes a latent state `s_t` and action `a_t` and predicts the *next image embedding* `h_{t+1}`. These ensemble members are trained alongside the main world model using mean squared error. The intrinsic reward `ir_t` is then defined as the **variance (disagreement)** among the predicted means `μ(w_k, s_t, a_t)` of these ensemble members:
 `D(s_t, a_t) = Var({μ(w_k, s_t, a_t) | k ∈ [1; K]})`.
 High disagreement indicates states where the model ensemble has differing predictions, signifying high uncertainty and thus high potential for new learning.

- **Planning for Expected Future Novelty:** The agent doesn't just react to observed novelty; it actively plans to *seek out future states that are expected to be novel*. This is achieved by training an **exploration policy (actor-critic network)** *inside the learned world model's imagination*. The policy is optimized to maximize the sum of these intrinsic (disagreement) rewards over imagined future trajectories. Gradients for the policy are backpropagated through the neural network dynamics model, allowing the policy to learn actions that lead to high-disagreement states. This process avoids additional real environment interaction during policy optimization.

- **Theoretical Foundation (Expected Information Gain):** The authors provide a theoretical link between latent disagreement and **Expected Information Gain (EIG)**. They argue that maximizing the variance of ensemble predictions approximates maximizing the EIG, which is a principled objective for active learning and Bayesian experiment design. EIG quantifies how much information a future observation `h_{t+1}` provides about the true underlying model parameters `w`, given the current state and action. This formalization strengthens the rationale for using disagreement as an intrinsic reward.

The exploration algorithm (Algorithm 1) iteratively:
1. Trains the world model `M` and the latent disagreement ensemble `E` on the collected dataset `D`.
2. Trains the exploration policy `π_LD` in imagination using `M` and the disagreement rewards from `E`.
3. Executes `π_LD` in the environment to collect new data, expanding `D`.

#### 3.3. Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Adaptation Phase

After the exploration phase, the learned global world model `M` and the collected dataset `D` are used to adapt to new tasks (Algorithm 2).

- **Reward Function Distillation:** When a new task-specific reward function `R` is provided, a latent-space reward predictor `R̂` is trained. This predictor learns to map latent states in the collected dataset `D` to their corresponding task rewards, effectively "distilling" the external reward function into the model's latent space.
- **Task Policy Learning in Imagination:** A separate **task-specific policy (actor-critic network)** `π_R` is then trained entirely in imagination, using the learned world model `M` and the distilled reward predictor `R̂`. This training process is identical to how Dreamer operates, maximizing expected task rewards over imagined trajectories.
- **Zero-Shot vs. Few-Shot:**
 - **Zero-shot:** The agent is evaluated immediately after training `π_R` in imagination, without any further real environment interaction.
 - **Few-shot:** Optionally, a small number of task-specific episodes are collected and added to `D`, allowing for slight refinement of `M` and `R̂` before `π_R` is further optimized. This provides a minor adaptation boost with minimal additional data.

This methodology ensures that the expensive data collection and world model learning are task-agnostic, while adaptation to specific tasks is rapid and data-efficient, capitalizing on the learned dynamics model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Plan2Explore was evaluated on 20 challenging continuous control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite, using only high-dimensional visual observations (64x64 pixels). The experiments aimed to answer key questions about its effectiveness compared to supervised oracles and other self-supervised exploration methods.

1. **Superior Zero-Shot Performance:**
 - Plan2Explore significantly outperformed prior state-of-the-art unsupervised exploration strategies (Curiosity, MAX, Retrospective Disagreement, Random) in terms of zero-shot task performance. This means that after a phase of self-supervised exploration (without any task rewards), Plan2Explore's learned world model enabled it to derive effective task policies purely in imagination, without any further interaction with the real environment, upon receiving a task reward function.
 - Remarkably, Plan2Explore's zero-shot performance was often competitive with, and in some cases even surpassed, Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2020), which is a state-of-the-art *supervised* RL agent that has access to task rewards *throughout* its training. This highlights Plan2Explore's ability to learn a highly general and useful world model through self-supervision.

2. **Rapid Few-Shot Adaptation:**
 - When allowed a small amount of task-specific interaction (e.g., 100-150 supervised episodes after 1 million exploration steps), Plan2Explore demonstrated rapid adaptation. It quickly matched or even outperformed the fully supervised Dreamer on several tasks (e.g., Hopper Hop), validating the hypothesis that self-supervised pre-training can lead to highly data-efficient fine-tuning. This showcases the potential for unsupervised learning to reduce the data requirements for new tasks drastically.

3. **Enhanced Generalization of Self-Supervised Models:**
 - The study specifically investigated whether self-supervised models generalize better than task-specific supervised models. In the Cheetah environment, Plan2Explore, after task-agnostic exploration, performed well on four different tasks (Run Forward, Run Backward, Flip Forward, Flip Backward) in a zero-shot manner.
 - In contrast, a Dreamer agent trained *only* on the "Cheetah Run Forward" task failed to generalize to the other three related tasks (Run Backward, Flip Forward/Backward), performing comparably to random exploration. This critical finding indicates that Plan2Explore learns a more "global" world model that captures general environment dynamics, whereas task-specific supervised agents can learn narrower models tailored to their reward function, limiting their transferability.

4. **Advantage of Maximizing Expected Novelty:**
 - The core hypothesis that planning to seek *expected future novelty* is superior to reacting to *retrospective novelty* was empirically supported. Plan2Explore, which uses multi-step planning within its world model to maximize future disagreement, achieved significantly better performance than a "Retrospective Disagreement" baseline (a one-step planning agent). This result validates the design choice of actively seeking out informative future states, rather than merely re-visiting past novelties.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that Plan2Explore represents a significant advancement in self-supervised RL. It provides a method that is not only highly efficient in exploration and data usage but also produces generalizable world models capable of rapid adaptation to diverse tasks, operating directly from high-dimensional visual inputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Plan2Explore marks a significant step forward in the field of reinforcement learning, addressing several long-standing challenges and opening new avenues for future research and application. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift Towards Task-Agnostic Pre-training:** Plan2Explore demonstrates the strong viability of a two-phase learning paradigm: extensive, self-supervised, task-agnostic exploration followed by rapid, zero-shot or few-shot adaptation to specific tasks. This represents a crucial shift from the traditional approach of training task-specific agents from scratch, which is often prohibitively expensive in terms of data collection and computational resources. This approach can drastically reduce the "cost per task" for complex agents.

2. **Increased Data Efficiency in Reinforcement Learning:** One of the most critical bottlenecks in deep RL is sample inefficiency. By learning a comprehensive world model during an unsupervised exploration phase, Plan2Explore enables policies for new tasks to be trained primarily in imagination. This ability to achieve near-oracle performance with zero or very few task-specific interactions after initial exploration provides a strong path towards more data-efficient RL systems, making them more applicable to real-world scenarios where physical interactions are costly or time-consuming (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving, drug discovery simulations).

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Transferability:** The empirical evidence that Plan2Explore learns a "global" world model, capable of generalizing to entirely new tasks within the same environment where task-specific supervised models fail, is profoundly significant. This highlights the power of self-supervised exploration in developing representations that capture fundamental environmental dynamics, rather than just features relevant to a single reward function. Such generalizable models are essential for building truly intelligent agents that can operate flexibly in diverse and changing environments.

4. **Principled and Proactive Exploration:** The method's foundation in expected information gain and its implementation through planning for "expected future novelty" provide a principled and highly effective approach to exploration. Moving beyond retrospective novelty, Plan2Explore's ability to foresee and seek out informative states demonstrates a more sophisticated form of intrinsic motivation, leading to better model learning and ultimately better task performance. This could inspire new directions in designing intrinsic reward signals.

5. **Scalability to High-Dimensional Inputs:** By successfully operating directly from high-dimensional pixel observations on complex control tasks, Plan2Explore confirms that advanced model-based and self-supervised techniques are robust enough for real-world sensory data. This removes the need for manual feature engineering or access to low-dimensional ground truth states, expanding the scope of problems that can be addressed by RL.

6. **Foundation for Future AI Systems:** The work contributes to the broader vision of autonomous agents that can learn continuously, build an understanding of their environment without explicit human supervision, and adapt to novel goals quickly. This aligns with goals in artificial general intelligence (AGI), where agents are expected to acquire a wide range of skills and knowledge through interaction and self-discovery.

In conclusion, Plan2Explore represents a compelling integration of world models, intrinsic motivation, and planning that delivers state-of-the-art performance in self-supervised reinforcement learning. Its demonstrated capabilities in data efficiency, generalization, and proactive exploration lay critical groundwork for developing more capable, autonomous, and broadly applicable RL systems, potentially accelerating the deployment of AI in complex real-world domains.
