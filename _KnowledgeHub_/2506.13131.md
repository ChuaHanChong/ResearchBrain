# AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for scientific and algorithmic discovery

> [!summary] Summary
> AlphaEvolve, from Google DeepMind, combines large language models with an evolutionary search framework to autonomously discover novel algorithms and optimize code. This system identified a faster procedure for 4x4 complex matrix multiplication, improved state-of-the-art for several open mathematical problems, and delivered tangible optimizations for Google's computing ecosystem, including recovering 0.7% of fleet-wide compute resources.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle to generate entirely novel and verifiable scientific or algorithmic discoveries in a single attempt, frequently producing "hallucinations."
- Automated discovery systems need to move beyond simple code generation to enable continuous, iterative improvement of complex algorithms based on empirical feedback.
- Optimizing critical computational infrastructure and solving open scientific problems often require designing sophisticated, multi-stage search algorithms or making subtle, high-impact code changes that are difficult for humans or prior AI systems to identify.

## Method

- AlphaEvolve implements a ==distributed, asynchronous evolutionary pipeline== that orchestrates state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) within a continuous feedback loop.
- LLMs are prompted to propose targeted ==code modifications (diff blocks)== to existing programs, which are then automatically evaluated against user-defined metrics.
- The system maintains a =='Program database'== inspired by MAP-elites to balance exploration and exploitation, iteratively refining solutions, and utilizes an ==ensemble of LLMs== for high-throughput and high-quality generation.

## Results

- Discovered a procedure for multiplying 4x4 complex matrices using **48** scalar multiplications, improving upon Strassen’s **49**-multiplication algorithm (a first in **56 years**) and enhancing **14** other matrix multiplication algorithms.
- Achieved new state-of-the-art results in approximately **20%** of over **50** open mathematical problems, including improving the lower bound for the kissing number in 11 dimensions from **592** to **593**.
- Deployed optimizations across Google’s computing ecosystem, recovering **0.7%** of fleet-wide compute resources in data center scheduling, accelerating Gemini training kernels by **23%**, and contributing to TPU hardware circuit design.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) into an evolutionary feedback loop allows for continuous, iterative refinement of code, transforming LLMs from one-shot generators into active agents for discovery.
> - LLMs can automate the design of complex 'evolution operators' and even evolve entire heuristic search algorithms, enabling adaptive, multi-stage problem-solving previously difficult to achieve.
> - Combining an ensemble of LLMs with efficient, parallelized evaluation cascades and multi-objective optimization strategies significantly boosts the system's ability to explore complex search spaces and identify subtle improvements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

AlphaEvolve is situated at the nexus of several rapidly advancing fields in artificial intelligence and computational science: evolutionary computation, large language models (LLMs) for code generation, and AI for scientific and mathematical discovery.

Historically, **evolutionary computation** and **genetic programming** have explored the idea of evolving programs through iterative mutation and selection. However, these methods traditionally relied on handcrafted "evolution operators," which are difficult to design and often lack the generalizability required for complex domains. AlphaEvolve addresses this limitation by using powerful LLMs to *automate the construction of these operators*, leveraging the LLM's vast world knowledge and coding abilities to propose diverse and effective mutations without predefined rules. This represents a significant leap from classical genetic programming.

The rise of **Large Language Models (LLMs)** has revolutionized code generation, optimization, and understanding. Projects like AlphaCode (Li et al., 2022) have demonstrated LLMs' proficiency in solving programming competition problems. AlphaEvolve goes beyond mere code generation; it positions LLMs as active agents within a feedback loop. Instead of one-shot generation, LLMs are used to iteratively *improve* existing code based on quantifiable performance metrics. This places AlphaEvolve within the growing domain of "code superoptimization," where AI systems continuously refine programs. It also aligns with the broader trend of "agentic AI," where LLMs are equipped with the ability to take actions, receive feedback, and learn from their environment over multiple steps (Yao et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023).

Crucially, AlphaEvolve is presented as a **substantial enhancement of DeepMind's FunSearch system** (Romera-Paredes et al., 2023). While FunSearch pioneered LLM-guided evolution for mathematical discovery, AlphaEvolve dramatically expands its scope and capabilities. Key improvements include:
- **Scale of Evolution:** FunSearch evolved single Python functions (typically 10-20 lines); AlphaEvolve can evolve entire code files (hundreds of lines) in any programming language.
- **Evaluation Flexibility:** AlphaEvolve can handle evaluations taking hours on accelerators in parallel, compared to FunSearch's need for fast evaluations (≤20 min on 1 CPU).
- **LLM Utilization:** AlphaEvolve benefits significantly from state-of-the-art LLMs (Gemini 2.0 Pro/Flash) and rich contextual prompting, whereas FunSearch used smaller, code-only LLMs with minimal context.
- **Efficiency:** AlphaEvolve achieves results with thousands of LLM samples, contrasting with FunSearch's millions.
- **Multi-objective Optimization:** AlphaEvolve can simultaneously optimize multiple metrics, a capability absent in FunSearch.

Finally, AlphaEvolve contributes to the burgeoning field of **AI for scientific and mathematical discovery**. This area has seen AI systems applied to protein structure prediction (AlphaFold), quantum physics, and materials science. AlphaEvolve distinguishes itself by focusing on *programmatic hypothesis representation and evaluation*. While other approaches like AI Co-Scientist (Gottweis et al., 2025) focus on natural language hypothesis generation, AlphaEvolve's code-centric approach allows it to sidestep LLM hallucinations more effectively and sustain long evolutionary processes. It demonstrates that AI can not only assist in hypothesis generation but also in the concrete, verifiable discovery of novel algorithms and mathematical constructions, pushing the boundaries of automated scientific research. The direct comparison and superior performance over specialized systems like AlphaTensor for matrix multiplication further underscore its general-purpose effectiveness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of AlphaEvolve is to **substantially enhance the capabilities of state-of-the-art LLMs in tackling highly challenging tasks, specifically open scientific problems and the optimization of critical computational infrastructure.**

The primary motivation stems from the recognition that while recent LLMs are powerful for code generation and hypothesis formulation, achieving entirely *new scientific or practical discoveries* through LLM pipelines remains a significant challenge. Traditional methods of scientific discovery involve a prolonged, iterative process of ideation, exploration, experimentation, and validation—a process LLMs alone cannot fully replicate in a single pass.

AlphaEvolve aims to bridge this gap by orchestrating an **autonomous pipeline of LLMs** within an **evolutionary framework**. The key idea is to enable LLMs to improve algorithms by making direct changes to code, continuously receiving feedback from automated evaluators. This iterative process, grounded in executable code and empirical evaluation, allows AlphaEvolve to overcome LLM limitations like "hallucinations" by verifying every proposed change.

The researchers' goals are manifold:
1. **Automate Algorithmic and Scientific Discovery:** Develop a general-purpose agent capable of discovering novel, provably correct algorithms and constructions that surpass existing state-of-the-art solutions across diverse domains.
2. **Optimize Real-World Computational Infrastructure:** Apply the system to mission-critical engineering problems within Google's compute stack to achieve tangible performance improvements, demonstrating practical utility beyond theoretical breakthroughs.
3. **Generalize and Scale Automated Discovery:** Create a system that is significantly more versatile, scalable, and powerful than previous LLM-guided evolutionary approaches (like FunSearch), enabling it to tackle larger, more complex codebases and problems with longer evaluation times.
4. **Promote AI-Human Collaboration:** Design the system to allow human experts to define evaluation criteria, provide initial solutions, and interpret the discovered code, fostering a synergistic partnership for research and engineering.
5. **Advance AI Capabilities:** Explore the "test-time compute" scaling of LLMs, demonstrating how machine feedback can sustain significant performance improvements over extended periods.

By focusing on problems where solutions can be *automatically evaluated*, AlphaEvolve seeks to unlock new frontiers in autonomous knowledge generation, driving both fundamental scientific progress and practical engineering advancements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AlphaEvolve is designed as a sophisticated, distributed, and asynchronous computational pipeline that orchestrates LLM interactions within an evolutionary search framework. Its methodology integrates several key components:

#### 3.1. High-Level Evolutionary Loop

The core of AlphaEvolve is an evolutionary algorithm that iteratively improves programs. It starts with an initial program (potentially rudimentary) and continuously generates, evaluates, and selects modifications to a pool of programs, aiming to maximize user-defined evaluation metrics. This loop is a "Scientist/Engineer" (human user) defining "What?" and AlphaEvolve figuring out "How?".

#### 3.2. Task Specification

The user defines the problem by providing:
- **Evaluation Mechanism (h):** A function (typically Python) that automatically assesses a generated solution, returning a dictionary of scalar metrics to be maximized. This function can range from simple checks for mathematical properties to extensive simulations or model training.
- **Input API:** Code blocks to be evolved are marked with special comments (`# EVOLVE-BLOCK-START` and `# EVOLVE-BLOCK-END`). This allows AlphaEvolve to modify specific parts of an existing codebase while preserving the overall structure.
- **Flexibility in Abstraction:** AlphaEvolve offers various ways to represent solutions: evolving raw strings, functions that construct solutions from scratch (as in FunSearch), or even bespoke search algorithms to find the solution within a budget. This choice allows adaptation to problem characteristics (e.g., evolving constructor functions for symmetric solutions vs. search algorithms for non-symmetric ones).

#### 3.3. Prompt Sampling

A "Prompt sampler" component is responsible for constructing rich prompts for the LLMs. These prompts incorporate:
- **Past Solutions:** Multiple high-performing solutions sampled from the "Program database" (evolutionary pool).
- **System Instructions:** Explicit guidance on how to propose changes to a specific solution.
- **Context:** Optional human-written instructions, equations, code snippets, or relevant literature about the problem.
- **Stochastic Formatting:** Template placeholders for increased diversity in prompts.
- **Rendered Evaluation Results:** Including program code, execution outcomes, and scores.
- **Meta-Prompt Evolution:** In some advanced configurations, instructions and context for the main prompt can themselves be evolved in a separate database, enabling AlphaEvolve to improve its own prompting strategy.

#### 3.4. Creative Generation (LLM Ensemble)

The "LLMs ensemble" receives the prompts and proposes new code modifications.
- **Output Format:** Changes are typically provided as `diff` blocks (`<<<<<<< SEARCH`...`=======`...`>>>>>>> REPLACE`), allowing for targeted modifications. For very short code or complete rewrites, full code blocks can be generated.
- **Models Used:** AlphaEvolve employs a combination of **Gemini 2.0 Flash** and **Gemini 2.0 Pro**. Gemini 2.0 Flash, with its lower latency, maximizes the rate of candidate generation (throughput). Gemini 2.0 Pro, being more capable, provides occasional higher-quality suggestions that can lead to significant breakthroughs. This ensemble strategy balances exploration volume with solution quality.

#### 3.5. Evaluation

New solutions proposed by the LLMs are automatically evaluated to track progress and select candidates for future generations.
- **Execution:** The user-provided `evaluate` function (`h`) is executed on the generated code.
- **Evaluation Cascade (Hypothesis Testing):** Solutions are tested on ensembles of test cases of increasing difficulty. Less promising solutions are pruned early by failing initial, smaller-scale tests, saving compute.
- **LLM-Generated Feedback:** For properties hard to quantify programmatically (e.g., code simplicity), separate LLM calls can grade these characteristics and add them to the scores, guiding evolution.
- **Parallelized Evaluation:** Evaluations are distributed asynchronously across a cluster to reduce wall-clock time, allowing for faster iterative mutations.
- **Multiple Scores:** AlphaEvolve can optimize multiple metrics simultaneously. This not only serves intrinsic multi-objective goals but also acts instrumentally, encouraging more diverse and robust solutions by exposing the LLM to different "definitions of good."

#### 3.6. Evolution (Program Database)

The "Program database" stores all generated solutions and their evaluation results. It implements a strategy inspired by **MAP-elites** and **island-based population models** to balance exploration (maintaining diversity across the search space) and exploitation (continuously improving the best solutions found). This mechanism ensures that previously successful ideas are resurfaced to inspire future generations while preventing premature convergence.

#### 3.7. Distributed Pipeline

AlphaEvolve is implemented as an asynchronous computational pipeline using `asyncio` in Python. This distributed architecture, comprising a controller, LLM samplers, and evaluation nodes, is optimized for throughput, maximizing the number of ideas proposed and evaluated within a given computational budget.

This comprehensive methodology allows AlphaEvolve to autonomously navigate complex search spaces, identify subtle improvements, and integrate them into coherent, executable programs, leading to novel discoveries and optimizations. A key insight for mathematical problems was to evolve *heuristic search algorithms* themselves, which then search for the final construction, allowing for adaptive, multi-stage problem-solving.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AlphaEvolve demonstrated remarkable success across a diverse range of challenging problems in both fundamental science and industrial engineering, often surpassing human-designed or previous AI-generated state-of-the-art (SOTA) solutions.

#### 4.1. Faster Matrix Multiplication via Finding Novel Algorithms for Tensor Decomposition

- **Significant Breakthrough:** AlphaEvolve discovered a procedure to multiply two 4x4 complex-valued matrices using **48 scalar multiplications**. This is the first improvement after **56 years** over Strassen’s algorithm (which uses 49 multiplications over any field of characteristic 0). While Fawzi et al. (2022) found a rank-47 algorithm for 4x4 matrices over a specific field (field with 2 elements), AlphaEvolve's general field solution is a foundational advance.
- **Broad Improvement:** Beyond the 4x4 case, AlphaEvolve improved the SOTA for **14 other matrix multiplication algorithms** across various matrix dimensions (e.g., reducing rank for 2x4x5 from 33 to 32, 3x4x6 from 56 to 54, etc.).
- **Complexity of Discovered Algorithms:** The changes made by AlphaEvolve to the initial gradient-based algorithms were highly non-trivial, involving modifications to optimizers, weight initialization, loss functions (e.g., adding gradient noise, cyclical annealing for clipping, hallucination loss, discretization loss, large value penalties), and hyperparameter sweeps. This demonstrates its ability to design sophisticated optimization routines.

#### 4.2. Finding Tailored Search Algorithms for a Wide Range of Open Mathematical Problems

- AlphaEvolve was applied to **over 50 open mathematical problems** spanning analysis, combinatorics, number theory, and geometry.
- **Rediscovery and Improvement:** It rediscovered the best-known constructions in approximately 75% of cases and, notably, **surpassed the SOTA in approximately 20% of cases**, discovering new, provably better constructions.
- **Key Mathematical Discoveries:**
 - **Autocorrelation Inequalities:** Improved best known bounds on several autocorrelation inequalities in analysis (e.g., C1 from 1.5098 to 1.5053, C2 from 0.8892 to 0.8962, C3 from 1.4581 to 1.4557).
 - **Uncertainty Principles:** Refined a construction for an uncertainty principle in Fourier analysis, leading to a slightly better upper bound (C4 from 0.3523 to 0.3521).
 - **Erdős’s Minimum Overlap Problem:** Established a new upper bound for this problem (0.380927 to 0.380924).
 - **Sums and Differences of Finite Sets:** Improved the lower bound for a problem concerning sizes of sumsets and difference sets (from 1.14465 to 1.1584).
 - **Kissing Number Problem:** Improved the lower bound for the kissing number in **11 dimensions from 592 to 593**, by finding a configuration of 593 non-overlapping unit spheres touching a central sphere.
 - **Packing Problems:** Achieved new results in various packing problems, including packing hexagons inside hexagons (e.g., 12 unit hexagons in a hexagon of side 3.942, down from 4.0), minimizing the ratio of max/min distances for points in 2D and 3D, and the Heilbronn problem for triangles and convex regions.
- **Methodological Innovation:** The success in mathematics largely stemmed from AlphaEvolve's ability to evolve *heuristic search algorithms* themselves, rather than directly evolving the constructions. These heuristics, evolved to improve upon previously found solutions, created multi-stage, adaptive search strategies that are hard to replicate manually.

#### 4.3. Optimizing Google’s Computing Ecosystem

- **Data Center Scheduling (Borg):** Discovered a simple yet highly effective heuristic function for job scheduling in Google's data centers. This heuristic, derived from historical workloads, was deployed fleet-wide and continuously recovers an average of **0.7% of Google's fleet-wide compute resources** that would otherwise be stranded. This represents significant real-world cost savings and increased efficiency.
- **Gemini Kernel Engineering:** Optimized tiling heuristics for critical matrix multiplication kernels (written in Pallas) used in training large models like Gemini. AlphaEvolve achieved an average **23% kernel speedup**, resulting in a **1% reduction in Gemini’s overall training time**. The optimization time was reduced from months of manual engineering to days of automated experimentation, and the heuristic has been deployed in production, meaning Gemini is optimizing its own training process.
- **Hardware Circuit Design (TPU RTL):** Found a simple code rewrite in Verilog for a key arithmetic circuit within Google's TPUs. This optimization, reducing area and power while preserving functionality, represents **Gemini’s first direct contribution to TPU arithmetic circuits** via AlphaEvolve.
- **Directly Optimizing Compiler-Generated Code:** Challenged to optimize highly optimized XLA-generated Intermediate Representations (IRs) for the FlashAttention kernel on GPUs. AlphaEvolve achieved a **32% speedup for the FlashAttention kernel** itself and a **15% speedup in pre-/post-processing code**. This demonstrated its ability to refine compiler-generated code, with potential implications for future compiler design.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments confirmed that all key components of AlphaEvolve (the evolutionary approach, rich context in prompts, meta-prompt evolution, full-file evolution, and the use of powerful LLMs) significantly contributed to its superior performance on challenging tasks like tensor decomposition and kissing number problems. Each component alone led to a measurable degradation in performance, validating the integrated system design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AlphaEvolve represents a significant advancement in automated discovery, demonstrating the profound synergistic potential when state-of-the-art Large Language Models are integrated within a robust evolutionary framework guided by automated evaluation. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Automated Discovery:** AlphaEvolve moves beyond simple LLM code generation or single-shot problem-solving. It establishes a powerful paradigm where LLMs act as intelligent agents within a continuous, self-improving loop. By allowing LLMs to propose changes, receive precise performance feedback, and iterate over many "generations," AlphaEvolve enables a form of "machine-driven scientific method" for problems with verifiable outcomes. This sustained, empirically grounded search significantly enhances the base LLM's capabilities.

- **Accelerated Scientific Progress:** The discovery of novel, provably correct algorithms (like the 4x4 complex matrix multiplication breakthrough after 56 years) and new mathematical constructions (e.g., improved kissing number bounds, solutions to Erdős' problems) highlights AlphaEvolve's capacity to push the frontiers of fundamental science. It provides a versatile tool that can rapidly explore vast search spaces, complementing human intuition and potentially leading to breakthroughs in areas previously deemed intractable for automated methods. The ability to evolve *search algorithms* themselves, tailored to specific problem landscapes, is a particularly impactful methodological innovation for mathematical research.

- **Tangible Economic and Engineering Impact:** The successful deployment of AlphaEvolve-discovered heuristics in Google's production infrastructure yields substantial, real-world benefits. Recovering 0.7% of fleet-wide compute resources, accelerating LLM training by 1%, and optimizing hardware circuits translates into significant cost savings, increased efficiency, and faster development cycles at Google's scale. This demonstrates that AI-driven discovery can directly impact critical business operations, moving beyond academic benchmarks to deliver measurable value.

- **Improved Interpretability and Deployability:** Unlike "black box" AI solutions, AlphaEvolve produces code as its output. This inherent interpretability of code makes the discovered solutions easier to debug, understand, verify, and deploy in mission-critical systems. This is a crucial advantage, particularly for engineering applications where reliability and accountability are paramount.

- **Feedback Loops for AI Improvement:** The instance of AlphaEvolve optimizing the training process of Gemini (the LLM underpinning AlphaEvolve itself) opens up fascinating possibilities for self-improving AI systems. While current feedback loops operate on a longer timescale, this "AI for AI" capability hints at a future where AI systems can autonomously refine their own infrastructure and capabilities, leading to accelerating improvements.

- **Future Directions and Broader Applicability:**
 - **Distillation:** The paper suggests distilling the "AlphaEvolve-augmented performance" back into the base LLMs, potentially creating more capable foundational models for future iterations.
 - **Hybrid Approaches:** The main limitation is the requirement for automated evaluators. However, the authors envision combining AlphaEvolve with LLM-provided natural language feedback for high-level ideas, before transitioning to programmatic evaluation for implementation, bridging the gap to domains like natural sciences where some experiments cannot be fully automated.
 - **Integration with Compilers:** The ability to optimize compiler-generated code suggests a long-term potential for integrating AlphaEvolve into compiler workflows, automatically discovering hardware-specific optimizations.

In conclusion, AlphaEvolve is a landmark achievement that showcases the power of fusing LLMs with evolutionary computation and automated evaluation. It significantly broadens the scope of problems amenable to AI-driven discovery, delivering both fundamental scientific insights and substantial real-world engineering efficiencies, thereby charting a promising course for future research in AI-accelerated innovation.
