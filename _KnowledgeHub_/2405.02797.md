# Adapting to Distribution Shift by Visual Domain Prompt Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> VDPG introduces a method for Few-Shot Test-Time Domain Adaptation (FSTT-DA) by generating domain-specific visual prompts to guide frozen CLIP features. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on WILDS benchmarks, demonstrating an 8.2% Macro F1 improvement on iWildCam over baselines, and outperforms existing methods on DomainNet by reaching 59.8% average accuracy, all while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Machine learning models often suffer significant performance degradation under distribution shift when test data distributions differ from training data.
- Existing Few-Shot Test-Time Domain Adaptation (FSTT-DA) methods are often inefficient, struggle to effectively leverage powerful foundation models like CLIP, or fail to extract truly transferable domain knowledge from limited target data.
- Directly finetuning large foundation models with few-shot target data can compromise their inherent robustness, is computationally expensive, and often restricted in black-box scenarios.

## Method

- ==VDPG== utilizes a frozen CLIP image encoder to preserve its robust generalized features, preventing performance degradation that can result from finetuning with limited data.
- A learnable ==Knowledge Bank== and a ==Conditional Domain Prompt Generator== synthesize domain-specific visual prompts for a target domain using a few unlabeled samples and cross-attention.
- A ==Domain Guidance Module== integrates the generated prompt into CLIP's visual features for all images in the target domain, and an ==episodic meta-learning== strategy, with domain-aware contrastive and correlation losses, trains the system to adapt effectively.

## Results

- VDPG achieved state-of-the-art performance on three out of four WILDS benchmarks (iWildCam, Camelyon17, FMoW), for example, improving Macro F1 on iWildCam by **8.2%** and achieving **96.0%** accuracy on FMoW.
- The method surpassed all compared approaches on DomainNet, reaching an average accuracy of **59.8%** across its six domains, demonstrating superior adaptation capabilities.
- VDPG is computationally efficient at test-time, requiring only **19.7T FLOPS** for inference on **512** images (using ViT/B-16) and avoids costly backbone finetuning, making it suitable for resource-constrained deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating and applying domain-specific visual prompts to guide frozen foundation model features provides an effective and efficient FSTT-DA strategy without compromising the model's intrinsic robustness.
> - Episodic meta-learning, combined with a transferable knowledge bank and a domain-aware contrastive loss, enables the distillation of pure domain-level characteristics from limited unlabeled data.
> - The generated prompts are critically important and truly domain-specific, exhibiting strong intra-domain similarity and inter-domain discrepancy, which is essential for effective adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses the critical and pervasive problem of **distribution shift**, where a machine learning model's performance degrades when the test data distribution differs from its training data distribution. This is a fundamental challenge for deploying AI models in dynamic real-world environments. The paper specifically operates within the paradigm of **Few-Shot Test-Time Domain Adaptation (FSTT-DA)**, a highly practical and challenging setting.

The broader research landscape related to distribution shift includes:
- **Domain Generalization (DG):** Aims to learn models that generalize well to *unseen* target domains *without accessing any target data during training or testing*. This is achieved by learning domain-invariant features or through data augmentation and meta-learning.
- **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA):** Assumes access to *unlabeled target data* during the *training phase* to align source and target distributions. This is often impractical due to privacy concerns or the inability to gather target data upfront.
- **Test-Time Adaptation (TTA):** Adapts a pre-trained model to *unlabeled target data* *at test time*. This is more practical as it doesn't require prior access to target domains during training, making it suitable for online or deployment scenarios.

FSTT-DA, the specific setting of this paper, is a more constrained and challenging variant of TTA. It aims to adapt the model using *only a few unlabeled data samples* from the target domain, and this adaptation happens *once* for the entire target domain. Previous FSTT-DA methods have faced several limitations:
1. **Inefficient knowledge storage:** Some store domain knowledge in large expert networks, leading to high computational and storage costs.
2. **Lack of foundation model (FM) utilization:** Many prior works do not leverage recent large-scale foundation models (like CLIP) which possess strong out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization capabilities due to web-scale pre-training.
3. **Inability to adapt FMs:** Directly finetuning FMs with few-shot data can degrade their inherent robustness. Moreover, access to FM weights for finetuning or gradient calculation is often restricted (black-box setting).
4. **Suboptimal knowledge extraction:** Previous methods might learn semantic patterns rather than transferable domain knowledge, often by separating source knowledge modeling and adaptation into disjoint training stages.
5. **Computational overhead at inference:** Some methods require continuous adaptation or text encoder inference, making them less efficient for resource-constrained deployment.

This work fits into this landscape by proposing a novel FSTT-DA approach that directly addresses these limitations. It positions itself at the forefront of leveraging **Visual Prompt Tuning** in conjunction with **frozen Foundation Models** and **meta-learning** for robust and efficient domain adaptation. The key innovations lie in creating a shared knowledge bank, a conditional prompt generator, and a domain-aware contrastive loss, all trained episodically, to efficiently extract and apply domain-specific knowledge without altering the robust FM backbone.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and efficient method for **Few-Shot Test-Time Domain Adaptation (FSTT-DA)**, allowing a pre-trained model to adapt to new, unseen data distributions using only a handful of unlabeled examples from the target domain.

The motivations behind this objective stem from critical gaps in existing approaches:
1. **Leveraging Foundation Models (FMs):** FMs like CLIP, trained on vast datasets, demonstrate remarkable out-of-distribution generalization. However, directly finetuning these massive models with limited target data can be detrimental to their robustness and is often infeasible due to computational cost or restricted access to model weights (black-box scenarios). The authors are motivated to harness the power of FMs' generalized visual features without finetuning their backbone, thereby preserving their intrinsic robustness.
2. **Effective Domain Knowledge Extraction:** From a few unlabeled target samples, it is challenging to extract rich, transferable domain-specific knowledge. Previous methods often fail to adequately learn transferable knowledge from source domains, or inadvertently focus on semantic patterns rather than domain-level characteristics. The goal is to purify this domain knowledge, ensuring it is category-agnostic and truly representative of the domain shift.
3. **Efficient Knowledge Transfer and Adaptation:** Storing domain knowledge in large, domain-specific expert networks is computationally expensive and scales poorly with the number of domains. Moreover, separating the process of learning source knowledge and adaptation into independent stages can compromise knowledge transfer. The authors aim for a more compact and flexible mechanism for knowledge transfer, ideally through a parameter-efficient approach like visual prompt tuning, and a unified training process.
4. **Practicality for Deployment:** The desired solution must be efficient enough for deployment on resource-constrained devices, meaning adaptation should be fast (preferably once per domain) and minimize memory and computational overheads.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Adapt foundation models for FSTT-DA while preserving their OOD generalization capabilities by *not* finetuning their weights.
- Develop a mechanism to effectively learn and condense transferable domain knowledge from multiple source domains into a domain-specific prompt using only a few unlabeled target samples.
- Integrate this domain-specific prompt to guide the frozen foundation model's features for improved performance on the target domain.
- Ensure the overall approach is parameter-efficient, computationally light, and robust, making it practical for real-world deployment in black-box and resource-constrained settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Visual Domain Prompt Generator (VDPG)**, tackles FSTT-DA by generating domain-specific visual prompts to guide a frozen Foundation Model (FM), specifically CLIP's image encoder. The approach is structured around a "knowledge bank," a "domain prompt generator," a "guidance module," and a meta-learning-based training scheme with specific loss functions.

1. **Foundation Model Backbone:**
 - VDPG leverages the powerful **CLIP image encoder** (e.g., ViT-L/14 or ViT-B/16) to extract robust, generalized image embeddings.
 - Crucially, the CLIP image encoder is **kept frozen** throughout training and inference. This preserves its intrinsic out-of-distribution generalization capabilities, which finetuning with limited data could impair. It also allows the method to work in black-box scenarios where FM weights are inaccessible.

2. **Transferable Knowledge Bank (KB):**
 - A learnable **Knowledge Bank (B)**, represented as a matrix $B \in R^{Z \times d}$, is introduced to store transferable domain knowledge from source domains. $Z$ is the number of knowledge vectors, and $d$ is the CLIP embedding dimension.
 - To ensure each vector in the KB captures unique characteristics and avoids redundancy, a **correlation loss ($L_{corr}$)** is applied. This loss minimizes the inner product between different knowledge vectors, encouraging orthogonality and diverse knowledge encoding.

3. **Conditional Domain Prompt Generator (G):**
 - This module is responsible for synthesizing a domain-specific visual prompt for a given target domain, conditioned on a few unlabeled images from that domain and the knowledge bank.
 - It's built using a **cross-attention mechanism**. The image embeddings $E(\mathbf{x})$ from the few-shot target data act as Keys (K) and Values (V), while the Knowledge Bank B acts as Queries (Q).
 - The output of the cross-attention is then average-pooled across the batch dimension to produce a single **domain-specific prompt P** for the entire target domain. This prompt encapsulates the extracted domain characteristics.

4. **Domain Guidance Module (GM):**
 - The generated domain prompt P (along with a learnable [CLS] token) is fed into a **Guidance Module (GM)**, which incorporates this domain-specific knowledge into the CLIP visual features $E(\mathbf{x})$ of all images in the target domain.
 - The GM also uses stacked **cross-attention layers**, allowing bi-directional interaction: prompt tokens attend to image features, and image features attend to prompt tokens. This effectively "guides" the generalized CLIP features towards the specific target domain distribution.
 - The output [CLS] token from the GM is then passed through a simple MLP Head to produce the final classification or regression prediction $y'$.

5. **Domain-Aware Contrastive Loss ($L_{dac}$):**
 - To ensure the generated prompts are truly domain-specific (category-agnostic, intra-domain similar, inter-domain discrepant), a **domain-aware contrastive loss ($L_{dac}$)** is employed.
 - This loss contrasts the generated domain prompts from different images. It pulls prompts from images within the *same domain* closer together and pushes prompts from images from *different domains* further apart, regardless of their semantic categories.

6. **Training Scheme (Episodic Meta-Learning):**
 - VDPG employs an **episodic training strategy**, inspired by meta-learning, to simulate the FSTT-DA setting during training.
 - In each episode:
 - A source domain $D_n^s$ is sampled.
 - A small **support set** (unlabeled, $x_S$) and a **query set** (labeled, $x_Q, y_Q$) are drawn from $D_n^s$.
 - A **contrastive batch X** is formed by combining $x_S$ with batches from several other randomly sampled source domains.
 - The domain prompt P is generated using $x_S$ and the KB via G.
 - P guides the features of $x_Q$ through GM, and predictions $y'_Q$ are made.
 - The overall loss function ($L_{all} = L_{task} + \lambda L_{corr} + \gamma L_{dac}$) is computed. $L_{task}$ (e.g., cross-entropy) is for the classification/regression task on the query set.
 - Parameters of G, KB, and GM are updated using SGD.
 - This episodic training aligns the learning objective (learning to adapt) with the test-time adaptation process, making the generated prompts and guidance module robust to unseen domains.
 - The method can also benefit from pre-training G and B on large-scale unlabeled data with domain IDs, further enhancing domain knowledge extraction.

7. **Inference:**
 - At test time, for each new target domain, a small set of unlabeled images (e.g., 16 images) is used to generate a single domain prompt P via the pre-trained G and KB.
 - This generated P is then cached and used to guide the CLIP features of *all subsequent images* from that target domain via GM for inference. This "generate once, apply to all" strategy makes it highly efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VDPG demonstrates superior performance across a range of challenging benchmarks, validating its effectiveness in Few-Shot Test-Time Domain Adaptation (FSTT-DA).

**Key Findings:**

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance on WILDS Benchmarks:**
 - VDPG significantly outperforms previous CNN-based and ViT-based methods (including ERM, CORAL, IRM, ARM, Meta-DMoE, MABN, Zero-shot CLIP, FLYP) on three out of four large-scale WILDS image testbeds: iWildCam, Camelyon17, and FMoW.
 - For example, on iWildCam (classification), VDPG achieves 78.8% accuracy and 46.5% Macro F1, surpassing the best baseline (MABN) by margins of 0.4% and 8.2% respectively. On FMoW, it achieves 96.0% accuracy, substantially higher than MABN's 92.4%.
 - On PovertyMap (regression), VDPG performs comparably to SOTA (0.51 Pearson r, 0.83 WC Pearson r) despite using only 3 out of 8 input channels due to CLIP's 3-channel input constraint.
 - Notably, VDPG consistently outperforms methods that finetune the CLIP backbone (e.g., FLYP), highlighting the benefit of keeping the FM frozen. Zero-shot CLIP often performs poorly, emphasizing the need for adaptation.

2. **Superior Performance on DomainNet:**
 - VDPG achieves an average accuracy of 59.8% across DomainNet's six domains, outperforming all compared methods, including CNN-based baselines, Meta-DMoE, MABN, and DoPrompt.
 - It notably surpasses CLIP-based finetuning (ERM on ViT-B/16) which performs worse than even zero-shot CLIP, further reinforcing the importance of not finetuning the FM backbone for robustness.

3. **Robustness to In-Distribution (ID) and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Shifts:**
 - On iWildCam, VDPG demonstrates strong performance on both ID (77.2% Acc, 60.2% F1) and OOD (78.8% Acc, 46.5% F1) settings.
 - It outperforms even ensembled versions of FLYP, indicating that its domain-specific guidance is more effective than ensemble strategies for balancing ID and OOD performance.

4. **Validation of Domain-Specific Prompt Generation:**
 - **Qualitative Analysis (t-SNE):** Visualization shows that features guided by VDPG's generated prompts are much better clustered and more discriminative than raw CLIP features, indicating effective domain adaptation.
 - **Ablation (Prompt Replacement):** Replacing the generated domain prompts with random, knowledge bank (KB), or zero prompts leads to significant performance drops (e.g., OOD F1 from 46.5% to as low as 24.5% for random prompts). This strongly confirms the critical role and meaningfulness of the generated prompts.
 - **Inter-domain discrepancy and intra-domain similarity:** Analysis of L2 distances between generated prompts confirms that prompts for different domains are diverse, while prompts for instances within the same domain (regardless of class) are similar.
 - **Correlation with Domain Diversity:** Swapping prompts between domains shows that larger distances (less correlation) between swapped prompts lead to greater performance drops, confirming that the generated prompts reflect true domain diversity.

5. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Effectiveness:**
 - **Episodic Training:** The ablation study shows that incorporating the prompt generator and knowledge bank *without* episodic training actually harms performance under ERM. Only when episodic meta-learning is applied (row 7 vs. row 6 in Table 5) does performance significantly boost, proving its necessity for learning transferable domain knowledge and adaptation mechanisms.
 - **Loss Functions:** Both the correlation loss ($L_{corr}$) for the knowledge bank and the domain-aware contrastive loss ($L_{dac}$) for prompt generation contribute positively to overall performance, with their combined use leading to the best results.
 - **Unlabeled Data Pre-training:** Utilizing large-scale unlabeled data with domain IDs for pre-training the generator and knowledge bank further improves performance.

6. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - VDPG is computationally efficient at test-time. It only requires 19.7T FLOPS for inference on 512 images (using ViT/B-16), which is lower than FLYP (26.2T FLOPS) and DoPrompt (36.7T FLOPS).
 - Crucially, VDPG does not finetune the backbone, making it memory-efficient and suitable for resource-constrained environments. The prompt is generated only once per target domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VDPG framework represents a significant step forward in addressing distribution shifts, particularly in the challenging and practical Few-Shot Test-Time Domain Adaptation (FSTT-DA) setting. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Robust and Efficient Adaptation of Foundation Models:** VDPG demonstrates a highly effective strategy for leveraging the strong out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization capabilities of large foundation models (FMs) like CLIP, *without* the need for finetuning their substantial weights. This is crucial because finetuning often compromises FM robustness and requires extensive computational resources and access to model internals. This "frozen FM + prompt generation" paradigm could become a standard for adapting FMs in various practical scenarios.

2. **Practicality for Real-World Deployment:** The method's efficiency is a major strength.
 - **Black-Box Compatibility:** It only requires pre-computed features from the FM, making it applicable even when access to FM weights or gradients is restricted (e.g., via APIs).
 - **Resource-Constrained Environments:** Generating a domain prompt only once for an entire target domain, followed by lightweight guidance, significantly reduces test-time computational overhead and memory footprint, making it ideal for on-device deployment or applications with limited computational budgets.

3. **Novel Domain Knowledge Extraction:** The introduction of a learnable knowledge bank, a conditional domain prompt generator, and a domain-aware contrastive loss provides a novel and effective mechanism for distilling and purifying transferable domain-specific knowledge from limited unlabeled data. This is a key methodological contribution that could inspire similar approaches in other knowledge transfer tasks.

4. **Meta-Learning for Improved Generalization:** The episodic training strategy effectively aligns the training process with the test-time adaptation objective, allowing the model to "learn to adapt" across diverse source domains. This enhances the generalizability of the prompt generation and guidance mechanisms to truly unseen target distributions.

5. **Broad Applicability:** While demonstrated on image classification and regression tasks, the core idea of generating domain-specific prompts to guide frozen features has potential for broader application across different modalities and tasks experiencing distribution shifts (e.g., medical imaging, remote sensing, natural language processing with vision-language models).

6. **Advancing Research in Domain Adaptation:** This work contributes to the growing body of research on prompt-based learning and parameter-efficient adaptation. It provides a strong baseline and novel insights into how to effectively combine these techniques with foundation models and meta-learning to tackle complex distribution shifts.

In conclusion, VDPG offers a practical, robust, and efficient solution to a critical problem in AI deployment. By effectively bridging the gap between powerful foundation models and the realities of real-world data variability, it has the potential to significantly impact the reliability and widespread adoption of AI systems in dynamic environments.
