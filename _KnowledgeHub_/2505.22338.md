# Text2Grad: Reinforcement Learning from Natural Language Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> TEXT2GRAD introduces a framework that converts free-form natural language critiques into actionable, span-level gradients to directly optimize large language model parameters, achieving state-of-the-art performance and faster convergence across summarization, code generation, and question answering tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) uses scalar rewards that obscure fine-grained reasons for an output's quality, leading to imprecise credit assignment and slow, opaque learning.
- Existing methods using natural language feedback (e.g., ReAct, Reflexion) rely on inference-time self-correction without internalizing lessons into the model's parameters, making feedback ephemeral and non-transferable.

## Method

- The framework defines a ==Natural Language Gradient== (NL-Gradient) that transforms textual critiques into token-level pseudo-rewards to guide precise policy updates.
- A dual-feedback annotation process, leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, generates both natural language critiques and structured span-level reward maps for training.
- A generative reward model is trained to jointly produce these critiques and span maps, which are then used with a modified PPO objective for fine-grained, token-level policy optimization.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across summarization (e.g., +**25.3%** BLEU over PPO), code generation (e.g., +5.8 on MBPP+ over PPO), and open-domain QA (e.g., +2.3 AlpacaEval 2.0 over PPO).
- Demonstrated faster convergence (**22%** faster than PPO) and improved interpretability, with qualitative evaluations showing TEXT2GRAD winning **12%** more often against PPO on summarization.
- The method generalizes across model architectures (Llama3.1, Mistral-**7B**) with modest computational overhead (9-**11%** per training step) and cost-efficient span-level annotation (70-**85%** token cost reduction).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Converting rich natural language feedback into targeted, span-level gradients provides more precise and interpretable learning signals than coarse scalar rewards.
> - Chain-of-Thought reasoning is crucial for generating high-fidelity, semantically aligned natural language critiques and structured span-level labels.
> - Token-level pseudo-rewards, when combined with Generalized Advantage Estimation, enable sharper and more discriminative advantage estimates, leading to more efficient and robust policy updates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "TEXT2GRAD" operates within the domain of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment, specifically addressing how LLMs can be trained to better adhere to human preferences and instructions. This field has seen significant advancements with the advent of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).

Traditional RLHF methodologies, such as those employing Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), typically convert human preferences or comparisons into scalar reward signals. While effective in enhancing helpfulness and safety, this scalarization process reduces complex, multi-faceted feedback into a single numerical value. This reduction can obscure the fine-grained reasons behind an output's success or failure, leading to challenges in precise credit assignment, slower learning convergence, and reduced interpretability of the model's behavior. Previous work, such as Process Reward Models (PRMs), has attempted to provide finer credit assignment by breaking down tasks into steps but still relies on scalar signals for each step.

A complementary line of research has explored retaining feedback in its natural language form. Methods like ReAct and Reflexion prompt LLMs to generate self-critiques and use these textual reflections to self-correct during inference. These approaches offer improved interpretability and, in some cases, enhanced task performance, drawing inspiration from human reasoning processes. However, a limitation of these methods is that the model's parameters remain frozen; the natural language feedback is not internalized into the model's weights. This means that lessons learned are not retained, requiring repeated corrections for similar errors and making the feedback ephemeral.

TEXT2GRAD positions itself by bridging these two paradigms. It aims to integrate the richness and interpretability of natural language feedback with the parameter-updating capabilities of reinforcement learning. Unlike traditional RLHF, it does not collapse feedback into a scalar. Unlike inference-time reflection methods, it uses natural language feedback to directly adjust model parameters. The paper proposes a framework that transforms free-form textual critiques into actionable, span-level gradients, allowing for targeted policy optimization within the training loop. This approach seeks to overcome the limitations of scalar rewards by providing precise, interpretable learning signals and to address the ephemeral nature of inference-time textual critiques by internalizing feedback into the model's architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the TEXT2GRAD framework stem from identified limitations in existing LLM alignment strategies.

**Motivation:**

1. **Limitations of Scalar Rewards in RLHF:** Current RLHF techniques predominantly rely on scalar rewards derived from human preferences. While effective for broad alignment goals like helpfulness and safety, these scalar rewards mask the specific reasons for an output's quality. For instance, a single negative score for a generated summary does not indicate *which* parts are inaccurate, *what* information is missing, or *how* to correct it. This coarse feedback leads to:
 - **Imprecise Credit Assignment:** The model receives a global signal for an entire sequence, making it difficult to pinpoint the exact tokens or spans responsible for the reward. This can cause the model to make broad, inefficient adjustments.
 - **Slow and Opaque Learning:** Without fine-grained guidance, the learning process can be slower as the model must infer the underlying causes of reward signals. The updates are less interpretable, making it challenging to understand why the model behaves in a certain way or how it improves.
 - **Need for Robust Regularization:** Scalar-reward RLHF often requires Kullback-Leibler (KL) penalties to maintain policy stability, indicating the inherent challenge in directing updates solely from a single numerical value.

2. **Ephemeral Nature of Inference-Time Natural Language Feedback:** Recent advancements have shown that LLMs can generate and utilize natural language critiques for self-correction during inference. Methods like ReAct and Reflexion enhance interpretability and can improve performance by allowing models to reflect on their outputs. However, these methods typically operate by prompting or internal reasoning without modifying the model's underlying parameters. Consequently:
 - **Feedback is Not Internalized:** The model does not "learn" from the critique in a lasting way. Each correction must be re-derived, leading to inefficient and non-transferable improvements across similar tasks or future interactions.
 - **Limited Impact on Core Capabilities:** While useful for immediate error correction, this approach does not fundamentally enhance the model's core policy or knowledge base, as its weights remain frozen.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations, TEXT2GRAD aims to achieve the following:

1. **Formulate Natural Language Feedback as Actionable Gradients:** The central objective is to transform free-form textual critiques directly into differentiable learning signals that can precisely update the policy parameters of a language model. This involves defining a "Natural Language Gradient" (NL-Gradient) that maps feedback phrases to relevant token spans and converts these alignments into numerical pseudo-rewards.
2. **Enable Fine-Grained, Span-Level Policy Optimization:** By deriving token-level pseudo-rewards from natural language critiques, the framework seeks to enable policy updates that are localized and targeted. This means adjusting only the "offending portions" of the model's output generation policy, rather than applying global nudges based on sequence-level scalar rewards.
3. **Enhance Interpretability and Efficiency of LLM Alignment:** The direct integration of human-readable feedback into the training loop is intended to make the learning process more transparent and understandable. Precise credit assignment is hypothesized to lead to faster convergence and more sample-efficient learning, as the model receives clear indications of what to improve and where.
4. **Develop a Robust and Scalable End-to-End Pipeline:** To realize the NL-Gradient, the project aims to construct a complete pipeline comprising:
 - A high-quality, scalable annotation process for generating paired textual critiques and span-level labels.
 - A reward model capable of jointly generating explanatory natural language critiques and structured span-level reward maps.
 - A policy optimizer that can effectively utilize these fine-grained reward signals for gradient updates.

In essence, TEXT2GRAD seeks to move beyond traditional scalar reward limitations and ephemeral inference-time critiques by converting the rich information within natural language feedback into a direct and precise training signal, thereby fostering more capable and aligned language models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TEXT2GRAD introduces a novel, three-stage pipeline to convert natural language feedback into actionable gradients for policy optimization. The core concept is the Natural Language Gradient (NL-Gradient), which transforms textual critiques into token-level signals to guide policy updates.

#### 3.1. Natural Language Gradient (NL-Gradient) Definition

The NL-Gradient is defined as $\nabla_{NL}(c \rightarrow y) = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \delta_t \nabla_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(y_t | x, y_{<t})$. Here, $y = (y_1, \ldots, y_T)$ is a generated sequence, $c$ is its textual critique, and $\{\delta_t\}_{t=1}^T$ are token-level pseudo-rewards derived by aligning $c$ to $y$. This formulation enables:
- **Fine-Grained Guidance:** Pseudo-rewards $\delta_t$ specifically highlight tokens needing improvement.
- **Interpretability:** Each update is grounded in human-readable feedback.
- **Transferability:** The model learns to map text to gradient signals, allowing generalization.

This approach is compatible with both Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) and RLHF. A theoretical analysis (Appendix A) suggests that token-level rewards lead to sharper and more discriminative advantage estimates than end-of-sequence rewards, particularly amplifying early feedback differences. For example, a token-level reward 20 steps before the end is weighted approximately 2.8 times more strongly than in an end-of-sequence setting with typical GAE parameters.

#### 3.2. Three-Stage Pipeline

TEXT2GRAD comprises three interdependent components.

#### 3.3. Dual-Feedback Reward Annotation

This stage generates dense, interpretable feedback structured for token-level learning. It jointly produces:
- **Free-form natural language critiques (c):** Explanations of strengths and weaknesses.
- **Structured span-level reward maps (A(y)):** Labels (positive, neutral, negative) assigned to specific token spans within the generated response $y$.

In the absence of human feedback, a large language model (LLM) such as GPT-4o is employed for automated annotation. This process uses **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting**:
- The LLM first performs step-by-step reasoning about the response quality.
- It then produces a critique ($c$) grounded in this reasoning.
- Finally, it derives a span-level reward map ($A(y)$) where each labeled span must be explicitly anchored to evidence in the critique, ensuring semantic alignment. Only positive and negative spans are explicitly labeled to reduce overhead, with neutral spans remaining implicit.

These labeled spans are then converted into **token-level pseudo-rewards ($\delta_t$)**:
- If a token $y_t$ is part of a positive span, $\delta_t = +1$.
- If $y_t$ is part of a negative span, $\delta_t = -1$.
- Otherwise (neutral or unlabeled), $\delta_t = 0$.
This yields a token-level reward vector $\delta = (\delta_1, \ldots, \delta_T)$, providing fine-grained supervision. The method is designed to be robust to varying span lengths and prioritizes precision in span selection to avoid noisy signals.

#### 3.4. Reward Model Learning

To operationalize NL-Gradient optimization, a dedicated reward model ($R_\phi$) is trained. Instead of predicting a scalar score, this model is a generative LLM (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct) fine-tuned to **jointly generate natural language critiques and structured span-level feedback** in a single autoregressive sequence.
- Given a prompt $x$ and response $y$, the reward model outputs a combined sequence $z = [c; A(y)]$.
- It is optimized using maximum likelihood with a cross-entropy loss: $L_R(\phi) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y,z)\in D_R} [\log p_\phi(z | x, y)]$.
This formulation offers flexibility across tasks, provides fine-grained gradient flow through tokenized outputs, and produces interpretable feedback in a unified model.

#### 3.5. NL-Gradient Policy Optimization

This stage leverages the token-level pseudo-rewards generated by the reward model to optimize the policy LLM ($\pi_\theta$). Traditional RL relies on sequence-level scalar rewards, which are unsuitable for fine-grained credit assignment. TEXT2GRAD addresses this by:
- **Token-Level Advantage Estimation:** For a given response $y$, the trained reward model $R_\phi$ generates the token-wise pseudo-rewards $\{\delta_t\}_{t=1}^T$. These are combined with an optional KL-penalty term $r_{KL_t}$ to form $r_{total,A_t} = \delta_t + r_{KL_t}$.
- **Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE):** Token-level advantages $A_t$ are computed using these dense rewards: $A_t = \sum_{l=0}^{T-t-1} (\gamma\lambda)^l \delta_{TD_{t+l}}$, where $\delta_{TD_t} = r_{total,A_t} + \gamma V_\psi(x, y_{<t+1}) - V_\psi(x, y_{<t})$, with $\gamma$ as the discount factor and $\lambda$ as the GAE parameter. $V_\psi$ is the value function.
- **PPO Objective with NL-Gradient:** These token-level advantages are integrated into a modified Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) objective:
 $L_{PPO}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_t [\min (\rho_t A_t, \text{clip}(\rho_t, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)A_t)] - \beta H(\pi_\theta(\cdot | x, y_{<t}))$.
 Here, $\rho_t$ is the importance ratio, $H$ is the entropy bonus, $\beta$ is the entropy coefficient, and $\epsilon$ is the clipping threshold.
By feeding these precise, feedback-aligned gradients, the policy model learns to make targeted adjustments, leading to more interpretable, efficient, and accurate improvements. The overall framework generalizes across tasks and primarily requires a token-weighting wrapper on top of PPO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TEXT2GRAD's effectiveness was evaluated across three distinct domains: summarization (SLF5K), code generation (KodCode), and open-domain question answering (UltraFeedback), demonstrating consistent improvements over established baselines.

#### 4.1. Reward Model Evaluation

The unified reward model, trained to generate both critiques and span-level labels, was crucial for the TEXT2GRAD framework.
- **CoT Impact:** The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategy significantly improved reward model performance. The CoT-based model achieved a 62% win rate against a non-CoT variant on SLF5K in pairwise comparisons and demonstrated robust alignment with human annotations (86% on SLF5K, 82% on UltraFeedback, 94% on KodCode).
- **Span-Level Metrics:** Precision for identifying positive spans was generally high (e.g., 58% on SLF5K, 66% on UltraFeedback), which is critical as false positive rewards can corrupt gradients. While recall for negative tokens was moderate (e.g., 43% on SLF5K), this was attributed to the high proportion of neutral tokens, meaning only a minority receive non-zero rewards.
- **Fidelity and Efficiency:** The annotation pipeline ensured high fidelity, with unmatched-span rates below 2.5% across all datasets, confirming that generated spans were exact quotes from model outputs. Span-level annotation reduced token costs by 70-85% compared to dense token-level labeling.

#### 4.2. Summarization (SLF5K Dataset)

TEXT2GRAD was evaluated on its ability to generate summaries aligned with human references.
- **Superior Performance:** TEXT2GRAD achieved state-of-the-art performance across all metrics, including ROUGE, BLEU, and BERTScore. It surpassed PPO by +25.3% BLEU and +6.7 ROUGE-L, DPO by +6.7 ROUGE-L, and SFT+Reflection by +3.3 ROUGE-L.
- **Faster Convergence & Interpretability:** Training curves indicated that TEXT2GRAD converged 22% faster than PPO. Qualitative evaluation using GPT-4-as-a-Judge showed TEXT2GRAD winning 12% more often against PPO, suggesting more coherent and informative outputs.
- **Span-based vs. Dense Labeling:** A dense token-level labeling baseline, despite maximal supervision, performed substantially worse (ROUGE-L: 0.196 vs. 0.291 for TEXT2GRAD), highlighting that precise span-based signals are more effective than noisy dense signals, especially when many tokens are functionally irrelevant.
- **CoT Importance:** Removing CoT reasoning from TEXT2GRAD consistently degraded performance across all metrics.

#### 4.3. Code Generation (KodCode Dataset)

The framework was tested on generating correct Python solutions, where precise error localization is critical.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** TEXT2GRAD outperformed all baselines, including proprietary and fine-tuned models, across HumanEval and MBPP benchmarks. It demonstrated superior robustness by gaining +5.8 on MBPP+ and +3.6 on HumanEval+ over PPO.
- **Critical Role of CoT:** The ablated variant without CoT reasoning underperformed by 6.9 points on average, underscoring that structured natural language feedback is essential for effective token-level credit assignment in code generation.

#### 4.4. Open-Domain Question Answering (UltraFeedback Dataset)

TEXT2GRAD was assessed for general-purpose alignment, factual accuracy, and conversational coherence.
- **Improved Alignment and Reasoning:** TEXT2GRAD consistently improved over the base model and PPO across AlpacaEval 2.0, ARC-Challenge, and MT-Bench. It showed a 2.3-point improvement over PPO on AlpacaEval 2.0 (instruction alignment) and a 1.7-point improvement on ARC-Challenge (reasoning).
- **CoT Reinforcement:** Similar to other tasks, excluding CoT reasoning during annotation led to a notable performance decrease (e.g., -6.1 points on AlpacaEval), reinforcing the significance of natural language explanations for generating effective token-level supervision.

#### 4.5. Cross-Model Generalization

TEXT2GRAD's gains were validated on Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, demonstrating its generalizability across different model families. It consistently outperformed baselines on code generation (average pass@1 improved from 42.9 to 45.3), QA (AlpacaEval from 26.17 to 29.40), and summarization (ROUGE-L reached 0.24).

#### 4.6. Case Study

A qualitative case study in code generation illustrated TEXT2GRAD's precision. When correcting a faulty `match_parens` implementation, a scalar reward model provided a generic negative score, while TEXT2GRAD's natural language reward model highlighted the exact faulty span and explained the error. This allowed for a targeted NL-Gradient update that rewritten only the highlighted lines, successfully passing all unit tests.

#### 4.7. Annotation and Training Efficiency

- **Modest Overhead:** The computational overhead introduced by TEXT2GRAD was modest, approximately 9–11% per training step compared to PPO. This was primarily due to a single additional forward pass of the reward model per sampled trajectory.
- **Cost Efficiency:** Span-level annotation reduced token costs by 70–85% compared to dense token-level labeling, with annotation costs on the order of $10^{-3}$ USD per sample.

#### 4.8. Span Length and Human Alignment

- **CoT and Span Quality:** CoT reasoning in annotation led to more targeted span selections, particularly for problematic regions, and maintained consistently high annotation accuracy (93.1%–96.4%) across all span lengths. Without CoT, accuracy varied more with span length.
- **Human Agreement:** Human evaluation showed high agreement with the reward model's critiques and span selections: 94% on KodCode, 86% on SLF5K, and 82% on UltraFeedback. This indicates the practical utility and robustness of the annotation pipeline even in subjective domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TEXT2GRAD framework introduces a novel approach to large language model alignment, addressing several limitations of current methods and offering implications for the development of more capable and interpretable AI systems.

**1. Bridging Feedback Modalities:** TEXT2GRAD's primary significance lies in its ability to bridge the gap between rich, expressive natural language feedback and the parameter-updating mechanisms of reinforcement learning. By converting free-form textual critiques into actionable, span-level gradients, it moves beyond the limitations of coarse scalar rewards, which often obscure the specifics of an output's quality. Concurrently, it internalizes feedback, overcoming the ephemeral nature of inference-time natural language critiques that do not modify model parameters.

**2. Enhanced Precision and Interpretability in LLM Alignment:** The core contribution of the Natural Language Gradient (NL-Gradient) is enabling fine-grained credit assignment. Instead of global policy adjustments, TEXT2GRAD allows for targeted modifications to specific tokens or spans responsible for errors or successes. This precision is significant for complex tasks like code generation or detailed summarization, where subtle inaccuracies can invalidate an entire output. The grounding of gradient updates in human-readable critiques also enhances the interpretability of the learning process, offering insights into *why* the model's behavior changes.

**3. Improved Learning Efficiency and Robustness:** The experimental results consistently demonstrate that TEXT2GRAD outperforms scalar-reward PPO and prompt-based baselines across summarization, code generation, and question answering. This indicates that fine-grained feedback leads to more efficient learning and faster convergence. In code generation, the method also showed superior robustness to adversarial test cases, suggesting that precise error correction translates to more reliable model outputs.

**4. Scalable and High-Fidelity Feedback Generation:** The use of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting with advanced LLMs (like GPT-4o) for dual-feedback annotation provides a scalable and high-fidelity mechanism for generating the necessary supervision. This automated approach reduces the reliance on extensive human labeling, making the framework practical for large-scale training. The generative reward model further streamlines this by jointly producing critiques and span maps, simplifying the pipeline.

**5. Generalizability Across Tasks and Architectures:** TEXT2GRAD's consistent performance gains across diverse domains (summarization, code generation, QA) and different model architectures (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2) suggests its broad applicability. This indicates that the principle of converting natural language feedback into localized gradients is a generalizable method for LLM fine-tuning.

**Potential Impact:**
The TEXT2GRAD framework has the potential to significantly influence the development of future language models:
- **More Controllable and Aligned LLMs:** By offering finer control over the learning process, TEXT2GRAD could lead to LLMs that are more precisely aligned with nuanced human instructions and preferences, reducing the occurrence of subtle errors that coarse rewards might overlook.
- **Facilitating Human-AI Collaboration:** The ability to provide and utilize natural language critiques directly as training signals could enhance human-AI interaction, allowing users to guide model behavior with descriptive feedback rather than just preference comparisons or ratings.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens new avenues for research into gradient-based optimization from unstructured text, potentially inspiring novel approaches to self-correction, knowledge injection, and even complex reasoning tasks where detailed, natural language explanations are paramount.
- **Reduced Training Costs in the Long Run:** While introducing some computational overhead, the improved sample efficiency and targeted learning might lead to overall cost reductions in achieving desired alignment outcomes, especially as model sizes and training datasets continue to grow.

Overall, TEXT2GRAD contributes a principled methodology for harnessing the expressive power of natural language feedback to build more precise, interpretable, and adaptable language models, moving towards a paradigm of "human-like" supervision.
