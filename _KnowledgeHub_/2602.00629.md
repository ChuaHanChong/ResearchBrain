# Action-Free Offline-to-Online RL via Discretised State Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called OSO-DecQN enables reinforcement learning agents to learn effectively from offline datasets completely devoid of action information, using a novel state discretization and conservative regularization. This method accelerates online learning convergence and improves asymptotic performance across diverse continuous and discrete control environments, outperforming transformer-based baselines.

## Problem

- Many real-world offline reinforcement learning datasets lack explicit action labels due to privacy, storage constraints, or sensor limitations.
- Existing offline RL methods primarily assume the availability of action labels, making them unsuitable for action-free scenarios.
- Prior attempts at action-free learning often incur high computational costs (e.g., transformer-based methods) or have not consistently shown robust improvements for online guidance.

## Method

- The **OSO-DecQN** pre-training stage learns ==state policies== by discretizing continuous state differences (s' - s) into discrete change vectors (increase, decrease, no change) using a scale-invariant transformation.
- It adapts the Decoupled Q-Networks (DecQN) framework to decompose Q-values across these discretized state-difference dimensions, integrating conservative regularization to mitigate overestimation bias and ensure state reachability.
- A guided online learning stage employs a lightweight Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM), trained concurrently, to translate desirable discretized state differences from the pre-trained policy into executable actions for online agents via a probabilistic policy-switching mechanism.

## Results

- OSO-DecQN consistently accelerated online learning convergence and improved asymptotic performance for TD3 and SAC agents across various D4RL and DeepMind Suite environments, including high-dimensional tasks like Quadruped-Walk (**78** state dimensions).
- The method significantly outperformed a transformer-based action-free baseline (AF-Guide), which frequently underperformed the basic online RL baseline, indicating OSO-DecQN's practical efficacy and reduced computational cost.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the scale-invariant state discretization and conservative regularization were critical for effective pre-training and for enabling the state policy to surpass initial offline dataset performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discretizing the continuous state increment space transforms a challenging regression problem into a stable discrete prediction task, which is crucial for learning effective state policies from action-free data.
> - Conservative regularization is essential for OSO-DecQN to effectively leverage reinforcement learning from action-free data, mitigating overestimation bias and enforcing plausible next-state predictions beyond simple imitation.
> - A simple, concurrently learned Inverse Dynamics Model, even when initialized from scratch, can reliably translate desired state transitions into actions, demonstrating that the primary performance driver is the quality of the pre-trained state policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has seen significant advancements, particularly in offline RL, which focuses on learning policies from pre-collected, fixed datasets without further online interaction. The majority of existing offline RL methodologies assume the comprehensive availability of action labels within these datasets. However, real-world applications often present scenarios where action data is absent or incomplete. This absence can stem from privacy mandates (e.g., healthcare treatment decisions, financial trading strategies), storage limitations (to reduce data overhead), sensor malfunctions, or simply the non-logging of specific control signals in industrial settings.

This paper addresses the specific and challenging paradigm of "action-free offline-to-online RL," where agents must derive actionable knowledge solely from observations consisting of state-reward-next_state (s, r, s') tuples. The work carves a niche within the broader offline RL landscape by focusing on the unique constraints imposed by the complete lack of action information.

Existing approaches to related problems exhibit limitations that this work aims to overcome:
- **Q-value formulations over target states** (e.g., Edwards et al., 2020; Hepburn et al., 2024) estimate the return of transitioning to a target state, but crucially, they still rely on action labels during their training process, rendering them unsuitable for action-free settings.
- **Transformer-based state policies** (e.g., Zhu et al., 2023) have attempted to remove this action dependency by learning state policies from action-free datasets. However, these methods often incur high computational costs and have not consistently demonstrated robust or generalizable improvements when guiding online RL across diverse environments and varying dataset qualities.
- **Existing discretization strategies** (e.g., Seyde et al., 2022) are primarily designed for continuous action spaces with bounded ranges. Such approaches are not directly applicable to action-free datasets, which necessitate a different paradigm for state-space discretization.

The authors identify a clear gap: there is a lack of practical and scalable approaches that can effectively (a) learn from action-free offline data, (b) scale to high-dimensional control problems, and (c) facilitate guided online learning to accelerate performance across a wide spectrum of environments. This research positions itself as a contribution towards filling this gap by adapting and extending concepts from decoupled Q-learning to the action-free domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and practical framework for "action-free offline-to-online RL," addressing the fundamental question of whether effective policies can be learned and transferred to online RL without ever observing offline actions. This objective is driven by significant practical motivations:

- **Data Scarcity and Privacy Concerns:** In numerous real-world domains, recording or accessing explicit action labels is often infeasible. For instance:
 - In **healthcare**, explicit treatment decisions might be deliberately omitted from patient records due to stringent legal or confidentiality requirements (Kushida et al., 2012; Yu et al., 2021). Researchers are left with sequential patient states (symptoms, vitals, lab results) and associated outcomes.
 - In **finance**, proprietary trading strategies or detailed order flows are frequently withheld to protect competitive advantages (Liu et al., 2022; Loukides & Gkoulalas-Divanis, 2012). Only partial portfolio states or market observations may be available.
 - In **robotics and industrial control**, sensor logs may be maintained without capturing precise torque commands or control signals to reduce storage overhead or due to proprietary information (Todorov et al., 2012).
- **Sensor Limitations and Data Corruption:** Actions can also be missing or corrupted due to sensor failures or limitations in the data logging infrastructure itself.

To address these challenges, the work sets forth several key objectives:
1. **Develop a novel pre-training strategy:** Introduce a simple yet effective method for learning state policies from action-free datasets. Instead of predicting actions, the goal is to recommend desirable next-state transitions.
2. **Ensure scalability and stability:** The proposed method must be capable of efficiently scaling to high-dimensional problems while avoiding the instability and overfitting issues commonly associated with continuous state prediction.
3. **Facilitate guided online learning:** Propose a mechanism that effectively leverages the pre-trained state policies to accelerate the learning process of online agents, improving both convergence speed and asymptotic performance.
4. **Demonstrate broad applicability:** Validate the approach across a diverse range of benchmarks, including those with high state dimensionality and varying data qualities.
5. **Analyze critical components:** Investigate the contributions of key algorithmic design choices, specifically the state discretization transformation and the conservative regularization technique, to the overall effectiveness.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to establish a robust framework that enables the utilization of vast, yet action-free, datasets for enhancing reinforcement learning agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for action-free offline-to-online RL consists of two complementary stages: pre-training an offline state policy and subsequently leveraging this policy for guided online learning.

#### 3.1. Pre-training: Offline State-Only DecQN (OSO-DecQN)

The pre-training stage focuses on learning state policies that recommend desirable next-state transitions (∆s) directly from action-free (s, r, s') tuples.

- **Discretizing the Target State Space:**
 - Instead of directly regressing to continuous next states (s')—an approach often prone to instability and overfitting—the method introduces a state discretization transformation.
 - For each state dimension `i`, the transformation `δ_ϵ_i(s, s')` quantifies whether the state dimension `s_i` has increased, decreased, or remained largely unchanged, mapped to `{-1, 0, 1}`. Specifically:
 `δ_ϵ_i(s, s') = -1 if s'_i - s_i < -ϵ`
 `δ_ϵ_i(s, s') = 1 if s'_i - s_i > ϵ`
 `δ_ϵ_i(s, s') = 0 otherwise`
 - Here, `ϵ` is a small threshold. This converts a continuous regression problem into a discrete prediction task, allowing the use of value-based Q-learning.
 - **Scale-Invariance:** Prior to discretization, z-score normalization is applied to each state dimension. This ensures the transformation is scale-invariant, making it robust across heterogeneous state spaces without requiring per-dimension tuning.
 - **Theoretical Guarantee:** Theorem 1 provides a value bound, demonstrating that discretizing the state increment space into `k` evenly spaced bins per coordinate preserves the optimal value function up to an error that scales with `O(H * sqrt(M) / k)`, where `H` is the range of mean increments and `M` is the state dimension. This suggests that finer discretization improves accuracy.

- **OSO-DecQN Algorithm:**
 - **Adaptation of DecQN:** The algorithm adapts the Decoupled Q-Networks (DecQN) framework (Seyde et al., 2022), which typically decomposes Q-values across action dimensions. OSO-DecQN reformulates this by decomposing Q-values across *state-difference dimensions*.
 - **Q-value Definition:** It learns Q-values of the form `Q(s, ∆s)`, where `∆s ∈ {-1, 0, 1}^M` represents the discretized change vector for all `M` state dimensions.
 - **Scalability:** This decomposition enables linear scalability with the state dimensionality `M`, addressing high-dimensional problems efficiently.
 - **Stability Enhancements:** An ensemble variant of DecQN is employed to improve sample efficiency and reduce variance. Double Q-learning (Van Hasselt et al., 2016) is integrated to stabilize learning and mitigate overestimation bias.
 - **Conservative Regularization:** A crucial regularization term `R_θ` is added to the critic's loss function. This term, adapted from conservative regularization techniques in offline RL (Kumar et al., 2020), helps mitigate overestimation bias and, critically, addresses *state reachability constraints*. In action-free learning, the agent must predict reachable next states; this regularization constrains predictions to state transitions observed or supported by the offline data. The regularization term is defined as `R_θ = Σ_{(s,∆s)~D} (log ||exp(Q_θ(s, ·))||_1 - Q_θ(s, ∆s))`.

#### 3.2. Guided Online Learning

This stage outlines how the pre-trained state policy can be utilized to accelerate online RL without direct environment interaction.

- **Leveraging Q-values for Guidance:** The pre-trained OSO-DecQN critic is used to inform the online agent's exploration strategy within a standard actor-critic algorithm (e.g., TD3 or SAC).
- **Policy-Switching Mechanism:** A probabilistic mechanism blends actions from the online agent's policy with actions derived from the offline state policy. For a given state `s`, an action `a` is chosen as:
 `a = I_ϕ(s, argmax_∆s Q(s, ∆s))` with probability `β`
 `a = π_on(s)` with probability `(1 - β)`
 where `ζ ~ U(0,1)` and `β` is a hyperparameter that controls the influence of the offline guidance. This allows for targeted exploration early in training and gradual reliance on the online policy.
- **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):**
 - A lightweight Inverse Dynamics Model, `I_ϕ`, is trained concurrently with the online agent from scratch.
 - Its function is to translate the desirable discretized state difference `∆s` (recommended by the pre-trained state policy) into an executable action `a`.
 - **Training:** The IDM is trained using supervised learning with an L1 loss, which is noted to be more robust to outliers than MSE. The loss function is `L(ϕ) = ||a_on,off - I_ϕ(s, ∆s_off)||1 + ||a - I_ϕ(s, ∆s)||1`.
 - To leverage the offline dataset, actions `a_on,off` are assigned to offline `(s_off, ∆s_off)` pairs using the online agent's current policy `π_on(s_off)`. This bootstraps the IDM's learning with offline experience, even without true action labels.
 - **Assumptions:** The approach assumes a sufficiently regular and locally smooth inverse relationship between actions and state transitions in the environment, allowing a simple IDM to learn this mapping.
- **Dynamic β Scheduling:** The hyperparameter `β` is linearly annealed from an initial maximum value to a minimum value over training timesteps. This strategy aims to balance aggressive offline-guided exploration early on, when the online agent is naive, with increased reliance on the maturing online policy later in training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of the proposed OSO-DecQN framework was conducted across various continuous and discrete action tasks from the D4RL suite (Fu et al., 2020) and the Action-factorised DeepMind Suite (Beeson et al., 2024). All action information was removed from these datasets for offline pre-training.

#### 4.1. Guided Online Learning Performance

- **Accelerated Learning and Improved Asymptotic Performance (Figure 1):** The method of guiding online agents (TD3 for continuous actions, ensemble DecQN N for discrete actions) using OSO-DecQN pre-trained state policies consistently improved both convergence speed and asymptotic performance. This was observed across various environments (Hopper, Halfcheetah, Walker2D, Cheetah-Run, Quadruped-Walk) and different dataset qualities (medium-replay, medium, medium-expert, expert).
- **Scalability:** For Quadruped-Walk, an environment with 78 state dimensions, the approach demonstrated consistent performance improvements during early learning stages, indicating its scalability to high-dimensional problems.
- **Robustness to Online Agent Choice:** The method showed comparable improvements when guiding Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) (Appendix F), demonstrating its compatibility with different base online algorithms without extensive re-tuning.
- **Comparison to Transformer-based Baselines (Figure 2, Figure 7):** OSO-DecQN consistently outperformed "AF-Guide" (Zhu et al., 2023), a transformer-based action-free guiding method. AF-Guide frequently underperformed the basic TD3 baseline, highlighting the practical efficacy of OSO-DecQN's approach. Additionally, OSO-DecQN offers reduced pre-training computational costs compared to transformer-based architectures.

#### 4.2. Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) Analysis

- **Minimal Impact of IDM Architecture (Appendix E.1):** Ablation studies varying IDM architecture (number of layers) and batch size showed that these factors had only a moderate impact on online guidance performance. This suggests that the primary driver of performance gains is the quality of the pre-trained state policy, rather than the IDM's specific design or optimization.
- **IDM Learning Dynamics (Figure 4, Table 13):** The IDM loss converged rapidly and remained stable during online training. Quantitatively, the final online IDM loss was comparable to that of an expert-level IDM, indicating that the concurrently trained, lightweight IDM can reliably translate state policy predictions into actions without becoming a limiting factor.
- **Sensitivity to β (Figure 3):** An annealed schedule for the policy-switching hyperparameter `β` (decreasing over time) generally yielded better asymptotic performance and stability compared to fixed `β` values. This schedule allows for robust offline-guided exploration early on and gradual reliance on the maturing online policy.

#### 4.3. Pre-training Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Necessity of Discretization (Table 1, Table 2):**
 - **Imitation Learning Performance:** Imitation learning directly mimicking continuous next-state `s'` or continuous state differences `s' - s` resulted in poor evaluation performance. In contrast, imitation learning targeting the *discretized state differences ∆s* yielded returns comparable to behavioral cloning with full action labels.
 - **Predictive Accuracy:** The analysis of discrete state difference error showed a strong correlation with evaluation performance. Policies trained on `s'` or `s' - s` exhibited high prediction errors (comparable to a random policy), while the `∆s` discretization strategy maintained low error, indicating accurate next-state prediction. This validates that discretization circumvents the difficulties of continuous regression.
- **Critical Role of Regularization (Table 1, Table 2):**
 - **Enhanced Returns:** OSO-DecQN, incorporating conservative regularization, significantly outperformed both action-based and discrete state-based imitation learning methods, demonstrating the ability to leverage reinforcement learning to surpass dataset performance.
 - **Mitigating Bias and Reachability:** A variant of DecQN without the regularization term (DecQN N) struggled to learn effectively and exhibited high prediction errors. This confirms that regularization is critical for mitigating overestimation bias and ensuring that the learned state policy adheres to state reachability constraints, thereby predicting plausible next states.
- **Robustness to Discretization Threshold ϵ (Appendix D.1, Table 9):** Performance was generally robust to varying values of `ϵ`, the threshold defining "no change" in state dimensions.
- **Impact of Number of Bins (Appendix D.2, Table 10, Table 11):** While using two bins (increase/decrease) was sufficient for learning, a three-bin approach (increase/decrease/unchanged) often led to slightly better performance and less variability, particularly in more complex environments. The three-bin approach provided a better balance between information loss and the ability to capture meaningful state transitions.

In summary, the main findings highlight that both the scale-invariant state discretization and conservative regularization are crucial for effective pre-training of state policies from action-free data. These policies, when coupled with a lightweight IDM and policy-switching mechanism, provide valuable and robust guidance for accelerating online reinforcement learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a critical and often overlooked problem in offline reinforcement learning: the absence of action labels. By proposing a principled and scalable framework for action-free offline-to-online RL, the work makes several significant contributions:

- **Enabling Learning from Challenging Datasets:** The primary impact is enabling the extraction of valuable knowledge from real-world datasets where actions are inherently missing due to privacy, storage, or sensor limitations. This opens up new avenues for applying RL in sensitive domains such as healthcare and finance, as well as in industrial settings where logging actions is impractical or proprietary.
- **Novel Algorithmic Design:** The introduction of OSO-DecQN, which combines a simple yet effective scale-invariant state discretization with conservative regularization within a decoupled Q-network architecture, represents a novel approach to tackle the challenges of continuous state prediction, overestimation bias, and state reachability in an action-free context. The theoretical bounds on value function approximation provide a formal basis for the discretization strategy.
- **Practical Utility and Scalability:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that the framework not only improves the convergence speed but also enhances the asymptotic performance of online agents across a diverse range of environments and dataset qualities. Crucially, its ability to scale to high-dimensional state spaces (e.g., 78 dimensions in Quadruped-Walk) underscores its practical applicability to complex control problems.
- **Efficiency Compared to Alternatives:** By not relying on computationally intensive transformer-based architectures, OSO-DecQN offers a more efficient pre-training process while delivering more consistent performance gains for online guidance, distinguishing it from prior action-free methods.
- **Foundational Insights for Future Research:** The detailed analysis of the roles played by state discretization and regularization provides valuable insights for the research community. This analytical contribution can serve as a benchmark and guide for the development and validation of alternative action-free state policies in the future.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across different online algorithms (TD3, SAC) and its robustness to IDM architecture variations and `β` scheduling indicate the generalizability and reliability of the proposed guidance mechanism.

**Potential Future Directions:**
The authors identify several promising avenues for future research building upon this work:
- **Adaptive Discretization:** Exploring adaptive discretization mechanisms, such as those proposed by Seyde et al. (2024), could allow for more fine-tuned control and potentially further enhance pre-training performance and online guidance in environments with varying state dynamics.
- **More Expressive Translation Mechanisms:** Investigating more advanced Inverse Dynamics Models that go beyond the current lightweight design could improve robustness and performance in domains characterized by highly discontinuous dynamics or multi-modal inverse mappings.
- **Learning from Visual Data:** Adapting the method to learn from suboptimal video demonstrations (image-based data) would be a significant extension, although it would require additional work on robustly extracting state and reward information from visual inputs.
- **Integration with Value Function Classification:** Combining OSO-DecQN with recent approaches that replace value-function regression with classification over discretized value supports (Farebrother et al., 2024) could further enhance the stability and scalability of the critic, particularly for larger architectures and more complex problems.

Overall, this research represents a notable step forward in making offline reinforcement learning more applicable to real-world scenarios where data completeness is a persistent challenge, thereby broadening the utility and impact of RL technologies.
