# Follow Your Pose: Pose-Guided Text-to-Video Generation using Pose-Free Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a two-stage training method for generating text-editable and pose-controllable character videos by integrating a pose encoder and temporal modules with a pre-trained text-to-image model. The approach achieved a CLIP score of 24.09 for video-text alignment and 93.36% frame consistency, enabling diverse character and background generation with stable motion.

## Problem

- Creating high-quality character videos that are controllable by both text descriptions and precise human pose sequences.
- Scarcity of comprehensive, large-scale datasets featuring paired video-pose captions for training complex generative models.
- Existing text-to-video generation methods struggle with maintaining temporal consistency and lack robust pose control or text-editing capabilities.

## Method

- A novel two-stage training scheme is introduced to decouple learning pose control from temporal consistency, building upon a pre-trained ==latent diffusion model==.
- The first stage trains a lightweight, zero-initialized multi-layer 3D convolutional ==pose encoder== using a new **LAION-Pose** dataset, injecting pose features into a frozen U-Net via ==residual connections==.
- The second stage fine-tunes the model on pose-free videos (HDVILA dataset) by introducing ==pseudo-3D convolutions== and learnable ==temporal== and ==cross-frame self-attention== layers to ensure temporal coherence.

## Results

- Qualitatively, the method generated diverse, text-customizable character videos with consistent appearance and background, and also produced stylized and multi-character videos.
- Achieved a CLIP score of **24.09** for video-text alignment, surpassing other methods, and a frame consistency of **93.36%**, indicating robust temporal coherence.
- Human evaluations preferred the generated videos **39.96%** of the time, demonstrating superior perceptual quality compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning of pose alignment and temporal coherence into separate stages effectively addresses data scarcity and complex video generation challenges.
> - Leveraging the strong generative priors of pre-trained Text-to-Image (T2I) models, rather than training from scratch, enables rich conceptual and stylistic control.
> - Residual injection of pose encoder features into the U-Net effectively adds control while preserving the original model's generative capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen substantial advancements in generative models, particularly in the domain of visual synthesis. Text-to-Image (T2I) synthesis systems have demonstrated proficiency in generating realistic and diverse imagery, performing editing tasks, composing scenes, and generalizing across various concepts. These systems, often built upon diffusion models like Stable Diffusion, have established a strong foundation for generating static visual content from textual descriptions.

However, extending these capabilities to Text-to-Video (T2V) generation presents additional complexities. Challenges include the high-dimensional nature of video data, the need for temporal consistency across frames, and a scarcity of high-quality, large-scale video datasets paired with detailed text captions and control signals like human poses. Early efforts in T2V generation utilized Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) but faced issues with training stability and scalability. More recent approaches have leveraged large language models and transformers, and increasingly, diffusion models, to synthesize videos. Models like Video Diffusion Models (VDM), Imagen Video, and Make-A-Video have demonstrated progress by extending image synthesis diffusion models with temporal modules.

Concurrently, research in pose-to-video generation has focused on animating human figures based on driving signals such as keypoints. Methods like Vid2Vid, Fewshot-Vid2Vid, LiquidWarpingGAN, and FOMM have achieved success in generating realistic human motion. However, these methods often require per-pair training or are limited in their ability to edit video content through text prompts, primarily focusing on motion transfer rather than diverse content generation.

More recently, controllable diffusion models, exemplified by T2I-Adapter and ControlNet, have enabled detailed control over image generation using various input conditions like depth maps, edges, or keypoints. While effective for images, directly applying these image-based control mechanisms to video often results in flickering and temporal inconsistencies, as they were not designed to maintain coherence across a sequence of frames.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these advancements, aiming to bridge the gap between high-quality text-to-image synthesis, precise pose control, and temporal coherence in video generation. It seeks to leverage the rich content generation capabilities of pre-trained T2I models while introducing robust pose control and ensuring temporal consistency, thus addressing the limitations posed by data scarcity and the inherent challenges of video synthesis. The paper proposes a method that can generate text-editable and pose-controllable character videos, a task that has remained a significant hurdle due to the absence of comprehensive paired video-pose caption datasets and specialized video generative prior models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method capable of generating high-quality character videos that are simultaneously controllable by textual descriptions and precise human pose sequences.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several key challenges and demands within the field:

1. **Demand for Digital Human Creation:** There is an increasing demand for generating various digital humans in applications such as animation, virtual reality, gaming, and content creation. The ability to control both the appearance (via text) and the motion (via pose) of these digital characters in video format is critical for such applications.

2. **Lack of Comprehensive Datasets:** A significant impediment to pose-controllable text-to-video generation is the absence of a large-scale, comprehensive dataset that features paired video-pose captions. Existing video datasets often lack detailed pose annotations, and text-video datasets may not include specific character pose information. This data scarcity makes direct end-to-end training of complex generative models for this task challenging.

3. **Limitations of Existing Generative Models for Videos:** While Text-to-Image (T2I) models have shown impressive generative prior capabilities, video-specific generative prior models for highly controllable, diverse character generation are not as mature. Many existing T2V models focus on general video synthesis without granular control over character pose, or they specialize in pose-driven generation but lack text-editing capabilities.

4. **Leveraging Pre-trained T2I Model Strengths:** Pre-trained T2I models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) possess strong capabilities in concept generation, semantic understanding, artistic styling, and composition. The researchers are motivated to harness these existing strengths rather than training a video model from scratch, which would be computationally intensive and data-demanding. The goal is to extend these T2I models to video generation while preserving their conceptual power.

5. **Addressing Temporal Inconsistency:** Directly adapting image-based conditional generation methods (like ControlNet or T2I-Adapter) to video often results in temporal flickering and inconsistencies in backgrounds or character appearance across frames. A key motivation is to devise mechanisms that ensure temporal coherence and stable video output when applying pose and text controls.

6. **Efficient Training Strategy:** Given the data limitations, the researchers aimed to design an efficient training scheme that could utilize readily available, "easier-to-obtain" datasets: image-pose pairs (for learning pose control) and pose-free videos (for learning temporal consistency). This approach decouples the learning of different aspects of video generation (pose alignment and temporal coherence) to overcome the dataset challenge.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to provide a practical and effective solution for creating text-editable and pose-controllable character videos, bridging critical gaps in the current landscape of generative AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a novel two-stage training scheme designed to overcome the challenges of data scarcity and the need for both pose control and temporal coherence in text-to-video generation. The approach builds upon a pre-trained latent diffusion model (LDM), specifically Stable Diffusion, and introduces targeted modifications and training strategies.

**Base Model Architecture:**
The core of the system is a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model (LDM), which utilizes a U-Net architecture for denoising. This U-Net comprises multi-stage downsampling and upsampling blocks, each containing spatial self-attention, cross-attention (for text conditioning), and feed-forward networks (FFN). The LDM autoencoder component projects images into a lower-dimensional latent space and reconstructs them.

**Two-Stage Training Strategy:**

The training is decoupled into two distinct stages:

**Stage 1: Pose-Controllable Text-to-Image Generation**
The objective of this stage is to instill pose control capability into the pre-trained T2I model.
- **Dataset:** The authors collected a new dataset called **LAION-Pose**. This dataset was created by processing the large-scale LAION image dataset using the MMpose algorithm to detect human skeletons. Only images with more than 50% detectable key points were retained, resulting in a dataset of diverse human-like characters with associated text captions and poses.
- **Pose Encoder Design:** To incorporate pose conditions, a lightweight, zero-initialized multi-layer 3D convolutional encoder is introduced. This encoder processes the input pose sequence (human skeletons).
- **Feature Injection:** The pose features extracted by the encoder are downsampled to match various resolutions within the U-Net (e.g., 64x64, 32x32, 16x16, 8x8). These features are then injected into each block of the pre-trained U-Net via a **residual connection**. This residual injection method is chosen to preserve the existing generative capacity of the pre-trained diffusion model, allowing only a few parameters to be updated to add control. The authors note that this approach outperformed direct channel concatenation of pose information. The condition can be effectively dropped by setting the residual to zero, restoring the original diffusion model behavior.
- **Training:** In this stage, all parameters of the original pre-trained U-Net are **frozen**. Only the parameters of the newly introduced pose encoder (convolutional layers) are updated. The training objective is the simple noise reconstruction loss, identical to that used in the original Stable Diffusion.
- **Outcome:** After Stage 1, the model is capable of generating pose-controllable images from text prompts and pose inputs, leveraging the semantic and stylistic knowledge of the base T2I model.

**Stage 2: Video Generation via Pose-Free Videos**
This stage focuses on learning temporal consistency to produce coherent videos and eliminate flickering.
- **Dataset:** The model from Stage 1 is further fine-tuned using a **pose-free video dataset**, specifically HDVILA (High-Resolution Video-Language-Audio). This dataset contains continuous in-the-wild video-text pairs.
- **Modifications for Video:** To adapt the image model for video inputs and ensure temporal coherence, several key modifications are made:
 - **Pseudo-3D Convolution:** The first convolutional layer of the pre-trained U-Net is "inflated" from a 2D convolution to a 1x3x3 pseudo-3D convolutional kernel. This allows the model to process sequences of frames.
 - **Temporal Self-Attention (SA) Layers:** Learnable temporal self-attention layers are appended to the network. These layers are crucial for modeling temporal dependencies across frames.
 - **Cross-Frame Self-Attention:** Inspired by previous one-shot video generation models, the attention mechanism is reshaped into cross-frame attention. This design promotes content consistency between frames.
- **Training:** During this stage, most parameters, including those learned in Stage 1 (the pose encoder) and the original LDM components (pseudo-3D convolution, cross-attention, FFN), are **frozen**. Only the parameters related to temporal consistency – specifically, the newly introduced temporal self-attention and cross-frame self-attention blocks – are made trainable. The training uses a noise reconstruction loss on video latents.
- **Longer Video Generation:** For generating videos longer than the training sequence length, a mechanism reusing noise from the previous DDIM sampling process is employed. Specifically, for T frames sampled at a time, the noise from the last T/2 frames is carried over as prior knowledge into the next sampling loop, ensuring continuity.
- **Outcome:** After Stage 2, the model can generate temporally coherent videos that maintain the pose control learned in Stage 1 and the conceptual richness from the base T2I model.

**Inference:**
During inference, a DDIM sampler is used with classifier-free guidance. The user provides a text prompt describing the target character and desired appearance, along with a sequence of poses representing the intended motion. The model then generates a temporally consistent and pose-aligned video.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative results to support the efficacy of the proposed method.

**Qualitative Results:**
The model demonstrates the successful generation of continuously pose-controllable character videos while preserving the conceptual generation and composition abilities inherited from the pre-trained text-to-image (T2I) model.
- **Diverse Character Generation:** The method can customize character appearances in generated videos by modifying the input text prompt. Examples include "Batman," "stormtrooper," and "Iron Man" performing the same motion, with consistent character appearance and background across frames.
- **Diverse Background Generation:** The approach allows for the synthesis of varied natural video backgrounds, such as "sea," "beach," "moon," or "grass," while maintaining character pose and temporal consistency.
- **Stylized Character Video Generation:** By appending style descriptions (e.g., "cartoon style," "Makoto Shinkai style," "cyberpunk style") to the text prompt, the model produces videos in the specified artistic style, consistent with poses and semantics. This capability leverages the broad knowledge embedded in pre-trained T2I models and LoRA adaptations.
- **Multiple-Characters Generation:** The method generalizes to scenarios involving multiple skeletons, generating videos with several characters (e.g., "astronauts" or "girls on the stage"). The generated video properties align with the input pose sequences and text prompts, demonstrating generalization capability and allowing for the integration of irrelevant characters into a shared video context.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Qualitative comparisons against methods like FOMM, Everybody Dance Now (EDN), Tune-A-Video (TAV), ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, and MasaCtrl indicate that the proposed approach yields superior performance in terms of content consistency (e.g., consistent backgrounds, character appearance) and artistic quality, and reduces flickering. For instance, ControlNet and T2I-Adapter showed inconsistent backgrounds and character appearances when applied to video sequences, an issue addressed by the proposed method's temporal coherence mechanisms.

**Quantitative Results:**
Quantitative comparisons were performed against various baselines across four metrics using 1024 video samples, as detailed in Table 1 of the paper.
- **CLIP Score (CS) for Video-Text Alignment:** The proposed method achieved a CLIP score of **24.09**, which is higher than all compared baselines, including Tune-A-Video (23.57), MasaCtrl (23.64), ControlNet (22.31), and T2I adapter (22.42). This indicates better alignment between the generated video content and the input text prompts.
- **Quality (QU) via Human Evaluation:** In a human evaluation involving 32 videos, evaluators preferred the videos generated by the proposed method 39.96% of the time, outperforming Tune-A-Video (23.81%) and ControlNet (6.69%). This suggests superior perceptual quality of the generated videos.
- **Pose Accuracy (PA) for Pose-Video Alignment:** The model achieved a pose accuracy of **34.92%**. This metric, based on the average precision of skeletons detected in generated videos compared to ground truth pose sequences, showed performance comparable to ControlNet (33.23%) and T2I adapter (33.47%), indicating effective alignment with the guiding pose.
- **Frame Consistency (FC) for Temporal Coherence:** The method achieved a frame consistency score of **93.36%**, measured by CLIP cosine similarity of consecutive frames. This score significantly surpasses ControlNet (54.35%) and T2I adapter (53.86%), demonstrating the effectiveness of the temporal designs in maintaining coherence. It is comparable to Tune-A-Video (93.78%), a method that typically overfits to an input video.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Effect of Residual Pose Encoder:** The study compared the proposed residual injection method for pose features against a direct channel concatenation approach. The residual method resulted in superior performance, preserving more of the pre-trained Stable Diffusion model's generative capabilities. The concatenation method required retraining the first convolutional layer, which compromised the high-quality image synthesis prior.
- **Number of Layers for Condition Control:** Increasing the number of layers where conditional signals are injected (multi-layer vs. single layer) led to improved pose-frame alignment. A single-layer injection resulted in noticeable mismatches between target and generated limb positions (e.g., arm of Iron Man).

These results collectively indicate that the proposed two-stage training scheme effectively addresses the core challenges, yielding high-quality, text-editable, and pose-controllable videos with strong temporal consistency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for pose-guided text-to-video generation that addresses several challenges in the field, contributing to the advancement of generative AI.

The primary significance lies in its ability to **decouple the learning of pose alignment and temporal coherence**, enabling the generation of complex, controllable videos despite the scarcity of comprehensive paired video-pose caption datasets. By leveraging readily available datasets (image-pose pairs and pose-free videos) and building upon the strong generative priors of pre-trained text-to-image (T2I) models, the work offers a practical solution to a data-intensive problem.

Potential impacts include:

- **Digital Human Creation and Animation:** The ability to generate text-editable and pose-controllable character videos has direct applications in creating digital humans for films, games, virtual reality, and metaverse environments. Animators and content creators could rapidly prototype character movements and appearances from simple text prompts and pose sequences, significantly reducing production time and costs.
- **Personalized Content Generation:** The method could enable personalized video content, where users describe characters and actions, and the system generates tailored videos. This could extend to educational content, interactive storytelling, or personalized virtual assistants.
- **Bridging T2I and T2V Gaps:** The research demonstrates an effective strategy for extending the powerful semantic and compositional capabilities of T2I models to the video domain, a significant step towards more versatile and controllable video synthesis systems. This could inspire further work on adapting other image-based generative techniques to video with robust temporal stability.
- **Foundation for Broader Conditional Video Generation:** The modular two-stage training scheme, particularly the strategy for injecting control signals and managing temporal consistency, offers a blueprint that can potentially be extended to other conditional video generation tasks. The authors explicitly mention its applicability to other control modalities such as depth maps, edges, or sketches, suggesting a versatile framework.
- **Resource Efficiency:** By intelligently utilizing existing pre-trained models and accessible datasets, the method offers a more resource-efficient approach compared to training large-scale text-to-video models from scratch. This can democratize access to advanced video generation capabilities.
- **Research Contribution:** The introduction of the **LAION-Pose dataset** provides a valuable resource for the research community, facilitating further studies in pose-conditioned image and video generation. The demonstrated superiority in quantitative and qualitative evaluations relative to existing methods establishes a new baseline for this specific task.

In summary, this work contributes a robust and efficient method for generating controllable character videos, addressing critical limitations in data and model design, and opening avenues for diverse applications in creative industries and beyond.
