# PccDiffuser: Multi-solution Motion Planning for Continuum Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> PccDiffuser is a conditional diffusion planner for continuum robots that learns a multimodal distribution over complete configuration-space paths under piecewise constant curvature, conditioning on initial configuration, target tip position, and a graph-encoded variable obstacle set. Analytical differential kinematics guides denoising for terminal accuracy and whole-body clearance; batched sampling yields parallel candidates, reaching 91% success on mixed obstacle scenes.

## Problem

- Sampling- and optimisation-based continuum planners return one solution per iterative run, so they cannot represent the inherently multimodal, redundant solution space of these robots.
- Configuration-space tree planners get little workspace task guidance, and nonlinear constant-curvature kinematics let a short configuration path yield an undesirable workspace path.
- Workspace planners describe tip motion naturally but repeatedly solving inverse kinematics causes discontinuities between adjacent nodes or convergence to local minima.

## Method

- ==Exponential-coordinate PCC parameterisation== removes the curvature-angle singularity at the straight configuration, giving a 6-dimensional configuration whose tip pose follows a product of exponentials.
- A temporal ==1D U-Net== denoiser predicts noise over a 64-waypoint path, conditioned via ==feature-wise affine modulation== on MLP embeddings plus a ==graph neural network== pooling a variable number of ball obstacles.
- Two analytical guidance schemes inject Jacobian-based terminal-error and backbone-repulsion gradients into ==DDIM sampling==: ==post correction== edits the predicted clean path, ==guided prediction== backpropagates into the noise estimate.

## Results

- On the mixed zero-to-four-obstacle test set the planner reached **91.11%** success versus roughly **70%** for workspace RRT/RRT* and potential-field baselines and about **65%** for configuration-space trees.
- Mean runtime was **34.0 ms** per successful path, orders of magnitude below the iterative baselines, and batching **10** candidates barely changed per-case sampling time.
- Post correction raised success to **99.62%** obstacle-free and **91.11%** mixed and cut normalised tip error **55.6%** and **45.4%**, at **15.1%** and **56.8%** runtime overhead, beating guided prediction's **60.4%** and **102.2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned path prior steers samples toward feasible regions far more reliably than uninformed exploration, and parallel denoising makes generating many alternative solutions nearly free.
> - Cheap post-hoc geometric correction on the predicted clean sample outperformed backpropagating gradients through the denoiser, giving better accuracy per unit of compute.
> - Choosing a singularity-free state parameterisation matters for generative planning, since discontinuous coordinates near common configurations corrupt the distribution the model must learn.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position Within the Broader Research Landscape

Continuum robots differ from conventional rigid-link manipulators because their bodies deform continuously rather than moving through a small number of discrete joints. Their compliance and ability to conform to confined spaces make them relevant to minimally invasive surgery, industrial inspection, maintenance, and human–robot interaction. These same properties create planning challenges: the robot has many possible body shapes, its kinematics are nonlinear, and collision avoidance must account for the entire backbone rather than only the endpoint.

Most existing continuum-robot motion planners are based on sampling or optimization. Rapidly-exploring random trees (RRT and RRT*) have been applied in both configuration space and workspace. Configuration-space planners can evaluate whole-body collisions through forward kinematics, but they may receive limited guidance from the desired workspace motion. Workspace planners more directly represent the intended tip path, but they often require repeated inverse-kinematics solutions, which can be discontinuous or trapped in local minima. Artificial-potential-field and related optimization methods provide task and obstacle guidance but can also suffer from local minima and generally produce one solution.

A central issue is that continuum robots are typically redundant. The same target tip position may be reached through different terminal configurations and substantially different backbone paths. This makes the planning problem multimodal: several geometrically and operationally distinct solutions may be valid. Conventional planners can generate alternatives by repeating a search, but they do not intrinsically learn or represent a distribution over possible paths.

PccDiffuser places this problem within the growing use of diffusion models for robot trajectory generation. Diffusion models learn to transform noise into samples from a conditional data distribution. In robotics, they have been used to generate action sequences and motion trajectories for rigid manipulators, often with task, goal, or collision guidance. The paper extends this approach to continuum robots by learning complete configuration-space paths, conditioning on obstacles represented as a variable-sized graph, and incorporating analytical robot kinematics into the sampling process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The main objective is to develop a motion planner that can generate **multiple feasible paths in parallel** for a continuum robot, rather than returning only one path from each planning attempt. The planner is intended to satisfy four requirements:

1. **Terminal accuracy:** The robot tip should reach a specified target position.
2. **Whole-body collision avoidance:** The complete robot backbone should remain clear of obstacles throughout the motion.
3. **Path diversity:** Different samples should represent distinct terminal configurations and body motions for the same initial state, target, and environment.
4. **Execution feasibility:** The generated geometric path should be converted into a time-parameterized trajectory that respects actuator constraints.

The authors separate geometric planning from time allocation. This distinction reflects the fact that obstacle avoidance and terminal position depend on the geometric sequence of configurations, whereas actuator velocity and acceleration limits determine how quickly that path can be executed. The first stage therefore generates a sequence of configurations, and the second assigns timing to the selected candidate.

The motivation for diffusion-based planning is that the task is underconstrained and inherently multimodal. Starting from different noise samples allows the model to explore different modes of the learned distribution. Batched denoising makes it possible to generate several candidates simultaneously, potentially reducing the cost associated with running a conventional planner repeatedly.

The paper also aims to address weaknesses associated with the chosen representation and with purely learned generation. Standard curvature and bending-plane coordinates can become singular or discontinuous when a section is straight. In addition, a learned diffusion model may generate paths with small terminal errors or residual collisions. PccDiffuser therefore uses a nonsingular exponential-coordinate representation and applies analytical differential-kinematics corrections during denoising.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Robot Model and Configuration Representation

The study considers a three-section piecewise constant-curvature (PCC) continuum robot. Each section has a fixed arc length \(L_i\), curvature \(\kappa_i\), and bending-plane angle \(\phi_i\). Instead of directly using curvature and bending-plane angle, the authors represent each section using two variable components of an integrated spatial twist:

\[
\xi_{i1}=-L_i\kappa_i\sin\phi_i,\qquad
\xi_{i2}=L_i\kappa_i\cos\phi_i.
\]

The full robot configuration is represented by six variables,

\[
\zeta=[\xi_{11},\xi_{12},\xi_{21},\xi_{22},\xi_{31},\xi_{32}]^T.
\]

This parameterization avoids the discontinuity associated with the bending-plane angle at the straight configuration. Forward kinematics are computed using a product of exponentials, and the translational part of the analytical Jacobian provides the derivative of tip position with respect to the six configuration variables. The bending curvature of each section is constrained by \(\kappa_i\leq\kappa_{\max}\).

#### 3.2. Planning Formulation

The environment is represented as a collection of spherical obstacles, each defined by a center and radius. Given an initial configuration and target tip position, the planner generates a discretized path containing \(H=64\) configurations. The first configuration is fixed to the current robot state, and the final configuration must produce the desired tip position. Collision checking is performed on sampled points along the complete robot backbone at each waypoint.

The authors assume that adjacent configurations are sufficiently close that interpolation between two collision-free waypoints remains collision-free. After geometric planning, a minimum-duration time schedule is assigned subject to actuator velocity constraints and acceleration limits. The configuration-space path is mapped to actuator space, time allocated, and then spline-interpolated for hardware execution.

#### 3.3. Conditional Diffusion Model

A clean configuration-space path is denoted by \(x_0\), while the condition \(c\) includes the initial configuration, target tip position, and obstacle collection. A forward diffusion process progressively adds Gaussian noise to the path. During training, the model learns to predict the noise added at each diffusion step.

The reverse process uses deterministic DDIM sampling. Although the model is trained with 1,000 diffusion steps, inference uses a respaced schedule of 50 steps. Each candidate starts from an independent Gaussian noise sample. Consequently, multiple paths can be produced in parallel from one planning condition.

The first configuration is restored through inpainting. A binary channel identifies the fixed initial configuration, and the noise-prediction loss excludes this configuration. This allows the model to generate the remaining path while preserving continuity with the robot’s current state.

#### 3.4. Network Architecture

The denoising network is a one-dimensional temporal U-Net operating on the sequence of six-dimensional configurations. The architecture is designed to retain temporal structure across the path. The diffusion timestep, initial configuration, and target tip position are embedded through multilayer perceptrons.

Obstacles are encoded using a graph neural network. Each obstacle forms a graph node with features consisting of its center and radius. Relative center information is used in graph messages, and graph pooling converts a variable number of obstacles into a fixed-dimensional representation. This enables the same model to process environments containing different numbers of obstacles. The obstacle embedding is combined with the other task conditions and used to modulate the U-Net residual blocks.

#### 3.5. Training Data and Guidance

The training robot uses section lengths of one and a maximum curvature of \(\pi/2\). Training paths contain 64 waypoints. Target positions are generated from randomly sampled configurations, and multiple inverse-kinematics solutions and initial configurations are paired. Straight workspace tip paths are mapped into configuration space using damped differential kinematics with a null-space continuity term.

For obstacle scenes, colliding paths are repaired using artificial-potential-field repulsion applied to backbone points. The resulting workspace path is simplified with the Douglas–Peucker algorithm and then spline-interpolated. Paths that satisfy bending, continuity, and clearance requirements are retained. The authors generate approximately **353,000 obstacle-free paths** and **243,000 paths in mixed scenes containing zero to four spherical obstacles**.

Two forms of analytical guidance are evaluated during the last 20 denoising steps. The first, **post correction**, modifies the predicted clean path directly. It applies a terminal correction based on the tip-position error and repulsive corrections at penetrating backbone points. The second, **guided prediction**, modifies the predicted diffusion noise using the gradient of a combined terminal-error and collision-repulsion objective. Both methods use the analytical Jacobian to convert workspace errors into configuration-space corrections.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Multi-solution Generation

The model generated ten candidates for individual test cases using different initial noise samples. In obstacle-free scenes, the paths reached the same target while converging to visibly different terminal configurations. In one-obstacle examples, candidates displayed different whole-body motions and configuration-space profiles despite having the same initial state and target. Feasible alternatives were also produced in scenes containing more obstacles.

These results indicate that the diffusion model did not simply produce small perturbations of one nominal trajectory. Instead, it represented multiple solution modes associated with the redundancy of the continuum robot.

#### 4.2. Analytical-Guidance Ablation

The authors compared pure diffusion, post correction, and guided prediction. On obstacle-free data, post correction achieved a **99.62% success rate**. On the mixed obstacle dataset containing zero to four obstacles, it achieved **91.11%**. The paper’s abstract summarizes the mixed-set result as approximately 91%.

Compared with pure diffusion, post correction reduced mean normalized terminal tip error by **55.6%** in obstacle-free scenes and **45.4%** in mixed obstacle scenes. Guided prediction also improved success rates, but produced only marginal additional gains in tip accuracy.

Post correction increased mean runtime by **15.1%** in obstacle-free scenes and **56.8%** in mixed scenes. Guided prediction incurred larger increases of **60.4%** and **102.2%**, respectively. The authors therefore identify post correction as the more favorable compromise between accuracy, success rate, and computational cost.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Conventional Planners

PccDiffuser was compared with configuration-space RRT and RRT*, workspace RRT and RRT*, and an artificial-potential-field method. All planners were evaluated on the same test set, with success requiring both acceptable terminal tip error and collision-free sampled backbone points.

PccDiffuser reached a success rate of **91.11%**, while the strongest conventional baselines, including workspace RRT/RRT* and APF, achieved approximately **70%**. Configuration-space RRT and RRT* achieved approximately **65%**. The mean runtime for a successful PccDiffuser path was **34.0 ms**. The paper reports substantially higher runtimes for the benchmark methods.

The advantage increased when multiple candidates were requested because PccDiffuser denoised candidates in parallel. Increasing the candidate count had little effect on total sampling time per planning case, whereas conventional methods would generally require repeated searches. RRT* also increased runtime without materially improving success within the fixed iteration budget.

#### 4.4. Hardware Demonstrations

Experiments were conducted on a three-section tendon-driven continuum robot. The hardware demonstrations covered consecutive planning, multiple solutions for one target, and obstacle avoidance.

For consecutive planning, the robot first moved from the zero configuration to one target and then replanned from the resulting terminal configuration to a second target. The measured configuration histories remained continuous across the replanning boundary, and the robot held its shape during the intermediate waiting interval.

For multi-solution execution, two different sampled paths were selected for the same initial configuration and target. Both reached the target but produced visibly different backbone shapes and configuration histories. In the obstacle experiment, a spherical obstacle was placed in the workspace. The robot executed a selected trajectory that passed around the obstacle while avoiding contact with the complete backbone and reaching the target.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PccDiffuser contributes a framework for treating continuum-robot motion planning as conditional distribution learning rather than single-solution search. Its principal significance is the combination of multimodal path generation, obstacle conditioning, analytical kinematic refinement, and actuator-aware time allocation.

The ability to produce several candidates in parallel could support runtime selection based on criteria not fully encoded during training, such as clearance, path length, energy consumption, or preferred robot shape. It may also benefit replanning applications in which one candidate becomes invalid because of changing constraints. The use of a graph representation for obstacles makes the planner adaptable to environments with different numbers of objects.

However, the reported evaluation also defines the current scope of the method. The model is trained for a three-section PCC robot and spherical obstacles, uses a static environment, and relies on discretized collision checking and the PCC approximation. Performance may degrade when hardware deformation, tendon friction, hysteresis, contact, or complex obstacle geometry differs substantially from the training assumptions. The hardware evaluation demonstrates feasibility but covers a limited number of scenarios. The authors identify dynamic environments and closed-loop replanning with online feedback as future directions.

Overall, the paper shows that conditional diffusion can provide diverse and computationally practical motion candidates for continuum robots, while analytical corrections help address the accuracy and safety limitations of purely learned trajectory generation.
