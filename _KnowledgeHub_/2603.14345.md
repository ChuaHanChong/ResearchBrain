# VIP-Loco: A Visually Guided Infinite Horizon Planning Framework for Legged Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru, developed VIP-Loco, a framework that integrates vision-based scene understanding with reinforcement learning and infinite-horizon Model Predictive Control to enable anticipatory legged locomotion in complex environments. It achieved superior and more stable performance on visually challenging terrains compared to baselines, demonstrating successful navigation across diverse robot morphologies at inference rates of 40-50 Hz.

## Problem

- Existing legged locomotion control often relies on reactive behaviors from proprioceptive sensing, limiting performance in complex terrains requiring foresight.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) struggles with integrating high-dimensional visual data, while vision-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically lacks explicit anticipatory planning at deployment.
- Robots require long-horizon predictive reasoning to plan stable and kinodynamically feasible trajectories in dynamic and visually challenging environments.

## Method

- A unified framework, **VIP-Loco**, is introduced, integrating a learned internal model, ==reinforcement learning== (RL) for policy acquisition, and an infinite-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) for deployment planning.
- An internal model, based on an RSSM architecture, learns a compact, vision-guided representation from depth images and proprioceptive states, providing imagined kinodynamic rollouts for RL training and serving as the dynamics and return oracle for MPC.
- Online planning is performed using a constrained Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) method, warm-started by the RL policy, to optimize action sequences while enforcing kinodynamic and safety constraints.

## Results

- VIP-Loco (Variational) achieved superior training stability and episodic returns, mastering terrains beyond difficulty level 6, and maintained over **90%** success on maximum difficulty stairs and over **60%** on "crawl" and "tilt" terrains where proprioceptive baselines failed significantly.
- Explicit planning in VIP-Loco consistently yielded higher average returns, particularly on "anticipatory" terrains (e.g., climb, gaps), with the wheeled-biped showing the most substantial improvements.
- The framework effectively generalized across quadruped (Unitree Go1), biped (Cassie), and wheeled-biped (TronA1-W) morphologies, demonstrating accurate prediction of CoM trajectories and supporting robust, morphology-specific behaviors at 40-50 Hz inference rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The explicit use of a variational objective for the internal model, focusing on depth image reconstruction, effectively captures crucial lateral geometry constraints for tasks like "crawl" and "tilt," outperforming consistency-based latent losses.
> - A synergistic integration of visual perception, adaptive reinforcement learning, and explicit anticipatory Model Predictive Control (MPC) is essential for robust and interpretable locomotion on highly challenging terrains.
> - The learned internal dynamics model accurately predicts kinodynamic states, enabling effective receding-horizon planning and contributing to the robot's anticipatory behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Legged locomotion research has seen substantial advancements driven by two primary control paradigms: Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). MPC offers interpretable control through constrained optimization over a prediction horizon, providing a structured approach to motion planning. Conversely, RL excels at achieving adaptability and generalization across diverse conditions by optimizing policies through interaction with an environment, often leveraging high-dimensional sensory inputs.

Despite their individual strengths, a common limitation in many current MPC and RL methods is their reliance on purely proprioceptive observations. This often leads to reactive control behaviors that are insufficient for tasks requiring foresight, such as anticipating complex terrain variations, planning stable trajectories across gaps, navigating confined spaces, or traversing obstacles with high penalties for error. Robots operating in real-world environments necessitate the ability to perceive upcoming terrain and integrate this information into proactive motion planning to ensure stability and kinodynamic constraint satisfaction.

This identified gap has motivated research into unifying perception and planning for legged locomotion. Prior works have begun incorporating exteroceptive sensing, such as depth cameras, LiDAR, or RGB images, to move beyond purely reactive control. Examples include perception-conditioned RL policies that learn dynamic maneuvers like those demonstrated in ANYmal Parkour and Robot Parkour Learning, or methods like WMP that utilize world models to learn recurrent visual representations for generalization. However, these vision-based RL approaches typically do not employ explicit planning at deployment, relying solely on the learned policy to implicitly encode foresight.

Concurrently, MPC frameworks have also evolved. While classical MPC struggles with high-dimensional visual inputs and generalization, infinite-horizon MPC variants [17–23] integrate learned dynamics and value models with trajectory optimization. However, these often depend on off-policy training pipelines, which may not be well-suited for massively parallel simulation. PIP-Loco, a direct predecessor, bridged on-policy RL training with MPC deployment but was limited to proprioceptive inputs.

VIP-Loco positions itself at the intersection of these research lines. It aims to develop a unified framework that combines visual foresight with explicit planning, addressing the limitations of existing methods that either lack visual perception for planning (e.g., proprioceptive MPC/RL like PIP-Loco) or lack structured, anticipatory planning despite having visual input (e.g., perceptive RL like WMP). The work seeks to integrate vision-based scene understanding with an internal model-driven predictive control strategy, enabling anticipatory planning in visually complex environments while maintaining the efficiency of on-policy training and real-time computational feasibility for onboard deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, VIP-Loco, that enables legged robots to perform robust, adaptive, and anticipatory locomotion in complex and dynamic environments. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations and desired capabilities:

1. **Addressing the Reactivity Limitation of Current Methods:** Both prevalent approaches, Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), often exhibit reactive behavior when solely relying on proprioceptive sensory inputs. This reactivity restricts robot performance in scenarios demanding foresight, such as traversing uneven terrains, crossing gaps, climbing obstacles, or navigating confined spaces. The motivation is to move beyond reactive control to a proactive planning paradigm.

2. **Integrating High-Dimensional Perceptual Information with Planning:** MPC traditionally struggles with directly processing high-dimensional perceptual inputs (like depth images) for real-time planning, while model-free RL, despite its ability to adapt to visually challenging scenarios, inherently lacks an explicit planning mechanism. A key motivation is to bridge this gap by effectively integrating rich visual context for terrain understanding into a structured planning framework.

3. **Achieving Long-Horizon Predictive Control:** Legged robots operating in real-world environments must anticipate future terrain variations and plan motion trajectories that satisfy critical stability and kinodynamic constraints over an extended horizon. Purely reactive controllers are insufficient for such tasks. The objective is to imbue robots with the capability for long-horizon predictive reasoning, enabling them to make proactive decisions based on anticipated future states.

4. **Maintaining Interpretability and Constraint Enforcement:** While RL policies offer adaptability, their black-box nature can sometimes hinder interpretability. MPC, in contrast, provides a more interpretable control mechanism due to its explicit optimization under constraints. The motivation is to combine the adaptability benefits of RL with the interpretability and constraint-satisfaction guarantees offered by structured planning, ensuring that robot actions are both effective and safe.

5. **Ensuring Computational Feasibility for Real-time Deployment:** Any proposed framework for robotic control must be computationally efficient enough for real-time execution on onboard hardware. Therefore, a critical objective is to develop a framework that can leverage complex visual information and perform predictive planning while remaining computationally feasible for deployment at typical control loop frequencies.

To achieve these objectives, VIP-Loco proposes a framework that:
- Learns a compact, vision-guided internal model from proprioceptive states and depth images during training.
- Utilizes this learned model within an infinite-horizon MPC formulation during deployment.
- Combines the adaptability and generalization capabilities of reinforcement learning with the structured, constraint-aware, and anticipatory planning capabilities of model predictive control.

Ultimately, the motivation is to develop a robust, adaptive, and interpretable legged locomotion system capable of navigating diverse and challenging environments by effectively unifying perception, learning, and planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VIP-Loco proposes a framework that integrates vision-based scene understanding with reinforcement learning (RL) and an infinite-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) planning component. The methodology is structured into distinct training and deployment phases.

**A. Framework Overview**
The core idea is to train an internal model that learns to map proprioceptive states and depth images into compact kinodynamic features. This model is then leveraged in two ways: (1) during training, it provides imagined kinodynamic rollouts to augment the RL policy, enabling anticipatory behavior; and (2) during deployment, it acts as the dynamics and return oracle for an infinite-horizon MPC planner, facilitating real-time trajectory optimization.

**B. Setup**
The problem is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). An Asymmetric Actor-Critic architecture is employed, where the expert actor and privileged critic are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), while the internal model is trained via supervised learning.

- **Observation Space:** Observations ($o_t$) include a 5-step history of proprioceptive data (joint angles, velocities, projected gravity, target velocity commands, previous action) and 2D depth images. For the privileged critic, additional "privileged observations" ($o^{priv}_t$) are provided, comprising proprioceptive terms along with scan dots (height field information), base linear velocities, reaction forces, mass, and friction parameters.
- **Action Space:** For quadruped and biped robots, actions are joint-angle perturbations. For the wheeled-biped, actions also include joint-velocity commands for the wheels. These commands are fed to a PD controller.
- **Reward Function:** The reward function encourages desired locomotion behaviors, including tracking commanded linear and angular velocities, maintaining body height and orientation (Core Locomotion Rewards), and penalizing undesirable states like collisions, stumbles, or immobility (Environment-Constraint Rewards). A simplified linear velocity tracking reward (Eq. 1) is used, allowing the agent to adapt speed to terrain demands.

**C. Model Architecture**
The framework's architecture consists of an Internal Model coupled with an Asymmetric Actor-Critic setup.

- **Internal Model:** This model is central to VIP-Loco. It is based on the RSSM architecture from Dreamer and serves two roles:
 1. **Imagination Augmentation (Training):** Provides the expert actor with imagined kinodynamic rollouts (sequences of future states) for anticipatory behavior.
 2. **MPC Oracle (Deployment):** Acts as the learned dynamics and return model for the MPC planner.
 The model state is defined as $y = [x, h, z]^T$, where $x$ is an explicit kinodynamic Center of Mass (CoM) state (body pose, twist, joint states) for interpretability and constraint handling, $h$ is a recurrent hidden state from a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) for temporal memory, and $z$ is a stochastic latent state for uncertainty representation.
 The Internal Model operates at 10 Hz due to depth image processing, while the actor operates at 50 Hz, reusing the hidden state $h$. Its components include:
 - **Internal Policy $\pi_\phi$**: Outputs action distributions conditioned on the model state $y$.
 - **GRU Cell $g_\phi$**: Updates the recurrent memory $h$.
 - **Encoder ($p^z_\phi, p^x_\phi$):** Estimates the future latent state $z$ and CoM state $x$ from an observation embedding $e$ (derived from $o$ via a preprocessing module) and $h$.
 - **Dynamics ($d^z_\phi, d^x_\phi$):** Predicts future latent state $z$ (via an MLP) and CoM state $x$ (via rigid body dynamics equations) from $h$.
 - **Decoder $q_\phi$**: Reconstructs proprioceptive observations and depth images from $y$.
 - **Reward and Value Models ($r_\phi, V_\phi$):** Estimate task reward and value for a given model state.
 Algorithm 1 details how a horizon $H$ sequence of kinodynamic states ($X$) is predicted using the internal model, with the first step using the encoder (posterior update) and subsequent steps using the dynamics (prior prediction).
- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:**
 - **Expert Actor $\pi_\theta$**: An MLP that interacts with the environment during training. It receives current observations ($o$) and the imagined kinodynamic rollout ($X$) along with the hidden state ($h$), with stop-gradient applied to $h$ and $X$ to prevent actor updates from affecting the internal model.
 - **Privileged Critic $V_\theta$**: Accesses privileged simulator information ($o^{priv}$) in addition to $h$ and $X$ (also with stop-gradient).

**D. Training**
The training jointly optimizes the Internal Model, Expert Actor, and Privileged Critic.

- **Supervised Learning of the Internal Model:** The internal model is trained using a composite loss function (Eq. 2) that includes:
 - Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) terms for reward and value estimation, aligning with ground-truth rewards ($r^{sim}_t$) and value targets ($V^{target}_t$).
 - A KL divergence term to align the encoder's posterior distribution with the dynamics' prior distribution for the latent state $z$.
 - Behavior cloning, training the Internal Policy $\pi_\phi$ to match actions of the Expert Actor ($a^{expert}_t$).
 - Kinodynamic CoM Loss, enforcing accurate prediction of ground-truth CoM states ($x^{sim}_t, x^{sim}_{t+1}$).
 - A reconstruction term (NLL) to ensure the decoder can reconstruct observations ($o_t$) from the model state.
 This approach uses a *variational objective* for the internal model (reconstructing the depth image), a departure from consistency-based objectives in prior works (e.g., TD-MPC). This is motivated by the limitations of privileged scan-dot inputs in capturing lateral geometry constraints crucial for tasks like "crawl" and "tilt," which the variational approach, by directly reconstructing depth, can overcome.

- **Policy Learning via PPO:** The Expert Actor $\pi_\theta$ and Privileged Critic $V_\theta$ are trained using the PPO algorithm on trajectories collected in simulation. The PPO objective (Eq. 3) includes a clipped policy loss, an entropy bonus for exploration, and a value loss term.

**E. Deployment: Infinite-Horizon Planning**
At deployment, the learned internal models are integrated into an online planning framework to generate safe and optimal actions. This addresses potential issues of unsafe or suboptimal actions from RL policies under distribution shifts.

- **Objective:** The planner aims to compute an optimal action sequence $a^*_{0:H-1}$ by maximizing the cumulative discounted reward over a receding horizon $H$, with value function bootstrapping at the terminal state to approximate infinite-horizon return (Eq. 4). This improves long-term planning compared to fixed-horizon MPC.
- **Solver:** A constrained version of the Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) method (Algorithm 2) is used. MPPI iteratively samples and refines action trajectories, updating a Gaussian distribution over action sequences using an "elite sampling" strategy. The RL policy provides a warm-start for this process to accelerate convergence.
- **Constraints:** The planning incorporates kinodynamic feasibility and safety constraints, including joint position and velocity limits, and base twist bounds.

This comprehensive methodology allows VIP-Loco to combine the strengths of vision-based learning with structured, anticipatory planning, designed for real-time robotic control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VIP-Loco was conducted through extensive simulation experiments and ablation studies using NVIDIA’s Isaac Gym. The framework was tested across three robot morphologies: a quadruped (Unitree Go1), a biped (Cassie), and a wheeled-biped (TronA1-W). Robots were trained to navigate diverse and challenging terrains including rough slopes, staircases, gaps requiring jumps, obstacles for climbing, narrow passages for tilting, and low-clearance slabs for crawling. Domain randomization and curriculum learning were employed to enhance robustness. The system operates at 40-50 Hz inference rates on a desktop GPU.

**A. Ablation Methods**
To systematically assess the contribution of different components, VIP-Loco was compared against:
- **HIM-Loco:** A proprioception-only RL baseline (no vision, no planning).
- **PIP-Loco:** A proprioception-only RL with infinite-horizon MPC (no vision).
- **WMP:** A visually-guided RL method with a world model, but without explicit MPC planning at deployment (vision, no planning).
- **VIP-Loco (Consistency):** A variant of VIP-Loco using a consistency latent loss instead of the variational loss for the internal model.
- **VIP-Loco (Variational):** The proposed full VIP-Loco method with variational loss and infinite-horizon MPC.

**B. Training Comparison**
Figure 3 illustrates training performance (episodic return and mastered terrain level) over 6000 iterations.
- All methods showed rapid initial improvements.
- **VIP-Loco (Variational)** achieved the highest and most stable episodic returns, consistently mastering terrains beyond difficulty level 6.
- **WMP** performed competitively in early stages but asymptotically regressed to easier terrains to maintain higher rewards.
- **VIP-Loco (Consistency)** plateaued at lower difficulty levels, particularly struggling with "crawl" and "tilt" terrains. The authors hypothesize this is due to an information asymmetry, where the privileged critic's sparse scan-dot inputs are insufficient for capturing lateral geometry constraints, leading the consistency loss to learn less effective representations for these tasks compared to the variational objective's direct depth image reconstruction.

**C. Deployment Performance Comparison**
Figure 4 and Table II present success rates and average episodic returns on various terrains at deployment.
- **Vision-based vs. Proprioceptive-only:** On visually demanding tasks (climb, gaps), proprioceptive-only methods (HIM-Loco, PIP-Loco) failed beyond difficulty level 4. Vision-based methods (WMP, VIP-Loco variants) demonstrated robust performance.
- **Stairs:** VIP-Loco variants maintained over 90% success at maximum difficulty, while proprioceptive baselines degraded sharply.
- **Crawl and Tilt:** Only WMP and VIP-Loco (Variational) sustained over 60% success, with the consistency variant degrading at higher levels. This corroborates the training phase hypothesis regarding the variational formulation's advantage in handling complex lateral geometry.
- **VIP-Loco vs. WMP:** While both VIP-Loco (Variational) and WMP exhibited similar raw success rates on several terrains, the paper argues that VIP-Loco's primary advantage lies in its interpretability and explicit constraint satisfaction enabled by MPC planning, which is elaborated in the next point.

**D. Why Combine MPC and RL?**
Table II specifically compares VIP-Loco with and without its planning component across all three robots and six terrain types.
- **Benefits of Planning:** VIP-Loco with planning consistently achieved higher average returns across all robots compared to its no-planning variant. These gains were most significant on "anticipatory" terrains (e.g., climb, gaps, crawl, tilt for Go1; climb for Cassie; and across most terrains for TronA1-W), where stability and precise foothold selection are critical. Marginal regressions were observed on some easier terrains where the no-planning policy already performed near saturation.
- **Morphology-specific Gains:** The TronA1-W (wheeled-biped) showed the most substantial improvements with planning, attributed to its hybrid morphology introducing additional non-holonomic constraints that benefit from explicit trajectory planning.
- **Synthesis of Components:**
 - **PIP-Loco (RL + MPC, no vision)** succeeded on proprioceptive terrains but failed on visually demanding ones.
 - **WMP (RL + Vision, no planning)** effectively processed terrain geometry but lacked the corrective horizon and constraint-aware action filtering of MPC.
 - **VIP-Loco (RL + Vision + MPC)** demonstrated that the synergistic combination of visual perception, learned dynamics, and explicit anticipatory planning is necessary for robust performance at the highest difficulty levels and for enabling interpretability and safety.

**E. Qualitative Rollouts and Model Accuracy**
Figure 5 presents qualitative evaluations, comparing MPC-predicted CoM height (red) with actual tracked profiles (black) for Climb and Crawl tasks across all robots.
- The close alignment between predicted and actual CoM height trajectories validates the accuracy of VIP-Loco’s learned dynamics model, providing insight into the receding-horizon planning.
- Snapshots illustrate successful, morphology-specific behaviors: the quadruped coordinating legs for challenging maneuvers, the biped adapting step height and posture, and the wheeled-biped seamlessly blending wheel and leg transitions.

In summary, the findings indicate that VIP-Loco effectively unifies vision-based learning with infinite-horizon MPC, leading to superior and more stable performance, especially on terrains requiring anticipatory decision-making and precise constraint satisfaction. The variational loss formulation for the internal model and the explicit planning component are identified as critical contributors to its robustness and interpretability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VIP-Loco framework represents a notable advancement in the field of legged robot locomotion by addressing the long-standing challenge of unifying high-dimensional visual perception, adaptable reinforcement learning, and structured model predictive control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Anticipatory and Robust Locomotion:** The integration of vision-based scene understanding with infinite-horizon planning allows legged robots to move beyond purely reactive behaviors. This capability for anticipatory planning is crucial for navigating complex, dynamic, and visually challenging environments (e.g., uneven slopes, stairs, gaps, narrow passages, obstacles) where foresight is indispensable for stability and mission success. This could lead to more autonomous and capable robots for exploration, disaster response, and industrial applications.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Learning and Planning:** VIP-Loco successfully combines the generalization and adaptability benefits of model-free reinforcement learning with the interpretability, constraint-satisfaction, and safety guarantees of model predictive control. This hybrid approach offers a robust control strategy that leverages the strengths of both paradigms while mitigating their individual weaknesses, particularly in scenarios requiring both visual perception and long-term trajectory reasoning.

3. **Enhanced Interpretability and Safety:** By employing a learned kinodynamic internal model within an explicit MPC framework, VIP-Loco provides a degree of interpretability that is often absent in purely data-driven RL solutions. The ability to predict and reason over kinodynamic states allows for explicit constraint enforcement and facilitates online filtering of potentially unsafe or suboptimal actions, thereby enhancing the safety and reliability of robot operation.

4. **Demonstrated Generalizability Across Morphologies:** The framework’s effectiveness was validated across three distinct robot morphologies—a quadruped, a biped, and a wheeled-biped—without requiring structural changes to the core method. This demonstrates a high degree of generalizability, suggesting that VIP-Loco could be applied to a wider range of legged platforms, potentially reducing development effort for new robot designs or applications.

5. **Practical Real-Time Feasibility:** Operating at inference rates of 40-50 Hz, the framework is compatible with typical onboard control loops for legged robots. The incorporation of domain randomization during simulation training also enhances its potential for transfer to physical hardware, a critical step towards real-world deployment. The direct predecessor, PIP-Loco, having shown hardware validation, provides a basis for anticipating similar transfer capabilities for VIP-Loco.

6. **Methodological Contribution to Representation Learning:** The explicit choice of a variational objective for the internal model’s latent loss, which reconstructs depth images directly, is a key methodological contribution. This approach proved superior to consistency-based losses, particularly for terrains requiring understanding of lateral geometry (like "crawl" and "tilt"), demonstrating how careful design of the representation learning objective can significantly impact performance in perception-intensive tasks.

The primary future direction identified is the extension of VIP-Loco to physical hardware, which will involve addressing real-world challenges such as depth sensor noise and constraints on onboard computing resources. Successful hardware deployment would solidify VIP-Loco's impact, paving the way for more capable and autonomous legged robots in a variety of complex real-world scenarios.
