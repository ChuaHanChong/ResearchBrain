# VIP-Loco: A Visually Guided Infinite Horizon Planning Framework for Legged Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru, developed VIP-Loco, a framework that integrates vision-based scene understanding with reinforcement learning and infinite-horizon Model Predictive Control to enable anticipatory legged locomotion in complex environments. It achieved superior and more stable performance on visually challenging terrains compared to baselines, demonstrating successful navigation across diverse robot morphologies at inference rates of 40-50 Hz.

## Problem

- Existing legged locomotion control often relies on reactive behaviors from proprioceptive sensing, limiting performance in complex terrains requiring foresight.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) struggles with integrating high-dimensional visual data, while vision-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically lacks explicit anticipatory planning at deployment.
- Robots require long-horizon predictive reasoning to plan stable and kinodynamically feasible trajectories in dynamic and visually challenging environments.

## Method

- A unified framework, **VIP-Loco**, is introduced, integrating a learned internal model, ==reinforcement learning== (RL) for policy acquisition, and an infinite-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) for deployment planning.
- An internal model, based on an RSSM architecture, learns a compact, vision-guided representation from depth images and proprioceptive states, providing imagined kinodynamic rollouts for RL training and serving as the dynamics and return oracle for MPC.
- Online planning is performed using a constrained Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) method, warm-started by the RL policy, to optimize action sequences while enforcing kinodynamic and safety constraints.

## Results

- VIP-Loco (Variational) achieved superior training stability and episodic returns, mastering terrains beyond difficulty level 6, and maintained over **90%** success on maximum difficulty stairs and over **60%** on "crawl" and "tilt" terrains where proprioceptive baselines failed significantly.
- Explicit planning in VIP-Loco consistently yielded higher average returns, particularly on "anticipatory" terrains (e.g., climb, gaps), with the wheeled-biped showing the most substantial improvements.
- The framework effectively generalized across quadruped (Unitree Go1), biped (Cassie), and wheeled-biped (TronA1-W) morphologies, demonstrating accurate prediction of CoM trajectories and supporting robust, morphology-specific behaviors at 40-50 Hz inference rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The explicit use of a variational objective for the internal model, focusing on depth image reconstruction, effectively captures crucial lateral geometry constraints for tasks like "crawl" and "tilt," outperforming consistency-based latent losses.
> - A synergistic integration of visual perception, adaptive reinforcement learning, and explicit anticipatory Model Predictive Control (MPC) is essential for robust and interpretable locomotion on highly challenging terrains.
> - The learned internal dynamics model accurately predicts kinodynamic states, enabling effective receding-horizon planning and contributing to the robot's anticipatory behaviors.
