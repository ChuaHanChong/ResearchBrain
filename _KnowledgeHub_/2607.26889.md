# StructureGS: Structure-aware Gaussian Splatting for Articulated Object Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for articulated object reconstruction, StructureGS, leverages oriented bounding boxes and structure-aware loss functions within a 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline to disentangle geometry, appearance, and motion, leading to clearer part decomposition, more accurate kinematics, and robust performance even with sparse input views.

## Problem

- Existing methods struggle with the entanglement of geometry, appearance, and motion parameters during optimization, leading to ambiguities.
- Reconstructions often suffer from poor part decomposition, blurry boundaries, and geometric artifacts, hindering accurate structural understanding.
- Inaccurate kinematic parameter estimation, such as joint axes and ranges of motion, results from insufficient guidance, especially with limited visual information.

## Method

- Each articulated part in **StructureGS** is represented by a group of ==3D Gaussian primitives== in a canonical coordinate space, coupled with affine and rigid transformation parameters.
- The framework explicitly incorporates ==oriented bounding boxes (OBBs)== derived from part transformations to model part structure and physical relationships.
- Optimization includes novel structure-aware loss functions: ==Part Fitting== (for spatial coherence), ==Part Contact== (for structural connectivity using OBB-based distances), and ==Extent Regularization== (for flexible OBB scaling).

## Results

- Achieved superior geometric (Chamfer-L1 Distance of **0.919**) and kinematic (angular error of **0.088°**) accuracy on synthetic datasets with full views, outperforming baselines.
- Demonstrated significantly improved robustness to sparse views, reducing angular error to **0.701°** from **8.553°-32.840°** for baselines with only **10** views.
- Produced reconstructions with well-defined part boundaries and geometrically coherent structures, successfully recovering accurate kinematics from real-world smartphone images even under noisy conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit OBB-based structural guidance effectively disentangles geometry, appearance, and motion, resolving ambiguities inherent in photometric-only reconstruction.
> - Constraining spatial coherence (parts within their OBBs) and structural connectivity (contact between OBBs) significantly improves reconstruction robustness under sparse input views.
> - The use of coarse OBB proxies, even for complex shapes, provides sufficient structural priors to enable the recovery of intricate geometry and accurate kinematics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D reconstruction aims to create digital models of real-world objects from various inputs, such as images or point clouds. Within this field, articulated object reconstruction represents a specialized and challenging area. Unlike static objects, articulated objects possess multiple movable parts that change configuration relative to each other, requiring the recovery of not only static geometry and appearance but also the underlying kinematic structure and per-part motion parameters.

Existing methods for articulated object reconstruction often leverage neural representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to model geometry and appearance, while parametrizing part motions as rigid transformations. These approaches typically rely heavily on photometric supervision, which optimizes model parameters to minimize discrepancies between rendered and observed images. However, a known limitation of photometric-only supervision in articulated contexts is the entanglement of geometry, appearance, and motion parameters. This entanglement can lead to ambiguities during optimization, where the system may incorrectly adjust geometry to compensate for motion-induced appearance changes, resulting in suboptimal outcomes such as blurred part boundaries, geometric artifacts, or inaccurate part decomposition.

Prior work has attempted to mitigate these issues through various auxiliary priors. Some holistic deformation-based methods, like A-SDF and REACTO, represent the entire object with a single continuous function, implicitly capturing part structure via latent variables or deformation fields. While capable of plausible whole-object reconstructions, they often lack explicit part-level control and struggle with recovering clean part boundaries. Part-wise approaches, such as PARIS, ArticulatedGS, ArtGS, SPLART, and ScrewSplat, explicitly decompose the object into distinct parts, each with its own geometry and motion. These methods may incorporate priors such as skeleton-driven deformations, clustering of Gaussians based on deformation similarity or mobility, or constrained motion models like screw motions.

The present work identifies a gap in these existing methodologies: while auxiliary priors assist in identifying how an articulated object moves or how its elements should be grouped, they do not explicitly constrain the *structure* of individual parts, including their shapes, boundaries, and physical contacts. This paper addresses this limitation by introducing a framework that integrates explicit structure-aware guidance directly into the 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction process, aiming to provide more robust disentanglement and higher-quality part-wise reconstructions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve high-quality articulated object reconstruction with clear part decomposition, accurate geometry, and precise motion estimation. This objective is motivated by several fundamental challenges inherent in reconstructing objects with movable components:

1. **Entanglement of Parameters**: Articulated objects require the joint optimization of per-part geometry, appearance, and motion parameters. The observed visual data (appearance) is a complex function of both the underlying geometry and its pose. When relying solely on photometric supervision, the optimization process can conflate these interdependencies, making it difficult to accurately estimate each component. For instance, an appearance change due to part movement might be incorrectly attributed to a change in the part's geometry, leading to geometric distortions or "leaking" of geometry across parts.
2. **Poor Part Decomposition**: Without explicit guidance on part structure, photometric-only methods frequently produce reconstructions with blurred boundaries between parts. This lack of distinct separation hinders the understanding of individual part identities and their functional roles. Geometric artifacts, such as scattered Gaussians or distorted shapes, can further compromise the quality of the reconstruction.
3. **Inaccurate Motion Estimation**: The ambiguity between geometry and motion can also lead to inaccurate estimation of kinematic parameters, such as joint axes and ranges of motion. This is particularly problematic in scenarios with limited visual information (e.g., sparse views), where the system has fewer observations to resolve the underlying kinematics. Such inaccuracies limit the utility of the reconstructed model for applications requiring precise physical interaction or simulation.
4. **Need for Physically Plausible Configurations**: An accurate articulated model must not only visually resemble the object but also adhere to physical plausibility. This implies that parts should maintain spatial coherence (remain compact within their designated regions) and structural connectivity (maintain contact where physically joined), properties that are not inherently enforced by photometric losses alone.

To address these motivations, the authors introduce structure-aware guidance designed to explicitly inject structural constraints into the optimization process. The core goals of this guidance are:
- To enforce **spatial coherence**: constraining each part's reconstructed geometry to remain compact and localized within its own defined space.
- To enforce **structural connectivity**: ensuring physically plausible contact relationships between adjacent, connected parts.

By addressing these challenges, the research aims to produce articulated object reconstructions that are not only visually faithful but also geometrically precise, kinematically accurate, and structurally coherent, thereby enabling more robust digital twins for applications in embodied AI and physical interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The StructureGS framework is designed for articulated object reconstruction from multi-view RGB images captured in two distinct configurations: an initial state (t=0) and an articulated state (t=1). The object is modeled as a collection of *K* rigid parts, P = {P_0, P_1..., P_{K-1}}, where P_0 is a fixed base and P_k (for k ≥ 1) undergoes rigid transformation between the two states.

#### 3.1. Part Representation

Each part P_k is represented by a group of *N_k* Gaussian primitives, G_k, defined in a normalized canonical coordinate space (a unit cube [−1, 1]^3). Each Gaussian is characterized by its canonical position (x_j), opacity (α_j), covariance (parameterized by q_j for rotation and scale), and spherical harmonic coefficients (c_j) for view-dependent appearance. To keep Gaussian positions within the canonical space, they are constrained using μ_j = tanh(x_j).
The framework includes transformation parameters:
- **T_k^0 = (s_k^0, R_k^0, t_k^0)**: An affine transformation that maps the canonical Gaussians of part P_k to the world coordinate system at the initial state (t=0). Here, s_k^0 represents per-axis scaling, R_k^0 is a rotation matrix, and t_k^0 is a translation vector. This transformation naturally defines an oriented bounding box (OBB) for the part at t=0, centered at t_k^0 with half-extents s_k^0 and orientation R_k^0.
- **T_k^1 = (R_k^1, t_k^1)**: A rigid transformation applied to part P_k's position from its initial state (t=0) to the articulated state (t=1). This transformation captures the part's rigid motion.

#### 3.2. Part Contactness and OBB-based Distance

The method explicitly leverages OBBs for structural guidance. An OBB for each part is extracted directly from the transformation parameters (T_k^0, and subsequently T_k^1 for the articulated state).
To quantify the physical relationship between parts, the **Separating Axis Theorem (SAT)** is employed to compute the distance between any two OBBs (P_i, P_j). This involves projecting both OBBs onto a set of 15 candidate axes (three face normals from each OBB and nine cross products of their edge directions). For each axis, a signed distance (d_a) is computed based on the overlap of the projected intervals. The overall distance d(P_i, P_j) is then defined as the maximum d_a across all axes. Positive values indicate separation, zero indicates contact, and negative values indicate overlap. This differentiable distance metric enables the enforcement of contact constraints.

#### 3.3. Optimization with Structural Guidance (Loss Functions)

The total optimization objective combines photometric loss with several novel structure-aware losses:
1. **Photometric Image Loss (L_image)**: This is a standard 3DGS loss, calculated as a weighted sum of L1 distance and D-SSIM (structural dissimilarity) between rendered images and ground-truth multi-view RGB images for both states (t=0 and t=1).
2. **Part Fitting Loss (L_fit)**: This loss enforces spatial coherence, ensuring each part's OBB tightly encloses its geometry. It comprises two complementary terms:
 - **Coverage Loss (L_coverage)**: Penalizes regions of the ground-truth object mask (M) that are not covered by the union of all part structure images (I_all_struct), derived from OBBs. This term encourages OBBs to expand and fully cover their respective parts.
 - **Margin Loss (L_margin)**: Penalizes regions within a part's individual structure image (I_k_struct) that lie outside the overall object mask (M). This term encourages OBBs to shrink and tightly fit the occupied volume, minimizing unused void space within each bounding box.
3. **Part Contact Loss (L_contact)**: This loss enforces structural connectivity by penalizing separation between adjacent parts. It uses the OBB-based distance d(P_i, P_j) calculated with SAT. Only positive distances (separation) are penalized, allowing for some OBB overlap, which is plausible in articulated contexts (e.g., a drawer inside a cabinet). The loss is computed as Σ max(0, d(P_i, P_j))^2 for adjacent pairs (i,j)∈E.
4. **Extent Regularization (L_ext)**: This regularization term promotes flexible OBB scaling along specific axes. It penalizes the logarithm of OBB scale components (s_k_0,i) with learned per-axis weights, allowing the OBBs to adapt to the underlying part geometry by expanding primarily along under-covered directions rather than uniformly.

The final optimization objective is L_total = Σ_{t∈{0,1}} (L_t_image + L_t_fit + L_t_contact) + L_ext.

#### 3.4. Optimization Process

- **Initialization**: 10,000 Gaussians are randomly sampled per part within the canonical cube. OBBs are initialized using coarse point clouds obtained from VGGT. Static and dynamic regions are identified based on nearest-neighbor distances between point clouds from the two states, followed by DBSCAN clustering to form part hypotheses. OBBs are then fitted to these clusters. Articulation transforms T_k^1 are initialized as identity.
- **Scheduling**: The Adam optimizer is used for 10,000 iterations. Structure-aware losses (L_fit, L_contact, L_ext) are active from the start. L_image is introduced at iteration 400. Once part structures are sufficiently established (iteration 1500), L_contact and L_ext are disabled, and refinement continues with L_image and L_fit.
- **Densification**: Standard 3DGS densification and pruning strategies are applied, with new Gaussians retaining the part assignment of their parent Gaussian.
- **Joint Type Estimation**: After optimization, the optimized transformation T_k^1 for each part is analyzed. A part is classified as prismatic if its rotation angle is below a 5° threshold, otherwise revolute. Corresponding joint parameters (motion axis, displacement for prismatic; rotation axis, pivot point for revolute) are then extracted.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental validation of StructureGS across synthetic and real-world datasets, comparing its performance against prominent baselines: PARIS, ArticulatedGS, and ScrewSplat. Evaluations focus on geometric accuracy, kinematic accuracy, and rendering quality.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **PARIS Dataset (Single Movable Part)**:
 - **Full Views (100 views)**: StructureGS consistently outperforms baselines across all metrics. For instance, it achieved an average angular error of 0.088° for motion (compared to 0.091°-7.787° for baselines), a whole-object Chamfer-L1 Distance (CD-w) of 0.919 (vs. 1.908-13.935 for baselines), and a PSNR of 43.39 (vs. 29.324-37.336 for baselines).
 - **Sparse Views (10 views)**: The performance gains are more pronounced under limited observations, highlighting StructureGS's robustness. Angular error was 0.701° (vs. 8.553°-32.840°), CD-w was 1.320 (vs. 19.847°-84.632°), and PSNR was 33.264 (vs. 22.363°-28.135°).
- **DTA Dataset (Multi-Movable Parts)**: StructureGS maintained high reconstruction quality and lower errors for objects with more complex, multi-part kinematic structures, demonstrating scalability. Baselines often struggled to decouple multiple movable parts, leading to merged components and degraded overall reconstruction quality.
- **ArtGS-Multi Dataset (4-7 Parts)**: Further scalability tests on this dataset showed consistent improvements over ScrewSplat in both geometric and kinematic accuracy, validating the approach for more complex articulated structures.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Observations

- **Part Decomposition and Geometry**: StructureGS produced reconstructions with well-defined part boundaries and geometrically coherent structures. Baselines, particularly in sparse-view settings, frequently exhibited ghosting artifacts, blurry boundaries, and incorrect part decomposition, resulting from the failure to disentangle geometry and motion.
- **Motion Estimation**: The method consistently yielded accurate joint axis and motion parameter estimates, ensuring physically plausible articulation animations.

#### 4.3. Real-world Evaluation

- On a custom dataset of four challenging real-world objects (kettle, stand, carrier, hole punch) captured with only 10 smartphone images and using off-the-shelf camera pose and mask estimation (COLMAP, SAM), StructureGS robustly recovered coherent part-level 3DGS and accurate kinematics. Photometric-only baselines struggled significantly under these noisy and sparse conditions, producing scattered Gaussians and inaccurate joint estimates.

#### 4.4. Efficiency

- StructureGS achieved competitive training times, approximately 13 minutes per scene, similar to other Gaussian Splatting methods (ArticulatedGS, ScrewSplat) and substantially faster than NeRF-based approaches like PARIS (40 minutes).

#### 4.5. Ablation Study (Loss Functions)

- **Photometric loss only (L_image)**: Resulted in severe geometry and kinematic errors due to unresolved shape-motion ambiguity.
- **Adding Part Fitting (L_fit) and Part Contact (L_contact) losses**: Led to significant reductions in geometric distortion and joint inaccuracies, enabling coherent part decomposition and stable kinematics.
- **Adding Extent Regularization (L_ext)**: Further improved geometric accuracy, particularly for movable parts (CD-m), by rectifying under-estimated bounding volumes and stabilizing part extents.

#### 4.6. Robustness Analysis

- **View Sparsity**: StructureGS maintained stable performance (angular error, Chamfer distance) when the number of training views was reduced from 100 to 10, whereas baselines like ArticulatedGS showed a sharp increase in errors. This indicates that OBB-based structural guidance effectively constrains the solution space when visual evidence is limited.
- **OBB Initialization**: The method demonstrated robustness to noisy and imperfect OBB initializations derived from coarse point clouds and clustering. Optimization curves showed convergence to similar fitting errors and part-aligned OBBs, even from varying initial qualities. It was also shown to be independent of the specific point cloud estimator used.
- **Non-OBB-shaped objects**: Despite using OBBs as a coarse structural proxy, the jointly optimized 3D Gaussians could successfully recover intricate geometric details even for objects with highly non-cuboidal shapes and large empty space ratios within their bounding boxes (e.g., the foldchair scene with 97% empty space).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

StructureGS represents a methodological advancement in articulated object reconstruction by systematically integrating explicit structural guidance into the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework. The introduction of oriented bounding boxes (OBBs) and the associated structure-aware loss functions (part fitting, part contact, and extent regularization) addresses a critical limitation of previous photometric-only methods: the entanglement of geometry, appearance, and motion parameters.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to produce high-quality articulated object reconstructions characterized by:
1. **Clearer Part Decomposition**: By enforcing spatial coherence and structural connectivity, the method yields models with well-defined part boundaries and reduced geometric artifacts, which is crucial for understanding an object's functional structure.
2. **More Accurate Kinematics**: The explicit structural constraints guide the optimization towards physically plausible articulation, leading to more precise estimates of joint axes and motion parameters.
3. **Enhanced Robustness**: A notable impact is the significantly improved performance and stability under challenging conditions, particularly with sparse input views and noisy initializations. This robustness expands the practical applicability of articulated reconstruction to real-world scenarios where dense, high-quality data capture may not be feasible.

The potential impact of StructureGS extends to several domains:
- **Embodied AI and Robotics**: Accurate 3D models with disentangled part structures and kinematics are fundamental for intelligent agents to understand object functionality, plan manipulation tasks, and interact physically with their environment. StructureGS could provide a more reliable foundation for such applications.
- **Digital Twin Creation**: The method facilitates the creation of high-fidelity digital twins of articulated objects, which are valuable for simulation, virtual prototyping, and digital content creation in industries ranging from manufacturing to entertainment.
- **AR/VR Applications**: Enabling robust reconstruction from limited real-world data could support more realistic and interactive augmented and virtual reality experiences involving complex objects.
- **Computer Vision Research**: The work contributes to the broader understanding of how to incorporate semantic and structural priors into neural rendering and reconstruction pipelines, offering insights for future research on complex scene understanding.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges a limitation: performance can degrade when articulated motion produces only weak visual changes, potentially leading to part collapse into static regions. This suggests that while OBBs provide strong structural cues, they might not fully compensate for extreme visual ambiguity. Future work exploring the integration of higher-level semantic features, such as those derived from models like DINO, is proposed to address this remaining challenge.
