# ImageDoctor: Diagnosing Text-to-Image Generation via Grounded Image Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ImageDoctor framework introduces a unified, multi-aspect evaluation for text-to-image (T2I) generation, providing both scalar quality scores and pixel-level flaw heatmaps via grounded image reasoning. It leverages this dense, spatially aware feedback to enable a novel DenseFlow-GRPO method, which significantly enhances T2I model performance and human preference alignment in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

## Problem

- Existing text-to-image (T2I) evaluation metrics rely on single scalar scores, lacking interpretability and specific diagnostic feedback on *why* an image is good or bad.
- Current reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) methods for T2I models use sparse, image-level rewards, which are insufficient for guiding fine-grained improvements or addressing localized visual artifacts.
- A need for an evaluation model that can provide comprehensive, interpretable, and actionable feedback, including spatially grounded flaw indicators, to effectively diagnose and improve T2I models.

## Method

- **ImageDoctor** employs a multi-modal large language model (MLLM) backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) with a lightweight heatmap decoder to generate both multi-aspect quality scores and pixel-level artifact/misalignment heatmaps.
- It utilizes a "look-think-predict" reasoning paradigm, first localizing flaws with bounding boxes ("look"), then generating structured textual reasoning ("think"), and finally producing quantitative scores and heatmap signals ("predict").
- A two-phase training pipeline involves cold start supervised fine-tuning (score prediction, Chain-of-Thought reasoning) followed by reinforcement fine-tuning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) guided by grounding, score, and heatmap rewards.

## Results

- ImageDoctor achieved state-of-the-art alignment with human judgments on the RichHF-**18K** dataset, with an average Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC) of **0.741** for score prediction and superior heatmap prediction performance.
- It demonstrated robust generalization across GenAI-Bench and TIFA datasets without specific fine-tuning, consistently outperforming previous human preference models.
- When used as a reward function within the novel DenseFlow-GRPO framework, ImageDoctor's combined scalar scores and heatmaps significantly improved T2I model generation quality, achieving the highest gains across various evaluation metrics for finetuning Stable Diffusion 3.5-medium.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining multi-aspect scalar scores with pixel-level flaw heatmaps provides a comprehensive and interpretable diagnostic tool for T2I models, surpassing the limitations of single-score metrics.
> - Dense, spatially grounded rewards derived from heatmaps are more effective for fine-tuning T2I models via RLHF than traditional sparse, image-level rewards, enabling targeted local refinements.
> - A "look-think-predict" reasoning paradigm, inspired by human diagnostic processes and supported by MLLMs, can significantly improve the accuracy and interpretability of automated image evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation has witnessed a rapid transformation, moving from early Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Auto-Encoders (VAEs) to more recent and powerful diffusion models, flow-based models, and autoregressive architectures. These advancements have enabled the creation of remarkably realistic and diverse images from textual prompts, opening up vast applications in creative industries, design, and content generation. However, this impressive progress simultaneously creates a critical challenge: how to reliably and comprehensively evaluate the quality of these generated images.

Traditional T2I evaluation metrics, such as CLIPScore, PickScore, ImageReward, and the HPS series, primarily quantify image quality using a single scalar score. While useful for broad comparisons, these single-number assessments lack interpretability. They cannot explain *why* an image is considered good or bad, nor can they pinpoint specific flaws. For instance, an image might score low due to poor semantic alignment despite high aesthetic appeal, or it might be largely faithful to the prompt but contain unrealistic artifacts. This limitation hinders effective diagnosis and targeted improvement of T2I models. Recent efforts, like RichHF and HELM, have begun to explore multi-dimensional evaluation, acknowledging the complex nature of image quality. ImageDoctor pushes this frontier by not only providing multi-aspect scores but also introducing *spatially grounded feedback* in the form of pixel-level heatmaps.

Furthermore, evaluators play an increasingly crucial role in the training of T2I models, particularly with the rise of reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) techniques. In RLHF, evaluation models often serve as reward functions, guiding the generation process towards human preferences. Current RLHF approaches for T2I, such as Flow-GRPO, typically rely on sparse, image-level rewards. These sparse signals overlook fine-grained details, meaning they cannot tell the model *where* in the image a problem lies or *which specific aspect* needs improvement. This can lead to inefficient training and sub-optimal refinement, especially when dealing with partial prompt adherence or localized visual artifacts. ImageDoctor directly addresses this by providing *dense, pixel-level rewards*, which offer precise, spatially aware supervision. This capability represents a significant advancement, bridging the gap between holistic image evaluation and actionable, localized feedback for generative model optimization. By leveraging powerful multi-modal large language models (MLLMs), ImageDoctor also aligns with the broader trend of harnessing large foundation models for diverse AI tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "IMAGEDOCTOR" is to develop a unified, multi-aspect evaluation framework for Text-to-Image (T2I) generation that transcends the limitations of existing scalar-based metrics. The authors are primarily motivated by the inadequacy of single-score evaluations, which fail to provide comprehensive, interpretable, and actionable feedback necessary for diagnosing and improving T2I models.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **To provide comprehensive and interpretable feedback:** Current methods quantify image quality with a single scalar, obscuring the nuanced reasons behind an image's perceived quality. ImageDoctor aims to decompose image quality into four complementary dimensions: plausibility, semantic alignment, aesthetics, and overall quality. This multi-aspect scoring offers a more holistic understanding of image generation performance.
2. **To offer spatially grounded flaw indicators:** Beyond numerical scores, there is a critical need to identify *where* flaws occur within an image. Many T2I generation failures stem from localized issues—e.g., incorrect details, unrealistic textures, or misaligned objects. ImageDoctor seeks to provide pixel-level flaw indicators in the form of heatmaps, highlighting misaligned or implausible regions. This spatially grounded feedback is crucial for precise problem identification.
3. **To enhance reasoning capabilities and detail sensitivity:** Inspired by the medical diagnostic process, the authors aim to build an evaluation model that can "look," "think," and then "predict." This paradigm is designed to improve the model's ability to localize potential flaws, generate structured reasoning about those flaws by integrating visual evidence with contextual understanding, and then conclude with quantitative scores. This mimics human evaluators, leading to more robust and explainable assessments.
4. **To serve as a dense reward for T2I model preference alignment:** With the increasing adoption of reinforcement learning (RLHF) for fine-tuning T2I models, there is a strong motivation to provide more effective reward signals. Existing RLHF frameworks use sparse, image-level rewards, which are insufficient for guiding fine-grained improvements. ImageDoctor is motivated to generate dense, pixel-level rewards from its heatmaps, enabling T2I models to learn not only global quality but also how to refine local regions, thereby significantly improving generation quality.
5. **To establish a versatile evaluation tool:** The authors intend for ImageDoctor to be more than just a metric; it should be a versatile tool capable of serving as a reliable verifier in test-time scaling scenarios (selecting the best image from multiple candidates) and an effective reward model for RL-based optimization of T2I generators.

In essence, ImageDoctor seeks to transform T2I evaluation from a coarse, unidimensional judgment into a fine-grained, multi-dimensional, and spatially intelligent diagnostic process, directly facilitating the development of higher-quality generative models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ImageDoctor is designed as a unified evaluation framework built on a multi-modal large language model (MLLM) backbone, specifically Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Its core methodology integrates advanced vision-language understanding with a novel "look-think-predict" paradigm and a two-phase training strategy, enabling both multi-aspect scoring and spatially grounded heatmap generation.

#### 3.1. Model Design

The architecture comprises an MLLM backbone and a lightweight heatmap decoder.
- **MLLM Backbone:** The MLLM receives the text prompt and the generated image. It is fine-tuned to jointly understand image details and image-text semantics. Its textual output is formatted to include the four scalar quality scores: semantic alignment, aesthetics, plausibility, and overall quality. Additionally, it generates special "task tokens" (<ART> for artifact, <MIS> for misalignment) which are crucial for guiding the heatmap decoder.
- **Heatmap Decoder:** This component is responsible for generating pixel-level artifact and misalignment heatmaps ($H_{art}, H_{mis}$). It takes three inputs:
 1. Image features extracted by the MLLM's visual encoder.
 2. A learned heatmap token.
 3. The task token generated by the MLLM backbone, which specifies whether to generate an artifact or misalignment heatmap.
 Inspired by the SAM mask decoder, it employs a bi-directional cross-attention mechanism to fuse the token and image embeddings. The refined image features are then upsampled via convolution layers to match the original image size, and an additional token-to-image attention mechanism dynamically predicts the heatmap through a spatial point-wise product. This design ensures that both semantic reasoning from the MLLM and localized visual evidence contribute to precise spatial diagnostics.

#### 3.2. Grounded Image Reasoning ("Look-Think-Predict" Paradigm)

ImageDoctor adopts a diagnostic process akin to a human expert:
- **"Look":** The model first localizes potential flawed regions by predicting bounding boxes. This initial spatial grounding directs subsequent, more detailed analysis.
- **"Think":** It then analyzes these localized regions, integrating visual evidence with its broader contextual understanding of the prompt and image. This step involves generating structured textual reasoning, evaluating the image across the four quality aspects.
- **"Predict":** Finally, based on its observations and reasoning, ImageDoctor produces the conclusive quantitative scores and signals for heatmap generation.

#### 3.3. Two-Phase Training Pipeline

To instill the "look-think-predict" capability, ImageDoctor undergoes a two-phase training process:

1. **Cold Start (Supervised Fine-tuning):**
 - **Stage 1 (Score Prediction):** The MLLM backbone is initially fine-tuned on the image evaluation task to directly predict the four quality scores.
 - **Stage 2 (CoT and Heatmap Learning):** The model is trained on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data to learn the full "look-think-predict" reasoning process for both scores and heatmaps. The CoT data is ingeniously prepared: human-annotated heatmaps are used to detect flaw regions and generate bounding boxes. Then, Gemini 2.5 Flash, a powerful VLM, is prompted with the original image, prompt, and visual cues from the heatmaps (e.g., circled outlines of flaws) to generate detailed, structured reasoning aligned with human annotations. This high-quality reasoning, along with ground-truth heatmaps, is used to fine-tune ImageDoctor. The loss function optimizes both the textual reasoning (cross-entropy for token prediction) and heatmap prediction (L2 distance).

2. **Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) with Group Relative Policy Optimization - GRPO:**
 After supervised fine-tuning, RFT further refines ImageDoctor's reasoning. Given an image-prompt pair, ImageDoctor generates multiple candidate responses. A suite of verifiable reward functions is designed to guide this process:
 - **Grounding Reward ($R_G$):** Encourages accurate localization of flaw regions. It has three components: Completeness (bounding boxes cover all highlighted areas), Compactness (minimal normal regions within boxes), and Uniqueness (minimal overlap between boxes).
 - **Score Reward ($R_S$):** Measures the L1 distance between predicted scores and ground-truth human scores, encouraging alignment with subjective human judgments.
 - **Heatmap Reward ($R_H$):** Measures the L2 distance between predicted heatmaps and human-annotated heatmaps, promoting precise and sharp flaw localization.
 The total reward ($R = R_G + R_S + R_H$) is used within the GRPO framework to update the policy model, enhancing generalization and reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.4. DenseFlow-GRPO (Downstream Application)

To address the limitations of sparse rewards in T2I RLHF, ImageDoctor introduces **DenseFlow-GRPO**. This novel framework leverages ImageDoctor's dense feedback to enhance T2I models (e.g., flow models). Instead of image-level rewards, DenseFlow-GRPO reformulates the likelihood ratio at each trajectory step to allow pixel-wise advantage customization. It combines ImageDoctor's image-level scores and pixel-level heatmap information ($R_D(x_0, c, h, w) = R(x_0, c) + R_P(x_0, c, h, w)$) to create a dense reward function. This dense signal enables more precise, spatially aligned supervision, teaching T2I models to refine local regions in a fine-grained manner, leading to more robust improvements in generation quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate ImageDoctor's superior performance in T2I evaluation and its effectiveness in downstream applications.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Performance on RichHF-18K

- **Score Prediction:** ImageDoctor achieved state-of-the-art alignment with human judgments across all four dimensions (plausibility, aesthetics, semantic alignment, and overall quality) on the RichHF-18K dataset. Its average Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC) reached 0.741, a substantial improvement over the previous best method, RichHF (0.586). Notably, ImageDoctor significantly enhanced semantic alignment prediction (PLCC: 0.808 vs. 0.474), indicating a much stronger correlation with human understanding of prompt adherence.
- **Heatmap Prediction:** ImageDoctor also demonstrated the best performance in heatmap prediction, as measured by Mean Squared Error (MSE), Correlation Coefficient (CC), KL Divergence (KLD), and Similarity (SIM). This highlights its strong ability to precisely localize both artifact and misalignment flaws within generated images, outperforming methods like RichHF and CLIP Gradient.

#### 4.2. Generalizability on GenAI-Bench and TIFA

- When evaluated on the GenAI-Bench and TIFA datasets *without any specific fine-tuning* on these benchmarks, ImageDoctor consistently outperformed previous human preference models. This robust generalization capability, despite differences in image sources and annotator subjectivity, underscores the model's effectiveness and broad applicability.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of ImageDoctor's key components:
- **Task Tokens for Heatmap Decoder:** Removing these tokens led to a notable decrease in heatmap prediction performance (e.g., CC dropped by 0.024 for artifact and 0.042 for misalignment), proving their importance in guiding accurate spatial localization.
- **"Look" and "Think" Paradigm:**
 - Removing the "look" component (bounding box prediction) caused a drop in heatmap prediction quality (e.g., misalignment CC fell from 0.224 to 0.160), highlighting its role in enhancing spatial localization.
 - Removing the "think" component (reasoning generation) led to a more significant decrease in score prediction accuracy (PLCC dropped from 0.720 to 0.708), confirming its critical role in semantic reasoning for accurate evaluation.
- **Grounding Reward:** The absence of the grounding reward during reinforcement fine-tuning resulted in a decline in both score prediction performance and artifact heatmap quality, emphasizing its necessity for precise flaw region localization.

#### 4.4. Applications in Downstream Tasks

- **Verifier for Test-Time Scaling:** When used as a verifier to select the best among 16 generated images from Flux-dev, ImageDoctor reliably picked candidates that better aligned with the prompt and preserved more realistic and coherent details, outperforming other verifiers like PickScore and ImageReward.
- **Reward Function for RLHF:**
 - In Flow-GRPO, using ImageDoctor's predicted scores as the reward function consistently yielded the highest gains across various evaluation metrics (ImageReward, CLIPScore, UnifiedReward) for finetuning Stable Diffusion 3.5-medium, surpassing rewards from PickScore and RichHF.
 - The proposed **DenseFlow-GRPO**, which leverages ImageDoctor's combined scalar scores and heatmaps as dense rewards, achieved the best overall results. This demonstrated that ImageDoctor's pixel-level feedback provides fine-grained supervision, leading to stronger preference alignment and significantly improved generation quality, particularly in refining local details and eliminating localized artifacts that sparse rewards often miss.

In summary, ImageDoctor not only sets new benchmarks for comprehensive T2I evaluation but also proves its practical utility in actively improving T2I generation models, particularly through its innovative dense reward mechanism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ImageDoctor represents a significant advancement in the evaluation and improvement of text-to-image (T2I) generation models, with profound potential impacts across several dimensions of generative AI research and development.

1. **Transformative T2I Evaluation:** By moving beyond single scalar scores to a unified framework that provides multi-aspect scores (plausibility, semantic alignment, aesthetics, overall quality) *and* spatially grounded heatmaps, ImageDoctor offers a diagnostic capability unmatched by previous methods. This detailed feedback is crucial for understanding *why* a generated image succeeds or fails, providing a level of interpretability that was previously lacking. This shift from mere quantitative assessment to qualitative diagnosis will allow researchers and developers to gain deeper insights into model behavior and limitations.

2. **Accelerated Model Development and Debugging:** The pixel-level flaw indicators from ImageDoctor can directly inform and accelerate the iterative development cycle of T2I models. Instead of struggling to infer the cause of poor performance from a single low score, developers can now pinpoint specific regions or aspects that need improvement. This actionable feedback allows for targeted model adjustments, leading to more efficient debugging and faster progress in achieving higher-quality and more faithful image generation.

3. **Revolutionizing Reinforcement Learning for T2I Models:** Perhaps the most impactful contribution is ImageDoctor's role in the proposed DenseFlow-GRPO. The ability to provide *dense, pixel-level rewards* to reinforcement learning algorithms for T2I models is a paradigm shift. Existing RLHF methods, which rely on sparse, image-level rewards, often struggle to address localized artifacts or fine-grained misalignments. ImageDoctor's dense rewards offer precise spatial supervision, enabling T2I models to learn to refine specific regions and details. This could unlock a new era of highly aligned and locally coherent generative models, pushing the boundaries of realism and prompt adherence. It paves the way for T2I models that are not only globally impressive but also meticulously accurate at the pixel level.

4. **Enhanced Human-AI Alignment:** By aligning its evaluations closely with human preferences across multiple dimensions and providing interpretable reasoning, ImageDoctor facilitates better human-AI collaboration. It translates nuanced human judgments into structured feedback that AI models can learn from, fostering a deeper understanding between human expectations and AI capabilities. This is critical for building trustworthy and user-centric generative AI systems.

5. **Broader Applicability Across Generative AI:** The "look-think-predict" paradigm and the concept of dense, multi-aspect, spatially grounded feedback are not limited to T2I. This methodology could be adapted to evaluate and improve other complex generative AI tasks, such as video generation, 3D content synthesis, or even multi-modal story generation, where localized inconsistencies or conceptual flaws are common challenges.

6. **Benchmarking and Standardisation:** As T2I models continue to evolve, robust and comprehensive evaluation metrics become essential for fair comparison and progress tracking. ImageDoctor's superior alignment with human judgment and its multi-faceted approach make it a strong candidate for a new standard in T2I evaluation benchmarks, influencing future research directions.

In conclusion, ImageDoctor is more than just an evaluation metric; it is a diagnostic tool and a training facilitator that promises to significantly advance the capabilities and quality of text-to-image generation. Its ability to provide interpretable, multi-dimensional, and spatially grounded feedback addresses fundamental limitations in current generative AI pipelines, paving the way for more sophisticated, controllable, and human-aligned creative AI systems.
