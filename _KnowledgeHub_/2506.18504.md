# Generalizing vision-language models to novel domains: A comprehensive survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey offers the first comprehensive review of knowledge transfer and generalization strategies for pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), including recent Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It systematically categorizes adaptation methods, compares their performance across diverse transfer learning settings, and identifies critical future research challenges.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs), despite strong zero-shot performance, often perform poorly on domain-specific or out-of-distribution (OOD) data.
- Directly fine-tuning large pretrained VLMs with limited target data can disrupt their carefully aligned multimodal representations, leading to performance degradation.
- There was a lack of a comprehensive overview of strategies for efficient knowledge transfer and generalization of VLMs to novel domains and tasks.

## Method

- Conducted the first comprehensive survey dedicated to VLM knowledge transfer and generalization, analyzing existing literature and methodologies.
- Proposed a novel ==classification framework== categorizing VLM adaptation methods into ==prompt-based==, ==parameter-based==, and ==feature-based== approaches.
- Reinterpreted traditional transfer learning settings (UDA, DG, TTA, FSL) through the unique lens of VLM-based generalization and integrated discussion of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

## Results

- The survey successfully established a novel categorization of VLM generalization methods: prompt-based, parameter-based, and feature-based.
- Performance comparisons indicate that Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones achieve approximately **15%** higher generalization accuracy than ResNet-based ones within VLMs.
- Test-Time Adaptation methods prove superior on hard Out-of-Distribution datasets, while Few-Shot Learning methods generally yield the best overall performance when limited labeled target data is available.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained VLMs, especially those with Vision Transformer backbones, fundamentally reshape transfer learning, often providing robust baselines in various adaptation settings.
> - Generalization methods demonstrate varying strengths: Test-Time Adaptation excels on hard Out-of-Distribution datasets, Few-Shot Learning achieves top performance with labeled data, and robust fine-tuning is effective for significant domain shifts.
> - The emergence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) marks a crucial advancement in VLM generalization, showcasing broad capabilities across diverse multimodal tasks via multi-stage training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in deep neural networks have led to the emergence of "foundation models" – models pretrained on massive datasets to acquire general-purpose knowledge, which can then be adapted to various downstream tasks. In the vision and natural language processing domains, models like ResNet and GPT-3 exemplify this paradigm. However, these unimodal models inherently lack the ability to perceive and reason across modalities.

The landscape was fundamentally reshaped by the introduction of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), pioneered by CLIP. VLMs integrate visual and textual modalities through contrastive pretraining on web-scale image-text pairs, exhibiting impressive zero-shot capabilities across a range of tasks. This breakthrough positioned VLMs as powerful, multimodal foundation models.

However, a critical challenge emerged: despite their strong performance on general tasks, VLMs often struggle when confronted with domain-specific, specialized, or out-of-distribution (OOD) data. Direct full fine-tuning with limited target data can disturb the carefully aligned vision-language representations, leading to performance degradation. This necessitates effective knowledge transfer and generalization strategies for VLMs.

This survey directly addresses this crucial gap in the research landscape. While existing surveys primarily focus on VLM *pretraining* (e.g., model structures, objectives, datasets), this paper distinguishes itself as the *first comprehensive survey* dedicated to the *knowledge transfer and generalization* of pretrained VLMs. It provides a timely and much-needed synthesis of strategies to adapt VLMs to novel domains and tasks with minimal computational and annotation expenses.

Furthermore, the survey acknowledges the revolutionary breakthroughs in modern large language models (LLMs) and their integration with VLMs to form Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), such as DeepSeek-VL. MLLMs represent the next evolutionary step in vision-language research, offering even broader generalization capabilities across a wider array of general visual-language tasks. By including MLLMs, the survey positions itself at the frontier of vision-language research, offering a holistic view of the progression from foundational VLMs to the latest multimodal AI systems. It serves as a vital resource for researchers seeking to navigate the complex and rapidly evolving domain of VLM adaptation and generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this survey stems from a recognized limitation of even the most powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP: while they excel at zero-shot generalization on broad tasks due to web-scale pretraining, their performance often significantly deteriorates when confronted with specialized, domain-specific, or out-of-distribution (OOD) data. Simply fine-tuning these large models can be problematic, potentially disrupting their carefully learned, aligned multimodal representations and leading to degraded performance, especially with insufficient target data. Thus, the overarching challenge is to elegantly and efficiently transfer the rich, pretrained knowledge embedded in VLMs to diverse downstream applications without compromising their inherent capabilities or incurring prohibitive computational costs.

In response to this critical challenge, the authors set forth several key objectives for their comprehensive survey:

1. **Systematically Summarize Generalization Literature:** To comprehensively review and organize the vast and rapidly growing body of research focused on adapting or generalizing pretrained VLMs. This includes documenting various generalization settings, the methodologies employed, standard benchmarking practices, and reported results.
2. **Categorize Methodologies by Transferred Modules:** To develop a novel classification framework for VLM generalization methods based on *which specific modules* within the typical dual-branch VLM architecture (e.g., CLIP) are transferred or adapted. This categorization divides methods into prompt-based, parameter-based, and feature-based approaches.
3. **Reinterpret Transfer Learning (TL) in the VLM Era:** To revisit and analyze traditional transfer learning settings (e.g., Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA), Domain Generalization (DG), Test-Time Adaptation (TTA), Few-Shot Learning (FSL)) through the lens of VLM-based methods, providing novel interpretations and highlighting the unique characteristics derived from VLMs' multimodal nature and powerful zero-shot abilities.
4. **Provide Rigorous Performance Comparisons:** To collect and introduce mainstream benchmarks used for evaluating VLM generalization methods and to offer a thorough, fair, and comprehensive comparison of performance among the reviewed techniques, considering generalization settings, model backbones, and method designs.
5. **Integrate Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** To discuss the relationship and differences between traditional VLMs and the emerging generation of MLLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-VL). This objective aims to summarize general frameworks for building MLLMs, their structures, training data, optimization objectives, and how they represent scaled applications of vision-language research for generalization.
6. **Outline Future Research Directions:** To analyze persistent key challenges in current vision-language research and discuss promising potential directions for future exploration, thereby contributing a clear landscape for the community.

By achieving these objectives, the survey aims to provide a much-needed, structured understanding of VLM generalization, offering a practical guide for researchers and practitioners in this dynamic field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors' approach to conducting this comprehensive survey is structured and systematic, aiming to provide a clear and practical landscape of VLM generalization research.

**A. Scope Definition and Foundational Model:**
The survey primarily focuses on the knowledge transfer and generalization of *pretrained* Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It aligns with the current research focus by discussing the prevalent "two-tower" VLM architecture, exemplified by CLIP. This involves detailing the pretraining objective (contrastive learning with image-text pairs), the model structure (vision and language encoders), and the zero-shot inference procedure (cosine similarity between image and text features). This foundational understanding sets the stage for analyzing how knowledge is transferred within or from these models.

**B. Categorization Framework for Generalization Methods:**
A central methodological contribution is the novel categorization of VLM generalization methods based on the "transferred modules" within the VLM architecture. This framework provides a structured lens through which to analyze diverse techniques:
1. **Prompt-based methods:** These techniques primarily tune the textual prompt embeddings, or introduce visual/context prompts, to control VLM behavior without altering core model parameters. This includes differentiating between train-time (few-shot, generalizable prompting) and test-time prompting.
2. **Parameter-based methods:** This category involves modifying the actual parameters of the VLM encoders. It is further divided into:
 - **Fine-tuning methods:** Strategically updating pretrained parameters, emphasizing "robust fine-tuning" (preventing forgetting) and "selective fine-tuning" (adapting specific, critical parameters).
 - **Generalizable distillation:** Learning new, often smaller, student models by distilling knowledge from the larger, pretrained VLMs for efficient deployment or domain-specific generalization.
3. **Feature-based methods:** These approaches operate on the high-dimensional features extracted by the VLM's encoders, introducing external modules for adaptation without directly modifying the VLM's parameters. This includes "feature refinement" using adapters or linear probing, and "cache models" that store representative features for efficient, train-free prediction.

**C. Reinterpretation through Transfer Learning Settings:**
The survey revisits the categorized VLM-based methods by integrating them with heavily-studied traditional transfer learning (TL) settings:
- **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA):** Transferring knowledge from a labeled source to an unlabeled target domain with distribution shift.
- **Domain Generalization (DG):** Training on source domain(s) to generalize to entirely unseen target domains.
- **Test-Time Adaptation (TTA):** Adjusting model behavior efficiently at inference time using only target data streams.
- **Few-Shot Learning (FSL):** Adapting with very limited labeled target samples.
This approach allows for a nuanced analysis of how VLM characteristics influence and reshape these classic TL problems.

**D. Comprehensive Benchmarking and Performance Analysis:**
The authors compile and introduce mainstream benchmarks for evaluating VLM generalization methods. This includes a thorough performance comparison, considering generalization settings, VLM model backbones (e.g., ResNet, ViT), and the specific design choices of each method. This empirical analysis helps to identify strengths, weaknesses, and trends across different approaches.

**E. Integration of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):**
Recognizing the rapid evolution of the field, the survey extends its analysis to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It outlines the general structure of MLLMs (vision encoder, VL adapter, LLM), discusses their training data (vast multimodal instruction-following data) and strategies (multi-stage training), and provides a detailed summarization of typical and cutting-edge MLLMs, distinguishing between research-oriented and commercial offerings. This inclusion ensures the survey's relevance to the most recent advancements.

**F. Identification of Future Directions:**
Finally, the survey concludes by synthesizing current research advances, identifying key challenges that persist, and discussing promising future directions. This forward-looking perspective aims to inspire and guide subsequent research efforts in the vision-language field.

Overall, the methodology is designed to offer a structured, comprehensive, and up-to-date overview of VLM generalization, from foundational concepts to frontier MLLM developments, grounded in both theoretical categorization and empirical performance analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey distills several key findings and results, offering a comprehensive overview of the current state of VLM generalization:

**A. Effective Categorization of Generalization Methods:**
The proposed categorization of VLM generalization methods into **prompt-based, parameter-based, and feature-based** approaches provides an effective and intuitive framework for understanding the diverse landscape. This framework clarifies how different strategies interact with distinct components of the VLM architecture, from abstract text prompts to core encoder parameters and derived features.

**B. VLM's Transformative Impact on Transfer Learning:**
VLMs, with their vast general knowledge acquired from web-scale pretraining and strong zero-shot capabilities, have fundamentally reshaped traditional transfer learning (TL) scenarios. They set new, robust baselines across various TL settings, including UDA, DG, TTA, and FSL. This highlights that VLMs are not just powerful standalone models but also potent initializers for downstream adaptation. Notably, CLIP's zero-shot performance often surpasses specialized single-modality baselines, underscoring its inherent generalization strength.

**C. Performance Trends Across Settings and Architectures:**
- **Backbones:** Vision Transformer (ViT) based architectures consistently exhibit significantly better generalization abilities (around 15% accuracy gain) compared to ResNet-based ones within VLMs. This indicates the crucial role of the underlying vision encoder's representational power.
- **Transfer Settings:**
 - **Few-Shot Learning (FSL):** Methods with access to even limited labeled target data (FSL) generally achieve the best overall performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of targeted adaptation.
 - **Test-Time Adaptation (TTA):** TTA methods prove superior on *hard Out-of-Distribution (OOD)* datasets (e.g., ImageNet-A, FGVC, EuroSAT) with large distribution shifts. Their ability to make quick, unsupervised adjustments directly on unseen samples helps mitigate the domain gap effectively. Train-free cache models are particularly suitable for TTA due to their efficiency in processing data streams.
 - **Domain Generalization (DG):** DG methods tend to perform better on *in-distribution* or less severely shifted datasets, benefiting from sufficient training on diverse source data to learn more generalizable representations.
 - **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA):** CLIP-based UDA methods show significant improvement over single-modality baselines. However, a notable finding is their *over-reliance on CLIP's pseudo-labels*, leading to similar accuracies for tasks with the same target domain, and indicating that source domain knowledge might still be *under-explored* in many current approaches.
- **Method Designs:**
 - **Prompt Tuning:** Mainstream and effective for FSL, but its performance may deteriorate in TTA contexts due to insufficient training and lack of ground truth.
 - **Fine-tuning (Parameter-based):** Robust fine-tuning methods (e.g., WiSE-FT, CLIPood, CaRot) often outperform other types on domain-level DG tasks, particularly those with significant domain gaps (e.g., TerraIncognita). This suggests that substantial parameter updates are necessary for larger distribution shifts, though it comes with higher computational costs. However, even these methods still show significant room for improvement on challenging datasets, with accuracies often around 60%, highlighting the inherent difficulty of large distribution shifts.
 - **Distillation:** Offers flexible transfer, suitable for black-box models, resource-constrained deployments, and for enhancing task-specific generalization (e.g., object detection, semantic segmentation). Distillation methods, especially when leveraging larger teacher models, can surpass other competitors.

**D. Emergence and Importance of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):**
MLLMs represent a significant leap in VLM generalization. By connecting language-aligned vision encoders with powerful LLMs, MLLMs exhibit astonishing capabilities in a vast array of multimodal tasks (e.g., visual QA, segmentation, captioning). Their development involves multi-stage training paradigms with enormous, often proprietary, high-quality multimodal instruction-following datasets, leading to models with broad and deep generalization abilities across modalities and tasks. Both research-oriented (e.g., BLIP, LLaVA, MiniGPT) and commercial (e.g., Qwen-VL, DeepSeek-VL, PaliGemma, Apple MM) families of MLLMs are rapidly advancing, pushing the frontiers of multimodal AI.

**E. Persistent Challenges:**
Despite the remarkable progress, the survey highlights persistent challenges:
- Significant performance drop remains on datasets with large distribution shifts, even for advanced fine-tuning methods.
- Over-reliance on pseudo-labeling in UDA can limit the full exploitation of source domain knowledge.
- Computational intensity of many fine-tuning and even some prompt-tuning methods hinders agile deployment on resource-constrained environments.

These findings collectively paint a dynamic picture of VLM generalization, showcasing the immense potential of these models while pinpointing critical areas for future innovation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey on generalizing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to novel domains carries significant importance and is poised to have a substantial impact on the field of artificial intelligence for several reasons:

**A. Filling a Critical Knowledge Gap:**
The most immediate significance is that this paper addresses a crucial void in existing literature. While numerous surveys have covered VLM pretraining, this is identified as the *first dedicated survey* focusing specifically on the *knowledge transfer and generalization* of pretrained VLMs. This distinction is vital because the utility of powerful foundation models like VLMs hinges not just on their initial capabilities, but on their adaptability to real-world, often domain-specific or out-of-distribution (OOD), scenarios. By filling this gap, the survey becomes an indispensable resource for researchers and practitioners.

**B. Providing a Structured and Practical Framework:**
The proposed categorization of generalization methods into prompt-based, parameter-based, and feature-based techniques offers a clear and intuitive framework for understanding a complex and rapidly evolving research area. This structure simplifies navigation for newcomers and provides a common language for experienced researchers to compare and contrast diverse methodologies. This practical organization will facilitate the design of new generalization techniques and help in identifying under-explored areas.

**C. Informing Research Direction and Strategy:**
By systematically reviewing existing methods, benchmarking their performance across various datasets and transfer settings, and analyzing the impact of different VLM backbones, the survey provides invaluable insights. Researchers can quickly identify state-of-the-art approaches for specific generalization tasks (e.g., TTA for hard OOD, robust fine-tuning for large domain gaps) and understand the trade-offs involved (e.g., computational cost vs. accuracy gain). This empirical foundation is critical for guiding future research endeavors and resource allocation.

**D. Bridging Traditional Transfer Learning with Modern AI:**
The survey's re-interpretation of classic transfer learning (TL) settings (UDA, DG, TTA, FSL) in the context of VLMs and MLLMs demonstrates how these modern foundation models elevate and reshape long-standing challenges in AI. It highlights how VLM's inherent zero-shot capabilities provide a powerful starting point, fundamentally altering the strategies for knowledge transfer and adaptation. This intellectual bridge enriches both the TL community and the VLM community.

**E. Illuminating the Path to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):**
By integrating a detailed discussion of MLLMs, the survey places VLM generalization within the broader context of multimodal foundation models. This forward-looking perspective is crucial, as MLLMs represent the current frontier of general-purpose AI. The summary of MLLM structures, training strategies, and key examples provides a condensed but comprehensive understanding of how these powerful models are constructed and generalized, potentially inspiring innovations in multimodal agent design and real-world applications.

**F. Identifying Critical Future Challenges:**
The "Future Directions" section is particularly impactful, as it pinpoints unresolved challenges such as black-box generalization, efficiency across all computational dimensions, the development of truly general multimodal agents, native vision-language architectures, and the generalizability of training data itself. These challenges serve as a roadmap for future research, directing efforts towards areas that promise the greatest advancements for more robust, efficient, and universally applicable intelligent systems.

In summary, this survey is more than just a compilation of literature; it is a synthesis that provides context, structure, empirical analysis, and foresight into a pivotal area of AI research. Its impact will be felt by providing clarity, fostering innovation, and guiding the development of the next generation of generalizable vision-language and multimodal AI systems capable of operating effectively across diverse and novel real-world domains.
