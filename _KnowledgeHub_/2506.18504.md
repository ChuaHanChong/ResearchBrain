# Generalizing vision-language models to novel domains: A comprehensive survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey offers the first comprehensive review of knowledge transfer and generalization strategies for pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), including recent Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It systematically categorizes adaptation methods, compares their performance across diverse transfer learning settings, and identifies critical future research challenges.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs), despite strong zero-shot performance, often perform poorly on domain-specific or out-of-distribution (OOD) data.
- Directly fine-tuning large pretrained VLMs with limited target data can disrupt their carefully aligned multimodal representations, leading to performance degradation.
- There was a lack of a comprehensive overview of strategies for efficient knowledge transfer and generalization of VLMs to novel domains and tasks.

## Method

- Conducted the first comprehensive survey dedicated to VLM knowledge transfer and generalization, analyzing existing literature and methodologies.
- Proposed a novel ==classification framework== categorizing VLM adaptation methods into ==prompt-based==, ==parameter-based==, and ==feature-based== approaches.
- Reinterpreted traditional transfer learning settings (UDA, DG, TTA, FSL) through the unique lens of VLM-based generalization and integrated discussion of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

## Results

- The survey successfully established a novel categorization of VLM generalization methods: prompt-based, parameter-based, and feature-based.
- Performance comparisons indicate that Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones achieve approximately **15%** higher generalization accuracy than ResNet-based ones within VLMs.
- Test-Time Adaptation methods prove superior on hard Out-of-Distribution datasets, while Few-Shot Learning methods generally yield the best overall performance when limited labeled target data is available.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained VLMs, especially those with Vision Transformer backbones, fundamentally reshape transfer learning, often providing robust baselines in various adaptation settings.
> - Generalization methods demonstrate varying strengths: Test-Time Adaptation excels on hard Out-of-Distribution datasets, Few-Shot Learning achieves top performance with labeled data, and robust fine-tuning is effective for significant domain shifts.
> - The emergence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) marks a crucial advancement in VLM generalization, showcasing broad capabilities across diverse multimodal tasks via multi-stage training.
