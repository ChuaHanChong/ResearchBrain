# SMAP: Self-supervised Motion Adaptation for Physically Plausible Humanoid Whole-body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The SMAP framework develops a systematic self-supervised approach to transfer diverse human motion data to humanoid robots, ensuring physical plausibility and real-world deployability. This method enables the Unitree H1 humanoid robot to perform a wide range of stable and natural human-like movements, from dancing to martial arts.

## Problem

- Training humanoid robots to perform natural and stable movements is challenging due to their high degrees of freedom.
- Directly retargeting human motion data to robots often leads to unstable or physically impossible movements.
- Significant differences exist between human and robot biomechanics, including action spaces, physical constraints, and control mechanisms.

## Method

- The **SMAP** framework introduces a ==Humanoid-Adapter==, a self-supervised module that transforms human motions into physically plausible robot motions.
- It employs a Vector-Quantized Periodic Autoencoder (VQ-PAE) to learn shared representations between human and robot motion domains using unpaired data.
- A progressive policy learning approach with teacher-student distillation trains robust control policies, enabling sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- Achieved faster convergence and higher final performance in simulation for joint position and keypoint tracking compared to existing baselines.
- Enabled the humanoid robot to perform a wide variety of stable and natural human-like motions, including complex dance movements and martial arts.
- Successfully deployed on a Unitree H1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust and stable real-world execution across diverse motion types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A shared, discrete codebook learned via vector quantization can effectively bridge the heterogeneity between human and robot motion domains.
> - Self-supervised learning on unpaired human and robot motion data can discover common behavioral patterns, enabling adaptation without direct correspondences.
> - Progressive teacher-student distillation, using privileged information for the teacher and proprioceptive observations for the student, facilitates robust sim-to-real transfer for complex whole-body control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Humanoid robotics has long pursued the goal of creating robots that can move and interact like humans in real-world environments. However, training these complex systems with dozens of degrees of freedom to perform natural, stable movements remains a significant challenge. The SMAP (Self-supervised Motion Adaptation) framework addresses this problem by developing a systematic approach to transfer human motion data to humanoid robots while ensuring both physical plausibility and real-world deployability.

#### 1.2. The Challenge of Human-to-Humanoid Motion Transfer

The core challenge lies in the heterogeneity between human and humanoid robot action spaces. Humans have evolved sophisticated biomechanical systems with muscle activation patterns, joint flexibility, and balance mechanisms that differ substantially from robotic actuators and control systems. When human motion capture data is directly retargeted to humanoid robots, several problems emerge:

1. **Kinematic Incompatibility**: Human joint ranges and configurations may exceed robot capabilities
2. **Dynamic Inconsistency**: Human balance strategies may not translate effectively to robot dynamics
3. **Control Frequency Mismatch**: Human neural control operates differently from robot control loops
4. **Physical Constraint Violations**: Retargeted motions may violate robot joint limits or stability requirements

Traditional approaches often rely on extensive manual tuning or simplified motion primitives, limiting the diversity and naturalness of achievable movements. SMAP addresses these limitations through a data-driven approach that learns the mapping between human and robot motion domains automatically.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Motivation

The research tackles a fundamental issue in humanoid robotics: while humans and humanoid robots may appear similar, their underlying action spaces and physical constraints differ significantly. Direct application of human motion data to robots often results in unstable or physically impossible movements. SMAP introduces a self-supervised learning approach that learns to adapt human motions into robot-executable actions while maintaining the natural characteristics of human movement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. SMAP Framework Overview

The SMAP framework consists of two primary components that work together to enable effective human-to-robot motion transfer:

1. **Humanoid-Adapter**: A self-supervised motion adaptation module that learns to transform human motions into physically plausible robot motions
2. **Progressive Policy Learning**: A teacher-student distillation approach that trains robust control policies for real-world deployment

The framework operates on the principle that while human and robot motions may differ in execution, they can share common underlying behavioral patterns. By learning these shared representations, SMAP can generate robot motions that maintain the semantic content of human movements while respecting robot-specific constraints.

#### 3.2. Humanoid-Adapter: Bridging Motion Domains

The Humanoid-Adapter represents the core innovation of SMAP, using a Vector-Quantized Periodic Autoencoder (VQ-PAE) to learn a shared representation between human and robot motion spaces. This approach addresses the fundamental challenge of learning cross-domain mappings without paired training data.

#### 3.3. Vector Quantization Architecture

The VQ-PAE architecture consists of:

- **Encoder E₁**: Maps human motion sequences to continuous representations
- **Shared Codebook**: Discrete vocabulary of atomic behavioral patterns
- **Decoder D₂**: Reconstructs robot-compatible motion from codebook representations

The vector quantization mechanism creates a discrete bottleneck that forces the model to learn compact, generalizable representations of motion patterns. The mathematical formulation involves:

```
z = E₁(s^h)
q = VQ(z)
ŝ^r = D₂(q)
```

Where `s^h` represents human motion, `z` is the continuous encoding, `q` is the quantized representation, and `ŝ^r` is the reconstructed robot motion.

#### 3.4. Self-Supervised Training Strategy

The Humanoid-Adapter is trained using a self-supervised approach that leverages unpaired motion data:

1. **Human Motion Reconstruction**: The encoder E₁ and decoder D₁ learn to reconstruct human motions
2. **Robot Motion Reconstruction**: The encoder E₂ and decoder D₂ learn to reconstruct robot motions
3. **Shared Codebook Learning**: Both domains contribute to learning the discrete behavioral vocabulary

This approach allows the model to discover common motion patterns across domains without requiring expensive paired motion capture and robot execution data.

#### 3.5. Progressive Policy Learning with Teacher-Student Distillation

The second major component of SMAP addresses the challenge of transferring simulation-trained policies to real-world robots. This is accomplished through a teacher-student distillation framework with progressive curriculum learning.

#### 3.6. Teacher Policy Training

The teacher policy is trained in simulation with access to privileged information:

- **Ground Truth State Information**: Perfect knowledge of robot pose, contact forces, and environmental conditions
- **Adapted Motion Targets**: Human motions processed through the Humanoid-Adapter
- **Progressive Curriculum**: Gradual introduction of diverse motion patterns

The teacher policy uses this privileged information to learn robust motion tracking while maintaining stability constraints.

#### 3.7. Student Policy Distillation

The student policy learns to replicate teacher behavior using only proprioceptive observations available on real robots:

- **Historical Observations**: Joint positions, velocities, and IMU data from recent timesteps
- **Teacher Supervision**: Distillation losses to match teacher policy outputs
- **Reality-Compatible Sensors**: Only information available on physical robots

#### 3.8. Decoupled Reward Design

SMAP employs a carefully designed reward function that balances motion tracking accuracy with stability requirements:

```
R = w₁R_velocity + w₂R_direction + w₃R_orientation + w₄R_keypoint + w₅R_joint + R_stability
```

The reward components are weighted to emphasize upper body precision for task execution while allowing lower body adaptation for balance maintenance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Motion Adaptation Results

The visualization demonstrates how the Humanoid-Adapter transforms potentially unstable retargeted human motions into stable, executable robot motions while preserving the essential characteristics of the original movement.

#### 4.2. Simulation Performance

The SMAP framework demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods in simulation environments:

The results show that SMAP achieves:
- **Faster Convergence**: Reaches target performance levels earlier in training
- **Higher Final Performance**: Superior tracking accuracy in both position and keypoint metrics
- **Improved Stability**: Better maintenance of balance during complex motions

#### 4.3. Motion Quality Analysis

The framework enables a wide range of human-like motions including:
- **Dance Movements**: Complex coordinated full-body sequences
- **Martial Arts**: Dynamic poses requiring precise balance
- **Expressive Gestures**: Upper body movements with stable lower body control

#### 4.4. Latent Space Analysis

The visualization reveals how the Humanoid-Adapter creates a structured latent space where similar motions cluster together, regardless of whether they originate from human or robot data. The adapted motions (green) serve as an effective bridge between the human and robot domains.

#### 4.5. Real-World Deployment

SMAP has been successfully deployed on a Unitree H1 humanoid robot, demonstrating the practical applicability of the framework. The real-world experiments validate the sim-to-real transfer capabilities and show robust performance across various motion types.

#### 4.6. Hardware Validation

The Unitree H1 robot features:
- **Full-body actuation**: 19 degrees of freedom enabling complex whole-body control
- **Onboard computing**: Real-time policy execution without external computation
- **Proprioceptive sensors**: IMU and joint encoders providing the observations used by the student policy

#### 4.7. Performance Metrics

Real-world deployment demonstrates:
- **Stable Execution**: Maintained balance across diverse motion sequences
- **Motion Fidelity**: Close correspondence between intended and executed motions
- **Robustness**: Consistent performance across multiple trials and environmental conditions

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Technical Contributions

The SMAP framework represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics by addressing several key challenges:

1. **Self-Supervised Motion Adaptation**: First framework to learn human-to-robot motion transfer without paired training data
2. **Scalable Training Pipeline**: Enables efficient use of large-scale human motion capture datasets
3. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Demonstrated successful deployment on physical hardware
4. **Generalizable Framework**: Applicable to different robot platforms and motion types

#### 5.2. Practical Applications

The framework opens possibilities for humanoid robots in:

- **Entertainment and Media**: Robots capable of performing choreographed dances and expressive movements
- **Healthcare and Therapy**: Assistive robots that can demonstrate human-like movements for rehabilitation
- **Manufacturing and Service**: Robots that can perform complex manipulation tasks requiring whole-body coordination
- **Research and Education**: Platforms for studying human movement and developing new control algorithms

#### 5.3. Future Directions

SMAP establishes a foundation for several research directions:

- **Multi-Modal Integration**: Incorporating visual and audio cues to enhance motion adaptation
- **Interactive Control**: Enabling real-time modification of robot movements based on environmental feedback
- **Personalized Adaptation**: Learning individual human movement styles for more natural robot behavior
- **Cross-Platform Transfer**: Adapting the framework to different robot morphologies and capabilities

The SMAP framework demonstrates that the gap between human motion capture and robot control can be effectively bridged through principled machine learning approaches. By combining self-supervised learning with progressive policy training, it provides a practical pathway for deploying natural, human-like movements on real humanoid robots while maintaining the stability and safety requirements necessary for real-world applications.
