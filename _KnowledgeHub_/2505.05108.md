# Multi-agent Embodied AI: Advances and Future Directions

> [!summary] Summary
> This comprehensive survey consolidates the state-of-the-art in multi-agent embodied AI, meticulously analyzing advancements, challenges, and future directions for systems that perceive and interact with physical environments. It highlights the shift from single-agent to multi-agent complexities and the transformative role of generative models in enabling complex coordination and distributed decision-making.

## Problem

- Most existing embodied AI research focuses on single-agent systems in static or simplified environments, diverging from the complexities of real-world multi-agent interactions.
- Multi-agent settings introduce substantial complexities such as exponential problem complexity, partial observability, non-stationarity, and difficult credit assignment.
- There was an absence of a comprehensive, systematic survey that meticulously reviews advancements, challenges, and future directions specifically within the multi-agent embodied AI domain.

## Method

- The paper provides the first systematic and comprehensive review of ==multi-agent embodied AI==, consolidating disparate knowledge and clarifying key concepts.
- It meticulously categorizes existing methodologies across ==classic control==, ==learning-based approaches==, and ==generative model integration== for both single- and multi-agent embodied systems.
- The authors identify and articulate the major theoretical and practical challenges, proposing concrete future research directions across various dimensions of the field.

## Results

- The survey confirms that embodied AI is transitioning from single-agent exploration to multi-agent mastery, a shift critically dependent on overcoming complex interaction dynamics.
- It documents the profound impact of generative models on multi-agent embodied AI, enabling advancements in explicit task allocation, distributed decision-making among LLM-based agents, and enhanced human-AI coordination.
- The paper highlights that multi-agent embodied AI benchmarks are still nascent, often targeting specialized tasks and making idealized assumptions, revealing a persistent gap between simulation and real-world deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-agent embodied AI requires sophisticated mechanisms for real-time learning, dynamic adaptation, and complex collaborative problem-solving, extending beyond single-agent paradigms.
> - Generative models, particularly large language and vision models, are becoming instrumental in enabling complex multi-agent behaviors, including task allocation, distributed decision-making, and human-AI coordination.
> - Overcoming challenges like asynchronous decision-making, heterogeneous team composition, and self-evolution in open environments is critical for advancing multi-agent embodied systems towards real-world applicability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence (AI) is rapidly advancing, with Embodied AI emerging as a particularly pivotal area. Embodied AI focuses on integrating AI systems with physical bodies, enabling them to perceive, reason, and interact dynamically with their environments using sensors and actuators. Driven by significant advancements in deep learning (DL), reinforcement learning (RL), and large language models (LLMs), embodied AI has gained substantial traction in both academia and industry, finding applications across diverse sectors such as robotics, healthcare, transportation, and manufacturing.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in the existing research landscape. The vast majority of current embodied AI research has predominantly concentrated on **single-agent systems operating within static, closed, or simplified environments**. This narrow focus significantly diverges from the complexities of real-world scenarios, where intelligent agents must not only interact with their surroundings but also **collaborate with, compete against, or adapt to other agents, including humans**. Such multi-agent settings necessitate sophisticated mechanisms for real-time learning, dynamic adaptation, and complex collaborative problem-solving, introducing unique challenges like exponential complexity, partial observability, non-stationarity, and credit assignment difficulties.

Despite a growing interest in multi-agent systems, existing research in this specific intersection of multi-agent and embodied AI remains limited in scope, often relying on simplified models that fail to capture the full complexity of dynamic, open environments. More importantly, the authors highlight a glaring absence of a **comprehensive, systematic survey** that meticulously reviews the advancements, challenges, and future directions specifically within the domain of multi-agent embodied AI. While related surveys exist, such as those on embodied Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) or the integration of generative foundation models in embodied multi-agent systems, these are noted to address only specific dimensions of the broader field.

This paper directly addresses and aims to fill this critical void. By providing the first systematic and comprehensive review of multi-agent embodied AI, it positions itself as a foundational resource. It seeks to consolidate disparate knowledge, clarify key concepts, categorize methodologies, and identify overarching challenges and promising future research avenues. In doing so, this work serves as a crucial bridge, guiding researchers from the established paradigm of single-agent embodied AI towards the more complex, yet ultimately more realistic and impactful, realm of multi-agent embodied intelligence. It recognizes that for embodied AI to truly reach its potential in real-world applications, understanding and mastering multi-agent interactions is indispensable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this comprehensive survey stems from the recognized importance and current limitations within the field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI). Embodied AI is presented as a pivotal technology for the intelligent era, allowing AI systems to perceive, reason, and interact with the physical world, learning and adapting from real-world feedback. The maturation of foundational AI techniques, particularly deep learning (DL), reinforcement learning (RL), and large language models (LLMs), has propelled embodied AI into a leading research domain with widespread applications.

Despite this progress, the authors pinpoint a critical discrepancy: most current embodied AI research is confined to **single-agent systems operating in static, closed environments**. This contrasts sharply with the inherent complexity of real-world applications, which frequently demand **multi-agent interactions, collaboration, and adaptive problem-solving in dynamic and unpredictable settings**. These multi-agent scenarios introduce substantial challenges, including the exponential growth in problem complexity due to enlarged joint action spaces, the partial observability stemming from decentralized information, the non-stationarity arising from concurrent learning processes, and the complexities of accurately assigning credit for individual contributions.

Recognizing these challenges and the increasing interest in multi-agent systems, the authors observe that existing research in multi-agent embodied AI remains narrow, often relying on simplified models. Crucially, they identify a significant gap in the literature: **the absence of a comprehensive survey that systematically reviews the advancements in this area.**

Based on this compelling motivation, the key objectives of this paper are clearly articulated:

1. **Review the Current State of Research:** To systematically explore and document the latest developments in multi-agent embodied AI.
2. **Analyze Key Contributions:** To dissect and evaluate the significant methodological breakthroughs and innovative applications within the field.
3. **Identify Challenges:** To clearly delineate the unresolved theoretical and practical obstacles that impede further progress in multi-agent embodied AI.
4. **Identify Future Directions:** To propose promising research avenues and conceptual shifts necessary to address current limitations and advance the field.
5. **Provide Insights:** To offer a structured and informed perspective that can guide innovation and foster further development in this rapidly evolving domain.
6. **Fill the Gap:** Ultimately, the overarching objective is to provide the much-needed comprehensive overview that consolidates knowledge, highlights critical areas for development, and stimulates further research in multi-agent embodied AI.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to deepen the understanding of multi-agent embodied AI, making it more accessible and providing a foundational reference for researchers working towards real-world, highly interactive AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology of "Multi-Agent Embodied AI: Advances and Future Directions" is rooted in a structured and systematic review of the existing literature. The authors adopt a layered approach, beginning with foundational concepts, progressing through single-agent advancements, then dedicating significant attention to multi-agent specific challenges and solutions, and finally outlining future research directions. This organizational strategy ensures that the reader builds a complete understanding from the ground up, recognizing how multi-agent complexities extend from single-agent paradigms.

The paper's approach can be broken down into several key methodological components:

1. **Foundational Preliminaries (Section 2):** The survey begins by establishing a common vocabulary and theoretical background. This includes a formal definition of Embodied AI, emphasizing its attributes of embodiment, interactivity, and intelligence improvement. It then defines Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) and introduces essential underlying techniques such as Optimal Control (OC), Reinforcement Learning (RL), Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), Hierarchical Learning, Imitation Learning (IL), and various Generative Models (Transformers, Diffusion Models, State Space Models). This provides readers with the necessary context and technical understanding before diving into specific applications.

2. **Structured Review of Single-Agent Embodied AI (Section 3):** To provide a baseline and highlight the evolution of the field, the authors first review mainstream research in single-agent embodied AI. They categorize existing approaches into:
- **Classic Control and Planning:** Discussing knowledge-driven, task-specific methods like constraint-based, sampling-based (e.g., RRT), and optimization-based (e.g., MPC, OC) approaches, acknowledging their strengths and limitations.
- **Learning-based Methods:** Covering paradigms such as end-to-end RL, hierarchical RL (integrating high-level planning with low-level control), and imitation learning (behavior cloning, inverse reinforcement learning, generative adversarial imitation learning) as solutions to overcome the limitations of classic methods.
- **Generative Model-based Methods:** Analyzing the impactful role of large generative models (LLMs, VLMs) in enhancing single-agent embodied intelligence across functions like end-to-end control, task planning, perception, reward design, and data-efficient learning.
- **Benchmarks:** A dedicated subsection extensively lists and describes representative benchmarks for single-agent embodied AI, providing detailed information on their characteristics (input views, data types, agent types, and evaluation metrics). This standardization is vital for reproducible research.

3. **In-depth Analysis of Multi-Agent Embodied AI (Section 4):** This section forms the core of the survey, extending the discussion from single-agent to multi-agent contexts. The authors systematically review advancements by categorizing methods based on their underlying paradigms:
- **Multi-agent Control and Planning:** Examining extensions of classic control techniques for multi-agent coordination, including centralized, distributed, and grouped control strategies.
- **Learning for Multi-agent Interaction:** Delving into learning-based approaches that address unique multi-agent challenges such as asynchronous decision-making, heterogeneous team composition, and self-evolution in open environments. This highlights how MARL principles are adapted.
- **Generative Models-based Multi-agent Interaction:** A crucial section emphasizing the transformative impact of generative models in facilitating complex multi-agent behaviors, including explicit task allocation, distributed decision-making among multiple LLM-based agents, human-AI coordination, and data-efficient multi-agent learning through world models.
- **Multi-agent Benchmarks:** Similar to the single-agent section, a comprehensive review of specialized benchmarks designed for multi-agent embodied AI is provided, highlighting their scope and limitations.

4. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions (Section 5):** The authors synthesize the insights from their review to pinpoint major challenges and propose concrete future research directions across several key dimensions: theoretical foundations, new algorithm design, learning efficiency, generative model integration, generalist multi-agent frameworks, adaptation to open environments, and evaluation/verification. This forward-looking perspective transforms the survey from a mere review into a strategic roadmap for the field.

Throughout the paper, the authors leverage a vast and highly current bibliography, including numerous arXiv preprints from as late as April-May 2025. This commitment to incorporating the most recent developments ensures the survey's relevance and timeliness in a rapidly evolving field. The use of clear figures (e.g., Figure 1 for the survey overview, Figure 2 for Embodied AI attributes, figures illustrating various methods and benchmarks) and structured tables aids comprehension and comparison of diverse methodologies. This systematic and comprehensive approach allows the survey to fulfill its objective of providing an integrated perspective on multi-agent embodied AI, identifying both achievements and critical research gaps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, the "main findings and results" of this paper are the structured overview and critical analysis of the current state of Multi-Agent Embodied AI, revealing key advancements, overarching trends, and persistent challenges. The authors systematically dissect the field, highlighting significant progress while rigorously identifying the limitations that necessitate future research.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Foundational Understanding of Embodied AI:** The survey firmly establishes Embodied AI as a pivotal interdisciplinary field, defining its core attributes: physical embodiment, continuous interactivity with the environment, and the capacity for intelligence improvement through learning and adaptation. It traces the conceptual origins from Turing and Brooks, emphasizing the shift from symbolic AI to active environmental engagement.

2. **Evolution of Single-Agent Embodied AI:** The paper details the progression of single-agent embodied AI, showcasing a transition from:
- **Classic Control and Planning:** Methods like RRT, MPC, and Optimal Control provide high precision but suffer from computational intensity, limited scalability, and poor generalization in dynamic, unstructured environments.
- **Learning-based Approaches:** End-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Hierarchical RL emerge as solutions for adaptability and generalization by directly mapping sensory inputs to actions or by decomposing complex tasks. Imitation Learning (IL) offers a practical alternative by leveraging expert demonstrations, circumventing the need for explicit reward design.
- **Generative Model Integration:** A significant finding is the increasing and transformative role of large generative models (LLMs, VLMs) in single-agent scenarios. They enhance capabilities in direct end-to-end control, high-level task planning, environmental perception (e.g., semantic understanding, multi-modal fusion), automated reward design, and crucial data-efficient learning through world models and diverse environment generation.

3. **Unique Challenges and Solutions in Multi-Agent Embodied AI:** The survey explicitly identifies that directly transferring single-agent methods to multi-agent settings is inefficient due to inherent complexities. It highlights three major multi-agent challenges and the emerging solutions:
- **Asynchronous Decision-Making:** Real-world delays and hardware heterogeneity necessitate approaches that account for non-synchronized interactions, such as macro-actions and adapted MARL algorithms (e.g., macro-action-based MAPPO).
- **Heterogeneous Team Composition:** Variations in agent capabilities, action spaces, and objectives demand specialized strategies like separate policy networks connected via graph-based communication or decomposed advantage functions for effective credit assignment.
- **Self-Evolution in Open Environments:** The dynamic, unpredictable nature of real-world settings requires robust training, continual coordination, and architectures (e.g., GNNs, Transformers) that adapt to changing team sizes and interaction patterns, often incorporating self-play for continuous improvement.

4. **Generative Models as a Catalyst for Multi-Agent Collaboration:** A central finding is the profound impact of generative models on multi-agent embodied AI, enabling previously difficult levels of coordination and intelligence:
- **Multi-Agent Task Allocation:** Generative models leverage prior knowledge and reasoning to explicitly decompose complex tasks into sub-tasks and assign them to agents, significantly reducing individual exploration spaces. This includes handling sub-task dependencies.
- **Multi-Agent Distributed Decision Making:** The advanced perception and reasoning of LLMs make it feasible for multiple LLM-based agents to engage in decentralized collaboration, communicating to complete observations or request assistance. Centralized critics or reflectors are used to facilitate credit assignment and policy evaluation in these distributed systems.
- **Human-AI Coordination:** Generative models, particularly LLMs, significantly improve human-agent collaboration by facilitating natural language understanding and generation, enabling agents to infer human intentions, seek clarifications, and adapt their behavior for more intuitive teamwork.
- **Data-Efficient Multi-Agent Learning:** Generative models are crucial for overcoming the high sample inefficiency in multi-agent settings by enabling the creation of multi-agent world models, generating synthetic data, and simulating diverse environments for policy learning.

5. **Benchmarking Gaps:** While progress in single-agent embodied AI benchmarks is noted, the survey reveals that multi-agent embodied AI benchmarks are still relatively nascent. They often target specialized tasks, lack broad applicability, and make idealized assumptions (e.g., ignoring partial observability, communication latency), leading to a significant sim-to-real gap. The paper provides an extensive list of existing multi-agent benchmarks, highlighting the need for more robust, versatile, and ecologically valid evaluation frameworks.

In essence, the paper confirms that Embodied AI is transitioning from single-agent exploration to multi-agent mastery, a shift critically dependent on overcoming complex interaction dynamics and, increasingly, on the integration of powerful generative models. The survey's core contribution is its structured articulation of this transition, the challenges it presents, and the diverse methodological responses emerging from the research community.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper, "Multi-Agent Embodied AI: Advances and Future Directions," carries significant implications for the future trajectory of AI and robotics. Its impact stems from its timely, comprehensive, and forward-looking analysis of a rapidly evolving and increasingly critical sub-field.

1. **Consolidating Disparate Knowledge:** The most immediate and profound significance of this work is its role in synthesizing and structuring the fragmented knowledge base surrounding multi-agent embodied AI. By bringing together diverse research streams—from classic control theory and reinforcement learning to the burgeoning influence of large generative models—the survey provides a unified conceptual framework. This is invaluable for new researchers entering the field, offering a clear entry point and a structured understanding of its complexities. For seasoned researchers, it provides a panoramic view, fostering cross-pollination of ideas and identifying synergistic research avenues that might otherwise be overlooked.

2. **Highlighting Critical Gaps and Challenges:** The paper meticulously identifies and articulates the key theoretical and practical challenges hindering the widespread deployment of multi-agent embodied AI. These include the fundamental theoretical gaps in understanding complex agent interactions, the need for new algorithmic designs beyond traditional centralized training paradigms, the pervasive issue of sample inefficiency in real-world settings, the difficulties in effectively integrating large generative models, the absence of truly generalist multi-agent AI frameworks, the challenges of adapting to open and dynamic environments, and the critical lack of robust, standardized evaluation and verification methodologies. By clearly delineating these bottlenecks, the survey provides a precise agenda for future research, directing efforts towards the most impactful problems.

3. **Charting Future Research Directions:** Beyond merely listing challenges, the paper offers insightful perspectives and concrete future directions for addressing them. This roadmap for innovation covers crucial areas such as developing new theoretical paradigms (e.g., causal inference, complex systems theory), designing novel algorithms (e.g., hierarchical coordination, agent-grouping mechanisms), enhancing learning efficiency (e.g., multi-agent world models, meta-learning, IL), strategically integrating large generative models (e.g., scalable multi-agent pretraining, RL-GM fusion), pursuing generalist multi-agent architectures, and prioritizing robust evaluation frameworks. This forward-looking guidance is essential for shaping the research agenda and accelerating progress in the field.

4. **Emphasizing the Role of Generative Models:** A particularly significant contribution is the detailed exploration of how large generative models (LLMs, VLMs) are becoming indispensable for multi-agent embodied AI. By demonstrating their utility across task allocation, distributed decision-making, human-AI coordination, and data efficiency, the survey underscores a major paradigm shift. This highlights that future breakthroughs will likely involve deeper integration of these powerful models, pushing the boundaries of what embodied agents can achieve in complex, interactive settings.

5. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap and Real-World Applicability:** The paper implicitly and explicitly emphasizes the persistent "sim-to-real gap" and the need for research that translates effectively to real-world applications. By discussing practical challenges like sensor noise, communication delays, and hardware heterogeneity, and by reviewing benchmarks that aim to bridge this gap, the survey encourages research with tangible real-world impact. Its discussion on applications in robotics (manufacturing, logistics, autonomous driving), healthcare, education, and smart cities further underscores the immense potential societal benefits that advancements in this field could bring.

6. **Promoting Standardized Evaluation and Ethical Considerations:** The detailed review of benchmarks and the call for more comprehensive evaluation frameworks are critical for establishing rigorous scientific standards. Furthermore, the explicit mention of safety and ethical considerations (e.g., transparent explainability, minimizing risks in sensitive environments) is crucial for responsible development and deployment of increasingly autonomous multi-agent systems.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a review; it is a critical analysis and a strategic compass for the emerging field of multi-agent embodied AI. By systematically summarizing current advancements, articulating core challenges, and delineating promising future directions, it empowers researchers to pursue higher levels of embodied intelligence that are capable of robust, adaptive, and collaborative behavior in the complex and dynamic real world. This will undoubtedly stimulate significant innovation and accelerate the transition of embodied AI from specialized laboratory settings to widespread practical applications.
