# Model-Based Reinforcement Learning for Atari

> [!summary] Summary
> Simulated Policy Learning (SimPLe) is an iterative model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that achieves superior sample efficiency on Atari games by training a policy within a learned stochastic world model. The approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art model-free baselines like Rainbow and PPO, sometimes by over 10 times, under a strict budget of 100,000 real-environment interactions, addressing a long-standing challenge in model-based RL on high-dimensional visual inputs.

## Problem

- Model-free deep reinforcement learning algorithms require vast amounts of environmental interactions (tens to hundreds of millions of steps), making them impractical for real-world applications where data is costly or risky.
- A long-standing challenge in model-based RL has been the lack of clear demonstrations of successful planning with learned models on complex, high-dimensional visual environments like Atari that are competitive with model-free approaches.
- Bridging the significant gap in sample efficiency between human learning (minutes) and AI agents (weeks of play) for visual control tasks.

## Method

- ==Simulated Policy Learning== (**SimPLe**) employs an iterative approach that alternates between collecting data from the real environment, training a neural network 'world model' on this data, and then training a policy extensively within the learned world model using a model-free RL algorithm (PPO).
- A novel stochastic video prediction model with discrete latent variables serves as the world model, incorporating techniques like clipped loss to focus on dynamic elements and scheduled sampling to combat compounding errors in long predictions.
- Policy training within the learned model utilizes short rollouts (e.g., 50 steps), critically starting each rollout from a state uniformly sampled from the real-environment data buffer to ground training in actual experience and mitigate compounding model errors.

## Results

- SimPLe consistently demonstrated superior sample efficiency, outperforming Rainbow and PPO on over half of 26 Atari games under a 100,000 real-environment interaction budget, with up to a **10x** improvement on games like Freeway.
- Ablation studies confirmed the stochastic discrete world model's superiority over deterministic or recurrent variants, and validated the critical role of iterative training with random starting states for simulated rollouts.
- The method showed robustness to artificially introduced environmental stochasticity (sticky actions) and excelled in low-data regimes, though its sample efficiency advantage diminished at much larger data budgets where model-free methods could catch up.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a sufficiently accurate stochastic world model with discrete latent variables is crucial for enabling effective model-based control in complex visual environments like Atari.
> - The iterative process of alternating between model learning and policy optimization, combined with grounding simulated rollouts in real experience (random starts from data buffer), is essential for robustness and performance.
> - Even with 'benign errors' (non-physical but harmless predictions), a learned world model can still be effective for training policies, indicating a degree of robustness to model inaccuracies.
