# Rethinking Reasoning in Document Ranking: Why Chain-of-Thought Falls Short

> [!summary] Summary
> Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning consistently degrades performance and increases inference costs in document reranking across pointwise and listwise paradigms, challenging its assumed benefits in this domain. Direct-output rerankers built on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B consistently surpassed their CoT-augmented counterparts, achieving up to +9.0 NDCG@10 higher for pointwise models on BRIGHT and competitive or superior state-of-the-art results.

## Problem

- A prevalent assumption that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is beneficial for Large Language Models (LLMs) in document reranking, often without rigorous comparative studies.
- Prior work on reasoning-augmented rerankers often lacks fair comparisons against non-reasoning baselines, leading to an unexamined implicit assumption.
- Limited understanding of CoT's role in listwise reranking or under reinforcement learning training paradigms.

## Method

- Developed a systematic and controlled experimental framework comparing four LLM-based ==reranker== variants: Direct-Point, Reason-Point, Direct-List, and Reason-List.
- Utilized Qwen3-4B/8B backbone models, trained with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for pointwise models and a two-stage SFT + GRPO (Generalized Reweighted Policy Optimization) approach for listwise models.
- Evaluated performance on diverse benchmarks (BRIGHT, BEIR) using NDCG@10, alongside analysis of calibration and rationale characteristics.

## Results

- Direct-output rerankers consistently outperformed reasoning-augmented counterparts across all settings, model sizes, and benchmarks, with Direct-Point-**4B** surpassing Reason-Point-**4B** by +9.0 NDCG@10 on BRIGHT.
- Reasoning models exhibited broken calibration (higher Expected Calibration Error) and a positive class bias, while Direct-Point models maintained better calibration and effective handling of non-relevant documents.
- Listwise reasoning models fit the training data better but exhibited impaired generalization, with GRPO-trained models still being outperformed by direct counterparts despite shortened rationales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit reasoning disrupts confidence calibration in pointwise rerankers, leading to systematic overconfidence and a detrimental "true" class bias in information retrieval contexts.
> - For listwise rerankers, CoT allows for better training set fitting but impairs generalization to new, out-of-domain data, even when reinforcement learning (GRPO) shortens rationales.
> - The "reasoning" beneficial for complex LLM tasks does not directly translate to improved performance or utility in the specialized task of document reranking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Document reranking is a critical component in modern information retrieval (IR) systems, acting as a second stage to refine initial, coarsely-ranked results from a first-stage retriever. Its primary goal is to improve the precision and overall quality of search results, which is vital for downstream applications like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and recommendation systems. The field has seen a significant shift with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs), which have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in reranking tasks, often by combining targeted prompts with task-specific fine-tuning.

A more recent development has been the emergence of "Large Reasoning Models" (LRMs), such as DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1, which are designed to explicitly generate step-by-step reasoning chains (often referred to as Chain-of-Thought, or CoT) before producing a final prediction. This explicit reasoning process has shown promise in improving performance across various LLM tasks by bridging the gap between input and output and smoothing token-by-token transitions. Consequently, the IR community has begun exploring the integration of CoT reasoning into LLM-based rerankers, with several recent studies incorporating explicit reasoning through supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. These works generally *assume* that CoT reasoning is beneficial for reranking, drawing parallels to its success in other complex reasoning tasks.

This paper critically intervenes in this evolving landscape by questioning the fundamental assumption that explicit reasoning is universally advantageous for reranking. It highlights a significant gap: prior work often lacks fair comparisons against non-reasoning baselines, and emerging evidence of "overthinking" in reasoning-augmented rerankers (e.g., Jedidi et al., 2025; Fan et al., 2025) has been limited in scope. This research therefore presents the first systematic and comprehensive study designed to rigorously evaluate the actual utility of explicit reasoning in reranking across diverse settings and paradigms. It aims to provide a definitive answer to whether the observed benefits of CoT in other LLM tasks translate effectively to the specialized domain of document reranking, thereby informing future research directions and practical system designs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to conduct the first systematic and comprehensive investigation into the effectiveness of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in document reranking. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Challenge an Unexamined Assumption**: Recent research in LLM-based reranking has increasingly incorporated explicit reasoning, often implicitly assuming its benefit based on successes in other LLM tasks. However, the authors note that these claims are rarely substantiated by fair, controlled comparisons against direct, non-reasoning baselines. This paper aims to directly test this assumption.
2. **Address Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing analyses questioning the utility of reasoning in reranking are limited in scope, typically focusing only on pointwise rerankers trained with supervised objectives. There is a lack of understanding regarding reasoning's role in listwise reranking or under different training paradigms like reinforcement learning.
3. **Provide a Rigorous and Comparable Study**: The authors observe inconsistencies across prior work regarding experimental design, making apples-to-apples comparisons difficult. A core motivation is to establish a unified experimental framework that allows for a clean and rigorous comparison of reasoning-augmented versus direct rerankers across different model types, training strategies, and benchmarks.
4. **Gain Deeper Insights into Failure Modes**: Beyond simply determining if reasoning helps, the research aims to uncover *why* explicit reasoning might fail or even degrade performance in reranking. This involves in-depth analysis of phenomena such as calibration errors, class biases, and generalization capabilities.
5. **Guide Future Research and Development**: By systematically analyzing the limitations of CoT in reranking, the study seeks to highlight more promising future directions for developing effective and efficient LLM-based rerankers, moving beyond potentially detrimental reasoning strategies.

In essence, the paper is motivated by a critical inquiry into the true efficacy of a popular LLM paradigm (CoT) within a specialized IR task (reranking), aiming to provide clarity and foundational insights for the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors design a rigorous, systematic, and controlled experimental setup to fairly compare reasoning-augmented rerankers against their direct-output counterparts.

#### 3.1. Model Variants and Paradigms

The study defines four core LLM-based reranker variants, covering the two primary reranking paradigms and two reasoning approaches:
- **Direct-Point (Non-Reasoning Pointwise)**: Directly outputs a binary relevance decision (TRUE/FALSE) for each query-document pair, with scores derived from answer token logits.
- **Reason-Point (Reasoning Pointwise)**: First generates an explicit reasoning trace (Chain-of-Thought) and then produces the binary relevance decision.
- **Direct-List (Non-Reasoning Listwise)**: Takes an entire candidate list as input and directly generates a ranked permutation of documents.
- **Reason-List (Reasoning Listwise)**: First generates a global reasoning trace that compares candidates, then outputs the final ranked list.

#### 3.2. Backbone Models

All rerankers are built upon the **Qwen3 series**, specifically Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B. This choice ensures consistency with prevailing practices in the reranking community and allows for direct comparison of reasoning strategies across different model scales.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- **Pointwise Rerankers (Direct-Point, Reason-Point)**:
 - Trained on the **RANK1 corpus** (derived from MS MARCO), containing approximately 386k query-passage pairs annotated by DeepSeek-R1 with rationales and binary answers.
 - **Reason-Point**: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on `⟨query, passage, rationale, answer⟩` quadruples.
 - **Direct-Point**: SFT on `⟨query, passage, answer⟩` triples, ablating the rationale.
 - Both minimize cross-entropy loss, trained for one epoch using LLaMA-Factory with LoRA (rank 32, α=64, learning rate 1e-4). Relevance scores are computed as the probability of the TRUE token via a two-way softmax.
- **Listwise Rerankers (Direct-List, Reason-List)**:
 - Trained on the **REASONRANK training corpus**, comprising about 13k query-candidate sets from MS MARCO, including rationales and gold ranking orders.
 - **Two-stage training**:
 - **Stage 1 (SFT)**: Teaches models to output ranking sequences. Prompts enforce structural validity with `<think> ... </think>` for reasoning (even if empty for Direct-List) and `<answer> ... </answer>` for the ranking.
 - **Stage 2 (RL with GRPO)**: Refines the SFT policy using Generalized Reweighted Policy Optimization (GRPO), following the ReasonRank (Liu et al., 2025) setup. A composite multi-view ranking reward is used, combining NDCG@10, Recall@10, and Rank-Biased Overlap (RBO), gated by format validators to stabilize learning. GRPO uses a clipped objective with a KL divergence penalty, implemented with Verl for one epoch.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- All rerankers operate on a fixed initial candidate pool of **k=100 passages per query**, generated by BM25, reflecting a standard two-stage IR architecture.
- Hardware: Two NVIDIA A100 (80 GB) GPUs.

#### 3.5. Baselines

Comparison is made against state-of-the-art reasoning-enhanced LLM rerankers from prior work, including:
- **Pointwise**: Rank1-7B/14B, TF-Rank-4B/8B.
- **Listwise**: Rank-R1-7B/14B, REARank-7B, ReasonRank-7B.

#### 3.6. Benchmarks and Metrics

- **Benchmarks**:
 - **BRIGHT**: A reasoning-intensive IR suite spanning diverse domains, including original and GPT-4 generated "reason queries."
 - **BEIR**: A standard heterogeneous suite of retrieval datasets.
- **Primary Metric**: **NDCG@10** (Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain at 10), which measures both relevance and position sensitivity for the top results.
- **Auxiliary Metrics (for analysis)**: Expected Calibration Error (ECE), True Positive Rate (TPR), True Negative Rate (TNR) for pointwise models; analysis of rationale length for listwise models.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that the findings are robust, generalizable across different reranking paradigms and training strategies, and directly comparable to existing literature.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic study yields striking and consistent results, challenging the prevailing assumption about the benefits of explicit reasoning in document reranking:

1. **Reasoning is Unnecessary and Often Detrimental for Reranking**:
 - Across all experimental settings (SFT and SFT+GRPO training), model sizes (4B/8B), and benchmarks (BRIGHT and BEIR), **direct-output rerankers consistently outperform their reasoning-augmented counterparts.**
 - **Pointwise**: On BRIGHT, Direct-Point-4B surpassed Reason-Point-4B by +9.0 NDCG@10, and Direct-Point-8B by +6.1. Similar gaps (+4.3 to +5.3) were observed on BEIR.
 - **Listwise**: The advantage for direct models was smaller but stable, ranging from +0.4 to +1.7 NDCG@10 on BRIGHT and +0.3 to +1.9 on BEIR. This held true even after GRPO training, which generally improved performance for both.
 - **Inference Cost**: Reasoning-augmented models incurred substantially higher inference costs due to generating explicit rationales, making their underperformance even more pronounced.
 - **State-of-the-Art**: Direct rerankers not only outperformed reasoning variants but also surpassed many existing reasoning-enhanced LLM rerankers, achieving competitive or superior results on both BRIGHT (e.g., Direct-List-8B at 27.1 NDCG@10) and BEIR (e.g., Direct-Point-4B at 45.4 NDCG@10).

2. **Pointwise Rerankers: Calibration Failure and True Bias with Reasoning**:
 - **Broken Calibration**: Explicit reasoning in pointwise rerankers disrupts score calibration. Direct-Point-8B maintained a clearer monotonic relationship between confidence and accuracy (ECE = 0.106), while Reason-Point-8B exhibited systematic overconfidence and larger departures from ideal calibration (ECE = 0.141). This miscalibration contributes to degraded ranking quality.
 - **"True" Proclivity**: Class-conditional analysis revealed that reasoning models consistently shift towards predicting the positive class. While they might achieve higher macro binary accuracy, this gain often stems from a higher True Positive Rate (TPR) coupled with a lower True Negative Rate (TNR). In information retrieval, where non-relevant documents (negatives) dominate candidate pools, an elevated False Positive Rate (due to low TNR) is highly detrimental, pushing irrelevant documents to the top of the ranked list.

3. **Listwise Rerankers: Improved Training Fit but Impaired Generalization**:
 - **Training Fit vs. Generalization**: Reasoning models showed a higher mean NDCG@10 on the *training split* (e.g., Reason-List SFT 8B: 82.57 ± 3.2 vs. Direct-List SFT 8B: 80.41 ± 2.1), but with markedly larger dispersion (higher variance). This indicates that CoT can better fit in-sample permutations but introduces instability.
 - **Out-of-Domain Performance**: This training-split advantage did *not* translate to stronger in-domain (MS MARCO DL19/20) or out-of-domain (BRIGHT/BEIR) performance. Direct-List models consistently outperformed Reason-List models on these evaluation benchmarks. This suggests that the "fit" provided by reasoning is superficial, not leading to genuine generalization.
 - **GRPO's Role**: Reinforcement learning with GRPO significantly improved performance for listwise rerankers over SFT alone. Crucially, GRPO also **markedly shortened the rationales** produced by reasoning models (e.g., from 397.7 tokens in Reason-List SFT to 172.3 in Reason-List GRPO). This indicates that excessively long CoT rationales are not necessary and that GRPO implicitly incentivizes concise outputs. However, even with shortened rationales and improved stability from GRPO, Direct-List models still maintained stronger generalization.

In summary, the core finding is that explicit reasoning, while beneficial in many LLM tasks, imposes significant performance penalties and increased inference costs in document reranking, primarily due to calibration issues in pointwise settings and poor generalization/increased variance in listwise settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the design and deployment of LLM-based information retrieval systems, challenging a prevalent assumption and offering clear guidance for future work.

1. **Re-evaluation of Chain-of-Thought in IR**: The most significant impact is its direct challenge to the widespread assumption that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is universally beneficial for LLMs, particularly in specialized tasks like document reranking. By demonstrating consistent underperformance and specific failure modes, the paper compels the IR community to critically re-evaluate the utility of explicit reasoning in this domain. This could lead to a shift away from blindly incorporating CoT and towards more targeted, evidence-based approaches.
2. **Emphasis on Efficiency and Directness**: The findings strongly advocate for efficient, direct-output reranking methods. Given that reasoning-augmented models incur substantially higher inference costs without offering performance gains (and often degrading performance), this research provides a strong practical argument for prioritizing simpler, direct models for real-world IR systems where latency and throughput are critical.
3. **Deeper Understanding of LLM Limitations**: The detailed analysis of *why* reasoning fails—specifically, calibration errors, positive class bias in pointwise models, and poor out-of-domain generalization in listwise models—offers valuable insights into the limitations of LLMs beyond just performance metrics. This contributes to a more nuanced understanding of how LLMs interact with task-specific structures and data distributions.
4. **Guidance for Future Research Directions**: The paper does not simply dismiss reasoning but offers concrete directions for future research:
 - **Calibration-aware Scoring**: For pointwise rerankers, the identified calibration issues suggest a need for training objectives or post-processing techniques that explicitly promote calibrated confidence scores, rather than just binary accuracy.
 - **Concise and Targeted Reasoning Strategies**: For listwise rerankers, the observation that GRPO shortens rationales while improving performance indicates that "overthinking" is detrimental. Future work should focus on designing reasoning strategies that are highly concise, targeted, and perhaps implicitly integrated, rather than verbose and explicit, to mitigate overfitting and improve generalization. This might involve different prompt engineering techniques or architectural modifications that encourage focused deliberation without extensive token generation.
 - **Domain Specificity of Reasoning**: The results imply that the "reasoning" required for document reranking might be fundamentally different from the reasoning beneficial in other LLM tasks (e.g., complex problem-solving). Future research could explore what *constitutes* effective "reasoning" for IR.
5. **Impact on LLM Benchmarking**: The work highlights the importance of fair, controlled baselines in LLM research. Future benchmarking efforts for LLM-based IR should ensure that direct-output models are always rigorously compared against reasoning-augmented variants to accurately assess progress and identify genuinely beneficial innovations.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust, evidence-based critique of Chain-of-Thought reasoning in document reranking, moving the field towards more efficient, effective, and principled LLM-based IR system design.
