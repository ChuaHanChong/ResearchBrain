# Rethinking Reasoning in Document Ranking: Why Chain-of-Thought Falls Short

> [!summary] Summary
> Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning consistently degrades performance and increases inference costs in document reranking across pointwise and listwise paradigms, challenging its assumed benefits in this domain. Direct-output rerankers built on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B consistently surpassed their CoT-augmented counterparts, achieving up to +9.0 NDCG@10 higher for pointwise models on BRIGHT and competitive or superior state-of-the-art results.

## Problem

- A prevalent assumption that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is beneficial for Large Language Models (LLMs) in document reranking, often without rigorous comparative studies.
- Prior work on reasoning-augmented rerankers often lacks fair comparisons against non-reasoning baselines, leading to an unexamined implicit assumption.
- Limited understanding of CoT's role in listwise reranking or under reinforcement learning training paradigms.

## Method

- Developed a systematic and controlled experimental framework comparing four LLM-based ==reranker== variants: Direct-Point, Reason-Point, Direct-List, and Reason-List.
- Utilized Qwen3-4B/8B backbone models, trained with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for pointwise models and a two-stage SFT + GRPO (Generalized Reweighted Policy Optimization) approach for listwise models.
- Evaluated performance on diverse benchmarks (BRIGHT, BEIR) using NDCG@10, alongside analysis of calibration and rationale characteristics.

## Results

- Direct-output rerankers consistently outperformed reasoning-augmented counterparts across all settings, model sizes, and benchmarks, with Direct-Point-**4B** surpassing Reason-Point-**4B** by +9.0 NDCG@10 on BRIGHT.
- Reasoning models exhibited broken calibration (higher Expected Calibration Error) and a positive class bias, while Direct-Point models maintained better calibration and effective handling of non-relevant documents.
- Listwise reasoning models fit the training data better but exhibited impaired generalization, with GRPO-trained models still being outperformed by direct counterparts despite shortened rationales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit reasoning disrupts confidence calibration in pointwise rerankers, leading to systematic overconfidence and a detrimental "true" class bias in information retrieval contexts.
> - For listwise rerankers, CoT allows for better training set fitting but impairs generalization to new, out-of-domain data, even when reinforcement learning (GRPO) shortens rationales.
> - The "reasoning" beneficial for complex LLM tasks does not directly translate to improved performance or utility in the specialized task of document reranking.
