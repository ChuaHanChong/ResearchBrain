# PhysVLM: Enabling Visual Language Models to Understand Robotic Physical Reachability

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysVLM integrates robotic physical reachability into Visual Language Models (VLMs) using a novel Space-Physical Reachability Map (S-P Map) and a dual-branch architecture. The model achieves enhanced performance in embodied visual reasoning and task planning by enabling robots to consider physical constraints, outperforming baseline models on new benchmarks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) deployed in robotics often produce impractical or unsafe actions due to a lack of intrinsic understanding of a robot's physical reachability.
- Existing approaches for understanding physical constraints are often task-specific or focus on environmental modeling, lacking a unified, generalizable integration with VLMs for diverse robotic platforms.
- A significant gap exists in large-scale datasets that explicitly incorporate robotic physical parameters to train VLMs for physical reachability understanding.

## Method

- Proposed the ==Space-Physical Reachability Map== (S-P Map), a robot-agnostic, unified spatial representation that abstracts complex robot kinematics into a visually interpretable format.
- Developed **PhysVLM**, a dual-branch VLM architecture that processes egocentric RGB images and S-P Maps through separate encoders, integrating both visual and physical reachability features into a language decoder.
- Constructed Phys100K, a large-scale multi-robot dataset for physical reachability question-answering, and introduced EQA-phys, a new benchmark for evaluating physical reachability in embodied QA tasks.

## Results

- PhysVLM-3B achieved an average score of **71.0%** on the EQA-phys benchmark, outperforming GPT-4o by **14%** (**57.0%**) and other embodied VLMs by 17-**20%**.
- The model demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization, scoring over **63%** on real-world UR3 and XArm6 robots, indicating the S-P Map's effectiveness in transferring reachability understanding to unseen platforms.
- In real-world task planning scenarios with unreachable objects, PhysVLM-3B achieved a **48.4%** success rate, significantly outperforming baselines (**21.5%** to **35.8%**) by planning to approach initially unreachable objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The S-P Map effectively abstracts complex robot kinematics into a generalized, image-like representation, allowing VLMs to reason about physical reachability independently of specific robot configurations.
> - Integrating physical reachability information via a dedicated feature encoder significantly enhances a VLM's ability to generate feasible and safe robotic task plans, bridging the gap between high-level perception and low-level physical constraints.
> - The constructed Phys100K dataset and EQA-phys benchmark provide essential resources for training and evaluating models that require an understanding of physical reachability, driving advancements in embodied AI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence, particularly in robotics, has seen tremendous advancements with the advent of large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in environmental perception, visual question answering, and high-level scene understanding. However, a significant limitation persists when these powerful perception systems are deployed in embodied agents like robots: a fundamental disconnect between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical reality, specifically the robot's physical reachability.

Existing VLMs applied in robotics, such as those used for Embodied Question Answering (EQA) or robot task planning (e.g., SayCan, Code as Policies), often excel at identifying objects and generating plausible action sequences based on visual context. However, they frequently produce "inaccurate or impractical responses" (as illustrated in Figure 1) because they lack an intrinsic understanding of whether a robot can physically interact with an object or reach a specific location. These models typically operate under an implicit assumption that all objects are within the robot's operational area, leading to failed task executions or unsafe maneuvers when real-world physical constraints are violated.

Concurrently, research specifically focusing on "Understanding Physical Reachability" has explored various representations and methods. Traditional approaches include reachability maps, occupancy grids for obstacle avoidance, and advanced control techniques like model predictive control with workspace analysis. More recently, efforts have been made to integrate physical constraints with open-vocabulary perception models, such as using voxel grids with OWL-ViT and VLMs (e.g., ReKep, VoxPoser). While these methods address aspects of environmental or physical constraint understanding, they largely focus on environment modeling or specific task execution rather than a *unified, generalizable integration of physical reachability into the visual reasoning pipeline of VLMs for diverse robots*. A crucial gap identified is the lack of large-scale datasets that incorporate explicit robotic physical parameters for VLM pretraining.

PhysVLM directly addresses this critical gap by introducing a novel, unified representation of physical reachability (the S-P Map) that is **robot-agnostic**. By integrating this representation into a VLM architecture, the work aims to bridge the chasm between a VLM's powerful perceptual and linguistic abilities and the physical realities of robotic embodiment. This allows robots to not only "see" and "understand" the world but also to "reason" about their own physical limitations within that world, thereby making more intelligent, feasible, and safer decisions in task planning and execution. This research contributes significantly by pushing VLMs beyond purely cognitive reasoning towards more physically-grounded intelligence, a crucial step for robust embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to effectively understand and reason about robotic physical reachability**, thereby enhancing their performance in embodied visual reasoning and task execution.

The motivation for this work stems from a critical limitation observed in current state-of-the-art VLMs when applied to robotic tasks: despite their advanced capabilities in environmental perception and natural language understanding, they often fail to generate accurate or practical responses because they lack a grasp of a robot's physical constraints, particularly its reachability. As highlighted in the introduction, attempting to grasp an unreachable object can lead to task failure or even equipment damage.

To address this overarching problem, the authors identify two key challenges:

1. **Developing a unified and efficient representation of physical reachability:** Robots exhibit significant variations in size, joint configurations, and operational characteristics. Directly encoding these robot-specific parameters into a VLM is challenging and hinders generalization across diverse robotic platforms. A representation is needed that can abstract these differences into a common, interpretable format.
2. **Enabling VLMs to integrate physical reachability understanding without compromising general vision-language capabilities:** Introducing a new modality (physical reachability) into existing VLM architectures requires careful design. The integration must allow the model to reason effectively about physical constraints while retaining its broad vision-language understanding, rather than becoming overly specialized or losing its generalizability.

Based on these challenges, the authors set forth the following specific objectives:

- To propose the **Space-Physical Reachability Map (S-P Map)**, a novel and unified representation that abstracts the physical reachability of diverse robots into a generalized spatial form, independent of specific robot configurations.
- To introduce **PhysVLM**, a new vision-language model architecture that extends traditional VLMs by incorporating an additional feature encoder specifically designed to process the S-P Map, thereby integrating physical reachability information into the visual reasoning process.
- To construct a **large-scale multi-robot dataset, Phys100K**, tailored for training models to understand physical reachability.
- To establish a challenging new benchmark, **EQA-phys**, to rigorously evaluate a model's understanding of physical reachability in embodied QA tasks across various simulated and real-world robotic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysVLM is designed as a large-scale vision-language model that integrates physical constraints into visual reasoning for embodied tasks. Its core methodology revolves around a novel representation of physical reachability and a specialized VLM architecture.

#### 3.1. S-P Map Encoding (Unified Representation)

The Space-Physical Reachability Map (S-P Map) is a central innovation, providing a unified and robot-agnostic spatial representation of a robot's physical reachability. The encoding process involves both offline precomputation and real-time processing:

1. **Offline Precomputation of Reachable Workspace:**
 - **Inputs:** Robot-specific parameters like Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) parameters (describing joint geometry) and joint angle ranges (θ_min, θ_max).
 - **Forward Kinematics:** These parameters are used to calculate the homogeneous transformation matrices for each joint. By sampling joint angles within their ranges and applying forward kinematics, the end-effector positions are computed.
 - **Voxel Grid Generation:** The computed end-effector positions define the robot's reachable workspace, which is then discretized into a voxel grid (W_voxel) and stored for efficient lookup. This voxel grid represents *all physically reachable points* by the robot's end-effector, independent of the current scene.

2. **Real-time S-P Map Generation:**
 - **Input:** Raw point cloud data (P_raw) from the robot's egocentric RGB-D camera and the extrinsic calibration matrix (E) to transform coordinates from the camera to the robot's base frame.
 - **Coordinate Transformation:** P_raw is transformed into the robot's coordinate system (P) using E.
 - **Reachability Filtering:** Each point in P is checked against the precomputed W_voxel to determine if it falls within the robot's physical reach. This step yields P_valid, a filtered point cloud containing only the physically reachable points in the current scene.
 - **Image Plane Projection and Masking:** P_valid is transformed back to the camera coordinate system and projected onto the image plane using camera intrinsic parameters. Regions on the original depth map corresponding to reachable points are marked, while unreachable areas are overlaid with a gray mask and their boundaries outlined.
 - **Output:** The resulting S-P Map is an image-like representation that visually highlights reachable and unreachable areas in the egocentric view, thereby abstracting complex robot kinematics into a straightforward spatial constraint.

#### 3.2. PhysVLM Model Architecture

PhysVLM employs a dual-branch architecture to integrate visual and physical reachability information seamlessly:

1. **Vision Branch:**
 - Utilizes a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT), specifically SigLip-400M, to extract high-level visual features from the egocentric RGB image.
 - A Max Pooling layer reduces computational overhead, followed by a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to transform the visual features into token representations suitable for multimodal fusion.

2. **Physical Reachability Branch:**
 - Processes the generated S-P Map using a separate feature encoder, also based on the SigLip-400M architecture. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of using an independent encoder for S-P Maps, suggesting distinct feature requirements compared to general visual features.
 - Similar to the vision branch, it employs Max Pooling and a feature fusion layer, followed by a two-layer MLP to refine these features into reachability-specific tokens.

3. **Language Decoder:**
 - The Qwen-2.5-Instruct-3B model serves as the large language model (LLM) decoder.
 - It takes natural language instructions (processed by the Qwen-2.5 tokenizer), visual tokens from the vision branch, and reachability tokens from the S-P Map branch.
 - The decoder integrates these multimodal inputs to generate coherent, contextually relevant textual responses that consider both visual understanding and physical reachability constraints.

#### 3.3. Training Data Construction

To train and evaluate PhysVLM, the authors created the **Phys100K dataset** and the **EQA-phys benchmark**.

1. **Phys100K Dataset:**
 - A large-scale multi-robot dataset focused on physical reachability question-answering.
 - Aggregates data from diverse sources: RoboVQA (20K samples), ScanNet (10K samples), OpenX-Embodiment (60K samples), and 10K samples collected from PyBullet simulations.
 - **Depth Map Generation:** For datasets lacking depth information, DepthAnything-v2 is used to generate depth maps.
 - **Object Segmentation:** Grounding DINO and SAM2 are employed to obtain 2D bounding boxes and segmentation masks for objects.
 - **PyBullet Data Generation:** In PyBullet, detailed robot configurations (for UR5, FR5, CR5, FRANKA robotic arms) are available, allowing for precise S-P Map generation and ground-truth reachability labels (via simulated motion).
 - **Pseudo-labeling for Other Datasets:** For datasets without precise robot parameters, pseudo-labels for reachability are approximated using segmentation results and depth values, leveraging the S-P Map's region-based abstraction.
 - **Question-Answer Pair Generation:**
 - **Embodied QA:** GPT-4 is used to generate general QA pairs for ScanNet and RoboVQA, covering various reasoning categories.
 - **Tasks Involving Physical Reachability:** Five fixed task templates are used with the "reachable" labels to generate specific QA pairs (e.g., "Is the [Object] in the robot's reachable space?").

#### 3.4. Training Pipeline

PhysVLM adopts a two-stage training process:

1. **Stage 1 (Multimodal Feature Alignment):**
 - Uses LLaVA-Pretrain and OpenX-Embodiment data from Phys100K.
 - Only the projection layers (converting encoder features to LLM tokens) are trained.
 - Goal: Establish a foundational understanding and alignment between visual inputs, S-P Maps, and language, independent of specific robot configurations.

2. **Stage 2 (Full Model Training):**
 - All model parameters are unfrozen.
 - Trains on the complete Phys100K dataset, along with ShareGPT4V and RoboVQA.
 - Goal: Enhance PhysVLM's ability to perform complex visual reasoning tasks while integrating physical reachability constraints and generalizing across diverse environments and robots.
 - Implementation details: Trained for 48 hours on eight A800 GPUs, with specific batch sizes and learning rates for each stage. The final model is PhysVLM-3B.

#### 3.5. EQA-phys Benchmark

A new benchmark, EQA-phys, is introduced to specifically evaluate a model's comprehension of physical reachability in embodied QA tasks:

- **Simulator Dataset:** 200 samples and 1,000 questions from the PyBullet validation set (involving UR5, FR5, CR5, FRANKA robots).
- **Real-world Zero-shot Evaluation Set:** 60 samples and 300 questions collected from real-world UR3 and XArm6 robots in two distinct scenarios. This set is manually annotated by domain experts to assess the model's zero-shot generalization to unseen robots and environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly demonstrate PhysVLM's superior performance in integrating physical reachability into visual reasoning and task planning, often outperforming both general-purpose API-based VLMs and specialized embodied VLMs.

#### 4.1. EQA-phys Benchmark Performance

(Table 1) PhysVLM-3B achieved an average score of 71.0% on the EQA-phys benchmark, significantly outperforming all baseline models. API-based VLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, GPT-4o-mini) scored in the range of 52.8% to 57.0%, while embodied VLMs (SpatialVLM, SpatialBot) scored 51.1% to 54.1%. This 14% improvement over GPT-4o highlights PhysVLM's enhanced capability in understanding and reasoning about robotic physical reachability.

Crucially, the study showed that the S-P Map itself is highly effective and compatible with existing VLMs. When API-based VLMs like GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.5, and GPT-4o were augmented with the S-P Map input, their performance significantly improved on EQA-phys (e.g., GPT-4o improved from 57.0% to 61.3%), demonstrating the S-P Map's ability to abstract physical constraints into an interpretable format for these models.

Furthermore, PhysVLM-3B exhibited strong zero-shot generalization, achieving scores over 63% on real-world UR3 and XArm6 robots, despite being trained on simulated PyBullet data. This underscores the effectiveness of the S-P Map's unified, transferable representation of reachability and the model's independent visual and constraint encoding branches in learning generalizable features.

#### 4.2. Embodied QA Performance

(Tables 2 & 3) To ensure that integrating physical reachability did not compromise general visual reasoning capabilities, PhysVLM was also evaluated on standard embodied QA benchmarks.
On the **RoboVQA-val benchmark** (Table 2), PhysVLM-3B achieved the best performance, surpassing RoboMamba by 7.2% in BLEU-4 score (43.5 vs. 36.3). This indicates that PhysVLM can effectively handle general embodied visual reasoning tasks while also incorporating physical constraints.
On the **OpenEQA benchmark** (Table 3), PhysVLM-3B achieved a score of 57.4%, outperforming existing embodied VLMs like SpatialVLM (43.8%), SpatialBot (49.1%), and GPT4V (55.3%). It ranked second only to GPT-4o (66.7%), which is a significantly larger and more general-purpose model.

#### 4.3. Robot Task Planning Performance

(Table 4) PhysVLM's ability to generate feasible action plans was assessed in real-world task planning scenarios. When all objects were within the robot's reach, PhysVLM performed comparably to other models. However, when some objects were outside the robot's physical reach, PhysVLM significantly outperformed baselines. For "Part Objects in Range" scenarios, PhysVLM-3B achieved a success rate of 48.4%, while GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, SpatialVLM, and SpatialBot ranged from 21.5% to 35.8%. This demonstrates PhysVLM's crucial ability to understand when an object is unreachable and to incorporate steps like "move close to [object]" into its generated plan, leading to more practical and successful task execution.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

(Tables 5, 6, 7)
- **Effectiveness of S-P Map:** Removing the S-P Map input led to substantial performance degradation on EQA-phys (a 16% drop in simulation and 9.3% in real-world evaluations), confirming its critical role. Replacing the S-P Map with a raw Depth Map also significantly worsened zero-shot performance, highlighting that the S-P Map's abstracted representation is superior to raw depth for understanding reachability.
- **Effectiveness of an Additional Feature Encoder:** Using an independent feature encoder for the S-P Map (as in PhysVLM) yielded better performance on both EQA-phys (71.0%) and OpenEQA (57.4%) compared to sharing weights with the visual feature encoder (68.2% and 56.5% respectively). This suggests that S-P Map features are distinct from general image features, warranting a dedicated processing pathway.
- **Effectiveness of Training Data (Phys100K):** Ablating components of the Phys100K dataset (e.g., removing PyBullet data or other embodied datasets) consistently led to reduced performance on EQA-phys, validating the importance and comprehensive nature of the constructed dataset.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

(Figure 4) Visual comparisons demonstrated that PhysVLM produced accurate responses regarding object reachability and proposed physically feasible next steps for tasks, unlike GPT-4o and SpatialBot, which often made incorrect assertions or impractical suggestions due to their lack of physical reachability understanding. This qualitative evidence further reinforces the quantitative findings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "PhysVLM: Enabling Visual Language Models to Understand Robotic Physical Reachability" makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the fields of embodied AI and robotics.

#### 5.1. Technical Advancements and Novelty

- **Unified Reachability Representation:** The introduction of the Space-Physical Reachability Map (S-P Map) is a significant technical leap. By abstracting complex robot kinematics and configurations into a generalized, robot-agnostic spatial representation, it provides a universal language for physical reachability. This allows models to learn features applicable across diverse robots without needing to understand intricate, robot-specific parameters.
- **Physically-Grounded VLM Architecture:** PhysVLM's dual-branch architecture, with a dedicated feature encoder for the S-P Map, effectively integrates this physical intelligence into a VLM without sacrificing general vision-language capabilities. This design solves a critical challenge of incorporating a new modality into foundation models.
- **New Datasets and Benchmarks:** The creation of the large-scale multi-robot dataset Phys100K and the challenging EQA-phys benchmark fills a crucial gap in the embodied AI community. These resources provide a standardized framework for training and evaluating future models focused on physical reachability, accelerating research in this area.

#### 5.2. Impact on Robotics and Embodied AI

- **Enhanced Reliability and Safety:** By explicitly reasoning about physical reachability, PhysVLM significantly improves the reliability and safety of robotic task execution. Robots equipped with this understanding are less likely to attempt physically impossible or unsafe actions (e.g., trying to grasp an object out of reach), leading to fewer task failures, reduced damage to equipment, and safer human-robot interaction in various settings (industrial, assistive, home).
- **More Intelligent Task Planning:** PhysVLM enables robots to generate more practical and feasible task plans. Instead of simply generating a sequence of actions, it can incorporate necessary intermediary steps, such as "move closer to the object," when an object is initially unreachable. This bridges the gap between high-level task goals and low-level physical execution constraints.
- **Improved Generalization Across Robot Platforms:** The robot-agnostic nature of the S-P Map allows PhysVLM to generalize its understanding of physical reachability to unseen robot configurations and real-world environments with different robots. This cross-platform adaptability is vital for widespread deployment of AI in robotics, as it reduces the need for extensive retraining or recalibration for each new robotic system.
- **Bridging the Perception-Action Gap:** This work represents a crucial step in bridging the long-standing gap between high-level perception and actionable intelligence in embodied AI. VLMs can now move beyond purely semantic understanding of the environment to incorporate the physical limitations and capabilities of the robot, leading to more truly "embodied" intelligence.

#### 5.3. Broader Research Implications

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The S-P Map could serve as a foundational "physical common sense" module that can be easily integrated ("plug-and-play") into various existing and future VLMs, allowing them to gain physical reasoning capabilities.
- **Dataset Development:** The methodology for creating Phys100K, especially the pseudo-labeling and diverse data sources, provides a blueprint for constructing other large-scale, physically-grounded datasets for complex robotic tasks.
- **Domain Adaptation:** While the paper notes a limitation in reduced zero-shot performance on real robots compared to simulations due to the domain gap, this also highlights a clear direction for future research in robust domain adaptation techniques for physically-grounded embodied AI.

In conclusion, PhysVLM contributes a novel and effective solution to a critical problem in embodied AI, empowering VLMs with a practical understanding of robotic physical reachability. Its potential to lead to safer, more reliable, and more intelligent autonomous robotic systems is substantial, paving the way for advanced applications across numerous domains.
