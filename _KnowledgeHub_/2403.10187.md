# Grasp Anything: Combining Teacher-Augmented Policy Gradient Learning with Instance Segmentation to Grasp Arbitrary Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Teacher-Augmented Policy Gradient (TAPG), a two-stage learning framework that combines reinforcement learning with perception-aware policy distillation to enable a robot to grasp arbitrary, user-selected objects from cluttered environments. On a real robot, TAPG achieved a 55% success rate in zero-shot transfer, outperforming a Policy Distillation baseline (25%) and direct Vision-based Reinforcement Learning (0%).

## Problem

- Interactive robotic grasping in cluttered environments faces challenges with unknown object geometries and diverse clutter, requiring sophisticated visual perception and precise motor control.
- Integrating high-capacity Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) with reinforcement learning (RL) is difficult due to RL's high sample inefficiency, making direct training of visual policies computationally prohibitive.
- Existing policy distillation methods for transferring skills from a privileged teacher often overlook the need for a vision-based student to adapt its behavior to maintain visual information, such as target visibility.

## Method

- The Teacher-Augmented Policy Gradient (TAPG) framework is introduced as a two-stage learning approach that combines ==reinforcement learning== and perception-aware policy distillation.
- A teacher policy is first trained in simulation using privileged, low-dimensional state information to efficiently learn dexterous grasping skills.
- A sensorimotor student policy then learns from high-dimensional visual observations (segmented point clouds) using a modified policy gradient with a gated behavior cloning objective and a "target visibility reward" to adapt its actions to maintain perceptual input.

## Results

- Teacher policies achieved high grasping success rates in simulation, reaching **95.1%** for single objects and **75.9%** for 5-object cluttered scenarios on unseen test objects.
- In real-world zero-shot transfer, TAPG policies achieved a **55%** success rate, significantly outperforming a Policy Distillation baseline (**25%**) and direct Vision-based Reinforcement Learning (**0%**).
- TAPG demonstrated superior performance for smaller, easily occluded objects in real-world trials (e.g., 5/5 for foam brick vs. 0/5 for PD), directly attributed to its perception-aware grasping strategies that maintain object visibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct visual reinforcement learning is extremely sample-inefficient for complex dexterous grasping, necessitating efficient learning paradigms like policy distillation.
> - Standard policy distillation, while efficient, is insufficient; a vision-based student policy requires adaptation to its visual observation space, for example, by avoiding self-occlusion, rather than merely imitating a privileged teacher.
> - The gated behavior cloning objective in TAPG, combined with perception-driven rewards, enables a vision-based student policy to learn robust motor skills while effectively adapting to maintain critical visual information, leading to improved real-world performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of interactive robotic grasping in cluttered environments, a long-standing challenge in robot learning. This task requires sophisticated visual perception, precise motor control, and an effective integration of these two capabilities. Traditional approaches to robotic grasping often struggle with unknown object geometries and diverse clutter scenarios.

Recent advancements in computer vision, particularly the emergence of Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM), have provided capabilities for semantic understanding and promptable object segmentation. These models offer a potential solution for identifying and isolating target objects based on user input. However, integrating these high-capacity vision models with reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks presents a significant challenge due to the notorious sample inefficiency of RL, especially when dealing with high-dimensional visual observations and complex action spaces. Training visual policies directly with RL often demands hundreds of millions of interactions, pushing computational and data generation requirements beyond feasible limits.

Previous efforts to mitigate this sample inefficiency have explored policy distillation (PD), where knowledge from a teacher policy, trained on low-dimensional, privileged state information, is transferred to a student policy operating on high-dimensional visual observations. While PD improves efficiency, existing methods often overlook the inherent differences between observation spaces and the resultant optimal behaviors. Specifically, a teacher policy trained with full knowledge of object poses might execute actions (e.g., self-occlusion) that are detrimental to a vision-based student policy reliant on visual tracking. Such actions can lead to loss of visual information for the student, resulting in suboptimal performance or failure.

This research introduces Teacher-Augmented Policy Gradient (TAPG) as a novel two-stage learning framework designed to address these limitations. TAPG aims to synergize reinforcement learning and policy distillation in a manner that allows a vision-based student policy to adapt its behaviors to the specific requirements and constraints of its visual observation space. By doing so, the work seeks to enable the effective integration of powerful VFMs for perception with RL for control, ultimately facilitating robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to real-world robotic systems for grasping arbitrary objects in cluttered scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a robot to grasp arbitrary, user-selected objects from cluttered environments with a level of dexterity comparable to humans. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and is motivated by current challenges in robotic manipulation:

1. **Effective Target Identification:** A core challenge in cluttered grasping is precisely identifying and isolating a target object from a multitude of unknown geometries based on user cues. The motivation here is to leverage modern Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) to provide this capability, representing the target as a segmented point cloud for the grasping policy.
2. **Proficient Motor Control Learning:** The robot needs to acquire sophisticated motor skills for dexterous grasping and retrieval. The motivation is to learn these complex behaviors efficiently by initially training a teacher policy on low-dimensional, privileged state information, as learning directly from high-dimensional visual inputs is prohibitively sample-inefficient for complex tasks.
3. **Seamless Integration of Perception and Control:** While VFMs offer powerful perception, their integration into RL frameworks for continuous control is difficult due to the computational demands and sample inefficiency of RL with high-dimensional visual inputs. The objective is to develop a learning framework that can bridge this gap, enabling the sensorimotor policy to benefit from visual perception without requiring massive amounts of visual training data from scratch.
4. **Adaptation to Visual Constraints:** Existing policy distillation methods often assume direct imitation of the teacher is sufficient. However, a teacher policy with privileged information might perform actions (e.g., occluding the target object with the hand) that would cause a vision-based student policy to lose track of the object. The motivation is to allow the student policy to adapt its learned motor skills to the nuances and limitations of its visual observation space, specifically by encouraging behaviors that maintain target object visibility throughout the grasping process.
5. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The ultimate goal is for the learned policies to operate effectively on a real robot without further domain adaptation (zero-shot transfer). This necessitates robust learning in simulation that accounts for real-world robotic dynamics and the practical application of VFMs for real-time perception.
6. **Generalization to Novel Objects and Clutter:** The system should demonstrate the ability to grasp previously unseen objects and operate reliably in cluttered scenarios, recovering from interactions and failures, thus achieving a high degree of versatility and robustness.

These objectives collectively aim to develop a robust, efficient, and adaptable framework for robotic grasping, enhancing the capabilities of autonomous systems in complex, real-world manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Teacher-Augmented Policy Gradient (TAPG) framework, a two-stage learning approach that combines reinforcement learning (RL) and policy distillation (PD) to achieve perception-aware control.

**A. Stage 1: Training the Teacher Policy (Figure 2a)**
The initial stage focuses on learning proficient motor control in a simulated environment using privileged, low-dimensional state information.
- **Objective:** To master the dexterous grasping task without the complexities of visual perception, framing it as a tractable RL problem.
- **Observation Space:** The teacher policy receives proprioceptive information from the robot (e.g., joint angles), the ground truth state of the object (represented by an oriented 3D bounding box), and its own previous action. This low-dimensional representation allows for efficient learning.
- **Learning Algorithm:** Standard policy gradient (PG) methods are employed, specifically a variant that estimates the policy gradient to optimize the discounted sum of rewards.
- **Environment:** Training is conducted in the Isaac Gym simulator, which provides a parallelized environment for efficient data generation.
- **Reward Function:** A multi-component reward function guides the teacher policy. It includes:
 - Sparse task reward for lifting the object.
 - Dense task reward based on the inverse distance of the object to a goal position.
 - Reward for bringing fingertips close to the object.
 - Reward for clearance between the object and the table.
 - Penalties for large actions and contact forces on the robot arm. (At this stage, the target visibility reward is not included).
- **Policy Architecture:** The teacher policy is implemented as a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with three hidden layers (1024, 512, 512 units) using ELU activations.

**B. Stage 2: Teacher-Guided Training of the Sensorimotor Policy (Student Policy) (Figure 2b)**
This stage transfers the motor skills learned by the teacher to a sensorimotor student policy that operates on high-dimensional visual observations, while also enabling adaptation to these observations.
- **Objective:** To learn a perception-aware control strategy that leverages visual information and adapts behaviors to maintain perceptual input, such as object visibility.
- **Observation Space:** The student policy receives proprioceptive information and a segmented point cloud of the target object (derived from ground truth segmentations in simulation).
- **Learning Algorithm (TAPG):** TAPG combines a policy gradient objective with a novel, gated behavior cloning objective.
 - The combined loss function is given by `L_PG(θ) + L_BC(θ, ϕ)`.
 - `L_PG(θ)` is the standard policy gradient loss, allowing the student to learn from its own experience and fine-tune its policy.
 - `L_BC(θ, ϕ)` is a behavior cloning loss modified with a gating term: `− E[log π_θ(a_t|s_t) * ˆA_T]`.
 - **Gating Term (`ˆA_T`):** `ˆA_T = [V_π^T(s_t^T) − V_π^θ(s_t)] > 0`. This term serves to selectively activate the imitation objective. It evaluates to 1 if the teacher's value function (`V_π^T`) in its privileged state is estimated to be better than the student's value function (`V_π^θ`) in its visual state, and 0 otherwise. This mechanism ensures that the student policy imitates the teacher only when the teacher is deemed superior, thereby enabling the student to adapt and diverge from the teacher's behavior when its own visual information necessitates it.
- **Perception-Driven Reward Design:** An additional "target visibility reward" (`r_v`) is introduced into the student's reward function. This term incentivizes behaviors that maximize the visible area of the target object in the camera's image plane, calculated by projecting points sampled on the object's surface to the image plane. This encourages the policy to avoid self-occlusion, which is critical for maintaining robust tracking by the vision system.
- **Data Generation:** During this stage, ground truth segmentations from the simulator are used to generate segmented point clouds for training, avoiding the computational overhead of running a VFM in the loop for every training step.
- **Policy Architecture:** The student policy employs a PointNet-like encoder to process the segmented point cloud, the output of which is then concatenated with vectorized observations (proprioception, previous action). This combined embedding is fed into an MLP structurally identical to the teacher policy.

**C. Sim-to-Real Adaptation and Deployment (Figure 2c)**
- **Hand Dynamics Calibration:** To mitigate the sim-to-real gap, the intricate dynamics of the anthropomorphic hand (Schunk SIH), particularly its tendon-actuated joints, are calibrated. Real-world hand movements are tracked using ArUco markers, and the simulated robot's response curves are adjusted via least squares polynomial approximation to match the real-world data.
- **Safety Reward:** A contact penalty term in the reward function discourages the robot arm from colliding with the environment, promoting manipulation solely with the hand.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** The trained student policy is directly deployed on a real robot without any further real-world training or fine-tuning.
- **Real-World Perception:** For real-world deployment, the Segment Anything Model (SAM) is used as a promptable object segmentation model. Given a human-understandable prompt, SAM provides real-time segmentation masks, which are then used to generate segmented point clouds for the deployed student policy.

**D. System Setup:**
- **Robot Hardware:** Universal Robots UR5 robotic arm, Schunk SIH five-finger hand (11 DoF).
- **Vision System:** RealSense D455 RGB-D camera mounted above the workspace.
- **Control Parameters:** Control frequency of 7.5 Hz, episode length of 75 steps.
- **Objects:** A subset of 60 graspable objects from the YCB object and model set, divided into 48 training and 12 test objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to assess the teacher policy's ability to grasp, TAPG's advantages over baselines, and the system's real-world deployability.

**A. Training the Teacher Policy (RQ1)**
- **Performance:** Teacher policies demonstrated proficiency in simulation. For single-object grasping, they achieved a success rate of 95.1% on training objects and 89.7% on unseen test objects. In cluttered scenarios, with 3 and 5 objects, success rates were 85.3% and 79.0% (training objects), and 82.2% and 75.9% (test objects), respectively.
- **Failure Modes:** Qualitative analysis indicated that most failures occurred with very flat objects, which presented difficulties for stable grasping.
- **Conclusion:** The teacher policies successfully mastered dexterous grasping given privileged simulator state information, affirming its role as a capable motor skills expert.

**B. Training the Sensorimotor Policy (RQ2)**
- **Comparative Performance:**
 - **Vision-based Reinforcement Learning (VRL):** Direct RL from visual observations made limited progress and failed to solve the task within the allotted training time, demonstrating its sample inefficiency for this complex problem.
 - **Policy Distillation (PD):** This baseline, relying solely on cloning the teacher's behavior, converged quickly and stably, yielding capable sensorimotor policies. However, its performance plateaued and did not surpass the teacher's implicit capabilities for a vision-based system.
 - **Teacher-Augmented Policy Gradient (TAPG - Ours):** TAPG initially took longer to match the teacher's performance compared to PD but subsequently surpassed it in terms of episode return (Figure 3). This suggests TAPG's ability to adapt and refine behaviors beyond mere imitation.
- **Behavioral Adaptation:** TAPG policies exhibited adaptations to the visual observation space, particularly in maintaining target object visibility. For larger objects (e.g., sugar box), both PD and TAPG maintained visibility. However, for smaller objects (e.g., strawberry), PD policies often severely occluded the object, while TAPG policies adjusted their grasping strategies to keep the object visible throughout the process (Figure 4).
- **Conclusion:** TAPG demonstrated a tangible advantage over VRL and PD by enabling the student policy to effectively adapt to the intricacies and requirements of visual perception, leading to improved performance.

**C. Real-World Deployment (RQ3)**
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** The policies were deployed directly on a real UR5 robot with a Schunk SIH hand and RealSense D455 camera, using SAM for real-time object segmentation, without any real-world training or fine-tuning.
- **Quantitative Results:**
 - VRL showed no successful grasps (0/80 trials).
 - PD achieved a 25% success rate (20/80 trials).
 - TAPG significantly outperformed both baselines, achieving a 55% success rate (44/80 trials) across single-object and cluttered scenarios.
- **Object-Wise Performance:**
 - For large objects that are difficult to fully occlude (e.g., bleach cleanser, sugar box), both PD and TAPG showed comparable high success rates (4/5 to 5/5).
 - For smaller objects that can be easily occluded by the hand (e.g., foam brick, small cups), TAPG's advantage was more pronounced. PD often failed due to vision pipeline losing track of the object (e.g., foam brick: PD 0/5 vs TAPG 5/5; cups-a: PD 0/5 vs TAPG 4/5). This indicates that TAPG's perception-aware behaviors were crucial for success with these objects.
- **Conclusion:** TAPG policies successfully transferred to the real world zero-shot, outperforming baselines and demonstrating robustness, particularly for objects susceptible to visual occlusion. The learned behaviors enabled the agent to grasp diverse objects, including pre-grasp manipulation and recovery from failures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a framework that addresses key challenges in interactive grasping. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Bridging the Gap Between VFMs and RL:** The proposed TAPG framework provides an effective method for integrating powerful Vision Foundation Models (like SAM) for real-time perception with reinforcement learning-based control policies. This integration is crucial for enabling robots to understand and interact with arbitrary objects from high-dimensional visual inputs, overcoming the typical sample inefficiency of end-to-end visual RL.
2. **Adaptive Knowledge Transfer in Policy Distillation:** TAPG introduces a novel aspect to policy distillation by allowing the student policy to adapt learned motor skills to the specific constraints and requirements of its visual observation space. The gated imitation objective and the perception-driven reward term explicitly encourage behaviors that maintain visual information (e.g., target visibility), which is a critical consideration often overlooked in prior policy distillation works that assume direct imitation is sufficient. This adaptability enhances the robustness and reliability of vision-based robot policies.
3. **Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstration of robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to a real robot for diverse objects in cluttered environments is a significant achievement. This capability reduces the need for extensive and costly real-world data collection and training, accelerating the deployment of advanced robotic manipulation skills.
4. **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation:** The learned policies exhibit emergent behaviors such as pre-grasp manipulation and recovery from failures, indicating a higher level of dexterity and adaptability in complex, interactive scenarios. This contributes to the development of more versatile and robust robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured human environments.
5. **User-Friendly Promptable Grasping:** By leveraging promptable object segmentation from models like SAM, the system allows human users to easily specify target objects. This "grasp anything" capability enhances the practical utility of the robot, making it more intuitive and flexible for human-robot interaction in various applications.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust foundation for future research in areas such as grasping from deeply cluttered containers, where complete occlusions might be unavoidable. Investigating how policies can handle or recover from such unavoidable occlusions could lead to further advancements in robot manipulation in highly constrained environments.

In summary, this research offers a practical and effective solution for enabling robots to grasp arbitrary objects from clutter, marking a step towards more intelligent and human-like robotic manipulation capabilities.
