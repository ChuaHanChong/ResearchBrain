# VIMA: General Robot Manipulation with Multimodal Prompts

> [!summary] Summary
> VIMA introduces a multimodal prompting formulation to enable a single robot agent to perform diverse manipulation tasks, achieving up to 2.9x higher task success rates and outperforming baselines trained with 10x more data on various zero-shot generalization levels.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation tasks often require specialized models for different input modalities (e.g., language, visual demos), leading to fragmented, "siloed robot systems."
- There was a lack of a unified, flexible, and expressive interface for instructing generalist robots across diverse manipulation tasks.
- Existing methods struggle with strong zero-shot generalization to novel objects, combinations, and new tasks, and often require extensive data.

## Method

- Develops a multimodal prompting formulation that unifies diverse robot manipulation tasks as a single sequence modeling problem by interleaving text and visual tokens.
- Introduces VIMA-BENCH, a comprehensive benchmark with 17 diverse tasks and a four-level generalization protocol, along with a 650K+ trajectory dataset.
- Proposes VIMA, a transformer-based encoder-decoder agent that uses object-centric representations (Mask R-CNN detections) and cross-attention between prompts and the robot controller.

## Results

- VIMA's architecture, leveraging object-centric tokens and cross-attention, achieved up to 2.9x higher task success rates compared to other transformer-based baselines.
- The agent demonstrated high data efficiency, performing comparably or better with just 1% of the training data than baselines trained with 10x more data.
- VIMA showed robust zero-shot generalization across novel placements, combinatorial settings, and novel objects (L1-L3), and its performance drop on entirely new tasks (L4) was half as severe as baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diverse robot manipulation tasks can be unified under a single, flexible multimodal prompting formulation, similar to prompt-based learning in NLP.
> - Object-centric representations combined with cross-attention for prompt conditioning are crucial for achieving strong zero-shot generalization and data efficiency in robot learning.
> - A comprehensive, procedurally generated benchmark like VIMA-BENCH is essential for systematically evaluating and accelerating research in generalist robot agents.
