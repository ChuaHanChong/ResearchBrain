# FlowPolicy: Enabling Fast and Robust 3D Flow-based Policy via Consistency Flow Matching for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowPolicy introduces a novel framework for robot manipulation that employs conditional consistency flow matching with 3D visual representations, enabling real-time policy generation. The method achieves a 7x speedup in inference time over state-of-the-art 3D diffusion policies while maintaining or improving success rates across 37 robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Diffusion models, despite their performance in complex robotic trajectory generation, suffer from slow, multi-step inference, making them unsuitable for real-time robot manipulation.
- Existing 2D-based imitation learning methods often lack the rich spatial understanding required for precise 3D manipulation tasks.
- The inherent efficiency-quality trade-off in advanced visuomotor policies limits their practical deployment in real-world robotic applications.

## Method

- **FlowPolicy** integrates conditional consistency flow matching into a 3D visual representation backbone, adapting the architectural strengths of 3D ==Diffusion Policy (DP3)==.
- It leverages 3D point clouds, derived from single-view depth observations, as robust visual conditional inputs for the policy, providing essential spatial information.
- The framework employs a two-segment Consistency Flow Matching approach, which enables one-step policy generation by directly predicting the target action trajectory from noise, bypassing iterative sampling.

## Results

- FlowPolicy achieved an average inference time of 19.9ms per step (NFE=1), demonstrating a more than 7-fold speedup compared to the state-of-the-art 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) which took 145.7ms.
- The method obtained an average success rate of **70.0%** across 37 Adroit and Metaworld tasks, outperforming or matching DP3 (**68.7%**) and significantly surpassing 2D-based baselines.
- FlowPolicy exhibited superior learning stability, faster convergence, and better robustness to varying numbers of expert demonstrations compared to diffusion-based policies, particularly breaking through performance bottlenecks on hard tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditional consistency flow matching can effectively model complex, high-dimensional action distributions for 3D robot manipulation with a single-step inference process.
> - Combining the efficiency of consistency flow matching with robust 3D visual representations (point clouds) successfully resolves the critical efficiency-quality trade-off in visuomotor policies.
> - The 'two-segment' consistency flow matching strategy enhances the quality and flexibility of action generation for robotic tasks without requiring additional distillation steps common in other consistency-based models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in robot learning: enabling robots to acquire complex manipulation skills from expert demonstrations, a field known as imitation learning or learning from demonstration. Historically, approaches to this problem have largely fallen into two categories:
- **Supervised Regression:** Directly mapping observations to actions. While straightforward, these methods often struggle to capture the multi-modal nature of human behaviors and lack the precision required for complex tasks.
- **Energy-Based Models:** Implicitly modeling action distributions. These approaches aim to identify actions that minimize an energy function but often face challenges related to training stability due to the necessity of negative sample extraction.

The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements with the advent of **generative models**, particularly **diffusion models**, which have revolutionized various domains, including image generation and increasingly, robotics.
- **Diffusion-based Policies:** Models like Diffusion Policy (DP) and its 3D extension, 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), have demonstrated powerful capabilities in modeling complex, high-dimensional robotic trajectories. DP3, in particular, leverages 3D visual representations to learn generalizable visuomotor policies with relatively few demonstrations.
- **The Diffusion Bottleneck:** Despite their power, a critical limitation of diffusion models in robotics is their **computational inefficiency during inference**. They typically require numerous recursive sampling steps to generate high-quality actions, leading to substantial runtime delays. This creates a challenging "efficiency-quality trade-off" that severely restricts their practical application in real-time robotic systems. Researchers have explored sampling acceleration techniques, but the core issue persists.

In parallel, **Flow Matching (FM)** has emerged as another class of generative models. FM defines probability paths via Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) to transform between noise and data samples. It offers advantages in numerical stability and inference efficiency compared to diffusion models, as it directly regresses to a vector field rather than estimating noise. However, its application to complex conditional generation, especially involving 3D vision in embodied tasks, has been underexplored.

The most recent development in this area is **Consistency Flow Matching (Consistency-FM)**, which further enhances efficiency by defining a straight-line flow from any point in time to a single endpoint, theoretically enabling one-step generation. While consistency models show promise for faster generation, they often require distillation processes, which can limit their applicability, especially in real-world robotics where robust teacher models are hard to find.

**FlowPolicy's contribution** specifically addresses the gap left by these prior works. It directly tackles the runtime inefficiency of 3D diffusion-based policies by being the first to apply and explore **conditional consistency flow matching** for 3D robotic manipulation tasks. By leveraging the efficiency of consistency flow matching and combining it with robust 3D visual representations (inspired by DP3), FlowPolicy aims to provide a solution that achieves both high-quality actions and real-time inference speed, without requiring distillation or complex numerical simulations. This work pushes the boundaries of efficient and effective policy learning, moving towards practical, real-time robot autonomy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind FlowPolicy stems from the significant limitations of current state-of-the-art vision-based imitation learning methods, particularly those based on diffusion models, when applied to real-time robot manipulation.
- **Inefficiency of Diffusion Models:** While diffusion models (e.g., DP, DP3) have shown impressive performance in generating high-quality, complex robotic trajectories, their recursive, multi-step inference process leads to substantial computational overhead. This "efficiency-quality trade-off" is a critical bottleneck, preventing their widespread adoption in applications requiring immediate and precise robot responses.
- **Underexplored Potential of Flow Matching:** Flow Matching (FM) and especially Consistency Flow Matching (Consistency-FM) offer theoretical advantages in terms of numerical stability and inference speed, with Consistency-FM promising one-step generation. However, their capability for conditional generation in complex embodied tasks using 3D visual representations remained largely unexplored. There was a clear opportunity to bridge this gap.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the FlowPolicy research are:
1. **Develop a Novel 3D Flow-based Policy Framework:** To introduce a new imitation learning framework, FlowPolicy, that leverages the efficiency of consistency flow matching combined with rich 3D visual representations for robot manipulation.
2. **Enable Real-time Policy Generation:** To significantly improve the inference speed of robot policies, allowing for real-time task execution, which is crucial for practical robotic applications. This includes achieving one-step inference for action generation.
3. **Maintain Competitive Policy Quality:** To ensure that the accelerated inference does not come at the cost of performance, meaning FlowPolicy must achieve success rates comparable to, or better than, existing state-of-the-art methods (especially 3D diffusion policies like DP3).
4. **Explore Conditional Consistency Flow Matching with 3D Vision:** To make the first successful attempt at applying and demonstrating the effectiveness of conditional consistency flow matching in embodied tasks where complex 3D visual representations serve as the primary conditioning input.
5. **Achieve a Superior Efficiency-Quality Trade-off:** Ultimately, to demonstrate that FlowPolicy can deliver high-quality, robust robotic actions with a dramatic reduction in inference time (specifically aiming for a 7x speedup over DP3) without requiring additional distillation steps common in other consistency-based models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FlowPolicy proposes a novel framework for fast and robust 3D flow-based policy generation, building upon the strengths of 3D visual representations and Consistency Flow Matching. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Underlying Architecture and Core Idea

FlowPolicy adapts the powerful 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) as its architectural backbone but replaces the diffusion-based policy generation core with a conditional consistency flow matching mechanism. The central idea is to construct a policy generator that leverages the efficiency of consistency flow matching to enable one-step inference while maintaining the high quality of task completion, guided by rich 3D visual input.

#### 3.2. 3D Visual Conditional Representation

To provide the flow model with essential spatial information for fine robot manipulation, FlowPolicy employs 3D point clouds as visual conditional representations:
- **Data Acquisition:** Single-view depth-observation images are captured.
- **Point Cloud Conversion:** Depth information is converted into a 3D point cloud using camera intrinsics and extrinsics. This offers a richer spatial understanding than 2D images.
- **Downsampling:** Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is applied to the point cloud. This reduces redundancy and computational load while preserving the overall spatial integrity of the scene. The paper specifies downsampling to 512 points for Adroit tasks and 1024 points for Metaworld tasks.
- **Encoding:** A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) encodes the downsampled point cloud into a compact 3D representation (dimension 64). This serves as the visual conditional input for the flow-based policy.
- **Robot State Integration:** The compact 3D visual representation is combined with a robot state embedding (also dimension 64) to form the complete conditional input.
- **Observation Horizon:** The model utilizes point clouds from the two most recent time frames as conditional inputs, allowing it to leverage temporal context.

#### 3.3. Conditional Trajectory Generation with FlowPolicy

The core of FlowPolicy lies in its conditional consistency flow matching mechanism. The policy function is defined as $\pi: O \Rightarrow A$, mapping visual observations ($o \in O$) to action sequences ($a \in A$).
- **Flow Matching Principle:** Flow Matching aims to learn a vector field $\nu_{\theta}(t, x_t)$ that transports a noise distribution ($p_0$) to a data distribution ($p_1$) via an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE).
- **Conditional Flow Matching:** In FlowPolicy, this is extended to conditional flow matching, where the target action distribution ($\Gamma_{tar}$) is conditioned on the observed robot state ($s$) and the 3D visual representation ($v$). The source distribution ($\Gamma_{src}$) is typically Gaussian noise $N(0, I)$.
- **Consistency Flow Matching Adaptation:** FlowPolicy adopts Consistency Flow Matching (Consistency-FM), which defines a straight-line flow from any time point to the same endpoint and imposes constraints on its velocity values. This formulation allows for one-step generation, bypassing the iterative steps of diffusion models.
- **Two-Segment Training:** To enhance the quality and flexibility of action generation, FlowPolicy employs a "two-segment" Consistency-FM approach. This technique splits the total time interval into subsets (here, two segments) and normalizes a consistent vector field within each segment. This relaxes the strict velocity consistency requirement, allowing for more adaptable and efficient data transmission across the probabilistic path.
- **Loss Function:** The model is trained by minimizing a specific loss function (Equation 8 in the paper) that enforces velocity consistency across the segments and ensures the learned flow model effectively transforms noisy trajectories into target action trajectories, conditioned on the visual and state inputs. The loss includes a term for flow consistency and a term for velocity consistency, with a positive weighting factor $\alpha$ and segment-specific weights $\lambda_i$.
- **Flow Model:** The FlowPolicy model (F) is represented as $F: a, t, s, v \rightarrow \Delta a$, where it learns to predict the change in action ($\Delta a$) based on the current action ($a$), time ($t$), robot state ($s$), and visual representation ($v$).

#### 3.4. Inference Process

During inference, FlowPolicy performs a single, non-iterative step:
1. **Noise Sampling:** A noise trajectory ($a_0$) is sampled from the source Gaussian distribution.
2. **One-Step Prediction:** The trained flow model directly predicts the target action trajectory ($a_1$) from $a_0$, conditioned on the current state and 3D visual representation, in a single forward pass.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Expert Demonstrations:** 10 expert demonstrations are used per task.
- **Image Resolution:** 84x84 pixels.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with a learning rate of 1e-4.
- **Batch Size:** 128.
- **Training Epochs:** 3,000 epochs.
- **EMA Decay Rate:** 0.95.
- **Normalization:** Action and state inputs are normalized to [-1, 1] during training and unnormalized before execution.
- **Hardware:** Evaluated on a single NVIDIA RTX 2080Ti GPU.

This meticulously designed methodology allows FlowPolicy to address the critical trade-off between inference efficiency and policy quality, delivering real-time, high-quality robot actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FlowPolicy was extensively evaluated on 37 robot manipulation tasks across two prominent simulation benchmarks: Adroit (3 dexterous manipulation tasks) and Metaworld (34 tasks of varying difficulty). The evaluation focused on comparing FlowPolicy against state-of-the-art 2D-based (DP, Adaflow, CP) and 3D-based (DP3, Simple DP3) policy models in terms of inference speed (runtime and Number of Function Evaluations - NFE) and success rate.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons on Runtime (Table 1 and Abstract)

- **Dramatic Speedup:** FlowPolicy achieves a remarkable average inference time of **19.9ms per step**, operating in a single inference step (NFE=1).
- **7x Faster than SOTA 3D Diffusion:** This represents a more than **7-fold increase in speed** compared to DP3 (145.7ms, NFE=10), the state-of-the-art 3D conditional diffusion policy.
- **3x Faster than Lightweight Baseline:** FlowPolicy is also over **3 times faster** than Simple DP3 (63.0ms, NFE=10), a lightweight version of DP3.
- **Reduced Variation:** The approach exhibits less variation in inference time across tasks and runs, indicating a more stable and deterministic sampling policy, which is highly beneficial for real-time applications.
- **Real-time Enablement:** The results unequivocally demonstrate that FlowPolicy enables real-time robot operations, a critical achievement for practical deployment.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparisons on Success Rate (Table 2)

- **Competitive Performance:** FlowPolicy achieves an impressive average success rate of **70.0%** across all 37 tasks.
- **Outperforms 3D Baselines:** This performance is competitive with, and even slightly **higher than**, DP3 (68.7%) and Simple DP3 (67.4%), despite FlowPolicy performing inference in a single step compared to DP3's 10 steps.
- **Superior to 2D Baselines:** Consistent with expectations, 3D-based methods (including FlowPolicy) generally outperform 2D-based baselines due to their richer spatial representations. FlowPolicy's success rate is significantly higher than DP (35.2%), Adaflow (35.6%), and CP (50.1%).
- **Effective One-step Inference:** The ability to achieve such competitive success rates with only one step of inference underscores the effectiveness of conditional consistency flow matching in balancing efficiency and generation quality.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparisons on Manipulation Tasks (Figure 3)

- **Adroit Pen Task:** In the complex "Pen-turning" task requiring dexterous manipulation, FlowPolicy generated higher-quality action sequences, accurately turning the pen to the target angle and achieving the desired position faster. DP3, while completing the task, showed minor inconsistencies, resulting in a slightly misaligned pen state.
- **Metaworld Pick-Place Task:** For a challenging "Pick-Place" task, FlowPolicy successfully located, picked up, and moved a red cube to the target blue location. In contrast, DP3 failed to pick up the cube, leading to task failure. This demonstrates FlowPolicy's "more delicate understanding and generative ability" for complex tasks.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Learning Curves (Figure 4)

- **Faster and More Stable Learning:** FlowPolicy exhibits higher stability, faster learning efficiency, and ultimately higher success rates compared to Simple DP3 and DP3.
- **Reduced Fluctuations:** While DP3 and Simple DP3 showed fluctuations in success rates during training, FlowPolicy maintained consistent and high performance once a policy was learned.
- **Overcoming Bottlenecks:** For medium-difficulty tasks, FlowPolicy was able to achieve higher success rates, surpassing performance bottlenecks observed in DP3 and Simple DP3.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies on Expert Demonstrations (Figure 5)

- **Data Efficiency and Robustness:** FlowPolicy generally maintained a higher success rate than DP3 across various numbers of expert demonstrations.
- **Improved Scaling with Data:** For non-hard tasks, FlowPolicy outperformed DP3 even with limited demonstrations. Crucially, for hard tasks, while both methods improved with more demonstrations, DP3 reached performance saturation more easily, sometimes even showing a decrease in success rate with sufficient data (e.g., 'Coffee Pull'). FlowPolicy demonstrated better scaling, breaking through these performance bottlenecks and performing more robustly with increased data.

In summary, FlowPolicy successfully addresses the core problem of efficiency in 3D vision-based imitation learning. It achieves a groundbreaking 7x speedup in inference time over state-of-the-art 3D diffusion policies while maintaining or even slightly improving success rates, enabling real-time robot manipulation without compromising quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of FlowPolicy represents a significant advancement in the field of robot imitation learning, particularly for visuomotor control, with profound implications for both research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Bridging the Efficiency-Quality Gap

The most critical contribution of FlowPolicy is its ability to dramatically narrow the long-standing gap between inference efficiency and policy quality in vision-based imitation learning algorithms. For years, diffusion models offered impressive generative capabilities but were hampered by their slow, iterative inference, making them unsuitable for real-time robotic control. FlowPolicy effectively resolves this by achieving a **7x reduction in inference time (down to 19.9ms)** while simultaneously maintaining or even slightly improving average success rates compared to state-of-the-art 3D diffusion methods. This breakthrough is crucial for practical robot deployment.

#### 5.2. Enabling Real-time Robotic Manipulation

The achievement of real-time policy generation (one-step inference in milliseconds) is transformative for robotic manipulation. Robots operating in dynamic, unstructured environments (e.g., manufacturing, logistics, service robotics) require instantaneous responses and precise control. FlowPolicy provides the necessary speed to enable:
- **Responsive Control:** Robots can react immediately to changes in the environment or task requirements.
- **Increased Throughput:** Faster decision-making allows for quicker task completion in industrial settings.
- **Safer Human-Robot Interaction:** Real-time perception and action can improve safety in collaborative environments.

#### 5.3. Novel Application of Consistency Flow Matching

FlowPolicy pioneers the application of **conditional consistency flow matching** using complex 3D visual representations for embodied tasks. While consistency models and flow matching have shown promise in other domains (e.g., image generation), their effective integration with high-dimensional 3D point cloud data for robust robot manipulation policies was largely unexplored. This work demonstrates the power and flexibility of consistency flow matching as a generative model framework for robotics, without the need for distillation often associated with consistency models.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Robustness and Learning Stability

Beyond just speed and success rates, FlowPolicy demonstrates superior learning stability and robustness. Its learning curves show faster convergence and reduced fluctuations compared to diffusion-based baselines. Furthermore, the ablation studies reveal that FlowPolicy is more robust to varying numbers of expert demonstrations, exhibiting better data efficiency with limited data and avoiding performance bottlenecks when more data is available. This robustness is highly desirable for real-world scenarios where high-quality expert demonstrations can be costly or limited.

#### 5.5. Potential for Broader Impact and Future Directions

- **New Research Avenues:** This work opens up significant research avenues for exploring consistency flow matching in other embodied AI and robotics tasks beyond manipulation, such as navigation, dexterous grasping, or even human-robot collaboration.
- **Generalizable Robot Policies:** The effective use of 3D point clouds contributes to learning more generalizable visuomotor policies, which is essential for robots to adapt to novel objects and environments.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation Robotics:** FlowPolicy establishes a strong foundation for developing the next generation of robot learning algorithms that are not only capable but also highly efficient, paving the way for more widespread adoption of intelligent robotic systems in various industries and daily life.

In conclusion, FlowPolicy represents a critical step forward, successfully marrying the expressive power of generative models with the real-time demands of practical robotics, making sophisticated robot manipulation policies more accessible and deployable.
