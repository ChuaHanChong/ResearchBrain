# UniGRPO: Unified Policy Optimization for Reasoning-Driven Visual Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified Reinforcement Learning framework, UniGRPO, jointly optimizes discrete language and continuous visual policies for interleaved multimodal generation, achieving superior performance on text alignment and compositional benchmarks. The framework integrates GRPO for text and FlowGRPO for images, while introducing key modifications for scalability and stability.

## Problem

- Developing a unified RL framework that can jointly optimize both discrete language actions and continuous visual actions within a single, coherent optimization loop for multi-round interleaved generation.
- Addressing the computational and gradient complexity introduced by Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and challenges of reward hacking in visual RL within a unified multimodal context.
- Creating a scalable and robust approach for post-training fully interleaved models with general-purpose prompts, rather than employing separate models or disjoint optimization loops.

## Method

- UniGRPO formulates the entire "Prompt → Thinking → Image" sequence as a single ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)== to jointly optimize text and image generation.
- It integrates standard ==GRPO== for autoregressive text and ==FlowGRPO== (which adapts RL to ==flow matching== via SDEs) for visual synthesis into a unified objective function.
- Critical modifications include eliminating ==Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)== during training for linear rollouts and replacing standard latent KL regularization with a ==velocity-based Mean Squared Error (MSE) penalty== to prevent reward hacking.

## Results

- UniGRPO achieved the highest performance across all evaluated methods, scoring **0.8381** on the Text Alignment benchmark and **0.90** on GenEval, outperforming baselines and isolated optimization approaches.
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated UniGRPO's ability to generate photorealistic and finely detailed images that accurately reflect complex prompts, and to optimize reasoning traces to be purposeful and task-oriented.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the proposed velocity-based MSE regularization ensured stable training and high-fidelity images, while CFG-free training provided comparable or superior final performance against standard CFG-enabled training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing both reasoning (text) and visual synthesis policies within a unified RL framework yields greater performance gains compared to optimizing either component in isolation.
> - Training without Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) can simplify the computational pipeline and support scalability for multi-round scenarios without compromising final performance when CFG is applied at evaluation time.
> - Velocity-based MSE regularization on unweighted velocity fields provides superior robustness against reward hacking and ensures stable training dynamics for flow-matching models in an RL setting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI is evolving towards unified multimodal models capable of interleaved generation, where models can alternate between generating different modalities, such as text and images, within a single interaction. This paradigm holds the potential to leverage iterative reasoning for complex tasks, allowing models to refine prompts, generate outputs, and reflect across multiple rounds. A architectural synergy has emerged, often combining Autoregressive (AR) models for text generation with Flow Matching models for visual synthesis. This combination aims to integrate the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the high-fidelity generation strengths of flow-based models.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become a established technique for aligning and refining both LLMs and text-to-image (T2I) models. For LLMs, methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are used for tasks such as alignment and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. In the context of T2I models, RL approaches like reward-driven optimization, Reward Weighted Regression (RWR), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and PPO-style policy gradients are employed to align models with human preferences. Adapting these RL paradigms to the deterministic Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) of flow matching architectures has led to developments like FlowGRPO, which reformulates generation into a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) to enable exploration.

Previous efforts to unify multimodal capabilities have explored various strategies, including vector quantization of visual signals for shared autoregressive training (e.g., Chameleon, Emu3) or combining autoregressive and diffusion objectives within a single transformer (e.g., Show-o, Transfusion). Models like Bagel and Mogao have scaled this hybrid approach with interleaved multimodal data. However, a significant challenge remains in developing a unified RL framework that can jointly optimize both discrete language actions and continuous visual actions within a single, coherent optimization loop, especially for multi-round interleaved generation.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing UniGRPO, a unified RL framework that integrates existing GRPO formulations for text and FlowGRPO for images into a single Markov Decision Process. It aims to provide a scalable and robust approach for post-training fully interleaved models, distinguishing itself from concurrent works that often employ separate models, disjoint optimization loops, or focus on benchmark-specific prompts. UniGRPO's focus on a single unified model trained with general-purpose prompts and its specific algorithmic adaptations for scalability position it as an advancement in the field of reasoning-driven visual generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a unified Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework capable of jointly optimizing both text and image generation policies for interleaved multimodal generation. The authors propose UniGRPO to achieve this.

The motivation for this research stems from several factors:
1. **Leveraging Iterative Reasoning:** The emerging paradigm of unified multimodal models offers the potential to use iterative reasoning, where a model refines prompts, generates images, and reflects on outputs across multiple rounds to solve complex image synthesis tasks. This requires an integrated approach to optimize the entire multimodal interaction.
2. **Architectural Synergy:** The community is converging on autoregressive (AR) models for text generation and Flow Matching models for visual synthesis. This combination brings together the reasoning strengths of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the high-fidelity generation capabilities of flow-based models. A unified optimization framework is needed to effectively combine these distinct but complementary modalities.
3. **Fundamental Unit Validation:** Rather than immediately tackling long-horizon, multi-turn generation, the authors chose to validate their framework on a fundamental unit of interleaved generation: a single round of reasoning-driven image generation. This "Prompt → Thinking → Image" sequence encompasses both text and image generation, serving as a principled testbed to develop and validate the core unified RL framework in the absence of fully open-source interleaved models.
4. **Scalability to Future Complex Scenarios:** A key motivation for the design choices within UniGRPO is to ensure scalability to future multi-round and multi-condition generation scenarios, such as complex image editing or multi-turn interactive dialogues. This necessitates specific modifications to standard RL training recipes to maintain computational efficiency and stability. Specifically, the authors aim to address the computational and gradient complexity introduced by techniques like Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and the challenges of reward hacking in visual RL.
5. **Enhanced Generation Quality:** The underlying motivation is to improve the quality of image generation by making the reasoning process more informative and aligned with the visual synthesis. By jointly optimizing both components, the framework aims to encourage the model to produce more effective reasoning texts that directly lead to higher-quality and more faithful image outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniGRPO formulates the entire "Prompt → Thinking → Image" generation sequence as a single Markov Decision Process (MDP) to jointly optimize discrete language actions (text generation) and continuous visual actions (image synthesis).

#### 3.1. Multimodal Generation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP)

The interleaved generation process is modeled as an MDP (S, A, P, R):
- **State Space (S):**
 - In the text generation phase, the state `s_txt_k` comprises the initial prompt `c` and all previously generated reasoning tokens `y_<k`.
 - In the image generation phase, the state `s_img_k` includes the initial prompt `c`, the completed reasoning trace `y`, the noisy image latent `x_t_k`, and the current flow time `t_k`.
- **Action Space (A):**
 - In the text phase, an action `a_txt_k` is a single token selected from the vocabulary `V`.
 - In the image phase, an action `a_img_k` is the denoised latent `x_t_k-Δt` at the next flow step, residing in a continuous space `R^d`.
- **Transition (P):** Both transitions are deterministic given the action: appending a token to the sequence for text, and advancing the latent from `x_t_k` to `x_t_k-Δt` for images.
- **Reward (R):** A sparse terminal reward `R(x_0, c)` is provided only after the image latent has been fully denoised to `x_0`. All intermediate steps receive a zero reward.

#### 3.2. UniGRPO Framework

The framework employs a unified model `π_θ` for interleaved generation. For a given prompt `c`, UniGRPO samples `G` reasoning chains `{y_i}` via `π_θ(a_txt_k | s_txt_k)`. Each reasoning chain then conditions the same model to generate a corresponding image trajectory `{x_i}` via `π_θ(a_img_k | s_img_k)` using a hybrid SDE-ODE integrator. Group-relative advantages `ˆA_i` are computed based on the terminal rewards of the completed multimodal trajectories. These advantages are then used to update `π_θ` through a unified objective function: `J = J_Text + λ J_Flow`, where `λ` is a hyperparameter for balancing objectives (set to 1 in experiments).

#### 3.3. Integration of Standard Components

- **Text GRPO:** For the autoregressive text component, the standard GRPO formulation is adopted. It computes advantages relatively within a group of `G` samples and optimizes an objective that maximizes expected reward while constraining policy updates via importance sampling clipping and a KL divergence penalty against a reference policy `π_ref`.
- **Flow GRPO:** For the visual component, FlowGRPO is utilized. It adapts RL to flow matching models by converting the deterministic ODE into a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) for exploration. A hybrid sampling strategy (SDE for a continuous time window, ODE for others) is used for efficiency. The objective `J_Flow` is computed over the SDE timestep subset `T_SDE`, incorporating importance ratio clipping and a KL penalty. Ratio Normalization (RatioNorm) is applied to standardize log-importance ratios, mitigating issues with inconsistent variance across timesteps that can lead to reward hacking.

#### 3.4. Critical Modifications for Scalability

To ensure scalability to multi-round and multi-condition scenarios, two modifications are introduced:
1. **Eliminating Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):**
 - **Motivation:** CFG typically requires multiple model evaluations per step (conditional and unconditional), scaling poorly with multi-conditions (e.g., image editing requires 3+ evaluations) and creating branched computation graphs that complicate gradient estimation in an RL setting.
 - **Approach:** UniGRPO is trained entirely without CFG, enforcing a linear, unbranched rollout.
 - **Compensation:** The RL framework explicitly maximizes the reward (which evaluates text-image alignment and visual quality), thereby internalizing alignment capabilities directly into the policy weights without needing CFG at training time. CFG is still applied at evaluation time for all settings.
2. **Velocity-Based Regularization:**
 - **Motivation:** The standard latent KL divergence penalty in the SDE formulation evaluates to the squared difference in predicted velocities weighted by the inverse noise variance (`1/σ^2_t_k`). This creates an uneven penalty across the generative trajectory, with smaller penalties at high noise variances, leading to temporal vulnerabilities that the RL optimizer can exploit (reward hacking).
 - **Approach:** The standard latent KL penalty is replaced with a Mean Squared Error (MSE) penalty applied directly on the *unweighted* velocity fields: `L_MSE(θ) = ||v_θ(x_t_k, t_k, y) - v_ref(x_t_k, t_k, y)||^2`.
 - **Benefit:** This unweighted formulation forces the RL-tuned vector field to remain uniformly close to the pre-trained reference model across all noise levels, providing a more robust and consistent constraint against reward hacking and preserving the base model’s generative priors.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Pretrained Model:** Bagel, initially further fine-tuned with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on an internal dataset to establish a strong baseline.
- **Reward Model:** A differentiable reward model based on InternVL, fine-tuned on user preference data, designed to measure image-prompt consistency. This choice allows for fair comparison with gradient-based baselines.
- **Baselines:** ReFL, FPO, FlowGRPO, TextGRPO, and hybrid approaches like "ReFL w/ Thinking" and "ReFL (w/ Thinking) + TextGRPO," and UniFPO.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Text Alignment (TA) Benchmark:** An internal set of 150 diverse prompts, evaluated by a VLM that assesses image outputs against multiple prompt-specific exam points (binary scoring).
 - **GenEval:** A standard benchmark for compositional capabilities in T2I models (e.g., object counting, spatial relations, attribute binding).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of UniGRPO in enhancing reasoning-driven visual generation.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance

- **Baseline Improvement:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on Bagel significantly improved its base capabilities, evidenced by its higher scores compared to the vanilla Bagel model.
- **UniGRPO Superiority:** UniGRPO achieved the highest performance across all evaluated methods, scoring 0.8381 on the Text Alignment (TA) benchmark and 0.90 on GenEval.
- **Stability of GRPO:** UniFPO, a unified framework analogous to UniGRPO but using the FPO objective for image synthesis, failed to converge during training, which highlights the stability advantages of the GRPO-based formulation for the visual component.
- **Joint Optimization vs. Isolated:** A comparison between UniGRPO, FlowGRPO, and TextGRPO indicated that jointly optimizing both the reasoning and visual synthesis policies yielded greater performance gains than optimizing either component in isolation. For instance, UniGRPO (0.8381 TA, 0.90 GenEval) surpassed FlowGRPO with thinking (0.8208 TA, 0.86 GenEval) and TextGRPO (0.8078 TA, 0.88 GenEval).
- **Effect of Reasoning Chains:** While enabling explicit reasoning chains ("Thinking") on the vanilla Bagel model did not consistently improve GenEval scores (attributed to its reasoning module being primarily for knowledge-based reasoning rather than short prompt rewriting), UniGRPO successfully leveraged the reasoning chain to achieve state-of-the-art performance, demonstrating its ability to optimize this multimodal interaction.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Image Quality Progression:**
 - The original Bagel model tended to produce images with oversaturated colors and noticeable synthetic artifacts.
 - SFT helped mitigate these synthetic artifacts but sometimes resulted in images with reduced sharpness or blurriness.
 - UniGRPO further enhanced both aesthetic quality and text-image alignment, generating photorealistic and finely detailed images that accurately reflected complex user prompts.
- **Reasoning Process:**
 - The base Bagel and SFT models generated detailed reasoning texts, but these traces could sometimes become unfocused or disconnected from the core visual generation task.
 - UniGRPO optimized the reasoning phase to be purposeful and task-oriented. By explicitly aligning the thought process with the final visual reward, UniGRPO produced reasoning traces that were tightly coupled with and effectively guided the subsequent image synthesis, leading to more coherent and accurate outputs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Impact of CFG-Free Training:**
 - Experiments showed that while using CFG during training initially yielded higher rewards during training steps, removing CFG entirely resulted in comparable or superior final performance when CFG was applied at evaluation time.
 - This finding suggests that CFG is not necessary for RL-based alignment during training, simplifying the computational pipeline and supporting the choice of linear, unbranched rollouts for scalability.
- **Regularization Strategies:**
 - **No Regularization:** Training without any KL penalty led to reward hacking, where metrics increased but image quality degraded, often producing unnatural or oversaturated textures.
 - **Latent KL (Standard Practice):** This strategy exhibited unstable training dynamics. Even with a sufficiently large KL coefficient, grid-like artifacts emerged early in training, indicating that the method was vulnerable to exploitation.
 - **Velocity MSE (Proposed):** Applying an MSE penalty directly on the unweighted velocity fields ensured stable training dynamics and produced high-fidelity images with realistic textures. This method demonstrated superior robustness against reward hacking by uniformly constraining the RL-tuned vector field to remain close to the pre-trained reference model across all noise levels.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniGRPO represents a significant step in the development of unified multimodal generative models, particularly those that integrate reasoning capabilities with visual synthesis. Its contributions have several implications and potential impacts:

1. **Unified Multimodal Optimization:** The framework provides a method for jointly optimizing policies across fundamentally different action spaces—discrete for language and continuous for images—within a single, coherent Reinforcement Learning loop. This addresses a core challenge in building truly interleaved multimodal agents.
2. **Scalability to Complex Interactions:** The critical design choices of eliminating Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) during training and employing velocity-based regularization are vital for scalability. By enforcing linear, unbranched rollouts, the framework reduces the computational and memory burden that would otherwise compound in multi-turn interactions or multi-condition generation scenarios (e.g., complex image editing). The robust regularization strategy also mitigates reward hacking, ensuring stable learning as models scale to more complex tasks and longer horizons.
3. **Enhanced Reasoning-Driven Generation:** The empirical results demonstrate that UniGRPO effectively leverages chain-of-thought reasoning to improve image generation quality. By optimizing the reasoning process to be task-oriented and tightly coupled with visual synthesis, the model generates more accurate, photorealistic, and contextually relevant images from complex prompts. This suggests an avenue for generative models to interpret and respond to user intent with greater fidelity.
4. **Robust Baseline for Future Research:** UniGRPO establishes a robust and scalable baseline for the post-training of future fully interleaved models. Its minimalist approach, integrating established training recipes with targeted modifications, offers a principled foundation upon which more advanced multimodal RL systems can be built.
5. **Future Directions:** The paper identifies two promising directions:
 - **Multi-Round Interleaved Generation:** Extending UniGRPO to complex, multi-turn interactions (e.g., interactive image editing, visual storytelling) could unlock new capabilities for generative AI, enabling sustained, context-aware dialogues and iterative refinement of multimodal outputs. The CFG-free approach is particularly advantageous here.
 - **Multimodal Process Reward Models (PRMs):** Developing PRMs that provide dense, intermediate feedback on reasoning steps (rather than just sparse terminal rewards) could significantly improve sample efficiency in RL training. This would allow for better credit assignment, ensure the logical soundness of generated "thoughts," and enhance the interpretability and alignment of the model's decision-making process.

Overall, UniGRPO advances the state of unified multimodal generation by providing a practical, scalable, and effective RL framework that bridges the gap between language reasoning and visual synthesis, paving the way for more intelligent and interactive generative AI systems.
