# Certified Gradient-Based Contact-Rich Manipulation via Smoothing-Error Reachable Tubes

> [!summary] Summary
> Georgia Institute of Technology researchers introduce a method for certified robust control of contact-rich manipulation by quantifying smoothing errors between regularized and true hybrid dynamics. It produces affine feedback policies that guarantee 0.0% constraint satisfaction and enable deployment on hardware for complex tasks.

## Problem

- Gradient-based trajectory optimization methods struggle with contact-rich systems due to nonsmoothness in hybrid dynamics, leading to discontinuous or vanishing gradients.
- Smoothing contact dynamics to obtain informative gradients introduces model mismatch with the true nonsmooth dynamics, causing failures (e.g., premature contact loss) when policies are deployed.
- Existing heuristic approaches (like Contact Trust Regions) lack formal guarantees on constraint satisfaction and performance for the actual, nonsmooth physical system.

## Method

- Employs ==differentiable simulation== using convex formulations of contact dynamics, allowing for a smoothed version (fκ) and the original nonsmooth version (f₀) amenable to gradient computation.
- Derives a tight, analytical, and differentiable bound for the deviation (==smoothing error==) between fκ and f₀, which is then propagated as a set-valued disturbance through the system.
- Synthesizes robust affine feedback policies within a ==Sequential Convex Programming== framework, utilizing **smoothing-error reachable tubes** based on the propagated smoothing error to guarantee constraint satisfaction for the true nonsmooth system.

## Results

- Closed-loop rollouts of the nonsmooth hybrid dynamics consistently remained within the predicted reachable tubes across simulation and hardware experiments (e.g., bimanual planar bucket manipulation).
- Achieved a **0.0%** constraint violation rate in all tested scenarios, significantly outperforming a TO-CTR baseline which experienced **1.2% to 3.1%** violations, while maintaining competitive goal-reaching accuracy.
- Successfully scaled to high-dimensional systems, synthesizing a policy for in-hand cube reorientation (**22 DoF** state, **16 DoF** control) in **610 seconds**, demonstrating applicability to complex dexterous manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The model mismatch between smoothed and true nonsmooth contact dynamics can be analytically bounded and propagated as a set-valued disturbance.
> - Integrating these smoothing-error reachable tubes into an optimization problem allows for certified robust constraint satisfaction and goal attainment for the actual nonsmooth system.
> - Optimizing for affine feedback policies provides robustness to uncertainties and enables more efficient online deployment compared to open-loop trajectory optimization requiring constant replanning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Contact-rich manipulation, involving objects whose motion is influenced by discrete contact events, presents significant challenges in robotics. Existing approaches, such as reinforcement learning (RL) and zero-order planning methods (e.g., CEM, CMA-ES, MPPI), have demonstrated success but are often characterized by low sample efficiency. These methods typically require extensive trial-and-error to learn the underlying physics.

In contrast, gradient-based trajectory optimization methods, leveraging differentiable simulation, offer the potential for greater sample efficiency by exploiting the physical structure of the problem. However, these methods encounter fundamental difficulties when applied to contact-rich systems. Hybrid contact dynamics inherently introduce nonsmoothness, leading to discontinuous or vanishing gradients. For instance, before an effector makes contact with an object, its actions may not affect the object's state, resulting in zero gradients that provide no guidance for optimization. While differentiable simulators exist that can model contact, the practical utility of the gradients they produce for manipulation tasks remains limited due to these issues.

Previous attempts to address vanishing gradients in RL have involved stochasticity or domain randomization, which can be viewed as forms of randomized smoothing. This has led to the exploration of analytical smoothing, where contact dynamics are explicitly regularized to yield continuous and informative gradients. However, a significant drawback of planning with naively smoothed dynamics is the introduction of model mismatch. This mismatch can lead to failures, such as premature contact loss, when controllers are deployed on real systems, particularly due to the unilateral nature of contact (forces can only be applied in one direction). Prior work has tried to mitigate this with local contact trust regions (CTRs), but these are often heuristic and lack formal guarantees on model accuracy.

The current research aims to bridge this gap by offering a method that exploits the informative gradients of smoothed dynamics while formally quantifying and compensating for the resulting model mismatch. This work integrates differentiable simulation with principles from set-valued robust control to provide formal guarantees on safety and task completion under the original, nonsmooth hybrid dynamics. It builds upon convex formulations of contact dynamics that ensure single-valued solutions, thus providing more consistent gradients than those derived from nonlinear complementarity programs (NCPs). Unlike many trajectory optimization methods that produce open-loop trajectories requiring continuous replanning, this work focuses on synthesizing affine feedback policies, which are more robust to disturbances and enable online deployment without repeated optimization. This positions the work as an advancement in developing reliable and efficient gradient-based control strategies for complex, contact-rich robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to resolve the fundamental trade-off between the computational efficiency offered by gradient-based optimization on smoothed dynamics and the need for formal safety and task completion guarantees with respect to the original, nonsmooth hybrid dynamics in contact-rich manipulation.

The motivation stems from the limitations of existing approaches:
- **Gradient-based methods are efficient but fragile**: While gradient-based trajectory optimization can efficiently exploit the physical structure of a problem through differentiable simulation, the hybrid nature of contact dynamics (discrete changes in contact state) leads to nonsmoothness. This causes gradients to be discontinuous or to vanish in significant regions of the state-action space, rendering them uninformative for optimization.
- **Smoothing introduces model mismatch**: To overcome the nonsmoothness, dynamics can be smoothed. This regularization restores informative gradients, making optimization tractable. However, the smoothed model deviates from the true nonsmooth dynamics. If controllers are designed purely based on the smoothed model, this model mismatch can lead to failures when deployed on physical systems, particularly due to the unilateral constraints inherent in contact.
- **Lack of formal guarantees**: Prior heuristic attempts, like Contact Trust Regions (CTR), lack a formal characterization of the allowed deviation to ensure accuracy, meaning that constraint satisfaction cannot be guaranteed for the true system.

To address these challenges, the authors define two specific problems:

1. **Problem 1: Reachability for contact dynamics**: This objective is to develop a method for finding a tight, efficiently computable, and differentiable bound on the smoothing error—the deviation between the smoothed dynamics, $f_\kappa(x, u)$, and the true nonsmooth hybrid dynamics, $f_0(x, u)$. This error must then be propagated through the system dynamics to obtain reachable state and control tubes, denoted as $\{R_x_k\}_{k=0}^N$ and $\{R_u_k\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$. These tubes are intended to formally guarantee that the actual executed trajectory, evolving under the nonsmooth dynamics, remains within these predicted bounds and satisfies predefined state and control constraints.

2. **Problem 2: Robust feedback motion planning**: This objective involves optimizing both a nominal state and control trajectory ($z$, $v$) that is feasible under the $\kappa$-smoothed dynamics, and a causal affine feedback controller $\pi$. The controller is designed to stabilize the nonsmooth contact dynamics ($f_0$) around the nominal trajectory and ensure robust satisfaction of all state and control constraints for the closed-loop hybrid system. This aims to enable online deployment without requiring repeated replanning, a common limitation of open-loop trajectory optimization.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to develop a robust and certifiable control strategy for complex robotic manipulation tasks that involve rich contact, combining the benefits of gradient-based optimization with formal assurances of performance and safety in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates differentiable simulation, a novel smoothing-error bounding technique, and set-valued robust control within a Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) framework to synthesize robust affine feedback policies for contact-rich manipulation.

**Overall Approach:**
The core idea is to leverage the informative gradients provided by a smoothed version of the contact dynamics for efficient trajectory optimization. Simultaneously, the inherent model mismatch introduced by smoothing is rigorously quantified and propagated as a set-valued disturbance. This disturbance is then used within a robust control framework to compute reachable tubes for the actual nonsmooth system, thereby ensuring constraint satisfaction and goal attainment with formal guarantees.

**A. Differentiable Simulator with Contact Smoothing:**
1. **Contact Model**: The approach adopts a quasistatic model of contact dynamics, suitable for manipulation tasks where transient accelerations are brief. This model, based on convex formulations, ensures single-valued solutions for contact forces, which is crucial for gradient consistency.
 - **Nonsmooth Dynamics ($f_0$)**: Defined implicitly as the argmin of a convex conic program (Equation 4), which incorporates conditions like non-penetration and Coulomb friction.
 - **Smoothed Dynamics ($f_\kappa$)**: Derived by relaxing the complementarity condition in the nonsmooth formulation (e.g., $s \circ z = 0$) to a non-zero value ($\kappa > 0$, specifically $s \circ z = \kappa e$). This is equivalent to solving a convex barrier-penalized problem (Equation 5).
2. **Differentiability**: The formulation of contact dynamics as convex conic programs allows for implicit differentiation through their Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions. This enables the computation of gradients of the smoothed dynamics with respect to states ($\partial x^+_\kappa / \partial x$), controls ($\partial x^+_\kappa / \partial u$), and crucially, with respect to the smoothing parameter $\kappa$ ($\partial \lambda_{\kappa,i} / \partial \kappa$). The latter is essential for quantifying the smoothing error.
3. **Contact Geometry Smoothing**: Beyond contact dynamics, the geometry of contact primitives (e.g., sphere-polyhedron interactions) can also introduce nonsmoothness (discontinuous surface normals). The method addresses this by formulating contact point queries as quadratic programs (QPs) and solving a relaxed version. While this smoothing improves gradient quality, its induced error is acknowledged but not formally incorporated into the main smoothing-error bound, leaving it for future work.

**B. Contact Dynamics Smoothing-Error Bound:**
1. **Quantification**: A key contribution is the derivation of a tight analytical bound for the deviation between the smoothed dynamics ($f_\kappa$) and the nonsmooth dynamics ($f_0$). Theorem 1 states this relationship: $f_0(x, u) = f_\kappa(x, u) - P(x)^{-1} \sum_i J_i(x)^T (\partial \lambda_{\kappa,i} / \partial \kappa) \kappa w_i$, where $w_i \in$.
2. **Set-Valued Disturbance**: This equation is compactly written as $x^+ = f_\kappa(x, u) + E_\kappa(x, u)w$, where $w \in^{n_c}$ (for $n_c$ contact pairs). The term $E_\kappa(x, u)$ represents the state- and control-dependent smoothing error. The bounds for $w_i$ (between 1 and 2) naturally capture the unilateral nature of contact. Both $f_\kappa$ and $E_\kappa$ are continuously differentiable, providing the necessary gradient information.

**C. Trajectory Optimization and Policy Synthesis:**
1. **Problem Formulation**: The goal is to optimize a nominal trajectory $(z, v)$ using the *smoothed dynamics* (Equation 8) and synthesize a causal affine feedback policy. An affine feedback policy takes the form $u_k = v_k + \sum_{j=0}^k K_{kj}(x_j - z_j)$, where $K_{kj}$ are feedback gains.
2. **Reachable Tube Prediction (Theorem 2)**: For the linearized dynamics (around the nominal trajectory), Theorem 2 establishes that the closed-loop behavior of the *nonsmooth hybrid dynamics* under such affine feedback policies can be analytically predicted as a tube: $x = z + \Phi_x w$ and $u = v + \Phi_u w$. Here, $w \in^{N \cdot n_c}$ (where $N$ is the horizon) aggregates the per-timestep smoothing errors. The matrices $\Phi_x$ and $\Phi_u$ capture how these errors propagate through the system, defined by recursive relations involving the linearized dynamics ($A_k$, $B_k$) and the disturbance matrix $E_k$. Importantly, a feedback policy $K = \Phi_u (\Phi_x)^{-1}$ achieves this desired closed-loop response.
3. **Robust Optimization Problem (Equation 12)**: The final optimization problem jointly minimizes a cost function (nominal trajectory cost for goal reaching and smooth controls, plus a tube cost to penalize tube size) subject to:
 - Nominal dynamics evolving under $f_\kappa$.
 - Disturbance propagation dynamics (from Theorem 2).
 - **Robustified constraints**: State and control constraints (e.g., joint limits, non-collision, object position) are robustly enforced. This is achieved by taking the worst-case scenario for $x_k$ and $u_k$ within their respective reachable tubes. Specifically, for a linear constraint $G_k[x_k; u_k] + g_k \leq 0$, the constraint is modified to account for the maximum possible deviation induced by the uncertainty $w \in^{N \cdot n_c}$. This involves adding a term $w_r \|G_k[\Phi_{x_k,j}; \Phi_{u_k,j}]\|_{row,q}$ to the nominal constraint, where $w_c = 1.5$ and $w_r = 0.5$ for the $p=\infty$ norm ball for $w$, resulting in $q=1$ for the row-wise $q$-norm (L1-norm).

**D. Solver Algorithm (Sequential Convex Programming - SCP):**
1. **Iterative Solution**: The robust optimization problem (Equation 12) is non-convex. It is solved using an SCP approach, iteratively linearizing the dynamics and constraints around the current nominal trajectory.
2. **Convex Subproblem**: At each iteration, a convex optimization problem (Equation 13) is solved. This subproblem optimizes for small deviations ($\delta z$, $\delta v$) from the nominal trajectory, alongside the tube propagation matrices ($\Phi_x$, $\Phi_u$). Trust region constraints ($||\delta z|| \leq \epsilon, ||\delta v|| \leq \epsilon$) are applied to ensure linearization validity.
3. **Specialized Solver**: A specialized solver (detailed in Appendix E) is employed to efficiently solve the convex subproblem. It partitions the KKT conditions into two subsets:
 - **Nominal Trajectory Optimization**: Solves for $y = (\delta z, \delta v)$ by solving a quadratic program (QP).
 - **Uncertainty Tube Optimization**: Solves for $\Phi_x, \Phi_u$ using N independent Riccati recursions, leveraging the problem's structure.
 This alternating optimization procedure continues until convergence.

This comprehensive methodology allows for efficient gradient-based policy synthesis that respects the complexities of hybrid contact dynamics while providing formal guarantees on the behavior of the true nonsmooth system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates its method on several contact-rich manipulation tasks in both simulation and hardware, focusing on five key research questions (RQs).

**RQ1: Do closed-loop rollouts of the nonsmooth hybrid dynamics remain within the predicted tube?**
- **Bimanual Planar Bucket Manipulation (Hardware & Simulation)**: The method largely validates RQ1. In hardware rollouts, the bucket's state remains within the computed tubes despite sim-to-real modeling errors (e.g., mass, joint stiffness). Any observed deviation is attributed to perception noise, which could be addressed by inflating the bounds. In simulation, this behavior is consistently observed.
- **In-hand Cube Reorientation (Simulation)**: For this high-dimensional task (22 DoF state, 16 DoF control), the true closed-loop trajectory robustly remains within the predicted reachable tubes, providing further validation for RQ1.
- **Bimanual Planar Box Manipulation (without geometry smoothing)**: The rollouts remain within the tubes.
- **Bimanual Planar Box Manipulation (with geometry smoothing)**: The closed-loop rollouts do not strictly stay within the tubes because the error from geometry smoothing is not fully accounted for in the core error bound $E_\kappa$. However, this deviation is observed to be a small, non-cumulative offset, rather than a growing error.

**RQ2: Does our method achieve lower goal-reaching error than the baseline while strictly enforcing constraints?**
- **Bimanual Planar Bucket Manipulation (Simulation vs. TO-CTR baseline)**:
 - **Goal-reaching accuracy**: Without replanning, the proposed method achieves lower average goal position (0.1447m vs. 0.1522m) and orientation (0.0946 rad vs. 0.1034 rad) errors compared to the TO-CTR baseline. The improvement is modest, attributed to the planning horizon not being long enough for smoothing errors to significantly invalidate TO-CTR trajectories.
 - **Constraint satisfaction**: Crucially, the proposed method achieved a **0.0% constraint violation rate** in all scenarios (without replanning and with MPC). In contrast, the TO-CTR baseline experienced 3.1% (without replanning) and 1.2% (with MPC) constraint violations, leading to robot collisions or the object falling off the table. This confirms that the method provides certified constraint satisfaction while improving goal-reaching accuracy. A comparison with a method using exact dynamics in the forward pass but smoothed gradients in the backward pass (similar to) also showed constraint violations, indicating its insufficiency.

**RQ3: Does performance degrade due to strict constraint enforcement?**
- **Bimanual Planar Bucket Manipulation**: No, strict constraint enforcement does not degrade performance. The proposed method achieved a lower nominal trajectory cost (299.43 vs. 313.51 for TO-CTR without replanning), indicating reduced control effort and lower state cost. This suggests that the tube-based reasoning allows the optimizer to select contact sequences less conservatively than the heuristic CTR, which might be overly conservative and prematurely mark transitions as infeasible.

**RQ4: Is contact geometry smoothing necessary in practice?**
- **Bimanual Planar Box Manipulation**: Yes, for objects with discontinuous surface normals. Without geometry smoothing, the method exhibited unstable gradients, leading to poor task performance (object failing to reach the goal, even moving further away). With geometry smoothing, a solution much closer to the target (9 mm error) was found, demonstrating its practical necessity for such objects.

**RQ5: Can our method scale to high-dimensional systems?**
- **In-hand Cube Reorientation (22 DoF state, 16 DoF control)**: Yes, the method successfully scaled to this challenging dexterous manipulation task. It synthesized a policy in 610 seconds, demonstrating its applicability to high-dimensional, contact-rich systems while maintaining reachability-based guarantees.

**Ablation Study on Smoothing Parameter ($\kappa$)**:
- The study confirmed that tube size scales proportionally with $\kappa$.
- Small $\kappa$ values hinder convergence to effective contact mode sequences, resulting in larger goal errors.
- Sufficiently large $\kappa$ values allow the solver to converge to favorable contact modes, yielding smaller goal errors that vary monotonically with $\kappa$.
- An optimal $\kappa$ must exceed a task-dependent threshold for reliable convergence while remaining small enough to prevent unnecessarily large reachable tubes.

In summary, the research demonstrates a method that produces robust affine feedback policies for contact-rich manipulation with certified constraint satisfaction under nonsmooth hybrid dynamics. It achieves this by effectively bounding and propagating smoothing errors and by leveraging smoothed contact dynamics for efficient gradient-based optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving rich contact interactions. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Smoothness-Guarantees Gap**: The work addresses a fundamental challenge in gradient-based control: how to leverage the computational efficiency of differentiable simulation on smoothed dynamics while retaining formal guarantees for the actual nonsmooth physical system. By explicitly quantifying and compensating for smoothing error via set-valued reachable tubes, the method offers a principled solution to this long-standing trade-off.

2. **Formal Certifications for Safety and Reliability**: A key impact is the provision of formal, certified guarantees on constraint satisfaction and task completion under the true nonsmooth hybrid dynamics. This is crucial for deploying robotic systems in real-world scenarios, where safety and reliability are paramount. The demonstrated 0.0% constraint violation rate, compared to baselines, underscores this strength.

3. **Efficient Robust Control Synthesis**: The method produces affine feedback policies that are robust to model mismatch and uncertainties. Unlike open-loop control or frequent Model Predictive Control (MPC) replanning, these synthesized feedback policies allow for significantly cheaper online execution. This makes robust feedback synthesis practical for complex, high-dimensional, contact-rich systems, a domain traditionally challenging for formal control methods.

4. **Scalability to Complex Tasks**: The successful application to high-dimensional dexterous in-hand manipulation (22 DoF state, 16 DoF control) demonstrates the scalability of the approach. This broadens the applicability of gradient-based optimization with formal guarantees to increasingly complex robotic manipulation tasks that involve intricate contact sequences.

5. **Theoretical Contributions**: The derivation of tight analytical bounds on smoothing error (Theorem 1) and the formulation of tube-valued predictions for nonsmooth dynamics under feedback control (Theorem 2) represent significant theoretical contributions. These insights provide a foundation for understanding and managing model uncertainties in hybrid systems.

6. **Insights into Contact Modeling**: The research highlights the practical importance of smoothing not only contact dynamics but also contact geometry for objects with discontinuous surface normals. This provides valuable guidance for developing more robust differentiable simulators and contact models.

**Potential Impact**:
- **Real-world Robotics Deployment**: By offering certified performance and safety, the method could accelerate the deployment of robots in unstructured and dynamic environments where contact is an inherent part of the task (e.g., assembly, logistics, hazardous material handling).
- **Advanced Manipulation Capabilities**: Enabling reliable control for complex, dexterous, and bimanual manipulation tasks opens doors for robots to perform more intricate and human-like interactions with objects.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The framework provides a strong foundation for future work, including incorporating other sources of uncertainty (e.g., parametric uncertainty, perception noise), exploring more robust optimization techniques (e.g., direct transcription, stochastic exploration), and extending the approach to fully dynamic manipulation or tool-use scenarios. The identified areas of limitation also serve as clear directions for advancing the field.

In conclusion, this work represents a substantial step towards realizing reliable, efficient, and certifiably safe autonomous manipulation in contact-rich environments, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with gradient-based control methods in robotics.
