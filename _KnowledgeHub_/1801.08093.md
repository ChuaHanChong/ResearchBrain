# Learning Symmetric and Low-energy Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a deep reinforcement learning framework that synthesizes robust, natural-looking locomotion controllers for virtual characters. The method produces symmetric and low-energy gaits across diverse morphologies, achieving this through a novel mirror symmetry loss and a curriculum learning approach with modulated physical assistance.

## Problem

- Standard deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for locomotion often yields jerky, high-frequency, excessively energetic, and visibly asymmetric motions, even for symmetric characters.
- Traditional character animation methods (motion capture, finite state machines, trajectory optimization) are often limited in their generality across character morphologies or require extensive manual engineering.
- Existing energy penalties in DRL reward functions are typically down-weighted to prevent hindering basic task learning, thus having negligible effect on promoting energy-efficient gaits.

## Method

- The researchers from ==Georgia Institute== of Technology introduce a novel Mirror Symmetry Loss (MSL) added directly to the DRL policy optimization objective, penalizing asymmetric actions produced by the policy.
- They propose a curriculum learning approach that incorporates a "virtual assistant" providing modulated physical assistance, which is gradually removed as the agent improves, allowing for a consistently high energy penalty.
- The framework is built upon Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a gradient-based reinforcement learning algorithm, and learns policies to control joint torques for character locomotion.

## Results

- The method produced significantly lower average actuation magnitudes (e.g., **2.01** for simplified biped walking vs. **5.96** for baseline) and highly symmetric gaits (e.g., Symmetry Index of **0.0026** vs. **0.4982** for biped running).
- It successfully generated robust, natural locomotion controllers for diverse morphologies including bipeds, quadrupeds, and hexapods, with speed-appropriate gait patterns emerging automatically.
- Curriculum learning was crucial for successful policy acquisition, and the Mirror Symmetry Loss consistently improved learning speed and ensured genuinely symmetric motion, demonstrating the effectiveness of each component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gait symmetry can be effectively encouraged by penalizing policy actions directly in the loss function, ratherating than relying on delayed state-based rewards.
> - A gradual curriculum with modulated physical assistance enables deep reinforcement learning to acquire complex locomotion skills while maintaining high energy penalties from the outset.
> - Natural, speed-appropriate gait patterns can emerge autonomously from fundamental biomechanical principles when integrated into a reinforcement learning framework, without explicit motion examples or specific gait planning.
