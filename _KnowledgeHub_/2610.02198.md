# FERPO: Forward Entropy-Regularized Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> FERPO is an on-policy maximum-entropy RL algorithm that improves policies using critic values without differentiating the critic with respect to actions. It derives a closed-form entropy- and KL-regularized target action distribution, then fits the actor with a forward-KL loss estimated by self-normalized importance sampling over rollout-policy actions. It matches or beats REPPO on DMC and ManiSkill, with better sample efficiency and faster updates.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art on-policy methods such as REPPO update actors with critic action gradients, yet critics are trained only to predict returns, not accurate action derivatives.
- Accurate value predictions do not guarantee accurate action derivatives, so biased critic gradients can mislead policy improvement and destabilize training as on-policy data distributions shift.
- Reverse-KL policy projections can collapse onto a subset of the target distribution's modes, missing better distant high-value regions and limiting exploration in continuous control.

## Method

- ==Regularized target distribution==: minimizing an ==entropy- and KL-constrained Lagrangian== yields a closed-form target proportional to exp(Q/(tau+lambda)) times the rollout policy raised to lambda/(tau+lambda).
- ==Forward-KL actor fitting== via ==self-normalized importance sampling==: K actions sampled from the rollout policy get softmax critic-value weights, and the actor maximizes weighted log-likelihood without any critic action gradients.
- ==Lagrange-multiplier control== plus cached updates: temperature tracks a target entropy, ==trust-region coefficient== holds ==normalized effective sample size==, and cached Q values or a one-step-simulation state-value critic cut computation.

## Results

- DMC **22**-task IQM return reaches **897.7**, versus REPPO **891.0**, FastTD3 **890.6**, PPO (Brax) **542.8** and PPO **309.8**; ManiSkill success IQM **95.67%** versus REPPO **95.52%** and PPO **84.61%**.
- At K=16 on an A100, combined actor-and-critic update time is **6.9%** lower than REPPO, and caching raises the saving to **22.2%**, at roughly **22**-26% higher GPU memory.
- Removing entropy regularization drops G1 return from **34.81** to **30.32**; however, FERPO trails REPPO on the G1/T1 locomotion aggregate, **33.22** versus **34.63**, with the gap concentrated on G1.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When critics are trained only on values, use their values rather than their action gradients for policy improvement; derivative accuracy is a separate, unsupervised property.
> - Making the regularized optimal target explicit lets you choose the projection direction; forward KL covers multiple high-value modes where reverse KL can lock onto one locally.
> - A KL trust region does double duty: it keeps the local surrogate valid and keeps importance weights well behaved, best monitored directly through effective sample size.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Online robot learning often improves policies by taking gradients of a learned critic with respect to actions, even though the critic is trained to predict values, not necessarily their derivatives. FERPO takes a different route. It uses critic **values** to reweight sampled actions, then trains the actor to fit those actions. This avoids critic action derivatives, but FERPO still depends on useful critic values and adequate sampling.

A simple two-well example shows how the two approaches can differ even with an exact critic. At reference mean B ($\mu = -0.6$), gradient descent moves REPPO left, while the FERPO target-fit direction moves right, toward a target mean of about −0.466. This is a toy, one-step direction comparison. It is not a general performance guarantee.

At B, the local FERPO direction is rightward and the REPPO direction leftward. The figure uses an exact critic and fixed-width Gaussian policies (σ=0.4). ### 2. From values to a target

The closest precedent is [REPPO (Voelcker et al., ICLR 2026)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/3c2fd72a28fb98facf98546727320249-Abstract-Conference.html). It improves an on-policy actor by differentiating a learned soft Q critic with respect to actions, while limiting policy changes.

FERPO starts from an objective that rewards high critic values while regularizing two things: entropy and deviation from the rollout policy, which is the frozen policy that generated the current batch. From this objective it derives a target action distribution. It then fits the actor to that target with **forward KL**, from target to actor. The authors argue this fit encourages coverage of multiple target modes, unlike reverse-KL fitting, which can favor a subset. Mode coverage is the method’s rationale, not a general empirical guarantee.

In practice, the update works per state:

- For each rollout state, FERPO samples K candidate actions from the rollout policy.
- It weights each candidate using its critic value and its rollout-policy log probability, then normalizes the weights across candidates at that state.
- It trains the actor to assign high likelihood to the weighted actions.

The weights are held fixed during this fit. Gradients update policy log probabilities, not the critic’s action derivative.

A KL multiplier is adjusted using the normalized effective sample size (ESS), a measure of how concentrated the weights are. Low ESS prompts a larger multiplier and flatter weights. ESS is a practical proxy, not a per-state trust-region guarantee. The implemented controller estimates ESS from finite samples and averages it across rollout states.

At B, the expected cost derivative under the rollout Gaussian is 0.284, while the target-weighted derivative is −0.0419. This difference explains the opposite directions. Because the critic is exact and the policy’s standard deviation is fixed, the example isolates how the KL direction can change a local direction. It does not show that FERPO solves the broader problem of inaccurate learned critic derivatives.

### 3. What the benchmarks establish

FERPO is evaluated on 22 DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) tasks, four G1/T1 locomotion tasks, and eight ManiSkill manipulation tasks. Scores are summarized with the interquartile mean (IQM), the middle half of the empirical score distribution. Each environment is weighted equally, and its seeds share that environment’s weight. DMC returns remain in their original units.

At 50 million environment interactions, FERPO’s DMC aggregate IQM return is **897.7**, versus **891.0** for REPPO. The authors’ adjusted pairwise interval for FERPO minus REPPO on DMC includes zero. The small lead therefore does not establish a reliable advantage. On the G1/T1 aggregate at the same budget, FERPO is behind REPPO.

The paper also reports that FERPO reaches high performance on DMC and ManiSkill with fewer environment interactions. This concerns interaction count, not end-to-end training time.

### 4. A different target, a qualified result

FERPO replaces the critic’s action slope with a value-weighted target fitted by forward KL. The toy example shows that this can change an update direction. The benchmarks support competitive performance, not superiority: the DMC lead is within the adjusted interval, and REPPO is higher on G1/T1. Learning still depends on critic-value accuracy and samples that cover the relevant target actions.
