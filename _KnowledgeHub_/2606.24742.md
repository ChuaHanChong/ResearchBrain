# World Value Models for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the World Value Model (WVM), which repurposes spatiotemporal priors from video world models to achieve a generalist robotic value estimator with enhanced temporal understanding. It establishes a new evaluation benchmark, Suboptimal-Value-Bench, demonstrating WVM's superior ability to track task progress in suboptimal scenarios and guide robust policy learning from mixed-quality data.

## Problem

- Existing robotic value models, largely dependent on Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones, struggle with deep temporal understanding and long-term future planning essential for accurate value estimation.
- Value learning is inefficient due to sparse scalar value supervision and limited generalization as models are often customized for specific tasks.
- Current evaluation benchmarks primarily rely on expert trajectories, failing to assess value models' robustness in handling suboptimal robot behaviors like hesitations and retries.

## Method

- The World Value Model (**WVM**) integrates video world models with value estimation, leveraging their spatiotemporal priors for superior temporal understanding and future planning.
- WVM formulates value as a ==distributional value chunk==, trained using flow matching, providing dense training signals and increased expressiveness.
- A new multi-embodiment benchmark, Suboptimal-Value-Bench, is introduced, featuring 800 human-annotated suboptimal trajectories with dedicated metrics (Hesitation-RMSE, Retry-VOC) for rigorous evaluation.

## Results

- WVM achieved state-of-the-art performance on Suboptimal-Value-Bench, with an average Hesitation-RMSE of **0.05** (vs. **0.14** for baselines) and a Retry-VOC of **0.78** (vs. **0.62** for baselines), demonstrating superior handling of suboptimal behaviors.
- On expert trajectories, WVM attained the highest average Value-Order Correlation (VOC) score of **0.95** across various datasets, indicating robust monotonic tracking.
- WVM-guided policy learning (e.g., AWR, Filtered BC) consistently outperformed vanilla Behavioral Cloning across simulated and real-world tasks, validating its utility in improving manipulation performance from mixed-quality data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Repurposing pretrained video world models as feature extractors significantly improves value estimation by providing inherent spatiotemporal understanding and predictive capabilities over VLM-centric approaches.
> - Training with flow matching for distributional value chunks offers denser, more expressive training signals compared to scalar supervision, enhancing the model's ability to capture fine-grained progress differentials.
> - Augmentations like video rewinding and prefix randomization are crucial for improving robustness to suboptimal behaviors and preventing temporal shortcut learning, respectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in scaling robotic policy learning from large, diverse datasets. Generalist value models are positioned as fundamental components for providing learning signals in large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) systems and for filtering offline data. Current robotic value models typically leverage Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones. However, these VLMs are primarily pretrained on static or temporally sparse visual data, which limits their capacity for deep temporal understanding and long-term future planning—capabilities essential for accurate value estimation in dynamic robotic tasks. This limitation manifests as: (1) inefficient value learning due to sparse scalar value supervision, (2) restricted generalization arising from task-specific customizations, and (3) compromised temporal understanding inherent in VLM architectures.

World models, conversely, have demonstrated proficiency in temporal dynamics modeling and future state forecasting across various domains, including video generation and robotic manipulation. Their inherent spatiotemporal understanding and predictive capabilities align with the requirements for a generalist value estimator. This paper introduces a paradigm that repurposes the spatiotemporal priors from world models as a foundation for value learning, diverging from the prevalent VLM-centric approaches. By doing so, it aims to overcome the temporal modeling deficiencies of existing value models and enhance their ability to accurately assess task progression, especially in the presence of suboptimal robot behaviors. The development of a new benchmark, Suboptimal-Value-Bench, also addresses a gap in existing evaluation practices, which predominantly rely on expert trajectories or indirect policy performance metrics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in existing generalist robotic value models, particularly their dependence on Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones. These limitations hinder the accurate and generalizable estimation of task progression, which is crucial for scaling robotic policy learning with large, mixed-quality datasets. The authors identify three specific bottlenecks:

1. **Inefficiencies in value learning:** Current models often rely on scalar value supervision, which provides a sparse and low-information signal for learning from high-dimensional observations. This leads to sample-inefficient training and brittle predictions, especially when applied to heterogeneous video corpora.
2. **Limited generalization:** Many existing value models are customized for specific tasks, restricting their applicability as generalist progress estimators across diverse manipulation scenarios.
3. **Impaired temporal understanding and future planning:** VLM backbones are primarily pretrained on static or temporally sparse images. This optimization for static visual representations means they lack the inherent capabilities for dense temporal modeling and long-term future planning, which are mathematically necessary for accurate value estimation that accounts for historical context and future outcomes.

Driven by these identified shortcomings, the key objectives of this work are:

- **Develop a novel generalist robotic value model (WVM):** The core objective is to construct a World Value Model (WVM) by integrating world models with value estimation. This aims to leverage the rich spatiotemporal priors of pretrained video world models to achieve superior temporal understanding and future planning capabilities compared to VLM-based approaches.
- **Enhance value estimation accuracy and expressiveness:** WVM formulates the value function as a distributional value chunk, trained using flow matching. This objective is designed to provide dense training signals and increased expressiveness, thereby improving value estimation performance beyond traditional scalar supervision and conventional categorical distributions.
- **Improve robustness to suboptimal behaviors:** The model is specifically designed to accurately predict task progress not only in optimal trajectories but also in suboptimal scenarios, such as hesitations and retries. This is achieved through architectural design and training augmentations like video rewinding and prefix randomization.
- **Introduce a comprehensive evaluation benchmark for suboptimal data:** Recognizing the limitations of existing evaluation metrics (e.g., Value-Order Correlation (VOC) primarily for expert data), a critical objective is to introduce Suboptimal-Value-Bench. This new multi-embodiment benchmark, featuring human-annotated suboptimal trajectories, aims to enable a more thorough assessment of value models' ability to detect and quantify deviations from optimal task progression.
- **Demonstrate utility in downstream policy learning:** A final objective is to validate WVM's practical utility by showing that it can robustly guide policy learning from mixed-quality data, leading to improved manipulation performance in both simulated and real-world environments across various policy extraction approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on integrating world models, known for their temporal dynamics modeling, into a framework for generalist robotic value estimation. The proposed World Value Model (WVM) utilizes a novel architecture and training regime complemented by a new evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The authors formulate value estimation as a chunk-wise prediction problem. Given an $h$-frame observation sequence $o_{t-h+1:t}$ and a language instruction $l$, the model predicts a conditional distribution over a length-$h$ sequence of per-frame values $\hat{v}_{t-h+1:t} \in^h$. Here, $v_t = t/T$ represents the normalized task progress. This chunk-wise approach, rather than predicting an isolated scalar, allows WVM to capture local progress profiles like plateaus and regressions, which are crucial for understanding temporal dynamics. The classical reinforcement learning value function, $V(o_t)$, in a sparse-reward setting (where reward is -1 for non-terminal steps and 0 at completion), simplifies to predicting the negative expected distance-to-goal, aligning value estimation with task-progress prediction. This perspective naturally motivates using a video world model $M_\omega$ as a feature extractor.

#### 3.2. WVM Architecture

WVM's architecture (Figure 2 in the paper) instantiates the problem formulation using a video Diffusion-Transformer (DiT) and a value DiT, coupled via a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) mechanism.
- **Video Stream:** WVM employs the video VAE and video DiT from Wan2.2 as its world-modeling backbone. For a value chunk spanning the time window $[t-h+1, t]$, the VAE encodes a longer video clip of length $(2h+1)$, comprising a prefix frame, the current observation frames, and target future frames. The prefix latent is discarded, the current latent serves as context, and the future latent is corrupted for video-generation denoising.
- **Value Stream and MoT Coupling:** A lightweight DiT, with significantly fewer parameters than the video DiT, constitutes the value stream. This stream predicts the value chunk $\hat{v}_{t-h+1:t}$ from noisy value tokens. It integrates with the video stream through MoT multi-modal self-attention. An asymmetric attention mask is applied: value tokens are allowed to attend to current video tokens, but video tokens do not attend to value tokens. This design enables the value stream to capitalize on temporal video features while minimizing interference with the video generation process during training.

#### 3.3. Training

WVM is trained using a combination of a novel objective and specific augmentations.
- **Training Objective:** Flow matching is applied to both supervised video and value tokens. For either future video latents or the value chunk, a velocity predictor $f_\psi$ is trained to predict the velocity field between a noisy sample $y_\tau$ and the true sample $y$, given noise $\epsilon$ and time step $\tau$. The overall objective is a weighted sum of the value flow matching loss ($L_{value}$) and the video flow matching loss ($L_{video}$), with a weighting factor $\lambda$. This distributional formulation provides dense training signals and enhanced expressiveness.
- **Prefix Randomization:** To mitigate the value stream from relying on prefix values as shortcuts during inference with chunk overlapping, a prefix randomization strategy is employed. With a probability $p$, the prefix value is replaced by a random scalar sampled uniformly from $$. Otherwise, the original prefix is retained. The loss is computed only on the non-prefix value tokens. This technique aims to maintain inter-chunk continuity while preventing shortcut learning.
- **Video Rewinding:** To expose the value model to suboptimal behaviors (plateaus, regressions) beyond typical monotonic expert progress, chunk-level rewind augmentation is applied. For each video window, one of three temporal patterns (rising, plateau, or descending) is sampled by preserving, repeating, or reversing frames. The corresponding value chunk $v_{t-h+1:t}$ is relabeled accordingly. This augmentation broadens the model's understanding of diverse progress profiles.

#### 3.4. Suboptimal-Value-Bench

A new benchmark, Suboptimal-Value-Bench, is introduced to evaluate value models on realistic suboptimal segments.
- **Composition:** It comprises 800 human-annotated trajectories across three robot embodiments (AgileX, ARX, RoboSuite) and 15 manipulation tasks. Each trajectory includes dense, frame-level human annotations focusing on two prevalent suboptimal modes: hesitation and retry.
- **Hesitation Segments:** These are periods where the robot remains stationary or performs task-irrelevant micro-movements, resulting in a flat value plateau. Evaluation uses **Hesitation-RMSE**, penalizing prediction drift from the constant ground-truth value.
- **Retry Segments:** These involve failed manipulation attempts followed by retraction, leading to a temporary decrease in task progress. Evaluation uses **Retry-VOC**, which calculates Value-Order Correlation specifically on windows exhibiting monotonically decreasing ground-truth progress.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments focused on evaluating WVM's value prediction quality, its impact on downstream policy learning, and the contribution of its design choices through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Value Estimation Quality

- **Performance on Suboptimal-Value-Bench:**
 - WVM demonstrated superior performance in handling suboptimal behaviors. On Hesitation-RMSE, WVM achieved an average error of 0.05, which is notably lower than the strongest baselines, GVL and Robometer, both at 0.14. This indicates WVM's ability to maintain stable and accurate value estimations during periods of task stagnation.
 - For Retry-VOC, WVM attained the highest average score of 0.78, surpassing the next best baseline (GVL) at 0.62. This suggests WVM effectively tracks and predicts downward trends in task progress during retry attempts.
 - Qualitative comparisons further showed that WVM's detection of hesitation and retry segments aligned closely with human judgment, corroborating its quantitative performance.

- **Performance on Expert-VOC:**
 - On standard expert trajectories, WVM also exhibited strong performance, achieving the highest average Value-Order Correlation (VOC) score of 0.95 across various datasets, compared to 0.88 for the next strongest baseline. It scored 0.99 on self-collected datasets, indicating robust monotonic tracking on clean demonstrations.
 - The only instance where WVM was slightly outperformed was on the EgoDex dataset (0.92 vs. RoboReward's 0.95), which the authors suggest highlights limitations of Expert-VOC as a standalone metric for value models.

#### 4.2. Downstream Policy Learning

- **Policy Improvement:** WVM was integrated into downstream policy learning experiments using π$_{0.5}$-base with suboptimal data (10 trajectories for simulated tasks, 50 for real-world tasks).
 - Three WVM-guided policy variants—Advantage Weighted Regression (AWR), binary Filtered BC (retaining segments with positive advantages), and percentile Filtered BC (preserving top 70% of segments by WVM values)—consistently outperformed vanilla Behavioral Cloning (BC) across three simulated RoboSuite tasks and three real-world AgileX bimanual manipulation tasks.
 - These results suggest that WVM can accurately differentiate genuine task progress from suboptimal behaviors, enabling policies to learn more effectively from imperfect datasets.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contributions of WVM's key design components:

- **Video Co-training (λ):** Omitting the video co-training objective ($L_{video}$) or freezing the video stream weights led to performance degradation across all Suboptimal-Value-Bench metrics. For instance, Hesitation-RMSE increased from 0.05 to 0.08 without $L_{video}$, and Retry-VOC dropped from 0.78 to 0.68. The most severe degradation occurred when video weights were completely frozen (0.12 Hesitation-RMSE, 0.45 Retry-VOC). This indicates that continuous co-training of the video world model is crucial for the value model's ability to capture underlying temporal dynamics.
- **Prefix Randomization (p):** The prefix randomization ratio $p$ was found to be important for balancing temporal shortcut suppression and inter-chunk continuity. Without randomization (p=0), Hesitation-RMSE worsened to 0.09 and Retry-VOC dropped to 0.67, even though Expert-VOC increased to 0.98. This disparity suggested an over-reliance on prefixes as a causal shortcut, underscoring the insufficiency of Expert-VOC alone. WVM achieved optimal robustness on both Suboptimal-Value-Bench and expert trajectories at $p=0.5$.
- **Value Head Design:** Replacing WVM's flow-matching head with an HL-Gaussian alternative resulted in a decline across all metrics. Hesitation-RMSE marginally increased (0.05 to 0.06), and discriminative scores saw a sharper decline. This finding supports the advantage of the flow-matching head in capturing a continuous return density and fine-grained local value differentials, which are critical for ranking temporally adjacent chunks, as opposed to categorical heads with fixed bin support.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation by presenting the World Value Model (WVM), which leverages the predictive capabilities of pretrained world models for generalist robotic value learning. This approach addresses several fundamental limitations of existing value models, particularly their reliance on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that often lack adequate temporal understanding.

The **primary significance** of WVM lies in its innovative integration of world models as foundational backbones for value estimation. By inheriting the intrinsic strengths of world models in historical grounding and future planning, WVM provides a more accurate and robust assessment of task progression. This enables it to deliver state-of-the-art results not only on conventional expert-only benchmarks but also, critically, on complex, suboptimal trajectories.

A **key contribution and potential impact** is the introduction of Suboptimal-Value-Bench. This multi-embodiment benchmark, with its 800 high-fidelity, human-labeled suboptimal trajectories and dedicated metrics (Hesitation-RMSE and Retry-VOC), fills a significant gap in the evaluation of robotic value models. Current evaluation suites predominantly focus on optimal performance, offering limited insight into a model's ability to identify and quantify deviations from desired behavior. Suboptimal-Value-Bench facilitates a more comprehensive and realistic assessment of value model robustness, which is crucial for real-world deployments where suboptimal actions are common.

The **practical utility** of WVM is demonstrated through its ability to improve downstream policy learning. By providing robust guidance derived from world-model-based value estimates, WVM-guided approaches (such as Advantage Weighted Regression and Filtered Behavioral Cloning) consistently enhance manipulation performance when learning from mixed-quality data in both simulated and real-world environments. This capability is vital for scaling robotic systems, as it reduces the need for exclusively expert demonstrations and allows for more effective utilization of large, heterogeneous datasets that often contain varied quality trajectories.

The findings from the ablation studies further reinforce the importance of WVM's design choices, such as continuous video co-training, strategic prefix randomization, and the use of flow-matching for distributional value estimation. These elements collectively contribute to the model's superior performance and robustness.

In summary, this work advances the state of robotic policy learning by offering a more capable and generalizable value model. By providing WVM and Suboptimal-Value-Bench to the research community, the authors contribute both a novel methodological framework and a critical evaluation tool, potentially accelerating the development of robust, adaptable robotic systems that can learn effectively from diverse and imperfect real-world data.
