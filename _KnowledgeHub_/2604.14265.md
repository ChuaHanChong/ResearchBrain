# Reinforcement Learning via Value Gradient Flow

> [!summary] Summary
> Value Gradient Flow (VGF) introduces a new paradigm for behavior-regularized reinforcement learning by framing the problem as an optimal transport process that implicitly enforces regularization. This approach demonstrates state-of-the-art or competitive performance across various offline RL benchmarks like D4RL and OGBench, and achieves improved win rates in RL from Human Feedback tasks such as TL;DR summarization.

## Problem

- Offline RL policies risk value over-optimization due to out-of-distribution (OOD) extrapolation, and RL from Human Feedback (RLHF) policies can suffer from reward hacking if they deviate too much from a reference.
- Current behavior-regularized RL methods struggle with scalability to large generative models (e.g., diffusion, flow models) due to the computational cost and instability of backpropagating through multi-step sampling processes.
- Reject sampling methods are often overly conservative, amplifying existing signals within the reference distribution but limiting the discovery of novel, potentially more optimal behaviors beyond its support.

## Method

- **VGF** reformulates behavior-regularized RL as an ==optimal transport== problem, aiming to move probability mass from an initial reference distribution to a value-induced optimal policy distribution.
- It employs a particle-based discrete gradient flow (Jordan-Kinderlehrer-Otto scheme, inspired by SVGD) to update actions, guided by value function gradients and a repulsive kernel to prevent mode collapse.
- Implicit regularization is achieved by controlling the "transport budget" via hyperparameters like the number of gradient flow steps, limiting how far particles deviate from their initial positions without explicit penalty terms.

## Results

- VGF achieved state-of-the-art performance on most D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks and significantly higher scores on challenging AntMaze navigation tasks compared to existing Gaussian, diffusion, and flow-based policy baselines.
- In RLHF settings, VGF demonstrated higher win rates, such as **68.1%** on TL;DR summarization (vs. **61.2%** for DPO), indicating better alignment of LLMs with human preferences.
- The offline policies learned by VGF serve as stronger initializations for online fine-tuning, leading to faster adaptation and higher final performance across challenging OGBench tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Behavior regularization can be effectively achieved implicitly by controlling the transport budget in an optimal transport framework, offering a flexible alternative to explicit divergence penalties.
> - The particle-based gradient flow mechanism allows the learned policy's support to extend beyond the original reference distribution, overcoming the inherent conservatism of many existing methods.
> - VGF enables adaptive control during test-time by allowing dynamic adjustment of flow steps, which fine-tunes the exploration-exploitation trade-off without requiring policy retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated efficacy in various domains, from robotics and game playing to fine-tuning large language models (LLMs). A persistent challenge in many of these applications is the need for policies to be regularized toward a reference distribution. This regularization is crucial in two primary contexts: offline RL, where learning from fixed datasets risks value over-optimization due to out-of-distribution (OOD) extrapolation, and RL from Human Feedback (RLHF), where excessive deviation from a supervised policy can lead to reward hacking. The core problem is to balance maximizing value with adhering to a reliable reference behavior.

Current approaches to behavior-regularized RL typically fall into two categories. The first involves reparameterized policy gradients, which add explicit divergence or distance penalties (e.g., KL-divergence, L2 distance) to the RL objective. While effective in constraining policy updates, these methods face difficulties in scaling to large generative models like diffusion or flow models. Computing policy gradients in such models requires backpropagating through iterative sampling procedures, which can be unstable and computationally expensive. Distilling multi-step policies into one-step models can mitigate this, but may reduce expressivity. Furthermore, explicit penalties often tie regularization strengths for value learning and policy improvement, making tuning challenging.

The second approach is reject sampling, often implemented as weighted behavior cloning or "best-of-N" sampling. These methods draw samples from a reference distribution and select actions based on their value. While simple, they tend to be conservative, primarily amplifying existing signals within the reference distribution without effectively exploring or exploiting behaviors beyond its support. This limits their ability to discover novel and potentially more optimal strategies.

Value Gradient Flow (VGF) is positioned as a novel paradigm that addresses the limitations of these existing methods. It aims to provide a scalable, stable, and expressive solution for behavior-regularized RL by reframing the problem as an optimal transport process, moving beyond explicit policy parameterization and fixed regularization coefficients. The work also relates to the growing interest in applying optimal transport theory to RL, as well as methods that leverage Q-action gradients for policy guidance, but differentiates itself by its unique formulation and implicit regularization mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Value Gradient Flow (VGF), a new paradigm for behavior-regularized reinforcement learning that offers scalability, expressivity, and stability without explicit policy parameterization. The motivation for developing VGF stems from several limitations observed in current behavior-regularized RL methods:

1. **Addressing Value Over-optimization and Reward Hacking:** In offline RL, learning from static datasets can lead to policies that exploit spurious correlations in OOD regions, resulting in optimistic but erroneous value estimates (value over-optimization). In RLHF, policies can deviate too far from a safe, pre-trained base model, leading to "reward hacking" where the policy learns to generate outputs that maximize a potentially imperfect reward model without truly aligning with desired human preferences. Behavior regularization is a critical mechanism to mitigate these issues.

2. **Scalability to Large Generative Models:** Existing methods, particularly those relying on reparameterized policy gradients, face significant challenges when applied to modern, expressive generative models such as diffusion models or flow-matching models. These models often involve multi-step sampling processes, making backpropagation for policy gradient computation unstable and resource-intensive. Distilling such policies into simpler, one-step models can compromise their inherent expressivity. A key motivation for VGF is to provide a method that can leverage the power of these generative models without incurring their associated computational and stability drawbacks during policy optimization.

3. **Overcoming Conservatism of Reject Sampling:** While reject sampling methods (like best-of-N) are simple to implement, they are inherently conservative. By sampling actions exclusively from the reference distribution, they are constrained to its support. This limits their capacity to discover or exploit novel, high-value actions that lie outside the known behavior, thereby hindering true policy improvement and skill acquisition beyond the dataset's coverage. VGF aims to enable exploration beyond the reference distribution's support.

4. **Decoupling and Adaptability of Regularization:** Traditional explicit regularization methods often couple the regularization strength for value learning and policy improvement using a single coefficient (e.g., `β` for KL divergence). This makes the regularization term difficult to tune, often leading to either overly cautious updates (preventing policy improvement) or insufficient control over OOD drift (leading to instability). VGF seeks to provide a more flexible and adaptive form of regularization that can be controlled during both training and inference.

In summary, VGF is motivated by the need for a behavior-regularized RL approach that is simultaneously scalable to complex generative models, capable of exploring beyond observed data, and robust in its optimization, thereby addressing critical shortcomings of current methodologies in both offline RL and LLM fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Value Gradient Flow (VGF) re-conceptualizes behavior-regularized RL as an optimal transport problem. The core idea is to transport probability mass from an initial reference distribution (e.g., the offline dataset's behavior policy or a base LLM's output distribution) to a target optimal policy distribution, where the "transport budget" implicitly enforces regularization.

**Optimal Transport Formulation:**
VGF begins by considering a maximum-entropy (MaxEnt) formulation of the RL objective, which augments the value maximization objective with a policy entropy maximization term: $E_{a \sim \pi}[R(s, a)] + \alpha H(\pi(\cdot|s))$. This MaxEnt objective yields an optimal policy distribution that takes the form of a Boltzmann distribution over the value function $R(s, a)$:
$\pi^*_R(a|s) = \frac{1}{Z_s} \exp(R(s, a)/\alpha)$
where $Z_s$ is a normalization factor. The problem then becomes transporting the reference distribution $\mu$ to this value-induced optimal policy distribution $\pi^*_R$.

**Discrete Gradient Flow Solution:**
To solve this optimal transport problem, VGF employs a discrete gradient flow mechanism, specifically the Jordan-Kinderlehrer-Otto (JKO) minimizing-movement scheme. This scheme minimizes a functional $F(q) = KL(q \Vert \pi^*_R)$ regularized by the 2-Wasserstein distance from the previous distribution $q_k$.
$q_{k+1} = \arg \min_q KL(q \Vert \pi^*_R) + \frac{1}{2h} W_2^2(q, q_k)$
Directly solving for $q_k$ is intractable as it's infinite-dimensional. Therefore, VGF approximates $q_k$ as an empirical measure over $N$ particles in the action space: $q_k \approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \delta_{a_i^{(k)}}$.

**Particle-Based Gradient Flow (SVGD-like):**
To obtain a practical solver for updating these particles, VGF utilizes a nonparametric functional gradient method derived from Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD). This yields an update rule for each particle $a_i^{(l)}$:
$a_i^{(l+1)} = a_i^{(l)} + \epsilon \cdot \phi(a_i^{(l)})$
where $\epsilon$ is the step size. The velocity field $\phi(x)$ is defined as:
$\phi(x) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \left( k(a_j, x) \nabla_{a_j} \log \pi^*_R(a_j|s) + \nabla_{a_j} k(a_j, x) \right)$
The term $\nabla_{a_j} \log \pi^*_R(a_j|s)$ simplifies to $\frac{1}{\alpha} \nabla_{a_j} R(s, a_j)$, which drives particles towards high-value regions. The second term, $\nabla_{a_j} k(a_j, x)$, where $k(\cdot, \cdot)$ is a kernel function (e.g., RBF kernel), acts as a repulsive force. This repulsion encourages dispersion among particles, helping to preserve the multi-modality of the distribution and prevent collapse to a single mode. When transforming back to the original objective (without MaxEnt), $\alpha \to 0$, and the term simplifies to $\frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N k(a_j, x) \nabla_{a_j} R(s, a_j)$, with $\alpha$ absorbed into $\epsilon$.

**Implicit Regularization:**
A key aspect of VGF is that it imposes behavior regularization implicitly by controlling the "transport budget." This budget is determined by hyperparameters such as the number of gradient flow steps ($L$), the MaxEnt temperature parameter ($\alpha$), and the step size ($\epsilon$). Intuitively, limiting the transport budget restricts how far particles can deviate from their initial positions, thus controlling the divergence from the reference distribution without explicit penalty terms.

**Theoretical Properties:**
- **Bounded MMD:** Theorem 1 states that the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) distance between the initial particles (from $\mu$) and the final VGF-generated particles ($\pi^L_N$) is bounded, providing a theoretical guarantee on the implicit regularization: $MMD^2(\mu, \pi^L_N) \le \frac{2\epsilon L}{\sigma \sqrt{e}} (\frac{c}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{e}})$.
- **Support Extension:** Theorem 2 demonstrates that VGF's policy support is not necessarily confined within the reference distribution's support ($\text{supp}_\epsilon(\pi^L_N(\cdot|s)) \not\subseteq \text{supp}_\epsilon(\mu(\cdot|s))$). This indicates VGF's ability to move beyond conservative behavior constraints and potentially discover novel, high-value actions.

**Adaptation for Large Language Models (LLMs):**
For discrete token spaces in LLMs, direct application of particle gradients is infeasible. VGF addresses this by operating in a continuous surrogate space (e.g., token embeddings or latent vectors of flow/diffusion models). Gradients of the reward model $R(x,y)$ with respect to the generated response $y$ are back-propagated to this continuous representation $u$ via the chain rule: $\nabla_{u_i} \log \pi^*_R(y_i^{(l)}|x) = \frac{1}{\alpha} J_i^T \nabla_y R(x, y_i^{(l)})$, where $J_i$ is the Jacobian of the decoder.

**Practical Implementation (Algorithm 1):**
- **Value Training:** A behavior cloning (BC) policy is trained to generate samples from the reference distribution $\mu$. A Q-function (or reward model $R$) is trained using TD-learning. For target Q-values, VGF is applied to future states $s'$, and the Q-values are averaged over all generated particles. A separate network can be trained to predict the Q-function's action gradient to accelerate training and inference.
- **Policy Generation:** At inference time, initial particles are sampled from the BC policy. These particles are then evolved for $L_{\text{test}}$ steps using the particle-based gradient flow guided by the Q-function (or reward model).
- **Action Selection:** From the final set of VGF-evolved particles, the action with the highest Q-value is selected (best-of-N sampling).
- **Small Particle Count:** A small number of particles ($N=5$) is used across experiments to manage computational cost while maintaining efficacy.
- **Adaptive Test-Time Scaling:** The number of test-time flow steps ($L_{\text{test}}$) can be adjusted dynamically during inference without retraining. Setting $L_{\text{test}}=0$ effectively reduces VGF to a best-of-N sampling method.

A toy 2D bandit example demonstrates VGF's ability to explore high-reward regions beyond the suboptimal offline dataset's support, outperforming FlowQL (reparameterized policy gradient) and FlowBC Best-of-N (reject sampling), which remain more constrained.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Value Gradient Flow (VGF) establishes competitive or superior performance across various benchmarks in offline RL and RL from Human Feedback (RLHF) settings.

**Offline RL Benchmarks:**

1. **D4RL Datasets (MuJoCo and AntMaze):**
 - VGF consistently outperforms a range of prior methods, including Gaussian-policy-based (TD3+BC, IQL, IVR), diffusion-policy-based (Diffusion-QL, SfBC), and flow-policy-based (FQL) baselines.
 - On the MuJoCo locomotion tasks (halfcheetah, hopper, walker2d across medium, medium-replay, medium-expert datasets), VGF achieves state-of-the-art results on most tasks, notably improving over FQL, which showed poor performance on some MuJoCo datasets.
 - VGF demonstrates significantly higher scores on the challenging AntMaze navigation tasks (umaze, umaze-diverse, medium-play, medium-diverse, large-play, large-diverse). These tasks require long-horizon planning and sparse rewards, where VGF's ability to explore beyond the behavioral support proves particularly beneficial. For instance, on `antmaze-u`, `antmaze-u-d`, `antmaze-m-p`, `antmaze-m-d`, and `antmaze-l-p` VGF substantially improved upon prior best scores.

2. **OGBench Datasets (Robotic Locomotion and Manipulation):**
 - VGF achieves competitive or superior performance on a diverse set of goal-conditioned tasks from OGBench, encompassing antmaze-giant, humanoidmaze, antsoccer-arena, cube (single/double), scene, and puzzle (3x3/4x4) environments.
 - VGF shows significant improvements, especially on tasks identified as particularly challenging, such as `cube-double`, `puzzle-3x3`, and `puzzle-4x4`, where baselines like FQL struggled to achieve 50% success rates. This indicates VGF's robustness in complex, long-horizon, and sparse-reward settings.

3. **Online Fine-tuning (OGBench):**
 - When agents are trained offline with VGF for 1M steps and then fine-tuned online for an additional 1M steps, VGF provides a stronger initial policy compared to FQL.
 - VGF agents adapt more rapidly during online interaction and converge to higher final performance across challenging tasks. This suggests that the offline-learned policy by VGF is a more robust and effective starting point for subsequent online learning.

**Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):**

- VGF was evaluated on the TL;DR Summarize and Anthropic Helpful and Harmless Dialogue (Anthropic-HH) datasets, comparing win rates (WR%) judged by GPT-4 against an initialized model and chosen responses.
- VGF achieved higher win rates than several standard RLHF methods, including Pythia-SFT, PPO, DPO, and Best-of-N sampling. For example, on TL;DR, VGF achieved a 68.1% win rate versus the reference, compared to 61.2% for DPO. This indicates VGF's effectiveness in aligning LLMs with human preferences while mitigating reward over-optimization.

**Understanding Properties and Behavior (Ablation Studies):**

1. **Hyperparameter Importance:**
 - The "train-time flow steps" ($L_{\text{train}}$) is identified as the most crucial hyperparameter. It directly influences the degree of deviation from the reference policy during training. Optimal $L_{\text{train}}$ values are task-dependent, requiring tuning to balance policy improvement and regularization.

2. **Adaptive Test-Time Scaling:**
 - VGF supports adaptive scaling during inference by adjusting the "test-time flow steps" ($L_{\text{test}}$) without requiring retraining.
 - Increasing $L_{\text{test}}$ is generally beneficial when the learned value function generalizes well to OOD regions and the offline dataset is of low quality, allowing for greater exploration beyond the reference policy.
 - When the value function exhibits large extrapolation errors, setting $L_{\text{test}}=0$ effectively reduces VGF to a best-of-N sampling method. Even in this conservative mode, VGF can outperform a simple reference policy due to its use of TD-learned value functions, which enables better "stitching" of diverse behaviors and in-distribution generalization.

These findings collectively indicate VGF's capability to address challenges in behavior-regularized RL, offering improved performance, stability, and adaptability across diverse tasks and domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Value Gradient Flow (VGF) represents a methodological contribution to the field of behavior-regularized reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Novel Paradigm for Behavior Regularization:** VGF reformulates behavior-regularized RL as an optimal transport problem, moving probability mass from a reference distribution to a value-induced optimal policy distribution. This approach implicitly incorporates regularization through the "transport budget" (number of flow steps, step size), offering an alternative to explicit divergence or distance penalties. This novel perspective may stimulate further research into optimal transport applications in RL.

2. **Enhanced Scalability and Stability for Generative Models:** VGF addresses a critical limitation of existing methods by eliminating the need for explicit policy parameterization and avoiding unstable backpropagation through multi-step sampling processes characteristic of large generative models (e.g., diffusion, flow models). This makes VGF a more scalable and stable solution for training policies within complex, expressive generative architectures, which are increasingly prevalent in LLMs and robotic control.

3. **Reduced Conservatism and Expanded Policy Support:** Unlike reject sampling or other methods strictly confined to the support of the reference distribution, VGF's theoretical analysis demonstrates its ability to generate policies whose support can extend beyond the initial behavior distribution. This allows VGF to discover and exploit novel, high-value actions not present in the original dataset, mitigating the "over-conservatism" typically seen in offline RL. This capability is crucial for achieving true policy improvement and skill acquisition.

4. **Adaptive Control and Flexibility:** VGF enables adaptive control during test-time by allowing dynamic adjustment of the transport budget (e.g., number of flow steps) without requiring retraining. This flexibility permits practitioners to fine-tune the policy's exploration-exploitation trade-off based on the reliability of the value function and the quality of the reference data. The ability to smoothly transition between highly exploratory behavior and conservative, in-distribution sampling (by setting test-time steps to zero) is a practical advantage.

5. **Strong Empirical Performance:** The consistent outperformance of VGF against established baselines across challenging offline RL benchmarks (D4RL, OGBench) and LLM RLHF tasks underscores its practical efficacy. Its superior performance on complex tasks, particularly in environments requiring long-horizon planning and those with sparse rewards (e.g., AntMaze, OGBench manipulation tasks), highlights its robustness.

6. **Towards Unified and Scalable RL:** By providing a single, adaptable framework that performs well in diverse behavior-regularized settings (offline RL, RLHF), VGF contributes to the development of more unified and scalable RL algorithms. This could simplify the development and deployment of RL solutions across various applications.

Potential future work, such as integrating distribution reweighting techniques to handle heavily skewed reference distributions or combining with advanced value function expressiveness, suggests avenues for further impact and generalization of the VGF framework.
