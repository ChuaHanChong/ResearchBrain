# Stackelberg Coupling of Online Representation Learning and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SCORER (Stackelberg Coupled Representation and Reinforcement Learning) framework introduces a hierarchical game-theoretic approach to deep Q-learning, decoupling representation and value function learning into leader and follower roles with distinct optimization objectives. This method improves sample efficiency and stability across various deep Q-learning algorithms and environments, including MinAtar, Atari, and MiniGrid, by mitigating issues like representation collapse and divergent learning.

## Problem

- Deep Q-learning agents suffer from instability due to the 'deadly triad' (function approximation, bootstrapping, off-policy learning) in monolithic neural network architectures.
- This instability leads to non-stationary value targets, dependence on shifting representations, high variance in bootstrapped targets, and potential representation collapse or catastrophic learning failures.
- Existing solutions like auxiliary losses can introduce conflicting gradients within a shared network, creating trade-offs between representation stability and value learning.

## Method

- Introduces the **SCORER** framework, reformulating representation learning and Q-learning as a ==Stackelberg game== with a control network (Q-function) as the leader and a perception network (representation encoder) as the follower.
- The leader (Q-network) minimizes the Mean Squared Bellman Error (MSBE) with slower updates, providing a stable target, while the follower (perception network) minimizes the sample variance of Bellman errors across transitions with faster updates, given the leader's fixed parameters.
- Approximates the Stackelberg equilibrium using a two-timescale stochastic approximation (TTSA) algorithm with differential learning rates and stop-gradient operations to enforce hierarchical updates and prevent direct gradient flow between the networks.

## Results

- SCORER consistently improved sample efficiency and final performance across MinAtar (e.g., tripling DQN's final score in Breakout) and achieved statistically significant gains when applied to PQN.
- Demonstrated scalability to high-dimensional Atari-5 environments, outperforming baselines in Phoenix and Qbert, and showed 25-**50%** faster convergence in partially observable MiniGrid tasks with SCORER R2D2.
- Maintained stability and converged on Baird's counterexample where baseline linear TD diverged, and achieved significantly faster solved rates in Deep Sea compared to Bootstrapped DQN, indicating enhanced robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling representation and control learning via a Stackelberg game with sequential updates and timescale separation effectively resolves the 'deadly triad's' destructive feedback loop.
> - Minimizing the sample variance of Bellman errors provides a robust unsupervised objective for representation learning, promoting features that are consistent and stable against noisy TD targets.
> - A hierarchical interaction, where the control objective leads and provides a stable signal for representation learning, promotes stable co-adaptation without needing auxiliary tasks or architectural changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly methods employing Deep Q-Networks (DQN), has achieved notable successes in complex environments. A common approach in value-based RL involves training representation learning and the value function within a single, monolithic neural network. This design aims to foster beneficial co-adaptation between learned features and value estimates. However, this integrated architecture frequently encounters an instability known as the "deadly triad," which arises from the interaction of function approximation with neural networks, bootstrapping, and off-policy learning from experience replay buffers. This can result in errors in the current value function being bootstrapped into its own learning targets, leading to potential unbounded divergence, representation collapse, and catastrophic learning failures.

Existing research efforts to mitigate this instability often involve augmenting the primary RL objective with auxiliary losses, drawing from advancements in self-supervised and representation learning. While such strategies can improve performance, integrating multiple objectives within a shared network can introduce challenges such as conflicting gradients, creating a trade-off where efforts to stabilize representations may interfere with the primary value-learning objective.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a fundamental restructuring of the optimization problem. Instead of augmenting a monolithic optimization, it introduces a hierarchical, game-theoretic framework. This approach aims to resolve the inherent instabilities and trade-offs by decoupling the perception (representation learning) and control (Q-learning) processes and defining their interaction within a Stackelberg game. The goal is to achieve stable co-adaptation without relying on auxiliary tasks or external learning signals beyond the task rewards, thus offering an unsupervised method based purely on the Mean Squared Bellman Error (MSBE) and its sample variance. This places the work within the domain of foundational deep RL research focused on improving training stability and sample efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the persistent instability observed in deep Q-learning agents, particularly those utilizing monolithic neural networks for both representation learning and value estimation. The authors identify the "deadly triad" (function approximation, bootstrapping, and off-policy learning) as the root cause of issues such as non-stationary value targets, dependence on shifting representations, high variance in bootstrapped targets, and subsequent bias in value estimation. These factors can lead to phenomena like representation collapse and catastrophic learning failures, hindering the reliability and efficiency of deep RL systems.

The paper outlines several key objectives to address these challenges:

1. **Resolve Core Instability:** The primary objective is to develop a framework that directly tackles the instability arising from the tight coupling of representation learning and Q-learning. This involves moving away from simultaneous parameter updates in monolithic solutions.
2. **Enhance Representation Robustness:** To enable the perception network to learn representations that are robust to the noisy targets inherent in Temporal Difference (TD) learning. This is specifically achieved by minimizing the variance of Bellman errors, thereby aiming to reduce bias in value estimation.
3. **Promote Stable Co-adaptation:** To establish a mechanism for stable and coordinated adaptation between the representation learning and value function learning processes. The authors hypothesize that a hierarchical dynamic, where one component provides a stable signal for the other to adapt to, can achieve this.
4. **Develop a Principled and Practical Algorithm:** To translate the game-theoretic formulation into a computationally efficient and implementable algorithm that requires minimal modifications to existing deep Q-learning architectures. The solution should avoid the introduction of complex architectural changes or additional loss functions that might lead to conflicting gradients.
5. **Demonstrate Generalizability:** To empirically validate the proposed framework across a variety of established value-based RL algorithms and diverse environments, including those with high-dimensional observations and partial observability, thereby showcasing its broad applicability and effectiveness.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to fundamentally improve the reliability, sample efficiency, and overall performance of deep Q-learning agents without increasing model complexity, addressing a core limitation in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces the **Stackelberg Coupled Representation and Reinforcement Learning (SCORER)** framework. This framework reinterprets the interaction between a perception network (responsible for learning state representations) and a control network (responsible for Q-value estimation) as a hierarchical Stackelberg game.

**Stackelberg Game Formulation:**
In this hierarchical game, two distinct roles are assigned:

- **The Leader: Control Network (Q_θ)**: The Q-function network assumes the role of the Stackelberg leader. Its primary objective is to learn an optimal action-value function Q_θ(z, a) by minimizing the Mean Squared Bellman Error (MSBE). As the leader, it commits to its strategy by updating its parameters (θ) on a slower timescale, which is intended to provide a stable learning target for the follower. The leader's objective function, L_leader, is defined as E_((s,a,r,s',d)~B)[(Y - Q_θ(f_ϕ*(θ)(s), a))^2], where Y is the Bellman target value and f_ϕ*(θ) represents the follower's optimal response given the leader's parameters.

- **The Follower: Perception Network (f_ϕ)**: The perception network, an encoder that maps raw observations *s* to latent representations *z = f_ϕ(s)*, acts as the Stackelberg follower. Given the control network's committed strategy (i.e., treating *θ* as fixed via a stop-gradient operation during its update), the follower learns on a faster timescale. Its objective, L_follower, is to minimize the sample variance of Bellman errors across a batch of transitions. Specifically, if δ_j(ϕ, θ) = Y_j - Q_θ(f_ϕ(s_j), a_j) is the Bellman error for transition *j*, the follower's objective is Var_j∈B[δ_j(ϕ, θ)]. This variance-minimization objective is designed to counteract the instability of noisy TD targets by promoting representations that are consistent across the data distribution, thereby reducing the epistemic variance component of the Bellman error. While minimizing MSBE was explored for the follower, variance minimization was empirically found to yield superior performance.

**Approximating the Stackelberg Equilibrium via Two-Timescale Gradient Descent:**
The bi-level optimization problem resulting from this Stackelberg game is approximated using a two-timescale stochastic approximation (TTSA) algorithm. This approach addresses the computational challenges of directly solving bi-level problems in deep learning contexts by leveraging timescale separation.

- **Timescale Separation:** This is achieved by assigning distinct, time-dependent learning rates to the two players: α_ϕ,k for the follower and α_θ,k for the leader. These rates decay over time, with the crucial condition that α_θ,k / α_ϕ,k approaches zero as training progresses (i.e., the follower's learning rate is significantly larger than the leader's). This ensures that the faster-learning follower can effectively track a best response to the slowly evolving strategy of the leader.
- **Update Mechanism (Algorithm 1):** Within each update step:
 1. The follower (perception network) first updates its parameters ϕ_k+1 based on the current, fixed leader parameters θ_k. A stop-gradient operation is applied to θ_k, preventing gradients from flowing from the follower's loss to the leader's parameters.
 2. Subsequently, the leader (control network) updates its parameters θ_k+1 using the follower's newly updated state ϕ_k+1 as a fixed input. A stop-gradient operation is applied to ϕ_k+1.
This sequential update, combined with stop-gradients, enforces the hierarchical dependency and strategic interaction of the Stackelberg game.

**Generality and Implementation:**
SCORER is designed to be generally applicable across various value-based RL agents, including replay-based methods like DQN, Double DQN (DDQN), Dueling DQN, Dueling DDQN, and R2D2, as well as replay-buffer-free methods such as Parallelized Q-Network (PQN). A core aspect of the methodology is that it achieves its benefits without requiring changes to the underlying network architectures, instead relying on modifications to learning rate schedules and gradient flow dynamics. This implies minimal computational overhead compared to baseline methods. The theoretical foundation, detailed in the appendix, provides convergence guarantees under standard smoothness and convexity assumptions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SCORER demonstrates consistent improvements across various benchmarks and baseline algorithms. The key findings are:

- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Performance on MinAtar:** SCORER consistently delivered performance benefits across all tested base algorithms (DQN, DDQN, Dueling DQN, Dueling DDQN, PQN) in the MinAtar suite. For the classic DQN family, SCORER variants showed substantial gains in sample efficiency and final performance, with, for instance, a standard DQN agent's final score in Breakout tripling. SCORER also enabled replay-based methods to achieve performance comparable to, or in some cases superior to, the PQN baseline. When applied to PQN, SCORER provided additional statistically significant performance gains, indicating its applicability beyond replay-based agents.
- **Scalability to High-Dimensional Visual Environments (Atari-5):** SCORER was integrated with PQN and evaluated on the Atari-5 benchmark. SCORER PQN statistically matched the baseline performance in BattleZone, DoubleDunk, and NameThisGame, while outperforming the baseline in Phoenix and Qbert. These results were achieved without increasing computational overhead or architectural complexity, indicating effective scalability to high-dimensional visual control tasks.
- **Performance in Partially Observable Environments (MiniGrid):** On MiniGrid environments (accessed via Navix), SCORER R2D2 consistently outperformed vanilla R2D2, achieving 25-50% faster convergence. In challenging tasks like "Dynamic Obstacles," SCORER R2D2 improved the success rate by 10 percentage points, and in "Four Rooms," it achieved a 97% success rate while the baseline R2D2 failed to solve the task.
- **Enhanced Stability:**
 - **Baird's Counterexample:** When tested on Baird's counterexample, a known domain for inducing divergence in off-policy learning, the baseline linear TD method diverged. In contrast, SCORER variants (using both MSBE and Bellman Error Variance follower objectives) remained stable and converged, indicating that the Stackelberg structure can disrupt the destructive feedback loop inherent in monolithic agents.
 - **Stochastic Deep Sea:** In both deterministic and stochastic versions of Deep Sea (depth 10), SCORER variants achieved near-complete solved rates significantly faster than the Bootstrapped DQN baseline in the deterministic case. In the stochastic version, SCORER maintained near-complete solved rates, while the baseline plateaued at approximately 20%, suggesting robustness to environmental stochasticity.
- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Stackelberg Role Assignment:** Inverting the hierarchy (Perception as Leader, Control as Follower) resulted in a collapse of performance, falling below the monolithic baseline. This supports the design choice of having the control objective lead representation learning.
 - **Follower's Objective:** Using a Bellman Error Variance objective for the follower substantially outperformed a variant where the follower minimized MSBE, validating that an explicit stabilization objective is critical for SCORER's performance.
 - **Hierarchical vs. Synchronous Coupling:** A "Synchronous Coupling" baseline (separate networks, independent MSBE updates at the same frequency) showed marginal, non-significant benefits over standard DQN. A "Monolithic (Higher Encoder LR)" baseline (standard DQN with faster updates for encoder layers) did outperform standard DQN, indicating a benefit from faster representation updates. However, SCORER outperformed both these alternatives, emphasizing that the hierarchical dynamic and timescale separation are crucial, beyond mere architectural separation or differential learning rates.
- **Representation Analysis (Breakout-MinAtar):** Analysis of learning dynamics indicated that SCORER maintained a near-maximal effective rank of penultimate layer activations (Srank) very early in training. In contrast, the baseline DQN exhibited a lower effective rank for approximately the first 40 million steps before recovering. This period of maximal rank divergence precisely correlated with SCORER's largest performance advantage, suggesting SCORER contributes to preventing "representation collapse." SCORER also exhibited faster growth and stabilization of the L2 parameter norm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SCORER framework represents a notable contribution to the field of deep reinforcement learning, particularly in addressing the long-standing problem of instability in value-based methods. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Foundational Instability:** The work directly confronts the "deadly triad" problem, a core challenge that has hindered the robustness and reliability of deep Q-learning for decades. By reframing the interaction between representation and control as a Stackelberg game, SCORER offers a principled and systematic approach to mitigate this instability, moving beyond ad-hoc solutions.
- **Novel Algorithmic Paradigm:** Introducing a game-theoretic, bi-level optimization framework with a two-timescale algorithm for internal agent dynamics is a novel conceptual advancement. This provides a theoretical lens for understanding and designing complex learning systems where distinct components have interdependent objectives.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** The consistent empirical improvements in sample efficiency, final performance, and stability across a wide range of deep Q-learning agents (DQN variants, PQN, R2D2) and diverse environments (MinAtar, Atari, MiniGrid) suggest that SCORER offers a broadly applicable enhancement. This could lead to faster development and deployment of RL agents in various domains.
- **Low Implementation Overhead:** The fact that SCORER achieves its benefits primarily through modifications to learning rates and gradient flow (via stop-gradients) rather than complex architectural changes or additional loss functions is significant. This makes it a practical solution that can be readily integrated into existing deep RL frameworks with minimal effort and computational cost.
- **Principled Approach to Representation Learning:** By tasking the perception network with minimizing Bellman error variance, SCORER provides a novel and effective unsupervised objective for representation learning within an RL context. This explicit stabilization objective contributes to more robust and consistent feature extraction, which is crucial for stable value estimation.
- **Strong Theoretical Grounding:** The connection to two-timescale stochastic approximation theory provides a mathematical foundation for the algorithm's convergence, lending credibility to its design and offering avenues for further theoretical analysis, such as sample complexity characterization.
- **Future Research Directions:** SCORER opens several promising avenues for future research, including its extension to actor-critic methods and continuous control, further theoretical investigation into sample complexity, and the exploration of alternative information-theoretic objectives for the follower. These directions suggest that SCORER could serve as a flexible foundation for a new class of deep RL systems.

In summary, SCORER's ability to enhance stability, efficiency, and performance in deep Q-learning without increasing model complexity positions it as a significant algorithmic advancement. Its principled approach to decoupling and coordinating representation and value learning has the potential to influence the design of more robust and efficient deep RL agents for a wide array of applications.
