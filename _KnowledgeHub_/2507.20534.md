# Kimi K2: Open Agentic Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Moonshot AI developed Kimi K2, a 1 trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts large language model, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in agentic intelligence tasks, including software engineering and tool use. The model, which incorporates the novel MuonClip optimizer for training stability and a multi-stage reinforcement learning framework, establishes a new benchmark for open-source LLMs.

## Problem

- Large language models faced limitations in performing complex, interactive agentic behaviors, primarily due to reliance on static training data for planning and autonomous action.
- Scaling LLM pre-training encountered challenges related to token efficiency and training instability, particularly when using token-efficient optimizers like Muon.
- Generating high-quality, diverse data for advanced agentic capabilities, such as multi-tool use, was difficult and costly to obtain from natural human-generated datasets.

## Method

- Kimi K2 utilizes a 1.04 trillion-parameter ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== transformer architecture with 384 experts, enabling efficient scaling for agentic tasks.
- The novel ==MuonClip optimizer==, featuring a ==QK-Clip mechanism==, ensures stable pre-training on 15.5 trillion tokens by preventing attention logit explosions and loss spikes.
- A multi-stage post-training process incorporates a large-scale ==agentic data synthesis pipeline== for tool use and a comprehensive reinforcement learning framework with verifiable rewards and a self-critique rubric reward.

## Results

- Kimi K2-Instruct achieved state-of-the-art results in agentic benchmarks, scoring **71.6%** on SWE-bench Verified (Agentic-Multi-Attempt) and **66.1%** micro Pass@1 on Tau2-Bench, surpassing open-source baselines.
- The model was ranked as the top open-source LLM and **5th** overall on the LMSYS Arena (July 17, 2025) based on over 3,000 user votes, indicating strong real-world preference.
- Kimi K2-Base set new state-of-the-art records for open-source models on **10 out of 12** English language benchmarks (e.g., MMLU **87.79%**), and led across all coding and most mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The QK-Clip mechanism within the MuonClip optimizer is crucial for stabilizing ultra-large-scale LLM pre-training, preventing attention logit explosions and enabling stable learning over trillions of tokens.
> - Large-scale agentic data synthesis, combined with a sophisticated reinforcement learning framework incorporating both verifiable and self-critique rubric rewards, can effectively imbue LLMs with complex interactive and reasoning capabilities.
> - Scaling sparse MoE architectures by increasing the number of experts while optimizing attention heads proves effective for improving performance and inference efficiency under fixed computational budgets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Kimi K2 is presented within the context of a "profound paradigm shift towards Agentic Intelligence" in LLM development. This shift signifies a move from models that merely generate text based on static training data to intelligent agents capable of perceiving, planning, reasoning, and acting within dynamic, interactive environments. This aligns with a major trend in AI, where researchers are increasingly focused on building AI systems that can acquire new skills through interaction, adapt their behavior, and leverage tools to solve complex, multi-step problems that go beyond the limitations of pre-defined datasets. This pursuit is seen as critical for achieving "superhuman capabilities" and enabling wide-ranging applications in software development, scientific research, and real-world automation.

Kimi K2 also fits into the broader trend of scaling LLMs through Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures. MoE models, like DeepSeek-V3 and Qwen3, are designed to achieve massive parameter counts (Kimi K2 boasts 1 trillion total parameters) while keeping the number of *activated* parameters per token much smaller (32 billion for Kimi K2), leading to more efficient inference and potentially better performance given computational constraints. The paper's contribution of the MuonClip optimizer directly addresses a key challenge in MoE training—stability—which is a common hurdle in scaling these complex architectures.

Furthermore, the work addresses critical challenges in both pre-training and post-training. In pre-training, the focus on "token efficiency" and novel data rephrasing techniques reflects the growing recognition that simply increasing data volume is becoming unsustainable, necessitating methods to extract more learning signal from available high-quality tokens. In post-training, the emphasis on large-scale synthetic data generation for tool use and advanced reinforcement learning (RL) techniques (including verifiable rewards and self-critique) positions Kimi K2 at the cutting edge of AI alignment and capability refinement, especially for agentic tasks. The direct comparison against leading open-source models (DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3) and proprietary models (GPT-4.1, Claude 4, Gemini 2.5 Flash) clearly places Kimi K2 within the highest tier of current LLM capabilities, particularly for tasks requiring complex reasoning, coding, and tool interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Kimi K2 are clearly articulated in the report:

1. **Advance Agentic Intelligence:** The overarching goal is to push the boundaries of agentic capabilities in LLMs, enabling models to autonomously perceive, plan, reason, and act in complex environments. This is seen as a fundamental step towards AI agents that can learn through interaction and acquire skills beyond static training data.
2. **Address Pre-training Challenges:** A key motivation was to overcome limitations in pre-training large MoE models. Specifically, the authors aimed to enhance "token efficiency"—the learning signal derived per token—due to the scarcity of high-quality human data. This involved developing stable and efficient optimization algorithms.
3. **Overcome Post-training Limitations:** The report highlights the difficulty of scaling agentic capabilities (multi-step reasoning, planning, tool use) due to their rarity in natural data. The team sought to develop scalable methods for synthesizing high-quality agentic data and designing general reinforcement learning frameworks that incorporate both explicit rewards and self-critique mechanisms.
4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** A direct objective was to demonstrate superior performance across a broad spectrum of agentic and frontier benchmarks, establishing Kimi K2 as one of the most capable open-source LLMs available.
5. **Contribute to Open Research:** By open-sourcing the base and post-trained model checkpoints, the Kimi Team aims to "spur further progress in Agentic Intelligence," enabling the wider research community to explore, refine, and deploy these advanced capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Kimi K2 is a 1.04 trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language model, featuring 32 billion activated parameters per token. Its development involved significant innovations across pre-training, model architecture, and post-training stages.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

Kimi K2 adopts an ultra-sparse MoE transformer architecture, drawing inspiration from DeepSeek-V3. It utilizes Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) and has a hidden dimension of 7168 and an MoE expert hidden dimension of 2048. Key architectural decisions include:
- **Increased Experts:** Kimi K2 expands the total number of experts to 384 (compared to 256 in DeepSeek-V3), with 8 experts active per token. This design was motivated by empirical "sparsity scaling law" analysis, which showed that increasing sparsity (total experts to active experts ratio) consistently lowers training and validation loss under fixed computational budgets.
- **Reduced Attention Heads:** The number of attention heads was halved to 64 (from 128 in DeepSeek-V3) to reduce inference overhead, especially for long context lengths, with minimal impact on performance as indicated by scaling law analysis.
- **Other Parameters:** It features 61 layers and uses 1 dense layer, contributing to its overall parameter efficiency despite its massive total parameter count.

#### 3.2. Pre-training Innovations

Kimi K2 was pre-trained on a massive 15.5 trillion tokens, with a strong focus on maximizing token efficiency and training stability.
- **MuonClip Optimizer:** This novel optimizer integrates the token-efficient Muon algorithm with a "QK-Clip" mechanism. QK-Clip addresses training instability by dynamically rescaling query and key projection weights to constrain attention logits, preventing their explosion. This enabled Kimi K2 to be pre-trained without any loss spikes.
- **Pre-training Data and Rephrasing:** To improve token utility and leverage limited high-quality data, Kimi K2 introduced domain-specialized rephrasing techniques.
 - **Knowledge Data Rephrasing:** Employs style- and perspective-diverse prompting and chunk-wise autoregressive generation to create varied rephrasings of knowledge-intensive texts, ensuring factual integrity through fidelity verification.
 - **Mathematics Data Rephrasing:** Rewrites mathematical documents into a "learning-note" style and translates materials from other languages to enhance mathematical reasoning capabilities.
- **Training Infrastructure:** The model was trained on a cluster of NVIDIA H800 GPUs. The infrastructure was optimized for scalability and research efficiency, utilizing a flexible parallelism strategy combining 16-way Pipeline Parallelism (PP) with virtual stages, 16-way Expert Parallelism (EP), and ZeRO-1 Data Parallelism. Activation reduction techniques like selective recomputation, FP8 storage for insensitive activations, and CPU offloading were used to manage GPU memory.
- **Training Recipe:** A 4,096-token context window was used, processed over 15.5T tokens with a WSD learning rate schedule. A long-context activation stage extended the context window to 128k using the YaRN method.

#### 3.3. Post-training Strategies

Post-training for Kimi K2 involved a multi-stage process to transform pre-trained priors into actionable agentic behaviors.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** A large-scale instruction-tuning dataset was constructed focusing on prompt diversity and response quality, using in-house expert models and LLM/human-based evaluation for filtering. The Muon optimizer was used for fine-tuning.
- **Large-Scale Agentic Data Synthesis for Tool Use Learning:** A sophisticated pipeline was developed to generate diverse, high-quality tool-use demonstrations.
 - **Tool Spec Generation:** Creates a repository of both real-world (MCP) and LLM-synthesized tool specifications.
 - **Agent and Task Generation:** For given toolsets, generates diverse agents and corresponding tasks with explicit rubrics for success criteria.
 - **Trajectory Generation:** Simulates realistic multi-turn tool-use scenarios through user simulation and a sophisticated tool execution environment that provides realistic, state-updating feedback, including controlled stochasticity.
 - **Quality Evaluation and Filtering:** An LLM-based judge evaluates trajectories against rubrics, retaining only high-quality, successful interactions.
 - **Hybrid Approach:** Supplements simulated environments with real execution sandboxes (e.g., for coding tasks) to ensure authenticity and grounded feedback.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** A general RL framework was designed to scale training in task diversity and FLOPs.
 - **Verifiable Rewards Gym:** An extensible, Gym-like framework was developed to facilitate RL across various domains with verifiable rewards, including Math, STEM, Logical tasks (diverse coverage, moderate difficulty), Complex Instruction Following (hybrid rule verification, multi-source generation), Faithfulness (sentence-level judge model), and Coding & Software Engineering (competition problems, GitHub issues, robust sandbox infrastructure). Safety enhancements were also incorporated through automated prompt evolution and red-teaming.
 - **Self-Critique Rubric Reward:** To extend alignment beyond verifiable tasks, this mechanism enables the model to evaluate its own outputs using core and prescriptive rubrics, generating preference signals for subjective tasks (creative writing, open-ended Q&A). A "Closed-Loop Critic Refinement" process continuously updates the critic using verifiable signals.
 - **RL Algorithm Enhancements:** Included Budget Control (per-sample token limits with penalties), PTX Loss (auxiliary loss on high-quality SFT data to prevent forgetting), and Temperature Decay (shifting from exploration to exploitation).
- **RL Infrastructure:** A hybrid colocated architecture was used where training and inference engines share workers. Optimizations included efficient engine switching via a distributed checkpoint engine (broadcasting parameters efficiently across devices) and efficient system startup to mitigate large-scale training failures. Agentic rollout was optimized for long-horizon, multi-turn tasks by deploying heavy environments as dedicated services and employing partial rollouts to manage long-tail trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Kimi K2 demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, particularly among open-source non-thinking models, and significantly closes the gap with leading proprietary models across a diverse set of benchmarks.

- **Agentic and Competitive Coding:**
 - Achieves 65.8% on SWE-bench Verified (Agentic-Single-Attempt) and 71.6% (Agentic-Multi-Attempt), substantially outperforming DeepSeek-V3-0324 (38.8%) and Qwen3-235B-A22B (34.4%) and nearing Claude 4 Sonnet (72.7%).
 - Scores 47.3% on SWE-bench Multilingual, again leading open-source models (DeepSeek-V3: 25.8%, Qwen3: 20.9%).
 - Achieves 53.7% on LiveCodeBench v6 and 27.1% on OJBench, leading all evaluated models, including proprietary ones like GPT-4.1 and Claude 4 Opus, in non-thinking settings.
 - Scores 85.7% on MultiPL-E and demonstrates strong performance on PaperBench Code-Dev (27.8%), TerminalBench (30.0%), and SWE-Lancer (39.1%).

- **Tool Use Tasks:**
 - Sets a new standard, achieving 66.1% on Tau2-bench (micro-average across retail, airline, telecom domains) and 76.5% on ACEBench (en), outperforming all open-source baselines and remaining highly competitive with closed-source models.

- **Math & STEM Tasks:**
 - Exhibits robust mathematical capabilities with 49.5% on AIME 2025, 69.6% on AIME 2024, and 97.4% on MATH-500.
 - Achieves 75.1% on GPQA-Diamond, outperforming DeepSeek-V3-0324 (68.4%) and other non-thinking baselines. Leads on SuperGPQA (57.2%), ZebraLogic (89.0%), and AutoLogi (89.5%).

- **General Capabilities:**
 - Demonstrates strong, balanced performance on MMLU (89.5%), MMLU-Redux (92.7%), and MMLU-Pro (81.1%), tying or surpassing open-source peers and remaining competitive with proprietary models.
 - Leads all models on instruction benchmarks (IFEval: 89.8%, Multi-Challenge: 54.1%).
 - Achieves 31.0% on SimpleQA and 76.4% on LiveBench.

- **Factuality and Long Context:**
 - Scores 88.5% on FACTS Grounding, outperforming all open-source rivals and even Gemini 2.5 Flash.
 - Achieves a low hallucination rate of 1.1% on HHEM v2.1 (reported as 98.9%) and 7.4% on FaithJudge (reported as 92.6%).
 - Shows strong long-context capabilities on DROP (93.5%) and MRCR (55.0%), though slightly lags on FRAMES and LongBench v2.

- **Open-Ended Evaluation:**
 - Ranks as the top open-source model and 5th overall on the LMSYS Arena leaderboard (as of July 17, 2025), based on over 3,000 user votes.
 - Achieves state-of-the-art win-rates on Arena-Hard-Auto v2.0 for both hard prompts (54.5%) and creative writing (85.0%).
 - In Chinese in-house benchmarks, Kimi K2-Instruct outperforms ChatGPT-4o-latest (65.4% win rate), Claude Sonnet 4 (64.6%), and DeepSeek-V3-0324 (59.6%).

- **Safety Evaluation:**
 - Under red-teaming evaluations using Promptfoo, Kimi K2 exhibits varying robustness across different attack scenarios and strategies. While performing well in basic harmful content scenarios, it showed vulnerabilities in complex attacks like "Iterative Jailbreak" for harmful (92.16% pass rate) and criminal content (57.57% pass rate), highlighting areas for further improvement in adversarial robustness, although its performance in some of these complex cases was relatively higher than other open-source models like DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Kimi K2 marks a significant advancement in the field of large language models, particularly for open-source AI. Its impact is multi-faceted:

- **Acceleration of Agentic AI Research:** By open-sourcing its base and post-trained checkpoints, Kimi K2 provides the research community with a powerful, frontier-level model specifically designed for agentic intelligence. This will undoubtedly accelerate research into complex tool use, planning, and interactive AI systems, allowing researchers to build upon its capabilities rather than starting from scratch. It democratizes access to advanced agentic intelligence, fostering innovation beyond well-resourced labs.
- **New Benchmarks for Open-Source Models:** Kimi K2's state-of-the-art performance, especially in agentic capabilities and competitive coding, sets a new benchmark for open-source LLMs. It demonstrates that open models can now rival or even surpass some proprietary systems in specific, critical domains, challenging the notion that top-tier performance is exclusive to closed-source development.
- **Enhanced Practical Applications:** The model's exceptional capabilities in software engineering (SWE-bench, LiveCodeBench) and tool use (Tau2-bench, ACEBench) have direct implications for real-world applications. Kimi K2 could power more sophisticated AI assistants for software developers, automate complex multi-step tasks, and enable more reliable interactions with external systems and APIs, leading to significant productivity gains in various industries.
- **Innovations in Training Methodology:** The MuonClip optimizer and the large-scale agentic data synthesis pipeline, combined with the novel RL framework incorporating self-critique, represent significant methodological contributions. These techniques address fundamental challenges in scaling and aligning LLMs, offering blueprints for future model development that prioritize stability, token efficiency, and robust learning from interaction.
- **Driving Further Competitive Development:** The strong performance of Kimi K2 puts pressure on other open-source and proprietary model developers to push the boundaries further. This competition is healthy for the field, leading to continuous improvements in model architectures, training strategies, and overall AI capabilities.
- **Acknowledged Limitations as Research Opportunities:** The authors' candid discussion of current limitations (e.g., generating excessive tokens, performance decline with unnecessary tool use, one-shot prompting in full software projects) provides clear directions for future research. These challenges present opportunities for the community, including Moonshot AI, to refine agent architectures, improve context management, and develop more robust, nuanced control mechanisms for AI agents.

In conclusion, Kimi K2 is a landmark open-source LLM that not only achieves impressive performance across a wide array of demanding tasks but also pushes the frontiers of agentic intelligence and innovative training methodologies. Its release is poised to significantly impact the trajectory of AI research and development, particularly in the domain of autonomous and interactive AI systems.
