# Identity-Preserving Text-to-Video Generation by Frequency Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> This work, ConsisID by Peking University and collaborators, introduces a tuning-free, Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based model that generates identity-consistent videos from text prompts and a reference image. The method uses frequency decomposition to inject identity signals into the DiT, demonstrating improved identity preservation and video quality compared to prior approaches.

## Problem

- Existing identity-preserving text-to-video (IPT2V) methods typically require computationally expensive, per-identity fine-tuning, limiting their practical scalability.
- Current tuning-free IPT2V solutions often struggle with maintaining consistent identity, generating complex actions, or producing high-quality general videos.
- Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), while powerful, inherently lack mechanisms for efficient low-frequency feature aggregation (like U-Net skip connections) and struggle with high-frequency detail perception, posing challenges for identity preservation.

## Method

- **ConsisID** introduces a tuning-free, Diffusion Transformer (==DiT==)-based approach for identity-preserving text-to-video generation.
- It employs a Global Facial Extractor to inject low-frequency facial contours (e.g., key points) and a Local Facial Extractor to inject high-frequency intrinsic identity features (from face recognition and **CLIP** backbones) into the DiT.
- A robust consistency training strategy, including coarse-to-fine training, dynamic mask loss, and dynamic cross-face loss, is implemented to enhance identity consistency and generalization across frames and unseen identities.

## Results

- ConsisID outperforms state-of-the-art tuning-free methods (e.g., ID-Animator) in identity preservation (FaceSim-Arc 0.58 vs 0.32) and text relevance (CLIPScore 27.93 vs 24.97) while generating full-body videos with complex actions.
- It produces high-quality, identity-consistent videos for up to 49 frames, maintaining identity better than tuning-based methods and standard image-to-video models which suffer from temporal decay.
- Ablation studies confirm that both frequency decomposition (Global and Local Facial Extractors) and consistency training strategies (Coarse-to-Fine, Dynamic Mask, Dynamic Cross-Face Loss) are crucial for superior performance and generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing facial identity into low and high-frequency components and injecting them into specific DiT layers effectively addresses DiT's inherent limitations for detailed facial perception and pixel-level convergence.
> - A coarse-to-fine training strategy, coupled with dynamic masking and cross-face loss, significantly improves model convergence, facial consistency, and generalization to unseen identities.
> - Combining a global facial extractor for low-frequency contours and a local facial extractor for high-frequency intrinsic features provides an optimal frequency-aware control scheme for identity preservation in DiT-based models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI, particularly text-to-video (T2V) generation, has seen rapid advancements, driven by large-scale pre-trained video diffusion models. These models have opened up numerous downstream applications, including the specialized domain of identity-preserving text-to-video (IPT2V) generation. This paper positions itself at the forefront of IPT2V by addressing critical limitations of existing methods and adapting to emerging architectural trends.

Historically, most identity-preserving generative models, both for images and videos, have relied on **tuning-based approaches**. Methods like DreamBooth, LoRA, and Textual Inversion require fine-tuning the entire network or a subset of parameters for each new identity. While effective, this process is computationally expensive and time-consuming, severely limiting their practical applicability for diverse and on-demand content creation.

To counter this, **tuning-free methods** have emerged, particularly in image generation (e.g., IP-Adapter, InstantID, PhotoMaker). These models integrate identity information without requiring per-instance fine-tuning. However, the development of tuning-free IPT2V models has lagged. Existing open-source solutions like ID-Animator are often limited to specific scenarios, such as talking heads, and struggle with maintaining identity consistency, generating complex actions, or producing high-quality general videos.

Furthermore, the generative model landscape is experiencing a significant architectural shift from the ubiquitous **U-Net** to **Diffusion Transformers (DiT)**. DiTs, exemplified by models like Sora, have demonstrated superior scalability and potential for simulating complex physical worlds. However, adapting DiTs for controllable generation tasks like IPT2V presents unique challenges. Prior research in frequency analysis of Vision/Diffusion Transformers [3–5, 53, 61, 66, 88] reveals two key findings:
1. **Shallow/low-frequency features are crucial for pixel-level prediction in diffusion models and aid training convergence.** U-Nets naturally aggregate these via long skip connections, a mechanism absent in DiTs.
2. **Transformers have limited perception of high-frequency information**, which is vital for preserving intricate facial details. U-Nets' encoder-decoder structure offers multi-scale features rich in high-frequency content, a feature also lacking in DiTs.

This work critically addresses these gaps. It seeks to overcome the high overhead of tuning-based methods and the quality limitations of existing tuning-free models, while simultaneously tackling the inherent architectural challenges of adapting DiT for robust identity preservation. By pioneering a tuning-free, DiT-based solution grounded in frequency decomposition, the paper pushes the frontier of IPT2V towards more practical, high-fidelity, and scalable video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to advance the state-of-the-art in Identity-Preserving Text-to-Video (IPT2V) generation by enabling the creation of high-fidelity videos with consistent human identity, overcoming the significant challenges faced by current generative models.

The authors identify two specific, unresolved technical frontiers they aim to push:
1. **Developing a Tuning-Free Pipeline:** A primary motivation is to eliminate the cumbersome and resource-intensive case-by-case fine-tuning required by most existing IPT2V methods. This high overhead severely diminishes their practical applicability and scalability. The goal is to provide a solution where a single trained model can generate identity-preserving videos for any new person without further training.
2. **Implementing a Frequency-Aware Heuristic Identity-Preserving DiT-based Control Scheme:** With the emergence of Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) as powerful architectures for video generation, the authors are motivated to adapt DiT for IPT2V. This is a challenging endeavor because DiTs inherently struggle with perceiving facial details (high-frequency information) and achieving training convergence for pixel-level prediction tasks compared to U-Net architectures, which traditionally leverage skip connections for low-level features. The motivation is to strategically inject identity-control signals into DiT by decomposing facial features into low- and high-frequency components, thereby addressing these architectural limitations and leveraging DiT's powerful capabilities.

In essence, the motivation stems from the need for a more practical, efficient, and higher-quality IPT2V solution that can seamlessly integrate with the cutting-edge DiT architecture, which represents the future direction of large-scale generative models. By addressing the tuning limitation and the DiT's architectural challenges from a frequency perspective, the authors aim to make significant strides towards highly effective and widely usable identity-preserving video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named **ConsisID**, is a tuning-free, DiT-based controllable IPT2V model designed to maintain consistent human identity in generated videos by leveraging frequency decomposition. Its core approach is inspired by prior findings in frequency analysis of Vision and Diffusion Transformers.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Leveraging DiT Findings

The authors base their methodology on two critical insights regarding DiT from a frequency domain perspective:
- **Finding 1 (Low-frequency Importance):** Shallow, low-frequency features are essential for pixel-level prediction tasks in diffusion models, facilitating training convergence. DiT lacks U-Net's skip connections that aggregate these features.
- **Finding 2 (High-frequency Limitation):** Transformers have limited perception of high-frequency information, which is crucial for preserving facial details and thus identity. DiT lacks the multi-scale features found in U-Net's encoder-decoder structure.

ConsisID directly addresses these findings by decoupling identity features into high- and low-frequency parts and injecting them into specific, optimized locations within the DiT.

#### 3.2. ConsisID Architecture and Components

The ConsisID framework processes a reference image and a text prompt to generate an identity-preserving video. It comprises three main components: a Global Facial Extractor, a Local Facial Extractor, and a Consistency Training Strategy, all built upon a large-scale pre-trained DiT (specifically, CogVideoX-5B).

#### 3.2.1. Low-frequency View: Global Facial Extractor (GFE)

To address Finding 1 and accelerate model convergence for IPT2V, the GFE focuses on providing low-frequency facial information.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of directly feeding the full reference face (which contains high-frequency noise like lighting/shadows) into the shallow layers, the authors extract **facial key points** from the reference image, convert them into an RGB image, and then concatenate this with the original reference image. This combined input is then concatenated with the input noise latent of the DiT.
- **Rationale:** This strategy guides the model's attention to low-frequency signals like facial proportions and contours, minimizing irrelevant noise and easing the training process. Experiments show that discarding this component significantly hinders convergence.

#### 3.2.2. High-frequency View: Local Facial Extractor (LFE)

To address Finding 2 regarding DiT's limited high-frequency perception, the LFE is designed to capture intrinsic, high-frequency identity features.
- **Dual-Tower Feature Extraction:**
 - **Facial Recognition Backbone (e.g., ArcFace):** Extracts high-frequency features that are invariant to non-identity attributes (expression, posture, age, makeup). The penultimate layer is used to retain more spatial information than the final output. This provides intrinsic identity markers.
 - **CLIP Image Encoder:** Captures semantically rich features necessary for editing capabilities (e.g., controlling age, makeup via text prompts), which the face recognition backbone lacks.
- **Feature Fusion and Injection:**
 - A **Q-Former** is used to fuse the features from the facial recognition backbone and the CLIP image encoder, creating intrinsic identity representations enriched with high-frequency semantic information.
 - **Dropout** is applied post-CLIP processing to mitigate the impact of irrelevant features.
 - Shallow, multi-scale features from the facial recognition backbone (after interpolation) are concatenated with the CLIP features.
 - These fused intrinsic identity features are then integrated into the **DiT's attention blocks via cross-attention** with the visual tokens. This injection strategy is crucial for enhancing the DiT's high-frequency perception.

#### 3.2.3. Consistency Training Strategy

To ensure robust identity consistency across frames and enhance generalization, ConsisID incorporates a multi-pronged training strategy:
- **Coarse-to-Fine Training:** A hierarchical approach to training:
 - **Coarse-grained phase:** Prioritizes the Global Facial Extractor (GFE), focusing on low-frequency features (contours, proportions) for rapid identity acquisition and consistency across the video.
 - **Fine-grained phase:** Activates the Local Facial Extractor (LFE), shifting focus to high-frequency details (eye/lip texture, intrinsic identification) to improve fidelity and similarity. This sequential learning mitigates training complexity.
- **Dynamic Mask Loss (DML):** Addresses the issue of background noise interfering with facial identity training.
 - **Mechanism:** A facial mask is extracted from a randomly selected training frame and mapped to the latent space. The loss computation (`L_c`) is then constrained by this mask (`M ⊙ L_c`).
 - **Dynamic Application:** This masked loss is applied with a probability `α` (e.g., 0.5). If not applied, the full `L_c` (considering the entire scene) is used. This dynamic approach prevents the model from failing to generate natural backgrounds during inference.
- **Dynamic Cross-Face Loss (DCL):** Improves generalization and prevents overfitting to training identities ("copy-paste" shortcut).
 - **Mechanism:** With a probability `β` (e.g., 0.5), slight Gaussian noise (`ζ`) is added to the reference images, or "cross-face" images (reference images from *outside* the training dataset) are used as inputs.
 - **Rationale:** This encourages the model to learn more generalized identity features rather than simply replicating specific training examples, improving fidelity for unseen faces.

By meticulously integrating these frequency-aware feature extraction and injection methods with robust consistency-focused training strategies, ConsisID aims to overcome the limitations of prior IPT2V models and effectively harness the power of DiT architectures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate ConsisID's significant advancements in identity-preserving text-to-video generation, particularly in its tuning-free nature and DiT-based architecture.

#### 4.1. Quantitative and Qualitative Comparisons with State-of-the-Art

- **Comparison with Tuning-Free Methods (ID-Animator):**
 - **Quantitative (Table 1):** ConsisID significantly outperforms ID-Animator across identity preservation metrics (FaceSim-Arc ↑ 0.58 vs 0.32, FaceSim-Cur ↑ 0.60 vs 0.33) and text relevance (CLIPScore ↑ 27.93 vs 24.97). FID is higher for ConsisID, but the authors note FID's limited alignment with human perception for this task.
 - **Qualitative (Figure 4):** ID-Animator is limited to generating only face regions, lacks the ability to respond to complex action or background prompts, and exhibits poor identity preservation (skin smoothing, wrinkles, distorted faces). ConsisID consistently generates high-quality, realistic videos that accurately match the reference identity and adhere to complex prompts, including full body, actions, and backgrounds, while maintaining identity over longer video durations (49 frames vs. 16 frames).

- **Comparison with Closed-source (Vidu 1.5) and Tuning-based Methods (DreamVideo, MotionBooth, Magic-Me):**
 - **Quantitative (Table 1, Table 2, Table 3, Figure 13):** ConsisID generally outperforms Vidu 1.5 in identity preservation and visual quality. It also consistently outperforms tuning-based methods across key metrics despite being tuning-free and having a significantly shorter inference time (e.g., ~100s vs. 500s-3600s+ for tuning-based models on single Nvidia H100). The authors attribute this to tuning-based models being designed for broader open-domain tasks, which dilute their ability to capture nuanced identity features.
 - **Qualitative (Figure 13):** Vidu 1.5 shows artifacts and struggles with intrinsic ID features. Tuning-based methods also show less fidelity compared to ConsisID.

- **Comparison with Image-to-Video (I2V) Methods (CogVideoX-5B-I2V, EasyAnimate v4, OpenSora-Plan v1.3, DynamiCrafter512):**
 - **Quantitative (Table 3):** ConsisID achieves higher FaceSim scores, indicating better identity preservation, as standard I2V models demonstrate significant "temporal decay" in identity.
 - **Qualitative (Figure 5):** Standard I2V models struggle to maintain consistent identity throughout the video, especially as motion increases. ConsisID consistently preserves identity across the entire video.

#### 4.2. User Study (Figure 3)

A human evaluation involving 103 valid questionnaires overwhelmingly preferred ConsisID over ID-Animator across all assessed dimensions: Identity Preservation, Visual Quality, Text Alignment, and Motion Amplitude. This strongly validates the perceived effectiveness and quality of ConsisID's generated videos.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies: Validation of Design Choices

- **Effect of Identity Signal Injection in DiT (Figure 6, Table 3, Figure 7):**
 - Injecting *only* high-frequency or *only* low-frequency signals leads to poor convergence or lack of intrinsic detail/control.
 - Combining **both high-frequency (LFE via cross-attention within attention blocks) and low-frequency (GFE via concatenation with noise latent)** is crucial for optimal performance.
 - The inclusion of facial key points in the low-frequency signal significantly helps focus training and improves performance.
 - Injecting high-frequency signals *into* the attention block (scheme 'c') is superior to injecting at the output ('e') or input ('f'-'g', which cause gradient explosion), as it guides DiT's inherent low-frequency processing to highlight intrinsic facial features.
 - Fourier analysis (Figure 7) visually confirms that ConsisID's strategic injection effectively enhances both high and low-frequency information in the generated faces.

- **Ablation on Consistency Training Strategy (Figure 5, Table 2):**
 - **Coarse-to-Fine Training (CFT):** Essential for successful convergence by managing the interplay between low- and high-frequency learning. Removing it leads to suboptimal convergence.
 - **Dynamic Mask Loss (DML):** Improves facial consistency by focusing training on face regions. Without it, background noise negatively impacts training and facial fidelity. The dynamic application prevents issues with background generation.
 - **Dynamic Cross-Face Loss (DCL):** Crucial for improving generalization to unseen identities and preventing the model from "copy-pasting" the reference image. Removing it impairs fidelity for novel faces.

- **Effect of Inference Steps (Figure 9, Table 6):** The study revealed that optimal visual quality peaks at around 50 inference steps. Too few steps lead to incomplete features, while too many steps can cause a degradation in quality, particularly losing low-frequency facial information and overall clarity, suggesting an optimal balance between low-frequency (outline) and high-frequency (detail) denoising stages.

#### 4.4. Efficiency and Generalization

- **Computational Overhead (Table 5):** ConsisID introduces minimal additional parameters (0.5B) and similar inference time compared to its baseline DiT, making it efficient.
- **Inference Speed (Figure 6):** ConsisID integrates seamlessly with existing training-free inference acceleration strategies (e.g., TeaCache), demonstrating strong scalability.
- **Higher FPS (Figure 4):** Generated videos maintain coherence, allowing for successful post-processing with frame interpolation to achieve smoother playback (e.g., 16 fps).
- **Style Transfer (Figure 8):** Despite being trained on realistic facial data, ConsisID shows remarkable zero-shot generalization to generate anime-style videos while preserving identity, indicating broad applicability.

In summary, the results comprehensively demonstrate ConsisID's superior performance across multiple dimensions, validating the effectiveness of its frequency-aware design, strategic identity signal injection, and robust consistency training strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Identity-Preserving Text-to-Video Generation by Frequency Decomposition" marks a significant step forward in the field of generative AI, particularly for personalized video content creation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Technical Breakthrough in Tuning-Free IPT2V:** ConsisID is the first proposed tuning-free, Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based model for identity-preserving text-to-video generation. By eliminating the need for tedious, case-by-case fine-tuning, it dramatically reduces the computational and time overhead associated with personalization, making IPT2V accessible and practical for a much wider range of applications and users.

2. **Overcoming DiT Limitations via Frequency Decomposition:** The paper offers a novel and effective solution to a fundamental challenge in adapting powerful DiT architectures for detailed, controlled generation tasks like IPT2V. By meticulously analyzing DiT's limitations in handling low and high-frequency information, and strategically injecting identity signals based on this decomposition, ConsisID successfully leverages DiT's scalability and generative power while ensuring high fidelity and consistency of facial features. This frequency-aware heuristic control scheme provides an optimal solution for DiT-based models, paving the way for future controlled generation tasks on Transformer architectures.

3. **High-Quality, Editable, and Consistent Video Generation:** ConsisID consistently generates highly realistic, high-quality human-centered videos that not only preserve the identity from a reference image but also accurately respond to complex text prompts. This includes controlling actions, expressions, attributes (like age and makeup), backgrounds, and even camera angles. The robust identity consistency across frames, coupled with editing capabilities, is a crucial advancement for personalized digital content.

4. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The proposed consistency training strategies, including coarse-to-fine training, dynamic mask loss, and dynamic cross-face loss, significantly improve the model's generalization capabilities. This allows ConsisID to generate high-fidelity videos for unseen identities and diverse scenarios without overfitting, expanding its practical utility.

5. **Broad Practical Applications:** The ability to generate personalized, high-quality, and editable videos has immense potential across various industries:
 - **Content Creation:** Democratizing personalized video generation for social media, marketing, and entertainment, allowing creators to rapidly produce engaging content with consistent characters.
 - **Virtual Avatars and Digital Twins:** Facilitating the creation of realistic digital representations for virtual reality, gaming, and online communication.
 - **Film and Animation:** Streamlining the production of character animations and special effects, reducing manual labor.
 - **Accessibility:** Potentially enabling personalized sign language interpretation or synthetic character generation for educational purposes.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The work also highlights existing limitations in the field, particularly the need for more accurate and human-aligned evaluation metrics for generative video models. This emphasizes a crucial area for future research, ensuring that advancements in generative capabilities are accompanied by robust and relevant evaluation frameworks.

In conclusion, ConsisID is a significant contribution that pushes the technical boundaries of identity-preserving video generation. Its tuning-free, DiT-based, and frequency-aware approach offers a powerful, practical, and highly impactful solution for creating personalized, high-fidelity video content, positioning itself as a foundational step towards next-generation intelligent video synthesis. The authors also responsibly acknowledge the ethical implications, particularly the potential for misuse, emphasizing the ongoing need for responsible AI development.
