# Identity-Preserving Text-to-Video Generation by Frequency Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> This work, ConsisID by Peking University and collaborators, introduces a tuning-free, Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based model that generates identity-consistent videos from text prompts and a reference image. The method uses frequency decomposition to inject identity signals into the DiT, demonstrating improved identity preservation and video quality compared to prior approaches.

## Problem

- Existing identity-preserving text-to-video (IPT2V) methods typically require computationally expensive, per-identity fine-tuning, limiting their practical scalability.
- Current tuning-free IPT2V solutions often struggle with maintaining consistent identity, generating complex actions, or producing high-quality general videos.
- Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), while powerful, inherently lack mechanisms for efficient low-frequency feature aggregation (like U-Net skip connections) and struggle with high-frequency detail perception, posing challenges for identity preservation.

## Method

- **ConsisID** introduces a tuning-free, Diffusion Transformer (==DiT==)-based approach for identity-preserving text-to-video generation.
- It employs a Global Facial Extractor to inject low-frequency facial contours (e.g., key points) and a Local Facial Extractor to inject high-frequency intrinsic identity features (from face recognition and **CLIP** backbones) into the DiT.
- A robust consistency training strategy, including coarse-to-fine training, dynamic mask loss, and dynamic cross-face loss, is implemented to enhance identity consistency and generalization across frames and unseen identities.

## Results

- ConsisID outperforms state-of-the-art tuning-free methods (e.g., ID-Animator) in identity preservation (FaceSim-Arc 0.58 vs 0.32) and text relevance (CLIPScore 27.93 vs 24.97) while generating full-body videos with complex actions.
- It produces high-quality, identity-consistent videos for up to 49 frames, maintaining identity better than tuning-based methods and standard image-to-video models which suffer from temporal decay.
- Ablation studies confirm that both frequency decomposition (Global and Local Facial Extractors) and consistency training strategies (Coarse-to-Fine, Dynamic Mask, Dynamic Cross-Face Loss) are crucial for superior performance and generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing facial identity into low and high-frequency components and injecting them into specific DiT layers effectively addresses DiT's inherent limitations for detailed facial perception and pixel-level convergence.
> - A coarse-to-fine training strategy, coupled with dynamic masking and cross-face loss, significantly improves model convergence, facial consistency, and generalization to unseen identities.
> - Combining a global facial extractor for low-frequency contours and a local facial extractor for high-frequency intrinsic features provides an optimal frequency-aware control scheme for identity preservation in DiT-based models.
