# SigLIP 2: Multilingual Vision-Language Encoders with Improved Semantic Understanding, Localization, and Dense Features

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind's SigLIP 2 introduces a family of multilingual vision-language encoders, integrating decoder-based pretraining, self-supervised losses, and active data curation to enhance semantic understanding, localization, and dense features. It consistently outperforms previous SigLIP models and other open-source baselines across zero-shot classification, retrieval, dense prediction, and localization tasks, while also reducing representation bias.

## Problem

- Traditional contrastive vision-language models often lacked strong fine-grained spatial understanding for dense prediction and localization tasks.
- Existing open-source vision-language model efforts had not yet unified the broad range of recent advancements (e.g., decoder-based pretraining, self-supervised learning, data curation) into a single, high-performing model family.
- There was a need for vision-language encoders with robust multilingual capabilities and reduced societal biases for broader real-world applicability.

## Method

- Extends the original SigLIP training recipe by integrating ==decoder-based pretraining== for auxiliary tasks like image captioning and referring expression prediction.
- Incorporates ==self-supervised losses==, including ==local-to-global consistency== and masked prediction, during the later stages of training to enhance dense feature representations.
- Leverages the WebLI multilingual dataset with fairness-mitigation filtering and employs ==active data curation== to boost the performance of smaller models, alongside introducing ==NaFlex== variants for variable resolution and native aspect ratio support.

## Results

- SigLIP 2 consistently outperforms SigLIP and other open-weight baselines across zero-shot classification, image-text retrieval (including multilingual XM3600), and serves as a stronger vision encoder for Vision-Language Models.
- It shows substantial improvements on dense prediction tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation, depth estimation) and localization tasks (e.g., referring expression comprehension, open-vocabulary detection), attributed to integrated self-supervised and decoder-based losses.
- The models exhibit improved cultural diversity understanding and significantly reduced representation bias (e.g., a reduction from **35.5%** bias to **7.3%** for the L/16 model), while NaFlex variants achieve better performance on aspect-sensitive document and OCR benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified training recipe combining diverse advanced techniques, such as decoder-based pretraining, self-supervised losses, and active data curation, can yield compounded performance improvements in vision-language models.
> - Integrating auxiliary decoder tasks and self-supervised losses during pretraining effectively enhances models' capabilities for dense prediction and fine-grained localization.
> - Fairness mitigation techniques applied during training can substantially reduce representation biases in vision-language models.
