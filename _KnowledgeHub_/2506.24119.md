# SPIRAL: Self-Play on Zero-Sum Games Incentivizes Reasoning via Multi-Agent Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SPIRAL framework trains Large Language Models (LLMs) to acquire transferable reasoning skills by engaging in self-play on two-player zero-sum language games. This approach automates curriculum generation and leads to substantial improvements, such as an 8.6% increase in mathematical reasoning and 8.4% in general reasoning for a Qwen3-4B model.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLM reasoning suffer from a 'scalability bottleneck,' relying heavily on human-curated problem-answer pairs, domain-specific reward engineering, and expert supervision.
- Prior applications of self-play to language models were limited to offline updates, simple word games, or single-turn tasks, failing to leverage the full potential of multi-turn competitive dynamics for extended strategic reasoning.
- The challenge of developing an autonomous and scalable method to enhance generalizable reasoning capabilities in LLMs without continuous human intervention.

## Method

- The SPIRAL framework implements a fully ==online, multi-turn, multi-agent reinforcement learning== system for LLMs using two-player zero-sum language games.
- It frames learning as a turn-level ==Markov Decision Process==, where each action is a complete, multi-token response, and both players share a single, role-conditioned policy.
- A key technical innovation, ==Role-conditioned Advantage Estimation (RAE)==, uses separate, exponentially moving average baselines for each game and player role to stabilize training and reduce variance in multi-agent REINFORCE.

## Results

- SPIRAL, trained exclusively on Kuhn Poker, improved Qwen3-4B-Base's mathematical reasoning by **8.6%** and general reasoning by **8.4%**, demonstrating transferability of skills from games to standard reasoning benchmarks.
- Self-play significantly outperformed training against fixed opponents, which either led to 'thinking collapse' with random opponents or exploitation and overfitting with strong fixed opponents.
- Multi-game training (TicTacToe + Kuhn Poker + Simple Negotiation) achieved superior average performance on out-of-distribution games (**44.9%** vs. **27.1-34.4%** for single-game specialists) and improved even a stronger DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-7B model by **2.0 percentage points**.
- Role-conditioned Advantage Estimation (RAE) was essential for stable self-play, preventing 'thinking collapse' (where reasoning traces ceased to be generated) and ensuring stable policy gradient norms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Competitive pressure within self-play on zero-sum games forces Large Language Models (LLMs) to develop genuine, transferable reasoning abilities rather than memorization or exploitation of static strategies.
> - Self-play's automatic, adaptive curriculum consistently outperforms fixed-opponent training, which leads to overfitting and poor generalization in LLMs.
> - Different types of zero-sum games cultivate specialized reasoning skills in LLMs, and training across multiple games yields synergistic benefits, enhancing performance on novel, composite reasoning challenges.
