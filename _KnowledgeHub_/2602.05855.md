# A Hybrid Autoencoder for Robust Heightmap Generation from Fused Lidar and Depth Data for Humanoid Robot Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Leibniz University Hannover developed a hybrid autoencoder that fuses LiDAR, depth, and IMU data to generate robust, robot-centric heightmaps for humanoid robot locomotion. This perception system enables more stable and anticipatory gait patterns, leading to a 70.1% reduction in falls and improved command tracking on complex terrains.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges achieving reliable and safe locomotion in unstructured environments due to limitations in terrain perception.
- Existing single-sensor solutions for terrain perception are susceptible to environmental variations, processing latency, or lack robustness across diverse conditions.
- Traditional mapping techniques often incur high computational costs, are prone to mapping drift, or require extensive manual configuration, hindering real-time application.

## Method

- A learning-based, modular perception-to-control framework was developed, featuring a hybrid ==Encoder-Decoder Structure (EDS)== for terrain reconstruction.
- Multimodal sensor data from an Intel RealSense depth camera, a LIVOX MID-360 LiDAR, and an IMU are integrated and efficiently processed into a robot-centric heightmap.
- A two-stage training process includes unsupervised pretraining of CNN encoders for robust feature extraction, followed by supervised training of the full EDS with a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) core for temporal consistency.

## Results

- Multimodal sensor fusion achieved a **2.19** cm Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in heightmap reconstruction, showing a **7.2%** improvement over depth-only and **9.9%** over LiDAR-only configurations.
- The optimized robot-centric heightmap and refined reward function in the DRL policy led to a **70.1%** reduction in falls and improved command tracking accuracy (**25.0%** less linear velocity error, **17.2%** less angular velocity error).
- The perception module, trained with a **3.2**-second temporal horizon, reduced reconstruction errors by approximately **30%** compared to shorter horizons, facilitating anticipatory gait patterns on complex terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal fusion of depth and LiDAR data significantly improves heightmap reconstruction accuracy and robustness compared to single-sensor approaches.
> - The incorporation of temporal context through a GRU core enhances perception stability by modeling short-term motion dynamics and mitigating sensor occlusions or noise.
> - Systematic optimization of the robot-centric heightmap's grid spacing and resolution (7 cm per cell) is crucial for enabling anticipatory locomotion behaviors and improving the performance of the control policy.
