# A Hybrid Autoencoder for Robust Heightmap Generation from Fused Lidar and Depth Data for Humanoid Robot Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Leibniz University Hannover developed a hybrid autoencoder that fuses LiDAR, depth, and IMU data to generate robust, robot-centric heightmaps for humanoid robot locomotion. This perception system enables more stable and anticipatory gait patterns, leading to a 70.1% reduction in falls and improved command tracking on complex terrains.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges achieving reliable and safe locomotion in unstructured environments due to limitations in terrain perception.
- Existing single-sensor solutions for terrain perception are susceptible to environmental variations, processing latency, or lack robustness across diverse conditions.
- Traditional mapping techniques often incur high computational costs, are prone to mapping drift, or require extensive manual configuration, hindering real-time application.

## Method

- A learning-based, modular perception-to-control framework was developed, featuring a hybrid ==Encoder-Decoder Structure (EDS)== for terrain reconstruction.
- Multimodal sensor data from an Intel RealSense depth camera, a LIVOX MID-360 LiDAR, and an IMU are integrated and efficiently processed into a robot-centric heightmap.
- A two-stage training process includes unsupervised pretraining of CNN encoders for robust feature extraction, followed by supervised training of the full EDS with a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) core for temporal consistency.

## Results

- Multimodal sensor fusion achieved a **2.19** cm Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in heightmap reconstruction, showing a **7.2%** improvement over depth-only and **9.9%** over LiDAR-only configurations.
- The optimized robot-centric heightmap and refined reward function in the DRL policy led to a **70.1%** reduction in falls and improved command tracking accuracy (**25.0%** less linear velocity error, **17.2%** less angular velocity error).
- The perception module, trained with a **3.2**-second temporal horizon, reduced reconstruction errors by approximately **30%** compared to shorter horizons, facilitating anticipatory gait patterns on complex terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal fusion of depth and LiDAR data significantly improves heightmap reconstruction accuracy and robustness compared to single-sensor approaches.
> - The incorporation of temporal context through a GRU core enhances perception stability by modeling short-term motion dynamics and mitigating sensor occlusions or noise.
> - Systematic optimization of the robot-centric heightmap's grid spacing and resolution (7 cm per cell) is crucial for enabling anticipatory locomotion behaviors and improving the performance of the control policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots in complex, unstructured, and human-centric environments necessitates reliable terrain perception for stable and safe locomotion. Traditional approaches to terrain-aware locomotion for bipedal systems frequently rely on manually engineered pipelines, often utilizing data from a single sensor modality. These single-sensor solutions present inherent limitations; for instance, depth camera data is susceptible to variations in lighting conditions and surface properties, while LiDAR data processing can introduce significant latency, particularly in dynamic scenarios. Furthermore, established mapping techniques such as Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), elevation mapping, and voxelization often incur high computational costs, are prone to mapping drift over time, or require extensive manual configuration to achieve robustness across varied conditions.

This research addresses these existing limitations by proposing a learning-based perception framework. It departs from traditional methods by employing an intermediate, robot-centric heightmap representation for terrain information, which is then fed to a control policy. The work contributes to the field by introducing a hybrid Encoder-Decoder Structure (EDS) that integrates multimodal sensor data and incorporates temporal consistency modeling, aiming to provide a more robust and efficient solution for terrain perception compared to prior single-sensor or purely spatial approaches. By focusing on a compact, optimized heightmap representation, the study also contributes to the ongoing effort to balance the detail required for precise foothold planning with the computational efficiency necessary for real-time robotic control. This approach seeks to enhance the reliability and efficiency of terrain perception for humanoid robots, thereby facilitating their operation in challenging environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the ongoing challenge of enabling humanoid robots to achieve reliable and safe locomotion in unstructured environments. While humanoid robots offer versatility for tasks in human-centered settings, their inherent morphology—characterized by a high center of mass and bipedal structure—makes stable locomotion complex. Effective control policies are therefore required to integrate proprioceptive feedback with exteroceptive perception, such as data from depth cameras or LiDAR sensors.

The key objectives of this work were to:

- **Overcome limitations of existing terrain perception methods:** Address the drawbacks of single-sensor solutions (e.g., sensitivity of depth cameras to lighting, latency of LiDAR processing) and traditional mapping techniques (e.g., computational demands, drift, reliance on manual engineering) that hinder robust terrain perception for humanoid robots.
- **Develop a learning-based perception-to-control framework:** Establish a modular system where a perception module is dedicated to reconstructing the environment from raw sensor inputs, providing an intermediate robot-centric heightmap representation to a control policy. This modularity is intended to simplify development and improve system robustness.
- **Design a hybrid Encoder-Decoder Structure (EDS):** Create a perception module that combines a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for effective spatial feature extraction with a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) core for modeling temporal consistency. This hybrid architecture aims to leverage the strengths of both network types for improved terrain reconstruction.
- **Enhance robustness through multimodal data fusion:** Integrate complementary data from an Intel RealSense depth camera, a LIVOX MID-360 LiDAR, and an onboard Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU). The objective is to utilize the strengths of each sensor to compensate for the weaknesses of others, thereby improving overall perception accuracy and resilience.
- **Implement efficient LiDAR data processing:** Develop a method for processing unstructured 3D LiDAR point cloud data into a structured 2D range image using spherical projection. This aims to reduce computational complexity, memory requirements, and processing latency for real-time applications.
- **Optimize the robot-centric heightmap representation:** Systematically investigate and determine the optimal grid point spacing and resolution for the heightmap. The goal is to identify a configuration that balances the precision required for detecting terrain features (e.g., stair edges) with the computational efficiency needed for stable policy learning, and to foster anticipatory locomotion behavior.
- **Demonstrate the impact on locomotion policy performance:** Evaluate whether the optimized heightmap, combined with refined reward shaping in a deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework, enables the humanoid robot to exhibit anticipatory behaviors, improve stability, and enhance command tracking precision on complex terrains.
- **Quantify the benefits of multimodal fusion and temporal context:** Provide quantitative results on the reconstruction accuracy of the perception module under different sensor configurations (depth-only, LiDAR-only, fused) and varying temporal sequence lengths to validate the design choices.

In essence, the research aimed to establish a validated, real-time-capable foundation for terrain-aware humanoid locomotion in complex environments by developing a robust, learning-based perception system that efficiently integrates multimodal sensor data into an optimized, robot-centric heightmap.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a learning-based perception-to-control framework structured into distinct modules for locomotion control and terrain perception.

#### 3.1. Locomotion Policy and Heightmap Optimization

The locomotion control system was realized through a deep reinforcement learning (DRL) policy. This policy was trained in the Isaac Lab simulation environment using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm within an actor-critic framework.
The policy’s observation space integrated proprioceptive signals, base velocity commands, and the robot-centric heightmap generated by the perception module. The action space comprised target joint positions for the robot's 23 actuated degrees of freedom, which were managed by low-level proportional-derivative (PD) controllers.

A critical component was the design and optimization of the robot-centric heightmap, serving as the primary exteroceptive input. The heightmap stores local elevation relative to the robot's base. Systematic investigation into grid point spacing and resolution identified an optimal range of 6–8 cm per cell. Specifically, a heightmap measuring 0.98 m in length and 0.7 m in width, with a resolution of 7 cm, was selected. To enhance the field of view in the robot's direction of travel, the heightmap’s center was shifted forward by 0.2 m. Deviations from this optimal spacing were observed to negatively affect policy performance: finer resolutions increased input dimensionality, hindering learning, while coarser resolutions failed to capture discrete terrain features, leading to reduced foothold precision.
The reward function for the DRL policy was refined to encourage terrain-aware behavior. Adjustments included a symmetry term for foot airtime and an increased penalty for sliding foot contacts, promoting deliberate foot lifting and anticipatory foothold placement.

#### 3.2. Encoder-Decoder Structure for Heightmap Reconstruction

The perception module, a hybrid Encoder-Decoder Structure (EDS), was responsible for transforming raw sensory data into the optimized heightmap.

- **Sensor Modalities:** The system integrates data from an Intel RealSense depth camera, a LIVOX MID-360 LiDAR, and an onboard IMU.
- **LiDAR Processing via Spherical Projection:** To process unstructured 3D LiDAR point cloud data efficiently within a convolutional neural network (CNN), a spherical projection was applied. This method maps Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) into a range-azimuth-inclination space, discretizing it into a 2D range image. The LIVOX MID-360 point cloud was projected into a 276 × 40 resolution grid, with an effective vertical field of view (FOV) from -7° to 52° and a horizontal resolution of approximately 1.3° at a 10 Hz update rate. Preprocessing steps included row-wise gap-filling, nearest-neighbor filling, and a 3 × 3 median filter to mitigate projection artifacts. Values were clipped to a range of 0.2 m to 3.0 m.
- **Architectural Components:** The EDS features a two-stage design with separate CNN encoders for each visual modality and a shared recurrent decoder core.
 - **CNN Encoders:** Separate CNN branches processed depth images (160 × 120 resolution) and spherical LiDAR range images (276 × 40 resolution). Each encoder comprised four strided convolutional layers with 3 × 3 kernels, progressively downsampling spatial resolution and increasing feature depth. The extracted feature maps were flattened and mapped through fully connected layers to produce a 256-dimensional latent representation for each modality.
 - **Multimodal Fusion:** The 256-dimensional latent vectors from the depth camera and LiDAR were concatenated with auxiliary inputs: a 15-dimensional robot state vector (IMU-derived linear and angular velocities, position, and orientation) and the 165-dimensional heightmap prediction from the previous timestep. These inputs were fused into a unified representation via linear transformations and layer normalization.
 - **Recurrent Decoder:** The temporal core consisted of two stacked Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) layers, each with a hidden size of 256. These GRUs were designed to capture temporal dependencies and short-term motion dynamics, stabilizing terrain predictions amidst sensor occlusions or noise. The resulting hidden state was passed to a decoder head, comprising two fully connected layers, which output the final 165-dimensional predicted heightmap.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Process

A staged training methodology was employed for the perception module.
- **Stage 1 (Pretraining):** Symmetric convolutional autoencoders (AEs) were pretrained in an unsupervised manner using noisy simulated sensor data from both depth camera and LiDAR. This stage aimed to stabilize feature extraction and compress terrain-relevant information into a compact 256-dimensional latent bottleneck. Input data was corrupted with Gaussian noise (standard deviation of 1 cm) and random-shaped occlusion masks (up to 3% of the image area) to enhance robustness. Training utilized a pixel-wise Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, which demonstrated superior performance compared to other loss functions in preliminary trials. U-Net style skip connections were intentionally excluded to enforce high information density within the latent representation.
- **Stage 2 (EDS Training):** The pretrained CNN encoders were integrated into the full EDS, replacing the original AE decoders with the recurrent temporal core. The perception module was then trained in a supervised setting using backpropagation through time, with ground-truth heightmaps from simulation as targets. A comprehensive dataset of 400,000 samples per modality was used, split into 70% for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing. Optimization was performed using the AdamW optimizer with a plateau-based learning rate schedule over 40 training epochs. The framework was designed to operate at 10 Hz for real-time terrain updates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation focused on the reconstruction accuracy of the perception module and the resulting stability of the locomotion policy.

#### 4.1. Perception Module Performance

The reconstruction accuracy of the Encoder-Decoder Structure (EDS) was quantified using Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across different sensor configurations and temporal sequence lengths.

- **Sensor Fusion Benefits:** The multimodal fusion setup achieved a reconstruction accuracy of 2.19 cm MAE. This represented a 7.2% improvement in accuracy compared to the depth-only configuration (2.36 cm MAE) and a 9.9% improvement compared to the LiDAR-only configuration (2.43 cm MAE). These results indicate that the integration of complementary sensor data enhances terrain perception robustness.
- **Temporal Context:** The incorporation of temporal context proved beneficial. Models trained with a 3.2-second temporal horizon (32 timesteps) exhibited approximately 30% lower reconstruction errors compared to those utilizing shorter horizons. Extending the sequence length to 6.4 seconds yielded diminishing returns, suggesting that a 3.2-second window provides a balance between accuracy and computational efficiency.
- **Terrain-Specific Accuracy:** High reconstruction accuracy, with MAE values consistently below 2 cm, was observed on flat or gently varying surfaces. However, accuracy decreased on discontinuous surfaces, such as stairs. In these instances, sharp height changes were often smoothed into gradual slopes, attributed to the pixel-wise loss function prioritizing global statistical fitting over high-frequency spatial details.

#### 4.2. Policy Performance and Heightmap Impact

The locomotion policy was evaluated to ascertain if the optimized robot-centric heightmap provided sufficient contextual information for terrain-aware control.

- **Anticipatory Behavior:** The customized heightmap, configured at 0.98 m × 0.7 m with a 7 cm resolution and shifted 0.2 m forward, facilitated the emergence of anticipatory gait patterns. The optimized policy learned to precisely lift the swing leg to clear anticipated elevation changes, such as steps and stairs, contrasting with a baseline setup.
- **Stability Metrics:** The transition from a blind baseline setup to one incorporating the customized heightmap and refined reward structure resulted in a 70.1% reduction in falls. Command tracking precision also improved, with a 25.0% reduction in linear velocity error and a 17.2% reduction in angular velocity error.
- **Noise Robustness:** The policy maintained stable locomotion even when Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of up to 2 cm was applied to the heightmap inputs. This indicates that the compact heightmap representation supports robust control under moderate perceptual uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Discussion of Limitations

The research identified several limitations. While the hybrid EDS accurately reconstructs global topology, it tends to smooth sharp vertical discontinuities, such as stair edges. This smoothing, attributed to the pixel-wise MSE loss, may reduce foothold precision by representing abrupt changes as gradual slopes. Locomotion stability was also found to be sensitive to reconstruction fidelity; applying Gaussian noise with a standard deviation exceeding 2 cm to heightmap inputs led to significant degradation of anticipatory behavior and increased fall rates. Additionally, the 2.5-D heightmap representation inherently cannot capture overhanging structures. A sim-to-real gap was observed, with pretraining failures occurring on cluttered real-world datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing critical challenges in terrain perception for robust locomotion in unstructured environments. The proposed learning-based, modular perception-to-control architecture offers several significant aspects and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Accuracy:** The quantitative results demonstrate that multimodal sensor fusion (depth camera and LiDAR) significantly improves heightmap reconstruction accuracy by 7.2% over depth-only and 9.9% over LiDAR-only systems. This enhanced robustness is critical for reliable robot operation in diverse and unpredictable environments where single-sensor modalities may fail. The integration of a GRU-based temporal core further contributes to perception stability, particularly when sensor data is noisy or temporarily occluded.
- **Optimized Terrain Representation:** The systematic optimization of the robot-centric heightmap's grid spacing and resolution (identifying an optimal 7 cm per cell for a 0.98 m × 0.7 m grid) is a key contribution. This tailored representation balances the need for detailed terrain information with computational efficiency, directly impacting the learning stability and performance of the locomotion policy. This optimization led to a 70.1% reduction in fall-related terminations and improved command tracking, signifying a more stable and effective control system.
- **Facilitation of Anticipatory Behavior:** The customized heightmap, coupled with refined reward shaping, enabled the emergence of anticipatory gait patterns. This capability allows humanoid robots to plan footholds and adjust leg trajectories in advance of encountering terrain changes, moving beyond reactive control. This is a fundamental step towards more agile and intelligent locomotion.
- **Real-Time Capability:** The framework is designed to operate at 10 Hz, supported by efficient LiDAR processing via spherical projection and the use of compact latent representations. This real-time capability is essential for dynamic robotic applications where rapid perception-action cycles are required, making the system suitable for implementation on resource-constrained onboard hardware.
- **Modular and Scalable Design:** Decoupling the perception module from the control policy provides a modular framework. This design allows for independent development and optimization of each component, potentially simplifying system integration and enabling the perception module to be adapted for different control policies or robot platforms.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Deployment:** The work establishes a validated foundation for autonomous humanoid operation in complex environments. By quantifying the benefits of multimodal fusion and temporal modeling, and by systematically optimizing terrain representation, this research informs future developments in edge-aware loss functions, hybrid neural architectures (e.g., CNN-Transformer), comprehensive domain randomization, and closed-loop hardware evaluations. These advancements are crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap and achieving the eventual deployment of humanoid robots in practical, real-world applications such as industrial inspection, logistics, or human assistance.
