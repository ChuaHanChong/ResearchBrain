# Cross-Modal Navigation with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northeastern University developed CRONA, a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning framework that enables decentralized, modality-specialized agents to perform cross-modal navigation. This framework achieved a 95.72% success rate in a 'Studio' scene and demonstrated improved robustness and parameter efficiency compared to monolithic multi-modal models in various complex environments.

## Problem

- Embodied navigation systems struggle with noise, incompleteness, and misalignment in multi-modal sensory data, leading to unstable policy learning and dominant modalities suppressing others.
- Monolithic multi-modal models for rich inputs are computationally expensive, difficult to deploy, and create complex policy spaces.
- Existing multi-agent navigation often assumes homogeneous agents or limited sensory configurations, leaving heterogeneous, cross-modal collaboration largely unexplored.

## Method

- Introduces **CRONA**, a Multi-Agent ==Reinforcement Learning== framework for ==cross-modal== navigation using lightweight, modality-specialized agents operating under a centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm.
- Incorporates control-relevant auxiliary beliefs, specifically target location and category, inferred from local audio observations to enhance collaborative capability.
- Utilizes a centralized multi-modal critic that integrates global state information to improve the stability and effectiveness of decentralized policies during training.

## Results

- CRONA achieved the highest success rate of **95.72%** in the 'Studio' scene, significantly outperforming the single-agent baseline (**32.66%** success rate) and other collaborative methods.
- Ablation studies indicated that removing location belief led to a substantial performance drop (e.g., CRONA's success rate decreased by **38.46%** on the 'Ranch' scene), underscoring its critical role.
- CRONA exhibited greater stability across different embedding sizes and maintained robustness (**42.76%** success) even at low visual resolutions (4x4 pixels), where homogeneous vision-based methods degraded considerably.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-modal collaboration with modality-specialized agents offers a scalable, flexible, and parameter-efficient alternative to monolithic multi-modal models, particularly when targets possess distinct, modality-specific cues.
> - The framework demonstrates enhanced robustness to varying qualities of sensory input, such as low visual resolution, as specialized audio agents can compensate for poor visual information.
> - Integrating accurate spatial belief prediction (location belief) and global state information into a centralized critic is crucial for stabilizing and improving learning in multi-agent policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation systems frequently rely on diverse sensory inputs, such as visual (RGB-D), acoustic, radar, LiDAR, and linguistic instructions, to enable agents to perceive and navigate complex environments. These multi-modal inputs can offer complementary cues, which is particularly relevant for applications like autonomous driving and robotic systems. However, real-world multi-modal data often presents challenges, including noise, incompleteness, and asynchronous or misaligned signals. This can lead to unstable policy learning and imbalanced joint optimization, where dominant modalities overshadow weaker ones. Furthermore, monolithic models integrating rich multi-modal inputs often require large architectures, which can be computationally expensive, difficult to deploy, and create complex representations with enlarged policy spaces.

In parallel, multi-agent collaboration has been explored to enhance navigation efficiency and robustness. Prior work in this area often focuses on homogeneous agents—where all agents receive inputs from the same modalities—or operates under limited sensory configurations. While centralized planning methods exist for multi-agent coordination, they generally suffer from scalability and robustness issues. Recent trends favor decentralized collaboration, allowing agents to act based on local observations, sometimes with limited or no inter-agent communication. However, the potential of heterogeneous collaboration, where agents possess distinct sensory modalities, remains largely underexplored.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a framework for heterogeneous, cross-modal collaboration among decentralized agents. It deviates from monolithic multi-modal models by employing lightweight, modality-specialized agents that collaborate, thereby offering a potentially more scalable and flexible paradigm. The work also extends the paradigm of centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) in cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) by incorporating auxiliary beliefs and global state information into a centralized critic. By investigating navigation scenarios within diverse Matterport3D environments, this study contributes to understanding the effectiveness and efficiency of different modality configurations in collaborative embodied AI, particularly focusing on visual-acoustic interactions without explicit inter-agent communication.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from several limitations observed in current embodied navigation systems:
- **Challenges with Multi-modal Data**: High-quality and perfectly aligned multi-modal data is often scarce. When integrating multiple modalities into a single, monolithic model, issues such as noise, incompleteness, and temporal or dimensional mismatch between modalities can lead to unstable learning and inefficient utilization of all available cues. Dominant modalities might inadvertently suppress the learning contribution of weaker or noisier ones.
- **Computational Complexity and Deployment of Monolithic Models**: Models designed to process rich multi-modal inputs typically require large architectures. This complexity leads to high computational costs during training and inference, making them difficult to deploy in resource-constrained environments. The policy space also expands significantly with complex multi-modal representations, increasing the challenge of learning robust policies.
- **Limitations of Existing Multi-Agent Navigation**: While multi-agent collaboration can enhance navigation, most existing approaches either assume homogeneous agents (all agents have the same sensory inputs) or operate under restricted sensory configurations. There is a lack of comprehensive investigation into heterogeneous collaboration, where agents leverage complementary modalities, and how such configurations impact navigation effectiveness and efficiency. Specifically, questions regarding which modalities are most beneficial for collaboration, what team configurations are optimal, and what cooperative behaviors naturally emerge from such setups remain largely unanswered.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:
- To propose CRONA (Cross-Modal Navigation with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning), a MARL framework for cross-modal navigation that facilitates collaboration among lightweight, modality-specialized agents. This framework aims to enable flexible deployment and parallel execution while preserving the strengths of individual modalities.
- To improve collaborative capability within the proposed MARL framework by leveraging control-relevant auxiliary beliefs, specifically target location and category, inferred from local observations.
- To enhance the training stability and effectiveness of decentralized policies by employing a centralized multi-modal critic that incorporates global state information.
- To construct a collaborative navigation benchmark based on diverse Matterport3D scenes, enabling rigorous evaluation of heterogeneous multi-agent systems in complex environments.
- To empirically evaluate the performance of cross-modal multi-agent navigation against single-agent and homogeneous multi-agent baselines in visual-acoustic navigation tasks.
- To identify and analyze modality-dominance patterns in various navigation scenarios, explaining when and why specific modalities or combinations of modalities contribute most effectively to task completion. This includes investigating the trade-offs between homogeneous and heterogeneous collaboration, and the impact of model capacity and input quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research approaches the problem of cross-modal navigation within a cooperative multi-agent setting using a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP) formulation. The proposed framework, CRONA, adheres to a centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The navigation task is modeled as a Dec-POMDP, defined by a set of `n` decentralized agents `I`, a global state space `S`, individual observation spaces `{O_i}`, individual action spaces `{A_i}`, a shared joint reward function `R`, a transition function `T`, a discount factor `γ`, and an episode horizon `H`. Agents make decisions based on local observations `o_i,t`, which include agent pose, natural-language goal descriptions, and modality-specific inputs (visual RGB-D and/or binaural audio). The objective for the joint policy `π = {π_1..., π_n}` is to maximize the expected cumulative reward over the episode.

#### 3.2. Acoustic Representation

To address the high-dimensionality and temporal complexity of raw audio signals, CRONA converts binaural audio waveforms into magnitude spectrograms using the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT). This representation highlights time-frequency features, making acoustic patterns more explicit for learning. The spectrograms `o_spec_i,t` have dimensions `R^2xKxF`, where `κ` denotes left/right channels, `K` is the number of frequency bins, and `F` is the number of time frames.

#### 3.3. Auxiliary Belief Predictor

To mitigate the challenges of learning directly from noisy and stochastic audio observations, CRONA incorporates auxiliary belief predictors for audio-based agents. These predictors infer control-relevant information:
- **Location Head**: A convolutional encoder processes the spectrogram `o_spec_i,t` to extract acoustic features `z_audio_i,t`. A location head then predicts an instantaneous sound-source goal `b_goal_i,t` in global coordinates. This global coordinate is transformed into a relative location `b_loc_i,t` within the agent's frame using a 2D rotation matrix based on the agent's current pose.
- **Category Head**: A fully connected layer predicts a belief `b_cat_i,t` over target categories based on `z_audio_i,t`.
These instantaneous predictions are smoothed over time using an exponential moving average to reduce variance. The belief predictor is optimized by minimizing a loss function comprising an L2 loss for location prediction against the ground-truth closest target goal point and a binary cross-entropy loss for category prediction against multi-hot category labels of all targets. These beliefs are inferred from local histories, maintaining consistency with decentralized policies.

#### 3.4. Attention-Based History Encoder

Each agent maintains a local observation-action history to inform its policy. To manage computational expense, a fixed-size memory cache stores a short-term history of `k` previous observation embeddings and actions.
- **Modality Encoders**: Convolutional encoders (ResNet-18 for visual, plain CNN for audio) process RGB-D images and spectrograms, generating latent features (`z_rgb_i,t`, `z_depth_i,t`, `z_audio_i,t`). Visual encoders are deeper due to higher dimensionality and richer spatial structures.
- **Observation Embedding**: Encoded features are concatenated with agent pose and goal instruction to form a latent observation embedding `z_o_i,t`.
- **History Representation**: The cached observation-action sequence is then processed by transformer blocks (multi-head attention layers) to produce a compact history representation `z_h_i,t`. This representation captures geometric, acoustic, and motion patterns, enabling agents to select actions `a_i,t` based on their local history.

#### 3.5. Centralized Critic with Global Information

CRONA uses a centralized critic `V_ϕ(z_h_t, b_t, s_t)` during training to estimate the joint value function. This critic leverages:
- **Joint History Embedding**: `z_h_t`, formed by concatenating all agents’ history embeddings.
- **Auxiliary Beliefs**: `b_t = {b_1,t..., b_n,t}` from audio-based agents.
- **Global State**: `s_t`, including target locations, agent positions/orientations, and task completion indicators.
The paper proves that augmenting the critic with these history-induced beliefs and global state provides an unbiased estimate of the value function conditioned only on history. The critic and actor policies share modality-specific encoders, the auxiliary belief predictor, and history transformers to improve representation learning and accelerate training, but use separate heads for policy and value prediction.
Training utilizes Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Advantages `A_t` are computed using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). The critic is trained by minimizing a clipped value surrogate objective against the return target `R_t`. Each agent's decentralized policy `π_θ_i(· | h_i,t, b_i,t)` is updated by maximizing a clipped surrogate objective using the shared advantage estimate `A_t`, with an entropy regularization term to encourage exploration. Gradients from both actor and critic objectives are backpropagated through shared modules, optimized by a weighted sum of the policy gradient and temporal difference loss.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted in Matterport3D scenes simulated using Habitat and libsora. Five representative scenes (Studio, Corridor, Apartment, Ranch, Maze) with varying layouts and difficulties are used. Collaborative navigation datasets with two agents are constructed, targeting one to three objects with distinct sound sources. Visual observations are restricted to depth maps (0–5m range, 16x16 pixels, 10° HFoV) to increase task difficulty and necessitate collaboration. Baselines include a single-agent model and homogeneous collaborative agents (VLA-Collab, ALA-Collab, AVLA-Collab), all implemented with comparable configurations for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate CRONA against baselines across five Matterport3D scenes, categorizing the findings into five modality-dominance patterns based on task completion and navigation efficiency.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison Across Scenes

- **No Clear Dominance (Studio)**: In this single-room scene with a visually salient target (picture with camera-shutter sound), all collaborative methods (VLA-Collab, ALA-Collab, AVLA-Collab, CRONA) perform well, achieving success rates above 85%. CRONA exhibits the highest success rate at 95.72%. All collaborative methods significantly outperform the single-agent baseline (32.66% success), demonstrating the benefit of decentralized agents covering larger areas and mitigating premature stopping.
- **Audio Dominance (Corridor)**: The Corridor scene involves locating a sink with dripping water in a passage connecting two areas. This task is dominated by audio cues. ALA-Collab, leveraging only audio and language, achieves the best success rate (25.31%). Vision-based methods (VLA-Collab, AVLA-Collab) perform poorly (around 14% success) due to the corridor geometry limiting informative visual cues. This indicates that vision is not universally reliable and audio can be more effective in specific environments. High timeout rates (over 74%) across all methods suggest the difficulty of recovery from early incorrect decisions.
- **Vision Dominance (Apartment)**: In the Apartment scene (bed and counter targets), collaboration between two vision agents (VLA-Collab) achieves the best performance (78.96% success). Targets are large and visually salient. Audio observations are less reliable, as evidenced by the lower success rate of ALA-Collab (38.23%), possibly due to mixed audio signals disrupting auxiliary belief prediction. CRONA outperforms AVLA-Collab (68.52% vs. 63.38%), suggesting that weak or unreliable modalities can hinder monolithic multi-modal policies by diverting representational capacity.
- **Cross-Modal Dominance (Ranch)**: CRONA demonstrates the strongest performance in the Ranch scene (64.62% success), involving a picture with camera-shutter sound and a table with silverware-dropping sounds. This is attributed to effective collaboration between agents specializing in complementary modalities. The audio agent localizes the picture via distinct sound cues, while the vision agent identifies the table visually. Surprisingly, AVLA-Collab performs worse (18.93%) than VLA-Collab and ALA-Collab (around 40%), indicating challenges in monolithic multi-modal models with limited capacity in aligning and exploiting diverse modalities. This pattern suggests that cross-modal collaboration is effective and parameter-efficient when targets have clean, modality-specific cues.
- **Multi-Modal Dominance (Maze)**: The Maze scene, the largest and most complex, highlights the benefit of richer multi-modal inputs and increased model capacity. AVLA-Collab achieves the best success rate (26.16%). CRONA performs moderately worse than homogeneous multi-modal baselines, suggesting its cross-modal approach can still effectively utilize partial, modality-specific inputs. Overall, CRONA shows robustness and efficiency compared to multi-modal collaboration.

#### 4.2. Aggregated Success Rate

Aggregated results across all scenes indicate that CRONA generally achieves a higher success rate over time compared to other collaborative baselines, demonstrating its robustness and overall effectiveness in diverse scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies conducted on the Ranch scene provide insights into model capacity, input signal quality, and the importance of CRONA's components:
- **Embedding Size**: The homogeneous multi-modal baseline (AVLA-Collab) is highly sensitive to embedding size. With a small embedding size (60), its success rate is significantly low (0.06%). Increasing the embedding size improves its performance substantially, eventually outperforming CRONA at larger capacities (e.g., 73.33% at 180 vs. CRONA's 68.75%). CRONA, however, demonstrates greater stability across embedding sizes, as its specialized agents process fewer modalities, simplifying representation learning.
- **Visual Resolution**: Homogeneous vision-based methods (VLA-Collab and AVLA-Collab) degrade considerably at low visual resolutions (e.g., 4x4 pixels), with success rates dropping to 12.76% and 15.43%, respectively. CRONA maintains robustness, achieving 42.76% success even at the lowest resolution, due to modality specialization where the audio agent can compensate for poor visual input.
- **Component Ablation**:
 - Removing the **category belief** from the auxiliary belief predictor results in a minor performance reduction for both AVLA-Collab (0.72% drop) and CRONA (2.04% drop).
 - Removing the **location belief**, either alone or with category belief, leads to a substantial performance decrease for both methods (e.g., CRONA's success rate drops by 38.46% without it). This indicates the critical role of accurate spatial belief prediction.
 - Excluding **state information** from the centralized critic results in near-complete learning failure for both AVLA-Collab and CRONA (less than 0.2% success rate), emphasizing its crucial role in stabilizing and improving centralized training.

Overall, the findings suggest that while richer multi-modal inputs can be beneficial with sufficient model capacity, especially in complex environments, cross-modal collaboration offers a robust and often more parameter-efficient alternative, particularly when specific targets exhibit distinct, modality-specific cues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces CRONA, a novel Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework that facilitates cross-modal navigation through decentralized, modality-specialized agents. The framework's core contributions lie in its architecture, which integrates control-relevant auxiliary beliefs and a centralized multi-modal critic with global state information, adhering to the centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm.

The research's significance is multifaceted:
- **Scalability and Flexibility**: By enabling lightweight, modality-specialized agents to collaborate, CRONA offers a scalable and flexible paradigm compared to monolithic multi-modal models. This reduces the burden of learning dense multi-modal representations within a single agent and simplifies deployment in scenarios where resources might be constrained or where a full suite of sensors on every agent is impractical.
- **Robustness to Sensory Limitations**: The experimental results demonstrate that cross-modal collaboration can enhance robustness to varying qualities of sensory input. For instance, CRONA maintains performance even with poor visual observations, as the audio-based agent can compensate. This is particularly relevant for real-world applications where sensors can be noisy, occluded, or limited.
- **Insights into Modality Utility**: The identification of five modality-dominance patterns (no clear dominance, audio dominance, vision dominance, cross-modal dominance, multi-modal dominance) provides valuable insights into when and why specific modalities contribute most effectively to navigation. This understanding can inform the design of more efficient and effective embodied AI systems, guiding sensor selection and agent specialization based on environmental characteristics and task requirements.
- **Advancements in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning**: The successful integration of auxiliary beliefs and global state into a centralized critic for decentralized execution represents an advancement in cooperative MARL. The ablation studies confirm the critical role of these components, particularly location belief and global state information, in stabilizing and enhancing learning for multi-agent policies.

Potential impacts of this research include:
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems**: The findings can inform the design of multi-robot systems where different robots are equipped with specialized sensors (e.g., one robot with a high-resolution camera, another with an advanced acoustic sensor). Such heterogeneous teams could perform complex navigation and search tasks more efficiently and robustly in diverse environments.
- **Embodied AI Research**: The benchmark and framework provide a valuable tool for future research in embodied AI, particularly for studying multi-modal perception, multi-agent coordination, and the trade-offs between different system architectures.
- **Resource-Efficient Deployment**: The paradigm of modality-specialized, lightweight agents could lead to more resource-efficient embodied agents, making advanced navigation capabilities accessible for platforms with limited computational power or sensor payloads.

The limitations, such as focusing primarily on vision and audio, using specific belief types, and conducting tasks in 2D navigation settings, also highlight important directions for future research. Extending CRONA to other sensory inputs, developing more generalized control-relevant belief representations, and applying the framework to full 3D embodied environments would further test its generality and practical applicability, paving the way for more sophisticated and capable embodied agents.
