# On-the-Fly VLA Adaptation via Test-Time Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TT-VLA) framework enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to adapt their policies online during inference by employing a value-free PPO variant and dense, progress-based rewards. This approach consistently improved the performance of various VLA models by up to 44.9% across unseen tasks in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation environments.

## Problem

- Current VLA models, trained via supervised fine-tuning or training-time reinforcement learning, produce fixed policies that struggle to adapt to dynamic, unpredictable, and unseen real-world scenarios due to environmental shifts.
- Existing deployed VLA models lack on-the-fly policy adjustment mechanisms during inference, leading to brittle behavior and task failures when environmental conditions evolve or novel variations appear.
- Traditional test-time training (TTT) methods are often inadequate for multimodal VLA tasks, as generic self-supervised or consensus-based objectives fail to provide sufficient task-aligned guidance for complex decision-making under severe data and latency constraints.

## Method

- Introduce Test-Time Reinforcement Learning for VLAs (**TT-VLA**), a framework enabling online policy adaptation during inference without requiring explicit fine-tuning, human intervention, or environment resets.
- Employ a ==dense, step-wise reward== mechanism calculated as the temporal difference in task progress, using a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action-Critic (**VLAC**) model to estimate progress.
- Utilize a ==value-free== variant of ==Proximal Policy Optimization== (**PPO**) by removing auxiliary losses and simplifying advantage estimation (A_t = r_t), which enables rapid policy updates based directly on immediate progress feedback.

## Results

- TT-VLA consistently improved the success rates of four diverse VLA models (Nora, OpenVLA, OpenVLA-RL, TraceVLA) across 15 unseen simulation tasks, with relative gains up to **44.4%** for Nora and **44.9%** for OpenVLA.
- In real-world experiments, TT-VLA enhanced OpenVLA's performance on nine unseen pick-and-place tasks, demonstrating effective generalization and recovery from execution errors by enabling policies to correct trajectories.
- Diagnostic experiments showed that TT-VLA's immediate progress-based reward (A_t = r_t) consistently outperformed standard GAE for test-time adaptation, and an update frequency of every 8 steps achieved optimal performance by balancing adaptation speed and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a reliable value function within a single episode at test time is impractical due to limited samples and latency constraints, making a value-free PPO variant more suitable for rapid, on-the-fly adaptation.
> - Dense, progress-based rewards provide immediate, task-aligned feedback essential for guiding policy adjustments mid-episode, outperforming sparse terminal rewards or generic self-supervised objectives for VLA tasks.
> - Simplifying advantage estimation to directly use immediate progress-based rewards (A_t = r_t) circumvents theoretical pitfalls of standard PPO with progress-difference rewards and enables efficient, stable policy refinement at test time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics and embodied intelligence has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models are designed to enable robots to interpret visual observations and natural language instructions, translating them into executable actions. This represents a step towards creating general-purpose intelligent agents capable of complex tasks.

Current VLA models, such as Nora, OpenVLA, and TraceVLA, are predominantly trained using two main paradigms: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on large, curated datasets, or training-time reinforcement learning (RL) in controlled environments. While these methods yield strong performance in static and well-defined settings, they exhibit limitations in their ability to adapt to dynamic, unpredictable, and previously unseen scenarios. This rigidity results in brittle behavior and reduced robustness when confronted with distribution shifts or evolving environmental conditions during real-world deployment.

The broader research landscape has started to explore test-time training (TTT) in other domains, such as natural language processing and computer vision, to enable models to adapt to incoming test data without requiring full retraining or ground-truth labels. However, applying TTT directly to VLAs presents unique challenges due to the multimodal nature of robotic tasks, which involve complex interactions between visual perception, language understanding, and physical actions. These interactions often lead to substantial and evolving distributional shifts that fixed, self-supervised TTT objectives may not adequately address.

This paper positions itself within this context by identifying the critical gap between static training regimes and the dynamic requirements of real-world robotic deployment. It aims to bridge this gap by introducing a method for *on-the-fly* policy adaptation for VLA models *during inference* (test time), thus enhancing their adaptability, stability, and task success in dynamic and novel environments. This contrasts with existing RL approaches that typically operate during the training phase, and self-supervised TTT methods that may not be sufficiently task-aligned for complex VLA tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to adapt their policies autonomously and continuously during inference (test-time) in dynamic, previously unseen simulated and physical environments. This framework, termed Test-Time Reinforcement Learning for VLAs (TT-VLA), aims to enhance the adaptability, stability, and task success of VLA models without requiring explicit fine-tuning phases, human intervention, or controlled data collection at deployment.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing VLA models:

1. **Rigidity of Fixed Policies:** Current VLA models are primarily trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or training-time reinforcement learning (RL). These methods produce fixed policies that, while effective in their trained environments, struggle when faced with the inherent variability and dynamism of real-world or challenging simulated deployments. This leads to a significant performance gap between training and deployment.
2. **Lack of On-the-Fly Adaptation:** Once deployed, existing VLA models typically lack mechanisms for real-time policy adjustment. If environmental conditions evolve, objects shift, or novel visual/semantic variations appear, the fixed policy cannot flexibly respond, leading to task failures. Retraining in such scenarios is often impractical due to time constraints, computational cost, and the unavailability of new labeled data or environment resets.
3. **Inadequacy of Traditional Test-Time Training (TTT) for VLAs:** While TTT has shown promise in other domains (vision, language) for adapting models to incoming test streams, its direct application to VLA tasks is challenging. The multimodal nature of VLA involves substantial and evolving distributional shifts across both visual and linguistic modalities, making generic self-supervised TTT objectives less effective at guiding task-oriented decision-making. Existing RL-based TTT methods like EVOLVE-VLA, while leveraging task progress, incur significant computational overhead (e.g., GRPO-style optimization), rendering them unsuitable for real-time robotic deployment with strict latency constraints.
4. **Need for Self-Improvement and Deployment-Readiness:** To realize truly general-purpose and robust robots, VLAs must be capable of continuous self-improvement and adaptation in diverse and unpredictable deployment scenarios. The current training-deployment separation paradigm leaves VLAs vulnerable to unanticipated dynamics.

To address these motivations, TT-VLA proposes to leverage reinforcement learning principles at test time. The goal is to derive dense, task-aligned reward signals from task-agnostic proxies (like a progress estimator) to efficiently update the VLA policy online. This approach seeks to preserve the strong priors learned during SFT/training-time RL while enabling flexible, within-episode policy refinement, thus offering a principled step toward self-improving, deployment-ready VLA systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Test-Time Reinforcement Learning for VLAs (TT-VLA), introduces a framework for online policy adaptation during inference. It addresses the limitations of traditional reinforcement learning (RL) in test-time scenarios by employing a value-free Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) variant and a dense, progress-based reward mechanism.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Preliminaries

Robotic manipulation is framed as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), defined by a state space (S), an action space (A), a multimodal observation space (O), and natural-language task instructions (L). A VLA policy π_θ receives an instruction `l` and observations `o` to generate a sequence of actions `a`. The core challenge is to adjust this pretrained policy `π_θ` online during deployment without access to training data, environment resets, or human intervention.

Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is an actor-critic policy-gradient method that optimizes a clipped surrogate objective to ensure stable policy updates within a trust region. The standard PPO objective includes a clipped policy loss, a value function loss, and an entropy regularization term. Advantage estimates, `A_t`, are typically derived using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), which involves a weighted sum of temporal-difference (TD) residuals.

#### 3.2. TT-VLA Design

TT-VLA adapts PPO for test-time adaptation by addressing the impracticality of learning a reliable value function with limited test-time samples and strict latency constraints.

##### 3.2.1. Dense Progress-Based Reward

Traditional RL-based VLAs often rely on sparse terminal rewards, which are unsuitable for test-time adaptation as they delay policy updates until task completion, preventing mid-episode corrections. TT-VLA introduces a dense, step-wise reward mechanism based on task progress.
- **Task Progress (p_t):** At each time step `t`, task progress `p_t` is estimated as a scalar value between 0 and 1 using a pretrained task progress predictor, Φ. This predictor is conditioned on the observation history `o_0:t+1` and the language instruction `l`. The paper instantiates Φ using the Vision-Language-Action-Critic (VLAC) model, a multimodal scalar regressor for task progress.
- **Per-Step Dense Reward (r_t):** The reward for an action taken at time `t` is defined as the temporal difference in progress: `r_t = p_t - p_t-1`. This reward design provides immediate feedback, encouraging actions that advance task completion and penalizing those that reverse progress.
 - This progress-based reward requires no external supervision during inference, provides dense feedback for continuous adaptation, and promotes monotonic progress.

##### 3.2.2. Training Objective (Value-Free PPO)

Recognizing that learning an accurate value function within a single episode at test time is impractical, TT-VLA adopts a value-free PPO variant.
- The auxiliary losses for value regression and entropy regularization are removed from the standard PPO objective (by setting `c_1 = 0` and `c_2 = 0`). The entropy term, which encourages exploration during training, is discarded because test-time adaptation prioritizes rapid fitting to the current task over broad exploration.
- The advantage estimate `A_t` is simplified to depend solely on the immediate reward `r_t`. This is achieved by setting the GAE parameters `λ = 0` and `γ = 0`. This collapses GAE into a one-step formulation where `A_t = δ_t = r_t`. This ensures that policy updates directly reflect the instantaneous progress, enabling rapid adaptation without a value function.
- The objective function focuses solely on the clipped surrogate policy loss, `L(θ) = E_t [L_CLIP_t(θ)]`.

#### 3.3. Overall Pipeline

1. **Initialization:** A pretrained VLA policy `π_θ` is loaded. The progress `p_0` is initialized, and an empty buffer `B` is created. The environment is reset to an initial state `s_0` and provided with an instruction `l`.
2. **Action Execution:** At each time step `t`, the current policy `π_θ` generates an action `a_t` based on the observation `o_t-1` and instruction `l`. This action is executed in the environment.
3. **Progress and Reward Computation:** A new observation `o_t+1` is received. The progress estimator Φ computes `p_t`. The dense reward `r_t` is then calculated as `p_t - p_t-1`.
4. **Data Storage:** The tuple `(o_t+1, a_t, r_t, log π_θ_old(a_t | o_t))` is stored in a buffer `B`.
5. **Policy Update:** Periodically (e.g., every `K` steps), the policy parameters `θ` are updated using the collected data in buffer `B`.
 - For each entry in `B`, the ratio `r_i(θ)` between the new and old policy likelihoods is computed.
 - The clipped policy loss `L_i` is calculated using `r_i(θ)` and `r_i`.
 - The policy parameters `θ` are updated via gradient ascent using a learning rate `η`.
 - The buffer `B` is then cleared.
6. **Continuous Refinement:** The updated policy `π_θ'` is used to generate subsequent actions, allowing continuous refinement throughout the episode.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis of TT-VLA

The paper provides theoretical justification for its design choices:
- **Proposition 1 and Corollary 1:** These show that for a progress-difference reward `r_t = p_t - p_t-1` and a value function `V(s_t) = 1 - p_t-1` (representing remaining progress), the TD error `δ_t` vanishes (if `γ=1`) or becomes systematically negative (if `0 < γ < 1`). Consequently, GAE advantages would be zero or negatively biased, rendering traditional PPO ineffective for learning with this reward type when a value function is used.
- **Lemma 1:** This lemma formally establishes that setting `λ = 0` in GAE simplifies `A_t` to `δ_t`, and further, setting `γ = 0` makes `A_t = δ_t = r_t - V(s_t)`. Critically, if the value function `V(s)` is set to zero (as in TT-VLA), then `A_t = r_t`. This justifies TT-VLA's value-free approach, ensuring that the learning signal directly corresponds to the immediate progress difference.

By discarding the value function and simplifying the advantage estimation to directly use the immediate progress-based reward, TT-VLA circumvents the theoretical pitfalls and practical challenges associated with traditional RL methods in test-time VLA adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates TT-VLA through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world robotic environments, comparing it against several state-of-the-art open-source VLA models.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Experiments:** Conducted in ManiSkill 3 using a WidowX-250S robotic arm, focusing on pick-and-place manipulation. Generalization was evaluated along three dimensions:
 - **Execution:** Randomization of initial robot/object/receptacle poses and mid-episode object repositioning.
 - **Vision:** Dynamic textures (weak/strong), unseen table surfaces, and image-level noise (weak/strong).
 - **Semantics:** Unseen objects, receptacles, instruction paraphrases, and multi-object/distractor scenarios.
 Each task involved 80 trials across three random seeds, using 640x480 RGB images.
- **Real-World Experiments:** Performed on a Franka Research 3 platform for pick-and-place tasks. Nine unseen tasks were designed to assess robustness to executional, visual, and semantic shifts. Each task was evaluated over 10 trials with fixed camera viewpoints (500x480 resolution), controlled lighting, and static backgrounds.
- **Implementation Details:** Policies were fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) with ranks {16, 32}, AdamW optimizer, learning rates from {1e-5, 5e-5, 1e-4}, and a clipping parameter `ε = 0.2` for PPO stability. Each episode had a fixed horizon of 160 steps.

#### 4.2. Baselines

TT-VLA was benchmarked against four diverse open-source VLA models:
- **Nora:** Based on Qwen-2.5-VL-3B, using FAST+ tokenizer. Further fine-tuned on a ManiSkill 3 dataset.
- **OpenVLA:** Built on Llama-2-7B, a widely used open-source VLA. Warm-up fine-tuning applied.
- **OpenVLA-RL:** An extension of OpenVLA that incorporates reinforcement learning during its training phase.
- **TraceVLA:** Designed for enhanced spatio-temporal reasoning by encoding state-action histories as visual prompts.

#### 4.3. Simulation Results

Table 1 presents the success rates for all models across the unseen simulation tasks.
- **Consistent Performance Improvement:** TT-VLA consistently improved the performance of all four baseline VLA models across nearly all 15 unseen tasks and generalization dimensions (Execution, Vision, and Semantics).
- **Quantitative Gains:**
 - For **Nora**, TT-VLA achieved gains in 14 out of 15 tasks, with relative improvements ranging from 5.26% to 44.40%. Notable improvements were seen in "Obj. Rep." (+44.4%) and "Noise-s" (+18.15%).
 - For **OpenVLA**, consistent improvements were observed, including significant gains such as +44.9% on "M Recep." and +18.4% on "Obj. Rep.".
 - **OpenVLA-RL** and **TraceVLA** also showed consistent, albeit sometimes smaller, gains (e.g., +6.15% on "Obj. Rep." for OpenVLA-RL).
- **Broad Applicability:** The results indicate that TT-VLA offers a generalizable solution for VLA adaptivity, demonstrating that substantial generalization can be achieved through streamlined test-time adjustments, rather than solely relying on complex training-time architectural improvements.

#### 4.4. Real-World Results

(Figure 3)
- **Improved Real-World Performance:** Using OpenVLA as the base policy, TT-VLA consistently improved performance on nine unseen real-world pick-and-place tasks, demonstrating effective generalization beyond simulation.
- **Case Study (Figure 5):** A qualitative example for "put banana on plate" illustrated TT-VLA's ability to recover from execution errors. When the gripper deviated from the target, the dense, progress-based reward mechanism enabled rapid detection of task regression and on-the-fly behavioral adjustments, allowing the policy to correct its trajectory and successfully complete the task. This highlights the benefit of instantaneous, progress-aware rewards in dynamic real-world scenarios.

#### 4.5. Diagnostic Experiments

- **Reward/Advantage Design (Figure 4):**
 - The study compared TT-VLA's progress-based reward (with `γ=0, λ=0`) against standard GAE (with `γ=0.99, λ=0.95`).
 - TT-VLA's approach of focusing on immediate progress (i.e., `A_t = r_t`) consistently outperformed standard GAE across various tasks and models (e.g., 57.08% vs. 55.00% for OpenVLA on a Vision task).
 - This suggests that long-horizon returns, as estimated by standard GAE, can be unreliable at test time and may assign optimistic advantage signals to ineffective actions, whereas instantaneous feedback is more effective for rapid adaptation.
- **Test-Time Training Steps (Table 2):**
 - The impact of update frequency (1, 4, 8, and 16 steps) was investigated.
 - Updating the model every **8 steps** yielded the best performance. More frequent updates (e.g., 1 step) were found to destabilize training, while less frequent updates (e.g., 16 steps) delayed policy improvement and reduced learning efficiency. An intermediate update frequency balanced rapid adaptation with stable optimization.

#### 4.6. Discussions on VLA Test-Time Training (Table 3)

The paper compared TT-VLA with two other representative TTT methods adapted for VLAs:
- **TLM (Test-Time Learning for LLMs):** A self-supervised method minimizing input perplexity. TLM showed lower performance gains than TT-VLA. This is attributed to VLA tasks involving complex multimodal interactions, where input likelihood optimization may prioritize representational consistency over task-oriented decision-making, making self-supervised objectives inadequate.
- **TTRL (Test-Time Reinforcement Learning):** A method using consensus-based pseudo-labels for rule-based rewards. TTRL underperformed TT-VLA. The consensus-based pseudo-labels were found to be less effective for VLA tasks, as majority voting does not accurately reflect action quality, leading to insufficient task-aligned learning signals.

These comparisons highlight that TT-VLA's progress-based reward is crucial for effective test-time adaptation in the VLA domain, outperforming methods that are either purely self-supervised or rely on generic consensus-based rewards.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, TT-VLA, that addresses a fundamental limitation in the deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their inability to adapt to dynamic and unseen environmental conditions during inference. The significance and potential impact of this work are substantial, particularly for the advancement of general-purpose robot learning.

1. **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** TT-VLA enables VLA policies to adapt on-the-fly, continuously refining their actions in response to evolving environments or distributional shifts. This significantly enhances the robustness and adaptability of robots, allowing them to succeed in scenarios where fixed, pre-trained policies would typically fail. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in complex, unstructured real-world settings that are characterized by inherent variability.

2. **Addressing the Training-Deployment Gap:** Current VLA models operate under a training-deployment separation paradigm, making them vulnerable to unexpected dynamics at test time. TT-VLA directly bridges this gap by facilitating continuous within-episode policy adaptation without the need for time-consuming retraining cycles, additional data collection, or human intervention. This makes VLA models more practical and deployment-ready.

3. **Novel Test-Time Reinforcement Learning Paradigm:** The introduction of a value-free PPO variant combined with a dense, progress-based reward mechanism offers a novel approach to test-time adaptation. By deriving immediate, task-aligned learning signals from a progress estimator and forgoing the complexities of learning a value function, TT-VLA demonstrates an efficient and stable method for policy refinement under severe data and latency constraints characteristic of inference time.

4. **Broad Applicability Across VLA Architectures:** The empirical results show that TT-VLA consistently improves the performance of diverse VLA backbones (Nora, OpenVLA, OpenVLA-RL, TraceVLA) across a wide range of unseen tasks in both simulated and real-world environments. This indicates that the proposed framework is not confined to a specific VLA architecture but offers a generalizable enhancement that can augment existing and future VLA models.

5. **Paving the Way for Self-Improving Robots:** By allowing robots to continuously refine their behavior based on real-time environmental feedback, TT-VLA contributes to the vision of self-improving robotic agents. This moves beyond merely executing pre-programmed or pre-learned policies towards agents that can learn and adjust autonomously during their operational lifetime.

6. **Insights into Test-Time Training for Multimodal Tasks:** The diagnostic experiments and comparisons with other test-time training (TTT) methods highlight the unique challenges and requirements of TTT for multimodal VLA tasks. The findings emphasize that generic self-supervised or consensus-based rewards are often insufficient, underscoring the importance of task-aligned, dense reward signals for effective adaptation in this domain.

In conclusion, TT-VLA represents a foundational contribution to VLA models, providing a practical and effective solution for enabling flexible, on-the-fly policy adaptation. Its impact is likely to be seen in the development of more robust, autonomous, and general-purpose robots capable of operating reliably in dynamic and unpredictable real-world scenarios.
