# Vesta: A Generalist Embodied Reasoning Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Vesta is a single generalist VLM (finetuned Qwen3-VL-8B) unifying localization, navigation, embodied reasoning, and long-horizon action planning, using a curated spatially-grounded SFT corpus plus a lightweight image-text memory harness. It beats specialist baselines by over 20% on average across benchmarks and improves real-robot memory-heavy task success by 38.3%.

## Problem

- Hierarchical planner-actor stacks rely on separate specialist VLMs per capability, making deployment computationally expensive and prone to cascading errors across modules.
- Navigation, memory, and reasoning specialists are trained and evaluated in isolation on narrow academic benchmarks, so they fail catastrophically out-of-domain.
- Prior VLA/VLM planners commonly lack explicit history handling or rely on ad hoc compression, limiting performance on non-Markovian long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- A ==base-tail curated SFT corpus== mixing large-scale grounding/detection data with embodied, navigation, and real-robot tail data to induce spatially grounded generalist capabilities in one model.
- A **minimalist multimodal memory harness** that caches sampled past image frames plus a running textual subtask log, re-injected into the prompt at each decision step.
- A ==four-phase chain-of-thought== action-planning formulation (Observation, Progress, Reasoning, Action) that conditions next-subtask prediction on compressed memory state rather than full history.

## Results

- **Vesta** averages **68.7** cognition and **69.9** localization scores, beating RynnBrain, RoboBrain-2.5, and Qwen3-VL baselines by over **20** points on average across four capability axes.
- On real-world bimanual robot tasks, Vesta as planner improves success rate by **38.3%** over actor-only and **25%** over a Qwen3-VL planner, with greater than 4-sigma significance.
- Ablations show the unified data mixture outperforms both nav-only and embodied-only specialists on their own tasks (**+1.4** SR on R2R, **+3.9** average on embodied), and image-text memory beats image-only or text-only variants (**75.9%** vs **63.1%/49.7%** overall).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly training spatially grounded capabilities in one generalist can produce positive transfer, outperforming specialists rather than merely matching them under equal compute.
> - A minimalist memory design (fixed frame sampling plus text log) can suffice for long-horizon reasoning, suggesting elaborate learned retrievers are not always the bottleneck.
> - Oversampling rare transition-phase decision points during training meaningfully improves planning accuracy, since naturally collected trajectories are dominated by steady-state execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotics research, particularly in embodied artificial intelligence (AI), has seen a rise in vision-language models (VLMs) and vision-language-action (VLA) models. The current paradigm often involves a hierarchical stack where a planner VLM generates high-level instructions, which are then executed by a specialized action VLA model. Within this framework, various robotic capabilities—such as spatial localization, memory, navigation, and world knowledge—are frequently addressed by specialized models. For example, navigation specialists are optimized for navigation benchmarks, memory specialists for long-horizon manipulation, and reasoning specialists for static question-answering.

While these specialist models can achieve high performance within their specific domains, their deployment in open-world robotic environments presents several challenges. A multi-model stack composed of individual specialists can be computationally expensive, introduce latency, complicate the inference process, and be susceptible to cascading errors where a failure in one specialist propagates throughout the system.

This work positions itself as an alternative to this specialist-driven approach by proposing a unified, generalist embodied model named Vesta. It challenges the conventional method of developing and deploying siloed specialist models by demonstrating that a single foundation model can consolidate multiple core robotic capabilities—localization, spatial reasoning, navigation, and long-horizon planning—without compromising, and in some cases, exceeding the performance of individual specialists. This contribution aims to simplify the robotic perception-action pipeline, improve scalability, and enhance robustness by reducing points of failure associated with modular, multi-specialist systems. The paper also contributes to the growing body of research on embodied foundation models that translate web-scale semantic knowledge into robotic control and spatial reasoning, and specifically addresses the challenge of multi-stage reasoning and cross-task adaptability through a unified architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and demonstrate a single, unified generalist model capable of performing diverse high-level planning tasks for robots in open-world environments. The authors posit that such a generalist approach can overcome the limitations inherent in current multi-model stacks composed of specialized agents.

The motivation for pursuing a generalist embodied reasoning model is rooted in several critical challenges faced by robots operating in complex, real-world settings:
- **Integration Complexity:** Robots require seamless integration of various capabilities, including localization (understanding where objects are in space), spatial reasoning (inferring relationships and properties), navigation (moving through an environment), and long-horizon planning (executing multi-step tasks). Traditional approaches often treat these as isolated problems, leading to a fragmented system design.
- **Computational Expense:** Deploying an ensemble of specialist models, each optimized for a particular task, incurs significant computational overhead. This is particularly challenging for resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- **Cascading Errors:** In a multi-model stack, an error in one specialist's output can propagate and amplify through subsequent modules, leading to overall system failure. This lack of robustness undermines reliable operation in dynamic environments.
- **Lack of Scalability:** Developing and maintaining separate specialist models for every required capability is not a scalable solution as the complexity and scope of robotic tasks expand.
- **Bridging Semantic and Physical Gaps:** Robots must bridge the gap between high-level semantic reasoning (e.g., "clean the store") and low-level physical execution (e.g., "scrub floors"). Current hierarchical frameworks, while beneficial, still often rely on multiple decoupled components for the planning VLM, which handles diverse skills.

The authors propose that these capabilities can and should be unified into a single "generalist planner" to address these issues. Their objective is to demonstrate that a unified model like Vesta can match or surpass the performance of individual specialists while offering benefits in terms of simplicity, scalability, and robustness, thereby presenting a feasible and preferable alternative to combining specialists.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Vesta is developed as a generalist embodied model by finetuning the Qwen3-VL-8B base model. The methodology centers on three core techniques: a curated supervised fine-tuning (SFT) corpus, a multimodal memory harness, and a structured action planning formulation.

#### 3.1. Base Model and Training Strategy

Vesta is built upon the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model. The training involves a full-parameter SFT approach, where the vision tower, multimodal projector, and language backbone are all unfrozen and trained jointly in bf16 precision. The optimization uses FSDP with full sharding, FlashAttention-2 kernels, and sequence packing to handle long-context, multi-image samples efficiently. The training consists of 1 epoch over the full data mixture, with a learning rate of 1e-5, weight decay of 0.01, and a batch size of 256 across 128 H100 GPUs.

#### 3.2. Curated Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Corpus

A significant effort was placed on curating a diverse and massive SFT corpus specifically designed to induce spatially grounded capabilities and broad cross-task generalization. The corpus mixture is intentionally biased toward spatial intelligence, comprising six categories:
- **Spatial Intelligence (27.1%):** The largest portion, focusing on understanding spatial relationships.
- **Navigation (21.8%):** Data from Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks, formulated as instruction-guided navigation. This includes R2R, RxR, and ScaleVLN datasets, with episodes rendered in simulation. The model predicts high-level navigation actions (pixel goal, turn sequence, or stop) based on instructions, current view, and visual history.
- **Grounding (20.8%):** Endows Vesta with capabilities to associate text descriptions with spatial regions and predict contact/manipulation points. This component uses large-scale grounding and detection datasets (Objects365, COCO, LVIS) for general priors, augmented with embodied and robotics-specific data (egocentric observations, manipulation-centric annotations, temporal interaction sequences) to adapt to partial observability and viewpoint changes. Structured outputs are decoded as text tokens.
- **General VLM (16.2%):** Preserves broad visual-language competence and prevents over-specialization.
- **Embodied Reasoning (9.8%):** Extends spatial localization to action-conditioned scene understanding. The corpus combines large-scale VQA, detection, and pointing data with embodied data from real-robot and human manipulation trajectories. This aims for a unified interface for "what, where, how, and when" reasoning, including affordance prediction, manipulation trajectory generation, and task progress estimation.
- **Real Robots (4.3%):** Provides task-level reasoning and real-world execution signals, aligning the model with downstream embodied settings.

#### 3.3. Multimodal Memory Harness

Vesta employs an explicit memory harness to enable reasoning over extended time horizons and handle non-Markovian planning problems. At each timestep, the memory builds a curated context ($\mathcal{M}_t$) from prior steps and injects it into the prompt. Each historical step is represented as a tuple: $\langle i, \tau_i, o_i, a_i, g \rangle$, where $i$ is the step index, $\tau_i$ the timestamp, $o_i$ the observation (image), $a_i$ the predicted subtask (text), and $g$ the overall goal.
To manage intractable context lengths, historical images are capped at $K$ via a sampling operator. Two sampling strategies are explored: uniform sampling and recency-biased sampling (exponential weighting), with the first frame always retained. The design is deliberately minimalist, combining visual and textual memory components.

#### 3.4. Action Planning Formulation

For long-horizon robot planning from egocentric video, Vesta predicts the next subtask as text, given a textual goal. The policy conditions on the current observation ($o_t$), the compressed context from the memory harness ($\mathcal{M}_t$), and the goal ($g$). Before predicting each subtask, the model generates a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) with four phases:
1. **Observation:** "What do I see?"
2. **Progress:** "How far has the task progressed?"
3. **Reasoning:** "What should happen next and why?"
4. **Action:** The predicted subtask.
Only the predicted action is written to memory.

#### 3.5. Evaluation

The model's performance is evaluated across a range of benchmarks:
- **Embodied Reasoning Benchmarks:** Scores are reported on various cognition (e.g., Open-X VQA, SAT, VSI-Bench) and localization (e.g., CrossPoint, EmbSpatial, Where2Place) benchmarks.
- **Offline Action Planning Benchmark:** This benchmark addresses the challenge of costly real-robot evaluation. It formulates planning as Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQ) and simulates continuous temporal rollouts. Predictions are weighted by temporal Intersection-over-Union (IoU) with ground-truth segments. The benchmark uses two datasets: AgiBot (standard robotic manipulation with five task categories) and Egocentric Human-Hand (diverse human manipulation scenarios, pushing cross-embodiment generalization). All tasks are strictly zero-shot and excluded from training.
- **Navigation Benchmarks:** Evaluation uses the NavSuite benchmark, specifically the R2R val_unseen split in the Habitat simulator. Standard R2R metrics are reported: Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), Oracle Success (OS), and Navigation Error (NE).
- **Real-world Robot Evaluation:** Vesta is deployed as a planner on a tabletop bimanual YAM grippers platform from I2RT robotics, with Gr00t-N1.6 as the low-level actor model. Three memory- and reasoning-heavy tasks are evaluated (Find Object, Count Fruits, Memorize Candy), each with 20 samples. Both synchronous and asynchronous planner-actor coupling modes are developed, with synchronous used for reported results.
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigate the impact of different training data mixtures (Nav-only, Embodied-only, unified Vesta mix), transition phase oversampling, and memory modalities (image-only, text-only, image-text hybrid).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding Vesta's performance as a generalist embodied reasoning model.

#### 4.1. Overall Benchmark Performance

Vesta demonstrates an average improvement exceeding 20% over individual state-of-the-art baselines and greater than 10% over an oracle ensemble of baselines (which selects the best specialist for each category) across diverse benchmarks for localization, navigation, embodied reasoning, and action planning. This indicates that a single generalist model can achieve or exceed the performance of multiple specialized models.

#### 4.2. Embodied Reasoning

On embodied benchmarks (Table 1), Vesta achieves the highest average scores in both cognition (68.7) and localization (69.9) categories, outperforming other 8B models such as RynnBrain (average 64.8 cognition, 61.9 localization), RoboBrain 2.5 (average 56.6 cognition, 69.4 localization), and Qwen3-VL (average 55.7 cognition, 57.3 localization). Vesta attains the best score on most cognition and localization benchmarks, maintaining competitive performance on the others.

#### 4.3. Action Planning (Offline)

In the offline action planning benchmark (Table 2), Vesta significantly outperforms baseline models. It achieves an average score of 75.4% across diverse zero-shot tasks from AgiBot and Egocentric Human-Hand datasets, compared to 38.5% for RoboBrain-2.5-8B, 33.6% for Qwen3-VL-8B, and 33.5% for RynnBrain-8B. This indicates strong performance in planning complex, multi-step tasks.

#### 4.4. Navigation

For navigation capabilities on the R2R-CE benchmark (Table 3), Vesta achieves a Success Rate (SR) of 55.5% and Oracle Success (OS) of 61.4%, effectively tying with the state-of-the-art navigation specialist InternVLA-N1 (SR 55.4%, OS 60.6%). Vesta also significantly outperforms other generalist models like UniNaVid and prior VLM baselines (RynnBrain, RoboBrain 2.5, Qwen3-VL), which show 0% success rates, suggesting catastrophic forgetting for out-of-domain tasks.

#### 4.5. Real-world Robot Evaluation

Deployment on a real bimanual robot platform (Figure 7) shows Vesta's practical utility. As a planner, Vesta improves the average task success rate by 38.3% over an actor-only baseline and by 25% over a Qwen3-VL-8B planner on tasks requiring memory and long-horizon reasoning (Find Object, Count Fruits, Memorize Candy). This improvement is reported with statistical significance exceeding 4 standard deviations, indicating a robust positive effect on real-world execution.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Generalist vs. Specialist Training (Table 4):** A unified training mix for Vesta results in performance that matches or exceeds specialists in their respective domains (e.g., 55.5 SR for navigation vs. 54.1 for Nav-only specialist; 70.5 cognition average vs. 64.3 for Embodied-only specialist). This suggests positive knowledge transfer between different capabilities within the generalist model.
- **Transition Sampling (Table 5):** Oversampling transition-phase steps in the training data from 1× to 2× leads to a notable increase in transition accuracy and overall accuracy (from 73.6% to 75.9%), demonstrating the importance of balancing data distribution for rare but critical events.
- **Memory Modality (Table 5):** The multimodal (image-text unified) memory harness yields the best overall accuracy (75.9%). Both image-only memory (63.1%) and text-only memory (49.7%) perform less effectively, with image-only struggling with progress understanding and text-only becoming over-reliant on textual shortcuts. The specific frame-selection policy (uniform vs. recency-biased) for the image-text hybrid memory shows comparable performance, suggesting that the modality mix is more critical than precise sampling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates that developing a single generalist embodied reasoning model is a feasible and effective approach, offering a compelling alternative to combining multiple specialized models.

#### 5.1. Key Demonstrations

The primary significance lies in the empirical demonstration that a unified generalist model, Vesta, can match or exceed the performance of domain-specific specialists across four distinct capability axes: localization, navigation, embodied reasoning, and action planning. This challenges the prevailing paradigm of creating separate models for each task.

#### 5.2. Technical Contributions

The work introduces a novel training recipe that combines a specific multimodal memory harness with a diverse supervised fine-tuning (SFT) corpus biased towards spatially grounded capabilities. This recipe is shown to yield superior cross-task generalization compared to naive supervised fine-tuning. The design of the minimalist multimodal memory harness, which intersperses historical image frames with a textual cache of past subtasks, is a notable aspect of this contribution.

#### 5.3. Impact on Robotics Development

- **Reduced Complexity:** A single generalist planner can significantly simplify the deployment of robotic systems by reducing the number of individual models that need to be integrated and managed.
- **Improved Robustness:** By unifying capabilities, Vesta can mitigate the risk of cascading errors that are common in multi-model stacks, leading to more robust and reliable robotic operation.
- **Enhanced Scalability:** The generalist approach offers a more scalable framework for developing embodied AI, as it avoids the need to train and maintain a growing array of specialists for new tasks.
- **Lowered Barrier to Entry:** Simplifying the model stack could potentially lower the engineering barrier for academic and smaller-team robotics research, making advanced capabilities more accessible.
- **Efficient Evaluation:** The proposed offline planning benchmark provides a low-cost and reliable method for evaluating planning performance, which can accelerate the development cycle, particularly for groups with limited access to physical robotic platforms.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Limitations

The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also point to future research avenues. These include expanding the real-robot evaluation to diverse embodiments (e.g., humanoids, mobile manipulators) to stress the planner-actor interface and study how subtask abstraction adapts to different actor controllability profiles. Further exploration of scaling effects in embodied SFT mixtures, which differ from web data, is also a stated direction. Finally, developing learned and lifelong memory mechanisms, potentially beyond episodic memory, to enable robots to operate across many episodes in the same environment, is highlighted as an area for improvement.

#### 5.5. Broader Impacts

The potential positive impacts of Vesta include simplifying the deployment of assistive robots in various settings (domestic, healthcare, retail, warehouse) and facilitating academic and small-team robotics research. The consolidation of capabilities into a single model can also make overall system behavior easier to audit. Potential negative impacts common to embodied AI research include physical safety risks from incorrect actions in shared spaces and the possibility of labor displacement in routine physical tasks. The compute-intensive nature of training and serving large VLM-based planners could also concentrate capabilities in well-resourced organizations. The authors suggest mitigation strategies such as applying rule-based or learned safety filters at the planner output and sandboxing the actor to a vetted action vocabulary. They also commit to providing documentation on intended uses, evaluated capabilities, known failure modes, and recommended deployment guardrails for future asset releases.
