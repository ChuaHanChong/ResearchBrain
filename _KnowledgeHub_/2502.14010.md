# Which Attention Heads Matter for In-Context Learning?

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from UC Berkeley demonstrates that Function Vector (FV) attention heads are the primary drivers of in-context learning in large language models, rather than induction heads. The study resolves discrepancies in prior work by emphasizing the importance of specific evaluation metrics and controlling for functional correlations between different attention head types, showing that many induction heads develop FV capabilities during training.

## Problem

- Understanding the internal mechanisms enabling in-context learning (ICL) in large language models (LLMs).
- Resolving conflicting hypotheses about whether induction mechanisms or function vector representations are more crucial for ICL.
- Identifying which specific attention heads within transformer models are most responsible for ICL capabilities.

## Method

- Analyzed **12** decoder-only transformer models (**70M** to **7B** parameters) from Pythia, GPT-2, and Llama 2 suites.
- Identified induction heads using an ==induction score== and ==Function Vector (FV) heads== using ==causal mediation analysis==.
- Conducted comprehensive ablation studies (mean ablation and ablation with exclusion) to measure the impact of different head types on ICL accuracy and token-loss difference, while also analyzing training dynamics.

## Results

- Ablating Function Vector (FV) heads consistently led to the largest degradation in ICL accuracy across various model sizes, affirming their primary role.
- When controlling for correlations, induction heads showed a **significantly smaller** impact on ICL performance compared to FV heads.
- Analysis of training dynamics revealed that many induction heads acquire FV functionality over time, and the relative importance of FV heads increases with model scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Function Vector (FV) heads are the primary drivers of in-context learning, consistently causing the largest performance drops when ablated.
> - Induction heads play a secondary role in ICL, especially when controlling for correlations with FV functionality.
> - Many attention heads that initially function as induction heads evolve to develop FV functionality during training, suggesting a developmental trajectory from simpler pattern matching to more sophisticated task representation.
