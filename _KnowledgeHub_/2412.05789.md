# InfiniteWorld: A Unified Scalable Simulation Framework for General Visual-Language Robot Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> InfiniteWorld is a unified Isaac Sim-based simulation framework for vision-language robot interaction. It combines language-driven scene generation and editing, depth- and normal-regularized Gaussian-splatting Real2Sim reconstruction, a web-based semi-automatic 3D point-cloud annotation tool, and format-unified open-source assets, yielding 10K scenes expandable to 2.36M, plus four benchmarks spanning loco-navigation, loco-manipulation, collaborative scene-graph exploration, and social mobile manipulation.

## Problem

- Embodied assets are fragmented across simulators with incompatible formats and no unified interface, making high-quality scene and object reuse labor-intensive and inefficient.
- Existing benchmarks focus on navigation, localization, or manipulation, and ignore scene-graph construction, the step by which agents build environmental knowledge before complex tasks.
- Social-interaction simulators grant helper NPCs a global God's-perspective view of the environment, which has no real-world counterpart and so misrepresents genuine human-robot interaction.

## Method

- ==Generation-driven asset construction== extends **HOLODECK** on Isaac Sim with 236 floor and wall textures plus object add/remove/replace editing, image-to-3D reconstruction, and controllable articulated-object generation.
- ==Depth-Prior-Constrained Real2Sim== adds depth-estimation and normal-vector regularization losses on top of ==PGSR== ==Gaussian splatting==, plus post-processing for axis alignment, noise, surface continuity, and scale.
- **Annot8-3D** is a three-stage web annotation pipeline: ==Point Transformer V3== coarse segmentation, ==SAM2Point== prompt-driven interactive refinement, manual fine-tuning; all assets converted into unified .usd format.

## Results

- LLM-based instruction following with GPT-4o reached **90.82%** success rate and SPL on object loco-navigation and **77.28%** on loco-manipulation; weaker LLMs scored **42-70%**.
- Zero-shot VLMs collapsed on both tasks, at most **0.06%** success rate with **0.00** SPL and navigation error of **11.67-26.53 meters**.
- Collaborative scene-graph exploration: Co-NavGPT with GPT-4 led at **0.3209** semantic exploration rate; every social mobile manipulation variant scored **0.00** success, even with walk/pick primitives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying heterogeneous assets into one format behind a single simulator interface multiplies their usable value more than generating yet another isolated asset set.
> - Scripted action interfaces mask model weakness: LLMs succeed when handed planning primitives, while VLMs acting directly from raw observations fail almost completely.
> - Coarse semantic maps propagate downstream: scene graphs too imprecise for instance-level grounding make dependent social manipulation tasks unsolvable regardless of planner quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) aims to develop intelligent agents that can perceive, reason, and act in physical environments. A critical challenge in this domain is the prohibitive cost and inherent limitations of collecting vast, diverse, and generalizable real-world robotic data. While recent breakthroughs in large-scale language models (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) have been driven by "scaling laws" enabled by internet-scale data, embodied AI has struggled to achieve similar progress due to data scarcity and the complexity of physical interaction.

Existing embodied AI simulators, such as Habitat, AI2-THOR, iGibson, ManiSkills, and GRUtopia, have made significant strides in physics simulation and task diversity. However, the authors identify several critical limitations that hinder widespread adoption and scalability:

1. **Fragmentation of Platforms and Assets:** Research efforts are often scattered across diverse simulation platforms, each with its own asset formats and interfaces. This leads to inefficient, repetitive work and poor interoperability, preventing the community from building upon shared resources.
2. **Lack of Unified, High-Quality Assets:** Creating varied, realistic, and interactive 3D assets (scenes and objects) is labor-intensive and often manual or semi-automated. There is a dearth of high-quality, physically accurate assets that can be easily scaled and integrated across different research projects.
3. **Limited Realism in Interaction Benchmarks:** While benchmarks for navigation and manipulation exist, simulating complex, human-like social interaction remains a significant gap. Current approaches, like GRUtopia's "God's perspective" NPC, deviate from real-world communication dynamics where agents often have limited, distributed knowledge.
4. **Absence of Systematic Knowledge Building:** Many existing tasks overlook the fundamental process by which robots build scene knowledge through exploration, a crucial prerequisite for complex interactions.

InfiniteWorld directly addresses these pain points by proposing a unified and scalable simulation framework built upon NVIDIA Isaac Sim, a powerful platform known for its physically based rendering and robust simulation capabilities. It positions itself as a solution to enable "scaling laws" in embodied AI by providing an "infinite world" of high-quality, diverse, and interactive data. By offering systematic asset construction, automated annotation, and novel, more realistic interaction benchmarks, InfiniteWorld aims to standardize and accelerate embodied AI research, reduce the sim-to-real gap, and foster the development of more generalist robotic agents. It contrasts with previous efforts by emphasizing generation-driven asset expansion and a more nuanced approach to multi-agent social interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "InfiniteWorld" project is to establish a unified and scalable simulation framework that allows robots to learn and interact freely in open-world environments, akin to human capabilities. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific aims:

1. **To build a unified and scalable simulation platform:** The authors aim to integrate diverse functionalities into a single, cohesive framework, leveraging NVIDIA Isaac Sim as its foundation. This unification seeks to overcome the fragmentation prevalent in current embodied AI research, where disparate simulation platforms and incompatible assets hinder collaborative progress.
2. **To provide comprehensive physical asset construction methods:** A core motivation is to alleviate the "dilemma of the lack of high-quality assets" for embodied AI. The project seeks to achieve "unlimited scaling of scene and object assets" by integrating advanced generative AI techniques (language-driven scene generation, controllable object generation, image-to-3D reconstruction) and sophisticated Real2Sim pipelines.
3. **To develop a web-based automated annotation framework:** Recognizing the high cost and labor intensity of manual data labeling, the authors aim to streamline the annotation process for 3D point clouds, crucial for robotic tasks. The goal is to provide an efficient, AI-assisted platform that supports distributed collaboration.
4. **To offer a unified 3D asset library:** The project aims to convert and integrate various existing open-source 3D scene and object datasets into a common format (.usd) compatible with Isaac Sim, thereby enhancing asset utilization and interoperability across the community.
5. **To introduce generalized free robot interaction benchmarks:** Moving beyond conventional tasks, a key objective is to simulate more realistic human-like interactions. This involves designing novel benchmarks that evaluate an embodied agent's capabilities in environmental understanding (scene graph construction), task planning and execution, and intelligent social interaction, including both hierarchical and horizontal communication paradigms.

The fundamental motivation behind InfiniteWorld stems from the observed inefficiencies and limitations in current embodied AI research. The authors highlight the disparity between the data-driven success of large language models and the data scarcity in robotics. They argue that expensive real-world data collection and fragmented simulation efforts impede the realization of "scaling laws" for embodied AI. By providing a rich, scalable, and standardized simulation environment, InfiniteWorld seeks to accelerate research, facilitate the training of more robust and generalist robotic agents, and ultimately bridge the gap between simulation and real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InfiniteWorld's methodology is structured around building a comprehensive and extensible simulation ecosystem. The core of its approach lies in four interconnected components: generative asset creation, Real2Sim pipelines, automated annotation, and unified asset management, all integrated within NVIDIA Isaac Sim, complemented by novel benchmarks.

#### 3.1. Generate-Driven 3D Asset Construction

To address the need for diverse and scalable 3D environments, InfiniteWorld integrates state-of-the-art generative AI techniques:
- **Language-driven 3D Scene Generation:** It extends HOLODECK to allow users to generate and modify indoor scenes using natural language. This includes applying over 200 different scene styles (e.g., floor and wall textures) and performing detailed object edits (e.g., adding/removing objects, replacing similar objects, changing textures, quantity). This method has been used to generate 10,000 diverse indoor scenes (household and social environments), which can be expanded to 2.36 million unique scenes through style variations.
- **Controllable Joint Object Generation:** The framework integrates methods for generating articulated objects, which are crucial for realistic manipulation tasks.
- **Image-to-3D Object Reconstruction:** Single-image to 3D object reconstruction techniques are included to further enrich the asset library with objects derived from real-world images.

#### 3.2. Depth-Prior-Constrained Real2Sim

To bridge the sim-to-real gap, InfiniteWorld incorporates an improved Real2Sim pipeline for reconstructing real-world scenes into high-fidelity 3D models.
- **Enhanced 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** Building upon PGSR, the method introduces depth and normal vector regularization losses. This addresses common challenges in 3D reconstruction, such as reflections on smooth surfaces, by using pre-trained depth estimation models (Depth Pro) and local plane assumptions.
- **Comprehensive Post-Processing:** The pipeline includes critical post-processing steps to optimize reconstructed models for simulation: SfM (Colmap-Glomap), Novel View Synthesis & Meshing (TSDF and Marching Cubes), Z-Axis Alignment (RANSAC), Denoising (connectivity-cluster), Hole-Filling (PyMeshFix), Recoloring (KDTree for color mapping), and Simplification (PyMeshLab) to reduce model complexity.

#### 3.3. Annot8-3D: Automatic Annotation Framework

To facilitate large-scale data labeling, Annot8-3D is proposed as a web-based, smart point cloud annotation framework:
- **Multi-Stage Annotation Pipeline:** It implements a progressive refinement approach:
 1. **Initial Coarse Segmentation:** Automated segmentation using Point Transformer V3 for initial object proposals.
 2. **Interactive Refinement:** Human reviewers use positive/negative prompts to guide SAM2Point for refining segmentations.
 3. **Manual Fine-tuning:** Manual tools for precise adjustments where AI falls short.
- **Key Features:** Supports distributed collaboration, AI-assisted labeling, human-in-the-loop capabilities, and captures comprehensive attribute schemas (physical properties like friction, manipulability flags, semantic labels, navigation data).

#### 3.4. Unified 3D Asset

InfiniteWorld tackles asset interoperability by creating a unified library:
- **Standardized Format:** All assets from various sources are converted to the .usd format, the native format for Isaac Sim.
- **Integration of Diverse Datasets:** It integrates popular 3D scene datasets (e.g., HSSD, HM3D, Replica, Scannet) and 3D object datasets (e.g., 3D Front, PartNet-mobility, Objaverse, ClothesNet), providing a rich collection of interactive objects and environments.
- **Realistic Physics:** The platform supports the simulation of special objects like soft bodies and transparent materials, enhancing physical realism for manipulation tasks.

#### 3.5. Robot Interaction Benchmarks

InfiniteWorld introduces four benchmarks, moving from basic navigation to complex social interactions:
- **Object Loco-Navigation:** Basic task: navigate to a target object given language instructions.
- **Loco-Manipulation:** Extended task: navigate to an object, grasp it, and move it to a new target location based on language instructions.
- **Scene Graph Collaborative Exploration (SGCE):** Novel multi-agent task where robots collaboratively explore an unknown environment to build a unified scene graph, sharing map data and semantic labels.
- **Open-World Social Mobile Manipulation (OWSMM):** Two levels of human-like interaction for complex manipulation tasks:
 - **Hierarchical Interaction:** Agents interact with an "administrator" (simulated with global knowledge from pre-built scene graphs) for task assistance.
 - **Horizontal Interaction:** Agents with equal knowledge acquire scene information through social dialogue when in proximity, simulating "passer-by" interactions.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Robot:** Stretch robot (mobile base, 7-DOF manipulator) is used as the embodied agent.
- **Task Generation:** GPT-4o combined with scene semantics from HSSD generates natural language instructions.
- **Simulation Assistance:** Provides an occupancy map, D* Lite path follower, joint-based physical manipulation, and an adhesion interface to simplify certain robotic actions.
- **Baselines:** Evaluates various LLM-based (GPT-4o, Qwen-turbo, Chat-GLM4-flash) and VLM-based (GPT-4o, Qwen-VL2, GLM-4v) approaches for instruction following and planning, alongside traditional methods like semantic mapping and random policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of InfiniteWorld's benchmarks reveals insights into the capabilities of current embodied AI agents, particularly LLMs and VLMs, in navigation, manipulation, and social interaction within complex simulated environments.

#### 4.1. Object Loco-Navigation

- **LLM-Based Instruction Following:** GPT-4o demonstrated superior performance with a Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 90.82% and Navigation Error (NE) of 1.0. This indicates strong ability to interpret instructions and navigate. Other LLMs (Qwen-turbo, Chat-GLM4-flash) performed significantly worse, showing varying trade-offs between action stability and accuracy.
- **VLM Zero-Shot:** All VLM models performed exceptionally poorly (SR near 0%). This suggests that current VLMs struggle to directly translate visual observations into effective navigation actions in a zero-shot setting without specific task-oriented fine-tuning or explicit navigation interfaces.

#### 4.2. Loco-Manipulation

- **LLM-Based Instruction Following:** GPT-4o again led among LLMs (SR 77.28%), showcasing its robustness in multi-stage tasks requiring both navigation and precise manipulation. The performance gap between GPT-4o and other LLMs widened compared to navigation-only tasks, emphasizing the importance of action accuracy for complex manipulation.
- **VLM Zero-Shot:** Similar to navigation, VLM performance remained near 0% SR. VLMs struggled not only with reaching targets but also with accurately determining graspable object boundaries, highlighting a significant challenge in perception and precise action generation for manipulation.

#### 4.3. Scene Graph Collaborative Exploration (SGCE)

- **Baseline Performance:** Using Co-NavGPT with GPT-4, the Semantic Exploration Rate (SER) reached 0.3209, and the Minimum Root Mean Square Error (MRMSE) was 6.1336. These results, while showing some capability, suggest that collaborative scene graph construction is still a challenging task, with significant room for improvement in both coverage and accuracy of environmental knowledge acquisition.

#### 4.4. Open World Social Mobile Manipulation (OWSMM)

- **Extremely Challenging Task:** Both hierarchical and horizontal interaction tasks proved to be highly difficult for the evaluated VLM (GPT-4o), yielding a 0% success rate even with the incorporation of action primitives (e.g., `walk`, `pick`).
- **Root Cause Analysis:** The authors attribute the failure primarily to the coarseness of the semantic maps constructed from Benchmark 3 (SGCE). The object instances required for tasks were either absent from these maps or their parsed positions had large discrepancies from actual locations, preventing successful task execution. This indicates a critical dependency on accurate and fine-grained environmental perception and mapping for complex, social mobile manipulation tasks. While MPL and LPL showed some actions were planned, they did not lead to task completion.

In summary, the results highlight the strong performance of advanced LLMs like GPT-4o when provided with structured action interfaces for embodied tasks. However, they also expose significant limitations of current VLMs in zero-shot settings for direct action generation, particularly in precise manipulation and complex social interaction scenarios. The low success rates in collaborative exploration and social mobile manipulation underscore the need for further research in robust semantic mapping, knowledge representation, and inter-agent communication within embodied AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InfiniteWorld presents a significant contribution to the embodied AI research landscape by addressing several critical bottlenecks that have hindered the field's progress towards generalist robots and the realization of "scaling laws."

#### 5.1. Enabling Scalability and Data Generation

The most profound impact of InfiniteWorld lies in its commitment to providing a unified and scalable simulation framework with virtually infinite, diverse, and interactive physics assets. By integrating generation-driven 3D asset construction, Real2Sim pipelines, and automated annotation, the platform offers a systematic solution to the perennial problem of data scarcity and the high cost of real-world data collection. This capability is crucial for generating the massive, high-quality datasets necessary to train large-scale embodied AI models, mirroring the success observed in natural language processing and computer vision with large foundation models. This could dramatically accelerate the development of more robust, generalizable, and adaptable robotic agents.

#### 5.2. Advancing Realism and Bridging Sim-to-Real

Built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, InfiniteWorld leverages its robust physics engine to simulate realistic robot-environment interactions. The improved Real2Sim pipeline, with its focus on high-fidelity mesh reconstruction from real-world data and comprehensive post-processing, significantly contributes to bridging the sim-to-real gap. By allowing researchers to easily import and use realistic assets, the platform facilitates the transfer of learned policies from simulation to the real world, a crucial step for practical robotic deployment. The inclusion of soft bodies and transparent object simulations further enhances the realism of physical interactions.

#### 5.3. Pushing the Boundaries of Embodied Interaction

Beyond traditional navigation and manipulation, InfiniteWorld introduces novel and challenging benchmarks for social interaction: Scene Graph Collaborative Exploration (SGCE) and Open-World Social Mobile Manipulation (OWSMM). These benchmarks force agents to develop more sophisticated capabilities in environmental understanding, knowledge building, communication, and multi-agent coordination. SGCE highlights the often-overlooked importance of systematic knowledge acquisition (scene graphs), while OWSMM's hierarchical and horizontal interaction paradigms simulate more realistic human-robot and robot-robot communication dynamics. By providing a comprehensive evaluation framework for these complex interactions, InfiniteWorld inspires new research directions in social robotics and collaborative AI.

#### 5.4. Fostering Community and Reducing Redundancy

By offering a unified asset interface and converting diverse existing datasets into a common format, InfiniteWorld provides a systematic resource that can be readily adopted by the embodied AI community. This reduces the fragmentation of efforts, alleviates the burden of creating proprietary assets, and fosters greater interoperability and collaboration among researchers. It enables faster iteration and comparison of models and algorithms, accelerating overall progress in the field.

#### 5.5. Identifying Future Research Directions

The experimental results, particularly the low success rates for VLMs in complex manipulation and social interaction tasks, and the challenges with coarse semantic maps, provide valuable insights into current limitations. These findings highlight critical areas for future research, including:
- Developing more robust and fine-grained semantic mapping techniques for embodied agents.
- Improving VLM capabilities for direct, precise action generation and complex multi-stage tasks.
- Enhancing natural language understanding and grounding for richer human-robot interaction.
- Exploring more sophisticated communication protocols for multi-agent collaboration.

In conclusion, InfiniteWorld is a timely and impactful contribution that aims to standardize and elevate embodied AI research. By addressing fundamental issues of data scarcity, platform fragmentation, and task realism, it provides a powerful, scalable, and accessible foundation for the next generation of intelligent, interactive robots.
