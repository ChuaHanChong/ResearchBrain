# ROBOGATE: Adaptive Failure Discovery for Safe Robot Policy Deployment via Two-Stage Boundary-Focused Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> ROBOGATE introduces an adaptive framework for validating robot manipulation policies, employing a two-stage boundary-focused sampling strategy to efficiently identify failure modes across high-dimensional operational parameter spaces. The study empirically quantifies a "cross-simulator gap," revealing that Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models performing well in academic benchmarks achieve 0% success in industrial simulation environments, underscoring the need for a dedicated validation layer in Physical AI deployment.

## Problem

- Traditional uniform random testing for robot policy validation is computationally inefficient in high-dimensional parameter spaces.
- Current evaluation frameworks often lack cross-embodiment validation, hindering the distinction between universal and robot-specific failure modes.
- Learned robot policies are often poorly characterized in edge cases or adversarial conditions, posing risks for real-world industrial deployment.

## Method

- A ==two-stage adaptive sampling== strategy is implemented in **ROBOGATE**, moving from ==Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS)== uniform exploration (Stage 1) to ==boundary-focused sampling== (Stage 2) that concentrates resources on the ==30–70% success-rate transition zone==.
- A ==logistic regression risk model== with interaction terms is developed to derive ==closed-form failure boundary equations== and quantify deployment risk with interpretable parameter thresholds and ==bootstrap confidence intervals==.
- The framework enables ==cross-embodiment validation== across multiple robot platforms (Franka Panda 7-DOF, UR3e/UR5e/UR10e 6-DOF) within an ==8-dimensional operational parameter space== using ==NVIDIA Isaac Sim== with ==Newton physics==, and produces a ==deployment-gate== leaderboard for VLA models.

## Results

- The two-stage adaptive sampling strategy improved risk prediction, achieving an AUC of **0.780** for the logistic regression model (vs **0.754** for Stage 1 alone), and effectively targeted failure regions with **63.9%** of boundary-focused samples resulting in failure.
- All **seven** state-of-the-art VLA models (GR00T N1.6, π0, OpenVLA, SmolVLA, Octo-Base, Octo-Small) achieved **0%** success on ROBOGATE's 68 industrial scenarios in Isaac Sim, with NVIDIA's GR00T N1.6 showing a **97.65 pp** cross-simulator gap (LIBERO MuJoCo **97.65%** → Isaac Sim industrial **0/68**) on a single fine-tuned checkpoint.
- A closed-form failure boundary equation $\mu^*(m) = (1.469 + 0.419m)/(3.691 - 1.400m)$ was derived for the friction-mass space, and **four universal danger zones** (e.g., object masses **0.935 kg – 2.114 kg**) were identified affecting multiple robot embodiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient and robust robot policy validation requires actively targeting success-failure boundary regions rather than relying on uniform or exhaustive sampling.
> - A significant "cross-simulator gap" exists, where high performance in one academic simulator (e.g., MuJoCo) does not guarantee readiness for deployment in another industrial simulator (e.g., Isaac Sim), necessitating a dedicated validation layer for Physical AI.
> - Interpretable risk models that provide closed-form failure boundaries and critical parameter thresholds are essential for translating validation results into actionable operational constraints and policy improvements.
