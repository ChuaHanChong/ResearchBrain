# Thinking with Looped Flows

> [!summary] Summary
> Looped flows train recurrent reasoning models with local denoising objectives instead of backpropagation through time. A stateful denoiser predicts the solution and updates a recurrent state at each step of a probability flow from noise to answer, with progressively decreasing noise levels tying the steps together. Inference integrates that flow on a finer grid, reaching 58.8% on ARC-AGI-1 and 12.2% on ARC-AGI-2.

## Problem

- Full backpropagation through time is costly and unstable for looped models, so HRM, TRM and FPRM truncate gradients to one or a few recurrent updates.
- Under truncated gradients, losses at later steps send no signal to earlier hidden states, so early updates cannot learn to support future computation.
- Resulting recurrences are unstable, falling into spurious attractors or failing to converge, and being deterministic they yield only one solution per problem.

## Method

- A ==stateful denoiser== jointly outputs the predicted clean solution and the next recurrent state, coupling flow-state integration with looped recurrence inside one shared network.
- ==Temporal alignment==: cross-entropy on interpolants at sorted decreasing noise levels that share a single problem, noise and solution triplet, with stop-gradients between steps.
- ==Adaptive computation time== head trained with binary cross-entropy halts saturated steps; inference uses a ==noise-backtracking SDE sampler==, finer temporal grids, and best-Q ensembling.

## Results

- Single-trajectory **looped flows** reach **97.9%** on Sudoku-Extreme, **86.7%** Maze-Hard, **58.8%** ARC-AGI-1 and **12.2%** ARC-AGI-2, versus TRM's **44.6%** and **7.8%** on ARC.
- Sudoku accuracy scales from **74.5%** at 8 inference steps to **97.9%** at 128, and looped flows resolve **90.9%** of TRM's failure cases across 65,000 test instances.
- Ablations confirm every flow component: dropping time conditioning and interpolants cuts ARC-AGI-1 to **43.6%**, and stochastic integration lifts 10x10 N-Queens coverage from **54.7%** to **61.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A noise-level schedule can act as a curriculum that makes purely local losses build globally useful recurrent state, substituting for backpropagation through time.
> - Sharing one noise sample across steps empirically helps despite theoretically permitting a linear shortcut, suggesting cross-step association matters more than objective independence.
> - Framing recurrence as probability transport yields solution diversity, inference-time scaling and off-the-shelf integrators for free; self-conditioning is a degenerate special case.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Solving complex problems often necessitates increased computational expenditure, a principle observed in both human cognition and algorithmic processes. In the field of artificial intelligence, particularly deep learning, two primary paradigms have emerged to address this:
1. **Looped Models:** These recurrent neural networks iteratively refine a distributed hidden state using shared parameters, effectively increasing their depth during inference. Examples include Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRM), Tiny Recursive Models (TRM), and Fixed-Point Reasoners (FPRM). While capable of accumulating computation over time, these models face significant training challenges. Full Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) is computationally expensive and susceptible to gradient instability. Consequently, practical training often relies on truncated BPTT or per-step objectives with stop-gradients, which localize supervision and hinder the ability of early updates to support future computations. This can lead to unstable recurrences, non-convergence, or convergence to spurious attractors. Furthermore, traditional looped models typically produce a single, deterministic solution, prompting research into stochastic variants like Probabilistic Tiny Recursive Models (PTRM) or Generative Recursive Reasoning Models (GRAM) to handle multi-solution problems.
2. **Flow and Diffusion Models:** This class of generative models increases inference-time computation by conceptualizing generation as a continuous trajectory of local transformations from noise to data. These models learn by solving a sequence of independent denoising objectives across different noise levels, allowing local losses to contribute to a globally coherent process. This approach enables scalable inference through finer temporal grids and advanced numerical integrators. However, prior work suggests that flow-based models, while effective at representing distributions, may be less proficient at learning certain inherently sequential or algorithmic computations compared to recurrent models. Attempts to introduce recurrence, such as self-conditioning, have been explored, but they typically operate within a single flow timestep or lack the temporal unfolding of a recurrent state over the flow trajectory.

The present work, "looped flows," aims to integrate the strengths of both paradigms. It proposes to leverage the stable, local training properties of flow models to improve the learning of recurrent states in looped architectures, thereby addressing the training stability issues of recurrent models while enhancing the sequential reasoning capabilities of flow models. This bridges a gap in current research by enabling a recurrent state to unfold its computation along a probability flow trajectory, a mechanism distinct from previous approaches to recurrence or self-conditioning in generative flows.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a framework for recurrent reasoning that enables neural networks to solve progressively harder problems by effectively scaling inference-time computation. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and opportunities within existing deep learning models:

1. **Mitigating Training Difficulties in Looped Models:** A primary motivation is to overcome the limitations of training conventional looped models. The authors observe that backpropagating through many recurrent updates is often unfeasible due to computational cost and gradient issues (vanishing/exploding gradients). The common practice of truncating gradients or applying per-step objectives prevents earlier computational steps from receiving direct learning signals that would improve later steps. This makes it challenging for looped models to learn stable and globally useful recurrent states. The paper seeks to remedy this by introducing a training strategy that encourages the development of such states even with local gradients.

2. **Learning Stable Recurrences:** A related objective is to address the observed instability in existing looped models, which can manifest as failures to converge, oscillations, or falling into "spurious attractors" (incorrect solutions that the model nevertheless converges upon). The authors aim to design a system that intrinsically promotes more robust and convergent recurrent dynamics.

3. **Enabling Scalable Inference Computation:** The work is motivated by the desire to enable models to improve performance by simply "spending more time on computation" during inference. For looped models, this typically means running more recurrent steps. For flow models, it involves using a finer temporal discretization for integration. The paper aims to combine these capabilities, allowing for increased computation via both recurrent updates and a more refined temporal integration grid.

4. **Supporting Diverse and Multiple Solutions:** Many real-world reasoning problems admit multiple valid solutions. Traditional deterministic looped models are ill-equipped for this. Flow and diffusion models, by modeling probability distributions, intrinsically support sampling diverse outcomes. A key objective is to leverage this aspect of probability flows to enable the recurrent reasoning model to generate a range of valid solutions for multi-solution problems.

The core motivation for "looped flows" is the hypothesis that training a recurrence through a sequence of progressively easier, local denoising objectives—similar to how flow models are trained—can provide the necessary temporal association and curriculum for the recurrent state to accumulate useful computation over time, even when gradients are locally truncated.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "looped flows" framework integrates a stateful denoiser into a probability flow model, allowing for recurrent updates of a hidden state alongside the denoising process. The model processes categorical sequences represented as one-hot encodings.

**Problem Formulation:**
The approach builds on continuous flow matching for categorical data. For a given problem `c` and solution `x_1`, a noise sample `x_0` is drawn. An interpolant `I_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1` defines a probability path `p_t` from noise to solution. The goal is to learn a denoiser function `D_t(x; c)` which approximates `E[x_1 | I_t = x, c]`. This denoiser implicitly defines the velocity field `b_t(x; c) = (D_t(x; c) - x) / (1-t)` of a probability flow, which can transport `x_0` to `x_1`.

**Stateful Denoiser Architecture:**
The core component is a stateful denoiser `D_hat_t` which takes the current flow state `x_t` and a recurrent state `z_t` (and problem context `c`) as input. It outputs a denoised prediction `x_hat_t` and an updated recurrent state `z_t+1`. The architecture is based on the Tiny Recursive Model (TRM), augmented with an input projection layer for `x_t` and an embedding for time `t`. The denoiser maintains two internal states: `h` (used for prediction decoding) and `ℓ` (used for updating `h`), which undergo several cycles of interaction within a single denoiser call.

**Training (Algorithm 1):**
The training objective `L_LF` is formulated as the expectation of cross-entropy losses between the denoiser's output `x_hat_ti` and the true solution `x_1` across a sequence of `k` timesteps. Key aspects of the training methodology include:
- **Local Denoising Objectives:** Similar to flow models, the denoiser is trained with local losses at each step, `CE(x_hat_ti, x_1)`. Crucially, gradients for the recurrent state are stopped (`sg(z_t(i))`) between steps, preventing backpropagation through time across the recurrent chain.
- **Temporal Alignment:** To encourage useful recurrent state transfer despite local gradients, a sequence of `k+1` ordered timesteps `t_0 < ... < t_k` is sampled. This ensures progressively decreasing noise levels (as `1-t_i` decreases). For each training rollout, the same problem context `c`, initial noise `x_0`, and true solution `x_1` are shared across all `t_i`. This "shared noise" and target are posited to create an implicit temporal association between adjacent denoising tasks, incentivizing the recurrent state to retain information.
- **Adaptive Computation Time (ACT):** An auxiliary binary classifier `q` is trained to predict whether the current denoised output `x_hat_ti` is a correct solution. This `q` head, trained with binary cross-entropy, allows the model to dynamically halt computation during training if a sufficiently accurate solution is predicted, preventing overfitting on later, less noisy steps that might provide redundant signals.
- **Pseudotargets (Regularization):** For single-solution problems where overfitting can be an issue, the interpolant for steps `i >= 1` can be constructed using `x_hat_t(i-1)` (the model's previous prediction) instead of `x_1` as the "clean" component. This `sg(x_hat_t(i-1))` is a pseudotarget, still supervised by the true `x_1`, which helps reduce direct exposure to `x_1` and promotes generalization.

**Inference (Algorithm 2):**
During inference, the model generates a solution by iteratively integrating the probability flow and updating the recurrent state over a temporal grid `t_0 < ... < t_n`:
- **Stochastic Integration:** The standard forward Euler integration is generalized to a stochastic differential equation (SDE) based scheme. This involves a "noise-backtracking" step where the current state `x_ti` is moved back to an earlier effective timestep `s` by adding fresh noise `epsilon`. The parameter `gamma >= 0` controls the level of stochasticity. `gamma = 0` reduces the scheme to deterministic ODE integration. This allows for diverse predictions from different initial noise samples.
- **Inference-time Scaling:** Performance can be scaled by using a finer temporal grid (more steps `n` than trained `k`). Additionally, Best-Q ensembling is employed, where multiple independent inference trajectories are generated, and the prediction with the highest confidence (as measured by the ACT head `q`) is selected.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the accuracy, scalability, diversity of solutions, and the individual contributions of flow and recurrence components.

**Q1: Accurate and scalable recurrent reasoning:**
- **Benchmark Performance:** Looped flows demonstrated improved performance on standard reasoning benchmarks compared to prior state-of-the-art looped models.
 - On **Sudoku-Extreme**, looped flows achieved 97.9% accuracy, surpassing FPRM (94.2%) and TRM (87.4%).
 - On **ARC-AGI-1**, accuracy reached 58.8%, exceeding GRAM (52.0%) and TRM (44.6%).
 - On **ARC-AGI-2**, accuracy was 12.2%, higher than GRAM (11.1%) and TRM (7.8%).
 - On **Maze-Hard**, looped flows achieved 86.7% accuracy, performing competitively with FPRM (87.0%).
- **Inference-Time Scaling:** The model exhibited scalability, with performance improving as the number of inference steps increased. For instance, Sudoku accuracy rose from 74.5% with 8 steps to 97.9% with 128 steps, indicating effective accumulation of computation over time.
- **Ensembling Benefits:** Inference-time ensembling (Best-Q with 5 trajectories) further boosted accuracy (e.g., Sudoku, Maze, ARC-AGI-1) or improved stability (ARC-AGI-2), outperforming PTRM.
- **Learned Recurrence Stability:** An analysis on Sudoku-Extreme revealed that looped flows learned more stable recurrences. Out of TRM's 12.6% failure cases, looped flows successfully resolved 90.9% of them, specifically recovering 89.9% of non-convergence cases and 98.0% of spurious attractor cases. This indicates that the proposed training method addresses common failure modes of recurrent reasoning models.

**Q2: Diverse solutions through probability transport:**
- **Multi-Solution Benchmarks:** Evaluated on N-Queens (8x8 and 10x10) and Graph Coloring (8-vertex and 10-vertex), which require generating multiple valid solutions.
- **Superior Diversity and Validity:** Looped flows achieved the best performance across all metrics (accuracy and coverage of distinct valid solutions), with more pronounced gains on larger problem instances.
 - On **N-Queens 8x8**, looped flows achieved 99.9% accuracy and 91.4% coverage (vs. GRAM's 99.7% and 89.7%).
 - On **N-Queens 10x10**, accuracy was 94.4% and coverage 61.5% (vs. GRAM's 89.7% and 57.5%).
 - On **Graph Coloring 8-vertex**, conflicts were 0.7 and coverage 89.4% (vs. GRAM's 2.7 and 85.8%).
 - On **Graph Coloring 10-vertex**, conflicts were 1.0 and coverage 55.2% (vs. GRAM's 3.3 and 51.3%).
- These results suggest that the probability transport mechanism through the learned flow effectively enables the discovery of diverse and valid solutions.

**Q3: Contribution of flow and recurrence components:**
- **Flow Component Ablation (Training-Time):** Ablation studies on the training-time components demonstrated the importance of the flow formulation. Removing elements like time conditioning, interpolant-based training, progressively decreasing noise levels, or shared noise across timesteps consistently led to performance degradation on ARC-AGI benchmarks. For example, removing both time conditioning and interpolant training resulted in a significant drop in ARC-AGI-1 accuracy from 58.8% to 43.6%.
- **Flow Component Ablation (Inference-Time):** Stochastic integration (`gamma > 0`) generally improved performance (e.g., N-Queens accuracy, Graph Coloring 10-vertex conflicts) and solution coverage compared to deterministic ODE integration (`gamma = 0`), although diversity was observed even with deterministic integration.
- **Recurrence Component Contribution:** Comparison with Flow Map Language Models (FLM, a flow model without recurrence) and self-conditioned variants (which carry denoised predictions or hidden features as recurrence) on Sudoku showed that while self-conditioning improved generalization over FLM (which tended to memorize training data), learning recurrent states across decreasing noise levels as implemented in looped flows yielded the best performance. This indicates the effectiveness of the proposed strategy for learning recurrent states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of "looped flows" represents a significant contribution to the development of deep learning models capable of complex reasoning and scalable computation.

- **Bridging Paradigms:** This work effectively bridges two distinct yet powerful paradigms—recurrent neural networks and generative flow models. By synergizing their strengths, looped flows offer a novel framework that can overcome the individual limitations of each, particularly the training instability of deep recurrent models and the potential weakness of pure flow models in sequential computation.
- **Enhanced Training Stability for Recurrence:** A primary impact is the provision of a more stable and effective method for training recurrent reasoning models. By using local denoising objectives aligned over time, the framework encourages the formation of globally useful recurrent states without the challenges associated with full Backpropagation Through Time. This capability to learn stable recurrences that avoid spurious attractors and non-convergence addresses a long-standing challenge in the field.
- **Improved Performance and Scalability:** The achievement of state-of-the-art or competitive results across a range of reasoning benchmarks—including Sudoku, Maze, and abstract reasoning tasks (ARC-AGI)—demonstrates the practical efficacy of the approach. The ability to scale performance by increasing inference-time computation, either through finer temporal grids or ensembling, offers a direct path to solving progressively harder problems.
- **Native Multi-Solution Capability:** By leveraging probability transport through the learned flow, looped flows intrinsically support the generation of diverse and valid solutions for problems with multiple correct answers. This is a valuable feature for many real-world applications where a single deterministic output may be insufficient.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Looped flows provide a new foundation for exploring more advanced reasoning architectures. The findings suggest that the integration of flow-based training curricula with recurrent computations is a promising direction. Future work could investigate simulation-free training algorithms that retain these benefits, further optimizing the balance between computational efficiency and reasoning capability. The conceptual link between progressively smoothed energy landscapes (implicit in flow models) and the optimization of recurrent processes could also stimulate new theoretical and empirical investigations.
