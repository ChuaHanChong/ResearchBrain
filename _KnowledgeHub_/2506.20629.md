# PLoP: Precise LoRA Placement for Efficient Finetuning of Large Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PLoP introduces a computationally lightweight method for precise LoRA adapter placement in large models by leveraging a novel "Normalized Feature Norm" (NFN) metric, which consistently achieves competitive or superior performance, often with fewer trainable parameters, across classification, text generation, and reinforcement learning tasks. This approach provides a principled way to identify which modules require adaptation, moving beyond heuristic placement strategies.

## Problem

- Practitioners face ambiguity and inconsistent guidance on optimal Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module placement, often resorting to trial-and-error.
- Current placement strategies (e.g., attention-only, MLP-only) are often suboptimal and task-dependent, leading to inefficient resource utilization.
- Applying LoRA to all modules (the 'ALL' strategy) increases computational and memory costs, diminishing LoRA's core efficiency benefits.

## Method

- The paper introduces **PLoP** (Precise LoRA Placement), a lightweight, automatic method for selecting LoRA target modules based on a novel measure called Normalized Feature Norm (NFN).
- PLoP computes NFN scores for each module type by averaging over a small sample of the fine-tuning dataset, quantifying the current alignment of the module's weights with the task's input data.
- LoRA adapters are strategically inserted into module types that exhibit the lowest aggregated NFN scores, hypothesizing that these less-aligned modules have the greatest capacity for adaptation to the new task.

## Results

- PLoP consistently outperforms or is highly competitive with traditional LoRA placement strategies (e.g., Attn, MLP, ALL) across diverse models (Qwen, Llama, Gemma) and tasks (classification, text generation, reinforcement learning).
- On Qwen3-**1.7B** for text generation, PLoP achieved **75.2%** accuracy with **27.5M** parameters, surpassing the 'ALL' strategy which achieved **73.9%** accuracy with **69.7M** parameters, demonstrating superior parameter efficiency.
- NFN scores reveal task-specific alignment, showing, for instance, that for Llama3.2-**1B**, MLP modules generally exhibit lower alignment (NFN ≈ 1), aligning with PLoP's decision to adapt them for math tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature norms of certain modules within large neural networks exhibit significant growth during training due to progressive alignment of module weights with input data, which can be quantified.
> - The Normalized Feature Norm (NFN) serves as a robust and computationally cheap metric to quantify module-data alignment, where lower NFN scores indicate modules with greater potential for adaptation to a new task.
> - Optimal LoRA adapter placement is not universal; it varies significantly across different module types, models, and fine-tuning tasks, necessitating a principled selection method.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial and often overlooked aspect within the rapidly expanding field of large model adaptation: the strategic placement of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules. It is situated at the intersection of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) and the theoretical understanding of deep neural network dynamics.

**Context of PEFT and LoRA:** The advent of increasingly large language and vision models has made full fine-tuning computationally prohibitive for most practitioners. PEFT methods, like LoRA, have emerged as indispensable tools to adapt these colossal models to specific downstream tasks with significantly reduced computational and memory footprints. LoRA achieves this by freezing the majority of the pretrained weights and injecting small, trainable low-rank matrices (adapters) into specific layers.

**Addressing a Gap in LoRA Best Practices:** While LoRA has gained widespread adoption, the community has grappled with inconsistent guidance regarding optimal adapter placement. The original LoRA paper suggested attention modules (Query, Key, Value), while other works found MLP modules to be more effective for certain models. This ambiguity forces practitioners to either rely on trial-and-error, follow potentially suboptimal general guidelines, or resort to placing adapters in *all* modules, which negates some of LoRA's efficiency benefits. This paper directly confronts this "confusion" by proposing a principled, automatic solution.

**Distinction from Other LoRA Improvements:** The research landscape around LoRA is vibrant, with efforts focusing on various aspects:
- **Hyperparameter optimization:** Adaptive rank selection (RA-LoRA, Adaptive Rank), learning rate strategies (LoRA+), and initialization schemes.
- **Structural modifications:** Weight-decomposed LoRA (DoRA), adaptive budget allocation (AdaLoRA), Vector-based Random Matrix Adaptation (VeRA), asymmetric architectures (HydraLoRA), and high-rank updating (MoRA).

Unlike these approaches that modify *how* LoRA operates or *what* its parameters are, PLoP focuses on *where* to apply LoRA, complementing existing advancements.

**Lightweight and Practicality Focus:** The paper explicitly acknowledges the practical constraints of LoRA finetuning. Existing adaptive parameter selection methods (e.g., gradient-based scoring) often require computing and storing gradients for the full model, or necessitate multiple forward passes through different configurations, which defeats LoRA's core purpose of memory efficiency. PLoP distinguishes itself by being a "lightweight method" that operates within these strict resource limitations, requiring only a few forward passes without gradient computations or extensive intermediate storage. This focus on practical applicability in resource-constrained environments is a significant differentiator.

**Theoretical Underpinnings:** Crucially, this work is not purely empirical. It draws inspiration from and contributes to the theoretical understanding of deep neural networks, particularly concepts like infinite-width limits (μP theory) and feature learning. The observation about feature norm growth during training and its connection to "module-data alignment" provides a novel theoretical lens through which to analyze the effectiveness of fine-tuning different parts of a model. This distinguishes PLoP from purely heuristic placement strategies and positions it as a method with a strong foundational basis.

In essence, PLoP provides a much-needed, theoretically informed, and computationally efficient solution to a practical problem in large model fine-tuning, thus enriching the PEFT research landscape by offering a principled approach to adapter placement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PLoP stems from the practical challenges and inconsistencies encountered in applying Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for fine-tuning large language and vision models. Despite LoRA's widespread adoption due to its memory and computational efficiency, practitioners face a significant hurdle: determining *which* specific modules within a large pretrained model should receive LoRA adapters for optimal performance.

The paper highlights the existing "confusion" in adapter placement strategies:
- The original LoRA paper suggested attention modules (Query, Key, Value).
- Subsequent research indicated that MLP modules might be superior for certain models and tasks.
- Even the original LoRA authors acknowledged that optimal placement is task- and model-dependent and difficult to ascertain.
- The default solution of applying LoRA to *all* modules, while ensuring broad coverage, comes at a higher finetuning cost, diminishing LoRA's core appeal.

This ambiguity forces practitioners into suboptimal choices, often relying on trial-and-error or general guidelines that may not apply to their specific model or task. This leads to inefficient resource utilization and potentially compromised performance.

Given these challenges, the **overarching objective** of this research is to introduce a principled, automatic, and computationally lightweight method for selecting target module types for LoRA adaptation, specific to a given pretrained model and a finetuning task. The method must operate efficiently within the resource-constrained environments where LoRA provides its greatest value, meaning it should not require full model gradient computation, multiple forward passes for evaluation, or extensive intermediate storage.

More specifically, the paper outlines three key contributions, which serve as its explicit objectives:

1. **Develop a theoretical framework to study module-data alignment in large neural networks:** This objective aims to move beyond empirical observations and establish a theoretical basis for understanding *why* certain modules might be more amenable to adaptation than others. The core concept here is "Normalized Feature Norm (NFN)," a measure of how well a module's weights align with the input data from a specific task.
2. **Develop PLoP (Precise LoRA Placement) based on this theoretical analysis:** This objective is to translate the theoretical insights into a practical algorithm. PLoP is designed to automatically identify which module types should be fine-tuned with LoRA by leveraging the NFN scores, with the hypothesis that modules with lower alignment (lower NFN scores) have more "potential for adaptation."
3. **Validate PLoP with extensive experiments across diverse scenarios:** This objective involves empirically demonstrating the effectiveness of PLoP. The authors test PLoP's performance in three distinct post-training scenarios: supervised fine-tuning for classification, supervised fine-tuning for text generation, and reinforcement learning for mathematical reasoning. This broad validation aims to show PLoP's generalizability and consistent superiority or competitiveness against commonly used placement strategies.

In essence, PLoP seeks to resolve a critical practical bottleneck in LoRA deployment by grounding a simple, efficient solution in theoretical principles of deep learning dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of PLoP's methodology is built upon an intuitive theoretical analysis of "module-data alignment" within large neural networks, specifically focusing on the behavior of activation norms.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Intuition: Feature Norm Growth

The authors start with a key observation: during training, the feature norms (specifically, $n^{-1}||Wz_{in}||^2$) of certain modules for inputs from the training data exhibit substantial increases, while others remain relatively constant. This phenomenon, empirically illustrated in linear networks, is attributed to two main factors:
1. **Large Network Width (n):** A common characteristic of modern LLMs, where theoretical predictions for infinite-width networks often approximate practical behavior.
2. **Progressive Alignment:** The module weights become increasingly aligned with their respective inputs.

To make this tractable, the authors simplify the analysis by considering a single trainable weight matrix ($W$) in a linear network, trained on a single data point using SignSGD (a momentum-less form of Adam). Under the Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) scaling rules, which ensure stability and maximal feature learning in infinite-width networks, they derive how the feature norm evolves. Initially, due to the randomness of initialization, the alignment term ($\langle W_t z_{in}, S(dz_{out}) \rangle$) is small, leading to a growth in the feature norm. As training progresses and alignment increases, this growth plateaus.

A critical insight from this theoretical analysis is that different modules exhibit different growth levels. This is explained by the fact that inputs to deeper layers change dynamically during training, introducing more complex update dynamics and varying degrees of alignment. Furthermore, the alignment is task-dependent: modules might show higher alignment for data similar to their pre-training distribution and lower alignment for novel tasks.

#### 3.2. Normalized Feature Norm (NFN) as an Alignment Score

Building on this intuition, the authors propose a novel measure called the **Normalized Feature Norm (NFN)** to quantify module-data alignment.

**Definition 1 (Normalized Feature Norm (NFN)):**
For a given pretrained model, a module with weight $W$, and an input $x$:
$NFN(W, x) = \frac{||Wz_{in}(x)||}{||W\tilde{z}_{in}(x)||}$
where $z_{in}(x)$ is the actual input to module $W$ for input $x$, and $\tilde{z}_{in}(x)$ is a random Gaussian vector with the *same dimension and norm* as $z_{in}(x)$, but with i.i.d. coordinates.

**Rationale for Normalization:** The denominator, $||W\tilde{z}_{in}(x)||$, serves as a crucial baseline. Since $\tilde{z}_{in}(x)$ is random and not aligned with $W$, its corresponding feature norm is expected to remain stable, independent of training. This normalization effectively removes the dependence on the magnitude of $W$ and $z_{in}$, isolating the "alignment" component. Intuitively, an NFN score greater than 1 indicates positive alignment, while a score close to 1 suggests minimal alignment.

#### 3.3. PLoP – Precise LoRA Placement Algorithm

PLoP leverages NFN scores to guide LoRA adapter placement:

1. **Step 1 (Scores): Calculate NFN(W, D):** For a given finetuning dataset $D$ (or a small subset of it), the NFN score is calculated for *each individual module* $W$ in the pretrained model by averaging the squared NFN values over the samples in $D$:
 $NFN(W, D) = |D|^{-1} \sum_{x \in D} \frac{||Wz_{in}(x)||^2}{||W\tilde{z}_{in}(x)||^2}$.
2. **Step 2 (Aggregation): Calculate NFN(T, D):** The individual module NFN scores are then aggregated by *module type* (e.g., Query, Key, Value, OutProj, GateProj, UpProj, DownProj) by averaging over all modules of that type. This provides a coarse-grained alignment score for each module type.
3. **Step 3 (Insertion): Insert LoRA in Low Scores:** The core decision rule: LoRA adapters are inserted into the module types that exhibit the *lowest* aggregated NFN scores. The hypothesis here is that modules least aligned with the new task (lowest NFN) have the greatest "room" or "need" for adaptation to improve performance on that task.

#### 3.4. Computational Efficiency of PLoP

A key strength of PLoP is its computational lightness. Calculating NFN scores requires only a single forward pass with a small batch of data from the finetuning task. It does not involve computing gradients with respect to full model parameters, nor does it require multiple forward passes for different configurations. This makes PLoP highly practical and consistent with LoRA's goal of memory-efficient fine-tuning, as it adds virtually no significant overhead.

#### 3.5. Experimental Validation

The methodology is validated through extensive experiments across diverse scenarios and models:
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) for Classification:** Tested on ANLI with Qwen2.5-0.5B and Llama3.2-1B.
- **SFT for Text Generation:** Trained on MetaMathQA and evaluated on GSM8K using Qwen3 models (0.6B and 1.7B).
- **Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning (GRPO):** Applied to Qwen3-1.7B and Gemma3-1B trained on MetaMathQA and evaluated on GSM8K, demonstrating its utility beyond standard SFT.

For comparison, PLoP is benchmarked against common placement strategies: inserting LoRA in Attention modules (Attn), MLP modules (MLP), all modules (ALL), and its inverse, PLoP-1 (inserting in highest NFN scores). The number of trainable parameters is often matched for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness and practicality of PLoP across various models and tasks.

#### 4.1. NFN Score Analysis and Insights

- **Task Sensitivity:** NFN scores are highly sensitive to the finetuning task. For instance, models show higher alignment with "history" data compared to "math" data, reflecting their pre-training distributions. This validates NFN as a task-specific alignment measure.
- **Module Type Variability:** There is significant variability in NFN scores across different module types. For Llama3.2-1B, Query and Key modules show higher alignment (NFN 2-3x baseline), while Value, Gate, Down, and Up (MLP) modules show lower alignment (NFN ≈ 1). For Qwen3-1.7B, Query, Key, and Gate show high alignment, but Value modules surprisingly exhibit "negative" alignment (NFN ≈ 0.75), suggesting alignment with smallest singular directions. These findings indicate that optimal placement is not universal.
- **Consistency Across Model Sizes:** Within the same model family (e.g., Llama3.2-1B vs. 3.2-3B), the ranking of module types by NFN scores remains largely consistent, suggesting that the underlying feature learning patterns within a model family are preserved across scales.
- **Specialized Model Alignment:** Specialized models (e.g., Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B for code) demonstrate overall higher NFN scores for their target domains compared to general instruction-tuned models, further confirming NFN's ability to measure module-data alignment.

#### 4.2. Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) for Classification (ANLI)

- **Qwen2.5-0.5B:** PLoP consistently and significantly outperforms both the MLP-only and Attention-only (Attn) placement strategies in terms of test accuracy. For this model, PLoP placed adapters in Value, Out, and Up projection modules.
- **Llama3.2-1B:** PLoP and MLP placement strategies yield roughly similar performance, both outperforming the Attn strategy. This aligns with the NFN map for Llama, where MLP modules generally have low NFN scores, placing them among PLoP's selected modules (V, O, D). This supports PLoP's intuition of adapting less aligned modules.

#### 4.3. Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) for Text Generation (MetaMathQA → GSM8K)

- **Qwen3-0.6B & Qwen3-1.7B:**
 - Placing adapters solely in attention layers (Attn) is consistently suboptimal, underscoring that for complex tasks like mathematics, adapting only attention layers has limited efficacy.
 - PLoP demonstrates highly competitive performance, often matching or slightly surpassing MLP-only placements.
 - Crucially, PLoP often outperforms the "ALL" modules strategy while using significantly fewer trainable parameters (e.g., 1.7B model: PLoP with 27.5M parameters achieves 75.2% accuracy vs. ALL with 69.7M parameters achieving 73.9%). This highlights PLoP's efficiency.
 - When parameter counts are equalized, PLoP often maintains a slight edge, indicating more effective parameter allocation.

#### 4.4. Reinforcement Learning for Enhanced Reasoning (GRPO)

- **Qwen3-1.7B (MetaMathQA → GSM8K):**
 - PLoP consistently achieves the highest performance in terms of both training rewards (Rwd/Format, Rwd/Answer) and final evaluation accuracy on GSM8K.
 - PLoP outperforms Attn even when Attn uses more trainable parameters (e.g., PLoP (r=16, 6.88M params, 74.52% GSM8K) vs. Attn (r=25, 7.17M params, 72.13% GSM8K)).
 - PLoP also outperforms MLP (r=16, 11.01M params, 73.61% GSM8K) with fewer parameters, indicating superior parameter efficiency.
 - Increasing the rank for PLoP (PLoP (r=25, 10.75M params) further improves performance to 75.03%.
- **Gemma3-1B:** While overall GSM8K scores remain low due to model limitations, PLoP still outperforms other strategies in this setting, suggesting its general applicability even for models struggling with the task.

In summary, the results consistently show that PLoP either outperforms or is highly competitive with traditional LoRA placement strategies, often achieving better performance with fewer trainable parameters, thus maximizing LoRA's inherent efficiency benefits. The empirical findings strongly support the theoretical premise that adapting less aligned modules is a beneficial strategy for fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PLoP represents a significant advancement in the practical and theoretical understanding of parameter-efficient fine-tuning, with a wide range of potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Democratizing Large Model Fine-tuning

- **Reduced Guesswork:** For practitioners, PLoP eliminates the need for arbitrary decisions, extensive trial-and-error, or reliance on often contradictory empirical guidelines for LoRA adapter placement. This directly addresses a major pain point in the fine-tuning workflow.
- **Maximized Efficiency:** By identifying the most impactful modules for adaptation, PLoP ensures that LoRA's computational and memory savings are fully realized. This enables effective fine-tuning of large models even on limited hardware, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible to a broader range of researchers and developers. This is particularly crucial as models continue to grow in size.
- **Cost Savings:** Optimizing parameter allocation translates directly into reduced GPU hours and energy consumption for fine-tuning, contributing to more sustainable AI development.

#### 5.2. Bridging Theory and Practice in PEFT

- **Novel Alignment Metric:** The introduction of Normalized Feature Norm (NFN) is a key theoretical contribution. It provides a principled, intuitive, and computationally cheap measure of module-data alignment. This offers a new lens through which to understand how pretrained models respond to new tasks and where adaptation efforts are most needed.
- **Insights into Feature Learning:** The theoretical analysis of feature norm growth and its connection to alignment contributes to the broader understanding of deep neural network dynamics during training and fine-tuning. It reinforces the idea that specific parts of a large model adapt differently based on their internal representations and their relevance to the new data distribution.
- **Principled Decision Making:** Unlike many heuristic approaches in PEFT, PLoP's method is grounded in a theoretical intuition about what makes a module "adaptable" for a new task. This provides a stronger foundation for future PEFT research.

#### 5.3. Broad Applicability and Generalizability

- **Versatility Across Models and Tasks:** The demonstrated effectiveness across diverse model architectures (Llama, Qwen, Gemma), model sizes, and distinct downstream tasks (classification, text generation, reinforcement learning for reasoning) highlights PLoP's robustness and generalizability. This suggests that the underlying principles of module-data alignment are broadly applicable.
- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** PLoP focuses on *where* to place adapters, making it complementary to other LoRA improvements that focus on *how* adapters are designed or optimized (e.g., adaptive rank, new initialization schemes). This allows for synergistic combinations to achieve even greater efficiency and performance.

#### 5.4. Facilitating Future Research

- **Granular Placement:** The paper notes its deliberate focus on module *type* selection, while acknowledging the potential for more granular (e.g., layer-level) placement. This opens clear avenues for future research into even more fine-grained adapter allocation, potentially leveraging the detailed NFN-maps presented.
- **Deeper Theoretical Exploration:** The observed "negative" alignment in some modules (e.g., Qwen's Value module) and the general patterns of NFN scores across layers invite deeper theoretical investigation into why certain modules are more or less aligned with specific tasks and how this relates to their functional roles within the network.
- **Reproducibility and Adoption:** The authors' commitment to releasing their code makes PLoP readily accessible to the wider research community, fostering reproducibility and encouraging its adoption and further development.

In conclusion, PLoP offers a significant practical tool for efficient large model fine-tuning and provides valuable theoretical insights into the adaptive capabilities of deep neural networks. Its lightweight nature, consistent performance gains, and principled foundation mark it as an important contribution to the evolving landscape of accessible and efficient AI.
