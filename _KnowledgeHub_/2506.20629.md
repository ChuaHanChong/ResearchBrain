# PLoP: Precise LoRA Placement for Efficient Finetuning of Large Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PLoP introduces a computationally lightweight method for precise LoRA adapter placement in large models by leveraging a novel "Normalized Feature Norm" (NFN) metric, which consistently achieves competitive or superior performance, often with fewer trainable parameters, across classification, text generation, and reinforcement learning tasks. This approach provides a principled way to identify which modules require adaptation, moving beyond heuristic placement strategies.

## Problem

- Practitioners face ambiguity and inconsistent guidance on optimal Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module placement, often resorting to trial-and-error.
- Current placement strategies (e.g., attention-only, MLP-only) are often suboptimal and task-dependent, leading to inefficient resource utilization.
- Applying LoRA to all modules (the 'ALL' strategy) increases computational and memory costs, diminishing LoRA's core efficiency benefits.

## Method

- The paper introduces **PLoP** (Precise LoRA Placement), a lightweight, automatic method for selecting LoRA target modules based on a novel measure called Normalized Feature Norm (NFN).
- PLoP computes NFN scores for each module type by averaging over a small sample of the fine-tuning dataset, quantifying the current alignment of the module's weights with the task's input data.
- LoRA adapters are strategically inserted into module types that exhibit the lowest aggregated NFN scores, hypothesizing that these less-aligned modules have the greatest capacity for adaptation to the new task.

## Results

- PLoP consistently outperforms or is highly competitive with traditional LoRA placement strategies (e.g., Attn, MLP, ALL) across diverse models (Qwen, Llama, Gemma) and tasks (classification, text generation, reinforcement learning).
- On Qwen3-**1.7B** for text generation, PLoP achieved **75.2%** accuracy with **27.5M** parameters, surpassing the 'ALL' strategy which achieved **73.9%** accuracy with **69.7M** parameters, demonstrating superior parameter efficiency.
- NFN scores reveal task-specific alignment, showing, for instance, that for Llama3.2-**1B**, MLP modules generally exhibit lower alignment (NFN \u2248 1), aligning with PLoP's decision to adapt them for math tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature norms of certain modules within large neural networks exhibit significant growth during training due to progressive alignment of module weights with input data, which can be quantified.
> - The Normalized Feature Norm (NFN) serves as a robust and computationally cheap metric to quantify module-data alignment, where lower NFN scores indicate modules with greater potential for adaptation to a new task.
> - Optimal LoRA adapter placement is not universal; it varies significantly across different module types, models, and fine-tuning tasks, necessitating a principled selection method.
