# Token Hidden Reward: Steering Exploration-Exploitation in Group Relative Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Token Hidden Reward (THR), developed by researchers at the University of British Columbia, Vector Institute, and Amii, introduces a token-level metric quantifying individual token influence on correct response likelihood within Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This method enables fine-grained control over the exploration-exploitation trade-off in RL-tuned LLMs, leading to up to 4.0% higher greedy accuracy for exploitation and 7.0% higher Pass@K accuracy for exploration on various math reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Effectively managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off in reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs) remains a persistent challenge.
- Existing methods for controlling this trade-off often rely on coarse-grained, question-level interventions or external verifiers, lacking fine-grained control at the individual token level.
- Prior analyses of RL-tuned LLMs, such as GRPO, primarily focused on negative gradients for exploitation, failing to provide a comprehensive framework for both exploration and exploitation, or account for positive gradients and cross-token interactions.

## Method

- The authors define **Token Hidden Reward** (THR), a novel token-level metric derived from GRPO's likelihood change analysis, to quantify each token's contribution to the probability of generating correct responses.
- A THR-guided reweighting algorithm is proposed to dynamically adjust GRPO's advantage scores based on the magnitude and sign of THR, allowing for explicit steering towards exploitation (by amplifying positive THR) or exploration (by amplifying negative THR).
- The method identifies and prioritizes "dominant tokens" (those with significantly high absolute THR values) to refine the learning signal, effectively masking out less influential tokens.

## Results

- For exploitation (`p > 0`), the THR-guided approach improved greedy decoding accuracy (Pass@1) by up to **4.0%** over baseline GRPO on the Qwen2.5-Math-**7B** model.
- For exploration (`p < 0`), THR consistently boosted Pass@K accuracy, achieving up to **7.0%** improvement over GRPO on Llama3.2-**3B**-Instruct and up to **5.0%** at Pass@256 on Qwen2.5-Math-**1.5B**, outperforming question-level baselines by over **1.1%**.
- The THR framework integrates seamlessly with other RL objectives like GSPO-token, yielding average Pass@K improvements of **0.9**-**1.4%**, and incurs minimal computational overhead during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A small subset of "dominant tokens" with disproportionately high absolute THR values significantly drives the learning dynamics in RL-tuned LLMs.
> - The sign of Token Hidden Reward (THR) directly correlates with the exploration-exploitation trade-off: positive THR reinforces confidence in correct outputs (exploitation), while negative THR preserves probability mass for alternative outputs (exploration).
> - Token-level interventions, such as THR-guided reweighting, offer more effective and fine-grained control over exploration and exploitation in LLMs compared to coarser question-level reweighting or entropy-based methods that neglect cross-token interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of large language models (LLMs) enhanced by reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for complex reasoning tasks. RLVR methods, particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and its variants like Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), have demonstrated efficacy in training LLMs for state-of-the-art performance in domains such as mathematics (DeepSeek-Math), general reasoning (DeepSeek-R1), and medical reasoning (Med-R1). Despite these advancements, a persistent challenge in RL-driven LLM training is the effective management of the exploration-exploitation trade-off.

Current approaches to address this trade-off exhibit limitations. Some methods, like Chow et al. (2024), rely on external verifiers or best-of-n objectives, which can complicate scalability. Other works, such as Chen et al. (2025), Mahdavi et al. (2025), and Walder & Karkhanis (2025), employ Pass@K-training to encourage exploration by reweighting questions based on hardness; however, these operate at a coarser, question-level granularity, overlooking finer-grained token dynamics. Cui et al. (2025) explore entropy-based regularization for exploration but primarily analyze a token's self-influence. Deng et al. (2025) examined GRPO's learning dynamics, specifically how negative gradients influence correct response confidence for exploitation, but their analysis was limited to negative gradients.

The current paper builds upon and extends this landscape by introducing a token-level metric, Token Hidden Reward (THR), to provide a more granular and explicit mechanism for steering the exploration-exploitation balance. By analyzing the intrinsic contribution of individual tokens to the confidence of correct responses, this work offers a fine-grained alternative to existing question-level or self-influence-based methods, aiming to provide more targeted control over the training objective. It seeks to establish THR as a principled tool for dynamically managing exploration and exploitation in RL-tuned LLMs, thereby offering new capabilities for targeted fine-tuning in reasoning-intensive applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged difficulty of explicitly steering the training of reinforcement learning (RL) models for large language models (LLMs) towards either exploration or exploitation. While RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR), particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has significantly advanced LLM reasoning capabilities, a systematic and fine-grained control mechanism for the exploration-exploitation trade-off remains an open problem. Existing methods often rely on coarser-grained interventions or are limited in their scope:
- **Coarse-grained control**: Approaches like Pass@K-training reweight entire questions, not individual tokens, limiting the precision of intervention.
- **Limited analytical scope**: Prior work, such as Deng et al. (2025), analyzed GRPO's learning dynamics but focused specifically on the role of negative gradients in exploitation, not offering a comprehensive framework for both exploration and exploitation or accounting for positive gradients and cross-token interactions.
- **External dependencies**: Some methods require external verifiers, complicating implementation and scalability.
- **Self-influence bias**: Entropy-based methods, while promoting exploration, often limit their analysis to a token's influence on itself, potentially missing critical cross-token interactions.

Recognizing these limitations, the authors aim to introduce a novel approach that provides principled, token-level control over training dynamics.

The key objectives of this research are detailed as follows:
1. **Define and Analyze Token Hidden Reward (THR)**: To introduce a novel token-level metric, Token Hidden Reward (THR), which quantifies the influence of each individual token on the likelihood of correct responses within the GRPO framework. The objective is to conduct a thorough analysis of THR's behavior, particularly identifying whether training dynamics are dominated by a subset of tokens and how the sign of THR correlates with confidence in correct outputs.
2. **Connect THR to Exploration-Exploitation**: To establish a conceptual link between the sign of THR values and the exploration-exploitation trade-off. Specifically, to hypothesize that tokens with positive THR strengthen confidence in correct outputs (favoring exploitation), while tokens with negative THR preserve probability mass for alternative outputs (enabling exploration).
3. **Develop a THR-Guided Reweighting Algorithm**: To design and propose an algorithm that leverages the magnitude and sign of THR to explicitly modulate GRPO's learning signals. This algorithm should allow for targeted biasing of training towards either exploitation (by amplifying positive THR tokens and weakening negative ones) or exploration (by reversing this strategy).
4. **Empirically Validate Efficacy on Math Reasoning Benchmarks**: To conduct extensive empirical evaluations on diverse math reasoning benchmarks. The objective is to demonstrate that the THR-guided reweighting algorithm effectively improves greedy-decoding accuracy (for exploitation, with p > 0) and Pass@K accuracy (for exploration, with p < 0).
5. **Demonstrate Generalizability and Integration**: To show that the THR framework integrates seamlessly with other RL objectives (e.g., GSPO) and generalizes across different LLM architectures (e.g., Llama), underscoring its broader applicability and robustness beyond specific GRPO implementations or model families.
6. **Compare with Existing Methods**: To provide a detailed comparison of THR with existing methods for managing exploration and exploitation, such as question-level Pass@K-training and entropy-based regularization (COV-KL), highlighting THR's advantages in terms of fine-grained control and effectiveness.

These objectives collectively aim to provide a fine-grained, interpretable, and effective method for controlling the exploration-exploitation balance in RL-tuned LLMs, thereby offering new capabilities for optimizing their performance in complex reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work is built upon a theoretical analysis of GRPO's learning dynamics, leading to the definition of a new token-level metric, and subsequently developing a reweighting algorithm based on this metric.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundation: GRPO Likelihood Change

The work initiates by leveraging a recent analysis (Deng et al., 2025) of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO is an RLVR algorithm that simplifies policy optimization by using group-relative rewards instead of a separate value network. For a given query, GRPO samples a group of responses, classifies them as correct (reward 1) or incorrect (reward 0), and computes normalized advantage scores ($ \hat{A}_{i,k} $) that are constant across all tokens in a given response. The GRPO objective minimizes a clipped surrogate loss involving these advantages and likelihood ratios.

Theorem 3.1 from Deng et al. (2025) is central, describing how the likelihood of correct responses ($ \ln \pi_{\theta(t)}(y^+_i | x) $) changes during GRPO training. This change is inversely proportional to a quantity that sums "Negative Token Hidden Reward" (terms comparing correct and incorrect responses) and "Positive Token Hidden Reward" (terms comparing correct responses). These terms involve weights ($ \alpha^{\pm}_{k,k'} $) that quantify the similarity of token-level prediction errors and the inner product of hidden embeddings ($ \langle h_{x,y^+_i,<k}, h_{x,y_j,<k'} \rangle $).

#### 3.2. Definition of Token Hidden Reward (THR)

Based on Theorem 3.1, the authors introduce **Token Hidden Reward (THR)** to quantify the contribution of individual tokens to the model's confidence in correct outputs.

- **Individual Token THR (Definition 4.1)**: For a specific correct response $ y^+_i $ and any token $ y_{j,k'} $ in another response $ y_j $, THR is defined as:
 $ \text{THR}(y^+_i, y_j, k') = (2r_j - 1) \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{|y^+_i|} \alpha_{k,k'} \cdot \langle h_{x,y^+_i,<k}, h_{x,y_j,<k'} \rangle $
 The $ (2r_j - 1) $ term assigns a positive sign for correct responses ($ r_j = 1 $) and a negative sign for incorrect ones ($ r_j = 0 $), reflecting GRPO's reward structure. A higher THR is associated with a larger increase in the likelihood of the correct response $ y^+_i $.
- **Group-level THR (Corollary 4.2)**: To account for multiple correct answers in a group, THR is extended by marginalizing over all positive responses:
 $ \text{THR}_{j,k'} \triangleq \text{THR}(y_j, k') \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^{N^+} \frac{1}{|y^+_i|} \text{THR}(y^+_i, y_j, k') $
 Here, the magnitude of $ \text{THR}_{j,k'} $ indicates the strength of a token's influence, while its sign shows whether it positively or negatively contributes to the likelihood of generating correct responses.

#### 3.3. Connecting THR to Exploration and Exploitation

The core hypothesis is that THR values encode exploration-exploitation dynamics:
- **Exploitation**: Encouraged by a higher increase in the likelihood of observed correct responses, strengthening confidence. This corresponds to amplifying positive THR tokens.
- **Exploration**: Encouraged by a lower increase in the likelihood of observed correct responses, preserving probability mass for alternative outputs. This corresponds to amplifying negative THR tokens.

#### 3.4. THR-Guided Token Advantage Adjustment

The insights from THR are then used to design a reweighting strategy for GRPO's advantage scores.

- **THR Analysis**: Empirical analysis of THR distributions (Figure 2) reveals that:
 - **Dominant Tokens**: A small subset of tokens exhibit significantly high absolute THR values, suggesting they dominate training dynamics.
 - **Significance of Sign**: Both correct and incorrect responses contain tokens with positive and negative THR scores, implying tokens can either strengthen or weaken confidence in correct outputs.

- **Dominant Token Training**: A threshold $ \tau $ (defined as the average influence of a correct response's tokens on other correct responses' likelihoods) is used to identify "dominant tokens." A modified advantage $ \hat{A}^{\text{THR}}_{i,k} = \mathbf{1}[|\text{THR}_{i,k}| > \tau] \cdot \hat{A}_{i,k} $ is introduced, effectively masking out non-dominant tokens.

- **Steering Exploration and Exploitation via THR Sign**: The main reweighting strategy modulates the advantage based on the sign of THR:
 $ \hat{A}^{\text{THR}(p)}_{i,k} = \mathbf{1}[|\text{THR}_{i,k}| > \tau] \cdot (1 + \text{sign}(\text{THR}_{i,k}) \cdot p) \cdot \hat{A}_{i,k} $
 - When $ p > 0 $, positive THR tokens are boosted, and negative THR tokens are dampened, reinforcing **exploitation**.
 - When $ p < 0 $, negative THR tokens are boosted, and positive THR tokens are dampened, encouraging **exploration**.

#### 3.5. Experimental Validation

- **Datasets**: MATH dataset (levels 3–5) for training. Evaluation on "Hard datasets" (AIME 2025, AIME 2024, AMC23) and "Standard datasets" (MATH500, Olympiad, Minerva Math).
- **Models**: Qwen2.5 (0.5B-Instruct, Math-1.5B, Math-7B) and Llama3.2-3B-Instruct.
- **Metrics**: Greedy decoding accuracy for exploitation (Pass@1) and unbiased Pass@K accuracy (K=2^1:8) for exploration.
- **Baselines**: GRPO, Base models, Pass@K-mixed training, COV-KL (entropy-based regularization), and clip-high.
- **Generalization**: The method is also applied to GSPO-token to demonstrate compatibility with other RL objectives.
- **Ablation Studies**: Investigations into the choice of parameter $ p $, positive-only/negative-only training, error-correction behavior, and computational overhead are conducted.

This comprehensive methodology, spanning theoretical derivation to extensive empirical validation, aims to establish THR as a robust and fine-grained control mechanism for managing the exploration-exploitation balance in RL-tuned LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings that support the efficacy and theoretical grounding of Token Hidden Reward (THR) for steering exploration-exploitation in RL-tuned LLMs.

#### 4.1. Dominant Tokens and Training Dynamics

- **Observation**: An analysis of THR scores reveals that a small subset of tokens possesses disproportionately high absolute THR values, while the majority cluster around zero (Figure 2). These high-THR tokens are identified as "dominant tokens."
- **Result**: Training exclusively with these THR-dominant tokens (i.e., setting other tokens' advantages to zero, Eq. 4) yields performance comparable to the original GRPO method, which uses all tokens. This indicates that a minority of tokens significantly drive the learning process.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness in Exploitation (p > 0)

- **Strategy**: Setting $ p > 0 $ in the THR-guided reweighting algorithm (Eq. 5) amplifies the learning signals from positive THR tokens and dampens those from negative THR tokens, thereby reinforcing exploitation by strengthening confidence in correct outputs.
- **Result**: On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B models, $ \text{THR}(p=0.1) $ or $ \text{THR}(p=0.05) $ consistently improves greedy decoding accuracy (Pass@1) compared to vanilla THR ($ p=0 $) and GRPO. For instance, on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, $ \text{THR}(p=0.1) $ achieves a 4.0% increase over GRPO in total average greedy accuracy. These configurations are noted as the most effective for exploitation. While prioritizing exploitation, these settings maintain competitive Pass@K results, especially at larger K values.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness in Exploration (p < 0)

- **Strategy**: Setting $ p < 0 $ in the THR-guided reweighting algorithm upweights negative THR tokens while down-weighting positive ones. This preserves probability mass for alternative generations, encouraging exploration.
- **Result**: $ \text{THR}(p < 0) $ consistently delivers improved Pass@K performance across all model sizes and benchmarks. For Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, $ \text{THR}(p=-0.1) $ surpasses the best baseline by 2.4% at Pass@128 and 5.0% at Pass@256. Qwen2.5-Math-7B shows average gains of approximately 1% across all K values. Additionally, $ \text{THR}(p < 0) $ maintains competitive greedy accuracy, even outperforming $ p > 0 $ configurations on challenging datasets like AIME and AMC, suggesting that increased exploration can be beneficial for harder problems. Ablation studies indicate that "negative-only" training is insufficient, implying that positive tokens also contribute to effective exploration when appropriately managed by THR. The method also increases the ratio of "reflection-related tokens," suggesting an encouragement of self-correction behaviors.

#### 4.4. THR vs. Pass@K Training (Token-Level vs. Question-Level Reweighting)

- **Comparison**: The paper contrasts THR's token-level reweighting with Pass@K-training's question-level reweighting (Eq. 6), which reweights advantages uniformly across tokens within a question.
- **Result**: THR ($ p < 0 $) consistently outperforms Pass@K-mixed training in Pass@K metrics across Qwen models (average improvement > 1.1%). Directly combining THR ($ p < 0 $) with Pass@K-mixed is found to be suboptimal, possibly due to Pass@K-mixed excessively downweighting "easy" questions. A modified combination using a constant weighting factor for Pass@K (static $ \chi $) leads to better performance, suggesting that THR and Pass@K can complement each other effectively if easy questions retain sufficient influence.

#### 4.5. Relation between THR and Entropy

- **Overlap**: There is a high overlap (around 90%) between tokens identified by high THR scores and those with high entropy (Figure 3), suggesting a strong association between the two metrics. The authors theoretically establish a link between THR reweighting and entropy regularization at the token level (Section D.2).
- **Comparison with COV-KL**: When compared to COV-KL (an entropy-based regularization approach that focuses on a token's self-influence), THR ($ p < 0 $) consistently outperforms COV-KL in Pass@K settings (Table 4). This superiority is attributed to THR's explicit capture of cross-token interactions, which COV-KL does not.

#### 4.6. Generalizability Across RL Objectives and Model Families

- **Other RL Objectives**: THR integrates seamlessly with Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO-token), yielding average Pass@K improvements (~0.9% over vanilla THR on GSPO and 1.4% over GSPO itself, Table 7).
- **Model Families**: THR demonstrates generalizability to Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, a model family with different characteristics (e.g., weaker math knowledge, reduced reasoning length). On Llama, THR still achieves substantial exploration gains (up to 7% Pass@K improvement over GRPO, Figure 5).

#### 4.7. Computational Efficiency

- **Overhead**: The computational overhead introduced by calculating THR is minimal compared to the overall training time, contributing only a small fraction to the total cost (Table 10).

In summary, the main findings establish THR as a principled, effective, and efficient token-level mechanism for fine-grained control over exploration and exploitation in RL-tuned LLMs across various models and objectives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces Token Hidden Reward (THR) as a fine-grained, token-level mechanism for dynamically controlling the exploration-exploitation trade-off in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for large language models (LLMs). This represents a significant advancement by providing a direct and interpretable method to influence model behavior at a granular level, which was previously less accessible.

The potential impact of this research is multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Control over LLM Behavior**: THR offers a principled way to explicitly bias LLM training towards either higher confidence (exploitation) or greater diversity and novelty (exploration). This granular control can lead to LLMs that are more precisely tailored for specific application needs. For instance, in domains requiring high-stakes, low-variance predictions (e.g., medical diagnosis, financial analysis), exploiting current knowledge is critical. Conversely, tasks demanding creativity, robust generalization, or problem-solving in novel scenarios (e.g., scientific discovery, code generation, complex mathematical proofs) benefit from enhanced exploration. THR provides the tools to fine-tune this balance.

- **Improved Performance in Reasoning Tasks**: The empirical results demonstrate that THR-guided reweighting leads to tangible performance gains. Increased greedy decoding accuracy for exploitation and consistent improvements in Pass@K accuracy for exploration across diverse math reasoning benchmarks suggest that this method can yield more capable LLMs for reasoning-intensive applications. The observation that exploration can be particularly beneficial for harder datasets highlights its potential to push the boundaries of LLM capabilities in challenging domains.

- **Finer-Grained Algorithmic Design**: By operating at the token level, THR reveals that RL for LLMs can benefit significantly from interventions that leverage the unique, sequential structure of language generation. This contrasts with coarser, question-level interventions and self-influence-focused entropy methods, suggesting a new avenue for algorithmic design that is more deeply aligned with how LLMs process and generate language. This shift in perspective could inspire further research into token-level dynamics in other aspects of LLM training.

- **Integration and Generalizability**: The demonstrated seamless integration of THR with other RL objectives like GSPO and its effectiveness across different model architectures (Qwen, Llama) underscore its broad applicability and robustness. This indicates that THR is not an isolated technique but a foundational concept that can enhance various existing RLVR frameworks.

- **Theoretical Insights and Complementary Approaches**: The work establishes theoretical connections between THR and established concepts like entropy regularization, and it compares favorably against approaches like Pass@K optimization. This interlinkage reinforces that dynamics-based, entropy-based, and objective-based analytical lenses can complement each other, potentially leading to the development of even more sophisticated and hybrid training methodologies in the future.

- **Future Research Directions**: The paper opens several promising avenues for future work, such as adaptive tuning of the parameter `p` based on training progress or question difficulty. This could lead to dynamic and intelligent agents that adjust their exploration-exploitation strategy in real-time. Exploring similar token-level interventions in other RLVR domains, beyond mathematical reasoning, like code generation or complex scientific reasoning, is another clear path forward.

In summary, THR provides a novel and effective mechanism for fine-grained control over LLM learning dynamics, offering substantial potential for improving their performance and adaptability in a wide array of reasoning-intensive applications, while also fostering new directions in RL algorithm design for language models.
