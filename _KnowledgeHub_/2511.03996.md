# Learning Vision-Driven Reactive Soccer Skills for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and ByteDance Seed developed a unified reinforcement learning controller that enables humanoid robots to acquire reactive soccer skills by directly integrating visual perception and motion control. The framework successfully transferred skills zero-shot from simulation to diverse real-world environments and led the Tsinghua Hephaestus team to championships in the RoboCup 2025 Adult-size Humanoid League and the 2025 World Humanoid Robot Games.

## Problem

- Humanoid soccer systems traditionally relied on decoupled modules for perception, planning, and control, leading to delayed responses and incoherent behaviors in dynamic environments.
- Onboard visual perception on humanoid robots is noisy, delayed, and spatially constrained, making it difficult to react accurately and robustly in real-world scenarios.
- Existing adversarial motion priors (AMP) focused primarily on proprioceptive imitation, struggling to integrate exteroceptive sensors like cameras for dynamic, vision-driven tasks.

## Method

- A unified asymmetric actor-critic (AAC) architecture with ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== was developed, integrating an encoder-decoder network to process historical visual observations into a control-relevant latent representation.
- A Virtual Perception System (VPS) was introduced in simulation to model real-world camera noise, latency, and detection probability, facilitating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) with a multi-critic framework and a symmetry loss were used to guide the policy towards human-like kicking behaviors while enabling task-specific objectives and bilateral kicking.

## Results

- The humanoid robot achieved robust and reactive soccer skills, contributing to championship wins at the RoboCup 2025 Adult-size Humanoid League and the 2025 World Humanoid Robot Games.
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer of policies from simulation to the real robot across diverse, unseen environmental conditions and achieved high kicking success rates comparable to simulation.
- The policy exhibited effective perception-action coordination, including active perception to keep the ball in view, predictive tracking when the ball left the field of view, and significantly reduced noise in ball position estimates (RMSE of 0.186m before kicking).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tightly coupling perception and action through an encoder-decoder processing historical noisy visual inputs allows the policy to robustly estimate ball state and adapt behavior.
> - Modeling real-world perceptual uncertainties via a Virtual Perception System in simulation is crucial for achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in dynamic vision-driven tasks.
> - Extending Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) to perceptual settings effectively bridges human-like motion imitation with visually grounded dynamic control for complex, reactive skills.
