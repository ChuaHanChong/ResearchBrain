# Learning Vision-Driven Reactive Soccer Skills for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and ByteDance Seed developed a unified reinforcement learning controller that enables humanoid robots to acquire reactive soccer skills by directly integrating visual perception and motion control. The framework successfully transferred skills zero-shot from simulation to diverse real-world environments and led the Tsinghua Hephaestus team to championships in the RoboCup 2025 Adult-size Humanoid League and the 2025 World Humanoid Robot Games.

## Problem

- Humanoid soccer systems traditionally relied on decoupled modules for perception, planning, and control, leading to delayed responses and incoherent behaviors in dynamic environments.
- Onboard visual perception on humanoid robots is noisy, delayed, and spatially constrained, making it difficult to react accurately and robustly in real-world scenarios.
- Existing adversarial motion priors (AMP) focused primarily on proprioceptive imitation, struggling to integrate exteroceptive sensors like cameras for dynamic, vision-driven tasks.

## Method

- A unified asymmetric actor-critic (AAC) architecture with ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== was developed, integrating an encoder-decoder network to process historical visual observations into a control-relevant latent representation.
- A Virtual Perception System (VPS) was introduced in simulation to model real-world camera noise, latency, and detection probability, facilitating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) with a multi-critic framework and a symmetry loss were used to guide the policy towards human-like kicking behaviors while enabling task-specific objectives and bilateral kicking.

## Results

- The humanoid robot achieved robust and reactive soccer skills, contributing to championship wins at the RoboCup 2025 Adult-size Humanoid League and the 2025 World Humanoid Robot Games.
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer of policies from simulation to the real robot across diverse, unseen environmental conditions and achieved high kicking success rates comparable to simulation.
- The policy exhibited effective perception-action coordination, including active perception to keep the ball in view, predictive tracking when the ball left the field of view, and significantly reduced noise in ball position estimates (RMSE of 0.186m before kicking).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tightly coupling perception and action through an encoder-decoder processing historical noisy visual inputs allows the policy to robustly estimate ball state and adapt behavior.
> - Modeling real-world perceptual uncertainties via a Virtual Perception System in simulation is crucial for achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in dynamic vision-driven tasks.
> - Extending Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) to perceptual settings effectively bridges human-like motion imitation with visually grounded dynamic control for complex, reactive skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid soccer, epitomized by the RoboCup challenge, is a widely recognized and challenging testbed for embodied intelligence. It demands a tightly coupled perception-action loop, integrating advanced locomotion, precise manipulation, real-time decision-making, and robust perception in a dynamic, adversarial environment. This task pushes the boundaries of autonomous robotics by compressing a wide array of essential robotic competencies into a single, continuous objective.

Historically, research in robot soccer, particularly within RoboCup, often relied on decoupled modular systems. Early learning-based approaches typically focused on acquiring isolated skills like running, dribbling, or shooting [3–9], or addressed high-level strategic planning separately. These modular designs, while simplifying individual components, often led to delayed responses and incoherent behaviors when integrating across modules, especially in fast-paced, real-world scenarios.

A significant shift occurred with the introduction of unified policies trained through reinforcement learning to acquire long-horizon humanoid soccer behaviors. This paradigm was further advanced by incorporating vision-based control, leveraging egocentric perception rendered via Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) during training. However, these approaches still faced limitations, including reduced reactivity to dynamic changes and heavy reliance on specifically modeled visual environments, which restricted their generalization to diverse, unconstrained real-match conditions.

Beyond soccer, the broader field of reinforcement learning has driven remarkable advancements in robust and agile robot behaviors, including running, jumping, parkour, and fall recovery. Concurrently, learning from demonstrations (LfD) has emerged as a powerful framework, enabling humanoid robots to replicate complex, human-like behaviors such as stylized walking, dancing, and acrobatic flips [20–25]. Within LfD, various methods have been explored:
- **Feature-based methods** like DeepMimic used dense tracking rewards and hand-crafted features for high-fidelity motion replication. However, their reliance on explicit motion matching and rigid temporal alignment limited adaptability in dynamic tasks.
- More recent approaches have tried to address these limitations through supervised distillation, high-level policies generating reference trajectories, or latent motion embeddings [4, 30–32], but these often involve cumbersome multi-stage training processes.
- **GAN-based methods** [33–37], particularly Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) and its variants like Wasserstein GAN, offered a more flexible alternative. They leverage a discriminator to provide an implicit reward signal, allowing for dynamic integration of task objectives with imitation without explicit temporal alignment. However, a key limitation of existing GAN-based methods was their primary focus on proprioceptive imitation in static scenarios, largely overlooking the integration of exteroceptive sensors (like cameras) crucial for dynamic tasks such as robot soccer.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. It extends the successful AMP framework to perceptual settings in real-world dynamic environments, thereby bridging the divide between motion imitation and visually grounded dynamic control. The work aims to develop a *unified, vision-driven reactive controller* that can generate coherent and robust soccer behaviors in previously unconstrained environments, overcoming the limitations of prior work in reactivity, generalization, and perception-action coupling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified reinforcement learning-based controller that empowers humanoid robots to acquire reactive soccer skills by directly integrating visual perception with motion control. The authors are motivated by several key challenges inherent in humanoid soccer:

1. **Overcoming Decoupled Systems:** Existing robot soccer systems typically rely on separate modules for perception, planning, and control. This modularity often leads to delayed responses and disjointed, incoherent behaviors in the fast-paced, dynamic environment of a soccer match. The goal is to create a tightly coupled perception-action loop for seamless operation.
2. **Mitigating Real-World Perceptual Limitations:** Onboard visual perception on humanoid robots is inherently noisy, delayed, and spatially constrained, especially during dynamic movements. These imperfections lead to distorted or incomplete observations, severely impacting a robot's ability to react accurately and robustly. The research aims to enable policies to function effectively despite these real-world sensory constraints.
3. **Achieving Reactive and Coherent Behaviors:** Human players exhibit intuitive perception-action coupling, enabling them to track fast-moving balls, anticipate actions, and adapt their movements in real-time. The motivation is to imbue humanoid robots with similar reactive capabilities, allowing them to consistently execute coherent and robust soccer behaviors across diverse scenarios, including searching for, chasing, and kicking the ball in various directions.
4. **Extending Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) to Perceptual Settings:** While AMP has been successful in enabling human-like motion imitation from proprioceptive data, its application to tasks requiring exteroceptive (visual) feedback in dynamic, real-world environments remained an open challenge. The authors aim to extend AMP to effectively bridge motion imitation with visually grounded dynamic control.
5. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Training complex robotic skills often occurs in simulation for efficiency and safety. However, transferring these skills to the real world is notoriously difficult due to disparities between simulation and reality. A key motivation is to design a training framework, including a virtual perception system, that effectively models real-world visual characteristics to enable robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
6. **Developing a Single-Stage Training Process:** Many previous approaches involving learning from demonstrations or complex skill compositions required multi-stage training. The motivation here is to develop a more streamlined, single-stage training process that allows the robot to autonomously compose versatile skills without explicit segmentation.

Ultimately, the core motivation is to advance humanoid robotics towards human-level embodied intelligence by demonstrating practical efficacy in a challenging, competitive setting like RoboCup.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a unified reinforcement learning (RL)-based controller that directly integrates visual perception and motion control to enable reactive soccer skills for humanoid robots.

#### 3.1. Overall Learning Framework

The system adopts an asymmetric actor-critic (AAC) architecture combined with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The actor (policy network) receives partial observations consistent with real-world sensor data, while the critic (value network) has access to privileged full state information available only in simulation, which enhances learning stability. The policy outputs desired joint positions, which are then tracked by a proportional-derivative (PD) controller on the robot.

#### 3.2. Perception-Action Coupling Architecture

To address real-world perceptual limitations and tightly integrate vision:
- **Encoder-Decoder Network:** The policy incorporates an encoder-decoder architecture. An MLP-based encoder processes a 1-second historical sequence (50 frames) of observations, compressing visual cues into a 64-dimensional control-relevant latent representation. A decoder then reconstructs auxiliary, privileged states (e.g., the ball's true position, robot's dynamic parameters) from this latent state. This guides the policy to extract meaningful, low-noise information from noisy, partial historical inputs, facilitating robust state estimation and adaptive behavior.
- **Virtual Perception System (VPS):** To bridge the sim-to-real gap, a VPS is introduced in simulation. It models real-world camera and perception processing characteristics: detection probability, noise distribution (Gaussian noise with variance dependent on ball distance), update frequency, and latency. This exposes the policy to realistic perceptual uncertainties during training, fostering robustness. Data for modeling the VPS was collected from a real robot observing a ball under diverse conditions.
- **Structured Observation Space:** Instead of raw images, the policy receives a compact, structured observation space: ball position (x, y), goal center coordinates (x, y), and goal normal direction (cos θ, sin θ), all in the robot's coordinate frame. This abstracts away irrelevant visual complexity, improving generalization.
- **Perception Pipeline:** On the real robot, YOLOv8 detects the ball and field landmarks (goalposts, line intersections) from camera images (25 Hz). Pixel coordinates are projected into Bird's Eye View (BEV) space using depth and geometric projection. Ball detections are fed to the policy. Field landmarks are fused with proprioceptive odometry to infer goal location.

#### 3.3. Reward Function and Multi-Critic Framework

- **Task Rewards:** The primary objective is ball kicking into the goal, resulting in a sparse terminal goal reward. To mitigate sparsity, dense auxiliary rewards are introduced: an approach-ball reward and a goal-progress reward (both using potential-based reward shaping).
- **Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP):** The AMP framework is used to guide the policy towards human-like kicking behaviors. A discriminator network is adversarially trained to distinguish state transitions generated by the policy from those in an expert demonstration dataset. The discriminator's output provides an implicit "style reward" (negative tanh-transformed score) that encourages the policy to imitate the reference motions. A Wasserstein GAN formulation with gradient penalty is used to stabilize adversarial training.
- **Multi-Critic Framework:** To address negative interference between heterogeneous reward components, a multi-critic approach is adopted. Separate critics estimate returns for two groups: goal-related rewards and auxiliary rewards (style, regularization). The final policy advantage for PPO updates is a weighted sum of advantages from these critics, enhancing training stability.
- **Regularization Rewards:** Includes penalties for falling, stagnation, head/leg action rate, joint position limits, base acceleration, collisions, and foot proximity.

#### 3.4. Learning from Demonstration

- **Reference Dataset:** A dataset of human motions is curated, comprising 76.28 seconds of omnidirectional walking from the ACCAD dataset and 30 seconds of custom motion-captured arch-based kicking. These human movements are retargeted to the robot's kinematics.
- **Symmetry Loss:** To encourage bilateral kicking capability and prevent asymmetric strategies, a symmetry loss is introduced. It penalizes differences between actions taken in an observed state and the mirrored action taken in the mirrored state, promoting symmetric motion patterns.

#### 3.5. Training Environment and Robot Platform

- **Simulation:** Training is conducted in NVIDIA Isaac Gym for parallel simulation (16,384 environments, 20,000 epochs, ~1 day on 8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs). The simulated environment features a RoboCup Adult-size field (14x9m), randomized ball properties, and small uneven terrain for robust locomotion. Random ball velocity/teleportation and external forces on the robot simulate competitive scenarios. Episodes continue with ball resets after a goal or out-of-bounds to facilitate continuous kicking.
- **Hardware:** The policy is deployed on the Booster T1 humanoid robot (1.2m height, 30kg mass), equipped with an Intel RealSense Depth Camera D435i on its head and an onboard Jetson AGX Orin for computation.
- **Hybrid Odometry:** For robust localization, a hybrid odometry system fuses proprioceptive data (using an MLP trained on simulated trajectories, with autoregressive feedback to mitigate drift) with vision-based corrections from detected field landmarks (using a particle filter).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments, conducted in both simulation and real-world scenarios, validate the effectiveness of the proposed unified controller:

1. **Robust and Reactive Soccer Skills:** The controller successfully enables the humanoid robot to acquire and execute reactive soccer skills, seamlessly integrating real-time ball tracking, agile locomotion, and accurate kicking.

2. **Exceptional Sim-to-Real Transfer and Generalization:**
 - The policy demonstrated **zero-shot transfer** to the real robot across diverse, previously unseen environmental conditions, including various surface types (grass, slabstone, soil, asphalt, rubber) and challenging visual conditions (varied color, illumination, detection failures).
 - It maintained robust ball tracking and locomotion even in scenarios lacking field landmarks, relying on proprioceptive odometry.
 - The robot successfully adapted to multiple balls and sustained extended rounds of continuous kicking with diverse and coherent behaviors.

3. **Competitive Performance Validation:**
 - The proposed controller was a key component of the Tsinghua Hephaestus team, which won championships in both the **RoboCup 2025 Adult-size Humanoid League** and the **2025 World Humanoid Robot Games**.
 - The team scored 76 goals and conceded only 11, demonstrating the controller's practical efficacy and agility under strict competition constraints (fully autonomous, onboard computation/sensing only, limited adaptation time).

4. **High Kicking Success Rates:**
 - Both simulation and hardware tests (Fig. 3A) showed high success rates, particularly in regions close to the goal. While performance decreased with distance or angular offset due to stricter accuracy requirements, the robot achieved successful kicks in most tested regions.
 - Hardware success rates were comparable to simulation, confirming the efficacy of the virtual perception system in bridging the sim-to-real gap. Crucially, the robot remained stable throughout all trials, with no falls recorded.

5. **Effective Perception-Action Coordination:**
 - **Active Perception:** The policy actively adjusts its torso and head orientations to keep the ball within the camera's field of view (FOV) for most of the time (Fig. 4C). This emergent behavior was present even without explicit tracking rewards, though such rewards improved robustness.
 - **Predictive Tracking:** When the ball moved out of FOV, the policy adaptively rotated its head and torso towards the predicted ball trajectory, successfully repositioning the ball within view (Fig. 4D, E).
 - **Perceptual Denoising and State Estimation:** The encoder-decoder network significantly reduced noise in ball position estimates. The policy's estimates had a minimum RMSE of 0.186m in the 1 second before kicking, a substantial improvement over the 0.344m from raw visual perception (Fig. 4B), which is critical for accurate strikes given the robot's foot arch length.
 - **Adaptive Ball Search:** The policy exhibited intelligent, context-aware ball-search strategies, such as reorienting towards the field center from edges, then performing rotational scans. Its predicted ball position estimates consistently guided these search behaviors (Fig. 3B, C).

6. **Agile and Versatile Gait Behaviors:**
 - **Behavioral Versatility:** UMAP analysis of joint-space trajectories revealed 5 distinct gait clusters (walking, turning left/right, left-foot kicking, right-foot kicking) (Fig. 5), demonstrating the policy's ability to produce a versatile set of behaviors and transition smoothly between them within a single policy.
 - **Generalization Beyond Demonstrations:** The policy's trajectories broadly covered the reference motion dataset but also extended to synthesize novel, task-specific behaviors, such as a "pivot hook kick" for backward goals, showcasing its capacity for adaptive composition.
 - **Superior Agility vs. Rule-Based:** Compared to a state-of-the-art rule-based strategy, the learned policy achieved consistently shorter kicking times across all orientations and exhibited higher angular velocities (Fig. 6), enabling faster ball possession and seamless transitions from approaching to kicking.
 - **Adaptive Gait for Chasing:** The policy dynamically adjusted its gait based on ball proximity and movement. It used slower, energy-efficient steps at a distance and increased step frequency with shorter, rapid strides closer to the ball for precise foot placement (Fig. 3D, E). For rolling balls, it demonstrated agile lateral steps or rapid rotational maneuvers to intercept and kick (Fig. 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in humanoid robotics and embodied artificial intelligence, with profound implications for the development of autonomous systems operating in dynamic, unstructured environments.

1. **Breakthrough in Humanoid Soccer Performance:** By achieving championship status in major RoboCup competitions, this work provides empirical evidence of the practical superiority of a unified, vision-driven RL controller over traditional methods. It demonstrates that humanoid robots can achieve highly reactive, agile, and robust soccer skills, moving closer to human-level performance in a complex sport.

2. **Unified Perception-Action Paradigm:** The paper successfully demonstrates the effectiveness of a tightly integrated perception-action loop, where visual inputs directly influence motion control. This unified approach inherently overcomes the limitations of decoupled, modular systems (e.g., delays, incoherence) that have traditionally hampered robot performance in dynamic tasks. The elimination of manual skill segmentation and reliance on a single policy simplify system architecture and foster seamless coordination.

3. **Advanced Sim-to-Real Transfer for Dynamic Tasks:** The introduction and validation of a virtual perception system, combined with an encoder-decoder network, represent a crucial contribution to bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex, dynamic, vision-driven tasks. This methodology allows policies trained in simulation to transfer robustly to real-world conditions despite significant environmental variations and perceptual noise, a long-standing challenge in robotics.

4. **Extension of Adversarial Imitation Learning:** The work successfully extends the Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) framework beyond its original scope of proprioceptive imitation in static environments. By incorporating visual feedback and enabling perception-action coordination, it broadens the applicability of GAN-based learning from demonstrations to complex, real-world dynamic tasks. This allows robots to learn human-like behaviors while integrating task-specific objectives in a flexible manner.

5. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The learned policy's ability to denoise visual information, predict ball movement, and actively coordinate perception with action (e.g., adaptive head movements and search strategies) significantly enhances the robot's robustness against partial observability, motion blur, and other real-world perceptual challenges. Its adaptive gait patterns further demonstrate a high degree of adaptability to varying ball positions and dynamics.

**Potential Broader Impact:**

- **Foundation for General-Purpose Humanoids:** The principles of tightly coupled perception-action, robust sim-to-real transfer, and learning from demonstrations for dynamic tasks are fundamental for developing general-purpose humanoid robots capable of operating in diverse human environments. This research paves the way for humanoids performing complex tasks beyond soccer, such as household chores, industrial assistance, or disaster response.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** This work contributes significantly to the field of embodied artificial intelligence by demonstrating how robots can effectively learn to interact with and react to their physical surroundings through integrated sensing and actuation.
- **Future Research Directions:** While highly impactful, the authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the current focus on individual soccer skills rather than multi-robot coordination or a broader repertoire of human soccer skills (dribbling, trapping, strategic passing). These limitations point towards exciting future research avenues involving expanding observation spaces for social/adversarial context and incorporating strategic priors for more diverse and context-aware behaviors, ultimately bringing robot performance closer to human-level capabilities in team sports and beyond.
