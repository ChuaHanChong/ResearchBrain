# Perpetual Humanoid Control for Real-time Simulated Avatars

> [!summary] Summary
> A physically-based humanoid controller is introduced that perpetually controls simulated avatars in real-time, achieving a 98.9% success rate on the AMASS dataset without external stabilizing forces and robustly handling noisy inputs with 91.9% success on video-derived keypoints. This system also enables high-fidelity recovery from arbitrary fallen or far-from-reference states, allowing continuous avatar operation.

## Problem

- Prior physics-based motion imitation methods relied on non-physical "residual forces" or external stabilization, compromising realism and leading to visual artifacts.
- Existing controllers for simulated humanoids typically require jarring resets when characters fall or deviate significantly, especially when driven by noisy real-world inputs.
- Training reinforcement learning policies on large, diverse motion datasets while integrating new tasks (like fall recovery) often leads to catastrophic forgetting, limiting scalability.

## Method

- A goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework, leveraging proportional-derivative (PD) control without external stabilizing forces, is developed for physically realistic humanoid imitation.
- ==The Progressive Multiplicative Control Policy (PMCP)== is introduced, training a series of specialized primitive subnetworks on progressively harder motion sequences to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- A dedicated fail-state recovery primitive is integrated, enabling the controller to naturally recover from arbitrary fallen or far-from-reference states and resume imitation.

## Results

- Achieved a **98.9%** success rate on the AMASS training dataset for motion imitation, crucially without employing any external stabilizing forces, ensuring high physical realism.
- Demonstrated high robustness to noisy real-time inputs, reaching a **91.9%** success rate when using 3D keypoints from off-the-shelf video pose estimators on the H36M-Test-Video dataset.
- Enabled high-fidelity recovery from diverse fail-states, including fallen (**98.8%** success) and far-from-reference conditions (**99.5%** success), within 10 seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity, physically realistic humanoid motion imitation from large datasets is achievable through end-to-end reinforcement learning without relying on external stabilizing forces.
> - A progressive learning approach with a multiplicative control policy effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, allowing a single controller to learn diverse behaviors and recover from failures.
> - Robust perpetual control from noisy real-time inputs (video, language) is feasible, enabling seamless and continuous operation of virtual avatars in dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of **physics-based character animation, reinforcement learning (RL), computer vision (specifically human pose estimation), and natural language processing (for motion generation)**. It addresses several critical challenges that have hindered the widespread adoption of physically simulated humanoids in real-time applications, such as virtual reality, gaming, and telepresence.

**Current Landscape and Challenges:**
- **Physics-based Motion Imitation:** Historically, creating realistic human motion in simulation has been difficult due to the high degrees of freedom (DoF) of humanoids and the complex dynamics involved. RL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training controllers to imitate motion. However, scaling these methods to large, diverse motion datasets (like AMASS, with tens of thousands of clips and 40 hours of motion) has been a significant challenge. Prior state-of-the-art methods, such as UHC, often resorted to applying "residual forces" or "external stabilizing forces" (termed a "hand of God" in the paper) to prevent characters from falling. While effective for stability, these forces compromise physical realism, leading to artifacts like "floating" or "swinging," which are undesirable for believable avatars.
- **Robustness to Noisy Input and Failure Recovery:** Real-world inputs, such as pose estimates from monocular video or motion generated from language descriptions, are inherently noisy, incomplete, or prone to errors (e.g., foot sliding, physically impossible poses, depth ambiguity). Existing physics-based controllers typically cope with such challenges by "resetting" the humanoid to a stable (often kinematic) pose when a failure condition (e.g., falling, large deviation) is detected. This resetting behavior is jarring, unnatural, and defeats the purpose of an immersive, continuous experience. There's a clear need for controllers that can gracefully recover from unexpected falls and resume tracking naturally.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting in RL:** When training a single policy on a very large and diverse dataset, or when incrementally adding new "tasks" (like fall recovery) to an existing policy, neural networks often suffer from "catastrophic forgetting." This means they forget previously learned skills or patterns as they learn new ones. This phenomenon limits the scalability and versatility of unified controllers.

**Contribution to the Landscape:**
"Perpetual Humanoid Control" (PHC) directly confronts these limitations. It aims to achieve:
1. **Uncompromised Physical Realism:** By successfully imitating motion from a vast dataset *without any external forces*, PHC pushes the boundaries of physical plausibility in character control, addressing a core critique of previous methods.
2. **Perpetual Operation:** The ability to naturally recover from arbitrary fail-states (fallen, faraway, or both) and seamlessly resume motion imitation fundamentally changes how virtual avatars can operate. This eliminates the need for disruptive resets, enabling continuous, "perpetual" control, which is essential for dynamic real-time environments.
3. **Scalability and Robustness:** The introduction of the Progressive Multiplicative Control Policy (PMCP) provides a novel solution to scale learning to large, diverse motion datasets and integrate new capabilities (like fail-state recovery) without catastrophic forgetting. Its robustness to noisy inputs from real-time video and language generators makes it highly practical for diverse applications.
4. **Simplified Input Requirements:** Demonstrating high-fidelity imitation using only 3D keypoints (instead of full joint rotations and positions) simplifies the input requirements, making it more compatible with off-the-shelf vision-based pose estimators.

In essence, PHC represents a significant step towards creating truly autonomous, robust, and physically realistic virtual avatars, moving beyond isolated demonstrations to enable continuous, interactive experiences. It bridges the gap between sophisticated physics simulations and the demands of real-world noisy data streams.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a **physics-based humanoid controller capable of perpetually controlling simulated avatars in real-time, even when driven by noisy, real-world inputs.** This goal is driven by the vision of highly realistic and interactive virtual avatars for immersive experiences, such as those envisioned for the metaverse, AR/VR applications, and advanced gaming.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be broken down as follows:

- **Motivation 1: Limitations of Existing Physics-Based Imitation Methods.**
 - **Problem:** Prior state-of-the-art methods, like UHC, often rely on non-physical "residual forces" to stabilize humanoids, especially when dealing with complex or large-scale motion datasets (e.g., AMASS). This compromises physical realism, leading to visual artifacts such as floating or unnatural movements.
 - **Objective:** Develop a controller that can achieve high-fidelity motion imitation on extremely large datasets (ten thousand motion clips, 40 hours) *without* resorting to any external stabilizing forces, thereby maintaining strict physical realism.

- **Motivation 2: Handling Noisy Real-World Input.**
 - **Problem:** When controlling simulated humanoids using real-time data sources like video-based pose estimators or language-based motion generators, the input is inherently noisy, prone to errors, and can lead to characters falling or deviating significantly. Current solutions typically involve resetting the humanoid, which is disruptive and unrealistic for continuous avatar control.
 - **Objective:** Design a fault-tolerant controller that can naturally and gracefully recover from unexpected falls and significant deviations from the reference motion, and then seamlessly resume imitation, eliminating the need for jarring resets. This "perpetual" operation is crucial for live, interactive avatar use cases.

- **Motivation 3: Scalability and Multi-Task Learning Challenges.**
 - **Problem:** Training a single reinforcement learning policy to imitate a vast and diverse set of motions, while also incorporating new capabilities like fall recovery, often leads to "catastrophic forgetting." The model struggles to retain previously learned skills when acquiring new ones or when exposed to harder sequences.
 - **Objective:** Propose an efficient and scalable learning mechanism that can handle large-scale motion databases and integrate diverse tasks (motion imitation, fail-state recovery) without suffering from catastrophic forgetting. This mechanism should enable the controller to learn progressively harder motion sequences.

- **Motivation 4: Practicality and Usability for Downstream Applications.**
 - **Problem:** Integrating physics-based controllers with real-time motion estimation or generation pipelines can be complex due to specific input requirements (e.g., joint rotations, specific kinematic structures).
 - **Objective:** Ensure the controller is "task-agnostic" and compatible as a "drop-in solution" with off-the-shelf video-based pose estimators (like HybrIK, MeTRAbs) and language-based motion generators (like MDM). Additionally, explore simplified input requirements, such as relying solely on 3D keypoint positions.

In summary, the authors are motivated by the clear gap between the potential of physics-based character animation and its practical limitations in real-time, noisy environments. PHC aims to close this gap by delivering a robust, physically accurate, and perpetually operating humanoid controller suitable for the next generation of virtual avatars.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Perpetual Humanoid Controller (PHC) is built upon a **goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (RL) framework**, incorporating several novel components to achieve high-fidelity, robust, and perpetual motion control.

#### 3.1. Core RL Framework

- **Markov Decision Process (MDP):** The task is formulated as an MDP (States, Actions, Transition Dynamics, Reward Function, Discount Factor).
- **Humanoid:** Uses the SMPL kinematic structure (24 rigid bodies, 23 actuated) with support for body shape variations. Physics simulation is performed in NVIDIA's Isaac Gym.
- **State Space:** Consists of humanoid proprioception (body pose, velocities, body shape) and a goal state.
 - For **rotation-based imitation**, the goal state includes differences in joint rotations, positions, and linear/angular velocities relative to the next reference state.
 - For **keypoint-based imitation**, the goal state simplifies to differences in keypoint positions and linear velocities. All quantities are normalized.
- **Action Space:** Utilizes a proportional-derivative (PD) controller at each DoF, where the policy directly outputs PD targets. Critically, unlike some prior methods (e.g., UHC's residual action), PHC *does not* use residual actions (adding to reference pose) or any external stabilizing forces, ensuring true physical realism.
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function guides learning:
 - **Task Reward ($r_{g_t}$):** Differs for motion imitation (penalizing deviations from reference joint positions, rotations, velocities) and fail-state recovery (point-goal reward for approaching the root reference).
 - **Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) Reward ($r_{amp_t}$):** A discriminator-based reward that encourages natural, human-like motion, crucial for both high-fidelity imitation and natural recovery behaviors.
 - **Energy Penalty ($r_{energy_t}$):** Penalizes high joint torques and angular velocities to prevent high-frequency jitter, especially important for controllers operating without external forces.
- **Training Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to learn the policy.
- **Initialization & Termination:**
 - **Reference State Initialization (RSI):** Episodes start by matching the humanoid's pose to a random point in the reference motion.
 - **Relaxed Early Termination (RET):** Episodes terminate if joints deviate more than 0.5m from reference *on average*, but crucially, ankle and toe joints are excluded from this strict termination condition. This allows the humanoid to use its feet for balance, accommodating dynamics mismatches between simulated and real human feet.
- **Hard Negative Mining:** During training, the system identifies motion sequences that the current policy fails to imitate and prioritizes training on these "harder" sequences. However, unlike prior work, this is integrated into the progressive learning scheme.

#### 3.2. Progressive Multiplicative Control Policy (PMCP)

This is the core innovation enabling scalability and multi-task learning without catastrophic forgetting.
- **Progressive Learning:** Instead of training a single monolithic policy, PMCP trains a series of "primitive" subnetworks, P(1), P(2)..., P(K), and P(F).
 - **Primitive P(1):** Initially trained on the full motion dataset ($\hat{Q}$).
 - **Subsequent Primitives P(k):** Once P(k-1) converges, its parameters are frozen. A *new* primitive P(k) is initialized (using weight sharing from P(k-1) or lateral connections) and trained *only* on the subset of "hard" motion sequences that P(k-1) failed to imitate. This progressively allocates new network capacity to increasingly challenging motions.
 - **Fail-State Recovery Primitive P(F):** A dedicated primitive is trained specifically for fall recovery. When the humanoid is in a fail-state (fallen, far from reference, or both), the state space is modified to remove detailed reference motion information (except root relative position/orientation), transforming it into a point-goal objective towards the root reference. P(F) is trained on simple locomotion data to bias AMP towards natural walking/running for recovery.
- **Multiplicative Control (MCP):** Once all primitives {P(1)...P(K), P(F)} are trained and frozen, an additional "composer" network (C) is trained. This composer dynamically combines the outputs of the learned primitives. Unlike a Mixture of Experts (MoE) that typically activates only one expert, MCP combines the action distributions of *all* primitives, allowing for a richer and more expressive action space. The composer learns to output weights to activate the primitives based on the current state, effectively leveraging the specialized knowledge embedded in each primitive.
- **Training Procedure:** The overall training process involves an iterative loop: train a primitive, identify hard sequences, freeze, initialize a new primitive on hard sequences, and finally, train a composer to combine all frozen primitives. Fail-state recovery training is interleaved.

#### 3.3. Connecting with Motion Estimators

PHC is designed to be **task-agnostic**, meaning it only requires the next time-step reference pose or keypoints. This allows seamless integration with:
- **Video-based Pose Estimators:** For real-time avatar control from video, PHC can consume outputs from methods like HybrIK (provides joint rotations) and MeTRAbs (provides 3D keypoints in metric space). A Gaussian filter is applied as a post-processing step to smooth noisy estimates.
- **Language-based Motion Generators:** The Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) is used, which generates 3D keypoints from text prompts. PHC's recovery ability is leveraged for "in-betweening" disjoint clips generated by MDM, enabling continuous motion from multiple text prompts.

This methodological approach uniquely combines progressive learning, specialized recovery policies, and a robust RL framework to create a highly capable and practical humanoid controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the superior performance and versatility of the Perpetual Humanoid Controller (PHC) across various challenging scenarios.

#### 4.1. Motion Imitation on High-Quality MoCap Data

- **Unrivaled Success Rate Without External Forces:** PHC achieves a remarkable 98.9% success rate on the AMASS training dataset and 96.4% on the AMASS test dataset. Crucially, this is accomplished *without applying any external stabilizing forces*, unlike previous state-of-the-art methods. This significantly improves physical realism.
- **Performance vs. Baselines:** Compared to UHC (Universal Humanoid Control), the previous SOTA method which relies on Residual Force Control (RFC):
 - PHC consistently outperforms UHC (with RFC) in success rate and achieves comparable or better MPJPE (Mean Per-Joint Position Error) across training and testing sets (AMASS-Train*, AMASS-Test*, H36M-Motion*).
 - UHC *without* RFC performs drastically worse (e.g., 84.5% vs. 98.9% success on AMASS-Train*), highlighting the critical role of RFC for UHC's stability and validating PHC's novel approach to achieve stability physically.
 - PHC exhibits significantly lower acceleration error compared to UHC without RFC, indicating smoother and more physically plausible motions without high-frequency jitter.
- **Keypoint-Based Imitation:** The keypoint-based controller (Ours-kp) shows performance on par with, and sometimes even outperforming, the rotation-based controller on MoCap data. This validates the possibility of using simpler input (3D keypoints) for high-fidelity imitation.

#### 4.2. Motion Imitation on Noisy Input from Video

- **Robustness to Real-World Noise:** PHC demonstrates exceptional robustness to noisy pose estimates from real-time video. It achieves an 88.7% success rate (rotation-based) and 91.9% (keypoint-based) on the H36M-Test-Video* dataset, where inputs are derived from off-the-shelf pose estimators (HybrIK, MeTRAbs).
- **Superiority Over Baselines on Noisy Data:** UHC (with RFC) only achieves a 58.1% success rate on this noisy dataset, and UHC without RFC drops to a mere 18.1%. This stark difference strongly validates PHC's design for handling real-world, imperfect inputs.
- **Keypoint-Based Advantage:** The keypoint-based controller (Ours-kp) performs particularly well with noisy video inputs (e.g., 91.9% success with MeTRAbs keypoints), suggesting that 3D keypoint estimation can be more stable or accurate than joint rotation estimation from monocular video.

#### 4.3. Fail-State Recovery

- **High Recovery Success:** PHC exhibits very high success rates in recovering from various fail-states:
 - Fallen-State: 98.8% success within 10 seconds.
 - Far-State (3m away): 99.5% success within 10 seconds.
 - Fallen + Far-State: 98.8% success within 10 seconds.
- **Natural Recovery:** While quantitative metrics measure success, the supplementary materials (videos) demonstrate that the recovery process is natural and human-like, with the humanoid rising from the ground and walking back to the reference motion.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Relaxed Early Termination (RET):** Improves performance by allowing minor foot deviations for better balance.
- **Progressive Neural Networks (PNN) within PMCP:** Significantly boosts robustness and imitation performance, proving crucial for scaling and avoiding catastrophic forgetting. Models without PMCP (e.g., a single primitive or MCP without progressive training) perform substantially worse on noisy data.
- **Multiplicative Control Policy (MCP):** Provides an additional boost over a simple Mixture of Experts (MoE) by allowing the combination of multiple primitive outputs.
- **Fail-State Recovery Integration:** PMCP successfully integrates fail-state recovery capabilities without compromising the existing motion imitation performance, demonstrating its effectiveness in multi-task learning.
- **Keypoint-Based Robustness:** Confirms that the keypoint-based imitator is highly competitive and robust, offering a simpler input formulation.
- **Number of Primitives:** Performance generally increases with the number of primitives, indicating effective learning of harder sequences. Four primitives were found to be optimal for the tested scenarios.

#### 4.5. Real-time Applications

- The paper successfully demonstrates PHC driving real-time (30 FPS) simulated avatars using live webcam video input (single and multi-person scenarios).
- It also shows seamless "in-betweening" of disjoint motion clips generated from language (MDM), allowing for continuous, physically valid motion from sequential text prompts.

In summary, the findings robustly support the paper's claims, demonstrating that PHC is a significant leap forward in physics-based character control, offering unparalleled realism, robustness, and perpetual operation capabilities for virtual avatars.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Perpetual Humanoid Controller" (PHC) represents a substantial advancement in the field of physics-based character animation and reinforcement learning, with profound significance and wide-ranging potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Overcoming a Fundamental Trade-off:** PHC successfully tackles the long-standing challenge of achieving high-fidelity, large-scale motion imitation without compromising physical realism. By eliminating the need for "residual forces" (a common crutch in previous methods), it sets a new standard for physically accurate character control. This achievement validates the potential of end-to-end RL for complex dynamical systems when combined with sophisticated learning architectures.
- **Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting:** The Progressive Multiplicative Control Policy (PMCP) offers a novel and effective solution to the catastrophic forgetting problem in reinforcement learning. Its ability to progressively acquire new, harder skills and integrate diverse tasks (like fall recovery) without degrading prior performance is a significant methodological contribution that can be applied beyond humanoid control.
- **Robustness to Real-World Imperfections:** Demonstrating high performance with noisy, real-time input from video and language models shows that physics-based controllers are not confined to idealized motion capture data but can robustly operate in real-world, uncertain environments. This bridges the gap between theoretical RL advancements and practical applications.
- **Validation of Keypoint-Only Control:** Proving that high-fidelity motion imitation can be achieved solely from 3D keypoint inputs simplifies the data pipeline for many computer vision tasks, as keypoint estimation can sometimes be more stable or easier than full joint rotation estimation. This opens new avenues for simpler and more robust integration.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Revolutionizing Virtual Avatars:** The most immediate and significant impact is on the development of virtual avatars for the metaverse, AR/VR, and gaming. The ability to control avatars perpetually without jarring resets, even when they fall or are driven by imperfect real-time inputs, is a game-changer for immersive experiences. This allows for more believable interactions, more dynamic virtual worlds, and a higher sense of presence for users. For instance, a user's avatar will naturally get up after tripping in a virtual world, rather than suddenly disappearing and reappearing.
- **Enhanced Telepresence and Remote Work:** PHC can enable more realistic and engaging telepresence systems. Imagine virtual meetings where avatars physically interact more naturally and expressively, mirroring real-world movements with greater fidelity and robustness.
- **Advanced Character Animation Tools:** For animators and game developers, PHC provides a powerful tool to generate highly realistic, physics-driven character animations with less manual effort and fewer artifacts. This can lead to more dynamic and interactive characters in games and films.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** While focused on simulated humanoids, the principles of robust, fault-tolerant control and progressive learning from diverse data have direct implications for robotics. Training robots to recover from falls, adapt to noisy sensor inputs, and learn complex motor skills in a progressive manner could accelerate progress in embodied AI and real-world robot deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** PHC lays a strong foundation for several future research directions:
 - **Full Scene Interaction:** Integrating awareness of complex terrains and objects will enable physically realistic human-object and human-environment interactions.
 - **Tighter Integration with Perception:** Deepening the connection between pose estimation/motion generation and the physics controller could lead to even more robust and adaptive systems, where the perception module is aware of the simulator's constraints.
 - **More Complex Behaviors:** Building on perpetual control, future work can focus on learning even more dynamic and high-level behaviors that require planning and anticipation (e.g., performing complex acrobatics consistently).

In conclusion, "Perpetual Humanoid Control" is a landmark paper that addresses core limitations in physics-based character animation, paving the way for a new generation of highly realistic, robust, and continuously interactive virtual avatars across various transformative applications.
