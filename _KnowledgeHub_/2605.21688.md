# Closed-Loop Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning for Deformable Microfiber Shape Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A closed-loop sim-to-real reinforcement learning approach enables autonomous shape control of deformable microfibers on a surface. It shows that a policy trained in a simplified, frictionless simulation transfers successfully to a physical system, achieving sub-millimeter shape control accuracy across various fiber geometries and initial configurations by using real-time visual feedback for continuous correction.

## Problem

- Micromanipulation of deformable objects is hindered by the dominance of unmodeled, history-dependent microscale surface and interfacial interactions (e.g., adhesion, friction).
- Accurate simulation of complex microscale physics is challenging, limiting the effectiveness of sim-to-real transfer for training autonomous, contact-rich robotic control policies.
- Current micromanipulation systems for deformable objects often rely on teleoperation or task-specific heuristic controllers due to difficulties in achieving high levels of autonomy.

## Method

- A ==reinforcement learning== policy is trained in a simplified MuJoCo simulation environment where surface friction is deliberately omitted to focus on learning geometric shaping skills.
- The trained policy is directly deployed on a physical dual robotic microgripper system without retraining or fine-tuning, relying on real-time visual feedback from camera images for iterative shape correction.
- The task is formulated as a partially observed Markov Decision Process, with the state incorporating gripper positions, fiber centerline, target shape, and previous actions, and a reward function penalizing shape error, fiber tension, and action roughness.

## Results

- The transferred policy achieved consistent real-world shape regulation, with a mean point-wise error of 270 

80 
m and a maximum error of 390 

100 
m for 50 
m diameter silk microfibers.
- The policy generalized across diverse initial configurations and to different fiber specimens (50, 80, 120 
m diameters and 10, 15, 20 mm manipulated lengths), maintaining median maximum shape error below 500 
m.
- Successful control was demonstrated even for target geometries more complex (higher bending energy) than those present in the training data, indicating robust learned skills adaptable to new configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Closed-loop control with real-time visual feedback can effectively compensate for significant sim-to-real mismatch arising from deliberately simplified physical models (e.g., frictionless simulation) in microscale manipulation.
> - Decoupling the learning of geometric shaping skills from the requirement for an accurate microscale contact model simplifies the development of autonomous controllers for deformable micro-objects.
> - Unmodeled microscale contact dynamics can be treated as short-impact disturbances that are correctable within a reactive, closed-loop feedback system without needing explicit modeling or long-term history tracking.
