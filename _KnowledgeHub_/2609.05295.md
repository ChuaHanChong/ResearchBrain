# RISE: Recursive Improvement via Self-Extrapolating Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> RISE (Recursive Improvement via Self-Extrapolating Policy Distillation) builds an on-policy distillation teacher from a model's own RLVR trajectory: it amplifies the displacement between the current checkpoint and a trailing anchor, in weight space or logit space, turning a sparse outcome reward into dense per-token targets. Refreshed every iteration, it beats GRPO and privileged self-distillation across math, STEM, code and agentic tasks at 1.3-1.6x wall time.

## Problem

- On-policy distillation gives dense per-token supervision but depends on teacher quality; external teachers score student prefixes they never generated, so their distributions grow unreliable exactly at novel reasoning steps.
- Self-distillation with privileged conditioning is capped by in-context learning capacity, and teacher and student see different inputs, creating another mismatch that need not transfer.
- RLVR and other self-improvement schemes supply only sequence-level signals, and remedies like token gating or DAgger sampling tolerate a flawed teacher instead of replacing it.

## Method

- **Self-extrapolated teacher**: a representation map over policies gives phi(future) = phi(anchor) plus beta times the post-==RLVR== minus anchor displacement, instantiated as ==weight-space task arithmetic== or a ==logit-space geometric mixture==.
- Two-phase loop: ==GRPO==-style ==RLVR== supplies the displacement direction, then ==OPD== distills the extrapolated teacher via top-K ==Jensen-Shannon divergence== on reused rollouts, adding no extra sampling.
- Stabilizers: a monotonically decaying ==beta schedule== from 1.2 toward 1, plus an ==EMA anchor== that lags the policy to smooth and enlarge the displacement across iterations.

## Results

- Math Avg beats GRPO everywhere: OLMo3-7B **47.6** to **56.4** with AIME'24 up **16.7** points, Qwen3-1.7B **45.4** to **50.2**, Qwen3-8B **60.0** to **62.7**; OOD averages hold or rise.
- Privileged-teacher baselines lag: GRPO+SDPO falls below GRPO (**55.9** vs **60.0** at 8B); multi-domain RISE reaches **44.8** Math and **47.5** STEM; agentic ALFWorld **+9.4**, WebShop accuracy **+10.9**.
- Ablations: removing RLVR collapses training to **2.4%** on MATH-500 with runaway length; adopting the extrapolated weights without OPD gains only **+0.3**; compute-matched GRPO-2x trails RISE by **2.2-2.9** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A training trajectory is itself a supervision source: low-rank, near-linear post-training dynamics let modest extrapolation synthesize a stronger teacher without external models or privileged context.
> - Extrapolation needs both a reward-verified direction and a trust-region projection; amplifying self-referential updates diverges, and merely taking a longer weight step shifts without improving.
> - Safe extrapolation range shrinks as a policy nears optimality, so the scale must decay; any reward hacking in the signal gets amplified too.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) is actively exploring methods for recursive self-improvement, enabling models to enhance their capabilities through self-generated data and experience, with minimal human oversight. Common approaches include reinforcement learning from human feedback or verifiable rewards (RLHF/RLVR), self-play, and rejection sampling fine-tuning. A shared characteristic of these methods is their reliance on sequence-level learning signals, such as an outcome reward or a binary label. This sparsity creates a credit assignment bottleneck, as the signal does not inherently indicate which specific tokens contributed to success or failure.

On-policy distillation (OPD) aims to address this by providing dense, per-token supervision. In OPD, a "teacher" model guides a "student" model by specifying a full next-token distribution at each position, theoretically offering a more granular signal for improvement. However, the effectiveness of OPD is constrained by the quality and availability of a suitable teacher. Existing strategies for teacher acquisition face significant limitations:
- **External teachers:** Distilling from a larger, pre-trained model can lead to distribution mismatch. As the student explores novel reasoning paths, the external teacher's distributions conditioned on these out-of-distribution prefixes may become unreliable, reflecting how the teacher would continue from an unfamiliar context rather than providing accurate guidance for the student's reasoning.
- **On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) with privileged conditioning:** This approach uses the same base model as both teacher and student, with the teacher provided privileged information (e.g., correct solutions). Its limitations include insufficient in-context learning (ICL) capability of the model to effectively incorporate the privileged context, and a different form of distribution mismatch where the teacher’s conditioned distribution may not be transferable or may even be detrimental to the student’s unconditioned generation.
- **Heuristic remedies:** Many recent works propose token-level gating, divergence mixing, DAgger-style sampling, or trajectory refinement to mitigate the symptoms of flawed teachers. These methods often accept a suboptimal teacher and focus on improving signal integration rather than addressing the fundamental challenge of teacher construction.

This work proposes a shift in perspective by constructing a teacher directly from the language model's own RLVR training trajectory. It leverages empirical evidence suggesting that post-training parameter updates and log-probability evolutions are low-dimensional and approximately linear, as observed in research on task arithmetic, linear mode connectivity, and low-rank weight dynamics in RLVR. By exploiting this structural property, the proposed method aims to generate a reliable, adaptive teacher that overcomes the limitations of prior OPD approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify and resolve the teacher quality bottleneck that limits the effectiveness of on-policy distillation (OPD) for language model self-improvement. The authors posit that existing teacher construction methods, whether relying on external models or privileged-conditioned self-distillation, introduce fundamental issues such as distribution mismatch and constraints imposed by in-context learning (ICL) capabilities.

The core motivation is to enable language models to recursively improve themselves from their own experience without external supervision or privileged information. To achieve this, the authors propose a novel approach for teacher construction by synthesizing a "future teacher" directly from the model's own RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) training trajectory. This concept is motivated by the idea that the optimal teacher for a current policy (π_θ) is its own converged future self (π*), which, though unknown, has its direction of convergence revealed by the ongoing training process.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:
1. **Eliminate external model dependency:** To bypass the distribution mismatch issues inherent when distilling from a separate, potentially larger or differently trained, external teacher model.
2. **Remove privileged conditioning:** To circumvent the limitations of on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), which struggles with inadequate in-context learning (ICL) capabilities and potential transferability issues arising from the teacher receiving privileged information unavailable to the student.
3. **Convert sparse outcome rewards into dense token-level supervision:** RLVR provides a sparse, sequence-level reward signal. The objective is to transform this sparse signal into a rich, per-token guidance that informs the model on how to improve individual token-level decisions.
4. **Establish a recursive improvement mechanism:** Rather than treating distillation as a one-shot compression of an existing teacher, the goal is to create a dynamic system where the teacher continuously refreshes and advances as the student policy improves. This allows the distillation process to become a recursive loop, enabling continuous self-enhancement.
5. **Leverage observed linearity in training trajectories:** The work is motivated by empirical findings that post-training updates often occur in low-dimensional, approximately linear subspaces of the model's parameter or output logit space. This linearity provides a principled basis for extrapolating the direction of recent improvement to synthesize a future teacher.
6. **Complementary roles of RLVR and OPD:** The approach is motivated by the understanding that RLVR can ground the training in verifiable outcomes, ensuring the direction of improvement is meaningful, while OPD can provide the fine-grained token-level refinement that sparse outcome rewards cannot. The objective is to combine these two signals in a synergistic manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **RISE (Recursive Improvement via Self-Extrapolating Policy Distillation)**, constructs a synthetic teacher by extrapolating the model's own RLVR training trajectory. The core concept is formalized by defining a self-extrapolated teacher `π_future` using a representation map `φ` into a vector space where linear operations are meaningful:

`φ(π_future) = φ(π_θ_n) + β * (φ(π_θ'_n+1) - φ(π_θ_n))`, where `β > 1`.

Here, `π_θ_n` is the policy at the beginning of the current iteration, `π_θ'_n+1` is the policy after the RLVR update in the current iteration, and `β` is an extrapolation scale factor that amplifies the recent displacement, projecting beyond the current state along the observed training direction.

**Instantiations of the Representation Map (φ):**
RISE offers two primary instantiations for `φ`, each with distinct computational and statistical properties:
1. **Weight-space extrapolation (φ = θ):** This sets `φ` to the identity function on the model's parameters. The future teacher's parameters are calculated as `θ_future = θ_n + β * (θ'_n+1 - θ_n)`. The teacher is then the model defined by these `θ_future` parameters. This approach yields a coherent model whose cross-position predictions are jointly consistent. It requires materializing these extrapolated parameters and performing a forward pass for the teacher during each OPD step.
2. **Logit-space extrapolation (φ = log π):** Here, `φ` maps each policy to its per-token output log-probabilities. The future teacher's log-probabilities are computed as `log π_future(·|s_t) = log π_θ_n(·|s_t) + β * (log π_θ'_n+1(·|s_t) - log π_θ_n(·|s_t)) + const`, where `s_t` is the context at position `t` and `const` ensures normalization. This is a geometric mixture that amplifies the probability ratio `π_θ'_n+1 / π_θ_n`. Logit-space extrapolation does not require parameter manipulation and can fix the teacher's distribution before the OPD phase begins, potentially offering computational advantages over weight-space.

**Distillation Loss Design:**
The RISE distillation loss (`L_RISE`) is based on minimizing the divergence between the student's policy `π_θ` and the self-extrapolated teacher `π_future`. Specifically:
`L_RISE = E_y∼π_θ [ Σ_t D_KL(π_θ(·|s_t) || sg[π_future(·|s_t)]) ]`.
The KL divergence is computed over a (K+1)-dimensional simplex, including the top-K tokens and a tail bucket for the remaining probability mass, to manage computational expense. `π_future` is treated with a stop-gradient (`sg`) to ensure it remains a fixed target during the student's gradient updates. Jensen–Shannon Divergence (JSD) is used in practice instead of reverse KL for numerical stability, as JSD is bounded.

**Training Procedure (Two-Phase Iteration):**
Each RISE iteration alternates between two phases:
1. **RLVR Phase:** Rollouts are sampled from the current policy `π_θ_n`. Outcome rewards are computed, and the policy is updated using a policy gradient method (e.g., GRPO) to obtain `θ'_n+1`. This phase is crucial for grounding the extrapolation direction in verifiable improvements derived from sparse outcome rewards.
2. **OPD Phase:** The `π_future` teacher is constructed using `π_θ'_n+1` and an anchor policy `π_θ_anchor` (which can be `π_θ_n` or an EMA of previous checkpoints). The student policy, initialized from `π_θ'_n+1`, is then refined by minimizing `L_RISE` against the `π_future` teacher, yielding `θ_n+1`. This phase provides the dense, per-token supervision that refines the policy according to the extrapolated future direction.

Importantly, the rollouts sampled for the RLVR phase are reused for the OPD phase, meaning RISE incurs no additional sampling cost.

**Key Design Elements:**
- **Extrapolation Decay (β_n):** The extrapolation scale `β_n` is dynamically decreased from an initial `β_0 > 1` towards 1 over training iterations (e.g., linearly). This adaptive scheduling is motivated by the observation that the "safe" extrapolation range narrows as the policy approaches optimality.
- **Anchor Dynamics (θ_anchor):** The anchor point used to compute the displacement vector can be either the immediately preceding checkpoint (`η=1`) or an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of previous checkpoints (`η<1`). An EMA anchor can smooth and effectively enlarge the displacement vector, which may be beneficial if per-step RLVR updates are noisy.
- **Role of OPD:** The distillation step projects the extrapolated teacher's distribution onto the student policy within a trust region. This is crucial because directly adopting the `θ_future` policy could be unstable or degrade performance due to potential overshooting or amplification of noise from the RLVR step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RISE spanned multiple model scales, architectures, and domains, demonstrating consistent improvements over baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Performance Across Diverse Tasks and Model Scales

- **Mathematical Reasoning:** RISE consistently outperformed RLVR-only training (GRPO) and on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) baselines across Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-1.7B, and OLMo3-7B-Instruct-SFT models trained on DAPOMath and OpenR1-Math-46K. Significant gains were observed on competition benchmarks, for example, improving AIME'24 accuracy on OLMo3-7B from 30.2% (GRPO) to 46.9% (RISE logit). Both logit-space and weight-space RISE variants achieved competitive results, with neither consistently dominating.
- **Multi-Domain STEM:** On Qwen3-4B-Base trained with mixed math and STEM data, RISE (weight) achieved higher average accuracy in both Math (44.8% vs. GRPO's 40.2%) and STEM (47.5% vs. 45.5%), demonstrating that extrapolation can aggregate improvements across heterogeneous domains without diluting gains.
- **Code Generation:** Using Qwen3-8B-Base, RISE variants converged faster than GRPO on HumanEval+ and MBPP+ benchmarks, reaching comparable final accuracy in fewer training steps.
- **Agentic Tasks:** On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct for ALFWorld and WebShop, RISE (weight) substantially outperformed GRPO, with a 9.4 percentage point increase in ALFWorld success rate and a 10.9 percentage point increase in WebShop accuracy, indicating effectiveness in sequential decision-making with sparse, delayed rewards.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

- **Limited Gains from Privileged-Conditioning OPSD:** Baselines using privileged-conditioning (GRPO+SDPO, SDAR, RLSD) generally showed limited or, in some cases, negative gains compared to GRPO-only training. This empirically supported the authors' hypothesis that ICL capacity limitations and distribution mismatch hinder the effectiveness of these teachers, even with varied integration strategies.
- **Out-of-Distribution Preservation:** RISE maintained or slightly improved performance on out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., GPQA, IFEval, MMLU-Pro), suggesting that the token-level refinement did not degrade general capabilities.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Extrapolation Mechanism

- **Necessity of RLVR Grounding:** Ablation studies demonstrated that removing the RLVR phase and extrapolating based solely on self-distillation led to training collapse (accuracy dropped, generation length exploded), confirming that outcome rewards are essential to ground the extrapolation direction in verifiable improvement.
- **Role of Distillation:** Directly adopting the extrapolated `θ_future` policy without the OPD phase resulted in negligible performance gains compared to GRPO. This indicated that the benefits of RISE stem from the token-level distillation process, which projects the extrapolated teacher's distribution back onto the student, rather than merely taking a longer step in parameter space.
- **Narrowing Safe Extrapolation Range:** Empirical testing of extrapolation factors (`β`) on saved checkpoints showed that while moderate `β` values yielded consistent gains early in training, the tolerable `β` range contracted sharply as the policy approached optimality. This justified the use of a decaying `β` schedule in RISE.
- **Broadening Solution Coverage:** RISE improved both average accuracy (avg@N) and best-of-N accuracy (pass@N) on AIME benchmarks, particularly for smaller models. This suggested that RISE not only sharpened the policy around existing solutions but also expanded the set of problems the model could solve, indicating increased solution coverage.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Training Cost

- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Performance was stable for `β_0` values in the range of 1.2 to 1.3, with aggressive values (e.g., 2.0) leading to divergence without proper decay. Various decay schedules (linear, cosine, exponential) performed comparably, but any decay schedule outperformed a fixed `β`.
- **Anchor Dynamics:** An EMA anchor (η < 1) improved performance for some models (Qwen), smoothing noisy RLVR updates, while a previous-checkpoint anchor (η = 1) was more effective for others (OLMo) with stable per-step updates.
- **Rollout Reuse:** Reusing RLVR rollouts for OPD proved as effective as generating fresh rollouts, contributing to computational efficiency.
- **Compute-Matched Comparison:** RISE outperformed GRPO even when GRPO was given a matched gradient budget (GRPO-2x), indicating that RISE's gains are attributable to the quality of its extrapolated supervision, not merely more optimization steps.
- **Training Cost:** RISE introduced a modest wall-time overhead of approximately 1.3–1.6× that of GRPO per iteration, with no additional sampling cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RISE contributes to the field of language model self-improvement by introducing a method for constructing an adaptive, high-quality teacher directly from a model's own training trajectory. This approach addresses a fundamental limitation of previous on-policy distillation (OPD) techniques, namely the dependence on external models or privileged information that can lead to distribution mismatch or be constrained by in-context learning (ICL) capabilities.

The significance of RISE lies in several aspects:
- **Enabling Recursive Self-Improvement:** By dynamically refreshing the teacher from the student's improving trajectory, RISE transforms distillation into a continuous, recursive improvement loop. This moves closer to the aspiration of building AI systems that can autonomously enhance their capabilities from their own experience.
- **Overcoming the Teacher Quality Bottleneck:** RISE provides a principled solution to the "teacher quality bottleneck" in OPD by synthesizing a "future self" teacher. This eliminates the need for external models or privileged conditioning, which were shown to limit the effectiveness of prior methods.
- **Efficient Knowledge Transfer:** The method effectively converts sparse outcome-based rewards from RLVR into dense, token-level supervision, offering a more granular signal for policy refinement. This improves sample efficiency, allowing models to achieve higher accuracy in fewer training steps.
- **Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across diverse tasks (mathematical reasoning, multi-domain STEM, code generation, agentic tasks) and various model scales (1.7B to 8B parameters) suggests that the underlying principle of trajectory extrapolation is robust and widely applicable in LLM post-training.
- **Computational Efficiency:** RISE achieves its performance gains with a modest increase in wall-time overhead (1.3–1.6x) and no additional sampling cost, making it a practical and resource-efficient enhancement to existing RLVR pipelines.

**Potential Impact:**
RISE's principle of leveraging a model's own training dynamics for self-supervision could have a broader impact on how LLMs are fine-tuned and continuously improved. It opens avenues for:
- Developing more robust and autonomous agents that can learn from sparse feedback in complex environments.
- Improving the scalability of fine-tuning processes by reducing reliance on costly human annotation or large external models.
- Extending this self-extrapolating teacher concept to other post-training paradigms, such as preference optimization, where the "future" preference model could guide token-level decisions, or multi-agent settings, where agents could recursively improve their strategies.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Reliance on Linear Trajectory Assumption:** RISE relies on the empirical observation that training trajectories are sufficiently low-dimensional and approximately linear for extrapolation to remain meaningful at moderate `β` values. While the `β`-decay schedule and EMA anchor provide empirical robustness, a principled method for detecting when extrapolation becomes unreliable or deviates significantly from the true optimal trajectory remains an open research question.
- **Inheritance of RLVR Biases:** RISE amplifies the direction provided by the RLVR reward signal. If the RLVR reward mechanism itself contains biases or is "hackable" (i.e., rewards can be achieved through non-desired behaviors), then extrapolation would amplify these spurious directions, potentially leading to undesired outcomes. Future work could explore integrating reward model uncertainty or multi-objective rewards to qualify the extrapolation process.
