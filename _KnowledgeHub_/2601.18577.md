# Self-Refining Video Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST, NYU, and NTU Singapore developed Self-Refining Video Sampling, an inference-time, training-free method that reinterprets flow matching as a denoising autoencoder to improve video realism. The approach yields over 73% human preference for motion quality and boosts robotics grasp success rates by 8.4-11.0% on benchmarks, making generated content more physically plausible.

## Problem

- Video generators often produce physically implausible, temporally incoherent, or spatially inconsistent outputs when depicting complex dynamics.
- Existing methods to improve realism, such as external verifiers or additional training, are computationally expensive, inefficient, or difficult to apply.
- Pre-trained generative models lack an inherent, efficient mechanism for self-correction or internal feedback to refine outputs.

## Method

- Reinterpreted the ==flow matching== objective as a generalized ==denoising== autoencoder (DAE) to enable inference-time self-refinement.
- Introduced Predict-and-Perturb (P&P) sampling, an iterative denoising and local resampling mechanism applied during the ODE solver process.
- Designed Uncertainty-aware P&P to selectively refine only spatio-temporal regions with high prediction variance, preventing artifacts in static backgrounds.

## Results

- Achieved over 73% human preference for motion quality and highest VBench scores on complex motion benchmarks, correcting physically implausible poses.
- Improved robotics grasp success rates by up to 11.0% and increased Robot-QA accuracy, outperforming computationally more expensive rejection sampling.
- Demonstrated an 84% human preference for physical commonsense and more accurate trajectories in free-fall experiments, showing better adherence to physical laws.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The flow matching objective can be reinterpreted as a denoising autoencoder, enabling self-refinement at inference time using the pre-trained model's internal signals.
> - Iterative Predict-and-Perturb steps effectively act as a pseudo-Gibbs sampler, driving video latents towards more physically realistic and coherent regions of the learned data distribution.
> - Uncertainty-aware refinement prevents over-saturation by selectively focusing corrections on dynamic or "uncertain" regions, preserving static background consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern video generative models, particularly those based on diffusion and flow matching, have demonstrated capabilities in generating video content. These models are increasingly viewed as foundational for early-stage world models, aiming to capture physical dynamics and causal structures. However, a significant challenge remains: current video generators frequently struggle with complex physical dynamics, resulting in outputs that lack physical realism, motion coherence, and temporal consistency. These inconsistencies limit their applicability in real-world scenarios, such as robot manipulation, where minor visual inaccuracies can lead to incorrect actions.

Prior attempts to address these limitations generally fall into two categories:
1. **External Models/Verifiers**: Approaches that employ external mechanisms, such as rejection sampling with verifiers, to filter or improve physically plausible outputs. These methods often suffer from inefficiency due to low acceptance rates and can be domain-specific, struggling with generalized temporal coherence and physical plausibility evaluation.
2. **Additional Training/Post-Training Strategies**: Methods that involve fine-tuning models on augmented datasets or incorporating reward models to capture fine-grained motion dynamics. These strategies typically demand substantial computational resources and high-quality, often domain-specific, external data, while still facing difficulties in accurately modeling complex motions.

This work positions itself as an alternative to these existing paradigms by proposing an inference-time, training-free self-refinement method. It aims to leverage the inherent knowledge within pre-trained video generators, which are trained on large-scale datasets and thus encode priors about realistic motion and structure. By reinterpreting the generator itself as a self-refiner, this research attempts to improve video quality without the need for external models, additional training, or extensive data curation, thereby offering a computationally less intensive and more generalizable solution within the current generative model landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Enhance Physical Realism and Motion Coherence**: Address the limitations of current video generators that struggle to model complex physical dynamics and often produce videos with inconsistencies or implausible motion. The goal is to generate videos that are more temporally coherent and physically plausible.
2. **Develop a Training-Free Self-Refinement Method**: Introduce a method that improves video generation quality at inference time, utilizing only the pre-trained video generator itself, without requiring external verifiers, additional training, or costly data augmentation.
3. **Mitigate Over-Refinement Artifacts**: Prevent artifacts such as over-saturation or simplification in static regions that can arise from iterative refinement, especially when combined with classifier-free guidance (CFG).

The motivation stems from several observations and challenges:
- **Inadequacies of Current Video Generators**: Despite advancements, video generators often fall short in complex scenarios, producing outputs that are not physically realistic. These failures hinder the deployment of video generation in critical applications like robotics, where accuracy is paramount.
- **Limitations of Existing Solutions**: Current approaches for improving physical realism are resource-intensive. External verifiers are inefficient and often domain-specific, while additional training requires significant computation and curated data. There is a need for a more efficient and broadly applicable solution.
- **Untapped Potential of Pre-trained Models**: Large-scale video generators already possess rich priors regarding realistic motion and structure due to their extensive training. The authors hypothesize that these learned priors can be leveraged internally to refine generated samples.
- **Lack of Internal Feedback in Video Generators**: Unlike large language models (LLMs) that can directly re-ingest and revise their outputs, video generators lack an explicit internal feedback mechanism for self-correction, particularly given the high-dimensionality and temporal coupling of video data. This motivates the development of a novel mechanism to enable internal self-critique and revision for video generation.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to make video generation more reliable and trustworthy for a wider range of applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed **Self-Refining Video Sampling**, is built upon reinterpreting the flow matching objective as a denoising autoencoder (DAE) and leveraging this property for iterative, inference-time refinement.

#### 3.1. Flow Matching as Denoising Autoencoder

The foundation of this method involves drawing a connection between flow matching and denoising autoencoders. The authors demonstrate that the flow matching objective, which trains a time-dependent vector field model to transform samples from a prior distribution to the data distribution, can be mathematically reformulated to align with the generalized DAE objective. In this interpretation, the flow matching model learns to denoise a corrupted input latent representation `z_t` back to a clean data sample `z_1` across various noise levels. This DAE perspective allows the model, at inference time, to perform a reconstruction at any given noise level `t`.

#### 3.2. Predict-and-Perturb (P&P)

Leveraging the DAE interpretation, the authors introduce Predict-and-Perturb (P&P), a training-free sampling method that enables iterative self-refinement within the inference loop.
- **Operators**: P&P defines two core operators:
 - **Predict (D_θ(z_t, t))**: This is the denoising step, where the flow matching model predicts the clean data `z_1` from a noisy latent `z_t` at a given noise level `t`.
 - **Perturb (R_ϵ(z, t))**: This is the corruption step, which adds Gaussian noise `ϵ` to a clean sample `z` to bring it to a specific noise level `t` via linear interpolation: `tz + (1 - t)ϵ`.
- **Iterative Refinement**: P&P iteratively refines the video latent `z_t` at a fixed noise level `t` through a pseudo-Gibbs sampling process. Each iteration consists of:
 1. **Predict**: The model predicts a clean endpoint `ˆz_1` from the current noisy latent `z_t`.
 2. **Perturb**: The predicted clean endpoint `ˆz_1` is then re-corrupted (perturbed) back to the *same* noise level `t` using new Gaussian noise `ϵ`. This results in a refined noisy latent `z_t`.
- **Mechanism**: This Predict-Perturb cycle steers the latent representation towards higher-density regions of the learned video distribution, which correspond to more temporally coherent and physically plausible videos. The local resampling performed by the Perturb step is noted to allow for exploratory moves, especially at early timesteps, which can mitigate "early lock-in" where initial decisions overly constrain subsequent temporal dynamics.
- **Integration**: P&P is designed to be plug-and-play, integrating into existing ODE solvers by replacing the current `z_t` with a refined `z_t` after a few (typically 2-3) P&P iterations. Empirically, applying P&P primarily at early noise levels (e.g., `t < 0.2`) is found to be sufficient for significant improvements in temporal coherence and physical plausibility.
- **Toy Experiment**: A 2D synthetic dataset (sine wave) was used to demonstrate that P&P generates samples closer to the data manifold compared to a standard Euler solver and that iterative P&P steps at a fixed timestep effectively pull predictions closer to the data manifold.

#### 3.3. Uncertainty-aware P&P

To address the issue of over-saturation or simplification in static regions when applying multiple P&P updates with classifier-free guidance (CFG), the authors propose Uncertainty-aware P&P.
- **Problem**: Repeated CFG updates can lead to guidance accumulation, particularly in static video regions that remain largely unchanged across P&P iterations, resulting in artifacts.
- **Solution**: This extension selectively refines only spatio-temporal regions where the model exhibits uncertainty, preserving stable regions.
- **Uncertainty Mask Creation**: An uncertainty map `U` is computed at each refinement step by measuring the L1 norm difference between the current predicted clean output `ˆz_1^(k)` and the previous one `ˆz_1^(k-1)`. Higher differences indicate higher uncertainty. This map is then thresholded with a confidence threshold `τ` (e.g., `τ = 0.25`) to create a binary mask `M`.
- **Selective Refinement**: The mask `M` is used to apply P&P only to the uncertain regions (where `M` is 1), while the confident regions (where `M` is 0) retain their state from the previous P&P iteration or base ODE step. This prevents artifacts in static areas while correcting inconsistencies in dynamic regions.
- **Efficiency**: The uncertainty mask is generated without additional Network Function Evaluations (NFEs) by reusing predictions computed during the P&P process.
- **Algorithm Integration**: The method is incorporated into the ODE solver, where the next latent `z_t_i+1` is a weighted sum of the P&P-refined latent and the previous latent, controlled by the uncertainty mask. This enables a targeted refinement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Self-Refining Video Sampling was evaluated across various benchmarks and tasks, demonstrating improvements in motion coherence, physical realism, spatial consistency, and certain visual reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Motion Coherence for Challenging Motions

- **Benchmarks**: Dynamic-bench (120 prompts designed for challenging motions, multi-object interactions, human motion, physics) and VideoJAM-bench. Evaluation included VBench metrics and human evaluation.
- **Qualitative Results**: The method produced videos with enhanced motions even for complex dynamics. For instance, it corrected duplicated arms and physically implausible poses in gymnastic motions, generating successful movements and realistic interactions.
- **Quantitative Results**:
 - **Human Evaluation**: On Dynamic-bench (Wan2.2 T2V), the method was preferred by over 70% of human evaluators for motion quality compared to the default sampler and FlowMo (a training-free guidance method). For text alignment, it was preferred by over 65% over the default sampler.
 - **Automated Metrics (VBench)**: Achieved higher scores on motion and consistency metrics compared to baselines, including NFE×2, CFG-Zero, and FlowMo.

#### 4.2. Physical Realism in Robotics Videos

- **Benchmarks**: PAI-Bench-G (174 robot-domain prompts, I2V generation). Evaluation used VQA questions (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) and grasp success rates (Gemini 3 Flash).
- **Qualitative Results**: Generated videos that showed realistic physical interactions with reduced artifacts. It corrected grasping failures and accurately followed instructions for object manipulation, such as moving a bowl onto a cloth.
- **Quantitative Results**:
 - **Grasp Success Rate**: Significantly improved grasp success rates, with a +11.0% increase on Cosmos-Predict-2.5 and +8.4% on Wan2.2-I2V compared to the base ODE sampler. It also outperformed a verifier-based rejection sampler (best-of-4).
 - **Robot-QA Accuracy**: Achieved the highest Robot-QA accuracy, indicating better prompt alignment.
 - **Computational Overhead**: Accomplished these improvements with only a moderate increase in inference time (e.g., 1.6× for Cosmos-Predict-2.5 and 1.5× for Wan2.2-I2V), significantly less than the 4.0× of the rejection sampler.

#### 4.3. Physics Alignment in the Wild

- **Benchmarks**: VideoPhy2 (action-centric, physics-related prompts), PhyWorldBench (kinematics and interaction dynamics), and PisaBench (free-fall I2V generation). Evaluated using Gemini 3 Flash and VBench.
- **Qualitative Results**: Generated videos that adhered more closely to physical laws, showing fewer visual hallucinations. For instance, it correctly depicted sand interactions and consistently generated realistic free-fall trajectories, in contrast to the base model's non-physical behaviors.
- **Quantitative Results**:
 - **Human Evaluation**: On VideoPhy2, the method was preferred by 84% of human evaluators for physical commonsense over the default sampler.
 - **Automated Metrics**: Outperformed baselines in physics commonsense (PC) metrics on both VideoPhy2 and PhyWorldBench. On PisaBench, it achieved more accurate trajectories in free-fall experiments (lower L2/CD distances, higher IoU).

#### 4.4. Improvement in Spatial Consistency

- **Benchmarks**: Custom experiments with 20 prompts involving large camera motions, evaluated by measuring visual distances between frames at revisited viewpoints after camera-pose-based warping (SSIM, L1, PSNR).
- **Qualitative Results**: Maintained stronger spatial consistency, preserving previously observed scene content even during large camera rotations, where default methods often produced inconsistent backgrounds.
- **Quantitative Results**: Demonstrated significantly improved SSIM, L1, and PSNR scores, indicating better spatial consistency.

#### 4.5. Application to Visual Reasoning

- **Tasks**: Graph traversal and maze solving from existing benchmarks.
- **Graph Traversal**: Showed a substantial increase in success rate (from 0.1 to 0.8) for graph traversal problems, attributed to reduced visual artifacts and improved temporal coherence.
- **Maze Solving**: Exhibited limited improvement for maze-solving tasks, with success rates remaining near zero. This suggests that the method, being a local search, is less effective for tasks requiring global planning or discrete semantic correctness that the generator fundamentally lacks.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Uncertainty-Aware Refinement**: Confirmed that the uncertainty-aware strategy mitigates over-saturation and simplification artifacts that arise from excessive P&P iterations without it.
- **Hyperparameters**: Demonstrated that hyperparameters (number of P&P iterations `K_f`, confidence threshold `τ`, P&P interval rate `α`) are robust across a range of settings. Applying P&P at earlier inference stages was found to be more effective for correcting motion errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a method for improving the physical realism and temporal coherence of videos generated by pre-trained video models. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Addressing a Core Limitation**: The method directly tackles a critical challenge in video generation: the struggle with complex physical dynamics and motion coherence. By providing a mechanism to refine these aspects, it contributes to the development of more robust and believable video outputs.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility**: Unlike many existing approaches that require external models, extensive re-training, or computationally expensive rejection sampling, this method operates entirely at inference time using the pre-trained generator itself. This makes it more resource-efficient and accessible, potentially broadening the practical application of advanced video generative models without incurring prohibitive computational costs.
- **Broad Applicability**: The approach is demonstrated to be effective across various state-of-the-art flow matching-based video generators (Wan, Cosmos) and different tasks (T2V, I2V), suggesting its generalizability. It also shows potential applicability to diffusion-based models, indicating a wider scope of impact.
- **Enhanced Reliability for Real-World Applications**: Improvements in physical plausibility, motion coherence, and spatial consistency are critical for applications such as robotics, simulation, and potentially other AI-driven systems that rely on accurate visual predictions. Generating more trustworthy videos could accelerate progress in these domains.
- **Novel Self-Correction Paradigm**: The concept of a generative model acting as its own self-refiner at inference time, particularly in the high-dimensional and temporally coupled video domain, represents a novel paradigm in generative AI. This extends ideas of self-refinement seen in LLMs to multimodal generation.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work highlights the potential of internal signals for self-improvement and points towards future research directions, such as combining local refinement with global search strategies, exploring specialized refinement models, and optimizing stochasticity control. This can foster further innovation in inference-time optimization for generative models.
- **Improved User Experience**: For end-users, this method leads to higher quality, more consistent, and visually appealing generated videos, enhancing the overall utility and creative potential of video generation tools.
