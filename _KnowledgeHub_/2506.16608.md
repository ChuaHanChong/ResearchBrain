# Distributions as Actions: A Unified Framework for Diverse Action Spaces

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Alberta and Amii developed the "Distributions-as-Actions" framework, which redefines an agent's output as continuous parameters of an action distribution, effectively unifying discrete, continuous, and hybrid action spaces into a continuous control problem. This approach, implemented as DA-AC with Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL), demonstrates improved training stability due to lower-variance policy gradients and achieves competitive or superior performance across a wide range of continuous, discrete, and hybrid control benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning algorithms are often specialized for either discrete or continuous action spaces, requiring different gradient estimators and architectures.
- Policy gradient methods, especially likelihood-ratio (LR) estimators, frequently suffer from high variance, hindering stable and efficient learning.
- A unified, low-variance policy gradient estimator akin to reparameterization tricks for discrete actions and stochastic policies has been lacking.

## Method

- The ==Distributions-as-Actions== MDP (**DA-MDP**) framework redefines the agent's action as the parameters of a probability distribution over primitive actions, treating action sampling as environmental stochasticity.
- The Distributions-as-Actions Policy Gradient (DA-PG) estimator, derived from applying DPG to the DA-MDP, offers lower variance than LR and RP estimators by operating in the continuous distribution parameter space.
- Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL) enhances critic training by updating at interpolated points between the policy's output and deterministic actions, improving gradient curvature and critic accuracy.

## Results

- DA-AC achieved competitive or superior performance against strong baselines (e.g., TD3, SAC, DQN) across continuous (MuJoCo, DMC), discrete (Gym, MinAtar), and hybrid (PAMDPs) control tasks.
- In high-dimensional discrete settings (discretized MuJoCo & DMC), DA-AC significantly outperformed baselines, with performance comparable to its original continuous action performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL) consistently improves DA-AC's performance across almost all environments, leading to more accurate critic value estimates and better policy optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Redefining the agent's output as continuous distribution parameters enables the unification of discrete, continuous, and hybrid action spaces into a single continuous control problem.
> - The DA-PG estimator rigorously generalizes DPG and theoretically achieves lower variance than both likelihood-ratio and reparameterization estimators, particularly for discrete and stochastic continuous policies.
> - Learning a critic over the continuous distribution parameter space with ICL provides more informative gradients, guiding the policy effectively towards optimal distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms are broadly categorized into value-based and policy-based methods. Value-based methods, such as Q-learning and its deep variants like DQN, are particularly suited for discrete action spaces where action values can be enumerated and compared. Policy-based methods, originating from the policy gradient theorem, are more commonly applied to continuous action spaces, though they can also operate in discrete settings.

A persistent challenge in the RL landscape is the inherent coupling between algorithm design and the specific structure of the action space. Different estimators and network architectures are often required for discrete versus continuous actions. For instance, likelihood-ratio (LR) estimators, while general, can suffer from high variance in discrete action spaces and often necessitate specific critic architectures. Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG) and reparameterization (RP) trick methods are primarily designed for continuous actions, offering lower variance but with specific applicability constraints. This divergence makes it difficult to develop unified RL algorithms capable of seamlessly handling diverse action types, including discrete, continuous, or hybrid action spaces.

Prior work has explored methods to reduce variance in policy gradients, such as baselines for LR estimators or the reparameterization trick for stochastic policies. Other approaches like expected policy gradient (EPG) aim for zero-variance gradients conditioned on a state but are often limited to low-dimensional discrete or specific continuous settings. For hybrid action spaces, existing methods typically involve ad-hoc adaptations or combinations of discrete and continuous algorithms.

This paper proposes a fundamental redefinition of the agent-environment boundary to address this fragmentation. Instead of the agent directly choosing an action or sampling it from an internally defined distribution, the agent now outputs the parameters of an action distribution, and the sampling process is considered part of the environment's stochasticity. This reframe aims to convert any action space (discrete, continuous, hybrid) into a continuous space of distribution parameters, enabling the application of continuous-action algorithms across a wider range of problems. This approach differentiates itself from existing methods by offering a more unified theoretical and algorithmic foundation, rather than modifying or combining existing estimators for different action types. It also connects to concurrent work on D-MDPs and dynamical system optimization, which explore similar shifts in the agent-environment boundary for theoretical analysis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

1. **Unification of RL Algorithms Across Diverse Action Spaces:** Current RL algorithms are often specialized for either discrete or continuous action spaces. This specialization necessitates different gradient estimators, critic architectures, and policy parameterizations. The authors aim to develop a single, generalized RL framework that can be applied consistently across discrete, continuous, and hybrid action domains without requiring significant architectural or algorithmic changes. This would simplify algorithm design and facilitate broader applicability.

2. **Addressing High Variance in Policy Gradient Estimators:** Policy gradient methods, particularly the likelihood-ratio (LR) estimator, often suffer from high variance, which can impede stable and efficient learning. While reparameterization (RP) tricks mitigate this in continuous settings, an unbiased, low-variance RP-style estimator for discrete actions has been lacking. The authors are motivated to develop a new policy gradient estimator that inherently offers lower variance, potentially improving learning stability and speed across different action types.

3. **Redefining the Agent-Environment Boundary:** The core motivation involves a conceptual shift in how the "action" of an RL agent is defined. Traditionally, the agent either deterministically chooses an action or samples an action from a policy distribution it maintains. The paper proposes to redefine the agent's action as the *parameters* of a probability distribution over primitive actions, rather than the primitive action itself. The subsequent sampling of a primitive action from this distribution is then considered part of the environment's stochasticity. This reparameterization transforms the problem into one with a continuous action space (the space of distribution parameters), regardless of the original action type.

4. **Enabling Deterministic Policy Gradient Methods for Stochastic Policies and Discrete Actions:** DPG methods are known for their low-variance gradients but are typically restricted to deterministic policies in continuous action spaces. By re-framing the agent's output as continuous distribution parameters, the authors aim to extend the benefits of DPG-like estimators (e.g., lower variance, differentiability through the critic) to stochastic policies and inherently discrete action problems, where DPG was previously inapplicable.

5. **Improving Critic Learning for Complex Input Spaces:** The proposed framework introduces new challenges for the critic, as it must now evaluate policies over continuous distribution parameters, which can be a higher-dimensional and more complex input space than primitive actions. This complexity could lead to increased bias if the critic is not accurately learned. A motivation is to develop specific strategies, such as Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL), to enhance the critic's ability to generalize and provide informative gradients in this new parameter space.

In summary, the motivation is to simplify and unify RL algorithm design, improve learning efficiency by reducing gradient variance, and expand the applicability of powerful policy gradient techniques by rethinking the fundamental interface between the agent and its environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around a novel conceptual framework and the development of algorithms derived from it.

#### 3.1. Distributions-as-Actions Framework (DA-MDP)

The central idea is to redefine the agent-environment boundary. In classical RL, the agent's policy $\pi_\theta(a|s)$ outputs or samples a primitive action $A$. In the proposed Distributions-as-Actions framework, the agent's policy $\bar{\pi}_\theta(s)$ directly outputs *distribution parameters* $U \in \mathcal{U}$ (e.g., mean and variance for a Gaussian, or probability vector for a categorical distribution). The actual primitive action $A$ is then sampled from a fixed, known function $f(\cdot|U)$ (e.g., $A \sim f(\cdot|U)$), and this sampling process is treated as part of the environment's stochastic transitions.

This redefinition transforms the original Markov Decision Process (MDP) $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, p, d_0, r, \gamma \rangle$ into a Distributions-as-Actions MDP (DA-MDP) $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{U}, \bar{p}, d_0, \bar{r}, \gamma \rangle$. In this DA-MDP:
- The action space is the continuous parameter space $\mathcal{U}$.
- The new transition function $\bar{p}(s'|s, u)$ is the expectation of the original transition over actions sampled from $f(\cdot|u)$: $\mathbb{E}_{A \sim f(\cdot|u)} [p(s'|s, A)]$.
- The new reward function $\bar{r}(s, u)$ is similarly the expectation of the original reward: $\mathbb{E}_{A \sim f(\cdot|u)} [r(s, A)]$.
The authors prove (Proposition 3.2) that the value functions in the DA-MDP are equivalent to the original MDP's value functions in expectation, specifically $\bar{v}_{\bar{\pi}}(s) = v_{\pi}(s)$ and $\bar{q}_{\bar{\pi}}(s, u) = \mathbb{E}_{A \sim f(\cdot|u)} [q_{\pi}(s, A)]$. The crucial aspect is that $\mathcal{U}$ is always continuous, even if the underlying primitive action space $\mathcal{A}$ is discrete, continuous, or hybrid.

#### 3.2. Distributions-as-Actions Policy Gradient (DA-PG)

With the DA-MDP formulation, the agent's policy $\bar{\pi}_\theta(s)$ becomes a deterministic mapping from states to distribution parameters $U$. This enables the direct application of the Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG) theorem to the DA-MDP. The resulting gradient estimator, DA-PG (Theorem 4.2), takes the form:
$\hat{\nabla}_{\text{DA-PG}}^\theta \hat{J}(\bar{\pi}_\theta; S_t) = \nabla_\theta \bar{\pi}_\theta(S_t)^\top \nabla_U \bar{Q}_w(S_t, U)|_{U=\bar{\pi}_\theta(S_t)}$
where $\bar{Q}_w$ is a learned critic approximating $\bar{q}_{\bar{\pi}_\theta}$. This form is mathematically identical to the DPG estimator, but with $\bar{\pi}_\theta$ outputting distribution parameters and $\bar{Q}_w$ evaluating expected returns over action distributions. The authors prove that DA-PG is a strict generalization of DPG (Proposition 4.3).

Crucially, the authors demonstrate theoretically that DA-PG results in lower variance compared to both the likelihood-ratio (LR) estimator (Proposition 4.4) and the reparameterization (RP) estimator (Proposition 4.5). This is because DA-PG can be viewed as the conditional expectation of both LR and RP estimators over action samples or noise variables, leveraging the law of total variance. This property holds even for stochastic policies and discrete action spaces, offering an unbiased RP-style estimator for discrete distributions. The paper also extends convergence analysis of DPG to DA-PG under linear function approximation (Theorem C.7 in Appendix C.3).

#### 3.3. Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL)

While DA-PG offers lower variance, the critic $\bar{Q}_w$ now needs to learn values over the potentially complex, continuous space of distribution parameters $\mathcal{U}$. This increased complexity can introduce bias if the critic is inaccurate. To address this, the authors introduce Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL).

ICL aims to improve the critic's ability to generalize and provide smoother gradients by leveraging the sampled primitive action $A_t$ from a given distribution $U_t$. Instead of updating $\bar{Q}_w(S_t, U_t)$ with the standard TD update, ICL trains the critic at an interpolated point $\hat{U}_t = \omega_t U_t + (1 - \omega_t) U_{A_t}$, where $\omega_t \sim \text{Uniform}$, and $U_{A_t}$ represents the deterministic distribution parameters corresponding to the sampled action $A_t$ (e.g., a one-hot vector for discrete actions, or mean=A and minimal variance for continuous actions). The motivation is that this interpolation encourages the critic to learn more informative curvature between the policy's current output and high-value deterministic actions, guiding the policy towards optimality. The authors acknowledge that this update is generally biased as it omits importance sampling weights.

#### 3.4. Distributions-as-Actions Actor-Critic (DA-AC)

The practical algorithm, DA-AC, integrates the DA-PG estimator and ICL into a Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3)-like architecture. TD3 is chosen as a strong DPG-based baseline for continuous control.
- The **actor** (policy network) $\bar{\pi}_\theta$ outputs the continuous distribution parameters $U$.
- Two **critics** $\bar{Q}_{w_1}, \bar{Q}_{w_2}$ are used to estimate the value of states and distribution parameters $\bar{Q}_w(S, U)$, similar to TD3's double Q-learning.
- The **actor update** uses the DA-PG estimator (Equation 10).
- The **critic update** incorporates ICL (Equation 13), using interpolated points $\hat{U}$ for training.
- Standard TD3 techniques like target networks (for critics only, as actor target network found not to improve performance) and delayed policy updates are retained.

This overall methodology provides a unified framework for diverse action spaces by converting all problems into continuous control tasks in the distribution parameter space, then applying DPG-like methods with specialized critic learning to this new formulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents both theoretical results and extensive empirical evaluations across various control settings.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Unification and Generalization:** The Distributions-as-Actions framework unifies discrete, continuous, and hybrid action spaces into a continuous parameter space ($\mathcal{U}$). The Distributions-as-Actions Policy Gradient (DA-PG) estimator is shown to be a strict generalization of the Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG).
- **Variance Reduction:** DA-PG is theoretically proven to have lower variance than both the likelihood-ratio (LR) policy gradient estimator and the reparameterization (RP) trick estimator. This is a significant finding, as it suggests a more stable learning process, especially in discrete and stochastic continuous settings where LR and RP estimators often exhibit higher variance. For discrete actions, DA-PG offers an unbiased, low-variance RP-style estimator.
- **Convergence Guarantee:** The paper extends the convergence analysis of DPG to DA-PG under linear function approximation, providing a theoretical guarantee for the proposed method in a simplified setting.

#### 4.2. Empirical Findings

The empirical evaluation of Distributions-as-Actions Actor-Critic (DA-AC) covers continuous, discrete, and hybrid control environments.

- **Continuous Control (MuJoCo & DeepMind Control Suite):**
 - DA-AC achieved competitive, and often superior, performance compared to baselines like TD3, RP-AC, SAC, and PPO.
 - In a comparison across 20 individual tasks, DA-AC performed better than TD3 in a majority of environments.
 - Aggregated results showed DA-AC outperforming most baselines, particularly on the DeepMind Control (DMC) Suite, especially in tasks with higher-dimensional action spaces (e.g., `quadruped-run`, `humanoid-run`).

- **Discrete Control (Gym Classic Control & MinAtar):**
 - DA-AC demonstrated competitive performance, often matching or exceeding strong baselines such as DQN, DSAC, ST-AC, LR-AC, and EAC. This indicates the framework's effectiveness even when the underlying action space is discrete.
 - DA-AC was among the best-performing algorithms in both classic control (e.g., CartPole) and MinAtar environments.

- **High-Dimensional Discrete Control (Discretized MuJoCo & DMC):**
 - By discretizing continuous action spaces into high-dimensional discrete spaces, the authors tested the scalability of DA-AC in such settings.
 - DA-AC achieved higher average performance than all baselines (ST-AC, LR-AC, PPO) in both discretized MuJoCo and DMC environments.
 - Notably, DA-AC's performance in these high-dimensional discrete settings was comparable to its performance in the original continuous action settings, suggesting robustness to the action space conversion.

- **Hybrid Control (Parameterized-Action MDPs - PAMDPs):**
 - DA-AC demonstrated competitive or superior performance against specialized baselines for hybrid control, including PATD3, PDQN, and HHQN, across 7 PAMDP environments. This highlights the unified nature of the framework, handling combined discrete and continuous actions effectively.

- **Effectiveness of Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL):**
 - An ablation study comparing DA-AC with and without ICL consistently showed that ICL significantly improves performance across almost all tested settings (continuous, discrete, high-dimensional discrete, and hybrid control).
 - Visualizations in bandit settings illustrated that ICL leads to a critic landscape with more informative curvature and more accurate value estimates, particularly at deterministic distribution parameters. This improved critic learning facilitates better policy optimization, preventing the policy from getting stuck in suboptimal regions.
 - Paired t-tests statistically confirmed the positive mean improvement of DA-AC with ICL across various settings.

Overall, the findings indicate that the Distributions-as-Actions framework, coupled with DA-PG and ICL, provides a robust and unified approach to RL across diverse action spaces, offering theoretical benefits in variance reduction and empirical competitiveness against specialized algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Distributions-as-Actions" framework represents a significant conceptual shift in reinforcement learning, with several potential impacts:

1. **Algorithmic Unification and Simplification:** By redefining the agent-environment boundary such that the agent always outputs continuous distribution parameters, the framework fundamentally transforms diverse action spaces (discrete, continuous, hybrid) into a unified continuous control problem. This simplifies algorithm design, allowing a single, continuous-action RL algorithm (like DA-AC, based on TD3) to be applied across a much broader range of tasks without ad-hoc modifications or specialized estimators. This could lead to more general-purpose RL agents.

2. **Improved Training Stability and Efficiency:** The theoretical demonstration that DA-PG offers lower variance than traditional likelihood-ratio and reparameterization estimators is crucial. Lower variance gradients typically lead to more stable and faster learning. This benefit is particularly notable for discrete and stochastic continuous settings, where high variance often hampers performance.

3. **Expanded Applicability of DPG-like Methods:** Deterministic Policy Gradient methods are powerful but traditionally limited to deterministic policies in continuous action spaces. This framework enables the application of DPG's low-variance properties to a wider class of problems, including those with stochastic policies and discrete action spaces, previously only amenable to higher-variance methods.

4. **Foundation for Future Research Directions:**
 - **Model-Based RL:** The framework opens avenues for model-based RL by allowing models to be learned over continuous distribution parameters, rather than disparate primitive actions. This could lead to more generalized planning and prediction.
 - **Hierarchical Control and Novel Hybrid Approaches:** The unified continuous parameter space might simplify the integration of hierarchical control strategies or more sophisticated hybrid algorithms that leverage properties of the distribution parameters.
 - **Advanced Critic Learning:** The challenges introduced in learning critics over distribution parameters highlight a new area for research, potentially leading to more sophisticated off-distribution updates, importance weighting schemes, or methods that integrate classical action-value functions to guide the distribution-parameter critic.
 - **Theoretical Development:** The connection to D-MDPs and dynamical system optimization suggests further theoretical exploration into the properties and equivalences within this reframed agent-environment interaction.

5. **Benchmarking and Reproducibility:** The empirical success across various benchmarks (MuJoCo, DMC, Gym, MinAtar, PAMDPs), coupled with open-source code and detailed hyperparameters, provides a robust baseline and promotes reproducibility in future RL research on unified frameworks.

Overall, the "Distributions-as-Actions" framework provides a novel and robust lens through which to approach the problem of diverse action spaces in reinforcement learning. Its conceptual simplicity, coupled with theoretical and empirical advantages, positions it as a significant contribution that could influence the design of future, more general and efficient RL algorithms.
