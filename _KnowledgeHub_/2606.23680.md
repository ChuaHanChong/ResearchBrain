# CoorDex: Coordinating Body and Hand Priors for Continuous Dexterous Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CoorDex facilitates continuous dexterous loco-manipulation for humanoids by coordinating separate latent priors for whole-body motion and multi-finger hand control within a residual reinforcement learning framework. This approach achieved successful dynamic grasping and object interaction while the robot was in continuous motion, outperforming direct joint-space control or monolithic latent policies on complex tasks.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid loco-manipulation systems often simplify complex tasks into "stop-and-go" processes, where the robot pauses walking to perform manipulation.
- Manipulation frequently relies on low-DoF end effectors, limiting the dexterity required for complex object interaction in dynamic environments.
- Coordinating high-dimensional whole-body balance, expressive locomotion, and intricate multi-finger dexterous hand movements simultaneously presents a significant control complexity challenge.

## Method

- A modular learning pipeline constructs specialized, proprioception-conditioned latent priors for the humanoid body (29 DoF) and the dexterous hand (20 DoF) via distillation from motion tracking teachers.
- A "==wrist-stabilized==" hand prior is trained to control only finger motion, preventing its latent space from allocating capacity to 6D wrist placement, which is instead managed by the body prior.
- A coordinated latent residual policy uses a shared "coordination trunk" and separate residual heads to predict compact latent corrections, enabling downstream reinforcement learning to jointly adapt and refine the pre-learned priors for continuous loco-manipulation.

## Results

- CoorDex achieved success rates of **0.55** for WALKGRAB (dynamic bottle grasping), **0.66** for OPENFRIDGE (opening a fridge door while moving), and **0.89** for WALKPICKTURN (picking up a cube and completing a 180° turn).
- On the challenging WALKGRAB task, CoorDex attained a **0.55** success rate with continuous forward motion (average **0.25** m/s) and no falls, whereas direct joint-space control and body-prior-only variants achieved **0%** success.
- The coordinated latent residual policy demonstrated smoother whole-body motion (action rate of **0.22**) and more reliable grasping compared to a monolithic latent residual variant, which failed on WALKGRAB with a higher action rate of **0.40**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing and independently training motion priors for the body and a wrist-stabilized hand significantly reduces the exploration complexity for high-DoF continuous loco-manipulation tasks.
> - Utilizing latent action spaces for both body and hand control is crucial for trainability, as direct joint-space control and body-prior-only variants failed completely on complex dynamic tasks like WALKGRAB.
> - A coordinated policy architecture with a shared task-level trunk and separate body/hand residual heads yields smoother whole-body motion and more reliable grasping compared to a monolithic latent residual policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots equipped with high degree-of-freedom (DoF) dexterous hands present a potential for navigating and interacting with human environments. However, realizing this potential involves challenges in coordinating locomotion and manipulation simultaneously. Existing approaches to humanoid loco-manipulation frequently simplify tasks into a "stop-and-go" process, where the robot pauses walking to perform manipulation, and often rely on low-DoF end effectors that function as simple grasp primitives. This simplification limits the fluidity and efficiency of robotic interaction in dynamic environments.

Recent advancements have progressed various components of humanoid control. Whole-body tracking methods have enabled stable locomotion and expressive upper-body movements in simulated and real robots, as evidenced by works like DeepMimic, BeyondMimic, Sonic, and Hover. Concurrently, physics-based motion priors, such as AMP, ASE, CALM, PULSE, and SLMP, have introduced structured latent action spaces, which can reduce the complexity of the reinforcement learning exploration problem.

In the domain of dexterous hand manipulation, research has focused on grasp synthesis, hand-object representation, and data generation for multi-finger grasps. Datasets and methods like DexGraspNet, GenDexGrasp, ManipTrans, DexMimicGen, and Jacta have contributed to generating diverse grasps and transferring manipulation skills. However, these methods commonly operate under the assumption that wrist or arm trajectories are externally provided, often from teleoperation, planning, or a stationary whole-body controller. Consequently, the challenge of coordinating complex finger-level interactions while the humanoid body is in continuous motion, where wrist placement dynamically emerges from whole-body movements, remains less explored.

This work integrates and extends these lines of research by addressing the specific gap of continuous, high-DoF dexterous loco-manipulation. While previous humanoid loco-manipulation systems have explored aspects such as optimization, residual learning, skill composition, force-adaptive policies, and vision-based control (e.g., ResMimic, Demohlm, SkillBlender, Falcon, ULC, VIRAL, WholeBodyVLA), they generally do not focus on the intricacies of multi-finger contact during ongoing locomotion. CoorDex aims to provide a framework for enabling such integrated control, distinguishing itself by explicitly decomposing and coordinating body-side wrist placement with hand-side finger motion. It builds upon established principles of motion tracking and latent prior distillation but applies them in a novel architecture designed to facilitate simultaneous, high-dimensional body and hand control during dynamic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that the full potential of humanoid robots with high-DoF dexterous hands is constrained by conventional control paradigms. Current humanoid loco-manipulation often simplifies complex tasks by segmenting them into distinct phases: the robot walks to an object, stops to manipulate it, and then resumes locomotion. Furthermore, manipulation is frequently limited by the use of low-DoF end effectors that function as basic open-close grasping mechanisms. This piecewise approach and limited dexterity hinder humanoids from seamlessly operating in dynamic human environments where continuous interaction and precise object handling are often required.

The specific challenges that motivate this work include:
- **High-Dimensional Control Complexity:** Simultaneously learning to control a humanoid's whole-body balance, expressive locomotion, and intricate multi-finger dexterous hand movements presents a high-dimensional exploration problem for reinforcement learning.
- **Dynamic Coordination:** Performing dexterous manipulation while in motion demands continuous coordination. The robot must maintain balance, position its hand and wrist optimally relative to a moving or fixed object, execute a precise grasp without disturbing the object, and then transport the object while managing the dynamic perturbations introduced by hand-object interactions.
- **Wrist Placement in Motion:** In a continuously walking humanoid, the wrist's position and orientation are dynamic outputs of the entire body's motion, influenced by foot timing, root motion, and torso posture. Existing dexterous hand control methods often bypass this by assuming a pre-defined or stationary wrist trajectory. Consequently, a hand prior that also attempts to account for 6D wrist motion may dedicate much of its capacity to gross body-side placement rather than focusing on fine finger coordination.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of CoorDex are:
1. **Enable Continuous Dexterous Loco-Manipulation:** To develop a learning pipeline that allows humanoids with high-DoF dexterous hands to perform manipulation tasks while actively walking or moving, rather than through a series of discrete stop-and-go phases.
2. **Decompose and Coordinate Body and Hand Control:** To address the high-dimensional control problem by separating the complexities of whole-body motion (which dictates wrist placement) from the intricacies of finger coordination. This involves modeling them with distinct, specialized priors.
3. **Facilitate Learning Through Latent Residual Control:** To convert high-dimensional body and dexterous hand control into a more tractable "coordinated latent residual control" framework. This approach aims to reduce the effective action space for downstream reinforcement learning by building upon pre-learned motion priors.
4. **Preserve Natural Whole-Body Motion and Improve Contact Reliability:** To ensure that the integrated control system maintains natural and stable whole-body motion while simultaneously improving the reliability of finger-level contact and grasping during dynamic tasks.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to advance the capabilities of humanoid robots towards more fluid, efficient, and human-like interaction with their surroundings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoorDex is a modular learning pipeline designed to enable continuous, high-DoF dexterous humanoid loco-manipulation through coordinated latent residual control. The methodology comprises three main stages: prior construction, coordinated latent residual policy design, and residual reinforcement learning training.

#### 3.1. Prior Construction

The pipeline begins by constructing two separate motion priors: one for the humanoid body and another for the dexterous hand. Both priors follow a similar distillation pipeline from motion tracking teachers.

- **Demonstration Collection:** Task-specific reference motions are collected in a simulated environment (Isaac Lab) using a teleoperation pipeline. Lower-body locomotion is generated by an AGILE-based controller, while a human operator provides right wrist and hand motion via an Apple Vision Pro and CloudXR interface. The human wrist pose serves as an end-effector target for an inverse-kinematics solver, and human hand motion is retargeted to the target dexterous hand using optimization-based dex-retargeting. These generated whole-body trajectories serve as reference motions for training the tracking teachers.

- **Humanoid Body Prior:** A whole-body motion tracking teacher, denoted $\pi_b^T$, is trained to track these reference motions. This teacher, based on designs like BeyondMimic, takes body proprioception and a reference goal as input and outputs joint position targets for the 29 body joints of the Unitree G1 humanoid. The reference trajectories are preprocessed to match the specific morphology and kinematic structure of the robot with its dexterous hands.

- **Dexterous Hand Prior:** A privileged hand tracking teacher, $\pi_h^T$, is trained in a "floating-hand" environment. This teacher uses ManipTrans-style retargeted hand-object motions. It observes hand proprioception and a reference goal, outputting joint position targets specifically for the 20 active finger joints of the WUJI hand. Crucially, during this training, the reference wrist pose and velocity are directly written into the simulation, meaning the teacher and the resulting prior control only finger motion. This "wrist-stabilized" design prevents the hand's latent space from allocating capacity to 6D wrist motion, allowing it to focus on finger coordination.

- **Distillation:** After training, each tracking teacher is distilled into a proprioception-conditioned latent prior, conceptually following the PULSE framework. For each subsystem (body or hand), the student network consists of an encoder $E_x$, a proprioceptive prior $R_x$, and a decoder $D_x$. The training objective combines three loss components: teacher-action reconstruction (minimizing the difference between decoded student action and teacher action), temporal smoothness of the encoder's latent means, and KL regularization between the encoder's posterior distribution and the proprioceptive prior. Following distillation, the prior networks ($R_x$) and decoders ($D_x$) are frozen, providing a default latent command ($\mu_{x,p_t}$) for subsequent residual reinforcement learning.

#### 3.2. Coordinated Latent Residual Policy

With the body and hand priors frozen, a downstream residual PPO policy learns to coordinate and adapt them for specific loco-manipulation tasks.

- **Latent Residual Action Space:** Instead of directly predicting joint-space targets, the actor predicts residual corrections in the two compact latent spaces: $\Delta z_b$ for the body and $\Delta z_h$ for the hand.
- **Policy Architecture:** The policy employs a coordinated latent residual structure:
 - At each control step, the proprioception-conditioned prior means ($\mu_{b,p_t}$, $\mu_{h,p_t}$) are obtained from the frozen prior networks.
 - A shared **coordination trunk** ($f_{coord}$) processes a comprehensive input, including body and hand proprioception, task-level state (e.g., object pose, goal, projected gravity, contact features), hand-object state (e.g., object pose in hand frame, fingertip-object contact), the prior means, and the previous latent residual. This trunk generates a shared task-level coordination feature.
 - Two separate **residual heads** ($f_b$ and $f_h$) then predict the body and hand latent corrections, respectively. These heads take the coordination feature, their respective proprioception, and prior means as input.
 - The predicted residuals are added to the corresponding prior means to form the corrected latent commands ($\tilde{z}_b$, $\tilde{z}_h$).
 - Finally, the frozen decoders ($D_b$, $D_h$) map these corrected latents, along with proprioception, to the final joint position targets ($a_b$, $a_h$). The body decoder outputs targets for all 29 body joints, while the hand decoder outputs targets for the 20 active finger joints.
- **Execution:** These joint position targets are then sent to a low-level PD controller for execution on the Unitree G1 humanoid equipped with a 20-DoF WUJI hand. This architecture maintains low-dimensional exploration while preserving separate control authority for locomotion and grasping, with the shared trunk facilitating task-aware coordination.

#### 3.3. Residual RL and Environment Design

Policies are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) while the body and hand priors remain frozen.

- **Observation Space:** The policy's observation includes body proprioception, hand proprioception, task state, object-relative geometry, fingertip contact features, the prior means, and the previous latent residual.
- **Reward Function:** The reward structure combines terms for locomotion and balance, palm-object reaching and alignment, fingertip contact and sustained contact, and task completion (e.g., lift height, carry distance, door-angle progress). Regularization terms penalize large latent residuals, abrupt changes in decoded actions, and high joint velocities.
- **Task Design:**
 - For shorter interaction tasks (e.g., WALKGRAB, OPENFRIDGE), a single-stage reward without reference state initialization is used. Phase transitions are handled by predicate gating within the reward terms.
 - For longer-horizon tasks (e.g., WALKPICKTURN), a lightweight staged reward mechanism is employed. Additionally, a "Demonstration-Free Reference State Initialization" (NoDemoRSI) curriculum is used. NoDemoRSI allows the policy to reset episodes from states it has itself reached in later stages of the task, thereby providing exploration support without requiring expert demonstrations. This curriculum helps overcome sparse exploration bottlenecks in multi-stage tasks.
- **Hardware and Simulation:** All experiments are conducted in Isaac Lab, utilizing a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid with a 20-DoF WUJI dexterous hand. The system operates at a control rate of 60 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated CoorDex across three dexterous humanoid loco-manipulation tasks: WALKGRAB (grasping and carrying a bottle while walking), OPENFRIDGE (opening a fridge door while stepping backward), and WALKPICKTURN (picking up a cube and completing a 180° turn while retaining the object). The evaluation focused on task success, fall rate, drop rate, and task-specific progress metrics over 50,000 episodes in 10,000 parallel simulation environments.

#### 4.1. Task Performance Across Skills (Q1)

CoorDex demonstrated the ability of its unified body-hand latent residual interface to support diverse loco-manipulation skills:
- **WALKGRAB:** Achieved a success rate of 0.55, with no falls. This task was identified as the most challenging due to the requirement for dynamic grasping during continuous whole-body motion.
- **OPENFRIDGE:** Achieved a success rate of 0.66, with no falls, opening the door to an average angle of 57.76° out of a target 60°. This task involved sustained articulated-object interaction.
- **WALKPICKTURN:** Achieved a success rate of 0.89 with a fall rate of 0.01, and a minimum heading error of 9.98°. This longer-horizon task was facilitated by NoDemoRSI, which provides exploration support by resetting episodes from policy-discovered states.

These results indicate that the CoorDex framework is applicable across tasks with varying interaction structures and difficulties, supporting the use of a unified latent residual interface.

#### 4.2. Action-Space Variants on WALKGRAB (Q2)

To assess the necessity of latent actions for improved exploration, CoorDex was compared against two variants on the WALKGRAB task under identical environment and reward settings:
- **All Joint Space:** This variant removed both latent priors, requiring the PPO policy to directly predict targets in the full body and hand joint space. It achieved a 0% success rate. The policy often resorted to unstable whole-body motions to avoid falling, and never successfully grasped the bottle. It exhibited a high "stop" rate (0.86) and a 0.04 fall rate.
- **Body Prior + Hand Joint Space:** This variant retained the body prior but controlled the hand directly in joint space. It also achieved a 0% success rate. While the body prior enabled the humanoid to walk towards the bottle and position its wrist, the policy struggled with high-DoF finger coordination. It frequently learned to slow down or stop (stop rate 0.90) near the bottle, attempting to solve it as a stationary grasping task, indicating that the body prior alone was insufficient for continuous dexterous loco-manipulation.

In contrast, CoorDex, using both body and hand latent priors, achieved a 0.55 success rate, a 0.55 grasp rate, and a 0% stop or fall rate. Analysis of the velocity profile showed that CoorDex maintained a forward velocity of approximately 0.25 m/s near the bottle, confirming that it performed non-stop grasping. This demonstrates that the latent-prior action interface for both the body and the hand is crucial for making high-dimensional, contact-rich tasks like continuous dexterous loco-manipulation trainable within a reinforcement learning framework.

#### 4.3. Coordinated Residual Prediction (Q3)

The importance of the policy's architecture for coordinated control was investigated by comparing CoorDex with a **Monolithic Latent Residual** variant on WALKGRAB. Both methods utilized the same frozen body and hand priors and shared the same latent dimensions and RL environment. The difference lay in the policy structure: Monolithic Latent Residual used a single Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict a concatenated latent residual vector, while CoorDex employed a shared coordination trunk followed by separate body and hand residual heads.
- **Monolithic Latent Residual:** Achieved a 0% success rate. Although it could approach the bottle, its body motion was less natural and more jittery, evidenced by a higher action rate of 0.40 compared to CoorDex. This lack of smoothness in whole-body motion likely contributed to its inability to reliably grasp the bottle.
- **CoorDex:** Achieved a 0.55 success rate and a lower action rate of 0.22, indicating smoother and more natural body motion.

These findings suggest that the coordinated actor architecture, with its shared trunk for task context and separate heads for body and hand residual predictions, is beneficial. It enables the policy to reason about the overall task state while maintaining distinct control pathways for body adaptation (stepping, torso motion, wrist placement) and finger adaptation (preshape, closure, contact refinement). This structural separation contributes to more reliable grasping and smoother whole-body motion in contact-rich tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoorDex addresses a fundamental challenge in humanoid robotics by enabling continuous, high-DoF dexterous loco-manipulation. Rather than treating locomotion and manipulation as sequential, independent phases, this work demonstrates a method for integrating them as coupled behaviors. The structured latent action space, derived from specialized body and hand priors, allows downstream reinforcement learning to jointly adapt whole-body movements, wrist placement, and fine-grained finger-level contact, which is a departure from common "stop-and-go" or low-DoF manipulation paradigms.

The significance of this research includes:
- **Advancing Humanoid Dexterity:** CoorDex moves beyond simplified manipulation by showcasing the ability to perform complex multi-finger grasps during continuous motion across a range of tasks, including bottle grasping, fridge opening, and cube manipulation.
- **Efficient Learning in High-Dimensional Spaces:** By converting high-dimensional joint control into coordinated latent residual control, the framework significantly reduces the effective action space for reinforcement learning. This approach makes previously intractable contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks trainable.
- **Principled Body-Hand Coordination:** The methodology proposes a structured way to decompose and coordinate whole-body mobility with dexterous hand control. Separating the body prior (for locomotion and wrist placement) from the wrist-stabilized hand prior (for finger coordination) allows each prior to specialize, while the coordinated latent residual policy ensures their effective coupling.
- **Modular and Adaptable Design:** The factorized body-hand prior design suggests a potential for adaptability to different hand morphologies, as qualitatively demonstrated in the appendix with the Dex3-1 hand. This modularity could streamline the deployment of new dexterous hands on existing humanoid platforms.

The potential impact of CoorDex extends to various applications where humanoids require fluid and versatile interaction:
- **Service Robotics:** Humanoids could perform household chores more seamlessly, such as opening doors while carrying items or continuously retrieving objects without pausing.
- **Logistics and Manufacturing:** Robots could efficiently handle diverse objects on the move, improving throughput and flexibility in dynamic environments.
- **Exploration and Rescue:** Humanoids might navigate complex terrains while simultaneously manipulating tools or samples, enhancing their utility in hazardous or unstructured environments.

However, the research also acknowledges several limitations. The current policies rely on privileged state observations (e.g., object poses, contact signals), indicating that the perceptual component and visual sim-to-real transfer remain future challenges. The experiments were conducted on a fixed Unitree G1 platform with a WUJI hand, and broader applicability would require evaluation across a wider range of objects, hands, and hardware configurations. Furthermore, longer-horizon tasks still necessitate task-specific exploration support like NoDemoRSI.

Future work aims to integrate the latent residual interface with perception capabilities, develop automatic curricula for more generalized task learning, and expand the scope of task-conditioned priors. This research contributes a foundational step towards enabling humanoids to perform truly continuous, dexterous loco-manipulation, treating whole-body mobility and dexterous contact as integral and coupled behaviors.
