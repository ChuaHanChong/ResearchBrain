# CoorDex: Coordinating Body and Hand Priors for Continuous Dexterous Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CoorDex facilitates continuous dexterous loco-manipulation for humanoids by coordinating separate latent priors for whole-body motion and multi-finger hand control within a residual reinforcement learning framework. This approach achieved successful dynamic grasping and object interaction while the robot was in continuous motion, outperforming direct joint-space control or monolithic latent policies on complex tasks.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid loco-manipulation systems often simplify complex tasks into "stop-and-go" processes, where the robot pauses walking to perform manipulation.
- Manipulation frequently relies on low-DoF end effectors, limiting the dexterity required for complex object interaction in dynamic environments.
- Coordinating high-dimensional whole-body balance, expressive locomotion, and intricate multi-finger dexterous hand movements simultaneously presents a significant control complexity challenge.

## Method

- A modular learning pipeline constructs specialized, proprioception-conditioned latent priors for the humanoid body (29 DoF) and the dexterous hand (20 DoF) via distillation from motion tracking teachers.
- A "==wrist-stabilized==" hand prior is trained to control only finger motion, preventing its latent space from allocating capacity to 6D wrist placement, which is instead managed by the body prior.
- A coordinated latent residual policy uses a shared "coordination trunk" and separate residual heads to predict compact latent corrections, enabling downstream reinforcement learning to jointly adapt and refine the pre-learned priors for continuous loco-manipulation.

## Results

- CoorDex achieved success rates of **0.55** for WALKGRAB (dynamic bottle grasping), **0.66** for OPENFRIDGE (opening a fridge door while moving), and **0.89** for WALKPICKTURN (picking up a cube and completing a 180° turn).
- On the challenging WALKGRAB task, CoorDex attained a **0.55** success rate with continuous forward motion (average **0.25** m/s) and no falls, whereas direct joint-space control and body-prior-only variants achieved **0%** success.
- The coordinated latent residual policy demonstrated smoother whole-body motion (action rate of **0.22**) and more reliable grasping compared to a monolithic latent residual variant, which failed on WALKGRAB with a higher action rate of **0.40**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing and independently training motion priors for the body and a wrist-stabilized hand significantly reduces the exploration complexity for high-DoF continuous loco-manipulation tasks.
> - Utilizing latent action spaces for both body and hand control is crucial for trainability, as direct joint-space control and body-prior-only variants failed completely on complex dynamic tasks like WALKGRAB.
> - A coordinated policy architecture with a shared task-level trunk and separate body/hand residual heads yields smoother whole-body motion and more reliable grasping compared to a monolithic latent residual policy.
