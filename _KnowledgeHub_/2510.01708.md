# PolySim: Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Humanoid Control via Multi-Simulator Dynamics Randomization

> [!summary] Summary
> PolySim introduces a framework for training humanoid control policies by simultaneously leveraging multiple heterogeneous physics simulators, effectively addressing the sim-to-real gap through dynamics-level randomization. This approach enables policies to generalize robustly to unseen simulator dynamics and facilitates zero-shot deployment on physical robots, increasing success rates by up to 52.8% on an unseen MuJoCo domain compared to single-simulator baselines.

## Problem

- Policies trained in a single simulator often overfit to its specific dynamics and inductive biases, leading to a significant "sim-to-real gap" when deployed on real robots.
- Existing domain randomization methods are limited to perturbing parameters within a single simulator's fixed physics model, failing to address fundamental model-class mismatches.
- Training complex, high-dimensional humanoid whole-body control policies directly on physical robots is impractical due to safety concerns, cost, and data collection difficulties.

## Method

- **PolySim** trains a single reinforcement learning policy concurrently across multiple heterogeneous physics simulators to achieve dynamics-level domain randomization.
- A distributed client-server architecture isolates the `TrainClient` from multiple `SimServers`, enabling elastic distributed execution and fault isolation.
- A `==Simulator Router==` harmonizes diverse simulator characteristics, providing a unified API through physics harmonization, API translation, and numerical normalization.
- A GPU-direct communication pipeline, using PyTorch RPC over NCCL, ensures high-throughput, low-latency data exchange between components.

## Results

- PolySim achieved superior zero-shot generalization to an unseen MuJoCo simulator, increasing success rates by **52.8%** over the IsaacSim baseline and **44.3%** over the IsaacGym baseline.
- Policies trained with PolySim demonstrated enhanced in-distribution robustness and precision, reducing global body position tracking error and joint position error on seen simulators while maintaining high success rates.
- The framework proved experimentally efficient, maintaining high training throughput with only a marginal overhead of +**0.1** to +**0.3** seconds per iteration compared to the slowest single simulator.
- Policies trained using PolySim were successfully deployed zero-shot onto a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, confirming its effectiveness in bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex whole-body motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training across multiple simulators effectively randomizes the underlying dynamics models themselves, rather than just their parameters, directly mitigating simulator inductive bias.
> - Theoretically, PolySim's mixed transition kernel is strictly closer (in Wasserstein distance) to the real-world dynamics than any individual simulator's kernel, leading to a lower sim-to-real gap.
> - Multi-simulator training offers synergistic benefits, improving generalization to unseen domains and enhancing in-distribution robustness and precision as a regularization effect, rather than just additive gains.
> - PolySim's parallel training avoids the "catastrophic forgetting" issues observed in sequential multi-simulator training approaches.
