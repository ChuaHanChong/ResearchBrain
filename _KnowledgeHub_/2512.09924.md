# ReViSE: Towards Reason-Informed Video Editing in Unified Models with Self-Reflective Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ReViSE framework from HKUST, Zhejiang University, Fudan University, and Tongyi Lab enables video models to perform edits requiring implicit reasoning by introducing a self-reflective learning paradigm. It achieved a 32% improvement in the "Overall score" on its new RVE-Bench for reason-informed editing and demonstrated robust performance on conventional video editing tasks.

## Problem

- Existing instruction-guided video editing models struggle with "reason-informed" editing, which demands deeper understanding of physical plausibility, causal dynamics, and common sense.
- Current video editing datasets primarily focus on literal transformations, lacking the complexity needed to train and evaluate models for reasoning-aware tasks.
- A disconnect exists between a model's internal Vision-Language Model (VLM) for understanding and its generative components, preventing effective transfer of semantic and world knowledge.

## Method

- The paper introduces the ==Reason-Informed Video Editing (RVE)== task and constructs ==RVE-Bench==, a new benchmark and training dataset created by re-captioning existing data with reasoning prompts and generating from movie data.
- It proposes ReViSE, a ==Self-Reflective Reasoning framework== built on a unified model architecture that leverages pre-trained VLMs and generative diffusion models.
- ReViSE employs its ==internal VLM as a critic==, providing intrinsic, QA-based feedback to refine the generator's reasoning behavior through ==Unified Semantic Optimization (USO)==.

## Results

- ReViSE achieved a **32%** improvement in the "Overall score" on the RVE-Bench's "Reasoning-Informed Video Editing" subset, with a **38%** improvement specifically for temporal reasoning.
- It consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines across all reasoning categories (Causal, Temporal, Spatial, Commonsense) on RVE-Bench and demonstrated generality on conventional Ditto-1M data with a **36.7%** gain in Overall score.
- Ablation studies confirmed the superiority of the Unified Semantic Optimization (USO) approach and validated the internal VLM's reliability as a critic, showing **74.6%** decision agreement with GPT-4o.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating an internal VLM as a self-reflective critic effectively bridges the gap between understanding and generation in video editing, eliminating the need for expensive external reward models.
> - The Unified Semantic Optimization (USO) objective, which combines flow matching loss with a distinct reasoning loss, is crucial for balancing generative fidelity with high-level semantic alignment.
> - Reason-informed video editing necessitates explicit evaluation metrics beyond pixel-level accuracy, leading to the development of a GPT-4o-based framework assessing semantic consistency and perceptual quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the rapidly evolving field of instruction-guided video editing and unified generative models. Recent advancements in this domain, particularly with diffusion-based models, have enabled high-quality, temporally consistent video synthesis and editing based on textual instructions. However, the existing approaches primarily focus on "literal editing," where the desired visual transformation is explicitly specified in the text prompt (e.g., "remove the boat").

This research identifies a significant gap: the inability of current models to perform "reason-informed" transformations. These transformations require inferring the intended outcome based on physical laws, temporal dynamics, world knowledge, or causal relationships, even when the instruction is underspecified (e.g., "Imagine the scene an hour after the boat departed"). Commercial state-of-the-art systems, such as Runway Gen-4 Aleph, reportedly struggle with these tasks, often leading to over-editing or loss of scene consistency. This indicates that reason-informed video editing represents an underexplored and unresolved challenge.

The paper distinguishes itself from related work in several ways:
- **Instructional Video Editing:** While prior work (e.g., Tune-A-Video, InsV2V, InsViE, Ditto) has introduced datasets and methods for instruction-guided video editing, these largely address pixel-level or surface-level manipulations rather than deeper reasoning. Unified frameworks like VEGGIE and Omni-Video integrate Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for better instruction comprehension, but their capacity for complex reason-informed edits is still limited. This paper aims to push beyond this literal instruction following.
- **Reason-Informed Visual Generation and Editing:** There have been efforts to incorporate reasoning into visual generation (e.g., WISE, R2I-Bench, WorldGenBench) and image editing (e.g., RISE, EditWorld, Science-t2i). These benchmarks evaluate models' abilities to reason about world knowledge, spatial relationships, and causal logic, often revealing deficiencies. However, these works primarily focus on *images* or general conceptual reasoning, not the specific challenges of *reason-informed video editing* that involve temporal dynamics and causality within a continuous video context.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Differentiable Reward Learning:** Some methods have leveraged RL (e.g., CSR) or differentiable rewards (e.g., ReFL, VADER, RewardDance) to improve model alignment or incorporate human preferences. However, many RL-based approaches rely on costly external reward models or policy-gradient estimators, and existing differentiable reward methods often utilize external critics or reframe reward as "Yes" token probability without fully addressing the intrinsic reasoning-generation disconnect in a self-contained manner. This work introduces an intrinsic, differentiable reward mechanism that leverages the model's *own* internal VLM, differentiating it from approaches requiring external critics or labeled preference data.

By introducing the Reason-Informed Video Editing (RVE) task, a dedicated benchmark (RVE-Bench), and a novel self-reflective learning framework (ReViSE), this research contributes to bridging the identified gap between literal instruction following and advanced reasoning in video editing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable unified video models to perform "reason-informed video editing," moving beyond merely executing literal instructions to understanding and implementing transformations that require deeper reasoning about physical plausibility, causal dynamics, and world knowledge.

The motivation for this work stems from two identified limitations in current unified video models:
1. **Inadequate Datasets:** Existing datasets are considered insufficient for both training and evaluating models that need to perform reasoning-aware video editing. Current instruction-based video editing datasets typically describe surface-level pixel manipulations, lacking the implicit reasoning cues necessary for real-world understanding. This deficiency prevents models from learning to infer underspecified outcomes based on contextual logic.
2. **Reasoning-Generation Disconnect:** There is an inherent disconnect between the reasoning capabilities of a model's internal Vision-Language Model (VLM) and its generative (editing) capabilities. While VLMs within unified architectures are capable of multi-modal reasoning, this understanding does not effectively guide the video generation process during standard training, which primarily relies on output-level reconstruction loss. This leads to models that can imitate visually plausible edits but lack the underlying physical or causal reasoning.

To address these motivations, the authors established the following key objectives:
- **Define and formalize the Reason-Informed Video Editing (RVE) task:** This involves specifying the requirements for editing that necessitate inferring outcomes based on physical laws, temporal dynamics, or world knowledge.
- **Construct a comprehensive benchmark (RVE-Bench) and a large-scale dataset (RVE-Dataset):** These resources are designed to facilitate systematic evaluation and training of models for the RVE task, covering diverse reasoning dimensions across both editing and generation scenarios. The benchmark aims to provide a robust evaluation standard, while the dataset aims to supply the necessary data for training models with reason-informed capabilities.
- **Propose a novel self-reflective learning framework (ReViSE):** This framework aims to bridge the reasoning-generation disconnect by enabling the model to evaluate and refine its own outputs during training. A key goal is to leverage the model's internal VLM as a self-reflective evaluator, generating differentiable feedback that directly refines the generator's reasoning behavior, thereby allowing understanding to guide the editing process without relying on external critics or policy gradients.

Ultimately, the motivation is to advance video editing capabilities to a level where models can perform complex transformations that are not explicitly described but logically inferred, reflecting a more sophisticated understanding of video content and its interaction with the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the identified limitations—data inadequacy and the reasoning-generation disconnect—through a two-pronged approach involving data construction and a novel self-reflective learning framework.

#### 3.1. Data Construction: RVE-Dataset and RVE-Bench

To overcome the lack of suitable training and evaluation data, the authors developed a new dataset and benchmark:

- **RVE-Dataset:** This large-scale corpus comprises 56,000 reasoning-enriched triplets designed to foster sophisticated video editing reasoning. It includes two subsets:
 - **Reasoning-Aware Video Editing (RAVE) Subset:** This subset builds upon existing instruction-based video editing datasets (e.g., Ditto-1M). GPT-4o is employed to rewrite original instructions, inferring a real-world rationale and reformulating it as an implicit reasoning cue. This process ensures the generated instructions are physically motivated and semantically consistent with source and target videos, while withholding literal pixel-level operations. Examples cover Temporal, Spatial, Physical Causality, and Semantic Inference reasoning types.
 - **In-Context Video-to-Video Generation (ICVG) Subset:** This subset focuses on generating plausible future video frames conditioned on contextual descriptions, using real movie data. The construction pipeline involves segmenting movies into shots, captioning with QwenVL-32B, and grouping clips into consecutive scene windows. Visual clustering is performed using SigLIP embeddings to form coherent sub-clusters. Source and target pairs are selected to maximize semantic similarity between captions and visual diversity between clips. GPT-4o then generates reason-informed instructions for these pairs, grounded in narrative, causal, or commonsense reasoning (e.g., Camera, Causal, Emotional, Commonsense Reasoning).
 - **Data Quality Control:** GPT-4o is used exclusively for text instruction generation and does not influence visual content, ensuring the visual data originates from human-annotated or algorithmically selected sources.

- **RVE-Bench:** This benchmark is specifically constructed for systematic evaluation of the RVE task. It consists of approximately 1,000 unique triplets (source video, textual instruction, edited video) held out from the RVE-Dataset, with all instructions manually verified for plausibility and alignment.
 - **Evaluation Framework:** A GPT-4o-based framework is used for automated evaluation, assessing generated videos across two main aspects:
 - **Semantic Consistency (SC):** Measured by Edit Accuracy (EA) – whether the edit adheres to instructions, and Preservation Consistency (PC) – consistency of non-edited regions.
 - **Perceptual Quality (PQ):** Measured by Generation Naturalness (GN) – smoothness and natural flow, and Generation Realism (GR) – visual fidelity and absence of artifacts.
 - Scores (0-10) are provided for each metric, with an Overall score computed as the geometric mean of SC and PQ sub-metrics. PC is explicitly excluded for ICVG tasks, as they involve generating new content rather than targeted edits.

#### 3.2. ReViSE: Self-Reflective Learning Framework

To bridge the reasoning-generation disconnect, the authors propose ReViSE, a self-reflective learning framework built upon connector-based unified video models (e.g., Omni-Video).

- **Base Architecture:** The framework utilizes a frozen VLM (U, e.g., ViLA) and a diffusion generator (G_ϕ, e.g., Wan2.1-1.3B DiT). A connector (f_C) fuses visual, textual, and multimodal VLM representations into a unified conditioning signal for the generator.
- **Self-Reflective Training Loop:** ReViSE introduces an intrinsic feedback loop:
 1. The generator G_ϕ produces an edited video.
 2. The *frozen* internal VLM (U) evaluates this generated output against the reasoning instruction.
 3. A differentiable signal derived from this evaluation is backpropagated to refine G_ϕ's parameters.
- **Clean Video Estimation:** Since the VLM cannot interpret noisy latents (z_t), a one-step clean estimate (ˆx₀) is derived from z_t and the predicted velocity. This estimate provides semantically coherent input for VLM evaluation. To ensure reliability, VLM evaluation is gated to low-noise timesteps (t ≤ σ_max = 0.5), and F frames from ˆx₀ are decoded by a frozen VAE decoder (D).
- **Self-Reflective Critic:**
 - Instead of hard-decoded binary "Yes"/"No" signals, ReViSE uses the VLM's token-level probability P("Yes") as the differentiable reward signal. This continuous signal preserves confidence and uncertainty gradients, which are crucial for effective backpropagation.
 - The VLM critiques the generated video (ˆy₀) across the four dimensions defined in the evaluation protocol (EA, PC, GN, GR). It is instructed to provide a "Yes" verdict only if all dimensions are satisfied, preceded by chain-of-thought reasoning for interpretability.
 - The reward signal is the logit difference between the "Yes" and "No" tokens, which is further refined by subtracting a no-gradient baseline (logit difference from comparing the source video to itself). This isolates the genuine editing contribution and provides a zero-centered, bias-free reward.
 - Crucially, the VLM (U) and VAE decoder (D) are kept frozen. Gradients flow directly from the frozen VLM's token logits through D and ˆx₀ back to the generator G_ϕ, creating a fully differentiable self-corrective loop without external reward models or policy-gradient estimators, thus avoiding reward hacking and additional inference cost at deployment.
- **Unified Semantic Optimization (USO):** The total training objective combines the standard flow-matching loss (L_FM) for generative fidelity with the proposed reasoning loss (L_reason) derived from the self-reflective critic: L_USO = L_FM + λ * L_reason. The parameter λ balances these two objectives, empirically set to 0.25.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate ReViSE's capabilities in reason-informed video editing, as well as its performance on conventional tasks and generalization.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results on RVE-Bench

Evaluations on the RVE-Bench, encompassing both RAVE (Reasoning-Aware Video Editing) and ICVG (In-Context V2V Generation) subsets, compared ReViSE against four state-of-the-art baselines: InsV2V, InsViE, VACE, and Omni-Video. Metrics included VLM evaluation scores (Edit Accuracy (EA), Preservation Consistency (PC), Generation Naturalness (GN), Generation Realism (GR), and Overall score) and traditional video quality metrics (ViCLIP\_T, Subject Consistency (SC), Background Consistency (BC), Overall Consistency (OC), Temporal Flickering (TF), Motion Smoothness (MS)).

- **RAVE Subset:** ReViSE achieved an Overall score of 4.6689, representing a 49% improvement over the Omni-Video baseline (3.1331). Notable gains were observed in EA (from 4.8506 to 6.1605, +27%) and PC (from 2.9716 to 5.4086, +82%). This indicates enhanced adherence to instructions and better preservation of unedited regions. Compared to non-unified baselines, which sometimes showed high PC due to conservative edits, ReViSE balanced effective editing with preservation.
- **ICVG Subset:** For in-context generation, ReViSE again outperformed Omni-Video in EA (from 5.1571 to 5.8235, +13%) and Overall score (from 3.8395 to 3.9576). A slight decrease in GR for ReViSE was noted, which the authors attributed to the increased difficulty of maintaining visual realism when generating entirely new content without strict source-target correspondence.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons illustrated ReViSE's ability to interpret complex reasoning instructions. For instance, ReViSE successfully transformed a background into a "depth forest" based on the instruction "ran into the depth forest," maintaining visual consistency. It also generated a foggy scene from an instruction describing "cold air meets wet ground and causes moisture to condense into a low mist," demonstrating logical alignment with physical processes. In ICVG, ReViSE accurately interpreted instructions like "Imagine if the logs underwent a mechanical . . . wood chips onto a growing pile," generating realistic video of logs transforming into chips, showcasing its capability in complex in-context generation.

#### 4.3. User Study

A human preference study involved 15 experts ranking the anonymized outputs of all five methods for 50 randomly sampled RVE tasks. ReViSE achieved the highest win rate against all four baselines across Edit Accuracy, Content Preservation, and Visual Quality criteria, indicating strong human-perceived performance.

#### 4.4. Conventional Editing and Cross-Dataset Results

- **Conventional Editing (Ditto-1M):** ReViSE was evaluated on a random selection of 800 samples from the Ditto-1M dataset for conventional video editing. It outperformed all baselines in ViCLIP\_T, EA, GN, GR, and Overall, achieving a 36.7% gain in Overall score (from 2.7871 to 3.8109) over the previous best, suggesting that the self-reflective learning mechanism does not compromise the model's ability to perform standard editing tasks.
- **Cross-Dataset Generalization (VIE-Bench):** Experiments on VIE-Bench, which features reasoning-informed instructions, demonstrated ReViSE's generalization capabilities. ReViSE achieved the best Overall score (6.3819) and led in EA (6.7312) and GN (7.3656), confirming its effectiveness on unseen reasoning-informed benchmarks.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Effect of Optimization Objective:** Comparison of training objectives (SFT, RWR, USO) on the RAVE subset showed that USO (ReViSE's objective) achieved the best Overall score. SFT (supervised finetuning) improved less on EA (1.8%) compared to USO (27%) and RWR (11%), indicating that USO's joint optimization of flow-matching and reasoning losses with independent gradients is more effective than solely focusing on reconstruction fidelity or scaling the flow-matching loss.
- **Effect of Reasoning Loss Weight (λ):** An analysis of the trade-off weight λ in L_USO = L_FM + λ * L_reason determined that λ = 0.25 yielded the best Overall performance. Lower λ (0.10) led to reduced EA and PC, while higher λ (0.75) improved EA but regressed ViCLIP\_T, indicating a compromise in visual generation quality with excessive semantic supervision.
- **Effect of Noise-Gating Threshold (σ_max):** Evaluation of σ_max, which controls the noise level at which the VLM critic is activated, revealed that σ_max = 0.5 offered the best balance. While lower σ_max (e.g., 0.1) yielded slightly higher Pearson correlation with reward signal quality, its low trigger rate (3.5%) provided insufficient training coverage. σ_max = 0.5 maintained strong signal fidelity (Pearson r = 0.451, 93.4% of clean-video upper bound) with a practical training coverage (25.0% trigger rate).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of generative AI and video processing by addressing a recognized limitation in existing video editing models. The introduction of the Reason-Informed Video Editing (RVE) task and the accompanying RVE-Bench and RVE-Dataset establishes a dedicated framework and resources for investigating and advancing reasoning capabilities in video generation. This formalizes a new direction for research, moving beyond literal instruction execution to more sophisticated semantic and logical understanding in video editing.

The proposed ReViSE framework introduces a novel self-reflective learning paradigm. By leveraging the model's *own* internal VLM as an intrinsic, differentiable critic, ReViSE bypasses several challenges of prior approaches. It eliminates the need for external reward models, costly human-labeled preference data, or computationally intensive policy-gradient methods. The use of token-level probabilities for differentiable feedback is a technical innovation that maintains the continuous semantic signal, facilitating effective gradient flow back to the generator. This intrinsic mechanism offers an efficient and self-contained approach to bridge the disconnect between a model's understanding and generation capabilities.

The extensive experimental results demonstrate that ReViSE achieves state-of-the-art performance on the RVE-Bench across diverse reasoning categories. Its sustained superiority in both reasoning-informed and conventional video editing benchmarks, alongside strong cross-dataset generalization, indicates the robustness and versatility of the approach. The user study results further corroborate the perceived quality and accuracy of ReViSE's outputs by human experts.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Advancing AI Capabilities:** ReViSE contributes to the development of more intelligent and autonomous generative AI systems that can reason about complex physical, causal, and narrative contexts. This could lead to AI assistants that interpret user intent more deeply, requiring less explicit instruction.
- **Enhancing Content Creation Tools:** For creative industries, this research could lead to more powerful and intuitive video editing software. Users could issue high-level, abstract instructions, and the system would intelligently infer and execute the appropriate transformations, significantly streamlining workflows and enabling novel creative possibilities.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The RVE task, RVE-Bench, and the self-reflective learning paradigm provide a strong foundation for future research in multimodal reasoning, generative modeling, and human-computer interaction. It opens avenues for exploring how models can acquire and apply common sense and world knowledge in dynamic visual environments.
- **Improved Model Alignment:** The intrinsic differentiable reward mechanism offers a generalizable approach for aligning complex generative models with high-level semantic objectives without external supervision, which could be adapted to other domains beyond video editing.
