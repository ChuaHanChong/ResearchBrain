# What Teaches Robots to Walk, Teaches Them to Trade too -- Regime Adaptive Execution using Informed Data and LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a novel framework for financial market prediction, leveraging a teacher-student reinforcement learning architecture inspired by robotic locomotion to adapt to dynamic market regime shifts. It utilizes large language models (LLMs) as a source of informed data and an RL from Market Feedback (RLMF) alignment technique, resulting in the UNReAL model achieving significantly higher accuracy on stock movement prediction tasks compared to state-of-the-art LLMs.

## Problem

- Financial markets exhibit dynamic "regime switching," where underlying correlations and covariances shift, causing traditional machine learning models to fail in out-of-domain generalization.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) to finance is hindered by the difficulty of creating realistic, randomized market simulations for effective agent training.
- Existing large language models (LLMs) in finance primarily focus on pre-training or instruction-tuning, without directly addressing the challenge of dynamic market regime adaptation.

## Method

- A dual-phase teacher-student architecture, inspired by robotic locomotion adaptation, is employed, utilizing large language models (**LLMs**) fine-tuned on market data as base policies.
- ==Reinforcement Learning== from Market Feedback (RLMF) is introduced as an LLM alignment technique that dynamically adapts pretrained LLMs using intrinsic, natural market rewards as feedback signals.
- The inherent "world knowledge" encoded within LLMs is leveraged as a form of "privileged information" in the financial context, circumventing the need for complex, randomized market simulators.

## Results

- The UNReAL model achieved significantly higher accuracy on FLARE benchmark stock movement tasks, outperforming GPT-4 and Llama-2-7b-chat by substantial margins (e.g., **0.6248** on ACL18, an improvement of ~15 percentage points over the next best model).
- On the proprietary NIFTY Stock Price Movement Prediction task, UNReAL demonstrated superior performance with **0.7191** accuracy and **0.7146** F1 score, representing an over 28 percentage point improvement in accuracy compared to GPT-4.
- Experiments revealed that larger LLMs consistently produced richer and more informative embeddings for financial news headlines, quantifiable by higher information gain and reduction in variance within clusters, indicating better semantic representation for predictive tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-stage teacher-student policy architecture, proven successful in robotics for rapid motor adaptation and zero-shot generalization, can be effectively transferred to financial market prediction for dynamic regime adaptation.
> - Large language models (LLMs) can effectively serve as a proxy for "privileged information" in finance, encoding the necessary "world knowledge" to enrich the learning process without relying on unrealistic market simulations.
> - Continuous dynamic adaptation through real-time market feedback (RLMF) allows the model to learn and adjust to current market dynamics, significantly improving generalization during regime shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research integrates advances from two distinct fields: reinforcement learning (RL) in robotics and large language models (LLMs) in financial market forecasting. The broader landscape is characterized by:

- **Transformative Impact of Deep Learning**: Modern machine learning (ML) has demonstrated significant success in complex domains, often supplanting or radically changing decades-old heuristic approaches. Examples cited include weather forecasting (GraphCast) and protein structure prediction (AlphaFold).
- **Breakthroughs in Reinforcement Learning**: RL has driven advancements in areas such as game playing (AlphaGo), drone racing, and notably, agile locomotion for quadruped robots. These robotic applications have shown remarkable zero-shot generalization capabilities, adapting to unseen terrains primarily through model-free RL techniques combined with simulation and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Challenges in Financial Market Forecasting**: Financial markets are characterized as a "hard problem" due to dynamic "regime switching," which involves underlying correlation and covariance shifts in hidden market variables. Traditional ML models often fail to generalize well to out-of-domain distributions (i.e., regime shifts), leading to models that perform well in backtesting but poorly in future predictions. A key bottleneck in applying RL to finance has been the difficulty of creating reliable, randomized market simulations, making it akin to a "one-shot learning task" with only one true history. Historically, market regime switching has been approached using Markovian Switching Models, but the paper advocates moving beyond such heuristic methods.
- **Emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs)**: The recent advent of RL-tuned LLMs, capable of encoding "world knowledge," is identified as a crucial enabling factor. These LLMs offer a proxy for the "privileged information" (true environmental state) that is essential for the teacher-student training paradigms successful in robotics. Prior work in financial LLMs (e.g., FinBERT, BloombergGPT) has focused on pre-training with financial texts or instruction-tuning for general tasks, but none have directly addressed dynamic regime adaptation via analogies to robotic locomotion.

This work specifically positions itself as a novel attempt to juxtapose the successes of RL-based adaptive quadruped locomotion (using two-stage teacher-student policies and "privileged information") with the challenge of dynamic market regime adaptation, utilizing LLMs as the source of "informed data" or "world knowledge" in the financial context. It aims to overcome the limitations of traditional ML and pure RL approaches in finance by leveraging insights from a seemingly disparate domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are detailed as follows:

- **Address the Financial Market Regime Adaptation Problem**: The central motivation is to overcome the persistent challenge of dynamic regime switching in financial markets. This phenomenon, characterized by shifts in underlying correlations and covariances of hidden market variables, causes traditional predictive models to lose efficacy and fail to generalize from historical data to future market conditions. The paper posits that current ML techniques, even deep learning solutions, struggle with this dynamic environment, leading to models that are brittle outside their training distribution.

- **Draw Inspiration from Robotic Locomotion**: The authors are motivated by the remarkable success of reinforcement learning in robotics, particularly in enabling quadruped robots to achieve robust zero-shot generalization and agile locomotion adaptation to unseen and challenging terrains. These robotic systems utilize a two-stage teacher-student training architecture where a teacher policy, trained with "privileged information" (true environmental data), guides a student policy, which then learns to adapt using only proprioceptive signals. The goal is to apply this successful adaptive framework to the financial domain.

- **Overcome the Market Simulation Bottleneck**: A significant impediment to applying RL in finance has been the difficulty in creating realistic and sufficiently randomized market simulations to train agents effectively. Unlike robotics where simulation environments are well-developed, financial markets are complex, partially observable, and have only one true historical trajectory, making extensive simulation-based learning challenging. The research is motivated to find an alternative to this bottleneck.

- **Leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) as "Privileged Information"**: The recent advancements and capabilities of RL-tuned LLMs, specifically their ability to encode and process vast amounts of "world knowledge," are identified as a critical enabler. This "world knowledge" is conceptualized as an analogous form of "privileged information" in the financial context, offering a rich source of underlying environmental knowledge that was previously unavailable. The objective is to integrate these LLMs into the adaptive framework.

- **Introduce Reinforcement Learning from Market Feedback (RLMF)**: A key objective is to propose and implement a novel LLM alignment technique dubbed Reinforcement Learning from Market Feedback (RLMF). This technique aims to dynamically adapt pretrained LLMs using intrinsic, natural market rewards as feedback signals, allowing them to continually learn and adjust to real-world market dynamics.

- **Develop a Dual-Phase Teacher-Student Architecture for Finance**: Based on the robotics paradigm, the research aims to develop and implement a dual-phase (training and deployment) teacher-student architecture for financial market execution. This involves fine-tuning LLMs as base policies and then adaptively executing them, with continuous updates based on real market feedback.

- **Empirically Validate Efficacy and Outperform Existing Models**: A core objective is to demonstrate, through strong empirical results, the effectiveness of the proposed method in adapting to financial regime shifts. This includes outperforming best-performing state-of-the-art LLM models on established stock movement (SM) tasks (e.g., FLARE benchmark) and newly proposed tasks (NIFTY SM task), validating the adaptive policy's superiority.

- **Analyze the Role of Language Model Embeddings**: The research also aims to analyze the quality of language model embeddings, specifically in terms of information gain, to understand how underlying LLM architectural blocks (encoder, decoder, encoder-decoder) and model size contribute to richer and more informative representations of financial news data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a dual-phase teacher-student architecture inspired by rapid motor adaptation techniques for quadrupedal robots. The core idea is to leverage the "world knowledge" of LLMs and adapt them dynamically using natural market rewards.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The task of predicting market movement direction is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). A policy's "belief state" ($b_t$) is considered a sufficient statistic of the history ($h_t$) for optimal action selection. An observation ($o_t$) at time $t$ is composed of an LLM query ($x_q$), which includes a prompt ($x_p$) and the action prediction label from the previous time step ($\hat{x}_{r, t-1}$).

**B. The NIFTY Dataset:**
A new, curated dataset, NIFTY (News-Informed Financial Trend Yield), is introduced, comprising two subsets:

1. **NIFTY-LM ($D_{LM}$)**: Used for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of the LLM policy.
 - **Content**: High-quality, processed one-turn conversational queries (prompt $x_p$ and response $x_r$).
 - **Prompt Components**:
 - **Instruction**: 20 variations of task instructions (e.g., "Anticipate the direction of the $SPY...") generated by GPT-4.
 - **Market Context**: 10 days of historical numerical market data for the $SPY index, including OHLCV (Open, High, Low, Close, Volume) prices and various technical indicators (MACD, Bollinger Bands, RSI, CCI, DX, SMAs).
 - **News**: Concatenated daily news headlines from Wall Street Journal and Reuters (Jan 2010 - Sep 2020). Reuters headlines were filtered for financial relevance using SBERT similarity to GPT-4 generated financial headlines. Prompts exceeding 3000 words were truncated using TF-IDF.
 - **Response ($x_r$)**: A qualitative market movement label ('rise', 'fall', 'neutral') derived from the daily percentage change of the $SPY closing price, thresholded at $\pm0.5\%$.
 - **Statistics**: 2111 data points, split into 1477 train, 317 test, and 317 evaluation.

2. **NIFTY-RL ($D_{RL}$)**: A preferences dataset for training the reward model and RL fine-tuning.
 - **Content**: Tuples of $(x_p, x_r^w, x_r^l)$, where $x_r^w$ is the "chosen" (preferred, correct) market movement label, and $x_r^l$ is a "rejected" (incorrect) label, randomly chosen from {'rise', 'fall', 'neutral', 'surrender'} excluding $x_r^w$.

**C. Training Phase (Algorithm 1):**

1. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Teacher Policy ($\pi_{teacher}^{\phi}$)**:
 - A pretrained LLM (e.g., Llama2-chat-7b) is initialized.
 - Fine-tuned using the NIFTY-LM dataset with an autoregressive cross-entropy loss (Equation 3) to adapt it to market prediction and instruction following.

2. **Train Reward Model ($f_{RM}^{\theta}$)**:
 - Initialized with the SFT language model.
 - Trained using the NIFTY-RL preferences dataset.
 - The reward model learns to assign scalar rewards to prompt-response pairs by optimizing a negative log-likelihood loss (Equation 4) based on the chosen/rejected preferences.

3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Fine-tune Teacher Policy**:
 - The SFT-tuned teacher policy is further fine-tuned using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) and the NIFTY-RL dataset. This step aims to align the LLM's generative behavior with the preferences learned by the reward model, incorporating a KL-divergence penalty against the reference (SFT) policy to maintain stability.

**D. Deployment Phase (Algorithm 2) - Regime Adaptive Execution with RLMF:**

- This phase implements dynamic adaptation.
- **Student Policy Initialization**: A student policy ($\pi_{student}$) is initialized from the current teacher policy.
- **Adaptive Loop (repeated every $T$ steps)**:
 1. **Collect Market Feedback Data ($D_{MF}$)**: During the execution period, a dataset of (prompt $x_p$, student's predicted action $\hat{x}_r^{\phi}$, and the true market reaction $x_{MF}^r$) is collected. This $x_{MF}^r$ serves as the "natural market reward."
 2. **Update Reward Model**: The reward model ($f_{RM}^{\theta}$) is updated using the MLE loss (Equation 4) on the collected $D_{MF}$, effectively recalibrating it to recent market conditions.
 3. **Update Student Policy**: The student policy is updated using a combined objective function called **RLMF loss** ($L_{RLMF}$) (Equation 7), which integrates:
 - The standard RL fine-tuning loss ($L_{RL}$) (Equation 6), which uses the *updated* reward model and a KL-divergence penalty.
 - A simple Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss ($L_{MF}$) (Equation 5) between the student's prediction and the true market reaction from $D_{MF}$. This directly incorporates the "market feedback."
 - A coefficient ($\gamma$) controls the strength of this direct market feedback.
 4. **Teacher Swap**: The updated student policy then becomes the new teacher policy for the next $T$ steps, enabling continuous adaptation to regime shifts.

**E. Role of Embeddings in Information Acquisition (Appendix A):**
An auxiliary experiment investigates whether larger LLMs generate "richer" embeddings.
- **Method**: Various sizes of GPT, T5, and BERT models produce embeddings for NIFTY news headlines. These embeddings are then reduced to 2D using t-SNE and clustered using HDBSCAN.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Information Gain (IG)** (Equations 8-11): Used for "location" and "genre" tasks, measuring the increase in information (reduction in entropy) after clustering based on headline tags.
 - **Reduction in Variance (RV)** (Equations 12-14): Used for the "market movement" task, quantifying the decrease in variance of daily market percentage change within clusters, indicating how well the model groups headlines predictive of similar movements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical results demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed Regime Adaptive Execution with Informed Data (RAEiD) methodology, specifically through the UNReAL model.

**A. Performance on Stock Movement Prediction Tasks:**

1. **FLARE Benchmark Performance (Tables 2 & 3):**
 - **General Tasks (Table 2)**: The UNReAL model (approx. 7B parameters) achieved notable performance in sentiment classification (FPB task), with an accuracy of 0.9329 and an F1 score of 0.9390. This performance surpassed larger models, including GPT-4 (Acc: 0.792, F1: 0.795), and is attributed to its exposure to market news data and trinary label generation alignment. UNReAL was not evaluated on tasks requiring different output formats (e.g., Headline classification, NER, FinQA).
 - **Stock Movement (SM) Tasks (Table 3 - Zero-shot)**: UNReAL demonstrated superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art LLMs, including GPT-4, Llama-2-7b-chat, Zephyr, and Mistral models.
 - **ACL18**: UNReAL achieved an accuracy of 0.6248. This is an improvement of approximately 15% points over the next best model (Llama-2-7b-chat at 0.5145) and significantly higher than GPT-4 (0.45).
 - **BigData22**: UNReAL achieved an accuracy of 0.6142, outperforming Llama-2-7b-chat (0.5442) and GPT-4 (0.4518).
 - **CIKM18**: UNReAL recorded an accuracy of 0.6011, higher than Llama-2-7b-chat (0.5407) and GPT-4 (0.4891).
 - **MCC (Matthews Correlation Coefficient)**: While UNReAL's MCC scores were generally low (e.g., 0.0013 on ACL18), they were competitive with or, in some cases, slightly better than other models, indicating that the task might involve class imbalance or inherent difficulty in prediction.
 - **Comparative Observations**: FinMA models, based on an older Llama-1 architecture and trained for strictly binary classification, performed poorly on these tasks. GPT-4, despite its general capabilities, showed lower performance in these specialized financial prediction tasks compared to UNReAL, suggesting that specialized alignment and data are beneficial.

2. **NIFTY Stock Price Movement Prediction Task (Table 4):**
 - On the proprietary NIFTY test split, UNReAL exhibited a significant lead over all other compared models.
 - **Accuracy**: UNReAL achieved 0.7191 accuracy, a substantial improvement of over 28% points compared to GPT-4 (0.4385). Other models like Zephyr and Llama-2-7b-chat also performed significantly lower.
 - **F1 Score**: UNReAL also showed a high F1 score of 0.7146, again outperforming GPT-4 (0.4515) and other models.

**B. Role of Embeddings in Information Acquisition (Appendix A, Table 6, Figures 6, 7 & 8):**

- **Correlation with Model Size**: The experiments demonstrated a consistent trend: models with a larger number of parameters generally produced richer (more informative) embeddings. This was quantified by higher information gain for categorical tasks ("location," "genre") and a greater reduction in variance for continuous tasks ("market movement").
- **Consistency Across Architectures**: This trend was observed across different LLM architectures, including decoder-only (GPT2), encoder-decoder (T5), and encoder-only (BERT) models. For instance, GPT2-XL (1.5B parameters) showed higher information gain than GPT2-SMALL (124M parameters) for location and genre tasks. Similarly, BERT-BASE (109M) generally outperformed BERT-TINY (4M).
- **Granularity of Semantic Clustering**: Visualizations using t-SNE (Figures 6 and 8) illustrated that larger models achieved a finer granularity of semantic clustering, indicating a more precise interpretation of news headlines.
- **Implication for Finance**: This finding supports the hypothesis that the quality of source embeddings from LLMs is crucial. Larger LLMs, by generating more informative embeddings, can enhance the precision of semantic clustering of financial news, which is relevant for improving predictive accuracy in market movement tasks.

In summary, the UNReAL model, powered by the RAEiD methodology, achieved substantial performance gains in financial stock movement prediction, particularly in adapting to dynamic market conditions. The study also provided evidence that the size and architecture of LLMs directly influence the richness and informativeness of their embeddings, impacting their utility in complex analytical tasks such as financial forecasting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a novel framework for financial market prediction with several significant implications:

- **Novelty in Regime Adaptation**: The primary significance lies in introducing a fundamentally different approach to addressing dynamic regime shifts in financial markets. By drawing a direct analogy from successful reinforcement learning techniques in quadrupedal robotics, the work offers a fresh perspective that deviates from conventional heuristic models and purely data-driven ML methods which typically struggle with out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Bridging Cross-Domain Methodologies**: The successful transfer of the teacher-student policy architecture and the concept of "privileged information" from robotics to the complex domain of finance highlights the potential for interdisciplinary inspiration in AI research. This demonstrates that insights from one domain can provide solutions to persistent challenges in another.
- **Empirical Validation and Superior Performance**: The achieved empirical results, where the UNReAL model significantly outperforms state-of-the-art general-purpose LLMs (including GPT-4) on both established (FLARE) and novel (NIFTY) financial stock movement prediction tasks, validate the efficacy of the proposed Regime Adaptive Execution with Informed Data (RAEiD) and Reinforcement Learning from Market Feedback (RLMF) methodology. This suggests a more robust and adaptive predictive capability for financial applications.
- **Leveraging LLM "World Knowledge" Effectively**: The work demonstrates a practical method for integrating the vast "world knowledge" encoded within LLMs into a dynamic, adaptive system. This addresses the challenge of market simulation in finance by treating LLM-derived information as a form of "privileged information," thereby enriching the learning process without requiring unrealistic market simulators.
- **Implications for Financial Modeling**: The enhanced predictive accuracy and adaptability to regime shifts could lead to more effective financial models for tasks such as trading strategy development, portfolio management, and risk analysis. Such advancements could potentially benefit institutional investors and quantitative finance professionals by enabling more informed and timely decision-making.
- **Insights into Language Model Embeddings**: The analysis on the role of language model embeddings provides valuable insights for the broader NLP and ML communities. The finding that larger model sizes and specific architectural blocks lead to richer, more informative embeddings for financial news has implications for the design and selection of LLMs for specialized domain applications, emphasizing the importance of detailed semantic representation.

**Potential Impact (Future Directions):**

- **Foundation for Advanced Financial AI**: The proposed adaptive policy can serve as a foundational "observation mapping module" for downstream financial tasks. This opens avenues for integrating UNReAL into larger reinforcement learning agents designed for automated stock trading or dynamic portfolio allocation, where performance could be measured by metrics such as the Sharpe ratio.
- **Further Performance Enhancements**: The acknowledged limitations, such as the use of Llama-2-7b and RL-based alignment, point to future research directions. Exploring larger or more recent LLMs (e.g., Mistral-7B for extended context windows), incorporating RL-free alignment techniques (like DPO), or integrating Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) could further enhance the model's performance and robustness. The significant gap between UNReAL and other models on the NIFTY task indicates substantial room for further improvement.
- **Broader Economic Implications**: While the paper adopts a neutral stance on societal implications, the development of highly accurate and adaptive financial forecasting tools could have broader economic effects, including influencing market efficiency, liquidity, and investment flows. Ethical considerations regarding responsible deployment and access to such powerful technologies remain relevant.
