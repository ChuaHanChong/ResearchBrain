# G0.5: One Autoregressive Stream for Robot Reasoning and Action

> [!summary] Summary
> G0.5 is an autoregressive vision-language-action model in which one transformer decoder emits chain-of-thought and action tokens under a single next-token loss. A learned cross-embodiment residual-VQ ActionCodec compresses actions into shared codes, active-part tokenization skips idle joints, and factorized spatio-temporal visual memory injects history. It leads seven evaluation regimes, including 82.5% held-out DROID transfer and 98.9% on LIBERO.

## Problem

- The dominant VLA recipe bolts a separately trained flow-matching expert onto a pretrained VLM, demoting the backbone to a condition encoder rather than the decision-maker.
- Early autoregressive VLAs binned actions per dimension per timestep, so token counts exploded with control frequency, horizon and degrees of freedom, making high-frequency control expensive.
- Prior tokenizers flatten whole action vectors regardless of morphology, entangling semantics across robots; cross-embodiment alignment is patched at action-vector level via padding or per-robot adapters.

## Method

- **ActionCodec**: a learned ==residual vector quantizer== over semantically aligned motion parts (left control, right control, lower body), trained with a ==temporal contrastive loss== and emitting only active DoF-group tokens.
- **Native chain-of-thought stream** interleaves four supervised targets, Subtask, BBox, Trace and ActionHint, with action codes in one decoder under a single ==next-token cross-entropy== objective.
- **Visual memory** inserts ==factorized spatial-temporal attention== every four ==ViT== layers, drops history tokens at the last layer with ==stochastic frame dropout==; an optional ==flow-matching== head attaches for comparison.

## Results

- Real-world R1-Lite/R1-Pro fine-tuning under matched compute reached **76.7%** average success versus **53.3%** for π0.5 and **24.4%** for GR00T-N1.7; held-out DROID transfer hit **82.5%** versus **57.5%**.
- A single checkpoint scored **0.3136** on the BEHAVIOR Challenge against **0.2626** for π0.5 at four epochs and **0.2605** for the four-checkpoint winner; LIBERO **98.9%**, RoboTwin **93.3%**, SimplerEnv-Bridge **87.3%**.
- Toggling inference config, chain-of-thought lifted autoregressive progress **2.4** to **3.8** on Air Fryer versus **2.1** to **2.7** for flow matching; GRPO from one demo reached **97.4%** versus **92.9%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Keeping actions inside the VLM's own token stream preserves prompt steering and exposes exact token log-probabilities, so ratio-based RL applies without stochastic reformulation of a denoising process.
> - Structural alignment belongs in the tokenizer: part-wise grouping plus sparse emission of only moving joints scales across morphologies better than padding action vectors, and cuts decoding cost.
> - In-stream reasoning pays off mainly on multi-stage tasks; single-grounding benchmarks moved under two points, so evaluate chain-of-thought where horizons are genuinely long.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for general-purpose robot control has evolved through different architectural paradigms. Initially, autoregressive (AR) interfaces cast robot control as token generation, allowing the Vision-Language Model (VLM) itself to act. However, this approach faced scalability challenges with increasing control frequency, action horizon, and action dimensionality, leading to slow and expensive high-frequency control. Examples of early AR systems include RT-2 and OpenVLA.

This bottleneck prompted a shift towards VLM-as-encoder architectures. In this paradigm, a pretrained VLM serves as a feature extractor, providing hidden states or KV caches to a separately trained action expert (e.g., flow-matching or diffusion models) that predicts continuous action chunks. While improving action efficiency, this approach fundamentally altered the VLM's role from a direct action generator to a condition encoder. Consequently, core generative capabilities of VLMs, such as chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, in-context learning, and prompt-based motion steering, could only indirectly influence behavior via a compressed conditioning bottleneck, rather than being native components of action generation. Notable VLM-as-encoder models include π0, π0.5, GR00T-N1, and SmolVLA.

A recurring issue in VLM-as-encoder models is the "anti-forgetting problem," where backpropagating gradients from the action expert can degrade the VLM’s pretrained perception and language capabilities. Solutions like Knowledge Insulation address this by preventing gradient flow into the VLM and reintroducing AR action prediction as an auxiliary representation-learning objective. Recent work, such as VLA-0, has suggested that unmodified VLMs trained autoregressively on actions-as-text can outperform some VLM-as-encoder counterparts without extensive pretraining, providing evidence for the viability of the AR paradigm.

In terms of action tokenization, methods have progressed from simple per-dimension/per-timestep binning (inefficient for high-frequency data) to more advanced techniques like Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) with byte-pair encoding (FAST) and neural/vector-quantized variants (VQ-VLA, BEAST). Cross-embodiment generalization typically involves unifying action spaces at the vector level through padding or per-embodiment adaptors (e.g., Being-H0.5, Green-VLA, HEX).

For integrating reasoning, two main families exist: "bolt-on" CoT, where a high-level VLM generates plans for a separate low-level controller (e.g., HAMSTER), and "in-stream" CoT, where reasoning and action are generated within the same autoregressive sequence by the same decoder (e.g., ECoT, CoT-VLA).

This work positions itself by returning to and refining the autoregressive formulation, aiming to overcome its historical inefficiencies while preserving the VLM as a unified generative actor and reasoner. It proposes to lift structural alignment into the action tokenizer itself and integrate diverse reasoning primitives natively within the action generation stream.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to establish a unified VLA model where a single set of weights, derived from a VLM backbone, is responsible for generating both reasoning and actions within a single autoregressive token stream. This approach contrasts with the prevailing VLM-as-encoder architecture, where the VLM primarily serves as a context encoder for a separate action expert.

The motivation for this shift stems from perceived limitations of VLM-as-encoder models:
- **VLM Role Restriction**: In VLM-as-encoder architectures, the VLM's role is reduced to a condition encoder rather than an active decision-maker. This implies that the VLM's inherent generative capabilities, such as chain-of-thought reasoning, in-context learning, and prompt-based motion steering, can only indirectly influence robotic behavior. The authors argue that these capabilities are structurally weakened when mediated through a compressed conditioning bottleneck rather than being integral to the action generation process.
- **Inefficiency of Prior Autoregressive Models**: Earlier autoregressive VLA systems faced scalability issues. As control frequency, action horizon, and action dimensionality increased, the number of per-timestep action tokens grew rapidly, rendering high-frequency control slow and computationally expensive. The current work aims to address this inefficiency without abandoning the autoregressive framework.

To make the autoregressive formulation tractable and effective at a foundation-model scale, the G0.5 model aims to integrate three key components:
1. **A learnable cross-embodiment action tokenizer**: This component is designed to compress heterogeneous robot actions from diverse embodiments into a compact, shared discrete token vocabulary, thereby reducing the decoding burden.
2. **A native chain-of-thought (CoT) stream**: This stream is intended to interleave task decomposition, object grounding, and action hints directly with action tokens. By sharing the same decoder, context, and objective as the action tokens, reasoning becomes an integral part of the generative process rather than a separate module.
3. **A visual memory module**: This module is incorporated to inject multi-second visual history through the vision encoder, providing persistent visual context beneficial for long-horizon control and closed-loop replanning.

Ultimately, the primary motivation is to enable the pretrained VLM’s robust capabilities to directly translate into physical behavior, allowing for close instruction following and prompt-driven action steering without the need for additional retraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The G0.5 model is designed based on the principle that perception, reasoning, and action should be unified within a single autoregressive process operating over a shared token vocabulary.

**Model Architecture and Initialization:**
G0.5 is initialized from Qwen3.5 2B, a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM), which provides a visual encoder, a multimodal token vocabulary, and an autoregressive decoder. At inference, the model takes as input:
- A short temporal window of multi-view RGB observations from multiple cameras.
- An embodiment identifier (e.g., R1-Pro).
- A natural-language task instruction.
- A proprioceptive state of the robot.

The model then autoregressively generates a structured output that concludes with a sequence of discrete action codes. This generation can optionally include Chain-of-Thought (CoT) segments for object grounding, subtask decomposition, or gripper traces, depending on the prompt template.

**Token Sequence and Loss Function:**
All inputs and outputs are serialized into a single token sequence, as detailed in Figure 2 of the paper. This sequence is divided into:
- **Conditioning segment:** Contains images, embodiment ID, task instruction, and proprioceptive state, wrapped in user-side chat tokens and masked from loss computation.
- **Generative segment:** Contains the optional CoT trace and action codes, wrapped in assistant-side chat tokens. The standard next-token cross-entropy loss is applied *only* to tokens within this segment. This unified loss jointly supervises both CoT generation and action generation, treating them as "just tokens" from the same vocabulary within the same forward pass.

**Key Components:**

1. **Unified Heterogeneous Action Codec (Section 3.1):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Existing action tokenization methods often lack structural decomposition, flattening the entire action space, leading to semantically entangled tokens that transfer poorly across embodiments. They also suffer from poor token consistency, where similar actions might map to token sequences with high Hamming distances, introducing optimization noise.
 - **Approach:** G0.5 adopts an action grouping strategy and training recipe from ActionCodec. It decomposes each robot into independent motion parts (e.g., left control, right control, lower body) and pads them to a shared maximum dimensionality. A residual vector quantization (RVQ) model is then trained over these grouped actions. A temporal contrastive objective is incorporated to enhance token consistency across temporally adjacent motions.
 - **Representation:** During tokenization, structural special tokens (e.g., `<left_control_r>`) are explicitly injected into the sequence. The action span unfolds into `R` residual rounds, each containing active DoF-group markers followed by 8 action codes. This allows for sparse prediction, where only actively involved motion parts are tokenized, reducing the decoding burden. The action space is a unified 27-dimensional representation across embodiments (e.g., 9 DoF for left control, 1 for left gripper, 9 for right control, 1 for right gripper, 7 for lower body). Inactive slots are filled with noop tokens.

2. **Native Chain-of-Thought (CoT) (Section 3.2):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Previous methods often treated auxiliary VQA tasks (like sub-task or object bounding box prediction) as mere training-time supervision, without explicitly integrating them into the action generation process.
 - **Approach:** G0.5 integrates these auxiliary tasks directly into the autoregressive action generation stream. The model is trained to optionally emit intermediate reasoning for four self-describing targets: `Subtask:` (atomic task decomposition), `BBox:` (key-object localization), `Trace:` (2D motion planning), and `ActionHint:` (frame-level gripper hints). Any subset of these targets can be emitted, and the model is trained with 8 curated combinations per step, all supervised by the single next-token objective. This design ensures that reasoning and action are coupled phases of one generative process.

3. **Visual Memory (Section 3.3):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Complex manipulation tasks are often non-Markovian, where single-frame observations are insufficient due to occlusions or the need for temporal context to recognize failures and replan. Naively stacking historical vision tokens leads to quadratic computational cost and susceptibility to error accumulation.
 - **Approach:** G0.5 incorporates factorized spatial and temporal attention modules every four layers within the Vision Transformer, following approaches like π0.7 and MEM. This mechanism efficiently fuses historical context by sequentially mixing information across time and spatial patches. To bound latency, historical tokens are discarded at the final layer. During training, historical frames are stochastically dropped as regularization to prevent overfitting. Proprioceptive inputs are synchronized with visual frames using continuous state embeddings.

**Pre-training (Section 4):**
G0.5 undergoes a single-stage pre-training on a heterogeneous mixture of robot demonstrations and web-scale vision-language data.
- **Robot Data Mixture:** Covers 14 embodiments, with DROID data *excluded* from this foundational pre-training. All data is adapted to the unified 27-dimensional action space.
- **Autolabeling Pipeline:** An automated multimodal pipeline enriches raw episodes with multi-granularity semantic (action hints, atomic tasks, episode instructions via multimodal model APIs like Gemini 3 and Doubao Seed 2.0 Pro), visual grounding (per-frame bounding boxes/segmentation via foundation models and SAM3 tracking), and action-trajectory (2D end-effector traces) annotations.
- **Web and VQA Co-training:** The model is co-trained with a large-scale vision-language mixture, including generic web VQA, embodied VQA, and in-house annotations. This is an action-heavy mixture, with VQA and action samples optimized with the same next-token cross-entropy loss.
- **CoT Supervision:** Each robot sample is assigned one CoT format from eight candidates, with subtask text having a higher sampling weight.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with a learning rate schedule (warmup, constant, cosine decay), unfrozen vision tower, sparse multi-second history input, and stochastic dropping of historical frames.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of G0.5 spans seven distinct regimes, designed to assess its deployability, transferability, scalability, language fidelity, and adaptability.

#### 4.1. DROID Environment- and Object-Level Zero-Shot Evaluation

- **Context**: After post-training on DROID data, G0.5 was deployed on a Franka Research 3 robot arm in a held-out physical environment with unseen object instances, ensuring environment- and object-level zero-shot generalization.
- **Performance**: G0.5-DROID achieved an average success rate of 82.5%, outperforming π0.5-DROID (57.5%) and MolmoAct2-DROID (52.0%). G0.5 showed stronger performance in tasks requiring precise object discrimination and multi-step reasoning.
- **Observation**: G0.5-DROID demonstrated sensitivity to low-contrast, semi-transparent surfaces (e.g., drawer localization), which was mitigated by adding high-contrast visual markers.

#### 4.2. Simulation Benchmarks

- **Bridge-SimplerEnv**: After post-training on BridgeData V2, G0.5 achieved the highest average success rate of 87.3% on four Bridge-style WidowX manipulation tasks, surpassing models like GR00T-N1.5 (61.9%) and π0.5 (57.1%).
- **RoboTwin 2.0**: On a suite of 50 simulated bimanual manipulation tasks, G0.5 achieved an average success rate of 93.3% (93.7% clean, 92.8% randomized), leading baselines such as π0.5 (79.8%).
- **LIBERO**: G0.5 demonstrated an overall success rate of 98.9% on this Franka robot arm simulation benchmark, achieving the strongest performance on the challenging Long suite and outperforming π0.5 (96.9%) and GR00T-N1.7 (97.0%).

#### 4.3. Long-Horizon Tasks (2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge)

- **Context**: Evaluated on 50 full-length household mobile manipulation tasks using an R1-Pro robot in the OmniGibson simulator. The evaluation used a single policy checkpoint.
- **Training Efficiency**: G0.5 with only one epoch of post-training achieved a Task Success Score of 0.2904, surpassing π0.5 trained for four epochs (0.2626). With four epochs, G0.5 reached 0.3136, demonstrating superior learning efficiency.
- **Single-Policy Generalization**: G0.5 (4 epochs) outperformed the first-place solution (RLC, 0.2605) which used multiple checkpoints, indicating G0.5 learned a general whole-body control prior.
- **Architectural Contributions**: Structured action decomposition (decoupling navigation and manipulation) was beneficial for mobile manipulation. The pre-training data distribution influenced strengths; G0.5 excelled in pick-and-place tasks but showed a gap in container-interaction tasks (which narrowed with more training). Visual memory pre-training improved performance on navigation-intensive long-horizon tasks, even with single-frame post-training input.

#### 4.4. Real-World Fine-Tuning Evaluation (R1-Lite/Pro)

- **Context**: Fine-tuned and evaluated on R1-Lite (6-DoF dual-arm) and R1-Pro (7-DoF humanoid upper-body) platforms across four tasks (towel folding, carton folding, pencil-case packing, box transfer and stacking), covering six settings.
- **Performance**: G0.5 achieved an average success rate of 76.7% and an average process score of 129.2, significantly outperforming π0.5 (53.3% success, 105.2 process score) and GR00T-N1.7 (24.4% success, 68.9 process score).
- **Balance Across Embodiments**: G0.5 demonstrated balanced performance, adapting effectively to both R1-Lite (77.8% success) and R1-Pro (75.6% success).

#### 4.5. Pick-and-Place Benchmark (PP Bench)

- **Context**: Evaluated on an R1-Lite robot in cluttered scenes, measuring language following rate and task success rate for in-distribution and out-of-distribution objects.
- **Zero-shot and Post-training**: G0.5 exhibited strong zero-shot language following (65.6%) and task success (59.4%), which improved with increasing post-training data (50H: 84.4% language following, 75.0% task success).
- **Comparison with π0.5**: G0.5 consistently achieved higher language following and task success rates across all post-training scales, notably outperforming π0.5 at 50H (G0.5: 84.4% LF, 75.0% TS vs. π0.5: 68.8% LF, 65.6% TS).
- **Referring Context**: Augmenting the input with cropped visual regions of target objects and containers (+ Target Visual) significantly improved language following to 98.4% and task success to 84.4%, whereas textual coordinate tokens (+ Box Coord.) did not show improvement.

#### 4.6. Zero-Shot Probe of CoT and Action Head

- **Context**: A single pretrained G0.5 checkpoint was used, with only inference-time configuration changes (AR vs. FM head, CoT on/off) on PP Bench and two new 5-stage long-horizon tasks (Air Fryer, Cook Bacon).
- **CoT Impact**: CoT showed minor impact on single-stage PP Bench. However, on long-horizon tasks, CoT significantly improved progress scores and language following rates for AR (e.g., Air Fryer progress score 2.4 to 3.8).
- **AR vs. FM with CoT**: The AR head benefited more from CoT than the FM head in terms of progress score and language following, suggesting AR's direct attention to the CoT stream might be more effective than FM's conditioning on a pooled hidden state summary.
- **Qualitative Observation**: The exact wording of per-stage instructions (e.g., adverbial qualifiers, spatial cues, synonymous verbs) qualitatively affected AR+CoT rollouts on out-of-distribution tasks without retraining.

#### 4.7. G0.5 Fine-Tunes with RL Out of the Box

- **Context**: Comparison of AR and FM policies under low-data Reinforcement Learning (RL) (single demonstration per task on LIBERO) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- **Performance**: The AR policy converged substantially faster, achieved a higher final success rate, and trained more stably with lower variance than the FM policy. This was attributed to the AR policy's native likelihood parameterization, which facilitates ratio-based RL algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents G0.5, an autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that unifies perception, reasoning, and action within a single token stream and objective. The work aims to re-establish the autoregressive paradigm as a foundational approach for VLA models, challenging the prevailing trend of VLM-as-encoder architectures coupled with separate action experts.

The findings suggest several structural advantages for the autoregressive route:
- **Preservation of VLM Capabilities**: The model's performance on the Pick-and-Place benchmark indicates that autoregressive action supervision can protect and enhance the VLM's instruction-following ability, rather than degrading it. G0.5's zero-shot language-following rate surpassed that of a post-trained π0.5 baseline, supporting the hypothesis that a VLM acting as a direct decision-maker better leverages its inherent reasoning capacities.
- **Generalist Mobile Manipulation Priors**: The results from the 2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge demonstrate that the pretrained G0.5 representations provide generalist mobile manipulation priors. A single G0.5 checkpoint, even with fewer post-training epochs, outperformed both a π0.5 baseline (with more epochs) and the challenge winner (which used multiple checkpoints), indicating effective transfer and broad generalization.
- **Consistent Performance Advantage**: In real-world fine-tuning experiments on R1-Lite and R1-Pro robots, G0.5 consistently surpassed π0.5 and GR00T-N1.7 under matched compute and evaluation protocols. This suggests that the advantages over VLM-as-encoder architectures are robust and not merely attributable to differences in training budget.
- **Integrated Reasoning and Action**: The native chain-of-thought (CoT) stream, combined with the autoregressive action generation, enables the model to perform intermediate reasoning and directly feed these insights into subsequent actions. This integration appears to enhance grounding and execution, particularly in long-horizon, stage-conditioned tasks. The observation that autoregressive policies may leverage CoT more effectively than flow-matching policies suggests a potential benefit of direct architectural alignment.

The development of G0.5, with its cross-embodiment action codec, native CoT stream, and multi-second visual memory module, contributes to making autoregressive VLA practical and performant at scale. The release of the pretrained backbone offers a resource for the research community.

Potential impacts include:
- **Rethinking VLA Architecture**: The work provides empirical evidence to encourage a re-evaluation of VLM architectures, advocating for the VLM to retain its role as an autoregressive reasoner that can also act, remember, and adapt in context.
- **Enhanced Robot Learning**: By improving instruction following, generalization to unseen environments, and robust performance across diverse tasks and embodiments, G0.5 could contribute to more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Further Research Directions**: The study identifies several areas for future work, including addressing limitations in specific task types (e.g., drawer insertion, transparent objects), extending visual memory for longer horizons, and systematically exploring the emergent prompt-level controllability, where subtle changes in instruction wording can influence robot behavior without retraining.
