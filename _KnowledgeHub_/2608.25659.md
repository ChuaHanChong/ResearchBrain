# GaussianDream++: Efficient 3D Gaussian World Modeling for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GaussianDream++ is a policy-native 3D Gaussian world model for vision-language-action manipulation. It inserts 16 World State and 4 World Prediction Tokens into a PaliGemma/pi-0.5 backbone, where a training-only World Representation Head decodes them into a Current World reconstruction and coupled residual Future Prediction with static-dynamic factorization. It reaches 98.6% on LIBERO and 87.8% on LIBERO-Plus with no runtime Gaussian decoding.

## Problem

- Action-imitation objectives in VLA policies supervise only the target action, leaving metric 3D structure, visibility, and contact-relevant spatial relations encoded implicitly in 2D features.
- Geometry-enhanced policies anchor only the current scene, while predictive policies model futures in RGB or latent space, where visual coherence need not imply metrically consistent physical change.
- The predecessor GaussianDream builds a dense 1024-token prefix via a runtime VGGT/TGE geometry pathway that entangles current state, dynamics, and action conditioning, raising deployment cost.

## Method

- **GaussianDream++**'s ==World State Tokens== (16) and ==World Prediction Tokens== (4) are embedded in the ==PaliGemma== prefix, so the ==flow-matching== Action Expert reads world-supervised representations natively without any world-to-action projector.
- A training-only ==World Representation Head== decodes state tokens into a renderable Current World via ==metric-depth unprojection== with geometry-appearance decomposition, supervised by RGB, depth, alpha, and regularization losses through ==differentiable Gaussian splatting==.
- ==Coupled Future Prediction== adds horizon-conditioned center residuals gated by a ==static-dynamic motion coefficient== over shared Current World primitives, trained with rendering, depth, 3D flow, and static-consistency terms, all removed at deployment.

## Results

- Under the matched protocol it hits **98.6%** LIBERO average and **87.8%** LIBERO-Plus Overall, beating GaussianDream by **0.8** points and reproduced pi-0.5 by **2.3**, with Camera rising from **73.2%** to **80.1%**.
- Real dual-arm robot pooled success rises from **29.2%** to **52.5%** over reproduced pi-0.5 across **120** trials, including **25.0%** to **50.0%** under Layout and **22.5%** to **42.5%** under Camera shift.
- Ablations: tokens without Gaussian supervision give only **86.3%** Overall versus **87.8%** full; removing metric depth costs **0.9** points and drops Camera to **76.5%**; latency rises from **286ms** to **330ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymmetric design works: rich geometric supervision can live entirely in training, leaving deployment with 20 extra tokens and 1.15x latency instead of a runtime world model.
> - Capacity is not the mechanism: adding tokens alone gained 0.8 points, while the structured Gaussian objectives, especially metric depth, supplied most of the robustness improvement.
> - Caution: gains concentrate on viewpoint, layout, and noise shifts; embodiment-level Robot robustness stays below the plain baseline, so geometric supervision does not transfer across morphology.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. VLA policies aim to enable embodied agents to interpret natural language instructions and convert visual observations into precise physical actions. Recent advancements in this area have leveraged large-scale vision-language pretraining and extensive robot demonstrations, leading to progress in instruction following and object-level generalization.

Despite these advancements, existing VLA paradigms face challenges in effectively incorporating metric 3D structure, object-relative geometry, and short-horizon physical evolution. These aspects are often only implicitly constrained by 2D visual features and action labels, making policies susceptible to errors in precise manipulation tasks like grasping and placement. Furthermore, traditional behavior cloning methods may underutilize the rich 3D and temporal information present in robot trajectories.

To address these limitations, prior research has explored several directions:
- **Geometry-enhanced VLAs** integrate explicit 3D information such as depth maps, point clouds, stereo observations, or pretrained 3D representations. While these methods improve current-scene spatial grounding, they typically offer limited supervision regarding how the physical state evolves after interaction.
- **Predictive policies and World-Action Models** learn from future observations, trajectories, or latent dynamics to provide richer temporal supervision. However, visually plausible predictions in RGB or latent spaces do not consistently encode metrically accurate physical transitions. Additionally, maintaining large generative models or performing explicit future rollout during deployment can increase the computational cost of closed-loop control.
- **Predictive geometry approaches** attempt to bridge spatial grounding and temporal modeling by introducing predictive 3D geometric representations. The predecessor to this work, GaussianDream (Zhang et al., 2026), demonstrated that training-time current Gaussian reconstruction and future Gaussian prediction can provide effective 3D supervision. It employed an asymmetric design, removing Gaussian decoding heads during deployment to maintain efficiency.

GaussianDream, while effective, used a dense, 1024-token prefix constructed from temporal geometric features via VGGT (Vision-Gaussian-Geometry Transformer) and TGE (Temporal Gaussian Evolution) modules. This prefix jointly carried current geometry, future evolution, and action-conditioning information, and its online construction still required a dedicated temporal geometry pathway at runtime. GaussianDream++ builds upon this foundation by proposing a more compact, policy-native, and structured world representation to enhance efficiency and robustness without sacrificing performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of GaussianDream++ is to improve the performance and robustness of VLA policies for robotic manipulation by integrating a structured, compact, and policy-native 3D world representation, while maintaining efficient deployment. This is motivated by several identified limitations in previous approaches, particularly those of its predecessor, GaussianDream.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Enhance Representational Compactness**: GaussianDream utilized a dense 1024-token prefix that carried diverse information (current geometry, future evolution, action conditioning). The motivation for GaussianDream++ was to develop a "substantially smaller, role-structured representation" to improve efficiency. This is achieved by replacing the dense external prefix with a compact set of 20 specialized tokens directly embedded within the VLA backbone.
2. **Achieve Policy-Native Integration**: GaussianDream's prefix was external to the main VLA backbone, requiring a dedicated temporal geometry pathway for its construction and subsequent conditioning of the action policy. A key motivation for GaussianDream++ was to make the world representation "native to the VLA policy." By embedding "World State Tokens" and "World Prediction Tokens" directly into the PaliGemma backbone, the policy's Action Expert can access these world-supervised representations through its native attention mechanism without additional projection layers or external modules during inference.
3. **Implement Role-Structured World Representation**: The prior GaussianDream prefix entangled current state, future dynamics, and action-conditioning information within a single dense representation. GaussianDream++ aims to explicitly separate these concerns by assigning "World State Tokens" to represent the current physical scene and "World Prediction Tokens" to represent short-horizon evolution. This role-structured approach is intended to provide a clearer and more organized understanding of the physical environment.
4. **Incorporate Static–Dynamic Factorization**: Robotic manipulation tasks involve both persistent scene structures and interaction-induced dynamic changes. The motivation was to leverage this distinction to "reduce redundant temporal modeling and suppress spurious changes in static regions." GaussianDream++ achieves this by preserving the reconstructed Current World as a shared template and focusing residual motion prediction (from World Prediction Tokens) on interaction-relevant regions, complemented by static-consistency supervision.
5. **Maintain Asymmetric Training and Deployment Efficiency**: GaussianDream established the principle that explicit 3D world modeling can enhance a feed-forward VLA policy without operating as an online simulator. GaussianDream++ adheres to this principle, aiming to provide "dense physical supervision during learning" (reconstruction, differentiable rendering, metric depth, visibility, motion objectives) but removing all heavy components (World Representation Head, Gaussian renderer, auxiliary objectives, VGGT/TGE pathway) at deployment. This ensures the deployed policy remains lightweight and efficient, utilizing only the learned compact world tokens.
6. **Improve Robustness and Generalization**: A core motivation is to enhance the policy's robustness to distribution shifts, particularly those affecting geometry and layout (e.g., camera viewpoint changes, object arrangement variations), and to improve its transferability to real-world manipulation tasks. The structured 3D supervision is hypothesized to yield a more stable and generalizable physical understanding.

In summary, GaussianDream++ aims to internalize the benefits of Gaussian-based 3D world modeling into a more compact, efficient, and robust VLA policy by carefully designing its representation and leveraging an asymmetric training-deployment paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GaussianDream++ proposes a compact, policy-native extension to VLA backbones, integrating 3D Gaussian world modeling through a distinct architectural and training strategy. The method retains the asymmetric training-and-deployment paradigm of its predecessor, GaussianDream, but fundamentally reconfigures the world representation.

#### 3.1. Policy-Native World Representation

The core of GaussianDream++ involves embedding two groups of learnable tokens directly into a VLA backbone, specifically PaliGemma.
- **World State Tokens ($Z_S$)**: Consisting of 16 tokens ($N_s=16$), these tokens are designed to represent the current physical scene. They retain a coarse spatial organization and receive calibrated spatial and ray embeddings.
- **World Prediction Tokens ($Z_P$)**: Comprising 4 tokens ($N_p=4$), these tokens are associated with short-horizon evolution and prediction slots.

These tokens are compact latent summaries rather than direct one-to-one representations of Gaussian primitives. During processing, PaliGemma jointly contextualizes these world tokens with the original visual and language tokens, generating contextualized hidden states $(H_{VL_t}, H_{S_t}, H_{P_t})$. The Action Expert, utilizing a flow-matching-based architecture (from $\pi_{0.5}$), directly conditions on this complete world-token-augmented prefix, eliminating the need for an additional world-to-action projection module. This direct conditioning ensures that the representations shaped by Gaussian world supervision are directly accessible and utilized for action generation.

#### 3.2. Current World Reconstruction

During training, a lightweight World Representation Head decodes the contextualized hidden states of the World State Tokens ($H_{S_t}$) to produce an explicit Current World representation ($G_t$).
- **Gaussian Feature Field**: The World State Tokens' hidden states, which retain a coarse spatial organization, are expanded into a dense Gaussian feature field ($F_{G_t}$) by a state-decoding branch ($D_{state}$) within the World Representation Head.
- **Geometry–Appearance Decomposition**: The system decomposes geometry and appearance prediction into separate branches. A geometry branch ($H_{geo}$) predicts metric depth ($\bar{D}_t$) and non-appearance Gaussian attributes (scale, rotation, opacity). An appearance branch ($H_{app}$) predicts spherical-harmonic appearance coefficients from stop-gradient geometry features, optionally fused with current image features. This separation helps prevent photometric cues from inadvertently altering the metric scene structure.
- **Gaussian Centers**: The predicted metric depth is unprojected using calibrated camera parameters to derive the 3D centers ($\mu_{t_i}$) of the Gaussian primitives.
- **Current World Representation**: The reconstructed Current World ($G_t$) is a collection of Gaussian primitives, each defined by its center, scale, rotation, opacity, and appearance coefficients.
- **Supervision**: The Current World is supervised through differentiable Gaussian splatting, rendering RGB ($\hat{I}_t$), metric depth ($\hat{D}_t$), and accumulated Gaussian coverage ($\hat{A}_t$). The loss function ($L_{cur}$) combines photometric (RGB), metric depth, alpha (coverage), and regularization terms. Depth and alpha losses are applied only in regions with valid supervision, indicating that the objective is to constrain metric geometry, visibility, and object layout rather than achieving photorealistic reconstruction.

#### 3.3. Coupled Future Prediction

For short-horizon anticipation, GaussianDream++ predicts future states by building upon the reconstructed Current World.
- **Shared Primitive Template**: It maintains the Current World ($G_t$) as a shared Gaussian primitive template, predicting only horizon-dependent geometric changes rather than regenerating the entire scene. This preserves current-to-future correspondence.
- **Decoding Future Changes**: For each prediction horizon $h$, the World Prediction Tokens' representation ($H_{P_{t,h}}$) is combined with Current World features and a learnable horizon embedding ($e_h$) to predict 3D center displacements ($\Delta\mu_{h_i}$).
- **Static–Dynamic Factorization**: An optional motion coefficient ($m_{h_i} \in$) is predicted to modulate the displacement, allowing for static–dynamic factorization. This mechanism helps concentrate residual motion on interaction-relevant regions while maintaining persistent structure from the Current World for static elements. The future center is computed as $\mu_{t+h_i} = \mu_{t_i} + m_{h_i} \Delta\mu_{h_i}$. Other Gaussian attributes (scale, rotation, opacity, appearance) are inherited from the Current World.
- **Future World Representation**: The predicted Future World ($G_{t+h}$) consists of Gaussians with updated centers but inherited attributes.
- **Supervision**: Each Future World is rendered using its corresponding horizon-specific camera parameters. The future prediction loss ($L_{fut}$) includes terms for RGB, metric depth, 3D flow (motion), and static consistency, applied over valid horizons and geometric correspondences. Future observations serve solely as supervision targets and are never fed into the policy's forward pass. This setup enables learning anticipated short-horizon evolution without performing counterfactual, action-conditioned simulation.

#### 3.4. World-Action Learning and Efficient Deployment

- **Joint Optimization**: The overall training objective is a combination of the action generation loss ($L_{act}$, from $\pi_{0.5}$) and the Gaussian world supervision losses ($L_{cur}$ and $L_{fut}$). This joint optimization ensures that the policy-native World Tokens are both physically structured and functionally useful for control, preventing the representation from collapsing into unconstrained action-only features.
- **Asymmetric Deployment**: During deployment, the World Representation Head, Gaussian renderer, target-construction modules, and all auxiliary supervision branches are removed. The deployed policy only retains the PaliGemma backbone, the World State and World Prediction Tokens, and the action adapters. This means inference involves only the processing of 20 compact World Tokens within the native VLA backbone, without online Gaussian decoding, rendering, future rollout, or the dedicated runtime VGGT/TGE pathway used by GaussianDream.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GaussianDream++ was evaluated on standard robotic manipulation benchmarks and real-world scenarios, demonstrating improvements in performance, robustness, and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Performance on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus

- **LIBERO (Standard Tasks)**: GaussianDream++ achieved an average success rate of 98.6%. This result indicates competitive performance among strong published policies and represents a gain of 0.2 percentage points over GaussianDream and 1.7 percentage points over the reproduced $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline under the matched GaussianDream-family protocol.
- **LIBERO-Plus (Zero-Shot Robustness)**: The model achieved an 87.8% Overall success rate, outperforming other published zero-shot policies and the matched GaussianDream and reproduced $\pi_{0.5}$ baselines by 0.8 and 2.3 percentage points, respectively.

#### 4.2. Robustness under Geometry-Sensitive Shifts

GaussianDream++ exhibited notable improvements under specific distribution shifts in LIBERO-Plus:
- **Camera Shift**: Success rate improved from 73.2% ($\pi_{0.5}$ reproduced) to 80.1%, and from 77.3% (GaussianDream) to 80.1%. This 2.8-point gain over GaussianDream suggests enhanced robustness to viewpoint changes, consistent with the objective of robustly representing scene geometry.
- **Layout Shift**: Success rate increased from 87.7% ($\pi_{0.5}$ reproduced) to 90.0%, and from 88.4% (GaussianDream) to 90.0%. This 1.6-point gain over GaussianDream indicates improved handling of variations in object-relative spatial relationships.
- **Noise Shift**: The model improved from 89.9% ($\pi_{0.5}$ reproduced) to 94.2%, suggesting that dense Gaussian supervision provides structural constraint beneficial under image degradation.
- **Robot Shift**: Performance remained below reproduced $\pi_{0.5}$ for the Robot shift category, indicating that embodiment-level generalization presents a distinct challenge not fully addressed by the current approach.

#### 4.3. Real-World Manipulation

Experiments on a dual-arm physical robot platform further corroborated the simulation findings:
- **Overall Success**: GaussianDream++ improved the pooled success rate from 29.2% (reproduced $\pi_{0.5}$) to 52.5% across two tasks and three conditions, an absolute gain of 23.3 percentage points.
- **Task-Specific Gains**: For "Bowl-Proximity" (precision placement), success increased from 25.0% to 46.7%. For "Eggplant-to-Pink-Plate" (long-horizon, semantic disambiguation), it rose from 33.3% to 58.3%.
- **Real-World Shift Robustness**: Under real-world Layout variation, the pooled success rate doubled from 25.0% to 50.0%. Under real-world Camera variation, success increased from 22.5% to 42.5%. These results are consistent with the simulation gains in geometry-sensitive shifts, supporting the benefits of the learned representation for robust physical manipulation.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Visualizations of RGB and depth decoded from the policy-native world representations indicated that:
- **Current Reconstructions**: Decoded from World State Tokens, these preserved the dominant spatial organization of observed scenes, including robot configuration, tabletop structure, and approximate object locations. While fine appearance details were not perfectly replicated, the recovered depth structures confirmed that the compact World State Tokens encoded decodable geometric information.
- **Future Prediction**: Decoded jointly from World State and World Prediction Tokens, these predictions were moderately less detailed but maintained consistent scene-level geometry and object correspondence, reflecting the coupled formulation where the Current World provides a persistent template for predicting residual short-horizon evolution.

#### 4.5. Inference Efficiency

- **Latency**: GaussianDream++ increased action-chunk latency from 286 ms (reproduced $\pi_{0.5}$) to 330 ms in a matched local deployment setting. This 44 ms overhead, representing approximately 1.15 times the latency of $\pi_{0.5}$, is attributed to contextualizing the 20 additional World Tokens within PaliGemma. Critically, no online Gaussian primitive construction, rendering, or future rollout is performed.
- **Comparison to GaussianDream**: GaussianDream's reported latency (531 ms) included its dedicated runtime VGGT/TGE pathway. GaussianDream++ eliminates this pathway, suggesting a potential improvement in real-time efficiency over its predecessor due to its policy-native representation.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies systematically investigated the contributions of different design choices:
- **Representational Structure**: Adding World Tokens without Gaussian supervision provided a modest gain over $\pi_{0.5}$ (86.3% vs. 85.5% Overall), but full performance required structured Gaussian supervision. Sequential additions of Current World reconstruction, Future Prediction, coupled decoding, and static consistency gradually increased the Overall success rate to 87.8%, confirming the incremental value of each structural component.
- **Supervision Objectives**: Removing metric depth supervision caused the largest drop in Overall performance (to 86.9%), particularly affecting Camera and Layout robustness. Removing metric 3D flow also reduced Overall performance (to 87.2%), specifically weakening Noise and Layout robustness. Removing RGB rendering or alpha supervision led to smaller but distinct performance degradations, affecting Lighting/Background and visibility-sensitive shifts, respectively. These results underscore that the complementary nature of appearance, metric geometry, spatial coverage, and short-horizon motion objectives is crucial for robust policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GaussianDream++ contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a method for integrating explicit 3D world modeling into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies with improved efficiency and robustness.

The work's primary significance lies in demonstrating that the benefits of dense 3D and temporal supervision, as explored in GaussianDream, can be internalized into a substantially more compact and structured policy representation. By replacing a dense, externally constructed prefix with 20 role-specific tokens directly embedded within the VLA backbone, GaussianDream++ achieves a policy-native integration of world modeling. This architectural design eliminates the need for a dedicated runtime geometry pathway and online Gaussian decoding/rendering, leading to a modest inference overhead compared to base VLA policies, which is a practical consideration for real-time robotic control.

The empirical results, particularly the improved robustness under camera and layout shifts in simulation (LIBERO-Plus) and in real-world experiments, highlight the impact of the structured Gaussian supervision. This suggests that the policy develops a more stable and generalizable understanding of scene geometry and object configurations, which is critical for successful manipulation in varied and unpredictable environments. The static–dynamic factorization and coupled current/future prediction further refine this understanding by distinguishing persistent scene structure from interaction-induced changes, allocating predictive capacity more effectively.

Potential impacts of this research include:
- **Enabling More Robust Robotic Systems**: The demonstrated gains in robustness against common real-world variations (viewpoint changes, object rearrangements) suggest that GaussianDream++ can contribute to the development of more reliable and adaptable robotic manipulation systems.
- **Advancing VLA Policy Design**: The methodology provides a blueprint for how explicit physical reasoning can be incorporated into neural policies without compromising inference speed, offering an alternative to heavy generative world models or purely implicit visual feature learning. This could inspire future work on compact, physically-grounded representations for embodied AI.
- **Efficiency in Deployment**: The asymmetric training-deployment strategy offers a practical solution for leveraging computationally intensive 3D supervision during development while maintaining low latency during operation, making advanced world modeling more accessible for real-world applications.

In conclusion, GaussianDream++ represents a step towards more physically-grounded, robust, and efficient VLA policies, addressing a critical challenge in general-purpose robotic manipulation.
