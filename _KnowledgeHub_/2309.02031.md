# A survey on efficient vision transformers: algorithms, techniques, and performance benchmarking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sapienza University of Rome and The University of Sydney present a comprehensive survey of efficient Vision Transformer (ViT) strategies, classifying them into four distinct categories and introducing a new "Efficient Error Rate" (EER) metric for normalized evaluation. The work benchmarks various models, identifying TinyViT-21M (Knowledge Distillation) as highly efficient with 83.1% Top-1 accuracy and 43.5% EER_all, and Castling-MViTv2-T (Compact Architecture) as providing a balanced trade-off with 84.1% Top-1 accuracy and 45.6% EER_all.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) achieve high performance in computer vision tasks but suffer from significant computational and memory demands, largely due to the quadratic scaling of their self-attention mechanism.
- The high resource consumption of large ViT models hinders their practical deployment on resource-constrained devices like embedded systems, mobile platforms, and edge AI applications.
- Existing surveys offered limited focus specifically on the architectural and algorithmic efficiency of ViTs for inference, lacking a comprehensive categorization and comparative evaluation of these methods.

## Method

- Systematically categorized efficient Vision Transformer (ViT) strategies into four main groups: ==Compact Architecture==, ==Pruning==, ==Knowledge Distillation==, and ==Quantization==.
- Provided mathematical formulations and detailed reviews of state-of-the-art methodologies within each efficiency category, illustrating their evolution and mechanisms.
- Introduced a novel =="Efficient Error Rate (EER)" metric== to offer a normalized and holistic benchmarking tool, accounting for multiple hardware-affecting features like parameters, FLOPs, bits, and model size during inference.

## Results

- Castling-MViTv2-T (Compact Architecture) demonstrated the best Paretian optimality, balancing **84.1%** Top-1 accuracy with an EER_all of **45.6%** across various hardware-affecting metrics.
- TinyViT-21M (Knowledge Distillation) was identified as the most overall efficient, achieving **83.1%** Top-1 accuracy with the lowest EER_all of **43.5%**.
- NoisyQuant-PTQ4ViT-Swin-B (Quantization) delivered the highest Top-1 accuracy at **84.7%** and smallest model size (**66.0MB**) among the top candidates, offering a strong option where memory footprint is a primary concern.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The four identified efficiency strategies offer distinct trade-offs between estimation performance and resource consumption, which the EER metric effectively unifies for comparison.
> - Compact Architecture (CA) models and Knowledge Distillation (KD) strategies generally achieve the most balanced trade-offs between high accuracy and overall efficiency for general-purpose applications.
> - Quantization (Q) provides the most drastic reductions in memory footprint and inference speed but its efficacy depends on the ViT architecture's robustness and often requires specialized hardware support for optimal gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of artificial intelligence (AI), specifically focusing on deep learning solutions in computer vision (CV). Traditionally, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) dominated CV tasks. However, the introduction and subsequent rise of Transformer models, initially groundbreaking in Natural Language Processing (NLP), have demonstrated superior performance in CV, primarily due to their self-attention mechanism's ability to extract global information and capture long-range dependencies. Vision Transformers (ViTs), pioneered by Dosovitskiy et al. (2020), have pushed the performance boundaries in image recognition.

Despite their exceptional performance, a significant challenge for ViTs is their high computational and memory cost. The self-attention mechanism, while powerful, scales quadratically with image resolution, making large ViT models computationally demanding and difficult to deploy on resource-constrained devices like embedded systems, autonomous vehicles, or low-power edge devices. This inherent inefficiency hinders their widespread real-world application, especially for real-time tasks requiring low latency.

This survey addresses this critical gap by systematically analyzing methodologies designed to make ViTs more efficient. While prior surveys (e.g., Han et al., 2022; Tay et al., 2022; Khan et al., 2022) have offered general overviews of Transformers in both NLP and CV, they typically dedicate only limited sections to computational efficiency in ViTs. More recently, Zhuang et al. (2023) focused on efficient *training* of Transformers. In contrast, Papa et al.'s work carves out a unique niche by:
- **Exclusive Focus:** Concentrating entirely on the *architectural and algorithmic efficiency* of ViTs for inference, providing a comprehensive overview of strategies to reduce their computational and memory footprint.
- **Systematic Categorization:** Organizing the diverse range of efficient ViT techniques into four distinct and well-defined categories (Compact Architecture, Pruning, Knowledge Distillation, and Quantization), offering a structured framework for understanding the field.
- **Novel Evaluation Metric:** Introducing the "Efficient Error Rate (EER)" to provide a normalized and more holistic comparison of models, considering multiple hardware-affecting features beyond just FLOPs or parameters.

By offering an in-depth, focused analysis and a new comparative tool, this survey directly contributes to the ongoing effort to bridge the gap between high-performance, resource-intensive ViTs and the practical demands of real-world AI applications, thereby accelerating the deployment of next-generation vision models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this survey are driven by the inherent trade-off between the superior performance of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and their significant computational and memory demands. The authors clearly articulate their goals:

1. **Address the Deployment Challenge:** The core motivation stems from the difficulty of deploying large, standard ViT architectures in real-world applications. Factors such as hardware limitations (processing and computational capabilities) and environmental restrictions (power consumption, thermal management) make the quadratic scaling of self-attention with image resolution a major bottleneck. The authors aim to investigate methodologies that enable ViT deployment while ensuring "sub-optimal estimation performances" – likely a typo, intended as "optimal" or "satisfactory" estimation performances under efficiency constraints.
2. **Provide a Comprehensive Overview of Efficient ViT Methodologies:** The paper seeks to offer an in-depth analysis of the most recent solutions for designing efficient ViT models. This includes mathematically defining these strategies, describing state-of-the-art techniques, and discussing their performance across various application scenarios.
3. **Categorize and Organize the Research Field:** To bring structure to the rapidly growing body of work on efficient ViTs, the authors categorize existing approaches into four distinct groups: Compact Architecture (CA), Pruning (P), Knowledge Distillation (KD), and Quantization (Q). This categorization facilitates a clearer understanding of different optimization paradigms.
4. **Introduce a Novel Performance Benchmarking Metric:** A crucial objective is the introduction of the "Efficient Error Rate (EER)." This new metric is designed to normalize and comprehensively compare models based on features that impact hardware at inference time, such as the number of parameters, bits, FLOPs, and model size. The EER aims to provide a fairer and more holistic evaluation of efficiency across diverse methods.
5. **Identify Best Trade-offs and Future Directions:** Through their analysis, the authors intend to identify which strategies best balance EER and estimation capabilities (accuracy). Furthermore, they aim to discuss open challenges and pinpoint promising research directions to guide future work in the field.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the theoretical promise of ViTs and their practical utility, making them accessible for ubiquitous deployment in scenarios where computational resources are limited.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a structured and comprehensive approach to survey efficient Vision Transformer strategies. Their methodology involves several key steps:

1. **Categorization of Efficiency Strategies:** The core of their approach is the classification of efficient deep learning algorithms for ViT structures into four distinct categories:
 - **Compact Architecture (CA):** Focuses on designing inherently smaller and computationally lighter ViT architectures, often by modifying or linearizing the self-attention mechanism to reduce its quadratic computational cost.
 - **Pruning (P):** Involves reducing the number of neurons and connections (weights) in a trained ViT model, typically by setting less important weights to zero, thereby creating sparser models with fewer operations during inference.
 - **Knowledge Distillation (KD):** A learning strategy where a smaller, "student" model is trained to mimic the outputs and behaviors of a larger, pre-trained "teacher" model, thereby transferring knowledge and improving the student's performance without increasing its size.
 - **Quantization (Q):** Aims to reduce the precision of ViT's weights and activation functions from higher-precision floating-point numbers (e.g., 32-bit) to lower-bit integer representations (e.g., 8-bit, 4-bit), significantly reducing memory footprint and speeding up computations.

2. **Background and Mathematical Formulation:** For each of the four categories, the paper provides a foundational "Background" section (Section 2). This section defines preliminary notions and offers mathematical formulations of the core concepts, such as the vanilla self-attention mechanism, the general pruning algorithm, the knowledge distillation loss function, and uniform quantization. This mathematical grounding helps to highlight the specific innovations introduced by state-of-the-art research in Section 3.

3. **Detailed Review of State-of-the-Art Methodologies:** Section 3 systematically reviews proposed architectures and strategies within each category. The methodologies are presented chronologically to illustrate their evolution and the gradual reduction of computational costs. The review discusses the strengths and weaknesses of each technique, explaining how they achieve efficiency (e.g., attention linearization, softmax-free attention, patch slimming, distillation tokens, mixed-precision quantization).

4. **Performance Benchmarking and Introduction of EER:**
 - **Standard Datasets:** The authors compare the estimation performances of the reviewed models primarily on the ImageNet-1K classification dataset (1.3M training, 50K validation images, 1K classes, 224x224 resolution). For CA strategies, additional results on COCO (object detection/instance segmentation) and ADE20K (semantic segmentation) datasets are provided in supplementary material.
 - **Evaluation Metrics:** Standard metrics are used: Top-1 accuracy for classification, average precision (APbox, APmask) for object detection/instance segmentation, and mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) for semantic segmentation. Efficiency metrics include the number of trainable parameters (#Par.), Floating Point Operations (FLOPs), weight/activation bit-widths (#Bit), and model size (Size).
 - **Efficient Error Rate (EER):** A novel metric is introduced, defined as EER = (1/||i||) \* Σ(Mi/Ri), where 'i' is the set of efficiency metrics (e.g., #Par., FLOPs, Size, #Bit), Mi is the value for the efficient model, and Ri is the value for a reference baseline model. The ViT-B/16 architecture is chosen as the baseline (R) for the ImageNet-1K classification task due to its representativeness as a shallower, non-optimized ViT. The EER aims to provide a normalized, single-value measure of efficiency, with lower EER indicating higher efficiency. A weighted variant of EER is also proposed to allow for prioritizing certain parameters.

5. **Comparative Analysis and Identification of Trade-offs:** The performance results are presented in tables within Section 4, enabling a quantitative comparison. The authors identify models that achieve the best "Paretian optimality" – the best trade-off between accuracy and efficiency (as measured by EER) – within each category and then across all categories.

6. **Discussion of Open Challenges and Future Directions:** The paper concludes by discussing limitations observed in current research, such as the heavy reliance on classification tasks, the need for more specialized evaluation metrics tailored for resource-constrained devices, and the underexplored potential of combining multiple efficiency strategies. These discussions aim to guide future research.

This comprehensive methodology allows the survey to not only summarize existing work but also to critically analyze, compare, and provide forward-looking insights into the field of efficient Vision Transformers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's analysis across the four categories of efficient Vision Transformer (ViT) strategies—Compact Architecture (CA), Pruning (P), Knowledge Distillation (KD), and Quantization (Q)—yields several key findings regarding their trade-offs between performance and efficiency, as quantified by traditional metrics and the newly introduced Efficient Error Rate (EER).

#### 4.1. Compact Architecture (CA) Strategies

- **Focus:** These methods primarily aim to reduce or linearize the quadratic computational cost of the self-attention mechanism.
- **Evolution:** Early approaches like PVT (Spatial-Reduction Attention) and Swin Transformer (shifted window attention) reduced quadratic cost but didn't achieve full linearity. Later works like SimA (softmax-free adaptive), Flowformer, Hydra Attention, Castling-ViT, and EfficientViT moved towards more linear computational complexities. PoolFormer demonstrated the effectiveness of non-parametric pooling.
- **Performance:** The MViTv2-L model achieved the highest accuracy (85.3% Top-1) but also the highest EER, indicating its heavier nature. For optimal trade-off, **Castling-MViTv2-T** emerged as a strong candidate, delivering 84.1% Top-1 accuracy with a significantly lower EER (26.7%), offering a 9.2x EER gain compared to MViTv2-L while maintaining comparable accuracy. For extremely constrained devices, models like EfficientViT-B1 and Castling-LeViT-128 showed promising low FLOPs (around 0.5G) and EERs (6.7-7.4%) with respectable accuracy (~79.5%).

#### 4.2. Pruning (P) Strategies

- **Focus:** Pruning aims to reduce the number of active connections and neurons by setting less important weights to zero, reducing FLOPs during inference without necessarily changing the parameter count.
- **Evolution:** Techniques like VTP focused on dimension-wise sparsity, while PS-ViT/DPS-ViT introduced patch slimming. WDPruning addressed both width and depth. More advanced methods like NViT (Hessian-based global pruning) and X-Pruner (XAI-based layer-wise pruning) refined importance score computation. DynamicViT uniquely combines pruning with Knowledge Distillation.
- **Performance:** Pruning is often applied to existing architectures (DeiT, Swin, LV-ViT). X-Pruner, for example, achieved an average 29.9% FLOPs reduction on Swin and 51.3% on DeiT with limited accuracy drops. **DynamicViT-LV-S/07** (leveraging a P+KD approach) was identified as the best trade-off, achieving 83.0% Top-1 accuracy with an EER of 28.9%, outperforming other pruning methods in balancing accuracy and efficiency. X-Pruner on DeiT-Ti offered extremely low EER (4.6%) for highly restricted computational settings, albeit with lower accuracy (71.1%).

#### 4.3. Knowledge Distillation (KD) Strategies

- **Focus:** KD transfers knowledge from a larger teacher model to a smaller student model, enabling the student to achieve higher performance than it would with direct training alone, without increasing its intrinsic size.
- **Evolution:** DeiT introduced the concept for ViTs with a distillation token. Subsequent works like Manifold Distillation (fine-grained patch-level), TinyViT (memory-efficient KD), DearKD (data-efficient), CivT (multi-teacher), and MiniViT (weight multiplexing) further refined KD for ViT students.
- **Performance:** KD consistently improved student model accuracy. Manifold Distillation and DearKD notably improved DeiT variants by 1.5-1.7% Top-1 accuracy. **TinyViT-21M** was identified as the most efficient model in this category, achieving 83.1% Top-1 accuracy with a low EER of 24.3%, representing an excellent balance of performance and computational lightness. Mini-Deit-Ti offered an option for extreme efficiency with very low EER (5.4%) at 72.8% accuracy.

#### 4.4. Quantization (Q) Strategies

- **Focus:** Quantization reduces the bit-width of weights and activations, drastically cutting memory and accelerating inference without changing the number of parameters.
- **Challenges:** Extreme quantization (e.g., 4-bit) can lead to significant accuracy drops, especially for smaller models. However, deeper architectures show more resilience.
- **Evolution:** Initial works like Liu et al. explored mixed-precision post-training quantization. PTQ4ViT introduced twin uniform quantization, while APQ-ViT focused on Matthew-effect preserving quantization for ultra-low bit-widths. NoisyQuant improved activation quantization with a noisy bias, and Auto-ViT-Acc developed FPGA-specific strategies. GPUSQ-ViT combined Q with P and KD for GPU-friendly deployment.
- **Performance:** Generally, 6/8-bit quantization offered a good balance for shallower models, while 4-bit could be viable for deeper ones (e.g., Swin-B achieving 76.5% at 4-bit). **NoisyQuant-PTQ4ViT-Swin-B (6-bit)** was deemed the most efficient Q model, achieving 84.7% Top-1 accuracy with a compact model size of 66.0MB and an EER of 19.4%. This highlights that a slightly larger but quantizable model (Swin-B has 88M parameters) can achieve very high accuracy and a small memory footprint.

#### 4.5. Overall Classification Performances and Paretian Optimality

- **Inter-Category Comparison:** When comparing the best performing models from each category (Table 8), a nuanced picture emerges.
 - **NoisyQuant-PTQ4ViT-Swin-B (Q)** achieved the highest Top-1 accuracy (84.7%) and smallest model size (66.0MB) among the top candidates, but its FLOPs are higher (15.4G) than some others, and its full efficiency might require specialized hardware. It has an EER_all of 56.5%.
 - **TinyViT-21M (KD)** demonstrated remarkable overall efficiency with the lowest EER_all (43.5%), achieving 83.1% Top-1 accuracy with low parameters (21.0M), FLOPs (4.3G), and a reasonable size (84.0MB).
 - **DynamicViT-LV-S/07 (P+KD)** also performed well, balancing accuracy (83.0%) with efficiency (EER_all of 47.4%).
- **Paretian Optimality (General Purpose):** For general-purpose settings, the **Castling-MViTv2-T (CA)** model was identified as achieving the best Paretian optimality between Top-1 accuracy (84.1%) and EER_all (45.6%). While not excelling in any single metric, it provides the most balanced trade-off between high accuracy and overall efficiency across all considered metrics in a hardware-agnostic manner.

In summary, the findings underscore that while quantization offers the most direct path to memory and computational savings, its efficacy depends on the model's robustness and hardware support. Compact architectures provide inherent efficiency, and knowledge distillation can significantly boost smaller models. Pruning offers a way to reduce inference-time operations in existing models. The EER metric proved valuable in quantitatively comparing these diverse approaches on a unified scale.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey on efficient Vision Transformers holds significant importance for both academic research and practical applications, primarily by systematically addressing the critical bottleneck of computational cost in high-performing ViT models. Its potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Guiding Future Research:** By categorizing, mathematically formalizing, and critically reviewing the vast landscape of efficient ViT strategies (CA, P, KD, Q), the paper provides a clear roadmap for researchers. It distills the core ideas, evolution, and state-of-the-art within each domain, enabling new researchers to quickly grasp the field and experienced ones to identify untapped opportunities.
2. **Facilitating Real-World Deployment:** The most direct impact is on accelerating the deployment of ViTs in resource-constrained environments. By showcasing and comparing methodologies that drastically reduce model size, FLOPs, and bit-precision, the survey empowers practitioners to select optimal architectures for embedded systems, mobile devices, autonomous vehicles, and other edge AI applications where energy consumption, latency, and memory footprint are paramount.
3. **Standardizing Efficiency Evaluation:** The introduction of the **Efficient Error Rate (EER)** is a significant contribution. It provides a novel, normalized, and comprehensive metric for comparing diverse efficiency techniques across multiple hardware-affecting parameters. This metric can foster more standardized and holistic benchmarking in the future, moving beyond single-point comparisons like FLOPs or parameter counts, and allowing for a better understanding of true hardware performance trade-offs. The flexibility of a weighted EER further enhances its applicability to specific real-world priorities.
4. **Highlighting Open Challenges and Promising Directions:** The discussion section is forward-looking, pinpointing crucial areas for future development:
 - **Broader Application Scenarios:** Encouraging research beyond traditional classification tasks to evaluate efficient ViTs in more complex computer vision domains (e.g., dense prediction, video analysis, 3D vision, robustness to adversarial attacks).
 - **Hardware-Specific Benchmarking:** Advocating for the development of benchmark tests on actual resource-constrained hardware (e.g., FPGAs, mobile GPUs, custom accelerators). This would provide more realistic insights into the degradation effects of optimizations and true inference-time performance, moving beyond theoretical FLOPs.
 - **Merging Multiple Strategies:** Emphasizing the underexplored potential of combining different efficiency techniques (e.g., quantization-aware pruning with knowledge distillation). The success of combined methods like DynamicViT suggests that hybrid approaches can yield superior accuracy-efficiency trade-offs, which is a significant research avenue.

In conclusion, this survey serves as a valuable resource for navigating the complexities of ViT efficiency. It not only consolidates existing knowledge but also provides analytical tools and clear directions to foster the development of a new generation of high-performing *and* practical Vision Transformers, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and sustainable across a wider range of applications.
