# CoRAL: Contact-Rich Adaptive LLM-based Control for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CoRAL introduces a neuro-symbolic framework for zero-shot, contact-rich robotic manipulation, decoupling high-level LLM reasoning from low-level control with online physical parameter adaptation and strategic refinement. The system demonstrated superior performance, achieving over 50% higher average success rates than VLA baselines on unseen contact-rich tasks in simulation and robust real-world transfer.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks struggle with contact-rich manipulation due to reliance on extensive demonstration data and a lack of explicit physical grounding or adaptive control.
- Existing VLA models are brittle and exhibit limited generalizability to novel physical situations that demand precise force management and adaptive strategies.
- The black-box nature of end-to-end VLA systems hinders explainability and makes adaptation to unforeseen physical dynamics challenging.

## Method

- **CoRAL** is a ==neuro-symbolic framework== that decouples high-level LLM/VLM reasoning from low-level, physically grounded control for zero-shot contact-rich manipulation.
- LLMs are employed as dynamic 'cost designers' and online diagnosticians for a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (**MPPI**) motion planner, creating adaptable objective functions.
- The framework incorporates nested feedback loops for ==online adaptation==, refining physical parameters via system identification and iteratively adjusting contact strategies based on real-time interaction feedback.

## Results

- CoRAL achieved high success rates across six challenging manipulation tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines (OpenVLA, π0.5) and the L2R cost-generation method, boosting success rates over **50%** on average in unseen contact-rich scenarios.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical Franka Emika Panda robot, effectively handling real-world uncertainties, such as varying friction, and regulating interaction forces.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of online refinement for diagnosing and correcting physical inaccuracies and the necessity of a dedicated pose estimator and modular VLM/LLM architecture for robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly separating high-level semantic reasoning (LLM/VLM) from low-level adaptive control significantly enhances robustness for contact-rich tasks, overcoming limitations of end-to-end VLA policies.
> - LLMs can effectively function as dynamic cost designers and online diagnosticians, providing adaptable task objectives and enabling real-time error correction and strategy refinement.
> - Online adaptation of physical parameters and strategic plans is crucial for overcoming initial uncertainties, handling sim-to-real gaps, and achieving successful deployment in dynamic real-world environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly exploring the integration of large foundation models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), to enhance robotic capabilities. These models have demonstrated abilities in high-level reasoning, semantic understanding, and multimodal processing, leading to their application in robotic control pipelines, primarily through imitation learning in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems. Examples include OpenVLA, π0, and RT-X, which aim to map multimodal inputs directly to low-level actions.

However, a significant challenge within this landscape is the effective handling of contact-rich manipulation tasks. These tasks, which are prevalent in daily interactions, require not only precise trajectory planning but also sophisticated interaction force management and adaptive control strategies. Existing VLA frameworks, often trained on large datasets of demonstrations, struggle with these scenarios due to their inherent lack of explicit physical grounding and inability to perform real-time adaptive control when faced with novel physics or unmodeled interactions. They typically rely on geometric collision avoidance rather than active regulation of extrinsic contact forces.

To address these limitations, a growing trend involves decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level control. Approaches like ThinkAct, Inner Monologue, and ECoT use LLMs to generate reasoning steps that guide separate, learned policies. Other works, such as VoxPoser, IMPACT, and VLMPC, utilize VLMs to generate static 3D cost maps or guide model predictive control (MPC) loops for planners. Language-to-Rewards (L2R) specifically generates reward functions for real-time MPC based on language.

CoRAL positions itself within this emerging trend of decoupled reasoning, but with a distinct neuro-symbolic approach. It aims to bridge the gap between the semantic understanding of foundation models and the physical requirements of contact-rich tasks. Unlike end-to-end VLA models that learn fixed policies from data, or static cost-generation methods that lack online adaptation, CoRAL integrates LLMs not as direct controllers but as adaptive cost designers for a high-frequency motion planner, enabling zero-shot planning and real-time physical adaptation without extensive prior demonstrations. It also differentiates itself by combining explicit LLM-driven strategy formulation with a dynamic, reactive controller that leverages real-time tactile and force feedback.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic manipulation framework capable of performing complex, contact-rich tasks in a zero-shot setting, effectively bridging the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level adaptive physical control. The motivation for CoRAL arises from several limitations observed in current robotic systems, particularly those incorporating foundation models:

- **Lack of Physical Grounding in Foundation Models**: While LLMs and VLMs excel at high-level reasoning and semantic understanding, they lack an inherent explicit physical understanding necessary for nuanced, contact-rich interactions. Directly applying them as controllers often results in brittle performance when physical parameters are uncertain or dynamic contact forces are critical.
- **Inability of End-to-End Policies for Adaptive Control**: Existing VLA frameworks, which typically learn general-purpose policies through imitation, struggle to generalize to novel physical scenarios involving complex contact dynamics. These methods are data-dependent and often cannot adapt their strategies in real-time to unforeseen physical deviations or unmodeled environmental effects. Tasks requiring active force regulation or the use of environmental fixtures (e.g., pushing an object against a wall to flip it) are particularly challenging for these systems.
- **Data Scarcity for Contact-Rich Tasks**: Achieving proficiency in contact-rich tasks typically requires extensive training data, often collected through labor-intensive teleoperation or detailed dynamic modeling. This creates a data bottleneck and limits generalizability to new objects or environments. The authors are motivated to explore zero-shot or few-shot learning paradigms that reduce this reliance on large, specialized datasets.
- **Fragility of Static Planning**: Methods that use foundation models to generate static cost maps or reward functions for planners often rely on fixed physical assumptions. They lack mechanisms to correct for model mismatches or adapt their strategies during execution when initial estimates are imperfect or the environment changes.
- **Need for Explainability and Robustness**: Black-box policies offered by many end-to-end models lack transparency, making it difficult to diagnose failures or understand the system's reasoning. A modular, interpretable approach is desired for more robust and trustworthy robotic systems.

To address these motivations, CoRAL sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

1. **Zero-Shot Planning for Contact-Rich Tasks**: Enable robots to perform dynamic, contact- and force-critical manipulation tasks based on natural language instructions and visual input, without requiring prior demonstrations or task-specific training.
2. **Adaptive Control through LLM-driven Cost Design**: Utilize LLMs not as direct action generators, but as "cost designers" that synthesize context-aware, adaptive objective functions for a high-frequency motion planner (MPPI), allowing for real-time adjustment of control strategies.
3. **Neuro-Symbolic Physical Understanding**: Introduce a mechanism for online system identification where VLMs provide semantic priors for environmental dynamics (e.g., mass, friction), which are then explicitly refined in real-time based on interaction feedback.
4. **Hierarchical and Modular Architecture**: Decouple high-level semantic reasoning from low-level reactive execution to ensure real-time control stability and maintain robustness even with imperfect initial visual estimates or slow LLM inference.
5. **Long-Term Adaptability and Experience Reuse**: Incorporate a retrieval-based memory unit to store and reuse successful physical parameters and contact strategies, facilitating faster adaptation and improved performance on semantically similar recurrent tasks.
6. **Enhanced Explainability**: Leverage the modular neuro-symbolic design to provide transparency, allowing the system to articulate diagnoses of failures and explain corrective actions taken during online adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoRAL is presented as a neuro-symbolic framework for zero-shot, contact-rich manipulation, characterized by its modular architecture and nested feedback loops. The system integrates various components that work synergistically to achieve adaptive control.

**A. Environment Perception and World Model Initialization**
This initial stage translates raw visual, textual, and geometric inputs into a structured, physics-aware internal world model.
1. **Pose Estimation and Tracking**: FoundationPose, a 6-DoF pose estimation model, is used to continuously track the poses of interactable objects from RGB-D camera images and known 3D geometric models. This provides geometric state information.
2. **Semantic Physics Priors**: A VLM generates semantic priors for physical properties (e.g., mass, friction) based on visual input and task context. These priors, while not ground truth, initialize the physical parameters in the world model, which are then subject to refinement. This approach acknowledges that direct, accurate estimation of dynamic parameters from static images is ill-posed.
The output of this pipeline is a set of world parameters (θ) for each object, including its semantic label, tracked pose, and semantic physics priors. These parameters are used to initialize and update the 'Planning World' used by the MPPI. The framework is designed to be parameter-agnostic, allowing for extensions to other physical properties.

**B. LLM-driven Task Formulation and Memory Retrieval**
This module formulates a concrete plan, leveraging past experiences if available.
1. **Memory Retrieval**: Before generating a new plan, the system queries a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) based memory unit. This unit stores successful "experience episodes," indexed by task definitions and environmental parameters. If a sufficiently similar and successful past experience is retrieved, its stored cost function (J_mem) and contact strategy (C_mem) are used, bypassing the initial LLM call.
2. **Plan Generation from Scratch**: If no suitable memory is found, an 'LLM (Task Formulation)' module acts as a high-level strategist. It translates the natural language task (T) and world parameters (θ) into an initial plan (J_0, C_0):
 - **Initial MPPI Cost Function (J_0)**: The LLM generates the mathematical structure and relative weights of a cost function (e.g., balancing distance-to-target, contact incentives, control effort penalties). This cost function can include any terms derivable from available state, pose, and action variables, and can specify conditions for transitioning between task stages.
 - **Initial Contact Strategy (C_0)**: The LLM predicts a coarse region of interest for contact, outputting a nominal reference point (c_ref) and a spatial expansion factor (r). A procedural helper then constructs an ellipsoid to define a valid contact manifold. Candidate target points are sampled from this manifold, and integrated into J_0 as soft cost attractors, biasing MPPI sampling towards semantically relevant regions while allowing dynamic optimization.

**C. Reactive Planning and Execution (The Inner Loop)**
This is a high-frequency, reactive execution cycle (10Hz) controlled by the MPPI controller, forming the inner loop of the framework.
- **Hierarchical Control Architecture**: To manage LLM inference latency, control is hierarchical:
 - **Tier 1 (Hardware)**: Joint impedance control (1kHz) ensures safety and compliance.
 - **Tier 2 (Trajectory)**: MPPI (10Hz) plans trajectories based on the current cost function.
 - **Tier 3 (Reasoning)**: The LLM operates asynchronously (∼1Hz) to update cost structures periodically.
- **MPPI Formulation**: MPPI solves a stochastic optimal control problem. Given a state-transition model and an LLM-generated running cost function (J_t), it samples K control sequences, evaluates their costs through rollouts in the 'Planning World', and computes an exponentially weighted average of perturbations to update the control sequence. Only the first action of the optimized sequence is executed.
- **Reactive Control Augmentation**: To mitigate the sim-to-real gap, the nominal planned action (u_t) is augmented with a real-time feedback term based on the error between desired and measured states (e.g., force/torque, proprioception), enabling continuous re-planning and correction.

**D. Online Adaptation via LLM-driven Refinement (The Outer Loop)**
After a predefined number of failed attempts (N_retry), the low-frequency outer loop is triggered, invoking the 'LLM (Online Adaptation)' module.
- **Diagnostician and Re-strategist**: This LLM analyzes logged episode data (E_t), which includes interaction history, used cost functions, and estimated physical parameters. Based on this context, it performs two functions:
 - **World Model Correction**: The LLM acts as a system identification agent, refining semantic priors by updating estimated physical parameters (θ) in the 'Planning World' (e.g., correcting an underestimated mass if an object moves less than expected).
 - **Strategy Refinement**: The LLM can modify the plan by adjusting cost function weights or proposing new contact strategies (J_t, C_t).
This refined world model and plan are fed back into the inner loop, enabling the system to learn from failures and adapt its approach mid-task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulated (ROBOSUITE/MUJOCO) and real-world (Franka Emika Panda robot) environments across a suite of six challenging manipulation tasks, including novel contact- and force-critical problems. Performance was primarily measured by success rate over 10 trials.

**A. State-of-the-Art Comparison (RQ1)**
CoRAL demonstrated superior performance compared to state-of-the-art VLA baselines and foundation-model-based planners, especially in tasks requiring complex physical reasoning.
- **VLA Baselines (OpenVLA-OFT, π0.5)**: These models performed well on simpler pick-and-place tasks (T2: Pick Box, T3: Pick and Place in Clutter, achieving 9/10 to 10/10 success), but their success rates drastically dropped (0/10 to 3/10) on contact-rich tasks (T1: Push and Pick Cutting Board, T4: Push with Constant Force, T5: Flip Box, T6: Flip with Wall). This indicates their limitation in generalizing to scenarios demanding explicit physical modeling or non-obvious maneuvers.
- **Cost-Generation Baseline (L2R)**: While better than VLA on some complex tasks (e.g., T4: 5/10, T5: 4/10), L2R exhibited fragility due to its reliance on static cost functions and lack of online adaptation or explicit contact strategy formulation. It achieved only 1/10 on T6.
- **CoRAL Performance**: CoRAL significantly outperformed all baselines in contact-rich tasks, achieving 5/10 on T1, 9/10 on T4, 9/10 on T5, and 7/10 on T6. It matched baseline performance on T2 and T3 (10/10 success). This suggests its integrated adaptive cost design and contact strategy enabled robust zero-shot execution across dynamic interaction regimes.

**B. Comparison to Human-Designed Cost Functions**
Two human expert baselines were used to approximate an upper bound for performance:
- **Expert (single-stage)**: Achieved strong performance on simpler tasks (T2-T4: 9/10 to 10/10) but struggled with multi-stage or highly contact-critical tasks (T1: 0/10, T6: 3/10).
- **Expert (FSM)**: The most robust, achieving high success rates (8/10 to 10/10) across all tasks by allowing explicit finite-state machines with phase-specific costs.
CoRAL's performance, while generally below the Expert (FSM) upper bound, significantly narrowed the gap on harder tasks (e.g., T5: 9/10 vs. 10/10, T6: 7/10 vs. 9/10). This indicates that CoRAL's LLM-based controller can automatically recover much of the structure of expert-designed costs, reducing manual tuning effort.

**C. Ablation Study Analysis (RQ2)**
The ablation studies highlighted the criticality of each component:
- **CoRAL (Unified VLM)**: Failed on nearly all complex tasks (0/10 to 2/10 success). This provided evidence that separating the VLM's role for perception from a dedicated LLM for strategy formulation is crucial for robust performance, as specialized modules provide more reliable and structured outputs.
- **CoRAL (w/o Refinement)**: Showed a substantial performance drop, particularly in multi-stage tasks like T1 (from 5/10 to 0/10) and T3 (from 10/10 to 3/10). This demonstrated the necessity of the online adaptation loop, allowing the LLM to diagnose physical outcomes, refine parameters (e.g., friction), and update plans mid-execution.
- **CoRAL (w/o Memory)**: Performance decreased on tasks where experience reuse would be beneficial (T1: from 5/10 to 2/10; T5: from 9/10 to 7/10). This validated the benefit of the retrieval-based memory unit for bootstrapping solutions and accelerating convergence on recurrent tasks.
- **CoRAL (w/o Pose Tracking)**: Catastrophic failure across all tasks (0/10 success). This definitively showed that a dedicated, high-fidelity 6-DoF pose estimator (FoundationPose) is an essential geometric foundation, as VLMs alone often produce imprecise or "hallucinated" pose estimations.

**D. Robustness Analysis (RQ3)**
- **LLM-Guided Contact Strategy**: An ablation on the "Flip with Wall" task (T6) showed that providing the LLM-generated initial contact strategy (C0) was crucial. The guided approach was 83.9% more efficient in control steps (32 vs. 199) and 63.9% shorter in end-effector path (1.33m vs. 3.69m) compared to an unguided variant. This highlighted the LLM's role in intelligently pruning the action search space.
- **Online Parameter Adaptation**: CoRAL's 'Online Adaptation' module successfully corrected intentionally overestimated mass (2.0kg vs. 0.25kg) and friction (0.9 vs. 0.5) parameters in both simulation and real-world experiments. The LLM progressively adjusted its beliefs, converging closer to true values and demonstrating the framework's ability to overcome initial environmental mischaracterizations.
- **Sensitivity to Pose and Physical-Parameter Estimates**: Sensitivity analysis on T1 revealed that physical-parameter errors (mass, friction) could largely be compensated through replanning and online adaptation. However, pose-estimation errors, particularly during occlusions caused by contact, were a dominant bottleneck. Ground-truth poses significantly improved success (from 5/10 to 7/10), indicating their critical role.

**E. Explainability and Automated Failure Recovery**
A key advantage observed was the inherent explainability of CoRAL's neuro-symbolic design. During failure recovery in the "Flip with Wall" task, the 'LLM (Online Adaptation)' module provided natural language diagnoses of failures (e.g., poorly weighted cost function) and detailed explanations of corrective actions (e.g., adjusting specific weights), contrasting with opaque end-to-end models.

**F. Real-World Validation (RQ4)**
CoRAL was deployed zero-shot on a physical Franka Emika Panda robot without real-world fine-tuning:
- **Sim-to-Real Robustness**: The system exhibited strong sim-to-real transfer, achieving perfect success on standard tasks (T2, T3: 10/10) and robust performance on contact-critical tasks (T1: 4/10, T4: 9/10, T5: 7/10, T6: 6/10). The online adaptation loop successfully diagnosed and corrected for real-world phenomena like varying surface friction, salvaging trials.
- **Force Regulation**: In Task 4 ("Push with Constant Force"), the MPPI controller, guided by the LLM's cost function, effectively regulated interaction forces within target bounds (approx. 5N) despite sensor noise, confirming its ability to close the loop on force dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoRAL represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing fundamental challenges associated with integrating large foundation models into physically grounded, adaptive control systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control**: The framework effectively bridges the gap between the semantic understanding of LLMs/VLMs and the real-time adaptive control requirements of contact-rich manipulation. By utilizing LLMs as "cost designers" rather than direct controllers, CoRAL leverages the reasoning capabilities of foundation models while maintaining the stability and reactiveness of model predictive control.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization for Contact-Rich Tasks**: A major impact is its demonstrated ability to perform complex, contact-rich tasks in a zero-shot manner. This contrasts with data-hungry, end-to-end VLA models that struggle to generalize to novel physical interactions or require extensive demonstrations. CoRAL's approach significantly reduces the need for large-scale, specialized datasets for new tasks.
- **Adaptive Physical Understanding and Robustness**: The neuro-symbolic adaptation loop, combining VLM-derived semantic priors with online system identification and LLM-driven strategy refinement, enables the system to adapt its internal world model and control objectives in real-time. This dynamic adaptation mechanism enhances robustness against uncertainties in physical parameters, unmodeled environmental effects, and the sim-to-real gap, which are common challenges in practical robotics.
- **Enhanced Explainability and Failure Recovery**: Unlike opaque end-to-end systems, CoRAL's modular design provides inherent explainability. The LLM's ability to diagnose failures in natural language and articulate its corrective actions (e.g., modifying cost function weights or physical parameter estimates) offers transparency. This interpretability is crucial for debugging, building trust, and potentially for human-robot collaboration.
- **Data Efficiency and Path to Lifelong Learning**: By operating in a zero-shot regime and incorporating a retrieval-based memory unit, CoRAL offers a more data-efficient paradigm. The memory unit allows the system to reuse successful strategies, suggesting a clear path towards few-shot performance improvements and lifelong learning as it accumulates experience.
- **Modular and Extendable Architecture**: The clear separation of concerns (perception, reasoning, planning, control) within CoRAL’s modular architecture makes it flexible and extendable. New perception modules, different LLMs, or more sophisticated physics models can be integrated without overhauling the entire system, facilitating future research and development.
- **Impact on Human-Robot Interaction**: The ability to reason, adapt, and explain its actions could significantly impact human-robot interaction, allowing for more intuitive task specification, better understanding of robot behavior, and more effective collaboration in complex manipulation scenarios.

While the framework’s performance currently relies on the fidelity of the vision-based world model and is subject to latency constraints of foundation model inference, CoRAL's hybrid paradigm—coupling pre-trained knowledge with rigorous real-time control—is positioned as a promising direction for developing more capable, autonomous, and physically intelligent robotic agents.
