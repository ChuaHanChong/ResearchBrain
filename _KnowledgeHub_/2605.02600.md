# CoRAL: Contact-Rich Adaptive LLM-based Control for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CoRAL introduces a neuro-symbolic framework for zero-shot, contact-rich robotic manipulation, decoupling high-level LLM reasoning from low-level control with online physical parameter adaptation and strategic refinement. The system demonstrated superior performance, achieving over 50% higher average success rates than VLA baselines on unseen contact-rich tasks in simulation and robust real-world transfer.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks struggle with contact-rich manipulation due to reliance on extensive demonstration data and a lack of explicit physical grounding or adaptive control.
- Existing VLA models are brittle and exhibit limited generalizability to novel physical situations that demand precise force management and adaptive strategies.
- The black-box nature of end-to-end VLA systems hinders explainability and makes adaptation to unforeseen physical dynamics challenging.

## Method

- **CoRAL** is a ==neuro-symbolic framework== that decouples high-level LLM/VLM reasoning from low-level, physically grounded control for zero-shot contact-rich manipulation.
- LLMs are employed as dynamic 'cost designers' and online diagnosticians for a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (**MPPI**) motion planner, creating adaptable objective functions.
- The framework incorporates nested feedback loops for ==online adaptation==, refining physical parameters via system identification and iteratively adjusting contact strategies based on real-time interaction feedback.

## Results

- CoRAL achieved high success rates across six challenging manipulation tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines (OpenVLA, 
							π
							0.5
							) and the L2R cost-generation method, boosting success rates over **50%** on average in unseen contact-rich scenarios.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical Franka Emika Panda robot, effectively handling real-world uncertainties, such as varying friction, and regulating interaction forces.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of online refinement for diagnosing and correcting physical inaccuracies and the necessity of a dedicated pose estimator and modular VLM/LLM architecture for robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly separating high-level semantic reasoning (LLM/VLM) from low-level adaptive control significantly enhances robustness for contact-rich tasks, overcoming limitations of end-to-end VLA policies.
> - LLMs can effectively function as dynamic cost designers and online diagnosticians, providing adaptable task objectives and enabling real-time error correction and strategy refinement.
> - Online adaptation of physical parameters and strategic plans is crucial for overcoming initial uncertainties, handling sim-to-real gaps, and achieving successful deployment in dynamic real-world environments.
