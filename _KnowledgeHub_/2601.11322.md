# Enhancing Vision Language Models with Logic Reasoning for Situational Awareness

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework integrates Vision-Language Models with traditional computer vision and explicit logical reasoning to provide justifications for VLM outputs and flag unreliable predictions in situational awareness applications. Its consistency-driven fine-tuning strategy consistently outperforms uninformed tuning, achieving comparable accuracy to label-intensive methods while reducing labeling effort.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack fine-grained, quantitative details crucial for precise situational awareness, despite providing rich semantic descriptions.
- Fine-tuning VLMs for specific, often rare, events is resource-intensive due to the need for large labeled datasets.
- Current VLMs do not provide justifications for their outputs or indicate when predictions might be unreliable, hindering deployment in critical systems.

## Method

- The proposed framework integrates VLMs and Traditional Computer Vision (TCV) with explicit logical reasoning using ==Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT)==.
- It employs a ==three-task activity categorization== (main, auxiliary, proxy) to structure knowledge and facilitate consistency checks between VLM outputs and TCV-derived facts.
- A ==consistency-driven fine-tuning algorithm== intelligently selects unlabeled video segments for VLM fine-tuning based on identified logical inconsistencies, reducing reliance on extensive prior labeling.

## Results

- The consistency-driven directed fine-tuning achieved up to **9 percentage points** higher accuracy compared to uninformed (random) fine-tuning (e.g., MiniGPT4 on TU DAT improved from **73.14%** to **82.14%**).
- Directed fine-tuning attained classification accuracy nearly identical to that of an accuracy-driven method requiring prior labels for selection, demonstrating efficiency without sacrificing performance.
- The Consistency Improvement Factor (CIF) was significantly higher for directed fine-tuning (e.g., X-CLIP on TU DAT at **74.25%**) compared to undirected fine-tuning (**54.5%**), indicating better resolution of inconsistencies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit logical reasoning via SMT effectively combines high-level VLM semantic understanding with fine-grained TCV details, enabling both justification for VLM outputs and reliability checks.
> - Consistency checks, derived from logical rules and TCV groundings, serve as a powerful, label-free signal to guide VLM fine-tuning, leading to substantial accuracy improvements with reduced labeling effort.
> - The integration of an auxiliary VLM, which performs a simpler related task, enhances the robustness of consistency checks and improves the effectiveness of directed fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within Artificial Intelligence and Computer Vision, primarily addressing crucial limitations of contemporary Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in real-world, high-stakes applications.

**The Rise of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** In recent years, VLMs, built upon Large Language Models (LLMs), have demonstrated impressive capabilities in generating high-level, semantically rich descriptions of visual scenes. They can process multimodal inputs (images/videos and text) and produce human-like narrative outputs, making them highly attractive for tasks requiring nuanced understanding. However, the paper highlights their inherent weaknesses in providing fine-grained, quantitative details, their resource-intensive fine-tuning requirements, and a lack of explicit justification for their outputs, particularly in the context of critical situational awareness (SA) applications.

**The Enduring Relevance of Traditional Computer Vision (TCV):** While VLMs offer high-level understanding, Traditional Computer Vision (TCV) methods (e.g., object detection, tracking, pose estimation using CNNs or Transformers) remain superior for extracting precise, quantitative, and fine-grained details like object locations, movements, and specific attributes. The broader landscape often sees a competition between these paradigms, but this work advocates for a synergistic integration.

**Neuro-Symbolic AI and Explainable AI (XAI):** The paper contributes significantly to the burgeoning field of Neuro-Symbolic AI, which aims to combine the strengths of data-driven neural networks with knowledge-driven symbolic reasoning. Unlike many neuro-symbolic approaches that attempt to embed logical constraints implicitly within neural network loss functions (e.g., Logic Tensor Networks), this paper proposes an *explicit* logical reasoning layer using Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT). This distinction is crucial for transparency and direct control over reasoning. Furthermore, by providing "justifications for VLM outputs" and indicating when an output "may not be reliable," the work directly addresses core concerns in Explainable AI (XAI), moving beyond mere explanation towards actionable "justifiability" for trustworthiness in critical decisions.

**Efficient Fine-tuning Strategies:** Fine-tuning (FT) VLMs for specific, often rare, "target activities" (like accidents or security breaches) is resource-intensive due to the need for large labeled datasets. The paper tackles this by proposing an "intelligent (or directed) FT" mechanism. This aligns with a broader trend in AI research to make large models more adaptable and efficient, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data or dynamic requirements.

**Novelty:** The authors explicitly state that, "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work of its type to integrate explicit logic reasoning with VLM and TCV to make three substantial contributions in the context of VLM-based situational awareness." This positions the research as pioneering in its specific combination of explicit logical reasoning, multimodal AI, and traditional computer vision for enhanced reliability and efficiency in SA. It shifts the paradigm from merely generating descriptive text to building truly robust and justifiable intelligent monitoring systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the critical need for reliable and accurate automated situational awareness (SA) in cyber-physical systems, such as traffic monitoring, security surveillance, or industrial automation. In these environments, the focus is on identifying infrequent yet highly significant events (e.g., accidents, security breaches, policy violations) with high reliability.

The paper identifies specific limitations of existing approaches that motivate its proposed framework:

1. **Semantic Richness vs. Fine-Grained Detail:** While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel at providing high-level, interpretable semantic descriptions of complex activities from images and videos, they often struggle to extract precise, quantitative, and fine-grained details (e.g., exact locations, distances, specific movements, object counts) crucial for comprehensive SA. Traditional Computer Vision (TCV) methods, conversely, are adept at these fine details but lack the high-level semantic understanding of VLMs. The primary motivation is to bridge this gap.
2. **Resource-Intensive Fine-tuning:** VLMs, like Large Language Models (LLMs), typically require extensive task-specific fine-tuning (FT) to achieve good accuracy. This process is resource-intensive, especially for identifying rare events common in SA applications. The motivation here is to develop an efficient, intelligent FT strategy that reduces resource expenditure and human labeling effort by focusing on areas where the VLM performs poorly.
3. **Lack of Justification and Reliability:** In critical SA applications, the trustworthiness of AI outputs is paramount. Current VLMs generally do not provide justifications for their decisions, nor do they explicitly indicate when their outputs might be unreliable. This lack of transparency and reliability check is a major hurdle for deployment in safety- and security-critical systems. The motivation is to provide a mechanism for generating justifications and flagging questionable outputs during inference.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve three key objectives:

1. **Augment VLM Output with Fine-Grained Details:** To enhance the descriptive power of VLMs by integrating them with TCV methods, allowing for the extraction and presentation of both high-level semantic understanding and precise, quantitative details of events.
2. **Develop an Intelligent Fine-Tuning (FT) Strategy:** To propose and demonstrate a consistency-driven FT mechanism that intelligently selects video segments for fine-tuning based on identified inconsistencies. This strategy aims to achieve substantially higher accuracy than uninformed (random) selection while being more efficient in terms of data labeling and processing.
3. **Generate Justifications for VLM Outputs during Inference:** To enable the system to provide explicit justifications for its recognized activities and, critically, to indicate when an output may be unreliable. This enhances the trustworthiness and utility of VLM-based SA systems for human operators.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a novel framework that integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Traditional Computer Vision (TCV) techniques, and explicit logical reasoning through Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT).

**A. Three-Task Activity Categorization:**
To facilitate the integration and consistency checks, the framework defines three types of tasks and associated activities:
1. **Main Task ($A_m$):** Consists of the primary "target activities" (situations) that need to be recognized and tracked (e.g., "Car hit by another from behind"). These are recognized by a **Main VLM ($VLM_m$)**.
2. **Auxiliary Task ($A_a$):** Contains variants or alternate descriptions of each activity in $A_m$. There's a one-to-one correspondence between $A_m$ and $A_a$ activities (e.g., for "Car hit by another from behind," $A_a$ might be "Car moving in same direction and one behind another"). These are recognized by an **Auxiliary VLM ($VLM_a$)**. $VLM_a$ often has simpler recognition tasks, making it more amenable to TCV verification.
3. **Proxy Task ($A_p$):** A set of simple, low-level activities that loosely characterize $A_m$ and $A_a$. These are typically fine-grained details like object presence, their attributes (poses), movements, and spatio-temporal relationships (e.g., "A car behind another car in the same lane," "A pedestrian in a traffic lane"). These proxy activities are primarily recognized by TCV methods and are represented as logic assertions. A key requirement is that if an activity $A_{m_i}$ (or $A_{a_j}$) is observed, then a corresponding subset of proxy activities, $S_{m_i}(A_p)$ (or $S_{a_j}(A_p)$), must also be observed (i.e., $A_{m_i} \Rightarrow S_{m_i}(A_p)$).

**B. TCV for Fine-Grained Details and Grounding:**
TCV algorithms (e.g., YOLO variants for object detection, augmented with layers for attributes, and frame-to-frame tracking) are employed to:
- Extract fine-grained, quantitative details of the situation.
- Identify key objects, their attributes, positions, distances, and relative movements.
- "Ground" the proxy activities into specific logic assertions (e.g., "car2 moving behind car1 in the same lane"). This forms the factual basis for logical reasoning.

**C. Explicit Logical Reasoning (SMT):**
Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT) solvers (like Z3 and YICES) are used for explicit logical reasoning. The reasoning model consists of:
- **Rules of Inference (RoIs):** Predefined logical implications and rules (e.g., "if car1 is hit from behind by car2, then car1 must be behind car2, and car1 and car2 must be very close").
- **Real-world Constraints:** General knowledge about the environment (e.g., "walking speed < 2 m/s").
- **Groundings/Facts:** Outputs from $VLM_m$, $VLM_a$, and TCV are translated into logic assertions, providing concrete truth values for the variables in the logic program.
SMT efficiently checks for consistency among these assertions, identifying contradictions.

**D. Consistency-Driven Fine-Tuning (FT) Algorithm:**
The core contribution is an intelligent (directed) FT mechanism, depicted as a loop:
1. **Initial FT:** A small initial fine-tuning of $VLM_m$ and $VLM_a$ is performed with a small set of labeled inputs.
2. **Evaluation Data (ED) Processing:** Video segments from an evaluation dataset (ED) are processed through $VLM_m$, $VLM_a$, and TCV.
3. **Consistency Checks:** The outputs from these models are fed into the SMT solver to perform three types of consistency checks:
 - (A) $VLM_m$ output class `i` is consistent with $VLM_a$ output class `i'`.
 - (B) All proxy activities in $S_{m_i}(A_p)$ are observed given $VLM_m$'s output.
 - (C) All proxy activities in $S_{a_i'}(A_p)$ are observed given $VLM_a$'s output.
4. **Inconsistency Detection and Directed Selection:** If any consistency condition fails, the SMT framework identifies the "offending assertions" and the VLM classes that require more fine-tuning. This allows for a "directed selection" of video segments from the Fine-Tuning Data (FTD) pool (and subsequent labeling for FT).
5. **Iteration:** The process repeats, focusing FT efforts on areas of weakness until performance saturates or resources are exhausted. Critically, the *selection* of data for FT does not require prior labeling of the ED, thus saving significant human effort.

**E. Justification during Inference:**
The same consistency-checking mechanism used during FT can be activated during inference. By running $VLM_m$, $VLM_a$, and TCV in parallel (or selectively for challenging scenarios) and performing SMT checks, the system can:
- **Confirm Validity:** If all consistency checks pass, the VLM output is deemed reliable and justified.
- **Indicate Unreliability:** If inconsistencies are found, the system flags the output as potentially unreliable, providing a crucial "sanity check" for critical applications.

**F. Experimental Setup:**
- **VLMs Evaluated:** A diverse set including MiniGPT-4, MiniGPT4-Video, X-CLIP, Video-LLaMA, and Video-Mamba, covering image-based, video-based, LLM-backed, non-LLM-backed, transformer-based, and SSM-based architectures.
- **Datasets:** TU DAT (road traffic accidents), Taekwondo (human movements), and Kinetics-100 (general human actions). Data augmentation was used for smaller datasets.
- **Metrics:** Classification Accuracy, Consistency Improvement Factor (CIF), and computational Overhead (FT preparation time, inference/justification time).
- **Implementation Details:** YOLO for object detection, Keras for data augmentation. Experiments run on NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed consistency-driven fine-tuning (FT) framework across various VLMs and diverse datasets.

1. **Achieved Classification Accuracy:**
 - **Directed vs. Undirected FT:** The intelligent, consistency-driven directed FT mechanism significantly and consistently outperforms uninformed (random) undirected FT across all tested VLMs (MiniGPT4, MiniGPT4-Video, Video-LLaMa, Video-Mamba) and all three datasets (TU DAT, Taekwondo, Kinetics). For instance, MiniGPT4 accuracy on TU DAT jumped from 73.14% (undirected VLMm) to 82.14% (directed VLMm). Similar substantial improvements are observed for $VLM_a$.
 - **Consistency-driven vs. Accuracy-driven FT:** The study compares the proposed consistency-driven FT (which doesn't require prior labels for data *selection*) against an accuracy-driven approach (which uses labels to select inputs for FT). The results show that the consistency-driven method achieves almost identical classification accuracy to the accuracy-driven one. This is a critical finding, validating that the consistency checks effectively guide FT without the need for pre-labeled evaluation data, thus saving significant labeling effort.
 - **Impact of $VLM_a$:** Even when the auxiliary VLM ($VLM_a$) is omitted (and consistency is checked between $VLM_m$ and proxy activities only), the directed FT still yields substantially higher accuracy than undirected FT, though slightly lower than when $VLM_a$ is used. This indicates flexibility in deployment based on resource availability.

2. **Consistency Improvement Factor (CIF):**
 - The CIF, which measures the reduction in inconsistencies, is dramatically higher for directed FT compared to undirected FT across all VLMs and datasets (e.g., X-CLIP on TU DAT: 74.25% directed vs. 54.5% undirected for $VLM_m$). This clearly shows that the directed approach is far more effective at identifying and resolving areas of inconsistency, leading to more robust models.
 - The improvement is robust across different video characteristics (road traffic, Taekwondo, complex human actions), affirming the generality of the approach.

3. **Analysis of Fine-Tuning Time:**
 - **FT Time per Epoch:** The actual FT time (training neural network weights) per epoch is nearly identical for both directed and undirected cases (around 10-12 seconds for both VLMs), as expected, since the amount of training data per batch is kept constant.
 - **Preparation Time:** The "preparation time" for directed FT (which includes YOLO, VLM querying, assertion generation, and SMT consistency checking) is only marginally higher than for undirected FT (which is mainly disk retrieval). This demonstrates that the overhead introduced by the logical reasoning and auxiliary VLM for intelligent data selection is minimal and efficient.

4. **Inference and Justification Time:**
 - The time required for "justifiability" (i.e., running consistency checks during inference) is found to be approximately equal to the VLM's core inference time (around 2 seconds on an NVIDIA A6000 GPU for MiniGPT4-Video).
 - The authors argue this overhead is acceptable for critical SA applications where accuracy and trustworthiness are paramount. They also highlight rapid advancements in AI processing hardware (e.g., NPUs in upcoming CPUs) and VLM efficiency (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL achieving <1 second inference), which will further reduce this overhead, making real-time justification increasingly feasible. They also emphasize that for critical applications, running on edge controllers rather than low-end IoT devices or cloud is preferred to avoid latency and ensure reliability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in developing trustworthy and efficient Vision-Language Model-based systems for situational awareness in critical cyber-physical environments. Its potential impact spans several domains:

1. **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness for Critical Applications:** For safety-critical (e.g., autonomous vehicles, industrial control) and security-critical (e.g., surveillance, anomaly detection) applications, the ability to obtain explicit justifications for VLM outputs and to be warned about potential unreliability is revolutionary. This moves beyond opaque "black box" AI, fostering greater trust among human operators and decision-makers, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
2. **Increased Efficiency in AI Model Development:** The intelligent, consistency-driven fine-tuning mechanism significantly reduces the reliance on large, meticulously labeled datasets, especially for infrequent but critical events. This makes the development and deployment of VLMs more agile, cost-effective, and scalable, as labeling efforts can be precisely targeted to areas of model weakness.
3. **Advancement in Neuro-Symbolic AI:** By explicitly integrating symbolic logic reasoning (via SMT) with data-driven deep learning models (VLMs and TCV), the work offers a practical and effective framework for neuro-symbolic AI. This approach avoids the limitations of purely neural or purely symbolic systems, paving the way for more robust, interpretable, and generalizable AI.
4. **Broader Applicability:** The framework's generality, demonstrated across diverse VLM architectures (transformer-based, SSM-based, LLM-backed, non-LLM-backed) and varied datasets (road traffic, human actions), suggests its wide applicability to various vision-based monitoring tasks beyond the specific examples in the paper.
5. **Addressing VLM Limitations:** The paper effectively addresses key limitations of current VLMs—lack of fine-grained detail, resource-intensive tuning, and lack of interpretability/justification—thus extending their utility into domains where these aspects are non-negotiable.
6. **Future-Proofing AI Systems:** The discussion on the decreasing latency of VLM inference and the increasing power of dedicated AI hardware (NPUs) highlights the practical feasibility of deploying such sophisticated, real-time reasoning systems. This ensures the proposed methodology is not just theoretically sound but also aligned with future hardware and software trends.
7. **Foundation for Human-AI Collaboration:** By providing clear justifications and reliability indicators, the framework facilitates better human-AI collaboration. Operators can more effectively understand, verify, and act upon AI-generated insights, improving overall system performance and decision-making in complex situations.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as the need for an auxiliary VLM (increasing resource needs) and potential TCV training requirements, the substantial benefits in reliability, efficiency, and interpretability for critical situational awareness applications strongly underscore the significant impact of this research.
