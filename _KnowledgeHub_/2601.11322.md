# Enhancing Vision Language Models with Logic Reasoning for Situational Awareness

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework integrates Vision-Language Models with traditional computer vision and explicit logical reasoning to provide justifications for VLM outputs and flag unreliable predictions in situational awareness applications. Its consistency-driven fine-tuning strategy consistently outperforms uninformed tuning, achieving comparable accuracy to label-intensive methods while reducing labeling effort.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack fine-grained, quantitative details crucial for precise situational awareness, despite providing rich semantic descriptions.
- Fine-tuning VLMs for specific, often rare, events is resource-intensive due to the need for large labeled datasets.
- Current VLMs do not provide justifications for their outputs or indicate when predictions might be unreliable, hindering deployment in critical systems.

## Method

- The proposed framework integrates VLMs and Traditional Computer Vision (TCV) with explicit logical reasoning using ==Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT)==.
- It employs a ==three-task activity categorization== (main, auxiliary, proxy) to structure knowledge and facilitate consistency checks between VLM outputs and TCV-derived facts.
- A ==consistency-driven fine-tuning algorithm== intelligently selects unlabeled video segments for VLM fine-tuning based on identified logical inconsistencies, reducing reliance on extensive prior labeling.

## Results

- The consistency-driven directed fine-tuning achieved up to **9 percentage points** higher accuracy compared to uninformed (random) fine-tuning (e.g., MiniGPT4 on TU DAT improved from **73.14%** to **82.14%**).
- Directed fine-tuning attained classification accuracy nearly identical to that of an accuracy-driven method requiring prior labels for selection, demonstrating efficiency without sacrificing performance.
- The Consistency Improvement Factor (CIF) was significantly higher for directed fine-tuning (e.g., X-CLIP on TU DAT at **74.25%**) compared to undirected fine-tuning (**54.5%**), indicating better resolution of inconsistencies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit logical reasoning via SMT effectively combines high-level VLM semantic understanding with fine-grained TCV details, enabling both justification for VLM outputs and reliability checks.
> - Consistency checks, derived from logical rules and TCV groundings, serve as a powerful, label-free signal to guide VLM fine-tuning, leading to substantial accuracy improvements with reduced labeling effort.
> - The integration of an auxiliary VLM, which performs a simpler related task, enhances the robustness of consistency checks and improves the effectiveness of directed fine-tuning.
