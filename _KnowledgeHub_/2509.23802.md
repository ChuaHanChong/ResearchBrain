# STAIR: Addressing Stage Misalignment through Temporal-Aligned Preference Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> STAIR introduces a Preference-based Reinforcement Learning framework that addresses "stage misalignment" in multi-stage tasks by dynamically approximating task stages using temporal distance and prioritizing stage-aligned human preference queries. This approach significantly enhances feedback efficiency, achieving faster convergence and near 100% success rates in complex robotic manipulation tasks while aligning with human cognitive processes.

## Problem

- Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) faces "stage misalignment" in multi-stage tasks, where comparing behaviors from different stages leads to ambiguous and uninformative human feedback.
- Human labelers experience increased cognitive load and provide less reliable feedback when asked to compare behaviors across natural task stage boundaries.
- A "stage reward bias" exists in human preferences, favoring segments closer to task completion, which theoretically leads to a quadratic increase in query complexity for conventional PbRL methods.

## Method

- Develops **STAIR**, a framework that learns a dynamic ==stage approximation== based on *temporal distance* (successor distance) between states, adapting to evolving policies without predefined, task-specific stage definitions.
- Employs contrastive learning to train an energy function that approximates the temporal distance, enabling on-policy updates as the agent's policy evolves.
- Introduces a "quadrilateral distance" to measure stage difference between *segments* and combines it with reward model uncertainty to prioritize informative, stage-aligned preference queries for human labeling.

## Results

- STAIR achieved near **100%** success rates and significantly faster convergence in multi-stage MetaWorld and RoboDesk robotic manipulation tasks, consistently outperforming several state-of-the-art PbRL baselines.
- Human studies confirmed that STAIR-generated queries were perceived as stage-aligned at a significantly higher ratio than queries from baseline methods, validating its temporal distance-based stage approximation.
- Demonstrated enhanced feedback efficiency, achieving a **52.01%** success rate on 'door-open' with 500 queries compared to **20.00%** for baselines, and exhibited robustness to hyperparameter choices and noisy human feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stage misalignment is a tangible problem in multi-stage PbRL, exacerbated by a measurable "stage reward bias" in human preferences, which can theoretically lead to inefficient learning and increased query complexity.
> - Dynamically approximating task stages using temporal distance effectively captures human cognitive perception of task stages, reducing cognitive load and improving the quality of human feedback.
> - Prioritizing preference queries that are both stage-aligned and where the reward model is uncertain significantly improves feedback efficiency and overall learning performance in PbRL for multi-stage tasks.
