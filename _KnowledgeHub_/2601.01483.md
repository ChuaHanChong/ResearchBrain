# Unified Generation and Self-Verification for Vision-Language Models via Advantage Decoupled Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and Ant Group developed Advantage Decoupled Preference Optimization (ADPO), a unified reinforcement learning framework that enables Vision-Language Models to jointly learn answer generation and self-verification. This method achieves significant performance gains in best-of-N selection across multimodal math reasoning, visual grounding, and mobile agent tasks, while reducing inference latency by up to 53.5% compared to dual-model approaches.

## Problem

- Existing parallel test-time scaling for VLMs often requires separate generator and verifier models, leading to high training costs and inefficient deployment.
- Serial test-time scaling approaches (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) show limited effectiveness in complex multimodal domains, necessitating better parallel strategies.
- Training a unified generation and self-verification policy faces challenges like class imbalance with binary rewards and reward hacking due to naive reward aggregation.

## Method

- **ADPO** extends ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) to jointly learn answer generation and self-verification within a single policy model.
- A Preference Verification Reward is introduced to reframe verification as a ranking problem, providing adaptive, relative supervision to overcome class imbalance.
- Advantage Decoupled Optimization uses separate advantages and disjoint token masks for generation and verification, preventing reward hacking and gradient interference.

## Results

- ADPO achieved superior best-of-N performance across all evaluated multimodal domains, including +**5.0%** accuracy on MathVista (N=4) compared to MM-Verifier.
- The method maintained comparable pass@1 generation quality while significantly improving overall reliability through self-verification.
- ADPO reduced inference latency by up to **53.5%** (e.g., from **5.6s** to **2.6s** for best-of-8 on MathVista) compared to a separate GRPO-as-judge model, for competitive or better performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing self-verification as a preference ranking task, rather than absolute correctness, provides more stable and informative gradient signals for training unified policies.
> - Decoupling the advantage signals for generation and verification objectives, combined with token-level masking, is crucial to prevent reward hacking and enable synergistic learning.
> - A single VLM can be efficiently trained to perform both robust answer generation and calibrated self-assessment, overcoming the computational and deployment overhead of multi-model systems.
