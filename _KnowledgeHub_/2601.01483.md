# Unified Generation and Self-Verification for Vision-Language Models via Advantage Decoupled Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and Ant Group developed Advantage Decoupled Preference Optimization (ADPO), a unified reinforcement learning framework that enables Vision-Language Models to jointly learn answer generation and self-verification. This method achieves significant performance gains in best-of-N selection across multimodal math reasoning, visual grounding, and mobile agent tasks, while reducing inference latency by up to 53.5% compared to dual-model approaches.

## Problem

- Existing parallel test-time scaling for VLMs often requires separate generator and verifier models, leading to high training costs and inefficient deployment.
- Serial test-time scaling approaches (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) show limited effectiveness in complex multimodal domains, necessitating better parallel strategies.
- Training a unified generation and self-verification policy faces challenges like class imbalance with binary rewards and reward hacking due to naive reward aggregation.

## Method

- **ADPO** extends ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) to jointly learn answer generation and self-verification within a single policy model.
- A Preference Verification Reward is introduced to reframe verification as a ranking problem, providing adaptive, relative supervision to overcome class imbalance.
- Advantage Decoupled Optimization uses separate advantages and disjoint token masks for generation and verification, preventing reward hacking and gradient interference.

## Results

- ADPO achieved superior best-of-N performance across all evaluated multimodal domains, including +**5.0%** accuracy on MathVista (N=4) compared to MM-Verifier.
- The method maintained comparable pass@1 generation quality while significantly improving overall reliability through self-verification.
- ADPO reduced inference latency by up to **53.5%** (e.g., from **5.6s** to **2.6s** for best-of-8 on MathVista) compared to a separate GRPO-as-judge model, for competitive or better performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing self-verification as a preference ranking task, rather than absolute correctness, provides more stable and informative gradient signals for training unified policies.
> - Decoupling the advantage signals for generation and verification objectives, combined with token-level masking, is crucial to prevent reward hacking and enable synergistic learning.
> - A single VLM can be efficiently trained to perform both robust answer generation and calibrated self-assessment, overcoming the computational and deployment overhead of multi-model systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their deployment for complex reasoning tasks, specifically addressing the paradigm of "test-time scaling." Test-time scaling aims to enhance model reliability and performance by leveraging additional computational resources during inference. The paper distinguishes between two main approaches:
- **Serial Test-Time Scaling**: This involves LLMs generating sequential thinking tokens (e.g., Chain-of-Thought reasoning), as seen in models like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI-o1. While effective in domains like mathematics and coding, the authors note its limited success in multimodal domains (image classification, video understanding, visual spatial understanding), motivating the need for alternative scaling principles.
- **Parallel Test-Time Scaling**: This approach generates multiple potential solutions simultaneously and then aggregates or selects the best one. Current methods often achieve this by deploying two separate models: a generator (to produce candidates) and a verifier (to evaluate and rank them). This dual-model approach, while effective, suffers from significant limitations: high training resource demands (separate datasets, two independent models) and deployment inefficiency (both models running concurrently at inference).

The paper addresses these limitations by proposing a novel, unified framework. It aims to integrate the generation and verification capabilities into a *single policy model*, moving beyond the computationally expensive and resource-intensive two-model setup. This positions the work as a significant step towards more efficient and practical deployment of high-performing VLMs for complex multimodal reasoning, particularly in agentic settings where robust self-correction is crucial. It also contributes to the broader field of reinforcement learning for large models, specifically in tackling challenges like class imbalance in reward modeling and gradient interference in multi-objective optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more efficient and reliable method for parallel test-time scaling in Vision-Language Models by unifying answer generation and self-verification into a single policy. The authors are motivated by several key challenges inherent in existing approaches:

1. **Computational Cost and Inefficiency of Separate Models**: Current state-of-the-art parallel test-time scaling often relies on training and deploying two distinct models – a generator and a verifier. This incurs substantial training costs (requiring specialized datasets and training pipelines for each) and significant inference overhead (requiring both models to run concurrently). The motivation is to reduce this complexity and cost.
2. **Limitations of Serial Test-Time Scaling in Multimodal Domains**: While serial approaches (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) have shown promise in language-only tasks, their performance benefits are limited in multimodal contexts. This highlights the need for more effective parallel scaling strategies that can better handle visual and other sensory inputs.
3. **Challenges in Unified Generation and Self-Verification**: The concept of a single policy performing both generation and self-verification introduces two specific technical hurdles:
 - **Class Imbalance with Binary Rewards**: Directly training a model to verify its own outputs using binary rewards (correct/incorrect) leads to severe class imbalance. As the model improves, correct answers dominate, causing verification scores to collapse and gradient signals to vanish, hindering further learning.
 - **Reward Hacking with Naive Reward Aggregation**: Simply combining answer and verification rewards for optimization can lead to reward hacking. The model might learn to intentionally produce incorrect answers but assign them very low verification scores to maximize the aggregated reward, thereby degrading its actual generation performance.

To overcome these challenges, the paper aims to develop a robust reinforcement learning framework that enables a unified policy to generate high-quality answers and produce reliable, calibrated self-verification scores, thereby allowing for effective "best-of-N" selection during inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Advantage Decoupled Preference Optimization (ADPO)**, a unified reinforcement learning framework built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). ADPO jointly learns answer generation and self-verification within a single policy model.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

ADPO extends GRPO, an RL algorithm that optimizes language models by estimating advantages based on group-normalized rewards. For a given multimodal query, the model samples a group of candidate responses, each receiving a sequence-level reward. GRPO uses a PPO-style clipped objective with group-normalized advantages to update the policy. Answer rewards ($R_a$) are defined based on task type: discrete rewards for exact matches (e.g., math, agent actions) and continuous rewards for similarity metrics (e.g., IoU for visual grounding).

#### 3.2. Unified Policy Architecture and Inference

The unified policy model, based on Qwen2-VL-7B or Qwen2.5-VL-7B, is trained to first generate an answer and then output a verification score (0-1) in a structured prompt format (e.g., `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer><score>...</score>`). At inference time, the model performs batch decoding to sample multiple candidate answers, each with its self-predicted verification score. The candidate with the highest verification score is then selected as the final output. This enables effective parallel test-time scaling via "best-of-N" selection.

#### 3.3. Preference Verification Reward

To address the "class imbalance challenge" with binary verification rewards, ADPO introduces a **Preference Verification Reward ($R_p$)**. Instead of using fixed thresholds, $R_p$ reframes verification as a ranking problem, providing adaptive, relative ranking supervision:
- **Adaptive Thresholds**: Samples are partitioned into contrastive sets (positive/negative) based on their answer quality ($R_a$).
- **Ranking Accuracy**: $R_p$ rewards the model when the ranking of verification scores ($s_i > s_j$) aligns with the ranking of answer qualities ($R_{a,i} > R_{a,j}$) within these contrastive sets.
- **Discrete vs. Continuous Tasks**: For discrete tasks, contrastive sets compare correct vs. incorrect answers. For continuous tasks, a margin ($\gamma$) is used to define contrastive sets for answers with sufficiently different qualities.
This approach ensures that informative gradient signals are maintained even when one class of answers becomes dominant, as it focuses on relative distinctions rather than absolute correctness.

#### 3.4. Advantage Decoupled Optimization

To tackle the "reward hacking challenge" and prevent gradient interference, ADPO introduces **Advantage Decoupled Optimization**:
- **Separate Advantages**: Instead of naively aggregating answer and preference rewards into a single total reward, ADPO computes separate advantages: $\hat{A}^{(a)}$ derived solely from answer rewards ($R_a$) and $\hat{A}^{(p)}$ derived solely from preference verification rewards ($R_p$).
- **Disjoint Token Masks**: Task-specific token masks ($M_a$ for generation tokens, $M_p$ for verification score tokens) are applied to isolate gradient flows. This ensures that:
 - Gradients from answer advantages ($\hat{A}^{(a)}$) only update the generation tokens (answer content and reasoning).
 - Gradients from preference advantages ($\hat{A}^{(p)}$) only update the verification score tokens.
This decoupling ensures that improvements in generation quality are driven by answer-based gradients, while verification calibration is shaped only by preference-based gradients, effectively preventing reward hacking and enabling synergistic optimization of both objectives.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

The methodology is evaluated across three diverse multimodal domains:
- **Multimodal Math Reasoning**: Trained on curated datasets, evaluated on MathVista (in-domain) and MMMU (out-of-distribution), using accuracy.
- **Visual Grounding**: Trained on RefCOCO, evaluated on ReasonSeg, using cIoU and ACC.
- **Mobile Agents**: Trained and evaluated on AndroidControl and GUI Odyssey, using step success rate (SR), type accuracy, and grounding accuracy.
Baselines include GRPO, GRPO with majority voting, ADPO with majority voting, and specific domain-expert models (e.g., MM-Verifier). Qwen2-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B serve as base models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ADPO consistently demonstrates superior performance across diverse multimodal tasks, highlighting its effectiveness in unifying generation and self-verification.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Test-Time Scaling

- **Superior Best-of-N Performance**: ADPO significantly improves best-of-N selection performance across all domains. For instance, on MathVista, ADPO achieves 64.8% (N=4), 65.0% (N=8), and 65.3% (N=12) accuracy, outperforming GRPO with majority voting by +1.4 to +2.1 percentage points and MM-Verifier by substantial margins (e.g., +5.0 at N=4). Similar gains are observed on MMMU (OOD performance), ReasonSeg (e.g., +1.7 to +2.2 cIoU over GRPO at N=4/8/12), AndroidControl (+1.7 to +1.9 SR over GRPO), and GUI Odyssey.
- **Maintained Pass@1 Generation Quality**: Crucially, ADPO achieves these gains without sacrificing initial generation quality (pass@1 performance). Its pass@1 accuracy/SR remains comparable to or slightly better than GRPO across all domains (e.g., MathVista 62.4% ADPO vs. 62.2% GRPO; ReasonSeg 59.1% ADPO vs. 59.5% GRPO; AndroidControl 70.9% ADPO vs. 71.0% GRPO). This indicates that the unified training effectively learns both generation and verification capabilities without degradation in either.
- **Efficiency Gains**: When compared to a separate GRPO-as-judge model, ADPO significantly reduces inference latency. On MathVista (best-of-8), ADPO achieves 65.0% accuracy with 2.6s latency, compared to GRPO-as-judge at 60.8% accuracy and 5.6s latency. This represents a 53.5% reduction in inference time for competitive performance.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Preference Verification Reward**: Ablation studies confirm the crucial role of the preference verification reward. It consistently improves both task performance (e.g., +1.6% best@8 for math) and self-verification quality (e.g., +1.3% to +11.8% AUC improvements across tasks) compared to a simple binary reward. This validates its ability to maintain informative gradient signals under class imbalance and produce better-calibrated confidence scores.
- **Effect of Advantage Decoupled Optimization**: Decoupled advantage computation demonstrably outperforms naive reward aggregation ("entangled advantage"). It leads to improvements in both task performance (e.g., +2.8% best@8 for GUI agent tasks) and significant gains in self-verification quality (e.g., +18.5% AUC for GUI agent tasks). This confirms that isolating gradient flows effectively prevents reward hacking and enables synergistic optimization.
- **Margin Parameter ($\gamma$) Analysis**: For continuous tasks like visual grounding, tuning the margin parameter $\gamma$ in the preference reward is important. An optimal $\gamma = 0.1$ was found to balance discrimination and signal density, achieving the best overall accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Advantage Decoupled Preference Optimization (ADPO) represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models, particularly for their reliable and efficient deployment in complex, real-world applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness of VLMs**: By jointly training a model for generation and self-verification, ADPO enables VLMs to not only produce diverse solutions but also accurately assess their own correctness. This "self-awareness" is crucial for tasks requiring high reliability, such as autonomous agents (mobile agent tasks), safety-critical applications, or expert systems where an incorrect answer without confidence could be problematic. The significant improvements in verification AUC and AP metrics confirm that ADPO's scores are genuinely better indicators of answer quality.
2. **Increased Efficiency and Reduced Deployment Cost**: ADPO addresses a critical practical limitation of current parallel test-time scaling methods: the need for separate generator and verifier models. By unifying these functions into a single policy, ADPO drastically reduces training complexity (no need for specialized datasets and training for two models) and deployment overhead (only one model needs to run at inference). The demonstrated reduction in inference time (up to -53.5%) is particularly impactful for real-time applications or those operating under strict latency constraints.
3. **Overcoming Fundamental RL Challenges**: The paper provides elegant solutions to two long-standing challenges in applying reinforcement learning to large models for self-correction:
 - **Class Imbalance**: The Preference Verification Reward offers a robust mechanism to maintain informative gradient signals even when positive samples become dominant, a common issue in tasks where models quickly learn to generate correct outputs.
 - **Reward Hacking**: The Advantage Decoupled Optimization effectively mitigates reward hacking by preventing conflicting gradient flows, ensuring that improvements in generation and verification are truly synergistic rather than exploitative.
4. **Broad Applicability Across Multimodal Domains**: The comprehensive evaluation across diverse tasks – multimodal math reasoning, visual grounding, and mobile agents – demonstrates the generality and versatility of ADPO. This suggests that the framework is not task-specific but provides a foundational approach for improving VLMs in any domain requiring both accurate generation and reliable self-assessment.
5. **Catalyst for Future Research**: ADPO's success in unifying generation and verification could inspire further research into monolithic, self-correcting AI systems. It paves the way for developing more capable and autonomous agents that can not only perform complex actions but also critically evaluate and refine their own performance, leading to more intelligent and trustworthy AI.

In conclusion, ADPO provides a principled, efficient, and highly effective framework for building more reliable and robust Vision-Language Models. Its ability to achieve superior performance with reduced computational complexity positions it as a significant step towards the next generation of intelligent multimodal AI systems.
