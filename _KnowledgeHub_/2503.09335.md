# NVP-HRI: Zero Shot Natural Voice and Posture-based Human-Robot Interaction via Large Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A system named NVP-HRI integrates natural voice commands, deictic (pointing) posture, and a Large Language Model with zero-shot semantic segmentation to enable robots to interact with unknown objects without prior training. This approach enhanced interaction efficiency by up to 65.2% compared to existing methods and improved collision-free trajectory success rates from 73% to 94%.

## Problem

- Existing human-robot interaction (HRI) frameworks struggle to interact with novel or unknown objects, often requiring extensive pre-trained detector networks and annotated datasets.
- Conventional HRI methods are often unintuitive and demanding for users, necessitating complex gestures or memorized commands, which limits accessibility for diverse demographics like the elderly.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) in robotics face limitations such as needing supervised models for object perception, struggling with semantic understanding of unknown objects, and risks of hallucination in untrained tasks.

## Method

- Developed **NVP-HRI**, a multi-modal HRI system that integrates natural voice commands with deictic (pointing) posture recognition to infer human intent.
- Incorporated the ==Segment Anything Model (SAM)== for zero-shot semantic segmentation, enabling the system to generate 3D structural representations of both known and unknown objects.
- Leveraged GPT-4-turbo for translating multi-modal human intent into robot action sequences, utilizing structured prompt engineering and a real-time cross-check mechanism for collision avoidance.

## Results

- NVP-HRI demonstrated a **59.2%** to **65.2%** improvement in interaction efficiency compared to state-of-the-art gesture-based, NLP-based, and VLM-based HRI methods.
- The system achieved high interaction accuracy, with **100%** in single-object manipulation and **90.9%** in multi-step, multi-object tasks involving unknown items across various age groups.
- A cross-check mechanism for LLM-generated action sequences enhanced the success rate of collision-free trajectories from **73%** to **94%**, improving operational safety and reliability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating zero-shot semantic segmentation with natural voice and deictic posture allows robots to robustly understand and interact with previously unknown objects without requiring prior training.
> - Employing a cross-check mechanism for LLM-generated action sequences significantly reduces the risk of hallucination and improves the reliability of collision-free robot trajectories.
> - A multi-modal HRI system leveraging natural communication methods offers substantial efficiency gains and maintains consistent performance across diverse user age groups.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Effective Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) is a critical area of research, particularly with the increasing demand for service robots in contexts such as aging societies. Current HRI frameworks frequently encounter limitations when interacting with objects that have not been explicitly pre-trained or cataloged within their systems. Many existing solutions rely on pre-trained detector networks (e.g., Mask R-CNN, YOLO, SSD) which require extensive annotated datasets and struggle with rare, unknown, or novel objects. This creates a domain gap that limits the adaptability and intuitiveness of robots in dynamic, real-world environments.

Furthermore, conventional HRI methods often require users to master complex gestures, memorize predefined language tokens, or provide detailed descriptions, which can be challenging for a broad user base, including the elderly or individuals with physical limitations. While language-based solutions, particularly those involving Large Language Models (LLMs), have shown increasing potential in embodied AI for tasks such as planning and control, they also face challenges. These include the need for supervised models for object perception, difficulties in understanding semantic meaning for unknown objects, the risk of hallucination for untrained tasks, and the requirement for specific syntax structures.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified gaps. It proposes a multi-modal HRI system that aims to enhance intuitiveness and adaptability, particularly when dealing with unknown objects. By integrating zero-shot segmentation capabilities, natural language processing, and deictic posture recognition, NVP-HRI seeks to overcome the limitations of existing methods that are either object-specific, cumbersome for users, or prone to errors when encountering novel situations. The paper explores a method to enable robots to understand human intent more naturally and robustly in dynamic environments without requiring prior object training or complex user commands, thereby contributing to the development of more accessible and capable service robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind NVP-HRI stems from the challenges associated with developing effective and intuitive HRI systems for service robots, especially in scenarios involving an aging population and a diverse range of objects, many of which may be unknown to the robot.

The key motivations are:
- **Addressing the "Unknown Object" Challenge:** Existing HRI solutions are often biased towards well-trained objects, creating a significant gap when robots encounter new or rare items. This is a crucial hurdle for effective robot assistance, especially for individuals who might rely on robots for daily tasks but struggle to name or describe unfamiliar objects.
- **Improving HRI Intuitiveness for Diverse Users:** Traditional HRI systems requiring users to recall complex commands, memorize hand gestures, or learn new object names are impractical. This is particularly true for elderly or sick individuals who may have cognitive or physical limitations. The goal is to make HRI intuitive and adaptable to a wide range of users and objects.
- **Mitigating LLM Limitations in Robotics:** While LLMs offer potential for language-based interaction and planning, they currently face issues such as requiring supervised models for object perception, struggling with semantic meaning for unknown objects, hallucination for untrained tasks, and requiring tedious syntax structures for control. The motivation is to leverage LLM capabilities while mitigating these drawbacks.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the NVP-HRI system are:
- **Develop an intuitive multi-modal HRI paradigm:** The system aims to combine natural voice commands with deictic (pointing) posture to provide a more natural and less burdensome interaction method for users.
- **Enable zero-shot interaction with unknown objects:** By utilizing advanced visual segmentation models like the Segment Anything Model (SAM), the system intends to analyze visual cues and depth data to precisely represent novel objects without prior knowledge or training.
- **Integrate Large Language Models for complex task execution:** The research aims to use an LLM to process multi-modal commands, coordinate them with object selection and scene distribution, and generate collision-free robot trajectories in real time.
- **Enhance the reliability of LLM-generated actions:** A specific objective is to regulate the action sequences generated by the LLM using essential control syntax and a cross-check mechanism, thereby reducing the risks associated with LLM hallucination and ensuring safer task execution.
- **Demonstrate efficiency and user-friendliness:** The system seeks to offer significant efficiency improvements over traditional HRI methods, particularly in terms of interaction speed and reduced reliance on syntax memorization, making it suitable for practical applications, including elderly and healthcare assistance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NVP-HRI system integrates several modules to achieve zero-shot natural voice and posture-based human-robot interaction. The overall architecture processes multi-modal inputs to generate real-time, collision-free robot action sequences.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The system takes verbal commands ($\mathcal{V}$) and human posture references ($\mathcal{B}$) as input. Verbal commands are mapped to text, and subsequently to action intentions ($I_a$) and metric references ($\lambda$). Posture references, in conjunction with structural object representations ($\Gamma$), are mapped to target intentions ($I_t$). All intentions ($I_a, I_t, \lambda$) are then fed into an LLM which, after processing, generates action sequences ($a$) for robot control.

#### 3.2. Structural Objects Representation

Objects within the scene are represented as 3D point cloud clusters ($\mathcal{P} \in \mathbb{R}^3$). For each cluster, a structural representation $\Gamma(\cdot) \in \mathbb{R}^7$ is derived, which includes a cluster index, height ($h$), width ($w$), thickness ($d$), and 3D centroid position ($\gamma$). The robot end effector's state at any time ($q(t)$) (6D pose and gripper opening angle) is also considered. The environment is thus represented as a tuple $\mathcal{E} = (\Gamma(i, h, w, \gamma), q(t))$.

#### 3.3. Intention Definition

Human intention ($I$) is inferred from a set of keywords ($\mathcal{S}$) and decomposed into:
- **Target references ($I_t$):** Identifies the specific object of interaction based on posture.
- **Action references ($I_a$):** Indicates the intended action based on verbal command.
- **Metric references ($\lambda$):** Optional parameters such as pouring angle or movement velocity, also derived from verbal command.

#### 3.4. Parallel Multi-modal Commands

- **Verbal Command Sequence ($\mathcal{V}$):** Verbal language input is categorized into:
 - **Action commands:** For prospective actions (e.g., "pick," "pour").
 - **Approval commands:** To confirm object selection.
 - **Metric references:** Optional variables (e.g., "angle 90 degrees").
 The mapping $\mathcal{L}$ converts audio input into text using the VOSK API, then extracts $I_a$ and $\lambda$.

- **Deictic Posture ($\mathcal{B}$):** This module infers the intended object of interest.
 - An RGBD camera captures the upper half of the human body.
 - OpenPose is utilized to track 2D human skeletons from the RGB image.
 - The aligned depth map estimates 3D human skeletons ($\mathcal{B}$) in the camera frame.
 - The extended line ($\ell$) of the operator's right forearm is designated as the deictic posture direction.
 - The target intention ($I_t$) is identified as the object closest to vector $\ell$, calculated using the distance formula $d_k = \frac{||\left(\ell_2 - \ell_1\right) \times \left(\ell_1 - \gamma_k\right)||}{||\ell_2 - \ell_1||}$.
 - The relevant object location is confirmed upon a verbal approval command.

#### 3.5. Zero-Shot Semantic Segmentation

This module extracts structural object representations.
- The pre-trained Segment Anything Model (SAM) (specifically vit\_l with 1024x1024x3 input) is used to generate 2D semantic masks. The system prioritizes SAM's ability to segment object contours over its potentially incorrect semantic labels for unknown objects.
- These 2D pixel data are reprojected into 3D point clouds in the camera frame ($\mathcal{P}_C$) using the camera's calibration values ($\mathcal{K}$).
- The 3D point clouds are then transformed into the robot base frame ($\mathcal{P}_B$) using a transformation matrix ($\mathcal{T}_C^B$).
- The centroid of each 3D point cloud cluster determines the object position ($\gamma_k$). Object width ($w$), thickness ($d$), and height ($h$) are calculated from the point cloud's bounding box; $w$ is used for gripper opening, while $w, d, h$ are used for path planning and trajectory cross-checking.

#### 3.6. Action Sequence Generation and Execution

GPT-4-turbo (version 2024-04-09) is employed to translate the human intention ($I$) into robot action sequences ($a$). GPT-4-turbo is selected for its ability to handle high-level environmental representations, complex natural language commands, 3D spatial understanding, and ease of integration. To mitigate LLM hallucination and manage syntax, specific prompt engineering is applied:
- **Action constraints:** Limits the set of actions the LLM can use.
- **Trajectory constraints:** Defines requirements for real-time trajectory planning, including units and coordinate axes.
- **Example tasks:** Provides demonstrations of similar tasks to guide the LLM's planning strategies.
The LLM reconstructs the scene from structural representations, determines safe heights, and generates action sequences conforming to the defined constraints.

#### 3.7. Cross-Check Mechanism

After generation, action sequences undergo a cross-check to ensure collision-free trajectories.
- The manipulated object ($\Gamma_M$) and potential obstacles ($\Gamma_O$) are simplified to rectangular shapes based on their structural representations.
- The swept volume ($\mathcal{A}$) of the manipulated object along the planned trajectory ($\psi$) is calculated.
- If the swept volume intersects with any obstacle volume ($\mathcal{A} \cap \mathcal{B} \neq \emptyset$), a collision is detected. GPT-4 receives detailed feedback on the failure (e.g., collision location) to adjust its subsequent reasoning.
- If no collision, the trajectory is executed. This mechanism reportedly improves the success rate of collision-free trajectories from 73% to 94%.

#### 3.8. Human-Robot Interaction

The interaction proceeds through multi-modal command sequences with visual feedback via a graphical user interface (ROS RVIZ):
1. The user issues a verbal action command.
2. Concurrently, the user makes a deictic posture towards the target object.
3. The system identifies the target object, and the user provides a verbal "approval command" to confirm selection.
4. Optional metric commands can be provided verbally.
5. Finally, the user confirms the overall intention.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates NVP-HRI through diverse experiments focusing on effectiveness, intuitiveness, precision, robustness, and user experience.

#### 4.1. Environment Setup and Scenarios

Experiments were conducted in a simulated living room environment using an Intel D435i RGBD camera, a Universal Robot manipulator, and a laptop microphone. Twenty participants of various age groups (18-65 y.o.) were involved. Scenarios included manipulating common objects (cups, bowls, plates) and novel, unrecognized items (charcoal briquettes, exotic tropical fruits).
The core experimental scenarios explored increasing complexity:
- **S1:** Picking up a specific cup.
- **S2:** Picking up a cup and placing it on a plate.
- **S3:** Picking up a cup and pouring its contents (at a 90-degree angle) into a bowl.
Multi-step tasks like "add water and pass" were also tested.

#### 4.2. Benchmarking and Baselines

The system's performance was benchmarked against state-of-the-art gesture-based (Vanc et al., 2023), NLP-based (Stepputtis et al., 2020), and VLM-based (Constantin et al., 2022) HRI methods. Baseline comparisons used commonly recognizable items, as other methods often failed with unknown objects. Two-way ANOVA was used to analyze the effects of age and interaction order on efficiency and precision.

#### 4.3. Comparison of Effectiveness and Intuitiveness (Interaction Duration)

Experiments demonstrated that NVP-HRI significantly reduced interaction time compared to baseline methods:
- **59.2% more efficient** than gesture-based HRI.
- **65.2% more efficient** than NLP-based HRI.
- **64.8% more efficient** than VLM-based HRI.
An analysis of interaction duration across different age groups (18-35, 36-50, 51-65 y.o.) revealed that for baseline methods, age had a significant effect on interaction duration ($P < 0.05$), with older participants generally taking more time. In contrast, for NVP-HRI, age groups had no significant effect on interaction duration ($P > 0.05$), suggesting its suitability across a wider age range. The order of interaction methods did not significantly affect duration in any case.

#### 4.4. Comparison of Precision and Robustness (Interaction Accuracy)

Accuracy tests involved participants interacting with 18 unknown charcoal briquettes arranged closely together.
- NVP-HRI demonstrated high interaction accuracy, with 100% in S1, 95.5% in S2, and 90.9% in S3 for the younger age group. Similar high accuracies were observed across all age groups.
- Visual feedback from the ROS RVIZ interface improved initial accuracy, allowing effective use without formal training.
- Gesture-based methods performed better than language-based methods due to clearer reference points, but NVP-HRI surpassed them.
- NLP and VLM-based methods struggled with discriminating similar objects or unknown objects, often requiring more precise descriptions or interactive dialogue.
Statistical analysis indicated that neither age group nor interaction order significantly affected interaction accuracy for any of the methods tested ($P > 0.05$).

#### 4.5. Field Survey on User Experience

An online survey involving 390 participants (professors, staff, students from diverse nationalities and age groups) indicated a strong preference for NVP-HRI, with over 97% of participants ranking it highest.
- Older participants valued the simplicity and comprehensibility of NVP-HRI, citing challenges in memorizing gestures for gesture-based systems.
- Younger and middle-aged participants highlighted NVP-HRI's efficiency, noting the inefficiency of VLM-based methods due to their reliance on interactive dialogue for object identification.
Onsite trials with survey participants confirmed the consistency between survey responses and real-world experience.

#### 4.6. Diverse Real-world Environment Tests

Qualitative tests in various home and elderly care center scenarios showed:
- Consistent detection of deictic posture across elderly and younger participants.
- Accurate deictic posture even when the lower body was obscured.
- Superior performance of NVP-HRI in identifying and manipulating novel objects compared to supervised models (e.g., YOLO), which often failed to detect or incorrectly categorized such items. The system successfully retrieved various previously unseen daily objects on demand.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NVP-HRI system represents a contribution to the field of human-robot interaction by addressing several persistent challenges.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Zero-Shot Interaction with Unknown Objects:** The integration of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) allows robots to perceive and interact with novel or previously unseen objects without requiring prior training or manual annotation. This significantly expands the operational versatility of service robots in dynamic, unstructured environments.
- **Enhanced User Intuitiveness and Accessibility:** By combining natural voice commands with deictic posture, NVP-HRI offers an intuitive interaction paradigm that reduces the cognitive load on users, eliminating the need to memorize complex gestures or specific vocabulary. This is particularly significant for elderly individuals or those with physical or cognitive impairments, for whom existing HRI methods may be impractical.
- **Improved Efficiency:** The evaluation demonstrates substantial efficiency gains (up to 65.2%) in interaction duration compared to baseline HRI methods, contributing to faster task completion and a more fluid user experience. The system's ability to maintain efficiency across different age groups highlights its robust design.
- **Robustness against LLM Hallucination:** The structured prompt engineering and the proposed cross-check mechanism for generated action sequences contribute to mitigating the known issue of hallucination in Large Language Models. This enhances the safety and reliability of robot operations by ensuring collision-free trajectory planning.
- **Multi-Modal Data Fusion:** The framework effectively fuses disparate data streams—visual (from RGBD camera and SAM), auditory (voice commands), and kinematic (human posture via OpenPose)—to derive a comprehensive understanding of human intent and the environment.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Service Robotics:** NVP-HRI has the potential to accelerate the deployment and adoption of service robots in homes, healthcare facilities, and other public spaces by making them more adaptable and user-friendly for a broader demographic.
- **Support for Aging Societies:** By providing an accessible and intuitive control method, the system can significantly enhance the independence and quality of life for elderly individuals and patients requiring robotic assistance for daily tasks.
- **Foundation for Future HRI Research:** The open-source availability of the code and design could serve as a valuable resource for the research community, fostering further development in multi-modal HRI, zero-shot perception, and robust LLM integration for robotics.
- **Safer Robot Operation:** The integrated collision-free trajectory planning and cross-check mechanism contribute to safer robot operation, which is a critical factor for public acceptance and deployment of autonomous systems in human-centric environments.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** The zero-shot capability for object recognition can potentially reduce the effort and cost associated with manually training object detection models for every new environment or item a robot might encounter.
