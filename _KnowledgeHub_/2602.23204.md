# Motion-aware Event Suppression for Event Cameras

> [!summary] Summary
> The Robotics and Perception Group at the University of Zurich introduces a framework for event cameras that suppresses non-informative events by jointly segmenting independently moving objects and predicting their future motion, achieving a 67% improvement in segmentation accuracy on EVIMO and operating at 173 Hz.

## Problem

- Event cameras generate a high volume of 'entangled' events originating from both camera ego-motion and independently moving objects (IMOs), posing a challenge for downstream perception systems.
- Existing methods either incur high latency and are susceptible to fragility (SLAM-based) or rely on manually tuned thresholds with limited generalizability and accuracy.
- There is a lack of frameworks capable of directly, adaptively, and anticipatorily suppressing motion events in a task-driven, generalizable, and real-time manner.

## Method

- The **Motion-aware Event Suppression** framework frames the task as two coupled steps: instantaneous event suppression using a binary mask and ==motion anticipation== to predict future scene evolution for anticipatory event filtering.
- A multi-task prediction architecture uses a ==convolutional GRU== encoder with parallel branches for mask decoding and optical flow decoding, featuring an ==Attention-based Time Conditioning (ATC)== module for future flow prediction.
- The model is trained end-to-end with a hybrid loss, combining supervised segmentation and flow losses with an ==unsupervised contrast maximization== objective to enhance event deblurring.

## Results

- The model achieves an inference rate of **173 Hz** with less than **1 GB** of memory usage, delivering effective zero-latency masks with a positive prediction age of **94.2 ms**.
- On the EVIMO dataset, the framework shows a **67%** improvement in R@0.5 and a **7%** improvement in mIoU over the EVIMO* (non-causal) baseline for future motion forecasting.
- Integrating the suppression method reduced the mean Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) in visual odometry by **8.7%** (to **0.21 m**) and accelerated Vision Transformer inference by approximately **10 FPS** with a negligible accuracy drop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly learning IMO segmentation and future motion prediction enables anticipatory event suppression, effectively delivering zero-latency masks despite processing delays.
> - Learning to forecast non-linear pixel paths through optical flow warping is crucial for robust anticipatory segmentation, especially for scenes with chaotic object motion.
> - A lightweight, GPU-optimized architecture can achieve high inference rates (173 Hz) and low memory usage, making real-time deployment feasible for latency-critical event-based systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Event cameras are biologically inspired sensors that asynchronously report per-pixel brightness changes with microsecond latency, generating a sparse stream of events encoding dynamic visual information. This high temporal resolution presents both an advantage and a challenge: it captures rapid motion with minimal latency and high dynamic range, but it also produces a significant volume of "entangled" events originating from both camera ego-motion and independently moving objects (IMOs). Efficiently decoupling these event sources is a key challenge for event-based perception systems.

Existing approaches to addressing this problem typically fall into several categories. Many methods focus on recovering intermediate representations such as camera poses, depth maps, or dense optical flow, which then feed into standard segmentation networks. While these pipelines can achieve benchmark performance, they often incur high latency and are susceptible to the inherent fragility of complex Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems, including issues like drift and depth estimation errors. Conversely, some bio-inspired filtering methods, offer low latency but rely on manually tuned thresholds and have primarily been demonstrated in controlled indoor environments, limiting their generalizability and accuracy compared to deep learning models.

Within the domain of learning-based event processing, significant research has explored event-based motion segmentation. Early works like Ev-DodgeNet focused on detecting and tracking moving objects for obstacle avoidance. EV-IMO introduced a structure-from-motion CNN for joint estimation of motion masks, depth, and camera motion, along with a dataset for indoor scenes. Subsequent methods have refined aspects of motion segmentation, such as MSRNN improving temporal dependencies and GConv using Graph Neural Networks for higher frequency predictions. Unsupervised methods, such as Un-EVIMO, have also emerged. However, many of these advanced architectures, particularly those extending to complex outdoor settings, demand considerable computational and memory resources, which can limit their suitability for real-time, low-latency applications.

A related area is low-latency optical flow estimation from event data, which aims to capture scene dynamics and object motion. Works like EV-FlowNet introduced self-supervised neural networks for optical flow, and later models like E-RAFT achieved state-of-the-art performance on larger datasets like DSEC. However, these methods primarily focus on flow estimation rather than the direct suppression of irrelevant events.

The gap identified by this work is the absence of a framework that can *directly*, *adaptively*, and *anticipatorily* suppress motion events in a task-driven, generalizable, and real-time manner. Current solutions either oversimplify with static thresholds or overcomplicate with dense 3D scene reconstruction, often addressing auxiliary sub-problems rather than the core challenge of motion-aware event suppression. This paper aims to provide a unified, principled solution to separate ego-motion-induced events from IMO events in real time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the inherent characteristics of event cameras: their high temporal resolution generates a large volume of events that combine signals from camera ego-motion and independently moving objects (IMOs). This "entanglement" of events poses a fundamental challenge for downstream perception systems, leading to computational overload and a degradation in accuracy as these systems must process irrelevant data. The paper defines its primary objective as "motion-aware event suppression": selectively filtering non-informative events while preserving those that are relevant to a specific task.

The specific objectives of this work are:
1. **Real-time Event Decoupling:** To develop the first framework capable of learning to filter events triggered by IMOs from those caused by ego-motion in real time.
2. **Anticipatory Suppression:** To jointly segment IMOs in the current event stream and predict their future motion, thereby enabling the anticipatory suppression of dynamic events *before* they occur. This addresses the latency issue inherent in reactive suppression methods.
3. **Lightweight Architecture:** To design a computationally efficient model suitable for practical deployment in latency-critical applications, characterized by low inference latency and minimal memory footprint on consumer-grade hardware.
4. **Downstream Task Enhancement:** To demonstrate that providing a "cleaner" event stream (either ego-motion only or IMO only) can significantly benefit the accuracy and efficiency of subsequent robotic perception tasks, such as visual odometry and Vision Transformer inference.

The motivation for these objectives is rooted in the critical requirements of emerging applications:
- **Augmented Reality/Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** These systems demand stable visual odometry for anchoring virtual content while simultaneously requiring responsiveness to user gestures. A reliable event suppression mechanism can ensure low latency and robust perception of relevant dynamics.
- **Autonomous Driving:** Autonomous vehicles need to accurately track their own motion and react instantly to pedestrians and other dynamic agents. Overloading perception systems with undifferentiated event data can hinder timely and accurate responses.
- **Data Imbalance and Latency:** In real-world scenarios, dynamic cues from IMOs often constitute a small fraction (e.g., less than 5%) of the total event rate, while static object edges generate a vast majority of events due to ego-motion. This imbalance creates a "chicken-and-egg dilemma": segmenting static background events requires knowing their motion context, but this context only becomes available after the system has been "flooded" by events, introducing latency.
- **Limited Information per Event:** Each event provides minimal information (pixel coordinate, timestamp, polarity), making meaningful classification impossible without additional spatio-temporal context.
- **Limitations of Prior Work:** Existing methods either oversimplify the problem using static thresholds or overcomplicate it by attempting dense 3D scene reconstruction, which is often computationally prohibitive and unnecessary for event suppression. These approaches do not directly address the anticipatory suppression challenge.

The authors propose to overcome these limitations by introducing a learning-based framework that unifies motion perception and prediction, thereby directly addressing the problem of motion-aware event suppression in a real-time, deployable manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces a framework for Motion-aware Event Suppression designed to decouple events caused by ego-motion from those originating from independently moving objects (IMOs) in real time. The core idea is to jointly learn to segment IMOs and predict their future motion, enabling anticipatory suppression of events.

**A. Anticipatory Motion Suppression**
The problem is framed as two coupled steps:
1. **Event Suppression:** Each incoming event is labeled as either IMO or ego-motion. This labeling is based on a binary mask that partitions events into "retain" or "suppress" categories, after converting the event stream into an image-like grid. The motion-suppression operator `S` filters an event stream `E` to produce `E'`, retaining events where a pixel `(x,y)` is marked dynamic by an instantaneous IMO mask `M_t(x,y)`.
2. **Motion Anticipation:** To counter the latency (`∆t_d`) inherent in processing, which leads to a lagging mask estimate (`M_{t-∆t_d}`), the method predicts how the scene will evolve. This involves estimating a per-pixel motion field (`ψ_{t+∆t}`) and warping the lagging binary mask forward in time to `M_f_t(x) = M_{t-∆t_d}(x - ψ_t(x))`. This warped mask is spatially aligned with the current scene, enabling anticipatory suppression based on past observations.

**B. Method Overview**
The model processes events `E` from a `∆t` temporal interval, converting them into a dense `B × H × W` stack representation using a two-bin temporal discretization. The network performs two simultaneous tasks:
- Generates a binary mask `M_t` (`H × W` resolution) segmenting IMO events from static background events.
- Predicts future dense optical flow `ψ_{t→t+t_p}` based on an input forecast time `t_p`.

**C. Multi-Task Prediction Architecture**
The architecture is inspired by prior work, and uses an `n`-stage convolutional GRU (conv-GRU) encoder for efficiency. Features from this encoder are processed by two parallel branches:
1. **Mask Decoder:** Raw encoder features are passed to a mask-specific decoder (`D_M`) to predict the current motion segmentation mask `M_t`.
2. **Optical Flow Decoder:** Encoder features are first processed by an **Attention-based Time Conditioning (ATC)** module to generate time-conditioned features. These are then fed to an optical flow decoder (`D_ψ`) to predict future flow `ψ_{t→t+∆t_p}`. Both decoders consist of `n` transposed convolutional layers with skip connections from the encoder blocks to retain spatial information.

**1. Attention-based Time Conditioning (ATC):**
This module modulates the current spatial feature embedding `E ∈ R^(B×C×H×W)` based on the target time delta (`∆t_p`) for optical flow forecasting. It employs a multi-head cross-attention mechanism:
- **Query (Q):** Derived from a temporal positional encoding `PE(∆t)` (a C-dimensional vector) broadcasted across all spatial positions.
- **Key (K) and Value (V):** Obtained by flattening the spatial dimensions of `E` into a sequence.
The cross-attention operation produces a modulated sequence, which is then reshaped back to the original spatial dimensions, yielding the time-conditioned embedding `E_{t+∆t}` for the optical flow decoder. The positional encoding uses a sinusoidal scheme for continuous time values `∆t`, mapping them to a `d`-dimensional vector.

**2. Mask Flow Warping:**
The predicted current mask logits (`M_t`) and the future optical flow (`ψ_{t→t+∆t_p}`) are combined. The mask logits are warped from time `t` to `t+1` using backward warping with the forward flow `ψ_{t_p}` (Equation 6), implemented via differentiable sampling with bilinear interpolation. Soft (logit) masks are propagated and trained with robust losses like Charbonnier to handle residual outliers near IMO boundaries and occluded regions.

**D. Training Losses**
The model is trained end-to-end with a hybrid loss `L_total = w_sup * L_sup + w_unsup * L_unsup`.
- **Supervised Loss (L_sup):**
 - **Segmentation Loss (L_mask):** A combination of Binary Cross-Entropy and Dice loss applied to both current (`M_t`) and future (`M_{t+∆t}`) predicted masks.
 - **Supervised Flow Loss (L_flow_sup):** An L1-norm data term for valid pixels and a Charbonnier smoothness loss on spatial gradients of the flow field, applied to both current (`ψ_t`) and future (`ψ_{t+∆t_p}`) flow predictions. For DSEC, this loss is applied only to static regions as provided flow often excludes IMOs.
- **Unsupervised Flow Loss (L_unsup):** Utilizes a contrast maximization objective. This encourages flow fields that "deblur" the event stream by warping events to a reference time `t_ref` to maximize the sharpness (focus metric) of the resulting Image of Warped Events (IWE).

**E. Training Setup**
The model is trained on the EVIMO and DSEC training splits. Events are aggregated into 50 ms windows as two-bin voxel grids. Mask loss supervision occurs at 100 ms intervals (matching ground truth frequency). Loss weights are configured for balancing the components, with specific considerations for datasets lacking ground-truth flow or having masked flow data (e.g., DSEC).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the Motion-aware Event Suppression framework demonstrates improvements across accuracy, efficiency, and utility in downstream tasks.

**A. Overall Performance and Efficiency**
The proposed model achieves an inference rate of 173 Hz on consumer-grade GPUs with less than 1 GB of memory usage. This efficiency enables anticipatory event suppression, where the model forecasts masks 100 ms into the future. By compensating for its 5.8 ms runtime, the system effectively delivers zero-latency masks with a positive prediction age of 94.2 ms. This contrasts with baseline methods that typically exhibit negative prediction ages (lag). Despite a higher theoretical computational complexity (32 GFLOPS) compared to EV-IMO (1.8 GFLOPS) and OMS (0.12 GFLOPS), the GPU-optimized architecture results in lower actual latency.

**B. Future Motion Segmentation on EVIMO**
The framework was evaluated for 100 ms future motion forecasting on the challenging EVIMO dataset. It consistently outperformed EVIMO\* (non-causal) and EVIMO† (causal) baselines:
- **R@0.5 (Ratio of Correctly Segmented Objects):** The method achieved an average 67% improvement over EVIMO† and 45% over EVIMO\*.
- **mIoU (Mean Intersection over Union):** It surpassed EVIMO\* and EVIMO† by 7% and 6% respectively, demonstrating effective segmentation of both IMO and ego-motion events.
- **Comparison to OMS:** The method achieved an average mIoU improvement of 61% over the OMS baseline, which struggled to capture true IMOs.

**C. Instantaneous Motion Segmentation on EVIMO**
On instantaneous motion segmentation on the EVIMO dataset, the approach demonstrated competitive performance:
- It outperformed unsupervised approaches by 55%, spiking neural network-based approaches by 40%, and state-of-the-art convolutional methods by 10% in point-based Intersection over Union (pIoU %).
- Frame-based methods like FlowSAM showed a 5.5x drop in accuracy compared to the event-based approach, indicating the advantage of event cameras for direct motion measurement.

**D. Motion Segmentation on EED**
Evaluated on the EED dataset (Event-based Ego-motion and Dynamic-object), which features low illumination and severe sensor noise, the model exhibited robustness:
- It achieved state-of-the-art results in Fast Drone (FD), Light Variation (LV), and Multiple Objects (MO) categories.
- Specifically, it outperformed the PMS baseline by 15.0% in low-light conditions (LV) and 4.3% in scenes with multiple dynamic agents (MO). This underscores the efficacy of the anticipatory suppression mechanism in isolating IMOs under challenging conditions.

**E. Ablation Studies**
- **Necessity of Non-linear Forecasting:** Comparisons against baselines (no forecasting, linear motion extrapolation, basic temporal summation without ATC) confirmed that the full model (ATC + Flow Warping) achieved superior mIoU, particularly in sequences with chaotic object motion. This indicates that learning to forecast curved pixel paths is crucial for robust anticipatory segmentation.
- **Model Capacity:** Scaling the architecture from XS (25% parameters) to L (100% parameters) showed consistent mIoU gains, especially in highly dynamic scenes. The 'L' model achieved peak accuracy while maintaining real-time performance.
- **Accuracy vs. Prediction Horizon:** A monotonic decrease in mIoU was observed as the prediction horizon `∆t_p` increased from 0 to 100 ms. This decay rate was scene-dependent, with faster degradation in scenes with rapid, non-smooth motion, confirming that performance is bounded by the prediction horizon.

**F. Application on Visual Odometry**
Integrating the anticipatory event suppression into the RAMP-VO pipeline, an open-source event- and frame-based visual odometry system, yielded accuracy improvements:
- The mean Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) was reduced to 0.21 m, an 8.7% improvement. This performance bridged the gap towards the maximum achievable gain of 13% observed when using ground-truth masks.
- Improvements were most pronounced in dynamic sequences (Boxes, Wall, Fast) where IMO features typically corrupt pose estimation, while performance remained stable in static scenes.

**G. Application on Token Pruning for Vision Transformers**
The dynamic IMO masks generated by the method were applied to guide token pruning in Vision Transformers (SViT), selectively focusing computation on dynamic agents:
- Using the original mask (SViT-0) increased inference speed by approximately 10 FPS compared to the full-token baseline (SViT-full), with a negligible drop of less than 7 points in Segmentation Average Precision (SegAP). This demonstrates a superior efficiency-accuracy balance without incurring additional time costs due to the masks being pre-forecasted.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework for real-time Anticipatory Motion Suppression, addressing a challenge in event-based perception: the efficient decoupling of events generated by ego-motion from those produced by independently moving objects (IMOs). The work offers a unified, end-to-end architecture that aims to balance accuracy and latency by learning to jointly segment IMOs and predict their future motion.

The significance of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Novelty in Event Filtering:** This is positioned as the first framework to directly address event suppression by anticipating motion, rather than merely reacting to it or relying on complex intermediate 3D reconstructions. This approach fundamentally alters how event data can be processed for downstream tasks.
- **Performance Advancement:** The framework establishes new performance benchmarks in event-based motion segmentation. The reported 67% improvement in segmentation accuracy on the EVIMO benchmark, coupled with a 53% faster inference rate (173 Hz), represents a substantial advancement over previous state-of-the-art methods.
- **Practical Applicability:** The lightweight architecture and high inference rate make the system suitable for real-world, resource-constrained applications. Its low memory footprint and ability to run on consumer-grade GPUs broaden its potential for deployment in various robotic and embedded systems.
- **Enabling Technology for Downstream Tasks:** By providing a "clean" event stream—either filtered for IMOs or isolated for ego-motion—the method enhances the robustness and efficiency of subsequent perception pipelines. The demonstrated improvements in visual odometry accuracy (13% ATE reduction) and Vision Transformer inference speed (83% acceleration via token pruning) highlight its practical utility.
- **Impact on Robotic Systems:**
 - **Autonomous Driving:** The ability to accurately and anticipatorily identify and track dynamic objects, while maintaining robust ego-motion estimation, is critical for safe and efficient navigation.
 - **AR/VR Devices:** Improved and stable visual odometry, free from the perturbations of scene dynamics, can lead to more immersive and responsive augmented and virtual reality experiences.
 - **General Robotic Perception:** The framework offers a foundational capability for any robotic system relying on event cameras to perceive and react to dynamic environments, including aerial platforms, legged robots, and manipulators.

The research also acknowledges limitations, such as potential mask misalignment under large ego-motion or fast occlusions, performance degradation with increasing prediction horizons, and sensitivity to event density (optimal range between 2x10^3 and 10^6 events). These aspects indicate areas for future research, including uncertainty-aware warping and broader validation across diverse robotic platforms and environmental conditions.

In summary, this work represents a step towards making event-based perception more robust, efficient, and practical, particularly for latency-critical robotic applications.
