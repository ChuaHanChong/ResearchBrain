# TIP: Token Importance in On-Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Princeton University and collaborators introduced a two-axis taxonomy for token importance in on-policy knowledge distillation, identifying previously overlooked "overconfident but wrong" error tokens and developing a parameter-free selection score to address this. This method achieved up to 58% peak memory savings and consistently improved student model performance in mathematical reasoning and agentic planning tasks.

## Problem

- Standard on-policy distillation applies supervision to all tokens, which can be computationally inefficient due to redundant or low-value signals.
- Existing token selection methods, often relying solely on student entropy, overlook a critical category of "confident but wrong" errors where the student is certain but incorrect.
- A comprehensive framework was lacking to systematically analyze different types of informative tokens based on student confidence and teacher-student agreement.

## Method

- A ==two-axis taxonomy== **TIP** categorizes tokens based on ==student entropy== and ==teacher-student divergence== into four quadrants.
- Theoretically proved a ==blind spot== for entropy-only methods, showing their inability to identify ==confident but wrong (Q3)== tokens.
- A ==parameter-free Soft-OR score== combines student entropy and teacher-student divergence, recovering the Q3 blind spot for comprehensive token selection.

## Results

- Entropy-based token selection at **50% retention** matched or exceeded baseline on math reasoning while reducing peak memory by up to **47%** (peak savings up to **58%**).
- Training on **<10%** of "overconfident" (Q3) tokens nearly matched or surpassed the all-token baseline accuracy, confirming their dense corrective signal.
- Soft-OR score outperformed entropy-only methods: **79.1%** vs **78.6%** for 50% retention on MATH-500 with Qwen3, with larger gains under aggressive retention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token importance in on-policy distillation can be robustly classified by considering both student uncertainty (entropy) and disagreement with the teacher (divergence).
> - "Overconfident and wrong" (Q3) tokens, where the student exhibits low entropy but high divergence, contain crucial corrective learning signals that are missed by entropy-only approaches.
> - Focusing training on a carefully selected subset of tokens based on a combined score (Soft-OR) can lead to both significant memory savings and improved student model performance.
