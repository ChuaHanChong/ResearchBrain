# Dual-Stream Diffusion for World-Model Augmented Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The DUAL-STREAM DIFFUSION (DUST) framework addresses the challenges of joint action and future observation prediction in robotic policy learning by using a dual-stream architecture and decoupled training. This approach yielded improved success rates by 18% on RoboCasa and 13% on real-world manipulation tasks compared to existing vision-language-action baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often fail to explicitly model world dynamics, leading to poor performance in tasks requiring sequential reasoning.
- Unified joint diffusion models for VLA suffer from a "modality conflict" due to the inherently different characteristics of low-dimensional actions and high-dimensional visual observations.
- Causal diffusion models for VLA lack bidirectional information flow, limiting comprehensive knowledge transfer between actions and their visual consequences.

## Method

- DUST introduces a Dual-Stream Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) architecture that processes action and vision tokens in separate pathways, allowing for bidirectional cross-modal attention without forcing a unified latent space.
- A decoupled diffusion training algorithm applies independent noise schedules to actions and future observation embeddings, enabling the model to learn diverse causal relationships.
- An asynchronous joint sampling strategy during inference allows for different denoising rates for vision and action tokens, providing a tunable trade-off between speed and accuracy.

## Results

- DUST consistently outperformed baselines on simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa and GR-1) and real-world Franka robot tasks, achieving an 18% higher average success rate on RoboCasa and 13% on real-world tasks compared to GR00T-N1.5.
- Pretraining DUST with action-free video data from BridgeV2 significantly improved data efficiency and generalization, boosting RoboCasa success rates by an additional 17% (from 0.501 to 0.585) with limited demonstrations.
- The asynchronous joint sampling strategy allowed for performance gains of 2-5% on both RoboCasa and GR-1 by increasing vision denoising steps without proportionally increasing action steps, demonstrating effective test-time scaling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly separating processing streams for different modalities while enabling shared cross-modal attention effectively resolves the "modality conflict" in multimodal generation.
> - Decoupled noise scheduling during training facilitates the learning of both forward and inverse causal dependencies between actions and future states.
> - Asynchronous denoising at inference time allows for efficient scaling, optimizing performance by dedicating more computational steps to high-dimensional modalities like vision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly leveraging Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to develop general-purpose robotic policies. These models build upon the extensive perceptual grounding achieved by internet-scale vision-language models (VLMs), enabling robots to interpret instructions and generate actions that generalize across various objects, scenes, and tasks. Current VLA models, such as those based on diffusion policies, demonstrate capabilities in precise, instruction-conditioned action generation.

However, a noted limitation in many existing VLA approaches is their infrequent explicit modeling of the physical processes that govern how generated actions transform the environment. This can lead to difficulties in understanding the underlying dynamics and predicting future states. To address this, a line of research has focused on augmenting VLAs with world-modeling objectives, aiming to jointly predict future observations and actions. The premise is that learning the joint distribution of these two modalities allows models to capture latent dynamics, thereby potentially improving performance and generalization.

Within world-model augmented VLAs, two primary architectural approaches have been explored:
1. **Unified Joint Diffusion Models:** These approaches, exemplified by works such as PAD and EnerVerse, typically concatenate action and vision tokens, forcing both modalities into a single latent space. A challenge with this approach is the "modality gap," which arises from the distinct statistical properties and dimensionalities of low-dimensional, temporally smooth action trajectories versus high-dimensional, spatially complex visual data. Jointly predicting these disparate data types within a single representation can lead to suboptimal learning.
2. **Causal Diffusion Models:** Approaches like Video Policy and Video Prediction Policy utilize disjoint architectures where modalities are separated, and information flow is primarily unidirectional (e.g., action generation conditioned on world-modeling outputs). While this helps in learning modality-specific structures, it inherently restricts bidirectional knowledge transfer, potentially limiting a comprehensive understanding of action-perception causality.

This research introduces DUST (DUal-STream diffusion) as an approach designed to reconcile the challenges faced by these existing paradigms. It aims to develop a framework that maintains the benefits of modality-specific processing while facilitating cross-modal integration. DUST specifically addresses the trade-off between cross-modal integration and modality-specific fidelity in world-model augmented VLA design, thereby contributing to the development of more physically grounded and effective robotic policies. The work also situates itself within the context of recent trends in world action models (WAMs) that repurpose video generation backbones for joint video and action prediction, noting DUST's orthogonal focus on augmenting VLAs directly with world-modeling for improved action generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to propose and evaluate DUST, a world-model augmented VLA framework that can effectively learn joint distributions of states and actions while mitigating the challenges associated with the modality gap inherent in such joint predictions.

The key motivations behind the development of DUST are:

- **Addressing the Modality Gap in Joint Prediction:** Existing world-model augmented VLAs often struggle to jointly predict low-dimensional action trajectories and high-dimensional visual observations. Unified models merge these distinct modalities into a single latent space, potentially causing a mismatch in learning. Causal models, while separating modalities, restrict bidirectional information flow. DUST is motivated to bridge these approaches by maintaining distinct modality streams for actions and observations, allowing specialized processing, while still enabling cross-modal information exchange through shared attention layers. This design aims to achieve better integration without compromising modality-specific fidelity.

- **Learning Cross-Modal Causal Relationships:** The ability for a robot to understand how its actions affect the environment (forward dynamics) and what actions are needed to achieve a desired future state (inverse dynamics) is crucial for robust policy learning and generalization. DUST is motivated to explicitly learn these causal relationships by introducing a decoupled diffusion training algorithm. This involves applying independent noise perturbations to each modality (actions and future observations) during training. By exposing the model to scenarios where one modality is clean while the other is noisy (and vice versa), the model is compelled to infer causal dependencies, moving beyond mere correlation.

- **Optimizing Inference-Time Efficiency and Accuracy:** Actions and visual observations have different requirements for denoising steps in diffusion models. High-dimensional visual data typically benefits from more denoising steps for refinement, whereas low-dimensional actions can converge in fewer steps and may even degrade with excessive steps. This disparity motivates the introduction of an asynchronous sampling method. This method allows for differential denoising rates for action and vision tokens, enabling a tunable trade-off between inference speed and predictive accuracy, which is critical for real-time robotic control.

- **Enhancing Transfer Learning Capabilities:** Acquiring large-scale, task-specific robot demonstrations is costly. The research is motivated to enable more effective transfer learning by leveraging diverse data sources. DUST aims to demonstrate its capacity for:
 - Pretraining on action-free videos, allowing the model to learn generalizable world dynamics without requiring explicit action labels, thereby utilizing inexpensive, large-scale video data.
 - Joint training with heterogeneous robot and human datasets, leveraging DUST's world-modeling capabilities to align structurally varied data within a shared representational framework, which could lead to more robust policies.

In summary, DUST seeks to develop a more principled and effective world-model augmented VLA by explicitly addressing the architectural, training, and inference challenges posed by jointly modeling action and vision, with the ultimate goal of improving robotic policy learning, generalization, and data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DUST is a world-model augmented Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that integrates a multi-modal diffusion transformer with specific training and sampling strategies to jointly predict states and actions.

**Overall Framework:**
The DUST framework utilizes a frozen, pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone (Eagle-2) to extract high-level semantic features (Φ_t) from the current visual observation (o_v_t) and task instruction (I). These features serve as conditioning signals for the core diffusion model, π_θ. The diffusion model's input consists of the robot's proprioceptive state (o_s_t), a noised action sequence (A_τ_t), and a noised future observation embedding (˜o_τ_t+k).

**DUST Architecture (Multimodal Diffusion Transformer - MMDiT):**
The core of DUST's architecture is a stack of Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) blocks, inspired by MMDiT models.
- **Dual Streams:** The architecture maintains separate token streams for actions and visual observations. These streams are processed independently for most operations within each block.
- **Cross-Modal Attention:** Information exchange between the action and vision streams occurs through shared cross-modal attention layers. After this interaction, the tokens are separated back into their respective streams.
- **Modality-Specific Timestep Embeddings:** To facilitate independent dynamics and support the decoupled training objective, each stream receives its own distinct timestep embedding via adaptive layernorm (AdaLN).
- **Specialized Denoising:** After traversing the shared MMDiT layers, the two token streams are routed into modality-specific DiT blocks for further specialized denoising. This final stage allows the vision pathway to focus on reconstructing semantically consistent future embeddings, while the action pathway refines low-level motor control. The diffusion backbone consists of 12 MMDiT blocks followed by 4 modality-specific DiT blocks for each stream.

**World-Modeling Target:**
Rather than predicting future visual observations in pixel space, DUST predicts future observation embeddings (˜o_t+k). This target is derived from a pre-trained vision encoder (specifically, SIGLIP-2 representations from the Eagle-2 VLM). This embedding-based approach aims to capture the semantic and structural content of the scene while abstracting away low-level visual noise, which can be less relevant for control and computationally intensive to model. Each image yields 256 tokens from the embedding model, reduced to 64 tokens via 2x2 average pooling. The diffusion module processes 1 state token, 16 action tokens, and 64 future image tokens.

**Joint Training Algorithm (Decoupled Diffusion):**
DUST employs a decoupled diffusion training algorithm, drawing inspiration from diffusion forcing.
- **Independent Noise Schedules:** During training, timesteps τ_A for actions and τ_o for future vision are sampled independently from a beta distribution. Corresponding Gaussian noises ϵ_A and ϵ_o are sampled.
- **Noised Modalities:** The ground-truth action sequence A_t and future observation embedding ˜o_t+k are independently noised to create A_τ_A_t = τ_A A_t + (1 - τ_A)ϵ_A and ˜o_τ_o_t+k = τ_o ˜o_t+k + (1 - τ_o)ϵ_o.
- **Velocity Network Prediction:** The diffusion model, V_θ, predicts the velocity field for each modality, conditioned on the VLM feature Φ_t, noised actions, noised future observations, and proprioceptive state. The outputs are V_A_θ and V_o_θ.
- **Modality-Specific Flow Matching Loss:** The model is optimized using separate flow matching losses for actions (L_A) and world-modeling (L_WM), which predict the ground-truth velocity fields (A_t - ϵ_A) and (˜o_t+k - ϵ_o), respectively.
- **Joint Objective:** The overall training objective is a weighted sum of these two losses: L_Joint(θ) = L_A(θ) + λ_WM * L_WM(θ), where λ_WM is a weighting hyperparameter set to 1.0. This decoupled noising forces the model to learn bidirectional causal dependencies by exposing it to various noise combinations.

**Vision-Action Joint Sampling (Asynchronous Denoising):**
During inference, DUST utilizes an asynchronous forward Euler sampling strategy to account for the differing denoising requirements of actions and vision.
- **Separate Noise Initialization:** Action noise A_0_t ∼ N(0, I_A) and future vision noise ˜o_0_t+k ∼ N(0, I_v) are initialized.
- **Asynchronous Step Sizes:** A fixed number of diffusion steps for actions, N_A (default 4), is set. The number of steps for vision, N_o, can be a multiple (q) of N_A (N_o = q × N_A).
- **Differential Updates:** The global timestep advances by ∆τ_o = 1/N_o. Vision tokens are updated at every fine-grained step. Action tokens are updated less frequently, only every q steps, corresponding to their larger effective step size ∆τ_A = 1/N_A.
- **Test-Time Scaling:** This asynchronous approach allows for test-time scaling, where increasing q (and thus N_o) can improve predictive accuracy for vision-intensive tasks at the cost of increased inference time, offering a tunable trade-off.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DUST involved extensive experiments across simulated, real-world, and transfer learning scenarios, comparing its performance against current VLA and world-modeling baselines.

#### 4.1. Main Results: Simulated and Real-World Environments

- **RoboCasa (Single-Arm Manipulation):** DUST demonstrated consistent outperformance across 24 kitchen-environment tasks with varying demonstration scales (100, 300, 1000 demos).
 - With 100 demonstrations, DUST achieved an average success rate of 50.1%, surpassing GR00T-N1.5 (41.7%) by 18% and FLARE (44.6%) by 5%.
 - These gains were maintained as the number of demonstrations increased, indicating data efficiency and scalability.
- **GR-1 (Humanoid Manipulation):** On this more challenging benchmark of 24 dexterous tabletop manipulation tasks, DUST also exceeded all baselines.
 - At 300 demonstrations, DUST achieved an average success rate of 36.0%, compared to GR00T-N1.5 (20.3%) and FLARE (33.7%).
 - At 1000 demonstrations, DUST reached 42.0% average success rate, outperforming GR00T-N1.5 (30.8%) and FLARE (36.3%).
- **Franka Research 3 (Real-World Tasks):** DUST was evaluated on 7 diverse real-world tasks (pick-and-place, insertion, tool-using).
 - DUST achieved the highest success rates on every task, with an average success rate of 59.9%.
 - This represents an average improvement of 13% over the GR00T-N1.5 baseline (46.5%) and 10.4% over the FLARE-enhanced model (49.5%). These results indicate robustness in physical environments.

#### 4.2. Transfer Learning

- **Joint Training with Heterogeneous Data:** DUST's ability to leverage mixed robot and human datasets was evaluated by augmenting RoboCasa training with GR-1 and EgoDex data.
 - When joint-trained, DUST's average success rate on RoboCasa increased from 58.5% (trained only on RoboCasa 300 demos) to 64.4%. This gain was also observed for baselines but DUST maintained its superior performance, suggesting effective utilization of heterogeneous data.
- **Pretraining with Action-Free Videos:** DUST was pretrained on the video component of the BridgeV2 dataset (action-free) before finetuning on RoboCasa (100 demos).
 - Incorporating video pretraining resulted in substantial gains, with DUST achieving an average success rate of 58.5% compared to 50.1% without pretraining. This indicates that large-scale passive video data can effectively transfer to downstream policy learning.

#### 4.3. Test-Time Scaling for Joint Sampling

- The asynchronous joint sampling strategy was evaluated by increasing the number of vision denoising steps (N_o) while keeping action steps (N_A) fixed at 4.
 - Increasing N_o from 4 to 64 led to performance gains of approximately 2–3% on RoboCasa and up to 5% on GR-1 (at N_o=32). This demonstrates a tunable trade-off between inference speed and accuracy.
 - A synchronous scaling approach, where both N_A and N_o were increased simultaneously, led to performance degradation, thus validating the necessity of asynchronous denoising.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Architecture and Noise Sampling:** Both the dual-stream MMDiT architecture and the decoupled training algorithm were found to be essential.
 - Removing the dual-stream structure resulted in an 8% performance drop.
 - Removing decoupled noise led to a 12% performance reduction. These findings suggest complementary contributions from both design choices.
- **Loss Weight Hyperparameter (λ_WM):** Performance remained stable within the range λ_WM ∈ [0.5, 2.0], indicating that a relatively even weighting of action and world-modeling objectives is beneficial.
- **MMDiT Layer Count:** Optimal performance was observed with 12 MMDiT layers (out of a total 16 layers), highlighting the benefit of substantial cross-modal processing.
- **Inference Time:** DUST achieved approximately 40Hz with 4 denoising steps, which is 3–10 times faster than several baseline VLA models. Even with 32 denoising steps for enhanced accuracy, DUST maintained about 8Hz, considered practical for closed-loop control.
- **Latent Embedder Choice:** Semantic embeddings (SigLIP-2 and DINOv2) performed comparably or marginally better than reconstructive latents (Flux.1 VAE) for the world-modeling target. This suggests that high-frequency pixel details are not critical for policy learning and can be irrelevant or distracting.
- **Action-Vision Causal Dependency Verification:** Conditioning action generation on ground-truth future states with varying noise levels showed a monotonic degradation in action accuracy as future state noise increased. This outcome supports the conclusion that DUST learned meaningful cross-modal causal dependencies.
- **Zero Vision Denoising Steps:** Even when vision denoising was disabled during inference (0 steps), DUST's performance (47.9% average success rate on RoboCasa 100 demos) still surpassed the GR00T-N1.5 (41.7%) and FLARE (44.6%) baselines. This indicates that the world-modeling training objective guides the internal representations to produce robust actions even without explicit visual reconstruction at inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DUST framework presents contributions to the field of robotic policy learning, particularly in the domain of world-model augmented Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The primary significance of this work lies in its proposed solutions to existing challenges in jointly modeling actions and visual observations for robotic control.

1. **Improved Handling of the Modality Gap:** By introducing a dual-stream architecture (MMDiT) that maintains separate token streams for actions and vision while enabling controlled cross-modal information exchange, DUST offers a method to reconcile the distinct characteristics of these modalities. This approach aims to avoid the limitations of both unified latent spaces, which can struggle with disparate data types, and purely causal models, which restrict bidirectional knowledge flow. This could lead to more accurate and robust joint predictions in VLA models.

2. **Enhanced Learning of Causal Relationships:** The decoupled diffusion training algorithm, which applies independent noise perturbations to actions and visual observations, is designed to explicitly teach the model about the causal dependencies between actions and their environmental outcomes. This explicit learning of forward and inverse dynamics could enable robots to develop a more grounded understanding of their environment, potentially leading to improved planning capabilities, better anticipation of action consequences, and increased adaptability to novel situations.

3. **Demonstrated Performance Gains and Generalization:** The consistent outperformance of DUST across diverse simulated benchmarks (RoboCasa, GR-1) and real-world tasks (Franka Research 3) compared to state-of-the-art baselines indicates that the proposed architectural and training strategies yield more effective robotic policies. These results suggest that DUST can lead to more reliable and generalizable robot behavior in complex manipulation scenarios.

4. **Advancements in Transfer Learning and Data Efficiency:** The research demonstrates DUST's capacity for effective transfer learning through two key avenues:
 - **Pretraining on Action-Free Videos:** The ability to leverage large-scale, inexpensive passive video data for world-modeling pretraining and subsequent transfer to downstream policy learning significantly reduces the reliance on costly, task-specific robot demonstrations. This could democratize access to advanced robot learning by lowering data acquisition barriers.
 - **Joint Training with Heterogeneous Data:** DUST's effectiveness in integrating diverse robot and human datasets within a unified framework suggests a scalable approach to leveraging the growing volume of varied robotics data. This can contribute to developing more robust policies that benefit from broad experiences across different embodiments.

5. **Flexible and Efficient Inference:** The asynchronous joint sampling strategy provides a practical mechanism to balance inference speed and predictive accuracy. This flexibility allows DUST to operate efficiently (e.g., ~40Hz at 4 denoising steps) for real-time closed-loop control while also offering an option for higher-accuracy predictions for less time-sensitive tasks. This adaptability is valuable for practical deployment in various robotic applications.

In summary, DUST contributes to the ongoing effort to create more intelligent and capable robotic systems by addressing fundamental challenges in VLA design and training. Its methods for integrating world models, learning causality, and enabling data-efficient transfer learning hold potential for advancing the development of generalist robot policies with a more comprehensive understanding of their physical surroundings.
