# MeRoPE: Metric Rotary Position Embedding for Camera-Controlled Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> MeRoPE is a norm-preserving relative camera positional encoding for camera-controlled video generation. It represents ray-local relative orientations with orthogonal rotation blocks, maps raw metric camera displacements into multi-frequency rotary phases, and adds disparity-anchored rotations along the spherical epipolar arc. Feature norms and pre-softmax logits stay bounded at any physical baseline, yielding the best joint rotation-translation consistency on nuScenes and PanShot.

## Problem

- Homogeneous projective camera encodings inject translation as an unnormalized inner product, so attention logits and feature norms grow unbounded with the physical translation baseline.
- Normalizing translation to a fixed scale discards physical magnitude, while world-frame or direction-only ray encodings either omit metric translation or depend on the chosen global coordinate frame.
- Depth-anchored, pixel-plane reprojection encodings identify translation only up to a depth scale and require dataset-specific metric-depth intervals or learned depth prediction.

## Method

- **Ray-local MinRot framing**: ==Rodrigues minimum-rotation matrices== carry the optical axis onto each calibrated unit viewing ray, giving orthogonal relative-orientation blocks singular only at the exact backward direction.
- **Query-frame metric translation RoPE** maps each Cartesian displacement axis onto logarithmically spaced ==rotary frequencies==; query-camera grouping relaxes strict ==per-token factorization==, which their impossibility theorem proves must be given up.
- **Disparity-anchored spherical encoding** samples anchors along the ==epipolar great-circle arc== between the transformed key ray and the epipole, block-diagonally summed with the backbone's native ==spatio-temporal RoPE==.

## Results

- On nuScenes with a matched Wan2.2 5B backbone, MeRoPE reaches the lowest CamMC **2.32** (UCPE **2.45**), best rotation error **1.37** degrees, and lowest FID **19.82** / FVD **134.15**.
- Component ablations isolate roles: dropping metric translation degrades tr% to **4.31** and CamMC to **3.66**, while adding ray-local rotation cuts rotation error from **1.66** to **1.37** degrees.
- On PanShot's **272** multi-lens clips, TransErr falls to **10.99** versus UCPE's **15.15**, and to **12.66** versus **17.91** when conditioning-pose near-depth normalization is disabled.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Orthogonality is the load-bearing property: norm-preserving attention operators stop long physical baselines from numerically drowning out rotational geometry and semantic visual content.
> - Full metric pose, strict per-token factorization, and norm preservation are provably incompatible, so pick which axiom to relax deliberately, here factorization, at extra attention cost.
> - Pixel-plane or depth-anchored relative encodings inherit monocular scale ambiguity; bounded angular anchors in ray space give correspondence priors without dataset-specific depth ranges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of camera-controlled video generation is a developing area within computer vision, with applications in domains such as autonomous driving simulation and robotics. Existing methodologies for achieving camera control in video synthesis can be broadly categorized into several pathways:

- **Feature fusion and adapters:** These methods integrate compact pose or ray representations into generative models, often by concatenating camera extrinsics with temporal features or embedding target extrinsics into transformer features. Examples include MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, ReCamMaster, and CamCo. These approaches offer control but may not inherently encode relative viewpoint relations within attention mechanisms.
- **Explicit 3D guidance and rendering:** This pathway derives structural conditions from estimated 3D geometry, for instance, by rendering point-cloud caches or using depth-based warping. Gen3C, Lyra 2.0, ViewCrafter, and TrajectoryCrafter are examples. While providing direct spatial guidance, their effectiveness can be limited by errors in estimated depth, pose, or scene motion.
- **Adaptive modulation:** This involves injecting camera-dependent affine transformations rather than directly fusing conditions. BulletTime and CompoSIA fall into this category.
- **Geometry-aware positional encodings (PEs):** This family of methods integrates camera relations directly into the attention mechanism of generative models, aiming to eliminate the need for external 3D proxies during inference. Early work in this area operated on camera-level extrinsic transformations (CaPE, GTA, PRoPE) or projective viewing frustums. Subsequent advancements introduced per-token 3D ray representations (UCPE, RayNova), and more recent efforts have incorporated depth cues (URoPE, RayRoPE, CRePE).

This paper specifically targets a limitation within the geometry-aware positional encoding paradigm. It identifies that existing schemes, particularly those based on homogeneous projective encodings (e.g., GTA, PRoPE, UCPE), encounter a scale-dependent failure mode when applied to real-world metric camera trajectories. In these formulations, unnormalized inner products for physical translation cause attention logits and feature norms to increase boundlessly with the length of the physical displacement baseline. This leads to translational terms dominating attention scores over large displacements, suppressing visual content, and ultimately degrading trajectory control.

MeRoPE positions itself as a development within geometry-aware positional encodings by proposing a novel, norm-preserving relative camera encoding. It aims to overcome the identified instability of previous methods while maintaining metric fidelity, thereby enabling more robust and accurate camera control in video generation, particularly for large-baseline movements and diverse camera optics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified deficiency in existing geometry-aware positional encodings used for camera-controlled video generation. Specifically, these methods exhibit instability when processing real-world metric camera trajectories, especially those involving large physical displacements.

The authors pinpoint a "scale-dependent failure mode" in current techniques, particularly those employing homogeneous projective encodings. In such schemes, physical translation is incorporated as an unnormalized inner product. As the physical translation baseline (distance between camera origins) increases, this term causes attention logits and feature norms to grow without bound. This unbounded growth can lead to several adverse effects:
- **Dominance of translational terms:** Over large displacements, the translational component can artificially outweigh other geometric and semantic information, leading to biased attention scores.
- **Suppression of visual content:** The numerical dominance of translation can suppress the contribution of visual features to the attention mechanism.
- **Degraded trajectory control:** The instability in attention can result in poorer consistency between the commanded camera motion and the motion recovered from the generated video.

To systematically address this problem, the authors formalize three desiderata for camera positional encodings:
- **(A) Depth-independent full metric relative-pose dependence:** The encoding should retain both the direction and the physical scale of camera motion, without relying on an assumed or estimated scene depth. This ensures fidelity to real-world metric relationships (T^-1_i T_j).
- **(B) Strict per-token factorization:** The encoding should permit independent transformations for each query and key token, maintaining compatibility with standard attention mechanisms.
- **(C) Norm-preserving actions:** The encoding should prevent baseline-dependent amplification of attention logits and feature norms, ensuring stability.

However, the paper presents Theorem 1, which states that no continuous finite-dimensional encoding can simultaneously satisfy all three desiderata while remaining sensitive to metric translation. This "trilemma" implies a fundamental trade-off. Given that accurate camera control necessitates both metric fidelity (A) and scale stability (C), the authors prioritize these two properties and, consequently, relax the requirement for strict per-token factorization (B) by adopting a query-camera grouping approach.

The key objective of this work is therefore to introduce MeRoPE (Metric Rotary Position Embedding), a positional encoding designed to:
- Achieve norm-preservation (satisfy C) to prevent the unbounded growth of attention logits and feature norms.
- Maintain full metric relative-pose dependence (satisfy A) by encoding raw metric camera motion without requiring normalization or making assumptions about scene depth.
- Resolve the trilemma by relaxing strict per-token factorization (B) through query-camera grouping.
- Improve camera control performance on challenging datasets characterized by large translation baselines and diverse camera optics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MeRoPE is designed as a norm-preserving relative camera positional encoding, integrated into a Diffusion Transformer framework. The methodology addresses the identified scale-dependent instability by decomposing the intrinsic relative camera pose into orthogonal operators and employing a query-camera grouping strategy.

**Geometry-Aware Attention and Metric-Scale Limitation (Analysis)**
The paper begins by formalizing how geometry-aware attention layers operate, using a pairwise transformation operator $U_{ab}$ that modulates query-key comparison and value aggregation. It analyzes existing methods like GTA, which use a direct sum of 4x4 homogeneous relative-pose blocks for $U_{cam}$. The analysis shows that the translation term within this formulation (specifically, $h_k(u_q)^T \Delta o_{j|i}$) is an unnormalized linear inner product that scales linearly with the physical translation baseline $\Vert o_j - o_i \Vert_2$. This directly causes the unbounded growth of attention logits. Similarly, the non-orthogonal nature of $U_{cam}$ leads to unbounded scaling of aligned value features. This analysis motivates the design of MeRoPE.

**Orthogonal Metric Pose Encoding**
To address the norm and logit explosion, MeRoPE decomposes the relative camera pose into two orthogonal operators:
1. **Ray-local minimum-rotation (MinRot) block ($U_{rot_{ab}}$):**
 - **Representation:** Each image patch is mapped to a calibrated unit viewing ray $d^c_{i,p}$ in its camera coordinate frame, providing a camera-model-agnostic representation for diverse optics.
 - **Local Framing:** A local coordinate frame $A_{i,p}$ is constructed for each token ray. This is achieved using the Rodrigues minimum-rotation formula, which rotates the optical axis $e_z$ onto the viewing ray $d^c_{i,p}$. This construction is presented as being more robust than prior methods like UCPE, which can degenerate for certain vertical ray angles.
 - **Relative Rotation Embedding:** The pairwise relative rotation between query ray (i, p) and key ray (j, q) is computed as $A^T_{i,p} R^T_i R_j A_{j,q}$. This relative rotation is then embedded into the attention mechanism as a direct sum of *m* repeated 3D rotation blocks, ensuring strict norm preservation.
2. **Query-frame metric translation RoPE block ($U_{trans_{ij}}$):**
 - **Encoding Metric Displacement:** To encode the query-frame metric displacement $\Delta o_{j|i} = R^T_i (o_j - o_i)$ without logit growth, each Cartesian coordinate of the translation is mapped into multi-frequency rotary phases.
 - **Frequency Construction:** Following RoPE principles, *K* logarithmically spaced wavelengths ($\lambda_k$) and their corresponding angular frequencies ($\omega_k$) are constructed across a predefined range $[\lambda_{min}, \lambda_{max}]$.
 - **Translation Operator:** For each Cartesian axis (x, y, z), the 1D displacement rotates a 2D feature subspace by an angle proportional to the displacement and frequency. The complete translation operator is a direct sum over all three axes and *K* frequencies, using standard 2D rotation matrices $Rot(\theta)$. This design ensures orthogonality, feature norm preservation, and prevents attention logits from scaling with the physical translation baseline.

**Disparity-Anchored Spherical Encoding ($U_{disp}$)**
Beyond the scene-independent relative camera framing, MeRoPE incorporates a mechanism for potential static-scene correspondences:
- **Correspondence Hypotheses:** Instead of relying on 2D pixel epipolar lines (like CamCo) or projecting metric depth anchors (like URoPE), MeRoPE constructs correspondence anchors in spherical ray space using bounded angular disparity fractions. This avoids camera-model-specific issues and heuristic depth choices.
- **Epipolar Arc Construction:** For a non-zero baseline, the key ray direction in the query camera frame ($u_{\infty} = R^T_i R_j d^c_{j,q}$) and the epipole unit vector ($e = \Delta o_{j|i} / \Vert \Delta o_{j|i} \Vert_2$) are identified. As 3D scene depth varies, points along the key ray sweep a great-circle arc on the unit sphere connecting $u_{\infty}$ and $e$.
- **Disparity Anchors:** *L* discrete disparity anchors ($u_{i,\beta_l}$) are sampled along this epipolar arc at specific angular fractions. The Rodrigues rotation $A_{i,\beta_l}$ carries the query-camera optical axis $e_z$ onto each candidate direction $u_{i,\beta_l}$.
- **Encoding Relative Rotation:** The disparity encoding computes the relative rotation $A^T_{i,p} A_{i,\beta_l}$ from the query ray $d^c_{i,p}$ to each candidate direction $u_{i,\beta_l}$, repeated across *n* coordinate triplets. These anchors act as fixed geometric reference points, allowing the network to infer correspondences from visual content.
- **Degeneracy Handling:** Special cases where the epipole or great-circle direction is undefined (e.g., coinciding camera centers, parallel $e$ and $u_{\infty}$) are handled by collapsing all anchor directions to $u_{\infty}$.

**Complete MeRoPE Operator and Integration**
The final MeRoPE operator $U_{ab}$ is formed as a block-diagonal direct sum of $U_{disp}$, $U_{rot}$, $U_{trans}$, and $U_{native}$ (the backbone's native spatio-temporal RoPE). This composite operator is designed to satisfy both metric fidelity (A) and norm preservation (C) while accommodating the relaxation of strict per-token factorization (B).

MeRoPE is integrated into pretrained video Diffusion Transformers via a lightweight parallel attention branch. This adapter scheme, similar to UCPE, allows the base model to retain its original self-attention, while the parallel branch performs camera-conditioned attention modulated by $U_{ab}$. The output features from this branch are then mapped through a zero-initialized linear projection and added residually to the backbone stream, ensuring no perturbation to the pretrained backbone features at initialization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of MeRoPE was evaluated on two distinct benchmarks: nuScenes for large-baseline driving video generation and PanShot for multi-lens camera control. Comparisons were made against various matched-backbone baselines, including GTA, PRoPE, UCPE, RayNova, RayRoPE, URoPE, and a CameraCtrl-style baseline.

**Evaluation Setup:**
- **Datasets:** Training combined 103.6k real clips from nuScenes with 244.1k synthetic trajectories from World Engine.
- **Metrics:** Camera control was assessed using geodesic rotation error (rot°), scale-aligned relative translation error (tr%), pairwise joint-pose AUC (AUC@3, AUC@10), and Camera Motion Consistency (CamMC). Visual quality was measured by FID and FVD.
- **Baselines:** All methods shared the same Wan2.2 TI2V-5B backbone, initialization, training mixture, and optimization schedule, with camera-conditioned methods retrained with a consistent 15-block alternating injection schedule.

#### 4.1. Pose-Controlled Video Generation on Driving Benchmarks (nuScenes)

- **Overall Performance:** MeRoPE achieved the lowest CamMC (2.32), indicating superior consistency between generated and commanded camera motion in both rotation and translation. This was an improvement over the strongest baseline, UCPE (2.45).
- **Rotation and Translation Accuracy:** MeRoPE demonstrated the best rotation accuracy (lowest rot°). It also ranked second in scale-aligned relative translation error (tr%) and AUC@10. URoPE led in tr% and AUC metrics.
- **Visual Quality:** MeRoPE also attained the lowest FID and FVD point estimates, suggesting that its improvements in camera control did not compromise the visual quality of the generated videos.
- **Baseline Stability:** Empirical observations (Figure 1 in paper) showed that, unlike UCPE, MeRoPE's positional contribution remained bounded, preventing an artificial shift of attention mass to distant frames as the translation baseline grew.

#### 4.2. Ablation of Encoding Components (nuScenes)

Ablation studies confirmed the complementary roles of MeRoPE's three sub-blocks:
- **Metric Translation:** Removing the metric translation component consistently degraded translation fidelity, resulting in substantially worse tr% and CamMC across all variants. This indicates its crucial role in capturing translation magnitude accurately.
- **Ray-local Rotation:** This component primarily influenced orientation control. Its addition reduced rotation error.
- **Disparity Anchors:** These anchors were shown to refine cross-view tracking. Adding them improved both AUC metrics and lowered tr% and CamMC, while largely preserving rotation accuracy.
- **Combined Effect:** The combination of all three components yielded the full model's best tr% and CamMC scores.

Further ablations on architectural design and injection strategies:
- **Adapter Compression (C):** A compression ratio of C=4 provided a balance between performance and adapter capacity, retaining similar CamMC to C=2 with half the capacity.
- **Translation Frequencies:** Using multiple frequency bands for metric translation was crucial; a single frequency variant degraded tr% and CamMC.
- **Injection Schedule:** While injecting into every block performed marginally better, the default 15 alternating blocks offered a similar CamMC with half the executions. Peak modulation was observed in middle layers (Blocks 11 and 13), where most of the camera modulation amplitude concentrated.
- **Integration Style:** A dedicated parallel camera self-attention (CamSA) branch, as used in MeRoPE, provided a better overall balance of camera control compared to carving low-frequency camera rotary channels into the backbone's native self-attention (ReRoPE style).

#### 4.3. Camera-Diverse Evaluation on PanShot

Evaluations on the PanShot benchmark, which features pinhole, wide-angle, and fisheye lenses, yielded the following:
- **Translation Control:** MeRoPE demonstrated a significant advantage in translation control, achieving the lowest TransErr and CamMC across both conditioning-pose settings (with and without near-depth normalization).
- **Rotation Control:** MeRoPE and UCPE remained competitive on RotErr, with MeRoPE showing a slightly higher error than UCPE in this specific metric.
- **Unified Camera Model Benefit:** The wider performance gap between UCPE/MeRoPE and GTA/PRoPE on PanShot (compared to nuScenes) supported the benefit of calibrated ray representations for handling diverse camera optics.
- **Robustness to Raw Displacements:** MeRoPE maintained the lowest errors and the smallest performance degradation when training and generation used raw conditioning displacements (without near-depth normalization), demonstrating its stability with respect to physical scale.

#### 4.4. Real-World Grounding with Retrieved History (Appendix)

Experiments involving the integration of retrieved history (calibrated same-location images) showed:
- **Performance Gains:** Both history integration pathways (History CA - cross-attention, and History SA - self-attention) provided modest further improvements in pose control (CamMC, FID, and FVD) compared to MeRoPE without history.
- **Self-Attention Effectiveness:** History SA, which concatenated retrieved and current video tokens for joint self-attention processing, achieved superior visual anchoring and overall performance (best CamMC, FID, FVD) compared to History CA.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MeRoPE carries several significances and potential impacts on the field of camera-controlled video generation:

- **Addressing Fundamental Instability:** The work rigorously identifies and formalizes a critical scale-dependent failure mode in existing geometry-aware positional encodings. By presenting the "trilemma" (depth-independent full metric relative-pose dependence, strict per-token factorization, and norm-preserving actions), the authors highlight a fundamental limitation that prior methods implicitly or explicitly overlooked. MeRoPE offers a principled solution by prioritizing metric fidelity and norm preservation, even if it necessitates relaxing strict per-token factorization. This provides a more stable foundation for camera control in scenarios with large physical displacements.

- **Improved Camera Control Accuracy and Consistency:** Through its design, MeRoPE demonstrates improved accuracy and consistency in controlling camera motion, both in rotation and translation, across challenging datasets like nuScenes and PanShot. Its superior performance on CamMC, which jointly evaluates rotation and translation trajectories, suggests a more robust and reliable coupling between commanded poses and generated visual content. The observed avoidance of attention shifts under large baselines also indicates enhanced stability.

- **Enhanced Generalizability to Diverse Camera Optics:** The strong performance on PanShot, a benchmark featuring a variety of camera lenses (pinhole, wide-angle, fisheye), indicates MeRoPE's ability to handle diverse camera intrinsics effectively. This is attributable to its use of a camera-model-agnostic ray representation and a design that is robust across different optical characteristics.

- **Broader Applicability:** The advancements in stable and accurate camera control directly benefit applications requiring precise viewpoint manipulation in generative models. This includes:
 - **Autonomous Driving Simulation:** Generating realistic and controllable driving scenarios for testing and training autonomous vehicles.
 - **Robotics:** Creating simulated environments for robot training and task planning where precise camera movements are essential.
 - **Content Creation:** Enabling artists and developers to generate videos with specified camera paths for film, gaming, or virtual reality.

- **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper explicitly points to future work, particularly reducing the computational overhead associated with query-camera grouping. This acknowledgment directs subsequent research towards optimizing the efficiency of metric-sensitive encoding without sacrificing performance, further integrating this robust approach into real-world applications. The exploration of real-world grounding with retrieved history also suggests avenues for integrating external contextual information to enhance generative consistency.

In summary, MeRoPE represents a significant step forward in the robustness and accuracy of camera-controlled video generation, providing a more stable and metric-faithful approach to encoding camera poses within generative models.
