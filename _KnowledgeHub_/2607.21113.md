# RL-MACRO: A Cybernetic Closed-Loop Intelligence Framework for Multimodal Adaptive Robotic Craniotomy

> [!summary] Summary
> RL-MACRO, a cybernetic closed-loop intelligence framework, enables autonomous robotic craniotomy by reconstructing inaccessible cutting temperatures from force and sound signals and adaptively coordinating multiple machining parameters. The system demonstrated real-time temperature tracking with an R^2 of 0.9272 and achieved complete, safe bone-flap removal on ex vivo goat skulls without visible thermal damage or dura mater injury.

## Problem

- Autonomous robotic craniotomy faces challenges due to complex, coupled interactions between high-speed tools and cranial tissue, leading to risks like thermal necrosis, bone fracture, or dura mater injury.
- Critical safety parameters, specifically real-time cutting temperatures at the tool-tissue interface, are unobservable during surgery dueulating closed-loop thermomechanical control.
- Current adaptive control methods often use a single-input single-output (SISO) paradigm, which is insufficient for coordinating multiple machining parameters (feed rate, spindle speed, cutting depth) to balance safety and efficiency objectives.

## Method

- The **RL-MACRO** framework integrates a ==CNN-LSTM multimodal observer== that reconstructs latent cutting temperatures in real-time from multimodal force and sound sensor feedback.
- An offline ==Reinforcement Learning (IQL)== agent, featuring a ==dual-head actor architecture== guided by an implicit state classifier, dynamically optimizes feed rate, spindle speed, and cutting depth to balance material removal rate and safety thresholds.
- A ==cybernetic execution module== dynamically re-plans trajectories and employs a ==velocity servo strategy== to translate the RL agent's discrete, high-level actions into kinematically smooth and spatially continuous robot motions on irregular cranial surfaces.

## Results

- The multimodal observer accurately tracked thermal evolution during cutting on bovine ribs, achieving an R^2 of **0.9272** and a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of **2.105°C** on unseen samples.
- RL-MACRO significantly mitigated maximum temperature and force excursions (p = **0.001**) and achieved higher episodic returns (p = **0.005**) compared to a non-adaptive baseline in rib line-milling experiments.
- The framework successfully performed complete spiral craniotomy on **six** previously unseen ex vivo goat skulls, demonstrating cross-specimen generalizability without visible thermal damage or dura mater injury.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inaccessible safety-critical states, such as cutting temperature, can be reliably perceived and reconstructed in real-time from partial, multimodal sensor feedback, enabling proactive thermomechanical regulation.
> - Offline Reinforcement Learning provides a robust mechanism to learn complex, coordinated control policies for multiple coupled machining parameters, achieving a dynamic trade-off between safety and efficiency objectives.
> - Decoupling the control frequencies for different parameters (e.g., stepwise cutting depth, high-frequency feed rate/spindle speed adjustments) within an RL agent can improve stability and responsiveness in dynamic interaction tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Craniotomy is a fundamental neurosurgical procedure, and the increasing adoption of robot-assisted neurosurgery has facilitated high-precision, stable bone removal. However, achieving full autonomy in robotic craniotomy remains challenging due to the intricate and coupled interactions between high-speed rotating tools and cranial tissue. These interactions involve mechanical loading, frictional heat generation, heterogeneous bone properties, and complex anatomical geometries. Uncontrolled interactions pose significant risks, including thermal necrosis from excessive temperature, bone fracture, tool damage, or dura mater injury from excessive cutting force, and increased infection risk from prolonged procedures. Therefore, autonomous craniotomy necessitates a simultaneous balance of mechanical safety, thermal safety, and surgical efficiency.

A primary limitation in regulating this process is partial observability. While various sensors can monitor intraoperative states (e.g., force, sound, vibration, temperature), critical information, such as real-time cutting temperatures, is often inaccessible due to physical occlusion by the tool and the presence of coolant. Existing temperature monitoring methods, such as infrared cameras or thermocouples, are not amenable to high-frequency in vivo measurement under these conditions. Previous studies have explored offline temperature estimation using theoretical models or machine learning approaches, but these do not provide the real-time latent state reconstruction necessary for closed-loop thermomechanical regulation.

Furthermore, current adaptive control strategies in robotic bone cutting often follow a single-input single-output (SISO) paradigm, adjusting one process variable to regulate a single physical response. This approach is insufficient for coordinating multiple machining parameters—such as feed rate, spindle speed, and cutting depth—to address coupled mechanical, thermal, and efficiency objectives. The nonlinear and time-varying nature of tool-tissue interactions further complicates the development of accurate analytical control models.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a method for learning coordinated state-to-action mappings directly from interaction data, offering a potential solution to these control challenges. Prior applications of RL in robotics include adapting cutting parameters for unknown materials, optimizing machining energy, and using offline RL to learn policies from historical data, thereby avoiding unsafe online exploration. This paper builds upon these advancements by integrating multimodal perception and offline RL within a cybernetic framework to address the specific challenges of partial observability and multi-parameter coordination in autonomous robotic craniotomy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties and safety requirements of autonomous robotic craniotomy. The interaction between a high-speed tool and cranial tissue is complex, involving coupled mechanical forces, heat generation, varying bone properties, and irregular anatomical shapes. This complexity necessitates continuous regulation of tool-tissue interactions to prevent hazards such as:
- **Thermal damage:** Excessive heat can lead to thermal necrosis of bone and surrounding tissues, impairing healing.
- **Mechanical overload:** High cutting forces risk bone fracture, tool damage, or sudden skull breakthrough, potentially injuring the underlying dura mater.
- **Surgical inefficiency:** Prolonged cutting duration can increase the risk of infection and other complications.

The fundamental challenge in achieving precise regulation is **partial observability**. The exact, time-varying properties of bone are often unknown, and critical safety-related parameters, particularly the cutting temperature at the tool-tissue interface, cannot be directly measured in real-time under surgical conditions due to physical occlusion and coolant flooding. This lack of complete state information prevents conventional controllers from accurately assessing the consequences of their actions and optimizing parameters effectively. Existing research has addressed aspects of sensing and control, but a comprehensive framework that integrates real-time latent state reconstruction with coordinated, adaptive control of multiple machining parameters in an uncertain environment has been lacking.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a comprehensive cybernetic closed-loop intelligence framework (RL-MACRO):** This framework aims to couple multimodal perception, adaptive decision-making, and robotic execution to enable autonomous, safe, and efficient robotic craniotomy.
2. **Enable autonomous perception of inaccessible states:** Specifically, to reconstruct the hidden, safety-critical temperature state from partial sensory feedback (force and sound signals). This is essential for achieving closed-loop thermomechanical regulation.
3. **Facilitate dynamic optimization of robotic behaviors under uncertainty:** To learn and execute coordinated control policies that dynamically adjust multiple machining parameters (feed rate, spindle speed, cutting depth) to balance safety and efficiency objectives.
4. **Ensure seamless execution of adaptive decisions:** To translate discrete policy decisions into kinematically smooth and spatially continuous robotic motions, particularly on complex and irregular cranial surfaces.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to establish a data-driven cybernetic paradigm for autonomous bone cutting that can robustly adapt to unknown, time-varying tissue properties and operational constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The researchers propose RL-MACRO, a cybernetic closed-loop intelligence framework structured around a "perception–decision–execution" cascade. This framework addresses the challenges of partial observability and coordinated control in autonomous robotic craniotomy.

**Problem Formulation:**
The study models autonomous craniotomy as a partially observable cybernetic regulation problem. The latent physical state (x_t), including local temperature and bone characteristics, evolves based on robotic control commands (u_t) and unknown environmental disturbances (w_t). Due to occlusion, the full state (x_t) is unobservable, and the robot relies on noisy sensory feedback y_t = [F_t, S_t]^T (force and sound features). The framework aims to approximate a belief state (ˆb_t) that includes reconstructed temperature and discrete cutting regime probabilities, from which an RL agent generates coordinated actions a_t = [v_f,t, n_t, a_p,t]^T (feed rate, spindle speed, cutting depth). These actions are then translated into continuous robot joint commands (u_t) by an execution module.

**Experimental Platform:**
The experimental setup consisted of a 7-DoF KUKA LBR iiwa 14 robot equipped with a high-speed spindle (Nakanishi NR3060-AQC) and a 4mm ball-end cutter. Multimodal sensors were integrated at the end-effector: a microphone (BSWA MPA201) and a 6-DoF dynamometer (Kistler 9306A) for real-time feedback, and an infrared (IR) camera (Infiray Xtherm T3Pro) for ground-truth temperature measurement during offline data collection. Hardware interfacing for spindle regulation and analog signal digitization was managed by a Beckhoff I/O module and an NI-9220 module, respectively. A ROS 2-based host computer synchronized and logged all data. Fresh bovine ribs, which possess a cortical–cancellous–cortical architecture similar to human bone, were used for offline data collection.

**A. Cybernetic Perception: Multimodal Latent Temperature State Observer**
To address the partial observability of cutting temperature, a **CNN–LSTM multimodal observer** (E_ω) was developed.
- **Inputs:** Historical sequences of synchronized force and sound feedback (y_t-H:t).
- **Architecture:**
 - **Causal 1D-CNN:** Multiple causal 1D convolutional blocks (each containing layer normalization, GELU activation, dropout, and Conv1D layers) with strict left-padding and dilated convolutions (rates 1 and 2) process the input sequence to extract features (H_c). Causality ensures no future information leakage.
 - **Gated Residual Fusion Module:** A learnable gating mechanism (G = σ(W_g [H_c; R] + b_g)) dynamically weighs the CNN-extracted features (H_c) against linearly projected residual features (R), producing a fused sequence (Z = G ⊙ H_c + (1 − G) ⊙ R). This aims to preserve critical sensory information while emphasizing nonlinear features as needed.
 - **Two-layer Unidirectional LSTM:** Processes the fused sequence (Z) to capture long-term temporal dependencies and thermal inertia.
 - **Regression Head:** Maps the LSTM's hidden state to the reconstructed temperature rise (∆ˆT_t).
- **Training:** The observer was trained offline on a dataset collected from 60 distinct parameter combinations (feed rates, spindle speeds, cutting depths) on bovine ribs, using IR camera measurements as ground-truth temperature.

**B. Cybernetic Decision-Making: Safety-Aware Regulation via Offline RL**
The problem of converting perceived latent states into coordinated adaptive behavior was formulated as a partially observable offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) task using **Implicit Q-Learning (IQL)**.
- **Offline Dataset:** D = {(o_t, a_t, r_t, o_t+1)} was collected.
- **Control-Oriented Observation (o_t):** This combines the reconstructed temperature (∆ˆT_t) with measurable mechanical features (RMS force F_rms,t and sound entropy S_ent,t).
- **Action Vector (a_t):** Consists of the three continuous machining parameters: feed rate (v_f,t), spindle speed (n_t), and cutting depth (a_p,t).
- **Reward Function (r_t):** Designed to balance material removal rate (MRR) for efficiency and penalize safety threshold breaches.
 - **Efficiency Reward:** r_eff,t = w_eff · MRR_t, where MRR is calculated based on cutter geometry and cutting parameters.
 - **Safety Penalty:** P_safe,t = w_safe · ((F_rms,t − F_max)_+^2 + (∆ˆT_t − ∆T_max)_+^2), a quadratic ReLU penalty applied when force or temperature exceeds predefined maximum thresholds (F_max, ∆T_max).
- **Implicit State Classification:** K-means clustering was applied to mechanical features (F_rms,t, S_ent,t) to identify distinct cutting regimes, generating pseudo-labels. An MLP classifier (C_ϕ) was trained to predict a discrete state probability vector (p_t) from these features.
- **Approximate Belief State (ˆb_t):** Formed by concatenating the control-oriented observation (o_t) and the state probability vector (p_t).
- **Dual-Head Actor Architecture (π_θ):** A novel architecture designed to handle actions with different operational frequencies.
 - One branch uses only the state probability (p_t) to predict the cutting depth (ˆa_p,t), enabling smooth, stepwise transitions to prevent mechanical chattering.
 - The second branch uses the full belief state (ˆb_t) to continuously fine-tune the feed rate (ˆa_v,t) and spindle speed (ˆa_n,t) at high frequency.
 - The final policy output is the concatenation of these actions: π_θ(ˆb_t) = [ˆa_v,t, ˆa_n,t, ˆa_p,t]^T.
- **IQL Algorithm:** The Value network (V_η) is updated via expectile regression, and two Critics (Q_ψ1, Q_ψ2) minimize the Bellman error using the extended belief state. The Actor is updated via Advantage-Weighted Behavioral Cloning with exponential weights. A **diversity regularization term (L_div)** is added to the Actor's loss for the depth head to prevent mode collapse, ensuring distinct depth outputs for different cutting regimes.

**C. Cybernetic Execution: Spatially Continuous Action Mapping and Servo Feedback**
This module translates the discrete, high-level machining actions (a_t) from the RL agent into kinematically feasible and continuous robot motions (u_t).
- **Dynamic Trajectory Re-planning:** Based on a prior contour-extraction framework, it uses a local adaptive blending method.
 - A blending coefficient α(ϕ) ensures the trajectory dynamically conforms to heterogeneous cranial geometry.
 - When the RL agent updates the cutting depth (pitch P_j), the accumulated depth (D_j) is measured from the current Cartesian pose, and the remaining trajectory is instantly re-parameterized.
 - This ensures C0 spatial continuity at action update points and uses quaternion hemisphere consistency to prevent rotational jumps.
- **Velocity Servo Strategy:** Implements composite arc-length interpolation for smooth trajectory tracking.
 - The control period is clamped (∆t_k ∈ [∆t_min, ∆t_max]) to mitigate operating system jitter.
 - The RL-commanded feed rate (v_ref) is smoothed via an acceleration-limited first-order filter to prevent mechanical shocks.
 - A composite Cartesian arc length (d_k) combines translational and rotational distances for step-length calculation.
 - An interpolation ratio (η_k) determines advancement along the trajectory, and inverse kinematics resolves expected joint angles.
 - An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) observer tracks the physically accumulated arc length (∆s_acc,k) to estimate actual velocity (¯v_k).
 - An adaptive gain law dynamically corrects the step-length multiplier (K_k) based on the commanded and estimated velocities, ensuring robust tracking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the RL-MACRO framework across latent temperature perception, adaptive policy response, continuous spatial execution, and cross-sample robustness.

**A. Rib Line-Milling Experiment:**
Experiments were conducted on fresh bovine ribs, which provide a repeatable cortical–cancellous–cortical structural transition and allow for continuous IR thermal tracking as ground-truth temperature reference. RL-MACRO was benchmarked against a constant policy (derived from its own temporal mean actions) lacking state-dependent regulation.
- **Online Temperature Observability (Fig. 8):** The perceived temperature reliably tracked the measured thermal evolution across distinct bone layers. There was an absence of significant phase lag during rapid heating. The cross-sample regression achieved an R^2 of 0.9272. The multimodal observer yielded a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 2.105 °C with a minor positive bias of 0.967 °C on unseen rib samples.
- **Dynamic Closed-Loop Response (Fig. 9):** RL-MACRO demonstrated dynamic adaptation, where tool–tissue interaction variations continuously updated the belief state, leading to immediate policy adjustments. The dual-head Actor architecture stabilized cutting depth through smooth stepwise transitions, preventing mechanical chattering, while fine-tuning feed rate and spindle speed at high frequency. The system exhibited a robust deviation–adaptation–recovery pattern, adjusting actions when force or temperature approached thresholds (27.0 N and 30.0 °C). In contrast, the non-adaptive baseline triggered severe safety penalty activations in hard cortical bone regions, leading to a significant drop in cumulative reward. Paired t-tests (n=9) confirmed that RL-MACRO significantly mitigated maximum temperature and force excursions (p = 0.001) and achieved a statistically significant advantage in episodic returns (p = 0.005).

**B. Cross-Specimen Closed-Loop Validation on Ex Vivo Goat Skulls:**
To assess generalizability and robustness under complex anatomical geometries, spiral craniotomy was performed on six unseen ex vivo goat skulls. The CNN–LSTM observer acted as the sole online temperature estimator.
- **Closed-Loop Temporal Responses (Fig. 10):** The skulls exhibited significant spatial anisotropy, leading to surgical durations varying from 210 s to 260 s. During initial penetration, low tangential velocity and dense outer cortex often caused force and temperature excursions. RL-MACRO promptly orchestrated compensatory adjustments in feed rate and spindle speed, occasionally overriding cutting depth. The rapid decay of thermomechanical responses following adjustments indicated a robust cybernetic loop. Periodic force/temperature fluctuations, possibly due to internal porosity in some skulls, were mirrored by periodic parameter oscillations, suggesting active engagement. A transient threshold breach in one skull due to aggressive feed-rate exploration was autonomously rectified by the agent.
- **Spatial Realization (Fig. 11):** The dynamic re-planning module ensured that all variable-pitch transitions maintained C0 spatial continuity. The trajectories conformed to the irregular cranial surfaces without generating kinematic singularities or unphysical spatial jumps.
- **Postoperative Outcomes (Fig. 12):** Complete bone-flap removal was achieved in all six goat skull samples. Postoperative inspection revealed no visible carbonization at the cutting margins and no macroscopic dura mater damage. These findings suggest the framework's capability for safe, autonomous cutting on heterogeneous and previously unseen cranial samples, though histological verification was not performed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents RL-MACRO, a comprehensive cybernetic closed-loop framework that advances autonomous robotic craniotomy, particularly in addressing the challenges of partial observability and coordinated adaptive control.

**Significance:**
1. **Addressing Partial Observability:** The development of a CNN–LSTM multimodal observer is significant as it successfully reconstructs inaccessible interfacial temperatures from force and sound feedback in real-time. This transforms critical thermal states from post-process evaluations into active online feedback, enabling closed-loop thermomechanical regulation, which was a major limitation in previous approaches.
2. **Adaptive and Coordinated Decision-Making:** The implicit state classifier-guided dual-head IQL architecture offers a mechanism for achieving a safety–efficiency trade-off. By decoupling the operational frequencies—macroscopic adaptation of cutting depth based on cutting regimes and high-frequency compensation of feed rate and spindle speed—the system demonstrates fine-grained, coordinated control previously difficult to achieve with single-input, single-output methods or complex analytical models.
3. **Seamless Spatial Execution:** The dynamic trajectory re-planning and velocity servo module ensures that discrete policy decisions are translated into kinematically smooth, C0-continuous spatial motions. This is crucial for maintaining precision and stability on irregular anatomical surfaces, preventing kinematic singularities or abrupt movements that could compromise safety.
4. **Robust Generalizability:** Extensive ex vivo validations on multiple bovine ribs and six previously unseen goat skulls demonstrated the system's robust temperature observability, proactive recovery from thermomechanical excursions, and cross-specimen generalizability without manual re-parameterization. This provides preclinical evidence for the framework's potential practical applicability in diverse anatomical scenarios.

**Potential Impact:**
The RL-MACRO framework has the potential to enhance the safety and efficiency of robot-assisted neurosurgery. By autonomously balancing critical safety (thermal and mechanical) and efficiency objectives, it could lead to:
- **Reduced Complications:** Mitigation of thermal necrosis, bone fracture, and dura mater injury, which are significant risks in manual and less-adaptive robotic craniotomy.
- **Improved Patient Outcomes:** Shorter surgical durations, potentially lowering infection rates and enhancing post-operative recovery.
- **Foundation for Intelligent Surgical Systems:** The data-driven cybernetic paradigm established in this work provides a robust foundation for developing more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems in complex surgical environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations. The data-driven offline RL methodology's performance is inherently bounded by the quality and state coverage of its training dataset. The current policy converged to safety thresholds for force (approx. 27 N) and temperature rise (approx. 30 °C ∆T) that are considered marginally aggressive, particularly if continuous saline irrigation—which typically attenuates actual temperature rise—is suboptimal. This indicates that the agent may have converged to a sub-optimal extremum constrained by the available offline exploration boundaries. Additionally, despite IQL's resilience to extrapolation errors, theoretical risks of out-of-distribution (OOD) anomalies persist, as evidenced by slightly amplified control fluctuations during the zero-shot domain transfer from bovine ribs to goat skulls.

Future work will focus on:
1. **Dataset Expansion:** Constructing large-scale, high-diversity offline datasets that encompass actual cranial domain data and a wider range of extreme machining scenarios to improve policy robustness and coverage.
2. **Simulation Model Development:** Creating a high-fidelity, thermodynamically coupled dynamic simulation model for human bone cutting. This model would allow for rigorous pre-validation of policy robustness and further guarantee the absolute safety of the RL agent prior to clinical deployment.
3. **In Vivo Validation:** Advancing towards in vivo and histological validations to definitively confirm the thermal safety and clinical efficacy of the framework.
