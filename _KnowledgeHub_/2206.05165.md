# Multifidelity Reinforcement Learning with Control Variates

> [!summary] Summary
> Argonne National Laboratory researchers developed a multifidelity reinforcement learning framework, MFMCRL, that uses control variates to reduce variance in state-action value function estimation. This approach leverages correlated low-fidelity data to achieve superior policy performance with fewer expensive high-fidelity interactions, demonstrated through theoretical bounds and empirical validation in synthetic environments and neural architecture search.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically suffers from high sample complexity, demanding a large number of interactions with the environment to learn effective policies.
- Acquiring sufficient high-fidelity data is often prohibitively expensive or time-consuming in many computational science and engineering (CSE) applications.
- Existing multifidelity RL methods primarily focus on transfer learning for exploration, often requiring prior knowledge about the relationship between low- and high-fidelity optimal policies.

## Method

- Proposes a novel ==multifidelity estimator== for the state-action value function by applying the statistical method of ==control variates==.
- Integrates this estimator into a **MFMCRL** (==Multifidelity Monte Carlo Reinforcement Learning==) algorithm, which utilizes both high-fidelity trajectories and mapped low-fidelity trajectories to form a control variate.
- Estimates the ==optimal control variate coefficient== from observed high- and low-fidelity returns to maximize variance reduction in the estimation process.

## Results

- Theoretically, the multifidelity estimator reduces the required number of high-fidelity samples by a factor of **(1 \u2212 \u03c1\u00b2_s,a)** for a given estimation accuracy, where \u03c1_s,a is the correlation coefficient between fidelity returns.
- Empirically, **MFMCRL** consistently outperformed standard Monte Carlo RL (MCRL) in synthetic MDPs, with performance gains increasing alongside the low-fidelity sample budget and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR).
- MFMCRL successfully improved learning in multifidelity Neural Architecture Search (NAS) environments, showing more pronounced gains when the low-fidelity environment closely resembled the high-fidelity one.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exploiting cross-correlations between low- and high-fidelity returns can significantly reduce the variance in state-action value function estimation, leading to more data-efficient RL.
> - Variance reduction in policy evaluation directly translates to improved policy improvement, increasing the probability of selecting truly optimal actions.
> - The effectiveness of the control variate approach is directly dependent on a meaningful correlation between the low- and high-fidelity environments; weak correlations diminish the benefits.
