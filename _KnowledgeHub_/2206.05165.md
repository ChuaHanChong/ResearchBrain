# Multifidelity Reinforcement Learning with Control Variates

> [!summary] Summary
> Argonne National Laboratory researchers developed a multifidelity reinforcement learning framework, MFMCRL, that uses control variates to reduce variance in state-action value function estimation. This approach leverages correlated low-fidelity data to achieve superior policy performance with fewer expensive high-fidelity interactions, demonstrated through theoretical bounds and empirical validation in synthetic environments and neural architecture search.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically suffers from high sample complexity, demanding a large number of interactions with the environment to learn effective policies.
- Acquiring sufficient high-fidelity data is often prohibitively expensive or time-consuming in many computational science and engineering (CSE) applications.
- Existing multifidelity RL methods primarily focus on transfer learning for exploration, often requiring prior knowledge about the relationship between low- and high-fidelity optimal policies.

## Method

- Proposes a novel ==multifidelity estimator== for the state-action value function by applying the statistical method of ==control variates==.
- Integrates this estimator into a **MFMCRL** (==Multifidelity Monte Carlo Reinforcement Learning==) algorithm, which utilizes both high-fidelity trajectories and mapped low-fidelity trajectories to form a control variate.
- Estimates the ==optimal control variate coefficient== from observed high- and low-fidelity returns to maximize variance reduction in the estimation process.

## Results

- Theoretically, the multifidelity estimator reduces the required number of high-fidelity samples by a factor of **(1 − ρ²_s,a)** for a given estimation accuracy, where ρ_s,a is the correlation coefficient between fidelity returns.
- Empirically, **MFMCRL** consistently outperformed standard Monte Carlo RL (MCRL) in synthetic MDPs, with performance gains increasing alongside the low-fidelity sample budget and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR).
- MFMCRL successfully improved learning in multifidelity Neural Architecture Search (NAS) environments, showing more pronounced gains when the low-fidelity environment closely resembled the high-fidelity one.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exploiting cross-correlations between low- and high-fidelity returns can significantly reduce the variance in state-action value function estimation, leading to more data-efficient RL.
> - Variance reduction in policy evaluation directly translates to improved policy improvement, increasing the probability of selecting truly optimal actions.
> - The effectiveness of the control variate approach is directly dependent on a meaningful correlation between the low- and high-fidelity environments; weak correlations diminish the benefits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of data efficiency in Reinforcement Learning (RL) within the context of computational science and engineering (CSE). In many CSE applications, data can be acquired at various levels of fidelity, with low-fidelity data being inexpensive and abundant, while high-fidelity data is costly and scarce. Examples include simulated versus real-world robotic data, or model evaluation on subsets versus full datasets. This multifidelity data paradigm is utilized in various "outer-loop" applications such as black-box optimization, inference, and uncertainty propagation to mitigate the prohibitive costs associated with using high-fidelity data exclusively.

Reinforcement Learning, while successful in complex decision-making tasks, typically suffers from high sample complexity, requiring a substantial number of interactions with the environment to learn effective policies. Previous efforts to leverage multifidelity environments in RL have primarily focused on transfer learning (TL). In TL, a policy learned in a low-fidelity environment is transferred to the high-fidelity environment to bootstrap learning, often making assumptions about the bounded deviation between optimal low-fidelity and high-fidelity value functions. These approaches necessitate a priori knowledge of such bounds to manage exploration-exploitation trade-offs.

This work introduces an alternative and complementary approach to multifidelity RL. Instead of focusing on transfer learning for exploration, it investigates multifidelity estimation to reduce the variance in the estimation of the state-action value function. The premise is that cross-correlations between low- and high-fidelity returns can be exploited to improve the learning agent's performance without explicitly altering or managing the exploration process. This distinguishes the proposed method from existing TL techniques, offering a novel direction for enhancing RL efficiency when multifidelity data sources are available.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two factors: the prevalence of multifidelity data sources in computational science and engineering, and the high sample complexity associated with state-of-the-art model-free reinforcement learning algorithms. Generating sufficient high-fidelity data for RL training can be prohibitively expensive or time-consuming in many real-world applications.

Against this background, the paper outlines several key objectives:

- **Problem Formulation:** To study the reinforcement learning problem in scenarios where an agent has access to multiple environments, each offering data at different levels of fidelity, for a given control task.
- **Performance Improvement:** The overarching goal is to enhance the performance of the RL agent within the high-fidelity environment by effectively utilizing multifidelity data. This involves making the learning process more efficient given a finite budget of expensive high-fidelity interactions.
- **Multifidelity Estimator Development:** To propose a novel multifidelity estimator for the state-action value function. This estimator is designed to leverage the cross-correlations between low-fidelity (cheap, abundant) and high-fidelity (expensive, scarce) returns to reduce variance in the estimation process. The method of control variates is adopted as the foundational framework for this estimator.
- **Algorithmic Design:** To integrate the proposed multifidelity estimator into a reinforcement learning algorithm. Specifically, a multifidelity Monte Carlo RL (MFMCRL) algorithm is developed with the aim of enabling the agent to learn superior policies in the high-fidelity environment, even with a limited number of high-fidelity data samples, by exploiting low-fidelity data.
- **Theoretical Analysis:** To theoretically investigate the impact of variance reduction, achieved through the multifidelity estimator, on both policy evaluation and policy improvement stages of RL. This involves deriving probability bounds to formally quantify the benefits.
- **Empirical Validation:** To empirically evaluate the performance gains of the proposed MFMCRL algorithm. This validation is conducted through numerical experiments on synthetic multifidelity environments and a practical application in neural architecture search (NAS).

In essence, the research aims to demonstrate that by focusing on variance reduction in value function estimation, rather than purely on knowledge transfer for exploration, multifidelity data can significantly accelerate and improve the learning of optimal policies in high-fidelity environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on developing a multifidelity estimator for the state-action value function in RL, building upon the statistical method of control variates.

**Problem Setup (Section 3.1):**
The agent operates in a multifidelity setup with two environments: a low-fidelity environment ($\Sigma_{lo}$) and a high-fidelity environment ($\Sigma_{hi}$). These are modeled as Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), $M_{lo}$ and $M_{hi}$, respectively. Key characteristics include:
- $\Sigma_{lo}$ is inexpensive to query and generative (allowing simulations from any state-action pair), but potentially inaccurate.
- $\Sigma_{hi}$ accurately represents the target system but is expensive to evaluate, with interactions typically restricted to full episodes.
- The environment dynamics and reward functions ($P, R$) for both fidelities are unknown to the agent.
- The action space $A$ is shared between environments. The state space $S_{lo}$ is assumed to be a subset of $S_{hi}$, with a known mapping $T: S_{hi} \rightarrow S_{lo}$ available.
- A crucial assumption is the existence of correlation between the low-fidelity reward $R_{lo}(T(s_{hi}), a)$ and the high-fidelity reward $R_{hi}(s_{hi}, a)$.
The objective is to learn a policy $\pi^*$ that is optimal with respect to the high-fidelity environment $\Sigma_{hi}$.

**Multifidelity Monte Carlo RL (Section 3.2):**
The core of the methodology lies in modifying the Monte Carlo (MC) method for value function estimation.
1. **Standard MC Estimator:** For a given state-action pair $(s_{hi}, a)$, the standard first-visit MC estimator for the high-fidelity state-action value $Q^{\pi}_{hi}(s_{hi}, a)$ is the sample mean of $n$ high-fidelity discounted returns ($G_{hi,i}$), denoted $\hat{Q}^{\pi,n}_{hi}(s_{hi}, a)$. This estimator is unbiased, with variance $\sigma^2_{hi}(s_{hi}, a) / n$.
2. **Control Variates Principle:** The paper reviews the method of control variates, a variance reduction technique that exploits the covariance between a target random variable ($W$) and a control variate ($Z$) whose mean ($\mu_Z$) is known. An estimator $\hat{\theta}_2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum (W_i + \alpha(Z_i - \mu_Z))$ is unbiased for $\mu_W$ and achieves minimal variance $(1 - \rho^2_{Z,W}) \text{Var}[\hat{\theta}_1]$ when $\alpha = -\text{Cov}[Z,W]/\sigma^2_Z$.
3. **Proposed Multifidelity MC Estimator ($\hat{Q}^{\text{MFMC}}_{\pi,n}$):** This estimator applies the control variate principle to RL.
 - The high-fidelity return $G_{hi,i}$ serves as $W_i$.
 - The low-fidelity return $G_{lo,i}$ (obtained from the same trajectory mapped through $T$) serves as $Z_i$.
 - The proposed estimator is:
 $\hat{Q}^{\text{MFMC}}_{\pi,n}(s_{hi}, a) = \hat{Q}^{\pi,n}_{hi}(s_{hi}, a) + \alpha^*_{s,a} (Q^{\pi}_{lo}(T(s_{hi}), a) - \hat{Q}^{\pi,n}_{lo}(T(s_{hi}), a))$
 where $\hat{Q}^{\pi,n}_{lo}(T(s_{hi}), a)$ is the sample mean of low-fidelity returns and $Q^{\pi}_{lo}(T(s_{hi}), a)$ is the *true* population mean of low-fidelity returns.
 - The optimal coefficient $\alpha^*_{s,a}$ is determined by the ratio of covariance between high- and low-fidelity returns to the variance of low-fidelity returns.
 - The variance of $\hat{Q}^{\text{MFMC}}_{\pi,n}(s_{hi}, a)$ is reduced by a factor of $(1 - \rho^2_{s,a})$ compared to $\hat{Q}^{\pi,n}_{hi}(s_{hi}, a)$, where $\rho_{s,a}$ is the correlation coefficient between the low- and high-fidelity long-term discounted returns. In practice, the covariance, variances, and $\alpha^*_{s,a}$ are estimated from data samples.

**Multifidelity Monte Carlo RL Algorithm (MFMCRL) - Algorithm 1 (Appendix A):**
The proposed algorithm integrates this multifidelity estimator into an on-policy first-visit MC control framework with $\epsilon$-soft policies.
- **Trajectory Generation:** In each episode, a high-fidelity trajectory $\tau_{hi}$ is generated following the current policy $\pi$. A corresponding low-fidelity trajectory $\tau_{lo}$ is derived by applying the mapping $T$ to states and using $R_{lo}$ for rewards.
- **Low-Fidelity Mean Estimation:** For each $(s_{lo}, a)$ pair encountered in $\tau_{lo}$, the agent collects $m$ *additional* trajectories by executing $\pi_a$ in $\Sigma_{lo}$ to estimate $Q^{\pi}_{lo}(s_{lo}, a)$. This step ensures a robust estimate of the control variate's mean, which is assumed known in the control variates formulation.
- **Value Function Update:** For each first-visited $(s_{hi}, a)$ pair in $\tau_{hi}$:
 - The corresponding high-fidelity ($G_{hi}$) and low-fidelity ($G_{lo}$) returns are appended to their respective lists.
 - Sample means, variances, and covariance of $G_{hi}$ and $G_{lo}$ are estimated from these lists.
 - The multifidelity estimator $\hat{Q}^{\text{MFMC}}$ is updated using these estimates and the estimate of $Q^{\pi}_{lo}$.
- **Policy Improvement:** The policy $\pi$ is updated to be $\epsilon$-greedy with respect to the newly estimated $\hat{Q}^{\text{MFMC}}$.
The algorithm's convergence properties are stated to align with those of standard on-policy first-visit MC control.

**Theoretical Analysis (Section 3.3):**
The theoretical analysis supports the empirical benefits.
- **Policy Evaluation (Theorem 1):** Using a Bernstein-type concentration inequality, the paper demonstrates that to achieve a specified estimation error $\xi$ with a given probability $1-\delta$, the multifidelity estimator requires $n \ge \frac{4(1-\rho^2_{s,a})\sigma^2_{hi}(s_{hi},a)}{\xi^2} \log(\frac{2}{\delta})$ high-fidelity samples. This is a reduction by a factor of $(1-\rho^2_{s,a})$ compared to the standard high-fidelity estimator, directly showing the efficiency gain.
- **Policy Improvement (Theorem 2):** This theorem analyzes the probability of selecting the truly greedy action using the estimated value functions. It provides lower bounds for this probability for both single and multifidelity estimators. The lower bound for the multifidelity estimator is higher, indicating that it is more likely to correctly identify the optimal action, especially as $|\rho_{s,a}|^2$ approaches 1. This suggests that the improved accuracy in value estimation translates to better policy improvement.

**Numerical Experiments (Section 4):**
The methodology includes empirical validation on:
- **Synthetic MDPs:** Random MDPs are generated, with low-fidelity environments created by adding Gaussian noise to high-fidelity dynamics and rewards, controlled by a Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). Experiments assess the impact of low-fidelity sample budget and SNR on MFMCRL's performance against MCRL.
- **Neural Architecture Search (NAS):** NAS-Bench-201 dataset is used to construct multifidelity RL environments. High-fidelity reward is validation accuracy at 200 training epochs. Low-fidelity rewards are validation accuracy at 10 epochs, with two scenarios: (i) identical state space, and (ii) a smaller state space (subset of high-fidelity).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded both theoretical and empirical findings that support the efficacy of Multifidelity Reinforcement Learning with Control Variates (MFMCRL).

**Theoretical Findings:**

- **Variance Reduction in Policy Evaluation:** Theorem 1 formally quantifies the benefit of the proposed multifidelity estimator for policy evaluation. It demonstrates that to achieve a desired level of accuracy ($\xi$) with a high probability ($1-\delta$), the required number of high-fidelity samples ($n$) is reduced by a factor of $(1 - \rho^2_{s,a})$. Here, $\rho_{s,a}$ represents the correlation coefficient between low- and high-fidelity returns for a given state-action pair. This theoretical result confirms that leveraging correlated low-fidelity data allows for more accurate estimation of the state-action value function with fewer expensive high-fidelity interactions.
- **Improved Policy Improvement:** Theorem 2 addresses the impact of variance reduction on policy improvement. It provides lower bounds on the probability that the agent selects the truly optimal (greedy) action based on the estimated value functions. The lower bound is higher for the MFMCRL agent compared to a standard RL agent. Specifically, as the correlation coefficient $|\rho_{s,a}|$ approaches 1, the probability of selecting the correct greedy action increases significantly, potentially approaching certainty. This indicates that more precise value function estimates lead to more reliable policy updates and convergence towards optimal policies.

**Empirical Findings:**

- **Superior Performance in Synthetic MDPs:**
 - The MFMCRL algorithm consistently demonstrated superior policy performance (measured by test episode rewards) compared to the standard Monte Carlo Reinforcement Learning (MCRL) agent, given the same budget of high-fidelity episodes.
 - The performance gains of MFMCRL were observed to increase with an increasing number of low-fidelity sample trajectories ($\#\tau_{lo}$) used for estimating the control variate. This indicates that more extensive use of cheap low-fidelity data directly translates to better learning outcomes for the high-fidelity task.
 - The performance of MFMCRL also improved significantly with a higher Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) in the low-fidelity environment. This implies that greater similarity and correlation between the low- and high-fidelity environments enhance the effectiveness of the control variates method.
 - Conversely, when the SNR was very low (e.g., -10 dB), indicating weak correlation, the benefits of MFMCRL diminished. In such cases, the variance reduction factor (Var[$\hat{Q}^{MFMC}$]/Var[$\hat{Q}^{hi}$]) was close to 1, confirming that multifidelity estimation offers little advantage if the fidelities are not sufficiently correlated.
- **Effectiveness in Neural Architecture Search (NAS):**
 - MFMCRL successfully improved learning over standard RL in the context of multifidelity NAS environments.
 - Performance gains were more pronounced when the low-fidelity environment was more similar to the high-fidelity environment (Case (i), where only the reward function fidelity differed).
 - Even in Case (ii), where both the state space and reward function fidelity differed (smaller search space for low-fidelity), MFMCRL still outperformed standard MCRL, although the gains were less substantial than in Case (i). These results suggest the approach is applicable to complex real-world problems and that the degree of similarity between fidelity levels influences the magnitude of improvement.

Overall, the main findings demonstrate that the control-variate-based multifidelity estimator effectively reduces the variance in state-action value function estimation. This reduction translates into improved data efficiency, requiring fewer expensive high-fidelity samples for accurate policy evaluation, and leading to more effective policy improvement, ultimately yielding superior policies under finite interaction budgets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Reinforcement Learning by addressing the critical issue of sample efficiency, particularly in applications where acquiring high-fidelity data is costly. The introduction of a multifidelity estimator based on control variates offers a novel and effective mechanism for leveraging readily available low-fidelity data to enhance learning in high-fidelity environments.

The **significance** of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Novelty in Multifidelity RL:** Unlike prior multifidelity RL approaches that often rely on transfer learning for exploration, this work focuses on variance reduction in value function estimation. This provides a complementary and distinct strategy for utilizing multifidelity data, expanding the toolkit for designing more efficient RL systems.
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** The core impact is the ability to achieve superior RL agent performance with a significantly reduced budget of expensive high-fidelity data samples. This is crucial for real-world computational science and engineering applications where high-fidelity interactions (e.g., physical experiments, detailed simulations, full model training) are time-consuming and resource-intensive.
- **Theoretical Foundation:** The paper provides rigorous theoretical analysis, including Bernstein-type concentration inequalities and probability bounds for policy evaluation and improvement. These theorems formally establish the benefits of variance reduction, providing a strong mathematical basis for the observed empirical gains.
- **Broad Applicability:** The method's effectiveness is demonstrated across diverse settings, from synthetic Markov Decision Processes to a practical application like Neural Architecture Search. This suggests its potential relevance to a wide array of problems in robotics, control systems, scientific discovery, and machine learning model optimization.

The **potential impact** of this research is substantial:

- **Resource and Cost Reduction:** By requiring fewer high-fidelity interactions, the proposed MFMCRL can lead to significant savings in computational resources, energy consumption, and real-world experimental costs. This aligns with broader efforts towards "greener" AI and more sustainable scientific computing.
- **Accelerated Discovery and Development:** In domains like materials science, drug discovery, or complex engineering design, where simulations or experiments are expensive, this method could accelerate the discovery of optimal control strategies or system designs.
- **Enhanced Real-World RL Deployment:** For applications involving physical robots or critical infrastructure, reducing the reliance on costly and potentially risky real-world interactions while maintaining performance can facilitate safer and faster deployment of RL agents.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for future research, particularly in integrating control-variate-based multifidelity RL with function approximation techniques (e.g., deep neural networks) to tackle continuous state-action space problems, further broadening its applicability.

A **broader impact** consideration mentioned by the authors highlights a caveat: the benefits are most pronounced when there is a meaningful correlation between the low- and high-fidelity environments. Running multifidelity RL training with weakly correlated environments could be wasteful of resources, suggesting that judicious selection or design of fidelity levels is important for optimal utility.
