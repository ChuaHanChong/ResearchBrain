# AgentWorld: An Interactive Simulation Platform for Scene Construction and Mobile Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentWorld, an interactive simulation platform for embodied AI, integrates procedural generation of diverse, physically realistic household scenes with a flexible mobile robotic teleoperation system. The platform was used to create the AgentWorld Dataset, demonstrating improved imitation learning performance on mobile manipulation tasks and exhibiting sim-to-real transfer capabilities for robotic control policies.

## Problem

- Existing simulation environments often lack sufficient visual realism, physical plausibility, and diversity, relying on static assets with limited material customization, hindering policy generalizability.
- Collecting high-quality, diverse robotic interaction data in simulation for training long-horizon, multistage tasks involving both mobile navigation and complex manipulation remains challenging.
- Current simulation platforms often provide only partial components, lacking a unified framework that integrates high-fidelity scene construction with flexible mobile robotic data collection.

## Method

- **AgentWorld** uses a ==four-stage procedural scene generation== pipeline, built on NVIDIA Omniverse Isaac Sim and Unreal Engine, to create diverse, visually realistic, and physically plausible household scenes.
- A flexible ==mobile-based teleoperation== system allows seamless 3-DOF control of various mobile bases (wheeled and legged) and robotic arms (grippers and dexterous hands) for efficient data collection of complex manipulation trajectories.
- The platform was used to construct the **AgentWorld Dataset**, comprising over 1,000 diverse trajectories across 150 unique scenes and multiple robot embodiments, for benchmarking and training embodied AI algorithms.

## Results

- Benchmarking on the AgentWorld Dataset showed Action Chunking Transformers (ACT) achieved **66-84%** success rates on basic tasks, while the vision-language-action model π0 achieved superior performance (up to **30%** success) on complex multistage tasks.
- A real-world experiment demonstrated sim-to-real transfer, with a π0 policy trained using a combined sim-real approach achieving a **29.3%** success rate on an object placement task.
- The platform's capability to generate a large-scale dataset (over **1,000 trajectories** across **150 unique household scenes**) validates its utility for scalable robotic skill acquisition and benchmarking imitation learning algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of procedural scene generation with diverse PBR materials and accurate physics significantly enhances scene realism and diversity, improving the generalizability of learned robotic policies.
> - A unified teleoperation system for both mobile bases and manipulators is crucial for efficiently collecting comprehensive, long-horizon, and multistage interaction data for training embodied AI agents.
> - Policies trained in AgentWorld show potential for sim-to-real transfer; while pure simulation data has limitations, targeted real-world fine-tuning can effectively bridge the reality gap for practical deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation increasingly relies on scalable and interactive simulation environments to train autonomous agents. Existing simulation platforms offer various functionalities, such as advanced scene generation or specialized datasets for manipulation tasks. However, many current solutions exhibit limitations, often providing only partial components of a comprehensive training system. Some platforms, like those based on Unity, may lack programmatic scene construction or accurate physics simulation. Others, such as ProcTHOR or Behavior-1K, excel in generating large-scale interactive environments or simulating human-like activities but may not fully integrate high-fidelity scene construction with flexible mobile robotic data collection. Platforms like ManiSkill2 and RLBench focus on manipulation tasks with physically realistic interactions or provide benchmarks for predefined tasks, but may not offer the same breadth in scene construction or diverse mobile robot support.

AgentWorld positions itself by addressing the identified gap: the need for a unified framework that integrates high-fidelity procedural scene construction with a flexible mobile robotic data collection system. It aims to provide a holistic solution for embodied AI research by combining diverse scene generation, including semantic object placement and realistic material configurations, with advanced teleoperation capabilities for both wheeled and legged mobile manipulators. This integration is designed to facilitate the acquisition of scalable robotic skills in complex home environments and to bridge the simulation-to-real-world deployment gap, differentiating it from platforms that focus on specific aspects like scene generation alone or task-specific manipulation datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce AgentWorld, an interactive simulation platform designed to facilitate the development of household mobile manipulation capabilities in autonomous agents. The motivation stems from two critical challenges identified in current embodied AI research:

1. **Stable and Diverse Scene Generation:** A key challenge is the need for simulated environments that are not only visually realistic but also physically plausible. Current systems often rely on static assets with limited material customization, which can hinder the generalizability of trained policies. The objective is to overcome this by enabling programmatic construction of diverse household scenes with abundant 3D assets, realistic visual materials, and accurate physical properties. This includes supporting various room layouts, semantic object placement, and fine-grained visual material configuration to enhance simulation fidelity and enable robust training.

2. **Comprehensive Data Collection System in Simulation:** Collecting high-quality, diverse robotic interaction data in simulation for training purposes remains a significant hurdle. Existing methods, such as scripted demonstrations or teleoperation, often lack the integration needed for long-horizon, multistage tasks that involve both mobile navigation and complex manipulation. The objective is to provide a flexible and comprehensive data collection system that allows seamless control of various mobile bases (wheeled and legged) and robotic arms (with grippers or dexterous hands). This system aims to enable the efficient collection of diverse manipulation trajectories, ranging from primitive actions to multistage activities, to support imitation learning and accelerate robotic skill acquisition for real-world deployment.

By addressing these challenges, AgentWorld seeks to provide an integrated solution that supports scalable robotic skill acquisition, thereby bridging the gap between simulation-based training and real-world application in complex domestic environments. The platform is specifically designed to support the generation of large-scale datasets suitable for benchmarking and training advanced imitation learning methods, ultimately aiming for successful sim-to-real transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AgentWorld simulation platform integrates procedural scene generation with a mobile-based teleoperation system for data collection. The platform is built upon NVIDIA’s Omniverse Isaac Sim, utilizing its physics engine and parallel training capabilities, and Unreal Engine, for its rendering capabilities and blueprint system.

#### 3.1. Procedural Scene Generation

The scene generation module follows a four-stage pipeline implemented using Unreal Engine for rendering and Isaac Sim for physics:

1. **Layout Generation:** This stage programmatically creates room layouts based on user-specified room types, currently supporting Living Room, Kitchen, and Bedroom. It automatically constructs architectural elements like walls, ceilings, and floors, accommodating both single-room and multi-room configurations, including multi-floor environments with staircases. The system extends ProcTHOR's rule-based algorithms with Unreal Engine’s Procedural Content Generation (PCG) framework for real-time scene generation and improved geometric realism (e.g., wall thickness, optimized room layouts, inter-room connectivity checks, multi-floor structures).

2. **Semantic Asset Selection and Placement:** The platform integrates an extensive collection of open-source 3D assets, sourced from repositories like ProcTHOR and Behavior-1K. Each asset is manually annotated with semantic metadata regarding its functional properties. Assets are categorized into "Basic Assets" (e.g., furniture, automatically placed) and "Interactable Assets" (e.g., appliances, tools, fruits, manipulable by robots). Interactable assets are further distinguished as articulated (e.g., microwaves, refrigerators) or rigid. Intelligent placement algorithms position assets according to semantic functions (e.g., food on dining surfaces), with interactive controls for fine-grained adjustments.

3. **Visual Material Configuration:** To enhance scene generalization and facilitate sim-to-real transfer, the platform incorporates a diverse library of high-fidelity Physically Based Rendering (PBR) materials. Material customization is supported for architectural components (e.g., marble, brick for walls/floors) and 3D assets (e.g., wood grains, ceramic finishes, metallic coatings), ensuring realistic visual appearances while maintaining physical accuracy.

4. **Interactive Physics Simulation:** This module utilizes NVIDIA Isaac Sim with its GPU-accelerated PhysX 5.0 engine to implement the robotic interaction layer. Physical properties (friction, restitution, mass) are automatically configured for all 3D assets based on semantic annotations, generating optimized collision primitives. For articulated mechanisms (e.g., doors, drawers), joint parameters (types, ranges, material-specific friction, actuation models) are precisely configured. The environment also features Isaac Sim’s advanced lighting system for photometrically accurate illumination with programmable color temperature and exposure.

#### 3.2. Mobile-based Teleoperation for Data Collection

The teleoperation system is designed to collect data for long-horizon, multistage tasks, integrating mobile navigation and manipulation control:

1. **Mobile-base Control:** This system enables 3-DOF control of mobile robots (velocity in x, y, and yaw angle). For wheeled or direct floating-base robots, keyboard control is directly applied. For legged humanoids, an IsaacLab’s reward-driven locomotion policy is employed, adapted for dual-arm stability, allowing keyboard-based movement. The robot can switch between locomotion and manipulation modes by fixing or freeing its base link. During training of the locomotion policy, arm positions are randomized to enhance robustness.

2. **Arm & Hand Control:** The OpenTeleVision system is used to control robotic arms. A VR headset captures hand keypoints, and its pose relative to the robot's head camera (or a fixed reasonable location) is calibrated. Arm joint poses are computed using a Closed-loop Inverse Kinematics (CLIK) algorithm based on Pinocchio. For end effectors, gripper actions are normalized based on thumb-to-index fingertip distance, while dexterous hands use dex-retargeting to convert hand keypoints into joint actions.

#### 3.3. AgentWorld Dataset

The platform was used to construct the AgentWorld Dataset, covering diverse household activities:

- **Scene & Task Description:** 150 unique household scenes were generated (10 distinct layouts for each of three room types – Living room, Bedroom, Kitchen, each randomly populated with interactable assets). Tasks are divided into "Basic Tasks" (e.g., Pick & Place, Open & Close, Push & Pull) and "Multistage Tasks" (e.g., Organizing books, Serving drinks, Heating up food, Cleaning dishes). Multistage tasks combine primitive skills to achieve higher-level goals.

- **Dataset Details:** Data was collected using four different embodiments: Unitree G1, H1, Franka Emika Panda with a wheel base, and a DOBOT X-Trainer with a fixed platform. End effectors included the 2F-85 Gripper and TRX-Hand5. For basic tasks, 10 assets per category were used, with 10 sequences per asset (80-150 steps). For multistage tasks, 30-50 sequences per task were recorded (300-800 steps). The control frequency was 30 fps. Each frame recorded robot joint poses, actions, and RGB-D outputs from wrist, head, and third-person cameras. The dataset currently contains over 1,000 trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Imitation Learning for AgentWorld Dataset

The AgentWorld dataset was benchmarked using several imitation learning algorithms to evaluate its effectiveness:
- **Algorithms Evaluated:** Behavior Cloning (BC), Action Chunking Transformers (ACT), Diffusion Policies (DP), and π0 (a vision-language-action model).
- **Inputs and Outputs:** All models processed synchronized multi-view RGB images. π0 additionally used language task descriptions. The unified output space included dual-arm joint/end effector actions, 3-DOF velocity commands for mobile base navigation, and a binary locomotion-manipulation mode selector.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** For Basic Tasks, per-category models were trained and evaluated on unseen assets/surroundings. For Multistage Tasks, models were trained on single tasks and evaluated with varied asset poses and materials. Success rates were calculated over 50 episodes per task for each model.
- **Quantitative Results:**
 - **Basic Tasks:** ACT achieved the most consistent performance, with success rates ranging from 66% for Pick & Place, 84% for Open & Close, and 72% for Push & Pull. Diffusion Policy also showed competitive results, particularly in Push & Pull (76%). BC served as a baseline with lower success rates (52-62%).
 - **Multistage Tasks:** π0 demonstrated superior performance, achieving 30% success in Living Room tasks, 20% in Kitchen tasks, and 18% in Bedroom tasks. Diffusion Policies also performed comparatively well (28% Living Room, 20% Kitchen, 24% Bedroom). ACT and BC showed lower success rates in these complex scenarios (4-16%).
- **Interpretation:** ACT's effectiveness on basic tasks is attributed to its action chunking mechanism for short-horizon sequences. π0's superior performance in multistage tasks is linked to its pretrained representations that capture long-horizon task structures, leveraging language instructions. However, all methods exhibited challenges with complex mobile manipulation tasks, indicating that both precise control and long-term planning simultaneously remain difficult.

#### 4.2. Real-world Experiment (Sim-to-Real Transfer)

A real-world experiment was conducted to validate the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of the platform.
- **Task:** The specific task involved placing various objects into a bowl.
- **Training Paradigm:** The π0 policy was trained using a sim-real-combined approach. This involved initial training on simulation data (10 assets, 10 trajectories each) followed by fine-tuning with limited real-world demonstrations (3 objects – banana, apple, mug – with 3 trajectories each, varying object positions).
- **Result:** This combined approach achieved a 29.3% success rate in the real-world task.
- **Interpretation:** The results suggest that AgentWorld's realistic material rendering and physical simulation properties provide a foundation for sim-to-real transfer. However, they also highlight the importance of targeted real-world fine-tuning to effectively bridge the reality gap for practical deployment. The limitation section further indicates that pure simulation data still struggles for real-world deployment, and multistage tasks require substantially more real-world demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AgentWorld represents a significant contribution to embodied AI research by providing a unified simulation platform that addresses key challenges in robotic learning. Its integration of procedural scene construction with a comprehensive mobile-based teleoperation system offers a scalable solution for training autonomous agents in complex household environments.

The platform's capabilities for generating diverse, visually realistic, and physically plausible scenes, coupled with its extensive semantic 3D asset repository and PBR material configuration, enhance simulation fidelity and are expected to improve the generalizability of learned policies. The flexible mobile teleoperation system, supporting both wheeled and legged robots with various end effectors, enables the efficient collection of large-scale, diverse manipulation trajectories, crucial for developing robust robotic skills, including long-horizon and multistage tasks.

The AgentWorld Dataset, generated using this platform, serves as a benchmark for evaluating imitation learning algorithms and demonstrates the utility of simulation-based training for real-world robotic applications. The successful, albeit limited, sim-to-real transfer experiments indicate the platform's potential in bridging the simulation-to-reality gap, suggesting that policies trained in AgentWorld can be effectively adapted for real-world deployment with targeted fine-tuning.

This work's impact extends to facilitating research in generalist robots, mobile manipulation, and human-robot interaction in domestic settings. By providing a comprehensive and interactive environment, AgentWorld has the potential to accelerate the development and evaluation of new robotic control policies and learning algorithms, ultimately contributing to the advancement of autonomous household robots. Future developments, such as expanded support for deformable objects and enhanced domain adaptation techniques, could further increase its utility.
