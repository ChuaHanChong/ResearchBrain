# Learning Robust Autonomous Navigation and Locomotion for Wheeled-Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical reinforcement learning framework enables a wheeled-legged robot to autonomously navigate diverse urban environments by integrating high-level navigation with low-level locomotion control, achieving an average speed of 1.68 m/s and a 6.5x speed improvement over purely legged systems.

## Problem

- Autonomous navigation for mobile robots in complex urban settings faces challenges with diverse terrains, dynamic obstacles, and energy efficiency.
- Traditional robot navigation systems often separate planning and locomotion, leading to suboptimal performance and instability.
- Wheeled-legged robots need a cohesive control strategy to fully leverage the benefits of both locomotion types across varying conditions.

## Method

- A hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) framework is developed, comprising a High-Level Controller (HLC) for navigation and a Low-Level Controller (LLC) for locomotion.
- The LLC is trained using privileged learning, providing access to additional environmental information during training to enhance robustness.
- The HLC generates velocity commands that the LLC reliably executes, enabling seamless coordination between navigation and locomotion.

## Results

- The robot successfully completed kilometer-scale autonomous navigation missions in diverse urban environments, including smooth pavements, stairs, and dynamic settings.
- Achieved an average speed of 1.68 m/s and a Mechanical Cost of Transport (COT) of 0.16, demonstrating energy efficiency and performance.
- The system exhibited real-time planning with a mean time of 0.34 ms and showed lower failure rates and zero collision rates compared to traditional methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex robot behaviors into hierarchical sub-tasks (navigation and locomotion) allows for more manageable and robust control.
> - Privileged learning effectively enhances the robustness and generalization capabilities of low-level locomotion controllers in varied environments without requiring additional sensory input during deployment.
> - A hierarchical approach with velocity commanding for the HLC offers the best balance of performance, adaptability, and training efficiency for wheeled-legged robot navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Autonomous navigation in complex urban environments presents significant challenges for mobile robots, particularly when dealing with diverse terrains, dynamic obstacles, and the need for energy-efficient locomotion. This research by Lee et al. introduces a comprehensive approach to autonomous navigation for wheeled-legged robots that combines the benefits of both wheeled and legged locomotion through advanced machine learning techniques.

Wheeled-legged robots represent a hybrid approach that leverages wheels for efficient locomotion on smooth surfaces while utilizing legs for agility on challenging terrain. Traditional approaches to robot navigation often separate navigation planning from locomotion control, leading to suboptimal performance and potential instabilities. This work addresses these limitations by developing an integrated system that seamlessly coordinates high-level navigation decisions with low-level locomotion control.

### 2. Technical Approach

The research employs a hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) framework that decouples navigation and locomotion while maintaining tight integration between these components. The system consists of two primary components:

1. **Low-Level Controller (LLC)**: A locomotion controller trained using model-free RL that manages the robot's movement and gait selection
2. **High-Level Controller (HLC)**: A navigation controller that generates velocity commands for the LLC based on environmental perception

The key innovation lies in the seamless integration of these controllers, where the HLC generates velocity targets that the LLC can reliably execute across various terrains and locomotion modes.

#### 2.1. Privileged Learning for Robust Locomotion

The LLC is trained using privileged learning, a technique where additional information is available during training but not during deployment. This approach allows the controller to learn more robust policies by accessing:

- Noiseless sensor data during training
- Terrain properties and friction coefficients
- Ground truth information about obstacles and terrain geometry

During deployment, the trained policy relies only on standard sensor inputs (IMU, joint encoders, etc.), but the privileged information during training enables more robust generalization to unseen conditions.

### 3. Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning Framework

The hierarchical structure addresses the challenge of controlling complex robot behaviors by decomposing the problem into manageable sub-tasks. The mathematical formulation can be expressed as:

```
HLC: π_h(a_h | s_h) → velocity commands
LLC: π_l(a_l | s_l, a_h) → joint torques
```

Where:
- `π_h` is the high-level policy mapping environmental observations to velocity commands
- `π_l` is the low-level policy mapping robot state and velocity commands to joint actions
- `s_h` includes elevation maps, goal information, and navigation context
- `s_l` includes robot proprioceptive state and immediate sensor data

#### 3.1. Action Space Design

The system explores different action space configurations:

- **Velocity-only commands**: The HLC outputs desired linear and angular velocities
- **Velocity and gait commands**: The HLC additionally specifies preferred locomotion modes (walking vs. driving)
- **End-to-end control**: A single policy directly maps from perception to low-level actions

The research demonstrates that the hierarchical approach with velocity commands provides the best balance of performance and training efficiency.

### 4. Simulation Environment and Training

The training environment is carefully designed to provide diverse and challenging scenarios that prepare the robot for real-world deployment. Key features include:

#### 4.1. Terrain Generation

The simulation uses Wave Function Collapse (WFC) algorithm to procedurally generate diverse terrain patterns. This ensures that the robot encounters a wide variety of surface types and obstacle configurations during training.

#### 4.2. Dynamic Obstacles

Moving obstacles are incorporated to simulate real-world dynamic environments, helping the robot learn to navigate around pedestrians and other moving entities.

#### 4.3. Navigation Graph

A graph-based navigation structure ensures that training scenarios are solvable while providing appropriate levels of challenge. This prevents the robot from being trained on impossible navigation tasks while maintaining sufficient difficulty.

#### 4.4. Elevation Mapping Integration

The system incorporates real-time elevation mapping to provide the HLC with detailed terrain information. This allows for informed decision-making about navigation paths and locomotion strategies.

### 5. Experimental Validation

The researchers conducted extensive real-world testing to validate their approach, deploying the system in urban environments in Zurich, Switzerland, and Seville, Spain. The testing involved:

#### 5.1. Kilometer-Scale Missions

The robot successfully completed autonomous navigation missions covering multiple kilometers through diverse urban environments, demonstrating the scalability and robustness of the approach.

#### 5.2. Performance Metrics

Key performance indicators included:
- Average speed: 1.68 m/s
- Mechanical Cost of Transport (COT): 0.16
- Success rate in various terrain conditions
- Energy efficiency compared to pure legged locomotion

#### 5.3. Terrain Adaptability

The robot demonstrated successful navigation across:
- Smooth pavements and roads
- Irregular steps and stairs
- Mixed surfaces combining different materials
- Obstacles such as poles and barriers
- Dynamic environments with pedestrians

### 6. Performance Analysis

The research provides comprehensive performance analysis comparing the proposed approach against traditional navigation methods:

#### 6.1. Computational Efficiency

The hierarchical RL approach demonstrates significant computational advantages:
- Mean planning time: 0.34 ms (compared to >1000 ms for traditional methods)
- Real-time execution capability
- Reduced computational overhead for onboard processing

#### 6.2. Success Rates

Comparative studies show:
- Lower failure rates compared to baseline methods
- Zero collision rates in controlled experiments
- Improved trajectory quality and smoothness

#### 6.3. Energy Efficiency

The wheeled-legged robot achieved substantial improvements in energy efficiency:
- 6.5× speed improvement over pure legged locomotion
- Significant reduction in mechanical cost of transport
- Adaptive gait selection optimized for terrain conditions

### 7. Contributions and Significance

This research makes several important contributions to the field of autonomous mobile robotics:

#### 7.1. Technical Innovations

1. **Integrated Navigation-Locomotion Control**: The seamless coordination between navigation and locomotion controllers addresses a long-standing challenge in mobile robotics
2. **Privileged Learning Application**: Demonstrates effective use of privileged learning for robust locomotion control in complex environments
3. **Hierarchical RL for Navigation**: Shows how hierarchical RL can effectively manage the complexity of autonomous navigation tasks

#### 7.2. Practical Impact

The work demonstrates the viability of wheeled-legged robots for real-world applications such as:
- Last-mile delivery systems
- Urban inspection and monitoring
- Search and rescue operations
- General autonomous mobility in complex environments

#### 7.3. Methodological Advances

The research provides a framework for developing autonomous systems that can operate reliably in unstructured environments, with applications extending beyond wheeled-legged robots to other hybrid locomotion platforms.

The successful deployment of RL-trained controllers in real-world urban environments represents a significant step toward practical autonomous robotics systems. The demonstrated energy efficiency and adaptability suggest that hybrid locomotion platforms could play an important role in future autonomous systems, particularly for applications requiring operation across diverse terrain types.

The integration of modern machine learning techniques with practical robotics engineering demonstrates how theoretical advances in RL can be successfully translated into working autonomous systems capable of operating in complex, real-world environments.
