# Learning Robust Autonomous Navigation and Locomotion for Wheeled-Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical reinforcement learning framework enables a wheeled-legged robot to autonomously navigate diverse urban environments by integrating high-level navigation with low-level locomotion control, achieving an average speed of 1.68 m/s and a 6.5x speed improvement over purely legged systems.

## Problem

- Autonomous navigation for mobile robots in complex urban settings faces challenges with diverse terrains, dynamic obstacles, and energy efficiency.
- Traditional robot navigation systems often separate planning and locomotion, leading to suboptimal performance and instability.
- Wheeled-legged robots need a cohesive control strategy to fully leverage the benefits of both locomotion types across varying conditions.

## Method

- A hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) framework is developed, comprising a High-Level Controller (HLC) for navigation and a Low-Level Controller (LLC) for locomotion.
- The LLC is trained using privileged learning, providing access to additional environmental information during training to enhance robustness.
- The HLC generates velocity commands that the LLC reliably executes, enabling seamless coordination between navigation and locomotion.

## Results

- The robot successfully completed kilometer-scale autonomous navigation missions in diverse urban environments, including smooth pavements, stairs, and dynamic settings.
- Achieved an average speed of 1.68 m/s and a Mechanical Cost of Transport (COT) of 0.16, demonstrating energy efficiency and performance.
- The system exhibited real-time planning with a mean time of 0.34 ms and showed lower failure rates and zero collision rates compared to traditional methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex robot behaviors into hierarchical sub-tasks (navigation and locomotion) allows for more manageable and robust control.
> - Privileged learning effectively enhances the robustness and generalization capabilities of low-level locomotion controllers in varied environments without requiring additional sensory input during deployment.
> - A hierarchical approach with velocity commanding for the HLC offers the best balance of performance, adaptability, and training efficiency for wheeled-legged robot navigation.
