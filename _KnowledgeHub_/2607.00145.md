# Iterated Invariant EKF for 3D Landmark-Aided Inertial Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers formulated and applied the Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF) for 3D landmark-aided inertial navigation in Micro Aerial Vehicles (MAVs), achieving an 80% reduction in position and velocity errors compared to the standard Invariant EKF while maintaining statistical consistency.

## Problem

- Classical Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs) in landmark-aided inertial navigation suffer from the "false observability problem," leading to overconfident and inaccurate state estimates.
- First-order linearization in standard Kalman filters, even in Invariant EKFs (IEKFs), can degrade accuracy in highly nonlinear measurement models.
- The Iterated Invariant EKF (IterIEKF) had not been systematically applied or evaluated for unconstrained 3D MAV landmark localization.

## Method

- The **IterIEKF** was formulated by embedding the MAV's state into a ==matrix Lie group== (SE2(3)×R6SE_2(3) × \mathbb{R}^6SE2(3)×R6) and defining the error state as a ==right-invariant error==, mitigating the false observability problem.
- The measurement update for the **IterIEKF** incorporates a ==Gauss-Newton iterative method==, performing multiple local optimization steps to refine the state estimate and reduce linearization errors.
- A systematic comparative study was conducted, evaluating **IterIEKF** against classical ==SO(3)-EKF==, ==Iterated SO(3)-EKF==, and standard ==IEKF== using Monte Carlo simulations on MAV trajectories with known 3D landmarks.

## Results

- IterIEKF achieved superior accuracy with an **81.12%** reduction in position error (**0.096 m** MAE) and an **80.14%** reduction in velocity error (**0.095 m/s** MAE) compared to the standard IEKF.
- The proposed filter maintained excellent statistical consistency, with the Average Normalized Estimation Error Squared (NEES) value closely aligning with the error-state dimension (**15**).
- While IterIEKF demonstrated superior convergence for IMU gyroscope biases, all filters, including IterIEKF, showed persistent difficulties in accurately estimating accelerometer biases, suggesting limitations in the current bias modeling approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Employing invariant error parameterization through Lie group actions inherently resolves the false observability problem, leading to more reliable uncertainty quantification.
> - Integrating iterative Gauss-Newton optimization steps into the invariant framework significantly reduces linearization errors, thereby improving state estimation accuracy in nonlinear measurement scenarios.
> - The IterIEKF combines the statistical consistency of invariant mechanics with the accuracy benefits of iterated updates, outperforming methods that rely solely on one or the other.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The estimation of a mobile system's state, encompassing its position, orientation, and velocity, represents a foundational challenge in navigation. Historically, celestial landmarks were utilized, evolving into the modern concept of fixed environmental features. The success of the Apollo missions underscored the necessity of fusing external landmark observations with internal inertial measurements (from accelerometers and gyroscopes), establishing principles for modern state estimation. Since the early 2000s, advancements in Micro Aerial Vehicles (MAVs) and sensing technologies like RGB-D cameras and LiDARs have enabled real-time state estimation and map reconstruction, leading to the Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) problem. This work focuses on a sub-problem of SLAM, assuming a pre-built map and solved data association for landmark observations.

Probabilistic filtering methods, particularly those based on the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), have been central to landmark-aided inertial navigation. Early applications included SO(3)-EKF (also known as Multiplicative-EKF or Quaternion-EKF) formulations for precision landing using known landmarks. However, these standard formulations were shown to suffer from the "false observability problem," where the filter becomes overconfident in unobservable directions (e.g., yaw and global position), resulting in overoptimistic covariance estimates and degraded accuracy.

In response to these consistency issues, the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) was introduced. By reformulating system dynamics as a group-affine system on a Lie group and parameterizing the state error using Lie group actions, the IEKF ensures that error kinematics are independent of the robot's absolute trajectory, inherently mitigating the false observability problem without artificial modifications. The IEKF has been applied to MAV navigation, demonstrating improved performance.

Parallel to the development of invariant filtering, iterated Kalman filters emerged to reduce linearization errors caused by highly nonlinear measurement models. These methods perform a sequence of local optimization steps to refine the state estimate. The Iterated Invariant EKF (IterIEKF) was recently proposed to combine Lie group invariance with manifold-aware Gauss-Newton iterations, aiming to solve the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation problem while confining uncertainty to the tangent space of the observed submanifold. Previous applications of IterIEKF demonstrated improved convergence properties for quadrupedal robots.

This paper extends the IterIEKF framework by applying it for the first time to a 3D MAV performing landmark-aided inertial navigation. It seeks to combine the statistical consistency benefits of invariant mechanics with the accuracy of iterated update steps, addressing the limitations of both traditional VIO methods (false observability) and conventional iterated manifold filters (lack of invariant error dynamics).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to formulate and apply the Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF) to the problem of 3D landmark-based inertial localization for Micro Aerial Vehicles (MAVs). The authors' motivation stems from identified limitations in existing state estimation methods:

- **Addressing the False Observability Problem:** Classical SO(3)-EKF approaches, commonly used in landmark-aided inertial navigation, suffer from the false observability problem. This issue causes the filter to become overconfident in unobservable directions, leading to inaccurate state and covariance estimates. The Invariant EKF (IEKF) was developed to mitigate this by exploiting system symmetries through Lie group theory, but still relies on a single, first-order linearization during its measurement update.
- **Overcoming First-Order Linearization Limitations:** While the IEKF improves consistency, its reliance on a first-order linearization of the measurement model can degrade estimation accuracy in scenarios with high nonlinearity. Iterated Kalman filters, including the recently proposed IterIEKF, aim to address this by performing multiple local optimization steps to refine the state estimate, thus reducing the impact of linearization errors. The authors are motivated to explore whether the IterIEKF, by combining invariant error parameterization with iterative updates, can achieve superior accuracy in such conditions.
- **First-Time Application to MAV Landmark Localization:** The IterIEKF has demonstrated improved performance in other robotic applications, such as quadrupedal robot odometry. However, its application to unconstrained, high-velocity 3D aerial robotics, specifically for MAVs equipped with IMUs and observing known environmental landmarks, has not been fully explored. This work aims to bridge this gap by deriving and evaluating the IterIEKF in this specific context.
- **Systematic Comparative Study:** A key motivation is to conduct a systematic comparative study. This involves analyzing the direct impact of iterative invariant updates on both trajectory accuracy and statistical consistency, comparing the IterIEKF against standard SO(3)-EKF, its iterated variant (IterSO(3)-EKF), and the standard single-step IEKF.
- **Detailed Analysis of IMU Bias Estimation:** The paper also aims to provide an in-depth analysis of online IMU bias estimation dynamics across the different filter formulations, recognizing its importance for long-term navigation accuracy.
- **Complete Algorithmic Derivations:** The authors intend to present complete mathematical formulations for both invariant and multiplicative iterated filters tailored for landmark-aided MAV state estimation, with a particular emphasis on detailing the IterIEKF's algorithmic derivation for this framework.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to demonstrate that the IterIEKF can deliver significant improvements in state estimation accuracy, consistency, and IMU bias estimation for MAVs in landmark-aided navigation scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves the development and comparative evaluation of four different Kalman filter variants for 3D landmark-aided inertial navigation in MAVs: the classical SO(3)-EKF, Iterated SO(3)-EKF (IterSO(3)-EKF), Invariant EKF (IEKF), and Iterated Invariant EKF (IterIEKF).

**System Model:**
The system models a 3D MAV equipped with an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) and capable of measuring its relative position to known environmental landmarks. The state of the robot at instant $t_i$ includes its orientation ($R_i \in SO(3)$), base velocity ($v_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$), position ($p_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$), and IMU gyroscope and accelerometer biases ($b_g, b_a \in \mathbb{R}^3$). Data association for observed features is assumed to be solved, meaning landmarks are uniquely identifiable from a prior map.

**State Representation:**
- **Invariant Filters (IEKF, IterIEKF):** The robot state $X_i$ is embedded into a matrix Lie group, specifically $SE_2(3) \times \mathbb{R}^6$. The error state $\xi_i$ is defined as a right-invariant error: $\xi_i := Log_{SE_2(3)\times\mathbb{R}^6}(X_i \bar{X}_i^{-1})$, modeled as a zero-mean Gaussian random variable.
- **Multiplicative Filters (SO(3)-EKF, IterSO(3)-EKF):** The robot state $x_i$ is represented as a tuple $\{R_i, v_i, p_i, b_{g,i}, b_{a,i}\} \in SO(3) \times \mathbb{R}^{12}$. The error state $\delta x_i$ is defined as a multiplicative error: $\delta x_i := Log_{SO(3)\times\mathbb{R}^{12}}(x_i \bar{x}_i^{-1})$, also modeled as a zero-mean Gaussian random variable.

**Discrete-Time Inertial Model (Prediction Step):**
The system dynamics are governed by IMU measurements (angular velocity $\tilde{\omega}_{I,i}$ and linear acceleration $\tilde{a}_{I,i}$), which are assumed piecewise constant and corrupted by additive white Gaussian noise and biases modeled as random walks.
- **Nominal State Propagation:** The nominal state $\bar{x}_{i|i-1}$ (or $\bar{X}_{i|i-1}$ for invariant filters) is propagated using a strapdown navigation approach, integrating IMU inputs and gravity, as described in Equation (11).
- **Error State Propagation:**
 - **Invariant Filters:** The right-invariant error state $\xi_{i|i-1}$ is propagated by linearizing the error kinematics, $\xi_{i|i-1} \approx A_{i-1}\xi_{i-1} + B_{i-1}w_{i-1}$, where $A$ and $B$ are system matrices (Eq. 18, 19) and $w$ represents grouped noise. The covariance $P_{i|i-1}$ is propagated accordingly (Eq. 20).
 - **Multiplicative Filters:** The multiplicative error state $\delta x_{i|i-1}$ is propagated by linearizing its discrete-time error kinematics, $\delta x_{i|i-1} \approx \bar{A}_{i-1}\delta x_{i-1} + \bar{B}_{i-1}w_{i-1}$, with $\bar{A}$ and $\bar{B}$ as system matrices (Eq. 40, 41). The covariance $\Sigma_{i|i-1}$ is propagated via Eq. (42).

**3D Landmark Measurement Model (Update Step):**
The MAV measures the relative position of known landmarks $b_k$ with respect to its IMU frame $I$, denoted $y_{k,i}$, corrupted by noise.
- **Invariant Filters:** The measurement model is expressed through the group action of $SE_2(3) \times \mathbb{R}^6$ (Eq. 13). The innovation $z_{k,i}$ is defined using the algebraic structure of the invariant error (Eq. 21). This is linearized to $z_{k,i} \approx H_k \xi_i + \hat{n}_{k,i}$ (Eq. 23), then reduced to a 3D subsystem (Eq. 24, 25) and stacked for multiple landmarks (Eq. 26).
 - **IEKF:** Performs a single update using this linearized innovation.
 - **IterIEKF:** Applies a Gauss-Newton iterative method to refine the innovation. It performs a sequence of local optimization steps (Eq. 28-34) to estimate the mode of the posterior distribution. This iterative process uses a current estimate of the error state to re-linearize the measurement model until a convergence criterion is met (e.g., $\| \xi^{j-1} - \xi^j \| < 10^{-4}$ or loss increase). The final estimated error state $\hat{\xi}_i$ updates the nominal state, and the covariance is updated (Eq. 35-37).
- **Multiplicative Filters:** The measurement model is given by $y_{k,i} = R_i^T(b_k - p_i) + n_{k,i}$ (Eq. 12). The innovation $\delta z_{k,i}$ is derived from the difference between the observed and predicted measurements (Eq. 43). The measurement Jacobian $\bar{H}_{k,i}(\bar{x}_i)$ explicitly depends on the absolute state estimate (Eq. 44) and is stacked for multiple landmarks (Eq. 46).
 - **SO(3)-EKF:** Performs a single update using this linearized innovation.
 - **IterSO(3)-EKF:** Employs a Gauss-Newton optimization scheme (Eq. 47-51) similar to IterIEKF, iteratively refining the multiplicative error state $\delta x_i$ until convergence, and then updating the nominal state and covariance (Eq. 52, 53).

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Simulation Environment:** The filters are evaluated using a structured Monte Carlo simulation framework with N=50 localized realizations.
- **Ground Truth:** True state trajectories are extracted from the V2 01 easy dataset of the EuRoC MAV repository, and ideal IMU inputs are reconstructed.
- **Landmarks:** Three artificial 3D landmarks are placed in the world frame.
- **Noise Model:** The initial state, proprioceptive IMU sequences, and exteroceptive landmark updates are corrupted with zero-mean white Gaussian noise with specified covariance matrices (Eq. 55). The initial state perturbation for invariant filters is $\bar{X}_0 = (-\xi_0) \oplus X_0$, and for multiplicative variants, $\bar{x}_0 = (-\delta x_0) \oplus x_0$.
- **Sensor Rates:** Simulated IMU feeds operate at 200 Hz, while landmark observations update at 1 Hz, with all three landmarks visible throughout the trajectory.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Statistical Consistency:** Assessed using the Average Normalized Estimation Error Squared (NEES) metric (Eq. 56), comparing it against the error-state dimension (15).
- **Trajectory Accuracy:** Quantified through the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) (Eq. 57) for position, body-frame linear velocity, global gravity vector orientation (angular displacement), and Euler attitude parameters (roll, pitch, yaw).
- **Bias Estimation:** The relative estimation errors of IMU gyroscope and accelerometer biases are analyzed over time for a representative single Monte Carlo realization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results from extensive Monte Carlo simulations, evaluating the performance of the four filter variants: SO(3)-EKF, IterSO(3)-EKF, IEKF, and IterIEKF.

**Overall Performance and Accuracy:**
- **Performance Hierarchy:** The IterIEKF consistently demonstrated superior performance across all evaluated metrics. It outperformed the IEKF, IterSO(3)-EKF, and classical SO(3)-EKF.
- **Position and Velocity Errors:**
 - The classical SO(3)-EKF exhibited the poorest accuracy, with a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 9.363 m for position and 4.918 m/s for linear velocity. This is attributed to the false observability problem.
 - The IterSO(3)-EKF showed improvement over the SO(3)-EKF, reducing position MAE to 2.1 m and velocity MAE to 1.331 m/s, but remained less accurate than the standard IEKF.
 - The IEKF achieved a position MAE of 0.511 m and velocity MAE of 0.48 m/s.
 - The proposed IterIEKF achieved the highest accuracy, with a position MAE of 0.096 m and a velocity MAE of 0.095 m/s. This represents an 81.12% reduction in position error and an 80.14% reduction in velocity error compared to the IEKF.
- **Gravity Vector Alignment:** IterIEKF also demonstrated superior accuracy in estimating the global gravity vector direction, with an MAE of 0.661 degrees, compared to IEKF (2.042 degrees), IterSO(3)-EKF (4.439 degrees), and SO(3)-EKF (11.244 degrees).
- **Convergence Characteristics:** IterIEKF exhibited rapid convergence, aligning with the ground truth trajectory within the first 10 seconds of operation for position, velocity, and gravity direction. The IEKF showed a slower convergence rate and larger steady-state errors.

**Statistical Consistency (NEES):**
- **IterIEKF:** Maintained an Average Normalized Estimation Error Squared (NEES) value close to 15, which corresponds to the error-state dimension. This indicates good statistical consistency, meaning the filter's estimated uncertainty closely matches the actual estimation error.
- **IEKF and IterSO(3)-EKF:** Showed higher NEES values compared to IterIEKF, suggesting overconfidence in their estimates.
- **SO(3)-EKF:** Exhibited the highest NEES values, confirming its poor consistency and reliability, particularly under initial state corruption.

**Attitude Estimation:**
- **Convergence Rate:** IterIEKF achieved the fastest convergence for attitude estimates (roll, pitch, and yaw angles), settling approximately 10 seconds faster than the standard IEKF.
- **Channel-Specific Performance:** While IterIEKF showed slightly prolonged settling for the roll angle, it exhibited highly accelerated convergence in the pitch channel.
- **Robustness:** The tracking profiles confirmed IterIEKF's superior robustness against severe initial state corruption, delivering reliable and consistent attitude estimation.

**IMU Bias Estimation:**
- **General Trend:** IterIEKF demonstrated superior convergence and lower residual errors for IMU biases compared to all baseline filters.
- **Gyroscope Biases:** Invariant filters, including IterIEKF, presented low steady-state errors for gyroscope biases.
- **Accelerometer Biases:** All evaluated filters, including IterIEKF, encountered difficulties in accurately identifying accelerometer biases. The relative error for accelerometer biases persistently remained above 1.0, indicating poor observability or estimation performance for these states. The authors attribute this limitation to the modeling approach, where biases are appended to the state space as standard vector additions ($\mathbb{R}^6$), potentially breaking the strict group-affine property of the system dynamics during propagation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in the field of state estimation for autonomous mobile robots, particularly Micro Aerial Vehicles (MAVs) operating in environments with known landmarks.

**Primary Significance:**
- **Novel Application of IterIEKF:** This paper marks the first explicit formulation and systematic evaluation of the Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF) tailored for 3D landmark-aided inertial navigation in MAVs. This extends the applicability of a geometrically principled and statistically consistent filtering framework to a new and demanding domain.
- **Superior Performance:** The study empirically demonstrates a significant performance leap provided by IterIEKF. It achieved an approximate 80% reduction in both position and linear velocity errors compared to the standard Invariant EKF (IEKF), while also establishing superior statistical consistency (as indicated by NEES values) and highly accurate gravity vector alignment.
- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations:** IterIEKF inherently addresses two key limitations of prior methods:
 1. **False Observability:** By parameterizing the system state error via right-invariant group actions on $SE_2(3)$, the framework structurally eliminates the false observability problem that affects classical non-invariant architectures like the SO(3)-EKF and IterSO(3)-EKF. This leads to more reliable uncertainty quantification.
 2. **Linearization Errors:** By integrating localized Gauss-Newton optimization loops directly into the invariant measurement update, the IterIEKF mitigates the approximation errors inherent in the first-order linearization used by the standard single-step IEKF, leading to improved accuracy in nonlinear scenarios.
- **Robustness and Consistency:** The IterIEKF demonstrates enhanced robustness against severe initial state corruption and prevents cross-state error corruption, providing a more dependable foundation for MAV navigation.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced MAV Autonomy:** The improved accuracy and consistency offered by IterIEKF could significantly enhance the capabilities of autonomous MAVs in various applications, including precision landing, inspection, delivery, and environmental monitoring in structured or pre-mapped environments.
- **Foundation for Advanced SLAM:** This work lays a robust algorithmic foundation for future advancements in more complex Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems. The authors suggest scaling the proposed filter into a full SLAM framework that incorporates online data association and unknown landmark map generation, which would extend its applicability to unmapped and dynamic environments.
- **Guidance for Filter Design:** The detailed comparative analysis provides insights into the advantages and disadvantages of different filter architectures, guiding future research and development in state estimation.
- **Challenges in Bias Estimation:** The identification of persistent difficulties in accurately estimating accelerometer biases, even with the IterIEKF, highlights an important area for future research. The suggestion to adopt advanced Lie group structures, such as the Two-Frame Group (TFG) framework, for structurally invariant embedding of biases, points towards a potential direction for resolving this known issue in IMU-centric navigation.

In summary, this research contributes a highly accurate and statistically consistent state estimation framework for MAVs, addressing long-standing challenges in inertial navigation and setting the stage for more capable autonomous robotic systems.
