# Push Anything: Single- and Multi-Object Pushing From First Sight with Contact-Implicit MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the GRASP Laboratory developed "Push Anything," a framework combining real-time perception with an enhanced Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (C3+) algorithm for high-precision planar pushing of single and multiple objects. The system demonstrated a 98% overall success rate across 928 trials with 33 diverse objects, achieving tight pose tolerances in complex, multi-contact scenarios previously intractable for such model-based approaches.

## Problem

- Prior Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC) methods faced limitations in handling the combinatorial complexity of multi-object interactions and relied heavily on precise knowledge of object geometries, often failing in real-time for cluttered environments.
- Existing CI-MPC algorithms suffered from computational bottlenecks due to local approximations of non-linear dynamics and the non-smoothness of complementarity constraints, hindering real-time performance and leading to entrapment in local minima.
- Robustly manipulating diverse objects without grasping, especially in cluttered environments with unknown physical properties, remained a significant challenge for robotic systems.

## Method

- The "Push Anything" framework integrates a perception pipeline for real-time object scanning, mesh reconstruction, and robust multi-object tracking (using FoundationPose and XMem) with an advanced control algorithm.
- A novel algorithm, Consensus Complementarity Control Plus (C3+), was developed, enhancing CI-==MPC== by introducing a slack variable to reformulate complementarity constraints. This allowed the non-convex component to decouple, enabling an ADMM-based optimization with an analytically solvable projection step.
- A sampling strategy for end effector positions, guided by pre-processed object meshes, provides candidate contact points, allowing the local C3+ planner to explore a broader range of actions and overcome local minima for long-horizon goals.

## Results

- The system achieved an overall **98%** success rate across 928 hardware trials with 33 diverse objects, successfully reaching pose goals within tight tolerances (translational error ≤ 2 cm, rotational error ≤ **0.1** rad).
- C3+ demonstrated a substantial speedup over its predecessor, C3, with its projection step being four to five orders of magnitude faster, which was critical for enabling real-time performance in multi-object scenarios.
- It successfully performed multi-object decluttering tasks with up to four objects, maintaining real-time control rates (e.g., ~9 Hz for 4-object tasks) and achieving mean time-to-goal metrics that scale with task complexity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Introducing a slack variable into the complementarity constraint of CI-MPC dramatically accelerates the projection step (by four to five orders of magnitude), transforming a costly coupled problem into many independent, constant-time computations.
> - Integrating a comprehensive real-time perception system with an enhanced CI-MPC allows for robust manipulation of arbitrary, diverse objects without prior CAD models, broadening the applicability of model-based control in unstructured environments.
> - Combining a global sampling strategy for end effector placements with a fast local CI-MPC planner effectively addresses the locality limitations of prior CI-MPC methods, enabling successful long-horizon multi-object manipulation.
