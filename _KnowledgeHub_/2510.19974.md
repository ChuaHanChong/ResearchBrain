# Push Anything: Single- and Multi-Object Pushing From First Sight with Contact-Implicit MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the GRASP Laboratory developed "Push Anything," a framework combining real-time perception with an enhanced Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (C3+) algorithm for high-precision planar pushing of single and multiple objects. The system demonstrated a 98% overall success rate across 928 trials with 33 diverse objects, achieving tight pose tolerances in complex, multi-contact scenarios previously intractable for such model-based approaches.

## Problem

- Prior Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC) methods faced limitations in handling the combinatorial complexity of multi-object interactions and relied heavily on precise knowledge of object geometries, often failing in real-time for cluttered environments.
- Existing CI-MPC algorithms suffered from computational bottlenecks due to local approximations of non-linear dynamics and the non-smoothness of complementarity constraints, hindering real-time performance and leading to entrapment in local minima.
- Robustly manipulating diverse objects without grasping, especially in cluttered environments with unknown physical properties, remained a significant challenge for robotic systems.

## Method

- The "Push Anything" framework integrates a perception pipeline for real-time object scanning, mesh reconstruction, and robust multi-object tracking (using FoundationPose and XMem) with an advanced control algorithm.
- A novel algorithm, Consensus Complementarity Control Plus (C3+), was developed, enhancing CI-==MPC== by introducing a slack variable to reformulate complementarity constraints. This allowed the non-convex component to decouple, enabling an ADMM-based optimization with an analytically solvable projection step.
- A sampling strategy for end effector positions, guided by pre-processed object meshes, provides candidate contact points, allowing the local C3+ planner to explore a broader range of actions and overcome local minima for long-horizon goals.

## Results

- The system achieved an overall **98%** success rate across 928 hardware trials with 33 diverse objects, successfully reaching pose goals within tight tolerances (translational error ≤ 2 cm, rotational error ≤ **0.1** rad).
- C3+ demonstrated a substantial speedup over its predecessor, C3, with its projection step being four to five orders of magnitude faster, which was critical for enabling real-time performance in multi-object scenarios.
- It successfully performed multi-object decluttering tasks with up to four objects, maintaining real-time control rates (e.g., ~9 Hz for 4-object tasks) and achieving mean time-to-goal metrics that scale with task complexity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Introducing a slack variable into the complementarity constraint of CI-MPC dramatically accelerates the projection step (by four to five orders of magnitude), transforming a costly coupled problem into many independent, constant-time computations.
> - Integrating a comprehensive real-time perception system with an enhanced CI-MPC allows for robust manipulation of arbitrary, diverse objects without prior CAD models, broadening the applicability of model-based control in unstructured environments.
> - Combining a global sampling strategy for end effector placements with a fast local CI-MPC planner effectively addresses the locality limitations of prior CI-MPC methods, enabling successful long-horizon multi-object manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly non-prehensile tasks involving diverse objects and complex contact interactions, represents a significant challenge in robotics. This challenge is compounded by factors such as unknown physical properties of objects and the combinatorial complexity of contact-rich scenarios.

Model-based approaches, such as Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC), have emerged as promising methods. CI-MPC embeds contact dynamics directly into real-time trajectory optimization, allowing for simultaneous optimization of control inputs and contact interactions without requiring pre-specified contact mode schedules. However, prior CI-MPC demonstrations have typically been limited by their reliance on local approximations of nonlinear dynamics, which restricts their effectiveness to narrow regions and simple, often pre-defined, examples. These methods also face scalability issues due to the non-smoothness and combinatorial complexity of complementarity constraints, rendering multi-object scenarios intractable for real-time application.

To address the locality limitation of CI-MPC, hybrid approaches have integrated sampling strategies to provide global guidance. These methods typically sample low-dimensional end effector positions or stable grasp configurations and then use CI-MPC as a local planner to generate dynamically feasible trajectories for each candidate. The robot then executes the plan corresponding to the lowest-cost solution, enabling it to overcome local minima and pursue long-horizon goals.

Concurrently, data-driven approaches, including reinforcement learning (RL), have gained traction for planar pushing tasks, demonstrating robustness for single-object repositioning and some improvements in generalization. However, these methods are often data-intensive and have not generally scaled to the complexity of multi-object rearrangement problems.

This work specifically builds upon the foundation of sampling-based CI-MPC, particularly the framework by Venkatesh, Bianchini et al.. It seeks to extend the capabilities of CI-MPC to real-world, multi-object scenarios involving diverse and arbitrary object geometries, which were previously largely intractable for such model-based approaches. By introducing a new, more efficient CI-MPC algorithm and integrating it into a comprehensive hardware system, this research aims to bridge the gap between theoretical CI-MPC advancements and their practical application in complex, cluttered environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to broaden the demonstrated capabilities of Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC) for precise planar pushing tasks. This involves extending its applicability to a wide range of object geometries and into multi-object domains, a setting where reasoning over numerous inter-object and object-environment contacts presents a significant challenge for prior CI-MPC methods.

The motivation behind this work stems from several limitations observed in existing robotic manipulation approaches:
- **Intractability of Multi-Object Interaction:** Prior CI-MPC methods have struggled with the combinatorial complexity arising from multi-object interactions, such as those required for de-cluttering environments. The problem complexity increases exponentially with the number of potential contacts, making real-time planning prohibitive.
- **Reliance on Known Geometries:** Previous demonstrations of CI-MPC have largely been restricted to single-object scenarios with precisely known geometries, often derived from CAD models. This limits their applicability to online, real-world settings where objects may have unknown or variable shapes.
- **Computational Bottlenecks:** While CI-MPC includes contact terms in real-time trajectory optimization, the underlying algorithms often rely on local approximations that can be computationally slow for complex, multi-contact scenarios, hindering real-time performance.
- **Locality of Solutions:** CI-MPC's dependence on local approximations can prevent the controller from taking short-term suboptimal actions necessary for long-term success, leading to entrapment in local minima and failure to reach distant goals.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this research are:
1. **Develop an enhanced CI-MPC algorithm (C3+):** The goal is to create a version of Consensus Complementarity Control (C3) that substantially accelerates solve times. This enhancement is intended to enable the system to reason efficiently over numerous inter-object and object-environment contacts across multi-step horizons, thus facilitating precise multi-object manipulation.
2. **Integrate C3+ into a complete manipulation pipeline:** The research aims to construct a fully-integrated system, named "Push Anything," that encompasses real-world object scanning, mesh reconstruction, robust object tracking, and real-time contact-rich motion planning. This pipeline is designed to work with arbitrary objects encountered in unstructured environments.
3. **Validate on hardware with diverse and multi-object scenarios:** A critical objective is to provide extensive real-world experimental validation, demonstrating high-precision planar pushing of a wide variety of objects and in complex multi-object decluttering tasks that were previously considered intractable for CI-MPC. This includes achieving high success rates and meeting tight pose tolerances.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to advance the state of the art in non-prehensile manipulation, making contact-rich planning more practical and widely applicable for robots operating in dynamic, cluttered, and uncertain real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Push Anything" framework integrates object perception with a novel control algorithm, operating in two main phases: an offline phase for object library creation and an online phase for real-time control.

**A. Offline Phase: Object Library and Perception Pipeline**
1. **Novel Object Mesh Reconstruction:** For new objects, a RealSense D455 RGBD camera records video. Initial object masks are manually selected in the first frame, and XMem automatically generates subsequent masks. BundleSDF then performs 3D mesh reconstruction from RGB images, depth images, masks, and camera intrinsics. The resulting meshes are post-processed to be watertight and oriented with the z-axis upwards for flat placement on a table. The system currently assumes identical mass and inertia for all objects, storing them in a URDF format.

**B. Online Phase: Control and Object Tracking**
The online phase uses robot and object state estimates to compute end effector trajectories, which are tracked by a low-level operational space controller (OSC).

1. **Multi-Object Tracking:** Multiple instances of FoundationPose are run in parallel, sharing memory for camera frames. To enhance robustness against occlusions, imperfect meshes, and drift over long horizons, XMem is integrated to enable periodic re-registration of object masks. Pose ambiguity, common in symmetric objects, is addressed by detecting and correcting implausibly large orientation changes between timesteps, ensuring temporal consistency.

2. **Sampling-Based CI-MPC Controller:** This component builds on the framework of Venkatesh, Bianchini et al. but incorporates targeted improvements for arbitrary and multi-object scenarios.
 - **Sampling Strategy of End Effector Positions:** Candidate end effector locations are generated by pre-processing object meshes to store body-frame face locations, areas, and normal vectors. During operation, an object is uniformly selected, then a face weighted by area, and finally a point on that face. This surface point is projected a fixed distance along its outward normal vector, then to a fixed world height. Samples too close to any object (due to non-convexity, multiple objects, or vertical normals) are rejected. This process is repeated until a desired number of candidates is obtained.
 - **Consensus Complementarity Control Plus (C3+):** This is an enhanced version of the C3 algorithm.
 - **Problem Formulation:** C3+ addresses a Quadratic Program with Complementarity Constraints (QPCC), derived from a linearized hybrid model (Linear Complementarity System or LCS) of contact dynamics. The state `x` includes end effector and object positions/orientations and velocities, `u` is Cartesian forces, and `λ` represents contact forces using 4-sided polyhedral friction cones.
 - **Core Enhancement – Slack Variable:** The key innovation in C3+ is the introduction of a slack variable, `η_k`, to represent the linear expression within the complementarity constraint `0 ≤ λ_k ⊥ Φ(x_k, u_k, λ_k) ≥ 0`. This reformulates the problem into `η_k = Ex_k + Fλ_k + Hu_k + c` and `0 ≤ λ_k ⊥ η_k ≥ 0`. This reframing allows the non-convex complementarity component to become decoupled across individual contact pairs, simplifying the subsequent optimization.
 - **ADMM-based Optimization:** C3+ employs the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) to solve the problem, which is reformulated into a consensus form. Each ADMM iteration consists of three steps:
 1. **Quadratic Step (z-update):** A convex Quadratic Program (QP) is solved to update the main decision variables. C3+ includes the additional linear equality constraint for `η_k` in this QP.
 2. **Projection Step (δ-update):** This step projects the updated variables onto the simple complementarity set. Due to the slack variable formulation, this projection decouples across all timesteps and contact pairs. Each sub-problem becomes an independent 1D Mixed-Integer Quadratic Program (MIQP) with a closed-form analytical solution. This transformation from a costly, coupled problem to many constant-time, independent computations is the source of C3+'s significant speedup.
 3. **Dual Update (w-update):** Scaled dual variables are adjusted to encourage agreement between the primal and auxiliary variables.
 - **Real-time Practicalities:** For real-time performance, ADMM iterations are terminated early after a small, fixed number of steps. In the final quadratic step, large weights are applied to end effector–object contact components to encourage dynamically feasible forces consistent with the complementarity constraints.

**C. System State and Contact Modeling:**
The system models contact geometries dynamically via collision detection at each control loop. Contact pairs are defined for the end effector-nearest object, object-ground (three pairs per object), object-wall (one pair per object, except in the 4-object setup), and between every pair of objects. For example, a 3-object setting results in 16 contact pairs, translating to `λ ∈ R^64`.

**D. Controller Parameters and Implementation:**
Controller parameters like planning horizon (N), timestep (Δt), number of ADMM iterations, and number of end effector samples are adjusted based on the number of objects to maintain real-time rates (e.g., shorter horizon for more objects). The system is implemented in C++ using Drake and utilizes three dedicated computers for the sampling-based controller, operational-space controller/robot drivers (on a real-time kernel), and object tracking, communicating via LCM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive hardware experiments to evaluate the "Push Anything" pipeline, including single- and multi-object planar pushing tasks.

**A. Overall Performance Summary**
Across all experiments, involving 33 diverse objects over 928 trials, the system achieved an overall success rate of 98%. Pose goals were reached within tight tolerances (translational error ≤ 2 cm and rotational error ≤ 0.1 rad, which is approximately 5.7 degrees).

**B. Single-Object Pushing Performance**
- **Trials:** 701 hardware trials were conducted with 25 distinct objects, each object tested until 28 successful trials were recorded.
- **Success Rate:** A 99.9% success rate (700 out of 701 trials) was achieved. The single failure occurred when a large object (egg carton) was pushed out of the robot's reachable workspace.
- **Time-to-Goal:** The mean time-to-goal across all single-object trials was approximately 31 seconds under tight success criteria.
- **Comparison to Prior Work:** For the established "Push T" task, the framework achieved a mean time-to-goal of 26.9 seconds, which represents an improvement of approximately 11.5% compared to the 30.5 seconds reported by the prior work on which this system builds.
- **Outliers:** Occasional outliers in time-to-goal were observed for certain objects (e.g., chicken broth, milk bottle) due to the robot needing to reposition objects that moved out of reach. The clamp, with its complex shape, mass, and friction properties, also contributed to longer manipulation times in some instances.

**C. Multi-Object Pushing Performance**
- **Trials:** A total of 227 trials were performed, comprising 10 experiments for 2-object scenarios, 6 for 3-object, and 5 for 4-object scenarios. Each experiment was run until 10 successful trials were achieved.
- **Success Rate:** Under tight tolerances, the multi-object tasks achieved a 92.5% success rate (210 out of 227 trials). Failures in multi-object tasks consistently resulted from objects moving beyond the robot's operational workspace.
- **Time-to-Goal:** The mean time-to-goal (tight tolerance) scaled with the number of objects:
 - 2-object tasks: approximately 96.4 seconds.
 - 3-object tasks: approximately 191.1 seconds.
 - 4-object tasks: approximately 315.7 seconds.
 These times do not scale linearly with the number of objects due to goal assignments being permuted across objects in each trial, which requires additional object rearrangements and execution time.
- **Control Rate:** The system maintained real-time control rates, approximately 14-15 Hz for 2- and 3-object tasks, and around 9 Hz for 4-object tasks, by adjusting the planning horizon.

**D. Comparison of Solve Times for C3 and C3+**
A benchmark comparison between the enhanced CI-MPC algorithm, C3+, and its predecessor, C3, demonstrated a substantial speedup for C3+.
- **Quadratic Step:** The quadratic step (z-update) in C3+ was marginally slower than in C3 (e.g., 3.09 ms vs. 1.67 ms for 1-object tasks).
- **Projection Step:** The projection step (δ-update) in C3+ was four to five orders of magnitude faster than in C3. For instance, in 1-object tasks, C3+ completed this step in approximately 0.007 ms compared to C3's 10.38 ms. In 3-object tasks, C3+ took about 0.006 ms versus C3's 40.39 ms.
This significant acceleration in the projection step by C3+ is identified as the key enabler for the overall real-time performance and the ability to handle multi-object, multi-contact scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in non-prehensile contact-rich tasks.

The introduction of **Consensus Complementarity Control Plus (C3+)** signifies a significant technical improvement in contact-implicit model predictive control (CI-MPC). By reforming the complementarity constraint with a slack variable, C3+ transforms a computationally intensive, coupled projection problem into many independent, analytically solvable sub-problems. This innovation results in a dramatic acceleration of solve times (four to five orders of magnitude faster in the projection step compared to its predecessor, C3), directly addressing one of the primary limitations of CI-MPC regarding real-time performance in complex multi-contact scenarios.

The **"Push Anything" pipeline** demonstrates the practical applicability of advanced CI-MPC in real-world settings. It integrates crucial components from perception (real-world object scanning and mesh reconstruction using RealSense, XMem, and BundleSDF) to robust object tracking (leveraging FoundationPose with XMem for drift correction) and ultimately to real-time, contact-rich control. This complete system addresses the challenge of manipulating objects with arbitrary and diverse geometries, moving beyond reliance on precisely known CAD models.

The extensive **hardware validation** involving 33 objects and 928 trials underscores the robustness and precision of the system. Achieving an overall 98% success rate and reaching tight pose goals in both single-object and complex multi-object decluttering tasks (with up to four objects) showcases the system's capabilities in scenarios previously considered intractable for CI-MPC. The demonstrated control rates and time-to-goal metrics provide quantitative evidence of the system's efficiency and effectiveness in practical operations.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** By enabling robots to robustly manipulate diverse objects without grasping, even in cluttered environments, this work contributes to more versatile and autonomous robotic systems. This capability is valuable in applications such as automated warehousing, manufacturing, and domestic assistance, where objects may not always be graspable or precisely known.
- **Advancement in Contact-Rich Planning:** The methodological improvements in C3+ provide a foundation for further research in contact-implicit optimization, potentially inspiring similar computational efficiencies in other complex contact-rich robotic tasks.
- **Bridging Research Gaps:** The research successfully bridges the gap between theoretical advancements in CI-MPC and their practical implementation in real-world hardware, demonstrating how model-based approaches can tackle the complexities of physical interaction.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations such as the performance being constrained by pose tracking accuracy (especially under occlusion), the assumption of identical mass and inertia for all objects, and the lack of high-level task planning. Addressing these limitations in future work, through improved perception, online model learning, higher-level reasoning, and extension to 3D non-prehensile manipulation, could further broaden the scope and impact of this research. Overall, this work represents a significant step towards enabling robots to perform flexible and robust manipulation in unstructured and dynamic environments.
