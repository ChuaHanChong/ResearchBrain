# EgoPressure: A Dataset for Hand Pressure and Pose Estimation in Egocentric Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoPressure is an egocentric dataset of bare-hand touch and pressure on a pressure-sensitive surface, pairing 4.3M RGB-D frames from seven static plus one head-mounted camera with per-contact pressure and MANO hand meshes annotated by a markerless multi-view differentiable-rendering optimization. Baselines show hand pose input improves pressure estimation, and PressureFormer predicts pressure directly as a hand-mesh UV map.

## Problem

- Gloves and force-instrumented hands used to capture contact forces obstruct natural tactile feedback and alter visual appearance, limiting generalization to bare hands and uninstrumented surfaces.
- Existing ground-truth pressure datasets record only static third-person cameras, so trained models do not transfer to head-mounted moving cameras with severe self-occlusion.
- Most hand-object datasets infer contact from hand-object mesh intersection, demanding sub-millimeter pose accuracy, and supply no pressure magnitude nor pressure localized on the hand.

## Method

- A multi-view sequence-based ==MANO annotation pipeline== initializes pose with ==HaMeR== and masks with ==Segment-Anything==, then refines pose, translation and per-vertex displacement through ==DIB-R differentiable rasterization== across seven calibrated cameras.
- A virtual orthogonal camera placed under the touchpad reprojects measured pressure onto the mesh as an optimized ==UV texture==, with a depth term enforcing physical contact at sensed pixels.
- **PressureFormer**, a transformer decoder cross-attending ==HaMeR ViT== image tokens from per-vertex tokens, predicts a quantized ==UV pressure map== rendered back to image space under coarse and pressure cross-entropy losses.

## Results

- On egocentric views, adding HaMeR-predicted 2.5D joints raised volumetric IoU from **38.64** to **40.52**, and ground-truth joints reached **41.39**, beating the RGB-only PressureVisionNet baseline.
- Cross-view generalization gained most: trained on cameras 2-5 and tested on held-out views 1, 6, 7, contact IoU rose **36.82** to **43.04** and MAE fell **62.22** to **49.45**.
- PressureFormer attained the best contact IoU at **43.04** versus **40.71** for PressureVisionNet, but its volumetric IoU **31.57** and MAE **71 kPa** trailed the pose-augmented baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D hand pose acts as a geometric prior for pressure inference, paying off most under occlusion and unseen viewpoints rather than in-distribution views.
> - Predicting pressure on a mesh UV map localizes force in 3D and sharpens contact boundaries, yet degrades magnitude accuracy: a localization-versus-calibration trade-off.
> - Instrumentation-free ground truth is obtainable by sensing the surface instead of the hand, preserving natural bare-hand appearance and tactile feedback throughout capture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "EgoPressure" contributes to the fields of computer vision, human-computer interaction, and robotics, specifically focusing on understanding human hand-object interactions. Estimating touch contact and pressure is critical for developing advanced applications in augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and robotic manipulation. Prior research has explored vision-based hand tracking and hand-object pose estimation, with significant progress due to deep learning and improved datasets. However, many existing datasets rely on additional instrumentation (gloves, markers), motion capture systems, or multi-view static camera rigs, which can interfere with natural interaction or limit generalization to real-world scenarios.

A key challenge identified in the existing landscape is the lack of comprehensive datasets that provide both accurate hand poses and detailed pressure information, particularly from an egocentric (first-person) perspective. While some datasets infer contact points based on intersecting hand and object meshes, and others provide ground-truth pressure maps (e.g., PressureVision and ContactLabelDB using pressure-sensitive surfaces), these often lack accurate hand pose and mesh annotations, or are limited to static camera views. Methods for estimating pressure from monocular images have also been explored, utilizing visual cues like fingernail alterations or surface deformations, but these often require specific object types or instrumentation.

"EgoPressure" extends this research by providing a dataset that addresses these limitations. It combines egocentric vision with accurate 3D hand pose and mesh annotations and synchronized ground-truth pressure data. This integration aims to bridge the gap between understanding hand-object interactions from a first-person perspective and precisely localizing applied pressure in 3D space, which is critical for developing more robust and generalizable models for AR/VR and robotic systems. The dataset's markerless capture and egocentric perspective differentiate it from much of the prior work, enabling research into scenarios where cameras are integrated into head-mounted devices.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce EgoPressure, a novel dataset designed to facilitate the estimation of touch contact and pressure during human hand-object interactions, specifically from an egocentric viewpoint. This objective is motivated by several identified needs in mixed reality and robotics:

1. **Enabling Natural Hand-Object Interaction in AR/VR:** Understanding touch contact and pressure from an egocentric perspective is crucial for creating more precise control and realistic haptic feedback in AR/VR environments. Current AR/VR systems often lack refined feedback mechanisms, such as varying sound based on pressure applied to virtual objects (e.g., a piano key). Accurate pressure estimation could provide a richer interaction experience, mimicking real-world tactile sensations.
2. **Advancing Robotic Manipulation:** Precise pressure information is essential for robots to accurately replicate human manipulation. Determining the appropriate pressure for grasping objects remains a significant challenge in robotics. Datasets that provide detailed human interaction data, including pressure, can aid in training robots for more dexterous and human-like manipulation tasks.
3. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Datasets:**
 - **Lack of Egocentric Pressure Data:** Previous datasets for pressure estimation are largely limited to exocentric (third-person, static camera) views. This makes it challenging to develop models that generalize to head-mounted or body-worn cameras common in AR/VR applications.
 - **Absence of Combined Pose and Pressure Data:** Many existing hand-object interaction datasets provide either hand pose or contact information, but rarely both with high-resolution pressure data. The concurrent availability of accurate 3D hand poses, meshes, and detailed pressure maps is identified as a critical missing component.
 - **Instrumentation Interference:** Prior methods often rely on instrumented gloves or surfaces, which can interfere with natural hand movements and tactile feedback, as well as alter visual appearance, limiting generalization to bare-handed interactions. The authors aim for a markerless, bare-handed approach.
4. **Establishing Benchmarks for Future Research:** By providing a comprehensive dataset, the authors aim to establish standardized benchmarks for evaluating models that estimate applied pressure from egocentric RGB images, with and without hand pose information. They also aim to introduce a benchmark for jointly estimating 3D hand pose, hand mesh, and pressure on both the external surface and the hand mesh itself, thereby enabling 3D localization of contact and pressure.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need for a high-quality, egocentric, markerless dataset that captures bare-handed interactions with precise 3D hand pose, mesh, and pressure annotations, thereby enabling the development of advanced models for AR/VR and robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves two primary components: the design and implementation of a specialized data capture setup to acquire multi-modal data, and the development of a marker-less optimization method for annotating accurate 3D hand poses, meshes, and pressure distributions. Subsequently, baseline models are introduced and evaluated to demonstrate the dataset's utility.

#### 3.1. Data Capture Setup

The EgoPressure dataset was collected using a custom-built data capture rig (Figure 3) designed to synchronize various sensors:

- **Pressure Sensing:** A Sensel Morph touchpad (240 × 169.5 mm) was used to capture high-resolution touch and pressure intensity data. Four different texture overlays (white, green, dark wood, light wood) were used on the touchpad surface.
- **Multi-view RGB-D Cameras:** The rig incorporates eight Azure Kinect cameras, seven of which are static and positioned around the interaction area, and one is head-mounted on a participant's helmet (egocentric view). All cameras record RGB images and depth maps at 30 Hz.
- **Synchronization:** All cameras and the Sensel Morph touchpad are connected to two workstations and synchronized using a Raspberry Pi CM4 via the Precision Time Protocol (PTP), ensuring frame rates are aligned at 30 fps.
- **Head-mounted Camera Tracking:** To obtain accurate poses for the head-mounted camera, nine active infrared (IR) markers are placed in an asymmetric layout around the Sensel Morph pad. These markers are detectable in the Azure Kinect's IR images, allowing for camera pose computation using the Perspective-N-Points (PnP) algorithm. Spherical linear interpolation (Slerp) is used for frames where tracking might be lost.
- **Participants and Gestures:** 21 participants (6 female, 15 male, ages 23–32) were recruited, providing anatomical diversity. Each participant performed 31 distinct gestures (e.g., touch, drag, pinch, press of varying strengths) with both left and right hands. Each gesture was repeated multiple times (3 or 5 repetitions), totaling 6216 gestures.
- **Data Volume:** The dataset comprises 5 hours of recorded interactions, resulting in 4.3 million RGB-D frames. Approximately 45.1% of these frames capture the hand in contact with the pressure-sensitive pad.

#### 3.2. Marker-less Annotation Method

A multi-camera, sequence-based optimization method (Figure 2) was developed to generate accurate, marker-less 3D hand pose and mesh annotations using the MANO hand model.

- **Automatic Hand Pose Initialization:**
 - Initial MANO hand pose (θ_init) and translation (t_init) are estimated for each static camera view using HaMeR, an RGB-based 3D hand pose estimator.
 - Scale-translation ambiguity from single-image HaMeR predictions is resolved by triangulating root joints from the seven static camera views.
 - Segmentation masks for the hand (M_gt) are obtained from each static camera image using Segment-Anything (SAM) with HaMeR’s bounding box and 2D hand root as input.
- **β-calibration:** A separate calibration sequence is performed for each hand of each participant. During these sequences, MANO shape parameters (β) are optimized and then fixed for all subsequent gesture sequences, accounting for individual hand characteristics.
- **Annotation Refinement (Two Stages):**
 1. **POSE OPTIMIZATION:** Focuses on refining hand pose (θ) and translation (t). The hand mesh (Θ) is derived from MANO(θ, β)+t. This stage minimizes a rendering objective (L_R) and a mesh intersection loss (L_insec) across all static camera views. The rendering objective includes Mask IoU Loss, RGB MSE Loss, and Depth Volumetric IoU Loss between rendered and ground-truth images/depth maps.
 2. **SHAPE REFINEMENT:** With pose (θ) and translation (t) fixed, this stage optimizes for vertex displacement (D_vert) along each vertex's normal vector. The refined mesh (Θ*) is expressed as Θ + n⃗ · D_vert. This stage minimizes the rendering objective (L_R), a geometry objective (L_G), and a virtual render objective (L_vR).
- **Virtual Render for Contact and Pressure (Section 3.2.1):**
 - A virtual orthogonal camera (vR) is positioned beneath the touchpad, oriented upwards, with its plane overlapping the touchpad's sensing surface.
 - Pressure is optimized as a UV texture map (T_P) on the hand mesh. The objective ensures that the rendered pressure (vR_P) on the hand mesh aligns with the ground-truth pressure (P_gt) from the Sensel Morph.
 - A contact area is inferred from P_gt using a pressure threshold, and the objective ensures that the masked rendered depth (vR_D) aligns with the distance to the touchpad (Z_v2p), enforcing physical contact.
- **Geometry Objective (L_G):** Comprises terms for mesh intersection (L_insec), as-rigid-as-possible (L_arap), normal consistency (L_n⃗), Laplacian smoothness (L_lap), and vertex offset regularization (L_offset) to ensure a physically plausible mesh.
- **Temporal Continuity:** The optimization processes batches of consecutive frames, incorporating a regularization term (L_temp) on the second-order derivative of hand joint positions to ensure smooth temporal transitions.
- **Depth Culling:** To handle partial occlusions by the touchpad, a pre-generated depth map of scene obstacles (D_o) is used to cull occluded parts of the rendered hand mesh, encouraging the hand-touchpad intersection to maintain lower mask IoU loss.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Evaluation

The research establishes two benchmarks using the EgoPressure dataset:

1. **Image-projected Pressure Baselines (Section 5.1):**
 - Evaluates PressureVisionNet on the EgoPressure dataset for both egocentric and exocentric views.
 - An extension to PressureVisionNet is proposed, incorporating an additional input channel for 2.5D hand keypoints (21 3D hand joints projected to image plane with depth). This is evaluated using both predicted (HaMeR) and ground-truth hand joints.
 - Metrics: Contact Intersection over Union (IoU), Volumetric IoU, Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Temporal Accuracy.
2. **Hand-projected Pressure Baseline (PressureFormer) (Section 5.2):**
 - Introduces PressureFormer, a novel model for jointly estimating 3D hand pose, hand mesh, and pressure as a UV map of the hand mesh, enabling projection onto the hand surface in 3D and back onto the image plane in 2D.
 - Architecture: Builds upon HaMeR by processing hand vertices and image feature tokens from HaMeR’s Vision Transformer (ViT). A transformer-based decoder maps features to a UV feature map, followed by convolutional layers to predict the quantized UV-pressure map.
 - Loss function: Combines coarse UV-pressure loss (L_c) between predicted and ground-truth UV maps, and pressure loss (L_p) between rendered pressure on the image plane and ground-truth pressure.
 - Metrics: Same as above, but extended to include UV map-based pressure metrics (UV Pressure Contact IoU, UV Pressure Volumetric IoU).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed findings regarding the EgoPressure dataset's characteristics, the quality of its annotations, and the performance of various baseline models for pressure and pose estimation.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics

- **Data Volume and Diversity:** EgoPressure comprises 4.3 million RGB-D frames (5 hours) from 21 participants performing 31 distinct gestures with both hands. This is noted as the first bare-handed egocentric pressure dataset with pose and mesh annotations.
- **Contact Information:** Approximately 45.1% of all frames capture the hand in contact with the pressure-sensitive pad. The dataset provides statistics on the ratio of contact frames per vertex and the maximum pressure measured per vertex (Figure 5b, 5c), demonstrating diverse contact patterns and pressure distributions.
- **Pose Diversity:** A t-SNE visualization (Figure 5a) illustrates the wide range of hand poses captured across different gestures.

#### 4.2. Annotation Fidelity Evaluation

The proposed multi-view, sequence-based optimization method yielded high-fidelity annotations:

- **Quantitative Improvement:** The annotation method significantly reduces Depth Volumetric IoU Loss and Mask IoU Loss (Table 5) from initial pose estimates (e.g., L_D reduced from 0.4443 to 0.1317, L_M from 0.3887 to 0.0558 after shape refinement).
- **3D Tip Error:** Compared to 3D tip positions triangulated from manual annotations, the method achieved a 3D tips error of 5.68 mm (Table 6). Ablation studies confirm that each loss term (appearance, depth, mesh intersection, rigidity, smoothness, normal consistency, offset regularization) contributes to the optimization performance.
- **Comparison to Learning-based Methods:** The annotation method produces robust and consistent hand poses across all camera views, outperforming state-of-the-art learning-based estimators like HaMeR in accuracy (e.g., 3D tips error of 5.68 mm for EgoPressure annotations vs. 12.37 mm for HaMeR triangulated predictions, Table 7). This highlights the value of optimization-based annotation for ground truth generation.

#### 4.3. Benchmark Evaluation Results

##### 4.3.1. Image-projected Pressure Baselines (Table 2)

- **Impact of Hand Pose:** Incorporating 2.5D hand keypoints as additional input to PressureVisionNet consistently improved pressure estimation performance.
 - Using HaMeR-estimated hand poses resulted in improvements over RGB-only input (e.g., Volumetric IoU increased by approximately 2% for egocentric views and 0.3% for exocentric views).
 - Using ground-truth hand poses led to further, more substantial improvements (e.g., Volumetric IoU increased by approximately 2.75% for egocentric views and 2.85% for exocentric views compared to RGB-only).
- **Generalization to Unseen Views:** Hand poses significantly enhanced the model's generalization. When trained on exocentric views (cameras 2,3,4,5) and evaluated on unseen views (cameras 1,6,7), Volumetric IoU improved from 25.05% (RGB only) to 28.10% (with predicted pose) and 31.39% (with GT pose), demonstrating over 6% improvement with ground-truth poses.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Qualitative results (Figure 8, 27, 28, 29) confirm that hand pose information aids in more accurate pressure and contact estimation, particularly for occluded hand parts.

##### 4.3.2. Hand-projected Pressure Baseline (PressureFormer) (Table 3, 4)

- **Joint Pose and Pressure Estimation:** PressureFormer is the first model to jointly estimate 3D hand pose, hand mesh, and pressure as a UV map of the hand, allowing 3D localization of pressure on the hand surface.
- **Performance:**
 - PressureFormer achieved the highest Contact IoU (43.04%) on the image-projected pressure map compared to PressureVisionNet baselines (40.71% and 42.52%).
 - On UV map-based pressure metrics, PressureFormer demonstrated superior performance with Contact IoU of 33.12% and Volumetric IoU of 24.54%, significantly outperforming image-based methods whose predictions were projected onto UV maps (e.g., PressureVisionNet with GT poses projected to UV map achieved 24.10% Contact IoU and 17.36% Volumetric IoU).
 - The inclusion of a coarse UV-pressure loss (L_c) was found to enhance the accuracy of UV map predictions (Table 4, Figure 13), preventing pressure predictions in non-contact areas.
- **Generalization to Diverse Objects:** Qualitative evaluations (Figure 15) suggest that PressureFormer, despite being trained on flat surfaces, demonstrates an ability to recognize pressure regions during contact events with unseen, more complex objects and environments, highlighting the potential of UV-pressure mapping for generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EgoPressure dataset and the accompanying annotation and benchmarking methodologies represent a notable contribution to the research on human hand-object interaction. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling Advanced Egocentric Vision Research:** By providing the first high-quality, bare-handed egocentric dataset with synchronized 3D hand poses, meshes, and detailed pressure information, EgoPressure facilitates the development of novel computer vision models that can interpret human interactions from a first-person perspective. This is particularly relevant for ubiquitous computing, wearable technologies, and mixed reality applications.
2. **Improving AR/VR Immersion and Interaction:** The availability of accurate pressure data, especially in 3D on the hand mesh, can lead to more intuitive and realistic user interfaces in AR/VR. This could enable more refined haptic feedback, allowing virtual objects to react more authentically to user input, such as varying audio feedback based on pressure applied to virtual instruments, or more precise manipulation of virtual objects.
3. **Advancing Robotic Dexterity and Control:** The detailed hand pose and pressure data can serve as a crucial resource for training robotic systems, particularly humanoid robots, to mimic human-like grasping and manipulation. Understanding the precise forces and contact areas involved in human touch can inform the design of tactile sensors and control algorithms for more dexterous and robust robotic manipulation in unstructured environments.
4. **Establishing Benchmarks and Fostering Future Research:** The introduced benchmarks for pressure estimation (both image-projected and hand-projected) provide a standardized framework for evaluating and comparing future models. The dataset's comprehensive nature, including multi-view RGB-D and egocentric data, encourages research into complex challenges such as estimating pressure on non-planar surfaces, handling occlusions, and generalizing to real-world settings with diverse backgrounds and objects. The qualitative results of PressureFormer on unseen objects indicate its potential for broader application beyond the flat-surface training data.
5. **Addressing Data Gaps with Marker-less Annotation:** The proposed marker-less annotation method provides a robust approach for generating high-fidelity ground truth data without interfering with natural human interaction, which is a persistent challenge in data collection for human pose and interaction. This method can potentially be adapted for annotating more complex hand-object interactions with varied geometries in future datasets.

In conclusion, EgoPressure is positioned to serve as a foundational resource, pushing the boundaries of machine understanding of touch and physical interaction, with direct implications for the development of more sophisticated AR/VR systems and advanced robotic capabilities.
