# Think Proprioceptively: State-Grounded Visual Token Selection for VLA Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ThinkProprio redefines the role of proprioception in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, transforming it from a passive conditioning signal into an active query for visual attention. This approach reduces visual token processing by 88% and inference latency by over 50%, while achieving higher success rates on long-horizon simulated and real-world robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current VLA models often treat proprioception as a passive, late conditioning signal, limiting its influence on instruction grounding or visual attention mechanisms.
- The method of proprioceptive integration is frequently entangled with other architectural choices in VLA designs, complicating isolated assessment of its specific impact.
- Existing methods struggle with efficiently selecting task- and state-relevant visual information from high-dimensional observations, leading to high computational costs during inference.

## Method

- **ThinkProprio** encodes raw proprioceptive states into ==VLM-vocabulary tokens== and integrates them early into the vision-language backbone, leveraging the VLM's native embedding capabilities.
- It implements an ==embodied visual token selector==, which uses both language and proprioceptive tokens as active query signals to dynamically gate and retain only task- and state-relevant visual patches prior to VLM processing.
- A ==diversity regularization loss== encourages complementary selection between the language and proprioception branches, and a ==global context token== is appended to selected visual tokens to preserve coarse scene understanding.

## Results

- Achieved an average completed chain length of **4.52** on CALVIN ABC→D, outperforming FLOWER and demonstrating a **15%** reduction in FLOWER's LH-5 failure rate.
- Reduced visual token processing by approximately **88%** (from **100 to ~12 tokens**) and inference latency by over **50%** (from **52 ms to 22 ms**) compared to FLOWER on CALVIN benchmarks.
- Demonstrated an **88.9%** overall success rate on a **14-task** real-world benchmark, surpassing FLOWER's **80.7%** and showing consistent performance gains across various manipulation tasks like drawer opening.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprioception, when encoded as VLM-vocabulary tokens and used as an active query early in the VLM pipeline, significantly improves manipulation performance and computational efficiency.
> - Language and proprioception serve as genuinely complementary query sources for visual attention; proprioception focuses on robot-centric and contact-relevant regions, while language addresses broader task objects and goals.
> - Effective visual token gating can substantially reduce the visual sequence length processed by the VLM and action head without performance degradation, provided a global context token compensates for potentially lost coarse scene information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which translate visual observations and natural language instructions into robot actions. These models leverage large-scale pretraining to enable robots to understand complex commands and perceive their environment. However, precise and robust manipulation, particularly in contact-rich scenarios, necessitates not only visual and linguistic understanding but also an awareness of the robot's own physical state, known as proprioception (e.g., joint configurations, end-effector pose).

Current VLA architectures typically incorporate proprioception as a late conditioning signal for the action head, or sometimes integrate it weakly with perception, or even omit it. This conventional approach often prevents proprioceptive information from actively grounding language instructions or directly influencing visual attention mechanisms within the VLA model. The functional role of proprioception in published systems is frequently entangled with other architectural choices, such as vision-language aggregation and action conditioning mechanisms, making it challenging to isolate and systematically assess its impact. Prior work has explored efficient visual-token selection and routing proprioception through the VLM token interface, but these aspects have generally been treated as separate developments.

This research addresses a specific design question within this landscape: whether robot state should primarily serve as a passive, late conditioning signal for action generation, or if it can function as an active participant in instruction grounding and visual attention. By investigating the encoding and entry point of proprioception, this work positions itself to refine VLA model design, aiming to improve both performance and computational efficiency by allowing proprioception to actively steer the model's visual processing. The paper contributes to the understanding of how multimodal inputs, specifically proprioception, can be more effectively integrated into large neural architectures for robotic control, moving beyond merely augmenting the action generation process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and redefine the role of proprioception within Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. The authors aim to move beyond conventional approaches where proprioception is typically treated as a passive, late-stage conditioning signal, exploring its potential as an active component in instruction understanding and visual attention.

The motivation for this investigation stems from several observations:
- **Requirement for Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Tasks involving physical contact (e.g., grasping, placing, opening drawers) are fundamentally dependent on the robot's embodiment and current configuration (proprioception), in addition to visual and linguistic cues. Existing VLA models often do not fully exploit this critical information.
- **Limitations of Passive Proprioception:** When proprioception is injected only as a late conditioning signal to the action head, it may not adequately influence how the VLM interprets instructions or directs its visual attention. This can lead to policies that fail to focus on action-relevant visual evidence or adapt their visual processing based on the robot's current state.
- **Entanglement in Current Architectures:** A review of existing VLA designs (Table 1) reveals that the method of proprioceptive integration is often coupled with other architectural choices (e.g., vision-language aggregation, action conditioning mechanism). This entanglement complicates efforts to isolate the specific functional benefits or drawbacks of different proprioceptive designs. The authors explicitly aim to disentangle these axes to systematically study the optimal encoding and entry point for proprioception.
- **Improving Efficiency and Performance:** The authors hypothesize that by actively involving proprioception in visual token selection, VLA models can become more efficient by discarding redundant visual tokens early in the processing pipeline. This early culling of irrelevant visual information is expected to reduce computational cost (inference latency, VRAM) while simultaneously improving manipulation performance by guiding the model toward task- and state-relevant visual evidence.

The research formulated three specific questions to guide its investigation:
1. **RQ1:** How should proprioception be represented and incorporated into a VLA policy? This addresses the optimal encoding (e.g., VLM-vocabulary tokens vs. learned projections) and entry point (e.g., late conditioning vs. early VLM input) for proprioceptive data.
2. **RQ2:** Can proprioception guide visual-token selection to retain task- and state-relevant evidence while reducing inference cost? This explores the feasibility and efficacy of using proprioception as an active query signal for visual attention.
3. **RQ3:** Do the resulting design choices improve manipulation performance and efficiency across simulation and real-world tasks? This evaluates the empirical impact of the proposed proprioceptive integration strategy on benchmark performance and computational resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces ThinkProprio, a VLA policy designed to integrate proprioception actively into the visual processing pipeline. The policy comprises a vision-language backbone and a separate action head.

**Proprioceptive State Encoding:**
ThinkProprio processes the raw proprioceptive state, which consists of multiple scalar values, by discretizing each value.
1. **Discretization:** Each scalar value q_t,k is uniformly binned over a clipped range [q_min, q_max] into B bins. The bin index b_t,k is calculated.
2. **Token ID Mapping:** Each bin index is mapped to a VLM-vocabulary token ID. A reverse mapping (V - 1 - b_t,k) is used to ensure these proprioceptive token IDs are disjoint from those generated by the language tokenizer.
3. **Embedding:** The corresponding embeddings H_q are retrieved from the VLM's existing token embedding table, thereby introducing no new embedding parameters and utilizing the native VLM lookup pathway.

**Embodied Visual Token Gating:**
A central component is the pre-VLM visual token selector, which uses two complementary guidance branches (language and proprioception) to gate visual patches. This selection occurs before the VLM processes the visual information.
1. **Branch-Specific Scoring:** For each branch (instruction and proprioception), normalized visual tokens (H_v) attend to their respective normalized guidance tokens (H_l or H_q) to generate context-dependent queries (Q_omega). These queries then score all visual tokens as candidates for retention, producing a branch-specific vote matrix S_omega ∈ R^(Nv × Nv). Each entry S_omega[i, j] quantifies how strongly visual token i (conditioned by guidance) votes for retaining visual token j.
2. **Vote-Based Gating with Straight-Through Relaxation:**
 - **Hard Forward Path:** During training, S_omega is perturbed with Gumbel noise. For each row i, a hard vote z_omega[i] = argmax(S_omega[i, j]) selects one candidate visual token. A token j is retained by a branch if it receives at least one vote (m_omega[j] = 1[∃ i : z_omega[i] = j]). The masks from the two branches are merged by a Boolean union: m[j] = m_lang[j] ∨ m_prop[j]. This union mask M is used at inference time and for the forward pass during training.
 - **Soft Relaxation for Backpropagation:** Parallel to the hard path, row-wise probabilities P_omega are computed via softmax. The probability that token j receives at least one vote within branch ω is calculated using a noisy-OR operation over rows: ρ_ω[j] = 1 - product(1 - P_ω[i, j]). Similarly, the aggregated soft probability across both branches is ρ[j] = 1 - (1 - ρ_lang[j]) * (1 - ρ_prop[j]).
 - **Straight-Through Gate:** The gate combines the hard union mask m[j] with the soft union probability ρ[j] using w[j] = m[j] + ρ[j] - sg(ρ[j]), where sg(·) is the stop-gradient operator. This ensures gradients flow through the soft probability ρ[j] while the forward pass uses the hard mask m[j].
3. **Diversity Regularization:** A diversity loss L_div is introduced to discourage the language and proprioception branches from selecting nearly identical visual token sets. It penalizes high cosine similarity between the softmax-normalized aggregated scores (r_omega) of the two branches, encouraging complementary selections.
4. **Global Context Token:** To preserve coarse, scene-level visual context that might not be strongly supported by individual token votes, a learned global context token H_ctx is appended to the selected visual tokens. This token is computed from the mean of the full pre-selection visual sequence.

**Training Objective:**
The VLM processes the compact multimodal sequence, which includes the retained visual tokens (H_Mv), the global context token (H_ctx), proprioceptive tokens (H_q), and language tokens (H_l), to generate conditioning features C. The action head, a rectified-flow / Diffusion Transformer, then predicts a continuous action chunk a_t:t+H conditioned on C. The model is trained end-to-end using a flow matching loss (L_fm) for action prediction, combined with the diversity regularizer (L = L_fm + λ_div L_div).

**Experimental Setup:**
ThinkProprio was evaluated across:
- **Simulation Benchmarks:** CALVIN (ABC→D, ABCD→D, D→D splits for long-horizon tasks) and LIBERO (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long suites for generalization).
- **Real-world Evaluation:** A 14-task benchmark involving a UR3 arm, a parallel gripper, and two RGB cameras (fixed third-person and wrist-mounted). Approximately 120 minutes of teleoperated demonstrations were collected using a customized GELLO-style interface.
- **Backbone:** Florence-2-Large, fine-tuned rather than frozen, consistent with FLOWER's approach.
- **Baselines:** A range of strong VLA models including OpenVLA, π0, π0.5, FLOWER, LightVLA, and others, as well as visual planning methods.
- **Hyperparameters:** Standard AdamW optimizer, tri-stage learning rate schedule, bf16-mixed precision, EMA, Gumbel scale annealing (1.0 to 0.01), and diversity loss weight (λ_div = 5×10^-4) and margin (γ = 0.75).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ThinkProprio yielded several key findings regarding its performance, efficiency, and the role of its design components:

**Performance:**
- **CALVIN ABC→D:** ThinkProprio achieved an average completed chain length of 4.52, outperforming FLOWER (4.44). It exhibited stronger performance at longer horizons (LH-3 to LH-5), reaching 86.4% at LH-4 and 79.1% at LH-5, which represented a reduction in FLOWER’s LH-5 failure rate by approximately 15%. This suggests that state-conditioned gating assists in retaining relevant visual evidence as the robot's configuration evolves over extended task sequences.
- **CALVIN ABCD→D & D→D:** ThinkProprio continued to demonstrate strong performance on these more challenging splits, achieving the best average length (4.74) and LH-5 result (88.5%) on ABCD→D, and exceeding prior baselines on D→D.
- **LIBERO:** ThinkProprio achieved the best overall success rate (97.7%), tying on LIBERO-Spatial and leading on LIBERO-Object and LIBERO-Long suites.
- **Real-world Evaluation:** ThinkProprio achieved an overall success rate of 88.9% compared to FLOWER’s 80.7%. It matched or exceeded FLOWER’s performance on 13 of 14 tasks, showing consistent gains across different objects and task categories (e.g., drawer manipulation showed uniform improvement).

**Computational Efficiency:**
- **Token Budget Reduction:** ThinkProprio retained approximately 12 of 100 visual tokens per step on average for CALVIN, significantly reducing the visual sequence length processed by the VLM and action head. For LIBERO, it retained about 7.8 of 34 tokens.
- **Inference Latency:** Despite the overhead of the visual token selector (~0.1 ms), ThinkProprio achieved lower end-to-end inference latency (22 ms on CALVIN, 23.8 ms on LIBERO) compared to FLOWER (52 ms). This reduction was primarily attributed to the VLM and action head processing shorter visual sequences.
- **VRAM Usage:** Peak VRAM usage for ThinkProprio grew marginally over FLOWER but remained significantly lower than other VLA models like OpenVLA.

**Token Selection Analysis:**
- **Qualitative Behavior:** Visualizations showed that retained tokens dynamically tracked task-relevant objects, targets, and robot–object interaction regions. Instruction-selected tokens covered objects and goal areas, while proprioception-selected tokens focused on the gripper and immediate contact areas. Overlap was highest during interaction moments.
- **Complementary Query Sources:** The overlap between tokens selected by the language and proprioception branches was consistently low (below one token across all benchmarks). This indicates that the proprioception branch contributes genuinely complementary evidence, rather than merely re-selecting language-grounded objects.
- **Selection Consistency and Adaptation:** The Intersection-over-Union (IoU) between retained-token masks was consistently above random baselines, confirming that selection is task- and state-conditioned. Phase-wise analysis on LIBERO-Spatial revealed that the selector is dynamic, with mean token count peaking during grasping/lifting (most visually dynamic phase) and masks showing significant drift across task phases.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Proprioceptive Integration:**
 - Injecting proprioception as VLM-vocabulary tokens, exposed to the visual selector, was the only method that preserved or improved performance.
 - Late action-head modulation of proprioception or projecting continuous state with an MLP into the VLM input either left baseline performance unchanged or degraded it, respectively.
- **Component Contribution:**
 - VLM-vocabulary proprioceptive tokens alone yielded modest but consistent gains.
 - Visual token selection, when implemented in isolation, slightly reduced performance, suggesting loss of coarse scene information.
 - Adding the global context token (H_ctx) recovered this performance loss.
 - The diversity loss (L_div) further improved results by encouraging complementary selection between branches.
- **Query Signal for Retention:**
 - Instruction-only or proprioception-only guidance for token selection resulted in substantially worse performance compared to their combination, underscoring the necessity of both signals for comprehensive visual grounding.
- **Proprioceptive Discretization:** The choice of bin resolution and clipping range for proprioceptive tokenization was found to be sensitive, with 256 bins over a [-3, 3] range performing optimally in the tested configurations.

**Qualitative Failure Cases:**
Real-world failures often involved control-side or contact-side limitations, such as misaligned gripper approach or accidental collision with nearby objects, even when the target object was visually identified. This suggests that while visual attention was effective, downstream control challenges persisted.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Think Proprioceptively" carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation and VLA model design:

**Redefining Proprioception's Role:** The most significant contribution is the re-conceptualization of proprioception from a passive conditioning signal to an active query signal that, alongside language, directs visual attention. This shift enables VLA models to ground instructions and perceive their environment with a more embodied awareness, which is crucial for complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks.

**Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** By actively guiding visual attention, ThinkProprio demonstrates improved performance on long-horizon, multi-stage tasks in simulation (CALVIN) and generalization tasks (LIBERO). The consistent gains, particularly at longer horizons and in real-world scenarios, suggest that state-grounded visual selection can lead to more robust policies that better handle accumulation of configuration drift and dynamically changing interaction contexts.

**Increased Computational Efficiency:** A major impact is the substantial reduction in end-to-end inference latency and visual-token budget (e.g., 12% of visual tokens retained on CALVIN), without compromising performance. This makes VLA models more computationally efficient and thus more practical for deployment on real robot systems where real-time responsiveness and limited computational resources are often critical constraints. The minimal overhead of the selector component further supports this.

**Improved Understanding of Multimodal Integration:** The systematic ablations provide valuable insights into optimal design choices for integrating proprioception. The finding that VLM-vocabulary tokens, rather than learned projections, are most effective when coupled with early VLM entry points, helps guide future architectural decisions in VLA models. The demonstrated complementarity of language and proprioception in visual selection further illuminates how diverse sensory inputs can contribute uniquely to a robot's understanding.

**Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for further exploration:
- **Broader Generalization:** Extending real-world evaluation to a wider range of embodiments, object categories, and environmental complexities beyond rigid tabletop objects.
- **Higher Visual Resolutions:** Addressing the O(N^2_v D) cost of the selector's vote matrix, which could become a bottleneck at higher visual resolutions. This may involve exploring more efficient token-selection mechanisms.
- **Interpretability and Control:** Enhancing the selector to consistently preserve human-interpretable, action-relevant visual evidence, potentially through explicit relevance signals rather than solely relying on the action objective. Furthermore, addressing the observed control-side and contact-side limitations in real-world failures remains crucial for full task success.
- **Dynamic Attention in Other Modalities:** The principles of state-grounded, active attention could potentially be extended to other modalities or stages of VLA processing, leading to more adaptive and efficient models.

In summary, ThinkProprio offers a validated architectural modification that significantly refines the role of proprioception in VLA models, contributing to more capable and efficient robotic manipulation systems.
