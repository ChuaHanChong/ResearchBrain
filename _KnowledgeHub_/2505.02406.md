# Token Coordinated Prompt Attention is Needed for Visual Prompting

> [!summary] Summary
> A Token Coordinated Prompt Attention (TCPA) module is proposed to enhance visual prompting for Vision Transformers by assigning specific, coordinated prompts to different token types and individual image tokens. This approach leads to the extraction of more discriminative and diverse features, resulting in consistent performance improvements across a wide range of visual classification benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing token-level visual prompting methods apply uniform prompts to all tokens, disregarding the distinct functionalities of CLS (classification) and image tokens.
- This indiscriminate application leads to redundant attention patterns and "indistinguishable and biased prompt-extracted features" due to the low-rank nature of self-attention.
- The uniform approach limits the Vision Transformer's representational capacity and its ability to capture comprehensive and diverse discriminative information for downstream tasks.

## Method

- Introduces ==Token Coordinated Prompt Attention (TCPA)==, a plug-and-play module that disentangles prompts based on token functionalities for attention-based interactions.
- Employs ==R-TCPA== to use separate, exclusive prompt sets for CLS and image tokens.
- Utilizes ==T-TCPA== by employing prompt pools and a matching function to assign appropriate prompts to individual tokens, then modifies the attention mechanism with a binarized mask to enforce token-specific interactions.

## Results

- TCPA improved average accuracy by **1.2%** to **2.0%** when integrated with existing visual prompting methods (VP, VPT, DAMVP, AutoVP, VFPT) across various datasets in the HTA benchmark.
- Consistent performance gains of **0.9%** to **2.1%** were observed across all categories of the VTAB benchmark (Natural, Specialized, Structured) for various base prompting methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both disentangling CLS/image prompts (R-TCPA) and coordinating individual image token prompts (T-TCPA) contributed to performance improvements with minimal additional computational cost (e.g., **0.05-0.13** seconds per epoch).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The distinct roles of CLS and image tokens, as well as varying information in different image tokens, necessitate token-specific prompt interactions for effective visual prompting.
> - Existing methods' uniform prompt application leads to redundant attention and limits the discriminative power of extracted features.
> - Disentangling prompts and coordinating their interaction with specific tokens can significantly enhance feature diversity and classification performance while maintaining computational efficiency.
