# Token Coordinated Prompt Attention is Needed for Visual Prompting

> [!summary] Summary
> A Token Coordinated Prompt Attention (TCPA) module is proposed to enhance visual prompting for Vision Transformers by assigning specific, coordinated prompts to different token types and individual image tokens. This approach leads to the extraction of more discriminative and diverse features, resulting in consistent performance improvements across a wide range of visual classification benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing token-level visual prompting methods apply uniform prompts to all tokens, disregarding the distinct functionalities of CLS (classification) and image tokens.
- This indiscriminate application leads to redundant attention patterns and "indistinguishable and biased prompt-extracted features" due to the low-rank nature of self-attention.
- The uniform approach limits the Vision Transformer's representational capacity and its ability to capture comprehensive and diverse discriminative information for downstream tasks.

## Method

- Introduces ==Token Coordinated Prompt Attention (TCPA)==, a plug-and-play module that disentangles prompts based on token functionalities for attention-based interactions.
- Employs ==R-TCPA== to use separate, exclusive prompt sets for CLS and image tokens.
- Utilizes ==T-TCPA== by employing prompt pools and a matching function to assign appropriate prompts to individual tokens, then modifies the attention mechanism with a binarized mask to enforce token-specific interactions.

## Results

- TCPA improved average accuracy by **1.2%** to **2.0%** when integrated with existing visual prompting methods (VP, VPT, DAMVP, AutoVP, VFPT) across various datasets in the HTA benchmark.
- Consistent performance gains of **0.9%** to **2.1%** were observed across all categories of the VTAB benchmark (Natural, Specialized, Structured) for various base prompting methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both disentangling CLS/image prompts (R-TCPA) and coordinating individual image token prompts (T-TCPA) contributed to performance improvements with minimal additional computational cost (e.g., **0.05-0.13** seconds per epoch).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The distinct roles of CLS and image tokens, as well as varying information in different image tokens, necessitate token-specific prompt interactions for effective visual prompting.
> - Existing methods' uniform prompt application leads to redundant attention and limits the discriminative power of extracted features.
> - Disentangling prompts and coordinating their interaction with specific tokens can significantly enhance feature diversity and classification performance while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of **parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)** for large pre-trained models, specifically **Vision Transformers (ViT)**. The core challenge in modern deep learning is adapting massive pre-trained models, such as ViTs, to diverse downstream tasks without incurring prohibitive computational and storage costs associated with full fine-tuning.

Historically, the pretraining-finetuning paradigm has been highly successful. However, with the exponential growth in model and dataset sizes, full fine-tuning becomes impractical. PEFT methods address this by only updating a small subset of parameters or introducing minimal additional learnable components. This field broadly categorizes into:
- **Partial tuning-based methods:** Fine-tuning only a portion of the pre-trained model's parameters (e.g., specific layers, biases).
- **Extra module-based approaches:** Adding small, learnable modules (like adapters) to the pre-trained backbone.
- **Prompt learning-based approaches:** Introducing a small set of learnable "prompts" that guide the pre-trained model to better adapt to specific tasks without modifying the original model weights.

Visual prompting, a sub-field of prompt learning, has emerged as a particularly promising PEFT strategy for Vision Transformers. Existing visual prompting methods typically fall into two main categories:
1. **Input-level prompting:** Learnable prompts are overlaid directly onto the input image, adjusting the model's input to guide its behavior (e.g., VP, DAMVP). These methods are architecture-agnostic but may have limited representational capacity as prompts are not used in intermediate layers.
2. **Token-level prompting:** Learnable tokens are introduced into the transformer's token sequence at various layers, interacting with the original image tokens and CLS tokens via self-attention mechanisms (e.g., VPT, E²VPT). These methods continuously influence feature extraction throughout the network.

The paper highlights a critical limitation of *existing token-level visual prompting methods*: they "learn and leverage the same prompt for all tokens without considering the different functionalities of CLS and image tokens, as well as the varying discriminative information conveyed by different image tokens." This uniform application of prompts leads to redundant attention patterns, where different tokens focus on similar regions, resulting in "indistinguishable and biased prompt-extracted features," thereby limiting the ViT's representational capacity. This limitation is theoretically supported by the "low-rank" nature of self-attention, suggesting that existing prompts guide attention to only a few dominant patterns.

"Token Coordinated Prompt Attention (TCPA)" directly addresses this gap by introducing token-specific, coordinated prompt interactions. It aims to make visual prompting more effective by aligning prompts with the specific needs and roles of individual tokens (CLS vs. image tokens, and even different image tokens). This work aims to push the boundaries of prompt learning by moving beyond static, shared prompts towards dynamic, token-aware prompt interactions, enhancing the discriminative power and diversity of features extracted by ViTs during fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the representational capacity and discriminative power of Vision Transformers (ViT) when fine-tuned using visual prompting techniques**, specifically by addressing the limitations of existing methods that use generic, shared prompts.

The core motivations are:

1. **Acknowledging Token-Specific Roles:** The authors recognize that within a Vision Transformer, different types of tokens serve distinct purposes. The **CLS (classification) token** is primarily responsible for aggregating global information and facilitating final classification, while **image tokens** (derived from image patches) are dedicated to extracting local, discriminative features. Existing visual prompting methods largely ignore this functional distinction, applying the same prompts to all tokens, which can hinder their specialized roles.
2. **Addressing Lack of Feature Diversity:** Even among image tokens, which correspond to different image patches, the information they need to extract varies significantly. Using a single set of prompts for all image tokens can lead to them focusing on similar aspects or regions, resulting in "indistinguishable and biased prompt-extracted features." This limits the model's ability to capture comprehensive and diverse discriminative information from an image.
3. **Overcoming Low-Rank Attention:** The paper theoretically grounds its motivation by referring to the low-rank nature of self-attention mechanisms, particularly after prompting. This implies that even with prompts, the attention matrix tends to focus on a limited set of patterns, leading to redundancy. The goal is to encourage more diverse and targeted attention interactions.
4. **Improving Performance and Efficiency:** The ultimate motivation is to develop a method that significantly boosts the performance of existing visual prompting techniques across various downstream tasks while maintaining their inherent efficiency (minimal additional learnable parameters and computational overhead).

In summary, the paper aims to introduce a more intelligent and granular way for prompts to interact with ViT tokens, moving from a "one-size-fits-all" approach to a "token-coordinated" strategy that respects the unique functions and information content of different tokens, thereby unlocking greater representational power for efficient fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel plug-and-play module called **Token Coordinated Prompt Attention (TCPA)**, designed to assign specific, coordinated prompts to different tokens for attention-based interactions within Vision Transformers. The methodology involves two key levels of prompt disentanglement and a modified attention mechanism.

#### 3.1. Coordinated Prompt Attention of CLS and Image Tokens (R-TCPA)

Recognizing the distinct roles of CLS tokens (global information aggregation) and image tokens (local feature extraction), TCPA first disentangles the prompts into two separate sets:
- **CLS Prompts ($P_c = \{p_{c_j}\}_{j=1}^L$):** A set of learnable prompts specifically designed to interact with the CLS token in each of the $L$ Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) blocks.
- **Image Prompts ($P_i = \{p_{i_j}\}_{j=1}^L$):** A set of learnable prompts dedicated to interacting with the image tokens in each MSA block.

Crucially, the interaction is *exclusive*. When the CLS token ($c_j$) and its corresponding CLS prompt ($p_{c_j}$) are fed into an MSA block along with image tokens, only the output CLS token ($c_{j+1}$) is retained for subsequent layers. The outputs corresponding to the image tokens that resulted from this CLS-prompted attention are discarded. Conversely, when image tokens ($h^j_1, \dots, h^j_N$) and their corresponding image prompt ($p_{i_j}$) are fed into the MSA block along with the CLS token, only the outputs corresponding to the image tokens ($h^{j+1}_1, \dots, h^{j+1}_N$) are retained. The outputs related to the CLS token in this context are discarded. This ensures that CLS prompts *only* influence CLS tokens and image prompts *only* influence image tokens, allowing them to better fulfill their respective functions.

#### 3.2. Coordinated Prompt Attention of Different Image Tokens (T-TCPA)

Further refining the approach, TCPA acknowledges that different image tokens (corresponding to distinct image patches) contain diverse information and therefore require different prompts. To address this, the CLS prompts ($p_c$) and image prompts ($p_i$) are further disentangled into **prompt pools**:
- **CLS Prompt Pool ($P_c = \{(p_{c_k}, \kappa_{c_k})\}_{k=1}^{N_c}$):** A pool of $N_c$ distinct CLS prompts, each with a learnable indicator $\kappa_{c_k}$.
- **Image Prompt Pool ($P_i = \{(p_{i_k}, \kappa_{i_k})\}_{k=1}^{N_i}$):** A pool of $N_i$ distinct image prompts, each with a learnable indicator $\kappa_{i_k}$.

For each individual token (CLS or image token), TCPA employs a **matching function** to automatically assign appropriate prompts from its respective pool. The matching is based on the cosine distance (or affinity) between the token's feature representation and the learnable indicator of each prompt in the pool. For an image token $h^j_m$ and an image prompt indicator $\kappa_{i_k}$, the affinity is calculated as $S(h^j_m, \kappa_{i_k}) = 1 - \cos(h^j_m, \kappa_{i_k})$.

This process yields an affinity matrix $A$ (e.g., between $N$ image tokens and $N_i$ image prompts). A **binarization step** is then applied: for each token (row in $A$), the top $K_i$ (or $K_c$ for CLS token) prompts with the highest affinity are selected, represented by setting their corresponding entries in a binary mask $\hat{A}$ to 1, and others to 0. This binarized mask is then dimensionally aligned with the attention map to create a final mask $M$.

#### 3.3. Modified Attention Mechanism

In the standard Vision Transformer attention module, the attention map is computed as $Attn = \text{Softmax}(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}})$. TCPA modifies this by performing an element-wise multiplication between the original attention map and the newly generated binary mask $M$:
$Attn' = Attn \odot M$.
This mask selectively suppresses attention weights for irrelevant token-prompt interactions while allowing relevant ones to pass through. This effectively guides each token to interact only with its assigned, coordinated prompts.

#### 3.4. Overall Optimization and Efficiency

TCPA introduces only a few additional learnable parameters: the CLS prompt pool $P_c$ and the image prompt pool $P_i$. The pre-trained ViT encoder remains frozen, with only the classification head and the new prompt parameters being trainable. The optimization objective includes a standard cross-entropy loss and regularization terms that encourage lower cosine distance (higher affinity) between tokens and their selected prompts.

A critical design choice for efficiency is that prompt selection (cosine distance calculation) is primarily through vector multiplication, incurring minimal overhead. Furthermore, the attention weights for all tokens and all prompts are computed *once* ($Attn = \text{Softmax}(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}})$). The token-specific interaction is then achieved by applying the binary mask $M$ (element-wise multiplication) to this computed attention map *before* multiplying by the value matrix $V$ (i.e., $Attn' \cdot V$). This avoids multiple, separate attention computations, ensuring that TCPA remains computationally efficient and plug-and-play.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate that TCPA consistently and significantly enhances the performance of existing state-of-the-art visual prompting methods across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance Improvements on HTA Benchmark

- Evaluated on ten diverse datasets (DTD, CUB, NABirds, Stanford Dogs, Oxford Flowers, Food101, Cifar100, Cifar10, GTSRB, SVHN) using ViT-B/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-21k.
- When integrated with DAMVP, DAMVP+TCPA showed an average accuracy improvement of **1.4%** across all ten datasets.
- Similar consistent improvements were observed when TCPA was applied to other prompting methods:
 - VP+TCPA: Average increase of **2.0%** (ranging from 0.9% to 2.8%).
 - VPT+TCPA: Average increase of **1.5%** (ranging from 0.2% to 2.2%).
 - AutoVP+TCPA: Average increase of **1.8%** (ranging from 0.6% to 3.1%).
 - VFPT+TCPA: Average increase of **1.2%** (ranging from 0.5% to 2.0%).
- These results highlight TCPA's ability to learn more task-specific and discriminative features by explicitly disentangling prompts based on token roles and enabling coordinated interactions.

#### 4.2. Performance Improvements on VTAB Benchmark

- Experiments on the VTAB benchmark, comprising 19 visual tasks categorized into Natural, Specialized, and Structured groups, further validated TCPA's robustness.
- VPT+TCPA showed improvements of **1.2%** (Natural), **1.9%** (Specialized), and **1.2%** (Structured) compared to base VPT.
- TCPA consistently enhanced VP, DAMVP, and AutoVP across these categories, demonstrating its broad applicability and effectiveness across diverse task types. For example, DAMVP+TCPA improved by **1.3%** (Natural), **2.1%** (Specialized), and **0.9%** (Structured).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Influence of Different Components:** The ablation studies confirmed the contribution of each proposed component:
 - Introducing **R-TCPA** (Coordinated Prompt Attention of CLS and Image Tokens) alone yielded a performance increase of **0.8%-0.9%**. This validates the benefit of disentangling CLS and image prompts.
 - Further incorporating **T-TCPA** (Coordinated Prompt Attention of Different Image Tokens) led to an additional **0.6%-1.1%** improvement, demonstrating the value of token-specific prompt selection from pools.
- **Computational Cost:** TCPA introduces only a minimal additional time cost. For example, on the CUB dataset, VPT+TCPA adds only 0.05 seconds per epoch compared to base VPT (2.36s vs 2.41s), and DAMVP+TCPA adds 0.13 seconds (2.14s vs 2.27s). This efficiency is crucial for PEFT methods.
- **Influence of Hyper-parameters:** Experiments on the size of the CLS prompt pool ($N_c$) and image prompt pool ($N_i$) showed that optimal performance is achieved with a moderate number of prompts. Too few prompts limit diversity, while too many can lead to overfitting due to limited downstream data. The image prompt pool generally requires a larger size than the CLS prompt pool, reflecting the greater variability in image token information.

#### 4.4. Feature Visualization (t-SNE)

- t-SNE visualizations of extracted features (e.g., on GTSRB, CUB, Cifar100, SVHN) compellingly demonstrated the effectiveness of TCPA.
- Compared to DAMVP, features extracted by TCPA from the same category were **tightly clustered**, and distinct categories were **clearly separated**. In contrast, DAMVP's features often appeared more scattered and intermixed. This visual evidence strongly supports the claim that TCPA enables the extraction of more diverse, comprehensive, and discriminative features.

In essence, the findings consistently validate TCPA's central hypothesis: by coordinating prompts specifically for different token types and even individual tokens, the model can learn more nuanced and effective representations, leading to significant performance gains without sacrificing computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Token Coordinated Prompt Attention (TCPA) module presents a significant advancement in the field of visual prompting and parameter-efficient fine-tuning for Vision Transformers. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Fundamental Improvement to Visual Prompting:** TCPA addresses a core limitation of existing visual prompting methods: their indiscriminate application of shared prompts to all tokens. By explicitly acknowledging and leveraging the distinct roles of CLS and image tokens, and further differentiating among image tokens, TCPA fundamentally improves how prompts interact with the model's internal representations. This shift from generic to token-coordinated prompting allows for a more granular and effective adaptation process.

2. **Enhanced Feature Discriminability and Diversity:** The experimental results, particularly the t-SNE visualizations, conclusively show that TCPA leads to the extraction of features that are more discriminative and diverse. This translates directly to improved classification performance across a wide range of tasks and datasets. The ability to capture richer semantic information from input images is critical for robust and accurate computer vision systems.

3. **Plug-and-Play Compatibility:** TCPA's design as a "plug-and-play" module is a major strength. It can be readily integrated into various existing visual prompting methods (both input-level and token-level) to boost their performance, as demonstrated by its consistent improvements across VP, VPT, DAMVP, AutoVP, and VFPT. This high compatibility facilitates its adoption by the broader research and development community.

4. **Computational Efficiency:** Despite its sophisticated token-aware prompt coordination, TCPA introduces only minimal additional computational overhead and a few extra learnable parameters. This efficiency is paramount for adapting large-scale models in resource-constrained environments or for applications requiring rapid deployment. TCPA retains the core advantage of PEFT while significantly enhancing its effectiveness.

5. **Theoretical Backing and Practical Validation:** The paper provides theoretical justification for the problem (low-rank nature of self-attention in existing prompting methods) and offers clear experimental validation that TCPA effectively mitigates this issue, leading to more diverse attention patterns. This combination of theoretical insight and comprehensive empirical evidence strengthens the contribution.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Wider Adoption of Efficient Fine-Tuning:** By offering a more powerful yet efficient method, TCPA can accelerate the adoption of PEFT techniques for Vision Transformers in various real-world applications, especially those requiring rapid deployment and adaptation to new visual tasks without needing to retrain massive models from scratch.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** TCPA's success in token-aware prompt interaction could inspire further research into fine-grained control mechanisms for large models, moving beyond simple input or token additions towards more intelligent, adaptive interaction strategies based on the internal workings of transformer architectures.
- **Applications in Diverse Domains:** The robust performance across different VTAB categories (Natural, Specialized, Structured) suggests that TCPA could be highly beneficial in diverse domains such as medical imaging, remote sensing, and industrial inspection, where data might be limited and efficient adaptation of powerful pre-trained models is crucial.
- **Democratization of Large Models:** By making large ViTs more accessible and adaptable to specific tasks with limited resources, TCPA contributes to democratizing the use of advanced AI models, enabling smaller teams or researchers with fewer computational resources to leverage the power of these models effectively.

In conclusion, TCPA represents a significant step forward in making visual prompting more effective and intelligent. By tailoring prompt interactions to the specific roles and information content of individual tokens, it unlocks greater representational capacity from pre-trained Vision Transformers, offering a robust, efficient, and versatile solution for adapting these powerful models to a myriad of downstream tasks.
