# Think Fast, Plan Selectively: Adaptive Deliberation for Efficient Data-Driven MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast-TD-MPC speeds up data-driven model predictive control by choosing, at every step, between a fast policy and a full planner. The fast policy is a small MLP that imitates TD-MPC2's MPPI planner. A Mahalanobis-distance out-of-distribution (OOD) gate on world-model latents calls the full planner only for unfamiliar states. Across 103 continuous-control tasks, inference is up to about 4x faster, with under 10% performance loss on three of four domains.

## Problem

- Data-driven MPC planners like TD-MPC2 sample and score hundreds of trajectories every step, so each decision is too slow for real-time control and actions go stale.
- Existing test-time planners spend the same compute budget on every timestep, even though most familiar states need no deliberation to choose a reliable action.
- Prior speedups either still run MPPI at every step (distillation, parallel rollouts) or replace it entirely with a feed-forward policy that has no fallback for out-of-distribution states.

## Method

- ==System 1 amortized policy==: a compact MLP trained with an ==L1 imitation loss== on TD-MPC2 planner rollouts in latent space, so one forward pass replaces iterative ==MPPI== optimization.
- ==OOD gate==: ==Mahalanobis distance== from the encoder latent to System-1 rollout statistics, with shrinkage-regularized covariance, two ways of labeling in-distribution states (all visited, or reward-gated), and a percentile threshold.
- System 2 fallback: the frozen TD-MPC2 MPPI planner, called only when the gate fires; its warm-start buffer is cleared on each switch, forcing a cold start from the policy prior.

## Results

- At the 90th-percentile threshold, inference is about **2.7x** faster on average (**4.2x** on DMControl) with under **10%** drop on three of four domains; the median threshold matches TD-MPC2 (**829** vs **820**).
- With all five disturbance types applied at once, the method keeps **95%** of TD-MPC2's DMControl return (**586** vs **616**) at **2x** speed, and planner use rises from **18%** to **45%**.
- State-aware gating beats fixed round-robin scheduling on DMControl at equal latency (**785** vs **743** at about **6.5** ms), but round-robin matches or beats it on MyoSuite and Meta-World.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Most control steps are routine, so a distilled reactive policy can handle them; deliberation should be spent only on novel states, not uniformly at every step.
> - A cheap novelty score in latent space can route compute: latency then scales linearly with how often the planner runs, giving an explicit dial between speed and robustness.
> - Gating pays off only when few states need planning; when difficulty is spread out, a fixed schedule does as well, and a reactive gate notices trouble only after it arrives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Model-predictive control (MPC) lets a controller use a learned model of the environment to evaluate possible action sequences before acting. That deliberation can make decisions more robust. However, sampling and scoring hundreds of trajectories at every step is slow, and the resulting action may be stale if the environment changes before it is applied.

Fast-TD-MPC trains a quick policy to imitate the planner on familiar states. A lightweight detector then calls the original planner when the current state looks unfamiliar. The planner is not removed; it becomes a fallback. On DMControl at the authors’ p90 threshold, Fast-TD-MPC’s mean return is **785 ± 58**, versus **820 ± 55** for full TD-MPC2. Mean per-step latency is **6.5 ± 2.0 ms**, versus **27.3 ± 1.1 ms**. That makes it about **4.2× faster**, with 96% of the baseline return. This is a simulation result and one point on a speed–performance trade-off.

Per-step latency (ms) against DMControl return and against success rate (%) for Meta-World, MyoSuite, and ManiSkill2. Threshold settings trace Fast-TD-MPC frontiers: red for the theoretical “all” OOD mode and purple for the reward-gated (RG) mode. The dashed horizontal line marks 90% of TD-MPC2 performance.

### 2. How the gate routes each decision

The starting point is **TD-MPC2**. Its MPPI planner repeatedly samples candidate action sequences, rolls them through its learned latent world model, and evaluates them. It then executes the first action of the selected sequence. Fast-TD-MPC leaves that world model and planner intact and adds an imitation-trained fast policy and a gate.

At each step, the controller first encodes the current state into a latent representation. It then uses Mahalanobis distance to score how far that representation lies from the familiar-state distribution. Here, “out of distribution” means unlike the latent states used to establish the familiar-state reference. If the score is below a threshold, a small feed-forward policy trained to imitate planner actions produces the action. Otherwise, TD-MPC2’s MPPI planner does. The threshold sets the speed–robustness operating point. The authors call the fast policy “System 1” and the planner “System 2.” This is their framing for the engineering design.

The proposed architecture. A state enters an encoder and becomes a latent state. An ID/OOD decision routes in-distribution states to System 1, a compact neural network, and out-of-distribution states to System 2, which evaluates candidate latent paths by reward and value. Both paths return an action to the environment.

### 3. What the comparisons establish

The DMControl figures above are means with 95% confidence intervals across 39 tasks, with three seeds per task. The speedup is lower elapsed per-step inference latency, which comes from invoking the planner less often. At p90, performance is within 10% of TD-MPC2 on DMControl, Meta-World, and MyoSuite. ManiSkill2 is the exception: its mean success is **32% versus 65%**.

The strongest mechanism test compares the gate with fixed scheduling. Fixed scheduling invokes the same planner at a set rate without regard to state. On DMControl, the p90 gate’s mean is **785 return at 6.5 ms**. Round-robin planning on 20% of steps reaches **743 at 6.6 ms**. This supports the authors’ interpretation that choosing *which* steps to plan can help. However, the paper reports no significance test for this pair, and the confidence intervals overlap.

The pattern also varies by domain. ManiSkill2 is comparable at intermediate latency, and round-robin does better at the high-latency end. On MyoSuite, round-robin at 10% planning reaches TD-MPC2’s 40% success at 6.6 ms, while the gate still plans on 60% of steps at p90.

The authors also tested disturbances applied together during evaluation. Under this combined setting at p90, DMControl return is **586 ± 101 at 13.6 ± 3.0 ms**, versus TD-MPC2’s **616 ± 104 at 27.2 ± 1.1 ms**. The authors report this as 95% retention at about half the latency. This shows robustness in simulation, not real-world validation.

### 4. Spend Compute Where Needed

A fast policy can stand in for repeated planning on familiar states. The original planner remains available when the latent-state score crosses a threshold. The evidence supports a controllable speed–performance trade-off in simulation. It does not show a universal win or demonstrated real-robot performance. The gate is reactive, and real-robot validation is deferred to future work.
