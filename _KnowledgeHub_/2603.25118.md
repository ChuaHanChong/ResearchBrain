# AnyDoc: Enhancing Document Generation via Large-Scale HTML/CSS Data Synthesis and Height-Aware Reinforcement Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> AnyDoc is a framework that leverages HTML/CSS as a unified, editable document representation, alongside a large-scale synthesized dataset (DocHTML) and height-aware reinforcement learning, to enhance automated document generation across multiple tasks while addressing content overflow.

## Problem

- Existing document generation methods produce either non-editable raster images or struggle with structural complexity using flat coordinate sequences.
- There is a persistent scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse datasets for training robust document generation models.
- Generative models frequently produce documents with elements that overflow beyond specified boundaries, reducing their practical usability.

## Method

- Utilizes ==HTML/CSS== as a unified, hierarchical, and fully editable document representation, leveraging its structural and styling capabilities (e.g., flexbox, grid).
- Develops an automated data synthesis pipeline to create DocHTML, a large-scale (265,206 samples), diverse dataset of HTML/CSS documents with comprehensive metadata.
- Fine-tune a Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) on DocHTML to handle Intention-to-Document, Document Derendering, and Element-to-Document tasks, followed by Height-Aware Reinforcement Learning (HARL) to minimize content overflow.

## Results

- AnyDoc achieved competitive or superior performance across all three document generation tasks (I2D, DD, E2D), outperforming general MLLMs like GPT-4o and specialized baselines on various quality metrics.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the HTML/CSS representation significantly reduced element overlap (e.g., Overlap metric of **0.0494**), while HARL substantially decreased height deviation (from **0.392** to **0.085**).
- The DocHTML dataset, comprising 265,206 samples covering 111 categories and 32 styles, proved effective for training, enabling strong model performance even with smaller data subsets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - HTML/CSS offers a robust and editable representation for document generation, outperforming raster images and flat coordinate sequences in handling structural complexity and ensuring post-generation utility.
> - Automated data synthesis using MLLMs and code generation models can effectively overcome data scarcity, providing high-quality and diverse datasets for training advanced generative models.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically tailored with height-aware reward functions, is effective in mitigating practical issues like content overflow, enhancing the functional quality of generated documents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of AI-driven content creation has seen growing interest in automated document generation. This area aims to streamline the creation of diverse documents, such as resumes, presentations, reports, and menus, which traditionally require significant manual effort to balance design principles like structural clarity, layout aesthetics, visual harmony, and stylistic coherence.

Existing approaches to document generation broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Raster Image Generation:** Methods that fine-tune text-to-image diffusion models to produce high-quality document images. While these methods excel in visual fidelity and text rendering accuracy, their primary limitation is the lack of editability in the output, restricting practical application. Some attempts have been made to decompose documents into multi-layer transparent images to partially address this, but they often lack comprehensive control over fine-grained styling attributes like text content, color schemes, and font selection.
2. **Flat Coordinate Sequence Generation:** Approaches that represent documents as sequences of elements with associated coordinates (e.g., JSON). These methods offer post-generation editability and have been applied across various document categories like academic posters, slides, and graphic designs. However, they encounter quality issues when dealing with structurally complex documents, as the flat representation necessitates extensive coordinate calculations to maintain proper layout structures, containment, spacing, and grouping relationships among elements.

A persistent challenge across both paradigms is **data scarcity**. Manually creating diverse, high-quality document datasets is labor-intensive and difficult to scale, leading to existing datasets having limited size and coverage. This data limitation hinders the full potential of document generation models. Furthermore, many current methods are specialized for narrow document categories, limiting their applicability to broader ranges of document types.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing **HTML/CSS** as a unified, hierarchical, and editable document representation. This approach positions AnyDoc as a framework that seeks to overcome the editability issues of raster outputs, the structural limitations of flat coordinate sequences, and the data scarcity problem by leveraging the scalable synthesis capabilities of HTML/CSS. It aims to unify diverse document generation tasks under a single framework, thus broadening the application scope beyond single-category solutions. The integration of reinforcement learning also addresses common practical issues, such as content overflow, which affect the usability of generated documents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the AnyDoc framework are to advance automated document generation by addressing several identified limitations in current methodologies:

1. **Establish a Unified, Editable Document Representation:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing document representations either result in non-editable raster images or use flat coordinate sequences that struggle with structural complexity, requiring extensive low-level coordinate management. This limits the practical utility and scalability of current systems.
 - **Objective:** To introduce HTML/CSS as a unified representation for documents. HTML inherently provides a hierarchical structure that is well-suited for complex document layouts and naturally reflects containment relationships. CSS offers powerful declarative styling capabilities with layout modules (e.g., flexbox, grid) to manage grouping, alignment, and spacing without precise numerical calculations, allowing models to focus on high-level design intent. Furthermore, HTML/CSS ensures full editability of all elements (replacement, restyling, repositioning).

2. **Develop a Scalable and Diverse Document Dataset:**
 - **Motivation:** The scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse document datasets limits the training of robust generative models. Manually curating such datasets is expensive and not scalable.
 - **Objective:** To create an automated data synthesis pipeline capable of generating a large-scale dataset of documents in HTML/CSS format. This pipeline aims to overcome the limitations of existing human-crafted datasets by producing a broad range of document categories and styles with comprehensive metadata, including design intentions, HTML/CSS source code, visual assets, and rendered screenshots. The resulting dataset, DocHTML, is intended to provide a solid foundation for training versatile document generation models.

3. **Enable Versatile Document Generation Across Multiple Tasks:**
 - **Motivation:** Many existing document generation methods are tailored for specific document categories or single tasks, limiting their generalizability and broader application.
 - **Objective:** To develop a framework that can handle multiple practical document generation tasks using a unified approach. Specifically, AnyDoc aims to support:
 - **Intention-to-Document (I2D):** Generating documents from natural language design intentions.
 - **Document Derendering (DD):** Reconstructing HTML/CSS code from a given document screenshot.
 - **Element-to-Document (E2D):** Composing a coherent document from a collection of text and image elements.

4. **Mitigate Content Overflow Issues in Generated Documents:**
 - **Motivation:** Even after initial training, generative models often produce documents where elements exceed specified boundaries, leading to truncated or undesirable visual outputs when rendered. This "content overflow" problem reduces the practical quality and usability of generated documents.
 - **Objective:** To incorporate a post-training procedure, Height-Aware Reinforcement Learning (HARL), to specifically address and penalize content overflow. By defining a reward function based on the difference between predicted and target document heights, HARL aims to guide the model to generate documents that comply with height constraints while maintaining overall visual quality.

These objectives collectively aim to create a more robust, versatile, and practically applicable document generation system that overcomes key limitations of previous research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AnyDoc framework integrates a multi-stage approach, combining large-scale data synthesis, multi-modal large language model (MLLM) fine-tuning, and a specialized reinforcement learning post-training procedure.

#### 3.1. Dataset: DocHTML Construction

The core of AnyDoc's methodology involves the creation of DocHTML, a large-scale, high-quality HTML/CSS document dataset. This is achieved through an automated data synthesis pipeline consisting of five key stages:

1. **Metadata Collection:**
 - A large-scale, professionally crafted document repository serves as the initial source.
 - Since this repository lacks textual metadata, InternVL3, an MLLM, is employed to generate semantic annotations for each document.
 - The model is prompted to extract two types of information: high-level design intentions (purpose/goal) and detailed descriptions (objective summary of visual content).

2. **HTML/CSS Code Generation:**
 - Using the collected metadata (document dimensions, category, style, intention, description) as input conditions, Qwen3-Coder-480B, a code generation model, is utilized to produce the corresponding HTML/CSS documents.
 - Specific prompting principles are enforced to ensure high-quality and compatible HTML/CSS:
 - Emphasis on modern CSS layout modules (e.g., flexbox, grid) for element positioning, spacing (e.g., `row-gap`, `column-gap`, `justify-content`, `align-items`), and sizing (e.g., `flex-basis`, `flex-grow`, `flex-shrink`).
 - Standardized format for `<img>` tags: `src` uses a unified placeholder URL (`https://picsum.photos/W/H`) embedding image width and height, and `alt` provides a detailed textual description of the desired image content. This mechanism ensures size coordination, semantic consistency, and contextual harmony.
 - The HTML `<body>` dimensions are set according to specified document width and height.

3. **Image Asset Synthesis:**
 - The widths, heights, and textual descriptions from the generated `<img>` tags are fed into FLUX.1-dev, a state-of-the-art image generation model.
 - This step synthesizes high-quality visual assets that correspond to the `alt` descriptions.

4. **Rendering:**
 - After image generation, the placeholder URLs in the HTML/CSS source code are replaced with the actual paths to the synthesized image files.
 - Playwright, a browser automation framework, renders the complete HTML/CSS code with the synthesized images into document screenshots. This step ensures that documents are available in both editable code and visual forms.

5. **Data Cleaning:**
 - Rigorous data cleaning rules are applied to maintain dataset quality. Documents are excluded if:
 - HTML body dimensions do not match specified values.
 - Image tags lack the required `src` placeholder or `alt` content.
 - Elements have zero height values.
 - Content overflows beyond document boundaries.
 - After cleaning, DocHTML comprises 265,206 high-quality samples, covering 111 document categories and 32 styles, each with metadata, HTML/CSS code, synthesized images, and rendered screenshots.

#### 3.2. Model Stage I: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

Building on the DocHTML dataset, AnyDoc fine-tunes multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) to acquire document generation capabilities across three tasks:

- **Intention-to-Document (I2D):** Input includes textual design intentions, document width, and height. Output is HTML/CSS code.
- **Document Derendering (DD):** Input comprises a document screenshot, document width, and height. Output is HTML/CSS code.
- **Element-to-Document (E2D):** Input consists of document width, height, and a set of randomly shuffled text and image elements extracted from a target document. Output is HTML/CSS code.

Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is used as the base model. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) fine-tuning is applied to reduce computational overhead, with a rank of 32. The global batch size is 128 and the learning rate is 1e-4.

#### 3.3. Model Stage II: Height-Aware Reinforcement Learning (HARL)

Following SFT, a post-training stage is introduced to address the "content overflow" issue, where generated elements extend beyond specified document heights.

- **Motivation:** SFT-trained models sometimes produce documents with elements that exceed the specified vertical boundaries, leading to truncation upon rendering.
- **Approach:** Height-Aware Reinforcement Learning (HARL) is implemented, based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
 - For each input query, GRPO samples a group of candidate HTML/CSS outputs.
 - Playwright is used to render each generated HTML/CSS code into a document image, from which its actual height (`ˆh`) is obtained.
 - A reward function `r` is computed based on the height ratio `ρ = ˆh / h`, where `h` is the specified target height:
 - `r = max(0, 1)` if `1 - γ ≤ ρ ≤ 1` (within tolerance or slight underflow).
 - `r = 1 - (1 - γ - ρ) = γ + ρ` if `ρ < 1 - γ` (significant underflow, penalized).
 - `r = 1 - α * |ρ - 1|` if `ρ > 1` (overflow, penalized proportional to deviation).
 - Here, `γ` is the tolerance factor and `α` is the overflow penalty coefficient. This function penalizes both underflow (to prevent reward hacking, e.g., generating empty documents) and overflow.
 - The model is optimized using the standard GRPO loss, encouraging the generation of documents that comply with height constraints.
- HARL uses full-parameter tuning for more effective policy updates, with a learning rate of 1e-6 and a batch size of 64. The GRPO rollout number is 5, KL divergence coefficient 0.01, and clipping ratio 0.2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of AnyDoc was evaluated through quantitative and qualitative experiments across the three specified document generation tasks, including ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setups

- **Dataset:** The DocHTML dataset was split into 8:1:1 for training, validation, and testing. A subset of 1,000 instances from the test set was randomly sampled for metric computation. HARL training utilized the top 20,000 most challenging (highest height deviation) samples from the SFT model's inference on the training set.
- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct was used for both SFT (LoRA fine-tuning) and HARL (full-parameter tuning).
- **Baselines:**
 - **General MLLMs (Zero-shot):** GPT-4o and InternVL3-78B.
 - **Specialized Models:** OpenCOLE and FLUX.1-dev for I2D; WebSight and Design2Code for DD; LaDeCo for E2D.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **I2D/E2D:** MLLM-based judge scores (InternVL3-78B) for Layout, Image, Typography, Content, Innovation, and an additional Intention metric for I2D (semantic consistency). Higher scores are better.
 - **DD:** Objective metrics: Block, Text, Position, Color, and CLIP. Higher scores are better.
 - **All Tasks:** Overlap (Ove), Alignment (Ali), and Height metrics for layout quality and content overflow. Lower values are better for these three.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Intention-to-Document (I2D) (Table 1):** AnyDoc either surpassed or matched baseline models, including general MLLMs (InternVL3-78B, GPT-4o) and specialized models (OpenCOLE, FLUX.1-dev), across almost all metrics. This indicates its capability to generate high-quality and semantically aligned documents from textual intentions. For example, AnyDoc achieved an Intention score of 8.95, comparable to GPT-4o (8.96) and higher than InternVL3-78B (8.02). Its Layout score was 8.64, surpassing GPT-4o's 8.59.
- **Document Derendering (DD) (Table 2):** AnyDoc demonstrated strong performance across derendering accuracy metrics (Block, Text, Position, Color, CLIP), with scores like 0.965 for Block and 0.910 for Position, outperforming most baselines including GPT-4o on several metrics. While its Height metric (0.309) was not the lowest, it was noted to be better than specialized derendering models like WebSight (0.438) and Design2Code (0.652), suggesting a relative strength despite the model size being smaller than general-purpose MLLMs.
- **Element-to-Document (E2D) (Table 3):** AnyDoc significantly outperformed GPT-4o across the five MLLM-based metrics (e.g., Layout 8.62 vs. 8.09, Image 8.73 vs. 8.38) and showed a lower Height metric (0.085 vs. 0.212), confirming its ability to generate high-quality documents from sets of input elements while managing content effectively. It also outperformed the specialized model LaDeCo across all MLLM-based metrics.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons (Figures 5, 6, 7) indicated that AnyDoc produced high-quality, visually appealing, and task-effective outputs:
- **I2D:** AnyDoc accurately captured input semantics, aligning with specified categories, styles, and intentions.
- **DD:** The framework successfully reconstructed original documents with fidelity in layout, color schemes, and text recognition.
- **E2D:** AnyDoc effectively combined provided elements into coherent documents, maintaining visual hierarchy and design principles.
In contrast, baseline methods frequently exhibited limitations such as poor element arrangement, significant overlap, unnatural spacing, and struggles with structural complexity or fidelity during reconstruction. Specialized models like FLUX and OpenCOLE produced raster outputs, which intrinsically lack editability, contrasting with AnyDoc's HTML/CSS output.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Table 4)

Ablation studies on the E2D task highlighted the contribution of key AnyDoc components:
- **Effect of HARL:** Removing HARL led to a notable degradation in the Height metric (from 0.085 to 0.392), indicating an increase in content overflow. A decline in MLLM-based scores also suggested that HARL contributes to overall document quality.
- **Effect of HTML/CSS Representation:** A variant using a flat coordinate representation (similar to LaDeCo) showed substantial deterioration in the Overlap metric (0.2627 compared to 0.0494 for HTML/CSS with HARL). This supported the advantage of HTML/CSS's hierarchical structure and layout capabilities over coordinate-based representations for complex document layouts.
- **Effect of DocHTML Dataset:** Increasing training data size from 10K to 50K and then to the full dataset showed stable and robust performance. Even with only 10K samples, the model outperformed GPT-4o on most metrics (Table 3), demonstrating the effectiveness and quality of the curated DocHTML dataset for document generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AnyDoc as a comprehensive framework that addresses several critical challenges in automated document generation, offering advancements with practical implications.

1. **Unified and Editable Document Representation:** By leveraging HTML/CSS, AnyDoc establishes a robust, hierarchical, and fully editable representation for diverse documents. This overcomes the limitations of previous methods that produced either non-editable raster images or structurally constrained flat coordinate sequences. The inherent structural and styling capabilities of HTML/CSS enable more precise control over layout, typography, and visual elements, significantly enhancing the utility of generated outputs for downstream editing and repurposing.

2. **Scalable Data Synthesis for High-Quality Datasets:** The development of the automated DocHTML synthesis pipeline is a significant contribution. This pipeline overcomes the persistent issue of data scarcity by efficiently generating a large-scale (265,206 samples), diverse (111 categories, 32 styles), and richly annotated dataset. This methodology provides a reproducible means to create high-quality training data, which is crucial for the development of advanced generative models. The effectiveness of DocHTML is demonstrated by AnyDoc's strong performance, even with a smaller subset of the data, indicating its high quality and relevance.

3. **Versatile Multi-Task Document Generation:** AnyDoc demonstrates the ability to handle multiple complex document generation tasks—Intention-to-Document, Document Derendering, and Element-to-Document—within a single, unified framework. This versatility, achieved by fine-tuning MLLMs on the curated DocHTML dataset, suggests a move towards more generalized and adaptable AI design assistants, capable of responding to various input modalities and design requirements.

4. **Enhanced Document Quality and Control via Reinforcement Learning:** The introduction of Height-Aware Reinforcement Learning (HARL) effectively mitigates the common problem of content overflow in generated documents. By explicitly penalizing deviations from target height, HARL improves the practical usability of the outputs, ensuring that documents render correctly without truncation. This refinement step is crucial for transitioning from aesthetically pleasing but functionally flawed outputs to practically usable documents.

5. **Foundation for Future Research and Applications:** AnyDoc provides a strong foundation for future research in several directions, including exploring more document generation tasks (e.g., document variation, image-based document generation, document question answering) and further refining content overflow management. Its modular approach, combining data synthesis, MLLM fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning, offers a blueprint for developing more sophisticated AI tools in graphic design, content creation, and web development. The framework's ability to produce editable, structured documents could significantly streamline creative workflows in various industries.
