# Towards Exploratory and Focused Manipulation with Bimanual Active Perception: A New Problem, Benchmark and Strategy

> [!summary] Summary
> A new problem of Exploratory and Focused Manipulation (EFM) is defined for robotic agents, complemented by the EFM-10 benchmark and a Bimanual Active Perception (BAP) strategy leveraging existing robot hardware. The research demonstrates that active vision, particularly when focusing on both the manipulated area and the end-effector, significantly enhances task success, and that integrating force sensing improves compliance and success rates in delicate manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation tasks often fail due to insufficient or poorly acquired sensory information, such as visual occlusion from head-mounted cameras, rather than solely motor control issues.
- A lack of a formal framework exists for tasks requiring robots to actively seek information through exploration or concentrate perception for precise, delicate operations.
- Current active vision approaches for manipulation typically rely on specialized hardware (e.g., high-DoF active necks) or are limited to single-arm setups, restricting broader application on existing bimanual robots.

## Method

- Formalized ==Exploratory and Focused Manipulation (EFM)== as a new problem requiring robots to actively gather information for tasks demanding exploration or concentrated perception, and established the **EFM-10** benchmark comprising 10 diverse and challenging tasks.
- Proposed a **Bimanual Active Perception (BAP)** strategy that utilizes a non-operating arm for ==eye-in-hand active vision== and the operating arm's force sensors for ==tactile feedback==, making it applicable to existing bimanual robots without specialized hardware.
- Created **BAPData**, a dataset of 1850 expert demonstrations with high-degree-of-freedom active vision and ==6D Force/Torque== information, to facilitate imitation learning for the defined EFM tasks.

## Results

- Active vision, specifically capturing both the manipulated area and operating end effector, substantially improved task success rates (e.g., Cup-Hang success increased from **26.7%** to **90.0%** compared to capturing only the manipulated area).
- Integrating force sensing into a policy increased success rates for delicate tasks (e.g., Light-Plug improved from **20.0%** to **36.7%**; Bread-Brush from **76.7%** to **90.0%**) and reduced the average maximum vertical force by **29%** and **22%** respectively.
- Benchmarking representative policies on EFM-10 revealed Pi-0's strong instruction-following (e.g., Toy-Find: **83.3%** success), but all tested models performed less effectively on extremely fine-grained operation tasks (e.g., Light-Plug: Pi-0 **23.3%** success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal active vision for manipulation tasks requires capturing both the manipulated area and the operating end effector, especially when manipulating hand-held objects, to provide crucial pose adjustment cues.
> - Integrating force sensing enables a form of "neural force compliance control" in learning-based policies, significantly enhancing performance and safety in delicate, contact-rich manipulation tasks.
> - Current state-of-the-art manipulation policies exhibit limitations in semantic conditioning, spatial perception, and autonomous optimal active viewpoint searching when addressing complex EFM tasks, indicating areas for future research.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses challenges in robotic manipulation, particularly for general-purpose applications involving humanoids. The paper identifies a shift in robotic camera placement from side-mounted to head-mounted configurations, which offers base-invariant flexibility but frequently results in visual occlusion. This issue is recognized as a specific instance of a broader problem: the absence of information essential for task completion.

Prior work in active vision has explored methods such as 7-DoF active necks to mitigate visual occlusion in humanoid manipulation. Active exploration has been a focus in navigation research and, to a lesser extent, in manipulation, with some studies aiming to identify system dynamics for model-based control or construct scene graphs. EyeRobot introduced active exploration for end-to-end manipulation policy learning in single-arm settings. Force sensing, specifically 6D Force/Torque and Visuo-Tactile, has also been integrated into both classical compliance control and learning-based approaches for contact-rich manipulation.

This paper extends beyond specific visual occlusion problems by introducing the more fundamental concept of Exploratory and Focused Manipulation (EFM). It departs from existing active vision approaches by proposing a Bimanual Active Perception (BAP) strategy, which utilizes the non-operating arm for active vision, making it applicable to existing bimanual robots without specialized active neck hardware. The work also contributes to the under-explored area of active exploration for bimanual manipulation, particularly in a learning-based context, and incorporates 6D Force/Torque sensing to address fine-grained contact tasks, building upon established methods for learning-based compliance. By defining EFM and providing a benchmark and dataset, the authors aim to consolidate and advance research on active information gathering for complex manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable robots to actively gather necessary information to complete challenging manipulation tasks, mirroring human capabilities in active exploration and concentration. This is motivated by the observation that visual occlusion, a common problem with head-mounted robot cameras, is fundamentally an "absence of information useful for task completion."

The authors expand this observation into a broader problem termed "Exploratory and Focused Manipulation (EFM)." EFM is defined by the need to actively seek information to complete manipulation tasks that require either exploration or concentration. This includes scenarios where critical information is hidden or difficult to perceive from a static viewpoint, and tasks requiring high precision and delicate contact where a focused, clear view and tactile feedback are beneficial.

Specific motivations for introducing EFM and the associated work include:
- **Addressing Information Deficiencies:** Recognizing that many complex manipulation tasks fail not just due to motor control issues but due to insufficient or poorly acquired sensory information.
- **Formalizing a Broader Problem:** Moving beyond the specific issue of visual occlusion to a more general framework that encompasses semantic exploration (e.g., finding an object of a specific color in a hidden compartment) and delicate operations (e.g., plugging a small USB cable or brushing a dough).
- **Inspiring Future Research:** The authors aim to establish EFM as a recognized problem area that warrants continuous research efforts, providing a common framework, benchmark, and initial strategies. They cite examples like plugging audio-video cables into small, invisible ports, which requires both active exploration for port color and focused perception (visual and force) for delicate insertion.
- **Leveraging Existing Robot Capabilities:** Proposing a strategy (BAP) that is readily applicable to many existing bimanual robots without requiring the integration of specialized, high-degree-of-freedom active necks, thus lowering the barrier for implementation.
- **Providing a Comprehensive Benchmark:** Creating EFM-10 to standardize the evaluation of different approaches to EFM, ensuring that future research can be quantitatively compared across a diverse set of challenging tasks.
- **Developing a Supporting Dataset:** Collecting BAPData to facilitate imitation learning, a proven paradigm for general-purpose manipulation, under the proposed BAP strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the problem of Exploratory and Focused Manipulation (EFM) through a multi-faceted approach involving problem definition, benchmark creation, strategy development, and data collection for empirical verification.

**A. Problem Formulation: Exploratory and Focused Manipulation (EFM)**
The core concept introduced is Exploratory and Focused Manipulation. This problem requires a robotic agent to actively seek information to complete challenging manipulation tasks. These tasks are characterized by two main needs:
- **Exploration:** The robot must actively gather information about hidden or unobserved aspects of the environment or objects to understand semantic properties or overcome visual occlusion.
- **Focus:** The robot must concentrate its perception on a specific area to perform delicate, fine-grained operations that require precise feedback.

**B. The EFM-10 Benchmark**
As an initial step to address EFM, a benchmark named EFM-10 was established. It comprises 10 challenging tasks grouped into four categories based on the abilities they require:
1. **Semantically Exploratory Tasks:**
 - **Toy-Find:** Find a specific colored toy from hidden compartments and place it on a table.
 - **Toy-Match:** Check the color of a hidden plate, then pick and place a toy of the same color onto it.
2. **Exploratory Tasks Involving Visual Occlusion:**
 - **Cup-Hang:** Hang a cup on a rack, where the hand-held cup can occlude the target.
 - **Cup-Place:** Place a cup on a small coaster, with potential occlusion during placement.
 - **Box-Push:** Push a box into a lined area, where the box itself can occlude the target area.
3. **Delicate Tasks Requiring Focus:**
 - **Light-Plug:** Plug a USB light into a small port, benefiting from a concentrated view.
 - **Bread-Brush:** Place dough and brush it, requiring a focused view for precise contact.
 - **Nail-Knock:** Place and knock in a nail, demanding precise perception of relative positions.
4. **Complex Tasks Requiring Both Exploration and Focus:**
 - **Cable-Match:** Check a hidden port's color, then pick and plug the matching cable.
 - **Charger-Plug:** Plug a USB charger into a specific power strip port, requiring active view for occlusion and focus for delicate insertion.

The tasks are defined with the aim of standardization, allowing researchers to replicate them in various robotic setups.

**C. Bimanual Active Perception (BAP) Strategy**
The proposed BAP strategy is designed for bimanual robots and does not necessitate a high-DoF active neck. The core idea is to:
- **Active Vision:** Leverage the non-operating arm (if available) to provide eye-in-hand active vision, offering dynamic viewpoints for exploration or focused observation.
- **Force Sensing:** Utilize the operating arm's built-in force sensors to provide tactile feedback during contact-rich, delicate manipulation.
The strategy is described as compatible with neck-based active vision, with potential for combination in future work.

**D. BAPData Dataset**
To verify the effectiveness of the BAP strategy, a dataset named BAPData was collected.
- **Size and Content:** 1850 expert demonstrations were recorded across all 10 EFM-10 tasks using a real-world bimanual robot.
- **Data Collection Process:** During teleoperation, human experts intentionally controlled the non-operating arm to provide active views that capture both the manipulated area and the operating end effector when exploration or focus was needed. Force/Torque sensory data were simultaneously recorded.
- **Hardware System:** A JAKA K-1 bimanual robot (7-DoF arms with built-in Force/Torque sensors) was used. It was equipped with an Orbbec Gemini 2L head-mounted camera, two Logitech C922Pro wrist-mounted cameras, and a Pico Ultra 4 VR headset for teleoperation.
- **Data Characteristics:** All data were recorded at 10 Hz. The dataset is notable for being large, incorporating high-DoF active vision, and including force information, as compared to other existing real-world humanoid manipulation datasets (Table I).

**E. Experimental Verification**
The methodology included three main experimental phases:
1. **Verifying Eye-in-Hand Active Vision:**
 - **Objective:** To determine the optimal visual context for active vision.
 - **Setup:** Policies were trained using ACT on 4 representative EFM-10 tasks under three active view conditions: 1) no active view (neither manipulated area nor end effector captured), 2) only manipulated area captured, and 3) both manipulated area and operating end effector captured.
 - **Evaluation:** Success rates over 30 random trials.
2. **Benchmarking Representative Policies:**
 - **Objective:** To evaluate the performance of current state-of-the-art manipulation policies on EFM tasks.
 - **Policies:** ACT, DP (CNN version), GR-MG, and Pi-0.
 - **Training:** Policies were trained using BAPData via imitation learning, initially without utilizing force sensing. Single-task algorithms were trained per task, while multi-task algorithms were trained for all tasks.
 - **Evaluation:** Success rates on all 10 EFM-10 tasks.
3. **The Effect of Force Sensing:**
 - **Objective:** To quantify the benefits of integrating force sensing for delicate EFM tasks.
 - **Method:** A variant of the GR-MG policy was devised to incorporate Force/Torque data as input and predict future Force/Torque values.
 - **Tasks:** Light-Plug and Bread-Brush, which involve fine-grained operation.
 - **Evaluation:** Success rates and the average of maximum vertical force (Avg. Fz Max) of the operating end-effector to assess compliance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several findings regarding the EFM problem, the BAP strategy, and the performance of current policies.

**A. Verification of Eye-in-Hand Active Vision**
Preliminary experiments demonstrated the impact of active view context on task performance.
- Policies failed EFM tasks when the active view captured neither the manipulated area nor the operating end effector.
- Capturing only the manipulated area yielded inferior performance compared to capturing both the manipulated area and the operating end effector. This was particularly evident in tasks like Cup-Hang (26.7% vs 90.0% success rate), Nail-Knock (13.3% vs 43.3%), and Charger-Plug (0.0% vs 20.0%).
- The results suggested that explicitly capturing the operating end effector in the active view is important, especially when manipulating hand-held objects, as it provides direct cues for end effector pose adjustment.

**B. Benchmarking Representative Policies**
Evaluation of various policy models on the EFM-10 benchmark using BAPData (without force sensing) revealed distinct capabilities and limitations:
- **Single-task policies (ACT and DP):** Demonstrated limitations in "language-driven semantically exploratory tasks" like Toy-Find and Cable-Match, as they lacked language conditioning. For example, ACT achieved 26.7% on Toy-Find, while Pi-0 (VLM-based) achieved 83.3%.
- **DP vs. ACT:** DP showed advantages in tasks with "multimodal action distributions" (e.g., Box-Push: DP 53.3% vs ACT 40.0%), but performed less effectively than ACT in "fine-grained tasks" (e.g., Nail-Knock: DP 36.7% vs ACT 43.3%), possibly due to the lack of temporal ensemble.
- **Multi-task policies (GR-MG and Pi-0):** Pi-0 exhibited stronger "instruction-following ability" than GR-MG, particularly in tasks that did not involve extremely fine-grained operations (e.g., Toy-Find: Pi-0 83.3% vs GR-MG 60.0%; Cup-Place: Pi-0 100% vs GR-MG 93.3%).
- **Overall Performance on Fine-Grained Tasks:** All tested policies, including the multi-task models, performed less effectively on "extremely fine-grained operation" EFM tasks such as Light-Plug (Pi-0 23.3%) and Charger-Plug (Pi-0 16.7%).

**C. The Effect of Force Sensing**
Experiments specifically evaluating the integration of force sensing into a GR-MG policy showed improvements in delicate manipulation tasks:
- **Success Rate Increase:** Success rates for Light-Plug increased from 20.0% to 36.7% and for Bread-Brush from 76.7% to 90.0% when force sensing was incorporated.
- **Force Compliance:** The average of maximum vertical force (Avg. Fz Max) of the operating end-effector reduced by 29% for Light-Plug and 22% for Bread-Brush. This reduction in peak forces suggests that the policy achieved a form of "neural force compliance control."
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visualization of rollouts demonstrated that the force-enhanced GR-MG policy could forecast changes in vertical force upon contact and adjust the end effector's control to prevent abrupt force increases, as well as correctly predict force decreases upon successful insertion.

**D. Analysis of Failure Cases**
Qualitative analysis identified common failure modes across EFM tasks:
- **Semantically Exploratory Tasks (e.g., Toy-Find, Toy-Match, Cable-Match):** Failures often stemmed from policies being unable to accurately condition actions on semantic context, such as picking an object of the incorrect color.
- **Exploratory Tasks Involving Visual Occlusion (e.g., Cup-Hang, Cup-Place, Box-Push):** Failures were diverse, including suboptimal gripper height after object transfer, policies failing to identify an optimal active view to avoid occlusion, and inadequate adaptation to dynamic boundary conditions.
- **Delicate Operation Tasks (e.g., Light-Plug, Bread-Brush, Nail-Knock, Charger-Plug):** Many failures were attributed to insufficient spatial perception and reasoning capabilities of the policies, frequently leading to subtle mispositioning during critical phases of the operation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a set of fundamental challenges related to information acquisition and utilization. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Problem Formalization:** By formally introducing and defining the problem of Exploratory and Focused Manipulation (EFM), the paper establishes a new conceptual framework that encompasses a broader range of real-world manipulation difficulties beyond simple visual occlusion. This formalization can guide future research towards more holistic solutions for intelligent robotic behavior.
- **Comprehensive Benchmark (EFM-10):** The creation of the EFM-10 benchmark provides a standardized, diverse, and challenging set of 10 tasks across four categories. This benchmark serves as a common platform for evaluating robotic manipulation systems, fostering reproducible research and enabling direct comparison of different algorithms and strategies. Its detailed task specifications aim to facilitate adoption by researchers globally.
- **Practical Active Perception Strategy:** The Bimanual Active Perception (BAP) strategy offers a practical and accessible approach to active vision and force sensing for bimanual robots. Crucially, it leverages existing hardware (two arms and wrist cameras) without requiring specialized, high-degree-of-freedom active necks. This pragmatic solution expands the applicability of active perception to a wider range of current robotic platforms.
- **High-Quality Dataset (BAPData):** The BAPData dataset, comprising 1850 expert demonstrations with both active vision and 6D Force/Torque information, is a valuable resource for the machine learning and robotics communities. It specifically supports imitation learning for EFM tasks and is noted as the largest dataset incorporating both high-DoF active vision and force information among comparable real-world bimanual manipulation datasets. This dataset can accelerate the development of learning-based manipulation policies.
- **Key Insights into Active Vision and Force Sensing:** The experimental results provide empirical evidence for the effectiveness of the BAP strategy. Specifically, the finding that active views should capture *both* the manipulated area and the operating end effector is a subtle yet important technical detail that can inform the design of active perception systems. Furthermore, the demonstrated "neural force compliance control" achieved by integrating force sensing highlights its crucial role in enhancing performance and safety in delicate, contact-rich tasks.
- **Identification of Current Policy Limitations:** The comprehensive benchmarking of representative manipulation policies (ACT, DP, GR-MG, Pi-0) reveals their strengths and weaknesses in addressing EFM tasks. The identified failure modes—such as difficulties with semantic conditioning, spatial perception/reasoning, and optimal active viewpoint searching—point to specific areas for future policy model improvements.
- **Directions for Future Research:** The paper lays a foundation for future work by clearly articulating remaining challenges. It encourages efforts to enhance policy models in areas like semantic conditioning, spatial perception and reasoning, and autonomous optimal active viewpoint searching. This comprehensive analysis can help direct the development of more robust and human-like manipulation capabilities in robots.
