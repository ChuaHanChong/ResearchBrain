# SmolVLM: Redefining small and efficient multimodal models

> [!summary] Summary
> Hugging Face and Stanford researchers develop SmolVLM, a family of compact multimodal models that process both images and videos while requiring less than 1GB of GPU memory for the smallest 256M parameter variant, achieving competitive performance through optimized architecture design and training strategies that specifically target resource-constrained environments.

## Problem

- Large vision-language models (VLMs) require significant computational resources, limiting their deployment on mobile and edge devices
- Simply scaling down existing architectures leads to inefficient small models with degraded performance
- Optimal parameter allocation between vision encoder and language model components for small-scale multimodal architectures was not well understood prior to this systematic study

## Method

- Systematically explore architectural choices and training strategies, including context length and training data composition, optimized for compact multimodal models
- Balance parameter allocation between vision encoder and language model components
- Employ aggressive ==visual token compression== and ==learned positional tokens==

## Results

- SmolVLM-**256M** achieves competitive performance using **<1GB** GPU memory, while SmolVLM-**2.2B** rivals state-of-the-art VLMs with significantly lower resource usage
- Models demonstrate strong generalization to video tasks without architecture modifications
- Successful deployment on consumer hardware enables practical on-device applications

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Smaller language models benefit less from larger vision encoders while larger models gain more from powerful encoders
> - Image splitting enhances performance for vision tasks but video frame averaging degrades results
> - System prompts and media tokens significantly improve compact VLM performance, while excessive chain-of-thought data harms it

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work fits into the broader research landscape of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Machine Learning. VLMs have gained significant attention due to their ability to process and understand both visual and textual information, leading to breakthroughs in various applications like image captioning, visual question answering, and document understanding.

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the VLM field: the high computational cost associated with large models. While large VLMs have demonstrated impressive performance, their size and resource requirements limit their deployment on mobile and edge devices. This creates a need for smaller, more efficient VLMs that can operate effectively in resource-constrained environments.

SmolVLM directly tackles this problem by introducing a series of compact multimodal models specifically designed for resource-efficient inference. The paper explicitly contrasts its approach with that of other VLMs, noting that many smaller VLMs simply mirror the architectures of larger models, leading to inefficiencies. The authors acknowledge the work of other efficient VLMs like Moondream and H2OVL-Mississippi, and positions SmolVLM as a further contribution to this line of research.

The paper also highlights the growing importance of video understanding within the VLM field, as VLMs expand beyond static images to process and interpret video data. This aligns with the increasing demand for applications that can understand and reason about video content, such as video captioning, video question answering, and action recognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the research are:

- **Develop compact yet powerful VLMs:** To create a family of small-scale multimodal models that achieve competitive performance without sacrificing capability.
- **Achieve efficient GPU memory usage:** To significantly reduce the memory footprint of VLMs, enabling deployment on resource-constrained devices.
- **Systematically explore architectural choices:** To investigate the impact of different architectural configurations, tokenization strategies, and training data composition on VLM performance.
- **Demonstrate robust video understanding:** To showcase the ability of SmolVLM models to generalize effectively to video tasks.
- **Release fully open-source resources:** To promote reproducibility and facilitate further research in the field.

The motivation behind this research is the increasing need for efficient VLMs that can be deployed on mobile and edge devices. The authors recognize that the high computational cost of large VLMs limits their practical applicability in many real-world scenarios. By developing smaller, more efficient models, they aim to lower the barrier to entry for VLM deployment and unlock new possibilities for real-time multimodal inference on resource-constrained platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic and iterative approach to explore the design space of small VLMs. The key elements of their methodology include:

- **Architectural exploration:** The authors investigate the impact of various architectural choices, including the balance between vision encoder and language model size, context length, and token compression techniques (pixel shuffling).
- **Tokenization strategies:** They explore different tokenization methods, including learned positional tokens and structured text prompts, and evaluate their impact on model performance.
- **Data curation:** The researchers carefully curate the training data, considering the composition of text and visual data, the inclusion of Chain-of-Thought examples, and the length of video sequences.
- **Instruction tuning:** They employ instruction tuning to fine-tune the models for specific tasks and improve their ability to follow instructions.
- **Experimental evaluation:** They conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the performance of SmolVLM models on a range of vision-language benchmarks, using VLMEvalKit for reproducibility.
- **On-device performance benchmarking:** They measure the throughput of SmolVLM models on different hardware platforms to assess their suitability for on-device deployment.

The authors adopt a modular architecture where encoded images are projected into a SmolLM2 backbone. They experiment with different sizes of the vision encoder and language model, finding that smaller language models benefit less from larger vision encoders, while larger language models gain more from powerful encoders.

To handle longer sequences, they extend the context capacity by increasing the RoPE base and fine-tuning the model on a mix of long-context and short-context data. They also employ pixel shuffling to reduce the number of visual tokens, finding that smaller VLMs benefit from more aggressive token compression.

For video understanding, they experiment with frame averaging, but find that it negatively impacts performance. Instead, they rescale video frames to the resolution of the image encoder.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The key findings of the research are:

- **Compact multimodal models benefit from a balanced encoder-LM parameter allocation.**
- **Compact VLMs significantly benefit from extended context lengths.**
- **Small VLMs benefit from more aggressive visual token compression.**
- **For small models, image splitting enhances performance for vision tasks, whereas video frame averaging does not.**
- **Learned positional tokens outperform raw text tokens for compact VLMs.**
- **System prompts and media intro/outro tokens significantly improve compact VLM performance, particularly for video tasks.**
- **Adding text from SFT blend proved worse than new text SFT data.**
- **Excessive CoT data harms compact model performance.**
- **Moderately increasing video duration during training improves both video and image task performance in compact VLMs.**

The experimental results demonstrate that SmolVLM models achieve strong performance on a range of vision-language benchmarks while maintaining significantly lower memory usage compared to other VLMs. The smallest model, SmolVLM-256M, requires less than 1GB of GPU memory, outperforming much larger models. The largest model, SmolVLM-2.2B, rivals state-of-the-art VLMs while consuming less GPU memory.

The authors also demonstrate the practicality of SmolVLM for on-device deployment, showcasing its ability to run efficiently on consumer-grade hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this research lies in its contribution to the development of efficient and accessible VLMs. By demonstrating that it is possible to achieve strong performance with small models and minimal resource requirements, the authors open up new possibilities for deploying VLMs in a wider range of applications and environments.

The potential impact of this work is significant:

- **Enabling on-device AI:** SmolVLM models can be deployed on mobile devices, laptops, and edge devices, enabling real-time multimodal inference without relying on cloud-based resources.
- **Democratizing AI:** By lowering the barrier to entry for VLM deployment, this research can make AI technology more accessible to a wider range of users and organizations.
- **Promoting energy efficiency:** Smaller models consume less power, contributing to more sustainable and environmentally friendly AI applications.
- **Facilitating further research:** The release of fully open-source resources will enable other researchers to build upon this work and accelerate innovation in the field of efficient VLMs.
- **Downstream applications:** As the paper highlights with ColSmolVLM, Smol Docling, and BioVQA, the models have the potential to be used in a variety of applications, including document processing, medical image analysis, and mobile-based AI assistants.

In conclusion, the "SmolVLM" paper presents a significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language Models by demonstrating the feasibility and benefits of developing small, efficient multimodal models. The research has the potential to enable new applications and democratize access to AI technology, while also promoting energy efficiency and facilitating further research in the field.
