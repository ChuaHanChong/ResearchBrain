# UniRelight: Learning Joint Decomposition and Synthesis for Video Relighting

> [!summary] Summary
> UniRelight introduces a video diffusion model that jointly predicts scene albedo and synthesizes relit outputs from a single input image or video under a target lighting condition. The method achieves state-of-the-art visual fidelity and strong generalization across diverse real-world scenes by leveraging a hybrid data strategy and implicit scene understanding.

## Problem

- Lack of large-scale, real-world multi-illumination datasets, hindering the training of general-purpose relighting models.
- Two-stage inverse rendering pipelines suffer from error accumulation (inaccurate G-buffers) and limited representation of complex materials like transparency or subsurface scattering.
- End-to-end relighting models struggle with generalization due to data scarcity and tend to "bake in" original scene lighting.

## Method

- Proposes **UniRelight**, a single-pass video ==diffusion model== (fine-tuned Diffusion Transformer) that jointly denoises and synthesizes target relit videos and scene albedo from a single input video.
- Employs a joint latent concatenation strategy, where latents for input, target relit video, and albedo are concatenated along the temporal dimension, enabling full ==self-attention== across modalities and using learnable type embeddings for differentiation.
- Utilizes a hybrid data strategy combining large-scale synthetic video data, pseudo-groundtruth albedo from the MIT multi-illumination dataset, and auto-labeled real-world videos to enhance diversity and generalization.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS scores on synthetic and real-world multi-illumination datasets, outperforming baselines, especially on scenes with complex materials.
- User studies consistently show a strong preference for UniRelight's visual quality and realism over all compared baselines, with up to 96% preference over DiffusionRenderer Cosmos.
- Performs joint relighting and albedo estimation in a single inference pass, reducing runtime (e.g., 445.5 seconds vs. 780 seconds for a 57-frame video) compared to multi-stage methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling albedo alongside relighting within a single generative framework significantly improves generalization and prevents "baked-in" lighting from the input image.
> - A hybrid data strategy, combining synthetic data with pseudo-groundtruth and auto-labeled real-world videos, is crucial for training robust relighting models that generalize to diverse "in-the-wild" scenes.
> - Implicit scene understanding learned by a video diffusion model can overcome the limitations of explicit G-buffer representations for handling complex materials and light transport.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of scene relighting – the ability to alter the illumination of an image or video while preserving its content – is a long-standing challenge in computer graphics and vision, with applications ranging from creative editing and visual effects to simulation and robust perception systems. Realistic relighting requires an accurate understanding of scene geometry, materials, and faithful light transport modeling. This paper addresses key limitations in the existing landscape:

- **Data Scarcity:** A fundamental bottleneck in learning-based relighting is the lack of large-scale, real-world datasets captured under multiple illumination conditions. Acquiring such data is prohibitively expensive and logistically complex, forcing many methods to rely on synthetic data or restrict themselves to narrow domains (e.g., portraiture), which limits generalization to diverse real-world scenes.
- **Limitations of End-to-End Models:** While conceptually appealing, training end-to-end relighting models has been difficult due to the data scarcity, often leading to poor generalization to unseen scenarios or a tendency to "bake in" lighting from the input image.
- **Challenges of Two-Stage Pipelines:** To circumvent data scarcity, many recent approaches decouple the relighting task into two stages: (1) **Inverse Rendering**, which estimates scene intrinsic properties (albedo, normal, depth – often called G-buffers) from an input image, and (2) **Forward Rendering**, which synthesizes the relit image using these estimated intrinsics and a new light source. Examples include DiffusionRenderer and RGB↔X. While this mitigates the need for multi-illumination supervision (as each stage can be trained separately), it introduces critical drawbacks:
 - **Error Accumulation:** Errors in the inverse rendering stage (e.g., inaccurate G-buffers) propagate and amplify in the forward rendering stage, significantly degrading the final relighting quality.
 - **Incomplete Scene Representation:** G-buffers often fail to capture complex material properties like transparency, subsurface scattering, and intricate reflections, limiting the realism of relighting for such materials. The forward renderer is highly sensitive to the explicit, discrete nature of these intermediate representations.
- **Generative Models in Inverse Rendering:** The rise of large-scale generative models, particularly diffusion models (e.g., DiT), has opened new avenues for inverse rendering tasks. Previous works like IntrinsicImageDiffusion and DiffusionRenderer have explored this, but often still within the two-stage paradigm or with domain-specific limitations.
- **Joint Generative Modeling:** More recently, concepts like VideoJAM and Matrix3D have demonstrated the power of diffusion models to jointly predict multiple modalities (e.g., pixels and motion, or pose, depth, and novel views). UniRelight builds upon this by extending the joint modeling concept to relighting and intrinsic decomposition.

UniRelight positions itself at the intersection of these challenges and advancements. It directly confronts the limitations of existing end-to-end and two-stage methods by proposing a **joint decomposition and synthesis approach** that implicitly reasons about scene intrinsics within a **single-pass video diffusion model**. By leveraging the generative power of Diffusion Transformers and a novel hybrid data strategy, it aims to achieve superior generalization, visual fidelity, and temporal consistency, addressing the shortcomings of error accumulation and limited material representation inherent in G-buffer-based pipelines. This work pushes the boundaries of general-purpose, realistic relighting beyond specific object types or controlled environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of UniRelight is to **enable high-quality, general-purpose relighting of single images or videos** by learning to understand and modify scene illumination. This goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** The prohibitive cost and impracticality of acquiring large, real-world multi-illumination datasets for training robust relighting models is a central challenge. The authors aim to develop an approach that can generalize effectively despite this limitation.
- **Addressing Limitations of Two-Stage Pipelines:** Existing decoupled inverse-forward rendering pipelines suffer from error accumulation (where mistakes in estimated G-buffers propagate to the final relit image) and struggle to model complex material interactions (e.g., transparency, subsurface scattering, intricate reflections) that are not adequately captured by simplified intrinsic representations. The motivation is to move beyond these discrete intermediate representations.
- **Achieving Implicit Scene Comprehension:** The authors hypothesize that a robust relighting model requires an intrinsic understanding of scene properties and illumination. Instead of explicit, potentially error-prone, G-buffer estimation, they seek an approach that implicitly learns these relationships within a generative framework, allowing for a more holistic and accurate representation of light transport.
- **Leveraging Generative Model Capabilities:** Recognizing the recent advancements in large-scale generative models, particularly video diffusion models, the authors are motivated to harness their power for complex tasks like joint decomposition and synthesis. They believe these models can learn implicit scene understanding and generate high-fidelity, realistic lighting effects.
- **Ensuring Generalization and Temporal Consistency:** Many previous methods are domain-specific or struggle with real-world, "in-the-wild" data. A key motivation is to develop a model that exhibits strong generalization across diverse scenes, materials, and lighting conditions, while also ensuring temporal consistency for video relighting.
- **Enabling Broader Applications:** By providing a more robust and general relighting solution, the authors aim to facilitate applications in creative editing, visual effects, virtual production, and potentially data augmentation for computer vision tasks in challenging real-world scenarios like robotics and autonomous driving.

In essence, the core motivation is to develop a robust, data-efficient, and highly generalizable relighting solution that can implicitly learn the physics of light transport and scene intrinsics, moving away from brittle multi-stage pipelines and limited datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniRelight introduces a novel generative framework that jointly predicts scene albedo and synthesizes relit outputs from a single input image or video under a target lighting condition. The methodology is built upon fine-tuning a state-of-the-art video diffusion model, combined with an innovative data strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Design: Joint Denoising Architecture

The core of UniRelight is a fine-tuned Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model, specifically Cosmos-Predict1, a large video diffusion model that operates in a lower-dimensional latent space.

- **VAE Encoder-Decoder:** A pre-trained VAE (Cosmos-1.0-Tokenizer-CV8x8x8) maps input RGB videos to compact latent representations and reconstructs them. All operations, including training and inference, occur in this latent space.
- **Joint Latent Concatenation:** Unlike some previous joint generative models, UniRelight adopts a simple yet effective strategy: the latent representations for the input video ($z_I$), the target relit video ($z_E$), and the corresponding albedo ($z_a$) are **concatenated along the temporal (frame) dimension**. This allows the DiT model to apply full self-attention across these distinct modalities, facilitating rich cross-modal information exchange and implicit learning of shared scene structure.
- **Token Embeddings:** To differentiate between the modalities within the concatenated latent sequence, the model uses:
 - Standard RoPE (Rotary Positional Embeddings) for spatial-temporal positioning within each individual video clip.
 - Learnable **type embeddings** (a unique embedding for input, relit, and albedo) concatenated along the channel dimension with their respective latent representations. This explicitly tells the model which modality each token belongs to.
- **Condition Mask:** A binary mask is attached to each frame to indicate whether it serves as a conditioning input (e.g., input source video, ground truth albedo) or a denoising target (e.g., relit video, predicted albedo). This allows for flexible training with partial supervision.
- **Encoding HDR Lighting:** High Dynamic Range (HDR) environment maps, which represent the target illumination, cannot be directly encoded by standard VAEs trained on LDR images. UniRelight follows prior work by representing lighting conditions using three complementary buffers:
 1. LDR panorama (Reinhard tonemapped)
 2. Normalized log-intensity map
 3. Directional encoding (unit vector per pixel)
 These three representations are passed through the VAE encoder, and their latent features ($h_E$) are concatenated along the channel dimension with the relit video latent ($z_E$). A binary condition mask indicates the presence of lighting features.

#### 3.2. Data Strategy: Hybrid Supervision

To overcome the limitations of data scarcity and improve generalization, UniRelight employs a multi-source data strategy:

- **Synthetic Data Curation:**
 - **Source:** A large-scale dataset of 108k procedurally generated video clips (57 frames each, 704x1280 resolution) rendered with a custom path tracer using OptiX.
 - **Content:** Scenes with randomized 3D objects (from Objaverse LVIS subset), PBR materials, and HDRI environment maps (from public resources like PolyHaven). Objects include up to three random 3D models and primitive shapes to increase variety.
 - **Motion:** Videos incorporate random camera orbits, oscillation, lighting rotation, and object motion. Each scene is rendered under two different illuminations for relighting pairs.
 - **Supervision:** Provides full supervision: input video ($I$), relit video ($I_E$), ground truth albedo ($a$), and environment map ($E$).
- **MIT Multi-illumination Labeling:**
 - **Source:** Dataset of 985 real-world indoor scenes (training) and 30 scenes (testing) from Murmann et al., each under 25 different lighting conditions.
 - **Process:** Pairs of (input video, environment map, target relit video) are extracted.
 - **Pseudo-Groundtruth Albedo:** Since real-world albedo is not readily available, the authors re-implement DiffusionRenderer's inverse renderer (fine-tuned on their synthetic data) to estimate high-quality pseudo-groundtruth albedo for each scene by averaging estimates from all 25 lighting conditions.
- **Real-world Auto-labeling:**
 - **Source:** 150k high-quality real-world video clips (57 frames, 704x1280) captured under single illumination.
 - **Process:** The same fine-tuned inverse renderer automatically annotates these videos with albedo maps, creating (input video, albedo) pairs.
 - **Purpose:** Crucially increases the diversity and realism of the training set, enabling improved generalization to "in-the-wild" scenes where multi-illumination data is not available.

#### 3.3. Training

The model is trained on a combination of these datasets, treating images as single-frame videos.

- **Loss Function:** The model optimizes a denoising score matching objective, minimizing the L2 loss between the predicted denoised latents and the ground truth latents for both the relit video and the albedo (weighted by $\lambda_a = 0.1$).
- **Conditioning Strategies:**
 - **Synthetic & MIT data:**
 - 12% of steps: Input video dropped ($z_I=0$), ground truth albedo ($z_a^0$) is the sole condition, predicting relit video.
 - 18% of steps: Conditioned on both input video ($z_I$) and ground truth albedo ($z_a^0$), predicting relit video.
 - 70% of steps (default): Input video ($z_I$) as condition, jointly predicting relit video ($z_E^\tau$) and albedo ($z_a^\tau$).
 Conditioning latents have their mask set to 1.
 - **Real-world auto-labeled data:** Since only (I, a) pairs are available (no new environment map or paired relit image), the provided albedo ($z_a^0$) is used as a condition, and the input RGB video is treated as the target relit video ($z_E^\tau$). Environment map embedding ($h_E$) and conditional input video representation ($z_I$) are set to zero.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance:** A 10% probability of dropping conditions is applied during training to enable classifier-free guidance at inference, though empirically, the authors found inference *without* CFG to yield more accurate results.
- **Training Schedule:** Two stages: first, training on synthetic data (image and video mix); then, training on all data sources with specific sampling ratios (synthetic video 8: synthetic image 1: real auto-labeled 3: MIT 2).

This comprehensive methodology, combining a novel joint modeling architecture with a strategic hybrid data pipeline, is designed to enable UniRelight to implicitly learn intricate scene properties and generalize effectively across diverse real-world relighting scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniRelight demonstrates significant advancements in video relighting and intrinsic decomposition, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines in both quantitative metrics and qualitative perceptual quality.

- **Superior Visual Fidelity:**
 - **Complex Materials:** UniRelight excels at handling intricate shadows, inter-reflections, and complex materials such as anisotropic surfaces, glass, transparent objects, fur, human skin, and car windows. Baselines, particularly those relying on explicit G-buffers (like DiffusionRenderer), struggle with these, often producing inaccurate material appearances or suboptimal results.
 - **Realistic Effects:** The method consistently generates plausible shadows, accurate specular highlights, and realistic light transport effects, which are critical for high-fidelity relighting.
 - **General-Purpose Application:** Qualitative comparisons on "in-the-wild" data (Figure 4) show that UniRelight produces more realistic and higher-quality results across diverse scenes (object-centric, urban streets) compared to domain-specific or G-buffer-dependent baselines.

- **Quantitative Performance:**
 - **Relighting:** On the SyntheticScenes and MIT multi-illumination datasets, UniRelight achieves state-of-the-art PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS scores for relighting (Table 1). While DiffusionRenderer (Cosmos) performs well on synthetic data, UniRelight significantly surpasses it on the more challenging MIT multi-illumination dataset, highlighting its ability to handle complex real-world materials.
 - **Albedo Estimation:** Although relighting is the primary focus, UniRelight's albedo estimation is strong, performing on par with or slightly better than dedicated intrinsic image decomposition methods like IntrinsicImageDiffusion and DiffusionRenderer (Table 2).

- **Perceptual Quality (User Studies):**
 - **Preference Over Baselines:** A user study on the MIT multi-illumination dataset consistently showed a strong preference for UniRelight's outputs over all baselines (e.g., 96% preferred over DiffusionRenderer Cosmos, 92% over DiLightNet, 84% over NeuralGaffer) when compared to ground truth. This indicates superior perceptual realism and accuracy.
 - **Ablation Study User Preference:** User studies on urban street scenes confirmed that the joint modeling approach is preferred over a pure relighting model, and adding real-world auto-labeled data further improves perceptual quality.

- **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **Joint Modeling (Albedo + Relighting):** This is a crucial component. The ablation study (Table 3, Figure 5) shows that jointly modeling albedo alongside relighting (compared to a "pure relighting" model) significantly improves generalization, particularly preventing "baked-in" shadows from the input image into the relit output for out-of-distribution scenes. This validates the hypothesis that albedo demodulation provides a strong prior for relighting.
 - **Real-World Auto-Labeled Data:** This data source, despite being sparsely labeled (only RGB and estimated albedo), is vital for generalization to natural, "in-the-wild" images, especially for outdoor scenes and out-of-focus regions where synthetic data might lack diversity. Without it, the model trained only on synthetic and MIT data exhibits noticeable artifacts (Figure 6).

- **Computational Efficiency:** UniRelight is more efficient during inference compared to two-stage pipelines like DiffusionRenderer. By performing joint relighting and albedo estimation in a single pass, it reduces overall runtime (e.g., 445.5 seconds vs. 780 seconds for DiffusionRenderer Cosmos on a 57-frame video), which involves multiple separate inverse and forward passes for the baselines (Table 6).

- **Application for Illumination Augmentation:** The model's strong generalization and ability to sample realistic relighting results under varying conditions make it suitable for data augmentation, e.g., generating diverse nighttime or dusk scenes from a single input for applications like autonomous driving (Figure 7).

In summary, UniRelight demonstrates state-of-the-art performance by effectively overcoming the limitations of previous relighting methods through its novel joint modeling approach and intelligent data strategy, leading to high-quality, temporally consistent, and perceptually realistic results across a broad range of scenes and materials.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniRelight represents a significant step forward in the field of image and video relighting, addressing fundamental limitations that have hindered the development of truly general-purpose solutions. Its impact can be assessed on several levels:

- **Advancing the State-of-the-Art in Relighting:** By introducing a robust joint decomposition and synthesis framework based on video diffusion models, UniRelight pushes beyond the limitations of traditional two-stage inverse/forward rendering pipelines and data-starved end-to-end approaches. The ability to implicitly reason about scene intrinsics, rather than relying on explicit, potentially error-prone G-buffers, allows for a more faithful and realistic representation of complex light transport phenomena (e.g., subsurface scattering, complex reflections, transparency) that were challenging for previous methods.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** The innovative hybrid data strategy, combining high-quality synthetic data with auto-labeled real-world videos and a smaller multi-illumination dataset, effectively mitigates the severe data scarcity challenge for relighting. This approach provides a blueprint for training complex generative models in domains where ground truth multi-condition data is hard to acquire.
- **Enabling Robust Generalization:** The strong generalization capabilities demonstrated on diverse "in-the-wild" scenes, beyond the training distribution, are particularly impactful. This means UniRelight is not confined to specific object categories or controlled environments, opening up its use to a much broader range of real-world scenarios.
- **Practical Applications:**
 - **Content Creation and Editing:** For filmmakers, photographers, and graphic designers, UniRelight offers unprecedented flexibility in manipulating lighting in existing footage, enabling creative freedom in post-production and visual effects. This could streamline workflows in virtual production and extend the capabilities of standard editing software.
 - **Virtual Production:** In virtual production environments, the ability to relight scenes or objects accurately and quickly is crucial for blending real and virtual elements seamlessly. UniRelight could provide tools for real-time adjustments and rapid iteration.
 - **Data Augmentation for AI:** The model's capacity to generate diverse lighting conditions for a given scene (as demonstrated in the illumination augmentation application) is highly valuable for training robust computer vision systems. For domains like autonomous driving, robotics, or surveillance, generating varied lighting scenarios can improve model resilience to different environmental conditions and enhance safety.
- **Shifting Paradigms in Inverse Rendering:** The success of UniRelight suggests a potential paradigm shift away from explicit, hard-coded inverse rendering steps towards more implicit, end-to-end learning within powerful generative models. This could lead to a new generation of inverse rendering techniques that are more robust, handle more complex scene properties, and generalize better.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors):**
The authors acknowledge that the current model cannot handle **emitting objects** (e.g., toggling lights within a scene), as its design focuses on environmental lighting. This is a significant area for future research. Another promising direction is integrating **text-based relighting** capabilities, allowing users to control lighting conditions using natural language prompts, which would greatly enhance usability and creative control.

**Broader Impact (from Appendix A):**
The authors responsibly discuss the broader implications, acknowledging that like all generative models, UniRelight may reflect biases present in its training data, potentially leading to generalization failures for underrepresented scene types or appearances (e.g., skin tones, races, gender identities). They also highlight the risk of misuse, such as altering visual content for misinformation or privacy violations. They explicitly discourage such applications and recommend careful dataset curation and debiasing efforts for human-centric use cases. This proactive stance on ethical considerations is commendable and crucial for responsible AI development.

In conclusion, UniRelight delivers a powerful and versatile solution for the complex task of relighting, leveraging cutting-edge generative AI to overcome long-standing challenges. Its impact is poised to extend across various creative industries and foundational AI research.
