# WIMLE: Uncertainty-Aware World Models with IMLE for Sample-Efficient Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Simon Fraser University's Apex Lab developed WIMLE, an uncertainty-aware model-based reinforcement learning framework that enhances sample efficiency in continuous control by employing an Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE) world model to capture multi-modal dynamics and integrate predictive uncertainty weighting. WIMLE demonstrated superior sample efficiency and competitive asymptotic performance across 40 complex continuous-control tasks, achieving over 50% improvement in sample efficiency on tasks like Humanoid-run.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning often requires a substantial number of environment interactions, leading to high costs and slow learning in real-world applications.
- Existing model-based RL (MBRL) methods struggle with compounding model errors and fail to adequately capture multi-modal dynamics, resulting in unrealistic "regression to the mean" predictions.
- Many MBRL approaches lack mechanisms to quantify or integrate predictive uncertainty, leading to overconfident predictions and biased policy learning from synthetic data.

## Method

- Develops an ==Implicit Maximum Likelihood== Estimation (IMLE)-based world model capable of learning stochastic and multi-modal transition dynamics without costly iterative sampling.
- Employs an ensemble of IMLE world models to estimate predictive uncertainty for each synthetic transition by measuring the standard deviation of their predictions.
- Integrates these uncertainty estimates into the RL objective as confidence weights, which inversely scale the contribution of synthetic data based on its predicted reliability.

## Results

- Achieved superior sample efficiency and competitive or improved asymptotic performance across 40 challenging continuous-control tasks, including the DeepMind Control Suite, MyoSuite, and HumanoidBench.
- Demonstrated over **50%** improvement in sample efficiency on high-dimensional and complex tasks such as Humanoid-run (DMC) and Myo-key-turn-hard (MyoSuite) compared to strong baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the IMLE-based multi-modality modeling and uncertainty-aware weighting are crucial for WIMLE's performance, stability, and ability to effectively utilize longer synthetic rollouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Uncertainty-aware weighting in the RL objective, with strictly positive weights, preserves the Bellman fixed point while reducing the variance of learned parameters, improving sample efficiency.
> - IMLE effectively captures the inherent multi-modal dynamics of complex environments, avoiding the "regression to the mean" problem without the computational overhead of iterative generative models.
> - Integrating predictive uncertainty allows for stable learning and mitigates the adverse effects of compounding model errors, enabling the use of longer synthetic rollout horizons without performance degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated effectiveness in addressing complex decision-making problems across various domains, including autonomous control and strategic game playing. A prevalent challenge in RL applications, particularly in real-world scenarios, is the substantial number of environment interactions required to train effective policies, leading to high costs, slow learning, and potential risks.

Model-based RL (MBRL) methods are designed to mitigate this sample efficiency issue. These methods operate by first learning a parametric world model from collected environment interactions. This learned model is then leveraged to reduce the need for real environment samples, thereby accelerating policy learning. Common applications of these world models include generating synthetic rollouts to augment policy training data and facilitating planning through the simulation of future trajectories.

Historically, MBRL has faced difficulties in consistently outperforming robust model-free baselines. This challenge is primarily attributed to several key issues:
- **Compounding Model Error:** Errors in the world model's predictions can accumulate over extended rollouts, leading to biased training signals and misguiding the policy.
- **Unimodal World Models:** Standard predictive models often struggle when the same state-action pair can lead to multiple, distinct next states due to factors such as partial observability, contact-rich dynamics, or inherent stochasticity. Such unimodal models tend to average over these multi-modal dynamics, producing "regression to the mean" predictions that can be implausible.
- **Lack of Uncertainty Awareness:** Many MBRL approaches do not adequately quantify or integrate predictive uncertainty into their learning process. This can result in overconfident predictions, particularly in regions with complex dynamics or limited data, which can further bias policy learning.

Despite ongoing research efforts to address these problems through various model architectures (e.g., autoregressive sequence models, latent-variable generators, diffusion models) and uncertainty-aware techniques (e.g., information-theoretic corruption measures for rollout truncation), MBRL methods have not consistently surpassed strong model-free baselines in practical applications. This paper introduces WIMLE, an approach that specifically targets the issues of multi-modal dynamics modeling and predictive uncertainty estimation within the MBRL framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an uncertainty-aware model-based reinforcement learning approach that overcomes critical limitations of existing MBRL methods, thereby improving sample efficiency and stability in continuous control tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from the identified challenges in model-based reinforcement learning:
- **Addressing Sample Inefficiency:** Reinforcement learning often demands extensive interactions with the environment, which is impractical or costly in many real-world applications. MBRL attempts to alleviate this by using learned world models, but its practical utility is hampered by model inaccuracies.
- **Mitigating Compounding Model Error:** The iterative nature of model rollouts leads to an accumulation of prediction errors, biasing the policy towards sub-optimal behaviors. A robust MBRL method needs to manage or reduce the adverse effects of these compounding errors.
- **Capturing Multi-modal Dynamics:** Real-world environments frequently exhibit stochastic or multi-modal dynamics, where a given state-action pair can lead to several possible next states. Traditional unimodal world models, often based on Gaussian regression, struggle to represent these diverse outcomes, instead predicting an average state that may not be physically realistic. This "regression to the mean" problem is particularly pronounced in complex settings like contact-rich manipulation or partially observable environments. The authors aim to develop world models that can capture this inherent multi-modality.
- **Integrating Predictive Uncertainty:** A lack of confidence estimation in model predictions can lead to over-reliance on inaccurate synthetic data, further exacerbating model bias. The motivation is to develop a mechanism to quantify the model's confidence in its predictions and use this information to temper the influence of potentially erroneous synthetic data during policy training.

To address these motivations, WIMLE sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- Develop an Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE)-based world model capable of learning stochastic and multi-modal transition dynamics without relying on iterative sampling, thus maintaining efficiency suitable for online RL.
- Establish a method for estimating predictive uncertainty from the learned world model that reflects its confidence in next-step predictions.
- Incorporate these uncertainty estimates into the RL objective as confidence weights, allowing for the differential weighting of synthetic transitions based on their predicted reliability.
- Demonstrate that this integrated approach leads to superior sample efficiency and competitive or enhanced asymptotic performance across a broad spectrum of continuous control tasks compared to existing model-free and model-based baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WIMLE (World models with IMLE) is an uncertainty-aware model-based RL approach that integrates three core components: an IMLE-trained stochastic world model, a predictive uncertainty estimation mechanism, and an uncertainty-weighted learning procedure.

#### 3.1. IMLE World Model

The world model in WIMLE is designed to address the limitations of unimodal models and the computational cost of iterative generative models like diffusion models. It employs Conditional Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE) to learn transition dynamics. The world model is represented as a conditional stochastic generator, $g_\theta(s_t, a_t, z)$, which maps a state-action pair $(s_t, a_t)$ and a latent noise vector $z \sim N(0, I)$ to the next outcome $( \tilde{s}_{t+1}, \tilde{r}_t)$. The latent variable $z$ enables the model to capture inherent stochasticity and multi-modality in the dynamics.

The **IMLE Training Procedure** involves two alternating steps:
1. **Assignment Step:** For each real environment transition target $y_i = [r_t, s_{t+1}]$, a pool of $m$ candidate latent vectors $\{z_j\}_{j=1}^m$ is sampled from $N(0, I)$. The latent $z_i^\star$ that minimizes the prediction error $||g_\theta(s_i, a_i, z_j) - y_i||^2$ is selected for each data point. This step is parallelizable and gradient-free.
2. **Update Step:** Stochastic gradient descent is performed on the empirical loss, using the assigned latents $z_i^\star$, to update the model parameters $\theta$.
This procedure ensures "mode coverage," meaning each data point is represented by at least one generated sample, preventing the model from collapsing to a single mean prediction (regression to the mean).

For **Inference and Rollouts**, after training, multi-step rollouts are generated by initializing from a real state $s_0$, and iteratively applying the policy $a_t \sim \pi_\phi(\cdot|s_t)$, sampling a new latent $z_t \sim N(0, I)$, and predicting the next state and reward $(s_{t+1}, r_t) = g_\theta(s_t, a_t, z_t)$. This non-iterative sampling process maintains fast rollout throughput.

#### 3.2. Uncertainty Estimation

To provide a measure of confidence in model predictions, WIMLE computes a predictive uncertainty for each synthetic transition. This is achieved by maintaining an ensemble of $K$ IMLE world models. For a given state-action pair $(s, a)$, each of the $K$ ensemble members generates $m$ latent samples, resulting in $K \times m$ predictions $\{( \tilde{s}_{t+1}, \tilde{r}_t)_{k,j} = g_{\theta_k}(s_t, a_t, z_j)\}$. The predictive uncertainty $\sigma(s, a)$ is then defined as the standard deviation across these $K \times m$ predictions. In practice, per-dimension standard deviations for reward and next state are averaged to obtain a single scalar uncertainty value. This $\sigma(s, a)$ decreases with model agreement and increases with high predictive variance, which can be decomposed into epistemic (ensemble variance) and aleatoric (latent variance) components.

#### 3.3. Uncertainty-Weighted Learning

The core idea of uncertainty-weighted learning is to scale the contribution of each synthetic transition in the RL objective based on its estimated confidence. High-confidence predictions are given greater influence, while uncertain ones contribute less. The weight $w(s, a)$ for a transition is defined as $1 / (\sigma(s, a) + 1)$, which ensures the weight is bounded between (0, 1] and inversely proportional to uncertainty.

This weight is integrated into the Temporal Difference (TD) loss for policy learning. The critic loss $L_{critic}$ is modified to $E_{(s_i, a_i, r_i, s'_i) \sim D} [w_i \cdot \delta_i^2]$, where $\delta_i$ is the TD error and $w_i$ is the corresponding uncertainty weight. For real environment data, $w_i=1$. This approach aims to leverage synthetic rollouts effectively by reducing the bias from uncertain predictions, without discarding them or altering the Bellman fixed point.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides two theoretical lemmas to support the uncertainty-weighted learning:
1. **Positive Weights Preserve the Bellman Target:** This lemma states that multiplying the squared Bellman error by any strictly positive weight function $w(s, a)$ does not change the unique minimizer of the population weighted Bellman squared loss, which remains the true conditional mean action-value function $Q^\star(s, a)$. This implies that the weighting re-emphasizes different regions of the state-action space but does not bias the optimal solution.
2. **Linear Critics, Inverse-Variance Weighting:** For a linear action-value function and independent noise in TD targets, choosing weights inversely proportional to the total target noise variance (i.e., $w_i \propto 1/\sigma_i^2$) yields the minimum-covariance linear unbiased estimator of the parameters. This suggests that inverse-variance weighting improves sample efficiency by reducing the variance of learned parameters.

#### 3.5. Algorithm Design Choices

- **Model Rollouts:** Rollout horizons up to $H=8$ were used, initialized from real environment states to ensure consistency with the data distribution.
- **RL Training:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) with distributional Q-learning (specifically, quantile Q-learning) was used as the underlying RL algorithm.
- **Ensemble Training:** An ensemble of $K=7$ IMLE world models was trained in parallel, each on bootstrap samples of the environment data, to enhance predictive uncertainty estimation and calibration.
- **Architecture:** The world model consists of an input layer, dense layers, three residual blocks (incorporating L2 normalization), and separate dense heads for reward and next-state predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WIMLE was conducted across 40 continuous-control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC), MyoSuite, and HumanoidBench. Performance was compared against strong model-free (MR.Q, PPO, SAC, Simba, SimbaV2, BRO) and model-based (TD-MPC2, DreamerV3) baselines. Experiments were run for 1 million environment steps with 10 random seeds, reporting interquartile mean (IQM) and 95% confidence intervals.

#### 4.1. Comparison to Baselines

- **Overall Performance:** WIMLE consistently demonstrated superior sample efficiency and competitive or improved asymptotic performance across the Dog & Humanoid subset, the full DMC suite, and HumanoidBench. On MyoSuite, WIMLE achieved asymptotic performance comparable to leading baselines, which already approached the maximum score.
- **Challenging Tasks:** The performance gains for WIMLE were most pronounced on high-dimensional and complex tasks, such as those within the Dog & Humanoid categories (e.g., Dog: |S|=223, |A|=38; Humanoid: |S|=67, |A|=24).
- **Specific Task Improvements:** On the Humanoid-run task, WIMLE improved sample efficiency by over 50% compared to the strongest competitor. On HumanoidBench, WIMLE successfully solved 8 out of 14 tasks, which compares to 4 tasks solved by BRO and 5 by SimbaV2. In sample efficiency comparisons for single tasks, WIMLE showed over 50% improvement over the strongest competing method on Humanoid-run (DMC), Myo-key-turn-hard (MyoSuite), and H1-slide-v0 (HumanoidBench).

#### 4.2. Method Analysis

- **Effect of Uncertainty-Aware Weighting:** An ablation study comparing WIMLE with uncertainty-aware weighting to an unweighted variant (where all synthetic transition weights were fixed to 1.0) on the Humanoid-run task revealed that the unweighted variant significantly lagged and sometimes underperformed a strong model-free baseline early in training. This indicated that ignoring predictive uncertainty biases learning. Furthermore, WIMLE's performance remained stable and improved with increasing rollout horizons (from H=1 to H=8), which typically causes severe degradation in traditional model-based methods. This suggests that uncertainty-aware weighting effectively mitigates model bias from error accumulation at longer horizons.
- **Impact of IMLE-based Multi-modality:** Another ablation study on Humanoid-run compared WIMLE (using an IMLE world model) with an otherwise identical variant employing a unimodal Gaussian world model (MBPO-style), both utilizing uncertainty-aware weighting. The IMLE-based variant significantly outperformed the Gaussian model, highlighting the importance and efficacy of modeling multi-modal transition dynamics for complex control. Analysis of weight dynamics showed that IMLE's weights were initially lower (reflecting higher uncertainty) and increased as training progressed, indicating growing model confidence and better calibration. In contrast, the unimodal Gaussian model's weights remained relatively flat, suggesting limited calibration and an inability to accurately capture the evolution of uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Additional Findings

- **Imagined-Only Training:** A variant of WIMLE where the critic and actor were trained exclusively on model-generated rollouts (without real environment transitions) showed performance comparable to the standard WIMLE configuration, underscoring the quality and utility of the synthetic trajectories generated by the IMLE world model.
- **Wall-Clock Efficiency:** Despite the computational overhead associated with ensemble training, WIMLE's superior sample efficiency resulted in a faster time-to-solution compared to model-free baselines like BRO (the fastest model-free competitor), particularly because it drastically reduced the time spent collecting real-world data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WIMLE represents an advancement in model-based reinforcement learning by addressing several fundamental challenges that have historically limited its practical applicability and performance relative to model-free methods.

The **significance** of this work lies in:
- **Effective Multi-modal Dynamics Modeling:** By integrating Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE), WIMLE successfully learns stochastic and multi-modal world models. This directly tackles the "regression to the mean" problem faced by unimodal models, which often yield implausible predictions in environments with complex or inherently stochastic dynamics (e.g., contact-rich manipulation or partially observable settings). The ability to capture diverse possible outcomes from a single state-action pair enhances the realism and utility of synthetic rollouts.
- **Robust Uncertainty Quantification and Integration:** WIMLE's ensemble-based approach to estimate predictive uncertainty provides a reliable measure of model confidence. Crucially, the theoretical analysis supports that using inverse-variance weighting in the RL objective preserves the Bellman fixed point while minimizing the variance of learned parameters. This reduces model bias and allows for stable learning even with long-horizon synthetic rollouts, a common failure mode for prior MBRL methods due to compounding errors.
- **Demonstrated Performance Gains:** The empirical results across 40 challenging continuous control tasks (DeepMind Control, MyoSuite, HumanoidBench) highlight WIMLE's superior sample efficiency and competitive or better asymptotic performance. Notably, its significant improvements on high-dimensional and complex locomotion tasks, such as Humanoid-run and HumanoidBench, indicate its robustness in difficult domains.
- **Computational Efficiency for Online RL:** Unlike some other advanced generative models (e.g., diffusion models) that rely on iterative sampling, IMLE's non-iterative inference allows for fast online rollouts, making WIMLE practical for real-time applications where rollout throughput is critical. The parallel training of ensembles further allows for scaling with available computational resources.

The **potential impact** of WIMLE is substantial:
- **Accelerated Real-World Learning:** By drastically reducing the need for real environment interactions, WIMLE could enable more efficient and safer deployment of RL agents in real-world applications where data collection is costly, time-consuming, or risky (e.g., robotics, autonomous systems, medical applications).
- **Enhanced MBRL Competitiveness:** WIMLE demonstrates that model-based approaches, when properly accounting for multi-modality and uncertainty, can consistently outperform or compete with state-of-the-art model-free methods, renewing interest in the potential of world models for challenging control problems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** WIMLE's methodology opens avenues for further research. For instance, exploring other uses of the uncertainty-aware world models beyond synthetic rollout generation (e.g., planning, policy-gradient formulations), extending the approach to handle image-based observations, or developing adaptive mechanisms for dynamic rollout horizon selection based on model confidence, could lead to even more capable and versatile MBRL systems.

In conclusion, WIMLE contributes a principled and empirically validated method for building uncertainty-aware, multi-modal world models, significantly advancing the state-of-the-art in sample-efficient continuous control.
