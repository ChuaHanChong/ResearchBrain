# WIMLE: Uncertainty-Aware World Models with IMLE for Sample-Efficient Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Simon Fraser University's Apex Lab developed WIMLE, an uncertainty-aware model-based reinforcement learning framework that enhances sample efficiency in continuous control by employing an Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE) world model to capture multi-modal dynamics and integrate predictive uncertainty weighting. WIMLE demonstrated superior sample efficiency and competitive asymptotic performance across 40 complex continuous-control tasks, achieving over 50% improvement in sample efficiency on tasks like Humanoid-run.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning often requires a substantial number of environment interactions, leading to high costs and slow learning in real-world applications.
- Existing model-based RL (MBRL) methods struggle with compounding model errors and fail to adequately capture multi-modal dynamics, resulting in unrealistic "regression to the mean" predictions.
- Many MBRL approaches lack mechanisms to quantify or integrate predictive uncertainty, leading to overconfident predictions and biased policy learning from synthetic data.

## Method

- Develops an ==Implicit Maximum Likelihood== Estimation (IMLE)-based world model capable of learning stochastic and multi-modal transition dynamics without costly iterative sampling.
- Employs an ensemble of IMLE world models to estimate predictive uncertainty for each synthetic transition by measuring the standard deviation of their predictions.
- Integrates these uncertainty estimates into the RL objective as confidence weights, which inversely scale the contribution of synthetic data based on its predicted reliability.

## Results

- Achieved superior sample efficiency and competitive or improved asymptotic performance across 40 challenging continuous-control tasks, including the DeepMind Control Suite, MyoSuite, and HumanoidBench.
- Demonstrated over **50%** improvement in sample efficiency on high-dimensional and complex tasks such as Humanoid-run (DMC) and Myo-key-turn-hard (MyoSuite) compared to strong baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the IMLE-based multi-modality modeling and uncertainty-aware weighting are crucial for WIMLE's performance, stability, and ability to effectively utilize longer synthetic rollouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Uncertainty-aware weighting in the RL objective, with strictly positive weights, preserves the Bellman fixed point while reducing the variance of learned parameters, improving sample efficiency.
> - IMLE effectively captures the inherent multi-modal dynamics of complex environments, avoiding the "regression to the mean" problem without the computational overhead of iterative generative models.
> - Integrating predictive uncertainty allows for stable learning and mitigates the adverse effects of compounding model errors, enabling the use of longer synthetic rollout horizons without performance degradation.
