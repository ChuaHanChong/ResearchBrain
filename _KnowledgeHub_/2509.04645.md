# Planning from Point Clouds over Continuous Actions for Multi-object Rearrangement

> [!summary] Summary
> A hybrid framework, Search over Point cloud Object Transformations (SPOT), enables robots to plan multi-object rearrangement tasks directly from point clouds using continuous actions. It integrates learned models within an A* search, achieving high task success rates in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Traditional symbolic planning struggles with real-world continuity, requiring extensive human effort for discrete object and action definitions.
- End-to-end learning policies often lack long-horizon reasoning capabilities and sufficient generalization for complex, multi-step manipulation tasks.
- Robots face challenges in planning from partial 3D observations and accounting for real-world discrepancies between planned and executed actions.

## Method

- Introduces **Search over Point cloud Object Transformations (SPOT)**, a hybrid framework combining ==A* search== with learned models.
- Plans directly in continuous ==SE(3) transformation== spaces from raw point cloud observations without symbolic abstractions.
- Utilizes learned ==Object and Placement Suggesters== to efficiently propose actions and a ==Model Deviation Estimator== to ensure physical plausibility.

## Results

- Achieved **88%** planning and **63%** execution success rates for complex block stacking tasks in simulation.
- Demonstrated **100%** planning and **86%** execution success rates for real-world table bussing scenarios.
- Outperformed end-to-end imitation learning (3D Diffusion Policy) and ablation baselines by a large margin on multi-step tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating learned deep learning models into classical A* search effectively guides exploration in high-dimensional continuous action spaces for long-horizon tasks.
> - Planning directly from raw point clouds avoids the need for manual discretization or privileged object pose information, increasing system flexibility.
> - Incorporating an explicit Model Deviation Estimator into the search cost improves plan robustness against real-world execution uncertainties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation, particularly for long-horizon tasks involving multiple objects, presents significant challenges in understanding 3D scenes and reasoning about sequences of actions. Traditional approaches to long-horizon robot manipulation often fall into two main categories: symbolic planning and learning-based methods.

Symbolic planning relies on discrete, symbolic descriptions of objects, their relationships, and actions, along with predefined preconditions and post-conditions. While effective for certain structured problems, this approach faces difficulties in real-world environments due to the challenge of obtaining complete scene knowledge, the need for human annotation for task-specific definitions, and the inherent discretization of continuous state and action spaces. These limitations restrict its applicability to open-set problems where predefined ontologies may not exist.

Another line of research involves planning from raw sensory observations, such as point clouds. Some works convert point cloud observations into relational state abstractions for symbolic planning, while others encode the scene into a learned latent space for planning. These methods often still operate with discrete sets of actions or object relationships or plan in an abstract, learned space. For instance, Simeonov et al. plan directly from sensory observations but require a high-level plan skeleton as subgoals, and Liu et al. use language commands to define multi-object relational constraints for goal configurations.

Multi-object rearrangement has also been approached through skill sequencing, often employing sampling-based optimization, or language-guided planning which typically assume libraries of skills and predicates. In contrast, end-to-end policy learning methods [e.g., 3D Diffusion Policy, 33] aim to learn direct mappings from observations to actions, but often struggle with long-horizon reasoning and generalizing across diverse goal configurations.

This paper introduces Search over Point cloud Object Transformations (SPOT) as a hybrid learning-and-planning approach that aims to address the limitations of these existing methods. SPOT differentiates itself by:
1. **Direct Planning in Continuous Space:** It plans directly over 3D transformations of raw point cloud observations, eliminating the need for discretizing actions or object relationships or converting to symbolic representations.
2. **Leveraging Learned Priors:** It integrates learned models (object and placement suggesters) trained on demonstrations to guide search in high-dimensional, continuous action spaces, thereby reducing search complexity.
3. **Operating on Partial Observations:** SPOT operates on partially observed point clouds without requiring privileged ground-truth information like object poses.
4. **Flexible Action Representation:** Unlike methods that assume a library of skills, SPOT assumes a fundamental "skill" of transforming an object from an initial pose to a target pose.
5. **Incorporating Execution Awareness:** It uses a Model Deviation Estimator (MDE) to account for potential discrepancies between planned actions and their physical execution, a common challenge in real-world robotics.

By combining the long-horizon reasoning capabilities of search-based planning with the ability of learned models to interpret complex sensory data and suggest relevant continuous actions, SPOT seeks to overcome the trade-offs inherent in previous approaches, providing a more robust and adaptable framework for multi-object rearrangement in realistic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties in achieving long-horizon robot manipulation, particularly in multi-object rearrangement tasks such as table bussing or packing items onto a shelf. These tasks require a robot to accurately perceive a 3D physical scene and predict the effects of a sequence of actions to reach a desired configuration.

The paper identifies several key challenges with existing methodologies:

- **Discretization of Continuous Spaces:** Traditional task planning methods necessitate the discretization of continuous state and action spaces into symbolic descriptions (e.g., "cup is on plate," "move_cup_to_table"). This abstraction is a significant bottleneck, as real-world environments are inherently continuous.
- **Symbolic Scene Descriptions:** Obtaining accurate and complete symbolic descriptions of objects, their relationships, and the dynamics of actions for a real-world scene is challenging. These descriptions often assume total knowledge and are task-specific, making them difficult to scale to diverse or open-set problems without extensive human annotation.
- **Limitations of Learning-Only Approaches:** Pure policy learning methods, while capable of operating directly from sensory data, often struggle with the long-horizon nature of rearrangement tasks. They may require vast amounts of task-specific data and can find it difficult to generalize to novel goal configurations or complex sequences of actions.
- **Partial Observability and Real-World Dynamics:** Robots operate with partial observations of the environment, and the actual effects of actions can deviate from simple models due to complex physics or unexpected interactions. Existing methods may not adequately account for these discrepancies.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are to develop a robust and generalizable framework for multi-object rearrangement that:

1. **Plans Directly from Point Clouds:** Eliminate the reliance on privileged ground-truth information or symbolic abstractions by planning directly from partially observed 3D point clouds.
2. **Operates in Continuous Action Spaces:** Reason over a sequence of continuous-valued actions (SE(3) transformations) without the need for manual discretization of actions or object relationships.
3. **Leverages Learned Priors:** Integrate learned models, trained on domain-specific demonstrations, to efficiently guide the search process in high-dimensional continuous action spaces. These priors aim to capture common sense about feasible object movements and placements without being explicitly task-specific.
4. **Ensures Physical Plausibility:** Incorporate a mechanism to assess the physical plausibility and predictability of proposed actions, thereby guiding the search towards transitions that are likely to execute reliably in the real world.
5. **Demonstrates Effectiveness Across Tasks and Domains:** Evaluate the proposed approach on a variety of multi-object rearrangement tasks in both simulated and real-world environments to demonstrate its generalizability and practical applicability.

The overall motivation is to create a hybrid system that combines the strengths of classical planning (systematic exploration, long-horizon reasoning) with the adaptability and perceptual capabilities of modern deep learning (interpreting raw sensory data, learning complex relationships and preferences), thereby addressing a fundamental challenge in robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Search over Point cloud Object Transformations (SPOT), is a hybrid learning-and-planning framework designed to solve multi-object rearrangement tasks. It operates by performing A* search directly within the continuous space of object transformations, guided by learned models that provide domain-specific priors.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is defined as finding a sequence of object pose reconfigurations to achieve a goal. The observation space is a partially-observed 3D point cloud ($o \in R^{n \times 3}$) of the scene, segmented into $M$ objects ($X = \{x_1, \dots, x_M\}$). Object segmentation and semantic class assignment are performed using an image segmentation method combined with an object detector. The action space is object-centric and continuous, where an action ($a_t = \langle x_t, T_t \rangle$) is an SE(3) transformation ($T_t \in R^{4 \times 4}$) applied to an object $x_t$. A task is complete when the scene point cloud satisfies a goal function $G: S \to \{0, 1\}$. The system assumes access to a limited, non-task-specific demonstration dataset $D = \{o_i, a_i, o_{i+1}\}$ for training learned components.

**System Overview (Figure 3):**
SPOT takes an RGB-D image, object names, and a goal function as input. It generates a segmented point cloud and then performs A* search over object transformations. Node expansion in the A* search is guided by two learned components: an object suggester and a placement suggester. A model deviation estimator further biases the search towards physically plausible actions. The output is a sequence of object transformations, executed by a robot manipulator using pick-and-place primitives and grasp pose generation (Contact-GraspNet for real world, heuristics for simulation).

**Learned Components for Guiding A* Search:**

1. **Learned Object Suggester ($p_\phi(x|o)$):**
 - **Purpose:** Predicts which object(s) in the current scene are feasible to move next, given the point cloud observation $o$. It learns a distribution over objects based on their movability observed in demonstrations.
 - **Training:** Supervised learning from the offline, environment-specific, non-task-specific dataset $D$. For each transition $(o_i, \langle x_i, T_i \rangle, o_{i+1})$, a binary query mask is created for $x_i$ (labeled 1) and other objects (labeled 0).
 - **Architecture:** PointNet++ network.
 - **Inference:** For each object $x_i$ in the scene, a query mask is generated, and the model outputs a score $\tilde{p}_{x_i}$. These scores are normalized into a probability distribution $p_\phi(x)$.

2. **Learned Placement Suggester ($p_\theta(T|x, o)$):**
 - **Purpose:** Given a selected object $x$ and the scene $o$, samples diverse and task-relevant SE(3) transformations $T$ representing potential placement locations.
 - **Implementation:** TAXPose-D, a method for relative placement tasks, based on a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE). It maps the input point cloud $o$ to a latent distribution over the cloud, conditioned on the object $x$. A latent encoding $z$ is sampled from this distribution and decoded into a transformation $T$.
 - **Multi-modality:** TAXPose-D represents the latent space as a discrete multinomial distribution, aiding in handling multi-modal placements (e.g., placing a cup inside a bowl vs. on a plate). Iterative re-scoring helps sample diverse placements by reducing the probability of sampling neighboring embeddings.
 - **Training Data:** The dataset $D$ consists of sequences of RGB-D images, with transformations extracted using Grounded Segment Anything and CoTracker3 to compute rigid transformations between stationary states of objects.
 - **Architecture:** Follows TAXPose-D specifications.

3. **Model Deviation Estimator (MDE, $\delta(o, a)$):**
 - **Purpose:** Predicts the deviation between an expected next state (from the placement suggester) and the actual next state if an action $a=\langle x, T \rangle$ is executed in state $o$. It guides the search away from actions that are unlikely to match environmental dynamics.
 - **Motivation:** The placement suggester assumes only the selected object moves, but in reality, other objects might be affected (e.g., a cup falling off a moved plate).
 - **Training:** Uses an offline dataset of robot-executed plans. Ground-truth deviation is calculated as a normalized sum of object-wise Chamfer distances between the expected next point cloud (after applying $T$ to $x$) and the actually observed next point cloud.
 - **Architecture:** PointNet++ network.

**A\* Search Over Scene Configurations:**

- **Node Definition:** A node $n$ in the search tree is defined by $[o_n, x_n, T_n, n_p]$, representing the point cloud state, the action taken to reach it, and its parent.
- **Node Expansion:** From a current node $n$, child nodes $n'$ are generated by sampling an object $x_{n'}$ using $p_\phi(x_{n'}|o_n)$ and a transformation $T_{n'}$ using $p_\theta(T_{n'}|x_{n'}, o_n)$.
 - For each object in the scene, $k$ potential transformations are sampled (e.g., $k=10$ for block stacking).
 - To prevent infinite loops, the object that was just moved is excluded from being chosen for the next action.
- **Value Function ($f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$):**
 - **Cost Function ($g(n)$):** A weighted sum of four components, designed to be task-agnostic:
 1. **Action Cost ($C_a(n)$):** A constant cost per step (0.01).
 2. **Collision Cost ($C_c(n)$):** Measures the voxel overlap percentage of the moving object with other objects, discouraging placements leading to collisions.
 3. **Deviation Cost ($C_d(n)$):** The output of the MDE, $\delta(o_n, a_n)$, which penalizes actions with high predicted deviation from expected outcomes.
 4. **Probability Cost ($C_p(n)$):** Calculated as $1 - p_\theta(T_n|x_n, o_{n_p})p_\phi(x_n|o_{n_p})$, favoring actions that are more likely according to the learned suggesters.
 - **Heuristic Function ($h(n)$):** Task-specific and calculated directly from the point cloud observation of node $n$, estimating object poses from point clouds.
 - **Block Stacking:** Discrete count of "out of place" blocks, determined by estimated pose, height, and alignment relative to the goal.
 - **Table Bussing:** Continuous sum of Euclidean distances (XY plane) of each object's center to a target point (e.g., center of a plate). The heuristic is designed to underestimate the distance to the goal.
- **Goal Function ($G(o_n)$):** Task-specific function: $h(n)=0$ for block stacking; for table bussing, it checks if all objects are stacked on a plate within a threshold.
- **Multi-goal A\*:** The search continues until the open list is empty, a maximum node expansion limit is reached, or a hyperparameter $m$ goals are found. Among the found goal nodes, one is selected based on a score function (e.g., lowest collision cost or best alignment), and its corresponding path is output as the plan.
- **Plan Execution:** The robot executes the sequence of actions. Grasp poses are generated, and a robot controller performs pick-and-place movements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SPOT focused on its ability to generate goal-satisfying plans and achieve reliable task execution success rates in both simulated and real-world environments.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Task Planning Success Rate:** Percentage of trials where SPOT generated a plan that satisfied the goal condition.
- **Task Execution Success Rate:** Percentage of trials where the robot successfully executed the generated plan to achieve the goal in the environment.

**Experimental Tasks:**
1. **Block Stacking (Simulation):** Stacking cubes in a specific order, involving both unstacking and restacking from randomized initial configurations.
2. **Constrained Packing (Simulation):** Placing four blocks inside a cupboard, requiring careful spatial reasoning.
3. **Table Bussing (Real World):** Arranging dinnerware (plates, bowls, cups) onto one plate, including unstacking, from randomized initial configurations.

**Simulation Results (Block Stacking):**
- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** SPOT achieved an 88% task planning success rate and a 63% task execution success rate.
- **Impact of Search (Table 2):** Compared to a **Beam Search** ablation (which performs myopic selection), SPOT demonstrated significantly higher execution success rates, especially for more complex tasks. For 3-step tasks, Beam Search achieved 7% execution success, while SPOT achieved 58%. For 4-step tasks, Beam Search failed completely (0%), whereas SPOT achieved 30% success. This highlights the importance of A* search in exploring diverse action sequences beyond immediate greedy choices.
- **Impact of Guided Search (Table 1):** Compared to **Random Rollouts**, which expands a similar number of nodes without guidance, SPOT exhibited substantially higher planning success (88% vs. 26%) and execution success (63% vs. 21%). This demonstrates that the A* search, guided by its cost and heuristic functions, is critical for efficient pathfinding.
- **Importance of Object Suggester (Table 1):** The ablation **"No Object Suggester"** (replacing the learned object probability distribution with a uniform one) showed slightly lower performance (86% planning, 60% execution) compared to SPOT, indicating that the learned object suggester contributes positively by guiding the search towards relevant objects to move.
- **Comparison to 3D Diffusion Policy (Table 2):** SPOT significantly outperformed the **3D Diffusion Policy** (DP3), an end-to-end imitation learning baseline, especially as task complexity increased. DP3 achieved 13% execution success for 2-step tasks but 0% for 3- and 4-step tasks. SPOT's performance for these complexities was 78%, 58%, and 30% respectively, demonstrating the advantage of a planning-based approach for long-horizon, multi-step tasks over purely reactive policies.

**Simulation Results (Constrained Packing):**
- **Comparison to Points2Plans (Figure 5):** SPOT achieved 100% task planning success and 84% task execution success on this task. This performance slightly surpassed that of Points2Plans and significantly outperformed eRDTransformer and Pairwise-RD.

**Real-world Results (Table Bussing):**
- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** SPOT achieved a 100% task planning success rate and an 86% task execution success rate in the real-world table bussing environment. This indicates that the method generalizes beyond simulation and effectively handles real-world sensory input and robot execution challenges.

**Search Metrics (Table 3):**
- Analysis of search time, generated nodes, and expanded nodes in the block stacking environment showed that these metrics generally increased with task complexity (number of steps in an optimal plan). For a 4-step task, SPOT averaged 49 seconds of search time, generating 1711 nodes and expanding 86.

**Qualitative Analysis: Efficient Path-finding (Figure 6):**
- A qualitative observation revealed that SPOT could sometimes find more efficient plans than the human demonstrations it was trained on. This is attributed to the learned object and placement suggesters capturing a goal-agnostic distribution of relevant actions, allowing the A* search to explore novel, shorter paths to the goal (e.g., strategically placing a cup aside to allow direct stacking of bowls, rather than explicitly moving it off a plate first).

In summary, the results indicate that SPOT successfully integrates learned priors with A* search to enable long-horizon, multi-object rearrangement planning directly from point clouds in continuous action spaces. It demonstrates superior performance compared to both ablation baselines (lacking search or specific learned components) and alternative policy-learning approaches, highlighting the efficacy of its hybrid learning-and-planning paradigm across diverse tasks and environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper introduces a significant contribution to the field of robot manipulation, particularly in addressing the challenges of long-horizon planning in complex, continuous, and partially observed 3D environments.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

- **Advancement in Hybrid Robot Intelligence:** SPOT effectively bridges the gap between classical symbolic planning and modern learning-based control. By integrating learned domain-specific priors (object and placement suggesters, model deviation estimator) within an A* search framework, it leverages the strengths of both paradigms. This hybrid approach enables robots to perform complex, multi-step reasoning while directly interpreting raw sensory data and generating continuous actions, a long-standing goal in robotics research.
- **Reduced Need for Manual Engineering and Discretization:** A core contribution is the elimination of the need for manual discretization of continuous state and action spaces or the explicit definition of symbolic object relationships. This reduces the significant human engineering effort traditionally required for complex manipulation tasks, making the system more scalable and adaptable to new environments and objects. The ability to plan directly from point clouds without ground-truth object poses further enhances this autonomy.
- **Improved Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate that SPOT significantly outperforms purely policy-learning approaches on tasks requiring multiple steps of reasoning, particularly as task complexity increases. This highlights the critical role of search-based planning for tasks that necessitate foresight and sequential decision-making beyond what reactive, end-to-end policies can typically achieve.
- **Robustness to Real-World Variability:** The incorporation of a Model Deviation Estimator (MDE) is crucial for improving the robustness of planned actions. By predicting discrepancies between expected and actual execution outcomes, SPOT can proactively guide its search towards more physically plausible and reliable action sequences, which is essential for deployment in unstructured real-world settings where ideal dynamics cannot always be assumed.
- **Efficient Exploration and Pathfinding:** The qualitative analysis suggests that SPOT can find more efficient plans than those demonstrated by humans. This indicates that by learning generalizable priors from demonstrations, the system can explore a broader solution space and discover novel, optimized action sequences rather than merely imitating. This capability is valuable for enhancing robot autonomy and efficiency.
- **Generalizability Across Diverse Tasks:** The evaluation across multiple rearrangement tasks (block stacking, constrained packing, table bussing) in both simulation and the real world demonstrates the versatility of the framework. This suggests that the underlying principles and learned components are generalizable, paving the way for applications in a wider range of manipulation scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies clear limitations and avenues for future work, such as optimizing search efficiency (e.g., using Partial Expansion A* or MCTS), implementing replanning for dynamic environments, and improving the learned suggesters. This indicates that SPOT provides a solid foundational framework upon which further advancements in continuous planning and manipulation can be built.

Overall, this work makes strides towards enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks more autonomously and adaptably, by elegantly combining advanced learning techniques with established planning methodologies.
