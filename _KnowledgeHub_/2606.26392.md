# MPC-Injection: Biasing Off-Policy Locomotion RL Toward Controller-Induced Behavior Basins

> [!summary] Summary
> MPC-Injection biases off-policy reinforcement learning for robot locomotion by strategically incorporating Model Predictive Control-generated trajectories into the replay buffer. This method guides RL agents toward stable, deployable gaits defined by an MPC, achieving locomotion quality comparable to reward shaping and adversarial imitation learning while substantially reducing engineering complexity, with a 25% injection ratio proving consistently effective.

## Problem

- Off-policy Reinforcement Learning for robot locomotion often produces high-reward policies that exhibit kinematically or dynamically undesirable behaviors, rendering them undeployable.
- Guiding RL towards designer-preferred behaviors typically involves high engineering overhead through extensive reward shaping or complex imitation learning pipelines.
- Locomotion tasks frequently offer multiple high-return behavior basins, making it challenging for designers to specify a desired qualitative behavior using only simple, underspecified reward functions.

## Method

- Introduce ==MPC==-generated trajectories, exhibiting a desired locomotion behavior, into the off-policy RL replay buffer.
- Recompute rewards for MPC transitions using the RL task reward function, and sample minibatches uniformly from the combined buffer.
- Train standard off-policy RL algorithms (e.g., SAC, TD3) on this augmented data, implicitly guiding the actor and critic towards the MPC-preferred state distribution without additional loss terms or networks.

## Results

- Successfully biased policies for a 2D walker and a quadruped toward stable, designer-preferred gaits (upright walking, trotting) using a **25%** MPC-injection ratio, while pure RL converged to undeployable behaviors.
- Achieved comparable locomotion quality to traditional reward shaping and Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) methods, but with substantially lower engineering effort required for behavior specification.
- Showed greater robustness in sim-to-real transfer on a Go2 quadruped and applicability to novel morphologies like a 3-legged half-cheetah, where alternative biasing data is scarce.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicitly biasing the replay buffer's state distribution with controller data effectively steers off-policy RL towards designer-preferred behavior basins, even under underspecified task rewards.
> - An optimal intermediate injection ratio (around 25%) reliably transfers the desired behavior, ensuring stability and convergence while higher ratios can lead to erratic policy learning.
> - Existing MPC controllers, designed for desired dynamics and kinematics, can serve as a low-cost, effective interface for specifying high-level behavioral preferences to an RL agent.
