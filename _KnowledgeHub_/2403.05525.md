# DeepSeek-VL: Towards Real-World Vision-Language Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepSeek-VL introduces a family of open-source vision-language models designed for real-world applications, achieving state-of-the-art or competitive performance against other open-source models on various benchmarks while successfully preserving the underlying language model's capabilities and efficiently processing high-resolution images. The models demonstrate strong natural image comprehension and logical understanding, often approaching the performance of proprietary solutions.

## Problem

- A significant performance gap exists between open-source Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) and state-of-the-art proprietary models in real-world scenarios.
- Existing open-source LMMs often suffer from insufficient pretraining, reliance on academic instruction tuning data, and low-resolution image processing.
- Multimodal training frequently leads to a degradation of the underlying Large Language Model's (LLM) language capabilities.

## Method

- Developed **DeepSeek-VL**, an open-source ==Vision-Language Model== (1.3B and 7B parameters) built upon the DeepSeek LLM series, focusing on real-world vision and language understanding.
- Curated extensive and diverse datasets for pretraining and ==supervised fine-tuning==, guided by a use-case taxonomy of real user scenarios including web screenshots, PDFs, OCR, and charts.
- Designed a ==hybrid vision encoder== combining ==SigLIP-L== for coarse semantics and ==SAM-B== for high-resolution (1024x1024) detailed features, integrated with a ==multi-stage training strategy== (with a ==modality warm-up==) to preserve language abilities.

## Results

- DeepSeek-VL-7B achieved state-of-the-art or competitive performance among open-source models on various multimodal benchmarks (e.g., **70.4** on SeedBench), outperforming most in natural image comprehension and mathematical logic.
- DeepSeek-VL models successfully preserved or even enhanced language abilities compared to their text-only DeepSeek-LLM counterparts on benchmarks like HellaSwag, MMLU, and AGIEval.
- Human evaluation indicated DeepSeek-VL-7B performed better than other open-source models and comparably to GPT-4V in categories like Recognition, Conversion, and Commonsense Reasoning, highlighting its robust real-world applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extensive pretraining with diverse, real-world data and a use-case-driven instruction-tuning approach are crucial for bridging the performance gap with proprietary LMMs.
> - A hybrid vision encoder effectively processes high-resolution images to capture both semantic and detailed information efficiently within a fixed token budget.
> - A multi-stage training strategy, including a modality warm-up and joint language-multimodal pretraining with a high language data ratio (e.g., 7:3), is vital for preventing language degradation and achieving balanced multimodal capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Placement within the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, driven by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-4, Gemini, and Claude. LMMs like OpenAI's GPT-4V and Google's Gemini have demonstrated impressive capabilities in comprehending and acting upon instructions spanning both vision and language, enabling more natural human-like interactions. This has led to a surge in open-source LMMs (e.g., LLaVA, Qwen-VL, CogVLM) attempting to narrow the performance gap with their proprietary counterparts.

Despite substantial strides, particularly in benchmark performance, a significant divide persists between most open-source models and state-of-the-art closed-source models when it comes to real-world performance and user experience. DeepSeek-VL directly addresses this "significant divide," identifying several key reasons for the gap:
1. **Insufficient Pretraining:** Many open-source solutions over-allocate resources to instruction tuning, neglecting the importance of extensive pretraining for general intelligence and rich world knowledge.
2. **Lack of Real-World Data:** Instruction tuning often relies on academic datasets, which may not translate to authentic real-world usage experiences.
3. **Low-Resolution Processing:** Most existing models operate on relatively low resolutions (e.g., 336x336), limiting their ability to capture intricate details crucial for tasks like OCR or tiny object discernment in complex real-world scenarios.
4. **Language Capability Degradation:** Multimodal training often leads to a degradation of the underlying LLM's language skills, compromising the generalist nature of the model.

DeepSeek-VL positions itself as an open-source solution designed to tackle these challenges head-on. By emphasizing extensive pretraining, careful data curation (based on a use-case taxonomy), a novel architecture for high-resolution processing, and a training strategy that preserves language abilities, DeepSeek-VL aims to push the boundaries of open-source LMMs towards robust, general multimodal capabilities for practical applications, thereby narrowing the gap with proprietary models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DeepSeek-VL is to develop an **open-source Vision-Language Model specifically designed for real-world vision and language understanding applications**. The motivation stems from the observed performance gap between existing open-source LMMs and state-of-the-art proprietary models in practical, real-world scenarios. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of current open-source approaches by focusing on three key dimensions:

1. **Data Construction:** To ensure comprehensive representation of practical contexts and superior user experience, the objective is to build a diverse, scalable dataset extensively covering real-world scenarios like web screenshots, PDFs, OCR, charts, and knowledge-based content. A crucial part of this is creating a use case taxonomy from real user scenarios to guide the construction of an instruction-tuning dataset.
2. **Model Architecture:** To efficiently process high-resolution images while maintaining low computational overhead, the objective is to incorporate a hybrid vision encoder capable of capturing critical semantic and detailed information within a fixed token budget. This addresses the need for high-resolution processing in complex tasks.
3. **Training Strategy:** To preserve the strong language abilities of the underlying LLM and achieve balanced integration of modalities, the objective is to investigate an effective VL pretraining strategy. This involves integrating LLM training from the beginning and carefully managing the competitive dynamics between vision and language modalities through a gradual adjustment of the modality ratio.

Ultimately, the goal is to develop a family of models (1.3B and 7B) that offer superior user experiences as vision-language chatbots, achieve state-of-the-art or competitive performance across a wide range of visual-language benchmarks, and maintain robust performance on language-centric tasks, all while being publicly accessible to foster innovation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepSeek-VL's approach is meticulously designed across three main pillars: data construction, model architecture, and a multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Data Construction

The data strategy emphasizes both breadth for pretraining and quality/relevance for fine-tuning.

##### 3.1.1. Vision-Language Pretraining Data

The pretraining dataset is a diverse collection of public and proprietary sources, aiming to imbue the model with fundamental cross-modal understanding. It includes:
- **Interleaved image-text data:** From MMC4, Wikipedia, Wikihow, and in-house PDF/Epub textbooks, enhancing in-context learning of multi-modality inputs.
- **Image caption data:** From high-quality datasets like Capsfusion, TaiSu, and Detailed Caption.
- **Table and chart data:** Covering a wide range of public sources (Chart2text, Geo170K, Unichart, Ureader, M-paper, ScienceQA, ScreenQA, SciGraphQA, Paper2figure100k, Widget Captioning, Screen2words, Refexp) to enable general table and chart image understanding.
- **Web Code data:** Leveraging Websight and processing Jupyter notebooks from Stack to reconstruct code from graphical interfaces or visual plots (2 million image-code pairs, filtered to 1.1 million high-quality instances).
- **Document Optical Character Recognition (OCR) data:** Addressing the lack of large-scale public datasets, they constructed a comprehensive English and Chinese dataset from 1.4 million arXiv articles (rendered markdown) and 860K English/180K Chinese e-books/educational materials (HTML rendering).
- **Scene text OCR data:** Augmenting text recognition from images integrated into environments using multiple public datasets (ArT, MLT-17, LSVT, UberText, Coco-text, RCTW-17, ReCTS, TextOCR, OpenVINO, HierText).
- **Text-only corpus:** The same 2T text corpus used for DeepSeek-LLM is included to maintain language proficiency, accounting for a significant 70% of the pretraining data.

##### 3.1.2. Supervised Fine-tuning Data

The SFT data is curated to reflect real-world usage scenarios and teach specific downstream tasks. It consists of:
- **In-house Data:** Meticulously designed high-quality multimodal SFT data (including Chinese), based on a comprehensive taxonomy (Recognition, Conversion, Analysis, Commonsense Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, Evaluation, Multi-graph, Safety) derived from authentic test cases for GPT-4V and Gemini.
- **General Multi-modality Data:** Well-known open-source datasets like ShareGPT4V, LAION-GPTV, LVIS-Instruct4V, textOCR-GPT4V, LLaVA1.6-GPT4V, and IconQA.
- **Table and Chart Data:** Partial data from pretraining datasets (Ureader, Geo170K, ScienceQA, ScreenQA).
- **Web Code Data:** UI Code dataset from Screen-to-code.
- **Text-only SFT:** Data from DeepSeek-LLM to ensure comprehensive dialogue proficiency.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

DeepSeek-VL employs a modular architecture comprising a hybrid vision encoder, a vision-language adaptor, and a DeepSeek LLM as the language backbone.
- **Hybrid Vision Encoder:** To overcome limitations of single encoders and low-resolution inputs, a hybrid approach combines:
 - **SigLIP-L:** A text-aligned encoder for coarse semantic extraction from 384x384 resolution images.
 - **SAM-B:** A vision-only self-supervised encoder (pre-trained ViTDet image encoder) for capturing detailed low-level features from high-resolution 1024x1024 images.
 This hybrid encoder efficiently condenses a 1024x1024 image into 576 tokens, striking a balance between rich visual representation and token economy. The SAM-B feature map (64x64x256) is interpolated and processed by convolutional layers to a 24x24x1024 map (576x1024 tokens), then concatenated with SigLIP-L's 576x1024 feature map to produce 576 visual tokens with 2048 dimensions, which are then passed through GeLU activation and an embedding layer.
- **Vision-Language Adaptor:** A two-layer hybrid MLP bridges the vision encoder and the LLM. Distinct single-layer MLPs process high-resolution and low-resolution features separately, then their concatenated outputs are transformed into the LLM's input space by another MLP layer.
- **Language Model:** Built upon the DeepSeek LLM series (1.3B and 7B variants), which follow LLaMA's design (Pre-Norm, RMSNorm, SwiGLU FFN, Rotary Embedding) and use the DeepSeek-LLM tokenizer. The DeepSeek-VL models are initialized from intermediate checkpoints of DeepSeek's pretrained LLMs (500B and 2T tokens respectively).

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

The training pipeline consists of three consecutive stages, with a focus on preserving language abilities and optimizing multimodal integration. Only next token prediction loss on the language part is calculated.

##### 3.3.1. Stage 1: Training Vision-Language Adaptor

- **Objective:** Establish a conceptual link between visual and linguistic elements in the embedding space.
- **Approach:** Vision encoder and LLM are frozen; only the vision-language adaptor's parameters are trained.
- **Data:** 1.25 million image-text paired captions from ShareGPT4V and 2.5 million Document OCR rendering pairs.
- **Observation:** Expanding data scale at this stage does not yield benefits due to the adaptor's limited capacity.

##### 3.3.2. Stage 2: Joint Vision-Language Pretraining

- **Objective:** Enable the LLM to comprehend multimodal inputs while preserving language capabilities.
- **Approach:** Vision encoder remains frozen; LLM and VL adaptor are trainable.
- **Challenge:** Direct training with multimodal data leads to severe degradation of language metrics ("catastrophic forgetting").
- **Solution: Joint Language-Multimodal Training:** A large proportion of language data (at least 70%) is incorporated alongside multimodal data. Experiments showed a 7:3 ratio of language to multimodal data effectively balances linguistic performance and multimodal ability.
- **Modality Warm-up Strategy:** To counter model instability at the beginning of training, the language data ratio starts at 1 and gradually decreases to the target ratio (e.g., 0.7), preventing initial decline in language capabilities.
- **Scaling Pretraining:** For stable development, experiments are conducted on the 1.3B model before scaling to 7B. To address fluctuating generative metrics in small models, Multi-choice PPL evaluation is used, and a small proportion of SFT data is mixed into pretraining.
- **Modality Group Training:** To enhance efficiency, different modalities are sampled separately at each global step, meaning batches are either all language or all multimodal. This avoids bottlenecks from disparate processing times and improves efficiency by 20%.

##### 3.3.3. Stage 3: Supervised Fine-tuning

- **Objective:** Bolster instruction following and dialogue capabilities to create DeepSeek-VL-Chat models.
- **Approach:** The low-resolution vision encoder (SigLIP-L), VL adaptor, and language model are trained. SAM-B remains frozen due to GPU memory constraints.
- **Data:** A blend of multimodal SFT data (Table 2) and pure text dialogue data from DeepSeek-LLM.
- **Loss:** Only answers and special tokens are supervised, with system and user prompts masked.

#### 3.4. Hyperparameters and Infrastructure

DeepSeek-VL is trained using HAI-LLM, a distributed training framework. The architecture allows adaptation of pipeline parallelism by treating visual encoders and text embeddings as a single initial module. Canonical 3D parallelism techniques (like Megatron) are utilized, with minor modifications to balance load due to non-uniform execution times. DeepSeek-VL-7B took 5 days on 64 nodes (8 Nvidia A100 GPUs each), and 1B took 7 days on 16 nodes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DeepSeek-VL demonstrates strong performance across various evaluations, often surpassing or competing with state-of-the-art open-source models and, in some cases, approaching proprietary solutions.

#### 4.1. Public Multimodal Benchmarks Evaluation

DeepSeek-VL models were evaluated using generation-based evaluation (greedy decoding) on a wide array of benchmarks:
- **Comprehensive Understanding:** MMMU, CMMMU, MMBench, MMBench-CN, SeedBench, MMV.
- **Chart/Table Understanding:** OCRBench.
- **Hallucination:** POPE.
- **Scientific Problem Solving:** ScienceQA, MathVista.

**Key results:**
- **DeepSeek-VL-7B:** Achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance among open-source models of similar size. It outperforms most open-source models on MMB, MMC, and SEEDbench (e.g., 70.4 on SeedBench vs. GPT-4V's 71.6, demonstrating powerful natural image comprehension). It also surpasses all open-source models in mathematical logic but still lags behind proprietary models like GPT-4V on MathVista (36.1 vs. 47.8).
- **DeepSeek-VL-1.3B:** Significantly outperforms models of comparable size, even achieving results comparable to 7B open-source models on MathVista (31.1), despite using almost half the parameters (1.3B vs. 2.7B MobileVLM V2). This highlights its robust natural image comprehension and logical understanding.

#### 4.2. Public Language Benchmarks Evaluation

Perplexity-based evaluation (for multiple-choice) and generation-based evaluation (for free text) were used for:
- **Multi-subject multiple-choice:** MMLU.
- **Language understanding and reasoning:** HellaSwag.
- **Language modeling:** Pile.
- **Math:** GSM8K.
- **Code:** MBPP.
- **Standardized exams:** AGIEval.

**Key results:**
- **Language Preservation:** DeepSeek-VL-7B generally performs comparably to or even surpasses the pure text DeepSeek-LLM-7B on benchmarks like HellaSwag (68.4 vs. 68.5), MMLU (52.4 vs. 49.4), and AGIEval (27.8 vs. 19.3). This indicates successful preservation of language abilities and even suggests that multimodal training can aid certain language tasks.
- **Limitations:** A decline in performance is observed in mathematics (GSM8K: 55.0 vs. 63.0 for DeepSeek-LLM-7B), suggesting a remaining competitive relationship between modalities, possibly due to the model's capacity (7B).

#### 4.3. Human Evaluation

An independent dataset of 100 questions, categorized by the in-house SFT taxonomy, was used for human evaluation, comparing DeepSeek-VL-7B with InternLM-XComposer2-VL, CogVLM, and GPT-4V.
- **Qualitative Assessment:** GPT-4V demonstrated exceptional performance. DeepSeek-VL-7B achieved better overall performance than other open-source models, coming close to GPT-4V in Recognition, Conversion, and Commonsense Reasoning. All open-source models lagged significantly behind GPT-4V in logical reasoning.
- **GPT-4V-based Evaluation:** A comparative assessment using GPT-4V (as a judge) across 99 test samples showed DeepSeek-VL's responses were preferred in over 60% of instances when compared to open-source models (Fuyu-8B, CogVLM-17B, InternLM-XComposer2-VL). It demonstrated comparably exceptional performance against GPT-4V itself.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Projector Training Scale:** Expanding the Stage 1 (projector warmup) dataset did not improve performance, indicating capacity limitations of the adaptor itself.
- **Training Stages:** Combining all three stages (adaptor warmup, joint pretraining, SFT) yielded significantly better results than any subset, confirming the effectiveness and necessity of each stage, especially multimodal pretraining.
- **Modality Group Training:** Grouping modalities by batch (either all language or all multimodal) improved training efficiency by 20% without compromising performance.
- **Modality Warmup:** Gradually decreasing the language data ratio during Stage 2 prevented initial declines in language capabilities and led to superior final performance in both language and multimodal domains.
- **Vision Encoder Selection:** The hybrid SigLIP+SAM encoder significantly enhanced performance (reduced training loss) compared to single encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP+DINO).
- **Vision-Language Adaptor Design:** A hybrid MLP strategy for combining visual features (concatenating separately processed high- and low-resolution features along the embedding dimension) provided stable and improved performance, outperforming sequence concatenation or shared MLPs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepSeek-VL represents a significant stride in the development of open-source Large Multimodal Models, addressing critical limitations that have historically separated open-source solutions from proprietary counterparts. Its core significance lies in:

1. **Advancing Open-Source LMMs:** By releasing the 1.3B and 7B models, DeepSeek-AI provides powerful, accessible foundation models that can democratize research and application development in multimodal AI, fostering innovation within the broader AI community.
2. **Robust Real-World Performance:** The emphasis on diverse, real-world data curation (including challenging scenarios like web screenshots, PDFs, and OCR), high-resolution image processing, and a training strategy that maintains language proficiency directly translates into models with superior user experiences and practical applicability. This moves LMMs beyond mere benchmark performance towards genuine utility.
3. **Preservation of LLM Capabilities:** The "modality warm-up" strategy and careful balancing of language and multimodal data during pretraining are crucial methodological innovations. They address the common pitfall of language degradation, ensuring that DeepSeek-VL models remain strong generalist AI agents capable of both vision and language tasks.
4. **Efficient High-Resolution Processing:** The hybrid vision encoder (SigLIP+SAM) is a practical solution for integrating detailed visual information from high-resolution images (1024x1024) within a manageable token budget, expanding the scope of tasks LMMs can effectively handle.

The potential impact of DeepSeek-VL is far-reaching. It could enable more sophisticated AI assistants, enhance applications in document understanding, visual question answering, code generation from UI, embodied AI, and scientific research. By providing a strong open-source base, it facilitates the exploration of new multimodal tasks and the development of specialized LMMs without the barriers of proprietary models. Looking ahead, DeepSeek-AI's plans to scale DeepSeek-VL to larger sizes and integrate Mixture of Experts (MoE) technology suggest a continued commitment to pushing the boundaries of LMM efficiency and effectiveness, promising further advancements in the field.
