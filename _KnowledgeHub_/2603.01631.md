# Learning Thermal-Aware Locomotion Policies for an Electrically-Actuated Quadruped Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huazhong University of Science and Technology developed a reinforcement learning-based locomotion policy that extends the continuous operational duration of an electrically-actuated quadruped robot by proactively managing motor temperatures. The policy achieved over 27 minutes of operation on a Unitree A1 robot, compared to 7 minutes for a baseline, by adapting its posture to reduce joint torque and heat generation without compromising locomotion performance.

## Problem

- Electrically-actuated quadruped robots are limited in continuous operation due to motor overheating under prolonged or demanding conditions.
- Existing hardware-level solutions increase thermal capacity but do not proactively prevent overheating, while control-layer approaches for dynamically balancing legged robots have not adequately addressed the tight coupling between thermal dynamics and stability.
- Motor overheating triggers protection mechanisms, leading to performance degradation or complete task interruption, which hinders the robot's practical utility for long-duration tasks.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning (RL) framework was used to train locomotion policies that integrate a comprehensive whole-body thermal model, explicitly making the policy aware of motor temperatures and their dynamic evolution.
- A thermal-constraint reward function, based on a Control Barrier Function (CBF), was designed to penalize high motor temperatures and encourage the policy to adapt actions to prevent temperature exceedance.
- Domain randomization, including randomized initial motor temperatures (biased towards maximum) and eccentric payloads, was applied during training to reduce the sim-to-real gap and facilitate learning thermal management behaviors.

## Results

- A Unitree A1 robot, equipped with the proposed thermal-aware policy and carrying a 3 kg payload, operated continuously for over 27 minutes without thermal interruptions (until battery depletion), a fourfold increase from the 7-minute performance of a baseline policy.
- Simulation analysis revealed that the thermal-aware policy led to substantially lower peak and root-mean-square (RMS) joint torques and reduced joint angle excursions compared to the baseline, directly correlating with reduced heat generation.
- The thermal-aware controller adopted a modified locomotion posture with a greater pitch angle and higher center of mass, which enhanced vertical force transmission efficiency, allowing the robot to maintain stable locomotion while reducing overall torque demands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating motor temperature information and a CBF-based thermal-constraint reward into an RL framework enables quadruped robots to learn emergent behaviors that proactively manage heat generation.
> - The robot can strategically adapt its locomotion posture, such as adopting a greater pitch angle and higher center of mass, to modify the geometric mapping between joint torques and contact forces.
> - This postural adaptation improves the efficiency of transmitting vertical forces from joints to the ground, allowing the robot to maintain equivalent support with reduced overall joint torque requirements and a lower thermal load on motors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Quadrupedal robots have demonstrated capabilities for navigating complex terrains and are being considered for applications requiring continuous operation, such as inspection, transport, and disaster response. A significant challenge in deploying these robots for extended periods is the thermal management of their electric motors. These motors often operate under cyclic, high-torque loads, which generate heat. Factors such as varying payloads, elevated ambient temperatures, and limited cooling can lead to rapid heat accumulation, potentially triggering overheat protection mechanisms. This results in performance degradation or complete task interruption, thereby limiting the robot's sustainable operational capability. Existing robot design specifications for endurance and payload are often based on ideal cooling conditions, which may not reflect real-world operational environments.

Current approaches to address motor thermal limitations primarily fall into two categories: hardware-level enhancements and control-layer constraint handling. Hardware-level methods focus on improving heat dissipation or increasing thermal mass to delay overheating and sustain higher torque outputs. While these methods increase a motor's thermal envelope, they do not inherently account for the dynamic coupling between thermal behavior and power delivery, meaning they cannot proactively predict or prevent thermal violations under specific task loads or environmental conditions. Consequently, protective control strategies remain necessary to ensure continuous operation.

Control-layer approaches, conversely, integrate thermal models or temperature feedback into the robot's cost functions or safety constraints. This enables the control policies or controllers to actively manage thermal safety during execution. Prior research in this area has largely focused on fixed-base systems, such as robotic arms, where thermal constraints can often be addressed by simply limiting torque or power for static or slowly varying trajectories. However, in dynamically balancing legged robots, motor torque and power are critically linked to maintaining stability, foot-ground contact, and overall task execution. The tight coupling between thermal management and stability control in quadrupedal locomotion presents a more complex challenge, and effective methods for proactive prevention of motor overheating in these systems have been less explored. This paper aims to bridge this gap by proposing a control-layer solution specifically designed for thermal management in legged locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that electrically-actuated quadrupedal robots, despite their high mobility, are constrained by motor overheating under prolonged or demanding operational conditions. This limitation significantly hinders their ability to perform long-duration tasks in realistic environments. The paper identifies a gap in existing control methodologies for legged robots, where the tight coupling between thermal dynamics and dynamic stability has not been adequately addressed for proactive thermal management.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Thermal-Aware Locomotion Control Method:** To propose a novel control approach that explicitly considers motor temperatures and their dynamic evolution during quadrupedal locomotion.
2. **Integrate Thermal Dynamics into Reinforcement Learning (RL) Policies:** To incorporate motor temperature information into the state space of an RL policy, allowing the policy to learn behaviors that inherently manage heat generation.
3. **Design Thermal-Constraint Reward Functions:** To introduce specific reward functions that penalize high motor temperatures and encourage the policy to adapt its actions to prevent temperature exceedance, thereby delaying the activation of overheat protection. This involves converting the temperature constraint into a Control Barrier Function (CBF) for improved responsiveness.
4. **Validate on a Real Quadrupedal Robot:** To conduct both simulation and real-world experiments on a Unitree A1 quadruped robot to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in extending continuous operation duration while maintaining comparable locomotion performance.
5. **Analyze the Underlying Mechanism:** To investigate how the thermal-aware policy achieves heat management, specifically by examining changes in joint torques, ground reaction forces, and robot posture, and relating these to motor heating and force transmission efficiency.

Ultimately, the goal is to enable sustainable and extended operation of quadrupedal robots under various task and thermal conditions without relying on additional hardware cooling, thus enhancing their practical utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a reinforcement learning (RL) framework to develop thermal-aware locomotion policies, integrating a comprehensive thermal model and specific reward functions.

**A. Motor Thermal Model for Quadruped Robots**
The methodology begins with establishing accurate thermal models for the robot's motors.
- **Single-Motor Thermal Model:** A first-order thermal model is used to describe the temperature evolution of an individual motor. This model accounts for Joule heating due to winding current and thermal interaction with the ambient environment, incorporating equivalent thermal capacitance and resistance. The model is represented by a differential equation relating the rate of temperature change to the temperature difference with the environment and the heat generated.
- **Whole-body Thermal Model:** Recognizing the compact structure and thermal coupling between densely distributed joint motors in quadruped robots, a whole-body thermal model is constructed. This model treats each motor and the onboard computer as thermal nodes, with additional nodes for the environment. Thermal resistances between neighboring nodes are considered to model heat transfer. The temperature evolution of each node accounts for interactions with its neighbors, internal heat generation (from winding current, joint friction, and motor drivers), and environmental exchange. This comprehensive model is formulated into a discrete-time state-space representation, where the state vector includes temperatures of all nodes (motors, computer, environment) and the input vector represents generated heat at each node. The model parameters for the Unitree A1 robot are adopted from previous work.

**B. Thermal-Aware Locomotion Policy Design**
The core of the control method is an RL policy trained within a custom framework.
- **Training Framework:** The policy is trained using an asymmetric Actor-Critic framework to handle the Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) inherent in real-world robot operation. The Actor network outputs a 12-dimensional action (corrections to nominal joint angles), which are then fed to joint PD controllers to generate torques (in simulation) or motor currents (on the real robot). The optimization process involves Hybrid Internal Optimization (HIO) for the encoder network and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for the Actor and Critic networks.
 - **Observations:** The Actor receives proprioceptive observations (commanded velocity, angular velocity, gravity vector, joint positions, velocities, motor temperatures, previous action), estimated velocity, and a latent feature vector generated by an encoder from past observations. The Critic, used only during training, receives additional privileged observations including linear velocity and external forces.
- **Temperature Randomization:** To reduce the sim-to-real gap and create conditions conducive to learning thermal management, domain randomization is applied during training. The whole-body thermal model is integrated into the simulator to update motor temperatures in real-time. Motor torque, which is approximately proportional to current, is used to calculate the root-mean-square (RMS) torque as the effective thermal input to the model. Initial motor temperatures are randomized within a specific range, biased towards the maximum allowable temperature (Tmax) to increase exposure to critical thermal states early in training. Additionally, eccentric payloads and persistent external forces are randomized to simulate realistic operating conditions that induce motor overheating. Other randomized parameters include payload mass, center of mass (CoM) displacement, ground friction, initial joint positions, system delay, motor strength, and environment temperature.
- **Reward Function:** A reward function is designed to guide the Actor in learning thermal-aware locomotion.
 - **Thermal-Constraint Reward:** To proactively manage motor temperature, an inequality constraint (motor temperature below Tmax) is converted into a Control Barrier Function (CBF). This CBF condition effectively requires the motor temperature derivative to be non-positive when the temperature approaches or exceeds the threshold. A clipped motor temperature is introduced to mitigate initialization bias and focus the penalty on motors near the threshold. The reward penalizes violations of this CBF constraint for each motor. The weight coefficient for the CBF (`γ_T`) is determined to ensure that the constraint can be satisfied even with zero input to a hot motor.
 - **Other Rewards:** In addition to the thermal reward, the overall reward function includes terms for linear and angular velocity tracking, minimizing vertical velocity and roll-pitch angular velocity, maintaining body orientation, penalizing joint accelerations, promoting a target body height, ensuring foot clearance, and encouraging smooth actions and action rates.

**C. Experimental Setup**
- **Simulation Environment:** Training is conducted in the Isaac Gym simulator using 8192 parallel robot agents over 6000 epochs, with each episode lasting 20 seconds. The policies are trained for omnidirectional walking on flat ground with slight terrain noise (up to 2% incline). For sim-to-sim validation and detailed analysis (e.g., ground reaction forces), the Gazebo simulator is used.
- **Real-world Platform:** The trained policy is deployed on a Unitree A1 quadruped robot. In real-world experiments, the trained encoder network estimates base velocity, and motor temperature feedback is directly used by the policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of the proposed thermal-aware locomotion policy demonstrates its effectiveness in extending continuous operation duration and mitigating motor overheating risks, both in simulation and on a physical robot.

**A. Real-world Locomotion Results**
Experiments were conducted on a Unitree A1 robot carrying a fixed 3 kg payload and commanded to walk forward at 1 m/s on flat terrain.
- **Baseline Policy Performance:** The baseline policy, trained without the explicit motor temperature reward, operated for approximately 7 minutes before a front-left knee motor overheated, triggering protection and forcing the robot to stop.
- **Proposed Policy Performance:** In contrast, the robot controlled by the proposed thermal-aware policy operated continuously for over 27 minutes under identical conditions until its battery was depleted. Throughout this extended operation, the temperatures of all motors remained below the predefined maximum threshold (Tmax). This demonstrates a significant improvement in sustainable operational capability, extending endurance by a factor of approximately four without thermal interruptions. The policy also maintained comparable command-tracking performance during this period.

**B. Analysis of Thermal-Aware Locomotion Performance (Simulation)**
To understand the underlying mechanisms of the thermal-aware policy, a detailed analysis was conducted in simulation, providing access to internal robot states. A more challenging scenario was used, involving a 4 kg lateral load applied to the torso while commanding forward locomotion, to accentuate behavioral differences.

- **Joint Positions and Torques:** The proposed controller resulted in reduced joint angle excursions compared to the baseline. Critically, both the peak and root-mean-square (RMS) values of joint torques were substantially lower. Since motor heating is directly related to torque magnitude and duration, this reduction in torque expenditure is consistent with the observed decrease in motor temperature accumulation.
- **Ground Reaction Forces (GRFs):** Analysis of GRFs showed that the proposed controller exhibited a higher stepping frequency. Despite this, the peak vertical forces and the time-averaged vertical impulse were comparable to those generated by the baseline policy. Horizontal force components remained relatively small. This indicates that the proposed policy maintained adequate vertical support capability, crucial for stable locomotion, even with reduced joint torques.
- **Robot Posture:** The thermal-aware controller adopted a modified locomotion posture. Steady-state locomotion under the proposed controller featured a greater pitch angle and a higher center of mass compared to the baseline. This postural adaptation suggests a strategic modification of the robot's kinematic configuration.
- **Joint-to-Foot Jacobian Analysis:** The altered posture was further analyzed through the joint-to-foot Jacobian matrix. By comparing the absolute Jacobian differences between the proposed and baseline policies at various stance phases, it was observed that the vertical (z) components of the Jacobian were consistently positive. This implies that for a given joint torque, the proposed policy's posture enabled the generation of greater force in the vertical direction. Concurrently, horizontal components of the Jacobian were slightly reduced. Given the dominance of vertical support forces in trotting locomotion, this enhanced vertical force transmission efficiency is a key finding.

**C. Mechanism Explanation:**
The collective results from the simulation analysis indicate that the thermal-aware controller leveraged the robot's locomotion redundancy. By adapting its posture (specifically, adopting a greater pitch angle and higher CoM), the policy modified the geometric mapping between joint torques and contact forces. This led to an improved efficiency in transmitting vertical forces from the joints to the ground. Consequently, the robot could maintain equivalent vertical support and command-tracking performance with reduced overall joint torque requirements, thereby directly leading to a lower thermal load on the motors without degrading locomotion capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in enhancing the sustainable operational capability of electrically-actuated quadrupedal robots by addressing a fundamental limitation: motor overheating.

**A. Core Contributions:**
- **Proactive Thermal Management:** The proposed method introduces a proactive, control-layer approach to motor thermal management. Unlike hardware-centric solutions, it dynamically adapts the robot's behavior to prevent overheating, rather than merely delaying it. This represents a step towards intelligent energy and thermal management in legged robotics.
- **Extended Operational Endurance:** The empirical results demonstrate a substantial extension of continuous operation time, from approximately 7 minutes to over 27 minutes, under challenging payload conditions in a real-world scenario. This quadrupling of endurance directly translates to a greater practical utility for tasks requiring prolonged activity.
- **Integration of Thermal Models with Reinforcement Learning:** The work successfully integrates complex whole-body thermal dynamics into a reinforcement learning framework. This allows the policy to learn intricate relationships between actions, heat generation, and overall system thermal state, leading to emergent thermal-aware behaviors.
- **Mechanism-Based Understanding:** The detailed analysis of joint torques, ground reaction forces, and postural adaptations provides insight into *how* the policy achieves thermal management. The finding that posture adaptation enhances vertical force transmission efficiency, thereby reducing torque requirements for equivalent support, contributes to a deeper understanding of energy-efficient and thermally-aware locomotion principles.

**B. Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Real-world Deployment:** By mitigating the risk of thermal shutdowns, the research directly impacts the feasibility and reliability of deploying quadrupedal robots in demanding, long-duration applications such as remote inspection, logistics, and disaster response where continuous operation is critical.
- **Reduced Hardware Reliance:** The approach minimizes the need for additional, potentially heavy or complex, active cooling hardware, which can add to robot mass, cost, and design complexity. This promotes the development of lighter, more agile, and potentially more energy-efficient robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future investigations into multi-objective robot control, balancing performance metrics (speed, agility, stability) with operational constraints (thermal limits, energy consumption).

**C. Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges a current limitation: the policy tends to adopt conservative walking postures even when motor temperatures are low. This conservative behavior, while beneficial for thermal management, can restrict the robot's mobility and hinder performance on challenging terrains such as steep slopes or stairs, where more aggressive gaits or postures might be required for optimal navigation.

Future work aims to address this by incorporating multi-mode recognition. This would enable the robot to dynamically adapt its behavior based on the current motor temperature:
- **Performance Maximization:** Under low-temperature conditions, the robot could prioritize locomotion performance, utilizing more agile or powerful gaits.
- **Thermal Safety Prioritization:** As temperatures approach critical thresholds, the robot would transition to behaviors that prioritize motor thermal safety, similar to the current proposed method.
This multi-modal approach seeks to strike a balance between maximizing locomotion performance and ensuring long-term operational safety, thereby further enhancing the robot's adaptability and utility in diverse real-world scenarios.
