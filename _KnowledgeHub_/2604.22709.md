# Thinking Without Words: Efficient Latent Reasoning with Abstract Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at IBM Research AI developed Abstract Chain-of-Thought (Abstract-CoT), a post-training framework that replaces lengthy verbalized rationales with a short sequence of discrete, abstract tokens. This method achieves comparable or improved performance over traditional Chain-of-Thought while reducing reasoning token usage by up to 12 times across various benchmarks and language models.

## Problem

- Large Language Models using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting incur high inference latency and computational costs due to generating verbose natural language rationales.
- Explicit verbalized CoTs might not always faithfully represent an LLM's true internal reasoning processes.
- The extended length of verbalized reasoning sequences can "bloat" reinforcement learning traces, diminishing training efficiency.

## Method

- Introduce **Abstract Chain-of-Thought (Abstract-CoT)**, a mechanism where LLMs generate a short sequence of ==discrete abstract tokens== from a ==reserved vocabulary== instead of natural language rationales.
- Employ a two-stage post-training approach: a ==policy iteration-style warm-up== phase to initialize abstract token embeddings and a subsequent ==warm-started reinforcement learning (GRPO)== stage.
- Utilize an ==attention mask== during the warm-up's supervised fine-tuning to create an ==information bottleneck==, forcing the model's answer generation to rely on the abstract tokens rather than the explicit verbal CoT.

## Results

- Abstract-CoT achieved up to **12x fewer reasoning tokens** compared to verbalized Chain-of-Thought, with comparable or superior performance across MATH, AlpacaEval, and HotpotQA.
- Demonstrated generalizability, maintaining efficiency and performance benefits across different LLM families (**Qwen3, Granite**) and various model sizes.
- Both the policy iteration warm-up and the subsequent RL stages were found to be **critical**: warm-up alone makes abstract tokens meaningful, and RL optimizes their use for high-quality responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively learn and utilize purely non-verbal reasoning pathways through post-training, bypassing the need for pre-training on abstract vocabularies.
> - The learned abstract token sequences exhibit structural properties, such as a power-law distribution similar to natural languages and sensitivity to token permutation, suggesting meaningful compositional reasoning.
> - A two-stage training process combining policy iteration warm-up with warm-started reinforcement learning is essential for successfully optimizing the abstract reasoning mechanism.
