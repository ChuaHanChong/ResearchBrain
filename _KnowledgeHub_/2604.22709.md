# Thinking Without Words: Efficient Latent Reasoning with Abstract Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at IBM Research AI developed Abstract Chain-of-Thought (Abstract-CoT), a post-training framework that replaces lengthy verbalized rationales with a short sequence of discrete, abstract tokens. This method achieves comparable or improved performance over traditional Chain-of-Thought while reducing reasoning token usage by up to 12 times across various benchmarks and language models.

## Problem

- Large Language Models using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting incur high inference latency and computational costs due to generating verbose natural language rationales.
- Explicit verbalized CoTs might not always faithfully represent an LLM's true internal reasoning processes.
- The extended length of verbalized reasoning sequences can "bloat" reinforcement learning traces, diminishing training efficiency.

## Method

- Introduce **Abstract Chain-of-Thought (Abstract-CoT)**, a mechanism where LLMs generate a short sequence of ==discrete abstract tokens== from a ==reserved vocabulary== instead of natural language rationales.
- Employ a two-stage post-training approach: a ==policy iteration-style warm-up== phase to initialize abstract token embeddings and a subsequent ==warm-started reinforcement learning (GRPO)== stage.
- Utilize an ==attention mask== during the warm-up's supervised fine-tuning to create an ==information bottleneck==, forcing the model's answer generation to rely on the abstract tokens rather than the explicit verbal CoT.

## Results

- Abstract-CoT achieved up to **12x fewer reasoning tokens** compared to verbalized Chain-of-Thought, with comparable or superior performance across MATH, AlpacaEval, and HotpotQA.
- Demonstrated generalizability, maintaining efficiency and performance benefits across different LLM families (**Qwen3, Granite**) and various model sizes.
- Both the policy iteration warm-up and the subsequent RL stages were found to be **critical**: warm-up alone makes abstract tokens meaningful, and RL optimizes their use for high-quality responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively learn and utilize purely non-verbal reasoning pathways through post-training, bypassing the need for pre-training on abstract vocabularies.
> - The learned abstract token sequences exhibit structural properties, such as a power-law distribution similar to natural languages and sensitivity to token permutation, suggesting meaningful compositional reasoning.
> - A two-stage training process combining policy iteration warm-up with warm-started reinforcement learning is essential for successfully optimizing the abstract reasoning mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated increased effectiveness on complex reasoning tasks through the use of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, where models generate explicit, natural language rationales before producing a final answer. While beneficial for performance, this verbalized CoT mechanism presents several drawbacks, including increased inference latency and computational cost due to longer generation lengths. Additionally, some research suggests that verbalized CoTs may not always accurately reflect the model's internal reasoning processes, potentially being unfaithful representations.

This work situates itself within a research landscape attempting to address these limitations by developing more efficient intermediate reasoning representations. Prior efforts in this area include:
- **CoT Compression and Distillation:** Methods aimed at shortening verbalized CoTs or distilling them into smaller models, sometimes involving multi-round refinement or learning to skip intermediate reasoning steps. Some approaches combine latent tokens (learned via vector quantization) with text tokens or gradually internalize explicit steps into implicit computation.
- **Filler Tokens:** The introduction of special, semantically uninformative tokens (e.g., `<pause>` tokens) to expand a model's computational capacity during the forward pass, thereby facilitating internalized thinking or summarizing context. These tokens are distinct from natural language.
- **Continuous and Hybrid Latent Reasoning:** Approaches that replace parts of the textual rationale with continuous thought states or latent vectors, aiming to balance efficiency and expressivity. Examples include models that derive latent vectors from hidden states or traverse between language and latent spaces. Hybrid methods interleave latent and text tokens.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Budget Control:** Using reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize the length or allocation of intermediate reasoning traces, learning when to expend additional reasoning steps versus generating an early answer, or optimizing for consistency with user-specified length constraints.

This paper specifically explores the possibility of replacing lengthy verbalized rationales with a short sequence of discrete, abstract tokens, aiming to achieve comparable performance while significantly enhancing token efficiency. It distinguishes itself from existing work by proposing a novel post-training mechanism that introduces a reserved abstract vocabulary and learns its utility through a two-stage training process involving policy iteration-style warm-up and warm-started reinforcement learning. This approach explores a discrete latent reasoning paradigm, differentiating it from continuous or hybrid latent reasoning methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether long, explicit verbalized chains-of-thought in LLMs can be effectively replaced by a short sequence of discrete, abstract tokens, serving as a latent "scratchpad," without compromising the performance gains associated with traditional CoT.

The motivations driving this objective are multi-faceted:
1. **Inference Efficiency and Cost Reduction:** Verbalized CoTs, while effective, are computationally expensive. Generating extended natural language rationales increases inference latency and the operational cost of deploying LLMs, as longer sequences require more computational resources. Reducing the number of generated tokens for reasoning directly translates to improved efficiency and reduced expense.
2. **Addressing "Bloat" in RL Traces:** The generation of lengthy reasoning sequences can "bloat" the length of traces used in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or other RL training paradigms, potentially making these processes less efficient.
3. **Investigating CoT Faithfulness:** Prior research suggests that verbalized CoTs might not always faithfully represent the underlying latent reasoning process of an LLM. This implies that the explicit natural language steps might be a post-hoc rationalization rather than the actual computational path. This work is motivated by the hypothesis that an abstract, non-verbal representation could potentially align more closely with or effectively leverage these latent processes.
4. **Exploring Alternative Reasoning Mediums:** The work seeks to explore a novel, non-verbal, and token-efficient alternative to natural language rationales. By introducing a new, reserved vocabulary of abstract tokens, the authors aim to enable LLMs to learn and utilize a compact, discrete "reasoning language" purely through post-training, bypassing the need for extensive pre-training on such a vocabulary. This explores the hypothesis that meaningful reasoning can occur without human-readable intermediate steps.
5. **Generalizability Across Models:** The research aims to demonstrate that such a mechanism can be generalized across different language model families and sizes, indicating a broadly applicable approach for efficient latent reasoning.

In essence, the core motivation is to develop a method that retains the performance benefits of CoT while overcoming its inherent inefficiencies and exploring the potential for learned, non-verbal reasoning pathways within LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Abstract Chain-of-Thought (Abstract-CoT), is a discrete latent reasoning mechanism implemented through a two-stage post-training recipe. It involves the LLM producing a short sequence of tokens from a reserved vocabulary (referred to as the "abstract codebook") instead of a natural language CoT, before generating a response.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup and Abstract Codebook

The base LLM's tokenizer is extended with a set of `M` previously unseen, reserved tokens (e.g., `<TOKEN_A>`, `<TOKEN_B>`, etc.) and two delimiters, `<beginabstract>` and `<endabstract>`. An abstract chain-of-thought is a bounded-length sequence (`m ≤ m_max`) of these abstract tokens enclosed by the delimiters. These new tokens are initially randomly initialized, presenting a "cold-start" problem where they are semantically uninformative.

#### 3.2. Warm-Up via Policy Iteration

The first stage addresses the cold-start problem and warms up the embeddings of the reserved tokens through a policy iteration-style loop over `T` iterations. Each iteration involves two alternating phases:

#### 3.2.1. Bottlenecked Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Abstract Tokens

- **Abstract Trace Generation:** In the first iteration (`t=1`), abstract sequences are randomly initialized for each training example, with a random number of abstract tokens sampled per verbal CoT step (e.g., `rand(1, |ℓ|/2)` for `|ℓ|` tokens in a verbal CoT step). For subsequent iterations (`t ≥ 2`), abstract sequences (`˜z^(t)`) are generated on-policy using the current model's policy (`π_abs_θ`) under constrained decoding.
- **Constrained Decoding:** During generation of abstract tokens, the model's policy is restricted to an allowed token set (`V_abs ∪ {<endabstract>}`). A hard cap (`m_max`) is enforced on the number of codebook tokens. If `m_max` is reached, the `endabstract` delimiter is forced.
- **Information Bottleneck with Attention Mask:** A critical component is a block-structured attention mask (`A`) applied during SFT. When training on a concatenated sequence `[x; c; ˜z; y]` (prompt, verbal CoT, abstract sequence, answer):
 - Abstract tokens (`Z_abs`) can attend to the prompt (`X`), verbal CoT (`C`), and previous abstract tokens.
 - Crucially, the answer (`Y`) is masked such that it can only attend to the prompt (`X`) and the abstract tokens (`˜z`), but *not* the verbal CoT (`C`). This design forces the abstract tokens to learn and encode useful information from the verbal CoT, effectively acting as a discrete latent bottleneck. The dependence of the answer on the verbal CoT is channeled *only* through the abstract segment.
- **SFT Objective:** The model is optimized using a masked SFT objective (`L_SFT`) that trains on the abstract sequence and the answer, incorporating the bottleneck attention mask.

#### 3.2.2. Self-Distillation Without Verbal CoT

- **Distillation Dataset Creation:** After the bottlenecked SFT, a distillation dataset is created. The model (`π_abs_¯θ^(t)`) generates new abstract sequences (`˜z'`) solely from the prompt (`x`) using constrained decoding. These `(x, ˜z', y)` triples (prompt, generated abstract sequence, gold answer) form the new training data.
- **Standard Causal SFT:** The model is then further fine-tuned with standard causal SFT on these `[x; ˜z'; y]` sequences using `L_Distill`. This phase trains the model to directly produce abstract tokens from the prompt alone, preparing it for inference without access to a verbal CoT teacher.
- These two phases are repeated iteratively for `T` rounds.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning from Warm-Start

Following the warm-up stage, the abstract-token policy is further optimized using reinforcement learning.
- **Generative Reward Model:** A generative reward model (specifically, `gpt-oss-20b` in experiments) is used to score outputs. This allows the approach to generalize to non-verifiable, natural language settings, not just math problems.
- **Trajectory Sampling:** For each prompt, `K` trajectories `{(˜z_k, y_k)}` are sampled: first `˜z_k` is sampled from `π_abs_θ(· | x)` using guided-regex constrained decoding, then `y_k` is sampled from `π_θ(· | x, ˜z_k)`. Rewards (`R_k`) are computed for these trajectories.
- **GRPO Optimization:** Advantages (`A_k`) are calculated based on these rewards, and the model parameters (`θ`) are updated using a GRPO (Generative Reinforcement Policy Optimization) objective (`J(θ)`). This objective includes log-probabilities for both the abstract trace and the answer tokens, applying KL regularization against a reference policy (`π_θ_ref`) to maintain policy stability. The RL stage aims to induce exploration over abstract token sequences and refine the abstract generation policy to maximize response quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings across various reasoning tasks and language model families.

#### 4.1. Token Efficiency and Performance

- **Comparable Performance with Reduced Reasoning Tokens:** Abstract-CoT achieves performance comparable to or exceeding that of traditional verbalized Chain-of-Thought (SFT + RL) across different benchmarks and models, while using substantially fewer reasoning tokens.
 - On MATH-500 (mathematical reasoning), Abstract-CoT (Warm-up + RL) for Qwen3-8B achieved an accuracy of 90.8% with an average of 144 tokens, compared to SFT+RL's 92.6% with 1671 tokens. This represents a 10.4-11.6x reduction in reasoning tokens.
 - On AlpacaEval (general instruction-following), Qwen3-8B with Abstract-CoT (Warm-up + RL) achieved a win-rate of 60.8% with 225 tokens, compared to SFT+RL's 58.4% with 496 tokens. This demonstrates a 1.9-2.2x reduction in tokens while also surpassing performance.
 - On HotpotQA (multi-hop question-answering), Qwen3-8B with Abstract-CoT (Warm-up + RL) achieved an F1 score of 58.8% with 171 tokens, compared to SFT+RL's 58.1% with 735 tokens, indicating a 4.0-4.3x reduction in tokens.
- **Generalizability Across Model Sizes and Families:** These trends are observed consistently across various models, including Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-4B, and Granite 4.0 Micro (3B), and Qwen3-32B (in appendix), suggesting the method's broad applicability.
- **Performance on Challenging Benchmarks:** On more reasoning-intensive benchmarks like GPQA-Diamond and AIME’25 (using Qwen3-8B), Abstract-CoT (Warm-up + RL) demonstrated competitive performance (50.5% vs. 51.5% accuracy for GPQA-Diamond and 24.4% vs. 25.6% for AIME'25, respectively) with significant token efficiency gains (7.9x and 2.7x fewer reasoning tokens, respectively).

#### 4.2. Efficacy of Warm-Up and RL Stages

- **Warm-Up is Crucial:** Cold-start RL (without the policy iteration warm-up) generally underperformed the base instruction-tuned model, indicating that randomly initialized abstract token embeddings are insufficient for effective reasoning.
- **Warm-Up Alone is Insufficient but Beneficial:** The warm-up stage alone (Abstract-CoT Warm-up) typically outperformed the SFT without CoT baseline but lagged behind SFT with verbal CoT. This suggests that while warm-up makes the abstract tokens meaningful, the subsequent RL stage is necessary to fully optimize their use for high-quality responses.
- **Synergy of Stages:** The combination of warm-up and warm-started RL is critical for achieving high performance with token efficiency, confirming the utility of both stages.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Baselines

- **Ineffectiveness of Pause Tokens:** The "Pause Tokens" baseline, despite generating a similar number of tokens as Abstract-CoT, performed worse than the initial baseline model across all settings, supporting prior findings that fine-tuning with pause tokens alone is ineffective without pre-training.
- **Stepwise Internalization Limitations:** While "Stepwise Internalization" offered relative efficiency, it was more compute-intensive (22 iterations) and did not match Abstract-CoT's performance.

#### 4.4. Analysis of the Abstract Reasoning Language

- **Emergent Power Law Distribution:** During RL training, the frequency distribution of abstract token usage evolves into a power law-like pattern, similar to Zipf's law observed in natural languages. This suggests that the model learns to prioritize certain abstract tokens, indicating the development of meaningful concepts and re-use patterns within the learned "reasoning language."
- **Sensitivity to Permutation:** Permuting the abstract token sequences significantly decreased Abstract-CoT's performance, indicating that the model learns compositional behavior and sequence ordering within the abstract vocabulary. The performance drop was substantial, though less severe than for verbal CoT, which has a stronger pre-training prior for ordered language.
- **Graceful Degradation with Truncation:** Truncating the abstract reasoning trace resulted in a performance decrease, but Abstract-CoT exhibited a more graceful degradation compared to verbal CoT. This is attributed to Abstract-CoT being trained to reason through a short, bounded trace, making it less dependent on extended sequences.

#### 4.5. Scaling Abstract Vocabulary Size

- Ablations on abstract vocabulary size (M) demonstrated that increasing M generally improved performance, with a plateau and slight decline at very large sizes (e.g., M=64 was often optimal). This suggests a balance between expressivity and complexity in the abstract vocabulary.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Abstract Chain-of-Thought as a notable development in efficient latent reasoning for Large Language Models. The findings highlight the viability of purely post-training mechanisms to induce new, non-verbal reasoning pathways.

#### 5.1. Practical Implications for Inference Efficiency

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that LLMs can achieve comparable performance to verbose, natural language-based Chain-of-Thought while generating significantly fewer reasoning tokens (up to 12x reduction). This directly addresses critical concerns regarding inference latency and computational costs, offering a practical solution for deploying high-performing LLMs in real-world applications where efficiency is paramount. By enabling "thinking without words," the method allows for faster response times and reduced operational expenses, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and scalable.

#### 5.2. Advancing Understanding of Latent Reasoning

The emergence of a power-law distribution over the abstract vocabulary suggests that LLMs can learn and internalize a structured, conceptual "reasoning language" without human interpretability constraints. This contributes to the understanding of how complex reasoning might be encoded and processed internally by neural networks, providing insights into the model's learned mechanisms beyond explicit natural language. The observed compositional behavior with permutation tests further supports the idea that these abstract sequences are not merely filler but convey meaningful, ordered computational steps.

#### 5.3. Bridging Explicit and Implicit Reasoning

Abstract-CoT offers a middle ground between fully explicit (and expensive) verbalized CoT and entirely implicit reasoning (which is difficult to control and analyze). The discrete nature of the abstract codebook provides a structured intermediate segment that can be both token-efficient and amenable to certain forms of analysis, potentially leading to new avenues for "chain-of-thought monitorability" and auditability studies. This could offer developers a controlled interface to probe or influence the model's internal thinking process without the overhead of natural language.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The work opens several avenues for future exploration:
- **Concept-level Understanding:** Further analysis of the power-law distribution and the specific abstract tokens could lead to a deeper understanding of concept learning and representation within the abstract vocabulary. This involves studying how these representations evolve during training and relate to different problem-solving strategies.
- **Budget-Adaptive Mechanisms:** Integrating Abstract-CoT with difficulty-aware adaptive sequencing, where the length of the abstract trace adjusts based on problem complexity, could further optimize reasoning for challenging tasks.
- **Hierarchical Codebook Structures:** Exploring hierarchical structures over the abstract codebook might enable the model to learn re-usable subroutines for particular tasks, potentially enhancing expressivity and efficiency for larger vocabularies and more intricate reasoning.
- **Integration with Pre-training:** While this work focuses on post-training, investigating the benefits of incorporating abstract tokens into the pre-training phase could yield further performance gains and insights into inherent reasoning capabilities.

In conclusion, "Thinking Without Words" introduces a robust and efficient latent reasoning mechanism that offers a promising direction for future LLM development, particularly in scenarios demanding high performance with constrained computational resources.
