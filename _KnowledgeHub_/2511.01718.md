# Unified Diffusion VLA: Vision-Language-Action Model via Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process

> [!summary] Summary
> UD-VLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that synchronously generates future images and robot actions using a Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process (JD3P) within a single transformer. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple robot manipulation benchmarks, including an average success rate of 92.7% on LIBERO, and demonstrates robust real-world generalization with 4.3 times faster inference than autoregressive methods.

## Problem

- Existing unified VLA models often suffer from modality misalignment and weak coupling between visual generation and action prediction due to modular designs or separate decoding processes.
- Many VLA approaches either treat future image prediction as an auxiliary task or do not fully integrate it as explicit guidance during action inference, limiting the effective use of visual foresight for action planning.
- Autoregressive generation methods process contextual information for each action token only once, restricting the extent to which image generation can iteratively refine action predictions.

## Method

- A Unified Diffusion VLA (UD-VLA) is developed, employing a single transformer architecture with a hybrid attention mechanism for intrinsic synergy between vision-language understanding, future image generation, and action prediction.
- The core is a Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process (JD3P) that enables parallel and synchronous generation of discrete future image and action tokens within the same denoising step.
- A two-stage training pipeline is utilized, initiating from a pre-trained VLM backbone and fine-tuning on robot action datasets with a shifted-operation strategy to reformulate autoregressive decoding as a JD3P diffusion process.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance in simulated environments, including an average success length of 4.64 on CALVIN ABCD4D, 92.7% average success rate on LIBERO, and 62.5% on SimplerEnv-WidowX.
- Demonstrated robust generalization in real-world experiments with a UR5e robotic arm, consistently outperforming baselines (GR00T N1, UniVLA) with success rates above 80% on varied tasks, even in unseen settings.
- The Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process (JD3P) enabled 4.3 times faster inference (219.3 tokens/s) compared to autoregressive methods, while also improving action prediction performance significantly.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tightly coupling future image generation and action prediction via a joint discrete diffusion process intrinsically strengthens their synergy, allowing actions to be iteratively refined under continuous and evolving visual guidance.
> - A hybrid attention mechanism, balancing intra-modal bidirectional interactions with causal cross-modal information flow, is crucial for effectively integrating multimodal tokens without leakage.
> - Explicitly modeling and utilizing future visual information as a 'chain-of-thought' through multi-step diffusion provides richer temporal cues, significantly benefiting robot action planning and improving generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to enable embodied agents to comprehend natural language instructions and visual observations to execute corresponding actions. A significant trend in recent VLA research involves integrating future images into the understanding-acting loop. These models, termed "unified VLAs," seek to perform joint understanding, generation (of future images), and action. The paper highlights two prevalent paradigms in constructing such unified VLAs and identifies their limitations.

Firstly, some VLA models unify modalities using external expert encoders and decoders. Examples include GR-1, SEER, DreamVLA, F1, and UP-VLA. These approaches encode images and language into intermediate tokens, which then condition separate visual and action generation models. The paper notes that this modular separation can introduce misalignment, increase complexity, and lead to weak coupling between visual generation and action prediction.

Secondly, other VLA models aim to unify the input and output spaces through visual and action tokenizers, allowing for token-level alignment across modalities. Examples include CoT-VLA, WorldVLA, UniVLA, and FlowVLA. However, these designs often maintain image generation and action prediction as separate processes, which can restrict the model's ability to leverage rich future visual information for action prediction. Some models in this category, such as UniVLA and FlowVLA, only predict images as an auxiliary task during training, thus not fully utilizing future images as explicit guidance during inference. Autoregressive methods within this paradigm also face limitations, as each action token integrates contextual information only once, constraining the extent to which image generation can guide action prediction iteratively.

The paper also acknowledges the emergence of discrete diffusion VLAs (e.g., PD-VLA, Discrete Diffusion VLA, LLADA-VLA, CEED-VLA), but points out that these models primarily focus on action prediction and largely overlook the interplay between visual and action tokens.

Against this background, the presented work introduces a VLA model that aims to address these limitations by proposing a unified framework where understanding, generation, and acting are intrinsically synergistic. It focuses on a joint, synchronous denoising process for both visual generation and action prediction, leveraging a discrete diffusion mechanism to enable actions to evolve under continuous and sufficient visual guidance. This approach seeks to provide a more integrated and effective solution for unified VLA development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Unified Diffusion VLA (UD-VLA) that tightly couples vision-language understanding, future image generation, and action prediction, enabling these processes to be intrinsically synergistic. The motivation for this objective stems from identified shortcomings in existing VLA models:

- **Addressing Modality Misalignment and Weak Coupling:** Current unified VLAs often employ external expert encoders and decoders or treat image generation and action prediction as separate processes. This modular separation can lead to misalignment between modalities, increased system complexity, and a weak coupling between visual generation and action prediction. The authors' motivation is to overcome these issues by designing a system where generation and action prediction are directly and synchronously optimized.
- **Leveraging Future Visual Information for Action Planning:** Many existing approaches either use future image prediction as an auxiliary task or do not fully integrate visual generation as explicit guidance during action inference. This limits the ability of the model to leverage rich future visual information for refining action predictions. A core motivation is to ensure that actions are formulated as implicit mappings to desired future observations, with actions evolving under constant and sufficient visual guidance derived from future image predictions.
- **Improving Iterative Refinement and Contextual Integration:** Autoregressive generation methods process contextual information for each action token through a single computation. This design restricts the extent to which image generation can iteratively guide action prediction, potentially leading to less precise or refined actions. The research is motivated by the need for an iterative scheme where action tokens are progressively refined through multiple computations, allowing them to benefit from evolving visual predictions across a denoising trajectory.
- **Enhancing Inference Efficiency and Performance:** While some approaches focus on improving one aspect (e.g., speed or success rate), a key motivation is to achieve both high performance and efficiency during inference. The authors aim to develop a model that not only achieves state-of-the-art results but also maintains faster inference times compared to alternative methods.
- **Establishing a Unified Tokenized Space for Multimodal Interaction:** The work is motivated by the idea that a truly synergistic VLA requires a unified representation space for all modalities. By discretizing language, vision, and action into a shared token space, the model can natively process and interact across these modalities without the need for additional, potentially misaligned, external components.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to move beyond models that superficially combine understanding, generation, and acting, towards a framework where these processes are deeply integrated and mutually reinforcing through a joint discrete denoising diffusion process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on the Unified Diffusion VLA (UD-VLA) and its core mechanism, the Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process (JD3P). This approach aims to achieve intrinsic synergy between vision-language understanding, future image generation, and action prediction within a single transformer architecture.

#### 3.1. Unified Diffusion VLA Architecture

- **Unified Tokenization:** The method unifies language, vision, and action modalities by converting each into discrete tokens and concatenating them into a single multimodal sequence.
 - Language tokens adopt the Emu3 design.
 - Visual observations are discretized using a VQ tokenizer into fixed-length `V_v` tokens.
 - Actions are represented using the FAST action tokenizer, yielding variable-length `V_a` tokens.
 - Special tokens (`<BOI>`, `<EOI>` for images; `<BOA>`, `<EOA>` for actions) mark the beginning and end of respective token sequences.
 - The complete token sequence is formatted as: `[text tokens; current image tokens; future image tokens; action tokens]`, where text and current images are inputs, and future images and actions are outputs.

- **Hybrid Attention Mechanism:** A hybrid attention mechanism is designed to manage interactions between multimodal tokens.
 - Input (text and current image) uses causal attention for text and bidirectional attention for the current image.
 - Output tokens are split into "generation" (future image) and "acting" (action) blocks.
 - Within each block, bidirectional attention enables comprehensive intra-modal token interactions, addressing issues like time-serial dependence in action tokens.
 - Across blocks, causal attention connects them: the generation block attends to the input, and the acting block attends to both the input and the generation block. This design prohibits action-to-vision pathways, aiming to prevent information leakage and ensure action grounding in predicted visual consequences. This factorization converts end-to-end action prediction into a foresight process (predicting next visual state) and an inverse kinematics process (inferring action conditioned on visual prediction).

#### 3.2. Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process (JD3P)

JD3P is the central mechanism enabling synchronous generation of images and actions.
- **Parallel Generation:** Actions and images are generated in parallel within the same denoising step. The complete sequence `v_0, a_0` concatenates fixed-length future image tokens and variable-length action tokens.
- **Masked Vocabulary:** The vocabulary is augmented with a special mask token `M` to facilitate the diffusion process.
- **Noising Process:** A Markov chain `{(v_t, a_t)}_{t=0}^T` is defined. At each step `t`, individual tokens are independently mapped to `M` with probability `β_t` or remain unchanged with `1-β_t`. This creates a corrupted distribution `q(v_t, a_t | v_0, a_0)` that factorizes position-wise.
- **Denoising Process:** The model learns to predict the reverse conditionals `p_θ(v_{t-1}, a_{t-1} | v_t, a_t, c)` where `c` is the input context. This process is decomposed to handle both masked and unmasked tokens. The UD-VLA selectively reconstructs masked positions, gradually annealing the mask ratio until the original `v_0` and `a_0` are recovered.
- **Loss Function:** A single-step mask-predict objective is employed. At each update, a mask ratio `ρ_t` is sampled and applied to `v_0` and `a_0`, creating `v_t` and `a_t`. The model `p_θ` is optimized via maximum likelihood, computing cross-entropy `L_CE(θ)` only on the masked positions to recover the original tokens. A weighting factor `ω` down-weights visual tokens to prevent their dominance over action tokens.

#### 3.3. Training Pipeline

The training proceeds in two stages:
- **Initialization:** The model is initialized from a pre-trained VLM backbone (e.g., Emu3), originally trained autoregressively with causal attention.
- **Stage (i) Post-training:** UD-VLA is post-trained on a large-scale video dataset. The token sequence includes text, current image, and future image tokens. This stage injects future image generation capabilities into the VLA, allowing it to model future states.
- **Stage (ii) Fine-tuning:** On downstream robot action datasets, the model jointly optimizes image and action generation using the full token sequence (text, current image, future image, action tokens). The autoregressive decoding from the backbone is reformulated as a JD3P diffusion process. A shift operation strategy (Gong et al., 2025) is adopted to predict the next token, aiming to retain knowledge from next-token prediction training while benefiting from bidirectional context and parallel decoding.

#### 3.4. Inference Techniques

Inference involves parallel decoding with adaptive masking:
- **Initialization:** All positions of `v_T` and `a_T` are initialized as `<MASK>`.
- **Iterative Prediction:** Token distributions for all positions are predicted in parallel, and this procedure is repeated for a small number of iterations.
- **Prefix KV Cache and Pre-filling Tokens:** Keys and values of current visual and prompt tokens are cached using Prefix KV-Cache. Special tokens (`<BOI>`, `<EOI>`, `<BOA>`) are pre-filled at their respective positions, which guides the denoising of both visual and action tokens and contributes to speed.
- **Confidence-Guided Decoding:** The denoising process iterates backward from `t=T` to `t=0` using a cosine mask schedule `ρ_t`. At each step, the model evaluates joint reverse conditionals. Confidence scores `q_{t-1,r}` are computed for currently masked positions. The top `(1-ρ_t)|M_t|` entries among masked indices are updated using tempered Gumbel sampling.
- **Decoding Space Mapping:** The classification space for image and action tokens is restricted to their designated codebook ranges, preventing cross-modality token generation. Once an `<EOA>` token is predicted, the action length is fixed, and subsequent tokens are replaced with `<MASK>` to prevent corruption.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of UD-VLA was evaluated on multiple simulated benchmarks and a real-world robotic platform.

#### 4.1. Main Results in Simulation

- **CALVIN Benchmark:** UD-VLA achieved an average success length of 4.64 on the CALVIN ABCD→D benchmark, surpassing all baselines. This performance was higher than MDT (4.52), UP-VLA (4.42), and UniVLA\* (4.26). This result suggests that explicit visual generation, acting as a chain-of-thought, and the multi-step diffusion process provide effective guidance for action prediction.
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** The model achieved an average success rate of 92.7%, establishing state-of-the-art performance. It notably achieved 95.7% on the Object suite and 89.6% on the Long suite, indicating robust generalization in object recognition and strong temporal reasoning through future-frame prediction. This performance exceeded approaches relying on extrinsic experts (e.g., DreamVLA, 92.6%) and those unifying input/output spaces with separate decoding (e.g., CoT-VLA, WorldVLA).
- **SimplerEnv Benchmark:** UD-VLA attained an average success rate of 62.5% on the SimplerEnv-WidowX benchmark, outperforming all baselines, including F1 (59.4%) and SpatialVLA (42.7%). On the "Stack Block" task, which demands precise manipulation, the model showed a 24.9% higher success rate than SpatialVLA. These results indicate its capability for precise motion and grasp target prediction through explicit future-frame modeling.

#### 4.2. In-Depth Analysis (Ablation Studies)

- **Effectiveness of Hybrid Attention Mechanism:** A comparison of attention schemes on CALVIN showed that the hybrid attention design achieved an average success length of 4.64. This was higher than purely causal attention (4.04) and purely bidirectional attention (4.32), indicating that the hybrid approach effectively balances intra-modal interactions with causal cross-modal information flow. Bidirectional attention across modalities led to a 0.3 lower average length, suggesting information leakage.
- **Effectiveness of Future Image Generation:** Experiments comparing different visual generation targets showed that predicting future images resulted in the best performance (4.64 average length). This outperformed not generating visual information (4.21) and reconstructing current images (4.39), highlighting that rich temporal cues from future visual predictions are beneficial for action planning.
- **Effectiveness of JD3P:** JD3P achieved an average success rate of 4.64 and an inference speed of 219.3 tokens/s, which is 4.3 times faster than autoregressive (AR) methods. This performance was superior to AR (4.18 avg. len., 50.2 tokens/s), Jacobi decoding (4.16 avg. len., 101.6 tokens/s), and independent diffusion (ID) processes for images and actions (4.35 avg. len., 144.4 tokens/s). This indicates that JD3P's joint denoising enables actions to be refined more effectively by leveraging image information from intermediate denoising steps.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiment

- **Setup and Tasks:** The real-world setup involved a UR5e robotic arm with an Inspire RH56E2 hand, using wrist-mounted and static cameras. Tasks included stacking bowls, putting blocks, and flipping towers, with varied objects, colors, shapes, and backgrounds, evaluated in both seen and unseen settings.
- **Results:** UD-VLA consistently outperformed GR00T N1 and UniVLA across all tasks, achieving success rates above 80%. It demonstrated strong generalization to unseen targets and backgrounds, outperforming UniVLA, which lacked explicit visual reasoning during inference. GR00T N1 exhibited poor generalization to unseen scenarios.

#### 4.4. Visualization of Future Image Generation

Visualizations showed that generated future frames generally followed instructions and captured task-level dynamics, aligning with ground-truth trajectories. This indicates that the model developed a temporal understanding of task logic, which enhanced action generation. However, the generated images sometimes lacked visual fidelity in fine-grained details, such as robotic arms and backgrounds. This limitation was attributed to the absence of large-scale generative pretraining and the use of compressed images for efficiency, yet the generated frames were sufficient for action planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the Unified Diffusion VLA (UD-VLA) and the Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process (JD3P) represents an advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) modeling.

#### 5.1. Core Contribution to VLA Design

The paper's central contribution is a framework that intrinsically couples understanding, generation, and acting. By implementing a synchronous denoising process for both future image generation and action prediction, UD-VLA addresses a limitation in prior VLA models where these processes were either modularly separated or only weakly coupled. This tighter integration allows actions to be iteratively refined under constant and evolving visual guidance, promoting a deeper synergy between visual foresight and motor control.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Performance and Efficiency

The model achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse and challenging robot manipulation benchmarks such as CALVIN, LIBERO, and SimplerEnv, as well as in real-world scenarios. Simultaneously, JD3P demonstrates a notable improvement in inference efficiency, achieving speeds up to 4 times faster than autoregressive methods. This combination of high performance and efficiency is significant for practical robotic deployments where real-time decision-making is crucial.

#### 5.3. Improved Generalization Capabilities

The experimental results, particularly on tasks requiring generalization to unseen objects, backgrounds, and long-horizon compositional skills (e.g., LIBERO Object and Long suites, SimplerEnv, and real-world unseen tasks), suggest that UD-VLA possesses enhanced generalization. The explicit modeling and utilization of future visual information as a "chain-of-thought" for action planning appear to contribute to the model's robustness in novel environments.

#### 5.4. Implications for Foundation Models in Robotics

By building upon a pre-trained VLM backbone and employing a unified tokenized space, UD-VLA provides a methodology for leveraging the knowledge embedded in large foundation models for robotics. The two-stage training pipeline allows for the activation of image generation capabilities and subsequent fine-tuning for joint visual and action generation, indicating a path towards adapting general-purpose VLMs for specific embodied tasks.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The identified limitation regarding the visual fidelity of generated future images, especially in fine-grained details, points to potential avenues for future research. Integrating larger-scale generative pretraining or developing more sophisticated image tokenization techniques could enhance the visual quality of predictions while maintaining or improving efficiency and task relevance. Further exploration of the theoretical underpinnings of joint discrete denoising diffusion processes could also yield insights into optimizing multimodal interaction and refining the denoising schedules.

Overall, this work contributes a robust and efficient framework for unified VLA modeling, offering a paradigm for developing more intelligent and capable embodied agents that can effectively understand, foresee, and act in complex environments.
