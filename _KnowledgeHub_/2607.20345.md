# Closing the Lab-to-Store Gap: A Data-Efficient Post-Training and Experience-Driven Learning VLA Framework for Retail Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> The DEED (Data-Efficient Post-Training and Experience-Driven Learning) framework is introduced for deploying VLA-based humanoids in dynamic retail environments. It demonstrates how systematic engineering practices combined with targeted post-training refinement can transform a non-functional foundation model into a competent system for tasks like supermarket chip-restocking, achieving a 32% success rate initially and improving to 42% after one iteration of experience-driven learning.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for humanoid robots perform well in controlled lab settings but exhibit a significant "lab-to-store gap" in reliability and robustness when deployed in dynamic real-world environments.
- Standard offline-trained VLA policies lack the inherent ability to adapt to unforeseen execution errors, environmental variability, and distribution shifts encountered during real-world operation.
- Successful deployment of pretrained VLA models is heavily influenced by practical engineering decisions and meticulous data management, aspects often secondary to core architectural advancements.

## Method

- A **Data-Efficient (DE)** post-training pipeline applying systematic engineering practices (e.g., ==frequency hierarchy==, data curation, action smoothing) and task-relevant visual highlighting to fine-tune a ==VLA== foundation model.
- An **Experience-Driven (ED)** learning strategy, adapting ==RECAP== for the ==GR00T== architecture, enabling the humanoid to iteratively refine its policy using self-generated experience and human corrections in the deployment environment.
- An **In/Out-Distribution (OOD)** analysis tool leveraging a ==Gaussian Mixture Model== on the VLA's latent space to provide real-time and offline monitoring of policy reliability and diagnose distribution shifts.

## Results

- The Data-Efficient (DE) post-training recipe improved the GR00T-N1.6 foundation model from a **0%** baseline to a **32%** task success rate on a supermarket chip-restocking task, with an average execution time of **24.30** seconds per bag.
- A single iteration of experience-driven learning (RECAP) further increased the success rate to **42%** and reduced mean execution time to **22.37** seconds, though a second iteration saw a decline to **22%** success, and both ED iterations reduced consecutive task completion from **4** bags to **1**.
- The in/out-of-distribution analysis tool revealed that performance degradation in later experience-driven iterations was associated with policy drift into regions underrepresented in the original, broader teleoperation data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bridging the "lab-to-store gap" for VLA-based humanoids is primarily a systems integration and engineering challenge, emphasizing the critical role of data quality and strategic post-training practices over purely architectural improvements.
> - Data quality and strategic engineering decisions during post-training (e.g., frequency alignment, careful data curation, action smoothing) can transform a non-functional VLA checkpoint into a competent system using limited demonstration data.
> - Uncontrolled iterative experience-driven learning can lead to distributional drift and performance degradation in real-world scenarios due to the diminishing influence of diverse expert demonstrations and a narrowing of the effective state-action distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a prominent paradigm for general-purpose robotic manipulation. Models such as OpenVLA, the $\pi$ family, and GR00T have demonstrated capabilities in generalization across objects, instructions, environments, and humanoid embodiments. As these models become more versatile, research attention has increasingly shifted towards their application in humanoid robots. Humanoid morphology is considered advantageous for leveraging existing infrastructure and performing manipulation tasks in human-designed environments, such such as retail stores.

Despite advancements in VLA model capabilities, a notable gap persists between their benchmark performance in controlled settings and their reliability in real-world deployments. Standard VLA models are often trained offline and deployed as fixed policies. However, practical deployment scenarios necessitate robustness to execution errors, adaptations to distribution shifts, and management of continual environmental variability.

Current research efforts address these challenges from various perspectives. Data-centric studies explore the impact of action diversity, transition diversity, and dataset quality on imitation learning performance. Other approaches focus on mitigating common failure modes through visual highlighting, long-horizon task decomposition, error recovery mechanisms, and improved evaluation protocols. Complementary to these are interaction-based methods, which aim to refine pretrained VLAs through online reinforcement learning. An example is RECAP, which integrates reinforcement learning signals into the imitation objective using advantage conditioning. Additionally, in/out-of-distribution detection methods contribute to monitoring deployment reliability.

This paper adopts a systems-level perspective for deploying VLA-based humanoids. It builds upon existing VLA foundation models, specifically GR00T N1.6, and integrates elements of data-centric strategies and experience-driven refinement. The work is motivated by practical constraints encountered in an industrial effort to deploy humanoid robots for restocking tasks in an operational supermarket. The proposed DEED framework positions itself as a comprehensive approach to address the engineering and learning challenges associated with real-world VLA deployment.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the gap between the performance of VLA-based humanoid robots in laboratory benchmarks and their reliable operation in real-world, dynamic environments, specifically addressing the "lab-to-store gap."

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in deploying advanced VLA models:

- **Robustness to Real-World Variability:** While VLA models exhibit strong generalization in controlled settings, practical deployment exposes them to execution errors, unforeseen distribution shifts, and continuous environmental variations (e.g., changes in lighting, object positions, or interaction dynamics). Standard offline-trained policies often lack the inherent robustness required for such conditions.
- **Importance of Engineering and Data Management:** The successful deployment of a pretrained VLA model is significantly influenced by practical engineering considerations. These include choices related to action representation, camera placement, control-frequency alignment, and data curation. Inconsistencies in data or even minor distribution shifts can accumulate into policy failures, as behavioral cloning is fundamentally limited by the quality and consistency of its training demonstrations.
- **Need for Continual Adaptation:** Offline policies, by definition, cannot learn or improve from their own experiences during deployment. This limitation underscores the need for post-training methods that enable robots to continually adapt and refine their behaviors based on real-world interactions and self-generated data.
- **Industrial Application Context:** The research is directly motivated by an ongoing industrial initiative focused on deploying humanoid robots for restocking tasks within an operational supermarket. This practical context imposes specific constraints and requirements, emphasizing data efficiency, reliability, and the ability to operate within complex, human-centric environments.

To address these motivations, the DEED framework was developed with the following specific objectives:

- **Develop a Data-Efficient Post-Training Pipeline:** To create a practical set of engineering practices and data design principles that can transform an "out-of-the-box" GR00T checkpoint into a reliable policy suitable for real-world operation, efficiently utilizing available data and computational resources.
- **Empirically Evaluate Experience-Driven Refinement:** To study the effectiveness of experience-driven learning methods, specifically an adapted RECAP-style refinement strategy, when applied to a real humanoid robot. This includes investigating how self-generated experience contributes to robustness and identifying potential limitations or degradation patterns.
- **Implement a Latent-Space Analysis Tool:** To provide a mechanism for real-time and offline monitoring of the robot's state relative to its training data distribution. This tool aims to diagnose in- and out-of-distribution behaviors, offering insights into policy reliability and potential failure modes.
- **Provide Practical Deployment Guidance:** To derive actionable insights and practical recommendations for deploying foundation-model-based humanoids in real-world retail environments, based on empirical findings from the implemented framework.

### 3. Methodology and approach

This research presents DEED (Data-Efficient Post-Training and Experience-Driven Learning), a systems-level framework designed for robust real-world deployment of VLA-based humanoids. The framework is instantiated on a supermarket chip-restocking task using a Unitree G1-Edu humanoid robot equipped with Dex-3 hands and leveraging the GR00T N1.6 foundation model.

The DEED framework comprises three main components: a data-efficient post-training pipeline, an experience-driven learning strategy, and an in/out-of-distribution analysis tool.

**A. Data-Efficient Recipe for VLA Post-Training**
This stage focuses on practical engineering and data curation techniques to fine-tune a VLA model for real-world deployment.

1. **Frequency Hierarchy:** A critical step involves aligning the camera frequency ($f_{cam}$), teleoperation frequency ($f_t$), recording frequency ($f_r$), and inference control frequency ($f_{ctrl}$) to satisfy $f_r = f_{ctrl} \le f_{cam}$ and $f_t \ge f_r$. For the experiments, $f_t = 50$ Hz, $f_{cam} = 30$ Hz, and $f_r = f_{ctrl} = 25$ Hz were used. This ensures smooth and reliable behavior by preventing learning from duplicated observations and matching temporal semantics between training and inference.

2. **Data Curation Rules:** To address the limitations of behavioral cloning, which learns directly from demonstrations, several rules were established:
 - **Balanced state coverage:** Prevent policy bias towards dominant states.
 - **Efficient, successful behavior:** Avoid hesitations, corrections, or inefficient paths in demonstrations; trim episodes at failure points.
 - **Incidental recoveries:** Retain segments where the expert recovers from deviations to teach the policy error response.
 - **Action consistency:** Prioritize consistent expert actions in similar states over broad but inconsistent state coverage.
 - **Minimize environmental variation:** Stabilize the physical setup during data collection to ensure consistent action-outcome relationships.
 - **Avoid no-operation states:** Start and end episodes with meaningful motion to prevent less dynamic behavior.

3. **Highlighting Task-Relevant Visual Cues:** An adapted IA-VLA framework is integrated to provide the VLA with task-relevant visual grounding. A large Vision-Language Model (VLM) highlights pertinent image regions via segmentation masks, which are propagated across frames, avoiding per-timestep inference. For the restocking task, this involved highlighting the empty shelf location for placement.

4. **Practical Tricks to Reduce VLA Dependence:**
 - **Binary Hand Controller:** For tasks primarily involving opening and closing the hand, a binary signal is used for gripper control instead of learning continuous, fine-grained joint control, acknowledging current VLA limitations in high-frequency, fine-grained physical interaction.
 - **Action Smoothing via Butterworth Filtering:** A Butterworth filter is applied to the predicted action sequence during inference. This mitigates jerky movements, maintains smooth execution, and keeps actions within the training distribution without sacrificing generalization.

5. **Continuous Episodes for Multi-Subtask Demonstrations:** Instead of segmenting long-horizon tasks into sub-tasks, continuous episodes are recorded that span full sub-task transitions. This allows the policy to learn transitions between sub-tasks naturally. Shared wording across subtask language prompts is avoided to maintain strong language signals.

6. **Policy Evaluation:** Evaluation focuses on qualitative analysis alongside quantitative metrics, distinguishing between in-distribution (ID), spatial OOD, and instance+spatial OOD scenarios to assess task-level understanding.

**B. Experience-Driven Learning Strategy**
This component addresses the limitation of offline policies by enabling them to refine their behavior from deployment experience, adapting the RECAP (RL with Experience and Corrections via Advantage-conditioned Policies) recipe for the GR00T architecture.

1. **GR00T Adaptation:** Unlike tightly coupled VLAs, GR00T decouples perception/reasoning from control. RECAP is adapted by conditioning the policy through a text-based instruction prefix ("Advantage=True/False") and restricting the value function to camera observations and text instructions. This avoids architectural modifications to GR00T.

2. **Pipeline Overview:** The strategy involves iterative refinement. An initial policy (from the Data-Efficient stage) collects autonomous rollouts. A human operator intervenes for corrections. The dataset is continuously updated with original teleoperation, successful autonomous, and human recovery episodes.
 - **Value Function ($V_\phi(o_t, \ell)$):** An independent value function is trained to estimate expected future return. It uses the frozen pretrained Eagle-3 Vision-Language encoder from GR00T followed by a lightweight MLP head. The head predicts a categorical distribution over 101 discretized return bins in $[-1, 0]$. Rewards are 0 for terminal success, -80 for terminal failure, and -1 otherwise, normalized by mean trajectory length. Training uses cross-entropy, with image embeddings cached for efficiency.
 - **Advantage Computation:** Offline advantage labels ($\hat{A}_t^{(N)}$) are computed using a multi-step estimator with a lookahead horizon N=25. Continuous values are converted into binary labels (True/False) at a threshold that labels 40% of the dataset as positive. Human corrective interventions are force-labeled positive. These labels are encoded as text prefixes and concatenated to the language instruction before fine-tuning, with a conditioning dropout of 0.3 applied during dataset generation. During inference, advantage is fixed to True.
 - **Optimization Stability:** Each refinement iteration initializes from the original GR00T checkpoint to mitigate optimization drift and prevent error accumulation.

**C. In/Out-Distribution Analysis Tool**
This tool is used across both DEED stages to measure the deviation of the robot's current state from the training data distribution.

1. **Latent Space Comparison:** States are compared in the latent space of the fine-tuned GR00T state encoder. This ensures that distances reflect task-relevant behavioral similarity.

2. **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM):** Every timestep of the training dataset is encoded into a latent representation. A GMM with K components is fitted to these embeddings to approximate the multi-modal empirical density of the training distribution.

3. **Mahalanobis Distance Scoring:** A new encoded state $z$ is scored by its Mahalanobis distance to the nearest GMM component. This metric accounts for each mode's covariance structure, penalizing deviations more strongly along low-variance directions.

4. **Interpretation Support:** Per-joint z-scores and empirical percentile checks provide attributable deviation information. PCA is used for a 3D low-dimensional projection of the training distribution and current state for live visualization. Thresholds for in-distribution, soft-OOD, and hard-OOD regimes are derived empirically.

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation was performed on a real-world chip-restocking task in a supermarket replica. The Unitree G1-Edu was equipped with a head-mounted Intel RealSense D435i and two wrist-mounted RealSense D405 cameras (640x480, 30 FPS). The action space was reduced to 20 dimensions (from 32 native), and the proprioceptive state was 17-dimensional.
81 teleoperation demonstrations (51.5 minutes) were collected, focusing on continuous episodes for multi-subtask learning. Subsequently, 116 autonomous rollout episodes (56.9 minutes) were gathered over two RECAP iterations. All models were initialized from the GR00T-N1.6-G1-PnPAppleToPlate checkpoint. Training and inference were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 workstation.
A naive SFT baseline was evaluated, trained on the same teleoperation data but without the DE engineering improvements. Evaluation metrics included task success rate, mean execution time for successful episodes, and maximum consecutive bags restocked, assessed over 50 episodes with slight initial pose variations.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research evaluated the DEED framework across its stages on a real-world supermarket chip-restocking task.

**Baseline Performance (Naive SFT):**
The initial naive supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of GR00T-N1.6, starting from the G1-specific public checkpoint (GR00T-N1.6-G1-PnPAppleToPlate) but without the DEED engineering improvements, resulted in a 0% success rate (0/50 episodes). This policy was unable to reliably grasp or place the chip bags, indicating that the base checkpoint alone, despite embodiment-specific initialization, was not functional for the target task without additional system-level considerations.

**Data-Efficient (DE) Policy Performance:**
The application of the Data-Efficient recipe transformed the non-functional baseline into a competent system. The DE policy achieved a 32% success rate (16/50 episodes) and a mean execution time of 24.30 seconds per bag for successful completions. This was the first configuration capable of executing the full pick-rotate-place sequence autonomously. Furthermore, it demonstrated a maximum of 4 consecutive bags restocked without requiring a manual reset. This improvement is attributed to the combined effect of a reduced action space, the addition of wrist-mounted cameras, matched control frequencies, curated demonstrations, and output smoothing.

**Experience-Driven (ED) Learning Performance:**
Building upon the DE policy, a single iteration of Experience-Driven refinement (RECAP iteration 1) resulted in an improvement in performance. The success rate increased to 42% (21/50 episodes), and the mean execution time per bag reduced to 22.37 seconds. Qualitatively, this iteration produced more direct approach trajectories and enhanced reliability in alignment during grasping and placement, suggesting that the advantage-conditioned objective successfully concentrated the policy's behavior around successful and human-corrected actions. However, the maximum number of consecutive bags restocked dropped to 1.

A second iteration of Experience-Driven refinement (RECAP iteration 2) did not continue the positive trend. The success rate declined to 22% (11/50 episodes), despite a slight further reduction in mean execution time to 21.09 seconds. The maximum consecutive bags remained at 1.

**Analysis of Performance Degradation in RECAP Iteration 2:**
The observed degradation in the second RECAP iteration is hypothesized to stem from distributional effects during iterative refinement. As self-generated rollouts increasingly dominated the training dataset, the influence of the original teleoperation demonstrations—which provided broader coverage of successful behaviors—diminished. This, combined with advantage relabeling becoming progressively tied to the policy's own sampling distribution, created a feedback loop that narrowed the effective state-action distribution. The in-distribution analysis tool supported this interpretation, indicating that the refined policies drifted towards regions underrepresented in the original teleoperation data, even if these regions were covered by the combined demonstration-plus-rollout dataset.

**Reliability and Value Function Design:**
- **Statistical Strength:** The 95% Wilson confidence intervals for the DE policy (20.8-45.8%) and RECAP iteration 1 (29.4-55.8%) showed substantial overlap. Consequently, the observed gain in iteration 1 is indicative rather than statistically conclusive, while the drop in iteration 2 (12.8-35.2%) showed less overlap. The qualitative trends were reported as consistent across runs.
- **Consecutive Placements:** The reduction in maximum consecutive bags to 1 for both RECAP iterations (from 4 for the DE policy) was attributed to the value function design. The terminal completion state of a single task was assigned a higher value than the intermediate poses required for subsequent resets, leading to reset transitions receiving low or negative advantage and thus being suppressed during training.
- **Value Function Ablations:** Ablation studies on the value function design revealed that using the Eagle-3 VLM backbone without state conditioning performed best. Prompt specificity had a negligible effect in the single-task context, and ensemble heads showed minimal disagreement. Encoding advantage as an additional state dimension significantly degraded performance, likely due to violating the zero-padded state convention of the pretraining.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the practical challenges of deploying VLA-based humanoid robots in real-world environments. Its primary significance lies in highlighting that bridging the "lab-to-store gap" for pretrained VLAs is predominantly a **systems integration challenge** rather than solely an architectural one. The work demonstrates that substantial improvements in real-world reliability can be achieved through judicious data design and targeted post-training without requiring modifications to the underlying foundation model architecture.

The **Data-Efficient (DE) stage** of the DEED framework demonstrates the critical role of practical engineering decisions. By implementing specific techniques such as frequency alignment, careful data curation, task-relevant visual highlighting (via IA-VLA), reduced VLA dependence through binary hand control, and inference-time action smoothing, the researchers transformed a non-functional VLA checkpoint into a competent system. This was achieved with limited demonstrations and a single GPU, underscoring that data quality and strategic engineering can be more impactful than sheer data volume in achieving real-world functionality. This finding can guide future research and development towards prioritizing robust deployment engineering alongside architectural advancements in VLA models.

The **Experience-Driven (ED) stage** provides empirical insights into the nuances of continual learning in real robot settings. While a single iteration of RECAP-style refinement improved performance, the subsequent iteration showed a degradation. This outcome suggests that gains from self-generated experience do not necessarily compound indefinitely and can lead to **distributional drift**. The observation that self-generated rollouts can narrow the effective state-action distribution, reducing the influence of broad teleoperation data, highlights a critical consideration for continual learning strategies. This has significant implications for designing robust, long-term deployment strategies, suggesting the necessity of maintaining a balanced mixture of diverse expert demonstrations and self-generated experiences to sustain performance, especially in high-variability humanoid tasks.

Furthermore, the research reveals a **structural finding regarding reset behavior** in multi-stage tasks. The observed suppression of reset transitions in refined policies, due to the value function prioritizing terminal completion over intermediate states, points to a need for more sophisticated reward definitions or explicit subtask incorporation within learning frameworks to enable seamless multi-item handling.

The **In/Out-Distribution Analysis Tool** is a notable contribution, offering a model-agnostic diagnostic for understanding policy behavior. By measuring shifts in the VLA's latent representation, this tool provided a concrete, testable explanation for observed performance degradation. Its potential impact extends beyond diagnosis; in principle, such a tool could be integrated into online deployment systems to detect when a policy deviates from its training distribution, triggering human intervention or automated data collection, thereby closing the loop in a self-improving robotic system.

In summary, this research offers practical guidance for deploying foundation-model-based humanoids in real retail settings by providing a structured framework that emphasizes systematic engineering and data management. Its findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the challenges and opportunities in bridging the gap between advanced AI models and reliable robotic autonomy, influencing future work on continual learning, data curation, and deployment monitoring in real-world human-robot interaction contexts.
