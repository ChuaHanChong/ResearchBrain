# Diffusion Models for Robotic Manipulation: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper provides the first comprehensive survey on Diffusion Models (DMs) in robotic manipulation, systematically classifying applications, architectures, and adaptations while highlighting their advantages in multi-modal distribution modeling and outlining future research directions.

## Problem

- Prior generative models like GANs, GMMs, and EBMs faced limitations in robotic manipulation, including training instability, mode collapse, bias towards specific modes, and difficulties with high-dimensional data or ensuring temporal consistency.
- The rapid emergence and increasing adoption of Diffusion Models in robotics since 2022 lacked a consolidated, structured overview, making it difficult for researchers to grasp the state-of-the-art and identify challenges.
- Robotic manipulation tasks inherently involve multi-modal solutions (e.g., multiple valid trajectories or grasp poses), which older models struggled to capture robustly.

## Method

- The paper provides a systematic review of state-of-the-art ==Diffusion Models== applied to robotic manipulation, categorizing them by network architecture, learning framework, and application.
- It introduces foundational mathematical concepts of DMs and details how they are adapted for robotic contexts, including conditioning on observations and handling temporal correlations.
- The survey discusses the advantages and existing limitations of diffusion-based methods in robotics, outlining promising future research directions.

## Results

- DMs are successfully applied across trajectory generation (Imitation Learning, Offline RL), robotic grasp generation (for 6-DoF poses), and visual data augmentation (for scaling datasets and scene reconstruction).
- While DMs offer improved training stability and temporal consistency, current applications face limitations in generalizability to highly novel or dynamic scenarios.
- Sampling speed remains a major challenge for real-time control, as the iterative denoising process can be computationally expensive, requiring further improvements in faster samplers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion Models excel at modeling complex, multi-modal distributions and are robust to high-dimensional data, making them suitable for tasks with multiple valid solutions like grasp generation and trajectory planning.
> - Common network architectures for DMs in robotics include Temporal U-Nets (for sample efficiency), Transformers (for long-range dependencies), and MLPs (for computational efficiency), each with specific trade-offs.
> - DMs are adapted for robotics by conditioning on multi-modal sensory inputs and handling temporal correlations, often using techniques like receding horizon control for trajectory generation.
