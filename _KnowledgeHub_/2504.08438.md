# Diffusion Models for Robotic Manipulation: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper provides the first comprehensive survey on Diffusion Models (DMs) in robotic manipulation, systematically classifying applications, architectures, and adaptations while highlighting their advantages in multi-modal distribution modeling and outlining future research directions.

## Problem

- Prior generative models like GANs, GMMs, and EBMs faced limitations in robotic manipulation, including training instability, mode collapse, bias towards specific modes, and difficulties with high-dimensional data or ensuring temporal consistency.
- The rapid emergence and increasing adoption of Diffusion Models in robotics since 2022 lacked a consolidated, structured overview, making it difficult for researchers to grasp the state-of-the-art and identify challenges.
- Robotic manipulation tasks inherently involve multi-modal solutions (e.g., multiple valid trajectories or grasp poses), which older models struggled to capture robustly.

## Method

- The paper provides a systematic review of state-of-the-art ==Diffusion Models== applied to robotic manipulation, categorizing them by network architecture, learning framework, and application.
- It introduces foundational mathematical concepts of DMs and details how they are adapted for robotic contexts, including conditioning on observations and handling temporal correlations.
- The survey discusses the advantages and existing limitations of diffusion-based methods in robotics, outlining promising future research directions.

## Results

- DMs are successfully applied across trajectory generation (Imitation Learning, Offline RL), robotic grasp generation (for 6-DoF poses), and visual data augmentation (for scaling datasets and scene reconstruction).
- While DMs offer improved training stability and temporal consistency, current applications face limitations in generalizability to highly novel or dynamic scenarios.
- Sampling speed remains a major challenge for real-time control, as the iterative denoising process can be computationally expensive, requiring further improvements in faster samplers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion Models excel at modeling complex, multi-modal distributions and are robust to high-dimensional data, making them suitable for tasks with multiple valid solutions like grasp generation and trajectory planning.
> - Common network architectures for DMs in robotics include Temporal U-Nets (for sample efficiency), Transformers (for long-range dependencies), and MLPs (for computational efficiency), each with specific trade-offs.
> - DMs are adapted for robotics by conditioning on multi-modal sensory inputs and handling temporal correlations, often using techniques like receding horizon control for trajectory generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself as the **first comprehensive survey specifically concentrating on Diffusion Models (DMs) within the field of robotic manipulation.** This is a critical contribution given the rapid emergence and significant success of DMs in broader visual domains (like image and video generation) and their relatively recent but growing adoption in robotics, particularly since 2022.

Before the advent of DMs, generative models such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs), and Energy-based Models (EBMs) were prominent in various applications. The authors highlight the limitations of these prior approaches in robotics:
- **GANs:** Often suffer from training instability, mode collapse, and hyperparameter sensitivity.
- **GMMs and EBMs (like Implicit Behavior Cloning - IBC):** While capable of modeling multi-modal distributions, they can be heavily biased toward specific modes and struggle with high-dimensional input spaces (GMMs) or complex discontinuous distributions (IBC in practice, despite theoretical capabilities).

In contrast, DMs offer distinct advantages:
- **Intrinsic ability to model any distribution:** This is crucial for robotic tasks that inherently involve multi-modal solutions (e.g., multiple valid trajectories or grasp poses for a given task).
- **Robustness to high-dimensional input and output spaces:** Essential for processing rich sensory data (images, point clouds) and generating complex actions (trajectories, 6-DoF poses).
- **Remarkable performance and stability:** Surpassing previous generative models in modeling complex and multi-modal distributions, with inherently smoother outputs for trajectories.
- **Foundation Model Integration:** Their compatibility with large visual and language models (VLMs, LLMs) facilitates the development of more generalizable and versatile robotic agents.

By surveying the state-of-the-art, the paper consolidates fragmented research, identifies common practices, highlights architectural choices, and points out the specific adaptations needed for DMs in robotics (e.g., conditioning on observations, handling temporal correlations). This systematic review is vital for new researchers entering the field, providing a structured understanding of the landscape, and for experienced researchers seeking to identify open problems and promising future directions. It establishes a foundational reference point for the rapidly expanding application of DMs in robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this survey paper are clearly articulated in its abstract and introduction:

**Motivation:**
1. **Rapid Emergence and Success of DMs:** Diffusion models have demonstrated "remarkable success in visual domains" and have "recently emerged as a promising approach in robotics." Their unique capabilities, such as modeling multi-modal distributions and robustness to high-dimensional data, make them particularly well-suited for robotic manipulation tasks where multiple valid solutions often exist (e.g., trajectory planning, grasp prediction).
2. **Addressing Limitations of Prior Generative Models:** Existing generative models like GANs, GMMs, and EBMs exhibit shortcomings such as training instability, mode collapse, bias towards specific modes, and difficulty with high-dimensional data or ensuring temporal consistency. DMs offer a superior alternative by providing stable training and inherently smooth distributions.
3. **Increasing Adoption in Robotics:** Since 2022, there has been a "noticeable increase in the implementation of diffusion probabilistic models within the field of robotic manipulation," across diverse applications. This rapid growth necessitates a consolidated overview.
4. **Identifying Key Adaptations and Challenges:** The application of DMs to robotics requires specific adaptations (e.g., conditioning on sensory inputs, handling temporal correlations in trajectories, addressing slow sampling). The survey aims to highlight these specific challenges and the methods developed to overcome them.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Comprehensive Review:** To provide a thorough review of the state-of-the-art diffusion models specifically applied to robotic manipulation.
2. **Systematic Classification:** To offer a structured classification of various DM methodologies based on:
 - Network architecture (CNNs, Transformers, MLPs)
 - Learning framework (Imitation Learning, Reinforcement Learning)
 - Application (Trajectory Generation, Grasp Synthesis, Visual Data Augmentation)
 - Evaluation metrics and benchmarks.
3. **Background Information:** To provide readers with the necessary fundamental mathematical concepts of DMs.
4. **Discussion of Architectures and Adaptations:** To detail the common network architectures and how DMs are adapted for robotic contexts, including improving sampling speed and quality.
5. **Highlighting Advantages and Challenges:** To discuss the benefits and existing limitations of current diffusion-based methods in robotics.
6. **Outline Future Research Directions:** To identify unsolved problems and potential avenues for future research.

In essence, the paper aims to serve as a foundational resource for the robotics community, synthesizing the current knowledge on DMs in robotic manipulation and guiding future research efforts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology focuses on a structured and comprehensive review of existing literature rather than experimental results. The authors adopt a systematic approach to organize and present the vast body of work on diffusion models in robotic manipulation.

The paper's methodological approach can be broken down into the following components:

1. **Foundational Background (Section 2):** The survey begins by establishing a necessary theoretical foundation for DMs. It introduces the two main mathematical frameworks: score-based DMs (Song and Ermon, 2019) and Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) (Ho et al., 2020). This section is crucial for readers to understand the underlying principles before delving into their robotic applications.
2. **Architectural Improvements and Adaptations (Section 2.2 & 3):**
 - **General Improvements:** The paper discusses general advancements in DM architectures aimed at improving sampling speed and quality, such as SDE/ODE formulations (Song et al., 2021b), Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) (Song et al., 2021a), DPM-solver (Lu et al., 2022), and knowledge distillation techniques (Salimans and Ho, 2022). It also introduces Flow Matching as an alternative to diffusion, highlighting its advantages in stability and sampling speed.
 - **Robotics-Specific Adaptations:** It then elaborates on how DMs are tailored for robotic manipulation tasks. Key adaptations include:
 - **Conditioning on Observations:** Explaining how DMs are conditioned on multi-modal sensory data (images, point clouds, language instructions) or proprioception, often using techniques like Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) or cross-attention.
 - **Handling Temporal Correlations:** Discussing strategies for generating actions/trajectories, particularly the popular "receding horizon control" for sub-trajectory generation, which balances efficiency and reactivity.
 - **Network Architectures (Section 3):** A dedicated section categorizes and compares the predominant neural network architectures used for the noise prediction network: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Transformers, and Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). It discusses their respective advantages (e.g., U-Net for temporal consistency, Transformers for long-range dependencies, MLPs for computational efficiency) and disadvantages (e.g., over-smoothing by U-Nets, high resource consumption of Transformers).
3. **Categorization of Applications (Section 4):** The core of the survey systematically categorizes DM applications into three main areas, providing detailed discussions within each:
 - **Trajectory Generation (Section 4.1):** Divided into Imitation Learning and Offline Reinforcement Learning. It discusses action and pose representations, visual data modalities (2D vs. 3D), trajectory planning as image generation, and methods for long-horizon, multi-task, and constrained planning, including integration with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
 - **Robotic Grasp Generation (Section 4.2):** Focuses on explicit diffusion on SE(3) grasp poses, diffusion in latent space, and DMs as feature encoders or image generators for grasp tasks. It also explores affordance-driven diffusion and object pose diffusion for reorientation/rearrangement.
 - **Visual Data Augmentation (Section 4.3):** Covers the use of DMs for scaling datasets, scene augmentation (e.g., changing textures, replacing objects), sensor data reconstruction (e.g., view completion), and object rearrangement.
4. **Experimental Evaluation and Benchmarks (Section 5):** The survey consolidates information on common benchmarks (e.g., CALVIN, RLBench, D4RL Kitchen) and baselines (e.g., Diffusion Policy, SE(3)-Diffusion Policy, Diffuser) used to evaluate DM performance in robotics. It also notes the prevalence of evaluation in both simulation and real-world setups.
5. **Challenges, Limitations, and Future Outlook (Section 6):** The authors critically analyze the common limitations of current DM applications in robotics, primarily focusing on generalizability and sampling speed. They then conclude by outlining promising future research directions based on these identified gaps.

The authors use tables (e.g., Tables 1-5) and figures (e.g., Figure 1, 2) to visually summarize taxonomies, technical details, and processes, enhancing the clarity and accessibility of the complex information presented.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey, the paper's "findings and results" are the syntheses and conclusions drawn by the authors from their extensive review of the literature. These insights reveal the current state-of-the-art, common trends, identified advantages, persistent challenges, and crucial open research questions in the application of Diffusion Models (DMs) to robotic manipulation.

**Key Strengths of DMs in Robotic Manipulation:**
1. **Superior Multi-Modal Distribution Modeling:** DMs excel at modeling complex, multi-modal distributions, which is inherently beneficial for robotics tasks where multiple distinct, valid solutions (e.g., different grasp poses, alternative trajectories) often exist. They address limitations of GMMs and EBMs in capturing all modes and avoiding bias.
2. **Robustness to High-Dimensional Data:** DMs demonstrate strong performance and stability when dealing with high-dimensional inputs (e.g., raw images, point clouds) and outputs (e.g., long-horizon trajectories, 6-DoF poses), overcoming challenges faced by earlier generative models.
3. **Training Stability:** Compared to GANs, DMs generally exhibit greater training stability, avoiding issues like mode collapse and hyperparameter sensitivity.
4. **Temporal Consistency and Smoothness:** DMs are noted for generating inherently smooth distributions, which is vital for continuous robot motions and maintaining temporal consistency in predicted trajectories.
5. **Integration with Foundation Models:** Their architecture naturally allows conditioning on multi-modal inputs, making them highly compatible with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) for enhanced generalizability and task specification.

**Dominant Architectures and Their Trade-offs:**
- **Temporal U-Nets (CNN-based):** Most frequently employed, especially for visuomotor policies. They are sample-efficient and generalize well with smaller datasets. While robust to hyperparameters, they might over-smooth high-frequency trajectories.
- **Transformers:** Proficient in capturing long-range dependencies and robust to high-dimensional data, making them suitable for long-horizon and high-level decision-making tasks. However, they are computationally intensive.
- **MLPs:** Offer superior computational efficiency and training stability, commonly used in Reinforcement Learning, but perform inferiorly with complex visual and high-dimensional inputs without prior encoding.

**Key Applications and Their Adaptations:**
1. **Trajectory Generation:**
 - **Imitation Learning (IL):** Most common. DMs are conditioned on sensory observations to generate smooth action trajectories. Receding horizon control is prevalent for planning sub-trajectories to balance efficiency and reactivity.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** DMs are integrated, primarily in offline RL, by incorporating reward signals (e.g., through classifier guidance or Q-learning). This has the potential to generalize better than IL in scenarios with diverse and suboptimal data.
 - **Multi-Task & Long-Horizon Planning:** DMs facilitate hierarchical planning, skill learning, and integration with VLAs, leveraging their ability to handle multi-modal inputs and learn complex action distributions for versatile robot behaviors.
 - **Constrained Planning:** Methods like classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance steer DM generation towards constraint satisfaction, though guaranteeing hard constraints remains an area of research.
2. **Robotic Grasp Generation:** DMs are used for generating 6-DoF grasp poses. The non-Euclidean nature of SE(3) poses requires specialized adaptations (e.g., using energy-based models on Lie groups or flow matching techniques for continuous velocity fields). Equivariance properties are crucial for efficient and robust grasp generation.
3. **Visual Data Augmentation:** Pre-trained DMs are powerful tools for augmenting robot datasets:
 - **Scaling Data & Scene Augmentation:** Generating semantically meaningful variations (e.g., object textures, backgrounds, positions) to increase data diversity and improve generalizability, especially for vision-based tasks.
 - **Sensor Data Reconstruction:** Inpainting missing depth or visual information from incomplete sensor data, particularly relevant for single-view camera setups and occluded scenes.
 - **Object Rearrangement:** Generating target arrangements from high-level language prompts, with subsequent methods focusing on ensuring geometric consistency.

**Identified Limitations and Challenges:**
1. **Generalizability:** Despite improvements, DMs in robotics still face limitations in handling highly dynamic, entirely novel, or out-of-distribution scenarios, largely due to dependence on training data quality (for IL) or the limitations of offline RL.
2. **Sampling Speed:** The iterative denoising process of DMs remains a major bottleneck for real-time control, even with advancements like DDIM or DPM-solver. While real-time control is *possible* with specific setups (e.g., DP with 10 denoising steps), further improvements are needed, and comparisons of faster samplers on robotics benchmarks are scarce.
3. **Computational Resources:** Training large DM architectures (especially Transformers) and generating high-dimensional outputs can be computationally expensive.
4. **Constraint Satisfaction:** While methods exist to guide DMs with constraints, guaranteeing hard constraint satisfaction (critical for safety-critical applications) is still an active research area.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance and has several potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation and the broader machine learning community:

1. **Consolidating Knowledge and Bridging Gaps:** As the first comprehensive survey explicitly focused on DMs in robotic manipulation, it serves as a critical resource for researchers. It brings together fragmented research from various sub-disciplines (computer vision, reinforcement learning, motion planning) into a unified framework. This consolidation helps new researchers quickly grasp the state-of-the-art and facilitates cross-pollination of ideas.
2. **Guiding Future Research:** By systematically categorizing methodologies, highlighting advantages, and rigorously detailing limitations, the survey acts as a roadmap for future research. It explicitly points out "unsolved problems" and "potential directions for future research," such as:
 - **Improving Sampling Efficiency:** Emphasizing the need to evaluate and adapt faster samplers developed in computer vision to robotics, and further develop robotics-specific fast sampling methods.
 - **Enhancing Generalizability and Adaptability:** Stressing the need for DMs to handle highly dynamic and unfamiliar environments, suggesting continual learning, improved data augmentation strategies, and more robust integration of VLAs.
 - **Robust Constraint Satisfaction:** Identifying the need for DMs that can guarantee constraint adherence, particularly for safety-critical applications.
 - **Addressing Data Scarcity:** Leveraging DMs' generative capabilities for data augmentation and scene reconstruction is crucial for reducing the burden of real-world data collection.
3. **Promoting Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The survey highlights how DMs, originating in computer vision, have been successfully adapted to robotics. This emphasizes the value of interdisciplinary approaches and encourages researchers to draw inspiration from advancements in related fields.
4. **Informing Application Development:** For practitioners and engineers, the survey provides a structured overview of which DM architectures and approaches are best suited for specific robotic manipulation tasks (e.g., U-Nets for positional control, Transformers for velocity control, MLPs for efficiency). This practical guidance can accelerate the adoption and deployment of DM-based solutions in real-world robotics.
5. **Benchmarking and Evaluation Standardization:** By summarizing common benchmarks and baselines, the paper indirectly promotes more consistent and comparable evaluations across different research efforts, leading to more rigorous scientific progress.
6. **Unlocking New Capabilities:** The intrinsic ability of DMs to model multi-modal distributions and handle high-dimensional spaces unlocks new possibilities for robotic systems, enabling them to handle complex, ambiguous tasks with greater dexterity and adaptability than previously possible with older generative models. The integration with Vision-Language-Action models holds particular promise for creating more intuitive and human-like robotic capabilities.

In conclusion, this survey is a timely and valuable contribution that organizes the burgeoning field of DMs in robotic manipulation. It not only chronicles past achievements but also critically assesses the current landscape, setting the stage for future breakthroughs in making robots more versatile, intelligent, and capable in complex real-world environments.
