# Enhancing Geometric Perception in VLMs via Translator-Guided Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University and Guangdong Laboratory of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Economy developed GEOPERCEIVE, a benchmark for geometric perception, and GEODPO, a translator-guided reinforcement learning framework. This approach enhanced Vision-Language Models' ability to interpret geometric diagrams, boosting downstream reasoning accuracy by up to 39% on MathVista and substantially reducing geometric hallucinations.

## Problem

- Vision-language models (VLMs) demonstrate limitations in geometric perception, often misinterpreting fundamental geometric primitives and their relationships (e.g., confusing an intersection with a tangency).
- Existing end-to-end benchmarks for geometric problem solving conflate visual misinterpretation with symbolic reasoning failures, making it difficult to precisely diagnose VLM limitations.
- Current domain-specific languages (DSLs) for geometric problems suffer from ambiguity or verbose syntax, which hinders rigorous evaluation and stable model training.

## Method

- Introduced ==GEOPERCEIVE==, an automatically generated benchmark featuring pixel-level geometric diagrams paired with unambiguous, canonical GEODSL programs, designed for isolated geometric perception evaluation.
- Developed ==GEODPO== (Translator-Guided Direct Preference Optimization), a reinforcement learning framework that uses an NL-to-GEODSL translator to provide fine-grained, DSL-level reward signals.
- GEODPO fine-tunes VLMs to generate improved natural language descriptions by leveraging ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)==, which addresses permutation-equivalence and maintains alignment with the VLM's natural language pretraining.

## Results

- GEODPO consistently improved geometric perception scores (in-domain) by **+14.2%** to **+26.46%** across various VLM backbones compared to raw models.
- The framework demonstrated superior generalization, improving out-of-domain perception by **+3.68%** to **+8.0%** and achieving substantial gains of up to **39.0%** on the MathVista geometry subset for downstream reasoning.
- Qualitative analysis showed a substantial reduction in geometric hallucinations, with GEODPO-trained models more accurately grounding visual elements and their relationships in diagrams.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A canonical and unambiguous Domain-Specific Language (GEODSL) is critical for precise, program-level evaluation and scalable, automated data generation in geometric perception tasks.
> - Employing a pre-trained NL-to-DSL translator effectively bridges the VLM's natural language output space to the formal geometric representation, enabling fine-grained supervision via reinforcement learning.
> - Trajectory-level reinforcement learning (DPO) applied to NL outputs provides superior generalization and robustness to distribution shifts compared to token-level supervised fine-tuning in improving VLM geometric perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated advancements in various multimodal reasoning tasks. However, the specific domain of geometric problem solving (GPS) continues to present a notable challenge for these models. This difficulty is primarily attributed to limitations in accurate geometric perception, which is the foundational ability to identify and ground geometric primitives (points, lines, circles) and their spatial relationships within a visual diagram. Current state-of-the-art VLMs frequently misinterpret these fundamental elements, for instance, confusing an intersection with a tangency.

Existing end-to-end benchmarks for GPS often conflate errors stemming from visual misinterpretation with those arising from symbolic reasoning failures. This integrated evaluation approach makes it challenging to pinpoint the precise source of model limitations and address specific perceptual deficiencies.

Prior efforts in formal geometric representation have introduced domain-specific languages (DSLs) such as GMBL, AlphaGeometry, and Inter-GPS. While these languages aim to specify geometric problems, they often suffer from issues such as ambiguity (multiple semantically equivalent representations for a single diagram) or verbose syntax, which can hinder rigorous evaluation and stable model training. Furthermore, existing datasets for GPS, while valuable, frequently rely on answer-aware metrics and lack a unified, controllable framework specifically for geometric perception evaluation.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has been applied to various natural language processing and mathematical reasoning tasks, including theorem proving and mathematical word problems. Within geometry, RL approaches have explored end-to-end problem solving. This paper distinguishes itself by employing RL to refine intermediate representations (Natural Language to DSL translation) with a focus on enhancing geometric consistency in perception, rather than direct end-to-end problem solving.

This work addresses two specific questions within this landscape: how to precisely measure geometric perception, and how to effectively improve it in VLMs. By introducing a new benchmark and a guided RL framework, the authors aim to isolate and enhance the geometric perceptual capabilities of VLMs, which is a prerequisite for robust geometric reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the geometric perception capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), recognizing that deficiencies in this area significantly impede their performance in geometric reasoning tasks. The authors identify two primary challenges that motivate their work:

**Challenge 1: Ambiguous Measurement of Geometric Perception**
- **Motivation:** Current evaluation paradigms for VLMs on geometry problems typically assess end-to-end performance, conflating errors in visual perception with errors in subsequent symbolic reasoning. This makes it difficult to diagnose whether a VLM fails because it cannot correctly "see" the geometric elements and their relations or because it cannot "reason" effectively given correct perceptions. For example, a VLM might misinterpret an intersection as a tangency, leading to incorrect reasoning, but the root cause is a perceptual error. There is a need for a mechanism to isolate and quantify geometric perception independently.
- **Objective:** To develop a benchmark, named **GEOPERCEIVE**, that enables the isolated and unambiguous evaluation of geometric perception. This benchmark must pair pixel-level diagrams with a formal, canonical, and complete representation of their geometric content. The representation must be unambiguous, meaning a single diagram corresponds to exactly one formal description, thus ensuring consistent and rigorous scoring. This also requires an efficient, automatic data generation pipeline to overcome the limitations and costs associated with human annotation.

**Challenge 2: Inefficient and Unstable Methods for Improving Geometric Perception**
- **Motivation:** Even with a benchmark providing structured data, directly improving VLM perception through conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) presents difficulties. Geometric diagrams often have permutation-equivalent representations (e.g., multiple valid orderings of elements describe the same diagram), which SFT struggles to handle as it typically optimizes for a single instance and can encourage surface-form patterns rather than invariant geometric properties. Furthermore, direct fine-tuning to emit formal DSLs can lead to a significant distribution shift from a VLM's natural language (NL) pretraining, requiring extensive data and training to stabilize performance. The goal is to enhance perception while keeping the VLM aligned with its NL-centric pretraining.
- **Objective:** To propose a training framework, named **GEODPO (Translator-Guided Direct Preference Optimization)**, that leverages the automatically generated data from GEOPERCEIVE to improve geometric perception. This framework aims to bridge the gap between natural language outputs and formal geometric descriptions without forcing the VLM off its NL manifold. It achieves this by introducing an NL-to-DSL translator, trained on synthetic NL–DSL pairs, to provide fine-grained, DSL-level reward signals. These signals are then used in a reinforcement learning (RL) paradigm (specifically Direct Preference Optimization, DPO) to guide the VLM's learning, addressing the order-permutation issue and mitigating distribution shift by operating on NL outputs.

In summary, the authors are motivated to create a robust system for both measuring and enhancing geometric perception in VLMs, focusing on clarity, scalability, and computational efficiency, thereby improving the fundamental understanding of visual geometry crucial for complex reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes GEOPERCEIVE, a benchmark for geometric perception, and GEODPO, a translator-guided reinforcement learning framework to enhance VLM capabilities.

#### 3.1. GEOPERCEIVE: Benchmark for Geometric Perception

GEOPERCEIVE is designed to evaluate geometric perception by pairing rendered diagrams with formal domain-specific language (DSL) representations. It consists of two engines: a GEODSL generation engine and a diagram solving engine.

##### 3.1.1. GEODSL: Domain-Specific Language for Geometry Perception

- **Design Principles:** GEODSL is designed for unambiguity and simplicity. Unlike existing DSLs, it ensures a one-to-one correspondence between a visual diagram and its formal representation, avoiding multiple semantically equivalent programs for a single diagram. This is achieved by using descriptive, relational statements (e.g., "points A, B, E lie on ℓ1") rather than constructive instructions. The language uses a minimal set of literals to ensure linear growth of program length with the number of elements.
- **Definitions:** A GEODSL program is a tuple G = ⟨P, L, C, R⟩, representing sets of Point, Line, Circle, and Constraint literals, respectively. Point-curve incidence relationships are inferred automatically within curve declarations. Examples of literals include `Point("A")`, `Line([A, B])`, `Circle(Point("O"), [Point("A")])`, and various constraints like `Parallel`, `Perpendicular`, `LineCircleTangent`, `CircleCircleTangent`, and `Equal` segment lengths.

##### 3.1.2. Benchmark Definitions and Metrics

- **Instance Representation:** Each instance is a tuple ⟨G, D⟩, where G is the ground-truth GEODSL program and D is the rendered diagram.
- **Model Output:** A VLM M takes D as input and predicts a GEODSL program ˆG = M(D).
- **Scoring:** `Score(G, ˆG)` is calculated as a weighted average of F1-scores for each predicate category (Points, Lines, Circles, Constraints).
 - F1-score for each category is derived from precision and recall, which are based on an aggregated similarity score `S(X, ˆX)`.
 - `S(X, ˆX)` is computed by solving a maximum-weight bipartite assignment problem (e.g., Hungarian algorithm) between ground-truth and predicted primitives, using element-wise matching scores `s(a,b)`.
 - `s(a,b)` depends on the primitive type: label identity for points, F1-score of constituent point sets for lines, a combination of center point similarity and constituent point F1 for circles, and specific `ConstraintScore` definitions for constraints. Weights for the overall score are set equally to 0.25 for each category.

##### 3.1.3. GEODSL Generation Engine

- **Purpose:** Automatically generate a large-scale, diverse, and complexity-controllable dataset of GEODSL programs.
- **Process:**
 1. **Primitive Initialization Stage:** Randomly selects a fundamental geometric configuration (e.g., a triangle, quadrilateral, or circle) as the starting point.
 2. **Iterative Construction Stage:** Progressively expands the GEODSL program by repeatedly selecting and applying geometric construction operations from a predefined pool (e.g., constructing an orthocenter, circumcenter, incenter, adding new segments, or points on curves). This process adds new primitives and constraints.

##### 3.1.4. Diagram Solving Engine

- **Purpose:** Render generated GEODSL programs into pixel-level diagrams.
- **Approach:** Implemented in PyTorch, it formulates geometric construction as a continuous, constrained-optimization problem.
 1. **Primitive Parameterization:** Initializes real-valued parameters for all points (coordinates), lines (implicit equation coefficients), and circles (center coordinates, radius).
 2. **Constraint Formulation as Losses:** Converts every explicit and implicit geometric constraint (e.g., point on a line, perpendicular lines) into a differentiable loss function.
 3. **Visual Plausibility Penalties:** Incorporates penalty terms into the objective function to ensure visually coherent diagrams, such as preventing point clustering (density penalty), unnatural scattering (distribution penalty), and ensuring elements remain within bounds (scale and boundary penalties).
 4. **Iterative Optimization:** Combines all weighted loss and penalty terms into a unified objective function. Uses an iterative optimization algorithm (e.g., Adam) to minimize this objective, determining the primitive parameters that satisfy the constraints and optimize visual plausibility. Programs that fail to converge are excluded.

#### 3.2. GEODPO: Translator-Guided Direct Preference Optimization

GEODPO is a framework to enhance VLM geometric perception by leveraging the GEOPERCEIVE data for reinforcement learning.

##### 3.2.1. NL2DSL Translator

- **Training Data Generation:** To train the translator, a parallel corpus of natural language (NL) descriptions and GEODSL programs is needed. This is generated by providing existing GEODSL programs to a capable VLM and instructing it to produce descriptive NL paragraphs, creating (NL, GEODSL) pairs.
- **Translator Function:** A language model (initialized from Qwen2.5-7B) is fine-tuned on these pairs to serve as the NL2DSL translator `T`. It takes an NL description `S` as input and outputs a corresponding GEODSL program `G_hat = T(S)`.
- **Role:** The translator bridges the NL output space of VLMs to the formal GEODSL space, enabling fine-grained, DSL-level scoring of NL descriptions.

##### 3.2.2. Translator-Guided Reinforcement Learning

- **Reward Model:** The translator `T`, combined with the GEOPERCEIVE scoring metric `Score(G_true, T(S))`, acts as a reward model, evaluating the quality of VLM-generated NL descriptions `S` for a given diagram `D` and ground-truth `G_true`.
- **Preference Pair Generation (Algorithm 1):**
 1. For each diagram `D` with ground-truth `G_true`, `N_samples` natural language descriptions `{S_1..., S_N_samples}` are sampled from the VLM (reference policy `π_ref`).
 2. Each `S_i` is translated to `ˆG_i = T(S_i)` and scored as `s_i = Score(G_true, ˆG_i)`.
 3. These (S_k, s_k) samples are sorted by score. Preference pairs `(S_w, S_l)` (winner, loser) are then constructed, where `S_w` has a higher score than `S_l` by at least a minimum difference `δ_min`.
- **DPO Training:** The VLM (policy `π_θ`) is fine-tuned using the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) loss. This objective function `L_DPO` drives `π_θ` to assign higher likelihoods to preferred responses `S_w` and lower likelihoods to dispreferred responses `S_l`, relative to the initial reference policy `π_ref`. This process aligns the VLM to generate NL descriptions that correspond to geometrically accurate GEODSL programs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments assessed GEODPO on various VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA-Next) across in-domain perception, out-of-domain perception, and downstream reasoning tasks, comparing it against raw models and supervised fine-tuning (SFT).

#### 4.1. NL2GEODSL Translator Performance

- The NL2DSL translator, initialized from Qwen2.5-7B and trained on the GEOPERCEIVE Translator split, consistently achieved 100% syntactic validity for generated programs.
- Average F1-scores were high: over 95% for points and lines, and approximately 90.8% for constraints, and 74.6% for circles.
- Performance showed graceful degradation with increasing geometric complexity (iterations 4-5), particularly for circular primitives and complex constraints. This indicates the translator effectively bridges NL to GEODSL.

#### 4.2. GEODPO Main Experiments (In-domain Perception)

- **Overall Performance:** GEODPO consistently surpassed both the raw (unfinetuned) models and SFT models across all three VLM families. Overall score improvements ranged from +14.2% to +26.46% relative to the raw baselines.
- **Element-wise Performance:** GEODPO demonstrated improvements across all perception types (Points, Lines, Circles, Constraints). Notably, for the "Constraints" category, SFT models sometimes exhibited performance degradation (e.g., InternVL3 and LLaVA-Next), whereas GEODPO consistently achieved substantial gains, ranging from +9.9% to +19.27%. This suggests GEODPO not only strengthens fundamental geometric feature perception but also stabilizes the more intricate aspects of constraint reasoning, which SFT might negatively impact.

#### 4.3. Out-of-Distribution Evaluation

- **GEOPERCEIVE-OOD (Perception):** On a hand-crafted OOD test set, SFT proved to be fragile, showing only marginal improvements or, in some cases, performance regressions. In contrast, GEODPO consistently enhanced OOD scores across all backbones (e.g., +3.68% for Qwen2.5-VL, +3.69% for InternVL3, +8.0% for LLaVA-Next), with concurrent category-wise gains.
- **MathVista (Downstream Reasoning):** GEODPO yielded substantial improvements on the geometry subset of the MathVista benchmark compared to both raw and SFT models. For example, InternVL3's accuracy increased from 29.06% to 40.39% (+39.0%). SFT, conversely, provided limited benefits in these downstream reasoning tasks.
- **Generalization:** These results collectively indicate that trajectory-level RL reward optimization (GEODPO) achieves stronger performance and superior generalization abilities compared to token-level log-likelihood SFT, with its advantages becoming particularly evident under distribution shifts.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis of Perception Performance

- A case study revealed that pretrained models often produced severe geometric hallucinations, failing to correctly perceive most geometric relations.
- In contrast, models trained with GEODPO accurately grounded the majority of visual elements, including point incidences on circumferences and the arrangement of chords. Minor errors, such as misidentification of a triangle due to point proximity, were observed but were less pervasive than in baseline models. This suggests GEODPO leads to substantially fewer geometric hallucinations.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study on Translator Quality

- Experiments with weaker translators (trained on less data) showed that the performance of the GEODPO framework significantly degraded. This confirms that the quality of the NL2DSL translator directly impacts the effectiveness of the reward signal and, consequently, GEODPO's overall performance.

#### 4.6. Exploration of Alternative RL Algorithms

- An additional experiment using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) showed that it performed on par with DPO but was slower due to the overhead of training an additional value model. This indicates the framework's flexibility in accommodating various RL algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by specifically addressing their limitations in geometric perception, a fundamental capability for advanced multimodal reasoning.

**Key areas of significance include:**

- **Addressing a Core VLM Weakness:** The work directly tackles the observed struggles of VLMs with geometric reasoning. By isolating geometric perception from reasoning, it provides a clearer pathway to improving VLM capabilities in this domain.
- **Novel and Scalable Benchmark:** GEOPERCEIVE introduces a canonical and ambiguity-free DSL (GEODSL) for representing geometric diagrams, enabling exact, program-level evaluation of perception. The automated data generation pipeline is a significant advancement, offering a complexity-controllable and scalable source of training and evaluation data, thereby drastically reducing the cost and effort associated with manual annotation. This benchmark can serve as a standardized tool for future research in geometric perception.
- **Effective Training Paradigm:** GEODPO offers a robust and data-efficient method for enhancing VLM geometric perception. By employing a translator-guided DPO approach, it effectively leverages synthetic data while mitigating common challenges of supervised fine-tuning (SFT), such as sensitivity to permutation-equivalent programs and distribution shifts from natural language pretraining. The strategy of keeping the VLM aligned with its natural language manifold while receiving DSL-level rewards is a notable design choice.
- **Demonstrated Performance Gains and Generalization:** The experimental results consistently show that GEODPO yields substantial improvements over both raw and SFT-tuned models across diverse settings. This includes significant gains in in-domain perception (+26.5%), out-of-domain perception (+8.0%), and particularly impressive improvements in downstream reasoning tasks (+39.0%). The superior generalization ability, especially under distribution shifts, indicates the robustness of the GEODPO framework.
- **Reduction in Geometric Hallucinations:** Qualitative analyses suggest that GEODPO leads to a substantial reduction in geometric hallucinations, meaning the models more accurately interpret and ground visual elements and their relationships within diagrams. This directly translates to more reliable and interpretable VLM outputs for geometric tasks.
- **Facilitating Future Research:** By releasing the code, the authors promote reproducibility and provide a foundation for further research into geometric perception, formal language integration, and advanced reinforcement learning techniques for multimodal models. The identified limitation regarding purely algebraic constraints also points to a clear and impactful direction for future work.
