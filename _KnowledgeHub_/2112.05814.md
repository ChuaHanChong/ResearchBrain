# Deep ViT Features as Dense Visual Descriptors

> [!summary] Summary
> Features extracted from self-supervised DINO-ViT models serve as powerful dense visual descriptors, encoding fine-grained, cross-category semantic part information at high spatial resolution. Leveraging these inherent properties with simple zero-shot methods achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance in co-segmentation, part co-segmentation, and point correspondence tasks.

## Problem

- Features from Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) were the standard for dense visual descriptors but exhibited limitations like texture bias, limited receptive fields, and a trade-off between spatial resolution and semantic depth.
- The emergence of Vision Transformers (ViTs) raised the question of whether their internal representations offer a superior, general-purpose alternative to CNN features.
- Prior work on DINO-ViT mainly focused on last-layer attention for object saliency, lacking an in-depth understanding of its internal features across various layers and self-attention facets.

## Method

- Conducted an in-depth empirical investigation of features from pre-trained supervised and self-supervised (==DINO==) ViT models, analyzing different encoder layers and self-attention facets (tokens, queries, keys, values).
- Developed lightweight, zero-shot methodologies for ==co-segmentation==, ==part co-segmentation==, and ==point correspondences==, which directly leverage the intrinsic properties of **DINO-ViT** features without any additional training or fine-tuning.
- Modified ViT at test time to extract overlapping patches and interpolate positional encodings for increased spatial resolution, and employed simple techniques like ==k-means clustering==, ==saliency-based voting==, and context integration (==binning==) for downstream tasks.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance in co-segmentation and part co-segmentation on intra-class, inter-class (PASCAL-CO), and video datasets, significantly outperforming unsupervised baselines.
- Demonstrated robust part co-segmentation in challenging inter-class scenarios (e.g., bicycle-unicycle, fox-leopard) and for novel domains (e.g., Pikachu), showing superior cross-category generalization.
- Significantly improved point correspondence accuracy compared to unsupervised baselines (e.g., NBB), exhibiting greater robustness to changes in appearance, pose, and scale, and substantially closing the gap with supervised methods on Spair71k.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DINO-ViT features encode well-localized semantic information, such as object parts, at a high spatial resolution that is maintained across all transformer layers.
> - Self-supervised DINO-ViT features capture semantic similarity of object parts *across different object categories* (e.g., 'ears' from various animals clustering together), a property not observed in supervised ViTs or CNNs.
> - Positional bias in ViT features evolves gradually across layers, with intermediate layers providing a valuable balance of both positional and semantic information; the 'keys' facet empirically offers cleaner representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental question at the intersection of deep learning and computer vision: the nature and utility of deep features as visual descriptors. For years, features extracted from pre-trained Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), such as VGG and ResNet, have served as the de facto standard for a wide array of visual tasks, including image generation, manipulation, tracking, and establishing correspondences. These "deep features" were celebrated for their hierarchical representation, capturing everything from low-level textures to high-level semantic concepts. However, CNNs have known limitations, including a bias towards texture over shape, limited receptive fields, and a trade-off between spatial resolution and semantic depth in deeper layers.

The emergence of Vision Transformers (ViTs) has recently provided a powerful alternative to CNNs, demonstrating superior performance and robustness in many visual tasks. This shift in architecture naturally leads to the crucial question posed by the authors: Do ViT's internal representations offer a superior alternative to CNN features as general dense visual descriptors? This work seeks to answer this by exploring the unique properties of features extracted from ViTs.

The paper specifically focuses on **self-supervised ViT models, particularly DINO-ViT**. Previous research on DINO-ViT has noted its ability to produce attention maps that highlight salient foreground objects, leading to its application in tasks like object discovery and segmentation, primarily utilizing features from the final layer. This paper distinguishes itself from these prior works in several key ways:

1. **In-depth Feature Analysis:** Instead of treating DINO-ViT as a black box and focusing solely on the last layer, the authors delve into the internal workings of the self-attention modules, examining different "facets" (tokens, queries, keys, values) across *various* layers of the transformer. This granular analysis aims to uncover a richer set of properties beyond simple saliency.
2. **Novel Feature Properties:** The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates new, striking properties of DINO-ViT features: (i) their ability to encode powerful, well-localized semantic information at high spatial granularity, such as object parts; (ii) the unexpected sharing of this semantic information across diverse, yet related, object categories; and (iii) a gradual evolution of positional bias across layers. These observations significantly expand our understanding of what ViTs learn.
3. **Zero-Shot Generalization:** A crucial contribution is the demonstration that these inherent properties can be leveraged with lightweight, zero-shot methodologies (e.g., simple binning and clustering) to tackle fundamental vision tasks without any additional training or fine-tuning. This contrasts sharply with most state-of-the-art methods that require extensive supervised training data for each specific task. This zero-shot approach highlights the intrinsic power and generalizability of the learned DINO-ViT representations.
4. **Challenging Scenarios:** The work pushes the boundaries of applications like part co-segmentation by demonstrating its effectiveness in highly unconstrained settings, such as across different object categories (e.g., a fox and a leopard, a bicycle and a unicycle) and even in videos, where training data is typically scarce or unavailable. This capability for cross-category and domain-agnostic part understanding is a novel achievement.

By providing a deeper empirical understanding of ViT features and demonstrating their power through robust, zero-shot applications, this research positions ViT features, especially from self-supervised models, as a strong contender and potentially superior alternative to traditional CNN features for dense visual descriptions, thus significantly advancing the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate and characterize the properties of deep features extracted from pre-trained Vision Transformers (ViTs) as versatile, dense visual descriptors. This investigation is primarily driven by a desire to understand if and how the recently emerged ViT architectures, particularly those trained with self-supervision, offer a more robust and semantically rich alternative to the long-standing CNN-based features.

The key motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Assessing ViT Internal Representations:** ViTs have demonstrated impressive performance and superior robustness to factors like occlusions, adversarial attacks, and texture bias compared to CNNs in various vision tasks. The authors are motivated to explore whether these external performance gains translate into equally powerful and distinct internal representations, and if these representations can serve as a general-purpose foundation for other vision problems. This addresses the fundamental question: "Do these properties reflect on the internal representations learned by ViTs? Should we consider deep ViT features as an alternative to deep CNN features?"
2. **Uncovering Novel Feature Properties:** Prior work on DINO-ViT primarily focused on its attention heads exhibiting foreground saliency. The authors are motivated to conduct a more granular investigation, examining features across different layers and various facets (keys, queries, values, tokens) within the self-attention modules, to uncover a more comprehensive set of properties beyond mere object-level saliency. They seek to understand the hierarchy of information (positional vs. semantic) and how semantics are encoded.
3. **Highlighting the Role of Self-Supervision:** The paper is specifically motivated to demonstrate the significant impact of the training supervision (self-supervised DINO vs. supervised ImageNet classification) on the learned ViT features. They aim to show that self-supervision fosters more generalizable and semantically coherent representations, especially across object categories.
4. **Demonstrating Intrinsic Power through Zero-Shot Applications:** A core motivation is to showcase the inherent power of DINO-ViT representations by applying them to fundamental computer vision tasks (co-segmentation, part co-segmentation, and semantic correspondences) using only simple, lightweight, and *zero-shot* methodologies. By deliberately avoiding additional training, fine-tuning, or complex design choices, the authors aim to "distill the power of ViT features from convoluted design choices," thereby proving the efficacy of the learned representations themselves.
5. **Addressing Challenging Generalization Scenarios:** The authors are motivated to push the boundaries of existing methods by applying their zero-shot framework to challenging, unconstrained scenarios. This includes demonstrating part co-segmentation across vastly different object categories, for an arbitrary number of input images, and even extending to video, domains where current supervised methods often struggle due to lack of diverse training data. This highlights the superior generalization capabilities offered by DINO-ViT features.

In essence, the research aims to provide a deeper understanding of ViT's internal mechanisms, unlock new capabilities through novel feature properties, and advocate for DINO-ViT features as a powerful, generalizable, and readily applicable alternative to CNN features for a broad spectrum of computer vision tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper revolves around a deep empirical investigation of ViT features and their application to various vision tasks using lightweight, zero-shot techniques.

#### 3.1. Model Selection and Feature Extraction

The authors focus on two pre-trained Vision Transformer models:
1. **Supervised ViT:** Trained for image classification on ImageNet.
2. **Self-supervised DINO-ViT:** Trained using a self-distillation approach without labels.
The DINO-ViT model emerges as the primary focus due to its superior properties. The methodology involves extracting features not just from the deepest layer, but across multiple transformer encoder layers. Critically, they explore different "facets" within each Multi-head Self-Attention (MSA) block: tokens, queries, keys, and values, which are directly associated with image patches. Empirically, they found **keys** to provide a slightly better representation for their tasks, exhibiting less sensitivity to background clutter.

#### 3.2. Empirical Observations and Feature Properties

A significant part of the methodology involves qualitative and quantitative analysis to understand the unique properties of ViT features, contrasting them with CNN features:

- **Spatial Resolution and Semantic Granularity:** Through PCA visualizations, they observe that ViTs maintain high spatial resolution throughout all layers, providing fine-grained semantic information, unlike CNNs which trade spatial resolution for semantic depth in deeper layers. ViT's global receptive field (each token attending to all others) contributes to this.
- **Hierarchical Representation in ViTs:** Unlike CNNs' low-to-high level semantic hierarchy, ViTs show a different progression. Shallow layers primarily encode positional information, while deeper layers become more semantic. Crucially, intermediate layers offer a beneficial blend of both positional and semantic information.
- **Semantic Information Across Super-Classes (DINO-ViT vs. Supervised ViT):** Using t-SNE visualizations on features from animal images in PASCAL-Parts, they demonstrate a key distinction: DINO-ViT features cluster by *semantic body parts* across *different animal categories* (e.g., all "ears" cluster together regardless of animal type). In contrast, supervised ViT features tend to group by *object class*, irrespective of specific parts. This highlights the superior cross-category semantic understanding of DINO-ViT.
- **Facet Comparison:** Empirical evaluation, including ablation studies, confirms that the "keys" facet often provides cleaner similarity maps and better performance for tasks compared to tokens, queries, or values.

#### 3.3. Zero-Shot Applications and Lightweight Methodologies

To showcase the intrinsic power of DINO-ViT features, the authors devise simple, zero-shot methods that require no additional training or fine-tuning.

- **Resolution Increase:** To benefit from finer spatial resolution, they modify the ViT at test time to extract overlapping patches, interpolating positional encodings accordingly.

- **Co-segmentation:**
 1. **Feature Extraction:** Extract DINO-ViT key descriptors and saliency maps (derived from the [CLS] token's attention in the last layer) for a set of input images.
 2. **Clustering:** Treat all extracted descriptors across all images and spatial locations as a single bag-of-descriptors and cluster them using k-means. This groups semantically common segments.
 3. **Voting:** A saliency-based voting procedure is used to identify foreground clusters. Each segment's saliency is calculated as the mean [CLS] attention of its patches. A cluster is marked "foreground" if a sufficient percentage of its segments across images are salient.
 4. **Refinement:** The binary co-segmentation masks are refined using GrabCut.

- **Part Co-segmentation:**
 1. **Foreground Segmentation:** First, the co-segmentation pipeline is applied to obtain foreground masks.
 2. **Part Clustering:** The clustering step (k-means) is then *re-applied exclusively to the descriptors within the identified foreground regions*. This forces the clustering to segment the foreground objects into common semantic parts.
 3. **Refinement:** Part masks are further refined using multi-label CRF.
 This approach is applied to challenging inter-class scenarios and video frames, with image augmentations (random crop/flip) used for stability on small sets.

- **Point Correspondences:**
 1. **Feature Selection:** To balance positional and semantic information, mid-layer features are chosen, as they retain more positional bias than the deepest layers.
 2. **Context Integration (Binning):** Log-binning is applied to each spatial feature to integrate information from adjacent features, adding contextual awareness.
 3. **Matching:** Robust correspondences are identified using the "Best Buddies Pairs" (BBPs) concept, where only mutual nearest neighbors in descriptor space (cosine similarity) are kept.

The overall approach is characterized by its simplicity and directness in leveraging the rich representations of DINO-ViT, demonstrating that sophisticated models can yield powerful zero-shot capabilities when their internal features are properly understood and exploited.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings based on empirical observations and extensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations across multiple vision tasks.

#### 4.1. Empirical Properties of DINO-ViT Features

- **Semantic Granularity at High Resolution:** DINO-ViT features encode well-localized semantic information, such as object parts, at a high spatial resolution, which is maintained across all layers. This contrasts with CNNs, where deeper layers typically sacrifice spatial detail for semantic abstraction.
- **Shared Semantics Across Categories:** A striking finding is that DINO-ViT features capture semantic similarity of object parts *across different object categories*. For instance, t-SNE visualizations show that features corresponding to "ears" from various animals (dogs, cats, horses) cluster together, rather than clustering by animal species. This property is strongly attributed to the self-supervised training (DINO) and is not observed in supervised ViTs or CNNs, which tend to group features by object class.
- **Gradual Positional Bias:** The positional bias in ViT features changes gradually across layers. Shallow layers retain more explicit positional information, while deeper layers become more semantic. Intermediate layers offer a valuable trade-off, balancing both positional and semantic cues, which is particularly beneficial for tasks like point correspondences.
- **Superiority of "Keys" Facet:** Through ablation studies, the authors empirically demonstrate that the "keys" facet from the self-attention mechanism generally provides a slightly better and cleaner representation than tokens, queries, or values, depicting less sensitivity to background clutter.
- **Influence of Self-Supervision:** The observed properties, especially the cross-category semantic part grouping, are shown to be a direct result of the DINO self-supervised training paradigm, significantly outperforming supervised ViTs.

#### 4.2. Part Co-segmentation Results

- **Challenging Small Sets and Novel Domains (Figure 6):** The zero-shot method successfully provides semantically consistent part segments for image pairs even in extremely challenging scenarios, including different appearances (cars, phones), different classes (bicycle-unicycle, cat-tiger), and domains lacking training data (Pikachu, Olaf). This is a novel capability not previously demonstrated.
- **Inter-Class Consistency (Figure 7):** On the AFHQ dataset (1.5K images of animal faces), the method consistently segments common parts (e.g., ears, forehead, whiskers) across diverse animal classes.
- **Video Part Co-segmentation:** The framework extends effectively to videos, yielding temporally consistent part segmentations across frames.
- **Quantitative Evaluation on CUB (Table 1, Figure 8):** On the CUB dataset, the method significantly surpasses previous unsupervised methods (ULD, DFF, SCOPS-paper) for key-point regression error, NMI, and ARI. It achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art *supervised* methods (SCOPS-model, Choudhury et al.), often providing more semantically coherent parts qualitatively.

#### 4.3. Co-segmentation Results

- **Quantitative Evaluation (Table 2):**
 - On intra-class datasets (MSRC7, Internet300, PASCAL-VOC), the unsupervised approach significantly outperforms other unsupervised methods (Hsu et al., DeepCO3, TokenCut, Faktor et al., Rubinstein et al.) in Jaccard Index (Jm) and Precision (Pm). It achieves competitive results with several state-of-the-art *supervised* methods.
 - **Superiority in Inter-Class Scenario:** On the newly introduced PASCAL-CO dataset (for inter-class co-segmentation), the method substantially surpasses all other methods, including supervised ones, demonstrating its robust generalization to semantically related but different categories (e.g., car-bus-train).
- **Ablation Study (Table 3):** Confirms that combining clustering with DINO-ViT features ("Ours") yields significantly better performance than simple saliency baselines (DINO/Sup. ViT/ResNet Saliency Baselines). It also reiterates the superior performance of using "keys" over other facets like tokens, queries, or values.

#### 4.4. Point Correspondences Results

- **Qualitative Robustness (Figure 10):** Compared to the VGG-based NBB method, the proposed approach shows greater robustness to changes in appearance, pose, and scale, in both intra- and inter-class scenarios.
- **Quantitative Evaluation on Spair71k (Table 4):** The method significantly outperforms the unsupervised NBB baseline in Percentage of Correct Keypoint (PCK). It also substantially closes the performance gap towards the supervised CATs method, achieving impressive results for a zero-shot approach.
- **Ablation Studies:** These studies confirm the benefits of using mid-layer features (e.g., layer 9) to balance positional and semantic information, and the crucial role of "binning" for incorporating context, both contributing to improved correspondence accuracy.

In summary, the main findings highlight that DINO-ViT features possess unique, powerful, and generalizable semantic properties that, when leveraged with simple zero-shot techniques, can achieve state-of-the-art or highly competitive results across a range of fundamental computer vision tasks, particularly excelling in challenging inter-class and low-data scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Deep ViT Features as Dense Visual Descriptors" carries significant implications for the field of computer vision and machine learning, with the potential to influence both fundamental understanding and practical applications.

1. **Re-evaluating Dense Visual Descriptors:** This work challenges the long-standing dominance of CNN-based features as the go-to dense visual descriptors. By demonstrating that ViT features, especially from self-supervised models like DINO-ViT, possess superior properties—such as high spatial granularity, localized semantic part information, and cross-category semantic consistency—it positions ViTs as a compelling alternative, potentially setting a new standard for feature extraction.
2. **Unlocking Zero-Shot Capabilities:** One of the most impactful contributions is the demonstration that these rich ViT features can be effectively leveraged with incredibly lightweight, zero-shot methodologies. This means that complex tasks like co-segmentation, part co-segmentation, and semantic correspondences can be solved without any additional task-specific training or fine-tuning. This dramatically reduces the need for large, labeled datasets for downstream tasks, making these methods highly practical and accessible, particularly in domains where data annotation is expensive or scarce.
3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The ability to perform part co-segmentation consistently across vastly different object categories (e.g., fox and leopard, bicycle and unicycle) and even on novel, unseen domains (e.g., Pikachus, Olaf) represents a significant leap towards truly generalized computer vision systems. This inherent cross-category semantic understanding, stemming from self-supervised training, allows for robustness against variations in appearance, pose, and scale, which is crucial for real-world applications.
4. **Deeper Understanding of ViT Mechanisms:** Beyond practical applications, the paper provides critical empirical insights into *what* Vision Transformers learn internally and *how* different training paradigms (supervised vs. self-supervised DINO) sculpt their feature spaces. The observation that DINO-ViT features cluster by *semantic parts across object categories* rather than by object class is a profound finding that enhances our interpretability of these complex models and can guide the design of future self-supervised learning objectives. It shows that DINO learns not just object saliency, but a fine-grained, compositional understanding of visual scenes.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens numerous avenues for future exploration:
 - **Broader Applications:** The demonstrated properties suggest DINO-ViT features could be beneficial for a wider range of vision tasks that require dense, semantic, and highly granular visual descriptors, such as scene understanding, generative modeling, or 3D reconstruction.
 - **Theoretical Analysis:** The empirical findings invite deeper theoretical investigation into why self-supervised ViTs learn such powerful and generalizable representations, potentially leading to a better understanding of attention mechanisms and their inductive biases.
 - **Efficiency and Optimization:** While already lightweight, further research could explore optimizing the feature extraction and downstream processing for real-time or embedded system applications.
 - **Multi-modal Integration:** The powerful visual representations could be integrated with other modalities (e.g., text, audio) within transformer frameworks, paving the way for more comprehensive AI systems.
6. **Impact on Low-Resource Domains:** The zero-shot, data-agnostic nature of the proposed methods holds immense promise for applications in fields with limited annotated data, such as medical imaging, scientific image analysis, or specialized industrial inspections.

In conclusion, "Deep ViT Features as Dense Visual Descriptors" is a landmark paper that not only provides novel empirical insights into the internal workings of Vision Transformers but also delivers concrete, high-performing, and highly generalizable solutions for fundamental computer vision tasks. Its emphasis on lightweight, zero-shot methodologies points towards a future where powerful pre-trained models can be readily adapted to new challenges without the prohibitive costs of extensive re-training or data collection.
