# Gravity-Aware Monocular 3D Human-Object Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> GraviCap jointly recovers 3D human motion and object trajectories in metric units from a single RGB video by exploiting gravity during an object's free flight. A Levenberg-Marquardt optimisation over initial object velocity/position, gravity vector, focal length, bone lengths and root translations, plus contact and mutual-localisation terms, yields 191.3 mm global root error and 6.75 cm height error.

## Problem

- Kinematic monocular capture methods ignore environmental constraints and physical laws, so they fail on non-trivial human-object interaction and show jitter, leaning and depth instability.
- Physics-based monocular methods model gravity and ground reaction forces but skip object interaction, and without body priors cannot recover posture or scene dimensions in absolute metric scale.
- Methods reconstructing humans with objects need 3D environment scans, shape priors, or interaction databases, and still leave monocular scale, bone lengths and ground orientation ambiguous.

## Method

- ==Projectile-motion model== parametrised by initial object velocity, initial position, gravity direction with fixed magnitude 9.81 m/s2, and focal length, fit through a ==re-projection energy== over spherical objects of unknown diameter.
- Human-object interaction constraints: a ==contact term== tying joint and object 3D positions at release, and a ==mutual-localisation term== matching sampled points along 2D and 3D torso-to-object vectors.
- Joint objective summing human re-projection, object re-projection, contact, mutual localisation, bone symmetry, multi-episode continuity and bone-length prior terms, solved by ==Levenberg-Marquardt== over pose initialisations from ==VNect==/==XNect==.

## Results

- Global root position error averages **191.3 mm** (9 DoF) versus **262.0 mm** for VNect and **309.7 mm** for PhysCap, winning on seven of eight sequences; bone-length MAE drops to **61.1 mm** from VNect's **78.4 mm**.
- Root-relative MPJPE reaches **122.8 mm** versus VNect **134.0 mm** and PhysCap **125.2 mm**; smoothness error **10.74** beats VNect **11.35** and VIBE **11.32**, but trails PhysCap's **7.72**.
- Gravity direction attains **0.972** cosine similarity, object trajectory error **445.5 mm**, human height error **6.75 cm**; in-the-wild shot put measured **18.557 m** and a basketball hoop **3.03 m** versus **3.05 m** actual.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A known physical constant observed in the scene, here gravity acting on a free-flying object, is enough to break monocular scale ambiguity without body scans or height priors.
> - Weak initialisation can be redeemed by physically grounded energy terms: a non-state-of-the-art pose initialiser still yields state-of-the-art global accuracy after constrained optimisation.
> - The gain is bought with brittle assumptions: no air drag, spherical objects, hand-held contact with known release timing, and sensitivity to 2D detection noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of markerless 3D human motion capture from monocular RGB video has seen advancements, yet challenges persist, particularly concerning the reconstruction of scenes involving complex human-environment interactions. Existing state-of-the-art methods frequently encounter difficulties when modeling environmental constraints or physical laws, often resulting in physically implausible poses characterized by jitter, unnatural body leaning, and depth instabilities. The joint 3D reconstruction of humans and interacting objects from monocular imagery remains a relatively underexplored area, with a limited number of prior works focusing primarily on geometric constraints to prevent interpenetration.

Recent physics-based approaches to monocular 3D human pose estimation have demonstrated improvements in biomechanical plausibility by incorporating gravity and ground reaction forces. However, these methods typically do not account for object interactions and are unable to estimate posture or scene dimensions in absolute metric scale without a priori information about the human body. A significant limitation of most monocular 3D reconstruction techniques is the inherent scale ambiguity, which prevents the recovery of absolute metric distances.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a framework that explicitly models physics, specifically the influence of gravity on objects undergoing free flight, and integrates human-object interaction constraints. By leveraging these principles, the work aims to enable metrically accurate 3D human and object trajectory reconstruction from a single monocular video. This approach differentiates itself by providing absolute scene scale (e.g., bone lengths, object trajectories in meters) and the orientation of the ground plane, thereby advancing the capabilities of monocular 3D reconstruction in complex, physically constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an approach, termed GraviCap, for joint markerless 3D human motion capture and 3D object trajectory estimation from monocular RGB videos. The work specifically focuses on scenarios where objects are partially observed during a free flight phase.

The detailed objectives and motivations are outlined as follows:

1. **Metric Scale Recovery and Ground Plane Orientation:** A central motivation is to overcome the scale ambiguity inherent in monocular reconstruction. The authors hypothesize that the known magnitude of the gravitational force acting on a freely flying object can serve as a metric reference. This allows for the recovery of absolute metric distances, such as human bone lengths and object trajectories in meters, as well as the orientation of the ground plane relative to the camera. This is particularly relevant in situations where strong prior assumptions about the scene, such as known bone lengths, cannot be made.

2. **3D Object Trajectory Reconstruction:** The work aims to accurately reconstruct the 3D trajectory of an object undergoing free flight solely from its 2D image coordinates. The motivation is based on the observation that projectile motion, governed by gravity, can be precisely parameterized. This enables the estimation of the object's initial velocity and position in 3D space, assuming knowledge of the camera frame rate and the gravity vector's magnitude.

3. **Improved 3D Human Motion Capture:** By localizing human poses relative to the metrically accurate 3D object trajectory, the research seeks to enhance the accuracy and physical plausibility of 3D human motion capture. The motivation stems from the limitations of existing kinematic methods that often yield geometrically consistent but physically implausible human poses. Integrating interaction constraints, such as contact points between the human and the object, is expected to provide stronger cues for accurate human pose estimation in depth.

4. **Novel Human-Object Interaction Constraints:** The development of new types of human-object interaction constraints is a key objective. These constraints are designed to ensure geometric consistency between the reconstructed human poses and object trajectories, leading to improved physical plausibility of human movements. The intention is to leverage these interactions to resolve camera-relative distances for both moving objects and interacting humans in metric units.

5. **Creation of a New Evaluation Dataset:** A practical objective is to establish a new dataset tailored for evaluating 3D human-object interactions involving free flights. This dataset is motivated by the need for a benchmark with ground-truth annotations for both 3D human poses and object trajectories to rigorously test and compare methods in this specific problem domain.

In summary, the work is motivated by the desire to leverage fundamental physical laws governing object motion to address long-standing challenges in monocular 3D reconstruction, specifically the recovery of absolute metric scale and the generation of physically plausible human poses in interactive scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed GraviCap approach is designed for joint 3D human motion capture and 3D object trajectory estimation from monocular RGB videos. The methodology is built upon a global optimization framework that integrates physics-based constraints with human-object interaction models.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The system takes 2D images as input and generates outputs in absolute metric units, including 3D object trajectory, final global 3D human pose, initial object velocity, focal length, gravity vector, bone lengths, and corrective root translation. The process involves three main steps: retrieving initial kinematic human poses, tracking the 2D object trajectory, and minimizing a global energy function.

#### 3.2. Recovering the 3D Object Trajectory

The method assumes that objects in free flight are solely influenced by gravity, neglecting air resistance. The 3D trajectory `B = {B_1, ..., B_N}` of an object is parameterized by its initial 3D position `B_0`, initial velocity vector `u`, and the gravity vector `g` (all viewed in the camera's frame of reference). Using Newtonian dynamics, the object's position at time `t` (frame `i` at frame rate `r`) is given by `B_i = B_0 + u*t + 0.5*g*t^2`.
The 3D object positions are projected onto the 2D image plane using the pinhole camera model: `x_i = f * X_i / Z_i + c_x` and `y_i = f * Y_i / Z_i + c_y`, where `f` is the focal length and `c = (c_x, c_y)` is the principal point.
A key aspect is the assumption that the magnitude of `g` is known (9.81 m/s^2), which enables the recovery of absolute metric scale. The system can operate in various modes depending on whether the direction of `g` and the focal length `f` are known or unknown, leading to 6, 7, 9, or 10 degrees of freedom (DoF) for the unknowns (`u`, `B_0`, `f`, `g`).
The 3D object trajectory is recovered by minimizing an `L2`-norm objective function `E_b` (Equation 4), which quantifies the reprojection error between the observed 2D object coordinates and the projected 3D trajectory. This optimization solves for `B_0`, `u`, and optionally `f` and `g`.

#### 3.3. Joint 3D Human-Object Reconstruction

This component associates the object's trajectory with human positions to estimate anthropometric information and refine human poses in absolute metric units.
Initial kinematic 3D human poses `P_kin` are obtained from off-the-shelf methods (e.g., VNect or XNect), while 2D joint locations `p` are extracted using a 2D pose estimator (e.g., AlphaPose). These initial 3D poses are typically root-relative and not in absolute metric scale.
The goal is to recover subject-specific bone lengths `l` and corrective root translations `t_corr` to produce absolute, camera-relative 3D poses `P = s(P_kin, l) + t_corr`.
A reprojection objective function `E_p` (Equation 5) minimizes the `L2` error between projected 3D human joints and observed 2D human joints. This term alone suffers from scale ambiguity.

To address this and improve physical plausibility, several constraints are introduced:
- **Constraints on Human Skeleton:**
 - **Bone Length Prior `E_bl` (Equation 6):** Encourages estimated bone lengths `l` to be close to average human bone lengths.
 - **Symmetry Constraint `E_s` (Equation 7):** Enforces left-right symmetry for bone lengths, correcting initial pose asymmetries.
- **Human Contact and Localisation Constraints:**
 - **Contact Term `E_c` (Equation 8):** Ensures that the 3D positions of the object and the corresponding body joints are close at moments of contact (e.g., throwing or catching). This term uses a set of known contact joints and timestamps.
 - **Mutual Localisation Term `E_m` (Equation 9):** Binds the human and object positions by ensuring that the 3D vectors connecting the object to specific torso joints (pelvis, spine, neck, shoulders) project consistently to their 2D counterparts in the image plane. This leverages the smooth and accurate 3D object trajectory to localize the human.

#### 3.4. Multi-Episodes and Multi-Person Settings

The framework extends to multi-episode scenarios (consecutive free flights by the same person) by assuming constant focal length, gravity, and human bone lengths across episodes, while individualizing `B_0` and `u`. An additional continuity constraint `E_co` (Equation 12 in supplementary) penalizes differences between the end position of one episode and the start position of the next. For two-person scenes, `E_p`, `E_m`, and `E_c` are modified to account for multiple persons and their respective contacts.

#### 3.5. Joint Energy Optimization

The complete GraviCap objective function `E` (Equation 11) is a weighted sum of all individual energy terms: `E = E_p + λ_b E_b + λ_c E_c + λ_m E_m + λ_s E_s + λ_co E_co + λ_bl E_bl`. The weights `λ` are empirically determined. This non-linear least squares problem is solved using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

- **Human Pose Estimation:** Initial 3D poses are obtained using VNect for single-person and XNect for multi-person cases. 2D joint positions are from AlphaPose, using an MPII 2D pose dataset skeleton with 16 joints.
- **Object Tracking:** OpenCV’s CSR Tracker is used for object tracklets, initialized via template matching. Episode switches are detected based on sudden changes in 2D object velocity direction.

#### 3.7. GraviCap Dataset

A new dataset was created for evaluation, featuring four subjects performing activities with four types of balls. It includes eight single-person sequences and one two-person sequence. Annotations consist of synchronized multi-view RGB videos, ground-truth intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters, ground-truth 2D/3D human poses (from a 101-camera motion capture system), and ground-truth 2D/3D object trajectories (from triangulation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GraviCap approach was quantitatively and qualitatively evaluated on a newly introduced dataset and demonstrated in-the-wild scenarios.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

Experiments compared GraviCap against existing state-of-the-art methods for 3D human pose estimation, including VNect, PhysCap, and VIBE. MotioNet was also initially considered but failed to produce reasonable estimates for comparison.

- **Global Root Positions (MPE):** GraviCap, in both its 9 DoF (gravity direction unknown) and 10 DoF (gravity direction and focal length unknown) modes, demonstrated improved accuracy in estimating global root translations. It outperformed VNect and PhysCap in seven out of eight sequences, achieving lower Mean Position Error (MPE). For instance, GraviCap (9 DoF) achieved an average MPE of 191.3 mm, compared to 262.0 mm for VNect and 309.7 mm for PhysCap.

- **Root-Relative Poses (MPJPE):** While VIBE showed the lowest overall root-relative Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) (113.2 mm), it does not estimate global root translations. GraviCap's root-relative MPJPE values (124.4 mm for 10 DoF, 122.8 mm for 9 DoF) were comparable to or slightly better than VNect (134.0 mm) and PhysCap (125.2 mm), indicating competitive accuracy for local pose estimation.

- **Bone Lengths (MAE):** GraviCap consistently outperformed VNect in estimating absolute human bone lengths, achieving lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across all tested cases. GraviCap (9 DoF) achieved an average MAE of 61.1 mm, compared to 78.4 mm for VNect. This suggests improved metric accuracy in anthropometric measurements.

- **3D Object Trajectories (MPE):** The method uniquely estimates 3D object trajectories in absolute metric units. The reported average MPE for 3D object positions was 445.5 mm for 10 DoF and 482.9 mm for 9 DoF, providing a baseline for future comparisons.

- **Gravity Direction (Cosine Similarity):** GraviCap demonstrated high accuracy in estimating the gravity direction, with an average cosine similarity of approximately 0.972 between the estimated and ground-truth gravity vectors. This implies accurate recovery of the ground plane orientation.

- **Smoothness (`e_smooth`):** The algorithm achieved a smoothness error (`e_smooth`) of 10.74, outperforming VNect (11.35) and VIBE (11.32). This improvement in motion consistency is attributed to the mutual localization constraint and more accurate bone length estimation. PhysCap, which assumes known body mass distribution and ground plane orientation, achieved a lower error of 7.72.

- **Human Height Estimation:** The method can estimate human heights as a by-product of scale disambiguation. Across four subjects in the new dataset, an average height estimation error of 6.75 cm was observed.

#### 4.2. In-the-Wild Experiments

The method was also applied to "in-the-wild" videos, such as a professional shotput throw and a basketball throw.
- For a shotput throw, GraviCap estimated a throw distance of 18.557 m and a maximum trajectory point of 5.46 m. The estimated gravity direction indicated an upward tilt of 13 degrees for the camera.
- For a basketball throw, the estimated height of the hoop was 3.03 m, which is close to the actual height of 3.05 m.

#### 4.3. Noise Sensitivity Analysis

A sensitivity analysis revealed that GraviCap's performance is affected by high levels of Gaussian noise applied to 2D object trajectories, particularly when the noise disrupts the parabolic nature of the trajectory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces GraviCap, a novel approach for joint 3D human motion capture and 3D object trajectory reconstruction from monocular video, making several contributions to the field of computer vision.

**Resolution of Monocular Scale Ambiguity:** A primary significance of GraviCap is its ability to resolve the fundamental scale ambiguity inherent in monocular 3D reconstruction. By leveraging the known physical laws governing projectile motion under gravity, the method can recover absolute metric distances for both human bone lengths and object trajectories, as well as the camera's focal length and the orientation of the ground plane. This is achieved without relying on extensive 3D training data for the core physics-based estimation, distinguishing it from many learning-based approaches.

**Enhanced Physical Plausibility and Accuracy:** The integration of physics-based constraints and human-object interaction terms significantly improves the physical plausibility and temporal consistency of reconstructed human poses. The method demonstrates state-of-the-art accuracy in global 3D human motion capture (camera-relative root translations) and competitive performance for root-relative poses when compared to established methods. The improved estimation of absolute bone lengths also represents an advance in anthropometric measurement from video.

**Novel Problem Formulation and Constraints:** The paper defines a new problem domain: joint 3D human-object reconstruction from monocular videos where objects undergo free flight. The proposed contact and mutual localization constraints are novel contributions that effectively bind human and object positions, leading to more coherent and accurate reconstructions.

**New Dataset for Research:** The release of the GraviCap dataset, which includes ground-truth annotations for 3D human poses and object trajectories in human-object interaction scenarios, provides a valuable benchmark for the community. This facilitates future research and standardized evaluation in this emerging subfield.

#### 5.1. Potential Impact

- **Sports Analytics:** The ability to accurately measure object trajectories and human kinematics in metric units could benefit sports analysis, providing precise data on throws, jumps, and other ballistic movements.
- **Biomechanics and Ergonomics:** Accurate estimation of bone lengths and absolute human poses from monocular video could support applications in biomechanical studies and ergonomic assessments, particularly in environments where specialized multi-camera setups are impractical.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** Detailed, metrically accurate understanding of human-object interactions can inform robotic systems designed to interact with humans and manipulate objects.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality:** Enhanced realism and physical consistency in virtual human-object interactions for VR/AR applications.

#### 5.2. Limitations

Despite its contributions, the method has limitations:
- Its accuracy is dependent on the quality of initial 2D human pose and 2D object detections, making it susceptible to noise and errors from upstream processes.
- The framework relies on identifying hand-object contact points, which may require specialized detection methods.
- The default assumption is that humans hold objects in their hands, and deviations from this (e.g., using an instrument) would require additional priors.
- The "f/Z" ambiguity persists if both camera intrinsics and initial absolute root translations are unknown, limiting full recovery in certain scenarios.
- For applications on other celestial bodies (e.g., Moon or Mars), the local magnitude of the gravity vector would need to be known.

Overall, GraviCap changes the perspective on joint 3D human-object reconstruction by demonstrating the power of integrating fundamental physics, opening new avenues for research into more robust and metrically accurate monocular 3D understanding.
