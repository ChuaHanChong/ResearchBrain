# Dr. Zero: Self-Evolving Search Agents without Training Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Dr. Zero, a framework developed by Meta Superintelligence Labs and UIUC, enables multi-turn search agents to self-evolve without any human-curated training data. It utilizes a proposer-solver co-evolution mechanism and a novel Hop-Grouped Relative Policy Optimization, allowing agents to match or surpass supervised baselines on open-domain QA tasks while drastically reducing computational overhead.

## Problem

- Reliance on expensive, human-curated training data for developing advanced search and reasoning large language models (LLMs).
- Limitations of prior data-free self-evolution frameworks in generating diverse, challenging open-domain questions for multi-hop reasoning.
- Computational inefficiency of existing reinforcement learning methods for optimizing multi-turn search agents that interact with external tools.

## Method

- A ==self-evolving proposer-solver framework== where both agents learn without human data, relying solely on an external search engine for factual verification.
- Introduces ==Hop-Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (HRPO)== for efficient proposer training, clustering questions by hop complexity and using group-level baselines to reduce computational overhead.
- Employs a ==difficulty-guided reward== for the proposer to generate diverse, challenging, yet verifiable multi-hop questions, fostering a dynamic curriculum.

## Results

- Dr. Zero matches or exceeds the performance of supervised baselines and significantly outperforms few-shot and other data-free methods on open-domain QA benchmarks, achieving up to **22.9%** higher EM on Natural Questions.
- The Hop-Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (HRPO) method reduces computational rollout cost by approximately **4x** compared to standard GRPO while maintaining or improving performance.
- The self-evolving framework consistently showed performance improvements over **2-3 iterations**, demonstrating autonomous learning, with optimal curriculum composition adapting to model size.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex open-domain reasoning and search capabilities can emerge entirely through self-evolution without human-curated training data, challenging traditional data dependency.
> - The proposed HRPO method significantly improves the computational efficiency of self-evolution for multi-turn search agents by grouping questions by hop complexity.
> - A dynamic, difficulty-guided curriculum, where the proposer creates challenging but solvable tasks, is essential for continuous improvement and generalization of the solver agent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dr. Zero operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas within artificial intelligence, specifically large language models (LLMs), reinforcement learning (RL), self-evolving agents, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) or search-augmented LLMs.

**Self-Evolving Language Agents:** The core paradigm Dr. Zero builds upon is "self-evolving language agents." This approach aims for LLMs to autonomously enhance their performance by iteratively generating problems, solving them, and learning from these experiences. This field gained traction due to the increasing difficulty and cost of obtaining high-quality human-curated training data. Earlier methods, such as those by Liu et al. (2025a) and Zhang et al. (2025a), relied on human-curated questions to drive exploration. More recent advancements, like those by Zhao et al. (2025) and Chen et al. (2025), introduced automated proposers to generate synthetic training data, making "data-free" self-evolution possible. R-Zero (Huang et al., 2025a) is a notable example, using a proposer-solver co-evolution framework. However, a significant limitation of these existing data-free methods is their primary focus on "mathematical or specific reasoning tasks" with narrowly defined questions, leading to limited question diversity and an inability to generalize to open-domain queries.

**Search-Augmented LLMs:** Another critical area is the integration of external search and retrieval mechanisms with LLMs. Models like RAG (Lewis et al., 2020) and IRCoT (Trivedi et al., 2023) have demonstrated that augmenting LLMs with external knowledge bases significantly improves their performance on knowledge-intensive tasks, especially multi-hop reasoning. Agentic LLMs further enhance this by incorporating search engines as optional tools for adaptive retrieval and reasoning (Jin et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025b; Zhang et al., 2025b). However, a pervasive limitation in this domain is the reliance on "supervised, verifiable settings" and "extensive human queries and annotations for training." Even concurrent work like Lu et al. (2025) which uses self-play, still requires ground truth labels and numerous human-written examples.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLMs:** RL serves as the underlying optimization mechanism for these self-evolving agents. Policy gradient algorithms (Sutton et al., 1999; Ouyang et al., 2022) like PPO and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) are commonly used to optimize LLMs based on rewards. More recently, group-based methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) have been adopted for their ability to construct low-variance baselines from multiple responses, enhancing training stability and efficiency.

**Dr. Zero's Contribution to the Landscape:** Dr. Zero distinguishes itself by uniquely combining these areas. It aims to bridge the gap between data-free self-evolution and search-augmented LLMs, specifically targeting **open-domain multi-turn search agents**. It addresses the limitations of prior data-free self-evolution frameworks which suffered from:
1. **Limited question diversity and difficulty:** They tended to generate simple, one-hop questions, leading to moderate gains only on trivial tasks.
2. **Computational inefficiency:** Methods relying on nested sampling (e.g., GRPO) become computationally prohibitive for multi-turn search agents with complex tool interactions.
3. **Reliance on human data:** Existing search-augmented LLMs still largely depend on human supervision.

By proposing a framework that leverages external search engines for self-supervision and introduces an efficient optimization method (HRPO), Dr. Zero pushes the frontier of truly autonomous, data-free learning for complex, open-domain reasoning tasks, effectively challenging the necessity of human supervision for advanced search and reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of Dr. Zero is to **enable search agents to effectively self-evolve without any human-curated training data.** This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** High-quality training data, especially for complex reasoning and multi-turn interactions, is becoming increasingly difficult and expensive to obtain. This bottleneck hinders the scalability and development of advanced LLMs. Dr. Zero aims to bypass this by creating a self-sufficient learning loop.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Data-Free Self-Evolution:** Prior data-free self-evolution frameworks (e.g., R-Zero, SQLM) have primarily focused on narrow domains like mathematics or coding. They often struggle with open-domain questions due to limited question diversity and a bias towards simpler, one-hop tasks. These methods fail to generate the challenging, multi-hop queries necessary for advanced search agent development. Dr. Zero seeks to overcome this by generating diverse and progressively difficult questions.
3. **Eliminating Human Supervision for Search Agents:** Current state-of-the-art search-augmented LLMs predominantly rely on extensive human-annotated questions and ground truth labels for training. This dependency prevents their truly autonomous development. Dr. Zero's motivation is to develop search agents that can improve their search and reasoning capabilities solely through self-evolution, leveraging only an external search engine for factual verification.
4. **Improving Computational Efficiency for Complex Interactions:** Self-evolution methods, particularly those involving multi-turn reasoning and tool use, can be computationally intensive. Techniques like standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) require nested sampling (multiple queries, multiple responses per query), leading to prohibitive compute requirements for search agents. A key motivation for Dr. Zero is to design a training mechanism that maintains performance and stability while drastically reducing computational overhead.
5. **Establishing an Automated Curriculum:** The goal is to create a dynamic feedback loop where the improving capabilities of the solver agent continuously incentivize the proposer agent to generate increasingly difficult yet solvable tasks. This establishes an automated, self-improving curriculum that refines both agents over time.

In essence, Dr. Zero seeks to demonstrate that complex reasoning and search capabilities can emerge entirely through self-evolution, free from human supervision, by efficiently generating a high-quality, self-paced curriculum for search agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dr. Zero employs a novel **proposer-solver self-evolution framework** designed to enhance multi-turn search agents in a strictly data-free setting. Both the proposer ($\pi_\theta$) and the solver ($\pi_\phi$) are initialized from the same base LLM and rely exclusively on an external search engine (R) for knowledge and supervision signals. The core idea is an iterative, symbiotic loop where the proposer generates diverse and challenging questions, and the solver learns to answer them, with the solver's improvement pushing the proposer to generate even harder tasks.

#### 3.1. Setup and Feedback Loop

The framework operates as follows (Figure 2):
- **Proposer:** Generates Question-Answer (QA) pairs (x, y) leveraging the search engine. Its objective is to maximize a "difficulty-guided reward" that incentivizes verifiable yet challenging questions.
- **Solver:** Learns to answer the questions (x) generated by the proposer, aiming to maximize a simple outcome-based reward (I(y = ŷ)), indicating correctness.
- **Self-Evolution Feedback Loop:** The solver's predictions ({ŷi}) are fed back to the proposer to compute its reward. As the solver improves, trivial questions yield diminishing rewards for the proposer, pushing it to synthesize more complex, multi-hop queries. Conversely, these increasingly difficult questions prevent the solver's training rewards from plateauing, continuously expanding its reasoning skills.

#### 3.2. Proposer Training with Hop-Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (HRPO)

A key innovation of Dr. Zero lies in its proposer training. Existing methods struggle with question diversity and efficiency for open-domain, multi-turn search.
- **Issue with Existing Methods:** Standard RL algorithms like GRPO become inefficient due to "nested sampling," requiring multiple queries and multiple responses for each question, leading to (m+1) × n rollouts per prompt. This is computationally prohibitive for multi-turn search agents.
- **HRPO Solution:** HRPO addresses this by:
 1. **Clustering Questions by Hop Complexity:** It groups generated QA pairs (x,y) by their cross-hop complexity, denoted by the number of hops (h). Questions with fewer hops are simpler, while higher-hop questions demand more extensive search and multi-step reasoning.
 2. **Group-Level Baseline for Advantage Estimation:** Instead of sampling multiple responses for a single prompt, HRPO computes advantages by standardizing the solver's reward scores *within each hop group*. This "hop-specific normalization" produces low-variance advantage estimates.
 3. **Efficiency:** HRPO generates a single question per prompt with multiple solver predictions (e.g., 5), significantly reducing computational cost compared to GRPO's nested sampling.
- **Difficulty-Guided Reward (Equation 4):** The proposer's reward function `r(y, {ŷi}n i=1)` is designed to encourage both:
 - **Verifiability (solvability):** The question must be solvable by the solver.
 - **Difficulty:** The question must not be trivially easy.
 The reward is maximized when `0 < k < n` (where `k` is the number of correct solutions out of `n` sampled attempts), implying the question is challenging but within the solver's capabilities. It linearly decays as `k` increases (becomes easier) and penalizes `k=0` (too hard) or `k=n` (too easy).
 - **Format Reward (`rf`):** An additional reward component (`rf`) is imposed to mitigate structural degradation, ensuring the generated QA pairs are well-formed and adhere to reasoning/search protocols. This includes adherence to `<think>` tags, valid tool usage, and extractable question/answer tags.

#### 3.3. Solver Training with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

The solver agent ($\pi_\phi$) is trained using the QA pairs generated by the proposer.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) is employed. This method computes advantages from empirical group statistics, reinforcing valid trajectories.
- **Reward Function:** The solver's optimization is driven by a simple outcome-based reward that solely evaluates the correctness of its final predictions against the synthesized ground truth (`I(y = ŷi)`).
- **Dynamic Curriculum:** As the proposer generates increasingly complex questions, the solver is continuously motivated to refine its search and reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.4. External Search Engine (R) and Base Models

- **Search Engine:** The external search engine is configured using the E5 base model (Wang et al., 2022) for embedding queries and an English Wikipedia dump as the corpus. During inference, it performs approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search to retrieve the top-3 relevant documents.
- **Base LLMs:** Qwen2.5 3B/7B Instruct models are used as the foundational LLMs for both the proposer and solver, which are initialized from these base models.
- **Strict Data-Free Setting:** Critically, no human-annotated demonstrations, questions, or answers are used throughout the framework.

#### 3.5. Training Protocol

Dr. Zero involves an alternating optimization schedule:
1. **Proposer Training:** 50 steps of HRPO to update the proposer.
2. **QA Data Generation:** The updated proposer generates a batch of QA pairs.
3. **Solver Training:** 50 steps of GRPO using the newly generated QA data to update the solver.
This cycle typically repeats for 2-3 iterations, as performance tends to plateau afterwards. Hyperparameters are minimally searched for stability and kept constant (detailed in Appendix A, Tables 5 & 6).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Dr. Zero's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on open-domain QA benchmarks, comparing it against both few-shot and supervised baselines, as well as other data-free methods.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Against Supervised Baselines (Table 1)

- **Matching/Surpassing Supervised Methods:** Dr. Zero consistently matches or exceeds the performance of strong supervised baselines (e.g., Search-R1, RAG, SFT) across various single-hop (Natural Questions, TriviaQA, PopQA) and multi-hop (HotpotQA, 2WikiMQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle) benchmarks. This is a remarkable achievement given Dr. Zero operates in a strictly data-free setting.
- **Significant Outperformance on Single-Hop Tasks:** For the Qwen2.5-3B model, Dr. Zero achieved 0.397 EM on NQ, outperforming supervised Search-R1 (0.323) by 22.9%. Similar gains were seen on TriviaQA and PopQA.
- **Competitive on Multi-Hop Tasks:** The 7B variant of Dr. Zero achieved roughly 90% of Search-R1's performance on complex multi-hop scenarios and even surpassed it on 2WikiMQA.
- **Robustness to Scaling:** Performance gains were robust as the base model scaled from 3B to 7B, particularly in multi-hop reasoning.

#### 4.2. Superiority Over Few-Shot Baselines (Table 1)

- Dr. Zero substantially outperformed all few-shot baselines (e.g., standard prompting, IRCoT, Search-o1, RAG) across nearly all benchmarks, indicating that its iterative training loop effectively refines search capabilities more dynamically and comprehensively than static few-shot examples.

#### 4.3. Comparison to Data-Free Baselines (Table 2)

- Dr. Zero consistently outperformed existing data-free methods like SQLM\* and R-Zero\* by significant margins (39.9% and 27.3% average gains, respectively, for the 3B model). This validates the effectiveness of Dr. Zero's proposer and training pipeline, particularly its multi-turn reasoning capabilities, difficulty-guided reward, and HRPO.
- Decoupling the proposer and solver (as in Dr. Zero and R-Zero) was observed to prevent model memorization and lead to better generalization.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics (Figure 3, Table 3)

- **Rapid Initial Gains:** Both 3B and 7B solvers achieved peak performance within approximately 50 steps of the initial training phase, suggesting quick acquisition of core capabilities.
- **Continuous Self-Evolution:** The second iteration consistently yielded improvements (average gains of 4.93% for 3B, 1.64% for 7B), confirming the continuous self-evolutionary trend.
- **Performance Plateau:** Beyond two to three iterations, performance gains became marginal or slightly decreased, indicating a plateau.
- **Failure Modes:** Training instability, particularly in the 7B model, was observed due to inconsistent token IDs in multi-turn search steps.

#### 4.5. Response Length and Entropy (Figure 4)

- **Solver Convergence:** The solver's entropy and response length decreased and stabilized, indicating that it quickly gained confidence and refined its reasoning and search capabilities.
- **Proposer Diversity:** The proposer's entropy and response length fluctuated, suggesting it maintained generation diversity and continuously explored ways to create increasingly complex challenges for the solver.

#### 4.6. Impact of Different Hop Ratios (Table 4)

- **Model-Specific Needs:** For the 3B model, a higher proportion of single-hop questions (4:3:2:1 ratio for 1/2/3/4-hop questions) yielded optimal multi-hop performance, implying that strengthening fundamental search capabilities is crucial for smaller models.
- **Larger Model Capacity:** The 7B variant performed better with a higher proportion of multi-hop synthetic data, indicating that larger models benefit from a more challenging curriculum to fully leverage their inherent capacity for complex, cross-hop reasoning.

#### 4.7. Ablation Studies (Table 7) and HRPO Efficiency (Table 8)

- **Component Importance:** Ablation studies validated the critical roles of the format reward, the difficulty-based reward, and the initial document in guiding the proposer to generate effective and challenging queries.
- **HRPO Efficiency:** HRPO achieved comparable or even superior aggregate performance (0.326 EM vs. 0.320 EM for GRPO with 3B backbone) while requiring significantly fewer computational resources (about one-fourth the rollout cost of GRPO). This makes HRPO a more efficient method for data-free self-evolution, especially for complex tool-use scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Dr. Zero represents a significant advancement in the field of large language models and autonomous AI agents, with several profound implications:

1. **Challenging the Data Dependency Paradigm:** The most significant impact of Dr. Zero is its empirical demonstration that complex reasoning and search capabilities can emerge *solely through self-evolution, without any human-curated training data*. This fundamentally challenges the long-standing reliance on vast, expensive, and often biased human-annotated datasets, paving the way for more autonomous and data-efficient AI development.
2. **Scalable AI Development in Data-Scarce Environments:** By showing that LLMs can generate their own curriculum and improve effectively, Dr. Zero provides a promising paradigm for developing and enhancing AI agents in domains where high-quality human data is scarce, inaccessible, or prohibitively expensive. This could democratize AI development and expand its applicability to novel areas.
3. **Enhanced Efficiency for Complex Agents:** The introduction of Hop-Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (HRPO) addresses a critical computational bottleneck in self-evolution for multi-turn tool-use agents. By drastically reducing sampling overhead while maintaining or improving performance, Dr. Zero makes the self-evolutionary paradigm more practical and scalable for agents requiring complex interactions with external tools like search engines.
4. **Advancing Open-Domain Reasoning and Search:** Previous data-free self-evolution methods were largely limited to narrow, specific reasoning tasks. Dr. Zero successfully extends this paradigm to open-domain question answering, enabling agents to develop sophisticated multi-hop reasoning and search capabilities, which are crucial for real-world applications.
5. **Robustness and Adaptability:** The framework's ability to create a dynamic, difficulty-guided curriculum allows agents to continually adapt and improve, pushing their performance boundaries. The observed model-specific scaling behaviors (e.g., smaller models benefiting from simpler tasks, larger models from more complex ones) suggest an adaptive learning process that caters to the inherent capabilities of the base LLM.
6. **Future Research Directions:** Dr. Zero opens up new avenues for research, particularly in addressing current limitations such as performance plateaus, entropy collapse in larger models, and safeguarding against reward hacking and bias amplification. Tackling these challenges could lead to even more robust and continuously improving self-evolving AI systems.

In conclusion, Dr. Zero establishes a powerful new benchmark for data-free self-evolution, offering a pathway towards truly autonomous and efficient development of advanced search and reasoning capabilities in language agents. Its success underscores the potential for AI systems to generate their own learning experiences, significantly reducing external dependencies and fostering more intelligent, self-improving agents.
