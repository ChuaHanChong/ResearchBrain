# TACO: TActile World Model as a Self-COrrector forScalable VLA Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> TACO is a tactile-aware world-model framework for scalable VLA post-training in contact-rich manipulation. A Recognize-Imagine-Label loop uses a unified progress-action model to find failure-adjacent states, a visuo-tactile diffusion model to jointly denoise future video and 6-DoF force sequences into local corrections, and knowledge-insulated tactile adaptation with advantage conditioning to train on them, lifting real-robot success 44% over the base policy.

## Problem

- VLA policies fail at contact transitions where visual observations barely change but tactile signals shift sharply from slippage, weak pressure, or abnormal torque.
- Collecting corrective demonstrations via human intervention needs repeated monitoring and manual recovery at failure states, so corrective post-training does not scale.
- Vision-only world models imagine rollouts that look visually plausible yet carry physically inconsistent contact dynamics, making them unreliable supervision for contact-rich recovery.

## Method

- **TACO**'s ==visuo-tactile generation model== on a ==Wan2.2-TI2V-5B DiT== jointly denoises video latents and 12-D force tokens via a ==joint flow-matching loss==, with ==temporal RoPE alignment== and a clean first-frame force anchor.
- A ==unified progress-action model== fuses a ==DINOv2== direction-aware visual pathway with an MLP force pathway to predict 7-DoF corrective actions and dense progress, driving anchor recognition and imagined-segment labeling.
- ==Knowledge-insulated tactile adaptation== stop-gradients the ==PaliGemma== prefix and injects force history plus binary advantage into the action expert through ==adaRMSNorm==, trained with ==classifier-free-guided advantage conditioning==.

## Results

- Across six real Franka contact-rich tasks over two iterations, TACO reached **0.82** average success versus **0.38** base policy, **0.43** Filtered BC, and **0.50** without knowledge insulation.
- Ablations: dropping tactile generation cut success to **28%** and dropping tactile labeling to **65%**; uniform anchors fell to **78%** and **25%**, and removing advantage conditioning to **83%** and **56%**.
- Scaling imagined data on Insert Flower raised success **70%** to **93%** to **97%** at 1:2, 1:4, 1:8 ratios; OOD background/object/position rose from **50%/20%/35%/13.5%** base to **80.5%/85.5%/55.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating tactile alongside video matters less as extra observation than as input to the labeler: force must enter action and progress prediction to pay off.
> - Where imagination is anchored dominates how much is imagined; progress-stalling anchors concentrate the budget on recoverable contact transitions rather than unrecoverable states.
> - Keeping failures with negative advantage beats filtering them out, and insulating pretrained VLM priors prevents tactile gradients from eroding pre-contact perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robotic manipulation have seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-1, RT-2, and π-series models, which exhibit capabilities in transferring vision-language priors to robot action generation. These models demonstrate generalization across various tasks but frequently encounter difficulties in contact-rich manipulation scenarios. In such tasks, minor physical perturbations, such as slippage or insufficient pressure, can lead to unrecoverable failures that are not readily detectable through visual information alone. These failures are often localized contact issues rather than higher-level semantic misunderstandings of the task.

The scalability of corrective supervision for such localized failures presents a challenge. Traditional methods of collecting corrective demonstrations involve human intervention, which is resource-intensive and difficult to scale for large-scale post-training. This limitation has prompted research into using world models to synthesize imagined rollouts for policy improvement. However, existing vision-only world models may generate visually plausible trajectories that lack physical consistency in contact dynamics, rendering them unreliable for tasks requiring precise physical interaction.

Furthermore, integrating tactile feedback into robot learning has been explored to enhance contact awareness and force-sensitive control. While tactile signals can improve the physical fidelity of imagined rollouts and policy conditioning, a naive integration of tactile inputs may compromise the pre-contact perception and semantic grounding provided by pretrained VLA models. This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to develop a scalable, autonomous post-training framework that leverages tactile-aware world models to address contact-rich manipulation failures, while simultaneously preserving the generalization capabilities of pretrained VLA models. It aims to bridge the gap between semantic task understanding and robust physical interaction by providing targeted, tactile-aware corrections.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for contact-rich manipulation tasks:** VLA models, despite their generalization capabilities, exhibit fragility in tasks requiring precise physical contact. The work aims to improve their recovery capabilities from localized contact failures caused by perturbations like slippage or inadequate force, which are difficult to discern from visual cues alone.
2. **Develop a scalable method for generating corrective supervision:** Traditional methods for collecting corrective demonstrations through human intervention are costly and do not scale efficiently. A key objective is to automate the generation of corrective data to enable iterative post-training without continuous human oversight.
3. **Address the limitations of vision-only world models in contact-rich environments:** Existing world models, when relying solely on visual information, may produce imagined trajectories that appear visually coherent but are physically inconsistent in terms of contact dynamics. The research seeks to develop a tactile-aware world model that generates physically accurate visuo-tactile corrections.
4. **Incorporate tactile feedback for improved contact awareness without degrading VLA priors:** Integrating tactile information is crucial for robust contact-rich manipulation. However, directly fine-tuning a VLA model with tactile-heavy data risks eroding its pretrained visual-language priors, which are essential for pre-contact perception and semantic grounding. An objective is to design a mechanism that effectively integrates tactile-aware corrections while preserving these existing priors.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several observations:
- Failures in contact-rich tasks are often localized and relate to insufficient or incorrect physical interaction, suggesting that targeted corrective supervision at these contact transitions is more efficient than retraining the entire task.
- The prohibitive cost of human-guided data collection for recovery behaviors necessitates an autonomous data generation mechanism.
- The inherent unreliability of vision-only world models in modeling complex contact dynamics underscores the need for multimodal (visuo-tactile) world models.
- The desire to maintain the broad generalization strengths of VLA models while enhancing their performance in specific contact-rich scenarios motivates a cautious approach to integrating new sensory modalities like touch.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TACO (TActile World Model as a Self-COrrector) is a tactile-aware world-model-driven framework designed for scalable VLA post-training in contact-rich manipulation tasks. The approach is structured around an iterative Recognize–Imagine–Label loop, coupled with a knowledge-insulated tactile adaptation strategy for policy refinement.

#### 3.1. Tactile-Aware World Model

The core of TACO is a tactile-aware world model composed of two main components: a visuo-tactile generation model and a unified progress-action model.

- **Visuo-Tactile Generation Model:** This model is responsible for producing correction segments from failure-adjacent states by jointly denoising future video and force sequences.
 - It is initialized from a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B backbone, pretrained on diverse robot datasets, and then fine-tuned on specific contact-rich demonstrations.
 - It processes video latent tokens (X_v) and tokenized force sequences (X_f), which represent left/right 6-DoF force-torque readings. These tokens are concatenated (X = [X_v; X_f]) to enable bidirectional video-force interaction within DiT (Diffusion Transformer) self-attention layers.
 - The model uses a joint flow-matching loss (L_joint) to predict both video (u_v_ψ) and force (u_f_ψ) flow fields, ensuring temporal alignment between visual and tactile dynamics. A weighting factor (λ_f) balances the force-denoising term.
 - To enhance stability, temporal RoPE (Rotary Positional Embedding) alignment is applied to force tokens, aligning them with the temporal axis of video latents. Additionally, the first frame's force readings (F_0) are kept clean as a first-frame anchor to reduce contact-state ambiguity.

- **Unified Progress-Action Model:** This model simultaneously predicts corrective actions and task progress from an RGB frame (I_t) and a force-tactile signal (F_t).
 - It integrates a DINOv2-based visual pathway, utilizing a direction-aware decoder for spatial grounding, and an MLP-based tactile pathway for encoding 12-dimensional force-torque readings.
 - The fused visual and tactile embeddings are fed into separate heads to predict a 7-DoF end-effector action (a_t) and a task progress score (p_t ∈).
 - Training uses a joint action-progress objective (L_UPA), combining SmoothL1 for actions and L2 for progress, with a mask (m_t) indicating progress label availability. Progress targets are derived from manually annotated task-stage labels, providing dense, stage-aware supervision.

#### 3.2. TACO Iterative Correction Framework

The framework operates in an iterative loop:

- **Recognize Failure-Adjacent States:** The current policy (π^(k)_θ) is deployed to collect real robot rollouts. The unified progress-action model evaluates these rollouts, predicting a dense progress score (p_t) at each timestep. Failure-adjacent states are identified where task progress stalls or decreases (p_t+Δ - p_t < ϵ over a short window Δ and threshold ϵ). Up to 10 anchors per failed trajectory are selected, concentrating on contact-sensitive stages. For these states, timesteps before the estimated failure onset receive a binary advantage label y=1, while those from the onset onward receive y=0.
- **Imagine Visuo-Tactile Corrections:** Starting from each recognized anchor state, the visuo-tactile generation model imagines a local correction segment (T=49 timesteps) by denoising future video and force sequences. This process is conditioned on the current visual observation, force, and language instruction, aiming to generate a trajectory that recovers from the failure.
- **Label Actions:** The unified progress-action model then labels these imagined visuo-tactile segments with executable corrective actions (a_t:t+T) and dense progress scores (p_t:t+T). All imagined correction segments are assigned a positive binary advantage label (y_t=1), signifying them as recovery-oriented corrections.

#### 3.3. Knowledge-Insulated Tactile Adaptation

To integrate the imagined tactile corrective supervision without degrading the pretrained visual-language priors of the VLA model, TACO employs a specific adaptation strategy:

- **Mechanism:** During post-training, a stop-gradient is applied to isolate the pretrained VLM (Vision-Language Model) backbone. This prevents tactile-action gradients from updating the VLM's core representations. Instead, tactile learning is routed primarily to the action expert, along with tactile and advantage encoders.
- **Advantage-Conditioned Post-Training:** The action expert is trained using an advantage-conditioned flow-matching objective (L_π). It takes as input VLM prefix representation (z_t), flow timestep condition (c_t), and force (c_f) and advantage (c_a) conditions. These conditions are injected through adaRMSNorm. Classifier-free guidance is used during training (randomly replacing conditions with a null embedding), and at inference, a positive advantage condition is used to encourage high-progress tactile recovery behaviors.
- **Iterative Loop:** After post-training, the updated policy (π^(k+1)_θ) is redeployed to collect new rollouts, closing the real-to-imagine-to-real loop and progressively reducing contact-sensitive failures.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

TACO was evaluated on a single-arm Franka Research 3 robot, equipped with a front-view Intel RealSense D455 camera and two Xense tactile sensors on the gripper, providing 6D force/torque readings. Six contact-rich manipulation tasks were used: Insert Flower, Wipe Whiteboard, Twist Bottle Cap, Play Xylophone, Toast Bread, and Move Hanoi Rings. Each task involved 50 expert demonstrations. The current policy was deployed to collect rollouts, which were then used to generate imagined correction data for post-training. Evaluation involved 40 independent episodes per task with randomized object positions, running for two iterations of post-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the efficacy of TACO in improving VLA policy performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks and its ability to generalize.

#### 4.1. Visualization of Imagined Correction Data

Qualitative visualization (Figure 4, Figure 13) indicates that the tactile-aware world model generates locally consistent imagined correction data. For instance, in "Move Hanoi Rings," where a real rollout failed due to misalignment, the imagined correction adjusted the contact to align and seat the ring. Similarly, in "Twist Bottle Cap," the imagined correction maintained firm contact and completed the twist, unlike the real rollout which slipped. These visualizations suggest that the generated corrections provide recovery supervision for contact transitions that are challenging to infer from visual data alone.

#### 4.2. Quantitative and Qualitative Results

Quantitative results (Table 1) show that TACO consistently improved success rates across two post-training iterations.
- After the second iteration, TACO achieved an average success rate improvement of **44% absolute** over the Base Policy (0.82 vs. 0.38) and **32% absolute** over a variant without knowledge-insulated tactile adaptation (TACO (w/o KI), 0.82 vs. 0.50).
- Compared to Filtered BC, which filtered successful real-world rollouts, TACO achieved a **39% absolute** improvement (0.82 vs. 0.43), indicating that reinforcing successful trajectories alone is less effective for contact recovery.
- TACO also resulted in fewer average completion steps (127.7 vs. 185.5 for Base Policy), suggesting smoother task execution.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies investigated the contributions of different components:
- **Generation of Imagined Correction Data (Figure 5):**
 - Removing tactile generation ("w/o tactile generation") resulted in a substantial drop in real-world success rate to 28%, compared to TACO's 82%. This indicates that visual imagination alone struggles to capture contact-state transitions.
 - Excluding tactile feedback from the labeling stage ("w/o tactile labeling") reduced performance to 65%, suggesting that tactile signals are effective when integrated into corrective action and progress prediction.
- **Scaling of Imagined Correction Data (Figure 5, Figure 10a):**
 - Increasing the real-to-imagined data ratio from 1:2 to 1:4 and 1:8 on "Insert Flower" led to success rates of 70%, 93%, and 97% respectively. Similar trends were observed on "Toast Bread." This suggests that larger scales of imagined corrections provide broader coverage of failure-adjacent contact states and contribute to improved policy post-training.
- **Effect of Advantage-Conditioned Training (Figure 10b):**
 - Removing advantage-conditioned training (which involved discarding failed rollouts and uniform supervision) led to a decrease in success rate from 93% to 83% on "Insert Flower" and 65% to 56% on "Wipe Whiteboard." This suggests that learning from failures via negative advantage signals is crucial, not just from successful and imagined corrective trajectories.
- **Effect of Failure-Adjacent Anchor Selection (Figure 10c):**
 - Uniformly sampling anchors from failed rollouts ("TACO (Uniform Anchor)") reduced success rates from 93% to 78% on "Insert Flower" and 65% to 25% on "Wipe Whiteboard." This indicates that progress-guided selection of failure-adjacent states is critical for generating relevant and effective imagined corrections.

#### 4.4. Analysis

- **Action Distribution Analysis (Figure 6):** On the "Insert Flower" task, TACO progressively yielded a broader end-effector pose distribution across iterations compared to the Base Policy and Filtered BC, which exhibited narrow distributions around the demonstration manifold. This suggests TACO enables the policy to explore more diverse successful adjustment behaviors for recovery.
- **Generalization Experiment (Figure 7, Figure 12):** TACO demonstrated generalization to unseen backgrounds, unseen objects (e.g., replacing an eraser with a sponge), and unseen object positions. While the base policy showed degradation in these out-of-domain (OOD) scenarios, TACO achieved consistently higher success rates after one adaptation iteration, by generating imagined corrections around OOD states. This indicates that the tactile-aware world model generalizes robustly even when OOD settings lie outside its original training distribution.
- **Failure Case Analysis (Figure 11):** Filtered BC tended to stall at contact transitions due to limited recovery behavior. TACO (w/o KI) suffered from degraded pre-contact perception due to the erosion of VLM priors. TACO, by combining imagined visuo-tactile corrections with knowledge-insulated tactile adaptation, addressed both issues, achieving reliable approach and effective contact recovery.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents TACO as a framework that addresses key challenges in scaling VLA models for contact-rich robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact lie in several areas:

1. **Scalable and Autonomous Correction:** TACO's iterative Recognize–Imagine–Label loop offers a method for generating visuo-tactile corrective supervision without requiring repeated human intervention. This capability substantially reduces the cost and time associated with data collection for policy improvement in scenarios where human demonstrations are difficult to obtain or scale, paving the way for more autonomous robot learning.
2. **Robustness in Contact-Rich Tasks:** By leveraging a tactile-aware world model that jointly denoises video and force sequences, TACO enables VLA policies to recover from localized contact failures that are often visually ambiguous. This directly enhances the robustness and reliability of robotic systems in tasks requiring precise physical interaction, such as assembly, grasping, or surface manipulation.
3. **Preservation of VLA Generalization Pries:** The introduction of knowledge-insulated tactile adaptation is crucial. It allows the integration of tactile-aware corrections without degrading the pretrained visual-language priors of VLA models. This ensures that robots can both perform fine-grained contact adjustments and maintain broad semantic understanding and pre-contact perception, thereby preventing new capabilities from compromising existing ones.
4. **Improved Generalization to Novel Scenarios:** The ability of TACO to generate effective corrections in out-of-domain settings (unseen backgrounds, objects, and positions) suggests that it can facilitate faster adaptation of VLA policies to novel environments and task variations. This contributes to the development of more versatile and adaptable robotic systems.
5. **Foundation for Future Robot Learning:** The approach of combining world models with offline reinforcement learning principles (advantage-conditioned training) for targeted policy refinement represents a direction for future robot learning paradigms. The detailed design of the visuo-tactile generation model and unified progress-action model contributes to the methodology of building sophisticated multimodal world models.

A limitation noted by the authors is that imagined corrections are generated offline rather than online during deployment. Future work may explore tighter coupling between the world model and policy for online correction generation, potentially enabling real-time failure recovery during task execution. Despite this, TACO provides a framework for enhancing robot capabilities in complex, real-world contact-rich environments by improving their ability to learn from and recover from physical interaction failures.
