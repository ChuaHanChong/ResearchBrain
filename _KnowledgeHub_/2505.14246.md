# Visual Agentic Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Lab and collaborating institutions develop Visual-ARFT, a reinforcement fine-tuning framework that enables Large Vision-Language Models to use web search and code execution tools for complex visual reasoning tasks, achieving up to 18.6% improvement in F1 scores over baselines while surpassing GPT-4o performance on image manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current Large Vision-Language Models lack effective multimodal agentic capabilities for combining visual and textual reasoning
- Existing supervised fine-tuning approaches for enabling tool use are costly and difficult to generalize
- Lack of standardized benchmarks for evaluating the tool use and visual reasoning abilities of multimodal agentic models

## Method

- ==Visual Agentic Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (Visual-ARFT)== framework using verifiable rewards
- ==Group Relative Policy Optimization== to maintain training stability
- New ==Multi-modal Agentic Tool Bench (MAT)== for standardized evaluation

## Results

- **18.6%** F1 improvement on MAT-Coding and **10.3%** F1 improvement on MAT-Search over baseline models
- **29.3%** F1 score gains on existing multi-hop QA benchmarks like 2Wiki and HotpotQA
- Surpassed GPT-4o performance on image manipulation tasks while using open-source models

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verifiable rewards based on format compliance and answer accuracy provide better training signals than learned reward models
> - Combining web search and code execution capabilities enables more complex visual reasoning tasks
> - Reinforcement fine-tuning can be effectively adapted for multimodal tool use

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Current Trends:** The paper directly addresses several key trends in AI research:
 - **Agentic AI:** The development of AI agents capable of reasoning, planning, and interacting with external tools is a major focus. This work aims to enhance the agentic abilities of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs).
 - **Multimodal Learning:** Integrating visual and textual information is increasingly important for AI systems to understand and interact with the real world. This paper tackles the challenge of building multimodal agents.
 - **Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) is emerging as a powerful technique for adapting LLMs to specific tasks and improving their ability to use tools. The paper proposes a novel RFT framework for LVLMs.
 - **Tool Use in LLMs:** Equipping LLMs with the ability to use external tools like web browsers and code interpreters is crucial for solving complex, real-world problems. This paper focuses on enabling LVLMs to use tools for search and image manipulation.
- **Gaps in the Literature:** The paper identifies several gaps in the current research landscape:
 - **Limited Multimodal Agentic Capabilities:** While significant progress has been made in language-only agentic systems, the development of multimodal agents that can truly "think" with images is less explored.
 - **Lack of Standardized Benchmarks:** There is a lack of standardized benchmarks for evaluating the tool use and visual reasoning abilities of multimodal agents.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning Limitations:** Supervised fine-tuning, the traditional approach for enabling tool use, is often costly and difficult to generalize. RFT offers a more scalable and adaptable alternative.
- **Related Work:** The paper references several relevant works:
 - **OpenAI's o3 and o1:** These proprietary models are cited as examples of agentic systems with native tool support and the use of RFT for rapid adaptation. The paper aims to replicate and improve upon these capabilities in open-source LVLMs.
 - **DeepSeek-R1:** This work highlights the effectiveness of verifiable reward signals for RFT, which is a key component of the proposed Visual-ARFT framework.
 - **Search-R1 and ReTool:** These papers demonstrate the successful application of RFT to agentic learning in language-only settings, inspiring the authors to explore RFT for multimodal agentic reasoning.
 - **Other related works:** They also draw comparison to other methods and models such as LLaVa, GPT-4o, InternLM, ZeroSearch and other LLM agents that make use of an external search engine.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objectives:**
 - To develop an effective framework, Visual-ARFT, for enabling flexible and adaptive reasoning abilities for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs).
 - To equip open-source LVLMs with the ability to browse websites for real-time information and write code to manipulate and analyze images.
 - To create a Multi-modal Agentic Tool Bench (MAT) for evaluating LVLMs' agentic search and coding abilities.
 - To demonstrate that Visual-ARFT can outperform baseline models and even surpass GPT-4o on the proposed benchmark.
- **Motivation:**
 - The authors are motivated by the potential of agentic AI systems to solve complex, real-world problems.
 - They recognize the gap between proprietary models like OpenAI's o3 and the capabilities of open-source LVLMs.
 - They aim to contribute to the development of robust and generalizable multimodal agents that can reason with both visual and textual information.
 - They aim to address the lack of standardized benchmarks for evaluating multimodal agentic reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Visual Agentic Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (Visual-ARFT):** The core of the paper is the proposed Visual-ARFT framework. This framework uses RFT to train LVLMs to:
 - **Reason:** Decompose complex tasks into smaller steps.
 - **Plan:** Decide which tools to use and when.
 - **Interact:** Invoke external tools (web search and code execution) to gather information and manipulate images.
- **Verifiable Rewards:** Visual-ARFT relies on verifiable rewards, which are derived from rule-based correctness checks rather than learned reward models. This simplifies the training process and provides explicit supervision signals for policy optimization.
 - *Format Rewards*: Rewards the model for following a pre-defined output format, to promote reasoning and tool usage.
 - *Accuracy Rewards*: Uses a modular format with F1 Score for the final answer, Semantic Similarity between generated and ground truth search queries, and a reward of 1 for executable code blocks.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** This algorithm is used to update the model's policy based on the reward feedback. GRPO incorporates a KL divergence term to prevent the updated policy from drifting too far from the reference model, ensuring training stability and generalization.
- **Multi-modal Agentic Tool Bench (MAT):** The authors introduce MAT, a new benchmark for evaluating multimodal agentic reasoning and tool use. MAT consists of two settings:
 - **MAT-Search:** Designed to measure LVLMs' agentic search abilities. It includes manually annotated multimodal multi-hop VQA examples.
 - **MAT-Coding:** Designed to measure LVLMs' agentic coding abilities. It includes VQA examples involving image distortions that require tool-based image processing.
 - The dataset is manually reviewed and filtered, and consists of a total of 350 high-quality examples.
- **Experimental Setup:** The authors evaluate Visual-ARFT on the MAT benchmark and existing multi-hop QA benchmarks using LVLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B. They use the Serper API as the retrieval tool for agentic search.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Visual-ARFT outperforms baselines on MAT:**
 - On MAT-Coding, Visual-ARFT achieves +18.6% F1 / +13.0% EM improvement over the baseline.
 - On MAT-Search, Visual-ARFT achieves +10.3% F1 / +8.7% EM improvement over the baseline.
- **Visual-ARFT surpasses GPT-4o on MAT-Coding:** This demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed framework.
- **Visual-ARFT generalizes well to existing multi-hop QA benchmarks:** It achieves +29.3 F1% / +25.9% EM gains on benchmarks like 2Wiki and HotpotQA, highlighting its strong generalization capabilities.
- **Reward Design Ablation:** The F1 Score for training provides better results than using Exact Match.
- **Comparison to CoT/RAG:** They compare Visual-ARFT to more traditional methods such as Chain-of-Thought and Retrieval Augmented Generation and show that the proposed model outperforms both.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Enabling Open-Source Multimodal Agents:** Visual-ARFT provides a promising path toward building robust and generalizable multimodal agents using open-source LVLMs.
- **Improving Tool Use in LLMs:** The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of RFT for enabling LVLMs to use external tools for search and image manipulation.
- **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning:** The proposed framework contributes to the advancement of multimodal reasoning capabilities in AI systems.
- **Providing a New Benchmark:** The MAT benchmark fills a gap in the research landscape by providing a standardized evaluation protocol for multimodal agentic reasoning.
- **Potential Applications:** The technology developed in this paper has the potential to be applied in various domains, including:
 - **AI assistants:** Enhancing the ability of AI assistants to understand and respond to visual information.
 - **Education:** Developing AI tutors that can reason with visual aids and provide personalized instruction.
 - **Scientific research:** Assisting researchers in analyzing images and extracting relevant information.
 - **Accessibility:** Creating AI tools that can help visually impaired individuals understand and interact with the world.
