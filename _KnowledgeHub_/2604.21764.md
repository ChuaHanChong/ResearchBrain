# Thinking with Reasoning Skills: Fewer Tokens, More Accuracy

> [!summary] Summary
> Thinking with Reasoning Skills (TRS) from Qiyuan Tech, Tsinghua University, The University of Hong Kong, and Peking University introduces a training-free framework that distills complex reasoning into compact skill cards to guide large language models, significantly reducing inference token usage and cost while maintaining or improving accuracy on math and coding tasks. It achieved up to a 53.8% cost reduction on Doubao Seed for mathematical reasoning and a +4.1 percentage point accuracy increase on GPT-OSS-120B for coding, particularly excelling on harder problems.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) generate substantial 'thinking' tokens during complex reasoning, leading to high inference costs and latency in practical deployment.
- Current LLM reasoning methods are inefficient, often requiring models to re-derive solution logic from scratch for each new problem, even when similar patterns exist.
- Attempts to reduce reasoning tokens frequently result in an efficiency-accuracy trade-off, where compressing deliberation can lead to lower correctness, especially on challenging problems.

## Method

- An ==offline phase== constructs a skill library by distilling LLM-generated reasoning traces from source problems into concise, structured ==skill cards== (Trigger, Do, Avoid, Check, Risk) and retrieval keywords.
- An ==online phase==, upon receiving a new query, retrieves the most relevant skill cards from the library using sparse, dense, or hybrid retrieval methods.
- Retrieved skill cards are injected into the target LLM's prompt with lightweight arbitration instructions, guiding its reasoning process and minimizing redundant exploration without modifying the model itself. **TRS** is fully training-free.

## Results

- On math reasoning, TRS reduced costs by up to **53.8%** (Doubao Seed) and **17.5%** (Gemini-3-Flash), while improving accuracy by up to **+1.8pp** (GPT-4o-mini).
- For coding tasks, the framework increased pass@1 accuracy by up to **+4.1pp** (GPT-OSS-120B) and reduced costs by up to **6.3%** (GPT-4o-mini).
- TRS consistently maintained or exceeded baseline accuracy on harder problem instances, outperforming other token-reduction methods that experienced significant accuracy degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling complex reasoning into compact, structured skill cards effectively serves as 'procedural shortcuts' that guide LLMs, minimizing redundant deliberation and improving efficiency.
> - The TRS framework successfully breaks the common efficiency-accuracy trade-off, demonstrating that significant token reduction can be achieved while preserving or enhancing accuracy, particularly on difficult problem instances.
> - Reasoning skills are transferable across different LLM models and related task domains, enabling modular knowledge reuse and offering a pathway to enhance the efficiency of less powerful models using expertise from more capable ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has increasingly emphasized their reasoning capabilities, particularly in complex domains such as mathematics and coding. Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) have been instrumental in eliciting multi-step reasoning, and subsequent advancements such as self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) and Tree-of-Thoughts (Yao et al., 2023) have further amplified these effects. Agent-style prompting, exemplified by ReAct (Yao et al., 2022), integrates reasoning with external actions and tool use. These deliberative reasoning paradigms have significantly improved LLM accuracy, allowing models to tackle problems that require intricate logical steps.

However, a notable consequence of these approaches is a substantial increase in test-time compute costs and latency. Generating intermediate reasoning traces often involves thousands of "thinking" tokens, which include redundant verifications and trial-and-error loops. Given that commercial LLM APIs typically bill by token, and often price output tokens higher than input tokens, the financial and infrastructure burden of reasoning-heavy inference becomes considerable. This creates a critical bottleneck for the practical deployment of reasoning-centric LLMs.

Prior research has attempted to mitigate this cost issue through various token-efficient reasoning strategies. These efforts can be broadly categorized into:
- **Prompt-only approaches:** These aim to compress intermediate traces (e.g., Chain-of-Draft by Xu et al., 2025) or enforce explicit token budgets (e.g., TALE by Han et al., 2024).
- **Decoding-time interventions:** Methods like NoWait (Wang et al., 2025) suppress reflection markers to prevent redundant "overthinking" loops, though these often require logit-level control over the model.
- **Model modifications:** Some approaches involve post-training models for budget awareness (Han et al., 2024) or shifting reasoning into alternative representations (e.g., latent-space reasoning in Coconut by Hao et al., 2024).

A common limitation across many of these existing methods is that they still operate under a "reasoning from scratch" paradigm for each new query. The model is asked to re-derive solution logic, albeit faster or shorter. This often leads to an efficiency–accuracy trade-off, especially on challenging problems, where forcibly compressing the reasoning space can cause models to omit crucial steps and fail.

This work draws inspiration from human problem-solving, where experts typically recall reusable skills distilled from past experiences rather than re-deriving fundamental logic for every problem. This concept of skill libraries and experience memory has also gained traction in LLM agent designs (Yao et al., 2022; Shinn et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023) to enable the reuse of prior reflections or executable skills. However, for pure reasoning tasks (e.g., math and coding) under token budget constraints, the "reasoning from scratch" regime has largely persisted. This paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a retrieval-augmented framework that externalizes and reuses distilled procedural knowledge, seeking to bypass the efficiency-accuracy trade-off inherent in prior token-reduction strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the practical and economic challenges associated with deploying highly capable, reasoning-centric Large Language Models (LLMs). While models that engage in extensive intermediate deliberation (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) demonstrate improved accuracy in complex tasks like mathematical problem-solving and code generation, this performance comes at a significant cost.

The key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Address High Inference Cost and Latency:** Modern LLMs, particularly those engaging in multi-step reasoning, generate a substantial number of "thinking" tokens. These tokens, which can include redundant verification steps, trial-and-error exploration, and verbose intermediate thoughts, directly translate to higher per-request costs when using commercial APIs (which often bill per token) and increased latency. The paper highlights industry reports confirming that reasoning-heavy inference amplifies infrastructure strain, making efficient reasoning a production-critical requirement.

2. **Overcome the Efficiency–Accuracy Trade-off:** Existing token-efficient reasoning methods, such as prompt compression (e.g., Chain-of-Draft) or explicit budget constraints (e.g., TALE), primarily focus on forcing models to generate shorter reasoning traces. However, these methods often treat each new query as a "blank slate," requiring the model to re-derive solution logic from scratch. This enforced brevity frequently leads to a compromise in accuracy, especially on difficult problems, where models may skip essential steps. The motivation is to break this trade-off, enabling models to achieve efficiency without sacrificing correctness.

3. **Mimic Human Expert Reasoning:** Humans, particularly experts, do not typically re-derive fundamental principles for every problem. Instead, they recall and apply reusable skills, patterns, and problem-solving strategies distilled from past experience. The paper aims to enable LLMs to emulate this behavior, replacing redundant internal rediscovery with the recall of externalized procedural memory.

4. **Decouple Expensive Exploration from Cheap Reuse:** The proposed framework seeks to shift the burden of extensive deliberation and trial-and-error exploration to an offline, one-time process. The expensive generation of long reasoning trajectories is used to distill reusable "reasoning skills." These compact skills can then be cheaply retrieved and applied at inference time, guiding the model toward efficient and effective solution paths without repeated, costly re-exploration.

5. **Develop a Practical, Training-Free, and Black-Box Compatible Framework:** A key objective is to create a solution that is readily applicable in real-world scenarios. This implies a framework that does not require retraining the large language model weights, making it compatible with black-box LLM APIs (where access to model internals is limited). The goal is to provide a method that can be seamlessly integrated into existing LLM-based systems to enhance their efficiency and performance.

In summary, the motivation is to deliver the benefits of sophisticated LLM deliberation—high accuracy on complex tasks—without incurring the prohibitive costs and latency associated with current "reasoning from scratch" paradigms, by introducing a system that allows LLMs to "think with recalled experience."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Thinking with Reasoning Skills (TRS), is a training-free, retrieval-augmented framework designed to improve the efficiency and accuracy of LLM reasoning. It operates by decoupling the acquisition of reasoning logic from its execution. The methodology consists of two main phases: offline skill library construction and online retrieval and skill injection.

#### 3.1. Offline Skill Library Construction

This phase involves creating a library of reusable reasoning skills by processing a dataset of source problems.
- **Experience Generation:** For each source instance (query `q_i`, answer `a_i`), a reasoning model (`M_r`) generates a detailed intermediate reasoning trace (`τ_i`) and a final output (`y_i`). The correctness of the output (`c_i = Eval(y_i, a_i)`) is determined (exact match for math, pass@1 for code). This forms an "experience record" (`q_i`, `a_i`, `τ_i`, `y_i`, `c_i`).
- **Skill Distillation:** A stronger summarizer model (`M_s`, e.g., Gemini 3 Flash Preview) is used to distill this experience record into two components:
 - **Compact Skill Card (`s_i`):** If `c_i=1` (correct solution), the skill card captures the essential solution pattern (e.g., minimal transformation, invariant, algorithmic template). If `c_i=0` (incorrect solution), it captures a reusable "failure-mode fix" or anti-pattern leading to a correction. Skill cards are standardized into a short, structured text format with five fields: Trigger (applicability cues), Do (actionable recipe), Avoid (anti-patterns), Check (constraints/invariants), and Risk (edge cases/failure modes). Abstraction, actionability, and compactness constraints are enforced during distillation to prevent copying instance-specific details and ensure conciseness.
 - **Retrieval Triggers (`K_i`):** A set of 10–20 keywords are generated to act as triggers for retrieving the skill card.
- **Key-Value Storage:** Each distilled skill card becomes a key-value entry in a library `L`. The value `v_i` is the skill card `s_i`, and the key `x_i` is a concatenation of the original query `q_i` and its retrieval triggers `K_i` (i.e., `Concat(q_i, K_i)`). This concatenation aims to improve recall for sparse retrieval methods while maintaining human interpretability.

#### 3.2. Online Retrieval and Skill Injection

When a new query `q` is presented, TRS performs the following steps:
- **Skill Retrieval:** A retriever component identifies the top-k most relevant skill cards from the constructed library `L`.
 - **Retrieval Backends:** The system supports three types of retrieval:
 - **Sparse Retrieval (BM25):** This method is based on lexical matching over the concatenated keys `x_i`. It is adopted as the default for mathematical reasoning tasks due to its effectiveness in matching formula patterns and theorem names.
 - **Dense Retrieval:** Utilizes embeddings (e.g., BGE-M3) for queries and keys, followed by nearest-neighbor search (e.g., FAISS). This is designed for semantic matching where lexical overlap might be weak, commonly seen in coding problems.
 - **Hybrid Retrieval:** Combines candidates from both BM25 and dense retrieval, merging them with a simple score fusion. This is the default for coding tasks.
- **Prompt Assembly and Skill Injection:** The retrieved top-k skill cards are prepended to the prompt of the reasoning model `M_r`. A lightweight arbitration instruction is included in the prompt, directing the model to "prefer the most directly applicable skill; ignore irrelevant or contradictory advice" and to "keep intermediate reasoning concise while maintaining correctness."
- **Gating and Budget Control:** To prevent prompt inflation and manage the impact of retrieved skills:
 - Each skill card is capped to a small budget (e.g., a few bullet points).
 - Only the top-k cards (by retrieval score) are included.
- **Token Reduction Mechanism:** TRS reduces reasoning tokens by providing the model with a "procedural shortcut" and explicit pitfalls distilled from past experiences. This guidance helps the model avoid redundant exploration, branching, detours, and repeated debugging, effectively replacing lengthy internal rediscovery with externalized knowledge. While the input prompt length slightly increases due to injected skills, the reduction in generated reasoning tokens typically leads to a larger overall decrease in end-to-end cost and latency.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Math Benchmark:** DEEPMATH-103K, with 93K problems for skill library construction and 10K held-out problems for evaluation. Default retrieval is BM25 with k=1.
- **Coding Benchmark:** NEMOTRON-COMPETITIVE-PROGRAMMING-V1 (34K problems, filtered to 26.6K for training and 1K for testing). Two generators (Gemini 3 Flash Preview and Doubao Seed 2.0 Pro) are used for trajectory generation. Skills are distilled with Gemini 3 Flash Preview. Default retrieval is hybrid with k=5. Evaluation uses local compile-and-run judge (pass@1).
- **Models:** Evaluated models include GPT-OSS-120B, Gemini-3 Flash Preview, GPT-4o-mini, and Doubao Seed (1.8 Pro for math, 2.0 Pro for coding).
- **Metrics:** Accuracy (exact match for math, pass@1 for coding), Reasoning tokens (completion tokens used as proxy), and Cost reduction (normalized percentage relative to baseline).
- **Difficulty Slicing:** Test sets are sliced based on the baseline model’s thinking length (`T_base_think(q)`) to analyze performance on harder instances (`Q_>=θ`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of Thinking with Reasoning Skills (TRS) across mathematical and coding reasoning tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Efficiency-Accuracy Trade-off

Table 1 demonstrates that TRS generally improves the efficiency-accuracy trade-off compared to direct reasoning.
- **Mathematical Reasoning:** For Gemini-3-Flash, TRS increased accuracy by +0.7 percentage points while reducing cost by 17.5%. For GPT-4o-mini, accuracy improved by +1.8 percentage points with a 6.9% cost reduction. Doubao Seed-1.8 Pro maintained accuracy (a minor reduction of 0.2 percentage points) while achieving a significant 53.8% cost reduction. GPT-OSS-120B preserved accuracy with a 16.9% cost reduction.
- **Coding Tasks:** TRS generally improved pass@1 accuracy with lower costs. GPT-4o-mini showed a +2.4 percentage point increase in accuracy with a 6.3% cost reduction. Doubao Seed-2.0 Pro improved accuracy by +0.8 percentage points with a 6.0% cost reduction. GPT-OSS-120B achieved a +4.1 percentage point increase in accuracy, though this was accompanied by a slight cost increase of 4.8%, attributed to larger prompts.
These results indicate that TRS often enables a reduction in per-request cost without sacrificing correctness, and in some cases, with an improvement in accuracy.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines on Difficulty Slices

Figures 4 and 5 illustrate the performance of TRS against other token-efficient reasoning methods (TALE-EP, Chain of Draft (CoD), NoWait) when problems are sliced by baseline thinking length (difficulty).
- **Breaking the Trade-off:** Unlike TALE and CoD, which exhibited a substantial accuracy collapse on harder problems (higher `θ` thresholds), TRS consistently matched or exceeded baseline accuracy. For instance, on the hardest GPT-OSS tasks (`θ > 10k`), TRS showed accuracy uplift from approximately 45% to 80%, suggesting that retrieved skills effectively guide the model through complex solution paths.
- **Efficiency Gains:** TRS achieved significant token reductions. For Doubao, it cut generated tokens for hard problems (θ ≥ 12k) from approximately 12k to 5k while maintaining high accuracy. For GPT-OSS, TRS approximately halved token usage (from ~15k to ~7k) without the accuracy degradation observed in speed-limit approaches.

#### 4.3. Controlled Representation and Coverage Analysis

Table 2 details an ablation study on the impact of skill card representation and library coverage. The findings suggest that the benefits of TRS are not merely due to simple retrieval of relevant context. Raw examples, raw CoT traces, free-form summaries, and smaller structured-card libraries all underperformed the full TRS approach, especially on GPT-OSS. The strongest performance was observed when structured skill reuse was combined with sufficient library coverage, indicating that the structured distillation of procedural guidance is crucial for TRS's effectiveness.

#### 4.4. Transfer to External Contest-Math Benchmarks

Table 3 and Figure 17 demonstrate the transferability of skills distilled from a separate contest-math corpus (AoPS) to external AIME and HMMT benchmarks.
- **Heterogeneous Transfer:** Out of 25 model-benchmark pairs, 13 showed improved accuracy and 20 showed reduced per-query cost. Doubao-1.8 was the clearest average beneficiary (+1.88 accuracy points, -2.8% cost), while GPT-OSS-120B showed smaller gains (+0.50 accuracy points, -6.5% cost). Gemini-3-Flash improved accuracy but increased cost in this setting.
- This indicates that reusable reasoning structures can transfer across nearby domains, though the benefits are not universally guaranteed and depend on the specific target model and workload.

#### 4.5. Cross-Model Skill Transfer

Figure 6 investigates the transferability of skills distilled from one model (Source) to another (Target).
- **Difficulty-Dependent Gains:** The benefits of TRS were more pronounced on harder problems, where baseline accuracy declined. TRS preserved performance better while reducing cost, suggesting that retrieved skills prune redundant exploration effectively.
- **Source Alignment:** Skills were transferable across models. However, using skills distilled from the same source model generally yielded the highest accuracy, likely due to stylistic alignment. Cross-source skills could sometimes lead to more aggressive token reduction.
- **Implication:** This supports a modular workflow where skills from powerful proprietary models can enhance more efficient deployment models, with model-specific distillation being preferable for maximal accuracy.

#### 4.6. Prompt Strategy for Skill Injection

Figure 7 explores the impact of different prompt strategies for injecting skills.
- **Model-Specific Optimization:** Optimal prompt strategies varied by model. For Doubao, the "Short" prompt (explicit budget) offered the best trade-off between token reduction and accuracy. For GPT-OSS, the "Draft" prompt (concise step-by-step) achieved the highest accuracy while maintaining strong efficiency. This indicates the importance of tailoring injection prompts to the specific LLM's characteristics.

#### 4.7. Summarizer-Choice Ablation and Non-reasoning Stress Test

- **Summarizer Choice:** An ablation with an alternative summarizer (GPT-5-mini) showed that summarizer choice matters, but the full TRS effect is not reducible to a single free-form summary backend.
- **Stress Test:** Injecting mismatched DeepMath skills into short-form classification tasks (Table 5, Figure 18) did not cause catastrophic accuracy degradation but consistently increased cost. This serves as a robustness control, suggesting that TRS is not inherently harmful in mismatched scenarios, but it is not recommended for non-reasoning workloads due to increased cost.

In summary, the main findings consistently demonstrate that TRS effectively addresses the token efficiency challenge in LLM reasoning by reusing distilled procedural knowledge. It often surpasses baseline performance in terms of both accuracy and cost-efficiency, particularly on complex tasks where other brevity-forcing methods tend to fail.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, "Thinking with Reasoning Skills (TRS)," introduces a novel framework with several significant implications and potential impacts on the development and deployment of large language models (LLMs).

1. **Breaking the Efficiency-Accuracy Trade-off:** The most significant contribution of TRS is its demonstrated ability to reduce the computational cost (in terms of reasoning tokens) of LLMs while simultaneously maintaining or improving accuracy on complex reasoning tasks. This directly addresses a critical challenge in the field, as previous token-reduction strategies often resulted in a compromise of performance. By breaking this trade-off, TRS makes sophisticated LLM reasoning more viable for real-world applications where both high performance and cost-effectiveness are essential.

2. **Practical and Economic Viability:** The reduction in reasoning tokens translates directly to lower inference costs and latency, particularly when utilizing commercial LLM APIs that bill by token. This economic advantage can significantly broaden the applicability of reasoning-centric LLMs, making them more accessible and affordable for deployment in various industries and services. Reduced latency also enhances user experience in interactive applications requiring quick responses.

3. **Black-Box Compatibility and Training-Free Deployment:** TRS operates as a training-free, retrieval-augmented framework, which is compatible with black-box LLMs. This means it can be implemented without requiring access to the internal weights or architecture of proprietary models, or undertaking costly retraining efforts. This ease of integration accelerates deployment and allows existing, powerful LLMs to benefit from the efficiency gains without fundamental modifications.

4. **Enhanced Robustness on Hard Problems:** The findings indicate that TRS particularly shines on more challenging problems, where baseline models struggle with extensive deliberation and other brevity-enforcing methods lead to accuracy collapse. By providing "navigation maps" through distilled skills, TRS guides models away from redundant detours and toward effective solution paths, thereby enhancing their robustness and reliability on difficult tasks.

5. **Modular and Transferable Knowledge:** The concept of distilling reusable reasoning skills into structured, retrievable cards creates a modular knowledge base. This skill library can be built once and then potentially reused across different LLMs or even adapted for related domains. The cross-model transfer analysis suggests that skills distilled from powerful proprietary models could be used to enhance the performance of more efficient, smaller deployment models, optimizing resource allocation.

6. **Inspiration for Future Research in Knowledge Representation:** The structured skill card format (Trigger, Do, Avoid, Check, Risk) offers a formalized approach to representing procedural knowledge for LLMs. This could inspire further research into more sophisticated methods for knowledge distillation, abstraction, and the representation of implicit reasoning patterns, moving beyond simple factual retrieval.

7. **Scalable Reasoning Paradigm:** By externalizing and reusing procedural memory, TRS shifts the paradigm from individual problem re-derivation to leveraging collective experience. As skill libraries grow, this approach has the potential to scale LLM reasoning capabilities more effectively, allowing models to benefit from an ever-expanding repository of problem-solving expertise.

In conclusion, TRS represents a significant step towards practical and scalable intelligent systems. Its ability to enable more accurate and cost-effective reasoning has the potential to accelerate the adoption of advanced LLM capabilities in diverse real-world applications, from automated code generation and mathematical problem-solving to more general intelligent agents.
