# PhyGile: Physics-Prefix Guided Motion Generation for Agile General Humanoid Motion Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyGile integrates a robot-native diffusion-based motion generator with an agile general motion tracking policy, guided by physics-prefixes, enabling humanoid robots to execute complex, physically feasible whole-body motions from natural language commands. The system demonstrates enhanced robustness for agile skills and successfully tracks diverse high-difficulty movements on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Text-to-motion models, designed for humans, often produce physically infeasible motions when retargeted to humanoid robots, violating dynamic constraints.
- Large motion datasets exhibit a long-tail distribution where agile, high-difficulty motions are rare, leading to fragile performance on these skills for general tracking policies.
- Existing hierarchical pipelines loosely couple motion generation and tracking, often failing if generated motions fall outside the controller's feasible execution region.

## Method

- A unified framework, **PhyGile**, integrates a ==two-stage Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) General Motion Tracking (GMT)== policy with a ==Token-level Parameter-mixing MoE (TP-MoE)==-conditioned robot-native diffusion generator.
- ==Physics-prefix guidance== conditions the diffusion model at inference with dynamically feasible motion segments, directing generation towards executable regions.
- A ==closed-loop refinement process== iteratively fine-tunes the GMT controller on prefix-conditioned rollouts, ensuring kinematic plausibility and dynamic consistency over long horizons.

## Results

- PhyGile achieved a **0.9401** success rate and **0.0720 rad** mean per-joint angle error on the AMASS test set, demonstrating improved robustness and tracking accuracy over baseline methods.
- The framework effectively resolves the semantics–physics trade-off, maintaining strong semantic fidelity (R@3 at **0.6176**) while achieving low physical violation metrics (e.g., **0.00 mm** penetration after fine-tuning).
- Successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, the system stably tracked a diverse set of agile, high-difficulty whole-body motions based on natural language commands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot-native motion generation combined with physics-prefix guidance effectively aligns generated motions with a robot's physical constraints, reducing infeasibility.
> - A two-stage MoE GMT training curriculum, featuring hard-biased routing and soft global post-training, allows the policy to specialize and robustly track agile, high-difficulty skills despite data imbalance.
> - Token-level parameter mixing in the diffusion generator enhances semantic fidelity by promoting fine-grained alignment between individual text tokens and generated motion segments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robot control is transitioning from task-specific locomotion to more scalable and general-purpose motion generation. This shift is driven by advancements in two primary research areas: General Motion Tracking (GMT) and text-driven motion generation.

GMT policies have demonstrated the capability to imitate large sets of reference motions and transfer them to real hardware, offering a foundation for robust robot control. Concurrently, text-driven motion generation, particularly using diffusion-based models, has shown progress in synthesizing semantically rich and diverse human motions from natural language commands. The convergence of these two paradigms aims to establish hierarchical control systems where text-to-motion models provide high-level motion priors, and GMT policies act as low-level executors.

Despite these advances, a fundamental misalignment persists between motion generation and physical execution in practice. Most text-to-motion models are trained on human motion datasets (e.g., SMPL representations), designed with human biomechanics and actuation in mind. Directly retargeting these motions to humanoid robots often leads to physically infeasible trajectories that, while kinematically plausible, violate physical constraints such as torque limits, contact consistency, and dynamic balance. These retargeting artifacts degrade policy learning and necessitate data filtering, impacting data efficiency and robustness.

A second challenge is the severe data imbalance in large motion datasets, characterized by long-tail distributions. Common datasets contain an abundance of simple motions (e.g., walking) but are sparse in complex, agile, or highly dynamic movements. Training general tracking policies on such skewed data tends to prioritize frequent, low-difficulty motions, leaving complex skills undertrained and leading to fragile performance during agile tasks.

Previous attempts to address these issues include aligning language with robot-controllable embeddings or directly modeling robot-native motion data. However, these methods typically optimize generation and tracking as loosely coupled modules, meaning that if generated motions fall outside the controller's feasible region, execution often fails without a mechanism to reconcile the two.

PhyGile contributes to this landscape by proposing a unified framework designed to explicitly bridge the gap between robot-native motion generation and agile GMT, aiming to resolve the practical misalignments and data imbalances that hinder robust execution of complex, text-driven humanoid motions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to execute agile and expressive whole-body motions in real-world environments stably and reliably, directly from natural language commands. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges identified in the broader research landscape:

1. **Addressing Physical Feasibility in Motion Generation:** Existing text-to-motion models, largely trained on human motion data, generate trajectories that often lack physical feasibility when retargeted to robots. These motions may satisfy kinematic constraints but fail to account for robot-specific dynamics, leading to unstable or impossible movements. The motivation is to generate motions that are inherently "robot-native" and physically realizable from the outset, thereby eliminating the issues associated with post-hoc retargeting.
2. **Overcoming Data Imbalance for Agile Motion Tracking:** Large-scale motion datasets typically contain a disproportionate number of simple motions compared to complex, agile, or high-difficulty skills. This imbalance biases policy learning towards easier motions, resulting in tracking policies that perform poorly on rare and dynamically challenging behaviors. The motivation is to develop a tracking policy that is robust across the full spectrum of motion difficulties, particularly for agile and highly coordinated movements, by explicitly addressing the long-tail distribution problem.
3. **Tightly Coupling Motion Generation and Physical Execution:** In many existing hierarchical pipelines, motion generation and tracking modules are loosely coupled. The generator produces a trajectory, and the tracker attempts to follow it, but there is no inherent feedback mechanism to ensure that the generated motion is within the tracking policy's executable capabilities. The motivation is to establish a closed-loop system where generation is guided by physical feasibility constraints derived from the robot's control capabilities, thereby ensuring that generated motions are not only semantically rich but also dynamically consistent and trackable by the robot.
4. **Enabling Stable Real-World Deployment of Complex Skills:** Ultimately, the research aims to facilitate the stable deployment of complex, agile whole-body skills on physical humanoid robots. This requires a robust and unified framework that can translate open-vocabulary language commands into dynamically feasible and consistently trackable robot movements, overcoming the fragility observed in prior methods when faced with high-difficulty tasks.

By addressing these motivations, PhyGile seeks to expand the capabilities of text-driven humanoid control, moving beyond low-dynamic motions to encompass a wider range of agile and expressive behaviors that can be reliably executed on hardware.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhyGile is a unified framework designed to couple robot-native diffusion-based motion generation with agile General Motion Tracking (GMT) through physics-prefix guidance. The methodology consists of three main components: a two-stage Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) GMT tracker, a robot-native diffusion generator with Token-level Parameter-mixing Mixture of Experts (TP-MoE), and a physics-prefix-guided fine-tuning stage.

**A. General Motion Tracking (GMT)**

The GMT component focuses on training a robust policy capable of tracking a wide range of motion difficulties, particularly agile and complex ones, by addressing data imbalance.

1. **Data Curriculum and Difficulty Annotation:** A dataset of approximately 45 hours of motion (including HumanML3D, AMASS, LaFAN1, and a private MoCap set) is retargeted to the target robot embodiment. Motions from HumanML3D are semantically analyzed using an LLM to assign them to one of 10 difficulty levels (grouped as Easy, Medium, Hard), representing increasing coordination and dynamic instability. Levels 11-12 are excluded due to environmental or physical mismatch with the simulation.
2. **Freeze-and-Drop Self-Purification:** During training, a motion file is temporarily "frozen" if its Exponential Moving Average (EMA) tracking error exceeds a threshold or its success rate falls below a threshold, after sufficient exposure. If a file repeatedly triggers freezing, it is "dropped" from the training set, preventing the policy from learning from persistently untrackable references.
3. **Look-ahead Motion Encoding:** The policy receives multi-scale future motion information. A short look-ahead window provides immediate targets, while a longer, downsampled horizon is encoded into a compact latent vector `z_t` via temporal convolution and pooling. `z_t` captures upcoming dynamics and serves as the sole input to the MoE gate.
4. **Two-stage Training with MoE Routing:** The actor is an MoE policy with `K` expert MLPs and a lightweight gate `G` that routes `z_t` to logits `l`. Low-frequency soft top-k routing selects `k` candidate experts, and actions are a convex mixture of their outputs.
 - **Stage I: Level-wise Curriculum with Hard-Biased Routing:** Training progresses level-by-level from difficulty 1 to 10, progressively unlocking higher-difficulty motions. This induces expert specialization. Difficulty-aware in-batch sampling maintains exposure to earlier levels. Hard-biased routing (with probability `ρ_hard = 0.8`) is applied for samples from the current hardest level, forcing them to route to the newly unlocked expert (`E_l_max`). New experts are initialized by copying the preceding expert, and an auxiliary cross-entropy loss (`L_route`) aligns gate output with motion semantics.
 - **Stage II: Global Soft Post-Training:** Curriculum masks and hard-routing constraints are removed. All `K` experts access the full dataset, enabling end-to-end optimization with differentiable soft top-k routing. A load-balancing objective (`L_bal`) prevents routing collapse, replacing strong level supervision. Dynamic expert addition is supported to handle distribution shifts in unlabeled data, spawning new experts for persistently difficult files and adjusting learning rates for adaptation.

**B. Motion Generation**

The motion generation component is a diffusion-based model that directly synthesizes robot-native joint trajectories from natural language commands.

1. **Robot-Native Motion Representation:** Motions are represented as a 262-dimensional per-frame descriptor `m_t` for the robot body. This descriptor includes root angular velocity (`dot_omega_root`), root linear velocity (`dot_v_root`), root height (`z`), local positions of 12 rigid bodies (`p_ric`), 6D rotation representations of 29 rigid bodies (`R_6d`), local velocities of 13 rigid bodies (`dot_p_local`), and binary contact indicators for feet (`c_foot`) and hands (`c_hand`). All features are extracted under a canonical heading.
2. **Conditional Denoising Diffusion Framework:** A DistilBERT encoder processes text into token embeddings. The denoising network is an AdaLN Transformer Decoder, injecting the diffusion timestep via AdaLN. The training objective is a standard denoising loss (`L_diff`).
3. **Token-level Parameter-mixing Mixture of Experts (TP-MoE):** This module is integrated after the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) of each decoder layer. For each text token embedding, a gating network produces expert weights, which are then used to mix an expert pool (`{e_1..., e_K}`) in parameter space. A spatial mask, based on cross-attention weights, is applied to the resulting token-specific mixed expert outputs, allowing fine-grained alignment between individual text tokens and specific motion segments. A load-balancing loss (`L_bal`) prevents expert collapse.
4. **Action-Semantic Frequency-aware Oversampling (ASFO):** To mitigate the long-tailed distribution in motion data, ASFO uses an LLM to extract action-semantic tags. An oversampling multiplier is calculated based on the empirical frequency of each tag relative to the median frequency. Rare-tag samples are augmented via left-right mirroring with a rarity-dependent probability, enhancing diversity for under-represented actions.

**C. Physics-Prefix-Guided Motion Generation Fine-tuning**

This stage bridges generation and execution by aligning generated motions with the physically executable manifold of the GMT controller.

1. **Physics-Guided Prefix Conditioning:** During inference, a physically executable motion segment (`x_prefix`), refined by the GMT simulator, is concatenated with a desired terminal constraint (`x_target`) and provided as conditioning to the diffusion model. This anchors the denoising process to a dynamically consistent initial state, steering sampling towards feasible regions.
2. **Closed-Loop Refinement and Diffusion Fine-tuning:** Each generated motion is validated in simulation using the trained GMT tracker. Trajectories exceeding a Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) tolerance are rejected and resampled. Feasible rollouts are retained. This generate–simulate–select loop filters out dynamically inconsistent samples. To further align the generative distribution with executable motion, the pretrained GMT controller (Stage II) is progressively fine-tuned using PPO on prefix-conditioned closed-loop rollouts, with the diffusion generator kept frozen. This involves a receding-horizon procedure: conditioned on the current prefix, a 1-second continuation is sampled and refined via end-to-end simulation. The refined continuation is appended to the prefix, and the process repeats until the designated suffix is reached. This iterative refinement ensures kinematic plausibility and dynamic consistency over long horizons. The fine-tuned GMT controller and physically executable motions are then deployed to the real robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PhyGile involved comprehensive offline benchmarks and real-robot experiments, demonstrating its ability to generate and execute agile, high-difficulty humanoid motions that are both semantically aligned and physically realizable.

**A. Motion Generation Evaluation (Offline)**

- **Semantics–Physics Trade-off Resolution:** PhyGile demonstrated a resolution to the common trade-off between semantic richness and physical feasibility. While human-motion generators (e.g., MLD, MDM) showed strong text alignment and diversity, they often produced severe physical artifacts (high penetration, floating, skating) after retargeting due to embodiment mismatch. Robot-space methods like TextOp reduced these violations but often compromised text alignment and diversity.
- **Superior Performance:** PhyGile achieved the best distribution matching (lowest FID) among compared methods, while maintaining competitive semantic fidelity (R@3, MM-Dist) and significantly reducing physical artifacts compared to retargeting-based approaches. For example, "Ours" achieved an FID of 0.1823, notably lower than MDM's 0.2550 or TextOp's 0.3074.
- **Impact of Physical Fine-tuning:** "Ours [Fine-tuned]" further improved contact safety, achieving 0.00 mm penetration and a skating percentage of 1.58% (compared to TextOp's 0.00 mm penetration and 7.5% skating), albeit with a minor impact on retrieval and distribution metrics. This indicates the effectiveness of the closed-loop refinement in enhancing physical realism.
- **Effectiveness of TP-MoE:** Ablation studies showed that removing the Token-level Parameter-mixing Mixture of Experts (TP-MoE) ("Ours w/o TP-MOE") degraded alignment and distribution matching, confirming its contribution to fine-grained text conditioning. Optimal performance (peak R@3) was observed with a moderate number of activated experts (around top-k=6).

**B. Motion Tracking Evaluation (Offline)**

- **Robustness and Stability:** The complete PhyGile pipeline exhibited the best overall robustness on the test set, achieving the highest success rate (0.9401) and improved stability compared to the GMT baseline (0.8914) and TextOp (0.8888).
- **Tracking Accuracy:** PhyGile attained the lowest mean per-joint angle error (E_mpjae, 0.0720 rad) and mean per-joint velocity error (E_mpjve, 0.4222 rad/s) among all compared methods. While TextOp achieved a slightly lower mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe, 0.2427 m vs. PhyGile's 0.2566 m), PhyGile demonstrated stronger robustness and more consistent performance across multiple metrics.
- **Component Contributions (Ablation Study):**
 - **PhyGile-C (Difficulty-stratified curriculum):** Showed improvement over GMT in tracking accuracy.
 - **PhyGile-CF (Freeze-and-drop):** Further enhanced stability and overall performance by filtering incompatible motions.
 - **PhyGile-CFM (MoE design in Stage I):** Improved success rate by enabling expert specialization across difficulty tiers.
 - **PhyGile (Global soft-MoE fine-tuning in Stage II):** Consistently strengthened robustness and reduced joint errors across the full dataset.
- **Scalability:** The success rate for all variants increased with scaling up the GMT module size, with the full PhyGile consistently outperforming others across all tested sizes.

**C. Real-world Deployment**

- **Hardware Execution:** The system was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot with 29 degrees of freedom. The fine-tuned GMT controller ran at 50 Hz, processing proprioceptive states and reference motion to output joint commands in real time.
- **Agile Whole-body Motion:** The physics-prefix-guided, fine-tuned GMT controller demonstrated stable and robust tracking of a diverse set of agile, high-difficulty whole-body motions (e.g., rolling, jumping, spinning, cartwheels) on the physical hardware.
- **Generalization:** A single fine-tuned GMT policy generalized across a wide range of complex behaviors without requiring per-skill retraining, indicating strong scalability towards complex whole-body skills in real-world environments. Trajectories were verified for physical executability via sim-to-sim validation before hardware deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhyGile presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robot control, particularly in bridging the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level physical execution for agile, whole-body motions. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Agile and Dynamically Feasible Humanoid Motions:** The framework directly addresses the long-standing challenge of physical feasibility in text-driven motion generation. By generating motions natively in the robot's skeletal space and incorporating physics-prefix guidance, PhyGile produces trajectories that are not only semantically aligned with natural language but also dynamically consistent and executable by real robots. This capability expands the operational envelope of humanoids to include highly dynamic and agile behaviors that were previously difficult or impossible to realize.
2. **Robustness Against Data Imbalance and Retargeting Artifacts:** The curriculum-based Mixture-of-Experts training for the GMT controller effectively tackles the data imbalance problem, ensuring robust tracking of rare and complex motions. Furthermore, the robot-native generation process inherently mitigates the physical inconsistencies and artifacts typically introduced by retargeting human motions, leading to a more reliable and stable control pipeline.
3. **Unified and Closed-Loop Control System:** PhyGile integrates motion generation and tracking into a unified, closed-loop framework. The physics-prefix-guided fine-tuning stage creates a principled interface, ensuring that the generative process is anchored by physically executable segments and that the tracking policy is refined to handle the generated motion distribution. This tight coupling reduces the "generation-execution discrepancy" and enhances overall system reliability.
4. **Advancing Text-Driven Humanoid Control:** The ability to translate open-vocabulary language commands into stable, real-world execution of complex, agile whole-body skills represents a substantial step forward for text-driven humanoid control. This opens new possibilities for intuitive human-robot interaction, allowing users to command robots with natural language for intricate tasks.
5. **Scalability to Complex Behaviors:** The demonstrated success in deploying a single fine-tuned GMT policy across a diverse range of complex behaviors without per-skill retraining highlights the framework's scalability. This is crucial for developing generalist humanoid robots capable of adapting to various tasks and environments without extensive re-engineering.
6. **Broader Applications:** The principles and methodologies introduced by PhyGile could have broader impacts beyond current applications. They could inform the development of more general-purpose humanoid robots for applications such as advanced manufacturing, logistics, exploration in unstructured environments, and even entertainment, where agile and expressive physical interaction is essential. The approach of learning directly in robot space with physically grounded feedback offers a template for future research in embodied AI and robot learning.

In summary, PhyGile establishes a new baseline for achieving both semantic richness and physical realism in humanoid motion generation and execution, paving the way for more capable and versatile humanoid robots.
