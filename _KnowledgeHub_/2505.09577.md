# VTLA: Vision-Tactile-Language-Action Model with Preference Learning for Insertion Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences develop a Vision-Tactile-Language-Action (VTLA) model that combines visual, tactile, and language inputs for robotic insertion tasks, achieving over 90% success rates on unseen peg shapes through vision-guided temporal enhancement and preference learning while demonstrating effective sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action models struggle with contact-rich manipulation tasks due to lack of tactile feedback
- Traditional robotic manipulation systems have limited generalization capabilities across different object shapes and configurations
- Existing tactile-language-action models lack visual modalities, and standard next-token-prediction training mismatches discrete language objectives against continuous robotic control

## Method

- Developed VTLA architecture integrating vision, tactile, and language inputs using pre-trained VLM (Qwen2-VL)
- Implemented Vision-Guided Temporally Enhanced Tokens (VGTE) to process temporal tactile information
- Applied Direct Preference Optimization to bridge gap between discrete language modeling and continuous control

## Results

- Achieved >90% success rate on unseen peg shapes in simulation
- Outperformed traditional imitation learning and existing multimodal baselines
- Demonstrated effective real-world performance on UR3 robot despite training only in simulation

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision guidance early in tasks combined with temporal tactile information improves manipulation performance
> - Preference learning significantly enhances generalization to unseen objects
> - Successful sim-to-real transfer possible through domain randomization in synthetic training data

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the rapidly evolving field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on:

- **Multimodal Perception in Robotics:** The paper addresses the limitations of relying solely on visual information for robotic tasks, particularly in contact-rich scenarios. It builds upon the growing body of research that emphasizes the importance of integrating tactile sensing with vision for robust manipulation. The research acknowledges the growing importance of Vision-Tactile learning frameworks that enhance grasping and manipulation capabilities.

- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models for Robotics:** The paper situates itself within the recent trend of leveraging large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) for robotic control. It recognizes the advancements made by models like RT-2 and RoboFlamingo in generalizing across different robotic platforms and tasks. It critically points out the limitations of existing VLA systems in contact-rich manipulation scenarios due to the lack of tactile feedback.

- **Tactile-Language Models:** The paper acknowledges the emerging area of tactile-language models, noting their success in tasks like texture and material recognition. It builds upon the previous work that combines tactile sensing and language for robot learning, suggesting that it aims to overcome specific limitations of TLA models by integrating vision.

- **Sim2Real Transfer:** A significant aspect of the research landscape is the challenge of transferring skills learned in simulation to real-world robots (Sim2Real). This work addresses Sim2Real by training the model on simulated data with domain randomization and evaluating its performance on a real robot.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations of this research are:

- **To develop a robust robotic manipulation system for contact-rich tasks:** The authors aim to create a system that can handle tasks requiring precise contact and force control, such as peg-in-hole insertion. This is motivated by the observation that many real-world manipulation tasks require robots to interact physically with their environment, making tactile feedback crucial.
- **To improve the generalization capabilities of robotic manipulation systems:** The authors seek to create a system that can generalize to different object shapes, sizes, and task configurations. This is motivated by the need for robots to be able to adapt to new situations without requiring extensive retraining.
- **To leverage the power of VLMs while incorporating tactile sensing:** The authors want to combine the reasoning capabilities of VLMs with the sensory information from tactile sensors to create a more versatile and robust manipulation system. The researchers believe VLMs offer significant advantages in terms of generalization and adaptability, but that these benefits need to be extended to contact-rich tasks.
- **To address the limitations of existing TLA models:** The authors identify specific weaknesses in existing tactile-language-action models, such as the absence of visual modalities and the need for improved tactile encoding and training frameworks.
- **To bridge the gap between classification-based language model training and continuous robotic control:** The authors aim to improve the performance of VLMs in robotic control by addressing the mismatch between the discrete nature of language model training and the continuous nature of robotic actions.
- **To achieve effective Sim2Real transfer:** The researchers aim to develop a model that can be trained primarily in simulation and then deployed on a real robot without significant performance degradation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper presents a multi-faceted approach:

- **VTLA Model Architecture:** The core of the research is the development of the VTLA model, which integrates vision, tactile, and language inputs to generate robot actions. The model builds upon a pre-trained VLM (Qwen2-VL) and incorporates a tactile encoding module.
- **Vision-Guided Temporally Enhanced Tokens (VGTE):** A key contribution is the design of VGTE, which addresses the limitations of VLMs in processing temporal information from tactile sensors. This involves emphasizing visual inputs and enhancing temporal fusion of tactile data before tokenization. The VGTE component highlights both vision guidance and temporal enhancement. Vision observations are prioritized in the early stages of tasks and VGTE uses temporally enhanced tactile inputs to improve VLM's reasoning on sequential data.
- **Simulated Data Collection:** The authors create a synthetic dataset of peg-in-hole insertion tasks using NVIDIA Isaac Gym. The dataset includes vision, tactile, and action data, and is designed to facilitate Sim2Real transfer. The assembly task is structured around gripping a peg, positioning it above the hole, and attempting insertion. The data is collected from both successful and unsuccessful attempts.
- **Instruction Tuning:** The VTLA model is trained using a next-token prediction (NTP) loss, where the model predicts the next action token based on the previous vision, tactile, and language inputs.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** The authors employ DPO to address the mismatch between the classification-based NTP loss and the continuous nature of robotic control. DPO involves generating diverse action predictions, creating a preference dataset based on proximity to the ground truth, and then optimizing the model to prefer actions closer to the ground truth.
- **Real-World Experiments:** The VTLA model, trained exclusively on simulated data, is evaluated on a real UR3 robot performing peg-in-hole insertion tasks. This demonstrates the Sim2Real transferability of the approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the VTLA model:

- **Superior Performance:** The VTLA model outperforms traditional imitation learning methods (Diffusion Policy) and existing multimodal baselines (TLA/VLA) in both simulated and real-world peg-in-hole insertion tasks.
- **High Success Rates:** The VTLA model achieves over 90% success rates on unseen peg shapes in simulation.
- **Effective Sim2Real Transfer:** The VTLA model demonstrates promising Sim2Real transfer performance, achieving high success rates in real-world insertion experiments despite being trained only on simulated data.
- **Impact of VGTE:** The integration of visual and tactile information within the VTLA framework leads to improved performance compared to models that rely solely on vision (VLA) or tactile (TLA) sensing.
- **Benefits of Preference Learning:** Preference learning using DPO significantly improves model performance, particularly in terms of generalization to unseen data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has significant implications for the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Enabling Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The VTLA model provides a foundation for developing robots that can perform a wider range of manipulation tasks requiring precise contact and force control. This could have applications in manufacturing, assembly, healthcare, and other industries.
- **Improving Generalization and Adaptability:** The VTLA model's ability to generalize to different object shapes and task configurations makes it a more versatile and adaptable robotic system. This reduces the need for task-specific training and allows robots to be deployed in more dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** The VTLA model's successful Sim2Real transfer demonstrates the potential for training robots in simulation and then deploying them in the real world. This reduces the cost and complexity of robot development and deployment.
- **Advancing Multimodal AI in Robotics:** This research contributes to the growing body of work that integrates multiple modalities of information (vision, tactile, language) to create more intelligent and robust robotic systems.
- **Inspiring Future Research:** The authors identify areas for further research, such as improving tactile-language alignment and exploring deeper integration of visual and tactile modalities in LLMs. This could lead to further advancements in the field of robotic manipulation.
