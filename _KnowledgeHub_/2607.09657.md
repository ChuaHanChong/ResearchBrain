# Scalable Visual Pretraining for Language Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Visual Pretraining (VP) trains a foundation model directly on rendered scientific PDF pages instead of parsed text. Pages are encoded by a frozen ViT, filtered to foreground patches, and the shared autoregressive backbone predicts the next visual latent with an InfoNCE loss, mixed with text cross-entropy. Under matched corpora VP beats text pretraining on scientific reasoning using roughly 25% of the tokens.

## Problem

- Pretraining corpora reduce visually rich documents to plain text via HTML/LaTeX parsing or neural PDF parsers, discarding figures, equation layout and page-level spatial structure.
- Multimodal models do ingest images, but only as conditioning context for text-token prediction, so visual content never enters the predictive objective itself.
- OCR-free document models like Donut and Pix2Struct read page images directly, yet their objectives target parsing and image-to-text generation, not general language intelligence.

## Method

- Sparse document representation: a frozen ==ViT== plus spatial merger encodes each rendered page; a variance and luminance ==foreground mask== drops blank patches, and retained latents are raster-ordered with preserved position indices.
- ==Next visual latent prediction==: the shared autoregressive LLM, under causal image-only masking, maps hidden states through a two-layer MLP head to predict the next foreground latent via ==InfoNCE== with in-batch negatives.
- Joint objective combining text cross-entropy and the visual loss interleaves one visual batch per text step (**VP** weight 0.1, temperature 0.07); only LLM, input projector and head train, vision tower frozen.

## Results

- VP beats matched text pretraining across four backbones: GPQA Diamond improves up to **3.22** points (**76.24** to **79.29** on Qwen3.5) and MMLU-Pro up to **2.1** points.
- VP consumes about **20B** visual tokens versus **80B** parsed text tokens, reaching **2.88x** normalized gain on AIME-25, **2.02x** on GPQA, **1.27x** on MMLU-Pro.
- Without paired supervision, alignment improves (centroid separation **1.665** to **0.661**, cosine **0.631** to **0.907**, retrieval R@1 **64** to **99**) and ChartQAPro gains **5.4** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Textualization loss is measurable: gains concentrate on visually dense pages and vanish on text-dominant ones, so document representation matters more than raw token count.
> - Predicting continuous latents in a frozen feature space suffices; adding a pixel-reconstruction decoder costs 1.4x training time for no consistent reasoning gain.
> - Unlabeled visual-document pretraining buys cross-modal alignment for free, but evidence covers only knowledge-dense scientific PDFs, not natural images or video.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large foundation models has been significantly advanced by pretraining on extensive text corpora. This text-centric paradigm implicitly assumes that all relevant knowledge can be represented effectively as a linear sequence of text tokens. However, many forms of human knowledge and communication, particularly in scientific and technical domains, rely on visual representations such as figures, typeset equations, tables, and page layouts. These visual cues encode rich information, including geometric constraints, symbolic topologies, and structural correspondences, which are not fully or faithfully captured by text alone.

Current pretraining approaches for language intelligence often discard this visual information by converting visually rich sources, such as documents and web pages, into plain text using methods like HTML/LaTeX parsing or neural document-parsing models (e.g., OCR systems) as a preprocessing step. The resulting text is then used exclusively for language model training. While recent multimodal foundation models incorporate visual modalities, they frequently treat visual inputs as conditioning context for text-token prediction, limiting the deep integration of visual content into the core predictive process. In these scenarios, the raw visual documents are consumed and then typically discarded, leaving the visual modality external to the model's primary learning objective.

This paper positions itself as a challenge to the prevailing assumption that language models must be trained solely on text-only representations. It investigates whether foundation models can directly learn from visual corpora without relying on text extraction or explicit image-text pairing supervision. The work proposes that "Visual Pretraining" (VP) can serve as a scalable learning paradigm for enhancing foundation model intelligence, particularly for content-rich documents where visual structure conveys critical information. It aligns with the "Platonic Representation Hypothesis," which suggests that representations learned across modalities converge towards shared abstract structures of reality, implying that richer underlying structures could be learned directly from raw visual documents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding this research are detailed below:

- **Challenge the Text-Only Pretraining Assumption:** The fundamental motivation is to question the implicit assumption that all knowledge relevant for language intelligence can be losslessly encoded and learned from linear sequences of text tokens. The authors argue that this assumption leads to significant information loss, especially when processing visually rich documents like scientific papers.
- **Mitigate Information Loss from Textualization:** Scientific documents communicate complex concepts through figures, tables, mathematical formulas, and spatial layouts. Converting these into plain text often weakens or distorts this critical information. A key objective is to demonstrate that visual pretraining can recover this otherwise discarded information, leading to stronger reasoning capabilities.
- **Develop a Scalable Visual Pretraining Framework:** The paper aims to introduce and systematically study an unsupervised visual pretraining paradigm, termed Visual Pretraining (VP). This framework is designed to directly leverage visual documents (rendered pages) without requiring explicit text extraction or image-text pairing supervision. The goal is for the foundation model to learn visual information deeply by training it to predict document patches in a latent space, thereby integrating visual latents into the predictive process.
- **Demonstrate Effectiveness, Efficiency, and Scalability:** The research seeks to empirically establish VP as an effective, efficient, and scalable alternative or complement to text-only pretraining. This involves conducting a unified empirical study using carefully matched data sources across multiple language model architectures and evaluating performance on various scientific reasoning and multimodal benchmarks. Specifically, the objectives include demonstrating that VP:
 - Consistently outperforms text-only pretraining on the same underlying corpora.
 - Achieves these gains with greater efficiency, consuming less computational budget (e.g., fewer tokens) than text-only methods.
 - Leads to emergent cross-modal alignment and improved multimodal reasoning capabilities even without explicit multimodal supervision.
- **Focus on Scientific Documents as a Prototypical Use Case:** The authors are particularly motivated by scientific documents, where figures, equations, and layouts are integral to conveying meaning and facilitating reasoning. These documents serve as a compelling domain to investigate the benefits of preserving the native visual form during pretraining.

In summary, the motivation stems from the recognition of inherent information loss in current text-only pretraining approaches for visually rich documents and the ambition to develop and validate a novel visual pretraining paradigm that can capture and leverage this previously discarded visual knowledge to enhance foundation model intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on **Visual Pretraining (VP)**, a framework designed to enable foundation models to learn directly from the visual content of documents. This approach integrates visual learning into existing text continued pretraining paradigms.

**A. Overall Framework**
VP extends standard text continued pretraining by introducing a "next visual latent prediction" objective. It operates on raw scientific document pages rendered as images, which are processed into sparse visual sequences for the language model.

**B. Sparse Document Representation**
1. **Frozen Visual Feature Extraction:** A frozen vision tower (composed of a ViT-style encoder and a spatial merger) processes a rendered document page. This tower extracts a sequence of visual features (latents). By keeping the vision tower frozen, the visual stream predicts stable document features rather than raw pixels.
2. **Foreground-Token Filtering:** To enhance efficiency and focus learning on relevant content, the method identifies and removes blank regions (margins, whitespace) from the document page. A patch-level foreground mask is computed based on pixel variance and average luminance. Only non-blank patches are retained.
3. **Raster-Scan Ordering:** The retained foreground features are ordered sequentially in a raster-scan manner, forming a sparse foreground sequence. This preserves the page-level spatial order while significantly shortening the visual context.
4. **Projection into LLM Space:** Each foreground visual feature from the sparse sequence is projected into the language model's (LLM) hidden space using a learned linear map. Position indices are reassigned according to the raster order, enabling the LLM to process the page as an ordered visual sequence.

**C. Next Visual Latent Prediction Objective**
1. **Causal Visual Prediction:** The projected visual sequence is fed into the shared autoregressive LLM backbone. A causal attention mask is applied over visual positions, preventing the model from attending to future tokens.
2. **Prediction Head:** The hidden state from the LLM at each position is passed through a lightweight MLP prediction head. This head maps the LLM's hidden state back to the frozen visual-latent space to predict the *next* foreground visual latent.
3. **Contrastive Loss:** The prediction is trained to match the actual next foreground visual latent using a contrastive loss (InfoNCE). Other visual features within the same batch serve as negative examples. This objective encourages the model to predict the correct subsequent document feature and distinguish it from other patches. This is a continuous analogue of language model next-token prediction.

**D. Joint Text and Visual Pretraining**
The final training objective combines the standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss for text tokens ($\mathcal{L}_{CE}$) with the next visual latent prediction loss ($\mathcal{L}_{VP}$). Text and visual examples are interleaved during training with a fixed mixing ratio. During this joint optimization, the LLM parameters, the visual input projection, and the visual prediction head are updated, while the vision tower remains frozen.

**E. Training Setup**
- **Continued Pretraining (CPT):** Implemented using the XTuner framework.
- **Matched Corpora:** Both VP and the Text Pretraining (TP) baseline use the same non-PDF text corpus and a matched additional scientific-PDF corpus. The key difference is the representation of the PDF corpus:
 - **TP:** PDF pages are converted into text using MinerU2.5 parsing.
 - **VP:** The *same* PDF pages are rendered as images and filtered into sparse foreground visual sequences.
- **Token Budget:** Due to the more compact visual representation, VP processes approximately 20B visual tokens from the scientific PDF corpus, whereas TP processes approximately 80B parsed text tokens from the *same* corpus. This setup isolates the effect of document representation.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** After CPT, all VP and TP checkpoints undergo identical supervised fine-tuning to adapt them to structured reasoning traces and specific answer formats required by benchmarks.

**F. Evaluation Protocol**
Models are evaluated in a zero-shot setting after SFT.
- **Text-Only Reasoning Benchmarks:** MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond, AIME 2025, and HLE. CoT (Chain-of-Thought) prompting is used with the instruction "think step by step." Metrics include pass@8 for GPQA, average score over 32 runs for AIME-25, and pass@1 for MMLU-Pro and HLE.
- **Multimodal Benchmarks:** MMMU-Pro, SFE, ChartQAPro, and MathVista. A direct-answer template is used without explicit reasoning guidance, reporting pass@1.
- **Cross-Modal Alignment Analysis:** Quantitative evaluation on 100 held-out scientific document image–text pairs, measuring:
 - *Global alignment:* Centroid Separation and Pairwise Cosine Similarity.
 - *Structural alignment:* Linear Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA).
 - *Local alignment:* Mutual k-Nearest Neighbor overlap (for k=1, 5, 10).
- **Qualitative Study:** Analysis of attention patterns in the LLM for both text and visual inputs on a math problem to understand how VP exposes reasoning-relevant visual evidence.

**G. Exploratory Study (Appendix C)**
An alternative generative-decoder variant, which includes an explicit generative decoder for pixel-level reconstruction supervision (MAR-style autoregressive decoder followed by a frozen VAE decoder), was explored. This variant incurred higher training costs (additional parameters, computation, and longer training time) but achieved comparable performance to the decoder-free VP formulation for downstream reasoning tasks. The decoder-free approach was therefore adopted for the main results due to its efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments establish three primary findings regarding Visual Pretraining (VP) compared to Text Pretraining (TP) on scientific reasoning tasks under matched corpora:

**A. Effectiveness: VP Improves Scientific Language Reasoning**
- **Consistent Outperformance:** VP consistently improved performance over the matched TP baseline across various scientific reasoning benchmarks. This gain was observed in both native multimodal models (Qwen 3.5, Llama 3.2 Vision) and language-only models (Qwen 3, Llama 3.1).
- **Specific Gains:** GPQA Diamond scores improved by up to 3.22 points (e.g., 76.24 to 79.29 on Qwen 3.5), and MMLU-Pro by up to 2.1 points. These improvements suggest that VP effectively internalizes knowledge from the visual content of scientific documents, especially information carried by geometric figures, physics schematics, and other visual elements that text extraction may not faithfully preserve.
- **Differential Impact:** HLE (Humanity's Last Exam) showed only marginal improvements (up to 0.97 points), indicating that its difficulty primarily derives from complex multi-step reasoning rather than knowledge gained from specific visual information.

**B. Scalability: Gains Scale Efficiently with VP**
- **Training Efficiency:** VP demonstrated superior scaling efficiency. When pretraining on the same scientific PDF corpus, VP used approximately 20 billion visual tokens, while TP processed roughly 80 billion text tokens. Despite using a smaller token budget, VP achieved steadily increasing normalized gains as training progressed, reaching 1.27× on MMLU-Pro, 2.02× on GPQA, and 2.88× on AIME-25, relative to the TP baseline's improvement over the base model.
- **Loss Dynamics:** During continued pretraining (CPT), VP and TP showed similar training loss curves, with VP reaching a slightly lower final loss. However, after supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with identical data, the VP checkpoint converged faster and reached a lower final SFT loss, suggesting better representation quality for downstream tasks.
- **Visual Grounding:** The gains were found to be visually grounded. When evaluation sets were categorized by visual-structure density, VP's advantage over TP increased with density. On low-density, text-dominant pages, performance was comparable, but on high-density pages (rich in figures, equations, tables), VP yielded substantially larger improvements.
- **Correlation with Visual Prediction Quality:** Better next-token visual-feature prediction, as measured by visual feature cosine similarity, correlated strongly with larger downstream gains, indicating that enhanced visual space modeling supports stronger reasoning by allowing richer knowledge extraction from visual content.

**C. Efficiency: Compact Visual Representations Preserve VP Gains**
- **Data Compression:** Visual representations effectively compressed the scientific documents. VP trained on approximately 20 billion visual tokens, compared to 80 billion parsed text tokens for TP from the same corpus.
- **Optimal Visual Token Budget:** Experiments varying the visual token budget showed that an 8,192-token setting (1×) offered the best trade-off in terms of downstream performance, under a fixed training context.
- **Resolution Robustness:** Lowering the maximum rendering resolution preserved strong performance, with VP still outperforming TP, further amplifying the efficiency of VP under compressed visual input.

**D. Cross-Modality: Multimodal Capabilities Emerge Without Paired Supervision**
- **Improved Cross-Modal Alignment:** VP, trained on unlabeled raw document pages without task-specific multimodal annotations, significantly improved cross-modal representation and alignment.
 - **Global:** Centroid separation between visual and text embeddings dropped from 1.665 to 0.661 (a 60% reduction), and paired cosine similarity rose from 0.631 to 0.907.
 - **Structural:** Linear CKA improved from 0.657 to 0.745.
 - **Local:** Mutual k-NN overlap improved at all evaluated k values (e.g., k=1 increased from 0.140 to 0.310). These metrics collectively indicated that VP fostered a shared representation space where visual and textual document embeddings became more compatible.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Benchmark Performance:** VP consistently outperformed both the base models and text-pretrained models on multimodal benchmarks (MMMU-Pro, SFE, ChartQAPro, MathVista) across different backbones. TP, by contrast, yielded negligible or even negative gains on these benchmarks. VP's advantage was most pronounced on visually heavy benchmarks (e.g., +5.4 on ChartQAPro, +4.8 on MathVista for Llama 3.2 Vision), reinforcing that its benefit stems from modeling visual content.
- **Reasoning Cues in Attention:** A qualitative study of attention patterns demonstrated that visual-token reasoning in VP-trained models attended to semantically relevant regions of a rendered math problem (e.g., question constraints, intermediate computation steps) in a manner parallel to how text-based reasoning models attended to corresponding textual evidence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for Visual Pretraining (VP) that offers several implications for the development and training of large foundation models.

**A. New Pathway for Foundation Model Training:**
The work introduces a novel route for foundation model training, particularly for domains characterized by high knowledge-density and visually native corpora. This includes scientific documents, charts, tables, mathematical formulas, and structured layouts. For such data, the visual-latent prediction objective serves as an effective complementary pretraining signal, allowing models to learn beyond the limitations of purely textual representations.

**B. Addressing Information Loss:**
The findings confirm that the conventional practice of reducing visually rich documents to plain text for language model pretraining incurs substantial information loss. VP demonstrates that directly learning from the native visual form of these documents can recover this discarded information, leading to enhanced scientific reasoning capabilities. This highlights the importance of multimodal inputs in capturing the full context and meaning embedded in complex documents.

**C. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:**
VP demonstrates efficiency by utilizing significantly fewer visual tokens (approximately 20 billion) compared to the text tokens (approximately 80 billion) derived from the same underlying document corpus, while achieving superior or comparable performance gains. This suggests a more efficient pathway to acquire both language and visual intelligence, potentially reducing the computational resources required for pretraining on visually rich datasets. The ability to maintain strong performance with compact visual representations and lower resolutions further amplifies its efficiency.

**D. Emergence of Multimodal Capabilities Without Paired Supervision:**
A notable impact is the demonstration that VP can foster improved cross-modal alignment and enhance multimodal reasoning capabilities without requiring explicit image-text pair supervision during pretraining. This suggests that the self-supervised learning from raw visual document pages, particularly their inherent structure and layout, is sufficient to guide the model towards a more compatible and integrated multimodal representation space. This could simplify the data annotation process for multimodal model development.

**E. Future Directions in Foundation Model Design:**
This work points towards a potential paradigm shift where foundation models could be primarily trained on large-scale visual streams, with only lightweight text alignment, to achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding current text-pretrained models. This could open avenues for exploring truly multimodal native architectures from early pretraining stages. Future research could investigate optimized loss scheduling, foreground-aware token selection, and parameter-efficient visual modules to further refine this approach. The generalizability of this strategy to broader visual corpora beyond scientific PDFs (e.g., natural images or video) remains an open question for future exploration.
