# Stubborn: A Streamlined and Unified Reinforcement Learning Framework for Robust Motion Tracking and Fall Recovery for Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Southern University of Science and Technology introduced "Stubborn," a unified reinforcement learning framework for humanoid robots that integrates robust motion tracking with autonomous fall recovery into a single policy. This framework achieved competitive motion tracking accuracy, demonstrated a 100% recovery success rate from strong perturbations in simulation, and successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods often treat motion tracking and fall recovery as separate problems, leading to complex multi-stage training procedures or specialized policies.
- Standard RL training typically employs "hard termination" of episodes immediately after falls or significant tracking failures, limiting the agent's ability to explore and learn recovery behaviors in unstable states.
- Uniform sampling of training data over long-horizon tasks inefficiently explores difficult motion segments and under-represented unstable states, hindering robust learning.

## Method

- A unified ==asymmetric Actor-Critic== RL architecture was developed, utilizing a drift-invariant yaw-aligned tracking representation to focus on local whole-body coordination and reduce sensitivity to global drift.
- A Bernoulli-based Probabilistic Termination (PT) mechanism replaces hard termination, allowing episodes to continue for an expected physical recovery time in unstable states, fostering emergent fall recovery behaviors without explicit recovery-specific rewards.
- A Probabilistic Termination and Tracking Error-Driven Sampling Strategy (AdpS) adaptively reweights the training data distribution, prioritizing challenging motion segments and unstable states to enhance learning efficiency and performance.

## Results

- Stubborn achieved competitive tracking accuracy on LAFAN1 (lowest MPBPE **48.85 mm**, MPJVE **624.03 rad/s** vs. baselines), and the adaptive sampling strategy (AdpS) further reduced key tracking errors (e.g. MPJPE **120.7**→**119.4**) especially in mid/late training.
- The probabilistic termination mechanism enabled a **100%** recovery success rate from strong external perturbations (**5 m**/s) under both strict and moderate thresholds, significantly outperforming variants without PT (77.5-**85.0%**).
- The framework was successfully deployed on a physical 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust motion tracking, disturbance resilience, and autonomous fall recovery in real-world outdoor environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating motion tracking and fall recovery into a single policy within a unified RL framework simplifies the overall control system and training process for humanoids.
> - Allowing the policy to probabilistically explore unstable and fallen states, rather than immediately terminating episodes, is critical for learning emergent, resilient recovery behaviors.
> - Dynamically adjusting the sampling distribution based on tracking errors and termination events allows the agent to efficiently focus learning on difficult segments, improving performance for complex, dynamic motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen progress in developing controllers for dynamic motion tracking, often leveraging reinforcement learning (RL). Existing works have enabled humanoids to perform complex movements such as dancing and martial arts. A recurring challenge in this area is maintaining precise motion tracking while ensuring robustness to external disturbances and enabling autonomous recovery from falls.

Many current RL approaches typically address motion tracking and fall recovery as distinct problems. This often leads to multi-stage training procedures, the design of specialized recovery rewards, or the implementation of separate policies for each task. Such methodologies can increase architectural and training complexity. Furthermore, a common practice in RL training is to terminate an episode immediately following a significant tracking failure or fall. This "hard termination" mechanism limits the agent's opportunity to explore and learn recovery behaviors in unstable or fallen states, as it prematurely ends the learning trajectory. While some studies have focused on improving robustness by designing specific push-recovery or standing-up policies, these are often decoupled from the primary motion tracking policy. Methods that aim to enhance general motion tracking sometimes struggle to efficiently learn from difficult motion segments or states that are under-represented in uniformly sampled training data.

This paper introduces "Stubborn" to address these limitations by proposing a unified framework. It aims to integrate robust motion tracking and fall recovery within a single policy and training paradigm, rather than treating them as separate or competing objectives. The work seeks to overcome the reliance on hard termination by enabling continued exploration in unstable states and to improve training efficiency by adaptively focusing on challenging motion segments. By proposing a streamlined approach, Stubborn aims to advance the development of more resilient and capable humanoid controllers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a streamlined and unified reinforcement learning framework, named Stubborn, that enables a single policy to achieve both robust motion tracking and autonomous fall recovery for humanoid robots.

The motivation behind Stubborn stems from several identified limitations in existing reinforcement learning approaches for humanoid control:

1. **Segregated Tasks:** Current methods often treat motion tracking and fall recovery as separate tasks. This typically necessitates multi-stage training processes, the creation of specialized recovery rewards, or the development of distinct recovery policies. This compartmentalization increases the overall complexity of the system design and training regimen. The researchers aim to overcome this by synthesizing a single policy capable of managing both objectives simultaneously.

2. **Limited Recovery-Oriented Exploration:** A common practice in RL-based humanoid control is the immediate termination of training episodes when severe tracking failures or falls occur. This "hard termination" strategy prematurely halts the policy's exploration in unstable or fallen states, thereby impeding its ability to learn effective recovery behaviors. The motivation is to enable the policy to continue exploring and learning from these challenging scenarios, fostering emergent recovery capabilities.

3. **Inefficient Learning from Difficult States:** Under long-horizon training, uniformly sampled data often under-represents difficult motion segments and unstable states. This can limit the policy's capacity to thoroughly explore and master these critical areas, which are essential for achieving high-fidelity tracking and robust recovery. The goal is to develop a mechanism that dynamically prioritizes learning from these under-represented and challenging segments.

4. **Desire for a Unified and Streamlined Solution:** The overarching motivation is to provide a more elegant and efficient solution compared to existing fragmented approaches. By integrating the learning of robust tracking and fall recovery into a single, streamlined framework, the authors aim to reduce complexity, improve sample efficiency, and facilitate the development of more versatile and robust humanoid controllers without requiring extensive reward tuning or complex architectural modifications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Stubborn employs an asymmetric Actor-Critic reinforcement learning architecture within a unified framework, integrating three main technical components to achieve its objectives. The policy network relies solely on proprioceptive observations, while the value network utilizes privileged simulation information.

The core components of Stubborn's methodology are:

1. **Drift-Invariant Yaw-Aligned Tracking Representation:** To reduce sensitivity to global drift and heading disturbances while retaining crucial gravity-related balance information, Stubborn adopts a yaw-aligned tracking representation. This approach defines a yaw-aligned frame (Ψ) relative to the world frame (W) based on the robot's base yaw angle. For any rigid body *i*, its pose in Ψ is derived by transforming its world-frame pose using the inverse of the yaw-only rotation and the position of the robot's base. Specifically, the local position *˜p<sub>i</sub>* and orientation *˜R<sub>i</sub>* in the aligned frame are computed as *˜p<sub>i</sub> = R<sup>T</sup><sub>Z</sub>(ψ)(p<sup>W</sup><sub>i</sub> - p<sup>W</sup><sub>B</sub>)* and *˜R<sub>i</sub> = R<sup>T</sup><sub>Z</sub>(ψ)R<sup>W</sup><sub>i</sub>*, where *R<sup>T</sup><sub>Z</sub>(ψ)* is the inverse yaw rotation and *p<sup>W</sup><sub>B</sub>* is the world-frame position of the robot's base. This transformation removes global translation and heading components, ensuring that tracking errors primarily reflect local whole-body coordination.

2. **Bernoulli-based Probabilistic Termination (PT) Mechanism for Fall Recovery Learning:**
 - **Conditional Bernoulli Termination:** Instead of immediate termination when tracking errors exceed predefined thresholds, Stubborn introduces a probabilistic termination mechanism. When the height error (*e<sub>p</sub><sup>root,z</sup>*) or orientation error (*e<sub>q</sub><sup>root</sup>*) of the robot's floating base surpasses their respective thresholds (*θ<sub>pos</sub>*, *θ<sub>quat</sub>*), the episode terminates with a probability *p<sub>term</sub>*, modeled by a conditional Bernoulli distribution. If errors are within thresholds, termination probability is 0.
 - **Design of Termination Probability:** The parameter *p<sub>term</sub>* is designed to allow a physically meaningful time window for fall recovery. The expected number of additional steps (*E[T]*) before termination is set to align with a physical recovery time (*t<sub>rec</sub>*), such that *E[T] = 1/p<sub>term</sub> = f<sub>ctrl</sub> ⋅ t<sub>rec</sub>*. For a control frequency *f<sub>ctrl</sub> = 50 Hz* and a physical recovery time *t<sub>rec</sub> = 4 s*, *p<sub>term</sub>* is set to 0.005. This design provides an expected exploration horizon of 200 steps in unstable regions, allowing the policy to learn resilient actions and emergent recovery behaviors without requiring auxiliary recovery-specific rewards. This approach mitigates temporal difference (TD) distortion caused by standard truncation and promotes stable, long-horizon optimization by ensuring that a significant majority of unstable episodes probabilistically terminate before the maximum episode length.

3. **Probabilistic Termination and Tracking Error-Driven Sampling Strategy (AdpS):**
 - **Addressing Weakened Correspondence:** The PT mechanism makes episode termination a less reliable indicator of segment difficulty, as it is influenced by stochasticity and external perturbations. To compensate, AdpS dynamically reshapes the sampling distribution for training data.
 - **Adaptive Weight Update:** For each episode starting at *t<sub>0</sub>* and lasting *T* steps, an average keypoint tracking error (*ē*) over the segment is calculated. The unnormalized sampling weight (*w<sub>t</sub>*) for reference frame *t* is then updated based on this *ē* and the episode's termination status:
 - If *ē* is above a success threshold (*θ<sub>success</sub>*), indicating a difficult segment, *w<sub>t</sub>* is increased.
 - If *ē* is below *θ<sub>success</sub>* and the episode reaches its maximum length (*T = T<sub>max</sub>*), indicating successful tracking, *w<sub>t</sub>* is decreased using a local decay operator (Φ<sub>att</sub>).
 - If *ē* is below *θ<sub>success</sub>* but the episode terminates probabilistically before *T<sub>max</sub>* (*T < T<sub>max</sub>*), a local redistribution operator (Φ<sub>dist</sub>) is applied. This operator prioritizes increasing weights for later parts of the segment where a fall might have been successfully avoided or recovered from.
 - **Normalization:** After weight updates, the weights are normalized to obtain the sampling distribution *p<sub>t</sub>*, which governs the selection of the starting frame for subsequent episodes. This ensures that training focuses more frequently on challenging motion segments and unstable states, improving learning efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Stubborn was conducted through both simulation experiments in IsaacLab/MuJoCo and real-world demonstrations on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot, using motions from the LAFAN1 and AMASS datasets.

**Q1: Comparison with Baseline Methods on Motion Tracking**
Stubborn's motion tracking accuracy was quantitatively compared against HoloMotion, Any2Track, BFM-Zero, and a From-scratch multi-motion RL baseline on the full LAFAN1 dataset. The results, reported as mean ± standard deviation, indicate Stubborn achieved competitive performance:
- **Mean Per-Body Position Error (MPBPE):** Stubborn recorded 48.85 ± 0.000 mm, the lowest among all methods, significantly outperforming From-scratch RL (62.68 ± 0.000 mm).
- **Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE):** Stubborn achieved 113.38 ± 0.000 rad, which was the lowest value, slightly better than From-scratch RL (115.43 ± 0.000 rad).
- **Mean Per-Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE):** Stubborn showed 624.03 ± 0.000 rad/s, a notable reduction compared to From-scratch RL (887.87 ± 0.000 rad/s), indicating improved joint velocity tracking.
- **Acceleration Deviation (∆acc):** Stubborn's ∆acc was 17.09 ± 0.000 rad/s², comparable to BFM-Zero (17.07 ± 0.000 rad/s²) and lower than HoloMotion, Any2Track, and From-scratch RL.

These results indicate that Stubborn achieves lower overall tracking errors across multiple metrics while maintaining stable and smoother dynamic motion performance compared to the evaluated baselines.

**Q2: Impact of the Probabilistic Termination (PT) Mechanism on Recovery**
An ablation study investigated the effect of the PT mechanism on disturbance and fall recovery under a strong external perturbation of 5 m/s. Stubborn (with PT) was compared to a variant without PT (w/o PT). Recovery success was defined as re-establishing stable tracking within predefined error thresholds (0.15 m for strict, 0.25 m for moderate).
- **Success Rate:** Under both the strict (0.15 m) and moderate (0.25 m) recovery thresholds, Stubborn with PT achieved a 100% recovery success rate. In contrast, the w/o PT variant achieved 77.5% and 85.0% success rates, respectively.
- **Recovery Steps:** During training, Stubborn with PT converged faster and exhibited more stable performance with fewer recovery steps, particularly under the stricter 0.15 m threshold, compared to w/o PT, which showed larger fluctuations.
The findings suggest that the PT mechanism substantially improves the policy's ability to recover from strong perturbations and falls.

**Q3: Impact of the Probabilistic Termination and Tracking Error-Driven Sampling Strategy (AdpS)**
Another ablation study assessed the contribution of the AdpS strategy to tracking performance by comparing Stubborn with a variant without AdpS (w/o AdpS).
- **Tracking Error Reduction:** The introduction of AdpS resulted in slight reductions across key tracking error metrics:
 - MPJPE decreased from 120.7 to 119.4.
 - MPJVE decreased from 548.5 to 541.5.
 - ∆acc decreased from 14.50 to 14.42.
- **Error Trends during Training:** Analysis of error trends over policy steps showed that AdpS maintained lower joint position and velocity errors in the middle and late stages of training. The acceleration error remained similar to that of the w/o AdpS variant.
These results indicate that AdpS contributes to improved overall tracking performance, particularly for long-horizon, highly dynamic motions, by adaptively increasing training frequency for difficult segments.

**Q4: Real-World Deployment**
Stubborn was successfully deployed on the physical 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot. Real-robot experiments demonstrated the policy's capability in motion tracking, robustness to disturbances, and fall recovery across diverse dynamic motions, including flips and acrobatic maneuvers, using retargeted motions from LAFAN1 and AMASS datasets. This confirms the practical applicability and sim-to-real transferability of the learned policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Stubborn presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid control by offering a unified and streamlined reinforcement learning framework for robust motion tracking and fall recovery. The proposed methodology addresses several long-standing challenges in training humanoid policies, potentially leading to more capable and reliable robotic systems.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Unified Framework:** By integrating motion tracking and fall recovery into a single policy trained within a unified framework, Stubborn reduces the architectural and training complexity associated with multi-stage training procedures, specialized rewards, or separate controllers. This simplification can accelerate the development and deployment of humanoid robots.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Fall Recovery:** The Bernoulli-based probabilistic termination (PT) mechanism is a core innovation. By allowing the policy to explore unstable and fallen states rather than immediately terminating episodes, it enables the learning of emergent recovery behaviors without explicit recovery-specific reward engineering. This directly addresses the limitation of hard termination, fostering more resilient and autonomous robots capable of handling strong external perturbations and recovering from falls. The experimental results quantitatively demonstrate a substantial improvement in recovery success rates compared to conventional termination strategies.
- **Improved Training Efficiency:** The probabilistic termination and tracking-error-driven sampling strategy (AdpS) enhances learning efficiency by dynamically focusing training on challenging motion segments and unstable states. This adaptive sampling mechanism ensures that the policy allocates more learning resources to areas where it performs poorly, leading to better overall tracking performance for complex, long-horizon dynamic motions.
- **Practical Applicability:** The successful real-world deployment on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot validates the framework's effectiveness and its potential for practical application. Demonstrating robust motion tracking, disturbance resilience, and fall recovery in a physical environment suggests that the learned policies can transfer from simulation to reality, which is often a bottleneck in robotics research.

**Potential Impact:**

The advancements introduced by Stubborn could have a notable impact on various aspects of humanoid robotics:

- **Development of More Autonomous Humanoids:** By allowing humanoids to autonomously recover from unexpected disturbances and falls, Stubborn contributes to the development of robots that are more robust and less reliant on human intervention in dynamic and uncertain environments.
- **Broader Application Domains:** Enhanced robustness and reliable fall recovery capabilities could expand the practical application domains for humanoid robots, enabling them to operate safely and effectively in complex scenarios such as disaster response, logistics, assistance in human environments, or even dynamic entertainment.
- **Streamlined Research and Development:** The unified and streamlined training approach could simplify the research and development pipeline for future humanoid control policies, allowing researchers to focus more on advanced behaviors rather than on managing disparate control modules for different tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The framework provides a robust foundation for further research into advanced humanoid behaviors, including more agile and smooth recovery motions, adaptation to diverse and challenging terrains, and the synthesis of increasingly dynamic and complex skills.

Future work outlined by the authors, focusing on improving the agility and smoothness of recovery motions and extending the framework to more challenging terrains and dynamic behaviors, indicates a clear path for building upon this significant contribution.
