# Stubborn: A Streamlined and Unified Reinforcement Learning Framework for Robust Motion Tracking and Fall Recovery for Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Southern University of Science and Technology introduced "Stubborn," a unified reinforcement learning framework for humanoid robots that integrates robust motion tracking with autonomous fall recovery into a single policy. This framework achieved competitive motion tracking accuracy, demonstrated a 100% recovery success rate from strong perturbations in simulation, and successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods often treat motion tracking and fall recovery as separate problems, leading to complex multi-stage training procedures or specialized policies.
- Standard RL training typically employs "hard termination" of episodes immediately after falls or significant tracking failures, limiting the agent's ability to explore and learn recovery behaviors in unstable states.
- Uniform sampling of training data over long-horizon tasks inefficiently explores difficult motion segments and under-represented unstable states, hindering robust learning.

## Method

- A unified ==asymmetric Actor-Critic== RL architecture was developed, utilizing a drift-invariant yaw-aligned tracking representation to focus on local whole-body coordination and reduce sensitivity to global drift.
- A Bernoulli-based Probabilistic Termination (PT) mechanism replaces hard termination, allowing episodes to continue for an expected physical recovery time in unstable states, fostering emergent fall recovery behaviors without explicit recovery-specific rewards.
- A Probabilistic Termination and Tracking Error-Driven Sampling Strategy (AdpS) adaptively reweights the training data distribution, prioritizing challenging motion segments and unstable states to enhance learning efficiency and performance.

## Results

- Stubborn achieved competitive motion tracking accuracy on the LAFAN1 dataset, recording the lowest Mean Per-Body Position Error (**48.85 mm**) and Mean Per-Joint Velocity Error (**624.03 rad**/s) compared to baseline methods.
- The probabilistic termination mechanism enabled a **100%** recovery success rate from strong external perturbations (**5 m**/s) under both strict and moderate thresholds, significantly outperforming variants without PT (77.5-**85.0%**).
- The adaptive sampling strategy (AdpS) slightly reduced key tracking errors (e.g., MPJPE decreased from 120.7 to 119.4) and maintained lower joint position and velocity errors during the middle and late stages of training.
- The framework was successfully deployed on a physical 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust motion tracking, disturbance resilience, and autonomous fall recovery in real-world outdoor environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating motion tracking and fall recovery into a single policy within a unified RL framework simplifies the overall control system and training process for humanoids.
> - Allowing the policy to probabilistically explore unstable and fallen states, rather than immediately terminating episodes, is critical for learning emergent, resilient recovery behaviors.
> - Dynamically adjusting the sampling distribution based on tracking errors and termination events allows the agent to efficiently focus learning on difficult segments, improving performance for complex, dynamic motions.
