# RobustVLA: Robustness-Aware Reinforcement Post-Training for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RobustVLA introduces an online reinforcement learning post-training method designed to enhance the resilience of pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models against environmental perturbations. This approach achieved an 82.1% success rate under joint observation and action perturbations on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing existing state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limited generalization in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenarios due to environmental disturbances like observation noise and action perturbations.
- Existing online reinforcement learning (RL) post-training methods primarily focus on task performance, lacking explicit mechanisms to guarantee robustness against these real-world uncertainties.
- Unaddressed environmental perturbations can amplify over time, significantly degrading VLA model performance and reliability in real-world robotic deployment.

## Method

- **RobustVLA** integrates two theoretically derived regularization terms, ==Jacobian regularization== and ==smoothness regularization==, into the ==Proximal Policy Optimization== (**PPO**) objective for online fine-tuning.
- Jacobian regularization penalizes the model's sensitivity to observation noise, while smoothness regularization controls the magnitude of policy updates to mitigate action perturbation effects.
- An adaptive noise scheduling mechanism based on ==curriculum learning== dynamically adjusts the levels of injected observation and action noise during rollouts to progressively build robustness.

## Results

- RobustVLA achieved an **82.5%** average success rate under various observation perturbations and a **54.8%** success rate under action perturbations on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming all baselines.
- Under challenging joint perturbations (image rotation and action noise), RobustVLA-C obtained the highest success rate of **82.1%**, demonstrating superior robust and stable decision-making in complex OOD scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Jacobian and smoothness regularization terms are essential and complementary, contributing to more robust latent representations and stable action distributions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Performance deviation under observation perturbations is primarily governed by the VLA model's Jacobian sensitivity, indicating that controlling this sensitivity is key for visual robustness.
> - Return degradation under action perturbations is driven by cumulative policy drift from online updates, highlighting the importance of smooth and stable policy evolution.
> - A combined strategy of Jacobian regularization, smoothness regularization, and curriculum-based noise exposure is crucial for simultaneously optimizing task performance and robustness in dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has recently seen a transformative shift with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Inspired by the success of large language and vision models, VLA models like RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0 have demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in learning general-purpose robotic policies from large-scale, multi-modal datasets. These models integrate visual observations, natural language instructions, and robot actions, enabling robots to perform a diverse range of manipulation tasks.

However, a significant challenge for VLA models, particularly in real-world deployment, is their limited generalization ability in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenarios. OOD conditions encompass various environmental disturbances, such as observation noise (e.g., sensor errors, lighting changes, occlusions) and action perturbations (e.g., actuation errors, hardware disturbances). Existing research has highlighted that VLA models can often rely on spurious correlations, making them brittle when faced with these unavoidable uncertainties.

Traditional solutions like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) are often data-intensive, requiring costly human demonstrations that are impractical to scale for diverse OOD tasks. In contrast, online Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training offers a more appealing alternative, allowing autonomous data collection and policy refinement directly in the deployment environment. While recent online RL methods (e.g., RIPT-VLA, VLA-RL) have shown promise in adapting pre-trained VLA models, they predominantly focus on maximizing task-specific rewards in nominal environments. A critical gap in these approaches is the *explicit lack of mechanisms to guarantee robustness* against environmental perturbations. Such reward-centric optimization can lead to policies that are over-adapted to specific training dynamics and thus highly sensitive to even minor environmental variations.

The broader field of Robust RL has explored various techniques, including robust Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), adversarial training, and domain randomization, to enhance stability and generalization under uncertainty. However, many of these methods either focus on offline settings, curriculum learning, or address corruption in training data. The challenge of simultaneously handling exploration and robustness in *online* VLA post-training, particularly concerning environmental shifts at test time, remains largely underexplored.

RobustVLA positions itself precisely at this intersection. It addresses the critical need for a principled approach to online RL post-training that not only optimizes for task performance but also explicitly constrains the VLA model's sensitivity to environmental perturbations. By doing so, it aims to bridge the gap between high-performance VLA models and their reliable, robust deployment in dynamic and uncertain real-world settings, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in generalist robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and evaluate **RobustVLA**, a lightweight online Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training method specifically designed to **explicitly enhance the resilience and robustness of pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models against environmental perturbations**.

The motivations driving this work stem from several critical observations and unmet needs in the field of robotic manipulation:

1. **Limited Generalization of VLA Models in OOD Scenarios**: While VLA models have achieved remarkable success in learning general-purpose robotic policies from large-scale multi-modal datasets, they frequently struggle with generalization when deployed in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) environments. These OOD scenarios inevitably involve disturbances such as observation noise (e.g., sensor errors, visual occlusions, lighting variations) and action perturbations (e.g., actuation errors, motor inaccuracies). The authors highlight that VLA models may rely on spurious correlations, making them susceptible to failure under these unseen conditions.
2. **Shortcomings of Existing Post-Training Methods**:
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: While a natural approach, SFT relies heavily on costly, high-quality human demonstrations, which are often scarce or prohibitive to acquire for diverse tasks. Its effectiveness diminishes sharply with limited data.
 - **Online Reinforcement Learning (RL) Fine-Tuning**: Although online RL enables autonomous data collection and policy refinement, existing methods primarily focus on maximizing task-specific reward signals in nominal environments. They *do not inherently incorporate mechanisms to guarantee robustness* against environmental perturbations. This leads to models that may be over-adapted to specific training dynamics and become brittle when faced with even slight real-world disturbances.
3. **Ubiquity and Amplification of Environmental Perturbations**: Environmental perturbations are inherent in real-world robotic systems. Observation perturbations lead to mismatches between perceived and actual states, while action perturbations cause deviations between intended and executed actions. If left unaddressed, these perturbations can amplify over time, significantly degrading model performance and reliability.
4. **Need for Principled Robustness-Oriented Post-Training**: There is a clear need for a principled approach that not only optimizes for task performance but also explicitly constrains the sensitivity of the VLA model's decision process to perturbations. Such a robustness-oriented post-training strategy is crucial for ensuring the reliable and stable deployment of VLA models in dynamic, uncertain real-world environments.

By addressing these motivations, RobustVLA aims to ensure that VLA models are not only capable of performing tasks but can do so reliably and consistently even in the presence of real-world environmental uncertainties.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of RobustVLA is built upon a principled theoretical analysis of robustness, which then informs the design of specific regularization terms integrated into an online RL post-training framework.

#### 3.1. Robustness Analysis to Environmental Perturbations

The core of RobustVLA's methodology lies in a systematic robustness analysis that quantifies how observation and action perturbations affect VLA model performance. This analysis leads to three key theoretical findings:

1. **Error Amplification Bound (Theorem 1)**: This theorem analyzes the performance deviation (robustness gap) under observation perturbations. It establishes that the gap is primarily governed by the product of the VLA model's Jacobian sensitivity (λ) to input changes and the level of observation noise (ϵs). A high λ means the model's output actions change drastically even for small input observation changes, leading to error amplification. This theoretical insight directly motivates the need for **Jacobian regularization** to reduce λ.
2. **Return Drift Control (Theorem 2)**: This theorem addresses the return gap under action perturbations, modeled as Gaussian noise. It reveals that the gap is driven by two main factors: the cumulative VLA model drift (Σδi) from online updates and an irreducible stochastic execution noise term (σ√d). The term Σδi indicates that overly aggressive policy updates can destabilize rollouts. This analysis underscores the importance of **smoothness regularization** to limit δi and decouple learning progress from robustness degradation.
3. **Robust Stability Guarantee (Theorem 3)**: This theorem combines both observation and action perturbations, demonstrating that the return gap depends jointly on the observation-sensitivity term (λϵs) and the accumulated update drift (Σδi). The interactions between these error sources imply that controlling only one type of perturbation is insufficient; a dual regularization strategy is necessary to prevent compounding error growth over long horizons.

These theoretical insights are further supported by corollaries (detailed in Appendix A.2) that show how these bounds specialize under contractive dynamics and discounted returns, providing more concrete conditions for robustness guarantees.

#### 3.2. Model Online Regularization Optimization Objective

Based on the theoretical analysis, RobustVLA introduces two complementary regularization terms to the standard PPO objective:

1. **Jacobian Regularization ($R_{Jac}$)**: This term addresses sensitivity to observation noise (derived from Theorem 1). It is implemented by penalizing the squared L2-norm of the gradient of the log-action probability with respect to the observation input:
 $R_{Jac}(\theta) = E_{(s,a) \sim D} [min (\| \nabla_s \log \pi_\theta (a|s) \|_2^2, G_{max})]$
 The use of log-action probability's gradient provides a stronger control over the model's sensitivity. For computational efficiency, this gradient is computed on low-dimensional embeddings of the observations, rather than raw image pixels. A clamping mechanism ($G_{max}$) prevents excessively large penalties.

2. **Smoothness Regularization ($R_{Smooth}$)**: This term aims to stabilize online updates and reduce sensitivity to action perturbations (derived from Theorem 2). It penalizes the L2-norm squared difference between the mean actions ($\mu_\theta(s)$) of the current policy and a reference policy ($\mu_{\theta-}(s)$) from the previous update:
 $R_{Smooth}(\theta) = E_{s \sim D} [\| \mu_\theta (s) - \mu_{\theta-} (s) \|_2^2]$
 This explicitly enforces gradual updates in the action means, complementing PPO's KL divergence penalty, which ensures distribution-level stability but may not prevent large shifts in mean actions.

These two regularization terms are combined with the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) objective ($L_{PPO}$) to form the complete robust online RL optimization objective:
$L_{RobustVLA}(\theta) = L_{PPO}(\theta) + \alpha R_{Jac}(\theta) + \beta R_{Smooth}(\theta)$
where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are hyperparameters controlling the strength of each regularization component.

#### 3.3. Robust Post-Training Algorithm Implementation

The RobustVLA algorithm (Algorithm 1) builds upon a standard PPO-like online RL fine-tuning loop:

1. **Initialization**: The algorithm starts with a pre-trained VLA model ($\pi_\theta$), initializes a reference model ($\pi_{\theta-}$), and sets an initial noise level for curriculum learning.
2. **Rollout Collection**: Trajectories are collected by interacting with the environment, which is perturbed with observation and action noise. The level of this injected noise is dynamically adjusted.
3. **Policy Update**: For multiple steps (N), minibatches of collected data are used to update the policy parameters ($\theta$). The update utilizes the $L_{RobustVLA}$ objective, incorporating $L_{PPO}$, $R_{Jac}$, and $R_{Smooth}$.
4. **Reference Model Update**: After a set number of internal updates, the reference model ($\pi_{\theta-}$) is updated to the current policy ($\pi_\theta$) to compute the smoothness penalty for the next iteration.
5. **Curriculum-based Adaptive Noise Scheduling**: A crucial practical component is the adaptive noise curriculum. The level of injected observation and action noise during rollouts is dynamically increased or decreased based on the model's smoothed success rate (pMA). If the success rate is high (above $\tau_{high}$), the noise level is increased; if it's low (below $\tau_{low}$), the noise level is decreased. This gradual exposure to increasing perturbation levels prevents premature destabilization while building robustness.

This integrated approach ensures that the VLA model not only learns to maximize rewards but also develops resilience against realistic environmental uncertainties, directly operationalizing the theoretical insights into a practical training scheme.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RobustVLA rigorously assesses its effectiveness across various perturbed scenarios, transfer learning tasks, and through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmark**: The experiments are conducted on the **LIBERO** (Liu et al., 2023) simulation platform, a long-horizon robotic learning suite comprising Object, Long, Spatial, and Goal task categories.
- **Perturbations**: A comprehensive set of environmental perturbations is introduced:
 - **Observation Perturbations**: Image Shift, Image Rotation, Color Jitter, Image Occlusions, and Image Erasing. These are applied to both first- and third-view inference images.
 - **Action Perturbations**: Zero-mean Gaussian action noise with standard deviations of 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3.
 - **Joint Perturbations**: A challenging combination of observation image rotation and action noise (level 0.1).
- **Evaluation Metric**: Average Success Rate (SR) over 50 held-out test contexts for each task.
- **Base Model**: OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025) is used as the base VLA model, adapted with LoRA checkpoints from RIPT-VLA (Tan et al., 2025a).
- **Baselines**: A wide array of state-of-the-art baselines are compared:
 - **Offline Imitation Learning (IL)**: π0, GEVRM, OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT.
 - **Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: RWR, ReinboT, ARFM.
 - **Online Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: RIPT-VLA.
- **Proposed Methods**: RobustVLA (without curriculum learning) and RobustVLA-C (with curriculum learning).

#### 4.2. Performance Evaluation Under Environmental Perturbations

1. **Observation Perturbation Settings (Table 1)**:
 - RobustVLA (82.5% SR) and RobustVLA-C (82.2% SR) significantly outperform all baselines.
 - The best performing baseline, RIPT-VLA, achieved 80.8% SR, and OpenVLA-OFT achieved 80.6% SR.
 - Offline IL and Offline RL methods generally performed worse (around 60% SR for Offline RL).
 - **Finding**: This highlights the crucial role of autonomous online interaction and the proposed Jacobian regularization in robustly resisting visual uncertainties.

2. **Action Perturbation Setting (Table 2)**:
 - RobustVLA (54.8% SR) and RobustVLA-C (54.7% SR) again demonstrate superior performance across varying action noise levels (0.1, 0.2, 0.3).
 - OpenVLA-OFT was the best offline method at 53.5% SR, slightly below RobustVLA. RIPT-VLA achieved 50.5% SR.
 - **Finding**: The results confirm that RobustVLA effectively improves the VLA model's resilience to execution-level action perturbations.

3. **Joint Perturbation Settings (Table 3)**:
 - Under the challenging joint perturbation (image rotation + 0.1 action noise), RobustVLA-C (82.1% SR) achieves the best performance, significantly outperforming RobustVLA (78.7%) and RIPT-VLA (78.2%).
 - Online RL algorithms generally outperform both offline IL and offline RL. OpenVLA-OFT was the best offline IL method (69.5%).
 - **Finding**: This confirms that autonomous online RL interaction, combined with the Jacobian and smoothness penalties and curriculum learning, is essential for robust and stable decision-making in complex OOD scenarios.

#### 4.3. Transfer Learning Performance Comparison (Figure 3 & Appendix Figure 6)

- RobustVLA demonstrates superior transfer learning capabilities to target domains with environmental uncertainty (image rotation and 0.15 action noise).
- For tasks like "open drawer" and "put bowl," RobustVLA achieved improvements of 8.0% and 16.0% respectively over direct zero-shot transfer. In contrast, the baseline RIPT-VLA often showed decreasing performance with more rollouts in perturbed scenarios.
- **Finding**: This indicates that RobustVLA can rapidly adapt and exhibit more robust and stable behaviors after only a few environment interactions, even in OOD conditions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Analysis (Figure 4 & 5)

1. **Hyperparameter Ablation (Figure 4a)**:
 - Omitting either the Jacobian weight ($\alpha=0$) or the action-smooth weight ($\beta=0$) leads to a noticeable drop in VLA model performance.
 - **Finding**: This confirms the necessity and complementary roles of both regularization terms. The algorithm also shows good insensitivity to hyperparameter values within reasonable ranges.

2. **Observation Representation Visualization (T-SNE) (Figure 4b-c)**:
 - T-SNE plots of observation representations show that RobustVLA maintains largely consistent clusters for successful task performances despite varying perturbations.
 - In contrast, the baseline RIPT-VLA exhibits overly separated and fragmented clusters for observation representations in some downstream tasks, indicating deviations leading to task failure.
 - **Finding**: RobustVLA's regularization helps to learn more robust and stable latent representations, making it less sensitive to visual input variations.

3. **Action Distribution Visualization (Figure 5a-b & Appendix Figure 7-8)**:
 - Visualization of action trajectories under different noise conditions reveals that observation and action perturbations have distinct impacts on robot manipulation.
 - Crucially, under joint perturbations, RobustVLA's action distribution is significantly closer to the noise-free action distribution compared to RIPT-VLA.
 - **Finding**: This demonstrates RobustVLA's ability to adaptively adjust its behavior to better resist environmental disturbances, leading to more robust and stable executed actions.

In summary, the experimental results consistently demonstrate that RobustVLA, particularly with its curriculum learning variant, significantly surpasses state-of-the-art methods in terms of robustness and reliability under diverse and challenging environmental perturbations, both for direct evaluation and transfer learning. The ablation studies further validate the essential contribution of both the Jacobian and smoothness regularization terms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "ROBUSTVLA" makes several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation and reinforcement learning, with a substantial potential impact:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in VLA Robustness**: RobustVLA tackles the crucial problem of VLA model brittleness in out-of-distribution environments. While VLA models excel in task performance, their lack of inherent robustness to common environmental perturbations (observation noise, action errors) has been a major barrier to real-world deployment. This paper provides a principled solution for this critical limitation.

2. **Theoretically Grounded Methodology**: A key strength of RobustVLA is its foundation in rigorous theoretical analysis. By deriving explicit upper bounds on performance deviation under different perturbation types (Jacobian sensitivity for observation noise, policy smoothness for action noise and update drift), the authors provide a strong mathematical justification for their proposed regularization terms. This moves beyond empirical heuristics, offering a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms governing robustness.

3. **Principled Dual Regularization Strategy**: The introduction of both Jacobian regularization and smoothness regularization as explicit terms in the online RL objective is a novel and effective strategy. These two terms address distinct but interacting sources of fragility, ensuring comprehensive robustness. The ablation studies definitively demonstrate that both components are necessary for optimal performance.

4. **Enhanced Reliability for Real-World Robotics**: Environmental uncertainties are unavoidable in real-world robotic operations. By enabling VLA models to adapt and perform reliably under these conditions, RobustVLA takes a significant step towards making general-purpose robot policies truly dependable. This increased reliability is paramount for the broader adoption of VLA models in industrial, service, and domestic applications.

5. **Superior Performance and Transferability**: The extensive experimental results on the LIBERO benchmark, covering various observation, action, and joint perturbations, conclusively show RobustVLA's superior performance over a wide array of state-of-the-art offline and online RL baselines. Furthermore, its demonstrated ability to transfer robust behaviors to new, perturbed tasks with minimal rollouts highlights its practical utility and efficiency.

6. **Curriculum Learning for Robustness**: The adaptive noise scheduling mechanism, which gradually exposes the model to increasing perturbation levels, is a practical innovation. This curriculum-based approach ensures stable learning while progressively building resilience, a valuable technique for training robust agents in complex environments.

7. **Future Research Directions**: The work also opens doors for future research, such as investigating robustness to other perturbation types (e.g., dynamic shifts, external disturbances). This signals an ongoing commitment to advancing the reliability of VLA models in increasingly complex and unpredictable settings.

In essence, RobustVLA significantly contributes to the vision of generalist robots that are not only skilled but also robust and trustworthy, marking a crucial advance in the journey towards deploying intelligent embodied agents in real-world scenarios.
