# Optimal Single-Policy Sample Complexity and Transient Coverage for Average-Reward Offline RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Wisconsin-Madison developed a model-based pessimistic algorithm for average-reward offline reinforcement learning that yields the first sample complexity guarantees dependent only on the target optimal policy's intrinsic complexity. Their work establishes optimal statistical rates and demonstrates the necessity of transient state coverage for such bounds in weakly communicating Markov Decision Processes.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) in the average-reward setting faced challenges from costly online data, distribution shifts, and non-uniform data coverage.
- Previous theoretical work relied on 'uniform' complexity measures for all policies, leading to loose performance guarantees and restrictive assumptions on Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).
- Existing algorithms often required prior knowledge of unlearnable environmental parameters, hindering practical application.

## Method

- The authors introduced 'Pessimistic Value Iteration With ==Quantile Clipping==,' a model-based algorithm that approximates the average-reward MDP using a discounted reduction with an adaptively chosen discount factor.
- A novel quantile clipping technique was integrated into the pessimistic Bellman operator to allow for sharp, span-based penalties while maintaining essential operator properties like monotonicity and contractivity.
- The algorithm is designed to operate without requiring prior knowledge of unlearnable environmental parameters such as bias span or effective dataset size.

## Results

- The paper provides the first 'fully single-policy' sample complexity guarantee for average-reward offline RL, bounded by 	ilde{O}}(√(S(\lVert h_{π^*) \rVert_{span} + 1)α / m}), where mmm is the effective dataset size.
- A theoretical lower bound demonstrates that the dependence of suboptimality on the effective dataset size mmm (specifically, the O(1/m)O(√(1/m))O(1/m) rate) is statistically optimal.
- Through hard examples, the research formally proves the necessity of transient state coverage for achieving single-policy complexity guarantees in the average-reward setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving sample complexity bounds dependent on the target optimal policy's specific complexity (e.g., its bias span) in average-reward offline RL requires data coverage that includes 'transient' states, beyond just its stationary distribution.
> - The quantile clipping mechanism enables the construction of a robust pessimistic Bellman operator that effectively mitigates distribution shift and leverages span-based penalties in the average-reward setting.
> - A new complexity measure, the 'policy hitting radius,' was introduced to characterize the transient state coverage requirements and link them to the bias span of the target policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in solving complex control problems, typically relying on extensive online interaction with an environment. However, many real-world applications in fields like robotics, education, and healthcare face significant barriers to online data collection, such as high costs, safety concerns, or the absence of high-fidelity simulators. Offline RL addresses these challenges by learning effective policies solely from pre-collected historical datasets, without further online interaction. This setting places a premium on statistical efficiency and necessitates robust methods to handle issues like distribution shift and non-uniform data coverage.

Distribution shift arises because a policy improved through offline learning may visit states and take actions that are under-represented or absent in the historical dataset, making its performance estimation unreliable. Non-uniform coverage refers to the uneven concentration of data across the state-action space, making it difficult to accurately estimate the value of all possible policies.

While significant theoretical progress has been made in offline RL, much of this has been confined to finite-horizon or discounted infinite-horizon settings. The average-reward setting, which aims to maximize the long-term average reward, has been comparatively less explored from a theoretical perspective. In this setting, the challenges of distribution shift and non-uniform coverage are amplified. Distribution shifts can occur over arbitrarily long timescales, and the intrinsic complexity of different policies (ee.g., as measured by the span of their relative value functions) can vary widely, unlike in discounted settings where the effective horizon provides an implicit upper bound.

Previous theoretical work in average-reward offline RL has typically relied on "uniform" complexity measures, such as the uniform mixing time or a uniform bound on the span of all policies. These measures quantify complexity across *all* policies, rather than being specific to the target optimal policy. This can lead to loose performance guarantees and often requires strong structural assumptions on the Markov Decision Process (MDP), such as assuming it is unichain or that all policies have a constant gain. Furthermore, existing algorithms often necessitate prior knowledge of unlearnable parameters (e.g., concentrability coefficients) for their implementation.

This research contributes by developing a theoretical framework and an algorithm that specifically addresses these limitations. It seeks to provide sharp performance guarantees in the average-reward setting that depend only on the complexity measures of the *target policy* and to operate in more general MDP structures (weakly communicating MDPs) without requiring prior knowledge of specific environmental parameters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding this research are:

- **Develop Single-Policy Dependent Performance Guarantees:** A central motivation is to overcome the limitations of prior average-reward offline RL theory that relies on "uniform" complexity measures. The authors aim to establish performance guarantees that are "fully single-policy," meaning they depend only on the intrinsic complexity characteristics of the *target optimal policy* itself, such as its bias span and a newly introduced "policy hitting radius." This is motivated by the desire for sharper, more instance-dependent bounds that avoid the potential looseness associated with worst-case uniform complexity measures.
- **Address Weakly Communicating MDPs:** Previous work often made restrictive assumptions, such as requiring MDPs to be unichain or for all policies to have constant gains. These assumptions do not hold in many realistic scenarios. A key objective is to extend theoretical guarantees and algorithmic solutions to the more general class of weakly communicating MDPs, where only the optimal gain is constant, but sub-optimal policies may exhibit state-dependent gains. This broadens the applicability of the theoretical framework.
- **Achieve Optimal Statistical Efficiency:** The authors are motivated to develop an algorithm that attains optimal statistical rates, particularly concerning the effective dataset size. This involves rigorously deriving both upper and lower bounds to demonstrate the tightness of their proposed sample complexity.
- **Eliminate the Need for Prior Parameter Knowledge:** Many existing offline RL algorithms, especially those with theoretical guarantees, require tuning parameters that are functions of unknown environmental complexities or coverage coefficients. A practical motivation for this work is to design an algorithm that can be implemented without any prior knowledge of such unlearnable parameters.
- **Investigate Transient State Coverage Requirements:** An explicit motivation is to deeply understand the data coverage necessary for achieving single-policy-dependent guarantees in the average-reward setting. This leads to exploring whether coverage assumptions solely based on the stationary distribution of the target policy are sufficient, or if data from "transient" (zero stationary probability) states is also critical. This exploration is driven by the unique long-horizon nature of the average-reward objective, which can magnify the impact of rare but impactful transitions.
- **Establish Theoretical Lower Bounds:** To validate the optimality of their approach and bounds, the authors aim to construct "hard" instances that demonstrate the fundamental limits of learning under their specified conditions. This includes proving the necessity of transient state coverage and the tightness of their statistical rates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on a model-based pessimistic approach, combined with a novel technique to address the unique challenges of average-reward offline RL.

**Algorithmic Framework: Pessimistic Discounted Value Iteration with Quantile Clipping**

The core of the approach is Algorithm 1, "Pessimistic Value Iteration With Quantile Clipping." This algorithm approximates the average-reward MDP by reducing it to a discounted MDP with a carefully chosen, very large discount factor.

1. **Discount Factor Selection ($\gamma$):** The algorithm sets the discount factor $\gamma = 1 - \frac{1}{n_{tot}}$, where $n_{tot}$ is the total dataset size. This choice makes the effective horizon of the discounted problem equal to $n_{tot}$, a large value necessary to approximate the long-term behavior of average-reward MDPs. Crucially, this choice of $\gamma$ requires no prior knowledge of environmental parameters.

2. **Empirical Model Construction:** An empirical transition matrix $\hat{P}$ is formed directly from the given dataset $\mathcal{D}$. The reward function $r$ is assumed to be known.

3. **Pessimistic Bellman Operator ($\hat{T}_{pe}$):** The algorithm uses an iterative pessimistic Bellman operator, $\hat{T}_{pe}$, to update Q-values. For a Q-function $Q$, the update for a state-action pair $(s,a)$ is defined as:
 $\hat{T}_{pe}(Q)(s,a) = r(s,a) + \gamma \max \left\{ \hat{P}_{sa} T_{\beta(s,a)}(\hat{P}_{sa}, MQ) - b(s,a,MQ), \min_{s'} (MQ)(s') \right\}$
 where $MQ$ denotes $\max_{a'} Q(s,a')$.
 - **Novel Quantile Clipping ($T_{\beta}$):** A critical component is the `Tβ` operator. For a vector $V$ and a probability distribution $\mu$, `Tβ(μ, V)` clips all entries of $V$ that are larger than the $(1-\beta)$-quantile with respect to $\mu$ down to this quantile value. This technique is designed to address a fundamental issue in constructing effective pessimistic operators for average-reward settings.
 - **Empirical-Span-Based Penalty Function (`b`):** The term $b(s,a,V)$ is a Bernstein-style penalty function, defined as:
 $b(s,a,V) = \max \left\{ \sqrt{\beta(s,a)V_{\hat{P}_{sa}}[T_{\beta(s,a)}(\hat{P}_{sa}, V)]}, \beta(s,a) \lVert T_{\beta(s,a)}(\hat{P}_{sa}, V) \rVert_{span} \right\} + \frac{5}{n_{tot}}$
 where $\beta(s,a) = \alpha/(n(s,a)-1)$ and $\alpha$ includes logarithmic factors of $S, A, n_{tot}, \delta$.
 The `Tβ` clipping allows the use of a sharp, span-based penalty term ($\lVert T_{\beta(s,a)}(\hat{P}_{sa}, V) \rVert_{span}$) while preserving crucial properties like monotonicity and $\gamma$-contractivity of $\hat{T}_{pe}$. This is essential because naive use of a span-based penalty would break these properties, and prior Bernstein-style penalties did not maintain the "constant shift" property vital for average-reward settings. The constant shift property ensures the penalty only scales with relative value differences, not the overall magnitude of value functions, which grow as $\frac{1}{1-\gamma}$ in discounted reductions.

4. **Value Iteration:** The algorithm performs $K = \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(\frac{n_{tot}}{1-\gamma})}{1-\gamma}\right)$ iterations of $\hat{T}_{pe}$ starting from $\hat{Q}_0 = 0$ to compute an approximate fixed point $\hat{Q} = \hat{Q}_K$.

5. **Policy Extraction:** The final policy $\hat{\pi}$ is chosen to be greedy with respect to $\hat{Q}$, i.e., $\hat{\pi}(s) = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \hat{Q}(s,a)$.

**Theoretical Approach:**

1. **Pessimism Guarantees:** A significant part of the theoretical analysis focuses on rigorously proving that the constructed $\hat{T}_{pe}$ operator is indeed pessimistic, meaning $\hat{T}_{pe}(\hat{Q}) \le T(\hat{Q})$ (where $T$ is the true Bellman operator). This guarantees that the learned Q-function acts as a lower bound for the true optimal Q-function, helping to mitigate distribution shift.
2. **Concentration Inequalities:** Leave-one-out and absorbing MDP techniques are heavily utilized to derive sharp Bernstein-style concentration bounds (Lemmas B.5, B.6, B.7, B.8). These bounds account for the statistical dependence between empirical estimates (like $\hat{P}$) and the learned value functions, which is crucial for robust guarantees.
3. **Policy Hitting Radius ($T_{hit}$):** A novel single-policy complexity measure, $T_{hit}(P, \pi) = \inf_{s^* \in S} \sup_{s_0 \in S} \mathbb{E}_{\pi}^{s_0}[\eta_{s^*}]$, is introduced. It quantifies the maximum expected time to reach a "center" (recurrent) state under policy $\pi$. This measure is instrumental in characterizing the transient state coverage requirements and relating them to the bias span.
4. **Relationship to Bias Span:** The paper establishes a theoretical link between the policy hitting radius and the bias span, showing $\lVert h_{\pi} \rVert_{span} \le 4 T_{hit}(P, \pi)$ (Lemma B.13).
5. **Lower Bounds:** To demonstrate optimality, the paper constructs "hard examples" (Theorems 3.3 and 3.4). These constructions rigorously prove the necessity of transient state coverage for single-policy complexity bounds and establish the optimal scaling with respect to the effective dataset size.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and theoretical results that advance the understanding of average-reward offline reinforcement learning:

- **First Fully Single-Policy Sample Complexity Bound (Theorem 3.2):** The paper provides the first sample complexity guarantee for average-reward offline RL that depends solely on the intrinsic complexity measures of the *target optimal policy* $\pi^*$. Specifically, the algorithm outputs a policy $\hat{\pi}$ such that, with high probability, its suboptimality is bounded by:
 $\rho^* - \rho_{\hat{\pi}} \leq \tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sqrt{S(\lVert h_{\pi^*} \rVert_{span} + 1)\alpha / m}\right)$
 where $S$ is the number of states, $\lVert h_{\pi^*} \rVert_{span}$ is the bias span of $\pi^*$, $\alpha$ incorporates logarithmic factors of system parameters and failure probability, and $m$ is an "effective dataset size." This bound is a significant departure from prior work that relied on uniform complexity measures (e.g., uniform mixing time or uniform span bound over all policies).

- **Handling Weakly Communicating MDPs:** The proposed framework and algorithm are shown to be effective in general weakly communicating MDPs. This is a broader class of MDPs compared to the unichain or constant-gain assumptions made in previous average-reward offline RL theoretical studies, thereby expanding the applicability of the results. The existence of a unichain gain-optimal policy is a mild assumption that always holds.

- **Necessity and Sufficiency of Transient Coverage:**
 - **Coverage Requirement:** The main theorem's sample complexity condition for state-action pairs $(s, \pi^*(s))$ is $n(s, \pi^*(s)) \ge m\mu_{\pi^*}(s) + \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T_{hit}(P, \pi^*)^2)$. This implies a requirement for data even for states $s$ which are transient under the target policy ($\mu_{\pi^*}(s) = 0$). The term $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T_{hit}(P, \pi^*)^2)$ represents a bounded amount of data from these transient state-action pairs, independent of $m$.
 - **Lower Bound on Transient Coverage (Theorem 3.3):** A surprising finding is formally demonstrated through a hard example: to obtain convergence rates dependent on single-policy complexity measures like $\lVert h_{\pi^*} \rVert_{span}$ and the novel policy hitting radius $T_{hit}(P, \pi^*)$, data coverage *beyond* the stationary distribution of the target policy is *necessary*. This means that even with arbitrarily large amounts of data, learning a near-optimal policy is impossible if only stationary states are covered. This result highlights a fundamental difference between single-policy complexity measures and previously studied uniform complexity measures in the average-reward setting.

- **No Prior Parameter Knowledge Required:** The algorithm (Algorithm 1) is designed such that its implementation does not necessitate prior knowledge of unlearnable parameters, such as the bias span $\lVert h_{\pi^*} \rVert_{span}$, the effective dataset size $m$, or environmental complexity parameters. The discount factor $\gamma$ is set adaptively based on the total dataset size $n_{tot}$.

- **Optimal Statistical Rate (Theorem 3.4):** The paper provides a lower bound demonstrating that the dependence of the suboptimality on the effective dataset size $m$ (specifically, the $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{1/m})$ rate) in their main theorem is tight. This establishes the statistical optimality of their proposed algorithm for large $m$.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the theoretical understanding and practical development of offline reinforcement learning, particularly in the challenging average-reward setting.

- **Theoretical Foundations of Average-Reward Offline RL:** The work establishes foundational theoretical guarantees for average-reward offline RL that are instance-dependent, relying on the intrinsic complexities of the target policy rather than worst-case uniform measures. This provides sharper, more informative bounds that are relevant to real-world scenarios where optimal policies may have specific, manageable complexities even if the overall MDP is complex.
- **Novel Algorithmic Techniques:** The introduction of the quantile clipping technique within a pessimistic value iteration framework is a novel algorithmic contribution. This technique enables the use of sharper, span-based penalties while maintaining crucial properties of the Bellman operator, such as monotonicity and contractivity. This method could potentially be adapted and applied to other complex RL settings or robust learning problems.
- **Deeper Understanding of Data Coverage:** The counter-intuitive finding regarding the necessity of transient state coverage for achieving single-policy complexity guarantees profoundly impacts the understanding of data requirements in average-reward offline RL. It highlights that the average-reward objective poses unique challenges related to "off-support" or rarely visited states, which are not captured by mere stationary distribution coverage. This insight guides future research in designing more appropriate data collection strategies or developing algorithms that can handle such coverage gaps under different assumptions.
- **Expansion to General MDPs:** By handling weakly communicating MDPs, the research extends the theoretical applicability of offline RL to a broader and more realistic class of environments. This is a step towards making average-reward offline RL practical for a wider range of real-world control problems.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The identified limitations and open questions in the conclusion (e.g., extending to function approximation, addressing the transient state coverage requirement in practice) directly suggest critical directions for subsequent research. Specifically, the strong requirement for transient data coverage prompts investigation into how this can be circumvented, perhaps through structural assumptions or by incorporating more nuanced prior information about the environment.
