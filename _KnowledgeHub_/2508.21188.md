# Mirage or Method? How Model-Task Alignment Induces Divergent RL Conclusions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, NUS, and HKUST reveal that the effectiveness of unexpected reinforcement learning behaviors in large language models, such as one-shot learning or robustness to noisy rewards, is contingent on the pre-trained model's inherent "Model-Task Alignment." Their analysis clarifies that these effects primarily occur when models already possess latent capabilities for a task, acting as an elicitation mechanism rather than driving genuine learning from scratch.

## Problem

- Several widely reported, counterintuitive reinforcement learning (RL) phenomena in large language models (LLMs)—e.g., one-shot training, robustness to spurious rewards, negative-only sample training—lack a clear, generalized explanation.
- Uncertainty exists whether these observations represent universal properties of LLM-RL or are artifacts of specific model-task interactions or potential data contamination.
- The ambiguity surrounding these phenomena hinders reliable application, efficient resource allocation, and targeted development in the burgeoning LLM-RL field.

## Method

- The authors propose and validate "**Model-Task Alignment**," quantified by `==pass@k==` accuracy, as the key explanatory factor for the emergence of counterintuitive RL phenomena.
- A systematic empirical investigation is conducted across diverse LLM architectures (Qwen, Llama) and reasoning task domains (mathematical, logical) to test the generality of these claims under varying alignment conditions.
- Contamination analysis is performed to differentiate between data leakage and inherent model capabilities as the primary drivers of observed RL effects, providing a distinct hypothesis.

## Results

- While ground truth rewards consistently improved performance across all alignment settings, the effectiveness of noisy reward signals (random, incorrect) was directly tied to strong model-task alignment, failing in weakly aligned scenarios.
- One-shot RL successfully generalized and achieved performance comparable to full-dataset training only in strongly aligned tasks, yielding minimal improvements in weakly aligned, challenging logical reasoning benchmarks.
- Both Negative Sample Reinforcement and Positive Sample Reinforcement performed similarly to full-signal RL in strong alignment, but in weak alignment, positive-only signals consistently outperformed negative-only signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL in LLMs operates via two distinct mechanisms: "capability elicitation" when a model has strong inherent alignment, and "genuine learning" when a model lacks foundational expertise for a task.
> - Many counterintuitive RL phenomena, such as one-shot training or effectiveness of noisy/negative-only rewards, primarily emerge in strongly aligned settings by eliciting latent model capabilities, rather than imparting new skills.
> - Model-Task Alignment provides a more fundamental and generalized explanation for these RL outcomes than data contamination alone, as demonstrated by the persistence of effects in contamination-free, strongly aligned scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Large Language Models (LLMs) has become a transformative force in AI, particularly for enhancing capabilities like instruction following, human preference alignment, and complex reasoning. Breakthrough models such as OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, QwQ, and Kimi-1.5 exemplify this trend, demonstrating remarkable performance in domains ranging from coding and mathematics to logical reasoning. This success mirrors RL's impact in traditional areas like game playing (e.g., AlphaGo).

However, the application of RL to LLMs has also surfaced several intriguing, often counterintuitive, empirical phenomena that diverge significantly from expectations in classical RL settings. These include:
- The surprising efficacy of single training examples, sometimes rivaling full-dataset performance.
- The reported dispensability of highly accurate reward signals, with random or incorrect rewards still yielding improvements.
- The effectiveness of training solely with negative samples, matching or exceeding reward-based methods.

While these observations have generated considerable excitement, their generalizability and the specific conditions under which they hold or fail have remained largely underexplored. Much of the evidence for these phenomena has been derived from limited experimental settings, predominantly involving Qwen models on mathematical tasks. This narrow focus raises concerns about whether these findings represent universal properties of LLM-RL or are artifacts of specific model-task interactions.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. It systematically investigates these "mirage-like" claims, moving beyond specific model-task combinations to determine their true scope. By proposing and validating "Model-Task Alignment" as a unifying explanatory factor, the work aims to provide a more robust understanding of LLM-RL dynamics. It also critically examines a concurrent "contamination hypothesis" (Wu et al., 2025) that attributes some of these phenomena to data leakage, offering a distinct and more generalized perspective. By clarifying the underlying mechanisms, this research aims to refine future RL practices, guiding resource allocation and opening new avenues for joint optimization of base model pre-training and RL fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate and clarify the conditions under which several widely reported, yet counterintuitive, phenomena in reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs) hold true. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Identify the critical factors** that differentiate the success or failure of unusual RL observations in LLMs, such as one-shot training, robustness to spurious rewards, and negative-only sample training.
2. **Propose and validate "Model-Task Alignment"**—the degree to which a pre-trained model's inherent capabilities align with task requirements, measured by `pass@k` accuracy—as a key determinant for these phenomena.
3. **Conduct a comprehensive empirical investigation** across diverse model architectures (Qwen, Llama) and task domains (mathematical and logical reasoning) to test the generality of these claims.
4. **Differentiate between true RL insights and artifacts** of specific, highly aligned model-task combinations, or potential data contamination.

The motivation behind these objectives is multifaceted:

- **Addressing Uncertainty:** The current landscape is characterized by remarkable but often anecdotal findings that lack a clear theoretical or empirical underpinning for their broad applicability. This uncertainty hinders reliable application and development in LLM-RL.
- **Guiding Resource Allocation:** If claims like one-shot training or noisy rewards are universally effective, they would necessitate significant shifts in research and development resources, such as prioritizing data selection over data volume or questioning the need for highly accurate reward models. A clear understanding of their validity is therefore paramount.
- **Unlocking New Opportunities:** By understanding the specific conditions under which these phenomena occur, researchers can either modify models (e.g., through targeted pre-training) to make these "easier" RL methods universally valid, or strategically allocate computational resources to the RL stage based on task difficulty and model alignment.
- **Clarifying the Role of Contamination:** By providing an alternative hypothesis to data contamination, the paper aims to provide a more robust explanation for observed RL behaviors, even in contamination-free settings.

In essence, the paper seeks to bring scientific rigor and clarity to a burgeoning area of LLM research, transforming potentially misleading "mirages" into actionable "methods" by precisely defining their scope and underlying mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around systematically testing the "Model-Task Alignment Dependency" hypothesis, which posits that the effectiveness of counterintuitive RL phenomena is contingent on how well a model's inherent capabilities match the task.

#### 3.1. Quantifying Model-Task Alignment

- The authors define Model-Task Alignment as the degree of inherent proficiency of a model on a given task.
- This alignment is quantitatively measured using **`pass@k` accuracy**, an unbiased estimator of the probability that at least one correct solution appears among `k` independent samples generated by the model. This metric effectively captures the model's pre-existing knowledge and reasoning patterns for a task.

#### 3.2. Strategic Model and Task Selection

- **Models:** Two representative LLM families were chosen for their differing baseline capabilities:
 - **Qwen2.5-7B-Base:** Known for strong performance, particularly in mathematical domains.
 - **Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct:** Another prominent model, allowing for comparison across architectures of comparable parameter scale.
- **Tasks:** A diverse set of reasoning tasks were selected to create varied alignment scenarios:
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** AIME24, MATH500, AMC23.
 - **Logical Reasoning:** SynLogic (synthetic puzzles), BBH (multi-step reasoning), BBEH (extended difficulty BBH), and subsets of KOR-Bench (Operation, Logic, Cipher, Puzzle, Counterfactual).
- **Categorization of Settings:** Based on `pass@k` measurements (illustrated in Figure 2 and Appendix B), the model-task combinations were classified into three categories:
 - **Red (Potential Contamination + Strong Alignment):** E.g., Qwen2.5 on mathematical tasks.
 - **Gray (No Contamination + Weak Alignment):** E.g., Llama3.1 on mathematical tasks, both models on SynLogic, BBH, BBEH, and KOR-Bench Logic, Cipher, Puzzle.
 - **Green (No Contamination + Strong Alignment):** E.g., Both models on KOR-Bench Operation and Counterfactual.

#### 3.3. Contamination Analysis

- To address the concurrent "contamination hypothesis" (Wu et al., 2025), the authors performed their own contamination analysis.
- **Method:** Original prompts were truncated at various ratios (0.4, 0.6, 0.8), and models greedily completed the remaining text.
- **Metrics:** ROUGE-L scores and Exact Match (EM) rates between model completions and actual remaining content were used to detect data leakage (high ROUGE-L/EM indicating contamination).
- This analysis (Table 1 and Appendix C) explicitly shows that strong alignment can exist even without contamination (Green category), supporting their hypothesis over contamination as the sole cause.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup and RL Algorithm

- **Training Datasets:** DeepScaleR for mathematical tasks and SynLogic-Easy for logical tasks.
- **Evaluation:** Zero-shot setting, with `avg@8` metrics for AIME 2024 and SynLogic.
- **RL Algorithm:** **DAPO** (Direct Advantage Policy Optimization) was used as the default, with specific hyperparameters for `epsilon_low`, `epsilon_high`, maximum prompt/generation lengths, batch sizes, and learning rate.
- **Modifications for Logical Tasks:** Adjustments were made to handle batches with few non-zero reward variance samples, reflecting the challenge of early-stage logical reasoning training.

#### 3.5. Research Questions (RQs) and Specific Experiment Designs

- **RQ1: Reward Signal: How Critical Is It?**
 - **Approach:** Evaluated performance with various reward signals:
 - **External Rewards:** Ground Truth (Correct), Random, Incorrect, Format-based.
 - **Self-Rewarded RL:** Majority Voting (Vote), Entropy Minimization (EM) on self-generated samples.
 - **Test-Time RL (TTRL):** Examined the effectiveness of TTRL (using majority vote on unlabeled test data) across different alignment settings, tracking `Maj@16` during training.

- **RQ2: Is One-shot Enough for RL to Work?**
 - **Approach:** Compared full dataset training with single-example training.
 - **Sample Selection:** Used samples selected by a variance-based algorithm (Wang et al., 2025) and randomly selected samples (random-1, random-2).
 - **Metrics for Logical Tasks:** Tracked `acc_1-shot` (rollout accuracy of the trained example), `acc_id` (in-distribution subtask accuracy), and `acc_ood` (out-of-distribution subtask accuracy) to assess generalization.

- **RQ3: Does RL Work with Only Negative Samples?**
 - **Approach:** Compared **Negative Sample Reinforcement (NSR)** (masking positive rewards) and **Positive Sample Reinforcement (PSR)** (masking negative rewards) against the full-signal DAPO baseline.
 - **Analysis:** Examined the impact on token-level entropy during training to understand exploration-exploitation dynamics.

This robust and multi-faceted methodology allows for a systematic and granular examination of the factors influencing RL efficacy in LLMs, providing strong empirical evidence for the Model-Task Alignment hypothesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's comprehensive experimental analysis yielded several critical findings, consistently supporting the central hypothesis that **Model-Task Alignment (measured by `pass@k`) is the fundamental determinant of when counterintuitive RL phenomena emerge in LLM reasoning.**

**Overall Observation:**
Standard RL training (with ground truth rewards) consistently provides robust performance improvements across all model-task alignment settings. However, many of the counterintuitive RL phenomena *only* arise when the pre-trained model already exhibits strong Model-Task Alignment. In challenging regimes with weak alignment, these specialized techniques largely fail, whereas standard RL remains effective.

#### 4.1. RQ1 – Reward Signal: How Critical Is It?

- **Ground Truth Rewards are Gold Standard:** Across all model families and task domains, using ground truth rewards consistently led to the highest performance improvements. For instance, Qwen2.5-7B achieved significant gains on AIME24 (+10.9) and MATH500 (+30.2) with accurate rewards, establishing them as the most reliable signal.
- **Model-Task Alignment Drives Robustness to Noisy Rewards:** The effectiveness of noisy (random, incorrect, format) reward signals was directly tied to alignment strength:
 - **Strong Alignment (Red & Green Categories):** Models showed surprising robustness. Qwen2.5-7B maintained reasonable performance on math tasks, and both models improved on KOR-Bench Operation and Counterfactual tasks even with random rewards.
 - **Weak Alignment (Gray Category):** Spurious rewards consistently failed to provide meaningful improvements, as seen with Llama3.1-8B on math and both models on challenging logical reasoning benchmarks.
- **Limited Effectiveness of Self-Rewarded Methods:** Self-Rewarded Reinforcement Learning (majority voting, entropy minimization) generally underperformed external ground truth rewards. While showing some promise for Qwen2.5-7B on math, they failed to generalize effectively to logical reasoning tasks.
- **Test-Time RL (TTRL) Efficacy:** TTRL, which leverages majority voting on test data for self-reward, was effective *only* in strong model-task alignment settings (Red and Green categories). In these cases, the `Maj@16` metric (majority vote accuracy) also consistently improved during training, further indicating that TTRL acts as an elicitation mechanism when the model already possesses latent capabilities.

#### 4.2. RQ2 – Is One-shot Enough for RL to Work?

- **One-shot RL Success is Alignment-Dependent:** The ability of models to generalize from a single training example was highly contingent on strong model-task alignment:
 - **Strong Alignment (Red & Green Categories):** Both Qwen2.5-7B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct demonstrated remarkable generalization, achieving performance comparable to full-dataset training on mathematical tasks for Qwen and substantial improvements on KOR-Bench Operation and Counterfactual for both. One-shot learning facilitated within-distribution generalization, quickly improving accuracy on similar subtasks.
 - **Weak Alignment (Gray Category):** Minimal improvements were observed across challenging logical reasoning benchmarks, and one-shot RL failed to generalize to different puzzle types.
- **Limited Impact of Sample Selection Strategy:** Contrary to prior work, the sophisticated reward-variance-based sample selection algorithm did not consistently outperform random sample selection. Performance levels were similar for both selected and random examples in strong alignment settings, and negligible differences were observed in weak alignment settings.

#### 4.3. RQ3 – Does RL Work with Only Negative Samples?

- **Strong Alignment Enables Both Negative and Positive-Only Learning:** In strong model-task alignment settings (Red & Green categories), both Negative Sample Reinforcement (NSR) and Positive Sample Reinforcement (PSR) achieved performance comparable to full-signal DAPO. This indicates that when models are already proficient, learning can be effectively driven by either penalizing errors or rewarding successes.
- **Weak Alignment Favors Positive-Only Signals:** In weak alignment settings (Gray category), PSR consistently outperformed NSR. PSR yielded meaningful improvements in logical reasoning tasks (e.g., SynLogic), while NSR showed minimal gains or even detriments.
- **Entropy Dynamics:** NSR helped maintain exploration by slowing entropy collapse, particularly on mathematical tasks. However, this increased exploration did not necessarily translate to accuracy gains in weak alignment settings (logical tasks), where a flatter entropy curve correlated with poorer final accuracy. This suggests that while negative signals can preserve output diversity, a larger exploration space is not always beneficial for learning new skills in unfamiliar domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a profound re-evaluation of the burgeoning field of reinforcement learning for large language models, shifting the understanding of several "miraculous" phenomena from universal truths to context-dependent occurrences. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

#### 5.1. Reconceptualizing RL in LLMs

The most significant contribution is the identification of **Model-Task Alignment** as the critical explanatory factor for many counterintuitive RL observations. This suggests that RL in LLMs operates under at least two distinct mechanisms:
1. **Capability Elicitation:** When a pre-trained model already possesses strong inherent capabilities (strong alignment), minimal or noisy RL signals can effectively *elicit* and refine these latent abilities. This explains the effectiveness of one-shot training, spurious rewards, and negative-only signals in such settings.
2. **Genuine Learning:** When a model lacks foundational expertise (weak alignment), standard RL with accurate, rich signals is still required to drive *genuine learning* and develop new reasoning skills. The "mirages" of minimal-data or noisy-signal RL largely fail here.
This distinction urges researchers to interpret RL phenomena in LLMs with greater caution, avoiding overgeneralization from specific high-alignment scenarios.

#### 5.2. Challenging the Contamination Hypothesis

The study directly contrasts its alignment hypothesis with the concurrent "contamination hypothesis" (Wu et al., 2025). By demonstrating that many counterintuitive RL effects persist in contamination-free, but strongly aligned, settings (Green category), the paper provides strong evidence that inherent model proficiency, rather than mere data leakage, is a more fundamental driver. This is crucial for guiding future research away from solely focusing on contamination as an explanation and towards understanding inherent model properties.

#### 5.3. Informing RL Best Practices and Resource Allocation

The findings have immediate practical implications for how RL is applied to LLMs:
- **Data Strategy:** For tasks where models show strong initial alignment, researchers might invest less in massive datasets for RL, focusing instead on carefully selected (even single) examples or leveraging self-supervised/noisy reward signals. For weakly aligned tasks, large, diverse datasets and precise reward modeling remain essential.
- **Reward Modeling:** The necessity of highly accurate reward models is clarified. While always the gold standard, less precise reward signals can suffice if the base model is already highly proficient. This could lead to more efficient development cycles in specific domains.
- **Pre-training / Mid-training Design:** The research opens up new avenues for jointly optimizing base model development and RL fine-tuning. One could strategically enhance a model's domain-specific capabilities during pre-training, enabling more efficient RL with limited data/rewards post-training. Alternatively, resources could be predominantly allocated to a robust RL stage with meticulously curated data and rewards for novel, challenging tasks.

#### 5.4. Guiding Future Research Directions

- **Understanding "Elicitation" vs. "Learning":** Future work can delve deeper into the precise mechanisms by which RL "elicits" capabilities from well-aligned models versus how it "learns" entirely new skills in misaligned scenarios.
- **Proactive Alignment:** Research can focus on developing methods to proactively improve model-task alignment during pre-training, making subsequent RL more efficient and robust across a wider range of tasks.
- **Dynamic RL Strategies:** Developing adaptive RL algorithms that dynamically adjust their reliance on reward accuracy, data volume, and exploration strategies based on real-time assessments of model-task alignment could be a fruitful direction.

In conclusion, "Mirage or Method?" provides a foundational framework for understanding the diverse outcomes of RL in LLM reasoning. By establishing Model-Task Alignment as a central explanatory principle, it transforms a collection of puzzling observations into a coherent, actionable understanding, paving the way for more informed and effective development of advanced LLM systems.
