# ClueTracer: Question-to-Vision Clue Tracing for Training-Free Hallucination Suppression in Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> ClueTracer, a training-free and architecture-agnostic plug-in, was developed to suppress hallucinations in multimodal large reasoning models (MLRMs) by tracing question-relevant visual clues. The approach localized task-critical visual evidence by leveraging the model's internal reasoning, leading to an average accuracy gain of 11.84% on reasoning-oriented hallucination benchmarks and improving performance across various MLRMs and non-reasoning MLLMs.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly Multimodal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs), frequently generate hallucinatory content not supported by input images or questions.
- Existing inference-time hallucination mitigation methods struggle with long reasoning chains due to accumulating biases (token-role-agnostic) or fail for complex reasoning tasks (task-agnostic priors).
- MLRMs exhibit 'reasoning drift,' where attention diffuses to visually irrelevant entities during clue gathering, weakening visual grounding and increasing hallucination propensity.

## Method

- Introduced ==ClueRecall==, a layer-wise visual-grounding metric, to identify the most reliable decoder layer for retrieving question-relevant visual clues.
- Proposed ==ClueTracer==, a training-free and parameter-free plug-in that recovers task-critical visual regions by tracing information flow from key question tokens to outputs and then to visual tokens.
- Developed an ==evidence-region construction process== that clusters identified task-critical visual tokens using DBSCAN to generate bounding boxes, which are then used to guide the MLRM's inference with focused visual evidence.

## Results

- ClueTracer consistently improved performance across all tested reasoning models on hallucination benchmarks, achieving an average accuracy gain of **11.84%** on reasoning-oriented datasets and **8.73%** on perception-oriented datasets.
- The method effectively mitigated reasoning drift by increasing task-relevant object mentions and visual attention to queried objects in generated text, while also reducing perceptual hallucination.
- ClueTracer outperformed prior inference-time baselines on reasoning tasks and demonstrated strong transferability to non-reasoning MLLMs like LLaVA-1.6-Mistral, boosting their accuracy significantly.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hallucination in MLRMs often stems from ungrounded and cluttered clue collection, rather than model capacity, as constraining attention to key visual areas can reduce hallucinations and recover correct answers.
> - Task-relevant visual clues are not isolated but become identifiable when conditioned on intermediate outputs that operationalize the query, indicating a consistent mediation pattern.
> - Key question tokens exhibit larger attention variance along the output dimension, providing a training-free signal for their detection and use in tracing visual evidence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has progressed from simple image description tasks to more complex, unified cross-modal reasoning, giving rise to Multimodal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs). These models aim to perform multi-step logical inference across various input modalities to solve intricate real-world problems. This advancement often involves strengthening inferential strategies through supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning on pre-trained multimodal backbones.

Despite these capabilities, a significant challenge in MLLMs and MLRMs is the phenomenon of hallucination, where the model generates content not supported by the input image or question, or produces factually incorrect information. In reasoning settings, this issue is often amplified because the generation pipeline remains language-dominant, leading to lengthy deliberations that may encourage over-reliance on linguistic priors and insufficient utilization of visual clues. This modal imbalance can weaken visual grounding and increase the propensity for fabricating non-existent objects or unsupported causal explanations.

Existing hallucination mitigation strategies for MLLMs generally fall into two categories:
1. **Token-role-agnostic grounding:** These methods apply step-invariant interventions, such as contrastive decoding, logit steering, or attention reallocation, to encourage the model to focus on visual signals globally. While effective for short responses, such approaches can introduce persistent biases that accumulate over long reasoning traces, potentially overlooking decisive clues embedded within intermediate steps.
2. **Task-agnostic priors:** These approaches prepend generic image descriptions or captions to the model's input, assuming that richer image descriptions will provide sufficient context. However, for complex reasoning tasks (e.g., multimodal math or logic), the question itself provides essential constraints and intermediate clues that cannot be fully captured by generic image descriptions alone.

This paper positions ClueTracer as a method developed to address these specific limitations, particularly the failure of prior inference-time methods to reliably isolate decisive clues within the long-chain multimodal reasoning characteristic of MLRMs. It aims to provide a step-aware and question-conditional inference-time mechanism for hallucination suppression.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research is primarily motivated by the observed phenomenon of "reasoning drift" in Multimodal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs). Reasoning drift describes a failure mode where, during the process of gathering visual clues, the model allocates excessive attention to entities that are irrelevant to the specific question posed. This diffusion of focus can dilute attention on task-relevant cues, progressively decoupling the reasoning trace from visual grounding and ultimately leading to hallucinations. The authors illustrate this by showing that constraining the model's attention to key visual areas can reduce hallucination and recover correct answers, suggesting that the model's capacity is not the bottleneck, but rather ungrounded and cluttered clue collection.

The paper identifies two main difficulties faced by existing inference-time localization or intervention methods when applied to reasoning models:
1. **Long-horizon output control:** Global, step-invariant biases (token-role-agnostic methods) can compound over lengthy reasoning traces, missing crucial clues that appear in intermediate steps.
2. **Limited generalization beyond object-centric tasks:** Task-agnostic prior methods, which rely on rich image descriptions, often fail to incorporate the indispensable constraints and intermediate clues supplied by the question itself in complex reasoning problems.

To address these issues, the authors conducted three targeted studies, leading to key observations that directly inform the design of ClueTracer:
- **Observation 1 (Bias accumulation and clue neglect):** Prior inference-time methods, such as direct steering of output logits or attention weights, can lead to premature termination or neglect task-critical visual clues in long-chain reasoning.
- **Observation 2 (Traceability of task-relevant clues via output):** Task-relevant visual clues are not directly separable from the query or the image in isolation. Instead, they become identifiable when conditioned on which intermediate outputs operationalize the query, indicating a consistent mediation pattern where question-aligned output steps also exhibit concentrated visual support.
- **Observation 3 (High attention variance for key question tokens):** Key question tokens show larger attention variance along the output dimension, providing a training-free signal for their detection.

Based on these insights, the primary objectives of this work are:
- To introduce **ClueRecall**, a novel, layer-wise visual-grounding metric to assess how effectively each layer of an MLRM retrieves question-relevant visual clues.
- To propose **ClueTracer**, a training-free, parameter-free, and architecture-agnostic plugin. ClueTracer aims to suppress hallucinations in MLRMs by localizing task-critical visual clues through explicit tracing of information propagation from the question to the outputs and then to the visual tokens.
- To demonstrate that ClueTracer can improve performance across various reasoning architectures on established hallucination benchmarks without additional training.
- To evaluate the transferability of ClueTracer to non-reasoning MLLMs, showcasing its robustness and generalizability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ClueTracer is an inference-time approach designed to isolate visual clues critical for answering a question, leveraging an MLRM's internal reasoning chain. The methodology consists of three main parts: ClueRecall, Task-Critical Token Identification (ClueTracer itself), and Evidence-Region Construction.

#### 3.1. ClueRecall: Assessing Visual Clue Retrieval

ClueRecall is a metric developed to evaluate the visual grounding capacity of each decoder layer within an MLRM. It quantifies how well a layer's visual attention aligns with question-relevant visual clues.
1. **Query Construction:** For a given image, bounding boxes and category labels (from annotations like COCO or segmentation models like Mask R-CNN) are used to instantiate templated queries, e.g., "Is there a/an {category} in this image?". These are tokenized as `X_q_cat`.
2. **Query-Aligned Regions:** The bounding box for the queried category (`cat`) is mapped to its corresponding visual token indices, `X_bbox`, representing the ground-truth visual clue.
3. **ClueRecall Calculation:** For each perception-labeled instance, the full input `X_c` (system prompt, visual tokens, question tokens) is fed into the MLRM. The attention tensor `A` is recorded. `ClueRecall(l)` for layer `l` measures the average recall of `X_bbox` by the visual attention `A_t,l,V` specifically at the output steps `t` where the model explicitly mentions the category token `cat`. The layer `L_max` with the highest `ClueRecall` is selected as the most reliable layer for clue retrieval for subsequent computations.

#### 3.2. ClueTracer: Task-Critical Token Identification

After identifying `L_max`, ClueTracer focuses on recovering task-critical visual regions without requiring external bounding-box annotations or fixed question templates. This involves a multi-stream tracing mechanism:
1. **Key Query Tokens via Output-Axis Variability:** Based on the observation that key question tokens exhibit localized attention peaks along the output axis, ClueTracer identifies these tokens. It collects query-side attention at layer `L_max` across all output steps for each query token. The variance of each token's attention trajectory (`a_nq`) along the output dimension is computed. Key query tokens (`X_q*`) are selected as those whose standardized variability (z-score) exceeds a predefined threshold `τ_q`.
2. **Tracing Visual Clues via Query-Output-Vision Propagation:** ClueTracer then seeks to identify the subset of visual tokens (`X_v*`) that best grounds these key query constraints. This is formalized as maximizing `P(x_v | X_q*)`. Recognizing that this dependency is mediated by intermediate outputs in an autoregressive decoder, the method leverages the model's reasoning chain (`y_1:T`) as latent variables. The relevance of a visual token `x_v` to the query constraints is formulated as a probabilistic trace, marginalizing over output steps: `P(x_v | X_q*) = sum_t P(x_v | y_t) * P(y_t | X_q*)`.
 - `P(y_t | X_q*)` (Query Alignment) is instantiated by summing the attention weights from key query tokens (`x_nq` in `X_q*`) to output token `y_t` at layer `L_max`.
 - `P(x_v | y_t)` (Visual Grounding) is instantiated by the attention weight from output token `y_t` to visual token `x_v` at layer `L_max`.
 - The **Trace Score** `T_R(x_v)` for each visual token `x_v` is calculated by summing the product of Query Alignment and Visual Grounding across all output steps.
3. **Statistical Filtering:** To suppress background noise, a statistical filtering function (z-score normalization) is applied to the `Trace Score`. Visual tokens (`X_v*`) whose filtered scores exceed a threshold `τ_v` are selected as the final set of task-critical visual clues.

#### 3.3. Evidence-Region Construction and Inference-Time Use

The identified task-critical visual tokens (`X_v*`) are organized into reusable clue regions:
1. **Clustering:** Each visual token in `X_v*` is mapped to its image-plane center. These points are then clustered using DBSCAN to group spatially coherent evidence.
2. **Bounding Box Generation:** Each cluster is enclosed by an axis-aligned bounding rectangle, resulting in a set of evidence regions `R`.
3. **Inference-Time Modes:** These regions `R` can be used in two ways:
 - **Offline:** Precompute and cache `R` for repeated evaluations.
 - **Online:** A two-stage pipeline where Stage 1 derives `R` from the model, and Stage 2 re-invokes the model with the original image and the generated regions `R` to refine reasoning with focused visual evidence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ClueTracer addressed four primary research questions, yielding the following findings:

- **RQ1: Performance on Reasoning Models and Layer-wise Understanding:**
 - ClueTracer consistently improved the performance of all tested reasoning models (R1-OneVision, Ocean-R1, MM-Eureka, ORSTA-R1) across various hallucination benchmarks.
 - On reasoning-oriented hallucination datasets (HallusionBench and VMCBench), ClueTracer achieved an average accuracy gain of 11.84%, with a maximum individual gain of 22.20%.
 - On perception-oriented hallucination benchmarks (POPE and MMVP), an average absolute gain of 8.73% was observed.
 - ClueRecall analysis revealed that for 7B architectures, the perception signal consistently peaked at layers 18–24, indicating similar perceptual capacities across models with different training architectures.

- **RQ2: Hallucination Analysis and Mitigation of Reasoning Drift:**
 - ClueTracer demonstrated efficacy in mitigating reasoning drift. Models equipped with ClueTracer showed a marked increase in the proportion of task-relevant object mentions in their generated text and allocated higher visual attention to the queried objects.
 - Qualitative examples illustrated that ClueTracer enabled models to focus directly on discriminative visual areas, avoiding exploratory descriptions of irrelevant regions common in baseline models.
 - ClueTracer also reduced perceptual hallucination, allowing models to correctly identify subtle or hard-to-perceive objects (e.g., a faint truck silhouette) that baseline models failed to detect.

- **RQ3: Component Studies and Comparison with Baselines:**
 - The performance trend over different attention layers in component studies mirrored the ClueRecall pattern, validating the selection of `L_max`.
 - DBSCAN was found to be preferable for clustering identified visual tokens compared to K-means, attributed to its robustness to noise and ability to discard spurious activations.
 - ClueTracer consistently outperformed prior inference-time baselines (e.g., VCD, ICD, AGLA, VDGD) designed for non-reasoning MLLMs when applied to reasoning tasks. These baselines often suffered from bias accumulation over long reasoning chains or provided task-agnostic priors that were insufficient for complex questions.

- **RQ4: Transferability to Non-Reasoning MLLMs:**
 - ClueTracer exhibited strong transferability to non-reasoning MLLMs, including LLaVA-1.6-Mistral and Qwen2.5-VL. When attention-guided crops generated by Ocean-R1 were provided to these models in an offline setting, performance on the MMVP benchmark improved.
 - The method was observed to significantly boost the accuracy of several models (LLaVA-1.6, R1-OneVision, ORSTA-R1) from near-random guessing levels towards the performance range of GPT-4V, underscoring its generalizability and architecture-agnostic nature.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ClueTracer represents a contribution to the field of multimodal reasoning by providing a practical and effective method for hallucination suppression. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Addressing a Core Challenge:** Hallucination is a critical and persistent problem in large multimodal models, particularly exacerbated in reasoning-intensive scenarios with long inference chains. ClueTracer directly tackles this issue by enhancing visual grounding.
- **Novelty in Approach:** The methodology introduces ClueRecall as a new internal metric for layer-wise visual grounding assessment and employs a unique "question → output → visual" tracing mechanism. This approach leverages the model's own intermediate reasoning steps as a conduit to connect question constraints to relevant visual evidence, moving beyond previous global or task-agnostic interventions.
- **Efficiency and Deployability:** ClueTracer is designed to be training-free, parameter-free, and architecture-agnostic. This characteristic minimizes deployment costs and allows it to be readily integrated as a plug-in with existing and future MLRMs and MLLMs without requiring retraining or architectural modifications.
- **Robust Performance Gains:** The consistent and substantial performance improvements across diverse reasoning models and even non-reasoning MLLMs, on multiple hallucination benchmarks, underscore the robustness and generalizability of the method.
- **Enhanced Trustworthiness and Interpretability:** By explicitly localizing and focusing on task-relevant visual clues, ClueTracer can make the reasoning process of MLRMs more grounded and interpretable. This could contribute to building more trustworthy AI systems, particularly in sensitive applications where factual accuracy is paramount.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** Improving the reliability of multimodal reasoning has implications for various real-world applications, including complex question answering, scientific discovery, medical diagnosis, autonomous systems, and content generation, where accurate visual understanding and logical inference are crucial.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The method of analyzing and intervening in internal attention patterns (ClueRecall, clue tracing) provides a framework that could inspire further research into understanding, diagnosing, and controlling the behaviors of complex neural networks, especially in multimodal contexts.
