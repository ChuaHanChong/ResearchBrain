# Reinforced Visual Perception with Tools

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ONE Lab, HUST, University of Washington, and Zhejiang University developed Reinforced Visual Perception with Tools (ReV PT), a framework that trains multimodal language models (MLLMs) to adaptively use visual tools via reinforcement learning. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance on perception-heavy visual reasoning benchmarks by learning to strategically select and interpret tool outputs, overcoming limitations of supervised finetuning approaches.

## Problem

- Bridging the gap between verbal reasoning in large language models (LLMs) and specialized computer vision (CV) for robust, general visual reasoning remains challenging.
- Prior tool-augmented MLLMs primarily relied on supervised finetuning (SFT), which is costly for data curation, requires extensive data filtering, and limits generalization to novel tool-use scenarios.
- MLLMs often struggle with complex visual perception tasks requiring granular analysis, such as spatial relationships, object counting, and depth estimation.

## Method

- ReV PT introduces a ==two-stage reinforcement learning (RL) framework==, built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to teach MLLMs adaptive visual tool usage.
- A =="cold-start" phase== leverages GPT-4.1 to synthesize high-quality, tool-augmented reasoning data, followed by SFT on this data, to establish a robust initial policy for sequential vision-tool invocation.
- The system integrates four ==perception-oriented visual tools== (Object Detection, Zoom In, Edge Detection, Depth Estimation) and a simple binary reward function to guide RL training and optimize tool selection.

## Results

- ReV PT achieves state-of-the-art performance on several perception-intensive benchmarks, including MMVP, MMStar, CV-Bench, BLINK, and BLINK-Hard, with an average improvement of over **5%** compared to instruct-tuned base models.
- The method outperforms SFT baselines and text-based RL agents, and surpasses commercial models like GPT-4.1 and Gemini-2.0-Flash on challenging visual reasoning subsets (e.g., BLINK-Depth and Relation).
- Ablation studies confirm that both the cold-start process and the integration of specific tools like object detection and depth estimation are critical for the framework's effectiveness, with their removal leading to significant performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly with the GRPO algorithm, offers a more adaptive and scalable approach to training MLLMs for visual tool use compared to supervised finetuning.
> - A "cold-start" phase, involving initial SFT on synthetically generated tool-use data, is crucial for preventing MLLMs from neglecting visual tools and for preserving their general capabilities during RL training.
> - ReV PT learns to optimize the strategic selection of visual tools, resulting in higher accuracy when tools are used, even if the overall frequency of tool calls decreases after RL optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has seen significant advancements in both Large Language Models (LLMs) for verbal reasoning and specialized Computer Vision (CV) models for perceptual tasks. However, bridging the gap between these two domains to achieve robust, general visual reasoning—a cornerstone of human intelligence—remains a formidable challenge.

Prior research has attempted to address this by augmenting LLMs with vision models, often referred to as "tool-augmented multimodal language models." Approaches like VisProg and VisualSketchPad demonstrated the potential of LLMs to leverage external vision specialists. More recently, open-source multimodal language models have also benefited from using visual tools and reasoning about their outputs, typically through **supervised finetuning (SFT)** on meticulously curated tool-use data.

However, the authors identify several critical limitations of the SFT paradigm for visual tool-use:
1. **Expensive Data Curation:** Generating high-quality tool-use and reasoning traces often relies on expensive commercial models (e.g., GPT-4) or manual annotation.
2. **Reliance on Careful Data Filtering:** Aggressive filtering based on format and correctness is often necessary to achieve performance gains, indicating fragility in the data generation process.
3. **Limited Generalization:** SFT relies on offline, pre-defined trajectories, offering no incentive for models to explore alternative tools or adapt to novel environmental outputs. This inherently limits generalization to unseen problems or tools.

"Reinforced Visual Perception with Tools" (ReV PT) directly positions itself as a solution to these limitations. By adopting a **reinforcement learning (RL)** approach, ReV PT aims to enable multimodal LLMs to learn to reason with visual tools adaptively and effectively. This paradigm shift moves away from static, human-curated demonstrations toward an environment where the model can explore, learn from feedback, and optimize its tool-usage strategy. This places ReV PT at the forefront of research exploring more robust, scalable, and generalizable methods for integrating specialized perception capabilities into general-purpose multimodal AI. It is part of a growing trend to leverage RL not just for robotics or game-playing, but also for enhancing the reasoning and problem-solving abilities of LLMs, especially in multimodal contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ReV PT is to **enhance multimodal language models' (MLLMs) visual perception and general question-answering abilities by training them to reason with visual tools via reinforcement learning (RL)**. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations stemming from the limitations of prior supervised finetuning (SFT) approaches:

1. **Overcome SFT Limitations:** The core motivation is to move beyond the shortcomings of SFT, which include the expensive and labor-intensive generation of tool-use data, the need for extensive data filtering, and the inherent lack of generalization to novel scenarios due to reliance on pre-defined trajectories.
2. **Enable Adaptive Tool Selection:** ReV PT aims to empower MLLMs to dynamically decide *when* and *which* visual tools to use, rather than following rigid, pre-scripted sequences. This adaptivity is crucial for tackling diverse and complex visual reasoning problems.
3. **Facilitate Exploration and Optimal Strategy Learning:** Through RL, the model is incentivized to explore different tool-use strategies and learn from the outcomes, thereby discovering potentially optimal or more efficient ways to solve problems that might not be present in human-generated SFT data.
4. **Improve Perception-Heavy Benchmarks:** A key practical objective is to achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks that heavily rely on strong visual perception, such as those involving spatial relationships, object counting, depth estimation, and complex visual analysis.
5. **Preserve General Capabilities:** While specializing in visual perception and tool-use, the project also aims to ensure that the RL training does not significantly degrade the model's general multimodal question-answering capabilities.
6. **Provide Open-Source Resources:** The authors are motivated to contribute valuable open-source code, datasets, and platforms to the research community, fostering further advancements in RL-based visual tool usage.

In essence, ReV PT seeks to unlock the latent potential of MLLMs by teaching them to "think with images and tools" in a more intelligent and flexible manner, analogous to how humans selectively employ external aids for complex visual tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReV PT introduces a novel two-stage framework to train multimodal language models for improved visual perception by integrating visual processing tools as reasoning steps. The methodology is built upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) reinforcement learning algorithm and involves a "cold-start" phase.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline (Figure 3)

The framework operates as follows: when presented with a multimodal query, the ReV PT agent first generates a reasoning process. This involves deciding whether to call a visual tool or answer directly. If a tool is called, the model-generated tool request is managed by a local environment-based Tool Controller, which independently deploys visual tool services (e.g., Depth, Object Detection). The outputs from these tools are then fed back to the Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) for iterative reasoning. This process involves K-turn rollouts where the model interacts with the tool environment to learn an adaptive policy, ultimately culminating in a final response.

#### 3.2. Visual Tools

The authors focus on four specific visual tools, deemed most suitable for perception-oriented tasks, after observing that models were reluctant to use a larger suite of tools:
- **Object Detection (Fu et al., 2025):** Performs open-vocabulary detection, taking an image and text query to locate and bound described objects. Useful for tasks requiring object localization and identification.
- **Zoom In:** Facilitates focused analysis by cropping and magnifying a specified region of interest. Helps models concentrate on pertinent areas and mitigate irrelevant information.
- **Edge Detection:** Identifies and delineates perimeters of objects and significant textural variations, transforming an image into an edge map. Useful for perceiving object silhouettes and surface patterns.
- **Depth Estimation (Yang et al., 2024):** Computes spatial depth from a monocular image, generating a depth map encoding object distances. Provides a nuanced understanding of 3D arrangements and relative positioning.

#### 3.3. Preliminary: GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)

ReV PT employs GRPO, an RL algorithm designed to evaluate policy performance by comparing a group of candidate responses.
1. **Response Sampling:** For a given prompt `q`, the policy `π_θ` samples `N` responses `{o_1..., o_N}`.
2. **Reward Assignment:** Each response `o_i` receives a reward `r_i = R(q, o_i)` from a reward function.
3. **Group-Relative Advantage:** An advantage `A_i` is calculated for each response by normalizing its reward against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the group. This helps in understanding a response's quality relative to other contemporaneous samples.
4. **Policy Update:** The policy is updated by maximizing a clipped surrogate objective function that leverages the importance sampling ratio (`s_i`) to measure policy change and includes a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty to regularize updates, preventing large deviations from a reference policy. This ensures stable and effective learning.

#### 3.4. Cold Start

A critical observation was that directly applying GRPO led to a decline in tool usage, likely because solving visual tasks often didn't *strictly* require tools, and tool-processed images represented a distribution shift. To counter this, a "cold-start" stage is introduced:
1. **Data Synthesis:** GPT-4.1 is used to synthesize high-quality, tool-augmented reasoning data in a predefined multi-turn format. This data explicitly encourages tool calls and reasoning about their results.
2. **Data Filtering:** Rollout trajectories generated by GPT-4.1 that contain errors are filtered out to ensure data quality.
3. **Supervised Finetuning (SFT):** The model is then finetuned on this curated cold-start dataset using a cross-entropy loss. This provides a robust initial policy for sequential vision-tool invocation, serving as a solid foundation before the RL phase. This addresses the initial reluctance of the model to use tools.

#### 3.5. Reward Design

The reward function for the RL phase is intentionally simple and rule-based to avoid reward hacking associated with neural network-based reward models:
- **Binary Reward:** A reward of `+1` is given if both the format of the response (e.g., thinking process in `<think></think>`, tool calls in `<tool_call></tool_call>`, answer in `<answer></answer>`) and the final answer are correct. Otherwise, a reward of `-1` is given.
This binary, verifiable reward scheme directly encourages both correct problem-solving and adherence to the structured tool-use format.

#### 3.6. Dataset Construction and Baselines

- **Training Data:** The SAT and TACO datasets are filtered to retain samples incorrectly answered by Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, creating a "difficult" dataset for RL. A subset is used for cold-start data synthesis by GPT-4.1.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against commercial models (GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.0-Flash), instruct-tuned MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct), SFT baselines (Qwen-SAT-SFT, Qwen-SFT cold-start), and a text-based RL agent (Qwen-GRPO) to isolate the impact of visual tool-usage and RL.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Evaluation spans eight multimodal benchmarks, with a strong focus on perception-centric tasks (CV-Bench, BLINK, MMVP, BLINK-Hard) and general capabilities (MMStar, MMMU, MMBench, MathVista).

This comprehensive methodology allows ReV PT to systematically build tool-use capability, first through an SFT-based cold-start, and then refine it through exploration and reward-driven optimization via GRPO, leveraging specific visual tools tailored for perception.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with ReV PT demonstrate significant advancements in multimodal visual reasoning, particularly on perception-heavy tasks.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on Visual Perception Tasks

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance:** ReV PT achieves SOTA performance on several perception-intensive benchmarks, including MMVP, MMStar, CV-Bench, BLINK, and BLINK-Hard.
- **Outperforming SFT and Text-based RL:** The method consistently outperforms SFT baselines and text-based GRPO (RL without visual feedback) counterparts. This highlights the effectiveness of visual tool integration and RL for learning adaptive tool-use policies.
- **Significant Gains over Instruct Models:** ReV PT-3B and ReV PT-7B notably outperform the original instruct-tuned models by significant margins. For example, on CV-Bench, ReV PT-3B and ReV PT-7B achieve improvements of 9.03% and 9.44% respectively. Across all benchmarks, an average improvement of over 5% is observed.
- **Specific Task Improvements:** Performance gains are particularly pronounced in depth and distance tasks, showing improvements of 9.5% and 13.23% compared to the base model, directly attributing these to the model's ability to utilize and interpret tool-generated information.
- **Outperforming Commercial Models:** ReV PT models surpass commercial models like GPT-4.1 and Gemini-2.0-Flash on challenging subsets such as BLINK-Depth and Relation.

#### 4.2. Insights from Ablation Studies

- **Cold-Start Process is Crucial:** Ablation studies on cold-start data construction reveal that synthesizing "hints" (reasoning about when and why to use tools) and including general data (from TACO) are critical for preserving the model's general capabilities while introducing tool-use. This structured pre-training prevents the model from neglecting tools or degrading general performance.
- **Individual Tool Contribution:** Removing frequently used tools like object detection and depth estimation significantly degrades performance. For instance, removing object detection led to a 5-point drop (8.2% decrease) on BLINK relation and a 12.33-point drop (9.6% decrease) on MMVP, confirming their fundamental importance to ReV PT's effectiveness.

#### 4.3. Tool Usage Analysis

- **Adaptive Tool Utilization:** ReV PT models exhibit high tool usage frequency on perception-intensive benchmarks (e.g., CV-Bench, MMVP) but less so on general benchmarks (MMSTAR), indicating adaptive tool selection.
- **RL Optimizes Tool Selection:** After RL training, object detection and edge detection usage decline, leading to an overall reduction in tool usage frequency. However, models achieve higher accuracy *when* using tools, suggesting that RL optimizes the *strategic* selection of more appropriate tools for visual perception tasks. The model learns to be more discerning in its tool calls.
- **Tool Bias:** Models show a bias toward object detection and depth estimation over zoom and edge detection, likely due to biases in the cold-start data construction.

#### 4.4. Identified Limitations and Failure Modes

- **Flawed Tool Results:** Visual tools themselves can sometimes provide incorrect outputs (e.g., misclassifying objects), leading the ReV PT model to an incorrect answer despite potentially inherent capability.
- **Incorrect Tool Analysis:** Even with correct tool outputs (e.g., depth maps), ReV PT may fail to correctly interpret or leverage this information in its reasoning chain.
- **Inappropriate/Unhelpful Tool Usage:** The model sometimes calls tools that are unhelpful or even interfere with its perception, leading to incorrect responses.
- **Incorrect Tool Selection:** In some cases, ReV PT selects a tool that is entirely unsuited for the given question.
These failure modes underscore that effective tool-use requires not only access to tools but also robust reasoning about *when* to use them, *how* to interpret their outputs, and *how to recover* from erroneous tool results.

In summary, ReV PT successfully demonstrates that RL can effectively train multimodal LLMs to leverage visual tools, leading to significant performance gains on perception-centric tasks and offering a more adaptive approach compared to supervised methods. However, challenges related to tool reliability and the model's interpretation of tool outputs remain areas for future improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Reinforced Visual Perception with Tools" (ReV PT) paper introduces a significant advancement in the capabilities of multimodal language models, with several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Tool Learning:** By effectively demonstrating the power of reinforcement learning for visual tool usage, ReV PT offers a compelling alternative to the prevailing supervised finetuning (SFT) paradigm. This shift from static, human-curated demonstrations to dynamic, reward-driven exploration addresses critical limitations of SFT, such as expensive data generation, reliance on filtering, and poor generalization. This could unlock more scalable and robust methods for integrating external knowledge and capabilities into AI systems.

2. **Enhanced Visual Reasoning Capabilities:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance, especially on perception-heavy benchmarks, signifies a substantial improvement in how MLLMs can "see" and "understand" visual information. The ability to strategically employ tools like object detection, depth estimation, edge detection, and zoom-in allows models to gather more granular and precise visual cues, leading to more accurate and nuanced reasoning. This moves MLLMs closer to human-like visual intelligence, which routinely relies on focused perception and external aids.

3. **Insights into RL for Multimodal AI:** The paper provides valuable insights into the practical aspects of applying RL to multimodal models for tool use. The necessity of a "cold-start" phase, the simplicity of the rule-based reward design, and the analysis of tool usage patterns offer crucial guidance for future research in this nascent area. The observation that RL optimizes tool selection, even if overall tool *frequency* decreases, suggests that models learn to be more efficient and purposeful in their tool calls.

4. **Open-Source Contribution:** The authors' commitment to fully open-sourcing ReV PT along with its code and datasets is a substantial contribution to the research community. This will accelerate further research, allowing other teams to build upon their work, replicate results, and explore new directions more easily. This fosters transparency and collaborative progress in a rapidly evolving field.

5. **Reframing the "Tool Specialist" Debate:** The discussion section critically examines the role of visual tool specialists, noting a non-monotonic relationship with model capability. For smaller, resource-constrained models, tools act as a "compelling shortcut," significantly boosting performance. However, as models scale, the marginal benefit may diminish, and sometimes tool outputs can even contradict a model's inherent intuition. This nuanced perspective challenges the simplistic notion that "more tools are always better" and opens up research questions about optimal tool integration strategies across different model scales and architectures. The idea of a "tool renaissance" for the most advanced models through dynamic code generation suggests an exciting future where AI can *create* its own tools.

6. **Addressing Human Bias in AI Systems:** The paper touches upon the "Bitter Lesson of Tool Use," highlighting the inherent human biases injected into AI training through cold-start data generation. This is a profound philosophical and practical point: while AI should ideally discover optimal strategies independently, current limitations force reliance on human-designed demonstrations. This discussion prompts deeper consideration of how to balance human guidance with autonomous discovery in the development of increasingly capable AI systems, especially as they interact with complex environments.

In conclusion, ReV PT is a landmark study that not only pushes the technical boundaries of multimodal AI by leveraging RL for visual tool-use but also stimulates critical discussions about the fundamental principles guiding the development of intelligent, tool-augmented agents. Its impact will likely be felt in both the practical application of more capable MLLMs and the theoretical understanding of how AI can best acquire and utilize external knowledge.
