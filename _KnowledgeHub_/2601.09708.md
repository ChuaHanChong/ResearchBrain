# Fast-ThinkAct: Efficient Vision-Language-Action Reasoning via Verbalizable Latent Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast-ThinkAct addresses the high inference latency of reasoning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by compressing explicit textual Chain-of-Thought reasoning into compact, verbalizable latent representations. This method achieves up to 89.3% reduced inference latency and improves performance across various robot manipulation and embodied reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- High inference latency in existing reasoning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, caused by generating lengthy explicit textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces.
- The slow decision-making (several seconds per decision) of current reasoning VLAs makes them impractical for real-time embodied AI applications requiring rapid control (e.g., 1-15 Hz).
- Challenges in directly applying efficiency techniques from large language models (LLMs) to embodied AI, which requires unique spatial-temporal understanding and grounded semantic reasoning.

## Method

- A ==teacher-student distillation== framework that compresses explicit textual CoT from a verbose teacher VLM into compact, continuous, and ==verbalizable latent vectors== generated by a student VLM.
- Preference-guided distillation using a verbalizer LLM and a DPO-like objective, optimizing the student to encode high-quality reasoning patterns from the teacher's varied CoT traces.
- Action-aligned visual plan distillation transfers spatial reasoning by aligning trajectory-level hidden states and directly predicting 2D waypoints, which then condition a ==diffusion Transformer== action model for low-level control.

## Results

- Achieved up to **89.3%** reduced inference latency (e.g., 9.3 times faster than ThinkAct-7B), making real-time embodied applications feasible.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art reasoning VLAs on robot manipulation benchmarks (LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin2.0) and embodied reasoning tasks (EgoPlan-Bench2, RoboVQA, OpenEQA).
- Demonstrated robust long-horizon planning, effective few-shot adaptation with limited demonstrations, and strong failure recovery capabilities in complex robotic scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dramatically reducing inference latency in embodied reasoning is achievable without performance loss by compressing explicit reasoning into compact, continuous latent representations.
> - Leveraging reward preferences and verbalizability during training effectively distills high-quality reasoning from explicit textual CoT into an implicit, interpretable latent space.
> - Integrating multimodal (linguistic and visual) planning into efficient latent representations, specifically through action-aligned visual plan distillation, is crucial for robust embodied control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Evolution of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models

Recent advancements in large vision-language models (VLMs) have enabled impressive capabilities in understanding multimodal data. Extending these capabilities to embodied tasks, such as robotic manipulation, has led to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA). These models learn to perceive complex visual scenes, interpret language instructions, and execute physical actions, often by training on large-scale robot demonstration datasets. While successful in acquiring basic skills, these imitation-based VLAs often struggle with generalization to novel scenarios, long-horizon planning, and self-correction due to the inherent limitations of demonstration data coverage.

#### 1.2. Rise of Reasoning VLAs and Their Challenges

To overcome the generalization limitations of basic VLAs, a new category of "Reasoning VLAs" has emerged (e.g., Embodied CoT, CoT-VLA, ThinkAct). These models incorporate intermediate "thinking processes" or Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, often through textual or visual sub-goal generation. This explicit reasoning has shown to significantly improve task generalization, long-horizon planning, and contextual adaptation. However, the primary drawback of these reasoning VLAs is their high inference latency. Generating lengthy textual CoT traces can take several seconds per decision, rendering them impractical for real-time embodied AI applications that require rapid decision-making at high frequencies (e.g., 1-15 Hz). This latency bottleneck is a critical safety and performance issue for time-critical robotic applications.

#### 1.3. Approaches to Efficient Reasoning

The need for efficient reasoning is not unique to VLAs; it's a prominent area of research in large language models (LLMs). Techniques like length penalties, latent reasoning in continuous spaces (e.g., Coconut, CODI), and reasoning dropout (e.g., ECoT-Lite) have been explored. However, direct transfer of these LLM techniques to VLA tasks is challenging because embodied AI requires integrating spatial-temporal understanding and grounding semantic reasoning in executable physical control. While ECoT-Lite introduced reasoning dropout for embodied CoT, it risked performance degradation due to information loss from directly shortening textual traces.

#### 1.4. Fast-ThinkAct's Position

Fast-ThinkAct directly addresses the critical efficiency-performance trade-off in reasoning VLAs. It aims to bridge the gap between effective, expressive reasoning and the real-time requirements of embodied AI. Instead of merely shortening explicit textual CoTs, Fast-ThinkAct proposes to compress reasoning into *compact, continuous, and verbalizable latent representations*. This approach attempts to retain the rich reasoning capabilities of explicit CoT while drastically reducing the inference time. By introducing preference-guided distillation and visual trajectory alignment, Fast-ThinkAct offers a novel solution that effectively captures multimodal (linguistic and visual) planning in an efficient, implicit manner, thereby advancing the practical applicability of reasoning-enhanced VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Fast-ThinkAct research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Address the Inference Latency Bottleneck in Reasoning VLAs:** The most significant motivation is to overcome the high inference latency inherent in existing reasoning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Current approaches that generate lengthy explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces (e.g., hundreds of tokens) are too slow for real-time embodied AI applications like robotic manipulation and autonomous driving, where decisions need to be made at high frequencies (e.g., 1-15 Hz). This latency creates a critical bottleneck, hindering real-world deployment and posing safety risks.

2. **Achieve Compact yet Performant Planning:** The goal is not merely to speed up reasoning but to do so *without sacrificing performance* in complex VLA tasks. Fast-ThinkAct aims to develop a planning mechanism that is both compact (efficient) and expressive enough to support robust long-horizon planning, few-shot adaptation, and failure recovery—capabilities that reasoning VLAs were designed to provide. The challenge is to preserve essential spatial and visual information crucial for embodied control while compressing the reasoning process.

3. **Enable Verbalizable Latent Reasoning:** To ensure that the compressed reasoning remains interpretable and learnable, the authors propose "verbalizable latent reasoning." This involves encoding reasoning into compact, continuous latent tokens that can still be decoded into natural language by a verbalizer. This verbalizability grounds the latent learning process in an interpretable textual form, facilitating learning from explicit textual reasoning traces.

4. **Transfer Linguistic and Visual Planning Capabilities to Latent Space:** The research aims to effectively distill both high-level linguistic reasoning patterns and low-level visual planning capabilities from a more verbose teacher model into the compact latent representations of a student model. This requires specific mechanisms, such as preference-guided distillation for linguistic reasoning and trajectory alignment for visual planning, to ensure the latents are comprehensive for embodied control.

5. **Bridge High-Level Planning with Low-Level Action Execution:** A crucial objective for any VLA model is to effectively translate abstract reasoning and planning into concrete, executable robot actions. Fast-ThinkAct seeks to achieve this by using the generated compact visual plan latents to guide a diffusion Transformer-based action model, ensuring that the efficient reasoning directly enhances the quality of action generation.

In essence, Fast-ThinkAct is motivated by the critical need to make advanced reasoning capabilities of VLAs practical for real-world robotics by resolving the efficiency-performance dilemma, thereby enabling more intelligent, adaptive, and responsive embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Fast-ThinkAct proposes an efficient embodied reasoning framework designed to compress lengthy textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into compact, continuous latent representations while preserving performance. The core approach involves a teacher-student distillation framework combined with a reasoning-enhanced policy learning strategy.

#### 3.1. Efficient Embodied Reasoning

The central idea is to move from explicit, verbose textual CoTs to implicit, compact continuous latent vectors. This is achieved through two main components:

#### 3.2. Verbalizable Latent CoT by Reward Preferences

To guide the learning of meaningful latent representations, Fast-ThinkAct introduces a novel preference-guided distillation approach:
- **Teacher Model:** A "Textual Teacher VLM" ($\mathcal{F}_T^\theta$) is first trained to generate explicit, lengthy textual CoTs. This teacher model is trained using a Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach, specifically GRPO (Group Rewards for Policy Optimization), which maximizes rewards derived from action-aligned visual cues. This process inherently produces textual CoTs of varying quality, where an "advantage function" ($A(\tau)$) serves as a quality indicator for each reasoning trace ($\tau$).
- **Preference Pair Construction:** From the teacher's rollouts, pairs of high-quality ($\tau^+$) and low-quality ($\tau^-$) reasoning traces are identified based on their advantage function scores.
- **Latent Student Model:** The "Latent Student VLM" ($\mathcal{F}^\theta$) is designed to autoregressively generate $M$ compact continuous latent vectors ($\mathbf{z} = \{z_m\}_{m=1}^M$) instead of textual tokens.
- **Verbalizer LLM:** A "Verbalizer LLM" ($\mathcal{V}^\psi$) is introduced. Its role is to decode the student's continuous latent vectors $\mathbf{z}$ back into natural language. This verbalizer acts as a bridge, grounding the latent space in an interpretable textual form.
- **Preference-Guided Optimization:** Inspired by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a verbalization loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{verb}}$) is formulated. This loss encourages the verbalizer to assign a higher likelihood to decoding the student's latents into high-quality reasoning ($\tau^+$) compared to low-quality reasoning ($\tau^-$). This implicitly trains the student VLM to encode only the most relevant and high-quality reasoning patterns into its compact latents.

#### 3.3. Action-Aligned Visual Plan Distillation

To ensure the latents capture the spatial reasoning critical for embodied control, an additional distillation mechanism is employed:
- **Spatial Reasoning Transfer:** The teacher model is trained with trajectory-level rewards (e.g., goal completion, trajectory alignment) that enable grounded visual planning, meaning its internal representations encode information about desired manipulation trajectories.
- **Hidden State Alignment:** The student model aligns its trajectory-level representations with those of the teacher by minimizing the L2 distance between the hidden states corresponding to the `<answer>` token (which encodes the visual plan) from both the teacher ($\mathbf{h}_T^t$) and the student ($\mathbf{h}_t$). This is the distillation loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}$).
- **Efficient Parallel Trajectory Prediction:** Unlike the teacher which might generate verbose textual sequences of waypoints, the student uses $K$ learnable "spatial tokens" appended to its latent reasoning sequence. Each spatial token's output hidden state is simultaneously projected via an MLP to predict a 2D waypoint. An additional loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{ans}}$) ensures these predicted waypoints align with ground-truth waypoints.

The total objective for training the student VLM ($\mathcal{F}^\theta$) combines these three components: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{student}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{verb}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{ans}}$.

#### 3.4. Reasoning-Enhanced Policy Learning

Once the student VLM ($\mathcal{F}^\theta$) is trained to perform compact latent reasoning and generate visual trajectory plans, these representations are used to guide a low-level action model.
- **Action Model:** A diffusion Transformer-based action model ($\pi_\phi$, e.g., RDT or DiT-Policy) is used for action prediction.
- **Conditioning with Visual Latent Planning:** The "visual latent planning" context ($\mathbf{c}_t$) is extracted from the Key-Value (KV) cache of the spatial tokens within the earlier layers of the student VLM. This $\mathbf{c}_t$ is then concatenated with KV pairs from the action model's state encoder. The action model's cross-attention mechanism then attends to both this visual planning context and the state observations.
- **Policy Post-Training:** The action model ($\pi_\phi$) is post-trained on action-annotated robot data using an imitation learning objective ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{IL}}$), while the trained student VLM ($\mathcal{F}^\theta$) and the state encoder are kept frozen. This process allows the action model to translate the high-level visual planning from the compact latent reasoning into precise, low-level robot actions.

#### 3.5. Learning Strategy and Inference

- **Training Stages:**
 1. **SFT and CoT-SFT:** Both teacher and student VLMs are initialized from a pre-trained VLM, then fine-tuned with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Chain-of-Thought SFT (CoT-SFT) on multimodal datasets.
 2. **Teacher-Student Training:** The teacher is further optimized with GRPO, and the student is trained with the combined $\mathcal{L}_{\text{student}}$ objective for compact latent reasoning distillation.
 3. **Reasoning-Enhanced Policy Learning:** The trained student VLM is integrated with an action model, which is then fine-tuned on large-scale robotic action data.
- **Inference:** During inference, only the student VLM ($\mathcal{F}^\theta$) and the action model ($\pi_\phi$) are required. The student performs compact latent reasoning, generates visual trajectories via spatial tokens, and the extracted visual latent planning context ($\mathbf{c}_t$) directly conditions the action model to predict actions. The verbalizer is only used for interpretability or during training, not for action execution.

This multi-stage approach systematically distills comprehensive reasoning and visual planning capabilities into an efficient, implicit latent space, which then directly guides action execution, addressing the critical efficiency needs of embodied AI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Fast-ThinkAct demonstrates significant advancements across various embodied manipulation and reasoning benchmarks, primarily achieving substantial efficiency gains without compromising performance.

#### 4.1. Efficiency Gains: Inference Latency

- **Dramatic Latency Reduction:** Fast-ThinkAct achieves up to **89.3% reduced inference latency** compared to state-of-the-art reasoning VLAs like ThinkAct-7B and MolmoAct-7B. Specifically, it is 9.3 times faster than ThinkAct-7B (7513ms down to 805ms) and 7 times faster than ThinkAct-3B (5674ms down to 805ms). This is a core contribution, making real-time embodied applications more feasible.

#### 4.2. Robot Manipulation Performance

- **Superior Performance on LIBERO and SimplerEnv:** Fast-ThinkAct consistently outperforms all baselines, including foundation VLAs (OpenVLA) and reasoning VLAs (CoT-VLA, ThinkAct, MolmoAct), across all LIBERO subtasks (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long) and SimplerEnv-Google benchmarks. For example, it improves average success rates on LIBERO from 86.8% (MolmoAct-7B) to 89.7% and on SimplerEnv-Google from 68.3% (ThinkAct-7B) to 68.7% (with its 3B model, achieving the same or better performance than 7B models at a fraction of the latency).
- **Robust Bimanual Manipulation:** On the challenging RoboTwin2.0 benchmark for bimanual manipulation, Fast-ThinkAct significantly outperforms previous VLAs and reasoning VLAs (e.g., ThinkAct), achieving 9.3% and 3.6% higher success rates on easy and hard settings, respectively, compared to RDT, and improving ThinkAct's success rates by 3.3% and 1.7%. This demonstrates its capability in complex, long-horizon tasks requiring coordinated control.

#### 4.3. Embodied Reasoning Capabilities

- **State-of-the-Art Reasoning:** Fast-ThinkAct surpasses all comparison methods, including powerful proprietary models like GPT-4V and Gemini-2.5-Flash, on embodied reasoning benchmarks: EgoPlan-Bench2 (multi-step planning), RoboVQA (long-horizon reasoning for manipulation), and OpenEQA (zero-shot understanding). It achieves the highest overall average score, demonstrating robust scene comprehension and multi-step task execution.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis: Long-Horizon Planning and Failure Recovery

- **Effective Long-Horizon Planning:** The visualizations show that Fast-ThinkAct successfully predicts feasible 2D visual traces for complex, long-horizon tasks (e.g., from SimplerEnv-Google, LIBERO-Long, and RoboTwin2.0). These predicted trajectories serve as effective visual planning guidance, enabling successful task execution.
- **Robust Failure Recovery:** Fast-ThinkAct exhibits strong failure recovery capabilities on the RoboFAC benchmark. It significantly outperforms baselines in identifying runtime failures and providing accurate, concrete corrective guidance (e.g., repositioning and aligning the robot arm after dropping an object). This highlights its ability to reason over manipulation errors and propose recovery steps.

#### 4.5. Few-Shot Adaptation

- **Enhanced Few-Shot Learning:** The method significantly improves few-shot adaptation on the RoboTwin2.0 benchmark. Using only 10 demonstrations per task, Fast-ThinkAct enhances the adopted RDT action model and outperforms other state-of-the-art VLAs (like $\pi_0$ and ThinkAct) on medium and long-horizon tasks, demonstrating efficient learning with limited data.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Validation of Key Components:** Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the proposed components:
 - Removing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{verb}}$ (preference-guided verbalization) leads to performance drops, indicating that explicit guidance for aligning latents with high-quality reasoning is essential.
 - Further removing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}$ (visual plan distillation) causes additional performance degradation, emphasizing the importance of transferring visual planning capabilities through trajectory-level alignment.
 - Comparison with efficient textual reasoning baselines (e.g., inference without thinking, fixed-length textual tokens, RL with length penalty) shows that Fast-ThinkAct's 6 latent tokens achieve superior performance (53.3 average score) compared to these methods (46.5-47.8), validating the effectiveness of latent reasoning over merely shortening text.
- **Optimal Latent Steps:** An ablation on the number of latent reasoning steps ($M$) shows that $M=6$ achieves optimal performance, balancing reasoning capacity and conciseness.

In summary, Fast-ThinkAct successfully addresses the core trade-off between reasoning capability and inference efficiency in embodied AI, delivering a highly performant and significantly faster VLA model suitable for real-world robotic applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Fast-ThinkAct represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI, addressing a critical bottleneck that has hindered the practical deployment of advanced reasoning capabilities in robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

1. **Bridging the Efficiency-Performance Gap:** The most immediate and profound impact is Fast-ThinkAct's ability to achieve high-performance reasoning in VLA tasks with vastly reduced inference latency (up to 89.3% reduction). This directly solves a fundamental challenge for real-time robotic control, where lengthy reasoning processes have previously rendered sophisticated VLA models impractical. By enabling compact yet expressive planning, Fast-ThinkAct makes complex, intelligent robotic behaviors feasible within tight operational deadlines.

2. **Enabling Real-World Embodied AI Applications:** With its enhanced efficiency, Fast-ThinkAct paves the way for the broader adoption of reasoning-enabled VLA models in real-world scenarios. This includes:
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** More responsive and adaptive robots for manufacturing, logistics, and service industries, capable of complex assembly, dynamic object handling, and intelligent error recovery.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Improved decision-making and planning for autonomous vehicles, drones, and other mobile robots, particularly in unforeseen or challenging situations requiring advanced reasoning.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** More fluid and intelligent interactions with robots that can quickly understand and execute multi-step instructions, and adapt to human cues and dynamic environments.

3. **Advancing Generalization and Robustness:** Fast-ThinkAct not only improves efficiency but also maintains or enhances key capabilities like long-horizon planning, few-shot adaptation, and failure recovery. These attributes are crucial for robots to operate effectively in diverse, unstructured, and dynamic environments beyond their training data. The ability to quickly adapt to new tasks with limited demonstrations and robustly recover from errors significantly improves the autonomy and reliability of robotic systems.

4. **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The proposed methodology introduces several innovative ideas:
 - **Verbalizable Latent Reasoning:** The concept of distilling complex textual reasoning into continuous, compact, and verbalizable latent representations provides a powerful mechanism for efficient information encoding while maintaining interpretability and learnability through a verbalizer.
 - **Preference-Guided Distillation:** Leveraging reward signals (via DPO-like objectives) to distill high-quality reasoning patterns from a teacher to a student in the latent space is an effective way to improve reasoning quality and conciseness.
 - **Action-Aligned Visual Plan Distillation:** Explicitly transferring visual planning capabilities by aligning trajectory-level representations and directly predicting waypoints ensures that the compact latents are grounded in executable actions, which is vital for embodied control.

5. **Setting a New Standard for VLA Efficiency:** Fast-ThinkAct demonstrates that it is possible to achieve superior performance with significantly smaller models (e.g., 3B parameters outperforming 7B baselines) and much faster inference times. This challenges the prevailing notion that larger models with lengthy explicit reasoning are always necessary for advanced VLA capabilities, encouraging future research into efficient and compact architectures.
