# InternW0: A Foundational Physical World Model for Efficient Real-World Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> InternW0 is a physical world model that couples future-video prediction with robot control in a mixture-of-transformers: a high-capacity video expert predicts slowly while a lightweight action expert acts fast, reusing cached video key/value context adapted by observation-conditioned routing. Trained on roughly 7,200 hours of robot and egocentric data, it reaches 98.6% on LIBERO with 60.73 ms action latency.

## Problem

- Existing world-action models regenerate expensive video predictions for every control update, so action latency grows and predictive context is already stale at execution time.
- Prior world-action models rarely unify heterogeneous embodiments with differing control and sensing semantics, limiting joint training across single-arm, dual-arm, mobile, and dexterous robot corpora.
- Vision-only policies leave contact conditions unresolved, so force- and tactile-critical steps such as tip attachment or precise insertion fail despite accurate visual prediction.

## Method

- ==Mixture-of-transformers== backbone jointly trains a slow video expert and fast action expert via ==video-action flow matching==, sharing layers while keeping separate parameters and token streams.
- An ==observation-conditioned chunk K/V editor== queries cached video context with ==DINOv3== observation features, then applies zero-initialized gated residual corrections, enabling ==asynchronous duplex inference== without regenerating predictions.
- ==Domain soft prompts== plus per-domain encoders and validity masks unify a 37-dimensional state-action interface; ==contact-aware post-training== jointly denoises future actions, six-axis wrenches, and tactile channels.

## Results

- LIBERO average reaches **98.6%** across four suites, ahead of LingBot-VA at **98.5%** and Fast-WAM at **97.6%**; RoboTwin 2.0-Full averages **93.12%** over 50 bimanual tasks.
- RoboTwin Clean2Random averages **75.60%**, exceeding GigaBrain-0.7 by **8.30 points**; real-world MoF synthesis progress rises from **50.2%** to **68.4%** and pipetting from **46.7%** to **65.3%**.
- Critical-path latency of **60.73 ms** yields **16.47 Hz** updates, a **3.13x** speedup over Fast-WAM; adding EgoLab video to 50-hour pretraining lifts randomized success **8.24 points**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating prediction and control timescales, with cached context cheaply re-adapted to each new observation, buys responsiveness without discarding long-horizon predictive influence.
> - Action-free egocentric video can supervise the predictive backbone and helps most out of distribution, suggesting generalization gains come from diverse observation rather than action labels.
> - Millimeter-precision terminal steps such as button pressing still collapse, and a 15-point clean-to-randomized gap persists; pretraining reduces but does not remove these failures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position Within the Broader Research Landscape

InternW0 is situated at the intersection of three research areas: world models, vision-language-action (VLA) models, and world-action models (WAMs). Traditional world models learn representations or predictions of how an environment evolves, often for planning and model-based control. VLA models instead focus primarily on mapping visual observations and language instructions directly to robot actions. WAMs attempt to combine these perspectives by modeling future visual states while also generating executable actions.

The paper argues that current WAMs face three related problems. First, physical interaction involves more than visual information; proprioception, force, tactile feedback, and embodiment-specific action semantics can be important, particularly during contact-rich manipulation. Second, visual prediction and action control operate at different temporal requirements. Long-horizon video prediction is computationally expensive, whereas action generation must respond quickly to new observations. Third, real-world robots operate under partial observation and external disturbances, meaning that a previously generated plan can become outdated while it is being executed.

InternW0 addresses these issues through a physical world model architecture that combines future video prediction with continuous robot control, while processing the two components asynchronously. It also extends pretraining across heterogeneous robot datasets and human egocentric laboratory videos. The work therefore reflects a broader movement toward general-purpose embodied foundation models that learn from diverse physical interaction data and transfer across robots, tasks, and environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Connecting Prediction to Action

The central objective is to construct a model in which predictions about future physical states remain useful for ongoing action. The authors define three criteria for a practical physical world model: predictive accuracy, decision relevance, and timeliness. A prediction is not sufficient if it is accurate but arrives too late to affect control, or if it does not preserve the information needed to choose an action.

InternW0 is designed to estimate a compact representation of the local physical state from incomplete observations, predict possible future developments conditioned on actions, and generate control commands based on both predicted context and newly observed feedback. The model is intended to maintain a closed-loop interaction cycle in which the robot observes, predicts, acts, observes the consequences, and updates its next action.

#### 2.2. Supporting Heterogeneous Embodiments and Modalities

The paper aims to train one shared model across single-arm, dual-arm, mobile-manipulation, simulated, and human egocentric data. Since these sources have different action spaces, sensor configurations, and robot morphologies, InternW0 uses domain-specific interfaces, validity masks, and learned soft prompts. The goal is to share general physical knowledge without assuming that every robot exposes identical sensors or uses identical action conventions.

The authors also seek to incorporate nonvisual physical information. Force and tactile measurements can reveal whether an object has been grasped, whether contact has been established, and whether a tool is experiencing excessive resistance. This is particularly relevant to pipetting, insertion, compliant grasping, and other tasks in which visual observations alone may not fully identify the state of contact.

#### 2.3. Improving Responsiveness During Interaction

A further motivation is reducing the control latency caused by repeatedly recomputing expensive future predictions. InternW0 separates a slower predictive process from a faster action process. The predictive component provides longer-horizon context, while the action component updates commands using the latest visual and physical observations. This arrangement is intended to preserve the benefits of future modeling without placing video generation on the critical path of every action update.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

InternW0 uses a mixture-of-transformers architecture with two principal experts:

1. A high-capacity Video-DiT expert predicts future visual dynamics.
2. A lightweight Action-DiT expert generates continuous robot action chunks.

The video expert processes visual observations and language to construct predictive context. The action expert receives this context together with the latest visual observation, proprioceptive state, language instruction, embodiment-specific inputs, and, during contact-aware post-training, force or tactile histories.

Video frames are encoded using a frozen Wan VAE, while current chunk-level observations are encoded using a frozen DINOv3 encoder. Language instructions are represented by precomputed text embeddings. Trainable projections map these inputs into the token spaces used by the two experts.

The model uses a unified 37-dimensional action and state interface. Each arm is represented by seven joint values, three end-effector translation dimensions, six rotation-6D dimensions, and one gripper dimension. Three additional dimensions represent mobile-base motion. Missing channels are zero-filled and accompanied by validity masks so that unavailable measurements do not contribute to the loss.

#### 3.2. Asynchronous Duplex Inference

The video expert periodically generates a longer-horizon predictive representation. Its layerwise key/value features are cached and reused by the action expert. Instead of regenerating video predictions for every action update, InternW0 applies an observation-conditioned context-routing mechanism.

For each action chunk, the latest visual observation is encoded and summarized into routing queries. These queries retrieve relevant information from the cached video context. A second attention operation then routes the retrieved information back to the original video-token positions. A lightweight projection produces residual corrections to the cached keys and values. A learned gate controls the magnitude of these corrections, and the residual branch is initialized as an identity mapping.

This mechanism allows each action chunk to obtain an observation-conditioned version of the same predictive context. The model can therefore retain long-horizon structure while adapting to deviations between the predicted state and the state actually observed.

#### 3.3. Training Objectives

Both experts are trained using continuous flow matching. The video expert receives future-video supervision, while the action expert receives action supervision when action labels are available. Egocentric videos contribute to the video objective even though they do not contain robot actions.

During action-supervised training, a second independently noised video stream provides conditioning features to the action expert. Gradients from the action loss propagate through the context-routing module into the shared video expert. Consequently, the visual representation is trained not only to reconstruct future video but also to provide information useful for control.

For contact-rich tasks, the action target is expanded to include actions, force or torque signals, and tactile representations. Historical contact signals are provided as conditioning, while future contact signals are predicted jointly with future actions. The action outputs are executed, whereas predicted contact signals serve as anticipated feedback for manipulation.

#### 3.4. Data and Training Pipeline

The pretraining corpus contains approximately 7,233.5 hours across seven datasets and 811,969 episodes. It includes:

- 3,494.3 hours of simulated InternData-A1 data.
- 2,620.0 hours of real AgibotWorld data.
- 438.1 hours of RoboCOIN data.
- 314.9 hours of Galaxea data.
- 275.4 hours of human egocentric laboratory video from the newly collected EgoLab dataset.
- 70.3 hours of MolmoAct data.
- 20.5 hours of RoboDojo simulation data.

EgoLab contains head-mounted video of laboratory activities such as pipetting, weighing, titration, filtration, grinding, stirring, and glassware cleaning. The videos are used through the video-only pathway and are intended to provide procedural knowledge about laboratory manipulation.

The datasets are sampled using square-root weighting based on the number of effective anchors, reducing the dominance of the largest corpora. Training uses a 15 Hz model-time convention. Robot samples contain 13 multi-view frames and 64 action steps divided into four 16-step chunks. The distributed training system uses Ray for execution management, FSDP2 for parameter sharding, BF16 computation with FP32 master parameters, and activation checkpointing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

On the LIBERO benchmark, InternW0 achieves an average success rate of 98.6% across spatial, object, goal, and long-horizon task suites. Its suite-level results are 99.4% for Spatial, 99.4% for Object, 98.6% for Goal, and 97.0% for Long.

On RoboTwin 2.0-Full, which includes clean and randomized demonstrations during training, InternW0 obtains 93.20% success on clean scenes and 93.04% on randomized scenes, for an average of 93.12%. The small difference between the two conditions indicates stable performance when randomization is present during both training and evaluation.

The Clean2Random setting is more demanding because only clean demonstrations are used for post-training and randomization is introduced at evaluation. InternW0 achieves 83.20% on clean scenes and 68.0% on randomized scenes, producing a 75.60% average. Although the 15.2-percentage-point gap shows that unseen visual variation remains difficult, the model records the highest average among the listed baselines in this evaluation.

A limited-data ablation indicates that adding 50 hours of EgoLab video to approximately 50 hours of robot data improves clean success from 72.13% to 76.40% and randomized success from 13.73% to 21.97%. The larger gain on randomized scenes suggests that egocentric video can contribute to generalization beyond the fine-tuning distribution.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot and Wet-Lab Tasks

InternW0 is evaluated on four dual-arm tasks and one dexterous-hand task. Its results are:

- Make Sandwich: 73.3% episode success.
- Pick Industrial Parts: 82.7% object-level success.
- Sort Tubes: 88.9% object-level success.
- Metal–organic framework preparation: 68.4% progress rate across 15 subtasks.
- Quantitative pipetting: 65.3% progress rate across five subtasks.

Compared with the reported π0.5 baseline, InternW0 performs similarly on sandwich assembly, improves industrial-part sorting from 53.1% to 82.7%, and improves the metal–organic framework workflow from 50.2% to 68.4%. In quantitative pipetting, it achieves higher performance on four of five subtasks, including tip attachment, aspiration, dispensing, and tip ejection. These tasks rely on contact-aware force–position control and dexterous hand–arm coordination.

The subtask analysis shows that InternW0 maintains higher completion rates during the later stages of the multi-step laboratory workflow, although it still experiences a decline during precise button pressing. The paper attributes several qualitative improvements to visual pretraining, predictive context, and force-aware online adjustment.

#### 4.3. Inference Efficiency

On the same RTX 5090D hardware, the reported critical-path latency is 60.73 milliseconds, corresponding to 16.47 action-generation updates per second. Fast-WAM records 190.00 milliseconds and 5.26 Hz, while Motus records 1,866.10 milliseconds and 0.54 Hz under the reported protocol. InternW0’s measurement excludes the slower asynchronous video-plan generation, which runs separately from the action-generation path.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InternW0 contributes an architectural approach for combining long-horizon physical prediction with responsive robot control. Its main significance lies in treating computation frequency as part of world-model design rather than solely as an implementation issue. Cached predictive context and observation-conditioned routing offer a way to reuse expensive predictions while still incorporating new observations.

The heterogeneous pretraining strategy also has implications for embodied foundation models. Combining robot trajectories with action-free egocentric video may allow models to learn physical and procedural priors from sources that do not provide identical supervision. EgoLab further demonstrates a pathway toward models specialized for scientific and laboratory workflows.

Potential applications include general-purpose manipulation, automated laboratory experimentation, compliant tool use, and cross-embodiment transfer. However, the results should be interpreted with several qualifications. Most simulation results require task-specific post-training, real-world evaluation uses 15 trials per task, and the paper does not provide exhaustive uncertainty, safety, or long-duration deployment analyses. The Clean2Random performance gap also indicates that environmental variation remains a significant challenge. Future work identified by the authors includes broader tactile and geometric sensing, uncertainty-calibrated prediction, continual closed-loop adaptation, transfer to new instruments and robots, and smaller models suitable for embedded deployment.
