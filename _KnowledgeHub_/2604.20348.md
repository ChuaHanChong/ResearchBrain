# Bimanual Robot Manipulation via Multi-Agent In-Context Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sapienza University of Rome and TU Darmstadt developed BiCICLe, a leader-follower framework enabling standard Large Language Models to perform bimanual robot manipulation tasks using in-context learning, without task-specific training. The system achieved a 70.5% average success rate on the TWIN benchmark, surpassing several supervised methods, and demonstrated real-world deployment on a bimanual Franka Panda robot with a 53.3% success rate across three tasks.

## Problem

- Bimanual robot manipulation tasks are challenging due to high-dimensional joint action spaces and critical inter-arm coordination requirements.
- Existing learning-based methods, like Imitation Learning, necessitate extensive, task-specific datasets for effective bimanual policy development.
- Directly extending single-arm In-Context Learning (ICL) to bimanual tasks leads to context window overload and inability to infer complex coordinated actions for two arms.

## Method

- The **BiCICLe** framework formulates bimanual control as a ==multi-agent leader-follower problem==, using ==standard LLMs without fine-tuning== (in-context learning).
- Bimanual action space is decomposed into ==sequential, conditioned single-arm predictions==, where the follower arm's trajectory is ==explicitly conditioned on the leader's complete predicted trajectory==.
- Action spaces are ==discretized into integer tokens== for end-effector position, orientation, and gripper state, while observations are ==text-based dictionaries of object 3D positions==.

## Results

- **BiCICLe** achieved a **70.5%** average success rate on the TWIN benchmark, outperforming training-free baselines by **6.1 percentage points** and several supervised methods.
- The framework demonstrated out-of-distribution generalization with a **54.5%** success rate on novel bimanual tasks, surpassing fine-tuned supervised baselines.
- Real-world deployment on a bimanual Franka Panda robot achieved a **53.3%** average success rate across three tasks, showing effective sim-to-real transfer without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing bimanual control into a leader-follower paradigm enables standard LLMs to effectively manage high-dimensional action spaces and maintain inter-arm coordination.
> - Explicitly conditioning the follower arm's actions on the leader's predicted trajectory is critical for achieving robust coordination in bimanual manipulation.
> - Text-based semantic object representations, including discretized 3D positions, provide more reliable information for LLM-driven in-context learning than visual keypoints or raw image inputs.
