# Bimanual Robot Manipulation via Multi-Agent In-Context Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sapienza University of Rome and TU Darmstadt developed BiCICLe, a leader-follower framework enabling standard Large Language Models to perform bimanual robot manipulation tasks using in-context learning, without task-specific training. The system achieved a 70.5% average success rate on the TWIN benchmark, surpassing several supervised methods, and demonstrated real-world deployment on a bimanual Franka Panda robot with a 53.3% success rate across three tasks.

## Problem

- Bimanual robot manipulation tasks are challenging due to high-dimensional joint action spaces and critical inter-arm coordination requirements.
- Existing learning-based methods, like Imitation Learning, necessitate extensive, task-specific datasets for effective bimanual policy development.
- Directly extending single-arm In-Context Learning (ICL) to bimanual tasks leads to context window overload and inability to infer complex coordinated actions for two arms.

## Method

- The **BiCICLe** framework formulates bimanual control as a ==multi-agent leader-follower problem==, using ==standard LLMs without fine-tuning== (in-context learning).
- Bimanual action space is decomposed into ==sequential, conditioned single-arm predictions==, where the follower arm's trajectory is ==explicitly conditioned on the leader's complete predicted trajectory==.
- Action spaces are ==discretized into integer tokens== for end-effector position, orientation, and gripper state, while observations are ==text-based dictionaries of object 3D positions==.

## Results

- **BiCICLe** achieved a **70.5%** average success rate on the TWIN benchmark, outperforming training-free baselines by **6.1 percentage points** and several supervised methods.
- The framework demonstrated out-of-distribution generalization with a **54.5%** success rate on novel bimanual tasks, surpassing fine-tuned supervised baselines.
- Real-world deployment on a bimanual Franka Panda robot achieved a **53.3%** average success rate across three tasks, showing effective sim-to-real transfer without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing bimanual control into a leader-follower paradigm enables standard LLMs to effectively manage high-dimensional action spaces and maintain inter-arm coordination.
> - Explicitly conditioning the follower arm's actions on the leader's predicted trajectory is critical for achieving robust coordination in bimanual manipulation.
> - Text-based semantic object representations, including discretized 3D positions, provide more reliable information for LLM-driven in-context learning than visual keypoints or raw image inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual robot manipulation tasks, such as lifting a tray or unscrewing a bottle cap, present challenges to robotic systems due to the need for synchronized positions, orientations, and forces between two arms. This complexity is amplified by a rapidly growing joint action space and the stringent temporal synchronization requirements, making error compensation between arms critical. Traditional learning-based methods, such as Imitation Learning (IL) and offline Reinforcement Learning (RL), typically necessitate large, task-specific datasets to adequately capture these dependencies.

The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) and In-Context Learning (ICL) offers an alternative paradigm for robot control. ICL allows LLMs to adapt to new tasks without explicit gradient updates or extensive training, by serializing scene information and robot actions as text within a prompt. This approach leverages the LLM's reasoning capabilities as a generalist planner, abstracting away raw perceptual details for broader generalizability. Prior work, including RoboPrompt and KAT, has demonstrated the utility of ICL for single-arm manipulation tasks. However, extending this paradigm to bimanual manipulation introduces significant challenges. A naive approach of concatenating both arms' commands into a single, monolithic joint action sequence can overwhelm standard LLM context windows due to increased token length and complexity, leading to incoherent plans. Conversely, treating the arms as entirely independent agents fails to account for the necessary inter-arm coordination, which is often crucial for successful bimanual task completion.

This research addresses the identified gap by proposing a framework that enables ICL for bimanual manipulation, specifically tackling the challenges of high-dimensional action spaces and inter-arm coordination without requiring task-specific training. It positions itself as a development in LLM-driven embodied control, moving beyond single-arm capabilities and traditional data-intensive learning methods for multi-arm systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable standard, off-the-shelf Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform few-shot bimanual manipulation tasks without requiring any task-specific fine-tuning. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the challenges of bimanual control:** Bimanual manipulation inherently involves a high-dimensional joint action space and requires tight inter-arm coordination. Errors in one arm can cascade and necessitate complex compensation from the other, making control policies difficult to learn and implement with traditional methods that demand extensive, task-specific datasets.
2. **Leveraging In-Context Learning (ICL) for robot control:** ICL with foundation models offers a compelling alternative to traditional learning paradigms by allowing robots to adapt to new tasks through prompt examples, eliminating the need for gradient updates and massive paired image-action datasets. This approach promises zero-shot or few-shot generalizability, which is a significant advantage in robotics.
3. **Overcoming ICL limitations in bimanual contexts:** Existing ICL frameworks for robotics, such as RoboPrompt and KAT, are primarily designed for single-arm manipulation. Direct extension to bimanual tasks encounters significant hurdles:
 - **Context window saturation:** Concatenating commands for two arms into a single sequence rapidly increases token length, potentially exceeding the LLM's context window limits.
 - **Increased pattern complexity:** The joint action space dramatically increases the complexity of patterns the LLM must infer from demonstrations, often leading to incoherent or ineffective plans.
 - **Lack of coordination:** Treating arms as independent agents fails to enforce the necessary inter-arm coordination, which is critical for many bimanual tasks (e.g., one arm needs to be positioned to receive an object from the other).
4. **Developing a structured approach for multi-agent coordination:** The research aims to develop a novel architectural design that facilitates coordination between robotic arms within an LLM-based ICL framework. This involves decomposing the complex bimanual control problem into a more manageable structure that LLMs can process effectively, thereby enforcing inter-arm consistency while reducing the reasoning burden on each agent.
5. **Demonstrating generalization and real-world applicability:** Beyond simulation, a key motivation is to demonstrate that such an ICL-driven bimanual manipulation policy can generalize to novel, out-of-distribution tasks without retraining and successfully transfer to real-world hardware, showcasing its practical utility and robustness to real-world complexities.

In summary, the research seeks to bridge the gap between the general reasoning capabilities of LLMs via ICL and the specific demands of bimanual robotic manipulation, providing a training-free and generalizable solution to a complex control problem.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Bimanual Coordinated In-Context Learning (BiCICLe), addresses bimanual manipulation by formulating it as a multi-agent leader-follower problem. This approach decouples the bimanual action space into sequential, conditioned single-arm predictions, leveraging standard LLMs without fine-tuning.

**Problem Formulation and Representations:**

- **Task Setup:** Experiments are conducted in the CoppeliaSim simulation environment using the TWIN benchmark, which features a bimanual Franka Panda robot. The robot observes the scene through six RGB-D cameras. Episodes conclude upon successful execution of a sequence of *K* keyframe actions for both arms.
- **Action Discretization:** The action space is discretized into integer tokens. Each arm operates in a 7-dimensional end-effector action space:
 - **Position:** The 3D workspace is bounded and discretized into a 100x100x100 voxel grid, mapping continuous positions to integer indices (0-99).
 - **Orientation:** End-effector orientations (quaternions) are converted to Euler angles and discretized into 5-degree bins, yielding rotation indices (0-71).
 - **Gripper State:** A binary value (0 or 1) represents closed or open gripper states.
 - A single-arm action is a 7-tuple. A bimanual action is the concatenation of two such 7-tuples (14-dimensional).
- **Observation Representation:** Observations consist of the 3D positions of task-relevant objects. Object positions are computed by fusing segmentation masks and point clouds from all six cameras. Masked point clouds are merged, downsampled using voxel grid filtering, and their centroids are discretized to voxel coordinates. The observation is represented as a text-based dictionary mapping object names to their discretized 3D positions (e.g., `{obj1: [x1, y1, z1]}`). Object orientations were intentionally excluded as they were found to degrade performance in simulation.
- **In-Context Demonstrations:** A demonstration D$_i$ comprises an initial scene observation o$_i$ paired with K$_i$ bimanual keyframe actions A$_i$. Keyframes are identified heuristically by detecting gripper state changes, zero joint velocities, or episode termination. For inference, N=10 demonstrations are serialized into a text prompt, prepended to the test observation (e.g., "o$_1$>A$_1$..., o$_N$>A$_N$, o_test>").

**BiCICLe: A Leader-Follower LLM Architecture:**

BiCICLe provides inter-arm coordination without increasing the per-agent action space or the context length for individual LLM calls. It factors the bimanual prediction problem into two sequential 7-dimensional predictions linked by explicit conditioning.

- **Phase 1: Leader Prediction:**
 - One arm is designated as the "leader" (default: right arm).
 - Bimanual demonstrations are processed to retain only the leader arm's actions.
 - The leader LLM receives a system prompt tailored for single-arm control, followed by the single-arm ICL demonstrations and the test observation.
 - The LLM generates the predicted full trajectory for the leader arm ($\hat{A}_L$). The LLM infers the task objective through pattern recognition in the demonstrations without explicit textual descriptions.
- **Phase 2: Follower Prediction:**
 - The follower LLM predicts its actions conditioned on the leader's predicted trajectory.
 - In-context demonstrations are restructured: the leader arm's ground-truth actions for each demonstration are embedded directly into the observation dictionary, creating an "augmented observation."
 - The demonstration actions are replaced with the follower's single-arm actions.
 - For the test observation, the leader's *predicted* actions ($\hat{A}_L$) are inserted to form the follower's prompt.
 - The follower LLM generates the predicted trajectory for the follower arm ($\hat{A}_F$).
- **Action Composition:** The leader and follower trajectories are combined into a bimanual action sequence. If the predicted sequences differ in length, the shorter sequence is extended by repeating its last action to match the longer one.

**Experimental Setup:**

- **Simulation:** The framework was evaluated on 13 tasks from the TWIN benchmark using CoppeliaSim. Experiments utilized GPT-5-mini as the primary LLM backbone, with ablations conducted using Qwen 2.5 7B.
- **Real-World Deployment:** BiCICLe was deployed on a physical bimanual Franka Panda robot system with a Stereolabs ZED X camera. For real-world tasks, 15 kinesthetic demonstrations were collected (10 used in prompt). Object poses were estimated using FoundationPose, and the workspace bounds were adapted. Object yaw was included in real-world observations due to its reliability. Predicted 6D end-effector poses and gripper states were executed via MoveIt.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding BiCICLe's performance in simulation and real-world scenarios, along with insights from ablation studies.

**Simulation Performance on TWIN Benchmark:**
BiCICLe achieved an average success rate of 70.5% across 13 tasks from the TWIN benchmark. This performance exceeded the best training-free baseline, RoboPrompt-DA, by 6.1 percentage points. Notably, BiCICLe also surpassed several supervised methods, including ACT (5.9%), PerAct$^2$ (16.8%), $\pi_0$-keypose (43.7%), and AnyBimanual (32.0%). On specific tasks like Push Box, Dual Buttons, and Handover, BiCICLe even outperformed the reported supervised state-of-the-art method, 3DFA. This indicates that a training-free ICL policy, predicting sparse keyposes from semantic object coordinates, can be competitive with methods requiring extensive training datasets.

**Analysis of Action Representation (SA vs. DA vs. BiCICLe):**
A comparison of monolithic joint action prediction (RoboPrompt-SA) and independent per-arm predictions (RoboPrompt-DA) revealed a trade-off. RoboPrompt-SA, which attempts a single 14-dimensional prediction, performed better on tightly coupled symmetric tasks (e.g., Push Box, Lift Ball) due to its ability to capture correlated motion. Conversely, RoboPrompt-DA, with two independent 7-dimensional predictions, was stronger on tasks requiring per-arm precision or distinct roles (e.g., Lift Tray, Pick Plate). BiCICLe addresses this trade-off by maintaining the 7-dimensional per-arm prediction format (like DA) but reintroducing coordination through explicit conditioning of the follower arm on the leader's trajectory. This approach resulted in an additional 6.1 percentage point gain over RoboPrompt-DA, particularly benefiting tasks requiring both coordination and manageable dimensionality, such as Straighten Rope.

**Observation Representations:**
ICL baselines utilizing DINO-ViT keypoint correspondences (KAT-SA, KAT-DA) or interleaved RGB+depth images (VLM-LF) significantly underperformed text-based methods. This suggests that semantic object identities, as provided in text, are more informative for ICL in these bimanual tasks than appearance-based keypoints or raw visual input, especially where precise object-relative positioning is crucial.

**Generalization to New Tasks:**
BiCICLe demonstrated out-of-distribution generalization on two novel bimanual tasks ("Close Jar" and "Take Item Out of Box") not present in the TWIN benchmark. It achieved an average success rate of 54.5%, substantially outperforming 3DFA-fft (10.0%) and 3DFA-LoRA (15.0%), even when these supervised methods were fine-tuned with N=10 demonstrations. This highlights BiCICLe's ability to adapt to entirely new tasks solely through prompt demonstrations, without requiring task-specific data or gradient updates.

**Real-World Deployment:**
The framework successfully transferred to a physical bimanual Franka Panda system without hardware-specific retraining. Across three real-world tasks (Lift Box, Open Pot, Cleanup), BiCICLe achieved an average success rate of 53.3%. This performance exceeded KAT-DA (23.3%) and RoboPrompt-DA (33.3%). KAT-DA's lower performance in the real world was attributed to the inherent limitations of appearance-based keypoint representations, which proved sensitive to real-world factors like texture, illumination, and reflectance. Failures for BiCICLe were primarily linked to grasp misalignments from coarse discretization and sensitivity to pose-estimation noise, particularly with small or occluded objects.

**Ablation Studies Insights (Appendix):**
- **Backbone Agnosticism:** BiCICLe's architectural benefits were maintained even with a smaller open-source LLM (Qwen 2.5 7B), though with an overall performance reduction, confirming its robustness across different LLM scales.
- **Leader Arm Assignment:** Switching the leader arm had a modest effect on overall performance, indicating that the sequential conditioning is largely agnostic to the specific arm assignment, with minor performance shifts attributable to the intrinsic handedness of certain tasks.
- **Sequential Arm Prediction:** Pure dimensionality reduction (Sequential Arms) without explicit leader-follower conditioning was found to be insufficient, performing 5.5 percentage points below BiCICLe, confirming the importance of the explicit conditioning channel for inter-arm coordination.
- **Test-Time Scaling:** While "Best-of-N" slightly improved performance (+0.7 pp) at a significant computational cost, "Multi-Turn Conversation" substantially degraded performance (-13.0 pp). This degradation was linked to corrupted leader predictions and context-induced drift, suggesting that simple accumulation of prior predictions in chat history is detrimental for this action-prediction interface.
- **Observation Details:** Including object rotations in observations degraded performance (-5.3 pp) due to high variance across demonstrations and issues with Euler-angle discontinuities, which disrupted pattern recognition. Increasing discretization resolution provided only modest improvements on precision-sensitive tasks (+2.8 pp), indicating other bottlenecks. "Prune" method for point-cloud extraction was most accurate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of BiCICLe represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for systems requiring bimanual coordination. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **First Multi-Agent ICL for Bimanual Manipulation:** BiCICLe is presented as the first framework that enables standard LLMs to address bimanual manipulation tasks using In-Context Learning. This addresses a recognized gap where prior ICL methods were largely confined to single-arm scenarios, struggling with the increased dimensionality and coordination demands of bimanual control.
2. **Training-Free Policy Development:** A core impact is the demonstration that sophisticated bimanual policies can be realized without any task-specific training or gradient updates. This capability drastically reduces the data requirements and computational overhead traditionally associated with developing policies for complex robotic tasks, offering a more agile and resource-efficient approach.
3. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The framework exhibits robust out-of-distribution generalization. Its ability to adapt to entirely new bimanual tasks (e.g., "Close Jar," "Take Item Out of Box") through prompt demonstrations alone, outperforming fine-tuned supervised methods, underscores the inherent generalizability of LLM-based ICL when appropriately structured. This suggests a pathway toward more adaptable and versatile robotic systems.
4. **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment of BiCICLe on a physical bimanual Franka Panda system without hardware-specific retraining validates its practical utility. This transferability from simulation to real-world hardware, across tasks demanding various levels of bimanual coupling, indicates the framework's robustness and potential for industrial and service robotics applications.
5. **Effective Solution to Coordination and Dimensionality Challenges:** The leader-follower decomposition is a key methodological contribution. By framing bimanual control as sequential, conditioned single-arm predictions, BiCICLe effectively manages the high-dimensional action space and preserves crucial inter-arm coordination, which is often a bottleneck in multi-arm robotics.
6. **Advancement in Embodied AI with LLMs:** This work contributes to the broader trend of integrating large language models into embodied AI. It moves beyond simple task-level planning, demonstrating LLMs' capacity for direct, low-level action prediction in complex multi-robot scenarios. This paves the way for future research into more intricate multi-agent coordination strategies and LLM-robot interfaces.

The limitations, such as reliance on accurate perception systems, the precision ceiling of discrete keypose interfaces, and challenges with conversational refinement, also highlight important avenues for future research. Nevertheless, BiCICLe establishes ICL as a viable and powerful paradigm for developing general-purpose, adaptable bimanual manipulation capabilities in robotics.
