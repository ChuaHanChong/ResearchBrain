# CausalFlow: Causal Attribution and Counterfactual Repair for LLM Agent Failures

> [!summary] Summary
> CausalFlow turns failed LLM-agent traces into minimal repairs by intervening on each logged step and re-executing downstream computation, scoring steps by whether the swap flips the verifier to success, then ranking corrections by token-level minimality and confirming them with a three-agent consensus check. It repairs 42.7% of failed executions across four benchmarks and yields reusable contrastive supervision pairs.

## Problem

- Agent supervision is usually outcome-level only, leaving it ambiguous which intermediate decision in a long reasoning-plus-tool trace actually caused the failure.
- Retry, self-critique and full-regeneration repair strategies never test causal responsibility, so they neither isolate the failure-inducing step nor guarantee minimal corrections.
- Preference-optimization signals are defined at trajectory or response level, and log-based failure attribution reaches only 14.2% step-level accuracy in prior benchmarks.

## Method

- Causal Responsibility Score: a binary interventional quantity, set to 1 if any of K=3 LLM-proposed step replacements flips the verifier after re-executing all affected descendants.
- Counterfactual repair ranks successful interventions by a position-wise token-match minimality score with length penalty, selecting the smallest edit and emitting a (wrong step, corrected step) pair.
- Three-agent validation (proposer, critic, meta-critic) forms a consensus score thresholded at 0.5, over TraceLogger typed steps (reasoning, tool call, response, memory, final answer) with explicit dependency indices.

## Results

- Repair rates span 21.9-52.4% across benchmarks, 555 of 1299 failed executions (42.7%): GSM8K 52.4%, MedBrowseComp 44.5%, MBPP 41.2%, SealQA Hard 21.9%.
- Only method improving accuracy on all four tasks: MedBrowseComp 30.8% to 61.6% (+30.8pp), SealQA Hard 42.5% to 55.1%, while critique baselines lose 1.4-3.5pp on browsing.
- Minimality stays 0.79-0.87 versus 0.01 for Self-Reflection on SealQA; Self-Refine repairs 80.3% of MBPP failures but at 0.68 minimality versus 0.82; judge audit precision 90.9%/86.2%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Most agent failures are localized, not systemic: one or two steps flip the outcome, so failed traces already carry step-level supervision.
> - Repair rate alone is a misleading metric; edit locality must be measured too, since critique loops win headline rates by regenerating whole answers.
> - Interventional debugging demands typed dependency-annotated traces, costing 13.1pp raw accuracy, and cannot fix failures whose root cause is missing retrieved information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly utilized for multi-step tasks that involve complex reasoning, tool usage, and interaction with environments. A common challenge in these systems is that when failures occur, the feedback typically provided is at the outcome level (e.g., final answer correctness). This outcome-level feedback often fails to identify the specific intermediate decision or step within the execution trace that caused the failure, leading to ambiguity in credit assignment. This ambiguity limits both the reliability of the agents and the potential for systematic learning from their failures.

Existing approaches to addressing LLM agent failures include heuristic retries, critique-driven rewriting, or regeneration of entire solutions. Examples of such methods are Reflexion, which uses verbal reinforcement learning for self-critique; Self-Refine, which employs iterative generation with model-produced feedback; and Self-Reflection, which generates structured error diagnoses. Other systems like ReAct, Tree-of-Thoughts, and CRITIC enhance reasoning, search, and external validation. While these methods can improve reliability, they generally do not explicitly test causal responsibility within structured execution traces. Consequently, they may not isolate the precise failure-inducing step or guarantee that repairs are minimal counterfactual corrections.

Within the landscape of step-level supervision and preference learning, techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and various preference optimization frameworks (e.g., DPO, RRHF, ORPO, ILQL) aim to improve credit assignment. However, these methods typically operate at the trajectory or response level, still leaving ambiguity about the specific intermediate step causing a failure.

More targeted work on failure diagnosis and causal intervention includes MAST, which taxonomizes failure modes, and Who&When, which benchmarks failure attribution accuracy from logs. DoVer is a closely related concurrent work that focuses on auto-debugging for multi-agent systems, requiring framework-level checkpointing and re-planning. CausalFlow differentiates itself from DoVer by targeting single-agent sequential traces without requiring framework modifications, computing a purely interventional Causal Responsibility Score (CRS) without an intermediate log-based hypothesis stage, and explicitly ranking repairs by minimality to produce validated contrastive pairs for offline preference optimization. Other causal tracing and model editing techniques (e.g., Meng et al., Chi et al., Kıcıman et al.) typically apply interventional ideas to internal model behavior rather than explicit execution traces.

CausalFlow positions itself by introducing an interventional framework designed to convert failed agent traces into minimal counterfactual repairs and reusable supervision. This approach addresses the limitations of prior methods by systematically identifying failure-inducing steps through counterfactual interventions, generating minimal corrections, and providing structured, localized feedback that can be used for both inference-time repair and training-time supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for CausalFlow stems from the observation that Large Language Model (LLM) agents, despite their capabilities, frequently encounter failures when executing complex, multi-step procedures that involve reasoning, tool invocation, and interaction with various environments. These failures are often recorded or addressed through simple retries, but the underlying causes of breakdown within the execution process remain opaque.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Systematic Causal Attribution:** To develop a principled framework that can identify which specific intermediate steps in an LLM agent's execution trace are causally responsible for a task failure. This moves beyond outcome-level feedback to pinpoint the "where and why" a failure occurred, rather than just "that" it occurred.

2. **Generate Minimal Counterfactual Repairs:** To create a mechanism for generating minimally edited counterfactual repairs for the identified causally responsible steps. These repairs are designed to alter the outcome from failure to success with the smallest possible change to the agent's behavior or reasoning, preserving the overall structure of the trace.

3. **Convert Failures into Reusable Supervision:** To transform failed execution traces into structured, learning-ready supervision signals. Specifically, the framework aims to produce validated contrastive pairs, consisting of a "wrong step" and its "corrected step." This supervision is intended to be valuable for offline preference optimization or reward modeling, enabling agents to learn from their mistakes without extensive manual annotation.

4. **Enable Targeted Test-Time Repair:** To provide a deploy-time mechanism that allows LLM agents to recover from failures during execution. By applying minimal, causally identified repairs, agents can improve their reliability with minimal behavioral drift, reducing the need for full re-execution or heuristic retries.

5. **Empirical Validation across Diverse Domains:** To demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of the CausalFlow framework by evaluating it across multiple benchmarks that span different types of tasks (mathematical reasoning, code generation, question answering, and medical browsing). This objective aims to show that the principles of causal attribution are necessary for reliable improvement across a variety of agent tasks.

In summary, the motivation is to address the ambiguity of failure attribution in LLM agents by providing a systematic, interventional approach that not only diagnoses the root cause of failures but also generates actionable, minimal corrections, thereby enhancing agent reliability and providing valuable learning signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CausalFlow is an interventional framework designed for causal attribution and counterfactual repair of multi-step agent executions. Its methodology comprises four main components: trace modeling, causal attribution via interventional scoring, counterfactual repair, and multi-agent validation.

**Problem Setup**
The framework models LLM agent executions as structured traces, $\tau = (s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_T)$, where each step $s_t$ contains an action (e.g., reasoning text, tool call) and possibly an environment observation. A task-specific verifier, $V(y(\tau), x) \in \{0, 1\}$, indicates success or failure of the induced task output $y(\tau)$ for a given task instance $x$.

The key assumption is a sequential dependency structure where each step $s_t$ depends on all preceding steps ($s_1, \ldots, s_{t-1}$). This implies that intervening on a step $s_i$ requires re-executing all subsequent steps ($s_{i+1}, \ldots, s_T$) to propagate the effects of the intervention. Dependencies are logged from the agent runtime, detailing how tool responses depend on calls, reasoning steps on previous reasoning and observations, and observations on prior actions.

A counterfactual intervention is defined as replacing a step $s_t$ with an alternative $\tilde{s}_t$ and re-executing subsequent steps, resulting in a new trace $\tau[t \leftarrow \tilde{s}_t]$. The goal is to identify steps where such an intervention successfully flips the verifier outcome from failure to success.

**Causal Attribution via Interventions**
Given a failed trace $\tau$ where $V(y(\tau), x) = 0$, CausalFlow identifies causally responsible steps using a Causal Responsibility Score (CRS).

1. **Sequential Intervention:** For each candidate step $s_i$, the framework generates $K$ alternative intervention proposals, $\{s'^{(k)}_i\}_{k=1}^K$. The LLM is prompted with the step's content, dependency context, and failure feedback to generate these minimally edited corrections. Reasoning steps might correct logical errors, while tool-call interventions might adjust arguments or tool selection.
2. **Causal Responsibility Score (CRS):** The CRS for a step $s_i$ is defined as:
 $CRS(s_i) = \max_{k \in \{1...,K\}} I[V(y(\tau[i \leftarrow s'^{(k)}_i]), x) = 1]$
 This means $CRS(s_i) = 1$ if at least one replacement of $s_i$ results in the verifier outcome flipping from failure to success, indicating that $s_i$ is a causally responsible step.
3. **Re-execution and Validation:** After an intervention, the modified trace $\tau[i \leftarrow s'^{(k)}_i]$ is re-executed. For tasks with deterministic executors (e.g., Python interpreter for code generation), validation is deterministic. For others (e.g., mathematical reasoning, question answering), predictive re-execution using an LLM-based outcome model is used.

**Counterfactual Repair**
For steps identified with $CRS(s_i) = 1$, CausalFlow generates validated counterfactual repairs.

1. **Minimality Ranking:** To ensure that repairs are localized and interpretable, successful interventions ($s'_i$ that flip the outcome to success) are ranked by a minimality score. This score is calculated using position-wise token matching with a length penalty:
 $Minimality(s_i, s'_i) = \frac{m}{L} \left(1 - \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{||x| - |y||}{L}\right)$
 where $x$ and $y$ are the token sequences of $s_i$ and $s'_i$ respectively, $L = \max(|x|, |y|)$, and $m$ is the count of position-wise token matches. Higher scores indicate smaller edits.
2. **Optimal Repair Selection:** The repair $s^{\star}_i$ is selected as the one maximizing the minimality score among all successful interventions for that step:
 $s^{\star}_i = \arg \max_{s'_i} Minimality(s_i, s'_i) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad V(y(\tau[i \leftarrow s'_i]), x) = 1$
Each selected validated repair $(s_i, s^{\star}_i)$ forms a contrastive supervision pair.

**Multi-Agent Validation**
To mitigate noise from LLM-generated attributions, a three-agent validation system is employed:
- **Agent A:** Proposes causal steps.
- **Agent B:** Critiques the proposed attributions.
- **Agent C:** Meta-critiques both A's and B's judgments.
Each critic returns an agreement label (AGREE, PARTIAL, DISAGREE) and a confidence score. These are mapped to numerical values ($1, 0.5, 0$ respectively). A Consensus Score is then computed:
$Consensus(s_i) = \frac{1}{3} \left(CRS(s_i) + \sum_{j \in \{B,C\}} c_j a_j\right)$
Steps with Consensus($s_i$) $\geq \tau_c = 0.5$ are retained as confirmed causal steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The CausalFlow framework was evaluated across four diverse benchmarks: GSM8K (mathematical reasoning), MBPP (code generation), SealQA Hard (complex web-based question answering), and MedBrowseComp (medical browsing), encompassing over 3,000 task instances.

**Agent Configuration and Baselines:**
For each benchmark, a representative LLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash Lite for GSM8K, GPT-5 Chat for MBPP, Gemini 3 Flash Preview for SealQA Hard and MedBrowseComp) was used with appropriate tools. CausalFlow was compared against Direct (single-pass chain-of-thought), Self-Refine, and Self-Reflection, with all baselines using the same model checkpoints and temperature settings. CausalFlow's intervention protocol used $K=3$ proposals per candidate step.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
The performance was assessed using:
- **Repair Rate:** Fraction of failed traces converted to correct executions.
- **Post-Repair Accuracy:** Overall task accuracy after applying each method.
- **Minimality Score:** Position-wise token similarity between original and repaired steps (higher indicates smaller edits).
- **CRS Precision:** How often CausalFlow-flagged steps admit a validated outcome-flipping intervention.

**Main Repair Performance (Table 1):**
- CausalFlow successfully converted a substantial fraction of failed executions into validated minimal repairs, with repair rates ranging from 21.9% (SealQA Hard) to 52.4% (GSM8K). In aggregate, 42.7% of failed executions (555 out of 1299) were repaired.
- Compared to baselines, CausalFlow demonstrated higher repair rates on GSM8K (52.4% vs. 2.9% for Self-Refine, 21.3% for Self-Reflection), MedBrowseComp (44.5% vs. 3.8% and 6.7%), and SealQA Hard (21.9% vs. 16.7% and 17.3%).
- MBPP was an exception, where Self-Refine (80.3%) and Self-Reflection (56.4%) achieved higher repair rates than CausalFlow (41.2%). However, this came at the cost of lower minimality scores.

**Minimality and Localization of Repairs (Table 1):**
- CausalFlow consistently achieved high minimality scores (0.79 to 0.87 across benchmarks), indicating that successful repairs involved small, targeted token-level modifications rather than extensive rewrites. This contrasts with baselines, where minimality scores could be as low as 0.01 (Self-Reflection on SealQA Hard), suggesting near-complete answer regeneration. On MBPP, Self-Refine (0.68) and Self-Reflection (0.65) had substantially lower minimality than CausalFlow (0.82), despite higher repair rates.

**Impact on Overall Accuracy (Table 2):**
- CausalFlow provided consistent accuracy improvements across all four benchmarks. It achieved the largest absolute gains on retrieval-heavy tasks: MedBrowseComp (+30.8 percentage points, from 30.8% to 61.6%) and SealQA Hard (+12.6 percentage points, from 42.5% to 55.1%).
- In contrast, Self-Refine and Self-Reflection showed negative accuracy deltas on MedBrowseComp (-1.4pp and -3.5pp respectively), indicating that global critique-based refinement could degrade performance in complex browsing tasks.
- On GSM8K, CausalFlow’s post-repair accuracy (88.1%) matched the Direct baseline, fully recovering the initial 13.1pp accuracy gap incurred by the structured trace logging requirements.

**Error Pattern and Skill Decomposition (Section 6.4, Appendix C.5):**
- Clustering causally responsible steps revealed interpretable, domain-specific skill deficits. Examples include arithmetic propagation errors, loop construction issues, query formulation errors, and medical terminology misinterpretations. This suggests that causal attribution exposes systematic weaknesses.

**Ablation Studies (Section 6.5, Tables 6-11):**
- **Intervention Sample Count (K):** Repair rates improved up to K=3, with diminishing returns thereafter.
- **Minimality Ranking:** This feature primarily enhanced repair localization rather than raw repair success rate.
- **Minimality Metric Sensitivity:** Lexical and edit-distance metrics showed high agreement (88% on average), while semantic cosine agreement dropped in browsing-heavy domains, indicating edit distance as a more robust choice.
- **Stochasticity:** Three independent runs on a GSM8K subset showed stable outcomes, with a standard deviation of 6.81pp for repair rate and 1.63pp for post-repair accuracy.
- **LLM Judge Accuracy:** Manual audits confirmed high precision for LLM-based grading (90.9% on SealQA Hard, 86.2% on MedBrowseComp).
- **Gold Reference in Repair Prompts:** Providing a gold reference was more beneficial for retrieval-heavy benchmarks (e.g., SealQA Hard, MedBrowseComp) where verifiers are less deterministic and the repair search space is large, but offered negligible or slight negative impact on structured reasoning tasks with executable verifiers.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that CausalFlow effectively converts agent failures into validated minimal repairs, improves downstream task success through targeted intervention, and generates localized supervision signals that are more precise than those from heuristic refinement methods, particularly in complex, retrieval-heavy environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CausalFlow introduces an interventional framework that offers a method for debugging and improving LLM agents. The research findings indicate that a significant proportion of LLM agent failures are localized to specific intermediate steps rather than stemming from systemic issues across an entire trajectory. This suggests that failures can be addressed with minimal modifications, rather than requiring extensive re-generation of solutions.

**Key Contributions and Operational Impact:**
- **Precision in Failure Diagnosis:** By identifying the exact causally responsible steps, CausalFlow provides granular insights into agent behavior, which is a departure from traditional outcome-level feedback or broad critique-based refinement. This precision can reduce the effort required for debugging and lead to more efficient solutions.
- **Minimal and Targeted Repairs:** The generation of minimally edited counterfactual repairs, validated through re-execution, minimizes behavioral drift in the agent. This is particularly valuable for maintaining desired characteristics of the agent's reasoning process while correcting errors.
- **Reusable Supervision for Learning:** The framework converts failed executions into structured contrastive pairs (wrong step, corrected step). These pairs can serve as step-level supervision for offline preference optimization or reward modeling, enabling agents to learn from their mistakes in a targeted manner without requiring extensive manual annotation. This could accelerate the development of more robust LLM agents.
- **Enhanced Reliability at Inference Time:** CausalFlow demonstrates that causal attribution can serve as an inference-time reliability mechanism. By applying targeted interventions, agents can recover from failures during deployment without requiring model parameter updates, leading to improved task success.
- **Effectiveness in Complex Domains:** The framework shows particular strength in retrieval-heavy environments (e.g., SealQA Hard, MedBrowseComp) where heuristic, global refinement methods often struggle or even degrade performance. CausalFlow's ability to isolate errors to specific query formulations or inference transitions makes it more effective in these complex settings.
- **Interpretability and Skill Assessment:** The clustering of causally responsible steps reveals systematic skill deficits (e.g., arithmetic errors, logical flaws in code, query formulation issues). This interpretability can inform targeted training strategies, curriculum design, or specialized fine-tuning efforts, moving beyond generic performance improvements to address specific weaknesses.

**Potential Broader Impact:**
CausalFlow's capabilities for structured causal debugging may contribute to the development of more reliable and interpretable multi-step AI systems. This could have positive societal impacts in domains such as:
- **Education and Programming Assistance:** By pinpointing errors in reasoning or code generation, CausalFlow could help in creating more effective tutoring systems or coding assistants.
- **Information Retrieval and Healthcare Support:** Improved accuracy and reliability in navigating and synthesizing information, especially in critical fields like medicine, could lead to more trustworthy AI tools for professionals and patients.
- **Human Oversight and Transparency:** The ability to understand *why* an agent failed and *where* the error originated can facilitate better human oversight and promote more transparent AI system behavior, which is crucial for high-stakes applications.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
While significant, the work has limitations. The quality of interventions depends on the LLM's ability to generate appropriate corrections, and computational overhead is incurred during re-execution. Retrieval-driven failures where essential information is genuinely absent cannot be fixed by local reasoning. Furthermore, the structured trace logging that enables CausalFlow introduces an initial accuracy cost, albeit recoverable, compared to unstructured approaches. Future work could explore lighter-weight trace formats, approximate CRS computation, integration with retrieval optimization, and extending causal debugging to multi-agent systems.

Overall, CausalFlow represents a step towards transforming LLM agent failures from mere logs into actionable, learning-ready signals, potentially paving the way for more robust, interpretable, and self-improving agent systems.
