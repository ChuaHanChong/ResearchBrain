# Disturbance-aware Motion Planning for Over-actuated Underwater Vehicles Exploiting Actuation Redundancy for High-fidelity 3D Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> An adaptive control framework for over-actuated underwater vehicles mitigates thruster-induced hydrodynamic disturbances during data acquisition, leveraging actuation redundancy to improve sensing performance. The system demonstrated a 55% reduction in 3D reconstruction RMSE and a 67% decrease in near-target particle velocity, achieving a 98.5% success rate for high-fidelity mapping.

## Problem

- Thruster-induced hydrodynamic disturbances (e.g., sediment resuspension, turbulence) degrade sensor data quality (motion blur, signal occlusion) for underwater robots, creating an "actuation-to-perception coupling."
- Conventional underwater robot controllers prioritize vehicle-centric objectives like trajectory tracking, often overlooking the robot's self-induced environmental impact on sensing performance.
- Existing disturbance mitigation strategies (hardware, operational, post-processing) have inherent limitations, failing to prevent physical information loss at the source or significantly prolonging mission times.

## Method

- A **disturbance-aware motion commander** integrates a custom-built, over-actuated eight-thruster ROV with a ==real-time optimization framework==.
- A computationally efficient ==thruster wake model==, derived from ==actuator-disk theory==, predicts hydrodynamic flow velocity at target locations for real-time control integration.
- A ==redundancy-resolving control allocator== exploits the ROV's actuation redundancy to minimize predicted hydrodynamic disturbance on target regions while simultaneously achieving desired motion commands, operating within a ==shared control architecture==.

## Results

- 3D reconstruction RMSE was reduced by **55%** (from **4.3 ± 1.8 mm** to **1.9 ± 0.4 mm**) with completeness increasing to **94.8%**, achieving a **98.5%** success rate for high-fidelity reconstructions.
- Mean target-surface particle velocity, a direct measure of disturbance, was reduced by **67%** (from **0.18 ± 0.05 m/s** to **0.06 ± 0.02 m/s**) compared to disturbance-unaware control.
- During static pose-holding, the method maintained comparable positioning accuracy while reducing target disturbance by **67%**, energy consumption by **31%**, and attitude variations by **45%** compared to standard Dynamic Positioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling thruster-induced hydrodynamic disturbance allows its integration as an optimization cost in real-time control, enabling proactive mitigation rather than reactive compensation.
> - Actuation redundancy in over-actuated underwater vehicles provides a null space that can be effectively exploited to achieve secondary objectives, such as minimizing environmental disturbance, without compromising primary motion goals.
> - The concept of "gentle stability," which prioritizes minimal thruster-induced flow on target regions while maintaining positioning accuracy, improves sensing outcomes, reduces energy consumption, and enhances platform stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Underwater robots are utilized in various applications, including infrastructure inspection, environmental monitoring, and scientific exploration, often requiring high-fidelity sensing of submerged structures. A fundamental challenge in these scenarios is the "actuation-to-perception coupling," where the robot's thrusters induce hydrodynamic disturbances. These disturbances can resuspend sediments and create turbulence, leading to reduced water clarity, motion blur, and signal occlusion, which degrade the quality of sensor data. This problem represents a mechatronic limitation that cannot be fully resolved through post-processing or algorithmic compensation alone.

Conventional underwater robot controllers primarily focus on "vehicle-centric objectives," such as trajectory tracking, stability, and dynamic positioning, without explicitly accounting for how the robot's own actuation impacts its surrounding environment or the quality of acquired sensor data. Standard six-thruster underwater vehicles typically provide exactly six control inputs for six degrees of freedom, offering no redundancy to pursue secondary objectives like minimizing environmental disturbance. This design physically couples motion control with environmental impact, often necessitating slower operations to reduce disturbance, which prolongs task duration.

Existing disturbance mitigation strategies operate at different system levels with inherent limitations. Hardware-level modifications (e.g., optimized propellers, reconfigured thruster placement) are fixed upon deployment and lack adaptability. Operational-level strategies (e.g., reduced velocity, wait-and-settle protocols) extend mission times. Algorithmic post-processing (e.g., image enhancement, neural restoration) cannot recover information that is physically lost due to blur or occlusion at the source.

The present work addresses this gap by operating at the control allocation level, specifically for over-actuated underwater platforms. Over-actuation provides redundancy, enabling multiple thrust allocations to yield the same vehicle motion. This redundancy creates a "null space" that can be exploited for secondary objectives, such as minimizing environmental disturbance, without compromising primary motion goals. The paper introduces a quantitative model for thruster-induced disturbance that is suitable for real-time control integration, a capability not typically found in prior qualitative descriptions or robust control methods focused on rejecting *external* disturbances.

In the context of 3D reconstruction, both classical Structure-from-Motion (SfM)/Multi-View Stereo (MVS) pipelines and modern neural implicit representations (e.g., NeRF, 3D Gaussian splatting) have strict input requirements regarding image clarity, texture, consistent appearance, and minimal motion blur. Underwater environments inherently challenge these requirements, and thruster-induced disturbances exacerbate these issues by scattering light and obscuring targets.

This research distinguishes itself by proposing an "acquisition-aware control" framework. Instead of attempting to compensate for degraded data post-acquisition, it actively preserves scene integrity during the data acquisition process. By formulating thruster-induced disturbance as an explicit optimization cost and leveraging the actuation redundancy of an eight-thruster platform, the framework aims to decouple motion tracking from environmental impact. This approach represents a departure from vehicle-centric control paradigms and contributes to the emerging field of robotics that considers the robot's self-induced environmental effects on sensing performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that underwater robots, particularly those operating in proximity to delicate targets for high-fidelity sensing, often inadvertently degrade the very data they seek to acquire. This "actuation-to-perception coupling" is a fundamental challenge where the hydrodynamic disturbances generated by thrusters (such as sediment resuspension and localized turbulence) corrupt sensor inputs, limiting the quality of tasks like 3D reconstruction. Traditional control systems are designed to optimize for vehicle-centric objectives (e.g., trajectory tracking accuracy, stability, disturbance rejection) and do not account for the environmental consequences of their thruster commands, thus remaining "blind to the environmental impact of their commands."

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Address Actuation-to-Perception Coupling:** The central objective is to mitigate the degradation of sensor data quality caused by the robot's own thruster actuation. This involves moving beyond reactive post-processing or indirect operational constraints to a proactive control strategy.
2. **Exploit Actuation Redundancy:** For over-actuated underwater platforms (specifically, an eight-thruster ROV), the objective is to leverage the inherent control redundancy. This redundancy allows for multiple thrust allocations to achieve the same desired robot motion. The goal is to systematically search this null space of thrust configurations to identify those that minimize hydrodynamic disturbance in a task-relevant target region, while simultaneously satisfying motion constraints.
3. **Develop a Real-Time Disturbance Model:** A key objective is to create a computationally efficient and control-oriented thruster-wake proxy. This model, derived from actuator-disk theory and incorporating directional attenuation, must be capable of predicting flow velocity at arbitrary spatial locations in real-time. This enables the explicit formulation of disturbance as an optimization cost within the control loop.
4. **Design a Redundancy-Resolving Control Allocator:** The research aims to develop a real-time control allocation optimizer. This optimizer's function is to resolve the actuation redundancy by selecting optimal thrust allocations from the null space. The objective of this selection is to minimize the predicted hydrodynamic disturbance on the target region while adhering to the desired motion commands.
5. **Integrate within a Shared Control Architecture:** The framework aims to be versatile, supporting both fully autonomous scanning operations and operator-assisted inspection scenarios. The objective is to design an architecture that interprets high-level operator intent (e.g., velocity commands) and translates them into disturbance-optimized trajectories, thereby automating low-level disturbance mitigation while preserving human decision-making authority over inspection strategy.
6. **Achieve "Gentle Stability":** This objective introduces a new control philosophy termed "gentle stability." Unlike conventional "tight stability" that prioritizes minimizing position error through aggressive, high-bandwidth corrections (potentially creating local turbulence), gentle stability aims to maintain positioning accuracy while explicitly minimizing thruster-induced flow on target regions, thereby preserving sensing objectives.

Through these objectives, the work aims to enable low-cost ROVs to perform high-fidelity 3D reconstruction tasks by actively preserving scene integrity during data acquisition, a capability previously limited to specialized equipment or requiring significant operational compromises.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a disturbance-aware motion commander that integrates a custom-built, over-actuated robot with a real-time optimization framework. The system operates under a shared control architecture supporting both autonomous and teleoperated modes.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The framework comprises four main components:
1. **Over-Actuated Underwater Robot:** A custom-designed eight-thruster Remotely Operated Vehicle (ROV) provides 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (DOF) control with actuation redundancy. The vehicle dimensions are 450×350×280 mm, weighing 4.5 kg, and it is equipped with eight M060 vectored thrusters (four horizontal, four vertical).
2. **Disturbance Model:** This component predicts hydrodynamic flow velocity at target locations induced by the thrusters.
3. **Real-Time Control Allocation Optimizer:** This module minimizes predicted disturbance while executing desired robot motion.
4. **Low-Level Controller:** This component translates optimized thrust commands into actual thruster forces.

For ground truth pose estimation, the robot employs a tightly-coupled Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) that fuses high-frequency monocular vision (62 Hz) with optical tracking (10 Hz, NOKOV Mars-3H), achieving a reported RMSE of 5.2 ± 1.1 mm.

#### 3.2. Disturbance Modeling

1. **Physical Foundation:** Each thruster is modeled as an idealized actuator disk. The model focuses on the downstream disturbance field rather than solely thrust generation. The induced flow velocity `v_i(x)` at a spatial location `x` from the `i`-th thruster is given by:
 `v_i(x) = (s * T_i / (2πρA_prop)) * (r_ref^2 / r_i^2) * n_i * g(θ_i)`
 where `T_i` is thrust magnitude, `ρ` is water density, `A_prop` is propeller disk area, `r_i` is distance from thruster center, `r_ref` is a reference distance (1 m), `n_i` is the unit vector along thrust direction, and `g(θ_i)` is a directional attenuation function.
2. **Directional Attenuation Function:** This function, `g(θ_i) = cos^4(θ_i)` for `|θ_i| ≤ 45°` and `0` otherwise, captures the jet-like flow patterns. The `cos^4(θ)` dependence is based on the hydrodynamic force scaling with dynamic pressure (velocity squared) and is consistent with propeller wake theory. The 45° cutoff angle defines the primary momentum flux cone.
3. **Model Validation:** The simplified model was validated against Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV) measurements. It demonstrated an R² value of 0.99 in the axial core region and R² = 0.82 in the full primary wake region, exhibiting a tendency to slightly overestimate disturbance at farther distances, providing a conservative estimate for optimization. The computational overhead is reported as less than 1 ms per control iteration.
4. **Aggregate Disturbance Metric:** The total flow disturbance `D(x_robot, T)` over a target region `R_target` is approximated by a weighted sum of squared induced velocities at `N` sample points {`p_j`}:
 `D(x_robot, T) ≈ Σ_{j=1 to N} w_j * Σ_{i=1 to 8} v_i(p_j)²`
 The weights `w_j` are determined by proximity to delicate features (`w_j = exp(-d_j / σ)`). The number of sample points `N` (150-300) is determined by target complexity, with minimal computational cost. Linear superposition is assumed for computational efficiency, providing a reliable relative ordering of disturbance.

#### 3.3. Shared Control Architecture

The framework supports two operational modes:
1. **Autonomous Scanning Mode:** The system executes pre-planned trajectories (hemispherical layered orbits) with integrated disturbance-aware optimization. This mode was the primary focus for quantitative experimental validation.
2. **Assisted Teleoperation Mode:** An operator provides high-level velocity commands (`v_des`) via a joystick, and the system autonomously optimizes thrust allocation to achieve the commanded motion while minimizing environmental disturbance. This mode was demonstrated qualitatively.

The core of the shared control is a mediated teleoperation paradigm where the optimizer exploits the actuation redundancy to find thrust configurations within the null space that minimize the disturbance cost `D(t)` while respecting operator intent. A weight `w_track` in the optimization formulation mediates the tradeoff between strict adherence to operator commands and disturbance minimization. An empirical tuning yielded `w_track = 5.0`.

#### 3.4. Optimization Formulation

The system solves a redundancy-resolving allocation problem formulated as:
`min_{p(t),u(t)} ∫_{0}^{T} (w_D * D(t) + w_u * ||u(t)||²) dt + w_T * T + w_track * ||p(T) - p_target||²`
Subject to:
- `˙x_robot = f(x_robot, u)` (robot dynamics)
- `u_min ≤ u(t) ≤ u_max` (actuator limits)
- `||˙u(t)|| ≤ ˙u_max` (rate limits)
- `||x(t) - x_obs|| ≥ d_safe` (collision avoidance)

Where `p(t)` is robot position, `p_target` is a soft position target derived from `v_des`, and `u` is the thrust vector.

#### 3.5. Real-Time Implementation

The non-convex nature of the disturbance cost `D(t)` is handled by:
1. **Local Linearization:** `D(t)` is approximated by its second-order Taylor expansion, converting the problem into a convex Quadratic Program (QP) subproblem.
2. **Warm-Starting:** Solutions from the previous 10 Hz control cycle initialize the current optimization, leveraging temporal continuity.

Constraints are enforced using active-set strategies for actuator limits, quadratic penalties for rate limits (soft constraints), and a log-barrier formulation for collision avoidance. An OSQP solver is used with a prediction horizon of `T = 2` seconds (20 steps). The average solve time on an Intel i7-10700K is 45 ± 12 ms, supporting a 10 Hz control rate.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted in a 6.0×5.0×1.5 m indoor freshwater tank with controlled illumination. Three artificial coral targets of varying geometric complexity were used (Model A, B, C). Ground-truth geometry was obtained using a desktop 3D scanner.

Four methods were compared: manual joystick teleoperation, a disturbance-unaware planner (`w_D = 0`), a smooth planner (penalizing control variation without disturbance modeling), and the full disturbance-aware system. For 3D reconstruction, COLMAP was used for Structure-from-Motion, and Instant-NGP for neural rendering. Images were acquired using hemispherical layered orbits at 20°, 40°, and 60° elevation angles.

The validation involved two phases:
- **Reconstruction Quality Comparison:** 240 scanning attempts (4 methods × 3 models × 20 trials). Quality metrics (RMSE, completeness, feature count) were computed from evaluable reconstructions.
- **Success Rate Validation:** An additional 200 trials using the disturbance-aware method under varied initial conditions to confirm a 98.5% success rate (RMSE < 3.0 mm and completeness > 85%).
Near-target flow was quantified using PIV on neutrally buoyant tracer particles to validate the disturbance proxy directly.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation, conducted across 440 trials, yielded the following main findings:

#### 4.1. Disturbance Model Validation

The proposed thruster wake model demonstrated strong agreement with Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV) measurements. It achieved an R² value of 0.99 in the axial core region (where `|θ| < 15°` and `r < 0.3 m`) and R² = 0.82 when considering the full primary wake region (`|θ| < 30°`). The model's computational overhead was reported to be less than 1 ms per control iteration, enabling its integration into real-time control loops.

#### 4.2. Dynamic Scanning Performance for 3D Reconstruction

Compared to baseline methods, the disturbance-aware planner significantly improved 3D reconstruction quality during dynamic scanning:
- **Accuracy:** The approach reduced the 3D reconstruction RMSE by 55% compared to a disturbance-unaware baseline (1.9 ± 0.4 mm vs. 4.3 ± 1.8 mm, p < 0.001).
- **Completeness:** Reconstruction completeness increased to 94.8% for the disturbance-aware method, compared to 68.5% for the disturbance-unaware baseline and 34.2% for manual teleoperation.
- **Feature Count:** The number of successfully extracted features for reconstruction was higher, with 523 ± 67 features for the proposed method compared to 342 ± 112 for the disturbance-unaware baseline.
- **Success Rate:** Across the comparison trials, the disturbance-aware method achieved a 98.3% success rate (59/60 trials), defined as meeting high-quality criteria (RMSE < 3.0 mm and completeness > 85%). This contrasts with success rates of 0% for manual teleoperation, 45% for the disturbance-unaware planner, and 85% for a smooth planner. An extended validation phase confirmed a 98.5% success rate over 200 additional trials under varied conditions.
- **Particle Velocity Reduction:** Direct measurement of near-target flow using PIV showed that the method reduced mean target-surface particle velocity by 67% (0.06 ± 0.02 m/s) compared to disturbance-unaware control (0.18 ± 0.05 m/s, p < 0.001). This reduction in particle velocity aligns with the observed improvements in reconstruction quality by minimizing blur and feature-matching errors.

#### 4.3. Gentle Stability: Static Pose-Holding Under External Disturbance

The concept of "gentle stability" was evaluated during static pose-holding tasks under a 0.3 m/s constant water current.
- **Positioning Accuracy:** The disturbance-aware controller maintained positioning accuracy comparable to a standard Dynamic Positioning (DP) controller (2.1 ± 0.6 mm vs. 2.3 ± 0.8 mm, p = 0.18).
- **Target Disturbance:** While maintaining similar accuracy, the method reduced target disturbance (measured as particle velocity) by 67% (0.06 ± 0.02 m/s vs. 0.18 ± 0.05 m/s, p < 0.001).
- **Energy Consumption:** The gentle thrust modulation resulted in a 31% reduction in energy consumption (11.2 kJ vs. 16.2 kJ) compared to the standard DP controller.
- **Attitude Stability:** The disturbance-aware controller improved attitude stability, reducing roll and pitch variations by 45% (RMS: 0.6° ± 0.2° vs. 1.1° ± 0.3° for standard DP). This indicates that reduced disturbance also contributes to platform smoothness.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study quantitatively assessed the contribution of individual components of the framework:
- **Absence of Disturbance Term:** Removing the disturbance term (`w_D = 0`) from the optimization objective degraded the reconstruction RMSE by 116% (4.1 ± 1.2 mm).
- **Six-Thruster Configuration:** Emulating a standard ROV without actuation redundancy (using a 6-thruster configuration) degraded RMSE by 100% (3.8 ± 1.1 mm).
- **Simplified Disturbance Model:** Replacing the detailed thruster wake model with a simplified linear proxy increased RMSE by 68% (3.2 ± 0.9 mm).
These results indicate the importance of explicit disturbance modeling, actuation redundancy, and the directional attenuation term for achieving performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses the "actuation-to-perception coupling" problem, identified as a mechatronic bottleneck in underwater robotics. By exploiting actuation redundancy in over-actuated platforms and integrating a real-time disturbance model into control allocation, the work offers a method to actively preserve scene integrity during data acquisition.

The quantitative results demonstrate that this approach enables low-cost ROVs to achieve high-fidelity 3D reconstruction, a capability previously associated with specialized equipment or operational compromises. The reported 98.5% reconstruction success rate, 55% improvement in accuracy, and 67% reduction in thruster-induced disturbance signify a notable advancement in reliable and precise underwater data collection. The achievement of "gentle stability," which maintains positioning accuracy while explicitly minimizing target disturbance and reducing energy consumption, represents a shift from purely vehicle-centric control to an acquisition-aware paradigm.

The framework's dual support for both autonomous scanning and operator-assisted inspection via shared control enhances its practical applicability across varied operational scenarios. By automating low-level disturbance mitigation, it reduces the cognitive workload on human operators while preserving their strategic decision-making authority.

The potential impact of this work extends to several critical applications requiring millimeter-scale fidelity in underwater environments:
- **Coral Reef Conservation:** Enabling detailed monitoring for the detection of subtle changes, such as millimeter-scale bleaching or damage.
- **Infrastructure Inspection:** Facilitating the identification of hairline cracks or corrosion in submerged pipelines, offshore structures, and cables.
- **Archaeological Preservation:** Supporting the high-fidelity digitization of fragile underwater artifacts and sites, which are highly susceptible to hydrodynamic disturbances.

Furthermore, the contribution of a validated, computationally efficient thruster wake model provides a tool for future research in environmental robotics. The planned release of the dataset, including robot states, images, and ground-truth 3D models, is intended to support further community research and development in this domain. Future work aims to address open-water deployment challenges, including learned disturbance prediction, multi-robot collaborative scanning, and integration with automatic target detection, thereby expanding the real-world utility of this disturbance-aware control paradigm.
