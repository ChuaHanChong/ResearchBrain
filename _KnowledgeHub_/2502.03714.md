# Universal Sparse Autoencoders: Interpretable Cross-Model Concept Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs), a method for jointly learning a shared, interpretable concept space across multiple distinct deep neural networks. It demonstrates the ability to discover a range of universal concepts, from low-level features to high-level abstractions, and shows effective cross-model activation reconstruction with positive R² scores, revealing a strong correlation between a concept's importance and its universality across different models.

## Problem

- Traditional interpretability methods primarily focus on single models, lacking scalability and efficiency for analyzing the growing number of deep neural networks.
- Existing techniques for identifying shared concepts across models are often post-hoc and computationally expensive, hindering direct concept translation or interaction.
- The absence of a shared conceptual space complicates multi-model applications and a unified understanding of diverse AI systems.

## Method

- Introduces ==Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs)==, which jointly learn a single, ==overcomplete sparse autoencoder== to reconstruct and interpret the internal activations of multiple pretrained deep neural networks simultaneously.
- The architecture uses ==model-specific encoders== to map individual model activations to a ==shared sparse code== representing the universal concept space, from which model-specific decoders can reconstruct activations.
- A universal training process is employed where, in each mini-batch, one model's activations are encoded into the shared code, and this same code is then used to reconstruct feature representations for all models, with loss aggregated across all reconstructions.

## Results

- Qualitatively, Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs) discover a broad range of semantically coherent and interpretable concepts (e.g., 'yellow', 'dog face', 'keypads') that activate consistently across diverse vision models like DinoV2, SigLIP, and ViT.
- Quantitative evaluation using **R²** scores demonstrates USAEs effectively reconstruct internal activations across models, with **positive** off-diagonal R² values confirming the shared latent space captures universal features.
- Analysis of firing entropy and co-fire proportion reveals a **strong positive correlation** between a concept's importance (reconstruction energy) and its universality (co-firing frequency), indicating the most important concepts are also highly universal.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep neural networks, even when trained for diverse tasks, converge on a range of semantically coherent and interpretable universal concepts, from low-level features to high-level abstractions.
> - The most important concepts, measured by their contribution to reconstruction energy, are also the most universal, consistently activating across different model architectures.
> - The universal training objective of USAEs can uncover shared representations that do not emerge when sparse autoencoders are trained on individual models in isolation.
