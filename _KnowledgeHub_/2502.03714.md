# Universal Sparse Autoencoders: Interpretable Cross-Model Concept Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs), a method for jointly learning a shared, interpretable concept space across multiple distinct deep neural networks. It demonstrates the ability to discover a range of universal concepts, from low-level features to high-level abstractions, and shows effective cross-model activation reconstruction with positive R² scores, revealing a strong correlation between a concept's importance and its universality across different models.

## Problem

- Traditional interpretability methods primarily focus on single models, lacking scalability and efficiency for analyzing the growing number of deep neural networks.
- Existing techniques for identifying shared concepts across models are often post-hoc and computationally expensive, hindering direct concept translation or interaction.
- The absence of a shared conceptual space complicates multi-model applications and a unified understanding of diverse AI systems.

## Method

- Introduces ==Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs)==, which jointly learn a single, ==overcomplete sparse autoencoder== to reconstruct and interpret the internal activations of multiple pretrained deep neural networks simultaneously.
- The architecture uses ==model-specific encoders== to map individual model activations to a ==shared sparse code== representing the universal concept space, from which model-specific decoders can reconstruct activations.
- A universal training process is employed where, in each mini-batch, one model's activations are encoded into the shared code, and this same code is then used to reconstruct feature representations for all models, with loss aggregated across all reconstructions.

## Results

- Qualitatively, Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs) discover a broad range of semantically coherent and interpretable concepts (e.g., 'yellow', 'dog face', 'keypads') that activate consistently across diverse vision models like DinoV2, SigLIP, and ViT.
- Quantitative evaluation using **R²** scores demonstrates USAEs effectively reconstruct internal activations across models, with **positive** off-diagonal R² values confirming the shared latent space captures universal features.
- Analysis of firing entropy and co-fire proportion reveals a **strong positive correlation** between a concept's importance (reconstruction energy) and its universality (co-firing frequency), indicating the most important concepts are also highly universal.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep neural networks, even when trained for diverse tasks, converge on a range of semantically coherent and interpretable universal concepts, from low-level features to high-level abstractions.
> - The most important concepts, measured by their contribution to reconstruction energy, are also the most universal, consistently activating across different model architectures.
> - The universal training objective of USAEs can uncover shared representations that do not emerge when sparse autoencoders are trained on individual models in isolation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The interpretation of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) is an area of increasing importance, driven by the need for risk mitigation, informed architectural design, and compliance with evolving regulatory frameworks. Traditional concept-based interpretability methods frequently concentrate on dissecting individual models for specific tasks. This isolated analysis approach presents challenges for scalable risk management in scenarios involving a proliferation of DNNs. The absence of a canonical basis for understanding model internals makes comprehensive interpretability difficult.

Recent developments in "foundation models" suggest that DNNs trained on extensive datasets may encode concepts that generalize across a range of downstream tasks within a given modality. Research by Moschella et al. (2022) indicates that models with different architectures, initializations, and tasks can develop semantically equivalent latent representations. Furthermore, studies by Dravid et al. (2023) and Kowal et al. (2024a) have identified shared concepts across various vision models, suggesting a broader universality than previously assumed.

However, existing techniques for identifying these universal features typically operate in a post-hoc manner. These methods extract concepts from individual models and then attempt to match them, often relying on computationally intensive filtering or optimization processes. This approach introduces limitations in terms of scalability, lacks the efficiency benefits of gradient-based training, and restricts the ability to directly translate concepts within a unified conceptual space across different models. Consequently, tasks requiring simultaneous interaction and comparison across multiple models, such as coordinated activation maximization, become cumbersome.

This research addresses these limitations by introducing Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs). USAEs are designed to jointly learn a shared, interpretable concept space that can simultaneously encode, reconstruct, and align the internal activations of multiple DNNs. By departing from post-hoc analysis and integrating concept alignment directly into an end-to-end training framework, this work aims to provide a more scalable, efficient, and unified approach to cross-model interpretability, thereby advancing the understanding of shared representations in complex multi-model AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to discover and align interpretable concepts that span multiple pretrained deep neural networks, ultimately aiming to learn a universal concept space. This universal space is intended to offer a unified perspective into the hidden representations of diverse models, treating "concepts" as fundamental abstractions captured by networks, ranging from low-level features (e.g., colors, textures) to high-level attributes (e.g., object parts, semantic ideas).

The motivation for this work is multifaceted:
- **Addressing Scalability and Risk Management**: With a growing number of capable DNNs being deployed, interpreting each network in isolation for risk mitigation and adherence to regulatory frameworks becomes unmanageable. The authors argue for the necessity of a canonical basis for understanding model internals to develop more tractable strategies for managing potential risks across multi-model AI systems.
- **Improving upon Existing Methods**: Current methods for identifying universal features are often post-hoc, computationally intensive, and lack the efficiencies of gradient-based training. These limitations hinder direct translation of concepts between models within a unified framework. The paper explicitly states the goal of overcoming these challenges by introducing an end-to-end trained system that inherently enforces concept alignment.
- **Deepening Insights into Model Representations**: Building on the premise of "foundation models" and evidence of semantically equivalent latent representations across diverse architectures, the research seeks to explore the extent of concept universality. By jointly learning a concept space, the authors aim to identify common factors of variation that transcend individual data points and specific model architectures, tasks, and datasets.
- **Enabling Novel Applications**: A key motivation is to unlock new interpretability applications that require simultaneous interaction across multiple models. Coordinated activation maximization is presented as an immediate example, allowing for the visualization of how a single universal concept manifests differently across various models, thereby providing insights into model-specific biases or shared understandings.
- **Qualitative and Quantitative Understanding**: The work is motivated by the need to both qualitatively visualize semantically coherent universal concepts across different levels of abstraction and quantitatively assess their universality, importance, and consistency with concepts learned by individual models. This comprehensive analysis aims to shed light on how large vision models, trained under varied paradigms, compare and diverge in their internal representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodological innovation presented is the Universal Sparse Autoencoder (USAE) framework, designed to learn a shared, interpretable concept space across multiple deep neural networks.

**Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) Background:**
The work builds upon Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), which aim to find a sparse re-interpretation of feature representations. Given network activations `A = f(X) ∈ R^(n×d)`, an SAE learns an encoder `Ψ_θ(·)` to map `A` to sparse codes `Z = Ψ_θ(A) ∈ R^(n×m)`, where `m > d` (overcomplete dictionary). A decoder `D ∈ R^(m×d)` then reconstructs `A` as `ZD`. The specific implementation uses a TopK SAE (Gao et al., 2024), where the `TopK(·)` operator enforces that each row of `Z` has at most `K` non-zero elements, promoting sparsity. The training objective for a standard SAE is the Frobenius norm of the reconstruction error: `L_SAE = ∥A - ZD∥_F`.

**Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs):**
USAEs extend the SAE concept to `M` different models, each producing activations `A^(i) ∈ R^(n×d_i)`. The central insight is to learn a *shared sparse code* `Z ∈ R^(n×m)` that allows *any* model's activations to be reconstructed from this common embedding.
1. **Model-Specific Encoding**: For each model `i`, activations `A^(i)` are encoded into the shared code space `Z` using a model-specific encoder `Ψ^(i)_θ`, defined as `Z = Ψ^(i)_θ(A^(i)) = TopK(W^(i)_enc (A^(i) - b^(i)_pre))`.
2. **Model-Specific Decoding**: Once encoded into `Z`, the activations of any model `j` can be reconstructed using a model-specific dictionary (decoder) `D^(j) ∈ R^(d_j×m)`, such that `bA^(j) = ZD^(j)`.
By jointly learning all encoder-decoder pairs `{(Ψ^(i)_θ, D^(i))}` across all `M` models, the USAE framework enforces a unified concept space `Z`. This ensures consistency and interpretability across different model architectures and allows for faithful recovery of each model's features from a common set of sparse concepts.

**Training USAEs:**
To manage the computational and memory demands of training multiple models simultaneously, an intermediate training strategy is employed:
1. **Random Model Selection**: In each mini-batch iteration, a single model `i` from the `M` models is randomly selected.
2. **Single Encoder Update**: A batch of features `A^(i)` from the selected model `i` is sampled and encoded into the shared code space `Z = Ψ^(i)_θ(A^(i))`.
3. **Multiple Decoder Reconstructions**: This single `Z` is then used to reconstruct the feature representations `A^(j)` of *every* model `j ∈ [M]` using its respective decoder `D^(j)`: `bA^(j) = ZD^(j)`.
4. **Universal Loss Calculation**: The total loss, `L_Universal`, is aggregated across all reconstructions: `L_Universal = Σ_(j=1 to M) ∥A^(j) - bA^(j)∥_F`.
5. **Parameter Update**: Backpropagation updates only the parameters of the chosen encoder `Ψ^(i)_θ` and its associated decoder `D^(i)`.
This strategy aims to strike a balance between training speed and memory usage while actively promoting concept alignment by ensuring that the shared `Z` must be sufficiently rich to reconstruct all models.

**Application: Coordinated Activation Maximization:**
The shared concept space learned by USAEs enables a novel application called Coordinated Activation Maximization. This extends traditional Activation Maximization (AM) by simultaneously optimizing inputs for multiple models to activate the *same* concept dimension `k`.
1. **Objective**: For a given universal concept index `k`, optimize an input `x^(i)` for each model `i` such that it maximally activates concept `k` within that model. This is defined as `Z^(i)_k(x) = [Ψ^(i)_θ(f^(i)(x))]_k`.
2. **Optimization**: Each `x^(i)` is independently optimized: `x^(i)_⋆ = arg max_(x∈X) Z^(i)_k(x^(i)) - λR(x^(i))`, where `R(x)` is a regularizer (e.g., total variation, ℓ2 penalty) to promote natural-looking inputs, and `λ` controls its strength.
This application allows for direct visual comparison of how a single universal concept is represented across different models, highlighting both consistencies and unique interpretations.

**Implementation Details**:
The USAE was trained on the final layer activations of three vision models: DinoV2, SigLIP, and ViT (ImageNet-trained), chosen for their diverse training paradigms (discriminative, contrastive, supervised). The ImageNet training set was used for USAE training, with the validation set reserved for evaluation. A dictionary size of 6144 (8 times the largest feature dimension) was used for the TopK SAE. An ℓ1 loss was used instead of ℓ2 for improved training dynamics and concept interpretability. Activations were standardized across models, and class tokens were removed for consistency. Training occurred on a single Nvidia RTX 6000 GPU, taking approximately three days.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative evidence for the efficacy and properties of Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs) in discovering and aligning interpretable concepts across multiple vision models.

#### 4.1. Universal Concept Visualizations

Qualitative analysis of the most important universal concepts, identified by their energy contribution to reconstruction, revealed a diverse range of features. Concepts spanned multiple levels of abstraction:
- **Low-level features**: Examples include fundamental colors like 'yellow' and 'blue', and basic shapes or patterns such as checkerboards. These concepts were observed to activate over broad spatial regions.
- **Intermediate-level features**: Concepts reflecting structural relationships like foreground-background contrasts (e.g., birds against the sky) and thin, wiry objects were discovered.
- **High-level features**: Object-part concepts like 'dog face' (excluding eye regions) and 'bolts' (activating across different materials) were identified. Fine-grained, compositional concepts like 'mouth-open animal jaws' and 'faces of animals in a group' also emerged, demonstrating generalization across ImageNet classes.
These visualizations suggest that USAEs capture robust, generalizable concepts that are invariant across different model architectures, training tasks, and datasets.

#### 4.2. Validation of Cross-Model Reconstruction

The ability of the USAE to reconstruct activations across models was quantitatively assessed using the R² score (coefficient of determination). A confusion matrix (Figure 5) illustrated the R² scores when activations from one model were encoded and then decoded to reconstruct another (or the same) model's activations.
- **Self-reconstruction**: The diagonal elements, representing self-reconstruction, showed the highest R² scores, confirming the USAE's capacity to faithfully reconstruct activations within a single model.
- **Cross-model reconstruction**: Crucially, positive off-diagonal R² scores were observed, indicating successful reconstruction of activations of one model from the sparse code derived from another. This provides quantitative evidence that the USAE effectively captures shared features among models.
- **Model Differences**: DinoV2 exhibited the highest self-reconstruction performance (0.77 R²), outperforming SigLIP (0.61 R²) and ViT (0.59 R²). This suggests that DinoV2's features might be inherently sparser and more readily decomposable by the sparse autoencoder.

#### 4.3. Measuring Concept Universality and Importance

The universality and importance of concepts were quantified using two metrics: Firing Entropy (FE) and Co-Fire Proportion (CFP), alongside concept energy.
- **Firing Entropy (FE)**: This metric measures the normalized entropy of a concept's firing across models. A high FE (closer to 1) indicates a concept that fires uniformly across all models (universal), while low FE suggests a model-biased concept.
 - The distribution of FE showed a bimodal pattern (Figure 6a), with over 1000 concepts exhibiting peak entropy (FE=1), confirming a substantial number of strongly universal concepts. A second peak indicated concepts favoring a subset of models, with very few concepts being purely model-specific (low FE).
 - Analysis of low-entropy concepts (Appendix A.2) revealed that DinoV2 uniquely encodes geometric features related to perspective, depth cues, and view invariance, while SigLIP encodes features related to both visual and textual elements.
- **Co-Fire Proportion (CFP)**: This metric quantifies how often a concept fires simultaneously across all models for the same input, relative to its total firing frequency in a given model. High CFP implies greater universality.
 - The top ~100 concepts exhibited high CFP (>0.5), indicating they co-fired 50-80% of the time (Figure 6b). The CFP gradually declined for less energetic concepts, suggesting a spectrum of universality.
 - DinoV2 showed a lower average CFP (0.266) compared to SigLIP (0.344) and ViT (0.326), reinforcing the observation that DinoV2 might possess more distinct features, possibly due to its unique architecture and training paradigm.
- **Universality vs. Importance**: A moderate positive correlation (r=0.63) was observed between co-fire frequency and concept importance (energy). This correlation became stronger (r=0.89) when focusing on concepts with a high number of co-fires (>1000), indicating that the most important concepts are also highly universal, consistently activating across models.

#### 4.4. Concept Consistency Between USAEs and SAEs

The study investigated the overlap between concepts learned by USAEs and those discovered by independently trained Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) for single models. Cosine similarity between decoder vectors was used to quantify consistency.
- ViT demonstrated the highest concept overlap, with 38% of its USAE-learned concepts having a cosine similarity > 0.5 with its independently trained SAE counterpart (Figure 7). This suggests that ViT's conceptual representations are relatively stable across universal and individual training objectives.
- USAEs significantly outperformed a random baseline (AUC=0.13), indicating that universal training generates meaningful concept alignments.
- Lower overlap for SigLIP (23%) and DinoV2 (26%) suggests that the universal training objective facilitates the discovery of shared representations that might not emerge when models are trained in isolation. When considering only the top 1000 co-firing concepts (Appendix A.5.1), the consistency between individual and universal concepts increased, further supporting that universal training prioritizes broadly represented concepts.

#### 4.5. Coordinated Activation Maximization

The application of Coordinated Activation Maximization (CAM) provided visual comparisons of how universal concepts manifest across different models.
- The method successfully produced interpretable visualizations for a broad range of concepts, including low-level primitives (e.g., 'curves', 'crosses', 'brown grass' texture), basic entities ('round objects'), and higher-level concepts ('objects from above', 'keypads') (Figure 8).
- CAM revealed model-specific differences, such as DinoV2 displaying low-to-mid level concepts at a larger scale and exhibiting stronger activation for larger curves compared to other models. Also, while 'brown grass' activated on grass in heatmaps, some models' AMs for this concept included birds, suggesting additional influencing factors.

#### 4.6. Discovering Unique Concepts with USAEs

By isolating concepts with low firing entropy (model-specific concepts), USAEs enabled the discovery of unique features for individual models:
- **Unique DinoV2 Concepts**: These included concepts related to perspective and depth cues (e.g., converging lines, angled scenes, object groupings at varying depths, uphill surfaces) and low-level 3D understanding (e.g., top faces of rectangular prisms, brim lines of domes, prism corners) (Figures 9, 10, 11). These findings are consistent with DinoV2's training regime, which emphasizes geometric consistency and view invariance through self-distillation and masked image modeling.
- **Unique SigLIP Concepts**: Concepts firing for both visual and textual elements of the same concept were identified (e.g., 'star' shape and text, 'capital C', musical instruments and notes) (Appendix A.3, Figure 12). This aligns with SigLIP's image-text contrastive learning objective, which aims to align image and text latent representations.

#### 4.7. Out-of-Distribution Generalization

The USAE framework, trained on ImageNet, demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution datasets like DTD (textures) and CelebA (faces). Consistent activation reconstruction accuracy (MSE and R²) and co-firing trends were observed (Appendix A.4, Table 1, Figures 13, 14, 15), suggesting that the learned dictionary captures generalizable structures beyond its training data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces Universal Sparse Autoencoders (USAEs) as a framework for interpretable cross-model analysis, representing an advancement in the field of machine learning interpretability. The development of USAEs offers several implications and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Scalable Interpretability**: By providing a method to discover and align concepts across multiple deep neural networks simultaneously, USAEs move beyond the limitations of single-model interpretability. This is crucial for managing and understanding the growing complexity of multi-model AI systems, offering a more tractable strategy for interpreting diverse architectures. Such scalability is essential for effective risk mitigation and for meeting the demands of evolving regulatory frameworks for AI.
- **Uncovering Universal Representations**: The research demonstrates that USAEs can identify a broad spectrum of semantically coherent concepts, ranging from low-level visual primitives to high-level abstractions, that are shared and consistently activated across different model architectures and training objectives. This sheds light on the commonalities in how diverse models encode knowledge, potentially contributing to a "canonical basis" for understanding neural network internals.
- **Revealing Model-Specific Distinctions**: Beyond commonalities, USAEs also provide a mechanism to identify unique conceptual features harbored by individual models. The discovery of DinoV2's distinct encoding of geometric and perspective cues, and SigLIP's integration of visual and textual elements, highlights the framework's capacity to diagnose model-specific biases and strengths, which can inform future model development and selection.
- **Facilitating Deeper Insights into AI Systems**: The ability to compare and contrast how different models represent the same universal concept, particularly through applications like Coordinated Activation Maximization, offers a novel avenue for gaining deeper insights into their internal workings. This comparative analysis can reveal subtle differences in how models interpret inputs, enhancing our understanding of their decision-making processes and potential vulnerabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Applications**: USAEs offer a powerful tool that could be extended to various applications beyond current demonstrations. For instance, the framework could be used to analyze concept evolution during fine-tuning, compare models across different modalities, or even guide the design of more robust and interpretable model architectures. The observed out-of-distribution generalization capabilities further underscore the potential for USAEs to extract fundamental visual structures.
- **Promoting Transparency and Accountability**: By providing a unified and interpretable concept space, USAEs contribute to greater transparency in AI systems. Understanding shared and unique representations can help practitioners diagnose and address issues like fairness, bias, and robustness more systematically across model portfolios, fostering a higher degree of accountability in AI deployment.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as hyperparameter sensitivity with increasing models and a current focus on final layer activations, the proposed USAE framework lays foundational groundwork for advancing the interpretability of complex multi-model AI systems.
