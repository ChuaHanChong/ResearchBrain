# Gemini Robotics: Bringing AI into the Physical World

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind introduces the Gemini Robotics family of models, which extend the Gemini 2.0 multimodal model to physical robots, enabling them to perform a wide range of dexterous manipulation tasks. This research demonstrates enhanced embodied reasoning, robust generalization to novel conditions, and rapid adaptation to new tasks and robot embodiments through extensive real-world robot data and specialized fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Bridging the generalist capabilities of large multimodal models (LMMs) from digital domains to practical applications on physical robots.
- Overcoming the limitations of traditional, highly specialized robotic systems that lack robust generalization to diverse, unstructured real-world environments.
- Developing robotic AI that can acquire robust embodied reasoning (e.g., 3D understanding, intuitive physics) and directly translate this understanding into precise physical actions.

## Method

- The Gemini Robotics team at Google DeepMind developed the ==Gemini Robotics== family of models, extending the Gemini 2.0 multimodal model to physical robotics.
- ==Gemini Robotics-ER==, a Vision-Language Model (VLM), was developed to enhance embodied reasoning capabilities, including open-world 2D/3D perception, trajectory, and grasp prediction, evaluated on a new ERQA benchmark.
- Gemini Robotics, a ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model, was built for low-latency real-time robot control, trained on thousands of hours of diverse real-world expert teleoperated robot action data alongside other multimodal data.

## Results

- Gemini 2.0 and Gemini Robotics-ER achieved state-of-the-art results on embodied reasoning benchmarks, including the newly introduced ERQA, with chain-of-thought prompting significantly boosting performance.
- Gemini Robotics demonstrated proficiency on **20** diverse dexterous tasks, surpassing state-of-the-art baselines, and showed robust generalization across visual, instruction, and action variations.
- Specialized Gemini Robotics achieved up to **79%** average success on six challenging long-horizon dexterous tasks (e.g., **55%** for origami fox), and demonstrated rapid adaptation to new tasks (over **70%** success with **100** demonstrations) and new robot embodiments (e.g., Apptronik Apollo humanoid).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enhancing large multimodal models with "embodied reasoning" capabilities is crucial for enabling them to understand and interact with the physical world effectively.
> - A generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model built on a powerful large multimodal model backbone and trained on diverse robot action data can achieve robust out-of-the-box dexterity and generalize to unseen visual conditions and instructions.
> - Such foundation models can be specialized and rapidly adapted with limited data for complex long-horizon tasks, new short-horizon tasks, and even completely different robot embodiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the integration of large multimodal models (LMMs) with robotic systems. Recent years have witnessed remarkable progress in LMMs, such as Google's Gemini, which demonstrate generalist capabilities across diverse digital modalities like text, images, audio, and video. However, a significant challenge remains in translating these digital proficiencies to physical agents that can competently and safely interact with the real world.

Historically, robotics has relied on highly specialized systems for specific tasks, often requiring extensive hand-engineering or task-specific data collection and training. The advent of foundation models in AI presents an opportunity to move towards more general-purpose robots capable of understanding and acting in diverse, unstructured environments. This paper directly addresses this paradigm shift by leveraging a cutting-edge foundation model, Gemini 2.0, as the core for robotic control.

The work builds upon and contrasts with several key trends in robotics AI:
- **Bridging Perception-Action Gap:** Traditional robotics often separates perception, planning, and control into distinct modules, sometimes requiring multiple specialized models. This paper explicitly aims to "unite all required capabilities in a single model," simplifying the pipeline and potentially improving end-to-end performance and generalization.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Gemini Robotics is presented as a state-of-the-art VLA model, a growing area of research that seeks to directly map multimodal observations and language instructions to robot actions. It is compared against contemporary VLA baselines like $\pi_0$ and multi-task diffusion policies, positioning it as a leading contender in this emerging space.
- **Embodied Reasoning:** The paper emphasizes "embodied reasoning" as a critical capability for physically grounded AI, encompassing 3D understanding, inter-object relationships, and intuitive physics. It introduces a new benchmark, ERQA, to standardize the evaluation of these often-overlooked reasoning abilities in VLMs, filling a gap in existing atomic capability benchmarks.
- **Generalization and Adaptability:** A major bottleneck for robot deployment is the lack of robust generalization to unseen environments, objects, and instructions. This research directly tackles these "distribution shifts" and explores rapid adaptation to novel tasks and robot embodiments, which are crucial for practical, real-world applicability of AI in robotics.
- **Safety in Robotics:** As AI models become more capable and autonomous, safety becomes paramount. This paper integrates safety considerations from the outset, moving beyond traditional physical action safety to address "semantic action safety" in open-domain environments, an area of growing importance for general-purpose robots.

In essence, Gemini Robotics represents Google DeepMind's ambitious push to extend the success of large AI models from the digital to the physical domain, aiming to set new benchmarks for generalist robot capabilities, dexterity, and real-world applicability while emphasizing responsible development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to bridge the significant gap between the remarkable generalist capabilities observed in large multimodal models within digital domains and their practical translation to physical agents like robots. The authors are motivated by the vision of creating "generally useful robots" that can robustly and safely interact with the physical world, moving beyond the limitations of highly specialized robotic systems.

The core motivation is encapsulated in the question posed by the authors: "What would it take to endow a state-of-the-art digital AI model with the embodied reasoning capabilities needed to interact with our world in a general and dexterous manner?" To answer this, the paper articulates two fundamental components necessary for physically grounded AI:
1. **Acquire Robust Embodied Reasoning:** This involves enabling the AI model to understand the intricate geometric, spatial, and temporal details of the physical world, encompassing capabilities such as perceiving 3D structure, interpreting object relationships, and grasping intuitive physics.
2. **Ground Reasoning in Physical Actions:** The model must learn to translate this understanding into direct physical actions, comprehending contact physics, dynamics, and the nuances of real-world interactions for fast, safe, and dexterous robot control.

Based on these foundational components, the paper sets forth several key objectives for the Gemini Robotics family of models:
- **Develop Gemini Robotics-ER:** To create a Vision-Language Model (VLM) specifically enhanced with strong embodied reasoning capabilities, including object detection, pointing, trajectory and grasp prediction, and 3D understanding (multi-view correspondence, 3D bounding box predictions). This model aims to be capable of supporting robotics applications without requiring explicit robot action data.
- **Develop Gemini Robotics:** To build a state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) generalist model that can directly control robots. This model aims to execute smooth, reactive, and dexterous movements for a wide range of complex manipulation tasks, demonstrating robustness to variations and the ability to follow diverse, open-vocabulary instructions.
- **Demonstrate Specialization and Adaptability:** To show that Gemini Robotics can be further specialized through fine-tuning to tackle highly dexterous, long-horizon tasks (e.g., origami, card games), learn new short-horizon tasks from limited demonstrations (few-shot learning), and adapt to completely novel robot embodiments (e.g., bi-arm platforms, humanoids).
- **Address Safety Considerations:** To discuss and mitigate important safety implications inherent in developing large robotics foundation models, ensuring responsible development aligned with Google AI Principles.

Ultimately, the motivation is to take a "substantial step towards developing general-purpose robots that realize AI’s potential in the physical world," fundamentally changing how robotic systems understand, learn, and are instructed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around developing a family of AI models, **Gemini Robotics**, built upon the advanced multimodal foundation of **Gemini 2.0**. The approach is two-pronged, addressing both embodied reasoning and direct robot action control.

#### 3.1. Gemini Robotics-ER: Enhancing Embodied Reasoning

- **Foundation:** Gemini Robotics-ER is a Vision-Language Model (VLM) that extends Gemini 2.0's inherent multimodal reasoning capabilities into the physical world. While specific architectural modifications for this extension are not detailed, the focus is on enhancing its spatial and temporal understanding.
- **Capabilities:** It enables advanced perception capabilities crucial for robotics, such as:
 - **2D Object Detection and Pointing:** Open-world detection of objects and precise pointing to explicit entities (objects, parts) or implicit notions (affordances, free space), using natural language queries.
 - **Trajectory and Grasp Prediction:** Leveraging pointing capabilities to predict 2D motion trajectories and top-down grasping poses.
 - **3D Understanding:** Multi-view correspondence (correlating 2D points across different camera views for 3D scene understanding) and direct prediction of metric 3D bounding boxes from monocular images (open-vocabulary).
- **Evaluation:** A new open-source benchmark, **ERQA (Embodied Reasoning Question Answering)**, was introduced. ERQA consists of 400 multiple-choice Visual Question Answering (VQA) style questions covering spatial reasoning, action reasoning, trajectory reasoning, state estimation, pointing, multi-view reasoning, and task reasoning. Gemini 2.0 models were evaluated on ERQA, RealworldQA, and BLINK.
- **Robotics Application (Zero/Few-Shot):** Gemini Robotics-ER's embodied reasoning capabilities are directly applied to robot control without any robot action data fine-tuning.
 - **Zero-shot Control via Code Generation:** The model generates Python code to control an ALOHA 2 bimanual robot using a predefined API (for moving grippers, opening/closing, detecting objects, getting grasp poses). It iteratively processes images, robot state, and execution feedback, outputting code for planning and reactive control.
 - **Few-shot Control via In-Context Learning:** The model is conditioned on a handful (e.g., 10) of teleoperated demonstrations (observations, actions, interleaved language descriptions) to directly generate end-effector poses. This approach adapts a method from Di Palo and Johns (2024), with Gemini Robotics-ER performing keypoint and object pose extraction internally.

#### 3.2. Gemini Robotics: Bridging to Physical Action

- **Architecture:** Gemini Robotics is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed for low-latency real-time control. It comprises two main components:
 - **Gemini Robotics backbone:** A distilled version of Gemini Robotics-ER, hosted in the cloud, optimized for low latency (<160ms).
 - **Local action decoder:** Runs on the robot's onboard computer, compensating for backbone latency to achieve an effective control frequency of 50Hz (via action chunks).
- **Data:** Training involves a massive, diverse dataset:
 - **Robot Action Data:** Thousands of hours of real-world expert teleoperated demonstrations collected on a fleet of ALOHA 2 robots over 12 months, covering diverse manipulation skills, objects, task difficulties, and dexterity requirements.
 - **Non-Action Multimodal Data:** Web documents, code, images, audio, and video, as well as embodied reasoning and visual question answering data, to enhance general understanding and reasoning.
- **Evaluation Baselines:** Comparisons are made against state-of-the-art models:
 - **$\pi_0$ re-implement:** A re-implementation of the open-weights $\pi_0$ VLA model (PaliGemma-based) trained on the same diverse data mixture.
 - **Multi-task diffusion policy:** A task-conditioned diffusion policy inspired by ALOHA Unleashed, also trained on the same data.
 - **Single-task diffusion policy:** Used for specialization/adaptation, trained from scratch per task.
- **Evaluation Procedure:** All experiments are real-world robot experiments, using A/B testing and statistical analysis to reduce bias. Performance metrics include binary success/failure rates and continuous "progress scores."
- **Key Capabilities and Specialization:**
 - **Out-of-the-Box Generalism:** Evaluation on 20 diverse short-horizon dexterous tasks sampled from the training dataset.
 - **Instruction Following:** Assessment of the model's ability to precisely follow 25 novel language instructions in various scenes (in-distribution and novel).
 - **Generalization Study:** Evaluation across visual (distractors, background, lighting), instruction (typos, multilingual, rephrasing, descriptive), and action (OOD object positions, new object instances) variations on an 85-task benchmark.
 - **Long-Horizon Dexterity:** Fine-tuning with dedicated datasets (2000-5000 episodes) for six highly challenging, long-horizon tasks (e.g., origami, lunch-box packing, card games).
 - **Enhanced Reasoning & Generalization:** Further fine-tuning with a re-labeled action dataset (bridging actions to embodied reasoning intermediates like trajectories) for tasks requiring single-step reasoning, semantic knowledge, and spatial understanding.
 - **Fast Adaptation:** Evaluating learning curves for new, shorter-horizon tasks with limited demonstrations (5, 20, 100 episodes).
 - **Cross-Embodiment Adaptation:** Adapting the ALOHA 2 trained Gemini Robotics model to control a bi-arm Franka robot and an Apptronik Apollo humanoid with small amounts of data on the target platforms.

#### 3.3. Responsible Development and Safety

- **Alignment with Principles:** Development aligns with Google AI Principles.
- **Content Safety:** Inherits safety training from Gemini 2.0. Additional supervised fine-tuning for embodied outputs (e.g., pointing) to prevent inappropriate generalizations and bias.
- **Semantic Action Safety:** Introduced **ASIMOV-datasets** (visual and text-only VQA instances) to evaluate and improve semantic action safety in open-domain environments. Models are post-trained on these instances, and constitutional AI methods are employed for mitigation and robustness against adversarial prompts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents a comprehensive set of findings demonstrating the advanced capabilities of the Gemini Robotics family of models across embodied reasoning, generalist robot control, and specialized dexterous tasks.

#### 4.1. Embodied Reasoning with Gemini 2.0 and Gemini Robotics-ER

- **State-of-the-Art ER Performance:** Gemini 2.0 (particularly Flash and Pro Experimental variants) achieves new state-of-the-art results on the newly introduced **ERQA benchmark**, as well as on RealworldQA and BLINK, with ERQA proving to be the most challenging.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Efficacy:** CoT prompting significantly boosts Gemini 2.0's performance on ERQA, indicating strong internal reasoning capabilities for complex problems.
- **Robust Perception:** Gemini Robotics-ER exhibits strong capabilities in:
 - **Open-world 2D Object Detection:** Identifying objects by name, category, spatial description, or affordance.
 - **Precise 2D Pointing:** Localizing objects, parts, spatial concepts, and affordances (e.g., grasp points) with high accuracy.
 - **2D Trajectory Prediction:** Generating meaningful motion trajectories grounded in observations.
 - **Top-Down Grasp Prediction:** Extending semantic pointing to predict grasping poses.
 - **3D Understanding:** Performing multi-view correspondence and predicting metric 3D bounding boxes from single images with performance comparable to, or exceeding, specialized expert models (Gemini Robotics-ER achieved a new SOTA on SUN-RGBD).
- **Zero and Few-Shot Robot Control (via Gemini Robotics-ER):**
 - **Zero-shot (Code Generation):** Gemini Robotics-ER achieves an average 53% success rate on simulated ALOHA 2 manipulation tasks, almost twice that of Gemini 2.0 Flash (27%), and 30% for real-world banana handover, highlighting the benefit of enhanced embodied reasoning for downstream robotics.
 - **Few-shot (In-Context Learning):** With just 10 demonstrations, Gemini Robotics-ER improves to 65% success in both simulation and real-world tasks (e.g., 56% for dress folding), showing effective adaptation for dexterous, bimanual tasks. A strong correlation is observed between embodied reasoning capabilities and robot control performance.

#### 4.2. Robot Actions with Gemini Robotics (Generalist VLA)

- **Out-of-the-Box Dexterous Manipulation:** Gemini Robotics demonstrates proficiency in a wide range of short-horizon dexterous tasks, achieving over 80% success on half of the 20 tasks tested. It significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines ($\pi_0$ re-implement and multi-task diffusion), particularly excelling in deformable object manipulation and challenging tasks like "open pink folder" where baselines had non-zero success.
- **Precise Language Instruction Following:** The model closely follows novel and precise natural language instructions in cluttered scenes, even with unseen objects. Gemini Robotics notably outperforms baselines in challenging scenarios with novel objects and fine-grained descriptions, attributed to its high-capacity VLM backbone and diverse data.
- **Robust Generalization:** Gemini Robotics consistently surpasses baselines across three critical generalization axes:
 - **Visual Generalization:** Robustness to distractors, different backgrounds, and lighting conditions.
 - **Instruction Generalization:** Understanding paraphrasing, robustness to typos, support for different languages (e.g., Spanish), and varying levels of specificity.
 - **Action Generalization:** Adapting to different initial object positions and novel object instances (shapes, sizes).
 - Crucially, Gemini Robotics achieves non-zero performance even in cases where baselines catastrophically fail (e.g., new languages).

#### 4.3. Specializing and Adapting Gemini Robotics

- **Long-Horizon Dexterity:** Through specialization with 2000-5000 episodes of high-quality data, Gemini Robotics achieves an impressive 79% average success rate on six highly challenging, long-horizon dexterous tasks (e.g., 100% for packing a lunch-box, 90% for spelling board game, 83% for playing cards, 55% for origami fox). It consistently outperforms specialized versions of baselines. The generalist model's strong foundation is shown to be crucial for this specialization.
- **Enhanced Reasoning and Generalization:** A reasoning-enhanced variant, fine-tuned to bridge action prediction with embodied reasoning, significantly improves success rates in novel, out-of-distribution scenarios requiring one-step reasoning, semantic knowledge, and spatial understanding. This variant also offers increased interpretability through predicted keypoint trajectories.
- **Fast Adaptation to New Tasks:** Gemini Robotics demonstrates rapid learning, achieving over 70% success on 7 out of 8 new, shorter-horizon tasks with as few as 100 demonstrations (15 minutes to 1 hour of data). It reaches 100% success on two tasks with this limited data, outperforming baselines on difficult tasks.
- **Adaptation to New Embodiments:** Preliminary experiments show successful adaptation of Gemini Robotics (trained on ALOHA 2 data) to a bi-arm Franka robot and an Apptronik Apollo humanoid. The adapted model maintains its robustness and generalization capabilities, outperforming single-task diffusion baselines on visual and action generalization tests on the new embodiments.

#### 4.4. Responsible Development and Safety

- The models inherit safety training from Gemini checkpoints for content safety.
- Additional supervised fine-tuning increased the rejection rate for bias-inducing pointing queries to 96%.
- Evaluation using the new ASIMOV-datasets demonstrates strong semantic understanding of physical safety, with performance improvements through constitutional AI methods and mitigation against adversarial prompts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Gemini Robotics family of models marks a profound and substantial step towards realizing the long-sought vision of general-purpose autonomous AI agents in the physical world. Its significance and potential impact span several critical dimensions:

#### 5.1. Advancing Generalist Robotics

By building upon the advanced multimodal capabilities of Gemini 2.0 and integrating them with extensive robot action data, this work pushes the boundaries of generalist robotics. The demonstrated ability of Gemini Robotics to perform a wide array of diverse and complex manipulation tasks "out of the box," coupled with its strong generalization capabilities to unseen visual conditions, instructions, and object variations, represents a significant leap from traditional specialized robotic systems. This heralds a future where robots can adapt to novel situations without extensive re-programming or re-training.

#### 5.2. Bridging AI and Physical Embodiment

The research effectively bridges the gap between the abstract reasoning and understanding of large AI models and the concrete realities of physical interaction. The introduction of Gemini Robotics-ER highlights the importance of "embodied reasoning" as a foundational capability for robots, providing them with a deeper understanding of the 3D world, spatial relationships, and affordances. This integrated approach, where perception, reasoning, and action are unified within a single model, promises more coherent and intelligent robotic behaviors.

#### 5.3. Enabling Dexterous and Long-Horizon Tasks

The paper showcases the unprecedented ability of specialized Gemini Robotics models to achieve high success rates on highly dexterous, long-horizon tasks like folding origami or playing cards. These tasks have historically been extremely challenging for robots, often requiring intricate hand-coded behaviors. The ability to learn such complex multi-step manipulations from demonstrations demonstrates a new level of robotic dexterity and autonomy.

#### 5.4. Practicality and Deployment Potential

The research directly addresses key barriers to widespread robot deployment. The demonstrated rapid adaptation to new, shorter-horizon tasks with minimal demonstrations (as few as 100) significantly reduces the data and effort required for deploying robots in new environments or for new purposes. Furthermore, the successful adaptation to completely novel robot embodiments (Franka, Apollo humanoid) suggests a path towards more universal robotic policies, reducing the need for model retraining for every new hardware platform. The optimized architecture with cloud-based backbone and local decoder also ensures low-latency control, critical for real-time physical interaction.

#### 5.5. Responsible AI in Robotics

The proactive integration of safety and ethical considerations from the outset is highly significant. By addressing not only traditional physical safety but also "semantic action safety" and demonstrating mitigation strategies using novel benchmarks like ASIMOV-datasets and constitutional AI, Google DeepMind sets a precedent for responsible development of powerful, general-purpose robotic AI. This approach is crucial for fostering public trust and ensuring that these technologies are developed and deployed in a manner that benefits society while mitigating potential risks.

In conclusion, Gemini Robotics is not just an incremental improvement; it proposes a fundamental shift in how robots can perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world. By leveraging the power of foundation models, it lays the groundwork for a future where intelligent robots are more versatile, adaptable, and capable of assisting humans in a myriad of daily activities, ultimately "radically chang[ing] what applications robotic systems are used for and by whom."
