# World-Gymnast: Training Robots with Reinforcement Learning in a World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The framework "World-Gymnast" enables training robot policies with reinforcement learning inside an action-conditioned video world model, achieving higher real-robot success rates than supervised fine-tuning and traditional software simulators while supporting diverse training scenarios. It yielded up to an 18-fold increase in real-robot success rate on challenging manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robot learning is constrained by the high cost and impracticality of extensive physical interaction, limiting experimental scale and behavioral diversity.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) from expert demonstrations is data-limited and often fails to cover complex error conditions or recovery behaviors.
- Traditional software simulators suffer from a significant "sim-to-real gap," causing policies to perform poorly when transferred to physical robots, and are costly to develop.

## Method

- World-Gymnast fine-tunes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies through Reinforcement Learning (RL) by generating imagined rollouts in an action-conditioned video world model (WorldGym).
- A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) (GPT-4o) assigns binary task completion rewards to these imagined rollouts, eliminating the need for manual reward engineering.
- The framework employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and includes optimizations like Key-Value caching for the world model to significantly reduce inference latency during rollouts.

## Results

- World-Gymnast achieved up to an 18-fold increase in real-robot success rates compared to SFT (e.g., 72% vs. 4% for 'Put eggplant into blue sink') and outperformed RL in state-of-the-art software simulators for 3 out of 4 tasks.
- Policies trained with synthesized distractors and novel language instructions improved robustness and generalization, with 'World-Gymnast-Language' reaching 81% success on held-out tasks.
- Test-time training on a single novel real-robot frame improved 'Close the drawer' success from 62% to 100%, and iterative world model refinement using real-world data further boosted policy performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training robot policies within world models learned directly from real-world video-action data can effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap and outperform traditional software simulators.
> - Reinforcement learning within these visually realistic, data-driven world models enables active exploration and the discovery of robust, generalizable behaviors beyond passive imitation.
> - World models facilitate novel training capabilities such as learning from diverse initial frames, adapting to novel language instructions, and improving robustness against visual distractors.
