# World-Gymnast: Training Robots with Reinforcement Learning in a World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The framework "World-Gymnast" enables training robot policies with reinforcement learning inside an action-conditioned video world model, achieving higher real-robot success rates than supervised fine-tuning and traditional software simulators while supporting diverse training scenarios. It yielded up to an 18-fold increase in real-robot success rate on challenging manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robot learning is constrained by the high cost and impracticality of extensive physical interaction, limiting experimental scale and behavioral diversity.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) from expert demonstrations is data-limited and often fails to cover complex error conditions or recovery behaviors.
- Traditional software simulators suffer from a significant "sim-to-real gap," causing policies to perform poorly when transferred to physical robots, and are costly to develop.

## Method

- World-Gymnast fine-tunes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies through Reinforcement Learning (RL) by generating imagined rollouts in an action-conditioned video world model (WorldGym).
- A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) (GPT-4o) assigns binary task completion rewards to these imagined rollouts, eliminating the need for manual reward engineering.
- The framework employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and includes optimizations like Key-Value caching for the world model to significantly reduce inference latency during rollouts.

## Results

- World-Gymnast achieved up to an 18-fold increase in real-robot success rates compared to SFT (e.g., 72% vs. 4% for 'Put eggplant into blue sink') and outperformed RL in state-of-the-art software simulators for 3 out of 4 tasks.
- Policies trained with synthesized distractors and novel language instructions improved robustness and generalization, with 'World-Gymnast-Language' reaching 81% success on held-out tasks.
- Test-time training on a single novel real-robot frame improved 'Close the drawer' success from 62% to 100%, and iterative world model refinement using real-world data further boosted policy performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training robot policies within world models learned directly from real-world video-action data can effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap and outperform traditional software simulators.
> - Reinforcement learning within these visually realistic, data-driven world models enables active exploration and the discovery of robust, generalizable behaviors beyond passive imitation.
> - World models facilitate novel training capabilities such as learning from diverse initial frames, adapting to novel language instructions, and improving robustness against visual distractors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot learning, particularly for complex manipulation tasks, is fundamentally constrained by the high cost and impracticality of extensive physical interaction. Every trial-and-error cycle on a real robot demands significant time, introduces wear-and-tear, and raises safety concerns, creating a bottleneck for scaling robot capabilities. This research directly addresses this critical challenge by exploring a novel paradigm for training robot policies.

Historically, two primary alternatives have been employed to mitigate the cost of real-world interaction:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) from Expert Demonstrations:** Robots are trained to imitate human or scripted trajectories. However, this approach is limited by the availability of high-quality expert data, which tends to cover only a narrow range of scenarios. It often fails to expose robots to complex error conditions or the recovery behaviors essential for robust deployment.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Software-Based Simulators:** Policies are trained in virtual environments that approximate real-world physics. While more scalable in terms of interaction data, traditional software simulators suffer from a significant "sim-to-real gap." This gap arises from discrepancies in visual features, physics parameters, and material properties between the simulated and real worlds, leading to policies that perform poorly when transferred to physical robots. Moreover, creating accurate and diverse software simulators for every new scenario is costly and time-consuming.

In recent years, the emergence of **world models learned directly from real-world video-action data** has opened a promising third avenue. These models predict how the visual environment evolves under a robot's actions, effectively acting as data-driven, action-conditioned video simulators. They hold the potential to alleviate the visual aspects of the sim-to-real gap and generalize to novel initial frames, as they are trained on real-world visual observations. However, questions remained about their physical realism and whether policies trained within these models could genuinely outperform existing alternatives on physical robots.

"World-Gymnast" directly tackles this ambiguity. It positions itself at the intersection of large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, video world models, and vision-language models (VLMs). By proposing to fine-tune VLA policies through RL *inside* a learned world model, the authors aim to leverage the best of both worlds: the broad generalization capabilities of foundation models and the active exploration benefits of RL, all within a visually realistic, data-driven simulated environment.

The paper differentiates itself from related work in several key ways:
- **Model-Based RL:** While model-based RL has a long history, much of it focused on lower-dimensional state spaces or visually simplistic simulated domains. This work specifically applies it to high-dimensional pixel observations and complex real-robot manipulation, leveraging the power of modern transformer architectures for world modeling.
- **Sim-to-Real RL:** Unlike traditional sim-to-real approaches that rely on domain randomization or adaptation to bridge the reality gap, World-Gymnast fundamentally bypasses manual asset creation and physics tuning by learning the dynamics directly from real-world data.
- **Video Generation for Robot Learning:** While many works use video generation for planning or imitation, World-Gymnast uses video generation primarily as the *environment* for RL, enabling self-improvement of policies.
- **Policy Evaluation vs. Improvement:** While several recent works have shown world models can *evaluate* robot policies, World-Gymnast focuses on using them for direct *improvement* of policy performance, with real-robot verification.

By demonstrating superior real-robot performance and novel training capabilities, World-Gymnast represents a significant step towards enabling scalable, data-efficient, and robust robot learning, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with current foundation models and reinforcement learning paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for the "World-Gymnast" project stems from the inherent and fundamental bottleneck in robot learning: the prohibitive cost and practical challenges associated with physical interaction in the real world. This cost limits the scale of experimentation, the diversity of learned behaviors, and ultimately, the robustness and generalization of robotic policies.

The authors identify the shortcomings of existing alternatives:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) from Expert Demonstrations:** While seemingly efficient, SFT is severely data-limited. Expert demonstrations rarely cover the long tail of challenging situations, nor do they provide examples of crucial recovery behaviors necessary for real-world robustness.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Software Simulators:** These environments, despite offering scalable interaction, are expensive to develop and maintain for diverse scenarios. More critically, they suffer from a "sim-to-real gap," where visual and physical discrepancies prevent effective transfer of learned policies to physical robots.

Against this backdrop, the emergence of **world models**—generative models learned from real-world video-action data—presents a compelling new frontier. These models predict future visual states conditioned on actions, effectively acting as "video simulators" derived directly from reality. The core question that motivates this research is: **Can training a policy in such a learned world model be more effective than supervised learning or RL in a traditional software simulator in achieving superior real-robot performance?**

Based on this foundational question, the key objectives of the "World-Gymnast" project are:

1. **Develop a Novel RL Framework:** To propose and implement World-Gymnast, a framework that performs Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies by generating imagined rollouts in an action-conditioned video world model and subsequently rewarding these rollouts using a Vision-Language Model (VLM).
2. **Demonstrate Superior Real-Robot Performance:** To empirically evaluate World-Gymnast on real robot hardware using an automated evaluation platform (AutoEval) and demonstrate that policies trained with this framework significantly outperform those trained with traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and RL in software-based simulators across a suite of manipulation tasks.
3. **Explore and Showcase Emergent Capabilities:** To highlight and validate the unique and "intriguing capabilities" enabled by world-model-based RL, which include:
 - Training from diverse initial image frames.
 - Training on novel, out-of-distribution (OOD) language instructions.
 - Training with visually distracting objects to improve robustness.
 - Performing test-time training from a single novel real-robot frame for rapid adaptation.
 - Implementing an online, iterative improvement loop for both the world model and the policy (Dyna-style).
4. **Advance Towards Generalist Robots:** Ultimately, to suggest that learning a world model and training robot policies in a scalable, cloud-based environment could be the pivotal step in bridging the gap between robots capable of executing specific demonstrations and truly generalist robots that can reliably operate in diverse, unstructured household environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

World-Gymnast proposes a sophisticated, model-based reinforcement learning framework designed to fine-tune Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies efficiently and robustly. The core idea is to replace costly real-world interactions or imperfect software simulations with imagined rollouts within a learned, data-driven world model.

The methodology is built upon several key components and a systematic training approach:

#### 3.1. Core Framework Components

1. **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Policy (π):** This is the robot control policy being trained. It takes an image observation ($o_t$) and a language instruction ($g$) as input and outputs a distribution over robot actions ($a_t$). For this work, the authors use `OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025)` as the base policy, which is an optimized version of `OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024)` pretrained on the `Open X-Embodiment dataset (Vuong et al., 2023)` and then fine-tuned on `BridgeData V2 (Walke et al., 2023)`. Modifications include disabling proprioception and secondary camera inputs and using a `LLAMA-2 (Touvron et al., 2023)` LM head for action probabilities.
2. **Action-Conditioned Video World Model ($\hat{T}$):** This model acts as the environment simulator. Given a current observation and a robot action, it predicts the next visual observation. The authors utilize `WorldGym (Quevedo et al., 2025)`, a 600M parameter model pretrained on the `Open X-Embodiment dataset`. WorldGym encodes 256x256 RGB images into a latent space using a VAE and uses a 16-layer transformer to predict latent frame sequences conditioned on actions. A critical optimization is the implementation of Key-Value (KV) caching for its temporal attention layers, significantly reducing inference latency during rollouts (10x reduction for 40-frame rollouts).
3. **Vision-Language Model (VLM) for Reward ($\hat{R}$):** Instead of manually engineering reward functions, a pre-trained VLM, specifically `GPT-4o (Hurst et al., 2024)`, is employed to assign a binary task completion reward to imagined rollouts. The VLM receives a downsampled sequence of predicted video frames from a rollout and the language instruction, then provides a brief reasoning and a binary score (0 for failure, 1 for success).

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm

World-Gymnast employs **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024)**, a policy gradient algorithm, to update the VLA policy.
- **Imagined Rollouts:** For a given task instruction and initial observation, the VLA policy generates a sequence of actions. These actions are fed into the world model ($\hat{T}$), which then generates K independent imagined trajectories (sequences of predicted frames). Rollouts are typically up to 40 steps.
- **Reward Assignment:** The VLM ($\hat{R}$) evaluates each of these K trajectories, assigning a binary task completion reward.
- **Advantage Calculation:** GRPO computes advantages by normalizing the rewards within each group of K trajectories using the group's mean and standard deviation. This group-based normalization acts as a baseline, improving training stability. The trajectory-level advantage is assigned to all steps within that trajectory.
- **Policy Update:** The VLA policy is optimized using a PPO-style objective with clipping based on the calculated advantages.
- **Training Tricks:** The authors incorporate several techniques for stable training and improved exploration, including discarding the KL penalty, dynamic sampling to filter groups with no reward variance, higher clipping ratios, and a higher temperature for action sampling during rollouts.

#### 3.3. Diverse Training Scenarios Enabled by the World Model

A significant advantage of World-Gymnast is the flexibility provided by the world model for data augmentation and diverse training:
- **Training from Any Frame:** Policies can be trained from arbitrary initial image frames, enabling learning of recovery behaviors and increasing the effective training data.
- **Training on Novel Language Instructions:** VLMs can be used to generate plausible new tasks and associated language instructions for existing scenes, allowing the VLA policy to generalize to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) language tasks.
- **Training with Distractors:** Image editing tools (e.g., Nano Banana) are used to synthesize additional, task-irrelevant objects into initial frames, enhancing policy robustness to visual clutter in real-world environments.

#### 3.4. Test-Time Training (TTT)

World-Gymnast supports test-time adaptation. When a novel real-robot frame is encountered, the policy can perform a short RL fine-tuning phase *in the world model*, starting from that specific test frame, before deployment. This allows rapid, on-the-fly adaptation without real-world data collection.

#### 3.5. Iterative World Model and Policy Improvement (Dyna-style)

Inspired by Dyna-style algorithms, World-Gymnast enables an iterative refinement process:
1. The current policy is rolled out in the real world (e.g., during AutoEval).
2. The collected real-world interaction data (frame-action sequences) is used to fine-tune and improve the world model.
3. The updated, more accurate world model then generates improved imagined rollouts for further policy optimization.
This feedback loop helps the world model adapt to the policy's evolving state distribution and provides the policy with increasingly accurate long-horizon predictions.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Robot Platform:** Bridge robot setup with a WidowX robot arm.
- **Evaluation Tasks:** A curated suite of 17 tabletop manipulation tasks from `OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024)` for WorldGym evaluation, and 4 specific manipulation tasks (open/close drawer, put eggplant in blue sink, put eggplant in yellow basket) from `AutoEval (Zhou et al., 2025)` for real-robot validation.
- **Computational Resources:** Training was conducted on 4 NVIDIA H200 GPUs (140GB each) for 1-2 days.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that World-Gymnast significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robot policy training by leveraging learned world models.

#### 4.1. Superior Real-Robot Performance

- **Outperforming Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** World-Gymnast achieved substantially higher real-robot success rates compared to SFT using the original Bridge data. For challenging tasks like "Put the eggplant into the blue sink," World-Gymnast attained a 72% success rate, an 18-fold improvement over SFT's 4%. Similarly, for "Put the eggplant into the yellow basket," it showed nearly a 10-fold improvement (78% vs. 8%). Even an iterative SFT approach (Iter-SFT), which used synthetic data from the world model as additional supervision, performed worse than World-Gymnast, suggesting that active RL exploration leads to more generalizable behaviors than passive imitation of synthetic data.
- **Outperforming RL in Software Simulators:** World-Gymnast outperformed RL training in `SIMPLER (Li et al., 2024)`, a state-of-the-art software simulator for the Bridge robot, for 3 out of 4 tasks. For instance, on "Put the eggplant into the blue sink," World-Gymnast achieved 72% success versus SIMPLER's 32%, and 78% versus 40% on "Put the eggplant into the yellow basket." This is particularly notable because SIMPLER was trained on "digital twins" of these exact tasks, yet struggled to transfer to the real world, highlighting the enduring sim-to-real gap that world models learned from real data can bridge. The only exception was "Close the drawer," where the base policy already performed well, making further RL improvements less pronounced.

#### 4.2. Diverse Training Capabilities

World-Gymnast leverages the flexibility of world models to enable training in novel and diverse scenarios:
- **Training with Distractors:** By generating training data with visually distracting objects using image editing tools, "World-Gymnast-Distract" policies became more robust. WorldGym evaluations showed "World-Gymnast-Distract" to be the most robust to visual perturbations, and surprisingly, it also improved success rates on the *original* OpenVLA tasks (78% vs. 74% for standard World-Gymnast). Qualitative analysis showed improved grasping and placement even amidst visual artifacts from the world model.
- **Training with Novel Language Instructions:** "World-Gymnast-Language," trained on new tasks defined by out-of-distribution language instructions (generated by a VLM) for existing scenes, demonstrated improved performance on held-out OpenVLA tasks (81% vs. 74% for standard World-Gymnast). This indicates that scaling data through language augmentation significantly improves VLA policy generalization.
- **Scaling the Number of Training Tasks:** By including five additional manipulation tasks from the Bridge dataset in training, "World-Gymnast-Scaled" further improved performance on the OpenVLA held-out split (81% vs. 74%), confirming the framework's ability to effectively leverage increased task diversity.

#### 4.3. Test-Time Optimization

The ability to perform Test-Time Training (TTT) was demonstrated on real robots. By fine-tuning the base policy with RL in the world model, starting from a novel test-time frame, the success rate for "Close the drawer" dramatically improved from 62% to 100%. While this rapid adaptation was powerful, the authors noted an overfitting effect to the specific task, causing performance degradation on other tasks—an area for future research.

#### 4.4. Iterative World Model and Policy Improvement (Dyna-style)

A compelling result was the qualitative and quantitative improvement through iterative world model and policy refinement. Real-world rollouts collected during AutoEval were used to fine-tune the world model. This updated world model (termed "WorldGym-Dyna") showed visibly higher fidelity and closer adherence to real-world outcomes compared to the original WorldGym and SIMPLER (Figure 3). Using this improved world model as the RL environment further boosted the "Close the drawer" success rate to 95%. This highlights a powerful feedback loop for continuous self-improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "World-Gymnast" research paper presents a significant stride in robot learning, offering a framework that has the potential to fundamentally transform how robotic policies are developed and deployed. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Democratizing Robot Learning and Reducing Costs

The most immediate impact is the substantial reduction in the dependency on expensive physical hardware and the laborious process of collecting human expert demonstrations. By enabling policy training primarily within a learned world model, World-Gymnast lowers the barrier to entry for robotic research and development. This cost-efficiency allows smaller labs, individual researchers, and even hobbyists to experiment with complex robotic tasks, potentially democratizing access to advanced robot learning capabilities.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap with Data-Driven Models

The work conclusively demonstrates that world models learned from real-world data can create a more effective training ground than traditional, manually engineered software simulators. By outperforming simulator-based RL, World-Gymnast showcases a powerful method to overcome the notorious "sim-to-real gap" that has long plagued robotics. This shifts the paradigm from trying to make simulators perfectly mimic reality to learning reality's dynamics directly from observation.

#### 5.3. Enhancing Policy Generalization and Robustness

World-Gymnast fosters policies that are more generalizable and robust. Its ability to train on:
- **Diverse initial frames:** Improves recovery behaviors and expands the policy's operational envelope.
- **Novel language instructions:** Empowers VLA policies to understand and execute tasks that were not explicitly demonstrated, moving towards truly flexible human-robot interaction.
- **Synthesized distractor objects:** Makes policies resilient to visual clutter and unpredictable environments, crucial for deploying robots in "anyone's messy household."

#### 5.4. Unlocking Novel Training and Adaptation Paradigms

The framework introduces and validates several innovative capabilities:
- **Test-Time Training (TTT):** The ability to quickly adapt a policy to a novel, unseen real-robot scene without collecting new real-world data is a game-changer for rapid deployment and on-the-fly customization. This offers a powerful mechanism for robot personalization and fine-tuning.
- **Iterative World Model and Policy Improvement (Dyna-style):** The demonstration of a virtuous cycle where real-world interactions improve the world model, which in turn improves the policy, represents a crucial step towards self-improving and continuously learning robot systems. This data flywheel approach promises ever-improving accuracy and performance.

#### 5.5. Paving the Way for Generalist Robots

The ultimate vision articulated by the authors—learning a world model and training policies "in the cloud"—points towards a future where generalist robots can acquire skills scalably and reliably. By enabling training on massive, diverse datasets within a realistic simulated environment, World-Gymnast brings closer the reality of robots that can perform a wide array of tasks in unstructured environments, significantly broadening the applications of robotics.

#### 5.6. Acknowledged Limitations and Ethical Considerations

The authors are transparent about current limitations, such as the world model's inability to generalize to initial frames far from its training distribution, and the reliance on VLMs for reward, which can suffer from hallucinations. These point to clear and important future research directions (broader pretraining, more robust reward models, dense rewards, preventing reward hacking). Furthermore, the impact statement thoughtfully addresses the ethical implications, emphasizing the need for rigorous safety verification of policies trained in generative world models before deployment in safety-critical real-world settings, given the potential for exploiting "hallucinated physics."

In conclusion, "World-Gymnast" is a pivotal contribution that leverages the power of generative AI and reinforcement learning to address long-standing challenges in robotics. It lays a robust foundation for building more capable, adaptable, and generalist robots, pushing the field closer to a future where intelligent robots can seamlessly integrate into human environments.
