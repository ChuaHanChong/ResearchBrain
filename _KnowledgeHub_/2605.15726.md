# Nudging Beyond the Comfort Zone: Efficient Strategy-Guided Exploration for RLVR

> [!summary] Summary
> The NUDGERL framework enhances reasoning in large language models (LLMs) trained with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by introducing strategy-guided exploration and an inter-intra group advantage mechanism. This approach achieves superior performance, outperforming brute-force methods with an 8x reduction in rollout budget and exceeding oracle-guided baselines across mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing RLVR exploration is fundamentally bottlenecked, as policy improvements are limited to trajectories that have already been sampled.
- Brute-force scaling of rollouts to discover rare, correct reasoning paths is computationally prohibitive and exhibits diminishing returns.
- Objective-based exploration (e.g., entropy regularization) lacks precise control over what is explored, often yielding unproductive trajectories, while oracle supervision is expensive and restricts diversity.

## Method

- **NUDGERL**'s ==Strategy Nudging== generates lightweight, heuristic text contexts to condition policy rollouts, forcing exploration of diverse reasoning paths with ==context dropout== for generalization.
- ==Inter-Intra Group Advantage== assigns credit based on both intra-context (relative to the same context) and inter-context (relative to global average) reward signals, balancing exploration among different strategies.
- A ==distillation-augmented RL objective== combines a PPO-style RL term with an advantage-weighted distillation term to effectively transfer context-conditioned strategies to the context-free base policy.

## Results

- NUDGERL achieved the best average pass@1 performance on Qwen3-4B and Olmo3-7B models using only **8 rollouts**, outperforming GRPO with **32 and 64 rollouts** (e.g., **0.489** pass@1 vs. **0.487** at 32 rollouts on Qwen3-4B).
- The framework consistently surpassed POPE, an oracle-prefix baseline, demonstrating the efficacy of structured exploration over injecting privileged solution signals.
- Strategy Nudging significantly increased the number of distinct reasoning approaches discovered and showed faster, more stable pass@1 improvement during training compared to GRPO variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured, lightweight strategy-level contexts can efficiently induce diverse and semantically meaningful reasoning trajectories, bypassing the limitations of brute-force sampling or expensive oracle supervision.
> - A robust credit assignment mechanism that disentangles intrinsic trajectory quality from context-specific biases is crucial for effective learning when policy rollouts are conditioned on diverse prompts.
> - Distilling effective behaviors discovered through context-conditioned exploration into a context-free base policy is essential to bridge the train-test gap and enable practical inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a methodology for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). Approaches like Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) leverage verifiable rewards to enable scalable post-training without requiring dense human supervision. This paradigm has found applications in various domains, particularly in mathematical reasoning.

Despite its successes, RLVR is fundamentally constrained by its exploration capabilities. Policy improvements are limited to trajectories that have already been sampled. Existing efforts to address this limitation generally fall into three categories:
1. **Brute-force scaling:** Increasing the number of sampled rollouts. While this can increase the probability of discovering rare trajectories, it is computationally expensive and quickly becomes prohibitive. Moreover, it exhibits diminishing returns for covering long-tail, low-probability correct reasoning paths.
2. **Modification of optimization objectives:** Methods such as entropy regularization or decoupled clipping encourage broader exploration at the distribution level. However, these approaches often provide limited control over the semantic content of what is explored and may fail to ensure coverage of meaningful reasoning strategies. Their stochastic nature can also lead to unproductive or incoherent trajectories.
3. **Leveraging privileged information:** This line of work utilizes external signals, such as oracle solutions or intermediate reasoning steps, to guide the policy toward correct trajectories. While effective for improving the *feasibility* of solving problems, these methods rely on strong supervision signals that are expensive to obtain and difficult to scale. Furthermore, by directing the policy toward predefined successful paths, they may inadvertently restrict exploration diversity and hinder the discovery of alternative reasoning strategies.

This research positions itself by addressing the exploration bottleneck in RLVR through a novel approach that structures the reasoning space scalably. It aims to develop a method that promotes diverse and meaningful exploration without incurring the high computational cost of brute-force scaling or the expense and potential limitations of oracle supervision. The work seeks to improve exploration efficiency and policy robustness by proactively inducing diverse reasoning strategies, contrasting with existing methods that primarily focus on distribution-level broadening or feasibility enhancement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the fundamental exploration bottleneck in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for large language models (LLMs) used in reasoning tasks. The authors identify that current RLVR methods are limited because policy improvement is contingent upon the discovery of successful reasoning trajectories, which can be rare and difficult to sample.

The motivation for addressing this bottleneck stems from several limitations of existing approaches:

- **Computational Cost of Brute-Force Exploration:** Simply increasing the number of rollouts (brute-force scaling) to discover rare, correct reasoning paths is computationally prohibitive. The probability of sampling a low-probability, "long-tail" correct trajectory decreases significantly with rollout budget, making full coverage infeasible in practice (as quantified by the inverse relationship between expected rollouts and trajectory probability, E[# rollouts] = 1/π(y|x_0)).
- **Limited Control in Objective-Based Exploration:** Modifying the optimization objective through techniques like entropy regularization or decoupled clipping encourages broader exploration but lacks precise control over *what* is explored. These methods do not guarantee that semantically meaningful or diverse reasoning strategies are sampled, often leading to stochastic perturbations that may not yield productive or coherent trajectories.
- **Expense and Narrow Focus of Privileged Information:** Approaches that rely on oracle solutions or intermediate reasoning steps, while improving the feasibility of finding correct trajectories, are expensive to obtain and challenging to scale. More critically, by guiding the policy towards known successful paths, they may inadvertently constrain the diversity of exploration and prevent the discovery of alternative or innovative reasoning strategies. These methods are primarily "feasibility-oriented" rather than "diversity-oriented."
- **Need for Context-Free Inference:** Even if exploration is enhanced through various means, the ultimate goal is for the LLM to perform effectively without external contexts or hints during inference. This necessitates a mechanism to transfer any improvements discovered during guided exploration back to the base policy.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the NUDGERL framework are:

1. **Induce Diverse Reasoning Trajectories:** To explicitly structure the reasoning space and efficiently expose a broader range of semantically meaningful strategies that the model might otherwise ignore.
2. **Scalable Exploration:** To achieve this diversity without relying on expensive, human-annotated oracle supervision or computationally intensive brute-force rollout scaling.
3. **Effective Credit Assignment:** To develop a robust credit assignment mechanism that can disentangle reward variations originating from intrinsic trajectory quality and context-specific biases when rollouts are generated under diverse, context-conditioned prompts.
4. **Transfer of Discovered Behaviors:** To bridge the "train-test gap" by distilling effective behaviors and strategies discovered during context-conditioned exploration back into the base policy, enabling the model to internalize these strategies for context-free inference.
5. **Outperform Existing Baselines:** To empirically demonstrate that the proposed structured, diversity-driven exploration method can achieve superior performance compared to both brute-force rollout scaling and oracle-guided baselines across challenging reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NUDGERL framework is designed to address the exploration bottleneck in RLVR by introducing structured and diversity-driven exploration. It comprises three interconnected components: Strategy Nudging, Inter-Intra Group Advantage, and a Distillation-augmented RL objective.

#### 3.1. Strategy Nudging: Structured Exploration via Strategy-Level Contexts

The core idea behind Strategy Nudging is to reduce the cost of discovering rare but useful reasoning trajectories by conditioning the generation process on specific contexts. Instead of relying on random sampling from a single policy, which often collapses to dominant modes, NUDGERL forces the model to explore diverse reasoning paths.

- **Context Generation:** For each problem (original prompt x_0), a pool of lightweight, heuristic text prompts, termed "strategy-level contexts" C(x_0) = {c_1..., c_M}, is generated. These contexts are typically keyword-level hints (e.g., "Pythagorean theorem") that suggest a specific mathematical concept or technique. In this work, these contexts are generated offline using a lightweight LLM (gpt-4o-mini) without additional verification. For the main experiments, two contexts are generated per problem.
- **Context-Conditioned Rollout Generation:** For each rollout, a context c^(i) is sampled uniformly from C(x_0). To ensure the policy can still learn to operate without explicit contexts and to maintain compatibility with the original prompt distribution, context dropout is applied. A binary mask b^(i) is sampled from a Bernoulli distribution with parameter (1 - p_drop). If b^(i) = 1, the sampled context c^(i) is used; otherwise, an empty context (∅) is used. The final prompt for generating a rollout is then x_1^(i) = (x_0, z^(i)), where z^(i) is the selected context or ∅. The rollout y_i is then sampled from the policy conditioned on this augmented prompt, y_i ∼ π_θ(· | x_1^(i)). By varying z^(i) across rollouts, this mechanism explicitly induces diversity at the input level.

#### 3.2. Inter-Intra Group Advantage: Learning to Balance Exploration between Strategies

Standard GRPO computes advantages by comparing rewards within a single group of rollouts sampled from the same prompt distribution. With Strategy Nudging, rollouts are conditioned on different contexts, leading to multiple "context groups." Applying a single group baseline in this scenario would entangle intrinsic trajectory quality with context-specific biases, leading to unreliable advantage estimates.

To address this, NUDGERL proposes the Inter-Intra Group Advantage, which assigns credit based on two signals:
- **Intra-context signal:** This component captures the quality of a specific trajectory relative to other trajectories generated under the *same* conditioning context. For each context group g ∈ G(x_0) (where G(x_0) is the set of unique contexts used for x_0), a context-level mean reward ¯r_g is computed.
- **Inter-context signal:** This component assesses the relative reliability or overall success rate of a given context itself, compared to the global average success rate across all contexts for the problem. The global mean reward ¯r is computed across all rollouts for the problem.

The advantage ˆA_i for a rollout y_i is calculated as:
A_i = (r_i - ¯r_z^(i)) + λ(¯r_z^(i) - ¯r), if z^(i) ≠ ∅
A_i = r_i - ¯r, if z^(i) = ∅
The final normalized advantage ˆA_i is derived from A_i by subtracting its mean (μ_A) and dividing by its standard deviation (σ_A + δ, for numerical stability).

The parameter λ controls the balance between exploration and exploitation among strategies. Proposition 3.1 states that for binary rewards, if λ ∈, a higher reward always translates to a higher advantage.
- If λ < 1, successes from lower-reward contexts are favored, encouraging exploration of less common strategies.
- If λ > 1, successes from higher-reward contexts are favored, emphasizing exploitation of more reliable strategies.
- If λ = 1, equal-reward trajectories are treated identically across contexts. The paper empirically finds λ = 1.1 performs best, suggesting a slight emphasis on more reliable contexts.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

Since the policy at inference time operates without external contexts, effective strategies discovered through context-conditioned exploration must be transferred to the base policy π_θ(· | x_0). NUDGERL achieves this through a combined objective:

- **Reinforcement Learning Objective (L_RL):** This term is a PPO-style clipped objective that optimizes the context-conditioned policy π_θ(· | x_1^(i)). It reinforces successful trajectories within each specific context.
- **Distillation Objective (L_Distill):** This term enables cross-context generalization by explicitly transferring effective behaviors to the base policy. It is an advantage-weighted distillation term, L_Distill(θ) = -E_{y~π_θ(·|x_1)}[ˆA log π_θ(y|x_0)]. This objective selectively emphasizes trajectories with high normalized advantage, ensuring that only beneficial behaviors contribute to updating the context-free policy π_θ(· | x_0).

The final training objective combines both terms: L_NUDGERL = L_RL + λ_distill L_Distill. The parameter λ_distill controls the strength of the distillation. This unified objective allows for both targeted exploration under context and the internalization of successful strategies by the base policy.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

NUDGERL was applied to two pre-trained LLMs: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Olmo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT. The training dataset used was DAPO-17k-Processed. Evaluation was conducted on five math benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, Apex Shortlist) using pass@1, estimated from 128 rollouts with an unbiased estimator, and solutions were graded by math-verify. Baselines included the base model, GRPO with varying rollout budgets (N ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64}), and POPE (an oracle-prefix method). For POPE, oracle solutions were generated using DeepSeek Reasoner v3.2 and truncated to 15% length.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of NUDGERL demonstrated its effectiveness in improving exploration efficiency and overall performance in RLVR for LLMs across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

- **Superior Performance with Fewer Rollouts:** NUDGERL achieved the best average performance on both Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Olmo3-7B-Instruct-SFT models while utilizing only 8 rollouts per prompt.
 - On Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, NUDGERL attained an average pass@1 of 0.489. This slightly surpassed the peak GRPO performance at 32 rollouts (0.487) and significantly outperformed GRPO at 64 rollouts (0.451), effectively achieving better results with an 8x smaller rollout budget.
 - On Olmo3-7B-Instruct-SFT, NUDGERL reached 0.285, outperforming the best GRPO result of 0.281 (at 32 rollouts).
 - The results also indicated that GRPO's performance could degrade with excessively large rollout budgets (e.g., GRPO-64 performed worse than GRPO-32 on both models), suggesting instability under brute-force scaling. In contrast, NUDGERL maintained robust performance by enhancing exploration quality.

- **Outperformance Against Oracle-Guided Baselines:** NUDGERL consistently surpassed POPE, an oracle-prefix method that relies on prefixes of oracle solutions. This finding suggests that structured exploration over diverse, lightweight strategies (generated by a lightweight LLM without verification) is more effective than injecting narrow, privileged solution signals, which typically require expensive supervision.

- **Efficient Coverage of Diverse Reasoning Modes:**
 - A comparison of reasoning structures (clustered by an LLM-as-a-judge) revealed that Strategy Nudging significantly increased the number of distinct reasoning approaches discovered compared to naive sampling under the same rollout budget (Figure 1). This indicates improved coverage of the reasoning space.
 - NUDGERL demonstrated faster and more stable improvement in pass@1 during training compared to GRPO variants across different rollout budgets (Figure 3b). By 200 steps, NUDGERL already exceeded 0.42 pass@1 on AIME24/25, while GRPO variants remained lower.
 - At inference time, NUDGERL consistently outperformed GRPO-8 across the full k-range for pass@k metrics (Figure 3c), indicating improved inference-time sample efficiency.

- **Internalization of Effective Strategies (Case Study):** A qualitative analysis of an AIME25 problem showed that the NUDGERL-trained model successfully discovered and exploited the "shoelace formula" strategy, leading to correct solutions. In contrast, the GRPO-trained model predominantly explored ineffective strategies, resulting in failed or truncated solutions. This illustrates that Strategy Nudging effectively exposes rare but potent reasoning modes, and the Inter-Intra Group Advantage reinforces their use.

- **Effect of Contexts During Training:** The training dynamics showed that both context-conditioned (hinted) and context-free (dropout) reward means improved together (Figure 5). This supports the efficacy of the distillation objective in transferring strategies discovered under context-conditioned exploration to the base policy. The observation that dropout reward occasionally exceeded hinted reward suggests that the primary role of context is to induce diversity, rather than solely simplify the problem.

- **Ablation Studies on NUDGERL Components:**
 - **Context Dropout (p_drop):** A moderate dropout rate (p_drop = 0.5) yielded the best average performance (Figure 6a). This balance enables exploration beyond fixed contexts while stabilizing group-wise statistics, indicating that a mixture of context-conditioned and context-free samples is beneficial.
 - **Hint Sampling Strategy:** Random sampling of contexts consistently outperformed "top-ranked" (oracle-selected) contexts (Figure 6b). This reinforces the idea that diversity is more critical than selecting the "best" specific hint, making the approach scalable with simple, unverified context generation.
 - **Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off (λ):** Setting λ = 1.1 (favoring successes from consistently higher-reward contexts) achieved the best performance (Figure 7a). This indicates that once diversity is induced through nudging, slightly emphasizing exploitation of more reliable contexts contributes to learning high-quality solutions.
 - **Distillation Coefficient (λ_distill):** The distillation term was found to be essential, as removing it (λ_distill = 0) led to a performance drop. A moderate coefficient of λ_distill = 0.1 yielded the best results (Figure 7b), suggesting that distillation should complement, rather than dominate, the RL objective.

- **Comparison with ϵ_high Scaling:** NUDGERL, using a standard ϵ_high = 0.2, consistently outperformed GRPO even when GRPO's ϵ_high was scaled across a range of values (Figure 7c). This indicates that improving exploration quality through structured guidance is more effective than merely increasing the magnitude of stochastic policy updates, which can lead to instability at extreme values of ϵ_high for GRPO.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces NUDGERL, a framework that provides a scalable and efficient approach to address the exploration bottleneck in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for large language models (LLMs). The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Exploration Efficiency:** NUDGERL offers a more efficient alternative to brute-force rollout scaling. By strategically guiding exploration with lightweight, strategy-level contexts, it reduces the computational resources required to discover rare but effective reasoning trajectories. This can lower the barrier to entry for training advanced reasoning LLMs, making such capabilities more accessible to researchers and organizations with limited computational budgets.
- **Improved Scalability of RLVR:** Unlike methods that rely on expensive human-annotated oracle solutions or intermediate steps, NUDGERL utilizes easily generatable, unverified keyword-level contexts. This approach is more scalable, as it avoids the prohibitive cost and effort associated with strong supervision signals, allowing for broader application across diverse and larger problem sets.
- **Promotion of Reasoning Diversity:** The framework explicitly encourages the exploration of a wider range of semantically meaningful reasoning strategies. This emphasis on diversity, rather than merely increasing the probability of known successful paths, may lead to LLMs that are more robust, adaptable, and capable of discovering novel or less obvious solution methods, similar to human problem-solving.
- **Bridging the Train-Test Gap:** The distillation-augmented RL objective is critical for ensuring that improvements gained during context-conditioned exploration are effectively transferred to the base policy for context-free inference. This mechanism is essential for practical deployment, as it enables the model to internalize diverse reasoning skills without requiring explicit hints or external guidance at test time.
- **Advancement in RLVR Methodology:** NUDGERL shifts the focus of exploration in RLVR from primarily "feasibility-oriented" (e.g., finding *a* correct solution with help) to "diversity-oriented" (e.g., finding *various* correct solution approaches). This methodological shift provides a new perspective on improving LLM reasoning capabilities.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** The principles of strategy-guided exploration and distillation could be applicable beyond mathematical reasoning. Any domain where LLMs engage in structured problem-solving, and where lightweight prompts can induce diverse reasoning paths (e.g., code generation, scientific hypothesis formulation, complex task planning), could potentially benefit from this framework.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitation of static context pools and the suggestion of model-adaptive context generation open promising avenues for future research. Dynamically generating contexts tailored to a policy's current blind spots could lead to even more consistent and targeted exploration gains throughout the training process.
