# DISCOVERSE: Efficient Robot Simulation in Complex High-Fidelity Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> A collaborative effort by researchers from multiple institutions, including Tsinghua University and Zhejiang University, introduces DISCOVERSE, a 3D Gaussian Splatting-based simulation framework designed to bridge the Sim2Real gap for robotics. This framework achieves hyper-realistic visual fidelity and accurate physics, leading to significantly improved zero-shot Sim2Real transfer performance, with policies achieving up to 86.5% success rates in real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- The 'Sim2Real gap' causes performance degradation when transferring robot policies from simulation to real-world tasks, primarily due to visual discrepancies and simplified simulated environments.
- Existing simulators struggle with high visual fidelity, either relying on artificial assets or failing to robustly reconstruct real-world complexity, especially for non-Lambertian surfaces and intricate geometries.
- Earlier neural representation-based simulators (e.g., 3DGS) faced limitations in precise geometry recovery, relightable appearances, view extrapolation, and robustness in complex, unstructured scenarios.

## Method

- **DISCOVERSE**, a unified, modular, and open-source ==3DGS==-based framework, integrates a high-fidelity neural renderer with the ==MuJoCo== physical engine and a ROS2 robotic interface.
- A hierarchical ==Real2Sim== pipeline generates hyper-realistic assets for both static backgrounds (using laser scanning and generative lighting models) and interactive scene nodes (employing a dual 3DGS-Mesh representation, 3D generative models, and ==PBR relighting==).
- The framework supports various robot models and diverse sensor modalities, offers high throughput, and incorporates generative ==domain randomization== techniques to mitigate domain shifts.

## Results

- DISCOVERSE significantly improved zero-shot Sim2Real transfer performance, with policies trained in the framework achieving up to **86.5%** success rates in real-world manipulation tasks using image augmentation.
- The framework consistently outperformed other state-of-the-art simulators (MuJoCo, RoboTwin, SplatSim), achieving an average success rate **11%** higher than SplatSim without data augmentation and up to **18.5%** higher with augmentation.
- Data collection efficiency was substantially enhanced, enabling approximately **100x** faster data gathering than real-world methods, completing 100 demonstrations in **1.5 minutes** via automated state generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) directly into a robot simulator provides an unprecedented level of visual fidelity for complex real-world scene representation, effectively addressing the visual aspects of the Sim2Real gap.
> - Utilizing a dual 3DGS-Mesh representation for interactive objects allows for both photorealistic rendering and accurate physics simulation, enabling precise physical interactions within visually complex environments.
> - A comprehensive Real2Sim pipeline, including laser scanning, 3D generative models, and physically-based relighting, enables the robust reconstruction and rendering of highly detailed and consistently appearing real-world environments and objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of fast and robust simulators is a central aspect of end-to-end robot learning, which aims to enable policy learning directly from raw sensor data, offering scalable, efficient, and cost-effective solutions. Despite significant advancements in simulation technologies, a persistent challenge is the "Sim2Real gap," characterized by performance degradation when transferring policies trained in simulation to real-world robots. This gap is primarily attributed to visual discrepancies, as conventional simulators often rely on artificial assets with simplified textures and lighting, which do not faithfully represent the complexity of real-world environments.

Prior attempts to address the Sim2Real gap have explored several avenues:
- **Real2Sim Approaches with 3D Reconstruction:** Some methods leverage 3D reconstruction techniques to create virtual replicas of real environments, intending to inherit rich appearance and structure from reality. However, traditional multi-view stereo (MVS) and RGB-D fusion approaches often struggle with non-Lambertian reflectance and thin geometries, leading to compromised visual quality and suitability mainly for navigation tasks.
- **Physically-Based Rendering (PBR) Simulators:** State-of-the-art simulators like Omniverse Isaac Lab provide high-quality PBR in real-time through GPU acceleration. While offering visual fidelity, these often require extensive manual configuration and lack inherent support for Real2Sim assets, especially for scenes with numerous primitives.
- **Neural Representation-based Simulators:** Recent works have begun to adopt advanced neural representations, such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), to build Real2Sim replicas. These approaches, however, have faced limitations, including difficulties in recovering precise geometry, generating relightable appearances, poor view extrapolation capabilities, and a lack of robustness in complex, in-the-wild scenarios with intricate geometry, textureless or non-Lambertian surfaces, or sparse imagery. Consequently, they have not yet matured into general-purpose robotic simulators suitable for diverse real-world applications.

The existing landscape indicates a need for a simulation framework that combines high visual fidelity derived from real-world captures with accurate physics, broad sensor support, ease of use, and scalability, while effectively mitigating the Sim2Real gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce DISCOVERSE, a unified, modular, and open-source 3DGS-based simulation framework designed to facilitate end-to-end robotic solutions by addressing the critical Sim2Real gap. The motivation stems from the observed limitations of existing simulators, particularly their inability to generate visually hyper-realistic environments that accurately reflect real-world complexity, thereby hindering effective policy transfer from simulation to reality.

The key objectives that DISCOVERSE aims to achieve include:
1. **High-fidelity Real2Sim Generation:** To synthesize hyper-realistic geometry and appearance of complex real-world scenarios. This involves developing a hierarchical Real2Sim pipeline for both static background elements and interactive scene nodes (objects and robots), leveraging advanced techniques like laser-scanning, generative models, physically-based relighting, and Mesh-Gaussian transfer. The goal is to produce replicas that overcome the limitations of traditional 3D reconstruction and earlier neural rendering methods, specifically in handling intricate geometry, textures, illumination, and non-Lambertian surfaces in large-scale and unstructured scenes.
2. **Efficient Simulation and User-Friendly Configuration:** To enable massively parallel simulation of multiple sensor modalities and accurate physics with high throughput. This includes seamlessly integrating a 3DGS rendering engine with a MuJoCo physical engine and a ROS2 robotic interface, providing an easy-to-use implementation for rapid deployment and flexible extension. An additional objective is to offer an automated state generation approach to streamline demonstration collection.
3. **Broad Compatibility and Inclusive Support:** To ensure compatibility with existing 3D assets and offer comprehensive support for various robot models (e.g., robotic arms, mobile manipulators, quadrocopters), diverse sensor modalities (RGB, depth, LiDAR, tactile sensors), and ROS plugins. This objective also includes incorporating various domain randomization techniques, including generative-based methods, to enhance robustness and aid in bridging the Sim2Real gap.

By achieving these objectives, DISCOVERSE aims to pave the way for a more robust and versatile framework for analyzing and ultimately closing the Sim2Real gap, empowering large-scale robot learning and complex robotic benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DISCOVERSE integrates an advanced neural renderer with a state-of-the-art physical engine and a user-friendly robotic interface within a unified, modular framework. The system is implemented with optimized code and provides Python APIs for deployment and extension.

#### 3.1. A. DISCOVERSE Engine

The core of the DISCOVERSE engine comprises:
- **Renderer:** The native 3DGS renderer is employed for high-fidelity visual simulation. It utilizes a fast tile-based Gaussian rasterizer with efficient sorting, splatting, spherical harmonics evaluation, and alpha-blending. All rendering operations are implemented using highly-optimized custom CUDA kernels to maximize GPU parallelization, balancing efficiency and fidelity.
- **Physical Simulator:** MuJoCo is selected for rigid body simulation due to its efficiency and accuracy in managing complex physical interactions such, as contacts, friction, and soft constraints. DISCOVERSE leverages MuJoCo’s capabilities to support precise force control, P-D control, and inverse dynamics, facilitating accurate collision detection, contact force simulation, and the modeling of articulated robots.
- **ROS2 Interface:** A ROS2 interface provides APIs for interacting with robots. This includes low-level control via encoder torques on joints and higher-level control through joint space and Cartesian coordinate space APIs, aiming to bridge the simulation-reality gap.

#### 3.2. B. DISCOVERSE Asset

The framework supports high-quality digital asset integration through a comprehensive Real2Sim pipeline:
- **Real-world Captures:** The simulator processes multi-view captures of real-world scenes and objects using COLMAP and 3DGS. It also extends support to laser scanners for more robust and accurate geometry reconstruction.
- **AIGC and Asset Library:** Compatibility is ensured with recent 3D AIGC techniques, including textured mesh generation, native 3DGS generation, and generation of articulated objects. Support for public 3D datasets such as ShapeNet, PartNet, and Objaverse is also provided.
- **Robot Models:** A range of robotic agents, including the AIRBOT Play robotic arm, AIRBOT MMK2 humanoid mobile manipulator, a wheeled loco-manipulator, and a quadrocopter, are supported.
- **Asset Formatting:** For interactive scene nodes (objects and agents), a dual 3DGS-Mesh representation is utilized: 3DGS (.ply) for high-fidelity visuals and a mesh counterpart (.obj/.stl) described in MJCF for accurate physics simulation. Each interactive element is linked to an `.xml` file for loading into simulation, with physical properties randomized or manually adjusted. Meshes are decomposed into convex parts for accurate contact simulation. Background nodes primarily use native 3DGS for rendering, as they do not require physical interaction.

#### 3.3. C. Sensor Modalities

DISCOVERSE simulates diverse sensor modalities:
- **Rendering-based sensors:** RGB and depth are simulated directly by alpha-compositing color and z-position of intersected Gaussian primitives. LiDAR simulation is achieved via a BVH-accelerated, native Gaussian ray tracing framework.
- **Physics-based sensors:** Integration with Tacchi enables optical tactile simulation, while MuJoCo provides other physics-based sensors (contact force, body and joint states, IMU).

#### 3.4. D. Actuator Model

The actuator model integrates MuJoCo's framework, supporting control inputs and optional activation states for various mechanisms (muscles, pneumatic cylinders) with first-order dynamics. It supports fixed or state-dependent gains and transmits forces through direct joint actuation, tendon-driven mechanisms, or slider-crank systems.

#### 3.5. E. Real2Sim Pipeline (Detailed)

The Real2Sim pipeline focuses on generating high-fidelity assets for both background and interactive nodes:
- **Background Node Real2Sim Generation:** Real-world scenes are reconstructed as 3DGS fields with geometry regularization, utilizing laser scanning (e.g., LixelKity K1) for reliable geometry measurements. Environmental lighting is estimated using generative models like DiffusionLight to generate HDR environment maps from single images, enabling realistic distant lighting effects via PBR for interactive nodes.
- **Interactive Scene Nodes Real2Sim Generation:** These nodes require a dual 3DGS-Mesh representation. The pipeline first focuses on classical textured mesh representation for 3D reconstruction, generation, processing, and pre-PBR.
 - **Laser-scanned 3D Reconstruction:** For objects with Lambertian surfaces, scanners like Artec Leo are used to reconstruct precise 3D geometry and textures. Multiple scans are registered to create complete textured meshes.
 - **3D Generation:** For objects with non-Lambertian specularity or thin structures, 3D generative models like CLAY are used to reconstruct challenging materials or shapes from single real-world images.
 - **Relighting:** To align the appearance of interactive nodes with the background radiance, PBR is performed as a preprocessing step using the estimated HDR environment map and Blender.
 - **Mesh-Gaussian Transfer:** An approach is proposed to convert meshes to 3DGS. One Gaussian is initialized for each mesh facet, with its centroid on the triangle's barycenter. Gaussians are flattened to 2D slanted planar primitives, and their local frames are aligned with face normals. Scaling values are initialized based on mean barycenter-to-vertex distance. Geometric constraints (depth, opacity) regularize the 3DGS field during optimization. For 3DGS to mesh conversion, multi-view depth maps are rendered from the 3DGS field, followed by TSDF fusion and decimation.

#### 3.6. F. Domain Randomization

To mitigate domain shifts and account for unmodeled real-world dynamics, DISCOVERSE incorporates several randomization mechanisms:
- Random video overlays (blending Internet video crops into the simulation stream).
- HSV-space image augmentation.
- Random gamma correction.
- Generative randomization leveraging ControlNet for conditioning, GPT-4V for text-prompt augmentation, and a hybrid flow-based pipeline, for frame interpolation.

#### 3.7. G. Throughput

The system reports a throughput of 650 FPS for rendering hyper-realistic RGB-D frames at 640x480 resolution with 5 cameras on a desktop (Intel Xeon w5-3435x CPU, Nvidia 6000 Ada GPU). On a laptop (AMD R7-5800H CPU, Nvidia GeForce RTX 3060 GPU), it achieves 240 FPS with the same setup.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DISCOVERSE in bridging the Sim2Real gap was evaluated through extensive experiments on imitation learning (IL) using ACT and Diffusion Policy (DP) across three real-world manipulation tasks. Comparisons were made against MuJoCo, RoboTwin, and SplatSim, as well as real-world performance.

#### 4.1. A. Data Collection Efficiency

- DISCOVERSE significantly improves data collection efficiency, enabling approximately 100 times faster data collection compared to real-world demonstrations. For 100 demonstrations, real-world collection took 146 minutes, while DISCOVERSE completed it in 1.5 minutes using an automated state generation approach.

#### 4.2. B. Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer Performance

The benchmark was conducted on three contact-rich real-world tasks: Close-Laptop, Push-Mouse, and Pick-Up-Kiwifruit. Policies were trained using 100 demonstrations for ACT and 2,000 for DP, with random initial gripper states, end-effector positions, and orientations. Success rate (%) over 50 trials was the primary metric.

- **Performance without Data Augmentation:**
 - **ACT:** Policies trained on MuJoCo and RoboTwin showed low Sim2Real success rates (average 14.5% and 6% respectively). SplatSim achieved an average success rate of 44%. DISCOVERSE demonstrated superior performance, achieving an average success rate of 55%, outperforming SplatSim by approximately 11% without data augmentation.
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP):** Similar trends were observed. MuJoCo and RoboTwin yielded average success rates of 0% and 7.3%. SplatSim achieved 45.3%, while DISCOVERSE achieved 56%, outperforming SplatSim by approximately 11% without data augmentation.

- **Performance with Image-Based Data Augmentation:**
 - All simulators showed improvements with data augmentation (random video overlays, HSV-space randomization, random gamma corrections).
 - **ACT:** DISCOVERSE achieved an average success rate of 86.5% with augmentation, representing a 31.5% increase compared to its non-augmented performance. It outperformed augmented SplatSim (68% average success rate) by 18.5%.
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP):** DISCOVERSE achieved an average success rate of 86% with augmentation, showing a 29.3% improvement. It surpassed augmented SplatSim (74.6% average success rate) by 11.4%.

These findings indicate that DISCOVERSE consistently yielded the best Sim2Real transfer performance across both ACT and DP policies, both with and without data augmentation, compared to the other evaluated simulators.

#### 4.3. C. Exemplar Applications

- **Navigation:** DISCOVERSE was demonstrated with an AIRBOT MMK2 agent navigating a large-scale indoor scene, updating a spatial map using ego-view renderings.
- **Multi-Agent Coordination:** The simulator facilitated cooperative exploration of a large-scale outdoor scene by a wheeled loco-manipulator and a quadrocopter.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DISCOVERSE represents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning by addressing the critical Sim2Real gap through its novel 3DGS-based simulation framework. The framework's ability to synthesize hyper-realistic geometry and appearance from complex real-world scenarios, combined with accurate physics simulation and extensive support for various robot models and sensor modalities, distinguishes it from existing solutions.

The primary significance lies in its demonstrated capability to improve zero-shot Sim2Real transfer performance substantially. The quantitative results from imitation learning experiments indicate that policies trained in DISCOVERSE achieve significantly higher success rates in real-world tasks compared to those trained in other state-of-the-art simulators. This suggests that the framework’s high visual fidelity, derived from its advanced Real2Sim pipeline and 3DGS rendering, effectively reduces the domain discrepancies between simulation and reality.

The potential impact of DISCOVERSE includes:
- **Accelerated Robot Learning:** By enabling efficient and high-fidelity data synthesis, DISCOVERSE can significantly reduce the cost and time associated with training robot policies, allowing for the generation of large-scale datasets that are prohibitively expensive to collect in the real world.
- **Facilitation of Complex Robotic Benchmarks:** The unified and modular nature of DISCOVERSE, along with its support for diverse robotic agents and environments, positions it as a suitable platform for establishing comprehensive Sim2Real robotic benchmarks across various tasks, including manipulation, navigation, and multi-agent coordination. This can stimulate further research and development in end-to-end robot learning.
- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap Towards Real2Real:** The superior zero-shot Sim2Real transfer performance achieved by DISCOVERSE indicates a step towards making simulation-trained policies directly deployable in real-world scenarios with minimal or no fine-tuning. This can alleviate the arduous process of real-world reinforcement learning and policy fine-tuning (RLPF).
- **Open-Source Contribution:** As an open-source framework, DISCOVERSE has the potential to foster community engagement and collaborative research, providing a robust foundation for future advancements in robot simulation and learning.

Future work on DISCOVERSE is envisioned to include advanced physical simulation and hybrid Mesh-GS PBR, aiming to further enhance its capabilities and solidify its role in the evolving landscape of Real2Sim2Real robot learning.
