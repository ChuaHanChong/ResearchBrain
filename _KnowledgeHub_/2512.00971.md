# H-Zero: Cross-Humanoid Locomotion Pretraining Enables Few-shot Novel Embodiment Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> H-Zero introduces a cross-humanoid locomotion pretraining pipeline that learns a generalizable policy across diverse robot designs. This approach allows for efficient few-shot transfer to novel embodiments, reducing controller development effort from tens of thousands of training epochs to a few hundred or thousand.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) controllers are typically tailored to specific humanoid robot designs, requiring extensive retraining for each new variant.
- The high degrees of freedom and complex dynamics of humanoid robots make control development resource-intensive and unscalable.
- A lack of efficient algorithms exists to achieve robust and versatile locomotion across the rapidly proliferating diverse humanoid embodiments.

## Method

- A pretraining pipeline learns a generalizable locomotion policy by exposing it to a diverse set of existing humanoid and quadruped robot models.
- Unified control semantics and embodiment descriptors standardize observation and action spaces across heterogeneous robot morphologies, leveraging descriptors as privileged information for the critic.
- An embodiment-aware learning strategy, including extended domain randomization, adaptive exploration, and dynamic loss reweighting, enhances generalization and balances learning progress across diverse robots.

## Results

- Pretrained policies achieved up to 81% mean normalized episode length on unseen robots when trained with broader embodiment diversity and extended domain randomization.
- H-Zero enables few-shot transfer, adapting to novel robots in hundreds to a few thousand fine-tuning epochs (approximately 30 minutes), a substantial reduction compared to tens of thousands of epochs for training from scratch.
- Qualitative evaluations on real-world robots like Aliengo, T1, and Adam Lite confirm that few-shot fine-tuning from the pretrained policy yields stable and responsive locomotion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretraining on a diverse set of real-world robot models, combined with extended domain randomization, is critical for a policy to generalize to unseen embodiments.
> - Providing embodiment-specific physical parameters as 'privileged information' to the critic, rather than directly to the policy, significantly improves performance and stability.
> - Adaptive exploration sampling variances and dynamic loss reweighting ensure balanced learning progress across morphologically diverse robots during the pretraining phase.
