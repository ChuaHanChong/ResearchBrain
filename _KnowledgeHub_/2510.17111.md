# Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models for Embodied Manipulation: A Systematic Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology conducted the first systematic survey on efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied manipulation. The survey categorizes existing techniques across model architecture, perception, action generation, and training/inference, highlighting methods to reduce computational load and improve real-time performance for robotic deployment.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art VLA models for robotic manipulation demand extensive computational and memory resources, leading to slow inference speeds.
- These high resource demands conflict with the real-time performance, limited onboard compute, and energy budget constraints of physical robotic platforms.
- A systematic review specifically focused on efficiency-improvement techniques for VLA models in embodied AI was absent from existing literature.

## Method

- The authors systematically reviewed and categorized existing VLA efficiency-improvement techniques into a novel four-dimensional taxonomy.
- This taxonomy covers efficient model architectures, perception feature processing, action generation strategies, and training/inference mechanisms.
- The survey identifies current limitations and outlines future research directions to bridge the gap between powerful VLA models and practical robotic deployment.

## Results

- The survey consolidates a wide array of efficiency techniques, demonstrating approaches for reducing VLA model parameters (e.g., downsizing to Pythia-1.3B, Qwen-2.5-VL-3B), faster inference, and improved memory utilization.
- It outlines five critical future research dimensions, including model-data co-optimization, efficient spatio-temporal perception, compact action generation, advanced learning paradigms, and the establishment of efficiency-centric evaluation criteria.
- The work provides a comprehensive roadmap for researchers to balance VLA model capabilities with practical deployment constraints, guiding the development of truly efficient embodied intelligence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA model evolution reveals a tension between increasing generalization (via large VLMs) and escalating computational demands, necessitating targeted efficiency solutions.
> - Efficiency techniques range from static model downsizing and dynamic computation pathways to selective perception feature processing and advanced action generation methods like action chunking.
> - Dual-system designs, inspired by cognitive science, offer a promising avenue by combining a large, slow reasoning system with a lightweight, fast reactive system for real-time control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey positions itself at the critical intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and embodied AI, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. It addresses a fundamental tension that has emerged in the rapid advancement of AI: the immense capabilities of large, multimodal models versus the stringent computational and real-time constraints of physical robots.

**Evolution of VLA Models:** The paper meticulously traces the evolution of VLA models:
1. **Early Exploration:** Initial attempts to apply deep learning to robot control (e.g., RT-1, Diffusion Policy) demonstrated feasibility but suffered from limited generalization due to small model sizes and datasets.
2. **VLM Integration:** A significant turning point was the integration of large pre-trained VLMs (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA), which leveraged web-scale data to imbue robots with superior semantic understanding and generalization. This "VLM-as-a-policy" paradigm became mainstream, often utilizing strong vision encoders, LLM backbones, and methods to convert control signals into discrete action tokens.
3. **Architectural Convergence & Performance Refinement:** More recent models (e.g., π0) have seen architectures converge towards using pre-trained LLMs for high-level planning and intent understanding, complemented by dedicated action expert modules (often diffusion-based) for precise, continuous action generation. This stage is characterized by growing model sizes (e.g., OpenVLA with 7B parameters, π0 with 3B parameters) and improving performance, but also highlights the escalating computational demands, with inference speeds still limited (e.g., 5-10 Hz on powerful GPUs).

**The Efficiency Bottleneck:** Despite their impressive potential for general-purpose control across diverse tasks and scenes, the reliance on massive computational and memory resources poses a severe limitation for VLA models. These demands conflict directly with the practical constraints of robotic platforms, which require real-time performance, limited onboard compute, and tight energy budgets. Existing efficiency techniques for general VLMs are often insufficient because VLA systems introduce additional challenges such as generating temporally consistent action sequences and ensuring physical reliability during real-time execution. Aggressive compression can lead to critical performance degradation in embodied contexts.

**Filling a Research Gap:** The authors explicitly identify a gap in the existing survey literature. While other surveys have provided broad overviews of VLA concepts, architectures, training, applications, or focused on specific aspects like action representations or autonomous driving, *none* have systematically addressed the critical issue of *efficiency*. This survey aims to fill this void by providing the first dedicated and systematic review of approaches to improve VLA efficiency, emphasizing reductions in latency, memory footprint, and training/inference costs. By doing so, it provides a much-needed roadmap for researchers and practitioners striving to deploy VLA models in real-world robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this survey stems from the fundamental conflict between the escalating computational demands of state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and the inherent resource constraints of embodied robotic platforms. While VLA models promise unprecedented generalization and semantic understanding for robot control, their massive parameter counts, high memory footprints, and slow inference speeds impede their practical deployment, particularly on edge devices like mobile manipulators that necessitate real-time performance and efficient resource utilization.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this systematic survey are threefold, as explicitly stated by the authors:

1. **To provide the first dedicated survey on efficient VLA models:** Recognizing the absence of a comprehensive review focused specifically on efficiency, the authors aim to consolidate and categorize the growing body of research in this crucial area. This objective underscores the perceived importance of efficiency as a primary bottleneck for real-world VLA adoption.
2. **To categorize efficiency-improvement techniques into a novel four-dimensional taxonomy:** The survey proposes a structured framework for understanding the diverse approaches to VLA efficiency. These dimensions are: efficient model architecture, efficient perception feature processing, efficient action generation, and efficient training/inference mechanisms. This categorization aims to bring clarity and order to a rapidly evolving field.
3. **To summarize mainstream approaches and analyze their strengths and weaknesses within this taxonomy:** Beyond mere listing, the survey seeks to critically evaluate the representative techniques within each category, offering insights into their practical implications, trade-offs, and suitability for different scenarios.
4. **To discuss future development trends and highlight key research priorities for enhancing VLA efficiency:** The authors intend to provide a forward-looking perspective, identifying promising avenues and open challenges that researchers should prioritize to advance the field towards truly efficient embodied intelligence.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to accelerate the transition of powerful VLA models from theoretical benchmarks to practical, deployable robotic systems by systematically dissecting and addressing their efficiency challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (for the Survey)

The methodology of this survey is structured and systematic, aiming to provide a comprehensive overview of efficiency optimization techniques for VLA models. The approach is defined by its categorization, flow-based organization, and critical analysis.

1. **Systematic Review:** The paper implicitly employs a systematic review methodology, collecting and synthesizing a broad range of recent research papers focused on VLA efficiency. The accompanying GitHub repository "Awesome Efficient VLA" further supports this by providing a curated list of relevant works, indicating a thorough collection process.

2. **Taxonomic Categorization:** A core aspect of the survey's methodology is its novel four-dimensional taxonomy for organizing efficiency-improvement techniques. This taxonomy serves as the primary framework for analysis and presentation:
 - **Efficient Model Architecture:** Focuses on the foundational structure of VLA models.
 - **Efficient Perception Feature:** Addresses optimizations related to processing visual and other sensory inputs.
 - **Efficient Action Generation:** Examines methods for generating robotic actions more effectively.
 - **Efficient Training and Inference Strategies:** Covers techniques applied across the model's lifecycle from learning to deployment.

3. **Pipeline-Oriented Structure:** The survey's organization directly mirrors the processing pipeline of a typical VLA system, as illustrated in Figure 1. This logical flow from input (perception) through processing (architecture, action generation) to output (training/inference) allows for a coherent and intuitive understanding of how efficiency techniques apply at different stages.

4. **Descriptive and Analytical Approach:** Within each category, the authors:
 - **Review Representative Techniques:** They identify and describe mainstream and notable approaches, providing concrete examples of algorithms and models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, RoboMamba, FastV, ECoT, Speculative Decoding).
 - **Summarize Strengths and Weaknesses:** For each technique or paradigm, they analyze its advantages and disadvantages, particularly in the context of efficiency-capability trade-offs. This critical assessment helps readers understand the nuances and limitations of different solutions.
 - **Provide Illustrations:** Figures are used effectively to visualize key concepts, such as VLA architecture, dual-system designs, token pruning, action chunking, and different decoding mechanisms, enhancing clarity and comprehension.

5. **Forward-Looking Discussion:** The survey concludes with a dedicated "Future Prospects" section. This involves synthesizing the identified challenges and trends to outline promising research directions across five key dimensions: model and data co-optimization, spatio-temporal perception, action generation, learning paradigms, and evaluation criteria. This provides a strategic roadmap for future work in efficient embodied intelligence.

By adopting this structured and comprehensive approach, the survey aims to offer a clear, systematic, and insightful understanding of the current state and future trajectory of efficient VLA models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a systematic survey, this paper's "findings" are a comprehensive distillation and structured presentation of existing research, rather than novel empirical results. The main results are the systematic categorization, summary, and critical analysis of efficiency techniques across the VLA pipeline, along with a forward-looking perspective on future research directions.

**Evolution of VLA Models (Section 2):**
The survey identifies three stages: early exploration with end-to-end policies, integration of pre-trained VLMs (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) marking a leap in generalization, and current architectural convergence (e.g., π0) involving specialized LLMs for planning and diffusion models for continuous actions. This evolution underscores the increasing model complexity and resultant efficiency challenges, motivating the survey.

**1. Efficient Model Architectures (Section 3):**
- **Static Backbone Selection:** The trend is towards downsizing. Researchers replace large VLMs with lighter LMs (e.g., Mamba, Pythia-1.3B, Qwen-2.5-VL-3B) or prune layers (SmolVLA) to reduce parameters and latency.
- **Dynamic Computation Pathways:** Solutions involve retaining large backbones but activating only necessary parts during inference. Techniques include static layer pruning (FLOWER), early-exit mechanisms (DEER-VLA) with multiple policy heads, Mixture-of-Experts (MoLE-VLA) for dynamic layer selection, and similarity-based skipping (Efficient-VLA) to bypass redundant computations.
- **Dual-System Designs:** Inspired by cognitive science, these architectures combine a "slow" (large MMLM for reasoning) and a "fast" (lightweight for rapid response) system. Examples include LCB, HiRT, RoboDual, OpenHelix, FiS, Hume, HyperVLA, and SP-VLA, which communicate via latent tokens or parameters. RT-Cache is noted as an experience-based retrieval system akin to a fast system.

**2. Efficient Perception Feature (Section 4):**
- **Selective Feature Processing:** Focuses on reducing visual input redundancy. Methods include token pruning based on attention scores (FastV, EfficientVLA), instruction relevance (EfficientVLA), spatial structure (SP-VLA), SVD-based importance (FlashVLA), query-driven selection (LightVLA), action-aware dynamic pruning (ADP, FASTDriveVLA, SpecPrune-VLA), and quantization-aware pruning (SQAP-VLA). Other approaches involve representational transformations like cross-attention pooling (OTTER) and unified discrete tokenization (UniVLA).
- **Temporal Sharing and Reuse:** Exploits temporal correlation in sequential data. Techniques include reusing KV-caches for static image patches (VLA-cache), selective temporal token fusion (TTF-VLA), reusing high-level perceptual representations (FlashVLA), and caching reasoning outputs (Fast ECoT).

**3. Efficient Action Generation (Section 5):**
- **Raw Action Generation:**
 - **Action Chunking:** Generates blocks of actions in one step (Action Chunk), reducing per-step inference cost and error accumulation. Real-Time Chunking (RTC) further ensures cross-chunk consistency.
 - **Action Token Compression:** Compresses discretized action sequences using techniques like Discrete Cosine Transform (FAST), B-spline and residual vector quantization (OmniSAT), special tokens representing entire chunks (VOTE), or polar coordinate transformations (SpatialVLA).
- **Reasoning-Aware Action Generation:**
 - **Language-based Reasoning:** Decomposes tasks into subtasks using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (ECoT). Efficiency improvements include reusing slow-evolving plans (Fast ECoT) and reasoning dropout during inference (ECoT-Lite).
 - **Vision-based Reasoning:** Extracts spatial-semantic cues (affordances, keypoints, goal states) from visual inputs. Examples include video prediction for goal states (UniPi), single subgoal image prediction (SuSIE), multi-scale feature extraction from video diffusion models (VPP), and dynamic region-based forecasting (DreamVLA).

**4. Efficient Training and Inference (Section 6):**
- **Training Efficiency Techniques:**
 - **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):** Methods like LoRA reduce trainable parameters, enabling adaptation with smaller datasets.
 - **Knowledge Distillation:** Transfers knowledge from larger "teacher" models to smaller "student" models (CEED-VLA, MoLE-VLA, VITA-VLA).
 - **Parameter Pruning:** Structured pruning combined with post-hoc recovery (GLUESTICK) to restore lost information.
 - **Quantization:** Quantization-aware training (QAT) guided by state-importance scores (SQIL) and ultra-low-bit QAT (BitVLA) for memory reduction and faster inference.
- **Inference Efficiency Techniques:**
 - **Non-Autoregressive (NAR) / Parallel Decoding:** Re-engineers architecture for parallel prediction of entire action sequences (openvla-oft) or uses iterative refinement (PD-VLA, CEED-VLA).
 - **Speculative Decoding:** Uses a lightweight draft model to generate candidate sequences, then verifies with a full model (Spec-VLA).
 - **Jacobi Iteration:** Refines all actions in parallel over multiple iterations, aided by consistency distillation (CEED-VLA).

**Future Prospects (Section 7):**
The survey concludes by identifying five critical dimensions for future research to achieve a systematic balance between capability and efficiency:
1. **Model and Data Co-Optimization:** Moving beyond brute-force scaling to data-centric efficiency frameworks, selective data pipelines, and joint scaling laws.
2. **Spatio-Temporal Perception:** Evolving from 2D frames to efficient 3D representations (point clouds, neural fields) and persistent memory, while containing token burden through task-aware summarization and semantic-guided filtering.
3. **Action Generation:** Shifting from chunked commands to compact, continuous, and context-aware control, with multi-level abstractions and reactive reasoning.
4. **Learning Paradigms:** Transitioning from pure imitation learning to efficient reinforcement adaptation through progressive training pipelines (IL + offline RL + online adaptation) and sample-efficient techniques.
5. **Evaluation Criteria:** Establishing efficiency-centric benchmarking frameworks that standardize reporting on resource usage, task performance, and interpretability (e.g., ImageNet, GLUE equivalents for VLA).

Overall, the survey successfully compiles and categorizes a wide array of methods, highlighting the ingenuity of researchers in tackling the VLA efficiency challenge from various angles, and offers a clear vision for future development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This systematic survey holds significant importance for the field of embodied AI and robotics, offering a foundational reference and a roadmap for future research in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

**1. Filling a Critical Research Gap:** The primary significance of this work lies in its unique focus. While VLA models have garnered immense attention, and various surveys exist, none have systematically addressed the crucial and increasingly pressing issue of *efficiency*. By meticulously categorizing and analyzing existing solutions for reducing latency, memory footprint, and computational costs, the authors provide the first dedicated comprehensive review, thus filling a critical void in the academic literature.

**2. Enabling Real-World Robotic Deployment:** The core problem addressed by this survey is the conflict between the immense capabilities of large VLA models and the stringent resource constraints of real-world robotic platforms (e.g., onboard compute, energy budgets, real-time demands). By synthesizing and evaluating efficiency techniques, the survey directly contributes to overcoming this bottleneck. Its insights will accelerate the transition of VLA models from laboratory demonstrations to practical, deployable applications in various domains, including manipulation, navigation, and autonomous driving.

**3. Providing a Structured Taxonomy and Research Roadmap:** The proposed four-dimensional taxonomy (model architecture, perception feature, action generation, training/inference strategies) offers a coherent framework for understanding and organizing the diverse landscape of VLA efficiency research. This structure is invaluable for new researchers entering the field, helping them quickly grasp the key areas of innovation. Furthermore, the "Future Prospects" section acts as a strategic roadmap, identifying critical open challenges and promising directions across data, perception, action, learning, and evaluation. This guides future efforts toward more impactful and sustainable research.

**4. Fostering Benchmarking and Reproducibility:** The call for "efficiency-centric benchmarking" and the establishment of "a large-scale, multi-scenario, and multi-task open benchmark for VLA" (similar to ImageNet or GLUE) is highly impactful. Standardized metrics for resource efficiency, performance, robustness, and interpretability are essential for fair comparisons, measuring true progress, and fostering a reproducible research ecosystem. The accompanying GitHub repository "Awesome Efficient VLA" is a practical step towards curating resources and fostering community collaboration.

**5. Promoting Sustainable AI Development:** By focusing on efficiency, this survey indirectly addresses broader concerns about the environmental and computational costs of increasingly large AI models. Promoting resource-conscious design and learning paradigms contributes to more sustainable and accessible AI development, ensuring that advanced robotic intelligence can be developed and utilized by a wider range of researchers and practitioners, not just those with access to vast computational resources.

In conclusion, this systematic survey is a timely and essential contribution to the field of embodied AI. By rigorously examining the landscape of efficient VLA models and outlining a clear path forward, it significantly aids in overcoming current deployment hurdles and paves the way for the widespread adoption of intelligent, general-purpose robots capable of operating effectively and autonomously in complex physical environments.
