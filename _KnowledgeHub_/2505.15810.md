# GUI-G1: Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training for Visual Grounding in GUI Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University of China and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab developed GUI-G1, a 3B-parameter model that refines R1-Zero-like training for visual grounding in GUI agents by addressing input, reward, and policy update biases. GUI-G1 achieves a 90.3% accuracy on the ScreenSpot benchmark, outperforming existing state-of-the-art models with a smaller training dataset and improved inference efficiency.

## Problem

- The direct application of general R1-Zero-like Reinforcement Learning paradigms to visual grounding in GUI agents may not be optimally suited, potentially due to confounding factors like backbone architectures and data sources.
- Current rule-based reward functions in R1-Zero-like training for GUI grounding (Hit-based, IoU-based) can lead to 'reward hacking' behaviors where models optimize for the reward rather than precise localization.
- The policy update objective (e.g., GRPO) used in R1-Zero-like training introduces biases, such as length bias (preferring longer incorrect responses) and difficulty bias (under-optimizing harder samples), which hinder effective learning for GUI grounding.

## Method

- Replaced 'Slow Thinking Templates' with a '==Fast Thinking Template==' to encourage direct answer generation, aligning with the finding that longer outputs harm grounding.
- Introduced a 'Box Size Constraint Reward' to regularize predicted bounding box sizes, used in conjunction with Hit and IoU rewards to mitigate reward hacking.
- Modified the GRPO objective by replacing length normalization with a constant and adding a difficulty coefficient (based on inverse relative box size) to address length and difficulty biases, respectively.

## Results

- GUI-G1-**3B** achieved **90.3%** overall accuracy on the ScreenSpot benchmark and **37.1%** on the ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark, surpassing prior state-of-the-art models including larger general-purpose and GUI-specific agents.
- The model achieved these results with a compact **3B**-parameter architecture, trained on only **17K** public samples, demonstrating efficiency gains in training data and model size.
- GUI-G1-**3B** showed enhanced inference efficiency by generating substantially fewer output tokens (e.g., 37 tokens vs. 107 tokens on average compared to InfiGUI-R1-**3B**), leading to reduced computational costs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit chain-of-thought reasoning (longer outputs) consistently degrades GUI grounding accuracy, indicating that visual pattern recognition benefits from direct answers ('fast thinking').
> - Existing hit-based and IoU-based reward functions for grounding lead to 'reward hacking,' incentivizing overly small or large bounding boxes respectively.
> - The standard GRPO objective, when applied to GUI grounding, suffers from a length bias (favoring longer incorrect responses) and a difficulty bias (under-optimizing harder samples).
