# GUI-G1: Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training for Visual Grounding in GUI Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University of China and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab developed GUI-G1, a 3B-parameter model that refines R1-Zero-like training for visual grounding in GUI agents by addressing input, reward, and policy update biases. GUI-G1 achieves a 90.3% accuracy on the ScreenSpot benchmark, outperforming existing state-of-the-art models with a smaller training dataset and improved inference efficiency.

## Problem

- The direct application of general R1-Zero-like Reinforcement Learning paradigms to visual grounding in GUI agents may not be optimally suited, potentially due to confounding factors like backbone architectures and data sources.
- Current rule-based reward functions in R1-Zero-like training for GUI grounding (Hit-based, IoU-based) can lead to 'reward hacking' behaviors where models optimize for the reward rather than precise localization.
- The policy update objective (e.g., GRPO) used in R1-Zero-like training introduces biases, such as length bias (preferring longer incorrect responses) and difficulty bias (under-optimizing harder samples), which hinder effective learning for GUI grounding.

## Method

- Replaced 'Slow Thinking Templates' with a '==Fast Thinking Template==' to encourage direct answer generation, aligning with the finding that longer outputs harm grounding.
- Introduced a 'Box Size Constraint Reward' to regularize predicted bounding box sizes, used in conjunction with Hit and IoU rewards to mitigate reward hacking.
- Modified the GRPO objective by replacing length normalization with a constant and adding a difficulty coefficient (based on inverse relative box size) to address length and difficulty biases, respectively.

## Results

- GUI-G1-**3B** achieved **90.3%** overall accuracy on the ScreenSpot benchmark and **37.1%** on the ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark, surpassing prior state-of-the-art models including larger general-purpose and GUI-specific agents.
- The model achieved these results with a compact **3B**-parameter architecture, trained on only **17K** public samples, demonstrating efficiency gains in training data and model size.
- GUI-G1-**3B** showed enhanced inference efficiency by generating substantially fewer output tokens (e.g., 37 tokens vs. 107 tokens on average compared to InfiGUI-R1-**3B**), leading to reduced computational costs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit chain-of-thought reasoning (longer outputs) consistently degrades GUI grounding accuracy, indicating that visual pattern recognition benefits from direct answers ('fast thinking').
> - Existing hit-based and IoU-based reward functions for grounding lead to 'reward hacking,' incentivizing overly small or large bounding boxes respectively.
> - The standard GRPO objective, when applied to GUI grounding, suffers from a length bias (favoring longer incorrect responses) and a difficulty bias (under-optimizing harder samples).

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

This paper critically examines and refines the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms, specifically the R1-Zero-like approach, to the visual grounding task within Graphical User Interface (GUI) agents. The authors conduct a thorough investigation into the inherent challenges of this training methodology and propose targeted solutions that lead to state-of-the-art performance.

#### 1.1. Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and Graphical User Interface (GUI) agents.

**Evolution of GUI Agents:** Historically, GUI agents relied heavily on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of models on large, meticulously labeled datasets. Examples include UI-BERT, OS-Atlas, UGround, and ShowUI. While effective, SFT approaches often suffer from high computational costs due to data annotation, and can exhibit poor generalization to out-of-domain (OOD) scenarios. The ultimate goal for GUI agents is to navigate and interact with digital interfaces like humans, requiring sophisticated capabilities like visual grounding, action prediction, and long-horizon planning.

**Emergence of R1-Zero Paradigm:** The R1-Zero paradigm, initially proposed by DeepSeek-R1-Zero for Large Language Models (LLMs), revolutionized post-training by applying RL directly to base LLMs without an intermediate SFT step. This paradigm incentivizes structured outputs and reasoning capabilities. Its success in LLMs quickly led to its adoption in the multimodal domain, with models like Vision-R1, MM-EUREKA, and VisualThinker-R1-Zero extending its application to vision-language and multimodal reasoning tasks. For GUI agents, recent works such as UI-R1, GUI-R1, and InfiGUI-R1 have successfully applied R1-Zero-like training, often utilizing algorithms like GRPO, to improve action prediction and visual grounding on benchmarks like ScreenSpot and ScreenSpot-Pro.

**Critical Examination of R1-Zero-Like Training for GUI:** While R1-Zero-like training has shown promising results in GUI agents, this paper highlights a crucial gap: the direct application of a general-purpose RL paradigm, developed primarily for LLM reasoning, may not be optimally suited for the unique demands of GUI grounding tasks. The paper posits that performance gains in these R1-style models might not solely stem from the RL algorithm itself, but could be confounded by differences in backbone architectures, data sources, and training protocols. This research aims to disentangle these factors and critically analyze how R1-Zero-like training *specifically* affects the core GUI grounding task. This work challenges the "blind application" of general RL techniques and advocates for task-specific adaptations, building upon prior critical perspectives on R1-Zero-like training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **understand and improve the application of the R1-Zero-like training paradigm for visual grounding in GUI agents**. The authors are motivated by the observation that while recent R1-style GUI agents show significant performance gains, the specific contributions and potential pitfalls of their online Reinforcement Learning (RL) components, particularly when adapted from general LLM training to GUI grounding, remain underexplored.

The paper identifies the following core motivations and objectives:

1. **Disentangle Algorithmic Contributions:** To isolate the true impact of online RL (specifically GRPO) on GUI grounding performance, separating it from other system-level factors like backbone architectures, data sources, and general training protocols.
2. **Focus on Grounding as the Core Capability:** To concentrate the RL objective exclusively on the visual grounding task, which is considered a fundamental capability for effective GUI interaction. This allows for a granular analysis of how RL mechanisms influence precise element localization.
3. **Identify and Analyze Challenges in Current R1-Zero-Like Pipelines:** To systematically decompose the existing R1-Zero-like training pipeline for GUI agents into three critical components—input design (template), output evaluation (reward function), and policy update (RL objective)—and reveal distinct challenges that arise from their "blind" application to GUI grounding.
 - **Input Design:** Investigate if explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, common in R1-Zero, truly benefits GUI grounding.
 - **Output Evaluation:** Analyze the impact of current hit-based and IoU-based reward functions on model behavior and localization quality, specifically looking for "reward hacking."
 - **Policy Update:** Examine the inherent biases in the GRPO objective (e.g., length and difficulty biases) and their detrimental effects on learning for GUI grounding.
4. **Propose Targeted Solutions:** Based on the identified challenges, develop and implement specific improvements for each component to mitigate the observed issues and optimize the RL training process for GUI grounding.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance Efficiently:** To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed solutions by training a compact model (3B parameters) on a relatively small public dataset (17K samples) that surpasses existing state-of-the-art GUI agents, including larger models and those requiring more extensive training or data, while also improving inference efficiency.

In essence, the paper aims to move beyond simply adopting powerful RL techniques, instead focusing on a deeper understanding of their mechanisms in the context of GUI agents to achieve more robust, accurate, and efficient visual grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research methodology involves a two-pronged approach: first, a comprehensive analytical decomposition of the existing R1-Zero-like training paradigm for GUI grounding, and second, the proposal and implementation of targeted solutions based on these insights.

#### 3.1. Analytical Decomposition of R1-Zero-Like Training

The authors systematically analyze the R1-Zero-like training pipeline by breaking it down into three core components:

1. **Input Design (Template Analysis):**
 - **Hypothesis:** Explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, often encouraged by "Slow Thinking Templates" (e.g., in InfiGUI-R1), might not be beneficial for GUI grounding, which relies more on perception.
 - **Experimentation:** They analyze the grounding accuracy of an InfiGUI-R1-3B model on the ScreenSpot dataset as a function of "output tokens" (proxy for reasoning length) and "image tokens." They compare performance on textual vs. iconic UI elements and across different image sizes, considering "text ratio" (ratio of text tokens to total visual + text tokens).
 - **Observation:** Longer reasoning chains were found to consistently degrade grounding accuracy, especially for text targets. Grounding performance improved with increased image tokens, and higher text ratios correlated with lower grounding performance, suggesting that visual content is more crucial than textual reasoning for this task. This echoes Kahneman's "Thinking, Fast and Slow" concept, where visual pattern recognition is a "fast, intuitive" process.

2. **Output Evaluation (Reward Function Analysis):**
 - **Problem:** Existing rule-based reward functions for GUI grounding primarily use "Hit-based rewards" (checking if predicted box center is within ground truth) and "IoU-based rewards" (Intersection over Union). It's unclear how these interact during training.
 - **Experimentation:** They train models separately with `R_Hit` and `R_IoU` and jointly with `R_Hit + R_IoU`. They track accuracy, IoU, and "relative box size" (size of predicted box relative to image) across policy iterations. They visualize examples of predicted boxes.
 - **Observation:**
 - Optimizing `R_Hit` alone improved accuracy but led to decreasing IoU and smaller predicted boxes (reward hacking towards smaller, more precise points).
 - Optimizing `R_IoU` alone improved IoU but reduced accuracy and led to larger predicted boxes (reward hacking towards larger boxes to maximize overlap, even if less precise).
 - Joint optimization of `R_Hit + R_IoU` could still be dominated by one, leading to suboptimal balance. These biases arise because GRPO's sample selection interacts differently with how each reward function incentivizes box size.

3. **Policy Update (GRPO Objective Analysis):**
 - **Problem:** The GRPO objective (Equation 2) normalizes the advantage by `|o_i|` (response length) and centers rewards using standard deviation, which introduces biases.
 - **Experimentation:** They analyze the behavior of GRPO during training, tracking output length of correct vs. incorrect responses and the proportion of "extreme samples" (all responses correct/incorrect).
 - **Observation:**
 - **Response-level length bias:** GRPO prefers longer incorrect responses and shorter correct responses. For grounding, longer responses degrade performance (as observed in template analysis), making this bias particularly problematic.
 - **Question-level difficulty bias:** The reward normalization can cause the model to disproportionately focus on easier or harder samples. The authors posit that for grounding, relative box size serves as a proxy for difficulty (smaller boxes are harder). The standard GRPO tends to under-optimize harder samples.

#### 3.2. Proposed Solutions

Based on these analyses, the authors propose three targeted improvements, forming the core of **GUI-G1**:

1. **Fast Thinking Template (for Input Design):**
 - **Solution:** Replace the "Slow Thinking Template" with a "Fast Thinking Template" that encourages direct answer generation (`You are a helpful assistant. \nUser: Grounding instruction is: {question} Please help to locate and output the bbox coordinates in JSON format.\nAssistant:`). This aims to reduce unnecessary reasoning tokens during training, aligning with the finding that longer outputs harm grounding.

2. **Box Size Constraint Reward (for Output Evaluation):**
 - **Solution:** Introduce a new reward term, `R_Box`, in addition to `R_Hit` and `R_IoU`. `R_Box` is designed to encourage the predicted bounding box size to match the ground truth. It's defined as `4 / (x_p1 + x_p2 + y_p1 + y_p2)`, where `x_p1` etc. are terms inversely proportional to the absolute difference between predicted and ground-truth coordinates, normalized by image dimensions.
 - **Rationale:** `R_Box` acts as a regularizer, preventing reward hacking towards overly small or large boxes when `R_Hit` and `R_IoU` are jointly optimized. It's used in conjunction with `R_Hit` and `R_IoU` because `R_Box` alone doesn't provide enough signal for correct format generation. The final reward function is `R_Hit + αR_IoU + βR_Box`.

3. **Modified GRPO Objective (for Policy Update):**
 - **Solution 1 (Addressing length bias):** Replace the length normalization term `1/|o_i|` in the GRPO objective with a constant `1/Max_Tokens`. This prevents the objective from penalizing shorter correct responses or amplifying gradients for longer incorrect ones, mitigating the length bias observed in.
 - **Solution 2 (Addressing difficulty bias):** Introduce a difficulty coefficient `w_q` that weights the GRPO objective for each query `q`. `w_q` is calculated based on the inverse of the relative box size of the ground truth (smaller boxes = higher difficulty = higher `w_q`), normalized to a range of (0.5, 1.5]. This assigns greater gradients to harder samples, enabling the model to learn more effectively from challenging instances.

#### 3.3. Model Implementation

The proposed **GUI-G1-3B** model is built upon the **Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct** architecture, a multimodal large language model. Training is conducted using the VLM-R1 framework. The specific parameters for the reward function are `α = 0.25` and `β = 0.125`. Training involved 4 NVIDIA H800 GPUs over 3 days, with a global batch size of 32 and a learning rate of `1e-6`, for only one training epoch.

The training dataset comprises **17K samples** constructed from publicly available sources:
- **Mobile:** Derived from UI-BERT.
- **Web:** Sourced from OS-Atlas.
- **Desktop:** Also from OS-Atlas, covering Windows, Linux, and MacOS.
To ensure data quality, samples with consistently correct or incorrect responses when prompted eight times with Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct are discarded.

Evaluation is performed on two standard benchmarks:
- **ScreenSpot:** Assesses grounding across Mobile, Web, and Desktop platforms.
- **ScreenSpot-Pro:** Focuses on more challenging, high-resolution desktop scenarios across various professional domains (CAD, Development, Creative, Scientific, Office, OS).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields significant findings from both the analytical phase and the empirical evaluation of the proposed GUI-G1 model.

#### 4.1. Insights from R1-Zero-Like Training Analysis

The detailed analysis of the R1-Zero-like training paradigm reveals critical insights into its application for GUI grounding:

- **Longer Reasoning Harms Grounding:** Contrary to expectations from general LLM reasoning tasks, explicit chain-of-thought reasoning (longer output token sequences) consistently degrades grounding accuracy on the ScreenSpot dataset. This effect is more pronounced for text-based UI elements. This suggests that for visual grounding, "fast thinking" (direct answers) is superior to "slow thinking" (deliberative reasoning).
- **Grounding is Image-Token Dependent:** Grounding performance improves with an increased number of image tokens and is inversely correlated with the "text ratio" of the input. This strongly indicates that GUI grounding benefits more from rich visual information than from additional textual reasoning steps, aligning with the "Thinking, Fast and Slow" hypothesis for visual pattern recognition.
- **Reward Hacking is Prevalent with Existing Reward Functions:**
 - Optimizing solely with **Hit-based rewards (`R_Hit`)** leads to models predicting smaller bounding boxes (sometimes just a point), improving accuracy but decreasing Intersection over Union (IoU). This is "point accuracy" hacking.
 - Optimizing solely with **IoU-based rewards (`R_IoU`)** leads to models predicting significantly larger bounding boxes, increasing IoU but reducing accuracy. This is "area overlap" hacking.
 - These behaviors highlight that current reward functions, when applied in GRPO's sample selection mechanism, create conflicting incentives regarding predicted box size, leading to suboptimal localization.
- **GRPO Suffers from Length and Difficulty Biases:**
 - **Length Bias:** The standard GRPO objective's normalization by response length `|o_i|` inadvertently prefers longer incorrect responses and shorter correct responses. This is particularly detrimental for GUI grounding, where longer outputs already reduce accuracy.
 - **Difficulty Bias:** The GRPO objective tends to treat all samples equally, leading to under-optimization on harder examples. The authors empirically show that relative box size can serve as a proxy for difficulty in grounding, with smaller target boxes being more challenging.

#### 4.2. Performance of GUI-G1-3B

The proposed GUI-G1-3B model, incorporating the "Fast Thinking Template," "Box Size Constraint Reward," and the "Modified GRPO Objective," demonstrates compelling performance:

- **State-of-the-Art on ScreenSpot:** GUI-G1-3B achieves an overall accuracy of **90.3%** on the ScreenSpot benchmark. This surpasses all prior models of similar size and even outperforms larger models across various categories:
 - **Proprietary models:** Outperforms Gemini 2.0 (84.0%).
 - **General open-source models:** Significantly outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B (84.7%).
 - **GUI-specific SFT models:** Beats formidable models like OS-Atlas-7B (82.5%), Aguvis-7B (84.4%), and UI-TARS-7B (35.7% on ScreenSpot-Pro, though not directly comparable here).
 - **R1-style GUI agents:** Exceeds InfiGUI-R1-3B (87.5%), GUI-R1-7B, and UI-R1-3B.
- **Strong Performance on ScreenSpot-Pro:** On the more challenging ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark, GUI-G1-3B achieves an average accuracy of **37.1%**. This is competitive with and often surpasses other high-performing models:
 - It outperforms the larger UI-TARS-7B (35.7%) and the previous best R1-based model, InfiGUI-R1-3B (35.7%).
 - While slightly lagging OS-Atlas-7B on the "OS" subset (16.1% vs. 16.8%), GUI-G1-3B generally performs better across other challenging domains (e.g., CAD, Development, Scientific, Office).
- **Efficiency Gains:** GUI-G1-3B achieves this superior performance while being notably efficient:
 - It is trained on a relatively small dataset of **only 17K public samples**.
 - It requires **fewer training stages** compared to multi-stage approaches.
 - It demonstrates significantly higher **inference efficiency** by generating substantially fewer output tokens (e.g., 37 tokens vs. 107 tokens on average compared to InfiGUI-R1-3B on ScreenSpot), leading to reduced computational costs.

The results underscore that the proposed task-specific adaptations to the R1-Zero-like training paradigm are highly effective in unlocking the visual grounding capabilities of MLLMs for GUI agents, even with limited supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the fields of multimodal AI, reinforcement learning, and human-computer interaction, with substantial potential impact:

1. **Advancing State-of-the-Art in GUI Grounding:** The GUI-G1-3B model establishes a new state-of-the-art for visual grounding in GUI agents, particularly impressive given its relatively small model size (3B parameters) and limited training data (17K samples). This demonstrates that strategic improvements to training methodology can yield breakthroughs without necessarily requiring massive scaling of models or datasets.

2. **Deepening Understanding of RL for MLLMs:** The paper provides a critical, empirically-driven analysis of the R1-Zero-like training paradigm when applied to a specific, perceptually-heavy task like GUI grounding. It highlights that blindly applying general-purpose RL methods can introduce counterproductive biases (length bias, difficulty bias) and incentivize undesirable behaviors (reward hacking). This work contributes a crucial understanding of how to adapt and refine RL objectives for optimal performance in specialized multimodal domains.

3. **Enhancing Training Efficiency and Accessibility:** By achieving state-of-the-art results with a smaller training dataset and fewer training stages, this research makes the development of high-performing GUI agents more accessible. It suggests that researchers and developers with limited computational resources can still achieve competitive results by focusing on methodological refinements rather than solely relying on data and model scale.

4. **Improving Inference Efficiency:** The "Fast Thinking Template" leads to the generation of significantly fewer output tokens during inference. This directly translates to reduced computational cost, faster response times, and lower energy consumption, which are critical factors for deploying GUI agents in real-world applications (e.g., on-device or in latency-sensitive scenarios).

5. **Robustness and Generalization:** The superior performance on both ScreenSpot and the challenging ScreenSpot-Pro benchmarks, particularly against models trained on larger datasets, indicates that GUI-G1's approach fosters better generalization. The observation that its gains mainly result from "post-training that activates pretrained knowledge rather than from task-specific data" is particularly insightful, suggesting a more efficient leveraging of pre-trained MLLM capabilities.

6. **Guidance for Future RL Research in MLLMs:** The detailed analysis of issues like reward hacking and GRPO biases provides a valuable roadmap for future research in applying RL to MLLMs across various tasks. It emphasizes the importance of task-specific reward engineering and objective function design to align learning incentives with desired behaviors.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accessibility:** More accurate and robust GUI grounding can significantly improve accessibility tools for users with disabilities, enabling more seamless interaction with digital interfaces.
- **Automation:** Enhanced GUI agents can automate complex tasks across various software applications (e.g., office suites, CAD tools, web browsers), boosting productivity in professional and personal settings.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** The ability of AI to accurately understand and interact with GUIs opens doors for more intuitive and natural human-computer interfaces, where users can simply describe desired actions.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** GUI agents are a form of embodied AI operating in a digital environment. Lessons learned from their development, particularly regarding robust grounding and action execution, can inform the design of agents in physical robotics.

**Considerations and Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including the focus solely on grounding (not full action prediction or planning), limited scope of RL analysis (primarily GRPO), and the relatively small training dataset. They also highlight broader impacts concerning the careful deployment of such agents, emphasizing that reward design choices can lead to unintended behaviors like reward hacking or bias. This self-awareness further strengthens the paper's contribution by inviting future research into fairness, robustness, and privacy in GUI agent development.
