# GARField: Group Anything with Radiance Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> GARField distills SAM's 2D masks into a scale-conditioned 3D affinity field over NeRF, letting each point belong to different groups depending on queried scale. Recursive clustering at descending scales yields a full hierarchical decomposition of a 3D scene, from whole objects down to fine subparts, without user input.

## Problem

- Grouping is inherently ambiguous across granularity levels, and prior 3D segmentation methods assume a flat hierarchy of objects or instances only.
- SAM produces per-view 2D masks that are neither multi-view consistent nor organized into any coherent hierarchy across scales.
- Prior NeRF segmentation approaches require ground-truth semantic labels, user scribbles, or slow-fast contrastive training to resolve conflicting masks.

## Method

- ==Scale-conditioned affinity field==: a NeRF-based feature field taking 3D position and a euclidean scale, trained so feature distance encodes group affinity at that scale.
- ==Margin-based contrastive loss== with densified scale supervision, augmenting scale between mask boundaries and adding a ==containment auxiliary loss== for coarse-to-fine consistency.
- ==Recursive HDBSCAN clustering== that decomposes the affinity field at descending scales into a hierarchical tree of 3D groups without a fixed cluster count.

## Results

- On 3D completeness across **5** scenes, GARField beats SAM by wide margins, e.g. bouquet fine-level mIOU rises from **17.4** to **76.0**.
- Hierarchical grouping recall mIOU improves substantially over SAM, e.g. keyboard scene from **23.2** to **77.9** with full method.
- Ablations show both scale-conditioning and dense hierarchy supervision are necessary, removing either drops mIOU (e.g. living room **76.6** down to **49.7** without scale).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical scale is an effective, learnable cue for resolving grouping ambiguity when 2D mask sources disagree across viewpoints or granularities.
> - Fusing many inconsistent single-view 2D proposals into one 3D field can exceed the fidelity of any individual 2D input, not just match it.
> - Distillation quality hinges on dense, continuous supervision across the conditioning variable; sparse discrete supervision leaves gaps that break hierarchy consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have emerged as a powerful tool for 3D scene representation, enabling high-quality novel view synthesis from a set of input images. However, standard NeRFs represent scenes as unstructured volumes without semantic understanding or meaningful decomposition, limiting their utility for scene understanding and manipulation. This is where GARField (Group Anything with Radiance Fields) makes a significant contribution.

GARField, developed by researchers at UC Berkeley and Luma AI, presents a method for decomposing 3D scenes into hierarchical groups of varying granularity. The method automatically discovers semantic structure in a scene without requiring user input, enabling powerful capabilities for scene understanding, editing, and interaction. By integrating scale-conditioned affinity fields with NeRFs, GARField addresses a fundamental challenge in computer vision: how to segment 3D scenes into meaningful hierarchical structures that reflect human understanding of object composition.

### 2. Background and Context

Hierarchical scene decomposition is a natural way for humans to understand the world. We perceive objects not just as whole entities but as compositions of parts that can be further broken down into smaller elements. For example, we understand that a desk has a surface and legs, and that the surface may hold various items like a keyboard, monitor, and decorative objects.

Traditional approaches to scene understanding in computer vision include:

1. **2D Image Segmentation**: Methods like Segment Anything Model (SAM) can identify regions in 2D images but lack 3D consistency across multiple views.

2. **3D Semantic Segmentation**: Assigns class labels to 3D points but typically produces a flat segmentation without hierarchy.

3. **Instance Segmentation in NeRFs**: Recent work enables identifying individual object instances in NeRFs but generally doesn't organize them hierarchically.

The key innovation of GARField is to introduce hierarchical grouping capability to NeRFs through a scale-conditioned affinity field that can represent different levels of grouping, from fine-grained parts to whole objects and even room-scale structures.

### 3. Methodology

GARField's pipeline consists of three main components:

1. **2D Mask Generation**: GARField leverages the Segment Anything Model (SAM) to generate a diverse set of 2D segmentation masks from input images. These masks serve as training data for learning 3D grouping.

2. **Scale Assignment**: Using depth information from a partially trained NeRF, GARField assigns a physical 3D scale to each mask based on the extent of 3D points within the mask. This scale information is crucial for resolving ambiguities in grouping.

3. **Scale-Conditioned Affinity Field Training**: The core of GARField is a neural network that learns to generate features for 3D points that determine their affinity (likelihood of belonging to the same group) at different scales.

The mathematical formulation involves:

- A NeRF-like MLP that maps 3D coordinates x and a scale parameter s to a feature vector F_g(x, s)
- A contrastive loss function that pulls together features of points within the same mask and pushes apart features of points in different masks
- Scale conditioning that allows the same points to be grouped differently at different scales

### 4. Scale-Conditioned Affinity Fields

The key insight of GARField is that the same scene can be decomposed at multiple levels of granularity, and the appropriate grouping depends on the scale at which we observe the scene. For example, at a fine scale, we might distinguish individual petals of a flower, while at a coarser scale, we might group all flowers in a bouquet together.

Two key innovations improve the quality of the learned affinity field:

1. **Continuous Scale Supervision**: Instead of training only at discrete scales observed in the SAM masks, GARField interpolates between scales to provide supervision throughout the scale space. This is implemented by sampling random scales during training.

2. **Containment Loss**: This ensures that groups observed at smaller scales are contained within groups at larger scales, maintaining a proper hierarchical structure.

### 5. Hierarchical Decomposition

Once the scale-conditioned affinity field is trained, GARField performs hierarchical decomposition by:

1. Beginning with a large scale (coarse grouping) and recursively decreasing the scale to reveal finer details.
2. At each scale, using HDBSCAN (Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) to cluster points based on their affinity features.
3. Constructing a tree structure where nodes represent groups, and edges connect groups to their subgroups.

This process creates a hierarchical decomposition where larger elements contain smaller ones, reflecting the natural composition of objects and scenes.

GARField demonstrates impressive results across various scenes, from room interiors to tabletop objects. The method successfully identifies meaningful object boundaries and hierarchical structures.

The authors evaluate GARField both qualitatively, through visual inspection of the hierarchical decompositions, and quantitatively, by measuring:

1. **3D Completeness**: GARField produces more complete 3D masks than SAM across viewpoints, resulting in higher mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) with multi-view human annotations.

2. **Hierarchical Grouping Recall**: GARField achieves higher mIoU than SAM in recalling ground truth hierarchical groups, demonstrating its ability to generate high-fidelity groupings by fusing information from multiple views.

### 7. Applications

GARField enables several applications:

1. **Automatic 3D Asset Extraction**: The hierarchical decomposition allows extracting complete 3D models at multiple levels of granularity.

2. **Interactive Segmentation**: Users can select a point and scale to extract the corresponding group.

3. **Scene Editing**: The hierarchical structure allows editing at different levels, from individual objects to groups.

4. **Dynamic Scene Understanding**: The object-centric representation could facilitate tracking and modeling of dynamic scenes.

### 8. Limitations and Future Work

While GARField represents a significant advance, it has several limitations:

1. **Dependency on SAM**: The quality of the hierarchical decomposition depends on the initial SAM masks. If SAM fails to identify certain objects, GARField may also miss them.

2. **Limited Semantic Understanding**: GARField groups based on visual similarity and spatial proximity but lacks deeper semantic understanding of object function or relationships.

3. **Computational Requirements**: Training the scale-conditioned affinity field requires significant computational resources.

Future work could address these limitations by:

1. Incorporating semantic information from language models to enhance grouping with semantic understanding.

2. Extending to dynamic scenes where objects move and interact.

3. Developing more efficient training and inference methods.

### 9. Conclusion

GARField represents a significant step forward in 3D scene understanding by enabling automatic hierarchical decomposition of NeRF scenes. By learning scale-conditioned affinity fields, GARField can generate meaningful object groupings at multiple levels of granularity without user input.

This capability opens up new possibilities for applications in computer vision, graphics, and robotics, where understanding the hierarchical structure of scenes is essential. The ability to automatically extract and manipulate 3D assets at different scales could transform workflows in content creation, scene editing, and interactive applications.

As shown in various examples (Figures 1, 6, 7), GARField successfully decomposes complex scenes like living rooms, office desks, and tables with food items, identifying not just object boundaries but also their hierarchical relationships. This demonstrates the method's versatility and effectiveness across diverse real-world scenarios.

By bridging the gap between low-level NeRF representations and high-level scene understanding, GARField takes an important step toward more intelligent and useful 3D vision systems.
