# Interactive Distillation for Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed HINT, a knowledge distillation framework for cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning that refines a hierarchical teacher through pseudo off-policy learning and filters expert demonstrations based on their long-term outcome. HINT demonstrated superior performance, including up to 60% higher success rates, over existing CTDE and KD baselines in complex multi-agent tasks like maritime logistics and wildfire management.

## Problem

- Existing knowledge distillation (KD) methods in MARL struggle to synthesize high-performing and reliable 'teacher' models in complex, dynamic environments.
- Teachers often provide inconsistent or suboptimal guidance when students encounter out-of-distribution (OOD) states not seen during initial teacher training.
- Observation mismatches between a centralized teacher's global view and decentralized students' local observations can degrade the quality of supervision.

## Method

- **HINT** utilizes a hierarchical teacher, pre-trained with ==HiT-MAC==, which decomposes decision-making into high-level coordination and low-level execution to manage complexity and provide structured guidance.
- The framework employs ==pseudo off-policy reinforcement learning==, using ==V-trace==, to enable the teacher to adapt its policy by learning from hybrid trajectories where students explore and the teacher completes the task.
- An extended ==DAgger== approach with performance-based filtering ensures that only expert actions leading to successful outcomes are included in the distillation dataset, improving data quality and robustness to noisy supervision.

## Results

- HINT consistently outperformed competitive online CTDE and KD baselines across medium and hard tasks in MARINE and FireCommander environments, achieving up to a **60%** higher success rate than the best KD baseline in MARINE-Hard.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both pseudo off-policy RL and performance-based filtering modules are critical for HINT's superior performance, leading to lower suboptimal demonstration rates and higher overall success.
- Analysis of student structure showed that the HetNet student model with heterogeneous graph attention (HetGAT) significantly improved performance (e.g., **84 points** in success rate over LSTM-Only in FC-Medium), highlighting the value of structured communication.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive teacher refinement via pseudo off-policy learning is crucial for teachers to provide robust and relevant guidance, especially when student policies diverge into out-of-distribution states.
> - Filtering expert demonstrations based on their empirical long-term outcomes significantly enhances the quality of distilled knowledge, effectively mitigating the challenges of an imperfect or OOD-exposed teacher.
> - A hierarchical teacher design provides a scalable and structured approach to managing coordination complexity in multi-agent systems, improving both sample efficiency and the tractability of distillation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is a field dedicated to developing autonomous agents capable of coordinating actions to achieve common goals in shared environments. Applications span diverse domains such as search and rescue, logistics, and warehouse management. A prevalent challenge in MARL, particularly in complex tasks with increasing team sizes, includes partial observability, non-stationary dynamics, and effective credit assignment among agents.

To mitigate these challenges, the paradigm of centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) has gained prominence. CTDE frameworks leverage global state information or joint observations during the training phase to stabilize learning, while individual agents execute policies based only on local observations, preserving scalability. Numerous algorithms have been developed within CTDE, including value-decomposition methods like QMIX and QTRAN, and policy gradient methods like MAPPO. Some CTDE approaches further incorporate communication mechanisms (e.g., CommNet, IC3Net, TarMAC) to enhance coordination during decentralized execution, though this can introduce training complexities related to noisy signals and policy gradient variance.

Knowledge Distillation (KD) has emerged as an alternative to CTDE to address training instability and credit assignment issues in MARL. KD allows a simpler "student" model (decentralized agents) to learn by mimicking a more capable "teacher" model (a centralized expert with global state access). This approach aims to provide stable training signals and simplify the learning process for students. However, existing KD methods in MARL often face limitations:
1. **Teacher Synthesis:** Creating a high-performing and reliable teacher in complex, dynamic environments is challenging, as MARL-based teacher training can be brittle and sample-inefficient.
2. **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) States:** Even capable teachers may encounter states generated by student policies that diverge significantly from the teacher's training distribution, leading to inconsistent or poor-quality guidance.
3. **Observation Mismatches:** Discrepancies between the centralized teacher's comprehensive observation space and the decentralized students' local observations can degrade the quality of supervision.

This research aims to address these specific bottlenecks within the broader context of cooperative MARL, by proposing a novel KD framework that integrates hierarchical supervision with interactive knowledge distillation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust knowledge distillation framework for cooperative MARL that overcomes the identified limitations of existing KD approaches. The authors propose Hierarchical INteractive Teacher-based transfer (HINT) to achieve this.

The motivation for HINT stems directly from the three key bottlenecks in current MARL KD paradigms:

1. **Synthesizing High-Performing Teachers in Complex Domains:** Existing KD methods often assume an "oracle-like" teacher that consistently provides optimal guidance. However, in complex, dynamic multi-agent tasks, training such a teacher is difficult due to coordination overheads, sample inefficiency, and brittleness. The authors are motivated to create a teacher that is both scalable and high-performing.
2. **Teacher Reasoning in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) States:** As student policies learn and explore, their trajectories can deviate from the data distribution encountered by the teacher during its initial training. This leads to the teacher providing unreliable or suboptimal guidance in unfamiliar OOD states, diminishing the effectiveness of distillation. The motivation is to enable the teacher to adapt to student behavior and provide richer, context-aware guidance in OOD scenarios.
3. **Mismatches Between Teacher and Student Observation Spaces:** The centralized teacher operates with a global view, while decentralized students have local observations. This inherent asymmetry can lead to teachers providing guidance that is not fully aligned with the student's limited context. The motivation is to improve the quality of supervision by ensuring that only outcome-relevant guidance is retained, thereby reducing observation mismatches and providing targeted feedback.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond static, oracle-level teacher supervision towards an adaptive, interactive teacher that can continuously refine its policy based on student experiences, provide robust guidance in diverse states, and offer high-quality, relevant demonstrations to facilitate stable and efficient decentralized student learning in complex cooperative MARL settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HINT (Hierarchical INteractive Teacher-based transfer) is a novel knowledge distillation framework designed for cooperative MARL in a centralized training, decentralized execution (CTDE) setup. It integrates three main components: a hierarchical teacher, pseudo off-policy reinforcement learning for teacher refinement, and performance-based filtering during dataset aggregation.

**A. Hierarchical Teacher with HiT-MAC**
The centralized teacher in HINT is pre-trained using Hierarchical Target-oriented Multi-Agent Coordination (HiT-MAC). This choice is motivated by HiT-MAC's robust performance in dynamic and complex environments and its ability to manage coordination overheads.
1. **Hierarchical Structure:** HiT-MAC decomposes decision-making into two levels:
 - **High-level Coordinator (π_H^T):** This policy observes the joint global state (¯o) and assigns subgoals (¯g) to individual agents. It leverages a self-attention mechanism over joint observations to capture inter-agent dependencies and evaluate the relative importance of agent-target pairs.
 - **Low-level Executors (π_L^T):** Each agent `i` acts as a low-level executor, selecting primitive actions (a_i) based on its local observation (o_i) and the assigned subgoal (g_i).
2. **Pre-training:** The teacher is pre-trained using a two-stage strategy. Low-level policies are trained independently to perform fundamental tasks, and then high-level policies learn to coordinate these behaviors. This sequential training improves stability and efficiency. The low-level policies are randomized with respect to agent positions and environmental factors to enhance generalization. The teacher's training is based on the A3C framework.
3. **Student Model (HetNet):** For decentralized execution, HINT adopts HetNet as the student model, which is designed for heterogeneous agents. Each agent's policy (π_S^i) processes local observations through a preprocessing module (CNN layers for spatial data), an LSTM encoder to mitigate partial observability, and heterogeneous graph attention (HetGAT) layers for structured inter-agent communication. A decoder integrates local features and aggregated messages to select actions. HetNet uses class-specific weights for efficiency with heterogeneous agents.

**B. Knowledge Distillation**
The core of HINT's student training is knowledge distillation, where decentralized student policies (π_S) learn by imitating the centralized hierarchical teacher (π_T). The objective for policy distillation minimizes the difference between the teacher and student policy distributions using a KL-divergence term, augmented with an entropy regularization term (H(π_S)) to encourage both imitation and policy diversity (Equation 3). Samples are stored in a replay buffer and updated using batch-based gradient optimization to ensure stable training. The distinct aspect here is that the dataset `D` for distillation is continuously augmented by an adaptive teacher, providing richer guidance.

**C. Pseudo Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning**
This component enables the teacher to adapt to student behavior and provide more relevant guidance, especially in out-of-distribution (OOD) states.
1. **Hybrid Trajectories:** Unlike traditional KD where teacher and student data are separate, HINT combines them within a single trajectory. The student initially explores, and then the teacher completes the trajectory (Alg. 2, lines 5-8). This "student explores, teacher resumes" structure allows the teacher to learn not only optimal actions but also how to recover from student mistakes.
2. **Stable Teacher Updates:** To update the teacher's policy (π_T) using these hybrid, off-policy trajectories, HINT employs the V-trace method from IMPALA. V-trace provides stable convergence through bounded off-policy corrections (Equation 4) to estimate corrected value targets (v_π_T^t).
3. **Policy and Value Function Updates:** The teacher's value function V_π_T(¯o_t) is trained by minimizing the squared error with the V-trace targets (Equation 5). The teacher's policy objective (J_θ) integrates samples from both teacher (on-policy) and student (off-policy, with importance weighting) trajectories (Equation 6). This hybrid learning scheme refines the teacher, enhances generalization, and maintains policy consistency. During this adaptive phase, only the high-level policy is refined, while the low-level policies (pretrained on diverse states) remain fixed to simplify optimization.

**D. DAgger with Performance-Based Filtering**
HINT extends the DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) approach to address distributional shift and improve the quality of expert actions.
1. **Iterative Data Aggregation:** The student policies run in the environment, and for each visited state (¯o_t), they query the teacher for an expert action (¯a*_t). These observation-action pairs are aggregated into a dataset `D` for subsequent student training.
2. **Performance-Based Filtering:** To counter the issue of an imperfect teacher providing suboptimal guidance in OOD states, a filtering mechanism is introduced (Alg. 3, Fig. 5). When a student queries the teacher, the teacher's trajectory is simulated from that query point to the end of the episode. Only if this simulated trajectory leads to a successful outcome is the queried expert action considered "good" and included in the dataset. Otherwise, the action is discarded. This domain-agnostic filtering ensures that students are trained only on empirically outcome-relevant demonstrations, enhancing data quality and robustness to noisy supervision.
3. **Dataset Management:** The dataset `D` retains a fixed number of initial demonstrations and periodically samples recent interactions, blending reliable foundations with fresh guidance aligned with the student's evolving capabilities.

These three components operate in a multi-threaded structure to enhance computational efficiency, with the cycle repeating until student policies converge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HINT was evaluated on two challenging cooperative multi-agent environments: MARINE (maritime logistics with routing and logistic agents, dynamic ocean conditions) and FireCommander (FC, grid-based wildfire management with action and perception agents, dynamic fire propagation). Performance was measured by success rate (proportion of successful episodes) and average steps taken (efficiency).

**A. Student State Distribution Analysis**
An initial analysis showed that as environment complexity and team size increased (e.g., MARINE-Hard, FC-Hard), the student state distributions diverged significantly from the teacher's training distribution (measured by KL-divergence). This indicated that teachers were increasingly exposed to out-of-distribution (OOD) states, motivating the need for adaptive teacher refinement.

**B. Benchmark Test against Online CTDE Baselines**
HINT was compared against several online CTDE methods, including those without communication (MAPPO, HAPPO) and with communication (TarMAC, IC3Net, CommNet, HetNet).
- **Performance:** HINT consistently outperformed all CTDE baselines across medium and hard tasks in both MARINE and FC. For example, in MARINE-Hard, HINT achieved a 53.33% success rate, while the next best, HAPPO warm-started, only reached 38.00%. In FC-Hard, HINT achieved 51.33% success, significantly higher than HetNet warm-started at 42.67%.
- **Efficiency and Robustness:** HINT showed higher success rates coupled with fewer steps taken, indicating both reliability and efficiency in challenging scenarios. It also demonstrated smaller standard deviations in FC-Hard compared to warm-started HetNet, suggesting a more stable training process.
- **Communication Benefits:** The performance improvements were partially attributed to HINT's structured communication (inherent in the HetNet student model), but also to the stable training signals and adaptive guidance provided by the hierarchical teacher, pseudo off-policy RL, and performance-filtered data.

**C. Benchmark Test against Online KD Baselines**
HINT was also compared against three online knowledge distillation (KD) methods: CTDS, IGM-DA, and PTDE.
- **Dominant Performance:** HINT consistently outperformed other KD baselines in both MARINE and FC environments across all difficulty settings.
- **Teacher Robustness:** In FC, where QMiX (the base policy for KD baselines) lacked a communication module, CTDS and IGM-DA teachers struggled, even with global information access. This highlighted the robustness of HINT's hierarchical teacher in more complex settings.
- **Hard Task Superiority:** In MARINE-Hard, HINT surpassed PTDE by 60% in success rate, despite PTDE showing comparable performance in easier tasks. This further supported the importance of an adaptive hierarchical teacher in the HINT pipeline for challenging scenarios.

**D. Ablation Study**
1. **Effect of Key Modules:** An ablation study evaluated the contributions of pseudo off-policy RL and performance-based filtering.
 - The full HINT model consistently outperformed variants with either or both modules removed, particularly in MARINE-Hard and FC-Hard tasks.
 - The full model exhibited a lower suboptimal demonstration rate, indicating that the adaptive teacher provided higher-quality guidance. This suggests that HINT's adaptability and filtering mechanisms are crucial for robust, high-quality policy learning.
2. **Student Structure:** Ablation on the HetNet student model components (LSTM, HetGAT) showed:
 - HetNet (LSTM + HetGAT) consistently outperformed LSTM-Only (no communication) and LSTM+GNN (generic graph neural network) variants.
 - The largest gains were in FC-Medium, where HetNet improved the success rate by 84 points over LSTM-Only and 18 points over LSTM+GNN. This emphasizes the value of heterogeneous attention in enabling structured communication for agents with limited sensing capabilities.
3. **Sensitivity Analysis:** HINT showed stability across a range of hyperparameters (student's entropy coefficient α, learning rate lr, episodes for teacher training N_pseudo, and episodes for expert queries N_query) in FC-Medium, with performance variations typically between -2.3% to +6.3% in success rate. Higher values generally led to modest gains from increased data or regularization.

**E. Limitations**
The scalability of the centralized teacher is inherently limited, though HINT scales to 20x20 grid environments with up to 10 agents. HINT's framework also includes more diverse modules, potentially leading to higher computational costs compared to simpler baselines, although multi-threading mitigated this, resulting in comparable overall training times. Finally, HINT relies on human expertise for defining hierarchical structures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of HINT represents an advancement in the field of multi-agent reinforcement learning, particularly for cooperative tasks requiring robust coordination in complex, dynamic, and partially observable environments. The work highlights the critical importance of addressing teacher policy suboptimality, which is often a neglected aspect in traditional knowledge distillation approaches for MARL.

The significance of HINT lies in several key contributions:
1. **Robustness in Complex Domains:** By achieving substantial performance improvements (e.g., 60% to 165% higher success rates) over competitive CTDE and KD baselines in challenging environments like MARINE and FireCommander, HINT demonstrates its capacity to enable more reliable and efficient multi-agent coordination under uncertainty. This suggests a pathway for deploying MARL systems in real-world scenarios that demand high-performance and adaptability.
2. **Adaptive Knowledge Transfer:** The core innovations—pseudo off-policy RL and performance-based filtering—allow the centralized teacher to adapt its guidance based on student experiences and only distill high-quality, outcome-relevant demonstrations. This shifts the paradigm from static, oracle-like supervision to dynamic, interactive learning, making knowledge distillation more practical and effective in open-ended environments where students often encounter out-of-distribution states.
3. **Scalable Teacher Design:** Leveraging hierarchical RL for the teacher provides a structured way to manage coordination complexity, improving sample efficiency and making the distillation procedure tractable for tasks with larger agent teams and environmental scales than typically handled by non-hierarchical teachers.
4. **Addressing Observation Mismatches:** The performance-based filtering mechanism implicitly mitigates observation mismatches by validating teacher actions against long-term successful outcomes. This ensures that the decentralized students receive context-aware and trustworthy feedback, even with their limited local observations.

The potential impact of this research is multi-faceted. It paves the way for future MARL systems that can learn more effectively from imperfect teachers, reducing the burden of creating perfectly optimal expert policies. The concepts of adaptive teacher refinement and selective data distillation could be applied to various imitation learning and human-robot interaction contexts. While limitations such as the reliance on human-defined hierarchies and computational overheads exist, the demonstrated robustness and performance gains suggest that HINT's principles could inspire further research into automated hierarchy construction, more efficient inference for teacher guidance, and asynchronous credit assignment mechanisms that further enhance the scalability and autonomy of multi-agent systems.
