# Audio Array-Based 3D UAV Trajectory Estimation with LiDAR Pseudo-Labeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University developed a self-supervised framework for 3D UAV trajectory estimation that leverages audio array data, using LiDAR to generate pseudo-labels for training. Their system achieved an average position error of 0.48 meters, maintaining performance in both ideal and dark environments, which is an improvement over visual and combined methods.

## Problem

- Current UAV tracking methods, primarily visual or RADAR/LiDAR, face limitations in terms of visibility, field of view, and cost-effectiveness for widespread deployment.
- The increasing use of UAVs presents challenges in public safety, privacy, and unauthorized airspace incursions, necessitating robust 3D trajectory estimation.
- Developing audio-based UAV tracking has been hindered by the difficulty and expense of collecting and annotating ground truth data in real-world scenarios.

## Method

- A self-supervised Teacher-Student Network framework is introduced, where a Teacher Network generates 3D pseudo-labels from LiDAR data.
- The Student Network, an Audio Perception Network, processes microphone array data by extracting time difference of arrival (TDOA) and intensity features from mel-spectrograms.
- Gaussian Process (GP) modeling is applied post-estimation to refine and smooth the predicted 3D UAV trajectories, enhancing spatiotemporal consistency.

## Results

- The framework achieved an average position error of **0.48** meters, surpassing established visual, audio, and multi-modal methods in 3D UAV trajectory estimation.
- Performance remained consistent in dark environments (0.48m APE), showcasing robustness against lighting conditions where visual systems severely degraded (e.g., VisualNet at 11.45m APE).
- The method exhibits high computational efficiency with **0.0065** GLOPs, making it suitable for practical deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LiDAR data can be effectively leveraged as a pseudo-ground truth source to train audio-based perception networks in a self-supervised manner.
> - An audio array, combined with specific convolutional feature extraction (TDOA and intensity), can accurately estimate 3D UAV trajectories across diverse environmental conditions.
> - Gaussian Process modeling significantly enhances the smoothness and precision of audio-derived 3D trajectory estimations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The proliferation of small Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) has led to increasing concerns regarding public safety, privacy violations, unauthorized surveillance, and risks in restricted airspaces, including civilian aviation and cross-border activities. Consequently, there is a recognized need for advanced UAV tracking and trajectory estimation solutions.

Current research in UAV tracking predominantly relies on visual information for 2D tracking or RADAR/LiDAR for 3D detection. However, these methods possess inherent limitations. Visual approaches are susceptible to poor visibility conditions, such as low light or adverse weather, and often suffer from restricted fields of view. RADAR/LiDAR systems, while capable of 3D detection, are typically characterized by high costs, making them less suitable for large-area surveillance or low-altitude tracking of smaller, often illicit, UAVs.

The utilization of audio data for UAV trajectory estimation remains less explored despite the accessibility and cost-effectiveness of microphones compared to cameras and LiDAR systems. Existing audio-based approaches face challenges related to data annotation and the filtering of ambient noise, particularly in complex real-world environments where labeled data is scarce.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing visual and LiDAR/RADAR systems, specifically by leveraging audio data for 3D UAV trajectory estimation. It aims to overcome the significant hurdle of data annotation in audio-based methods through a self-supervised learning paradigm. By integrating LiDAR data to generate pseudo-ground truth labels, the proposed framework seeks to enable robust and cost-effective UAV tracking solutions that operate independently of visual conditions and high-cost sensors during deployment. This approach contributes to the development of more versatile and practical UAV surveillance technologies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework for 3D UAV trajectory estimation that utilizes audio array data. The system aims to provide robust and accurate UAV tracking capabilities without requiring manual data labels or continuous reliance on expensive sensors like LiDAR during operational deployment.

The motivation behind this work stems from several key challenges and needs:

1. **Addressing UAV-related Security Concerns:** The widespread availability and use of UAVs have introduced significant security and privacy concerns. Effective tracking and trajectory estimation are crucial for mitigating threats such as unauthorized surveillance, incursions into restricted airspace, and illicit activities.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Tracking Technologies:** Current dominant methods for UAV tracking, primarily visual and RADAR/LiDAR systems, have inherent drawbacks. Visual systems are compromised by poor visibility and limited fields of view, while RADAR/LiDAR systems are expensive for wide-area or low-altitude surveillance. There is a need for a more versatile and economical solution.
3. **Leveraging Underutilized Audio Information:** Audio data offers an accessible and cost-effective alternative for UAV tracking, given the ubiquity and affordability of microphones. However, its potential for 3D trajectory estimation has been largely underexploited due to the complexities of signal processing and the difficulty of obtaining annotated data.
4. **Tackling Data Annotation Challenges:** A major impediment to developing supervised learning models for audio-based 3D trajectory estimation is the scarcity and high cost of collecting and annotating ground truth data in real-world scenarios, especially for 3D trajectories. The authors are motivated to eliminate this dependency.
5. **Enabling Self-Supervised Learning for Audio-Based Systems:** To overcome the annotation challenge, the research aims to implement a self-supervised learning model. This approach is designed to train an audio perception network using automatically generated pseudo-labels, thereby reducing the need for human intervention in data labeling.
6. **Enhancing Trajectory Smoothness and Accuracy:** Beyond initial estimation, the research is motivated by the need for continuous and smooth trajectory predictions. This requires incorporating advanced filtering or smoothing techniques, such as Gaussian Process modeling, to refine the estimated paths.

In summary, the specific contributions highlighted by the authors, which reflect their objectives, are:

- Presenting a novel self-supervised Teacher-Student Network for audio vectoring-based 3D trajectory estimation to achieve robust UAV tracking across diverse lighting conditions without manual labels.
- Proposing a cost-effective unsupervised method for 3D drone trajectory estimation using LiDAR scans as pseudo-ground truth for the Audio Perception Network.
- Benchmarking the method against various state-of-the-art approaches to demonstrate superior accuracy in inferring drone 3D trajectories.
- Open-sourcing their work to contribute to the broader research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for 3D UAV trajectory estimation utilizes a novel self-supervised learning model structured around a Teacher-Student Network architecture. This architecture allows for the training of an audio-based trajectory estimation system without requiring labeled ground truth data.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The system comprises two main components:
1. **Teacher Network:** This component employs an unsupervised method to process LiDAR point clouds and generate high-precision 3D UAV trajectory estimates. These estimates serve as pseudo-labels.
2. **Student Network (Audio Perception Network):** This network processes audio array data to predict UAV trajectories. It is trained in a self-supervised manner using the pseudo-labels provided by the Teacher Network.

Once trained, the Student Network can operate independently, using only audio signals for 3D trajectory prediction, without requiring LiDAR data or external ground truth during deployment. Gaussian Process modeling is integrated to enhance the spatiotemporal smoothness and accuracy of the estimated trajectories.

#### 3.2. A. Unsupervised Teacher Network (LiDAR-Based Trajectory Estimation)

The Teacher Network is responsible for generating accurate 3D UAV trajectory pseudo-labels from LiDAR data. It integrates data from two types of LiDAR sensors: Livox Mid360 and DJI Livox Avia.

- **Livox Mid360 Data Processing:**
 - The Livox Mid360 provides a 360° horizontal and 59° vertical field of view, capturing both background and UAV point clouds up to 40 meters.
 - To isolate UAV point clouds from noise and background, 20 consecutive LiDAR scans are grouped into "chunks."
 - **DBSCAN Clustering:** The DBSCAN algorithm (with `eps = 1.0` and `min-samples = 10`) is applied to cluster points.
 - **Connected Component Analysis:** Adjacent clusters are merged using connected component analysis (with `distance-threshold = 2.5` and `min-component-size = 20`) to form larger, coherent regions.
 - **Noise Removal:** The largest clusters (typically background) and smallest clusters (typically noise) are removed.
 - **UAV Point Cloud Identification:** The density of remaining clusters is analyzed, and the cluster corresponding to the UAV's point cloud is selected.

- **DJI Livox Avia Data Processing:**
 - The DJI Livox Avia LiDAR, with a 70-degree conical upward-facing field of view, tracks UAV movement up to 350 meters.
 - **Farthest Point Sampling:** This technique is applied to the Avia data to reduce the number of points while preserving the spatial distribution of the UAV point cloud.

- **Data Fusion and Pseudo-Label Generation:**
 - The dynamic portion of the Livox Mid360 point cloud is extracted and fused with the downsampled Avia data to form a complete UAV point cloud.
 - **DBSCAN (re-application):** DBSCAN is applied again to remove any remaining solar noise.
 - **B-Spline Fitting:** Finally, B-Spline fitting is used to interpolate a smooth curve through the aggregated 3D UAV point cloud. This smooth curve represents the estimated UAV trajectory, which serves as the pseudo-label for training the Student Network. The mean error of these pseudo-labels compared to actual UAV trajectories is reported as 0.5 meters.

#### 3.3. B. Self-Supervised Audio Feature Learning (Student Network)

The Student Network processes audio array information to estimate UAV trajectories.

- **Audio Data Collection and Preprocessing:**
 - Audio waves are collected from a microphone vector.
 - Signals are segmented into 2-second clips, sampled at 48,000 samples per second.
 - **Mel-Spectrogram Conversion:** Audio segments are converted into mel-spectrograms using a sweep window size of 2048 and a hop length of 1024.
 - **Resizing:** Mel-spectrograms are resized to 64 × 64 pixels for efficient feature extraction.

- **Audio Feature Extraction (Time Difference of Arrival - TDOA):**
 - The core idea is to leverage the Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA) principle among the four microphones to determine UAV location.
 - **Convolutional Kernels:**
 - One type of convolutional kernel sweeps the spectrogram horizontally to extract time difference information, reflecting relative arrival times of the UAV sound at different microphones.
 - Another type of convolutional kernel scans the spectrogram vertically to capture intensity information across different frequencies within the same spectrogram.
 - **Feature Concatenation and Transformation:** The extracted time-domain and frequency-domain features are concatenated to form a comprehensive audio feature. Two fully connected layers are then applied to learn higher-level features and transform the feature dimension to 3, corresponding to the 3D trajectory estimation (X, Y, Z coordinates).

#### 3.4. C. Gaussian Process Smoothing

To enhance the smoothness and continuous nature of the estimated UAV trajectory, Gaussian Processes (GPs) are applied. GPs are capable of modeling complex relationships and uncertainties without requiring fixed knot lengths, unlike B-splines.

- **GP Model:** Given a set of estimated trajectory points `{(t_i, y_i)}`, where `t_i` is the timestamp and `y_i` is the 3D position, GP regression is used to predict a smooth trajectory.
- **Predictive Distribution:** The predictive distribution for a new timestamp `t*` is a Gaussian distribution characterized by a mean `μ*` and variance `Σ*`.
- **RBF Kernel:** The Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel, known for its smoothness properties, is employed: `k(t, t') = σ_f^2 * exp(- ||t - t'||^2 / (2l^2))`.
 - `σ_f^2` controls the vertical variation (amplitude).
 - `l` (length scale parameter) controls the smoothness.
- **Parameter Optimization:** Through experimentation, an optimal length scale `l` of approximately 300, noise variance `σ_n^2` of 2.0, and signal variance `σ_f^2` of 1.0 were determined to provide the best results for UAV trajectory estimation.

#### 3.5. D. Trajectory Estimation Head

The framework directly estimates the 3D trajectory of UAVs.

- **Loss Function:** The training loss for the trajectory estimation head is a composite function designed to leverage both pseudo-labels and, if available, ground truth.
 `Loss = (1 - α)/N * Σ(||P^(i) - ξ^(i)||^2) + α/N * Σ(||P^(i) - Ξ^(i)||^2)`
 - `P` is the predicted trajectory.
 - `ξ` is the ground truth trajectory.
 - `Ξ` is the pseudo-label trajectory generated by the Teacher Network.
 - `α` is a weighting parameter:
 - If `α = 1`, the framework operates in a fully self-supervised mode, relying solely on pseudo-labels.
 - If `α = 0`, the system transitions to a supervised mode, using ground truth data. This flexibility allows the system to adapt to different real-world scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed audio array-based 3D UAV trajectory estimation framework was evaluated through a series of experiments and benchmark comparisons.

#### 4.1. A. Dataset and Implementation Details

- **Dataset:** The model was trained using the Multi-Modal Anti-UAV Dataset (MMAUD), which includes data from five types of drones: Avata, M300, Mavic2, Mavic3, and Pham4. The drones performed flights within a 10 m × 30 m × 25 m region, simulating border crossing scenarios.
- **Training Parameters:** The Adam optimizer was used with a batch size of 32 and a learning rate of 1×e⁻⁴ for 100 epochs. All experiments were conducted on a GeForce GTX 3090 GPU.

#### 4.2. B. Evaluation Metrics

The performance was assessed using the following metrics:
- **Center Distance (Dₓ, Dᵧ, D₂):** Measured as the L1 Loss between the predicted trajectory and the ground truth on the X, Y, and Z coordinates, respectively. Lower values indicate better estimation.
- **Average Position Error (APE, E):** Calculated as the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) to provide an overall measure of trajectory estimation performance. Lower values indicate better performance.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Represented by GLOPs (Giga Floating-Point Operations per Second), implicitly indicated by the inference time in the comparative table.

#### 4.3. C. Trajectory Estimation Performance

- **Visual Alignment:** The estimated trajectories generally showed close alignment with the ground truth trajectories (Figure 3).
- **Error Distribution:** For the Phantom 4 drone, the error between the estimated and ground truth trajectories had a mean of 0.28m and a standard deviation of 0.22m (Figure 4).
- **Dynamic Maneuvers:** While the system performed well in most instances, slight distortions were observed during sharp turns, suggesting minor challenges in highly dynamic maneuvers.

The proposed method was compared against several state-of-the-art approaches, including:

- **Overall Accuracy:** The proposed method demonstrated a significantly lower average position error (E) of 0.48 meters compared to all other methods. This indicates a higher level of precision in trajectory prediction.
- **Ideal Light Conditions:** Under ideal lighting, the proposed method achieved the lowest overall error (0.48m) and exhibited competitive or superior performance across individual dimensions (Dₓ: 0.14, Dᵧ: 0.26, D₂: 0.25) when compared to other methods, including AV-FDTI (E: 0.58).
- **Dark Environment Performance:** Crucially, the performance of the audio-based model was unaffected by lighting conditions, maintaining an average position error of 0.48 meters. This contrasts with visual-based methods (e.g., VisualNet and DarkNet), whose errors increased substantially in dark environments (11.45m and 8.31m, respectively). The proposed method also outperformed other audio-based and audio-visual methods in dark conditions.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The proposed method registered a GLOPs value of 0.0065, which is competitive with other audio-based methods (e.g., AudioNet and VorasNet at 0.005) and substantially lower than visual or audio-visual fusion methods (e.g., ASDNet at 15.30, AV-FDTI at 11.37), indicating efficient inference.

These results indicate that the self-supervised audio-based framework provides a robust and accurate solution for 3D UAV trajectory estimation, particularly in environments where visual information is compromised, and does so with computational efficiency suitable for practical deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for 3D UAV trajectory estimation that addresses several critical needs and limitations within the field of aerial surveillance and security. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced UAV Tracking Capabilities:** The proposed method provides a robust and accurate solution for tracking UAVs in 3D space. Given the growing concerns related to UAV misuse in public safety, privacy, and unauthorized activities, an effective tracking system is a foundational requirement for mitigation.
2. **Overcoming Environmental Limitations:** By relying primarily on audio information, the system demonstrates consistent performance irrespective of lighting conditions. This is a significant advantage over vision-based systems, which often fail or degrade in dark environments, fog, smoke, or other poor visibility scenarios. This enhances the operational versatility and reliability of UAV tracking.
3. **Reduced Cost and Increased Accessibility:** The framework leverages readily available and cost-effective microphone arrays, eliminating the continuous need for expensive LiDAR or RADAR systems during deployment. This makes advanced 3D UAV tracking more accessible for broader applications and large-scale surveillance efforts, which may currently be economically unfeasible with high-cost sensor modalities.
4. **Addressing Data Annotation Challenges with Self-Supervision:** One of the primary obstacles in developing robust machine learning models is the laborious and expensive process of collecting and annotating large datasets. This work introduces a self-supervised learning approach that utilizes LiDAR-generated pseudo-labels, effectively bypassing the need for manual ground truth annotations. This paradigm shift can accelerate research and development in audio-based perception systems.
5. **Establishing a New Performance Benchmark:** The demonstrated performance, characterized by a low average position error (0.48m) in both ideal and challenging (dark) conditions, establishes a new benchmark for 3D UAV trajectory estimation using self-supervised audio-only methods. This can serve as a reference point for future research and development in this domain.
6. **Potential for Practical Applications:** The framework has potential applications in various sectors, including:
 - **Border Security and Airspace Management:** Detecting and tracking unauthorized drones in sensitive or restricted airspaces.
 - **Public Safety and Event Security:** Monitoring for rogue drones during large gatherings or in urban environments.
 - **Critical Infrastructure Protection:** Surveillance around power plants, airports, or other vital facilities.
 - **Environmental Monitoring:** Tracking drones used for various tasks where visual conditions might be challenging.
7. **Contribution to the Research Community:** The commitment to open-sourcing the work indicates a contribution to fostering further research and innovation in multi-modal perception, self-supervised learning, and UAV tracking technologies.

In essence, this research provides a practical, efficient, and resilient solution for 3D UAV trajectory estimation, potentially broadening the applicability of drone detection and tracking systems across diverse operational environments.
