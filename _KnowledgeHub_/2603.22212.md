# Omni-WorldBench: Towards a Comprehensive Interaction-Centric Evaluation for World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Omni-WorldBench introduces a new benchmark and evaluation protocol to assess the interactive response capabilities of video-based world models in 4D settings. The study evaluates 18 models, revealing their strengths in visual fidelity but identifying limitations in causal consistency and robust joint control over camera and objects.

## Problem

- Existing evaluation benchmarks for world models primarily focus on visual fidelity or static 3D consistency, lacking systematic assessment of interactive response capabilities in 4D environments.
- Current methods do not adequately capture how world models generate plausible and consistent responses under diverse interaction conditions, which hinders progress in areas like counterfactual reasoning and planning.
- A comprehensive framework is absent for evaluating how interaction actions drive state transitions across both spatial structure and temporal evolution in video-based world models.

## Method

- Introduces **Omni-WorldBench**, the first benchmark dedicated to ==interaction-centric evaluation== for world models, offering a comprehensive and holistic framework.
- Develops ==Omni-WorldSuite==, a hierarchical prompt suite with 1,068 prompts covering general daily-life and task-oriented scenarios across three levels of interaction complexity.
- Establishes ==Omni-Metrics==, an ==agent-based evaluation protocol== that quantifies generated video quality, camera-object controllability, and crucial interaction effect fidelity, including ==long-horizon consistency==, ==causal faithfulness==, and ==event chronology==.

## Results

- Image-to-Video (IT2V) models like Wan2.2 and Cosmos achieved the highest overall AgenticScores (**75.92%** and **75.42%**, respectively), indicating their stronger potential for interaction-centric generation.
- Models with explicit camera control demonstrated clear advantages in camera-object controllability, but some, like WonderWorld, showed trade-offs, achieving high long-horizon consistency (**84.96%**) while having low non-target region stability (**24.89%**).
- While most models achieved high scores in conventional video quality metrics (e.g., >**95%** for temporal flickering), substantial variance was observed in dynamic degree and content alignment, highlighting areas for improvement in generating active and precise content.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current world models are generally proficient in basic video quality metrics like temporal smoothness but exhibit clear limitations in more complex interactive capabilities, such as action-conditioned world evolution and maintaining causal interaction consistency.
> - Incorporating richer conditional inputs (e.g., initial frames, explicit camera control) can enhance overall world modeling performance but may present trade-offs in simultaneously upholding interaction fidelity across all scene elements.
> - Evaluating specific aspects like long-horizon temporal consistency, non-target region stability, causal faithfulness, and event chronology is critical for truly assessing a world model's understanding of underlying physical and logical dynamics beyond mere visual generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models, which aim to characterize the temporal evolution of environmental states under specific interaction conditions, form a foundational component for advanced AI capabilities such as counterfactual reasoning, planning, and decision-making. Historically, early world models often relied on multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to represent world states through textual abstractions. However, recent advancements in video generation have propelled a shift towards video-based world models. This paradigm offers more expressive and grounded representations of complex environments, finding applications in diverse domains including autonomous driving (for traffic scene evolution and vehicle agent decision-making), embodied intelligence and robotics (for object-centric dynamics and manipulation control), and game environments (for constructing highly interactive virtual worlds).

Despite rapid progress in world model design and video synthesis, the development of dedicated evaluation benchmarks has been identified as a lagging area. Existing evaluation methods largely depend on conventional video generation metrics, such such as FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) and FVD (Fréchet Video Distance), or adapt general-purpose evaluation benchmarks like VBench. While these metrics effectively assess visual fidelity and text-video alignment, they do not adequately capture the core capability of world models: their ability to generate consistent and plausible responses under varying interaction conditions. Some prior work, such as WorldScore, has addressed world model evaluation by focusing on geometrically consistent 3D scene generation under viewpoint changes, emphasizing spatial coherence. However, this largely restricts the form of interaction to camera motion, neglecting the broader range of interaction types that contemporary world models increasingly emphasize.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the absence of a systematic evaluation framework for the *interactive response capabilities* of world models in 4D settings, which jointly model spatial structure and temporal evolution. The authors contend that the future of world modeling lies in such 4D generation, where the ability to accurately reflect how interaction actions drive state transitions across space and time is a central competency. Omni-WorldBench positions itself as the first benchmark specifically designed to address this deficiency by providing a comprehensive, interaction-centric evaluation framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the critical absence of standardized evaluation protocols for assessing the interactive response capabilities of world models. The authors argue that while current world models, often leveraging video synthesis, have shown progress in visual fidelity and text-video alignment, existing benchmarks are insufficient for evaluating their capacity to generate consistent and plausible responses to diverse interaction conditions. This deficiency hinders the development of world models capable of robust counterfactual reasoning, planning, and decision-making in dynamic environments.

The motivation behind Omni-WorldBench stems from several observations:
- **Limitations of Current Evaluation Paradigms:** Existing benchmarks either focus narrowly on visual fidelity and text-video alignment for generative models, or they rely on static 3D reconstruction metrics that do not account for temporal dynamics. These approaches fail to capture the critical ability of a world model to realistically predict and reflect the temporal evolution of states in response to interactions.
- **Emphasis on 4D Generation and Interactive Response:** The paper posits that the future of world modeling lies in 4D generation, which requires models to jointly represent spatial structure and temporal evolution. In this paradigm, the core capability is "interactive response" – the faithful reflection of how actions drive state transitions across space and time. No existing benchmark systematically evaluates this specific, critical dimension.
- **Need for Comprehensive Assessment:** World models are applied across varied domains (e.g., autonomous driving, embodied robotics, gaming), each with distinct interaction requirements. A comprehensive benchmark is needed to assess performance across these diverse interaction levels and scenario types, moving beyond narrow capability tests.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By systematically evaluating current models and revealing their limitations in interactive response, the benchmark aims to provide actionable insights. These insights are intended to guide and accelerate future research towards developing more interactive and causally consistent 4D world models.

To achieve this overarching goal, the research proposes specific contributions:
1. **Introduction of Omni-WorldBench:** The first benchmark dedicated to assessing the interactive response capabilities of world models, offering a comprehensive and holistic evaluation framework.
2. **Establishment of a Rigorous Evaluation Infrastructure:** This includes Omni-WorldSuite, a hierarchical prompt suite spanning diverse interaction levels and scenario types, and Omni-Metric, an agent-based evaluation protocol that quantitatively measures the impact of actions on both final outcomes and intermediate state transitions.
3. **Comprehensive Evaluation and Analysis:** Conducting an extensive evaluation of 18 representative world models to systematically analyze their performance, uncover critical limitations in 4D interactivity, and identify key areas for improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research is centered around the development and application of Omni-WorldBench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating the interactive response capabilities of world models. Omni-WorldBench comprises two main components: Omni-WorldSuite and Omni-Metrics.

#### 3.1. Omni-WorldSuite: The Prompt Suite

Omni-WorldSuite is a systematic collection of evaluation prompts designed to assess model performance across diverse interaction levels and scenario types.
- **Prompt Dimensions:**
 - **Scene Coverage:** Prompts span both general daily-life scenarios (indoor, outdoor) and task-oriented environments (autonomous driving, embodied AI, gaming). These collectively cover aspects such as physical laws, commonsense reasoning, causality, camera motion, closed-loop dynamics, and spatial constraints.
 - **Interaction Hierarchy (Three Levels):**
 - **Level 1 Interaction:** Actions whose effects are confined to the acting object, without altering other objects or the surrounding environment (e.g., viewing fields through a crystal ball, continuous spatial navigation).
 - **Level 2 Interaction:** Localized interactions where one object directly affects another (e.g., heating a metal rod in a campfire, ego-vehicle navigation alongside dynamic traffic).
 - **Level 3 Interaction:** Complex interactions that influence multiple objects and lead to broader environmental changes (e.g., snapping spaghetti, tidying a room, a robotic arm grasping and handing an object).
- **Prompt Definition:** Each prompt consists of a textual description of interaction-driven world-state evolution and an initial frame image specifying the starting world state. For some prompts, explicit camera trajectories are also provided to constrain viewpoint transition.
- **Prompt Construction Strategies:**
 - **Dataset-grounded Prompt Generation:** This strategy enhances realism and complexity. Camera motion trajectories and first video frames are extracted from open-source datasets (e.g., DriveLM for autonomous driving, InternData-A1 for embodied robotics, Sekai for gaming). Initial captions are generated using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Qwen-VL, followed by manual verification and refinement to ensure consistency.
 - **Concept-driven Prompt Generation:** This strategy focuses on synthesizing specific interaction scenarios. Prototype concepts spanning scene domains, target objects, and actions under different interaction levels are defined. Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT-5.2 are used to generate textual prompts and camera trajectories, which are then cross-checked by other LLMs (Gemini, DeepSeek-R1) and manually refined. High-fidelity initial frames are generated via a multi-stage pipeline using models like FLUX.1-dev, with manual screening for plausibility and quality.
- **Auxiliary Metadata:** For each prompt, metadata is provided, including:
 - Enumeration of entity objects categorized into affected and unaffected sets, with expected coarse motion direction and magnitude for affected entities.
 - A list of key events ordered by their temporal occurrence, extracted from the textual prompt.
 - Expected camera motions (direction, magnitude, and challenging return-to-origin settings) for a subset of prompts.
- **Statistics:** Omni-WorldSuite contains 1,068 evaluation prompts. It exhibits a multi-label distribution across six dimensions: Physics Principles, Commonsense, Causality, Camera Motion, Loop-Closure Consistency, and Spatial Constraints. Level 2 interactions are the most frequent.

#### 3.2. Omni-Metrics: The Evaluation Protocol

Omni-Metrics is a comprehensive evaluation protocol designed to holistically assess the fidelity and consistency of world state representations, moving beyond static visual fidelity to dynamic, controllable, and interaction-aware generation.
- **Structured Information Extraction:** Before metric computation, structured representations are extracted from the generated video:
 - **Entity Trajectories:** GroundingDINO and SAM are used to extract temporally consistent segmentation mask sequences for each entity.
 - **Optical Flow:** RAFT is used to estimate the optical flow field for motion intensity and dynamic variations.
 - **Relative Camera Motion:** Optical flow variations are used to approximate relative camera motion.
- **Evaluation Dimensions:**
 - **Generated Video Quality:** This dimension assesses visual fidelity using established metrics from VBench (imaging quality, temporal flickering, motion smoothness, dynamic degree) and WorldScore (content alignment).
 - **Camera-Object Controllability:** This dimension evaluates the coherence of static elements and object behavior, particularly under camera instructions.
 - **Camera Control:** Measures discrepancies in generated camera trajectories (rotational and translational components) against specified trajectories, adapted from WorldScore.
 - **Object Control:** Reformulated as a Visual Question Answering (VQA) problem, where a multimodal model (VLM) determines the presence of target objects in uniformly sampled frames. This replaces rule-based matching with semantic robustness.
 - **Transitions Detect:** Uses PySceneDetect's ContentDetector to identify scene boundaries/transitions, penalizing videos with more than one detected scene.
 - **Interaction Effect Fidelity:** This is a core contribution, quantifying long-term content consistency, causal logical ordering, and adherence to physical laws.
 - **InterStab-L (Long-horizon Temporal Consistency):** Assesses consistency across user-specified temporal revisit pairs by integrating low-level structural fidelity (SSIM) and high-level semantic consistency (CLIP visual embeddings). Includes a dynamics gating mechanism to penalize static sequences.
 - **InterStab-N (Non-target Region Stability):** Measures the stability of non-target regions by quantifying their motion energy using optical flow magnitudes.
 - **InterCov (Object-level Causal Faithfulness):** A VLM-based semantic verifier checks if interaction-affected entities exhibit semantically consistent responses (e.g., dynamic for affected, stationary for unaffected).
 - **InterOrder (Event Chronology Alignment):** A VLM assesses the occurrence and relative temporal precedence of event pairs, comparing against ground-truth sequences.
- **AgenticScore (Aggregated Metric):** To account for varying evaluation focuses across prompts, an agent-based aggregation framework is introduced. Each evaluation metric is treated as an agent. Interaction effect fidelity (A_I), generated video quality (A_G), and camera-object controllability (A_C) are averaged from their sub-metrics. An MLLM analyzes the evaluation prompt to determine the relative importance of these three dimensions and assigns adaptive weight coefficients (w1, w2, w3). The final AgenticScore is a weighted sum: `AgenticScore = w1*A_I + w2*A_G + w3*A_C`.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Models Evaluated:** 18 representative world models across Text-to-Video (T2V), Image-to-Video (IT2V), and camera-controlled generation paradigms (diffusion-based, autoregressive, hybrid).
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Omni-Metric's 15 sub-metrics grouped into the three main dimensions.
- **Test Sets:** 410 diverse prompts from Omni-WorldSuite for T2V and IT2V models; a dedicated set of 120 prompts with explicit camera trajectories for camera-conditioned models.
- **Implementation Details:** All inference on NVIDIA H20 GPUs, with software environments configured according to official model guidelines to ensure fair comparison. Specific generation parameters (frames, resolution, FPS, inference steps, guidance scales) were meticulously documented for each model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The quantitative evaluation of 18 representative world models on Omni-WorldBench revealed several findings regarding their interactive response capabilities:

- **Overall Performance Distribution:**
 - Image-to-Video (IT2V) models demonstrated the highest performance potential, with Wan2.2 achieving the highest overall AgenticScore (75.92%), closely followed by Cosmos (75.42%).
 - Among pure Text-to-Video (T2V) models, HunyuanVideo performed best (73.96%).
 - For models supporting explicit camera control, HunyuanWorld (74.36%) and WonderWorld (74.02%) led their category. This suggests that incorporating richer conditional inputs (like initial frames) can enhance overall world modeling performance.

- **Interaction Effect Fidelity:**
 - The IT2V group generally exhibited high consistency in handling complex physical and logical interactions, with Wan2.2 scoring highest in this dimension (67.34% average).
 - Models in the "With Camera" group showed notable trade-offs across different interaction sub-metrics. For example, WonderWorld achieved a high InterStab-L (long-horizon consistency) of 84.96% but a significantly lower InterStab-N (non-target region stability) of 24.89%. This indicates a challenge for current models in simultaneously maintaining consistent underlying interaction logic and managing complex camera scheduling, suggesting that explicit camera control might sometimes divert resources from upholding interaction fidelity in other scene elements.

- **Generated Video Quality:**
 - Most evaluated models achieved high scores in conventional visual quality metrics, particularly Temporal Flickering and Motion Smoothness (frequently exceeding 95.00%). This indicates a maturity in generating temporally coherent and fluid video sequences.
 - However, a substantial variance was observed in the Dynamic Degree across models, indicating that the ability to generate dynamic and active content remains a key differentiator. ViewCrafter and WonderWorld achieved perfect 100.00% scores in this metric, while others in the same category varied significantly. Content alignment also showed considerable differences among models. The results imply that while basic temporal smoothness is largely solved, dynamic responsiveness and precise content alignment are still areas of ongoing development.

- **Camera-Object Controllability:**
 - Camera-aware methods demonstrated clear advantages in this dimension. WonderWorld showed a high Camera Control score of 96.12%, outperforming other models in its category. HunyuanWorld achieved the best average controllability score (79.67%) within its group.
 - In the IT2V group, Cosmos (94.90%) and Wan2.2 (94.01%) excelled in Object Control, indicating their proficiency in maintaining and controlling specified objects within the generated scenes.

- **Qualitative Evaluation:**
 - **Non-Camera-Controlled Interaction (e.g., baseball throw):** Wan2.2 demonstrated superior performance, synthesizing a complete, anatomically reasonable throwing motion with maintained structural integrity and scene coherence. In contrast, Matrix-Game2.0 struggled, exhibiting an incomplete action, temporal degradation, and object collapse. These qualitative observations aligned with the quantitative Interaction Effect Fidelity scores.
 - **Camera-Controlled Interaction (e.g., left strafe camera motion):** HunyuanWorld exhibited stable performance throughout the sequence. ViewCrafter, however, introduced a spurious building that degraded visual consistency, resulting in a lower score. This also supported the quantitative Camera-Object Controllability results.

**Summary of Findings:** The evaluation revealed that current models are generally proficient in conventional video quality metrics like temporal flickering and motion smoothness. However, they exhibit clear limitations in more complex capabilities such as action-conditioned world evolution, maintaining causal interaction consistency, and achieving robust joint control over both camera and objects. These findings underscore the necessity of moving beyond passive video quality assessment towards agent-centric interactive generation evaluation for world models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Omni-WorldBench addresses a recognized gap in the evaluation of video-based world models by focusing on their interactive response capabilities in 4D settings. This emphasis represents a significant shift from traditional metrics that primarily assess visual fidelity or text-video alignment, which the authors argue are insufficient for characterizing true world modeling capabilities.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Establishing a Standardized Benchmark:** By introducing Omni-WorldBench as the first benchmark dedicated to interactive response, the paper provides a standardized and holistic framework for evaluating a critical dimension of world models. This can serve as a common ground for researchers to compare models and track progress.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation Infrastructure:** The dual components, Omni-WorldSuite (a hierarchical prompt suite covering diverse interactions, physical principles, and task-oriented scenarios) and Omni-Metrics (an agent-based protocol that quantifies action impact on outcomes, intermediate states, and causal consistency), offer a robust and nuanced assessment tool. This level of detail in evaluation can provide more granular insights than existing, more general benchmarks.
- **Revealing Current Limitations and Guiding Research:** The systematic evaluation of 18 representative world models comprehensively reveals the performance boundaries and critical limitations of current systems in handling 4D interactivity. These findings provide "actionable insights" by highlighting specific areas where current models fall short (e.g., causally grounded interaction dynamics, joint camera-object control despite good visual fidelity), thereby directing future research efforts.
- **Fostering Progress in Interactive 4D World Modeling:** By publicly releasing Omni-WorldBench, the authors aim to accelerate advancements in the field. A shared, comprehensive benchmark can stimulate innovation by providing clear targets for model improvement and facilitating fair comparisons. It creates a testbed for diagnosing current limitations and advancing research on more interactive and causally consistent world models.
- **Advancing the Understanding of World Model Capabilities:** The benchmark's focus on aspects like long-horizon consistency, non-target region stability, causal faithfulness, and event chronology helps to deepen the understanding of what makes a world model effective beyond mere visual generation. It pushes the research community to consider the underlying physical and logical coherence of generated worlds.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the suite not fully capturing the complexity of open-world, long-horizon, and highly dynamic interactive environments, and the future plan to release human-aligned evaluation results for further validation, Omni-WorldBench is positioned as a foundational tool. Its impact is expected to be in moving the field of world modeling towards a more rigorous, interaction-centric evaluation paradigm, ultimately fostering the development of AI systems that can reason and operate effectively in dynamic, interactive environments.
