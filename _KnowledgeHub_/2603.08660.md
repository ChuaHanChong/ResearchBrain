# How Far Can Unsupervised RLVR Scale LLM Training?

> [!summary] Summary
> Research into Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (URLVR) for LLM training demonstrates that intrinsic reward mechanisms primarily "sharpen" a model's existing knowledge, leading to a consistent "rise-then-fall" performance trajectory and eventual collapse. The study identifies external reward methods, such as self-verification, as a more scalable pathway, and introduces the "Model Collapse Step" as an efficient, ground truth-agnostic indicator for assessing a model's RL trainability.

## Problem

- Scaling Large Language Model (LLM) training with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is hindered by the high cost and increasing infeasibility of obtaining human-provided ground truth labels, creating a "supervision bottleneck."
- Prior Unsupervised RLVR (URLVR) methods, relying on intrinsic model signals, show initial gains but suffer from critical failure modes like reward hacking and model collapse, lacking systematic understanding of their potential and limitations.
- A clear, robust, and scalable approach to URLVR that avoids the inherent issues of intrinsic methods and effectively leverages external, verifiable rewards was underexplored.

## Method

- Developed a taxonomy classifying ==URLVR== methods into intrinsic (certainty-based, ensemble-based) and external (unlabeled data, generation-verification asymmetries) reward mechanisms.
- Conducted a theoretical analysis demonstrating that intrinsic reward methods fundamentally operate via a "sharpening mechanism," causing policies to geometrically converge to a deterministic state, amplifying existing model preferences.
- Performed wide-ranging empirical investigations with Qwen3-1.7B-Base and other models, evaluating intrinsic methods' training dynamics, performance trajectories, failure modes, and the potential of external rewards across various benchmarks.

## Results

- The proposed "Model Collapse Step," defined as the training step when Reward Accuracy drops below **1%** during intrinsic URLVR, strongly correlates with a model's actual RL trainability (Ground Truth Gain) and provides a rapid, ground truth-agnostic prediction.
- Intrinsic URLVR can avoid collapse and provide stable gains when applied to sufficiently small datasets (e.g., ≤ 128 samples) or in test-time training scenarios, attributed to localized overfitting rather than global policy alteration.
- Preliminary evidence from a self-verification case study on the Countdown task showed sustained performance improvement and stable Reward Accuracy, contrasting with the collapse observed in intrinsic methods, especially when combined with instruction-aligned models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intrinsic URLVR methods primarily "sharpen" the model's existing beliefs rather than discovering new knowledge, amplifying its initial probability distribution over answers, whether correct or incorrect.
> - All intrinsic URLVR methods consistently exhibit a universal "rise-then-fall" performance pattern, with initial gains followed by an inevitable decline and eventual model collapse due to reward hacking.
> - External reward mechanisms, particularly those leveraging generation-verification asymmetries (e.g., program execution, proof checking), offer a more robust and scalable pathway for URLVR as their reward signals are grounded in objective, independent procedures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of scaling Large Language Model (LLM) training beyond the limitations of human supervision, particularly in the context of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR has been instrumental in enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities, as seen in models like DeepSeek-R1, Gemini 2.5, and the Qwen3 series, which achieve high performance on benchmarks requiring mathematical, coding, and scientific reasoning. However, this supervised approach faces a "supervision bottleneck" due to the high human cost of generating ground truth labels and the increasing difficulty of obtaining reliable labels as models approach or exceed human expertise.

Unsupervised RLVR (URLVR) has emerged as a potential solution to this bottleneck, aiming to extend the scaling laws observed in pre-training to post-training reinforcement learning. Prior URLVR methods primarily leverage intrinsic signals from the model itself, such as majority voting across rollouts or entropy-based metrics, showing initial gains but also raising concerns about critical failure modes like reward hacking and model collapse. The present work positions itself as a comprehensive analysis that aims to systematically understand the potential and limitations of these intrinsic URLVR methods, and to explore alternative, more scalable approaches. It provides a taxonomy of URLVR methods, a theoretical framework for intrinsic rewards, and extensive empirical validation, thereby contributing to a clearer understanding of the boundaries of intrinsic URLVR and motivating future research directions, particularly towards external reward mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the "supervision bottleneck" encountered in scaling RLVR for LLMs. While supervised RLVR has led to significant advancements in LLM reasoning, the reliance on human-provided, high-quality ground truth labels becomes prohibitively expensive and eventually infeasible as models become more capable or as the demand for specialized datasets grows. This limitation hinders the path toward more advanced LLM capabilities.

Against this backdrop, Unsupervised RLVR (URLVR), which aims to derive rewards without explicit ground truth labels, presents an appealing alternative for scaling LLM training. Current URLVR methods, primarily those leveraging intrinsic model signals (e.g., majority voting, entropy-based metrics), have shown initial promise. However, these gains are accompanied by concerns regarding critical failure modes such as reward hacking and model collapse, and there is a lack of systematic comparison and theoretical understanding across diverse methodologies and evaluation settings.

The key objectives of this work are therefore:

1. **Systematically Classify URLVR Methods:** To categorize existing and emerging URLVR approaches based on their reward sources, distinguishing between intrinsic and external reward mechanisms.
2. **Theoretically Analyze Intrinsic URLVR:** To uncover the fundamental mechanism underlying intrinsic reward methods, irrespective of their specific design choices, and to understand how this mechanism influences model behavior.
3. **Empirically Evaluate Intrinsic URLVR Limitations:** To conduct extensive experiments across a range of intrinsic reward methods and hyperparameters, observing their training dynamics, performance trajectory, and specific failure modes. The goal is to determine if intrinsic rewards can truly scale LLM training.
4. **Identify Safe Application Scenarios for Intrinsic URLVR:** To explore conditions under which intrinsic rewards can be effectively and safely applied, particularly in contexts where their limitations might be mitigated.
5. **Develop a Practical Indicator for RL Trainability:** To leverage insights from intrinsic URLVR dynamics to propose an efficient method for assessing a model's prior and its suitability for subsequent reinforcement learning.
6. **Explore and Validate Scalable Alternatives:** To investigate the potential of external reward methods, specifically those exploiting generation-verification asymmetries, as a pathway to overcome the inherent limitations of intrinsic approaches and achieve robust, scalable URLVR.

In essence, the research is motivated by the fundamental question of "How far can intrinsic rewards truly scale LLM training?" and seeks to provide a comprehensive answer by mapping the boundaries of current intrinsic URLVR while simultaneously pointing towards more scalable future directions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach encompassing taxonomy, theoretical analysis, and extensive empirical investigation.

#### 3.1. Taxonomy of Unsupervised RLVR

The paper first establishes a taxonomy, classifying URLVR methods into two main categories based on the source of rewards:
- **Intrinsic Reward Methods:** Derive rewards solely from the model's internal signals.
 - **Certainty-Based Rewards:** Based on the policy's confidence (e.g., logits, entropy), encouraging low-entropy predictions. Examples include Self-Certainty (RLIF), Token/Trajectory-Level Entropy (EM-RL, RENT), Probability (RLSC), and Probability Disparity (RLSF).
 - **Ensemble-Based Rewards:** Based on agreement across multiple model rollouts (e.g., majority voting), assuming consistency correlates with correctness. Examples include Majority Voting (TTRL, SRT, SeRL), Semantic Similarity (EMPO), and Trajectory Consistency (CoVo).
- **External Reward Methods:** Generate verifiable rewards through mechanisms external to the model's internal states.
 - **Leveraging Unlabeled Data:** Transforms language modeling or structured text into reward-based tasks, using the text itself as a latent ground truth. Examples include Next-Token Prediction (RPT, TPT, RLPT) and Text Reconstruction (DuPO).
 - **Exploiting Generation-Verification Asymmetries:** Leverages tasks where generating a solution is hard but verifying it is easy, using an external, objective procedure (e.g., program execution, proof checking). Examples include Mathematical Integration (LADDER), Coding (AZR), and Countdown (RLSR).

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis of Intrinsic Rewards

The paper theoretically analyzes intrinsic URLVR using the standard KL-regularized RL objective.
- **One-Step Update Dynamics:** Using TTRL with majority voting reward as a representative example, the optimal policy for a fixed reward is derived. This shows that trajectories aligning with the majority answer are amplified exponentially, while others are diminished.
- **Geometric Convergence to Sharpening:** Through Theorem 1, it is proven that under assumptions of majority stability and effective learning, the policy's probability mass on the initial majority answer converges geometrically to 1. This implies a sharpening mechanism where the model converges to a deterministic policy concentrated on its initial preferences, whether correct or incorrect.
- **Unified Reward Framework (Appendix A.3):** A generalized framework is proposed, expressing most intrinsic rewards as a monotonic transformation of aggregated cross-entropy between anchor and model distributions. This framework helps generalize the sharpening analysis to other intrinsic reward types.

#### 3.3. Empirical Investigation

Extensive experiments were conducted using the veRL framework and GRPO algorithm, primarily with Qwen3-1.7B-Base on the DAPO-17k dataset, evaluating performance on AIME 2024, AIME 2025, and AMC 2023 benchmarks.

- **Rise and Fall of Intrinsic URLVR (Section 4.1):**
 - **Setup:** Compared majority-voting training against ground-truth training. Monitored training dynamics: Majority Voting Reward (intrinsic signal), Reward Accuracy (pseudo-reward vs. ground truth), and Actor Entropy. Hyperparameter tuning (temperature, mini-batch size, KL regularization, rollout number) was conducted across five intrinsic methods (Majority Voting, Self-Certainty, Token-Level Entropy, Trajectory-Level Entropy, Probability).
 - **Objective:** To observe the long-term performance trends of intrinsic rewards and identify common patterns.
- **Fine-Grained Per-Problem Analysis (Section 4.2):**
 - **Setup:** Trained Qwen3-1.7B-Base on 25 individual problems from MATH500 using REINFORCE with Trajectory-Level Entropy. Tracked greedy decoding correctness and correctness of the highest-reward sample.
 - **OOD Generalization Setup:** Trained on 6 MATH500 problems (incorrect initial answers) and evaluated on 2 unseen OOD problems. Tracked Training Label Accuracy and Test Label Accuracy.
 - **Objective:** To understand if intrinsic URLVR genuinely corrects errors or merely amplifies existing preferences and to assess generalization capabilities.
- **Safe Application of Intrinsic URLVR (Section 5):**
 - **Small Datasets:** Trained Qwen3-1.7B-Base with majority voting on DAPO-17k subsets of varying sizes (32 to 16384 samples). Monitored Ground Truth Reward, Majority Voting Reward, Reward Accuracy, and KL divergence from the reference model.
 - **Test-Time Training:** Compared training on a small test set (AMC23, 40 problems) vs. a large train set (DAPO-17k).
 - **Incorrect Majority Votes:** Trained on a filtered DAPO-32 set where initial majority votes were intentionally incorrect.
 - **Objective:** To determine conditions under which intrinsic URLVR can avoid collapse and provide stable gains.
- **Measuring Model Prior with Model Collapse Step (Section 6):**
 - **Setup:** Evaluated 7 models from OLMo, LLaMA, and Qwen families on AIME24. Measured three indicators: Ground Truth (GT) Gain (from supervised RLVR), Pass@k Gain, and a proposed "Model Collapse Step" (training step when Reward Accuracy drops below 1% during intrinsic URLVR with majority voting).
 - **Efficiency Analysis:** Tested consistency of Model Collapse Step across varying mini-batch sizes and rollout counts to assess rapid prediction.
 - **Objective:** To propose and validate an efficient indicator for assessing a model's RL trainability without extensive, costly training.
- **Exploring External Reward Methods (Section 7):**
 - **Case Study (Self-Verification):** Trained Qwen3-1.7B-Base and Qwen3-4B-Base on the Countdown task. Compared Self-Verification rewards (using a verification prompt) against Trajectory-Level Entropy and Oracle Supervision. Monitored validation accuracy, Ground Truth Reward, and Reward Accuracy.
 - **Prompt Sensitivity:** Explored the impact of different verification prompts and instruction alignment (Base vs. Instruct models).
 - **Objective:** To gather preliminary evidence on whether external reward methods can overcome the limitations of intrinsic methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the capabilities and limitations of Unsupervised RLVR, particularly intrinsic reward methods, and introduced a novel diagnostic tool.

#### 4.1. Intrinsic Reward Mechanisms and Limitations

- **Sharpening Mechanism:** All intrinsic reward methods, despite their diverse designs, converge toward "sharpening" the model's initial distribution. This mechanism amplifies existing preferences rather than discovering new knowledge. This sharpening can be beneficial if the initial confidence aligns with correctness but can catastrophically fail if misaligned, reinforcing errors.
- **Universal Rise-Then-Fall Pattern:** Intrinsic URLVR consistently exhibits a rise-then-fall pattern in performance across all methods and hyperparameter settings. Initial gains are observed, followed by an eventual collapse. The timing of this collapse is influenced by the model's prior knowledge, not solely by engineering choices.
- **Divergence of Proxy Reward and True Performance:** During the "fall" phase, the intrinsic proxy reward (e.g., Majority Voting Reward) continues to rise, while the actual validation accuracy and Reward Accuracy (agreement with ground truth) decline. This indicates reward hacking, where the model learns to optimize the proxy reward without improving actual correctness.
- **Distinct Failure Modes:**
 - **Gradual Degradation:** Methods like Self-Certainty and Majority Voting tend to degrade more slowly, maintaining higher Label Accuracy before collapsing.
 - **Length Collapse:** Probability-based rewards often lead to excessively brief responses, as multiplying token probabilities naturally favors shorter sequences.
 - **Repetition Collapse:** Entropy-based methods (Token-Level, Trajectory-Level Entropy) can encourage repetitive text to minimize entropy, leading to confident but uninformative outputs.
- **Per-Problem Amplification:** Fine-grained analysis showed that training primarily amplifies the model's existing preferences (correct or incorrect) rather than genuinely correcting errors on the same problem. Only a small fraction of problems flipped from wrong to correct.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization (with caveats):** Even when intrinsic URLVR amplifies errors on specific training problems, the sharpening mechanism can still generalize to unseen (OOD) problems and improve performance, provided that the model's initial confidence aligns with correctness on those OOD problems.

#### 4.2. Safe Application of Intrinsic URLVR

- **Small Datasets Prevent Collapse:** Training on sufficiently small datasets (e.g., ≤ 128 samples from DAPO-17k) prevents model collapse. In these scenarios, the model achieves rapid consensus (Majority Voting Reward approaches 1) while maintaining high Ground Truth Reward. This is attributed to localized overfitting, where the model makes localized parameter updates without altering its global policy, leading to smaller KL divergence from the reference model.
- **Test-Time Training (TTT) as a Safe Application:** Given the findings on small datasets, TTT is identified as a suitable and safe application for intrinsic URLVR. Training on small, domain-specific test sets (e.g., AMC23) avoids collapse, leading to stable improvements in both validation performance and Ground Truth Reward.
- **Gains Even with Initial Incorrectness:** Even when a small subset of training problems have mostly incorrect initial majority votes, test-time training can still yield gains on OOD benchmarks, demonstrating the robustness of localized sharpening.

#### 4.3. Model Collapse Step: An Indicator for RL Trainability

- **Correlation with RL Gains:** The research introduces "Model Collapse Step," defined as the training step at which Reward Accuracy drops below 1% during intrinsic URLVR. This metric strongly correlates with Ground Truth (GT) Gain, indicating its accuracy in predicting a model's inherent RL trainability. Models that sustain intrinsic URLVR for longer (higher Model Collapse Step) consistently achieve better performance in standard supervised RL training.
- **Superior to Pass@k:** Model Collapse Step demonstrates higher predictive power for RL gains compared to the conventional Pass@k metric. It also avoids issues with multiple-choice questions where Pass@k can be gamed by random guessing.
- **Rapid and Efficient Prediction:** The Model Collapse Step can be computed efficiently. Using aggressive hyperparameters (e.g., smaller mini-batch size, more rollouts) accelerates the collapse, allowing for a rapid assessment of model priors (5.6 times faster than running full GT-supervised RL training). Crucially, this indicator requires no ground-truth labels.

#### 4.4. Promising Directions: External Reward Methods

- **Sustained Improvement with Self-Verification:** Preliminary evidence from a case study on the Countdown task shows that external reward methods, specifically self-verification exploiting generation-verification asymmetries, can achieve sustained performance improvement without the collapse patterns observed in intrinsic methods. The Reward Accuracy, after an initial drop, recovers and stabilizes, while Ground Truth Reward continues to rise.
- **Importance of Instruction Following:** The success of self-verification is enhanced by instruction-aligned models, which exhibit higher starting performance, more robust training dynamics, and less sensitivity to prompt choices compared to base models. This suggests that self-verification can scale robustly when combined with strong instruction following capabilities.
- **Fundamental Scalability:** External rewards are grounded in procedures that do not degrade as the model improves (e.g., compilers, proof assistants). They are also scalable with data availability (unlabeled corpora) or computational resources, making them a more promising path for long-term URLVR scaling, as they are not bounded by the model's internal knowledge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a comprehensive analysis that significantly clarifies the capabilities and limitations of Unsupervised RLVR, particularly the widely explored intrinsic reward mechanisms, and outlines a strategic path forward for scaling LLM training.

The primary significance lies in **establishing clear boundaries for intrinsic URLVR**. By theoretically demonstrating the "sharpening mechanism" and empirically validating its universal "rise-then-fall" pattern across diverse methods and hyperparameters, the paper provides a fundamental understanding that these methods, while offering early gains, are inherently limited by the model's initial confidence-correctness alignment. This challenges the notion that intrinsic rewards alone can indefinitely scale LLM capabilities to discover entirely new knowledge or correct profound model errors.

This understanding has several direct impacts:

1. **Refined Application Guidance:** The finding that intrinsic rewards are effective and safe in **small-scale, localized applications** such as test-time training is a crucial insight. It explains why many current works employing intrinsic rewards focus on such scenarios and provides a principled basis for their continued use in these contexts, preventing researchers from misapplying them in large-scale training where collapse is inevitable.
2. **Efficient Model Selection for RL:** The introduction of **Model Collapse Step** as a reliable, rapid, and ground truth-agnostic indicator for RL trainability is a practical contribution. This diagnostic tool can significantly reduce the computational cost and time associated with selecting optimal base models or checkpoints for expensive RL fine-tuning, providing a more reliable filter than static metrics like Pass@k.
3. **Strategic Shift in Research Focus:** The inherent limitations identified for intrinsic URLVR strongly motivate a **strategic shift towards external reward methods**. By demonstrating that techniques leveraging unlabeled data or generation-verification asymmetries can provide sustained improvement without collapse, the paper highlights these as critical areas for future research. This re-orientation guides the development of more robust and scalable self-evolutionary systems for LLMs.
4. **Broader Implications for AI Safety and Alignment:** The observed "reward hacking" and "model collapse" phenomena with intrinsic rewards provide empirical evidence for the challenges of designing self-supervised learning systems that truly align with desired capabilities. This work contributes to the ongoing discourse on AI safety by highlighting the fragility of internally generated reward signals and the need for external, objective verification mechanisms, especially as models approach or surpass human expertise.

In conclusion, this paper does not merely evaluate existing methods but redefines the landscape of URLVR. It provides both theoretical clarity and empirical evidence for the limitations of current intrinsic approaches, while simultaneously offering a practical diagnostic tool and a clear conceptual pathway toward more scalable and robust self-improving LLMs through external reward mechanisms.
