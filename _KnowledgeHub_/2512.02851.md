# SwarmDiffusion: End-To-End Traversability-Guided Diffusion for Embodiment-Agnostic Navigation of Heterogeneous Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Skoltech developed SwarmDiffusion, an end-to-end conditional diffusion model that jointly predicts traversability maps and generates physically plausible trajectories directly from a single RGB image. This approach achieves 80-100% navigation success across diverse indoor environments and robot types with a 0.09s inference latency, demonstrating embodiment-agnostic generalization using minimal adaptation data.

## Problem

- Existing robot navigation methods struggle with embodiment dependence, requiring extensive retraining for new robot types or environments.
- Current learning-based approaches often rely on costly expert demonstrations or external planning modules, introducing latency and limiting generalization.
- Vision-Language Model (VLM) based navigation is sensitive to prompts, lacks pixel-accurate outputs, and does not directly generate trajectories.

## Method

- A novel end-to-end conditional diffusion model, SwarmDiffusion, that simultaneously infers a traversability map and generates a feasible trajectory from a single RGB image and robot proprioceptive state.
- A "planner-free" trajectory construction pipeline that generates diverse, supervision-free synthetic training trajectories using Gaussian perturbations and B´ezier curves.
- A dual-component network architecture consisting of a Traversability Student (utilizing a DINO-v2 ViT backbone and FiLM modulation) and a Diffusion Policy (a conditional UNet), both integrating embodiment-specific priors.

## Results

- Achieved 80-100% navigation success rates across indoor environments using both quadruped and aerial robot embodiments.
- Demonstrated real-time performance with an inference latency of 0.09 seconds for trajectory generation.
- Showed robust generalization, adapting to new robot embodiments with only a few hundred additional samples and maintaining an 80% success rate in unseen simulated environments without specific training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stable motion priors for trajectory generation can be learned without relying on expert demonstrations, using a novel synthetic, planner-free trajectory construction pipeline.
> - FiLM modulation with a ViT-adapter effectively imbues the model with embodiment-specific constraints, facilitating generalization across heterogeneous robot platforms.
> - A single, end-to-end diffusion model can jointly infer traversability and generate physically plausible trajectories in real-time, eliminating the need for separate perception and planning modules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reliable autonomous navigation is a cornerstone of modern robotics, particularly for indoor applications ranging from warehouse automation to search and rescue. Historically, this field has relied on classical geometric pipelines that use precise metric maps and hand-tuned parameters, rendering them brittle in unstructured or dynamic environments.

More recently, self-supervised learning methods have emerged, allowing robots to infer traversability directly from visual perception by observing their own interactions. While these methods improve robustness, they suffer from significant embodiment and environment dependence, requiring extensive retraining for each new robot or setting. The rise of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has introduced prompt-driven navigation, which offers better generalization but is highly sensitive to prompt wording, lacks pixel-accuracy, and often requires additional planning steps, struggling with uncommon viewpoints or challenging visual conditions.

Path planning itself has also evolved, moving from classical optimization-based methods (which demand precise maps and high computational cost) to reinforcement learning (RL) approaches. RL provides adaptability but demands extensive training, often struggles with generalization across embodiments, and is sensitive to reward design.

Diffusion models have recently shown promise in motion generation, with applications in quadruped pose generation, goal-conditioned exploration, and cost-aware trajectory planning (e.g., DiPPeST, NoMaD, MPD, VENTURA, NaviDiffusor). However, most existing diffusion-based navigation methods still rely on precomputed costmaps, external traversability estimates, or separate perception-planning modules, and none jointly infer traversability and generate physically plausible trajectories in a single, end-to-end model that generalizes across heterogeneous robot platforms.

"SwarmDiffusion" addresses this critical gap by proposing a unified, end-to-end diffusion framework that jointly predicts a traversability map and generates a feasible trajectory from a single RGB image. Unlike prior work, it aims for "embodiment-agnostic" navigation without relying on expert demonstrations, explicit 3D mapping, human-provided prompts, or computationally expensive external planners. This positions SwarmDiffusion as a significant advancement towards truly generalized and scalable robot autonomy, particularly for heterogeneous robot teams operating in complex, unstructured indoor environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SwarmDiffusion is to enable robots to directly infer both traversable regions and a feasible long-horizon trajectory from a single RGB image. This is motivated by the limitations of existing navigation paradigms:

- **Addressing limitations of VLM-based methods:** Current VLM-based approaches require hand-crafted prompts, generalize poorly across different robot types (embodiments), and output only traversability maps, leaving trajectory generation to slow, external planners. SwarmDiffusion aims for a prompt-free, end-to-end solution.
- **Overcoming embodiment-dependence:** Self-supervised methods require retraining for every new robot, limiting their deployment. SwarmDiffusion seeks to learn stable motion priors that transfer across heterogeneous platforms with minimal adaptation.
- **Eliminating reliance on expert demonstrations/annotations:** Traditional learning-based planners often need vast datasets of expert trajectories, which are costly and time-consuming to create. The paper aims to develop a planner-free trajectory construction pipeline, learning motion priors without demonstrations.
- **Achieving real-time, unified planning:** Separating perception from planning often introduces latency and breaks down in dynamic or ambiguous environments. The goal is a single model that functions as both a global and local planner, providing low-latency inference.
- **Ensuring physical consistency and safety:** The generated trajectories must be physically consistent, collision-free, and adhere to embodiment-specific constraints. The system should avoid local minima and oscillations that plague classical optimization methods.
- **Scalability and generalization:** The solution should generalize reliably to unseen environments and new robot platforms using minimal additional data, offering a scalable approach to unified traversability reasoning and trajectory generation.

In essence, the core motivation is to achieve "scalable, prompt-free, cross-embodiment navigation by learning a shared visual traversability representation that supports both global planning and local collision avoidance" within a single, lightweight diffusion model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SwarmDiffusion proposes a novel, end-to-end conditional diffusion model that jointly predicts traversability and generates feasible trajectories from an RGB image and the robot's proprioceptive state.

**A. Planner-Free Trajectory Construction Pipeline:**
A key innovation is the generation of synthetic, supervision-free training trajectories. Instead of relying on human annotations or external planners, the method samples waypoints using Gaussian perturbations, interpolates them with B´ezier curves for smoothness, and then rasterizes them into a 64x64 heatmap. This process ensures diversity, connectivity, safety, directionality, and path thinness, allowing the model to learn stable motion priors without demonstrations.

**B. Network Architecture:**
The proposed framework comprises two interconnected components:

1. **Traversability Student Model:**
 - **Visual Encoder:** Utilizes a frozen DINO-v2 ViT backbone for robust visual feature extraction.
 - **ViT-adapter:** A trainable ViT-adapter specializes the backbone for embodiment-aware traversability prediction without full fine-tuning.
 - **State Encoder:** The robot's proprioceptive state (position, orientation) `s_t ∈ R^6` is processed by an MLP to generate FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) parameters `(γ_t, β_t)`.
 - **FiLM Modulation:** These parameters modulate the ViT feature map (`F`) via `˜F = F ⊙ (1 + γ_t) + β_t`, injecting embodiment-specific constraints directly into the visual representation.
 - **Decoder:** A lightweight convolutional head decodes the FiLM-adapted tokens into a dense 64x64 traversability map `ˆT_t`.
 - **Traversability Teacher:** Supervision for the student model comes from the AnyTraverse teacher model, which provides platform-specific traversability maps and regions of interest, removing the need for prompt engineering at inference time for the student model.

2. **Diffusion Policy (Trajectory Generator):**
 - **Conditional UNet:** A standard UNet architecture `f_θ` serves as the trajectory generator, progressively denoising a random trajectory sample.
 - **Conditioning Inputs:** At each denoising step, the UNet is conditioned on:
 - **Noisy Trajectory Sample (`x_t`):** The current noisy path.
 - **Spatial Conditioning Maps (`C`):** This is a concatenation of `x_t`, the start-goal tensor `S_g` (a two-channel heatmap encoding start and goal locations), and the predicted traversability map `ˆT_t`. This provides geometric and traversability guidance.
 - **FiLM-based Token Conditioning (`Z_film`):** The same FiLM-modulated embodiment tokens generated by the traversability student are supplied to the UNet’s cross-attention layers, embedding embodiment priors into the attention mechanism.
 - **Noise Prediction:** The UNet predicts the injected noise `ˆε_t` using the standard DDPM objective `L_DDPM = ∥ˆε_t − ε∥^2_2`.
 - **Reverse Diffusion:** Trajectories are generated by iterating a reverse diffusion process from `x_T ∼ N(0, I)` for `N` denoising steps, yielding a clean trajectory heatmap `x_0`.

**C. Learning Objectives:**
The training process combines several loss components:
- `L_noise`: Standard DDPM denoising loss.
- `L_dir`: Directional Forward–Flow Loss penalizes backward flow relative to the start-goal direction.
- `L_trav`: Traversability Expectation Reward encourages trajectories to pass through high-traversability regions.
- `L_mass`: Mass-based regularization enforces geometric plausibility by penalizing probability near obstacles, promoting local path thinness, entropy compactness, and spatial smoothness.
- `L_distil`: Traversability distillation Loss trains the student head to match the teacher's traversability map using an `ℓ_1` regression loss.
The final objective is a weighted sum: `L = L_noise + L_dir + L_trav + L_mass + L_distil`.

**D. Safety Layer:**
For real-world deployment, a lightweight Artificial Potential Field (APF) refinement is incorporated. This layer uses point-cloud distances to perform small, local corrective adjustments to the generated trajectory, ensuring safety without requiring intermediate mapping or re-running diffusion inference.

**E. Dataset Generation:**
The dataset consists of synchronized RGB and depth streams from an Intel RealSense D435i mounted on a Unitree Go1 quadruped and a custom aerial robot. For each RGB frame, synthetic training trajectories are automatically generated (planner-free) with start points near the lower image border and goals in distant high-traversability regions (predicted by the teacher). This yields 4000 images per embodiment, totalling 8000 self-supervised samples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SwarmDiffusion demonstrates significant performance gains and novel capabilities:

- **Unified End-to-End Generative Navigation:** It is the first diffusion-based framework to jointly generate traversability maps and feasible trajectories from a single RGB image without expert demonstrations.
- **Embodiment-Agnostic Planning:** The lightweight state-vector adaptation mechanism allows generalization to new robot embodiments with only a few hundred additional samples (e.g., 500 visual samples for a new robot), keeping network weights frozen.
- **Robustness Beyond Classical Optimization:** The diffusion model implicitly encodes scene geometry and traversability, producing globally coherent trajectories that avoid local minima and oscillations common in classical optimizers.
- **High Navigation Success and Real-time Performance:** Across indoor environments and two embodiments (quadruped and aerial), the method achieves 80–100% navigation success. It boasts a very low inference latency of 0.09s (with 20 denoising steps), making it suitable for real-time control.
- **Reliable Generalization:** The model generalizes reliably to unseen environments in simulation (80% success rate across five challenging obstacle configurations without training on simulation data) and real-world trials, including layouts with newly introduced obstacles.
- **Superior Safety and Clearance vs. Baseline:** A comparison with a supervised baseline (conditioned on traversability and start-goal heatmaps, inspired by) shows that while the baseline achieves lower MSE due to pixel-aligned supervision, SwarmDiffusion consistently achieves superior safety and clearance (e.g., higher minimum traversability, greater free-space margins) despite using synthetic, non-aligned supervision. The baseline also required 100 denoising steps (0.24s latency) compared to SwarmDiffusion's 20 steps (0.09s latency).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Component Contribution:** All architectural components (traversability input, start-goal encoding, FiLM-based visual modulation) contribute positively, with their removal leading to significant degradation in clearance, cost, and safety.
 - **Guidance Strategy:** Classifier-free guidance (CFG) offered no measurable benefit and increased latency, as the model's strong spatially-aligned conditioning signals make it redundant.
 - **Denoising Steps:** 20 denoising steps provided the optimal balance between accuracy, safety, and real-time performance. Fewer steps led to poor safety, while more steps yielded diminishing returns with increased latency.
 - **Noise vs. Sample Prediction:** Predicting the diffusion noise (ϵ-prediction) was empirically superior to predicting the clean sample (x₀-prediction), leading to more stable optimization, faster convergence, and higher-quality trajectories under sparse and ambiguous supervision.
- **Real-World Embodiment-Specific Learning:** Experiments on a Unitree Go1 quadruped showed 100% success rates even with 50% of the training data. With 30% data, the success rate dropped to 67%, indicating robustness even under data limitations. Similar data efficiency was observed for the aerial robot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SwarmDiffusion represents a significant step forward in autonomous navigation for heterogeneous robotic systems, with broad potential impact:

- **Paradigm Shift in Robot Navigation:** By demonstrating prompt-free, planner-free, and annotation-free trajectory generation, SwarmDiffusion challenges conventional wisdom that often requires extensive manual effort for data labeling or explicit planning algorithms. This approach could drastically reduce the development time and cost for deploying robots in new environments or with new embodiments.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy in Unstructured Environments:** The ability to infer traversability and generate feasible paths directly from visual input, without relying on precise maps or 3D reconstruction, makes robots more robust and adaptable to complex, unstructured, and dynamic indoor settings where classical methods struggle.
- **Scalability for Heterogeneous Robot Teams:** The embodiment-agnostic nature and efficient adaptation mechanism are crucial for multi-robot systems where different robot types (e.g., ground robots and aerial drones) may need to operate collaboratively or take over tasks from each other. A shared underlying model simplifies deployment and coordination.
- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** The unified framework for perception and action, integrating traversability reasoning with motion synthesis, lays a scalable foundation for future research. This could extend to more complex tasks such as multi-step and long-horizon planning, richer 3D geometric reasoning (beyond 2D image-space planning), and advanced multi-robot coordination.
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** The synthetic, planner-free trajectory generation pipeline significantly mitigates the reliance on large, meticulously annotated datasets or computationally intensive expert planners, making the approach more accessible and scalable for various applications.
- **Real-time Decision Making:** The low inference latency (0.09s) is critical for real-time robotic control and agile navigation, especially in dynamic environments where rapid responses are necessary for safety and task completion.

Overall, SwarmDiffusion's success in fusing traversability estimation and trajectory generation within a single, efficient, and adaptable diffusion model sets a new benchmark for embodied-agnostic navigation. It promises to democratize advanced robotic capabilities, making complex autonomous behaviors more achievable for a wider range of platforms and applications, and pushing the boundaries of what is possible in generalized robotic intelligence.
