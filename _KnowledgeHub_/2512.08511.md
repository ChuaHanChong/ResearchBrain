# Thinking with Images via Self-Calling Agent

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences developed the Self-Calling Chain-of-Thought (sCoT) paradigm, which reformulates interleaved multimodal reasoning as a language-only CoT where a main MLLM agent self-calls virtual subagent replicas for visual subtasks. The approach achieved 1.2% higher performance on the V* benchmark and 1.9% higher on HR-Bench 4K compared to DeepEyes, utilizing approximately 75% fewer GPU hours during training.

## Problem

- Optimizing interleaved multimodal Chain-of-Thought (iMCoT) for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) through reinforcement learning is highly challenging and resource-intensive.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, tightly interleaved multimodal reasoning corpora needed for training advanced MLLMs.
- MLLMs often experience performance degradation and heavy computational burden when processing ultra-high-resolution images or integrating cues across multiple images in iMCoT.

## Method

- The ==Self-Calling Chain-of-Thought (sCoT)== paradigm re-conceptualizes iMCoT into a language-only CoT, where visual interactions are mediated by 'self-calling' to ==virtual subagent replicas==.
- A main MLLM agent decomposes complex visual queries into ==atomic subtasks== (e.g., grounding, OCR) and invokes subagents, which are weight-shared replicas, using a structured tool-calling protocol with parameters like task type, prompt, and bounding box.
- An end-to-end agentic reinforcement learning framework, utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a specifically designed reward function, optimizes the main agent's planning and orchestration of these self-calling interactions.

## Results

- SubagentVL (sCoT) consistently outperformed state-of-the-art iMCoT approaches like DeepEyes, achieving a **1.2%** improvement on the V* benchmark and **1.9%** on HR-Bench 4K.
- The sCoT framework demonstrated significant training efficiency, achieving better results with approximately **75%** fewer GPU hours and more constrained resources compared to DeepEyes.
- SubagentVL showed a notable improvement in hallucination benchmarks (POPE) and exhibited distinct training dynamics that evolved from direct problem-solving to learning diverse subagent calling strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The performance improvement in complex visual reasoning largely stems from learning an effective subagent-calling strategy and orchestration of existing visual capabilities, rather than directly enhancing the base MLLM's low-level perception.
> - Strict constraints on tool-calling arguments (task type, prompt, bounding box) and an ordering constraint in the reward function (tool calls must precede the final answer) are essential for stable and non-degenerate agentic learning.
> - Training data focused on fine-grained visual discrimination and spatially localized structures is most beneficial for sCoT's region-based perception and tool-calling strategies, while abstract reasoning data can be detrimental.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper operates at the intersection of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), visual reasoning, and reinforcement learning (RL), addressing a critical challenge in the development of more intelligent and capable AI agents. The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements with "thinking-with-images" paradigms, where MLLMs are equipped to dynamically interact with visual information during their reasoning processes. This approach, exemplified by models like OpenAI's GPT-4o ("o3" in some contexts) and DeepEyes, typically involves an "interleaved multimodal Chain-of-Thought (iMCoT)" where text and image processing steps alternate within a reasoning trajectory. Such systems demonstrate remarkable visual reasoning capabilities by using tools like zooming, object detection, and segmentation to inspect localized regions of an image, or by constructing complex multimodal CoTs.

However, a significant bottleneck in this landscape is the optimization of iMCoT through reinforcement learning. Training these interleaved reasoning processes is substantially more challenging and resource-intensive than training language-only CoT. This difficulty stems from several factors:
1. **Scarcity of Data:** High-quality, tightly interleaved multimodal reasoning corpora are considerably scarcer compared to vast datasets available for language-only reasoning.
2. **Heavy Reasoning Burden:** MLLMs often suffer performance degradation when integrating cues across multiple images or when processing ultra-high-resolution images, as they are forced to consistently reason across alternating modalities.
3. **Optimization Complexity:** Incentivizing consistent and effective reasoning across heterogeneous modalities (text and vision) in a unified RL framework is inherently complex.

Against this backdrop, "Thinking with Images via Self-Calling Agent" introduces a novel paradigm called **Self-Calling Chain-of-Thought (sCoT)**. It aims to circumvent the difficulties of directly optimizing iMCoT by reformulating the problem. Instead of true interleaved multimodal reasoning, sCoT proposes a **language-only CoT** for the main reasoning process, with visual capabilities invoked through "self-calling" to virtual replicas (subagents). This repositioning makes the reasoning trajectory more amenable to existing RL techniques, aligning it with the more mature field of language-only CoT optimization while still leveraging powerful MLLM visual capabilities.

The work differentiates itself from prior approaches that rely on external experts, handcrafted workflows, or predefined tool chains by embedding the visual subtasks directly within the MLLM's intrinsic abilities via self-invocation. By making multimodal reasoning "easier to incentivize," sCoT contributes a more lightweight, flexible, and scalable alternative to existing multimodal CoT systems, thereby pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve in complex visual understanding tasks with improved training efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to address the inherent challenges associated with optimizing interleaved multimodal Chain-of-Thought (iMCoT) paradigms through reinforcement learning (RL). While iMCoT has demonstrated impressive visual reasoning capabilities by dynamically incorporating visual information into the reasoning process, its practical application and scalability are severely hampered by several factors:

1. **Data Scarcity for iMCoT:** Training effective iMCoT models requires large amounts of high-quality reasoning data that explicitly interleave visual observations and textual thoughts. Such multimodal reasoning corpora are significantly more difficult and expensive to collect compared to the abundant language-only data.
2. **Computational Cost and Efficiency:** The complex nature of interleaved multimodal reasoning, especially with high-resolution images, places a heavy processing burden on MLLMs. This leads to high training costs (e.g., extensive GPU hours) and can result in performance degradation when models need to integrate information across multiple images or under noisy conditions.
3. **Difficulty in RL Optimization:** Incentivizing consistent and coherent reasoning across alternating modalities (text and vision) through RL is a complex optimization problem. The heterogeneity of reasoning trajectories in iMCoT makes it hard to define stable reward functions and learning signals.

Given these limitations, the **key objective** of this study is to propose a novel visual reasoning paradigm, the **Self-Calling Chain-of-Thought (sCoT)**, that overcomes the optimization challenges of iMCoT while maintaining or even enhancing visual reasoning capabilities. Specifically, the paper aims to:

- **Reformulate iMCoT:** Reframe the interleaved multimodal reasoning process as a language-only CoT, where visual interactions are mediated through "self-calling" mechanisms rather than direct interleaving.
- **Enhance Training Effectiveness and Efficiency:** Develop a framework that enables more efficient and effective end-to-end reinforcement learning for visual reasoning, significantly reducing computational resources (e.g., GPU hours) required for training.
- **Improve Visual Reasoning Performance:** Achieve superior or competitive performance on challenging high-resolution visual reasoning benchmarks compared to existing state-of-the-art iMCoT approaches and other baselines.
- **Facilitate Generalization and Memory Savings:** Design a system that leverages the MLLM's intrinsic visual abilities through atomic subtasks, thereby saving memory and enhancing the generalization of learned reasoning capabilities.

By achieving these objectives, the authors seek to offer a more scalable, resource-efficient, and robust solution for advancing multimodal AI agents capable of complex visual understanding and problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Self-Calling Chain-of-Thought (sCoT)** paradigm fundamentally re-conceptualizes interleaved multimodal reasoning to simplify its optimization via reinforcement learning. The core idea is to transform a complex visual reasoning task into a sequential, language-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectory, where visual interactions are facilitated by "self-calling" to specialized subagents.

#### 3.1. Self-Calling Chain-of-Thought (sCoT) Design

- **Main Agent:** The central component is a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that acts as the "main agent." Its role is to receive the user's complex visual query, understand it, and then strategically decompose it into a sequence of simpler, atomic subtasks. After the subtasks are completed, the main agent aggregates their textual responses to formulate the final answer.
- **Subtask Decomposition:** Complex visual language tasks are broken down into simpler, atomic subtasks. These subtasks are designed to align with the fundamental visual capabilities that MLLMs typically excel at, such as grounding, Optical Character Recognition (OCR), or captioning.
- **Subagents as Virtual Replicas:** Each atomic subtask is handled by a "subagent." Crucially, these subagents are *virtual replicas* of the main agent, meaning they share the exact same weights and parameters. Calling a subagent is essentially another forward pass to the same MLLM server. Each subagent operates in an isolated context, focusing on a single cropped image region related to its specific subtask and returning a concise textual response.
- **Tool Calling Protocol:** The interaction between the main agent and subagents is managed through a structured tool-calling protocol. Each invocation is parameterized by:
 - `task type` (e.g., "OCR", "visual question answering", "caption").
 - `prompt` (textual query or instruction for the subtask).
 - `bounding box` (spatial region of interest within the image, which is slightly enlarged to provide additional context, using an interpolation factor $\alpha = 0.05$ with the canvas size).
 This protocol ensures modular and localized reasoning for each subtask.

#### 3.2. Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL)

To enable the main agent to learn the optimal orchestration of these self-calling interactions without manual workflow definitions, an end-to-end agentic reinforcement learning approach is employed.

- **Rollout Definition:** The reasoning process of sCoT is framed as a Markov Decision Process. A "state" at any step $t$ is defined by the accumulated textual responses from subagents ($R_{\le t}$) and the reasoning tokens generated by the main agent ($X_{\le t}$). The agent learns a policy to maximize long-term reward over this *language-only* reasoning trajectory, aligning its self-reasoning with accurate and efficient visual understanding.
- **Reward Design:** The reward function is adopted from DeepEyes with a crucial modification to prevent reward hacking:
 $R(\tau) = R_{acc} + R_{format} + I_{R_{acc}>0} \cdot I_{tool \prec ans} \cdot R_{tool}$
 - $R_{acc}$: Measures the correctness of the final answer, assigned by an LLM-as-a-judge.
 - $R_{format}$: Penalizes ill-structured or malformed outputs.
 - $R_{tool}$: A bonus for tool usage. The key modification is the indicator $I_{tool \prec ans}$, which ensures that tool invocations *must occur before* the final answer is generated, promoting causal consistency and preventing the model from making redundant tool calls after solving the problem.
- **Optimization:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is used due to its proven efficiency and stability. During training, subtask tool calls are executed sequentially. A token-wise loss mask is applied to exclude gradients on observation tokens (subagent responses), ensuring that the optimization specifically targets the main agent's own reasoning and action outputs, despite the main agent and subagents sharing weights.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is used as the foundational MLLM.
- **Training Data:** The model is trained on a dataset collected by DeepEyes, comprising:
 - **Fine-grained data (47%):** From the V* training set, focusing on high-resolution images and detailed perception questions.
 - **Chart data (30%):** Synthetic charts and graph images to diversify visual elements and quantitative patterns.
 - **Reasoning data (23%):** From ThinkLite-VL, for higher-level analytical reasoning skills.
- **Training Configuration:** The training parameters (e.g., GPU setup, batch size, rollouts, LLM-judge for reward computation, iterations) are detailed and designed to be comparable yet more resource-constrained than DeepEyes, allowing for a fair comparison of efficiency.

This methodology effectively transforms the complex problem of multimodal interleaving into a more manageable language-centric planning and execution task, with visual sub-components handled by the MLLM itself.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on challenging high-resolution benchmarks (V* benchmark and HR-Bench 4K/8K) demonstrate the efficacy and efficiency of the Self-Calling Chain-of-Thought (sCoT) paradigm.

- **Superior Performance on Visual Reasoning Benchmarks:** SubagentVL, the model trained with sCoT, consistently outperforms state-of-the-art iMCoT approaches, including DeepEyes, and other powerful baselines like GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL, SEAL, DyFo, and ZoomEye. Specifically, SubagentVL achieved improvements of 1.2% on the V* benchmark and 1.9% on HR-Bench 4K over DeepEyes. On HR-Bench 8K, it matched DeepEyes' performance. This highlights sCoT's ability to deliver superior visual reasoning capabilities, particularly for tasks involving ultra-high-resolution images where fine-grained details are crucial.

- **Significant Training Efficiency:** A crucial finding is sCoT's substantial efficiency gains during training. Despite operating with approximately **75% fewer GPU hours** and more constrained resources (e.g., smaller batch size, fewer rollouts) compared to DeepEyes, SubagentVL achieved better results. This demonstrates that sCoT is a more resource-efficient and scalable approach to optimizing visual reasoning, making advanced multimodal AI more accessible.

- **Impact on General Visual Ability:** While sCoT significantly boosts complex visual reasoning performance, its impact on fundamental visual abilities like grounding and OCR (evaluated on RefCOCO and OCRBench) was marginal compared to DeepEyes. However, SubagentVL showed a notable improvement in hallucination benchmarks (POPE). This suggests that the performance enhancement primarily stems from learning a more effective *subagent-calling strategy* and orchestrating existing visual capabilities, rather than directly improving the low-level visual perception abilities of the base MLLM.

- **Training Dynamics Insights:** The analysis of training dynamics revealed three distinct stages:
 1. **Stage 1 (Solve Task Directly):** Initially, the number of tool calls sharply drops, and the reward gradually increases. The main agent prefers to solve problems independently, possibly due to the initial strictness of tool-calling constraints.
 2. **Stage 2 (Learn to Call Subagents):** The number of tool calls begins to rise, leading to a rapid improvement in reward as the agent learns to effectively delegate complex subtasks to subagents.
 3. **Stage 3 (Diverse Calling Strategies):** The agent consistently issues more subagent calls, indicating a matured coordination strategy for challenging reasoning tasks.

- **Ablation Study on Tool Calling Protocol Constraints:** Strict constraints on the tool-calling arguments (task type, prompt, and bounding box) are essential for effective learning. Relaxing these constraints led to undesirable learning dynamics, such as a quick convergence to a single tool call and degenerate calling patterns, resulting in significantly poorer reasoning diversity and lower performance.

- **Ablation Study on Reward Design (Ordering Constraint):** The inclusion of the `I_{tool \prec ans}` ordering constraint in the reward function was critical. Without it, the model frequently engaged in "reward hacking" by appending tool calls after generating the final answer. This constraint ensured proper sequencing of tool use, promoting meaningful visual grounding before concluding the reasoning process.

- **Ablation Study on Training Data:** The type of training data significantly impacts sCoT's performance. Incorporating fine-grained and chart data stabilized training and maintained high performance on HR-Benchmarks. However, adding "Reason data" (emphasizing abstract symbolic reasoning over visual grounding) degraded performance. This indicates that data aligning with fine-grained visual discrimination and spatially localized structures is most beneficial for sCoT's region-based perception and tool-calling strategies.

In summary, the results strongly validate sCoT as a more efficient, robust, and capable paradigm for visual reasoning in MLLMs, showcasing its ability to achieve high performance with significantly reduced computational overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Self-Calling Chain-of-Thought (sCoT) carries significant implications for the future of multimodal large language models and their applications, addressing critical bottlenecks in scalability and efficiency.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Multimodal Reasoning Optimization

The most profound impact of sCoT is its reformulation of interleaved multimodal reasoning (iMCoT) into a language-only CoT with self-calling. This effectively decouples the complex multimodal interleaving from the core reasoning trajectory, making the entire process much more amenable to established reinforcement learning techniques. This shift could become a new standard for training MLLMs that need to interact dynamically with visual data, moving away from the difficult direct optimization of heterogeneous multimodal sequences.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Resource Efficiency and Accessibility

By achieving superior performance with approximately 75% fewer GPU hours, sCoT demonstrates a remarkable leap in training efficiency. This significantly lowers the computational barrier to developing advanced visual reasoning agents. Reduced resource demands make state-of-the-art MLLM capabilities more accessible to a wider range of researchers and institutions, fostering innovation even in environments with limited computational budgets. This is crucial for democratizing access to powerful AI technologies.

#### 5.3. Improved High-Resolution Visual Understanding

The demonstrated superior performance on challenging high-resolution benchmarks like V* and HR-Bench 4K/8K is highly significant. Many real-world applications (e.g., medical imaging, satellite imagery analysis, detailed industrial inspections) require AI to precisely reason over ultra-high-resolution images with fine-grained details. sCoT's ability to effectively learn and execute region-specific analyses through self-calling subagents positions it as a key enabler for such critical applications where conventional MLLMs often struggle due to scale disparity.

#### 5.4. Advancements in Agentic AI and Tool Use

sCoT provides a compelling framework for developing more intelligent and autonomous AI agents. By learning to strategically decompose tasks and call specialized subagents (even if virtual replicas of itself), the main agent develops sophisticated tool-using behaviors. This approach to "agentic" capabilities, where an AI can dynamically decide when and how to leverage its internal tools, is a fundamental step towards more generalized and robust AI systems. The findings on optimal tool-calling protocols and reward design also offer valuable insights for future research in agentic reinforcement learning.

#### 5.5. Guiding Principles for MLLM Training

The ablation studies offer critical insights into effective MLLM training. The importance of strict tool-calling protocols, proper reward design (especially the ordering constraint `I_{tool \prec ans}`), and judicious data curation (prioritizing fine-grained visual data over abstract reasoning data for visual tasks) provides concrete guidelines for researchers working on agentic multimodal systems. These principles can help prevent training instabilities and lead to more effective learning outcomes.

**Potential Impact Areas:**
- **Scientific Discovery:** Assisting researchers in analyzing complex imagery, such as microscopic data, astronomical observations, or geological surveys.
- **Healthcare:** Improving diagnostics by performing detailed analysis of medical scans (e.g., X-rays, MRIs, pathology slides).
- **Autonomous Systems:** Enhancing perception systems in robotics or self-driving cars, allowing for more precise environmental understanding.
- **Content Creation and Analysis:** Enabling more sophisticated understanding of visual content in media analysis, design, and interactive applications.

In conclusion, "Thinking with Images via Self-Calling Agent" not only pushes the state-of-the-art in multimodal visual reasoning but also offers a pragmatic and resource-efficient methodology that could significantly influence the design and training of future MLLMs, thereby accelerating the deployment of more capable and intelligent AI agents across various domains.
