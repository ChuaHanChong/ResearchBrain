# CoFL-S: Spatially Queryable Sector Flow Fields for Local Language-Conditioned Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Tokyo developed CoFL-S, a framework that leverages spatially queryable sector flow fields to enable language-conditioned local navigation for robots using ego-centric RGB-D observations and local instructions. This approach generated continuous local trajectories, achieving an average success rate of 0.37 and SPL of 0.33 on the continuous-time Habitat benchmark, and demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer to real-world environments with a 0.75 success rate and 0.42 collision times.

## Problem

- Prior Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) research focused heavily on high-level instruction reasoning, neglecting the low-level action representation and execution layer ('how to do it').
- Existing low-level action interfaces (discrete actions, waypoints, action chunks) are inflexible, failing to adapt motion across varying workspace locations, obstacle configurations, or different replanning frequencies.
- Standard VLN benchmarks primarily use discrete action spaces, which do not reflect continuous robotic control complexities or allow for comprehensive evaluation across different planner frequencies.

## Method

- **CoFL-S**, a framework that predicts a language-conditioned, spatially queryable ==sector flow field== from ego-centric RGB-D observations and local language instructions to generate continuous local trajectories, using a frozen ==SigLIP2== encoder, a ==FiLM== depth-modulation pathway, and query-based ==Transformer decoders==.
- A method to convert standard VLN-CE episodes into dense, frame-level local supervision, complete with aligned sub-instructions and ==potential-field==-derived target flow fields (==cost-weighted geodesic attraction== plus ==invalid-region repulsion==).
- A ==continuous-time Habitat benchmark== designed to evaluate low-level action interfaces under continuous control across various planner frequencies, allowing for decomposition-independent comparisons.

## Results

- CoFL-S achieved the strongest task-completion performance on the continuous-time Habitat benchmark, with the best Navigation Error, Oracle Success, Success Rate (SR), and SPL across all tested planner frequencies (**2, 5, 10 Hz**) for R2R-CE and RxR-CE datasets (e.g., 5Hz average SR **0.37**, SPL **0.33**).
- The framework demonstrated robust zero-shot real-world transfer, achieving a **0.75** Success Rate and **0.42** Collision Times, significantly outperforming Action Token (SR **0.46**, CT **0.63**) and Action Chunk (SR **0.33**, CT **1.42**) baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that training with instruction-diverse data, using an action module for termination prediction, and integrating depth modulation consistently improved all task-completion metrics and reduced the Blocked-Step Rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatially queryable sector flow fields provide a richer, more robust representation of local motion intent, enabling continuous corrective behavior superior to discrete actions or fixed-horizon action chunks.
> - Dense, workspace-level supervision, derived from existing VLN benchmarks by combining geodesic attraction and obstacle repulsion, significantly enhances the learning of geometry-aware and continuous navigation policies.
> - The integration of geometric cues via depth modulation and a dedicated action module for discrete STOP signals are crucial for improving task completion and collision avoidance in continuous navigation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is an area of embodied AI where an agent is tasked with navigating an environment based on natural language instructions. Prior research in VLN, particularly following the establishment of benchmarks like R2R, RxR, and VLN-CE, has focused on enhancing high-level capabilities such as semantic grounding, memory construction, global map reconstruction, and long-horizon instruction decomposition. These advancements address the "what to do" aspect of navigation.

However, the "how to do it" — the low-level action representation and execution layer that translates high-level intent into physical robot motion — has received comparatively less exploration. Existing low-level action interfaces for VLN typically include discrete actions (e.g., forward, turn-left, turn-right), waypoints, or finite-horizon action chunks. These representations primarily specify an action for the current state or a short sequence of future actions. They generally do not describe how desired motion should adapt across varying nearby workspace locations, obstacle configurations, or different replanning frequencies.

This work draws inspiration from classical navigation and collision avoidance methods, such as potential fields and velocity-space planners, which provide spatially indexed guidance by defining how motion should change across a workspace. It also connects to recent advancements in learned flow-field policies, which represent motion as a flow field over a globally observed Bird's-Eye View (BEV) workspace, offering dense workspace-level supervision. However, these prior flow-field methods often assume third-person BEV observations, a constraint not always applicable to robots operating with first-person ego-centric sensors.

CoFL-S distinguishes itself by specifically addressing the low-level action representation gap in VLN. It proposes a spatially queryable control field that operates on ego-centric RGB-D observations and local language instructions, aiming to provide a more flexible and robust representation of local motion intent compared to traditional discrete or chunk-based action schemes. This approach seeks to isolate and improve the execution layer, complementing ongoing research in high-level instruction reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a low-level vision-language-action framework, named CoFL-S, that can effectively translate local language instructions and ego-centric observations into continuous robot trajectories. The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations and unexplored areas in current VLN research:

- **Underexplored Low-Level Action Representation:** While high-level reasoning in VLN has advanced significantly, the underlying action representation that converts semantic progress into executable motion remains an area with potential for improvement. Current VLN evaluations often couple instruction understanding, progress estimation, and motion grounding, making it difficult to isolate and study the impact of the action interface itself.
- **Limitations of Existing Action Interfaces:** Conventional action interfaces like discrete actions, waypoints, and action chunks define current-state actions or finite-horizon sequences. These representations do not inherently describe how desired motion should vary across adjacent workspace locations, different obstacle arrangements, or varying replanning frequencies. This can lead to less flexible and less robust navigation, particularly in complex or dynamic environments.
- **Need for Spatially Indexed Guidance:** Classical navigation paradigms employ concepts like potential fields to define control as a function of spatial location, allowing for continuous corrective behavior. The authors propose that a similar "spatially queryable control field" could offer a more robust and adaptable low-level interface for language-conditioned navigation, providing dense guidance over the robot's local observable workspace.
- **Lack of Continuous-Control Benchmarks:** Standard VLN benchmarks predominantly use discrete action spaces and fixed transition models (e.g., fixed forward/turn steps), which do not fully reflect the complexities of continuous robotic control or allow for evaluation across different planner frequencies. There is a need for benchmarks that allow decomposition-independent, closed-loop comparison of continuous action interfaces.
- **Enhancing Generalization and Real-World Transfer:** A low-level representation that explicitly models spatial guidance, rather than just single-step actions or fixed trajectories, is hypothesized to improve robustness against minor perturbations and facilitate better zero-shot transfer from simulation to real-world deployment, where sensory input and actuation often deviate from training conditions.

In pursuit of these objectives, the work aims to:
1. Propose CoFL-S, a framework that predicts a language-conditioned flow field over the robot's local visible sector.
2. Develop a method to convert existing VLN-CE episodes, initially designed for discrete actions, into frame-level local supervision with matched sub-instructions, trajectories, and dense flow-field targets.
3. Introduce a continuous-time Habitat benchmark to evaluate low-level action interfaces under continuous control and across various planner frequencies, allowing for a focused comparison independent of high-level instruction decomposition.
4. Demonstrate the effectiveness of CoFL-S in simulation and its potential for zero-shot real-world closed-loop deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoFL-S operates as a low-level planner, generating continuous local trajectories by integrating a predicted language-conditioned flow field. The methodology involves a novel architecture, a dense supervision strategy, and a new evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. CoFL-S Framework

The framework takes ego-centric RGB (I), depth (D) observations, and a low-level language instruction (ℓ) as input, aiming to output a continuous local trajectory τ(t) in the robot-centric ground-plane Cartesian frame.

- **Flow Field Representation:** Instead of directly regressing the trajectory, CoFL-S predicts a learnable flow field, v_ϕ(q | I, D, ℓ), which provides Cartesian velocity vectors at any normalized polar sector coordinate q. This polar sector is aligned with the robot's camera field of view, and velocities are expressed in the ego-centric Cartesian frame.

- **CoFL-S Architecture:**
 - **Vision-Language Encoder:** A frozen SigLIP2 encoder (f_vis, f_txt) processes RGB images and language instructions into visual (Z_v) and text (Z_ℓ) tokens.
 - **Depth Module:** A convolutional network (f_dep) patchifies depth information (D), and a FiLM pathway (f_FiLM) injects these depth features into the visual tokens via bounded residual modulation. This aims to integrate geometric cues without overfitting the semantic RGB representation.
 - **Context Fusion:** The modulated visual tokens and text tokens are fused using Transformer decoder-style blocks (f_fuse), where visual tokens query text tokens to form a language-conditioned context (C).
 - **Sector Decoder:** For field prediction, each sector query q is embedded using Gaussian Fourier features (ψ) and linear projection, forming coordinate tokens. A query-based Transformer decoder (f_dec) refines these tokens over the fused context C to predict a Cartesian velocity v(q | I, D, ℓ), consisting of a positive magnitude and a unit direction.
 - **Action Module (for Termination):** An auxiliary module, also a query-based Transformer decoder, shares the fused context C. It uses learnable "action queries" corresponding to discrete VLN-CE action IDs to predict a softmax probability distribution over these actions. In CoFL-S, this module primarily serves to predict a STOP signal.

- **Learning Objective:** The model is trained by sampling N_s sector queries and supervising the predicted flow fields. The overall loss function L comprises three components:
 - **L_dir:** Enforces angular alignment between predicted and target velocities using cosine similarity.
 - **L_mag:** Matches the velocity magnitudes between predictions and targets. The target magnitude is scaled by the distance-to-go.
 - **L_act:** A cross-entropy loss for the auxiliary action module, supervising the predicted action probability distribution against a one-hot target derived from ground-truth action IDs.

- **Inference:**
 - **Termination:** If the action module predicts STOP with the highest probability, execution terminates.
 - **Motion Generation:** Otherwise, a motion trajectory is generated by numerically integrating the predicted sector flow field. For efficiency, the decoder is queried once on a regular polar grid to cache the flow field, and subsequent rollout steps sample velocities via bilinear interpolation from this grid.
 - **Rollout:** The trajectory is generated by forward Euler integration in Cartesian space over T steps. A bounded inverse-time rescaling (s(j, T)) is applied to the predicted velocities during rollout to convert distance-to-go scaled vectors into fixed-horizon rollout steps.

#### 3.2. Continuous-Control Supervision from VLN-CE Replay

To obtain dense supervision for the flow field, the authors convert standard VLN-CE episodes (which typically provide whole-episode instructions and discrete action sequences) into frame-level local supervision.

- **VLN-CE Replay:** Official R2R-CE and RxR-CE training episodes are replayed in Habitat to record ego-centric RGB-D observations, agent poses, navigation mesh information, and semantic observations at each frame.
- **Fine-Grained Human Anchors:** Existing fine-grained annotations (Fine-Grained R2R and Landmark-RxR) are used to align local sub-instructions with specific frames and route segments.
- **K-Slot Instruction Expansion:** For each replayed frame, multiple instruction-conditioned slots (K) are generated. The first slot is the human-written anchor. Additional slots are procedurally generated by sampling semantically grounded alternatives, including object-grounded (e.g., "toward the sofa"), region-grounded (e.g., "enter the poolroom"), and rare terminal commands. All K slots share the same RGB-D observation but induce different local goals and target behaviors.
- **Procedural Annotation Pipeline (Algorithm 1):** For each frame-instruction slot, a target flow field is generated:
 1. **Ego-BEV Raster & Free Space:** An ego-centric Cartesian BEV raster is constructed, and an "effective free-space mask" (M_eff) is computed by intersecting the walkable navigation mesh and the depth-visible mask.
 2. **Goal Grounding:** The instruction is grounded to a reachable local goal within the M_eff.
 3. **Cost-Weighted Geodesic Attraction:** A traversal cost is defined based on distance to invalid cells. A multi-source Dijkstra search from the grounded goal generates a cost-weighted distance-to-go field (D_w_g) and a predecessor map (pred).
 4. **Invalid-Region Repulsion & Potential Field:** Distance to invalid regions (D_obs) is calculated. A combined potential field (Φ) is formed, attracting the agent towards the goal in free space and repelling it from invalid regions.
 5. **Cartesian BEV Velocity Field:** The negative gradient of the potential field yields a direction field (u). This direction field is scaled by the metric distance-to-go in free space or by an escape magnitude in invalid regions to produce the dense target velocity field (V*).
 6. **Baseline Target Extraction:** For comparative baselines, a target trajectory (τ*) is extracted by backtracking the Dijkstra predecessor map, and a discrete action label (a*) is derived by bucketing the trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CoFL-S was conducted in two primary settings: a continuous-time Habitat-based benchmark and zero-shot real-world closed-loop deployment.

#### 4.1. Continuous-Time Habitat Benchmark

- **Setup:** All methods were evaluated on R2R-CE and RxR-CE val-unseen episodes in a continuous-time Habitat environment. The agent state updated at 60 Hz, RGB-D observations at 30 Hz. All methods received the same ego-centric RGB-D and local sub-instructions, and their outputs (action token, action chunk, or flow field) were converted into velocity commands for a shared low-level controller. Performance was assessed across different planner frequencies (2 Hz, 5 Hz, 10 Hz).
- **Baselines:**
 - **Action Token:** Predicts discrete actions (FORWARD, TURN-LEFT, TURN-RIGHT, STOP), similar to standard VLN-CE, but executed via continuous velocity control. Uses a matched query-based action module.
 - **Action Chunk:** Predicts a finite-horizon sequence of local displacement vectors (a trajectory chunk) using a Transformer-style diffusion policy head. Uses the shared action module for STOP prediction.
- **Metrics:** Standard VLN metrics (Navigation Error (NE), Oracle Success (OS), Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) using reference path length), along with Blocked-Step Rate (BSR) and Heading Smoothness (HS).

- **Main Results (Table 1):**
 - **Task Completion:** CoFL-S consistently achieved the strongest task-completion performance, obtaining the best NE, OS, SR, and SPL across all tested planner frequencies (2 Hz, 5 Hz, 10 Hz) and both R2R-CE and RxR-CE datasets. For example, at 5 Hz, CoFL-S achieved an average SR of 0.37 (R2R: 0.40, RxR: 0.32) and SPL of 0.33 (R2R: 0.33, RxR: 0.30), outperforming Action Token (SR: 0.26, SPL: 0.235) and Action Chunk (SR: 0.235, SPL: 0.21) significantly.
 - **Geometric Awareness & Smoothness:** CoFL-S also showed competitive performance in BSR and HS. While Action Token at 10 Hz achieved the lowest BSR and highest HS, CoFL-S maintained low BSR and high HS while achieving substantially higher SR/SPL. This indicates that the sector-field interface improves task completion without compromising local stability or collision avoidance.
 - **Comparison with Baselines:** The gains were particularly notable over Action Chunk, which had a larger prediction head but showed weaker task completion and geometric awareness (higher BSR). The results suggest that dense, workspace-level supervision of flow fields provides a richer signal, offering a family of corrective motion targets compared to single-state actions or one rollout for action token/chunk training.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Instruction-diverse Data:** Training with the full augmented dataset (including object, region, and terminal instructions) provided moderate but consistent gains in NE, OS, SR, and SPL compared to using only human-annotated anchor instructions.
- **Action and Depth Modulation:**
 - The action module for termination prediction significantly improved task completion (SR, SPL), indicating that a dedicated discrete STOP signal is more effective than a heuristic rollout-stationarity rule, which often led to premature termination.
 - Depth modulation, when combined with the action module, further reduced BSR and improved all task-completion metrics (NE, OS, SR, SPL), suggesting that geometric cues from depth are complementary to the sector-field interface, enhancing geometry-aware execution.

#### 4.3. Real-World Zero-Shot Validation

- **Setup:** CoFL-S was deployed on a physical mobile robot with an Intel RealSense D435i camera and an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, without any real-world fine-tuning. Evaluation involved human-assigned local subtasks across eight sequences in indoor, outdoor, and hybrid indoor-outdoor environments, repeated three times at 5 Hz inference frequency.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR), manually counted Collision Times (CT), Success-conditioned Completion Time (T-S), Success-conditioned Path Length (PL-S), and inference Latency (Lat.).
- **Results (Table 2):** CoFL-S achieved the highest SR (0.75) and the lowest CT (0.42), T-S (111.50 s), and PL-S (42.43 m) compared to Action Token (SR: 0.46, CT: 0.63) and Action Chunk (SR: 0.33, CT: 1.42).
- **Real-World Advantage:** CoFL-S demonstrated stable transfer and robust closed-loop navigation in real-world settings. Action Token often suffered from oscillations near obstacles due to latency-amplified feedback mismatch. Action Chunk frequently collided or became stuck, failing to capture free space effectively. CoFL-S, by coupling local instruction semantics with geometric constraints through its spatially queryable field and integrated rollout, proved more robust to onboard latency and environmental complexities. Despite its higher latency compared to Action Token, its multi-step trajectory rollout made it less sensitive to moderate observation-control delays than single-step action prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper introduces CoFL-S, a framework for language-conditioned local navigation that leverages spatially queryable sector flow fields. This work offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of embodied AI, particularly Vision-Language Navigation.

- **Advancement in Low-Level Action Representation:** The core contribution is the proposal of a sector flow-field as a low-level action representation. Unlike discrete actions, waypoints, or action chunks, this representation explicitly models how desired motion should vary across nearby workspace locations. This provides a dense, spatially indexed guidance mechanism that can offer corrective behavior under different offsets and obstacle configurations. The empirical results demonstrate that this approach leads to more robust and higher-performing navigation compared to current-state action or finite-horizon action sequence predictions.
- **Dense Workspace-Level Supervision:** The methodology for converting standard VLN-CE episodes into frame-level local supervision with aligned sub-instructions and dense flow-field targets is a novel contribution. This dense supervision, combining geodesic attraction with obstacle repulsion, provides a richer training signal than sparse action labels, enabling the model to learn a more comprehensive understanding of local motion intent and geometric constraints. This approach effectively provides numerous corrective motion targets per training instance, contributing to the observed performance gains.
- **Standardized Continuous-Time Evaluation Benchmark:** The introduction of a Habitat-based, sub-instruction-aligned continuous-time benchmark is significant. This benchmark isolates the low-level action interface from high-level instruction decomposition, allowing for a decomposition-independent, closed-loop comparison of different planner frequencies and action interfaces using a shared velocity-command controller. This provides a more realistic and granular evaluation environment for continuous control policies in VLN, addressing a gap in existing discrete-action benchmarks.
- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Real-World Transfer:** A key finding is CoFL-S's strong zero-shot transfer capabilities to real-world environments (indoor, outdoor, hybrid settings) without any domain-specific fine-tuning. Its superior performance in terms of success rate, collision avoidance, and path efficiency in real-world deployment, especially when compared to baselines affected by real-world latency and dynamic environments, highlights the robustness of the flow-field representation. This demonstrates progress towards more practical and deployable embodied AI agents.
- **Understanding the Role of Low-Level Interfaces:** The consistent outperformance of CoFL-S over strong baselines, even when matched in terms of encoders and training settings, underscores that the choice of low-level action representation is a critical factor in VLN. This research validates that improving the low-level execution layer is a problem worthy of dedicated study, distinct from and complementary to advancements in high-level reasoning.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Robust and Flexible Embodied Agents:** The improved low-level control offered by CoFL-S can lead to more robust navigation for robots in complex and cluttered environments, handling minor perturbations and unexpected obstacles more gracefully.
- **Foundation for Modular VLN Architectures:** CoFL-S's focus on low-level execution makes it a suitable component for modular VLN systems. It could be integrated with advanced high-level task decomposers, global planners, and memory systems, allowing each component to specialize and perform more effectively.
- **Advancing Real-World Robot Deployment:** The demonstrated zero-shot transfer capability is crucial for reducing the sim-to-real gap, potentially accelerating the deployment of vision-language guided robots in diverse real-world applications without extensive real-world data collection or fine-tuning.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for future research into 3D spatial fields for full 3D collision avoidance and exploration, as well as developing adaptive scaling functions for flow-field integration. It also encourages further investigation into how to best couple high-level reasoning with continuous, geometrically aware low-level control.
