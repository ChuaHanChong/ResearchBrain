# What Makes a Reward Model a Good Teacher? An Optimization Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Princeton University analytically and empirically investigated reward model properties for efficient large language model (LLM) alignment through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). They demonstrated that while accuracy is important, high reward variance – the model's ability to differentiate between probable outputs – is crucial for rapid policy optimization, and a reward model's effectiveness is contingent on the specific language model it guides.

## Problem

- Empirical observations indicated that reward models (RMs) with higher ranking accuracy do not consistently lead to improved downstream language model performance after RLHF.
- The prevailing method for evaluating reward models focuses primarily on accuracy, which is insufficient to capture how well an RM facilitates efficient policy learning within an optimization context.
- A gap existed in understanding what characteristics, beyond mere ranking accuracy, contribute to an effective reward model in an optimization-driven RLHF pipeline.

## Method

- Developed a theoretical framework grounded in an optimization perspective, analyzing the time required for reward maximization during policy gradient optimization, treating it as gradient flow.
- Empirically validated theoretical findings using large language models (Pythia, Llama-3.2) by training and evaluating custom reward models with varying on-policy data mixes, and public RMs, on a simulated ground truth reward.
- Defined and analyzed key properties of reward models, including accuracy and ==reward variance==, and investigated their impact on the rate of policy learning and reward maximization.

## Results

- Theoretically proved that low reward variance implies an extremely slow increase in both proxy and ground truth rewards during policy gradient training (Theorem 1).
- Demonstrated theoretically that a perfectly accurate reward model can lead to arbitrarily slower ground truth reward maximization compared to a substantially less accurate but higher-variance model (Theorem 2).
- Empirical experiments showed a strong positive correlation between reward variance and the rate of increase in both proxy and ground truth rewards across various LLMs and RMs, and confirmed that the 'best' RM varies by initial policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low reward variance within the reward model, even if perfectly accurate, leads to vanishing gradients and a flat objective landscape, causing arbitrarily slow policy optimization.
> - Reward model effectiveness is not universal but is highly dependent on the specific language model (initial policy) it is guiding, challenging the concept of universal RM benchmarks.
> - While accuracy is important for mitigating issues like reward hacking, it is insufficient for efficient RLHF optimization; sufficient reward variance is a necessary, complementary property.
