# AgentMath: Empowering Mathematical Reasoning for Large Language Models via Tool-Augmented Agent

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentMath, developed by researchers from Tsinghua University and Tencent Hunyuan, introduces a framework to enhance large language models' mathematical reasoning by integrating LLM reasoning with code interpreters. The system employs a two-stage training approach, combining automated tool-augmented data synthesis with an agentic reinforcement learning paradigm, which enabled it to achieve state-of-the-art performance on challenging mathematical competition benchmarks like AIME and HMMT.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with precise computational or symbolic mathematical tasks, leading to frequent errors and inefficiencies when relying solely on text-based reasoning.
- Existing tool-augmented approaches face limitations including scarcity of high-quality tool-use data, unexplored potential of agentic reinforcement learning for strategy optimization, and inefficient training for ultra-long reasoning sequences with numerous tool calls.

## Method

- A two-stage training architecture begins with supervised fine-tuning (==SFT==) using automatically generated, high-quality tool-augmented data, synthesized from pure-text Chain-of-Thought (CoT) solutions.
- An agentic reinforcement learning (RL) paradigm, utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and dynamic generation-execution cycles, optimizes tool-use strategies and self-correction through multi-round interactive feedback.
- A scalable RL infrastructure, featuring decoupled asynchronous rollout scheduling, agentic partial rollout, and prefix-aware weighted load balancing, accelerates training for complex, long-sequence mathematical problems.

## Results

- AgentMath achieved state-of-the-art performance on challenging mathematical competition benchmarks including AIME24, AIME25, and HMMT25 across various model scales, outperforming many frontier open-source and proprietary models.
- The **30B**-A3B version, with **3B** active parameters in a Mixture-of-Experts architecture, rivaled the performance of much larger dense models and proprietary systems like OpenAI-o3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro.
- RL training improved accuracy by 7-**10%** over the SFT baseline and demonstrated a **4.0x** efficiency gain compared to text-based baselines, while the scalable RL infrastructure achieved a 4-**5x** speedup in training throughput for ultra-long sequences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automated synthesis of tool-augmented data, coupled with multi-faceted refinement and self-correction injection, is crucial for building robust initial tool-use competence and addressing data scarcity in LLMs.
> - Reinforcement learning with dynamic generation-execution cycles allows models to autonomously learn optimal tool-use strategies, adapt code, and recover from execution errors, fostering emergent debugging capabilities.
> - Decoupling model inference from environment interactions and implementing agentic partial rollouts are essential architectural innovations for achieving a 4-5x speedup and making large-scale agentic RL training feasible for computationally intensive, long-sequence tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) have demonstrated progress in natural language reasoning through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT). However, these models encounter limitations when addressing mathematical problems that require precise computation or intricate symbolic manipulation, such as large-number arithmetic, complex equation solving, or geometric reasoning. Pure text-based reasoning in these contexts can lead to frequent computational errors, necessitate redundant corrections, and result in overall inefficiency and inaccuracies.

To mitigate these limitations, recent research has explored the integration of external tools, particularly code interpreters, to delegate complex and error-prone computational steps. Models like OpenAI's o3 and o4-mini have shown improved mathematical reasoning accuracy by invoking such tools. Despite these advancements, existing tool-augmented approaches face several challenges:
1. **Data Scarcity:** There is a lack of high-quality tool-use data. Methods for generating tool-augmented trajectories, such as START, can exhibit delayed code computation and a distrust of code results. Manual annotation, as used in CoRT, is effective but does not scale to large datasets. Furthermore, under supervised learning, models struggle to learn autonomous debugging from code execution failures.
2. **Untapped Reinforcement Learning Potential:** The capacity for continuous performance improvement and tool-use strategy optimization through agentic reinforcement learning (RL) remains largely unexplored in this specific domain.
3. **Training Inefficiency:** Competition-level mathematical problems often involve extensive reasoning chains and numerous tool invocations (e.g., up to 96k tokens and 96 tool calls). Traditional batch-synchronous RL training frameworks prove inadequate for learning at this scale, primarily due to substantial long-tail effects and prolonged rollout times for ultra-long sequences.

AgentMath is positioned as a framework designed to address these three challenges by introducing innovations in data synthesis, agentic reinforcement learning, and an efficient training infrastructure. It aims to bridge the gap between language models' reasoning capabilities and computational precision, thereby advancing the field of mathematical reasoning for large language models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop AgentMath, an agent framework that integrates the reasoning capabilities of large language models with the computational precision of code interpreters to solve complex mathematical problems efficiently and reliably. This overarching goal is motivated by the inherent limitations of pure text-based reasoning in handling intricate mathematical operations and is broken down into three specific objectives:

1. **To develop an automated method for generating high-quality tool-augmented supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data.**
 - **Motivation:** The scarcity of high-quality training data that demonstrates effective tool utilization is a fundamental bottleneck. Existing methods are either labor-intensive (manual annotation) or yield suboptimal data with issues like inefficient tool usage or delayed computation. The goal is to transform raw natural language chain-of-thought (CoT) into structured trajectories that include executable code blocks and authentic execution feedback, thereby alleviating data scarcity and providing a robust foundation for initial model competence.

2. **To design a novel agentic reinforcement learning (RL) paradigm that facilitates autonomous learning of optimal tool-use strategies.**
 - **Motivation:** Supervised learning alone is insufficient for models to adaptively choose tools, refine their code, or recover from execution errors. The objective is to enable models to dynamically interleave natural language generation with real-time code execution through multi-round interactive feedback. This paradigm is intended to foster emergent capabilities such as autonomous debugging, code refinement, and strategic error correction, thereby improving continuous performance.

3. **To construct an efficient training system capable of handling ultra-long sequences and massive tool calls during reinforcement learning.**
 - **Motivation:** Training agentic models for complex mathematical reasoning often involves extremely long reasoning chains and frequent interactions with external tools, which can overwhelm traditional batch-synchronous RL training frameworks. The current inefficiencies lead to prohibitively long training times and resource underutilization. The objective is to implement a scalable infrastructure that incorporates advanced techniques to significantly speed up RL training, making large-scale agent learning feasible in computationally intensive scenarios.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to pave the way for building more scalable and capable mathematical reasoning agents that combine the strengths of natural language understanding with computational exactitude.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AgentMath employs a two-stage training architecture: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to establish foundational tool-use competence, followed by large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize tool-use strategies and self-correction. The problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where an LLM-based policy interacts with a sandboxed execution environment. A structured markup protocol (`<think>`, `<code>`, `<interpreter>`) governs communication between the agent and environment, enabling dynamic generation-execution cycles.

#### 3.1. Tool-Driven Data Synthesis (SFT Data Generation)

A three-stage automated pipeline generates high-quality tool-augmented trajectories from pure-text Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data.

**Stage 1: Agentic Trajectory Generation via Code Injection**
The process begins with a large corpus (`D_text`) of mathematical reasoning solutions (e.g., AM-Thinking, Open-Thoughts) distilled from powerful models like DeepSeek-R1-0528. This corpus is filtered using n-gram overlap against benchmark datasets to prevent contamination. A teacher model (DeepSeek-V3), guided by specific prompts, systematically partitions each extensive CoT sequence (`τ_text`) into segments. A function `F_inject` then replaces computationally intensive reasoning steps (`s_calc`) within these segments with executable code blocks (`c`) and teacher-simulated execution results (`o_sim`). This injection focuses on complex operations (e.g., exponential computations, matrix manipulations, equation solving), while elementary calculations remain in textual form to preserve the model's understanding of tool invocation rationale. Code blocks are delineated by `<code>` and `</code>` tags, with execution results enclosed in `<interpreter>` and `</interpreter>` tags.

**Stage 2: Multi-Faceted Quality Refinement**
Automatically synthesized trajectories often contain formatting issues, code defects, or logical inconsistencies. Four procedures are applied:
- **Format consistency correction:** Regular expressions and, for complex cases, teacher model regeneration enforce adherence to the structural markup.
- **Code executability verification:** Each embedded code snippet is executed in a controlled sandbox. Failed executions trigger a bounded resampling loop for executable alternatives; if unsuccessful, the block reverts to its original textual form.
- **Environmental feedback alignment:** Teacher-simulated outputs (`o_sim`) are replaced with ground-truth execution results (`o_real = E(c)`). A verifier model (Qwen3-32B) assesses contextual consistency, and incoherent samples are either removed or downgraded to text.
- **Tool-usage rationality assessment:** Heuristic constraints on code complexity (e.g., line count, Abstract Syntax Tree depth) filter out unnecessary code invocations.

**Stage 3: Self-Correction Capability Injection**
To enhance robustness, trajectories that initially failed during refinement due to execution errors are sampled. For each failed program (`c_fail`) and its error output (`o_error`), the teacher model generates a structured self-correction trace (diagnose error → repair code → re-execute → continue reasoning). A small fraction of these "negative-to-positive" corrections is injected into the final SFT instruction set (`D_SFT`) to cultivate debugging capabilities.

#### 3.2. Agentic Reinforcement Learning

This stage builds upon the SFT baseline to achieve continuous performance improvement and optimize tool-use strategies.

**Agent-Specific Reinforcement Learning**
The core optimization algorithm is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which simplifies training by not requiring critic models and improving efficiency via group-wise trajectory sampling and reward normalization. The framework incorporates techniques from DAPO, such as dynamic sampling, asymmetric gradient clipping, token-level loss computation, and KL divergence removal. Key innovations for code-integrated agents include:
- **Agentic trajectories with interleaved code execution:** Rollouts follow a generate–pause–execute–resume loop, constructing hybrid traces. Tool invocations are capped (`T`) per instance.
- **Loss Masking for Policy Gradient Updates:** Advantage signals are applied exclusively to tokens within `<think>` and `<code>` segments, masking `<interpreter>` tokens to focus learning on the agent's actions.
- **Adaptive Batch Construction:** Batches are filtered to exclude problems where all trajectories are uniformly correct or incorrect. Backfilling maintains consistent batch sizes by randomly sampling additional filtered instances.

**Reward Design**
The reward function integrates answer correctness and tool-usage efficiency:
- `R_acc`: Binary (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) based on mathematical equivalence verification.
- `R_tool`: Awarded only if `R_acc = 1`, calculated as `min(R_max, α + β * N_code)`, where `α` is a base reward, `β` scales with invocation count (`N_code`), and `R_max` is a cap.
- `R_total = R_acc + I(R_acc = 1) * R_tool`. (Defaults: `α=0.1`, `β=0.01`, `R_max=1`).

#### 3.3. Scalable Agentic RL Infrastructure

To overcome challenges posed by ultra-long sequences (avg. 24k tokens) and frequent tool invocations (avg. 27 calls) during RL training, a high-performance, scalable system is designed.

**Decoupled and Asynchronous System Architecture**
Model inference (GPU-intensive) is decoupled from agent logic and environment interactions (CPU/IO-intensive).
- **Distributed code execution sandbox cluster:** A cluster of isolated worker pods handles concurrent tool invocations, offloading CPU work and reducing tool-call latency (from 175s to 1.2s).
- **Request-Level Asynchronous Rollout Scheduling:** Static batch-synchronous processing is replaced by a coroutine-driven scheduler. Each rollout is an independent request, allowing the inference engine to process other requests when one suspends for tool invocation, maximizing GPU parallelism.

**Agentic Partial Rollout**
This mechanism decomposes long trajectories into budget-limited segments, each constrained by a maximum generation length (`L_seg`) and tool invocations (`T_seg`). Segment generation terminates upon an End-of-Sequence token, reaching segment limits, or cumulative global limits. This prevents individual trajectories from monopolizing resources and smooths computational load, yielding a 2.2–2.5x speedup.

**Prefix-Aware Weighted Load Balancing**
To manage the increased KV-cache memory and prefill costs associated with long prefixes from partial rollouts, requests are assigned dynamic weights based on prefix length. These weighted requests are then routed to the least-loaded inference engine. Sticky sessions via LRU caching ensure consecutive segments of the same trajectory are routed to the same engine, maximizing KV-cache reuse. This system improves training throughput by 4–5x.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AgentMath underwent extensive evaluation on challenging mathematical competition benchmarks, including AIME24, AIME25, and HMMT25. A key evaluation metric used was `avg@32`, representing the pass@1 accuracy over 32 independent inference runs per test sample. The inference configuration consistently utilized a 96K maximum sequence length, 96 maximum tool calls, a 1024-token code interpreter output limit, 0.6 temperature, and 0.95 top-p.

- **Overall Performance (Table 1 and 4):**
 - AgentMath consistently outperformed existing tool-augmented and text-only frontier reasoning models across all three benchmarks at comparable parameter scales.
 - **Small Scale (1B–2B):** AgentMath-1.7B achieved 59.6%, 48.1%, and 40.2% accuracy on AIME24, AIME25, and HMMT25, respectively. This surpassed tool-augmented models like CoRT-1.5B (43.1%, 30.2%, 20.1%) and text-only models such as OpenReasoning-1.5B (55.5%, 45.6%, 31.5%).
 - **Medium Scale (7B–8B):** AgentMath-8B achieved 89.8%, 84.7%, and 71.3%, outperforming tool-augmented CIR-Qwen3-NT8-8B and text-only DS-0528-Qwen3-8B (86.0%, 76.3%, 61.5%).
 - **Large Scale (30B–32B):** AgentMath-30B-A3B attained 90.6%, 86.4%, and 73.8%, exceeding tool-augmented STILL-3-TOOL-32B (81.7%, 64.2%, 45.4%) and text-only Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 (87.7%, 85.0%, 71.4%).
 - Notably, AgentMath-30B-A3B, with 3B active parameters in a 30B total parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture, rivaled the performance of much larger models like DS-671B-0528. It also surpassed OpenAI-o3-mini and Claude-Opus-4.0-Thinking, while remaining competitive with OpenAI-o3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro.
 - **Ultra-Large Scale (>32B):** AgentMath-235B-A22B-SFT (trained solely via SFT due to computational constraints) achieved 93.4%, 90.8%, and 81.7%, surpassing DS-671B-0528 (91.4%, 87.5%, 77.0%) and approaching the performance of OpenAI-o4-mini and Gemini-2.5-Pro.

- **Cognition Analysis (Section 3.5 and Appendix A.7):**
 - **Tool-Augmented vs. Text-Based Data (Table 2, Figure 4, 5):**
 - In the SFT stage, tool-augmented data improved accuracy by 3.4% on AIME24 and 4.1% on AIME25 compared to text-based data (60.5% vs. 57.1% on AIME24).
 - In the RL stage, AgentMath-RL achieved superior results (76.2% on AIME24, 67.5% on AIME25) in approximately 400 steps, demonstrating a 4.0x efficiency improvement over the text-based baseline, which required around 1600 steps for inferior outcomes (68.7%, 57.5%).
 - Inference efficiency was also improved, with tool-augmented models reducing sequence lengths by approximately 4k tokens (~14%) and exhibiting slower length growth due to the precision of code execution.
 - **Multi-Stage RL Training (Figure 3, Table 5):** An adaptive multi-stage RL strategy progressively expanded sequence length (from 48k to 96k tokens) and tool-call budgets (from 48 to 96) as truncation rates exceeded 10%. This led to increased average trajectory lengths (24k to 30k), higher tool invocation frequency (27 to 31 calls), and improved code utilization (70% to 95%). Accuracy on AIME24 rose from 78.4% to 89.8% (+11.4%), and on AIME25 from 72.2% to 84.7% (+12.5%). The model also demonstrated emergent code self-correction capabilities (Appendix A.8.2, Figure 9).
 - **Synthetic Data Refinement and Scaling Law (Table 3, Figure 7):**
 - Progressive multi-dimensional refinement steps (format correction, executability verification, environmental feedback alignment, self-correction injection, SFT with masking) were critical, improving AIME24 accuracy from 35.3% (initial unrefined data) to 60.5%.
 - Scaling the tool-augmented dataset from 2k to 300k samples significantly boosted accuracy, for example, on AIME24 from 27.2% to 78.4%.
 - **Efficiency of RL Training Framework (Table 6, 7):**
 - The combined use of request-level asynchronous rollout scheduling, agentic partial rollout, and prefix-aware weighted load balancing achieved a 4–5x speedup in training throughput, reducing the time per step from 3600–4000s to 750–900s.
 - The partial rollout mechanism proved effective, with N=4 segments offering an optimal balance of speedup (22h for 100 steps vs. 62h for N=1) without compromising model performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AgentMath framework addresses several critical challenges in empowering large language models for complex mathematical reasoning, demonstrating its significance and potential impact across multiple dimensions:

- **Advancement in Mathematical Reasoning Capabilities:** AgentMath achieves state-of-the-art performance on highly challenging mathematical competition benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, HMMT25). This advancement is attributed to its systematic integration of language model reasoning with the precision of code interpreters, surpassing many frontier open-source and even competitive proprietary models of comparable scale. The performance of AgentMath-30B-A3B, a Mixture-of-Experts model with only 3B active parameters, remaining competitive with much larger dense models, highlights a significant step forward in achieving high performance with computational efficiency.

- **Resolution of Data Scarcity for Tool-Augmented Learning:** The proposed automated tool-augmented data synthesis pipeline is a key innovation. By converting natural language Chain-of-Thought into structured, executable trajectories with authentic feedback and incorporating multi-dimensional quality refinement, the framework effectively mitigates the long-standing issue of data scarcity. This pipeline demonstrates strong scalability, with performance consistently improving as the dataset size increases, establishing a robust foundation for high-performance reasoning agents.

- **Innovation in Agentic Reinforcement Learning:** The novel agentic RL paradigm that dynamically interleaves natural language generation with real-time code execution enables models to autonomously learn optimal tool-use strategies. This approach fosters emergent capabilities in code refinement and error correction, allowing models to adapt and improve beyond supervised training. The observed consistent performance gains from RL training (typically 7-10% over SFT) underscore the effectiveness of this learning strategy.

- **Scalable and Efficient Training Infrastructure:** A major bottleneck in agentic RL for long-sequence, tool-intensive tasks is training efficiency. AgentMath introduces a high-performance, scalable training system with request-level asynchronous rollout scheduling, agentic partial rollout, and prefix-aware weighted load balancing. These innovations achieve a 4-5x speedup, making efficient RL training feasible for scenarios involving ultra-long sequences (up to 96k tokens) and numerous tool calls (up to 96). This infrastructure provides a critical enabler for exploring more complex and demanding agentic learning tasks.

- **Broader Impact and Reproducibility:** By providing a clear, transparent, and comprehensive pipeline for tool-augmented data synthesis and large-scale agentic RL training, AgentMath offers a reproducible technical roadmap for the open-source community. This contribution can accelerate research and development in agentic LLMs, particularly for applications requiring high precision and complex problem-solving. Furthermore, the demonstrated generalizability of the data synthesis and cleaning pipeline to other scientific domains, such as GPQA, suggests broader applicability beyond mathematics.

In conclusion, AgentMath represents a substantial contribution to the field of AI, particularly in empowering large language models for complex mathematical reasoning. Its innovations in data generation, learning paradigms, and training infrastructure collectively pave the way for building more capable, efficient, and robust AI agents.
