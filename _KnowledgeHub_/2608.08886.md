# Estimation of Spacecraft Inertia Tensor Using Attitude-Only Data from Torque-Free Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> An attitude-only estimator recovers a spacecraft's normalized inertia tensor from torque-free motion without gyroscopes or known torques. A Karush-Kuhn-Tucker constrained least-squares warm start seeds nonlinear shooting refinement that propagates states with the exact Jacobi-elliptic solution of Euler's equations and a second-order Magnus quaternion map. It cut inertia error roughly tenfold versus an Extended Kalman Filter while running about sixty times faster.

## Problem

- Existing inertia identification relies on gyroscope rate measurements or known control torques, both unavailable for non-cooperative targets and unreliable on low-cost MEMS units.
- Reconstructing angular velocity by numerically differentiating noisy quaternion telemetry amplifies measurement noise and biases least-squares and filter-based inertia estimates, especially off-diagonal terms.
- Kalman-type refinements carry iterative matrix-propagation overhead too heavy for resource-constrained onboard processors, and prior torque-projection formulations still depend on accurate angular-rate data.

## Method

- ==Karush-Kuhn-Tucker constrained least-squares== initialization stacks homogeneous torque-free Euler constraints over all samples, enforcing unit trace through a ==null-space parameterization== to yield a fast linear seed.
- ==Elliptic shooting refinement== minimizes quaternion vector-part residuals, propagating angular velocity by the exact ==Jacobi-elliptic flow map== and attitude by a ==second-order Magnus expansion== with separatrix-proximity fallback.
- ==Joint multi-arc estimation== across three torque-free segments, plus a photorealistic rendezvous pipeline: ==Mitsuba==-rendered JASON-1 imagery, ==YOLO== detection, ==RTMPose== keypoints, and ==EPnP== with ==RANSAC== pose recovery.

## Results

- On a single **500-second** arc over **100** runs, mean absolute error reached **3.56e-7** versus **2.27e-6** for the Kalman filter and **2.65e-5** for initialization alone.
- Runtime fell from **11.10 seconds** for the Kalman refinement to **0.185 seconds**; three joint **100-second** arcs gave **1.12e-6** error while staying about **twenty** times faster.
- With image-derived attitudes over **2,000-second** arcs, median tensor error rose from **2.0e-4** to **6.5e-4**, yet **10-hour** attitude prediction held **6.7 degrees** median error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exploiting analytically integrable dynamics through exact flow maps beats generic numerical filtering on both noise robustness and compute, enabling onboard parameter identification.
> - Observability is excitation-limited: error grows algebraically near stable principal axes but only logarithmically near the unstable intermediate axis, where separatrix dynamics preserve information.
> - Sampling cadence and spin rate trade off; too little rotation starves excitation while too much aliases fixed-rate imagery, favoring intermediate spin regimes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate knowledge of a spacecraft's inertia tensor is a fundamental requirement for various aspects of spacecraft operations, including attitude prediction, maneuver design, control system implementation, propellant consumption estimation, and monitoring structural changes. The existing body of research on inertia identification primarily relies on direct measurements of angular velocity from gyroscopes, information about applied control torques, or both.

Prior methods include batch least-squares estimation, extended linear regression for noisy measurements, and various Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) and Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) formulations. These often leverage data from angular momentum, rotational kinetic energy, or direct differentiation of angular velocity. Specialized approaches have also been developed for axisymmetric bodies, observation-based optimization, and combined sensor data from star trackers, gyroscopes, and control mechanisms. Challenges with EKF-based estimates, such as sensitivity to data quality and potential biases in off-diagonal inertia terms, have led to the exploration of instrumental-variable methods and active experiment design to enhance observability.

A notable challenge in this field arises when dealing with small spacecraft equipped with low-cost gyroscopes or, more critically, in non-cooperative space-domain-awareness scenarios where direct rate telemetry is unavailable. In such cases, only attitude histories, potentially derived from onboard star trackers or external imagery, may be accessible. Recent work has begun to explore direct use of image sequences, for instance, through physics-informed neural networks to learn rotational dynamics and inertia from images, primarily for future image prediction.

This paper addresses the gap of estimating the normalized inertia tensor solely from attitude histories during torque-free motion, explicitly removing the dependency on gyroscope measurements, known control torques, or active excitation maneuvers. This focus on attitude-only data, particularly from passive optical observations, positions the work as a contribution to the challenges of non-cooperative spacecraft characterization and resource-constrained onboard computation, where traditional sensor suites may be absent or unreliable. The method's ability to operate without reconstructing angular velocity via differentiation of noisy quaternion data, relying instead on analytical propagation, differentiates it from many existing approaches that process angular velocity information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an attitude-only framework for estimating a spacecraft’s normalized inertia tensor based solely on attitude histories during torque-free rotational motion. This objective is motivated by several specific challenges and requirements in spacecraft operations and space domain awareness:

- **Elimination of Gyroscope Dependency:** Many existing methods heavily rely on gyroscope measurements for angular velocity information. This dependency is problematic for resource-constrained small spacecraft, which may be equipped with less accurate MEMS gyroscopes, or for non-cooperative spacecraft where gyroscope data is entirely unavailable. The paper aims to remove this requirement, allowing for inertia estimation using more widely available attitude data.
- **Independence from Known Control Torques:** Conventional inertia identification often requires knowledge of applied control torques to infer the spacecraft's dynamic response. This work seeks to enable inertia estimation in torque-free conditions, where control torques are either absent, unknown, or cannot be actively applied (e.g., for inoperative spacecraft).
- **Support for Non-Cooperative Spacecraft and Space Domain Awareness:** A significant motivation is the capability to characterize the rotational state and mass properties of non-cooperative targets, such as defunct satellites or space debris. Accurate inertia estimates are crucial for predicting their long-term tumbling motion, which is essential for rendezvous, docking, recovery, or debris-removal operations. This capability is particularly valuable when only passive optical observations, which yield attitude histories, are feasible.
- **Robustness to Measurement Noise:** Reconstructing angular velocity by differentiating noisy quaternion data can amplify errors. A key objective is to reduce sensitivity to such measurement noise by analytically propagating the underlying rotational dynamics. This is achieved using the exact torque-free rigid-body solution and a quaternion mapping based on the Magnus expansion, which offers a more precise propagation method compared to numerical differentiation or iterative integration.
- **Computational Efficiency for Onboard Implementation:** The proposed estimator's structure, which involves a fast linear initialization followed by a nonlinear shooting refinement, aims to avoid the iterative matrix-propagation overhead characteristic of conventional EKF-based approaches. This design is intended to make the method suitable for efficient onboard computation, especially on systems with limited processing power.
- **Evaluation with Realistic Image-Derived Attitude Errors:** The research also aims to evaluate the framework under realistic, non-Gaussian attitude errors that arise from vision-based pose estimation (e.g., from monocular images). This involves simulating and processing photorealistic imagery to understand the method's performance in practical scenarios where errors are influenced by spacecraft geometry, illumination, and viewing conditions.

In essence, the paper strives to offer a robust, computationally efficient, and sensor-agnostic method for spacecraft inertia tensor estimation, particularly relevant for scenarios involving passive observation of uncooperative or resource-limited space objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named KKT+Elliptic, estimates a spacecraft's normalized inertia tensor from attitude-only data during torque-free rotational motion. It consists of a two-stage process: a fast linear initialization using a Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) formulation, followed by a nonlinear shooting refinement based on the exact analytical solution of Euler's equations and a Magnus-expansion-based quaternion map.

**A. Core Mathematical Framework**

1. **Body-Frame Angular Velocity Propagation via Jacobi Elliptic Functions:**
 - The method leverages the exact, closed-form solutions of Euler's equations for torque-free rigid-body motion. These solutions are expressed using Jacobi elliptic functions, as detailed in Appendix A.
 - The equations (Eq. 1) describe the evolution of body-frame angular velocity ($\mathbf{\Omega}$) and attitude ($g \in SO(3)$), constrained by conservation of kinetic energy ($E$) and angular momentum magnitude squared ($L^2$) (Eq. 2).
 - The analytical solution partitions into cases based on the relationship between $L^2$ and $2T I_2$ (where $I_2$ is the intermediate principal axis of inertia), defining orbits around the major or minor principal axes.
 - This provides an exact angular-velocity flow map, $\mathbf{\Omega}(h) = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{\Omega}_0, \mathbf{I}, h)$, which propagates angular velocity over a time step $h$ without numerical integration, minimizing truncation errors.

2. **Normalization of the Inertia Tensor:**
 - Torque-free dynamics are invariant under a uniform scaling of the inertia tensor ($\mathbf{I} \to \alpha\mathbf{I}$). Therefore, attitude-only observations can only determine the relative mass distribution, not the absolute scale.
 - To remove this scalar ambiguity, the method estimates a normalized inertia tensor by imposing a unit-trace constraint: $\text{tr}(\mathbf{I}) = 1$ (Eq. 5). This provides a symmetric treatment for all coordinate axes.

3. **Approximation of Attitude through Magnus Expansion:**
 - While angular velocity is exactly integrable, a closed-form solution for the attitude quaternion $\mathbf{q}(t)$ is generally not available for asymmetric rigid bodies.
 - The quaternion kinematic equation $\dot{\mathbf{q}}(t) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{\Omega}_K(t) \mathbf{q}(t)$ (Eq. 6), where $\mathbf{\Omega}_K(t)$ is a skew-symmetric matrix (Eq. 7), is solved using the Magnus expansion (Eq. 8).
 - The Magnus expansion (Eq. 9) represents the solution as an infinite series of nested commutators (Eq. 10). The paper truncates this series at the second order ($m=2$).
 - Assuming the angular velocity matrix $\mathbf{\Omega}_K(t)$ varies linearly over the time interval $[0, h]$ (Eq. 11), the first-order (Eq. 12) and second-order (Eq. 13) terms of the Magnus expansion are evaluated analytically. This provides a closed-form expression for the cumulative matrix exponential parameter $\mathbf{\Theta}^{(2)}(h)$ (Eq. 14) to propagate the quaternion state, maintaining the unit-norm constraint and geometric properties.

**B. Two-Stage Estimation Algorithm**

1. **Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) Estimate for Initialization:**
 - A reliable initial estimate of the inertia tensor is crucial for the nonlinear shooting refinement. This is obtained from a linear least-squares formulation of the torque-free Euler equations.
 - The symmetric inertia tensor is parameterized by a vector $\mathbf{Y} = [I_{xx}, I_{yy}, I_{zz}, I_{xy}, I_{xz}, I_{yz}]^T$.
 - By expressing $\mathbf{I}\dot{\mathbf{\Omega}}_k$ and $\mathbf{I}\mathbf{\Omega}_k$ as linear functions of $\mathbf{Y}$ (Eq. 16) and substituting into the Euler equation, a homogeneous linear constraint $\mathbf{A}_k \mathbf{Y} = 0$ is derived (Eq. 17).
 - Stacking these constraints yields an over-determined system $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{Y} = 0$. The scale invariance is addressed by imposing the unit-trace constraint $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Y} = 1$ (Eq. 18).
 - This constrained problem is transformed into an unconstrained least-squares problem (Eq. 20) using a particular feasible vector and a basis for the null space of $\mathbf{C}$, providing an initial estimate for $\mathbf{Y}$.

2. **Nonlinear Shooting Refinement:**
 - The initial guess from the KKT method is refined using a nonlinear optimization procedure (Figure 1).
 - The objective function $L(\mathbf{\Omega}_0, \mathbf{q}_0, \mathbf{I})$ (Eq. 15) minimizes the sum of norm-squared vector parts of the error quaternions between the simulated trajectory $\mathbf{q}_j$ and the measured telemetry $\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_j$. Minimizing this is dynamically equivalent to minimizing the true angular error.
 - The optimization procedure estimates the initial angular velocity $\mathbf{\Omega}_0$, initial quaternion $\mathbf{q}_0$, and the reduced vector $\mathbf{z}$ encoding the inertia matrix, thereby enforcing the trace constraint.
 - For the image-based single-trajectory case, an unknown midpoint state ($\mathbf{\Omega}_0, \mathbf{q}_0$) is estimated and then propagated both backward and forward to improve numerical stability against isolated pose-estimation errors.

**C. Data Generation and Preprocessing (for Controlled-Noise Simulations)**

1. **Ground Truth Trajectory Generation:** A normalized inertia tensor matching the MYRIADE micro-satellite (Eq. 21) was used. Trajectories were simulated for 500 seconds with a 1.0 s sampling interval using a high-order DOP853 Runge-Kutta integrator.
2. **Noise Injection:** Simulated attitude errors, representative of star tracker measurements, were injected by perturbing each true quaternion with a random rotation. The error angle was drawn uniformly from 0 to 30 arcseconds (Eq. 22).
3. **Preprocessing and Smoothing:** Noisy quaternion sequences were unwrapped, and angular velocity was approximated from successive attitude increments using the logarithmic map (Eq. 23). Both angular velocity and acceleration (for KKT) were smoothed with a Savitzky-Golay filter.

**D. Observability Studies**

- **Global Observability Analysis:** Initial angular-velocity directions were sampled on a unit sphere (Fibonacci grid) to analyze direction-dependent information losses, especially near principal axes or the separatrix. Trajectories near the separatrix were handled by switching to a numerical integrator to prevent gradient divergence.
- **Observability Limits Near Principal-Axis Rotations:** Analytical models were used to quantify the degradation of observability as trajectories approach stable (maximum/minimum inertia) and unstable (intermediate inertia) principal axes.

**E. Image-Based Attitude Measurements using Observations from a Chaser Spacecraft**

1. **Image Simulation Framework (Figure 5):**
 - A photorealistic rendering pipeline (Mitsuba 3) was developed to generate synthetic images of a JASON-1 spacecraft (Eq. 31) under various orbital, attitude, and illumination conditions in a simplified rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO) scenario.
 - Three datasets were created: Random-view (for training pose estimation), Single-arc RPO (continuous torque-free motion, 100 trajectories, 2000 images each), and Multi-arc RPO (two impulsive maneuvers, three torque-free arcs, 120 trajectories, 900 images each, stratified by initial spin and maneuver increment) (Table 2, Table 3).

2. **Image-Based Pose Estimation:**
 - Relative pose was estimated from 2D–3D keypoint correspondences. Keypoints were manually defined on the JASON-1 model (Figure 7a).
 - A two-stage vision pipeline (YOLO-based object detector, RTMPose-based keypoint detector) detected keypoint locations.
 - The perspective-n-point (EPnP) algorithm with RANSAC outlier rejection was used for pose estimation (Eq. 32).
 - The resulting pose sequence was post-processed with Hampel filtering and SLERP interpolation to suppress frame-to-frame jitter.

**F. Multi-Trajectory and Multi-Arc Inertia Tensor Estimation**

- The KKT+Elliptic method was extended to jointly estimate the inertia tensor from multiple short torque-free arcs, where distinct initial conditions allow sampling different regions of phase space to mitigate observability degeneracies. This involved estimating separate initial states ($\mathbf{\Omega}_0, \mathbf{q}_0$) for each arc.
- Performance was compared against KKT initialization alone and KKT+EKF refinement in both controlled-noise and image-derived multi-arc scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the accuracy, computational efficiency, and observability characteristics of the proposed KKT+Elliptic method, both with synthetic and image-derived attitude data.

**A. Performance with Controlled Attitude Noise (Single and Multi-Trajectory)**

- **Single Trajectory (500-second arc, 30 arcsecond noise):**
 - KKT+Elliptic achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of $3.56 \times 10^{-7}$ for the normalized inertia tensor. This represents approximately two orders of magnitude improvement over the initial KKT estimate ($2.65 \times 10^{-5}$) and one order of magnitude improvement over the KKT+EKF refinement ($2.27 \times 10^{-6}$).
 - Computationally, KKT+Elliptic was significantly faster, requiring 0.1849 seconds, compared to 11.1043 seconds for KKT+EKF, and 0.0027 seconds for the KKT initialization alone (Table 1).
 - Monte Carlo simulations (10,000 runs) showed the inertia-tensor error for KKT+Elliptic to be consistently bounded between $1 \times 10^{-7}$ and $8 \times 10^{-7}$ (Figure 2).
- **Multi-Trajectory (three 100-second arcs, 30 arcsecond noise):**
 - KKT+Elliptic maintained its performance advantage, achieving an MAE of $1.12 \times 10^{-6}$, compared to $8.22 \times 10^{-5}$ for KKT and $1.28 \times 10^{-5}$ for KKT+EKF.
 - Computational time for KKT+Elliptic was 0.2794 seconds, which was approximately 20 times faster than KKT+EKF (6.8997 seconds) (Table 5).
 - The error distribution over 10,000 Monte Carlo runs showed MAE primarily between $1 \times 10^{-6}$ and $3 \times 10^{-6}$ (Figure 11).

**B. Observability Studies**

- **Global Error Map (Figure 3):** The error map of the inertia tensor estimation revealed that accuracy was high over most of the energy ellipsoid. However, accuracy degraded near the principal inertia axes (max, intermediate, min) and separatrices, indicating regions of reduced observability.
- **Observability Limits Near Principal-Axis Rotations (Figure 4):**
 - For stable principal axes ($I_1, I_3$), the estimation error followed an $O(\epsilon^{-1})$ algebraic degradation trend as the transverse angular-velocity offset ($\epsilon$) from the principal axis decreased. This is attributed to the $O(\epsilon^2)$ scaling of the Gauss-Newton curvature for transverse inertia structure.
 - Near the unstable intermediate principal axis ($I_2$), the degradation was weaker, following a logarithmic trend, $O(|\log \epsilon|)$. This is due to the logarithmic divergence of the separatrix period, leading to fewer observable macroscopic phase-space traversals over a fixed observation window.

**C. Image-Based Pose Estimation Results**

- The image-based pose estimation pipeline achieved sub-degree median attitude accuracy: 0.660$^\circ$ for the single-arc RPO dataset and 0.964$^\circ$ for the multi-arc RPO dataset (Table 4, Figure 8).
- Median position errors were below 0.4%, and median reprojection errors were below one pixel for both datasets.
- The multi-arc dataset showed broader error distributions, particularly in attitude and reprojection error (e.g., 99th-percentile attitude error increased from 3.296$^\circ$ to 8.291$^\circ$), indicating more challenging conditions due to diverse rotational states and impulsive maneuvers.

**D. Inertia Tensor Estimation from Image-Derived Attitudes**

- **Single Trajectory (2000-second arc):**
 - Replacing true attitudes with image-derived estimates increased the median inertia-tensor MAE from $2.0 \times 10^{-4}$ to $6.5 \times 10^{-4}$ (Figure 9, left panel). The 90th percentile similarly increased from $1.8 \times 10^{-3}$ to $8.3 \times 10^{-3}$. This indicates that pose-estimation errors contribute to degradation, especially in less favorable cases.
 - Despite this, the estimated inertia tensor supported long-horizon attitude prediction. After 10 hours, the median attitude prediction error was 6.7$^\circ$, demonstrating the feasibility of long-term tumbling prediction for non-cooperative targets (Figure 9, right panel, Figure 10).
- **Multi-Arc Evaluation (30–300 seconds per arc):**
 - KKT+Elliptic consistently achieved lower median inertia-tensor error than both KKT and KKT+EKF across all tested arc lengths and maneuver modes (Figure 12b).
 - Estimation accuracy was strongly dependent on rotational excitation and temporal sampling (Figure 12a).
 - Largest errors occurred in the low angular-velocity regime, particularly for short arcs and small maneuvers (SS mode), due to insufficient excitation.
 - Errors generally decreased with increasing arc length in low- and medium-angular-velocity regimes.
 - In the high angular-velocity regime, the trend was less monotonic, as excessively rapid rotation reduced effective temporal resolution given the fixed 1 s image cadence.
 - The medium angular-velocity regime provided the most favorable balance, yielding the lowest errors for arc lengths of 100 frames or more.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for spacecraft inertia tensor estimation that operates exclusively on attitude-only data derived from passive observations of torque-free motion. This approach has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Non-Cooperative Spacecraft Characterization:** The method eliminates the need for gyroscope measurements, known control torques, or active excitation, which are often unavailable for uncooperative targets. This capability directly supports space domain awareness activities, allowing for the characterization of defunct satellites, tumbling debris, or other non-responsive objects. Accurate inertia information is critical for predicting their long-term motion, informing rendezvous, proximity operations, and potential debris removal missions.
- **Reduced Sensor Requirements and Onboard Computation:** By relying solely on attitude data (which can be obtained from star trackers or external imagery), the method lowers the sensor suite requirements for inertia estimation. The proposed KKT+Elliptic algorithm also demonstrates significantly higher computational efficiency compared to EKF-based methods, making it a candidate for deployment on resource-constrained small satellites or for real-time onboard processing.
- **Robustness to Measurement Noise and Analytical Precision:** The use of the exact Jacobi-elliptic solution for angular velocity propagation and the Magnus expansion for quaternion mapping provides a high-precision, analytically driven approach that is less susceptible to measurement noise amplification (e.g., from numerical differentiation of attitude data) compared to conventional methods. This inherent precision contributes to the method's accuracy.
- **Insights into Observability:** The detailed observability analysis provides fundamental insights into the conditions under which inertia estimation from torque-free motion is most effective. Identifying the algebraic and logarithmic degradation trends near principal axes highlights the importance of sufficient rotational excitation for reliable estimation and guides mission planners in selecting appropriate observation strategies or maneuver designs.
- **Integration with Vision-Based Navigation:** The successful evaluation with image-derived attitudes from photorealistic simulations demonstrates the method's practical applicability in conjunction with vision-based pose estimation pipelines. This integration represents a step towards fully autonomous inspection and characterization of space objects using passive optical sensors.
- **Potential for Long-Horizon Prediction:** The capability to achieve sub-degree median attitude prediction error over a 10-hour horizon using estimated inertia tensors underscores the utility of this method for operational planning and situational awareness, allowing for extended prediction of tumbling motion beyond the observation window.

In summary, this work offers a robust and efficient solution for a challenging problem in astrodynamics, extending the capabilities for understanding and interacting with non-cooperative and uninstrumented spacecraft, with potential benefits across space exploration, satellite servicing, and space traffic management.
