# Agent-World: Scaling Real-World Environment Synthesis for Evolving General Agent Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Renmin University of China and ByteDance Seed researchers introduced Agent-World, a framework for advancing general agent intelligence through scalable real-world environment synthesis and a continuous self-evolving training mechanism. The approach enables agents to learn and adapt by autonomously discovering and constructing a diverse ecosystem of stateful, executable tools and environments from real-world sources, achieving consistent performance improvements across 23 challenging agent benchmarks and outperforming prior environment-scaling methods.

## Problem

- Existing LLM agents struggle with complex, external, and stateful tool environments, often limited to stateless or single-tool interactions.
- A scarcity of diverse, realistic, and scalable interactive environments hinders the training of general-purpose agents, with manual creation being resource-intensive and simulated environments prone to hallucinations.
- Current training paradigms lack principled mechanisms to diagnose agent weaknesses and drive continuous self-improvement in scalable environments, preventing lifelong learning.

## Method

- **Agent-World** introduces ==Agentic Environment-Task Discovery==, which autonomously mines environment themes and databases from real-world sources, generates and verifies executable tool interfaces, and synthesizes verifiable tasks with controllable difficulty (both graph-based and programmatic).
- Implements a ==Continuous Self-Evolving Agent Training== mechanism that uses ==multi-environment reinforcement learning== and a ==dynamic diagnostic arena== to identify agent weaknesses and generate targeted training tasks.
- Constructs a ==hierarchical taxonomy== of synthesized environments to ensure broad coverage and systematic organization for evaluation and targeted task generation.

## Results

- Agent-World consistently outperformed prior environment-scaling baselines across **23** challenging agent benchmarks, including tool-use, AI assistant, reasoning, and coding tasks.
- Agent-World-14B achieved **65.4%** on τ²-Bench, **55.8%** on BFCL V4, and **38.1%** on MCP-Mark after two rounds of self-evolution, competitive with larger proprietary models in some areas.
- Scaling analyses showed a positive relationship between environment diversity and agent performance, with the average score on representative tool-use domains more than doubling (from **18.4%** to **38.5%**) when scaling environments from 0 to **1,978**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying scalable real-world environment synthesis with continuous self-evolution provides a robust framework for improving general agent intelligence, addressing limitations of static training.
> - Generating a diverse ecosystem of database-grounded executable environments and verifiable tasks from real-world sources is crucial for training agents capable of handling complex, stateful interactions.
> - A closed-loop self-evolving training mechanism, where agents diagnose their weaknesses and drive targeted environment/task expansion, enables continuous, lifelong agent learning and co-evolution with the training environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has progressed from basic text generation to more sophisticated capabilities, including decision-making and language understanding. A current frontier in this field involves evolving LLMs into general-purpose agents capable of interacting with complex, external, and stateful tool environments. This transition necessitates agents that can integrate real-world interaction with verbal reasoning and learn continuously from experience.

Existing research into agentic reinforcement learning (Agent RL) has explored agent systems using static tool environments. However, real-world environments are characterized by their compositional and stateful nature, requiring agents to manage multi-tool usage and track state transitions. Prior work often falls short in these areas, being limited to stateless or single-tool settings. The Model Context Protocol (MCP) and broader agent skills have emerged as standards to facilitate a unified interface for connecting agents with scalable real-world services.

A significant challenge for training general-purpose agents is the scarcity of diverse, realistic, and scalable interactive environments. Manual environment creation is resource-intensive, prompting two main research directions:
1. **Simulated environments**: These use LLMs as textual world models to generate environment feedback. While scalable, they are susceptible to hallucinations and may not accurately reflect real-world dynamics.
2. **Realistic environments**: These combine executable tools with real databases, providing stronger grounding for complex interactions. Recent benchmarks, such as $\tau^2$-Bench and ClawEval, have moved towards stateful environments. Some initial steps have been taken to synthesize programmatic environments and tasks for agent training.

Despite these advancements, two primary bottlenecks persist in the broader landscape:
- **Scalable realism and complex environment synthesis**: Current synthetic environments often lack the complexity and realistic interaction logic required to train agents for long-horizon, state-intensive tasks, often being derived from limited open-source toolchains or purely LLM-generated.
- **Continuous self-evolving training mechanisms**: While realistic environments can serve as training arenas, existing work has largely focused on environment construction without developing principled mechanisms to diagnose agent weaknesses and drive continuous self-improvement within these scalable environments.

Agent-World addresses these gaps by proposing a unified framework that combines scalable real-world environment synthesis with a continuous, self-evolving training mechanism, aiming to foster a co-evolution of agent policies and environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance general agent intelligence by creating a self-evolving training arena that addresses the limitations of current environment synthesis and agent training paradigms. This overarching goal is broken down into two main specific objectives:

1. **Developing scalable realism and complex environment synthesis**: The motivation here stems from the observation that existing training environments for LLM agents often lack the necessary realism, diversity, and complexity found in real-world scenarios. Purely LLM-generated environments can hallucinate, while those derived from limited toolchains may not accurately mirror real-world interaction logic. This restricts the ability to train agents on tasks requiring long-horizon planning and state-intensive interactions. The authors aim to overcome this by autonomously discovering and constructing a vast ecosystem of realistic, database-grounded executable environments and then synthesizing diverse, verifiable tasks with controllable difficulty on top of them. This is crucial for equipping agents with robust, transferable interaction logic across a broad spectrum of real-world contexts.

2. **Establishing a continuous self-evolving training mechanism**: Current agent training methodologies primarily emphasize environment construction and scaling, often overlooking mechanisms for agents to diagnose their own weaknesses and systematically improve over time. The motivation is to move beyond single-round training to a life-long learning paradigm, similar to human intelligence. The authors seek to integrate multi-environment reinforcement learning with a dynamic diagnostic arena. This arena would iteratively synthesize new tasks, automatically identify capability gaps in trained agents based on executable evidence, and then guide targeted environment-task expansion to address these deficiencies. This closed-loop process is designed to enable the co-evolution of agent policies and the training environments themselves, leading to more capable and adaptable general-purpose agents.

In summary, the work is motivated by the need for more realistic, diverse, and complex interactive environments and a principled, continuous learning framework to train LLM agents that can operate effectively as general-purpose assistants in dynamic, stateful real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Agent-World is structured around two interconnected components that form a closed-loop training process: Agentic Environment-Task Discovery and Continuous Self-Evolving Agent Training.

#### 3.1. Agentic Environment-Task Discovery

This component focuses on building a scalable and realistic environment ecosystem and synthesizing high-quality, verifiable agent tasks.

- **Environment Theme Collection**: The process begins by gathering environment themes from three real-world sources:
 1. **MCP Servers**: Specifications from Smithery, including structured JSON documents with source-data descriptions and standardized tool definitions.
 2. **Tool Documentations**: Open-source datasets of real tool-use scenarios, with tool definitions inversely mapped to environment topics using an LLM.
 3. **Industrial PRDs (Product Requirement Documents)**: Used as theme anchors due to their inclusion of domain workflows and system interfaces.
 These sources are merged to form an initial seed topic set.

- **Agentic Database Mining**: For each collected topic, a deep-research agent (G), powered by a policy model ($\pi_\theta$) and an external toolset (search, browser, code compiler, OS tools), autonomously mines and processes web data. This involves iterative information retrieval and data structuring. To enhance scale and complexity, a database complexification process ($\phi$) iteratively prompts the deep-research agent to expand and enrich topic-specific databases over multiple rounds, resulting in high-quality, realistic databases.

- **Tool Interface Generation and Verification**: A coding agent ($\psi$), equipped with a code compiler and OS tools, generates executable Python tool interfaces and associated unit test sets for each mined database. A cross-validation procedure filters these tools, retaining only those that compile successfully, pass over 50% of their unit tests, and belong to an environment with at least one valid tool and test case. This ensures the executability and quality of the tool ecosystem.

- **Environment Taxonomy Construction**: To organize the synthesized environments systematically, a hierarchical taxonomy is built. Hierarchical clustering is applied to thousands of environment themes to identify 50 second-tier cluster centers, which are then summarized by an LLM (GPT-OSS-120B) into descriptive labels. Three human annotators further merge these into 20 first-tier categories, creating a taxonomy with 20 first-tier, 50 second-tier, and over 2,000 third-tier labels.

- **Verifiable Task Synthesis**: High-quality agentic tasks are synthesized using two complementary strategies:
 1. **Graph-Based Task Synthesis**: This models sequential tool dependencies. For each environment, a weighted directed tool graph is constructed, with edges representing strong, weak, or independent call dependencies, assigned by an LLM. A random walk on this graph generates raw tool-call sequences, which are then instantiated with input parameters from the database. An LLM refines these sequences into executable tool chains ($\tau^*$). A final task description ($q_{final}$) is drafted, and the chain is executed in a sandbox to record interaction traces and generate ground-truth answers ($a^*$) and structured evaluation rubrics ($R$). Tasks are verified for consistency by running a ReAct agent five times and retaining tasks only if successful in at least two runs. Difficulty is scaled by increasing random walk step counts, sampling weak dependencies, and abstracting task descriptions.
 2. **Programmatic Task Synthesis**: This captures complex, non-linear reasoning. An LLM generates a complex task query ($q_{prog}$) and a corresponding executable Python script ($\pi_{code}$) that uses the environment's tools with complex control flows. The script is debugged and repaired by a ReAct agent in a sandbox until successful execution yields the ground-truth answer. An executable verification script ($V_{code}$) is also generated by an LLM to robustly evaluate candidate answers and database states. Similar consistency verification and difficulty scaling (e.g., increasing unique tools, intricate logic) are applied.

- **Static Statistics of Environment-Task Data**: Comprehensive statistics are provided for the generated environments and tasks, including diversity in environment types (1,978 environments, 19,822 tools, diverse file types) and task difficulty (average of over 20 interaction turns, with a small fraction solvable by a strong proprietary model).

#### 3.2. Continuous Self-Evolving Agent Training

This component employs the scalable environment ecosystem as both a training source and a dynamic diagnostic arena for continuous self-improvement.

- **Multi-Environment Agent Reinforcement Learning**: Agents are trained using multi-environment "agent–tool–database" interaction rollouts. This involves a closed loop between an LLM policy ($\pi_\theta$), a tool interface/runtime, and a database state. The policy generates actions, which are executed in a sandboxed environment to read/update the database, returning structured observations. Structured verifiable rewards are assigned: for graph-based tasks, an LLM-as-judge evaluates against a rubric; for programmatic tasks, an executable validation script verifies the output or database state. Policy updates are performed using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize these verifiable returns.

- **Self-Evolving Agent Arena**: The environment ecosystem also functions as a dynamic diagnostic arena.
 - **Arena Construction**: An evaluation arena ($E_{arena}$) is constructed by stratified sampling, selecting K environments from each first-tier category of the hierarchical taxonomy to ensure broad coverage.
 - **Dynamic Evaluation Task Synthesis**: At each iteration, a fresh batch of verifiable tasks and validators is synthesized for the arena environments, preventing overfitting to static evaluations.
 - **Agentic Diagnosis**: A trained agent policy is evaluated on these dynamic tasks. An auto-diagnosis agent ($\delta$), equipped with a Python interpreter and search tools, analyzes failure patterns (e.g., tool logs, error distribution, environment metadata). It outputs a ranked set of "weak environments" ($W^{(r)}$) and environment-specific task-generation guidelines ($G^{(r)}_{guide}$) characterizing missing capabilities (e.g., erroneous tool use, state-update mistakes).
 - **Agent-Environment Co-Evolution**: Based on the diagnosed weak environments and guidelines, the verifiable task synthesis pipeline is re-run to generate a targeted training set. Database complexification may also be performed if insufficient state diversity is identified as a weakness. Multi-environment agent RL is then applied to this augmented data to improve the policy. This iterative process forms a self-evolving loop, enabling agents and environments to co-evolve, turning scalable environments into an automated curriculum engine.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Agent-World was conducted across 23 challenging agent benchmarks, categorized into core agentic tool-use suites, advanced AI assistant benchmarks, general reasoning benchmarks, agentic search & coding benchmarks, and knowledge & MCP benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Agentic Tool-Use Benchmarks

- **Limitations of Foundation Models**: Proprietary models such as GPT-5.2 High and Gemini-3 Pro achieved moderate performance on challenging benchmarks like MCP-Mark (53.1% and 50.8% respectively). Open-source foundation models like GPT-OSS-120B and Qwen3-235B-A22B showed lower performance (4.7% and 5.8% on MCP-Mark), indicating difficulties with long-horizon tool use, multi-step planning, and state tracking in diverse stateful environments.
- **Uneven Gains from Existing Environment-Scaling Methods**: Previous environment-scaling methods (e.g., Simulator-8B, EnvScaler-8B, AWM-8B/14B) showed inconsistent improvements. Simulator-based methods performed well on certain benchmarks (e.g., $\tau^2$-Bench) but poorly on others (e.g., MCP-Mark), suggesting that simulated environments may not capture real-world state transitions adequately. Programmatic methods showed broader gains but still exhibited weaknesses in specific environments like GitHub and Notion.
- **Agent-World's Consistent Generalization**: Agent-World consistently outperformed prior environment-scaling baselines across the three core agentic tool-use suites. Agent-World-8B achieved 61.8% on $\tau^2$-Bench, 51.4% on BFCL V4, and 8.9% on MCP-Mark, surpassing EnvScaler-8B and ScaleEnv-8B. Agent-World-14B further improved performance by approximately 5% over Agent-World-8B, outperforming all prior environment-scaling baselines and achieving competitive results with large open-source LLMs like DeepSeek-V3.2-685B on BFCL-V4 (55.8% vs. 54.1%). This suggests that Agent-World’s integrated framework leads to more consistent gains across diverse benchmarks and environments.

#### 4.2. Generalization on Long-horizon Agentic Reasoning Scenarios

- **Preservation of General Reasoning**: Agent-World-8B demonstrated strong overall performance across seven general reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH500, GSM8K, MATH), showing improvements on most dimensions without degrading core mathematical reasoning capabilities.
- **Strong Gains in Search and Coding Tasks**: Significant improvements were observed on agentic search and coding benchmarks (e.g., WebWalkerQA, SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench 1.0/2.0, GAIA, HLE). These gains indicate that Agent-World facilitates the acquisition of transferable agentic strategies for iterative planning, software engineering, deep information retrieval, and multi-tool coordination.
- **Robustness in Knowledge and MCP Environments**: Agent-World-8B substantially outperformed baselines on five MCP-Universe sub-domains (e.g., Browser Automation, Web Searching) and maintained improvements on knowledge-centric benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, SuperGPQA), suggesting stronger compositional generalization and adaptation to structurally diverse external tools.

#### 4.3. Generalization on Advanced Agentic Assistant Scenarios

- **Superior Performance on Advanced Benchmarks**: Agent-World-8B achieved scores of 9.2% on SkillsBench, 6.5% on ARC-AGI-2, and 30.5% on Claw-Eval, outperforming open-source baselines (Qwen3-8B, EnvScaler-8B, AWM-8B) in these challenging assistant settings that require long-horizon planning and execution.
- **Stable Cross-Scale Gains**: Agent-World exhibited consistent performance improvements from the 8B to 14B parameter scale (e.g., SkillsBench: 9.2% to 12.6%), unlike some baselines that showed unstable or uneven scaling trends. This indicates the method's effectiveness and robust transferability to complex, integrated assistant scenarios.

#### 4.4. Scaling Analysis of Training Environments

- **Positive Scaling Relationship**: Increasing the number of training environments from 0 to 1,978 consistently improved downstream agentic tool-use performance across representative domains (MCPMark (Postgres), BFCL (WebSearch), BFCL (Multi-Turn), and $\tau^2$-Bench (Airline)). The average score more than doubled (from 18.4% to 38.5%, an increase of 20.1 points).
- **Stage-Wise Gains**: Performance gains were most pronounced from 10 to 100 environments and again from 100 to 500 environments, indicating that initial and moderate-scale environment expansion rapidly improves the coverage of critical interaction patterns. Diminishing, though still positive, returns were observed from 500 to 2,000 environments, suggesting that early expansion addresses high-impact diversity, while later expansion contributes finer-grained robustness.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Continuous Self-Evolution

- **Monotonic Gains from Self-Evolving Loop**: Running a two-round self-evolving arena loop demonstrated monotonic gains on all three evaluation suites for both Agent-World-14B and the EnvScaler-8B base model. Agent-World-14B performance on $\tau^2$-Bench/BFCL-V4/MCP-Mark improved from 60.2%/52.4%/29.5% to 65.4%/55.8%/38.1% after two rounds. EnvScaler-8B also improved from 37.9%/47.6%/9.5% to 41.6%/50.0%/15.1%.
- **Targeted Improvement for Challenging Scenarios**: The largest gains were observed on MCP-Mark, a benchmark requiring stronger state tracking and deeper interaction with realistic MCP server environments. This supports the self-evolving objective of localizing environment-specific weaknesses and generating harder instances to address failures.
- **Sustained Effectiveness**: Second-round gains were smaller but remained positive, indicating diminishing but effective returns. The early rounds primarily address pattern-level errors in unfamiliar interactions, while later rounds focus on residual and complex failures.

#### 4.6. Training Dynamics

- **Stable Training**: Reinforcement learning curves showed consistent upward reward trends for both Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B backbones, indicating steady policy performance improvement across different environment complexities.
- **Maintained Exploration**: Tool-use training exhibited relatively stable entropy growth, suggesting that as models adapt to unseen APIs and heterogeneous state transitions, they maintain or expand their exploration space, learning new interaction patterns rather than collapsing into narrow exploitation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Agent-World represents a development in the training of large language models for general agent intelligence. By unifying scalable real-world environment synthesis with a continuous self-evolving training mechanism, the work addresses key limitations in current agentic AI research.

The primary significance lies in its ability to generate a diverse and realistic ecosystem of interactive environments from real-world sources. This environment-task discovery pipeline, which autonomously mines topic-aligned databases and executable toolsets from the web and synthesizes verifiable tasks with controllable difficulty, offers a foundation for training agents that can operate in complex, stateful scenarios. The demonstration of Agent-World's consistent performance gains across numerous challenging benchmarks, including tool-use, AI assistant, reasoning, and coding tasks, suggests that this approach can improve the robustness and generalization capabilities of agents.

The continuous self-evolving training mechanism, which integrates multi-environment reinforcement learning with an agentic diagnostic arena, establishes a principled method for ongoing agent improvement. The ability of the arena to dynamically synthesize evaluation tasks, identify capability gaps, and drive targeted learning in a closed loop provides a framework for life-long agent learning. This co-evolution of agent policies and environments contrasts with static training paradigms and offers a pathway for agents to adapt to new challenges and refine their skills continually.

The scaling analyses, which demonstrate a positive relationship between environment diversity, evolution rounds, and agent performance, provide empirical insights for future research and development in general agent intelligence. These findings suggest that scalable, realistic environments are not merely data sources but constitute infrastructure for advancing agent capabilities.

The potential impact of Agent-World extends to practical applications requiring adaptable and robust AI agents, such as advanced AI assistants, software engineering tools, and deep research systems. By facilitating the training of agents that can effectively interact with a broad array of real-world tools and data, this research contributes to the development of more autonomous and intelligent systems.
