# Hearing Touch: Audio-Visual Pretraining for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces a method for enhancing robotic manipulation in contact-rich tasks by using contact microphones as tactile sensors and pre-training an audio encoder on large-scale internet audio-visual datasets. The approach significantly improves success rates and generalization on real-world tasks by providing crucial information not captured by vision alone.

## Problem

- Large-scale pre-training, effective for visual representations in robotics, has not been extended to non-visual modalities like tactile sensing due to data scarcity.
- Traditional tactile sensors lack internet-scale datasets, making it challenging to apply powerful self-supervised learning techniques for robust tactile representations.
- Robot policies struggle with generalization and robust performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks, especially in low-data regimes, without effective tactile feedback.

## Method

- Employs inexpensive Piezo contact microphones mounted on the robot gripper, re-framing tactile input as an audio signal.
- Leverages large-scale internet audio-visual datasets (AudioSet) to pre-train an audio encoder using an Audio-Visual Instance Discrimination (AVID) model.
- Fuses these pre-trained audio features with pre-trained visual features (R3M from Ego4D) via a transformer decoder for behavior cloning, with both encoders unfrozen for fine-tuning.

## Results

- The method achieved an average 23% higher 0-1 success rate and an average 76% increase in reward across three real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks compared to the best performing baseline.
- It demonstrated enhanced generalization, experiencing only a ~20% drop in success rate between train and test environments for the flipping task, significantly better than the 60% drop observed in the vision-only baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the inclusion of contact audio (vision-only was worst) and the large-scale nature of the audio pre-training are critical for the observed performance improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contact microphones provide vital information for contact-rich manipulation that vision alone cannot capture, making them a beneficial complementary modality.
> - Large-scale audio-visual pre-training is crucial for learning effective audio representations, significantly outperforming in-domain self-supervised pre-training, especially in low-data regimes.
> - Pre-trained audio representations enhance policy generalization by mitigating overfitting to specific visual features and enabling more robust task execution in varied environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant progress driven by two key trends: (1) pre-training on large datasets to obtain reusable representations and (2) incorporating multisensory input, particularly tactile sensing, for more robust control. This paper critically addresses a notable gap at the intersection of these trends.

While large-scale pre-training has become a dominant paradigm for learning visual representations in robotics, leveraging vast internet video datasets to create universal visual encoders like R3M and VIP, this approach has largely bypassed other modalities. Tactile sensing, despite its proven importance for contact-rich tasks, has traditionally been limited to training representations from scratch using relatively small, task-specific datasets. The fundamental challenge lies in the scarcity of "internet-scale" data for traditional tactile sensors, making it difficult to apply similar pre-training strategies.

This work ingeniously bypasses this data scarcity bottleneck by redefining the tactile sensing problem. The authors propose using contact microphones as an alternative tactile sensor. The crucial insight is that contact microphones capture structural vibrations as inherently *audio-based* information. This allows the researchers to leverage the wealth of existing *large-scale audio-visual internet datasets*, such as AudioSet, for pre-training tactile representations. By doing so, they become the first to leverage large-scale multisensory pre-training for robotic manipulation, marking a significant advancement.

The paper carefully situates itself within existing literature:
- **Audio in Robotics:** Previous research has demonstrated the utility of audio for tasks like object recognition, material classification, and even occluded manipulation. This paper builds on that foundation but focuses on using contact audio specifically as a tactile modality for fine manipulation, distinguishing itself from work like SHF by mounting the microphones directly on the robot gripper and emphasizing large-scale pre-training.
- **Tactile Sensing:** The authors acknowledge various traditional tactile sensors (GelSight, DIGIT, optical/magnetic-based) but highlight the advantages of contact microphones, particularly their high-frequency capture capabilities (32-48 kHz compared to 30-400 Hz for others) and lower cost.
- **Audio-Visual Representation Learning:** The methodology directly draws from advancements in self-supervised audio-visual representation learning, particularly contrastive learning methods like Audio-Visual Instance Discrimination (AVID), which learn from cross-modal correspondence in videos.
- **Representation Learning for Robotic Manipulation:** While prior works have focused on visual pre-training for robotics, this paper extends the pre-training paradigm to a non-visual modality. It directly contrasts with "That Sounds Right", which also explores contact audio for manipulation but relies on *in-domain* self-supervised learning with much larger task-specific datasets (5,000 data points per task). "Hearing Touch" demonstrates the benefits of *large-scale internet pre-training* in a *low-data regime* (fewer than 100 demonstrations per task), highlighting a key differentiating factor and a significant contribution to generalization and efficiency.

In essence, this work represents a crucial step towards developing truly universal and robust robotic agents by broadening the scope of large-scale pre-training beyond vision, creatively addressing data scarcity for tactile sensing, and enhancing generalization capabilities in real-world manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of "Hearing Touch" is to significantly enhance the performance and generalization capabilities of robotic manipulation policies by integrating large-scale pre-training for tactile sensing, a modality traditionally underserved by such methods.

The primary motivations for this research stem from several critical challenges in robot learning:

1. **Limited Scope of Large-Scale Pre-training:** While large-scale pre-training has proven immensely beneficial for visual representations in robotics, boosting performance and reusability, it has not been effectively extended to other crucial modalities like tactile sensing. This creates a bottleneck where non-visual representations are typically learned from scratch on limited, task-specific data.
2. **Data Scarcity for Tactile Sensors:** Traditional tactile sensors generate data that is difficult to collect at a scale comparable to internet video datasets. This lack of abundant, diverse data makes it challenging to apply powerful large-scale self-supervised learning techniques to tactile representations.
3. **Cruciality of Multisensory Input for Manipulation:** For many contact-rich manipulation tasks, tactile feedback is indispensable for precise control, successful interaction with objects, and robust performance. Relying solely on vision often leads to policies that struggle with subtle interactions or generalize poorly to variations.
4. **Performance in Low-Data Regimes:** Robotic data collection is notoriously expensive, time-consuming, and prone to safety issues. Therefore, robotic policies often operate in low-data regimes. In such scenarios, leveraging pre-trained, generalizable representations becomes even more critical for achieving acceptable performance.

The paper's core insight and hypothesis address these motivations directly: by utilizing contact microphones as a tactile sensor, the output signal becomes inherently audio. This allows researchers to exploit the vast, readily available *audio-visual data* on the internet to pre-train highly generalizable representations for touch, effectively transforming a tactile data scarcity problem into an audio-visual data abundance opportunity.

To validate this hypothesis and address the stated motivations, the authors set out to answer two key questions through their experiments:
1. **Do contact microphones mounted on a robot arm capture interactions difficult to perceive with vision alone?** This question probes the fundamental utility of the chosen sensor in a robotic context, justifying its inclusion as a complementary modality.
2. **Does large-scale pre-training for audio-based tactile sensors yield representations that are useful for robot manipulation?** This question directly tests the central hypothesis—whether leveraging internet-scale audio-visual data for pre-training can indeed lead to superior performance and generalization in downstream robotic manipulation tasks.

By answering these questions affirmatively, the research aims to pave the way for a new paradigm of multisensory pre-training in robotics, leading to more capable, robust, and data-efficient robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of "Hearing Touch" revolves around a two-stage training process that effectively integrates large-scale pre-trained audio-visual representations with behavior cloning for robot manipulation tasks.

**A. Sensors and Data Collection:**
- **Hardware:** A Franka Emika Panda Arm is used, controlled via inverse kinematics for 6-DoF delta end-effector actions. Four Piezo contact microphones are mounted on the robot's gripper, recording audio at 32 kHz. A fixed third-person Intel D435 RealSense camera captures image observations at 30 Hz.
- **Data Preprocessing:** Audio waveforms from the four contact microphones are averaged and downsampled to 16 kHz, then normalized to generate mel spectrograms, following the preprocessing in AVID. Images are collected at 480x640 resolution.
- **Demonstration Collection:** Human demonstrations for robot tasks are collected via teleoperation using an Oculus Quest headset, specifically designed to be in a low-data regime (40-60 demonstrations per task).

**B. Audio and Visual Representation Pretraining (Two-Stage Model Training):**
The core of the approach lies in initializing encoders with weights from models pre-trained on large internet datasets (Figure 2).
1. **Audio Encoder Initialization:** The audio encoder is extracted from a pre-trained Audio-Visual Instance Discrimination (AVID) model. AVID is a self-supervised learning approach trained on AudioSet, a vast dataset containing over 2 million 10-second video clips with human, animal, and environmental sounds. AVID learns representations by discriminating between training instances using cross-modal agreement, encouraging the network to learn video features that match corresponding audio features and vice-versa.
2. **Visual Encoder Initialization:** The visual encoder is initialized with weights from R3M, a well-established method for pre-training visual features for robotic applications. R3M uses a ResNet18 backbone, pre-trained on the Ego4D human egocentric video dataset through time contrastive learning and video-language alignment.
- Crucially, both the audio and visual encoders are kept *unfrozen* during subsequent policy learning. This allows for fine-tuning the pre-trained weights to adapt to the specific nuances of the in-domain robotic data, mitigating domain shift.

**C. Audio-Visual Behavior Cloning:**
- **Policy Architecture:** The policy, trained via behavior cloning, takes as input a two-second audio clip (as a mel spectrogram) and a sequence of `i` images (spanning the same two-second window). These inputs are passed through their respective pre-trained encoders. Learned positional embeddings are applied to the encoded features.
- **Multisensory Fusion:** The encoded audio and visual features are then fed into a transformer decoder network, similar to. This network leverages self-attention to effectively fuse the multi-modal representations, allowing the policy to reason over combined audio-visual cues.
- **Action Prediction:** The output of the transformer block is concatenated and passed to a 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). This MLP predicts a sequence of `h` delta-end effector actions (6-dimensional continuous values: Cartesian displacement (x,y,z) and Euler angle changes (α,β,γ)) over a short horizon.
- **Execution Strategy:** The policy operates in a quasi open-loop manner: at each timestep `t`, it predicts `H` steps of actions, but only `h` (specifically `h=2` in inference) of these actions are executed before re-planning. This balances responsiveness to sensory input with temporal consistency.
- **Loss Function:** The network is optimized using a standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between the predicted actions and the ground-truth actions from the demonstrations.

**D. Real-World Manipulation Tasks:**
Experiments are conducted on three real-world manipulation tasks, chosen for their contact-rich nature and significant visual variations between training and testing environments to rigorously evaluate generalization:
1. **Flipping Task:** Using a spatula to flip a bagel. This task requires subtle contact sensing to slide the spatula under the object.
2. **Scooping Task:** Using a spoon to scoop material from a bowl. This involves precise depth estimation and interaction with the bowl's edges and material.
3. **Zipping Task:** Manipulating a zipper. This task involves direct contact between the gripper and the object.

**E. Baselines for Comparison:**
To isolate the contribution of large-scale audio pre-training, the authors compare their method against three baselines, all using the R3M pre-trained ResNet18 for the visual encoder where applicable:
1. **Vision-Only:** A policy that uses only image frames as input, to ascertain the benefit of adding contact audio.
2. **Scratch:** An audio-visual policy where the audio encoder weights are randomly initialized, to assess the impact of *any* audio pre-training.
3. **BYOL-A (Bootstrap Your Own Latent for Audio):** A self-supervised approach to learning audio representations, but trained *only on in-domain, task-specific audio data*. This baseline directly compares the efficacy of large-scale internet pre-training versus in-domain self-supervised learning for audio.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate compelling evidence for the efficacy of leveraging large-scale audio-visual pre-training for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks, particularly in low-data regimes and when generalizing to novel environments.

**A. Superior Performance and Robustness:**
- **Overall Outperformance:** The proposed method, which utilizes large-scale audio-visual pre-training, significantly outperforms all baselines across all three real-world manipulation tasks (Flipping, Scooping, Zipping). It achieves an average 23% higher 0-1 success rate and an average 76% increase in reward compared to the next best-performing baseline (Table I, Fig. 4).
- **Enhanced Generalization:** A crucial finding is that the method displays much less variation in performance between different configurations of each task (e.g., varying objects, lighting, textures). This indicates a substantially increased ability to generalize to drastic visual differences, suggesting that useful audio representations prevent the model from overfitting to specific visual features present in the training data.

**B. Validation of Contact Audio and Large-Scale Pre-training:**
- **Benefit of Contact Audio:** The "Vision-Only" baseline consistently yields the worst performance across all tasks, often resulting in 0% success (e.g., on the flipping task). This conclusively demonstrates that the signal from contact microphones provides vital information that vision alone cannot capture, making it beneficial for these manipulation tasks.
- **Cruciality of Large-Scale Audio Pre-training:** Comparing the baselines, "Scratch" (randomly initialized audio encoder) and "BYOL-A" (in-domain self-supervised audio pre-training) show mixed and generally poorer performance. While BYOL-A involves an additional pre-training phase, its comparable or sometimes inferior performance to "Scratch" suggests that in-domain SSL, particularly with a small amount of data, is insufficient to learn effective audio representations for manipulation. In stark contrast, the proposed method, leveraging AVID pre-trained on the vast AudioSet, consistently and significantly improves performance. This confirms that the *large-scale aspect* of the audio-visual pre-training is the critical component for its success.

**C. Qualitative Insights and Representation Analysis:**
- **Reduced Overfitting:** Qualitative analysis shows that baselines struggle with domain shift, exhibiting jerky or off-target movements even before contact. The proposed method, however, is less affected by significant visual differences, suggesting that robust audio representations guide the policy more effectively and prevent overfitting to visual specificities.
- **Improved Task Execution:** For instance, in the scooping task, the proposed method consistently learns to push the spoon deeper into the bowl and tilt it effectively, leading to more successful scooping, closely mimicking the human demonstrations. Baselines often misestimate depth or scoop too shallowly.
- **Structured Embedding Space (t-SNE):** t-SNE visualizations (Fig. 5) of the transformer output embeddings reveal that the proposed method produces a more well-structured embedding space. While representations might start separated due to visual differences, they converge over the course of successful trajectories. This indicates that the pre-trained audio representations, combined with visual input via the attention mechanism, allow for a more coherent understanding of the task state.

**D. Ablation Studies Confirm Key Components:**
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** Freezing the pre-trained AVID audio encoder weights during policy learning only slightly diminishes performance, yet still outperforms the next best baseline on the zipping task. This highlights the strong generalizability and applicability of representations learned from large-scale internet audio-visual data.
- **Generalization Performance Gap:** A direct comparison between train and test settings for the flipping task shows that the Vision-Only baseline suffers a 60% drop in success rate, indicating severe overfitting to training visual features. In contrast, the proposed method experiences only a ~20% drop, further reinforcing that pre-trained audio features provide a crucial regularization effect, enhancing generalization.
- **Importance of Self-Attention:** Replacing the transformer with a simpler MLP (while maintaining similar parameter count) results in a nearly 50% drop in success rate and average reward on the scooping task. This confirms that the self-attention mechanism, used for fusing audio and visual features, is a critical architectural component for achieving high performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Hearing Touch" paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation and multimodal learning, with substantial potential for broader impact:

1. **Breaching the Tactile Data Scarcity Barrier:** The most profound impact of this work is its innovative solution to the long-standing challenge of data scarcity for tactile sensing. By cleverly repurposing contact microphones as audio-generating tactile sensors, the authors unlock the ability to leverage massive, readily available audio-visual internet datasets for pre-training. This paradigm shift fundamentally alters how tactile representations can be acquired and scaled, moving beyond the limitations of expensive, domain-specific data collection.

2. **Expanding the Scope of Large-Scale Pre-training:** This research successfully extends the highly effective paradigm of large-scale representation pre-training, previously dominated by vision, to a crucial non-visual modality. This sets a precedent and opens new avenues for exploring and integrating other sensory modalities (e.g., force, proprioception, haptics) with large-scale pre-training by finding analogous "bridges" to existing internet data. This is essential for building truly comprehensive and intelligent robotic systems.

3. **Enhanced Robot Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated significant improvements in manipulation success rates, rewards, and crucially, generalization capabilities across varied environments, are critical for real-world robotic deployment. Robots that can perceive subtle contact interactions and adapt to novel visual conditions without extensive re-training will be far more versatile and robust in unstructured environments. This directly addresses one of the major hurdles in practical robotics.

4. **Efficiency in Low-Data Regimes:** By achieving high performance with a remarkably small number of in-domain demonstrations (40-60 per task), the method showcases exceptional data efficiency. This drastically reduces the labor, cost, and safety concerns associated with collecting large robotic datasets, making advanced manipulation skills more accessible and faster to develop.

5. **Cost-Effective Sensing:** Utilizing relatively inexpensive Piezo contact microphones as a high-frequency tactile sensor makes this approach economically attractive. This could enable more widespread adoption of advanced tactile capabilities in various robotic platforms.

6. **Insights into Multimodal Learning:** The qualitative analysis and t-SNE visualizations provide valuable insights into how pre-trained audio features, when fused with visual inputs via attention, contribute to a more structured and generalizable understanding of the task space. The ablations underscore the importance of both large-scale pre-training and sophisticated fusion mechanisms (like self-attention).

7. **Future Research Directions:** The paper inspires several promising avenues for future work, including:
 - Investigating optimal properties of pre-training datasets for learning effective audio-visual representations for manipulation.
 - Exploring the synergistic combination of contact microphones with traditional visuotactile sensors for an even richer understanding of the environment.
 - Further expanding this "data-bridging" methodology to other robotic tasks and modalities to continuously "widen the data scarcity bottleneck."

In conclusion, "Hearing Touch" represents a pivotal step towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents. By ingeniously leveraging the vast resources of internet audio-visual data, it provides a practical and highly effective solution for advancing tactile perception in robotics, ultimately pushing the boundaries of what robots can accomplish in complex, contact-rich environments.
