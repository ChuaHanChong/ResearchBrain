# Scaling Bimanual Household Manipulation from 1,500 hours of Demonstrations to On-Policy Corrections

> [!summary] Summary
> This work releases 1,500 hours of bimanual household manipulation data - 531.7 hours of real-robot teleoperation across 32,518 trajectories plus ~1,000 hours of in-the-wild UMI handheld-gripper demonstrations - and trains XR-2, a 5B vision-language-action model with a Qwen3-VL backbone and a flow-matching action expert. Expert-data scaling lifts clothes-folding success to 84%, and three rounds of failure-weighted DAgger post-training push it from 58% to 93%.

## Problem

- Generalist bimanual policies are bottlenecked by scarce large-scale, high-quality human demonstration corpora covering long-horizon household tasks with fine-grained language supervision.
- Existing corpora are offline expert-only, so they never cover the deployment-time states a trained policy actually visits, leaving test-time failures unaddressed.
- In-the-wild bimanual handheld-gripper data at scale remains rare, and fixed robot workcells cannot afford the scene, object, and lighting diversity real homes provide.

## Method

- ==Dual collection pipeline==: SOP-standardized VR teleoperation on the 25-DoF **X2W** mobile bimanual robot, plus encoder-free vision-only UMI grippers recovering 6-DoF trajectories via ==visual-inertial SLAM==.
- **XR-2**, a 5B ==Mixture-of-Transformer== VLA: Qwen3-VL-4B backbone plus an 18-block ==flow-matching action expert== with ==depth-aligned cross-attention==, ==adaLN-zero timestep conditioning==, and ==action-prefix conditioning== for asynchronous inference.
- ==DAgger post-training== with trajectory-count-balanced 1:1 expert mixing and a closed-form ==failure-weighted allocation rule== that floors each sub-task's share to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- Clothes-folding success rises monotonically with expert data from **34%** at 30 hours to **84%** at 120 hours, then saturates, with 160 hours giving **82%**.
- Three DAgger rounds from a **58%** checkpoint raise success to **74%**, **82%**, then **93%**, a **35-point** gain over the expert-only baseline.
- Policies evaluated under generalization shifts in material size, color, and initial robot pose; an **85%** generalized success rate corresponds to over **95%** in-distribution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expert-data scaling has a measurable ceiling: once demonstrations densely cover the task distribution, residual failures live in policy-visited states outside that distribution.
> - Corrective on-policy data and offline demonstrations are complementary levers, not competitors - human intervention keeps paying off after expert scaling flattens.
> - Balance correction budgets by trajectory count rather than frames, and floor every sub-task's share, or rebalancing toward weak skills erodes already-strong ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressively moving towards developing generalist policies capable of executing a wide array of complex tasks in diverse environments. A significant approach in achieving this generality involves the use of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and large-scale robot datasets, mirroring trends seen in other domains of artificial intelligence such as natural language processing and computer vision. Bimanual manipulation, which involves coordinated use of two robotic arms, is particularly critical for tasks that demand intricate interaction, long-horizon execution, and fine-grained control, such as household chores involving deformable objects like clothes or operating articulated appliances.

Previous research has demonstrated the potential of VLA models and large datasets in enhancing robot capabilities. However, a persistent challenge in developing robust bimanual manipulation policies has been the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale human demonstration data. While some datasets exist, they often lack the scale, diversity, or specific bimanual coordination required for real-world household tasks. Furthermore, as data scales increase, a more nuanced question emerges: not merely the quantity of data, but the *type* of data that offers the most value. Offline expert demonstrations provide examples of successful task completion, but they may not adequately cover states encountered during real-world policy deployment, particularly failure states. Corrective, on-policy data, collected during actual robot operation and human intervention, offers a complementary source of supervision that specifically targets these deployment-time challenges.

This work contributes to the landscape by directly addressing the data bottleneck for bimanual manipulation and investigating the interplay between large-scale offline expert data and targeted online corrective data. It builds upon previous efforts to scale robot learning and VLA models by providing a substantial new dataset and an empirical analysis of data scaling laws in a complex domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were:

1. **To assemble and release a large-scale, high-quality corpus of bimanual household manipulation demonstrations.** This dataset aims to overcome the existing scarcity of data for complex bimanual tasks, particularly those encountered in everyday household environments.
2. **To train a powerful Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, named XR-2, using this comprehensive dataset.** The objective was to demonstrate the feasibility of training a generalist policy for bimanual tasks that exhibits strong performance and efficient data utilization, enabled by a purpose-built data pipeline and multi-stage training.
3. **To systematically investigate the scaling properties of both offline expert demonstration data and online human-in-the-loop corrective data.** This includes analyzing how increasing amounts of expert data affect policy performance and how post-training with DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) correction data from real-time human interventions contributes to further performance gains, particularly in addressing policy-induced failure states.
4. **To open-source the collected dataset and related resources.** This objective aims to support reproducible research in bimanual robot manipulation learning and provide a shared foundation for the community.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several factors:

- **Necessity of Bimanual Manipulation:** Many household tasks (e.g., folding clothes, operating appliances, handling baskets) intrinsically require two-arm coordination, which is often more efficient and robust than single-arm approaches.
- **Data Bottleneck:** Existing approaches to learning generalist manipulation policies are frequently limited by the lack of sufficiently large and diverse datasets of bimanual human demonstrations, especially real-world data rather than simulated data.
- **Advancement of VLA Models:** Recent progress in VLA models has highlighted their potential for complex robotic control. However, their full potential can only be realized with appropriate training data that reflects the complexity of real-world interactions.
- **Understanding Data Value:** There is a recognized need to understand the relative contributions and scaling behaviors of different types of supervision—specifically, the foundational knowledge provided by extensive expert demonstrations versus the targeted improvement offered by on-policy corrective data. This understanding is crucial for designing efficient and effective data collection strategies for future robot learning systems.
- **Bridging Simulation-to-Reality Gap:** The use of real-robot teleoperation and "in-the-wild" UMI data aims to provide data that is representative of real-world conditions, thus facilitating the deployment of learned policies.

By pursuing these objectives, the researchers aimed to advance the capabilities of bimanual robotic systems and contribute foundational resources and insights to the broader robotics and machine learning communities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved the creation of a large-scale bimanual manipulation dataset, the development of a 5-billion parameter Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model named XR-2, and a multi-stage training paradigm incorporating both expert demonstrations and online corrective data.

**A. Robot System (X2W)**
The robot used for real-robot data collection was the X2W, a mobile bimanual platform with 22 controllable joints (two 7-DoF arms, a 4-DoF waist, and a 2-DoF head) mounted on a three-wheel omnidirectional base, totaling 25 control variables. It is equipped with multiple cameras (head-mounted, wrist-mounted) for multi-view perception and proprioceptive sensors (joint positions, velocities, torques, end-effector poses, wheel states). All sensory data and actions are synchronized and recorded at 30 Hz.

**B. Data Collection Pipeline**
Two complementary pipelines were established for data collection:

1. **Expert Demonstration Collection:**
 - **Process:** Long-horizon tasks were decomposed into sub-tasks. Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) were designed for each sub-task to standardize scene setup, object arrangement, robot initialization, and manipulation workflows. Remote human operators used VR devices to tele-operate the X2W robot, recording multimodal trajectories.
 - **Post-processing:** Raw data underwent automatic post-processing, format conversion, automated quality validation, VLM-powered task and language annotation, and a final manual review. This pipeline aimed to produce consistent, high-quality, frame-level language-annotated demonstrations.
 - **Scale:** This pipeline yielded 531.7 hours of real-robot teleoperation data across 32,518 trajectories.

2. **Online DAgger Collection:**
 - **Process:** During real-world policy deployment, multiple robots executed tasks using the trained policy. When the policy failed or required correction, a human operator temporarily took control, corrected the sub-task, and returned control to the policy.
 - **Recording:** Human intervention segments were automatically recorded as corrective demonstrations, associated with sub-task prompts, and explicitly distinguished from policy-generated segments. This closed-loop system allows continuous conversion of deployment failures into new corrective demonstrations without re-annotation.

**C. Dataset Overview**
The combined dataset, named the PrimeBot Household Manipulation Dataset, totals 1,500 hours:

- **Real-Robot Dataset:** 531.7 hours, 32,518 trajectories, 57.4 million frames, frame-level language annotations.
 - **Scene Coverage:** Four household settings: Folding Station, Laundry (Washer), Living Room (Sofa), and Laundry (Basket), covering tasks like garment manipulation, washer interaction, object transfer, and laundry-basket handling.
 - **Long-horizon & Skill Coverage:** Contains both complete long-horizon tasks (up to 150s) and skill-level demonstrations, with language-aligned sub-step annotations for 11 atomic skills (e.g., "Fold clothes," "Load clothes into washer").
- **UMI Dataset:** Approximately 1,000 hours of in-the-wild bimanual demonstrations, primarily for household garment manipulation.
 - **Diversity:** Collected in over 200 households by 100+ collectors, involving 5,000 distinct garments, varied scenes (beds, tabletops, drying racks), lighting conditions, and folding strategies.
 - **Capture Method:** Utilizes an encoder-free Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) handheld gripper system, providing synchronized dual egocentric video, 6-DoF end-effector trajectories (recovered by visual-inertial SLAM), continuous gripper aperture (recovered visually), and frame-level language annotations (item-level and fine-grained action steps). Wrist-view-only observation is used, designed for endurance and comfort.

**D. Model Architecture (XR-2)**
XR-2 is a 5-billion parameter VLA model with a Mixture-of-Transformer architecture:

- **Backbone:** A pre-trained Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct model for multimodal perception.
- **Action Expert:** An 18-block diffusion transformer trained with flow matching objectives. It has a hidden dimension of 1024, 8 attention heads (grouped key-value attention), and depth-aligned cross-attention to the backbone's hidden states. Denoising-timestep information is conditioned via adaLN-zero modulation.
- **Action Generation:** The model generates a K-length action chunk (a_t:t+K) conditioned on visual observations, language instructions, and proprioceptive states.
- **Asynchronous Execution:** Action prefix conditioning is employed to handle asynchronous execution, with the delay (d) dynamically computed based on recent inference rounds. This allows for low-latency, smooth real-time control through 5 steps of Euler forward integration for synthesizing action sequences.

**E. Training Recipe**

1. **Post-training on Expert Data:**
 - The model is trained using the conditional flow matching objective (L_CFM) to learn multiple sub-tasks jointly. This objective minimizes the difference between the policy's prediction of the target flow and the actual flow, conditioned on noisy actions, observations, robot state, and language instructions.

2. **Post-training on DAgger Data:**
 - Collected DAgger trajectories are streamed to the cloud for further policy optimization on failure cases.
 - **Challenges:** The training strategy addresses "catastrophic forgetting" of existing skills and ensures balanced training across sub-tasks with different learning dynamics.
 - **Data Allocation:** DAgger and expert data are mixed at a 1:1 *trajectory ratio* (rather than frame ratio) to preserve the expert skill distribution.
 - **Failure-Weighted Budget:** The DAgger budget is biased toward sub-tasks where the policy performs poorly. A formula (Equation 4) calculates the fraction (w_i) of DAgger trajectories allocated to sub-task *i*, based on its current success rate (1-s_i), with parameters to control concentration (α), ensure positivity (ϵ), and set a minimum share (w_min) for all sub-tasks to guard against forgetting.

**F. Experiment Settings**
Experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. The policy performed asynchronous inference at 10 Hz, generating a 50x25 action chunk. Whole-body joint commands, temporally ensembled, were issued at 30 Hz, and then optimized by a whole-body motion controller at 1000 Hz. Test environments included slight generalizations in material size, color, and initial robot position.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings regarding data scaling and policy performance in bimanual household manipulation:

1. **Dataset Release and Coverage:**
 - The project successfully released a large-scale dataset of 1,500 hours of bimanual household manipulation data. This includes 531.7 hours of real-robot teleoperation across 32,518 trajectories and approximately 1,000 hours of in-the-wild UMI demonstrations.
 - The real-robot data covers diverse household settings (Folding Station, Laundry Washer, Sofa, Laundry Basket) and a range of long-horizon tasks and 11 atomic skills, annotated with frame-level language.
 - The UMI data provides high-diversity, in-the-wild demonstrations of garment manipulation, collected from numerous households and collectors, featuring a variety of objects and environmental conditions.

2. **XR-2 Model Performance and Data Utilization:**
 - The XR-2, a 5-billion parameter VLA model, trained on this corpus, demonstrated strong manipulation performance, retaining favorable training efficiency and high data utilization.

3. **Scaling Behavior of Expert Data:**
 - Experiments on the clothes-folding task showed a clear scaling trend with increasing amounts of expert demonstration data.
 - The success rate monotonically increased from 34% with 30 hours of data to 84% with 120 hours of data.
 - However, the performance tended to saturate beyond this point; increasing the expert data from 120 hours to 160 hours resulted in a slight decrease to 82%, indicating that additional expert demonstrations yielded diminishing returns. The researchers suggest that at this scale, expert data sufficiently covers the expert-visited state distribution, but residual failures originate from states the policy encounters during deployment that lie outside this distribution.

4. **Effectiveness of DAgger Correction Data:**
 - DAgger experiments were initialized with a policy checkpoint trained on 18,695 expert trajectories (which had a 58% success rate for the clothes-folding task).
 - Three rounds of DAgger post-training, utilizing a failure-weighted data allocation strategy for corrective demonstrations, led to substantial performance improvements.
 - The success rate increased progressively: from 58% to 74% after the first round, to 82% after the second, and finally to 93% after the third round. This represents a 35-percentage point gain compared to the expert-only baseline (58%).
 - This result demonstrates that corrective on-policy data is effective in addressing policy-induced failure states and overcoming the saturation observed with expert data alone.

5. **Complementary Roles of Data Sources:**
 - The findings validate that expert demonstrations and corrective on-policy data play complementary roles in scaling bimanual policy learning. Expert data builds a broad base of skills and successful behaviors, while corrective data targets specific failure modes encountered during deployment, leading to continued performance gains even when returns from additional expert data diminish.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

1. **Addressing a Critical Data Bottleneck:** The release of a 1,500-hour, high-quality, diverse dataset for bimanual household manipulation directly addresses a major limitation in developing generalist robot policies. This corpus, combining real-robot teleoperation and in-the-wild human demonstrations, provides a foundational resource that was previously scarce, enabling more robust and capable bimanual robot learning.

2. **Advancement of Vision-Language-Action Models:** The development and successful training of XR-2, a 5-billion parameter VLA model, on this extensive dataset, showcases the potential of large-scale models to handle complex, long-horizon bimanual tasks. Its architecture, incorporating a pre-trained V-L backbone and an action diffusion transformer with specialized features like depth-aligned cross-attention, contributes to the ongoing evolution of generalist robot control systems.

3. **Empirical Insights into Data Scaling:** The systematic investigation into the scaling behavior of both expert and corrective data provides valuable empirical evidence. The finding that expert data eventually saturates, while DAgger-based on-policy corrections yield further substantial gains, offers a critical insight for designing future data collection and training strategies. This suggests that efficient policy improvement requires a balanced approach, moving beyond simply collecting more expert demonstrations to strategically gathering data that targets real-world deployment failures.

4. **Support for Reproducible Research:** By open-sourcing the complete dataset, including temporally aligned sub-task language annotations, the authors enable other researchers to reproduce their findings and build upon their work. This fosters a collaborative research environment and accelerates progress in bimanual manipulation by providing a common benchmark and training resource.

5. **Practical Implications for Domestic Robotics:** The focus on household manipulation tasks, from garment folding to appliance interaction, positions this work as a step towards more capable and autonomous robots in domestic environments. The demonstrated success in tasks like clothes folding, which involve deformable object manipulation and bimanual coordination, points to the potential for robots to assist with everyday chores.

6. **Foundation for Future Work:** The dataset, model architecture, and the demonstrated training paradigm offer a strong foundation for future research. This includes exploring more advanced multi-modal learning, optimizing data collection pipelines, investigating generalization to novel tasks and environments, and further refining human-robot collaboration paradigms for continuous policy improvement. The insights gained could also inform the development of more robust error recovery mechanisms and adaptive robotic behaviors.
