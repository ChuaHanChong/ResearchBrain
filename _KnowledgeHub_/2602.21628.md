# RuCL: Stratified Rubric-Based Curriculum Learning for Multimodal Large Language Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The RuCL framework enhances Multimodal Large Language Model reasoning by applying a stratified rubric-based curriculum learning approach that reorients curriculum from data selection to dynamic reward design. This method yielded an average accuracy of 60.06% across seven visual reasoning benchmarks, improving the base model's performance by 7.83% and setting a new state-of-the-art for 7B-scale open-source models while mitigating reward hacking.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for MLLMs suffers from "reward hacking," leading to unreliable reasoning processes despite correct final answers.
- Existing fine-grained, rubric-based supervision methods are computationally costly due to instance-specific rubric generation.
- These methods also exhibit inefficient training dynamics by treating all reasoning skills (rubrics) as equally challenging, generating noisy gradients.

## Method

- The RuCL framework constructs ==generalized rubrics==, filtering and stratifying them into foundational and advanced levels based on empirical model pass rates.
- A ==dynamic, stability-aware curriculum mechanism== progressively weights foundational and advanced rubric rewards during reinforcement learning.
- It employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to optimize a hybrid reward function combining rule-based outcome and curriculum-modulated rubric supervision.

## Results

- Attained state-of-the-art average accuracy of **60.06%** across seven visual reasoning benchmarks, representing a **+7.83%** improvement over the baseline 7B-scale model.
- Showed significant performance enhancements in multimodal mathematical reasoning (**+12.97%** on WeMATH) and complex logical deduction (**+10.40%** on LogicVista).
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of stratified rubrics and dynamic, sigmoid-based curriculum scheduling in improving training stability and overall reasoning performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Curriculum learning can be effectively applied to the reward function itself, dynamically adjusting supervision complexity based on model proficiency.
> - Stratifying reasoning skills (rubrics) by empirically determined difficulty levels improves training stability and mitigates gradient noise from premature penalization.
> - Generalized rubric construction offers a computationally efficient method for providing fine-grained process supervision, crucial for addressing reward hacking.
