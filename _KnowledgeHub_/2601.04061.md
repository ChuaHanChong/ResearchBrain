# CLAP: Contrastive Latent Action Pretraining for Learning Vision-Language-Action Models from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLAP framework enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to learn robot manipulation skills from diverse human videos by explicitly aligning visual latent actions with physically executable robot trajectories using contrastive pretraining. This approach, which includes a dual-formulation VLA, achieves state-of-the-art performance on real-world bimanual tasks and the LIBERO benchmark, demonstrating improved skill transfer and robustness to environmental perturbations.

## Problem

- Scarcity of high-quality, diverse robotic data severely limits the scalability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for real-world manipulation.
- Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) learned from human videos suffer from visual entanglement, failing to disentangle pure manipulation skills from irrelevant visual noise.
- Developing VLA models that simultaneously provide high-level semantic reasoning for task planning and precise, high-frequency control for physical interaction is challenging.

## Method

- Introduces **Contrastive Latent Action Pretraining (CLAP)** to align visual latent actions inferred from human videos with a ==physically grounded, quantized latent action space== derived from robot trajectories using ==contrastive learning==.
- Develops a ==dual-formulation VLA framework==: **CLAP-NTP** for high-level reasoning and **CLAP-RF** for high-frequency, precise continuous control.
- Integrates a ==Knowledge Matching (KM) regularization== strategy to mitigate catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning on specific tasks or embodiments.

## Results

- **CLAP-RF** achieved a **61.0%** mean success rate on real-world bimanual manipulation tasks with the Astribot S1 robot, outperforming generalist baselines.
- Established new generalist SOTA on the **LIBERO benchmark** with **91.0%** average success rate, including **82%** on LIBERO-Long for complex long-horizon tasks.
- **CLAP-NTP** demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to unseen objects, boosting "Make Bouquets" success to **35%** with human data integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly aligning visual dynamics from human videos with physically executable robot actions via contrastive learning is key to learning disentangled, transferable manipulation skills.
> - A dual-formulation VLA architecture, combining an autoregressive model for high-level reasoning (CLAP-NTP) and a continuous flow-based policy for precise control (CLAP-RF), effectively balances different robotic requirements.
> - Utilizing a quantized latent action codebook derived from robot trajectories provides a robust and shared "physical language" for grounding actions across heterogeneous data sources.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a significant direction in robotics, building upon the successes of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in semantic understanding and visual perception. VLAs aim to equip robots with the ability to follow natural language instructions across diverse environments and tasks.

A primary challenge in scaling VLA models is the scarcity of high-quality robotic training data compared to the abundance of human video demonstrations. Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) have attempted to bridge this data gap by using self-supervised learning to construct a latent space from video data, often through inverse dynamics models that predict latent actions for visual transitions. However, a limitation of these LAMs is their susceptibility to visual entanglement, where the learned latent space encodes extraneous visual factors (e.g., background shifts, object deformations) rather than precisely capturing manipulation skills. This entanglement necessitates complex post-hoc training steps to map visual latents to robot controls, thereby limiting direct skill transfer from human videos.

This work addresses this limitation by proposing a method that explicitly aligns the latent action space derived from human videos with a physically grounded action space derived from robot trajectories. It connects the paradigm of latent action learning, which focuses on learning latent dynamics from visual transitions, with advancements in action tokenization, which emphasize compact action vocabularies for efficient VLA models. By doing so, this research contributes to the development of more scalable and robust VLA models capable of leveraging large-scale human demonstrations for general-purpose robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop a generalist, language-conditioned bimanual manipulation policy that effectively unifies large-scale human video demonstrations with precise robotic data. This overarching goal is motivated by the significant disparity between the limited availability of high-quality robotic data and the vast abundance of human video demonstrations.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Domain Gap:** Robotic datasets, while beneficial, are still limited in scale, diversity, and semantic richness compared to human data. The research aims to leverage widely available, unlabeled human videos to augment robot learning, thereby overcoming the data scarcity bottleneck. A key challenge is the domain gap between human videos (which offer semantic diversity but lack explicit action labels or kinematic states) and robot trajectories (which provide precise dynamics but are limited in scale).
- **Mitigating Visual Entanglement in Latent Action Models:** Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) learn latent spaces primarily from visual dynamics. This often leads to "visual entanglement," where the learned representations encode noise or irrelevant visual factors (e.g., background changes, object deformations) rather than the underlying manipulation skills. A central motivation is to overcome this limitation by ensuring the learned latent representations are isomorphic to executable robot commands, filtering out extraneous visual noise.
- **Establishing a Physically Grounded Latent Action Space:** The paper aims to construct a latent action vocabulary that is explicitly aligned with the robot's physical action space. This involves translating continuous robot trajectories into discrete, executable action tokens, creating a "physical language" that VLA models can interpret and execute.
- **Enabling Effective Skill Transfer from Human Videos:** By creating a shared, physically grounded latent action space, the research seeks to enable direct and effective transfer of manipulation skills from human videos to robotic execution. This means allowing VLA policies to learn from human demonstrations without requiring complex post-hoc mapping to robot controls.
- **Preserving Instruction Following and Object Generalization:** The work aims to develop a VLA policy that can absorb semantic evidence from human videos while maintaining the reasoning and instruction-following capabilities of a large VLM backbone. This is intended to facilitate robust object generalization, allowing the robot to manipulate novel objects based on learned semantic priors from videos without requiring continuous robot actions for every new object instance.
- **Developing an Efficient Post-Training Strategy:** For practical deployment and adaptation to target domains, the research is motivated to introduce a strategy that provides low-latency continuous action prediction while preserving the semantic knowledge acquired during pretraining. This involves converting token-space representations into continuous control signals and regularizing the adaptation process to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

In summary, the paper aims to develop a robust and scalable framework for learning generalist robotic manipulation policies by efficiently integrating heterogeneous data sources, specifically by grounding human visual dynamics in a precise, executable robotic action space.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Contrastive Latent Action Pretraining (CLAP) framework addresses the challenge of unifying human video demonstrations with robotic data through a three-stage training pipeline: Cross-Modal Alignment via CLAP, Token-Space Policy Pretraining (CLAP-NTP), and Post-Training for Continuous Control (CLAP-RF with Knowledge Matching).

**Problem Formulation:**
The framework operates on two distinct data sources:
- **Robotic Data (D_rob):** Consists of expert robot trajectories (observations `o_t` and actions `a_t`) conditioned on natural language instructions `I`. The action space `A ∈ R^14` represents bimanual control (end-effector position, orientation, and gripper aperture for each of two arms).
- **Human Video Data (D_hum):** Comprises human video demonstrations (visual observations `V`) and task annotations `I`, but lacks explicit action labels or kinematic state information.

The goal is to learn a policy `π(a_t | o_t, I)` that infers a latent control manifold shared between human visual changes and robot physical actions.

**A. Cross-Modal Alignment via CLAP:**
This stage bridges the supervision gap by establishing a shared, discrete latent action manifold.

1. **Semantic Action Quantization (Act-VAE):**
 - A Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE), termed Act-VAE, is trained to quantize continuous robot action trajectories `a_t:t+H-1` into a compact, executable action-token vocabulary.
 - It comprises a Transformer-based encoder `E_φ` and a decoder `D_ψ`. The encoder maps action trajectories to continuous latents `z_e`, which are then discretized by mapping to the nearest entry in a learnable action codebook `C_act = {e_k}_K_k=1`, yielding quantized latents `z_q` and discrete tokens `z_a`.
 - The objective `L_Act` minimizes reconstruction error `∥a - D_ψ(z_q)∥^2_2` combined with codebook and commitment losses, ensuring fidelity and compactness. The encoder `E_act` is frozen after training.

2. **Cross-Modal Dynamics Alignment (VD-VAE):**
 - The Vision-Dynamic VQ-VAE (VD-VAE) infers latent actions from visual transitions in both robot and human videos. It functions as an inverse dynamics model, mapping visual transitions `(o_t, o_t+H)` to the `C_act` codebook.
 - Visual features `f_t, f_t+H` are extracted using a frozen visual backbone (e.g., DINOv3). An inverse dynamics encoder `E_inv` decomposes the transition into an action-relevant latent `z_v,a` and an action-irrelevant latent `z_v,i`.
 - `z_v,a` is quantized using the *frozen* `C_act` from Act-VAE, ensuring alignment with robot control. `z_v,i` is quantized using a separate learnable environment codebook `C_env` to capture nuisance variables.
 - A forward decoder `D_fwd` reconstructs the future visual feature `f_t+H` from `f_t` and the quantized latents `(z_q,a, z_q,i)`.
 - **Contrastive Loss (SigLIP):** To ground `z_v,a` in physical actions, a contrastive loss aligns `z_v,a` with a source-specific reference `z^+_a`. For robot samples, `z^+_a` is the Act-VAE encoding of the ground-truth action chunk. For human videos (lacking ground-truth actions), `z^+_a` is a stop-gradient pseudo-positive constructed by an EMA teacher encoder from the clean transition. The SigLIP loss optimizes pairwise binary classification against a set of negative samples from the batch.
 - **L1 Regularization:** Applied to `z_v,i` (`∥z_v,i∥_1`) to enforce sparsity and disentanglement, forcing it to capture only nuisance information.
 - The total objective `L_VD` combines reconstruction, VQ constraints, contrastive alignment, and L1 regularization. The discrete pseudo action tokens inferred from human videos are denoted as `ˆz_a`.

**B. CLAP-NTP: Token-Space Policy Learning:**
- A token-space generalist policy, CLAP-NTP (Next-Token Prediction), is trained using a VLM backbone (Qwen3VL-4B).
- It models action tokens `z_a` as a continuation of the language sequence, predicting the joint sequence of sub-tasks and action indices `Y = [y_sub, z_a]` from observations `o_t` and instructions `I`.
- The policy is trained with a next-token prediction objective `L_NTP`, leveraging both ground-truth `z_a` from robot demonstrations and pseudo action tokens `ˆz_a` from human videos. This preserves the VLM's reasoning and instruction-following capabilities.

**C. Efficient Post-Training Strategy:**
For deployment and target-domain adaptation, a unified post-training strategy is introduced:

1. **CLAP-RF: Flow Matching Head for Fast Inference:**
 - To overcome the latency of autoregressive token decoding, a Rectified Flow (RF) action head (`f_a`), based on a Diffusion Transformer (DiT), is attached to the CLAP-NTP backbone.
 - The RF action head predicts continuous action chunks. It is trained by minimizing a rectified flow loss `L_RF` on robot trajectories, predicting a vector field `v = a_t:t+H-1 - ϵ` from a noised action chunk.
 - The RF head conditions on internal representations `h_t` from the NTP backbone, thereby benefiting from human-video pretraining.

2. **Knowledge Matching (KM):**
 - A regularization strategy is used to prevent catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning. KM anchors the trainable token policy (`φ_policy`) that conditions the RF head to a frozen reference NTP policy (`φ_ref`).
 - It applies a reverse KL divergence term `L_KL` between the distributions predicted by `φ_policy` and `φ_ref` over discrete tokens.
 - The post-training loss `L_post` combines `L_RF` with `αL_KL`.

**Post-Training Procedure (Algorithm 4):**
- **Stage 1 (NTP adaptation):** The pretrained CLAP-NTP policy (`φ_pre`) is adapted on all tokenized target data (robot action tokens and human pseudo action tokens). The adapted policy is then frozen as `φ_ref`.
- **Stage 2 (Robot-only RF post-training with KM):** The trainable policy branch (`φ_policy`) and RF action head (`f_a`) are initialized from the adapted model. They are then trained *only* on robot trajectories (due to continuous action chunk availability) using the `L_post` objective, with KM preserving the knowledge from `φ_ref`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the CLAP framework involved extensive experiments on both real-world robotic platforms and simulation environments, alongside ablation studies.

**A. Real-world Robot Deployment (Astribot S1)**
Experiments were conducted on the Astribot S1 dual-arm robot across five tasks: Pick and Place (Seen/OOD), Pack the Doll, Fold T-shirt, and Make Bouquets. Baselines included π₀, π₀.₅, and UniVLA.

- **Overall Performance and Precision:** CLAP-RF achieved the highest mean success rate (62.7%), outperforming π₀ (54.0%) and π₀.₅ (60.0%). CLAP-RF demonstrated higher precision on tasks requiring fine-grained control, such as "Pack the Doll" (90% P&P) and "Fold T-shirt" (40% success, compared to CLAP-NTP's 30%). This indicated that while CLAP-NTP excelled in high-level reasoning, CLAP-RF provided the necessary continuous control for complex dynamics.
- **Robust Generalization to OOD Objects:** CLAP-NTP maintained high performance on "Pick and Place (OOD)" (85% pick/place success), matching or exceeding its performance on seen objects. CLAP-RF showed a slight drop in OOD placement (70%), suggesting some sensitivity to visual distribution shifts, but remained competitive.
- **Semantic Understanding and Instruction Following:** Both CLAP-NTP and CLAP-RF performed strongly on "Make Bouquets," a task requiring language-conditioned object selection, achieving up to 50% success and matching or exceeding baselines.
- **Generalization via Human Demonstrations:** On "Make Bouquets (OOD)" and "PnP (OOD)," models trained solely on teleoperation data overfit. After fine-tuning with human pseudo-labeled data generated by VD-VAE, CLAP-RF improved significantly (e.g., "Make Bouquets (OOD)" from 10% to 45%; "PnP (OOD)" from 70% to 85%). UniVLA showed marginal gains (e.g., "Make Bouquets (OOD)" from 5% to 10%). Failure-case analysis showed a reduction in semantic errors (e.g., attempt failures reduced from 8 to 0 on "Make Bouquets (OOD)" with human data), confirming the effectiveness of human video data for semantic generalization.
- **Robustness Evaluation:** CLAP-RF demonstrated superior robustness to environmental perturbations (background changes, lighting variations, novel objects), achieving a mean success rate of 70.0% compared to π₀.₅ (56.7%) and UniVLA (16.7%). This robustness was attributed to CLAP's reduction of sensitivity to action-irrelevant visual factors.

**B. Simulation Results (LIBERO benchmark)**
CLAP-RF was evaluated in a generalist setting (single policy across all four task suites: Spatial, Object, Goal, Long).

- **Competitive Performance:** CLAP-RF achieved a strong average success rate of 97.2%, outperforming several state-of-the-art generalist models (e.g., SmolVLA at 88.8%, π₀ at 86.0%, π₀.₅ at 96.9%, FLOWER at 96.9%) and nearly matching X-VLA (98.1%). It also slightly exceeded some specialist baselines (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%), demonstrating its effectiveness in a challenging unified training setup.

**C. Ablation Studies**

- **Act-VAE Rate-Distortion:** An analysis showed that an Act-VAE configuration with `N_q = 16` and `K = 256` tokens provided an optimal balance between high reconstruction fidelity (PSNR) and semantic compactness, enabling effective downstream VLM training.
- **Backbone and Data Recipe:** Replacing the UniVLA-style Prismatic-7B backbone with Qwen3VL and incorporating the custom data recipe improved average success rates from 36.3% to 45.8%. However, the core CLAP alignment mechanism was identified as the primary source of the remaining performance improvement.
- **Contrastive Learning and Human Data:** Removing pseudo-positive labels resulted in a modest drop (from 60.0% to 58.8%), while removing the entire contrastive objective led to a larger degradation (to 53.8%, particularly on OOD tasks). Excluding human video data caused a severe performance collapse (average drop of 12.5%, "Make Bouquets (OOD)" down to 5%), confirming its crucial role in semantic generalization.
- **Post-Training Strategy:** Combining low-level and mid-level features for the RF head was found to be most effective. Knowledge Matching (KM) regularization proved crucial for robust target-domain adaptation, achieving 97.2% on LIBERO and 70% on PnP OOD, outperforming direct VLM fine-tuning (96.2% LIBERO, 60% PnP OOD) which showed weaker generalization.

**D. Additional Analysis**

- **Action Latent Space:** Visualizations of clustered action tokens confirmed strong semantic consistency across diverse robot (Astribot, AgiBot) and human (Ego4D) domains (e.g., "move right," "put down"). t-SNE analysis showed that contrastive learning produced more compact and well-separated clusters for semantically similar actions, indicating a better organized latent space.
- **Human Data Role:** An ablation on "Make Bouquets" indicated that human videos primarily reduced semantic failures (task/object-selection errors). Directly converting human videos to robot actions via estimated hand poses (e.g., WiLoR) proved unreliable and could degrade policy performance due to localization noise.
- **Inference Speed:** CLAP-RF significantly reduced inference latency to 70.1 ms, making it suitable for real-time applications, compared to the autoregressive CLAP-NTP at 382.0 ms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLAP framework contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by proposing a method that addresses key limitations in leveraging diverse data sources for generalist robot policies.

- **Enhanced Data Utilization:** The work demonstrates a viable approach to effectively incorporate large-scale, unlabeled human video demonstrations into robot learning. This is significant because it allows robot policies to benefit from the vast semantic diversity and richness of human data, mitigating the long-standing challenge of data scarcity in robotics.
- **Physically Grounded Latent Representations:** By explicitly aligning visual dynamics from human videos with an executable, quantized robot action space, CLAP successfully overcomes the "visual entanglement" issue prevalent in previous latent action models. This ensures that the learned representations encode genuine manipulation skills rather than irrelevant visual noise, which is fundamental for reliable robotic control.
- **Improved Generalization and Skill Transfer:** The established cross-modal alignment facilitates robust skill transfer from human demonstrations to robotic execution. The empirical results show improved generalization to unseen objects and varying environmental conditions, primarily driven by the ability to absorb semantic priors from human videos. This reduces the need for extensive, object-specific robot data collection.
- **Hierarchical Policy Formulation:** The dual formulation with CLAP-NTP and CLAP-RF offers a comprehensive approach, combining the semantic reasoning and instruction-following capabilities of autoregressive token-space models with the low-latency, high-precision continuous control offered by flow-matching action heads. This hierarchical design provides both cognitive flexibility and fine-motor control for complex manipulation tasks.
- **Robust Adaptation Strategy:** The introduction of Knowledge Matching regularization provides a method for efficiently fine-tuning pretrained policies on target embodiments without catastrophically forgetting the acquired knowledge. This contributes to practical deployment by enabling robust and stable adaptation.

The potential impact of this work includes accelerating the development and deployment of more capable and generalist robot manipulation systems. By making human video data more accessible and useful for robot learning, CLAP could reduce the reliance on costly and time-consuming robot-specific data collection. This could lead to robots that can learn new tasks and adapt to novel environments more readily, enhancing their utility in diverse applications from domestic assistance to industrial automation. Future research directions suggested by this work include exploring generalization to entirely new tasks, addressing morphological discrepancies between human and robot end-effectors, and simplifying the multi-stage training pipeline into an end-to-end learning paradigm.
