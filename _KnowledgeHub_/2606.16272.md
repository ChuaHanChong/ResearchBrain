# TopoRetarget: Interaction-Preserving Retargeting for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TopoRetarget, developed by researchers at Tsinghua University, provides a framework for translating human hand-object demonstrations into robot references that meticulously preserve local interaction structures and ensure kinematic feasibility. This method significantly enhances downstream reinforcement learning policies, achieving an 87.5% success rate on contact-rich manipulation tasks and enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to robot hardware.

## Problem

- The 'embodiment gap' between human and robot hands hinders the effective use of human hand-object demonstrations for robot dexterous manipulation.
- Existing retargeting methods often fail to preserve precise local hand-object interaction structures, leading to contact artifacts, penetrations, and kinematically infeasible robot poses.
- Poorly retargeted reference trajectories degrade the performance and robustness of downstream reinforcement learning policies, particularly for contact-rich and dynamic tasks.

## Method

- **TopoRetarget** formulates hand-object retargeting as a constrained optimization problem, integrating initial bone-direction alignment, interaction mesh construction, and ==topology-aware Laplacian optimization==.
- A Delaunay tetrahedralization is performed on the source (human) configuration to define an 'interaction edge set,' which is then reused for the robot configuration, preserving local hand-object neighborhood structure.
- Robot poses are refined through an optimization process minimizing weighted Laplacian coordinate differences, enforcing kinematic constraints, and robustly handling penetrations using inequality constraints and slack variable penalties.

## Results

- Achieved the lowest object-relative position displacement (**7.71** mm) and orientation error (**15.67**°) for contact preservation, demonstrating superior fidelity compared to baselines.
- Improved downstream RL policy performance, reaching an **87.5%** success rate on agile, contact-rich pen-spinning tasks, representing over 40 percentage points improvement compared to baselines.
- Enabled successful zero-shot transfer of policies, trained with TopoRetarget's references, to real-world Wuji Hand hardware for complex manipulation tasks, including scenarios with repeated in-hand contact changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving the local topological relationships and geometric fidelity between hand links and object surfaces is critical for generating high-quality and physically plausible robot references for dexterous manipulation.
> - Utilizing Laplacian coordinates, combined with an interaction mesh derived from source demonstrations, offers a robust approach to optimize for detailed interaction preservation and kinematic feasibility.
> - A single, fixed set of optimization parameters can effectively generalize across different robot embodiments and object scales, simplifying deployment and enhancing adaptability without task-specific tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of dexterous manipulation using reinforcement learning (RL) frequently leverages human hand-object demonstrations to provide dense reference motions for policy training. This approach helps to overcome the challenges of learning complex, contact-rich skills with high-dimensional robot hands. However, a significant hurdle in this process is the "embodiment gap" between human and robot hands. Directly applying human motion to robots often results in kinematic inconsistencies, physical impossibilities, or loss of crucial interaction details.

Existing dexterous hand retargeting methods have primarily focused on aligning robot hand poses with human hand poses. Techniques such as DexPilot, AnyTeleop, GeoRT, and studies analyzing various hand-centric objectives (e.g., fingertip correspondences, pinch relations) have enabled practical teleoperation and demonstration collection. While effective for basic pose transfer, these methods often fall short in preserving the intricate local hand-object interaction structures essential for contact-rich tasks. Specifically, they may not accurately specify where and how the robot should contact an object, leading to contact-region drift, invalid grasps, or unnatural joint configurations.

More recent research has attempted to integrate object motion, contact consistency (e.g., DexFlow, FunGrasp), or physical feasibility (e.g., SPIDER) into retargeting or policy learning pipelines. While these efforts have improved grasp or contact transfer, the generated motions frequently still necessitate downstream refinement, policy correction, or task-specific tuning before they can serve as reliable tracking references for RL. This dependency introduces additional complexity and potential for performance degradation in subsequent RL policy training.

TopoRetarget addresses this specific bottleneck by focusing on preserving the *topology* of local hand-object interaction. It aims to generate robot references that intrinsically maintain object-relative positional and directional relationships between hand links and object regions, alongside intra-hand relationships. This approach seeks to reduce the reliance on post-retargeting corrections or extensive task-specific tuning, thereby providing higher-fidelity references that are more directly usable for training robust RL policies, especially for dynamic and contact-intensive manipulation tasks like pen spinning. The work positions itself as an interaction-preserving reference generation method, aiming to enhance the quality and robustness of human-to-robot motion transfer for dexterous manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind TopoRetarget stems from the recognized utility of human hand-object demonstrations for training dexterous manipulation RL policies, coupled with the limitations of existing retargeting methods. While human demonstrations offer a rich source of reference motions, converting these into actionable robot trajectories without compromising task-critical hand-object interactions remains a challenge. Current retargeting approaches, largely hand-centric, often fail to adequately capture the nuances of contact structure, leading to artifacts such as contact misalignment, hand-object penetrations, or kinematically infeasible robot poses. These artifacts can significantly degrade the performance of downstream RL policies that rely on these demonstrations for tracking.

The key objectives of this research are formally stated as three primary contributions:

1. **To develop an interaction-preserving retargeting framework:** The primary objective is to create a method that precisely maintains local hand-object interaction during the retargeting process. This involves preserving object-relative positional and directional relationships between robot hand links and object regions, while simultaneously enforcing dexterous hand kinematic constraints and handling potential penetrations. A critical aspect of this objective is to achieve this with a *single set of parameters* that can be applied consistently across diverse retargeting conditions, thereby enhancing the method's generality and ease of use.

2. **To establish a lightweight reference-based RL tracking pipeline:** The research aims to demonstrate that the high-quality references generated by TopoRetarget can directly facilitate effective RL policy learning. This involves designing a minimal RL setup capable of tracking these retargeted trajectories and showcasing its ability to enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex, contact-rich dexterous skills, such as pen spinning and cube reorientation. This objective seeks to validate the practical utility of the interaction-preserving retargeting approach by demonstrating improved policy learning and transfer capabilities.

3. **To provide a dexterous hand-object interaction dataset:** A supplementary but important objective is to contribute to the research community by making available a dataset comprising retargeted trajectories, task references, and trained policies. This dataset is intended to promote reproducibility, allow for further comparative studies, and support future advancements in reference-based dexterous manipulation research.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more robust and interaction-aware retargeting solutions that can effectively bridge the human-robot embodiment gap, enabling RL policies to learn intricate dexterous manipulation skills from human demonstrations without extensive post-processing or task-specific tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TopoRetarget approaches the problem of interaction-preserving hand-object retargeting as a constrained optimization framework designed to convert human hand-object demonstrations into robot references. The method operates on a per-frame basis, building upon an initial guess and refining it through a topology-aware Laplacian optimization.

The overall algorithm involves three main stages: initialization, interaction mesh construction, and topology-aware Laplacian optimization.

#### 3.1. Relative Bone-Direction Initialization

The process begins by generating an initial estimate for the robot hand configuration at each time step. This is achieved by matching the local finger articulation of the human (source) hand to the robot (target) hand. For each non-terminal finger keypoint, a unit vector representing the bone direction to its child keypoint is defined. An energy term, $E_{bone}(q)$, quantifies the mismatch between the relative bone directions of the source and robot hands in their respective wrist-centered frames. The initial robot configuration $\tilde{q}^r_t$ is obtained by minimizing this bone-direction mismatch, regularized by a temporal consistency term that penalizes deviations from the previous frame's initial estimate $\tilde{q}^r_{t-1}$. This regularization promotes smooth transitions in the initial guess.

#### 3.2. Interaction Mesh Construction

To capture the local hand-object interaction structure, the method constructs shared interaction graphs for both the source (human) and target (robot) configurations at each frame. This involves:
- Defining hand-object vertex sets: These sets combine the 21 human MediaPipe hand keypoints (or corresponding robot keypoints) with $N_o$ surface samples drawn from the object mesh.
- Building a shared graph connectivity: Delaunay tetrahedralization is performed on the source vertices to establish an interaction edge set $I_t$. This same connectivity is then reused for the robot vertices, ensuring that both graphs ($G^s_t$ for source, $G^r_t(q)$ for robot) share an identical local neighborhood structure.
This shared graph allows for direct comparison and preservation of the demonstrated local hand-object relationships and facilitates augmentation across different object scales or hand embodiments by simply re-sampling object surfaces and rebuilding the graph.

#### 3.3. Topology-Aware Laplacian Optimization

Starting from the initial estimate $\tilde{q}^r_t$, the robot configuration is refined using a topology-aware Laplacian optimization.
- **Distance-Weighted Laplacian Coordinates:** For each edge $(i, j)$ in the shared interaction graph, distance-aware weights are computed based on the Euclidean distance between source vertices $v^s_{i,t}$ and $v^s_{j,t}$. These weights (normalized by their neighbors) introduce a spatial decay parameter $\kappa$, giving more importance to closer interactions. The weighted Laplacian coordinate $\Delta_t(V)_i$ for a vertex $i$ in a vertex set $V$ is defined as the weighted sum of vectors from $v_i$ to its neighbors $v_j$.
- **Interaction-Mesh Energy ($E_{IM}$):** This energy term aims to minimize the difference between the Laplacian coordinates of the robot configuration $V^r_t(q)$ and the source configuration $V^s_t$. Minimizing $E_{IM}$ effectively encourages the robot hand-object mesh to deform in a way that matches the local topological relationships observed in the human demonstration.
- **Final Optimization Problem:** The refinement is formulated as a constrained optimization problem (Equation 8). The objective function combines:
 - $E_{IM}(q)$: The primary term for interaction-mesh preservation.
 - $E_{bone}(q)$: A bone-direction prior, similar to the initialization, but with a different weight ($\lambda_{bone}$).
 - $E_{reg}(q; q^r_{t-1})$: A regularization term that enforces temporal smoothness in the optimizer coordinates and incorporates a lightweight prior for the robot's floating base pose (translation and rotation).
- **Penetration Handling:** The optimization includes explicit constraints to prevent hand-object penetration. For a query set $Q_t$ of hand-object pairs, $\phi_i(q)$ represents the signed distance. A soft tolerance $\tau$ and a hard bound $b$ are defined, along with slack variables $s_i$ and a penalty weight $w_s$. These constraints ensure that the robot hand does not excessively penetrate the object while allowing for minor, controlled contact.
Crucially, all parameters for this optimization are fixed across different experiments, emphasizing the framework's robustness and generality without requiring per-case tuning.

#### 3.4. Minimal RL Tracking Controller

To evaluate the quality of the retargeted trajectories, a minimal reinforcement learning setup is employed.
- **MDP Formulation:** The hand-object tracking task is defined as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **Policy Optimization:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to train policies.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs residual joint-position commands, which are added to the reference joint positions $q^{\theta,ref}_k$ to form the target joint positions $q^{\theta,tar}_t$. This residual parameterization allows the policy to correct tracking errors while leveraging the reference as a strong prior.
- **Observation Space:** Observations include proprioceptive information (joint state, previous action), object state (axis points), and lookahead reference features (joint, object, and hand-link references in the robot base frame). Noise is added to observations during training for robustness.
- **Reward Function:** The reward is a weighted sum of several terms:
 - Object tracking: A Gaussian kernel applied to the error between current and reference object axis points.
 - Hand-link tracking: A Gaussian kernel applied to the error between current and reference positions of tracked hand links (palm, fingers).
 - Joint tracking: A Gaussian kernel applied to the normalized finger joint error.
 - Action smoothness: Penalizes large changes in actions.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive randomization is applied during training (e.g., object mass, contact friction, actuator gains, joint damping, external perturbations) to enhance the policy's robustness and facilitate sim-to-real transfer.
- **Reference Initialization:** At each reset, a start frame is uniformly sampled from the reference trajectory, and the environment is initialized to the corresponding hand and object states, preventing overfitting to the trajectory's beginning.

This comprehensive methodology aims to create high-fidelity robot references that accurately preserve the subtle interaction patterns from human demonstrations, thereby simplifying and improving the downstream process of learning dexterous manipulation skills.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from three experimental categories designed to evaluate the effectiveness of TopoRetarget: interaction-preserving retargeting quality, downstream RL tracking policy performance, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Interaction-Preserving Retargeting Quality

To assess how well TopoRetarget preserves local hand-object interactions, the method was quantitatively evaluated against four baselines (OmniRetarget, Mink, DexPilot, GeoRT) on the ContactPose Dataset. The evaluation focused on contact precision ($E_{prec}$), contact alignment ($E_{align}$), and hand-object penetration.
- **Contact Precision and Alignment:** TopoRetarget achieved the lowest mean object-relative position displacement for contact links (7.71 mm) and the lowest mean object-relative orientation error (15.67°). These values indicate superior preservation of the relative spatial and directional relationships between the robot hand and the object at contact points compared to all baselines.
- **Penetration Handling:** The method demonstrated effective penetration handling, with a maximum hand-object penetration of 1.07 mm and 0% of frames exhibiting penetration greater than 2 mm. In contrast, several baselines showed significant interpenetration (e.g., Mink 20.12 mm max penetration, 84% frames with >2mm penetration; GeoRT 22.22 mm max penetration, 96% frames with >2mm penetration).
- **Solve Time:** TopoRetarget achieved an average solve time of 4.70 ms per frame, which is suitable for real-time retargeting, although some baselines (DexPilot, GeoRT, Mink) were faster.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visual comparisons highlighted that TopoRetarget effectively reduced common retargeting failure modes observed in baselines, such as contact-region drift, invalid grasps, misplaced non-tip contacts, and unnatural joint configurations (Figure 3).

#### 4.2. Downstream Tracking Policy Performance

The quality of retargeted trajectories was further assessed by their impact on downstream RL tracking policy training. Policies were trained using references generated by each retargeting method on two distinct datasets: Ho-cap (grasp-centric) and a self-collected MoCap Pen-Spin Dataset (dynamic, contact-rich). Success rate, object position error, and object rotation error were reported.
- **Ho-cap Dataset (Grasp-Centric):** TopoRetarget achieved the highest success rate at 84.4% (27/32 clips) and the lowest object position error (0.87 cm). Object rotation errors were broadly comparable across methods, suggesting that for relatively stable grasp-centric motions, most methods can generate trackable references to some extent.
- **MoCap Pen-Spin Dataset (Contact-Rich):** For the more challenging pen-spinning task, characterized by rapid motion and frequent non-tip contact transitions, TopoRetarget demonstrated a significant performance advantage. It achieved an 87.5% success rate (28/32 clips), which was over 40 percentage points higher than the best-performing baseline (OmniRetarget at 46.9%). TopoRetarget also yielded the lowest object position error (0.98 cm) and object rotation error (9.25°). This wider performance gap underscores the method's applicability and effectiveness in dynamic, contact-rich manipulation scenarios where precise interaction preservation is critical.
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Policies trained with TopoRetarget's references successfully transferred zero-shot to the physical Wuji Hand hardware for both cube reorientation and pen spinning tasks (Figure 4). This demonstrates the practical utility of the generated references for deploying learned contact-rich skills in the real world without further tuning.

#### 4.3. Generalization and Augmentation without Per-Case Retuning

The research also investigated TopoRetarget's ability to generalize across different robot hand embodiments and object scales using a single, fixed set of retargeting parameters.
- **Parameter Consistency:** The same human demonstrations were retargeted to different dexterous hand models and objects of varying scales without any modifications to the method's parameters.
- **Effective Transfer:** The results (Figure 5) visually confirmed that TopoRetarget successfully transferred both the general hand motion and, critically, the local hand-object interaction patterns under these changes. This is attributed to the interaction-mesh formulation, which allows for robust adaptation by rebuilding the local hand-object graph with recomputed edge weights based on the new object geometry. This capability facilitates the augmentation of human demonstrations for diverse robotic setups.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TopoRetarget addresses a fundamental challenge in robot learning from human demonstrations: the generation of high-fidelity, interaction-preserving robot trajectories. Its contributions have several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation Learning:** By providing robot references that accurately maintain the local hand-object interaction observed in human demonstrations, TopoRetarget directly improves the quality of data available for training reinforcement learning policies. This is particularly crucial for complex, contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks, where subtle contact patterns and transitions are vital for task success. The demonstrated significant increase in success rates for tasks like pen spinning indicates that the method enables more effective and robust policy learning.

2. **Reduced Bottlenecks in RL Pipelines:** The framework's ability to produce high-quality references with a single set of parameters across diverse conditions simplifies the overall RL pipeline. It reduces the need for extensive post-retargeting refinement, policy correction, or task-specific tuning, which are common limitations of prior methods. This efficiency can accelerate research and development in dexterous manipulation.

3. **Facilitation of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer demonstrated on the Wuji Hand for complex tasks highlights the practical utility of TopoRetarget. By generating physically plausible and kinematically consistent trajectories that preserve crucial interaction details, the method helps bridge the reality gap, allowing policies trained in simulation to perform effectively on physical hardware without substantial real-world data or tuning. This capability is critical for deploying robots in real-world applications.

4. **Improved Generalization and Data Augmentation:** The interaction-mesh formulation enables the method to adapt human demonstrations to different robot hand embodiments and object scales without requiring re-tuning of parameters. This capacity for generalization and augmentation is valuable for expanding the applicability of a limited set of human demonstrations across various robotic platforms and task variations, potentially reducing the data collection burden.

5. **Advancement in Motion Retargeting Research:** TopoRetarget contributes to the field of motion retargeting by emphasizing the topological preservation of local hand-object interactions rather than solely focusing on hand pose. This paradigm shift offers a robust approach for handling the complexities of multi-contact and dynamic manipulation.

6. **Support for Reproducible Research:** The commitment to providing a dataset of retargeted trajectories, task references, and trained policies fosters reproducibility and provides a valuable resource for the research community, encouraging further exploration and comparison of methods in dexterous manipulation.

In conclusion, TopoRetarget represents a substantial step towards making human demonstrations a more robust and direct source of learning signals for dexterous robot manipulation, paving the way for more capable and autonomously learning robotic systems.
