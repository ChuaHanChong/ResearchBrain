# Reset-Free Lifelong Learning with Skill-Space Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Lifelong Skill Planning (LiSP) tackles reset-free lifelong RL by learning unsupervised skills (extending DADS) via a model-free skill policy plus a learned skill-practice curriculum, then doing long-horizon MPC planning over the skill space instead of raw actions. Skills work online or offline. LiSP avoids catastrophic sink-state failures where SAC/MBPO/MOReL collapse, on gridworld and MuJoCo benchmarks.

## Problem

- Standard model-free RL (SAC, MBPO) fails catastrophically once resets are removed, collapsing into unrecoverable sink states despite near-perfect pretrained initialization.
- Prior model-based MPC planners are restricted to short horizons because compounding dynamics-model errors make long-horizon action-space planning inaccurate and unstable.
- Existing skill-discovery and offline RL methods assume episodic resets or single-task stationary settings, so they cannot learn or act safely in non-stationary, non-episodic lifelong environments.

## Method

- ==Model-based skill discovery== extends DADS with a learned skill-practice distribution that acts as a curriculum, proposing which skills to practice from each state using short model-generated rollouts.
- Offline skill learning adjusts intrinsic reward with a ==model-disagreement penalty== (à la MOReL), penalizing skills whose ensemble prediction variance exceeds a threshold to stay within dataset support.
- **Skill-space planning** (Algorithm 3) runs ==MPPI-based MPC== over sequences of latent skills rather than raw actions, letting the skill policy constrain the search space for accurate long-horizon (H=180) planning.

## Results

- On Lifelong Hopper and Lifelong Ant, **LiSP** remains stable across nonstationary task changes while SAC and MOReL fail catastrophically and plain action-space MPC underperforms it.
- Skill-space planning cuts one-step dynamics-model prediction error variance versus uniform-action planning and enables stable **180-step** horizons where action-space MPC diverges.
- Removing the skill-practice curriculum degrades 2D Minecraft asymptotic return sharply, and fully-offline LiSP scores **0.81-0.88** normalized performance across three Hopper tasks versus **0.27-0.49** for MPC baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining a planner's action space to a learned, distinguishable skill set reduces compounding model error, letting MPC extend to horizons impractical for raw actions.
> - Gradient-based policy/critic updates, not the environment itself, are the main source of instability in reset-free settings; planning that avoids relying on a live critic is inherently more robust.
> - A learned curriculum over what to practice matters as much as what is learned: naive uniform skill sampling starves short-rollout skill discovery of usable training signal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Reinforcement Learning (RL) has made progress in optimizing agents for specific tasks, typically within episodic environments where agents are reset to a starting state after each trial. However, a significant challenge arises when applying RL to real-world scenarios, which are often non-stationary and non-episodic. In such "lifelong" learning settings, agents must continuously adapt to changing environments and goals without the benefit of manual resets.

Prior work in lifelong RL has identified several limitations of existing RL algorithms in these challenging contexts. Standard model-free and model-based RL methods, when deployed in non-episodic settings, have been shown to suffer catastrophic failures. These failures can be attributed to issues such as succumbing to "sink states" – states from which agents cannot escape and where learning signals are low – and inherent instability of gradient-based updates in continuous, non-episodic interaction. Previous research has highlighted that even with access to ground truth dynamics models, model-free RL algorithms struggle to adapt to nonstationarity.

This paper addresses the specific gaps related to reset-free and non-stationary lifelong learning, particularly the need for algorithms that can learn from both online and offline interactions. While existing model-based planning approaches like Model Predictive Control (MPC) offer some benefits for long-term reasoning, they often struggle with compounding model errors over extended horizons and require careful tuning. Skill discovery methods, such as Dynamics-Aware Discovery of Skills (DADS), learn high-level behaviors, but their application in reset-free, non-episodic contexts or their integration with long-horizon planning for robustness has been less explored. Offline RL, which learns from logged data without real-world interaction, typically focuses on single-task, stationary environments and grapples with issues like value overestimation.

This work integrates elements from model-based RL, skill discovery, and offline RL to propose a framework designed to overcome the stability and adaptation challenges in reset-free lifelong learning. By focusing on planning in an abstract skill space rather than raw action space, the research aims to improve the robustness and effectiveness of RL agents in environments that more closely resemble real-world operational conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an algorithmic framework that enables reinforcement learning agents to continuously adapt and interact effectively in non-stationary and non-episodic environments, a setting referred to as "reset-free lifelong learning."

The motivation for this objective stems from several key challenges observed in current RL methodologies:

1. **Failure in Non-Episodic Settings:** Standard RL algorithms, including state-of-the-art model-free (e.g., Soft Actor Critic - SAC) and model-based (e.g., Model-Based Policy Optimization - MBPO) methods, exhibit catastrophic performance degradation when trained in non-episodic, reset-free environments, even after extensive pre-training. This instability suggests that current gradient-based RL is not inherently robust to the continuous, unreset environment dynamics of real-world scenarios.

2. **Susceptibility to Sink States:** A core issue identified is that agents can fall into "sink states," which are configurations from which recovery is difficult or impossible, leading to a halting of learning progress or permanent failure. In the absence of resets, escaping such states is critical for an agent's long-term survival and continued learning.

3. **Real-world Applicability:** Real-world agents (e.g., robots, autonomous vehicles) typically operate continuously without external resets. They also encounter non-stationary dynamics and goals, requiring constant adaptation. The goal is to develop autonomous agents that can learn without laborious human supervision in these high-stakes, non-stationary environments.

4. **Inefficiency of Online Data Collection:** Interacting with real-world environments can be expensive and time-consuming. Leveraging existing offline datasets alongside online interactions is therefore a desirable capability for efficient learning.

5. **Limitations of Action-Space Planning:** While model-based planning offers benefits like long-horizon reasoning, planning directly in the high-dimensional action space can suffer from compounding model errors over longer horizons, leading to unstable or inaccurate predictions.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes to:
- Develop a framework that combines model-free policy learning with model-based planning.
- Introduce the concept of planning in an abstract "skill space" to enable longer planning horizons and mitigate model errors.
- Incorporate mechanisms for unsupervised skill discovery from both online and offline data.
- Demonstrate the robustness of the proposed framework in challenging non-stationary, reset-free benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithmic framework, Lifelong Skill Planning (LiSP), addresses reset-free lifelong reinforcement learning by combining model-based planning with unsupervised skill discovery. LiSP operates continuously, updating its components from online interactions while also being capable of learning from offline data.

**Core Components:**

1. **Primitive Dynamics Model (fφ):** LiSP employs a probabilistic ensemble of neural networks, {fφi}, to model the environment's transition dynamics, P(s'|s, a). This model predicts the mean and variance of the next state given a current state and action. It is continuously updated to maximize the log probability of observed transitions in the true replay buffer (D). This model is crucial for both generating synthetic rollouts for policy training and for planning.

2. **Low-Level Skill Policy (πθ):** A skill-conditioned policy, πθ(a|s, z), is learned. This policy maps a state `s` and a latent skill `z` to an action `a`. Skills are abstract, higher-order behaviors, and `z` typically resides in a fixed, simple prior space (e.g., uniform distribution).

3. **Discriminator (qν):** A discriminator network, qν(s'|s, z), is trained to approximate the intractable posterior p(s'|s, z). This discriminator is used to compute an intrinsic reward, ˜r(s, z, s'), which is a variational approximation to the mutual information I(s'; z|s). This reward encourages the discovery of predictable and distinguishable skills, promoting empowerment.

4. **Skill-Practice Distribution (pψ):** This component is a "teacher" network, pψ(z|s), which proposes which skills `z` are most useful or relevant to practice at a particular state `s`. It generates a curriculum for skill learning, enabling more efficient and targeted skill discovery, especially with short model rollouts.

**Learning Process (Algorithm 1: Lifelong Skill Planning):**

The LiSP framework proceeds as follows:
- **Initialization:** The agent initializes its dynamics model ensemble, skill policy, discriminator, and skill-practice distribution. An optional pretraining phase can occur if offline data is available.
- **Continuous Learning Loop:** While the agent is active, it continuously performs the following steps:
 - **Dynamics Model Update:** The dynamics model fφ is updated using real environment transitions stored in the replay buffer D.
 - **Policy and Skill Updates (Algorithm 2: Model-Based Skill Discovery):**
 - **Synthetic Rollout Generation:** Short rollouts are generated using the learned dynamics model fφ. For each rollout, a state s0 is sampled from D, and a skill z is sampled from the skill-practice distribution pψ(·|s0). An action a is taken according to πθ(·|s0, z), and the next state s1 is predicted by fφ (sampling from one of the ensemble members). The transition (s0, a, z, s1) is added to a generated replay buffer, D̂.
 - **Discriminator Update:** The discriminator qν is updated on these generated transitions to maximize log qν(s1|s0, z).
 - **Intrinsic Reward Calculation:** Intrinsic rewards ˜radjusted are calculated for the transitions in D̂ using the updated qν.
 - **Policy and Skill-Practice Update:** Both πθ and pψ are trained using Soft Actor Critic (SAC) to optimize ˜radjusted based on minibatches from D̂.
 - **Action Execution:** The agent obtains an action using skill-space planning (Algorithm 3) and executes it in the environment. The resulting real environment transition is added to D.

**Offline Skill Learning (Section 3.1.1):**
For offline learning, the intrinsic reward ˜r is adjusted to ˜radjusted. This adjustment penalizes actions if the disagreement among the ensemble of dynamics models (indicating epistemic uncertainty) exceeds a threshold αthres. This mechanism encourages the policy to operate within the support of the offline dataset, mitigating value overestimation common in offline RL.

**Skill-Space Planning (Algorithm 3: GetAction):**
- **MPC with MPPI:** LiSP utilizes Model Predictive Control (MPC) with the Model Path Predictive Integral (MPPI) optimizer to select actions.
- **Planning in Skill Space:** Instead of planning over primitive actions, LiSP plans over sequences of skills (z), where each skill is executed by the low-level policy πθ. This constrains the search space, focusing on meaningful behaviors learned by the skill policy.
- **Long-Horizon Planning:** The use of skills enables more accurate planning over longer horizons (H) compared to action-space planning.
- **Process:** For each planning iteration, MPPI samples skill sequences, estimates their returns using the dynamics model fφ and skill policy πθ, and then reweighs and updates the distribution of skill sequences. The first skill from the optimal sequence is then used to derive the action a to be executed in the environment.

**Lifelong Learning Benchmarks:**
The authors extended standard gridworld and MuJoCo OpenAI Gym environments into "Lifelong" benchmarks. These environments feature non-episodic interaction, non-stationary dynamics (e.g., changing target velocities), and the presence of "sink states" (e.g., pitfalls, conditions leading to agent collapse) to specifically test the robustness and adaptation capabilities of lifelong RL algorithms. These benchmarks optionally include an offline pretraining phase from a starting dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations focused on LiSP's effectiveness in lifelong benchmarks, the advantages of skill-space planning, the role of the skill-practice distribution, and offline skill learning.

1. **Effectiveness on Lifelong Benchmarks:**
 - **Nonstationary MuJoCo (Lifelong Hopper, Lifelong Ant):** LiSP agents demonstrated stability and adapted to changing target velocities throughout their lifetime, maintaining performance despite nonstationarity and the presence of sink states. In contrast, baseline algorithms such as SAC (model-free RL), MOReL (offline model-based RL), and action-space MPC exhibited significant instability and catastrophic failures. LiSP consistently outperformed these baselines.
 - **Minimizing Resets (2D Volcano Environment):** In a gridworld environment with permanent sink states (pitfalls), model-based methods, including LiSP, effectively avoided getting trapped. LiSP and other model-based approaches required fewer "resets" (interventions to untrap the agent) compared to SAC, indicating their robustness in environments with critical failure states.

2. **Advantages of Long-Horizon Skill-Space Planning:**
 - **Constraining Model Error:** An analysis of one-step dynamics model prediction error in Lifelong Hopper showed that actions generated by the skill policy exhibited significantly lower variance in error compared to uniformly sampled random actions. This reduction in error variance helps to mitigate compounding model errors over long planning horizons.
 - **Constraining Planning Search Space:** Skill-space planning enabled stable long-horizon planning (e.g., H=180) in Lifelong Hopper. Action-space MPC, without the skill-based constraint, proved unstable with such long horizons due to accumulated unconstrained model errors, leading to catastrophic failures.
 - **Model Update Stability:** Experiments indicated that if only the dynamics model is updated, planning remains stable. This contrasts with the instability observed when policy/critic updates are continuously applied in reset-free settings for agents relying on value functions. This supports the notion that long-horizon planning provides stability over reliance on value function estimates.

3. **Online Skill Learning with a Model:**
 - **Hierarchical Skill Learning (2D Minecraft):** The skill-practice distribution (pψ) was critical for learning useful hierarchical skills in a 2D Minecraft environment. LiSP, utilizing this curriculum-generating distribution, significantly outperformed baselines that sampled skills uniformly or minimized intrinsic reward. The results suggested that directed practice sampling is necessary for learning useful skills in complex, hierarchical tasks.
 - **Learning with Short Rollouts:** The skill-practice distribution was found to be important for maintaining skill diversity and learning signal when using short, model-generated rollouts. It helps associate specific states with relevant skills, which is particularly crucial when long model rollouts are unstable due to compounding errors.

4. **Learning Skills Entirely from Offline Data:**
 - LiSP demonstrated the ability to learn a set of skills entirely from an offline dataset and subsequently use these skills for planning across multiple tasks (e.g., different target velocities in Lifelong Hopper) without further online gradient updates. In these offline settings, LiSP achieved higher performance compared to action-space MPC with both long and short planning horizons. Even with a reduced offline dataset size, LiSP maintained stability, although with some reduction in task performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Lifelong Skill Planning (LiSP) as a framework addressing critical challenges in the deployment of reinforcement learning agents in real-world scenarios. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Advancement in Lifelong RL:** LiSP contributes to the development of agents capable of continuous adaptation in non-stationary, non-episodic environments, which is a key requirement for autonomous systems operating over extended durations without human intervention. This moves beyond traditional episodic RL setups, which often do not reflect real-world learning conditions.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Stability:** The framework demonstrates a method to overcome catastrophic failures and susceptibility to "sink states" that plague standard RL algorithms in reset-free settings. By integrating skill-space planning, LiSP offers a more stable and robust approach to decision-making, which is crucial for safety-critical applications.

3. **Improved Long-Horizon Planning:** Planning in an abstract skill space rather than the primitive action space allows for more accurate and stable long-horizon planning. This addresses a known limitation of model-based RL, where compounding model errors often restrict planning horizons, thereby enabling more complex and temporally extended behaviors.

4. **Flexible Skill Acquisition:** The ability to learn skills from both online interactions and offline datasets broadens the applicability of the framework. This flexibility is valuable in real-world contexts where extensive online interaction might be costly or impossible, and significant amounts of historical data are available. The introduction of a skill-practice distribution further enhances the efficiency of online skill learning by generating a targeted curriculum.

5. **New Benchmarks for Future Research:** The introduction of new "Lifelong" benchmarks for gridworld and MuJoCo environments provides a standardized testbed for evaluating future lifelong RL algorithms under realistic reset-free and non-stationary conditions, fostering further research in this challenging area.

6. **Practical Implications for Autonomous Systems:** The principles and methods introduced by LiSP could lead to the development of more adaptable, resilient, and continuously learning autonomous robots, intelligent agents, and other AI systems that can operate effectively and safely in dynamic and open-ended real-world environments. This includes applications in areas such as robotics, industrial automation, and adaptive control systems.
