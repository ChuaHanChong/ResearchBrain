# MetaTune: Adjoint-based Meta-tuning via Robotic Differentiable Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Huazhong University of Science and Technology and National University of Singapore introduce MetaTune, a unified adjoint-based meta-learning framework that jointly and automatically tunes robotic feedback controllers and disturbance observers using differentiable dynamics. This approach achieved superior tracking accuracy, reducing RMSE by 15-20% at higher speeds and up to 40% under strong disturbances in sim-to-sim transfer experiments on a quadrotor.

## Problem

- Conventional tuning of coupled feedback controller and disturbance observer parameters is difficult and often suboptimal, particularly for nonlinear systems and time-varying disturbances.
- Existing adaptive control methods lack generalization under unknown disturbances, relying on offline designs or predefined conditions.
- Differentiable physics approaches for gradient-based tuning face computational inefficiency, with complexity scaling quadratically for long-horizon and high-dimensional problems.

## Method

- Proposes **MetaTune**, a unified ==meta-learning== framework for the joint, automatic tuning of feedback controller gains and disturbance observer parameters.
- Utilizes a ==neural network policy== to generate adaptive, ==time-varying gains== based on task features, enabling flexible gain scheduling.
- Implements a ==discrete adjoint method== for meta-gradient computation, allowing efficient propagation of gradients backward in time with ==linear complexity== in the data horizon.

## Results

- The adjoint method reduced gradient computation time by over **50%** compared to forward-mode methods, while yielding identical RMSE for fixed-gain tuning.
- MetaTune achieved the lowest RMSE across all tested methods in differentiable simulation, demonstrating superior tracking accuracy through policy-augmented adaptation.
- Exhibited robust zero-shot sim-to-sim transfer, achieving **15-20%** RMSE reduction at higher speeds and approximately **40%** under extreme disturbances on a quadrotor in PX4-Gazebo compared to a fixed-gain controller.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adjoint-based gradient computation is essential for computational efficiency and physical consistency in differentiable control, especially for long horizons and complex systems.
> - Joint optimization of coupled controller and observer parameters, rather than decoupled tuning, is critical for superior disturbance rejection and stable tracking performance.
> - Neural policies, leveraging physics-informed adjoint gradients, can achieve highly adaptive gain scheduling that generalizes well across tasks and environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The control of robotic systems operating in complex and uncertain real-world environments presents challenges, necessitating robust strategies against stochastic disturbances and modeling uncertainties. Disturbance observer-based control, leveraging frameworks such as Kalman filters, Luenberger observers, disturbance observers (DOBs), extended state observers (ESOs), and sliding-mode observers (SMOs), has emerged as a method to robustify robotic systems. However, a significant limitation of these architectures is their critical dependence on the coordinated tuning of control and estimation parameters, particularly in nonlinear systems or those subjected to time-varying disturbances.

Traditional approaches have included fixed-gain control, which offers limited adaptability, and variable-gain strategies like gain scheduling, LQR-based tuning, and fuzzy control. While these methods introduce some adaptability, they often rely on offline design rules or predefined operating conditions, which restricts their generalization capabilities under unknown disturbances. Adaptive control, such as Model Reference Adaptive Control (MRAC), offers a more principled approach by adjusting gains based on real-time system behavior, but still depends on predefined adaptive laws or expert design.

More recently, learning-based approaches have sought to enhance robustness by leveraging data-driven representations. These can be broadly categorized into disturbance-aware learning (explicitly estimating disturbances or implicitly updating policies) and intelligent parameter adaptation. The latter includes reinforcement learning (RL) for gain tuning and meta-learning, which aims for rapid adaptation and generalization across tasks.

A notable recent development is the integration of differentiable physics, where system dynamics are modeled as differentiable computational graphs. This allows for gradient-based optimization of controller or estimator parameters using analytical gradients of task-level losses propagated through the control loop. Examples include DiffTune and Neural MHE.

This work addresses a specific gap within these differentiable tuning methodologies: the interplay between control and estimation. Existing disturbance-aware methods often focus on disturbance compensation while overlooking feedback controller optimization or are constrained by fixed models. Parameter adaptation strategies, while efficient for gain tuning, frequently treat control and estimation as decoupled processes. This decoupled perspective fails to account for the intrinsic coupling in disturbance-observer-based architectures, potentially leading to suboptimal performance or instability by not balancing disturbance rejection and noise sensitivity effectively. MetaTune positions itself by proposing a unified framework that explicitly addresses this coupling through joint tuning using differentiable closed-loop meta-learning, and by introducing an adjoint-based method to overcome computational scaling issues associated with existing forward-mode differentiable physics approaches for complex, long-horizon problems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop MetaTune, a unified framework for the joint, automatic tuning of feedback controllers and disturbance observers. This framework aims to integrate a portable neural policy with physics-informed gradients derived from differentiable system dynamics, thereby enabling adaptive gain across various tasks and operating conditions.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in robust robotic control:

- **Coupled Parameter Tuning Challenge:** Disturbance observer-based control systems are effective in robustifying robots against uncertainties. However, the gains of the feedback controller and the parameters of the disturbance observer are strongly coupled. Tuning these parameters conventionally (e.g., manually or through isolated analyses) is difficult and often leads to suboptimal performance, especially in the presence of time-varying and nonlinear disturbances that are hard to model explicitly. This research seeks a coordinated solution in the joint parameter space to optimize overall closed-loop performance.
- **Limitations of Existing Adaptive Methods:** While variable-gain and adaptive control strategies exist, they often rely on offline-designed rules, predefined operating conditions, or expert-designed adaptive laws. These limitations restrict their ability to generalize effectively under unknown disturbances and evolving environments. Learning-based approaches offer a promising alternative by leveraging data-driven representations, but existing methods often fall short in addressing the intrinsic coupling between control and estimation.
- **Decoupled Control and Estimation:** Many learning-based approaches for robust control either focus on explicit disturbance compensation, sometimes overlooking the feedback controller's optimization, or treat control and estimation as isolated processes when adjusting gains. This decoupled perspective is problematic in disturbance-observer-based architectures where the controller operates on the observer's output rather than the true state, meaning the effective plant includes the estimator dynamics. Consequently, balancing trade-offs like disturbance rejection and noise sensitivity becomes challenging, leading to potentially unstable or suboptimal closed-loop behavior. The research aims to address this by explicitly formulating the tuning problem as a differentiable optimization over closed-loop executions, ensuring controller and observer are jointly optimized.
- **Computational Inefficiency of Gradient-Based Tuning:** Recent differentiable physics frameworks have enabled gradient-based parameter optimization. However, methods relying on forward sensitivity analysis (like DiffTune) face a fundamental limitation: their computational complexity for computing trajectory-level gradients scales quadratically with the horizon length and depends on the parameter dimension. This becomes prohibitive when observer dynamics are incorporated, significantly amplifying computational costs due to augmented state and gain dimensions. The motivation here is to develop a more efficient gradient computation method to enable scalable learning for high-dimensional, long-horizon problems.

To achieve these objectives and address these motivations, the authors propose a novel adjoint-based method for computing meta-gradients. This method aims to efficiently propagate gradients backward in time, reducing computational complexity to be linear in the data horizon and independent of the parameter dimension. This efficiency is critical for integrating observer dynamics into the differentiable tuning process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of MetaTune centers on a differentiable, adjoint-based meta-learning framework for the joint tuning of feedback controller gains ($\theta_k$) and disturbance observer parameters ($\psi_k$).

**A. Problem Formulation**
The system considered is a discrete-time nonlinear dynamical system subject to generalized disturbances $d_k$:
$x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k) + d_k$
where $x$ is the system state and $u$ is the control input. The disturbance $d_k$ is unknown and unstructured. To mitigate disturbances, a disturbance observer is constructed with an augmented state $\hat{x}_k = [\hat{x}_{sys,k}, \hat{d}_k]^\top$:
$\hat{x}_{k+1} = o(\hat{x}_k, u_k, y_{k+1}; \psi_k)$
where $y_k = g(x_k)$ are measurements, and $\psi_k$ are time-varying observer parameters. The control input $u_k$ is calculated using the estimated state $\hat{x}_k$ and a reference trajectory $x_{d_k}$ via a parameterized feedback law:
$u_k = h(\hat{x}_k, x_{d_k}; \theta_k)$
where $\theta_k$ are time-varying controller gains. The functions $f, g, o, h$ are assumed differentiable. The joint tuning task is formulated as minimizing a cumulative cost $J = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \ell(\hat{x}_k, x_k, u_k)$ subject to these closed-loop dynamics, where $\ell$ is a per-step loss function. This formulation treats controller and observer parameters as decision variables that are intrinsically coupled.

**B. Control-oriented Meta-Learning with Neural Parameterization**
MetaTune adopts a control-oriented meta-learning perspective where the time-varying controller and observer gains (collectively $\eta_k = [\theta_k, \psi_k]$) are outputs of a neural network policy $\pi_\xi(T_k)$. Here, $\xi$ represents the neural network parameters, and $T_k$ are task features (e.g., current observation, desired state, ESO state, trajectory coefficients, current time) that influence system behavior. The policy flexibly adjusts $\eta_k$ for different task features, enabling adaptation. The raw neural network outputs are mapped through a sigmoid function and scaled to ensure gains remain within predefined physical safety limits. The overall objective is to find optimal neural network parameters $\xi^*$ that minimize the expected closed-loop performance over a distribution of tasks.

The gradient of the total loss $J$ with respect to the network weights $\xi$ is computed using the chain rule:
$\nabla_\xi J = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left( \frac{\partial \eta_k^i}{\partial \xi} \right)^\top \frac{\partial \ell^i}{\partial \eta_k^i}$
where $M$ is the number of tasks, $N$ is the horizon length, $\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial \eta_k}$ is the sensitivity computed via the adjoint method, and $\frac{\partial \eta_k}{\partial \xi}$ is the Jacobian of the neural network obtained via backpropagation. An optimizer (e.g., Adam) iteratively updates $\xi$.

**C. Adjoint-based Gradient Computation**
To overcome the computational limitations of forward sensitivity analysis (which scales quadratically with horizon length and depends on parameter dimension), MetaTune employs a discrete adjoint method for backward sensitivity analysis. The core idea is to compute gradients of the task-level loss with respect to the adaptive gains by propagating adjoint variables backward in time.

1. **Augmented Lagrangian:** The closed-loop system is represented by an augmented Lagrangian:
 $L = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left[ \ell(x_k, \hat{x}_k, u_k) + \lambda_{x,k+1}^\top (f(x_k, u_k) - x_{k+1}) + \lambda_{\hat{x},k+1}^\top (o(\hat{x}_k, x_k, u_k; \psi) - \hat{x}_{k+1}) + \lambda_{u,k}^\top (h(\hat{x}_k, x_{d_k}; \theta) - u_k) \right]$
 Here, $\lambda_{x,k}$, $\lambda_{\hat{x},k}$, and $\lambda_{u,k}$ are adjoint variables associated with the plant dynamics, observer dynamics, and control input constraint, respectively.

2. **Adjoint Recursions:** Taking stationarity conditions of the Lagrangian with respect to $u_k$, $\hat{x}_k$, and $x_k$ yields the coupled adjoint recursions:
 - **Control Channel Adjoint ($\lambda_{u,k}$):** Obtained from $\nabla_{u_k} L = 0$. It aggregates the marginal contribution of $u_k$ through the immediate loss, plant dynamics, and observer update.
 - **Observer State Adjoint ($\lambda_{\hat{x},k}$):** Propagated backward through the observer and control policy dynamics.
 - **Plant State Adjoint ($\lambda_{x,k}$):** Propagated backward through the plant and observer dynamics.
 These adjoint variables are propagated backward in time, typically initialized to zero at the terminal step if no explicit terminal cost exists. This backward recursion efficiently encodes the cumulative cost-to-go effect.

3. **Parameter Gradients:** The gradients with respect to the tunable parameters $\theta$ and $\psi$ are obtained by accumulating their contributions along the trajectory:
 $\nabla_\theta L = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left( \frac{\partial h}{\partial \theta} \right)^\top \lambda_{u,k}$
 $\nabla_\psi L = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left( \frac{\partial o}{\partial \psi} \right)^\top \lambda_{\hat{x},k+1}$
 These gain-level gradients are then used in conjunction with the neural network's Jacobian (via backpropagation) to compute the full gradient $\nabla_\xi J$, as per the chain rule, for updating the meta-policy parameters $\xi$.

**D. Experimental Setup**
The framework is evaluated on a quadrotor platform with fully differentiable dynamics, a geometric SE(3) controller, and a nonlinear extended state observer (ESO). Controller gains ($k_p, k_v, k_R, k_\Omega \in \mathbb{R}^3$, total 12D for $\theta$) and ESO gains ($\psi \in \mathbb{R}^6$, total 6D for $\psi$) are jointly tuned, resulting in an 18-dimensional parameter vector $\Theta$. The implementation uses Python with JAX for differentiable simulation.
Comparison methods include various forward-mode DiffTune baselines (DT-Base, DT-Fixed, DT-Adaptive (History), DT-Adaptive (CTG)) and adjoint-based baselines (Adj-Fixed, Adj-Adaptive, which is MetaTune). The objective function is a quadratic per-step cost on state error, observer state error, and control input. The learnable policy is a single-hidden-layer MLP with 128 ReLU units, outputting the 18-dimensional gain vector, which is updated at 20 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated MetaTune on a quadrotor platform in both a differentiable JAX simulator and a high-fidelity PX4-Gazebo hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) simulator.

**A. Physical Gradient Validation and Computational Efficiency**
- **Gradient Correctness:** The Adj-Fixed method (adjoint-based tuning with fixed gains) demonstrated RMSE values identical to DT-Fixed (forward-mode tuning with fixed gains), numerically validating the derived adjoint equations and confirming that the backward propagation yields physically consistent gradients.
- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - Adj-Fixed reduced gradient computation time by over 50% compared to DT-Fixed.
 - DT-Adaptive (History), a forward-mode adaptive variant, showed training instabilities due to incorrect temporal credit assignment.
 - DT-Adaptive (CTG), another forward-mode adaptive variant, recovered performance but incurred prohibitive computational costs, rendering it impractical.
 - The proposed Adj-Adaptive (MetaTune) method achieved the lowest RMSE across all variants while maintaining computational efficiency, performing orders of magnitude faster than DT-Adaptive (CTG). This validates the adjoint method's advantage in scalability for high-dimensional, time-varying parameter optimization.

**B. Policy-Augmented Adaptation and Generalization in Differentiable Simulation**
- **Adaptability under Disturbance:** The DiffTune baseline, which optimizes only fixed controller gains, exhibited persistent steady-state errors and limited adaptability under disturbances. This suggests that without joint optimization and proper temporal credit assignment, disturbance effects accumulate.
- **Improved Tracking with Adjoint Methods:** Adjoint-based tuning (Adj-Adaptive) enabled more effective joint adaptation of controller and observer parameters. By propagating cost-to-go sensitivities backward, disturbance-related errors were attributed correctly to earlier parameter choices, leading to improved disturbance rejection and more consistent tracking.
- **MetaTune's Performance:** The full MetaTune framework, incorporating the MLP-based gain scheduling policy trained with the adjoint method, achieved the tightest tracking accuracy among all tested methods (Fig. 3). This indicates that the neural network parameterization effectively leverages the joint gradient information for precise control, outperforming direct gradient-based tuning methods without neural policies.

**C. Sim-to-Sim Transfer to PX4-Gazebo**
The policy trained in the simplified differentiable JAX environment was deployed to a high-fidelity PX4-Gazebo HIL simulator without further fine-tuning, demonstrating zero-shot transfer capabilities. Performance was compared against an adjoint-optimized fixed-gain controller (Baseline).

- **Adaptation under High-Speed Flight:** At low speeds (2 m/s), MetaTune and the Baseline showed comparable performance. However, as flight speeds increased (3-4 m/s), MetaTune demonstrated superior generalization, achieving an average RMSE reduction of approximately 15-20% compared to the fixed-gain Baseline across various trajectories and wind conditions. The Baseline's fixed-gain structure could not adequately compensate for nonlinear aerodynamic effects during aggressive maneuvers, while MetaTune's adaptive policy showed better stability and responsiveness.
- **Robustness under Strong Disturbances:** MetaTune's robustness advantage became more pronounced under strong external wind torque disturbances (1 N·m and 2 N·m). The Baseline controller experienced significant performance degradation and even crashes under these persistent loads, especially during dynamically demanding Figure-8 trajectories. In contrast, MetaTune maintained a more consistent tracking profile. In extreme scenarios combining high-velocity flight with maximum disturbance, MetaTune achieved an RMSE reduction of approximately 40% compared to the Baseline. This indicates the learned joint controller-observer adaptation effectively mitigates tracking divergence by real-time gain tuning in response to external torques. (Fig. 5 and 6 illustrate improved disturbance estimation and reduced position errors).
- **Sim-to-Sim Generalization Capability:** The consistent and robust performance of MetaTune across the sim-to-sim gap, without any fine-tuning, suggests that the physics-informed meta-learning framework learned fundamental closed-loop dynamics rather than overfitting to specific numerical traits of the training simulator. This indicates that the learned gain-scheduling laws remain effective even when exposed to unmodeled actuator dynamics and sensor noise present in more realistic simulation environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "MetaTune" offers several significant contributions and implications for the field of robotic control:

**Significance:**
- **Unified Joint Optimization:** The proposed framework provides a unified methodology for the simultaneous and end-to-end optimization of both feedback controller gains and disturbance observer parameters. This addresses the long-standing challenge of strong coupling between these components, which conventional tuning methods often treat in isolation, leading to suboptimal performance. By acknowledging and leveraging this coupling, MetaTune facilitates a more holistic and effective robust control design.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The introduction of an adjoint-based meta-gradient computation method is a key technical advancement. This approach significantly reduces the computational complexity of gradient calculation to be linear in the data horizon and independent of the parameter dimension. This is a notable improvement over existing forward sensitivity methods, which scale quadratically with horizon length and are sensitive to parameter dimensionality. This efficiency is critical for applying differentiable control to complex systems with high-dimensional augmented states, especially when integrating observer dynamics over long trajectories.
- **Physics-Informed Adaptive Policy:** By embedding differentiable system dynamics into a learnable neural policy, MetaTune generates adaptive gains that are grounded in the underlying physics of the system. This physics-informed approach, combined with meta-learning, allows the policy to adapt gains intelligently across diverse tasks and operating conditions.
- **Robust Generalization and Zero-Shot Transfer:** The experimental validation on a quadrotor platform, particularly the successful zero-shot sim-to-sim transfer from a differentiable JAX environment to a high-fidelity PX4-Gazebo simulator, demonstrates robust generalization capabilities. This suggests that the learned policy captures fundamental closed-loop dynamics rather than simulator-specific artifacts, which is a crucial step towards bridging the simulation-to-real-world gap.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy in Uncertain Environments:** MetaTune's ability to provide robust and adaptive control under strong disturbances and varying flight conditions has the potential to significantly improve the performance and reliability of robotic systems operating in complex and uncertain real-world settings. This could enable more effective applications in areas such as offshore UAV inspection, search and rescue, rough-terrain locomotion, and agile aerial maneuvers.
- **Reduced Manual Tuning Effort:** By automating the joint tuning process for controllers and observers, MetaTune can reduce the substantial manual effort and expert knowledge typically required for designing and fine-tuning robust control systems. This could accelerate the development and deployment of advanced robotic platforms.
- **Foundation for Future Differentiable Control Research:** The adjoint-based differentiable framework provides a powerful tool that can be extended to other areas of robotic control and learning. Future work, as hinted by the authors, could involve incorporating safety-critical constraints into the differentiable optimization framework, further enhancing robustness and reliability in sensitive applications. It also opens avenues for exploring more complex control architectures and learning tasks within a computationally efficient differentiable paradigm.
- **Bridge Between Model-Based and Learning-Based Control:** MetaTune effectively combines the strengths of model-based control (through differentiable physics) with data-driven learning (through neural meta-policies). This hybrid approach could lead to more robust, interpretable, and adaptable control systems than purely model-based or black-box learning methods.
