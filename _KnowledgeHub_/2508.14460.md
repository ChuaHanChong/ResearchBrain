# DuPO: Enabling Reliable LLM Self-Verification via Dual Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DuPO introduces a self-supervised dual preference optimization framework that enables reliable LLM self-verification without external annotations by utilizing a generalized duality principle based on complementary tasks. The method achieves substantial performance gains in multilingual translation and mathematical reasoning, bringing smaller models to state-of-the-art levels and significantly improving inference-time reranking.

## Problem

- Existing LLM alignment techniques like RLHF and RLVR rely on costly human annotations or restrictive verifiable answers, limiting scalability and general applicability.
- Traditional dual learning is restricted to strictly invertible task pairs and suffers from bidirectional competence asymmetry, making it unsuitable for most non-invertible LLM tasks.
- Many real-world LLM tasks, such as creative writing or mathematical reasoning, are non-invertible, preventing the use of self-supervised methods that require full input reconstruction.

## Method

- DuPO redefines task duality using a =='complementary consistency principle'== where a primal task input is decomposed into known and unknown components, and a dual task reconstructs only the unknown component.
- ==Self-supervised reward signals== are generated based on the consistency between the original and reconstructed unknown components, incentivizing the LLM to produce outputs that facilitate accurate reconstruction.
- The self-supervised rewards are integrated into a preference optimization framework, specifically ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, to continually improve the LLM without external supervision.
- An ==inference-time reranking mechanism== leverages the dual task to select the best reasoning trajectory from multiple LLM samples, boosting performance without additional training.

## Results

- DuPO boosted the Seed-X-7B-Instruct model by an average of **2.13** COMET points across **756** translation directions, making it comparable to ultra-large state-of-the-art systems.
- It achieved consistent performance improvements in mathematical reasoning, increasing scores by **6.4** percentage points for OpenReasoning-Nemotron-7B and Qwen3-4B on challenging benchmarks.
- DuPO lifted the average score of vanilla LlaMA-3.1-8B by **24.0** percentage points on AMC23 and MATH500 benchmarks, surpassing oracle-labeled SimpleRL-Zoo by **13.1%**.
- As an inference-time reranking mechanism, DuPO improved the average performance of Qwen3-4B by **9.3** points on AIME benchmarks, outperforming larger models like DeepSeek-R1 without additional training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalized duality, based on complementary consistency rather than strict invertibility, effectively extends self-supervised learning to a broad range of non-invertible LLM tasks.
> - Decomposing task inputs into known and unknown components provides a strong contextual anchor for the dual task, mitigating competence asymmetry and ensuring reliable self-supervision signals.
> - Dual preference optimization can activate and enhance complex reasoning capabilities even in base models without initial supervised fine-tuning, demonstrating the method's ability to unlock latent skills.
> - Reliable self-verification can be used effectively as a training-free inference-time reranking strategy, significantly improving model performance and extracting more capability from existing LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen remarkable progress across diverse tasks, but effectively enhancing and aligning their capabilities remains a core challenge. This paper positions DuPO as a critical advancement in the field of LLM optimization, particularly in addressing the limitations of existing reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms.

The current landscape of LLM alignment is dominated by approaches such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLHF, while effective for aligning models with human preferences, suffers from the high cost and inconsistency of human annotations. RLVR attempts to mitigate this by using binary rewards from verifiable answers (e.g., in math or code generation), but its applicability is limited to objective tasks with definitive solutions, and it still relies on external supervision for acquiring these verifiable answers, presenting a scalability bottleneck. Newer techniques like AI-Feedback (RLAIF) and Constitutional AI merely shift the dependency from human labels to teacher models or predefined rules, without fundamentally resolving the external supervision bottleneck.

Traditional dual learning offers a promising self-supervised alternative. It leverages task duality (e.g., translation and back-translation) to generate intrinsic feedback, validating outputs through cycle consistency without external labels. However, applying traditional dual learning to general LLM optimization faces two significant hurdles:
1. **Limited Duality in Irreversible Tasks:** Many real-world LLM tasks, such as creative writing or mathematical reasoning, are non-invertible. The output (e.g., a math solution) typically does not contain sufficient information to reconstruct the original input (e.g., the problem statement), thereby breaking the duality cycle.
2. **Bidirectional Competence Asymmetry:** LLMs often exhibit uneven performance across primal and dual tasks. A strong primal task performance coupled with a weak dual task can lead to noisy self-signals, hindering effective optimization.

DuPO directly addresses these limitations, carving out a novel niche within the broader landscape. It seeks to bridge the gap between the efficiency of self-supervised dual learning and the general applicability required for diverse LLM tasks. By introducing a "generalized duality" framework, DuPO aims to make self-verification applicable to non-invertible tasks and robust against competence asymmetry, thereby offering a scalable, general, and annotation-free paradigm for LLM optimization that transcends the current reliance on costly or restrictive external feedback mechanisms. This positions DuPO as a significant step towards more autonomous and efficient LLM training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DuPO stems from the inherent limitations of current LLM optimization techniques, particularly their dependence on external, often expensive or restrictive, forms of supervision. The authors identify two key shortcomings in the existing methods:

1. **The High Cost and Limited Applicability of External Supervision:**
 - **RLHF's reliance on human labels:** Human feedback, while valuable for subjective alignment, is inherently costly to acquire at scale and can suffer from inconsistencies across annotators, especially for complex tasks.
 - **RLVR's dependence on verifiable answers:** While less costly than human feedback, RLVR requires definitive, verifiable ground truth answers, which restricts its use to a narrow set of objective tasks (e.g., math, coding). It cannot be readily applied to open-ended or non-invertible tasks like multilingual translation, creative writing, or complex dialogue where multiple valid outputs exist.
 - **AI-Feedback's substitution of dependencies:** Approaches using LLM-as-a-judge or teacher models merely swap one external dependency for another, potentially introducing new biases and still requiring a strong, well-aligned "judge" model.

2. **The Inadequacies of Traditional Dual Learning for General LLM Tasks:**
 - Traditional dual learning, while annotation-free, is fundamentally restricted to *strictly dual task pairs* where the primal and dual tasks are mutually invertible (e.g., translation and back-translation). This strict invertibility requirement makes it unsuitable for the vast majority of real-world LLM tasks that lack such perfect symmetry.
 - Even for tasks with conceptual duality, LLMs often exhibit *bidirectional competence asymmetry*, meaning their performance might be strong in the primal direction but weak in the dual, leading to noisy and unreliable self-supervision signals.

Given these challenges, the overarching **key objective** of this work is to develop a novel, scalable, and annotation-free LLM optimization framework that enables reliable self-verification across a broad spectrum of tasks, including those that are non-invertible or open-ended. Specifically, DuPO aims to:
- Generate annotation-free feedback for LLM training.
- Overcome the reliance on costly labels and the restricted applicability of RLVR.
- Broaden the applicability of dual learning beyond strictly invertible task pairs.
- Mitigate bidirectional competence asymmetry in LLMs to ensure robust self-supervision.
- Synergize with LLMs' diverse capabilities to instantiate both primal and dual tasks within a single model, converting outputs into self-supervised reward signals for continual improvement without external annotations.

In essence, DuPO is motivated by the need for a more general, efficient, and autonomous method for aligning and enhancing LLMs, moving beyond the bottlenecks of manual supervision or task-specific verifiability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DuPO introduces a novel **generalized duality framework** at its core, designed to overcome the limitations of traditional dual learning and enable self-supervised preference optimization for a wider range of LLM tasks. The methodology is structured around redefining task duality, generating self-supervised rewards, and integrating with preference optimization algorithms.

#### 3.1. Generalized Duality Framework via Complementary Task

The most significant innovation in DuPO is the conceptual shift from strict mutual invertibility to a *complementary consistency principle*. This is achieved by:
- **Input Decomposition:** For any primal task input `x`, it is decomposed into two disjoint components: a **known component (x_k)** and an **unknown component (x_u)**, such that `x = x_k ∪ x_u`. The specific choice of `x_k` and `x_u` is flexible and task-dependent, allowing for adaptation to various domains.
- **Redefined Dual Task (T_cd):** Instead of attempting to reconstruct the entire primal input `x` from the primal output `y` (as in traditional dual learning), the *complementary dual task* `T_cd` is designed to reconstruct *only the unknown component x_u*, by leveraging both the primal output `y` and the known component `x_k`. Formally, `T_cd: (y, x_k) → x_u_hat`.
- **Complementary Consistency Principle:** The framework defines consistency by comparing the reconstructed `x_u_hat` with the original `x_u`. A domain-specific distance metric `d(·)` quantifies this consistency, ensuring `d(x_u, T_cd(y, x_k)) ≤ ε`.

This generalized duality resolves the previously identified challenges:
- **Overcoming Invertibility Constraints:** By only requiring reconstruction of `x_u` (which is designed to be reconstructible) rather than the entire `x`, DuPO bypasses the stringent invertibility requirement, making it applicable to inherently non-invertible tasks like mathematical reasoning or creative generation.
- **Mitigating Competence Asymmetry:** The `x_k` component acts as a strong contextual anchor for the dual task, simplifying its complexity and constraining the solution space. Furthermore, `x_u` can be strategically selected to be feasibly reconstructible and serve as a faithful reward signal, reducing the "weak dual" problem.
- **Broad Applicability:** The flexible decomposition allows the framework to be adapted to diverse tasks where partial or conditional input-output relationships exist.

#### 3.2. Self-Supervised Reward Generation

Based on the complementary consistency, DuPO constructs a self-supervised reward signal:
`r(y) ∝ exp(-λ · d(x_u, T_cd(y, x_k)))`, where `λ` controls the sensitivity of the reward. This reward is maximized when `x_u_hat` closely matches `x_u`, incentivizing the primal task model to generate outputs `y` that facilitate accurate reconstruction of `x_u`. The distance metric `d(·)` is flexible; for instance, BLEU scores are used for multilingual translation, and binary equality checks are used for mathematical reasoning.

#### 3.3. Preference Optimization

DuPO integrates this self-supervised reward into a standard preference optimization framework. The objective is to maximize the expected reward `J(θ) = E_y~π_θ(y|x) [r(y)]`, where `π_θ(y|x)` is the LLM's policy. While compatible with various RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, ReMAX, REINFORCE++), the authors specifically employ **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** in their experiments, citing its stability in high-dimensional parameter spaces relevant for LLMs and its compatibility with rule-based rewards.

#### 3.4. Construction of Dual Questions in Mathematical Reasoning (Appendix A)

For mathematical reasoning, the `x_u` component (e.g., a numerical variable) is generated by:
1. **Pattern Recognition and Exclusion:** Identifying numerical candidates in a math problem while excluding context-specific numbers (e.g., subscripts, exponents, function arguments).
2. **Variable Generation and Replacement:** Substituting the selected numbers with unique variable identifiers.
3. **Dual Question Generation:** Formulating an inverse problem (e.g., "Given the correct answer is [answer], determine the value of [variable]").
To ensure robust duality, generated dual questions are filtered based on two principles:
- **Answerability:** At least one candidate answer for the primal question must correctly solve the dual question.
- **Uniqueness:** At most one candidate answer for the primal question should correctly solve the dual question. This ensures a reliable one-to-one correspondence for the reward signal.

#### 3.5. Inference-Time Reranking

Beyond training, DuPO can also be used as a **training-free, inference-time reranking mechanism**. Given a primal task input, multiple reasoning trajectories are sampled from the LLM. For each trajectory, its final answer is used to construct and solve the corresponding dual question. The dual task is typically run with multiple samples (e.g., K=8) for a more reliable reward estimate. The trajectory with the highest "backward accuracy" on its dual questions is then selected as the final output. This allows for performance boosting without additional finetuning.

This comprehensive methodology allows DuPO to leverage the self-supervision paradigm of dual learning while adapting it to the complexities and diversity of modern LLM tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical validation of DuPO demonstrates its substantial efficacy across diverse tasks, model scales, and application scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance Boost on Multilingual Translation

- **Significant Gains:** When applied to Seed-X-7B-Instruct, a strong open-source translation LLM, DuPO achieved an average gain of **2.13 COMET points** over 756 translation directions across 28 languages. It also improved BLEU by 1.55 and BLEURT by 1.95, resulting in an average score of 64.66.
- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Comparability:** This performance enhancement elevates the 7B model to a level comparable to, and in some metrics surpassing, ultra-large state-of-the-art systems like Doubao1.5-thinking (+2.75) and Qwen3-235B-22B (+0.98), and is on par with the latest DeepSeek-R1.
- **Human Evaluation:** Human assessments on the challenging Seed-X-Challenge benchmark confirmed that DuPO-enhanced Seed-X-7B-Instruct achieved performance comparable to GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1-0120, significantly outperforming commercial closed-source systems like Google Translate.

#### 4.2. Robust Gains in Mathematical Reasoning

- **Consistent Improvements:** DuPO yielded consistent and significant performance improvements across various models (from 1.5B to 7B parameters) and on challenging benchmarks (AMC23, AIME24, AIME25).
- **Notable Examples:**
 - **OpenReasoning-Nemotron-7B:** Average score increased by **6.4 percentage points** (from 83.9% to 90.3%), achieving impressive performance.
 - **Qwen3-4B:** Average score boosted by **6.4 percentage points** (from 77.2% to 83.6%), notably surpassing the ultra-large DeepSeek-R1-0120 model.
 - **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B:** Even on this smaller, less powerful model, DuPO delivered a **3.9 percentage point** increase in average accuracy (from 35.8% to 39.7%).
- **Robustness:** These results demonstrate DuPO's robustness and stability in enhancing mathematical reasoning capabilities across different model scales and baseline abilities.

#### 4.3. Scalability to Diverse Backbones

- **Architecture Agnostic:** DuPO's effectiveness was further validated on the LlaMA architectural family.
- **Impressive LlaMA-3.1-8B Gains:** It lifted the average score of vanilla LlaMA-3.1-8B by **24.0 percentage points** (from 8.1% to 32.1%) on AMC23 and MATH500 benchmarks, surpassing SimpleRL-Zoo (which relies on oracle-labeled answers) by 13.1%.
- **Enhanced OctoThinker-8B:** For OctoThinker-8B-Hybrid-Base (a model with middle training on math reasoning), DuPO achieved even more substantial improvements: +50.0 points on AMC23 and +27.4 points on MATH500, leading to an average performance of 62.5%.

#### 4.4. Incentivizing Reasoning Capability from Base Models

- **Awakening Latent Abilities:** DuPO successfully activated and enhanced complex reasoning capabilities directly from a base model (Qwen3-4B-Base) without any preliminary supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
- **Clear Learning Dynamics:** Training dynamics showed the "Forward Acc" on the primal task soaring from 15.2% to 56.5%, demonstrating the effectiveness of the dual-task reward signal.
- **Strong Generalization:** This acquired skill generalized robustly, with performance on unseen test sets (AMC23, AIME24, AIME25) leaping significantly (e.g., AMC23 from 20% to 70%).

#### 4.5. Inference-Time Scaling Without Training

- **Effective Reranking:** DuPO's mechanism proved effective as a training-free, inference-time reranking strategy.
- **Significant Reranking Boosts:**
 - **Qwen3-4B:** Improved average performance by **9.3 points** (from 68.4% to 77.7%) on AIME benchmarks, allowing it to outperform DeepSeek-R1 and Claude-Sonnet4-Thinking without any additional training.
 - **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B:** Saw an even more pronounced **18.7 point** increase (from 20.0% to 38.7%). This highlights DuPO's ability to extract more capability from existing models at inference.

#### 4.6. Effects of Task Duality (Ablation Study)

- **Cruciality of Generalized Duality:** An ablation study removing the unknown component selection mechanism (thereby introducing poorer duality) confirmed its importance.
- **Performance Degradation:** For the 1.5B model, removing filtering led to a 3.6 percentage point drop in performance. For the 4B model, the drop was 5.4 points on average. These results empirically validate that the carefully designed generalized duality framework, particularly the unknown component selection strategy, is crucial for achieving superior performance by ensuring high-quality task duality and reducing training noise.

In summary, DuPO consistently demonstrates its ability to significantly enhance LLM performance across diverse tasks and model sizes, not only during training but also as an inference-time optimization, all while alleviating the dependency on costly external annotations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DuPO represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model optimization, offering a scalable, general, and annotation-free paradigm with far-reaching implications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Annotation-Efficient LLM Development:** The most immediate and profound impact of DuPO is its ability to **eliminate the reliance on costly human annotations and handcrafted rewards**. This directly addresses a major bottleneck in LLM development, democratizing access to high-quality alignment techniques. By generating self-supervised feedback from intrinsic task structure, DuPO drastically reduces the financial and logistical burden associated with training advanced LLMs, making robust optimization more accessible to a wider range of researchers and organizations.

2. **Broadened Applicability to Diverse Tasks:** DuPO successfully tackles the long-standing limitation of traditional dual learning by extending its applicability to **non-invertible and open-ended tasks**. This includes complex domains like mathematical reasoning, code generation, creative writing, and dialogue systems, where direct input-output reversal is not feasible. The generalized duality framework, with its input decomposition into known and unknown components, unlocks dual learning for a vast new class of problems, significantly expanding the scope of self-supervised optimization.

3. **Enhanced Model Robustness and Generalization:** The consistent and significant performance gains observed across various models (from 1.5B to 7B parameters, and across different architectures like LlaMA) and diverse benchmarks demonstrate DuPO's ability to improve LLMs' fundamental capabilities. It not only boosts performance on specific tasks but also incentivizes the learning of more robust and generalizable reasoning abilities, even from base models without prior supervised fine-tuning.

4. **Flexible and Scalable Optimization Paradigm:** DuPO's model-agnostic design and compatibility with various reinforcement learning algorithms (like GRPO) make it a highly flexible framework. Its proven effectiveness across different model scales positions it as a scalable solution for future LLM development. The concept of generating internal feedback directly from the model's own outputs enables continuous improvement without external intervention, paving the way for more autonomous learning systems.

5. **Efficient Inference-Time Scaling:** Beyond training, DuPO's utility as a **training-free inference-time reranking mechanism** is a game-changer. It allows smaller models to achieve performance comparable to, or even surpass, much larger counterparts without requiring expensive parameter scaling or additional finetuning. This has immense practical value for deploying powerful LLMs in resource-constrained environments or for quick experimentation, offering a cost-effective way to extract more capability from existing models.

6. **Addressing Critical Bottlenecks in LLM Alignment:** By effectively overcoming the limitations of RLHF (cost/inconsistency), RLVR (limited applicability/external supervision), and traditional dual learning (strict invertibility/competence asymmetry), DuPO pushes the boundaries of LLM alignment. It provides a systematic and principled way to generate high-quality feedback, moving the field closer to truly self-improving and adaptable language models.

In conclusion, DuPO offers a powerful and elegant solution to several critical challenges in LLM optimization. Its ability to enable reliable self-verification across diverse, high-stakes tasks without external annotations makes it a pivotal contribution that could fundamentally change how LLMs are trained, optimized, and deployed in the future, fostering more autonomous, adaptable, and cost-effective AI systems.
