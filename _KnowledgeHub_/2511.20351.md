# Thinking in 360°: Humanoid Visual Search in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> This research prototypes humanoid visual search, enabling multimodal large language models to actively explore 360° panoramic environments for object and path finding, similar to human perception. A new benchmark, H*Bench, evaluates models in complex real-world scenarios, revealing that post-training significantly improves open-source models, allowing them to outperform proprietary models in object search.

## Problem

- Most multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are passive observers, analyzing static 2D images, which fundamentally differs from active human visual search in 3D environments.
- Current embodied AI research often relies on expensive 3D simulators or physical hardware, limiting scalability and generalization to complex "in-the-wild" human environments.
- Existing models lack the ability to dynamically change their perspective or couple visual reasoning with physical actions in a closed-loop manner.

## Method

- Developed ==Humanoid Visual Search (HVS)== where an MLLM agent actively rotates a virtual "head" within 360° panoramic images to perform object and path finding tasks.
- Introduced ==H*Bench==, a benchmark comprising 3,000 richly annotated real-world panoramic task instances across diverse scene types to evaluate embodied visual search in complex scenarios.
- Employed a ==two-stage post-training pipeline==, combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), to adapt and enhance MLLMs for HVS tasks.

## Results

- State-of-the-art proprietary MLLMs like Gemini2.5-Pro and GPT-4o achieved only approximately **30%** success rates on both Humanoid Object Search (HOS) and Humanoid Path Search (HPS) tasks on H*Bench.
- The proposed HVS-3B model (a Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model with post-training) improved its HOS success rate over threefold from **14.83%** to **47.38%**, surpassing the best proprietary model, Gemini2.5-Pro (**31.96%**).
- HVS-3B's HPS success rate increased over threefold from **6.44%** to **24.94%**, demonstrating significant gains, though it still trailed Gemini2.5-Pro's performance on this more demanding task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs trained primarily on static 2D data exhibit a substantial performance drop when transferred to active 3D embodied visual search tasks, indicating a significant gap between passive reasoning and embodied interaction.
> - Active visual search, where the agent dynamically changes its perspective, is empirically superior to passive analysis of a full panorama, mimicking human visual efficiency and reducing reliance on models' generalization to distorted wide-angle views.
> - While post-training significantly enhances MLLMs for embodied tasks, higher-order spatial and commonsense reasoning, especially for complex path search, remains a persistent challenge, exposing limitations in current learning paradigms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses critical limitations within the current landscape of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), visual search, and embodied AI.

**Evolution from Passive to Active Perception:**
Historically, visual search in AI has largely been a "passive" exercise, where models analyze static 2D images to identify objects or information. While MLLMs have significantly advanced contextual understanding and generalization capabilities in visual tasks, they mostly operate on a fixed visual canvas. The paper argues that this passive approach fundamentally differs from human visual search, which is inherently "active" and "embodied." Humans dynamically coordinate head and eye movements (cephalomotor and oculomotor control) to explore 360° environments, actively gathering information and refining their perspective. This paper proposes a crucial transition for AI agents: from passive captioners and describers to active searchers.

**Bridging Gaps in Embodied AI and Visual Navigation:**
Current embodied AI research, particularly in visual navigation, often relies on expensive 3D simulators or real physical hardware. These platforms are typically constrained to simplistic "household scenes" (e.g., Gibson, Habitat, ProcTHOR), making it challenging to scale experiments or generalize to the "in-the-wild" complexities of human environments. The authors identify two key gaps:
1. **Non-interactive:** Existing models cannot change their perspective beyond an initial field of view.
2. **Disembodied:** Models lack physical embodiment, preventing them from coupling visual reasoning with actions in the physical world.

This work specifically tackles these issues by introducing "humanoid visual search" (HVS), a paradigm where an agent actively rotates its head within a 360° panoramic image. This novel approach enables a closed-loop perception-action cycle without requiring a full 3D simulator or physical robot, making the research scalable and hardware-free. It focuses on critical decision points in navigation and manipulation, abstracting complex full-body motion into atomic head rotations, which simplifies the problem while retaining the core cognitive challenge.

**Advancements in MLLMs and Tool Use:**
MLLMs have shown remarkable abilities in understanding and reasoning across modalities. Recent trends involve empowering MLLMs with external tools (e.g., web browsing, code execution, image manipulation like cropping/zooming) to address their limitations in semantic grounding and visual perception. While these tools enable "active perception" on a 2D canvas, this paper pushes the boundary by coupling tool use with *physical* actions – specifically, active head rotation. This transforms computational manipulations of static images into continuous visual chains of thought that mimic embodied interaction, bridging the gap between symbolic linguistic representations and physical world grounding.

**Novel Benchmark for Real-World Complexity:**
A significant contribution is the introduction of H*Bench, a new benchmark designed to evaluate visual search in complex, visually crowded "in-the-wild" scenarios. This moves beyond the typical sterile or simplified environments of existing benchmarks, encompassing diverse real-world locations such as transportation hubs, large retail spaces, and urban streets. This focus on realistic complexity pushes the boundaries of what MLLMs need to understand and interact with, necessitating advanced visual-spatial reasoning, physical commonsense, and even socio-spatial commonsense, which are often overlooked in simpler settings.

In summary, this research positions itself at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and embodied AI, aiming to evolve MLLMs from passive observers to active, embodied agents capable of intelligent interaction within the rich complexity of the human world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent efficiency and adaptiveness of the human visual system in navigating and interacting with complex 3D environments. Humans are not passive observers; they actively control their head and eye movements to gather necessary visual information, minimizing cognitive load and efficiently executing goal-directed behaviors. The authors pose a fundamental question: **"Can an AI agent actively search for objects or paths in a 3D world like a human, rather than just passively describe what it sees?"**

To address this overarching question, the paper outlines several key objectives:

1. **Develop Embodied Visual Search Agents Comparable to Humans:** The core objective is to create AI agents that can perform visual search tasks with the efficiency and intelligence akin to humans, especially in dynamic, 360° environments. This involves moving beyond the limitations of static 2D image analysis prevalent in most existing MLLM-based visual search methods.

2. **Bypass Real-World Hardware Constraints:** A significant practical motivation is to develop a scalable framework for studying embodied visual search that does not rely on expensive and hard-to-scale real-world hardware or highly complex 3D simulators. The proposed solution is to leverage 360° panoramic images as lightweight, hardware-free simulators, enabling a closed-loop perception-action cycle through virtual head rotations. This approach democratizes research in embodied AI by reducing resource barriers.

3. **Prototype Humanoid Visual Search (HVS):**
 - **Interactive:** The agent should be able to actively change its perspective by rotating its "head" (camera) within a 360° panoramic environment.
 - **Embodied:** Visual reasoning must be tightly coupled with physical actions (head movements), making search a goal-directed behavior rather than an abstract perceptual exercise.
 - **Task-Driven:** The search should be motivated by practical embodied tasks, specifically:
 - **Humanoid Object Search (HOS):** Locating and foveating a target object, crucial for manipulation.
 - **Humanoid Path Search (HPS):** Identifying a navigable path to a destination and aligning body orientation, a prerequisite for locomotion.

4. **Create a Challenging "In-the-Wild" Benchmark (H*Bench):** The researchers are motivated to move beyond simplistic household scenes, which are often the focus of current embodied AI platforms. They aim to establish a benchmark that captures the structural, semantic, and volumetric complexities of real human-made environments, such as transportation hubs, large retail spaces, urban streets, and public institutions. This benchmark should necessitate advanced visual-spatial reasoning, commonsense understanding, and robust generalization capabilities.

5. **Evaluate and Enhance MLLMs for Embodied Visual Search:** A key objective is to systematically evaluate the performance of state-of-the-art MLLMs on these challenging embodied tasks. Furthermore, the motivation includes developing effective post-training techniques (Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning) to adapt and enhance these MLLMs to function as capable visual search agents in a 3D world.

6. **Quantify Remaining Challenges:** Finally, the research aims not only to demonstrate progress but also to rigorously quantify the inherent difficulties of embodied visual search in the wild. By analyzing failure cases and performance gaps, the authors seek to highlight the "immense challenge that remains" in building MLLM agents that can seamlessly integrate into everyday human life, thereby guiding future research directions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "Thinking in 360°" is meticulously designed to prototype, benchmark, and improve humanoid visual search capabilities in complex, real-world environments. It combines a novel task formulation, a unique dataset, and a two-stage MLLM post-training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Humanoid Visual Search (HVS)

The core idea is to abstract full-body motion to atomic head rotations, capturing critical decision points in human spatial intelligence. The environment is modeled as a single 360° panoramic image, from which the agent observes narrow field-of-view (FoV) perspective images by rotating its virtual head.
- **Objective:** To identify the optimal direction (azimuth $\phi^*$, polar angle $\gamma^*$) that maximizes task success given a language instruction and current visual observation.
- **Humanoid Object Search (HOS):** The agent must find a target object and center it within its foveal region, submitting the final viewing direction. This serves as a prerequisite for manipulation.
- **Humanoid Path Search (HPS):** The agent must identify a navigable path to a target location and align its body orientation with this path, submitting the optimal azimuth $\phi^*$. This acts as a high-level planning step for locomotion.

#### 3.2. MLLM as the Embodied Agent

The agent is modeled as a tool-augmented MLLM, where its policy dictates the generation of both a textual chain of thought ($y_t$) and an action ($a_t$) at each timestep $t$.
- **Action Space:** The agent can perform two types of actions:
 - `rotate(∆yaw, ∆pitch)`: Adjusts the viewing direction by specified angular increments. This simulates active exploration.
 - `submit(yaw, pitch)`: Commits the current viewing direction as the final estimate and terminates the episode.
The search episode is a sequence of rotation actions culminating in a submission.

#### 3.3. H*Bench: The Benchmark for In-the-Wild Visual Search

To systematically study HVS, the authors introduce H*Bench, a novel benchmark designed for visually crowded, real-world scenarios.
- **Dataset Overview:** It comprises approximately 3,000 richly annotated task instances derived from high-resolution panoramic videos (up to 7680x3840 pixels). These videos are sourced from global metropolitan areas (New York, Paris, Amsterdam, Frankfurt) and open platforms, ensuring broad geographical and scene diversity across 6 major categories and 18 fine-grained scene types (e.g., transportation hubs, large retail spaces, public institutions, urban streets). Each task instance generates four distinct starting orientations, yielding 12,000 search episodes.
- **Task Annotation:** Annotators use a perspective-view interface to freely rotate a virtual camera within the panorama. They identify embodied search tasks, write natural-language instructions, and mark target objects (for HOS) or path directions (for HPS) with bounding boxes. These bounding boxes' centers define the optimal ground-truth directions ($\phi^*, \gamma^*$).
- **Cold-Start Data Curation:** For supervised fine-tuning (SFT), a subset of annotated tasks is augmented with structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales. GPT-4o is initially prompted to generate these rationales based on instructions, observations, and human-provided optimal actions. A human-in-the-loop protocol is used to review and refine these rationales, ensuring accuracy, grounding in visual evidence, and stylistic consistency. This results in 2,000 multi-turn trajectories for SFT.
- **Difficulty Taxonomy:**
 - **HOS Difficulty:** Categorized as Easy, Medium, or Hard based on the initial visibility ratio ($d$) of the target object in the agent's initial narrow FoV (e.g., $d \geq 0.5$ for Easy, $d=0$ for Hard).
 - **HPS Difficulty:** Defined by two factors: the presence of textual cues and the alignment between visual/textual cues and the actual path direction, resulting in Easy, Medium, Hard, and Extreme levels.

#### 3.4. MLLM Post-Training Pipeline

To adapt MLLMs, which are typically trained on static Internet data, for active 3D planning, a two-stage post-training pipeline is employed (using Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct as the base model).
- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The model undergoes full-parameter SFT on the curated multi-turn dataset. This stage is crucial for instilling basic task-oriented reasoning, grounding multi-modal inputs, and teaching structured action plan generation, establishing a strong behavioral prior.
- **Stage 2: Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The policy is further refined using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This RL stage encourages long-horizon reasoning, strategic exploration, and robustness beyond the imitation learning baseline established by SFT. The reward function is rule-based, incorporating correctness and format rewards, with an optional distance-to-goal reward for HPS.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

A trial is considered a success if the agent's submitted final viewing direction ($\hat{\phi}, \hat{\gamma}$) falls within a pre-defined tolerance region around the ground-truth optimal direction ($\phi^*, \gamma^*$).
- **Tolerance:** For HOS, $\tau_\phi = 30^\circ$, $\tau_\gamma = 20^\circ$ (mimicking human foveation). For HPS, $\tau_\phi = 10^\circ$ (requiring precise motion direction). Success rate (%) is reported across tasks and difficulty levels.

This comprehensive methodology allows for rigorous evaluation of MLLM capabilities in a novel, embodied setting while providing a clear pathway for improvement through targeted fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on H*Bench reveal significant challenges for existing MLLMs in embodied visual search and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed post-training pipeline, while also highlighting persistent limitations.

1. **Baseline Performance of MLLMs is Low:**
 - Even state-of-the-art proprietary models like Gemini2.5-Pro and GPT-4o struggle considerably, achieving only around **30% success rate** on HOS and HPS tasks (Gemini2.5-Pro: 31.96% HOS, 33.00% HPS; GPT-4o: 19.75% HOS, 23.69% HPS).
 - Open-weight models (InternVL3.5, Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma-3) perform even worse, with initial Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct scoring only 14.83% for HOS and 6.44% for HPS.
 - Interestingly, for some open-weight models (Gemma-3, Qwen2.5-VL), smaller model sizes (4B/3B) sometimes outperform their larger counterparts (12B/7B) in HOS, suggesting that raw parameter count does not always translate to better embodied reasoning in these tasks.
 - This starkly contrasts with performance on disembodied 2D visual search benchmarks (e.g., V*Bench), where similar MLLMs achieve near-saturation (e.g., 88% success), underscoring the fundamental difficulty introduced by embodiment and 3D interaction.

2. **Significant Performance Gains from Post-Training:**
 - The proposed two-stage post-training pipeline (SFT + RL) dramatically improves performance for the open-source Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model (referred to as HVS-3B).
 - **HOS:** Success rate increases over threefold, from 14.83% to **47.38%**. Our HVS-3B model even surpasses the best proprietary model, Gemini2.5-Pro (31.96%), in object search.
 - **HPS:** Success rate also increases over threefold, from 6.44% to **24.94%**.
 - **Contribution Breakdown:** SFT contributes the majority of these gains (HOS: 14.83% $\rightarrow$ 40.83%; HPS: 6.44% $\rightarrow$ 23.00%), establishing foundational task capabilities. RL provides additional but more modest refinement (HOS: $\uparrow$6.55%; HPS: $\uparrow$1.94%).

3. **Task-Dependent Efficacy and Persistent Challenges:**
 - **HOS vs. HPS:** While HVS-3B excels in object search, outperforming proprietary models, its absolute score for path search (24.94%) remains lower and falls short of Gemini2.5-Pro (33.00%). This highlights the inherent difficulty of path search, which demands more sophisticated spatial and commonsense reasoning.
 - **Negative Impact of RL on Complex HPS:** Paradoxically, for medium and extreme difficulty levels in HPS, the RL stage can sometimes degrade performance (e.g., from 23.03% to 20.18% for Medium HPS). This is attributed to potential "reward hacking," where the model exploits the reward signal without genuinely improving its reasoning, suggesting limitations in current reward function designs for complex, implicit reasoning.
 - **Error Analysis:**
 - **HOS Errors:** Primarily due to limited visual grounding (failing to identify targets in clutter) and a perception-action gap (detecting but not precisely foveating).
 - **HPS Errors:** More complex, including vision-action mismatch (perceiving cues but failing to translate to physical actions), lack of physical commonsense (e.g., attempting to pass through walls), and lack of socio-spatial commonsense (e.g., ignoring stairs, traffic norms, functional layouts in built environments).
 - **Key takeaway:** MLLMs can form linguistically grounded spatial models but struggle to translate this into physically grounded models necessary for embodied interaction.

4. **Cross-Task Generalization and Synergies:**
 - Training on a mixed dataset (both HOS and HPS) yields the best overall performance, though improvements can be uneven across specific task splits.
 - There's a clear bidirectional synergy: training on HOS boosts HPS performance (6.4% $\rightarrow$ 20.7%), and vice-versa (HPS training boosts HOS: 14.8% $\rightarrow$ 29.5%). This suggests transfer of general exploration and visual grounding skills. Notably, HOS-trained models perform surprisingly well on *easy* HPS, implying that simple path tasks can sometimes be solved via object-finding skills.

5. **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **Reward Shaping for HPS:** Simple rule-based reward functions (correctness, format, distance-to-goal) only improve easy HPS tasks and often degrade harder ones, confirming the challenge of designing effective rewards for complex reasoning.
 - **Active vs. Passive Search:** Active visual search (agent rotates perspective view) is empirically superior to passive analysis of a full panorama. This is attributed to mimicking human efficiency and avoiding distortions that conflict with MLLM training priors.
 - **Embodied vs. Disembodied Benchmarks:** Methods performing near-perfectly on 2D disembodied benchmarks (V*Bench) plummet to very low success rates on the embodied H*Bench (e.g., 88% $\rightarrow$ 2.5% for Mini-o3, 88% $\rightarrow$ 11.6% for Chain-of-Focus). This unequivocally demonstrates the critical gap between 2D passive reasoning and 3D active embodied interaction. Importantly, HVS-3B maintains a satisfactory 65.5% on V*Bench, indicating it learns 3D embodied search without significantly compromising 2D abilities.

In summary, the paper successfully demonstrates a significant step towards enabling MLLMs for embodied visual search in complex real-world environments. While post-training yields impressive gains, especially in object search, it also exposes fundamental limitations in higher-order spatial and commonsense reasoning for tasks like path search, highlighting substantial remaining research challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Thinking in 360°" carries substantial significance and potential impact across several domains of artificial intelligence and robotics.

1. **Pioneering a New Paradigm for Embodied AI:** The introduction of "humanoid visual search" (HVS) as a novel task is a crucial step forward for embodied AI. By abstracting full-body motion to head rotations within 360° panoramas, the authors have created a tractable yet realistic framework to study the core cognitive processes of embodied visual search. This task moves AI agents from passive perception to active, goal-directed interaction with the 3D world, mirroring human-like exploration and decision-making.

2. **Scalable and Accessible Embodied Research:** A major impact lies in the development of a "hardware-free" platform for embodied reasoning. By using readily available 360° panoramic images as lightweight simulators, the research bypasses the prohibitive costs and logistical complexities associated with real-world robotics or high-fidelity 3D simulators. This democratizes embodied AI research, making it accessible to a broader scientific community and accelerating innovation in this field.

3. **H*Bench: A Rigorous Benchmark for Real-World Complexity:** The creation of H*Bench is a significant contribution. It is the first systematic benchmark to rigorously evaluate embodied visual search in diverse, visually crowded "in-the-wild" environments, moving beyond the often-simplified household scenes. This benchmark pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities, revealing that even top-tier models falter in real-world scenarios requiring complex visual-spatial, physical, and socio-spatial commonsense. Its existence will drive future research towards more robust and generalizable embodied agents.

4. **Quantifying the Gap in MLLM Capabilities:** The paper provides quantitative evidence of the substantial performance gap between MLLMs' 2D visual reasoning abilities and their capacity for 3D embodied interaction. The dramatic drop in performance of strong 2D visual search models on H*Bench unequivocally demonstrates that capabilities learned from passive Internet data do not directly transfer to active, embodied settings. This critical insight helps to redirect research efforts towards building truly grounded and interactive MLLMs.

5. **A Promising Path for MLLM Enhancement:** The research demonstrates that post-training techniques, particularly supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, can significantly enhance MLLMs for embodied tasks. The over three-fold increase in success rates for both object and path search, with our HVS-3B model even outperforming proprietary models in object search, highlights a promising methodology for adapting powerful pre-trained models to embodied intelligence. The finding that the model retains its 2D visual search ability while learning 3D embodied search suggests progress toward unified models capable of operating in both physical and digital realms.

6. **Identifying Key Bottlenecks and Future Directions:** Beyond demonstrating success, the paper meticulously dissects failure cases, revealing fundamental bottlenecks in MLLMs' higher-level reasoning. The challenges related to physical commonsense (e.g., not passing through walls) and socio-spatial commonsense (e.g., understanding traffic norms or functional layouts) are critical insights. The observed "reward hacking" in complex RL scenarios for path search points to the need for more robust reward function design and advanced learning algorithms. These identified challenges provide clear, actionable directions for future research, including:
 - Designing more sophisticated reward functions.
 - Developing efficient vision tokenizers.
 - Creating pre-training methods that instill action-oriented spatial world knowledge.
 - Scaling up the collection of embodied search data.
 - Balancing performance across varying task difficulties.

7. **Enabling Future Robotics and AI Applications:** The advancements made in humanoid visual search are foundational for a wide range of real-world applications. These include:
 - **Humanoid Robots:** Equipping robots with human-like active perception to navigate and interact intelligently in unstructured environments.
 - **Assistive Technologies:** Developing more intuitive and effective AI assistants for people with disabilities, capable of understanding and responding to visual cues in their surroundings.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR):** Enhancing AR systems with active visual search capabilities, allowing them to dynamically adapt to user needs and environment changes.
 - **Open-World Navigation and Mobile Manipulation:** Providing the perceptual and reasoning prerequisites for AI agents to perform complex tasks in diverse and unfamiliar real-world settings.

In essence, this work does not merely present incremental improvements; it redefines a critical component of intelligent behavior for AI, laying a robust foundation for building truly embodied and human-like artificial intelligences that can perceive, reason, and act effectively in the dynamic complexities of our world.
