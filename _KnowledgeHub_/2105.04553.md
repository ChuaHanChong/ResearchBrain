# Self-Supervised Learning with Swin Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University, Xi'an Jiaotong University, and Microsoft Research Asia introduced MoBY, a self-supervised learning framework for Vision Transformers. The method, applied to Swin Transformers, achieves 75.0% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K linear evaluation and competitive transfer performance on COCO object detection and ADE20K semantic segmentation, providing the first comprehensive evaluation of SSL for general-purpose Transformer backbones on dense prediction tasks.

## Problem

- Previous self-supervised learning (SSL) evaluations for early Vision Transformers (ViT/DeiT) were limited to ImageNet-1K linear classification, failing to assess their utility for dense prediction tasks.
- Existing SSL methods for Vision Transformers often required computationally intensive "heavy tricks" or very large batch sizes, which hindered accessibility and efficiency.
- The generalizability of SSL-learned representations from Transformer architectures to diverse downstream tasks, particularly dense prediction, remained underexplored.

## Method

- Proposed ==MoBY== (MoCo v2 + BYOL), an efficient self-supervised learning approach combining ==asymmetric encoders==, a momentum update strategy, and a key queue for contrastive learning.
- Integrated MoBY with the ==Swin Transformer== architecture, leveraging its hierarchical and shifted window design as a general-purpose vision backbone suitable for various tasks, including dense prediction.
- Conducted comprehensive evaluations of MoBY-pretrained Swin Transformers on ImageNet-1K linear classification, COCO object detection/instance segmentation, and ADE20K semantic segmentation.

## Results

- MoBY with a Swin-T backbone achieved **75.0%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K linear evaluation after **300** epochs, surpassing DeiT-S by **+2.2%**.
- On COCO object detection and instance segmentation, MoBY-pretrained Swin-T models achieved performance on par with supervised ImageNet-1K pre-trained models (e.g., **43.6** box AP vs. **43.7** supervised with Mask R-CNN **1x**).
- For ADE20K semantic segmentation, MoBY-pretrained Swin-T models performed slightly worse than their supervised counterparts (e.g., **44.06** mIoU vs. **44.51** mIoU supervised).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MoBY, using "lighter tricks" and a smaller batch size (512), achieves competitive ImageNet-1K linear evaluation performance with Vision Transformers, obtaining 75.0% top-1 accuracy with Swin-T.
> - Asymmetric Drop Path, where drop path is applied only to the online encoder but not the target encoder, is crucial for preventing collapse and improving self-supervised learning performance in Transformers.
> - Unlike ResNet-based SSL, self-supervised Swin Transformers do not yet consistently outperform supervised pre-training on downstream dense prediction tasks, indicating a clear research opportunity for specialized SSL objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has undergone two significant transformative shifts in recent years: the rise of self-supervised learning (SSL) and the adoption of Transformer architectures as backbones, displacing traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). This paper sits precisely at the crucial intersection of these two trends.

**Background of the Two Trends:**

1. **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** Pioneered by methods like MoCo, SSL has revolutionized visual representation learning. By training models on pretext tasks derived from unlabeled data (e.g., predicting image rotations, contrasting different views of the same image), SSL has demonstrated the ability to learn highly transferable representations, often outperforming supervised pre-training on ImageNet-1K for various downstream tasks. Key methods in this space include MoCo, BYOL, SimCLR, and their subsequent improvements like MoCo v2 and MoCo v3.
2. **Vision Transformers (ViTs):** Originating from Natural Language Processing (NLP), Transformers were adapted for computer vision by ViT, which showed strong performance in image classification by treating image patches as sequences. Follow-up work like DeiT refined training strategies for ViT, making it more data-efficient. While powerful for classification, initial ViT/DeiT models struggled with dense prediction tasks (like object detection and semantic segmentation) due to their global attention mechanism and lack of inductive biases (locality, hierarchy) inherent in CNNs. This limitation was addressed by the **Swin Transformer**, which introduced hierarchical representations and shifted window attention, making it a general-purpose backbone suitable for a wide array of vision tasks.

**The Research Gap and This Paper's Contribution:**

Prior to this work, the intersection of SSL and Vision Transformers was relatively underexplored, especially concerning the transferability of learned representations to downstream dense prediction tasks. While works like MoCo v3 and DINO began investigating SSL with ViT/DeiT backbones, their evaluations were primarily confined to ImageNet-1K linear classification performance. This limitation arose because ViT/DeiT architectures, in their original forms, were not "tamed" or optimized for dense prediction tasks, preventing a comprehensive assessment of the general-purpose utility of their self-supervised representations.

This paper explicitly aims to bridge this critical gap. By adopting the **Swin Transformer** as the backbone, the authors can evaluate self-supervised learning methods not just on linear classification but also on object detection and semantic segmentation. This makes the evaluation significantly more comprehensive and relevant for real-world applications where general-purpose visual understanding is required. The paper not only introduces a new self-supervised learning approach, **MoBY**, but also meticulously compares its performance against supervised pre-training on various downstream tasks, providing crucial insights into the current state and future directions of SSL with Transformer architectures. It sets a new benchmark for comprehensive evaluation, moving beyond the isolated ImageNet-1K linear evaluation that characterized much of the early work on SSL for Transformers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the "Self-Supervised Learning with Swin Transformers" paper are multifaceted, addressing critical needs in the evolving landscape of computer vision:

1. **Enable Comprehensive Evaluation of SSL for General-Purpose Vision Transformers:**
 - **Motivation:** Previous self-supervised learning efforts with Vision Transformers (e.g., MoCo v3, DINO) largely focused on ViT/DeiT architectures, which, while effective for image classification, were not designed for or well-suited to dense prediction tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation. Consequently, the evaluations of these SSL methods were limited to ImageNet-1K linear classification, providing an incomplete picture of their general utility.
 - **Objective:** To overcome this limitation by leveraging the Swin Transformer, an architecture specifically designed as a general-purpose vision backbone, to facilitate a full-spectrum evaluation of self-supervised representations on a broader range of downstream tasks. This aims to provide a more holistic understanding of how SSL representations perform when transferred to real-world applications requiring fine-grained spatial understanding.

2. **Develop an Effective and Efficient Self-Supervised Learning Approach (MoBY) for Transformers:**
 - **Motivation:** While existing SSL methods for Transformers (MoCo v3, DINO) showed promising results, they often relied on "heavy tricks" (e.g., specific architectural modifications, large batch sizes, complex temperature schedules) that could be computationally intensive or difficult to reproduce. There was a need for a simpler, yet highly effective, approach.
 - **Objective:** To propose a new self-supervised learning method, MoBY (MoCo v2 + BYOL), that combines proven techniques from successful SSL frameworks with "lighter tricks" and more accessible computational requirements (e.g., smaller batch sizes). The goal was to achieve competitive or superior performance on ImageNet-1K linear evaluation while maintaining simplicity and efficiency.

3. **Establish Robust Baselines and Insights for Future Research:**
 - **Motivation:** As the integration of SSL and Transformers was still relatively nascent, the community lacked established baselines for how self-supervised Transformer backbones perform across various tasks, especially dense prediction. Understanding architectural adaptations and hyperparameter sensitivities was also crucial.
 - **Objective:** To provide well-tuned baselines for self-supervised Swin Transformers on ImageNet-1K linear evaluation, COCO object detection/instance segmentation, and ADE20K semantic segmentation. Through detailed ablation studies (e.g., on drop path, queue size, temperature, momentum), the authors aimed to offer valuable insights into optimal training strategies and architectural considerations for SSL with Transformers, thereby accelerating future research in this exciting domain. The open-sourcing of code and models directly supports this objective.

4. **Investigate the Performance Gap Between Supervised and Self-Supervised Transformers on Downstream Tasks:**
 - **Motivation:** For ResNet architectures, SSL methods often demonstrated significant performance gains over supervised pre-training when transferred to downstream tasks. It was unclear if this trend would hold for Transformer architectures, especially given that supervised Transformer training often employs very strong data augmentations.
 - **Objective:** To quantitatively compare the transfer performance of MoBY-trained Swin Transformers against their supervised counterparts on object detection and semantic segmentation to identify potential improvements or existing performance gaps. This comparison helps understand the current maturity of SSL for Transformers and highlights areas where further research is most needed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work lies in proposing a new self-supervised learning approach called **MoBY** and integrating it with the **Swin Transformer** backbone to enable comprehensive evaluation across various computer vision tasks.

#### 3.1. The MoBY Self-Supervised Learning Approach

MoBY is designed as a principled combination of two highly successful existing self-supervised learning frameworks: **MoCo v2** and **BYOL**. The name "MoBY" itself is a portmanteau of these two methods.

- **Architectural Design (Inspired by BYOL):**
 - **Asymmetric Encoders:** MoBY employs two main encoder branches: an "online encoder" and a "target encoder."
 - Both branches consist of a **backbone** (e.g., Swin-T or DeiT-S) followed by a **projector head** (a 2-layer MLP).
 - The online encoder additionally incorporates a **prediction head** (another 2-layer MLP) after its projector, making the two branches asymmetric. This asymmetry is a key feature of BYOL, preventing collapse.
 - **Momentum Update for Target Encoder:** The online encoder is updated via standard gradient descent using an optimizer (AdamW). The target encoder, however, is a momentum-weighted moving average of the online encoder weights. This means its weights are updated smoothly based on the online encoder's progress, typically using a momentum coefficient that gradually increases to 1 over the course of training. This stable target encoder provides consistent training signals.
 - **Asymmetric Data Augmentations:** Similar to BYOL, two different augmented views (v1, v2) of the same input image are fed into the online and target encoders, respectively.

- **Loss Function (Inspired by MoCo v2):**
 - **Contrastive Loss:** MoBY utilizes a contrastive loss, specifically the InfoNCE-like loss, which is central to MoCo v2. For a query feature `q` (from the online encoder's output for view v1), the loss encourages it to be close to its positive key `k+` (from the target encoder's output for view v2 of the same image) and far from a set of negative keys `k_i` (from a memory bank or queue).
 - **Key Queue:** Like MoCo v2, MoBY maintains a dynamically updated "key queue" that stores representations of previous mini-batch samples. This queue provides a large and diverse set of negative samples without requiring extremely large batch sizes, thus making the training more computationally accessible. The default queue size is K=4096.
 - **Symmetric Loss:** The overall loss function is symmetric, meaning it is computed for both (q1, k2) and (q2, k1) pairs, summing the individual contrastive losses.

- **Training Details:**
 - **Optimizer:** AdamW is used, which is standard for Transformer-based models. This contrasts with SGD or LARS often used for ResNet-based SSL.
 - **Hyperparameters:** Fixed learning rate (0.001) and weight decay (0.05) are used. Key hyperparameters such as key queue size (K), starting momentum for the target branch, and temperature (τ) are tuned.
 - **Asymmetric Drop Path:** A crucial regularization technique found to be beneficial. Drop path is applied to the online encoder at a certain rate, but *not* to the target encoder. This asymmetry helps prevent overfitting and improves performance.
 - **Batch Size:** A relatively small batch size of 512 is used, making the approach more affordable than methods requiring very large batch sizes (e.g., 4096 in MoCo v3).

#### 3.2. Swin Transformer as Backbone Architecture

- **Choice Rationale:** The Swin Transformer (specifically, the tiny version, Swin-T) is selected as the primary backbone. This choice is pivotal because Swin Transformer is designed as a hierarchical, general-purpose Vision Transformer suitable for various tasks, including dense prediction. This is a deliberate departure from ViT/DeiT, which were less amenable to these tasks without significant modifications.
- **Complexity:** Swin-T has comparable complexity to ResNet-50 and DeiT-S, making it a fair and relevant choice for comparison.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocols

The research employs a multi-faceted evaluation strategy to thoroughly assess the learned representations:

- **ImageNet-1K Linear Evaluation:** This standard protocol involves pre-training the backbone using MoBY on ImageNet-1K, then freezing its weights and training a simple linear classifier on top. Top-1 accuracy on the validation set is reported. This measures the quality of features for general object recognition.
- **Transferring Performance on Downstream Tasks (Fine-tuning):**
 - **COCO Object Detection and Instance Segmentation:** The pre-trained Swin-T backbone is fine-tuned within popular object detection frameworks like Mask R-CNN and Cascade Mask R-CNN. Performance metrics such as box AP and mask AP are reported.
 - **ADE20K Semantic Segmentation:** The pre-trained Swin-T backbone is fine-tuned with the UPerNet segmentation framework on the ADE20K dataset. Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) is used as the evaluation metric.

These comprehensive evaluations allow the authors to assess not only classification ability but also the transferability and utility of MoBY-learned features for tasks requiring dense, pixel-level understanding, which was a key objective of the paper.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive set of results demonstrating the effectiveness of MoBY with Swin Transformers and providing critical insights into self-supervised learning for Transformer architectures.

#### 4.1. ImageNet-1K Linear Evaluation

- **Competitive Performance with Lighter Tricks:**
 - Using DeiT-S as the backbone and 300 epochs of training, MoBY achieves **72.8% top-1 accuracy**. This is reported to be slightly better than MoCo v3 and DINO (without multi-crop augmentation) under similar training epochs, despite MoBY employing "much lighter tricks" and a smaller batch size (512 vs. 4096 for MoCo v3). This highlights MoBY's efficiency and simplicity.
- **Swin-T Outperforms DeiT-S:**
 - When using the Swin-T backbone, MoBY achieves **75.0% top-1 accuracy** after 300 epochs, surpassing the DeiT-S backbone by a notable +2.2%. This performance gain is even larger than the +1.5% gap observed between supervised DeiT-S and Swin-T, suggesting a strong synergy between MoBY and the Swin Transformer architecture.
- **Potential for Further Improvement:**
 - Initial ablation studies indicated that some "tricks" from MoCo v3/DINO, such as replacing LayerNormalization layers with BatchNormalization before MLP blocks, could further boost MoBY's performance by +1.1% (from 70.9% to 72.0% with 100-epoch training). This suggests MoBY has substantial untapped potential for higher accuracy.

#### 4.2. Transferring Performance on Downstream Tasks (with Swin-T Backbone)

The evaluation on downstream tasks is a crucial differentiator of this work, enabled by the general-purpose Swin Transformer.

- **COCO Object Detection and Instance Segmentation:**
 - MoBY-pretrained Swin-T models achieve **on-par performance** with their supervised ImageNet-1K pre-trained counterparts. For instance, using Mask R-CNN (1x schedule), MoBY yields 43.6 box AP and 39.6 mask AP, which is virtually identical to the supervised baseline's 43.7 box AP and 39.8 mask AP. Similar parity is observed with Cascade Mask R-CNN and 3x training schedules.
 - **Key Observation:** Unlike SSL methods with ResNet backbones, which often show significant performance gains over supervised methods on downstream tasks, MoBY with Swin Transformers does not exhibit such gains. The authors hypothesize this is partly because supervised pre-training of Transformers already uses strong data augmentations, narrowing the gap. This finding highlights a remaining "space to improve" for SSL with Transformers.

- **ADE20K Semantic Segmentation:**
 - On the ADE20K dataset using UPerNet, MoBY performs **slightly worse** than the supervised baseline. For example, MoBY achieves 44.06 mIoU compared to 44.51 mIoU for the supervised method. Even with multi-scale testing, the supervised method still holds a slight edge (45.81 mIoU vs. 45.58 mIoU for MoBY).
 - This further reinforces the observation from COCO that while MoBY with Swin-T performs well, it has not yet surpassed supervised pre-training for dense prediction tasks, indicating an active research area.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Asymmetric Drop Path:** This regularization strategy proved to be crucial. Applying drop path to the *online encoder* (e.g., 0.1 or 0.2 rate) while keeping the *target encoder* without drop path (0.0 rate) significantly improved performance (75.0% top-1 acc with 300 epochs). Conversely, applying drop path to *both* encoders led to a notable performance drop (e.g., 69.0% when both had 0.1 dpr vs. 70.9% when only online had 0.1 dpr for 100 epochs), emphasizing the benefit of asymmetry.
- **Other Hyperparameters:**
 - **Key Queue Size (K):** MoBY was found to be stable across a wide range of queue sizes (from 1024 to 16384), with 4096 being the default.
 - **Temperature (τ):** An optimal temperature of 0.2 yielded the best results, demonstrating its importance for the contrastive loss.
 - **Starting Momentum of Target Encoder:** A starting momentum value of 0.99 for the target encoder proved optimal, highlighting the sensitivity of the momentum update strategy.

In summary, the main findings establish MoBY as a robust and efficient self-supervised learning method for Vision Transformers, particularly Swin Transformer, achieving strong linear evaluation performance with simpler tricks. More importantly, it provides the first comprehensive evaluation of SSL Transformer representations on dense prediction tasks, revealing that while performance is competitive, there's still room for SSL to surpass supervised methods in this domain, unlike the trend observed with CNNs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Self-Supervised Learning with Swin Transformers" carries significant implications for the future direction of computer vision research, particularly in the realm of visual representation learning and general-purpose models.

1. **Setting a New Benchmark for Comprehensive Evaluation:** The most immediate and significant impact of this work is the establishment of a more comprehensive evaluation standard for self-supervised Vision Transformers. By demonstrating how Swin Transformers can be effectively used for SSL and subsequently evaluated on downstream dense prediction tasks (object detection and semantic segmentation), the paper moves beyond the limited ImageNet-1K linear evaluation that characterized earlier SSL-Transformer studies. This is crucial because real-world computer vision applications frequently require dense prediction capabilities, and the generalizability of learned features across tasks is paramount. This work will encourage future research to adopt similar comprehensive evaluation protocols, leading to the development of truly general-purpose SSL models.

2. **Robust and Accessible Baseline (MoBY):** MoBY provides a strong, yet conceptually simpler and more computationally accessible, baseline for self-supervised learning with Transformer architectures. By combining established techniques from MoCo v2 and BYOL and requiring "lighter tricks" and smaller batch sizes than contemporary methods like MoCo v3 or DINO, MoBY lowers the barrier to entry for researchers. Its open-source availability (code and models) further facilitates its adoption, enabling other researchers to build upon this foundation and accelerate the development of more advanced SSL techniques for Transformers.

3. **Highlighting a Critical Research Gap:** The finding that self-supervised Swin Transformers, while competitive, do not yet consistently outperform supervised pre-training on downstream dense prediction tasks (unlike the trend observed with ResNet-based SSL) is a highly significant insight. This gap suggests that the interaction between Transformer architectures, their inherent inductive biases, and various SSL objectives might be different from that of CNNs. It explicitly points to a rich area for future research:
 - Developing novel SSL objectives specifically tailored to Transformer architectures.
 - Exploring how to better leverage Transformer-specific properties (e.g., attention mechanisms) within SSL.
 - Investigating the interplay between data augmentation strategies in supervised vs. self-supervised Transformer pre-training.

4. **Confirming Swin Transformer's Versatility and Robustness:** This paper further solidifies the Swin Transformer's position as a highly versatile and robust backbone for computer vision. By showcasing its efficacy not only in supervised settings but also as a powerful architecture for self-supervised representation learning, it reinforces its potential as a foundational model for future vision systems. This will likely lead to even broader adoption of Swin Transformers across various research and application domains.

5. **Accelerating Future Research in Self-Supervised Vision Transformers:** By providing strong baselines, detailed ablation studies, and an open-source implementation, the authors have laid crucial groundwork. The insights gained regarding hyperparameters (e.g., asymmetric drop path, optimal temperature, momentum scheduling) and architectural considerations will directly guide subsequent investigations. This enables the community to focus on innovative aspects rather than re-establishing basic settings, thereby accelerating progress in developing more powerful and generalized self-supervised Vision Transformers.

In conclusion, "Self-Supervised Learning with Swin Transformers" is a timely and impactful paper that critically advances the understanding and application of self-supervised learning in the era of Vision Transformers. It not only delivers a capable new SSL method but, more importantly, provides a comprehensive framework and critical insights that will shape future research directions in this rapidly evolving field.
