# Re$^2$MoGen: Open-Vocabulary Motion Generation via LLM Reasoning and Physics-Aware Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> Re²MoGen is a framework designed for open-vocabulary human motion generation that combines Large Language Model reasoning with physics-aware refinement. The method leverages LLMs to plan initial keyframe sequences and then refines full-body motions using reinforcement learning, achieving superior semantic alignment and significantly reduced physical implausibilities (e.g., Float error of 2.46 mm) on unseen text descriptions.

## Problem

- Existing Text-to-Motion (T2M) models struggle to generalize to open-vocabulary text descriptions, performing poorly on inputs outside their training data distribution.
- Previous T2M approaches often neglect crucial temporal dynamics or accumulate errors through multi-stage processes, leading to unrealistic or disjointed motions.
- Generated motions frequently lack physical plausibility, exhibiting artifacts such as foot sliding, ground penetration, or floating.

## Method

- An ==MCTS-enhanced Large Language Model== reasoning stage translates natural language into ==sparse keyframe positions== for critical human joints, systematically exploring optimal sequences.
- A motion completion and finetuning stage generates smooth, full-body motions from these sparse keyframes by optimizing full-body poses using a ==VPoser prior== and finetuning a ==diffusion model== with ==dynamic temporal matching (soft-DTW)==.
- A ==physics-aware refinement stage== frames the diffusion denoising process as a ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)== and uses ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== with ==physics-aware rewards== to correct implausibilities like foot sliding, floating, and ground penetration. The full framework is named **Re²MoGen**.

## Results

- **Re²MoGen** achieved the highest semantic alignment scores on open-vocabulary descriptions (CLIP S of **23.64 ± 0.49** and VLM S of **2.72 ± 0.21**), outperforming existing methods.
- The framework significantly reduced physical implausibilities, demonstrating the lowest Float (**2.46 ± 1.05 mm**) and Penetration (**21.70 ± 2.70 mm**) errors among compared baselines.
- Refined motions showed improved tracking accuracy in physics simulators (e.g., E_mpbpe decreased from **68.20 mm** to **51.21 mm**) and were successfully deployed on both simulated and physical humanoid robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enhancing LLM reasoning with Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) significantly improves the semantic consistency of generated keyframe plans for open-vocabulary motion descriptions.
> - Dynamic temporal matching, integrated into the motion completion process, is crucial for flexibly aligning generated motions with sparse keyframes, ensuring natural and continuous sequences.
> - Framing the diffusion model's denoising as an MDP and using physics-aware reinforcement learning effectively mitigates physical implausibilities, leading to more realistic and executable motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of Text-to-Motion (T2M) generation, a domain focused on synthesizing human character movements from textual descriptions. Existing T2M models typically achieve high-quality and semantically consistent motion generation when operating within the distribution of their training data, relying on large datasets of text-motion pairs. However, a significant challenge in this area is **open-vocabulary motion generation**, where models must generate motions corresponding to textual descriptions that differ substantially from their training data. This limitation arises from the inherent disparity between the finite nature of motion datasets and the expansive, diverse possibilities of natural language.

Previous approaches to address open-vocabulary T2M generation have explored several avenues:
- **CLIP-based approaches:** Methods such as MotionCLIP and AvatarCLIP map motion data into visual-language embedding spaces (e.g., CLIP) to leverage cross-modal alignment. A limitation of these approaches is their tendency to deconstruct motion sequences into individual static poses for alignment, which can lead to the neglect of crucial temporal dynamics, often resulting in unrealistic or disjointed motions.
- **LLM-decomposition approaches:** Works like PRO-Motion and MCD employ Large Language Models (LLMs) to break down unseen action descriptions into combinations of known textual elements. These elements are then used with pre-trained models for generation and splicing. However, these methods are constrained by the textual coverage of their pre-training data, and their multi-stage planning and splicing processes are susceptible to error accumulation, causing generated actions to deviate from the original text description.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based exploration:** Approaches such as AnySkill and GROVE utilize the exploration capabilities of RL agents, guided by specially designed reward functions, to discover action states that conform to text descriptions. These online exploration methods can yield unstable results and are highly dependent on the quality and design of the reward functions.

This work positions itself by seeking to overcome the limitations of existing methods, particularly the inability to generalize to novel, out-of-distribution text prompts while maintaining both semantic fidelity and physical plausibility. It capitalizes on recent advancements in LLMs, specifically their enhanced spatial reasoning and action planning capabilities, to provide a more robust and generalized solution for open-vocabulary motion generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of generating semantically consistent and physically plausible human motions from open-vocabulary textual descriptions. This objective directly addresses the generalization limitations observed in many existing text-to-motion (T2M) models, which perform well within their training data distribution but falter when encountering novel or out-of-distribution commands.

The specific key objectives include:
- **Enhancing generalization capability:** To enable motion generation models to interpret and execute diverse, unseen textual instructions beyond the scope of their training datasets.
- **Ensuring semantic alignment:** To generate motions that accurately reflect the semantic meaning of complex and novel natural language descriptions.
- **Maintaining physical plausibility:** To ensure that the generated motions adhere to physical laws and appear natural, avoiding artifacts such as foot sliding, ground penetration, or floating.
- **Leveraging LLM reasoning for motion planning:** To exploit the advanced reasoning and planning capabilities of Large Language Models to formulate initial motion plans from high-level text.
- **Refining motions for physical realism:** To integrate physics-aware mechanisms to correct and enhance the physical realism of LLM-generated motion plans, which might initially lack fine-grained physical consistency.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations of current T2M generation techniques:
- **Disparity between finite data and infinite language:** Traditional models, trained on finite text-motion paired datasets, struggle to generalize to the infinite diversity of natural language, leading to performance deterioration with open-vocabulary descriptions.
- **Temporal coherence and realism issues:** CLIP-based methods, by treating motions as static poses, often discard crucial temporal dynamics, resulting in unrealistic or discontinuous sequences.
- **Error accumulation in staged generation:** LLM-based decomposition and splicing methods are prone to accumulating errors across stages, leading to deviations from the original text.
- **Instability and reward dependency in RL approaches:** Purely RL-based methods often produce unstable results and heavily depend on meticulously designed reward functions.
- **Lack of concrete spatial planning in LLMs:** While LLMs have shown abstract spatial understanding, they typically lack the capability for precise, concrete spatial planning (e.g., exact 3D joint movements), which is essential for human motion.

Re²MoGen aims to bridge these gaps by combining the high-level reasoning power of LLMs with detailed motion synthesis and physics-based refinement, offering a more robust and generalized solution for generating human motions from varied textual inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Re²MoGen, operates in three sequential stages: MCTS-enhanced LLM Reasoning, Motion Completion and Finetuning, and Physics-aware Refinement. The approach is designed to translate open-vocabulary textual descriptions into executable, physically plausible human motions.

#### 3.1. MCTS-enhanced LLM Reasoning

This initial stage focuses on translating a natural language motion description into a sequence of keyframe positions for specific human joints. To simplify the LLM's task, only the positions of the root joint (pelvis) and four end-effector joints (left/right wrist, left/right ankle) are planned.
- **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS):** MCTS is employed to enhance the LLM's reasoning by systematically exploring and identifying promising keyframe sequences. A Motion Keyframe Tree (MKT) is constructed, where each node stores key joint positions for a segment of keyframes. The tree grows iteratively, with sequences rewarded based on the quality of complete keyframe generation.
 - **Selection:** The algorithm traverses the tree by selecting child nodes with the maximum Upper Confidence Bound (UCT) value, balancing exploitation (high reward) and exploration (less visited nodes).
 - **Expansion:** Upon reaching a leaf node, the LLM is prompted to complete the keyframe sequence, and the first new keyframe segment forms a child node.
 - **Simulation:** The quality of each complete keyframe sequence generated by the LLM is evaluated. Full-body poses are reconstructed using a human pose model, and corresponding keyframe images are rendered. A CLIP-based evaluation model calculates the semantic consistency score between the text prompt and the rendered images.
 - **Backpropagation:** The computed score is propagated back up the search path, updating visit counts and accumulated rewards for all traversed nodes, and subsequently their average reward (Q-value) and UCT values.

#### 3.2. Motion Completion and Finetuning

This stage takes the LLM-planned keyframes and generates a smooth, full-body motion sequence.
- **Full-body Pose Optimization:** Since LLMs only provide sparse key joint positions, a human pose model (VPoser, enhanced with Motion-X dataset training) is used as a prior to optimize the full-body poses for these keyframes. Instead of directly optimizing raw pose parameters, the process operates in VPoser's latent space, where a latent code `z` is optimized. The objective `L_pose` minimizes the distance between FK-derived joint positions and the LLM-specified `j_key`, regularized by `||z||^2` to keep `z` within the learned latent distribution.
- **Spatiotemporal Motion Completion:** A pre-trained Motion Latent Diffusion (MLD) model is fine-tuned to generate a complete, fine-grained motion sequence between the optimized key poses.
 - **Dynamic Temporal Matching:** To address potential temporal misalignment between planned key poses and prior-consistent motions, a dynamic temporal matching objective `L_temporal` is introduced, based on soft Dynamic Time Warping (soft-DTW). This allows flexible alignment by calculating a cumulative cost matrix based on pairwise pose distances.
 - **Reconstruction Loss:** An additional reconstruction loss `L_recon` is used to ensure precise adherence to the planned key poses at their aligned timesteps.
 - The final optimization objective `L_MLD` is a weighted sum of `L_recon` and `L_temporal`.

#### 3.3. Physics-aware Refinement

The final stage addresses physical implausibility in the generated motions, which might still arise from reasoning errors or limitations of the generative model.
- **MDP Formulation:** The reverse denoising process of the MLD model is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
 - **State Space (S):** Comprises the motion description (`c`) and the motion at a given denoising timestep (`m_T-t`).
 - **Action Space (A):** The motion at the next denoising timestep (`m_T-t-1`).
 - **Reward Function (R):** A sparse reward function where only the final state (the generated motion `m_0`) receives a non-zero reward, based on its physical plausibility.
 - **Transition Function (P):** Governed by the diffusion model's dynamics.
- **Physics-aware Reward Functions:** Three metrics from prior work are adopted to define the reward `r(m_0)`:
 - **Foot Sliding Reward:** Penalizes undesirable foot movements when ground contact is expected.
 - **Floating Reward:** Penalizes postures where feet are off the ground when contact is required.
 - **Ground Penetration Reward:** Prevents body parts from sinking below the ground plane.
- **Post-training Objective:** The objective is to maximize the expected physics-aware reward using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. A Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence objective is also incorporated to regularize policy updates and prevent excessive deviation from the original MLD policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate Re²MoGen's ability to generate reasonable motions for open-vocabulary descriptions, the impact of MCTS and dynamic temporal matching, and the effectiveness of physics-aware refinement.

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

- **Datasets:** HumanML3D, AMASS, and Motion-X were used. HumanML3D was reconstructed for open-vocabulary testing by partitioning 40 texts as "unseen" and filtering the remaining for training a base MLD model. Motion-X was used to enhance VPoser.
- **Baselines:** MDM, MLD, MotionGPT (diffusion-based, fit training distribution), MotionCLIP (CLIP-aligned), and AnySkill (RL-based exploration for unseen texts) were used for comparison.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Semantic Alignment:** CLIP Score (CLIP S, average CLIP similarity between text and rendered motion frames) and VLM Score (VLM S, a weighted score from QWen-VL for semantic alignment and naturalness of rendered videos). Higher scores indicate better alignment.
 - **Physical Plausibility:** Floating (Float, average distance feet are above ground when they should be touching) and Penetration (Pene, average distance body parts are below ground). Lower values indicate better physical plausibility.

#### 4.2. Main Results (Q1: Open-vocabulary Motion Generation)

Re²MoGen demonstrated superior performance compared to existing methods for open-vocabulary descriptions.
- **Quantitative Comparison (Table 1):**
 - Re²MoGen (full) achieved the highest CLIP S (23.64 ± 0.49) and VLM S (2.72 ± 0.21), indicating better semantic alignment with unseen texts.
 - Traditional methods (MDM, MLD, MotionGPT) showed limited generalization. MotionCLIP, despite leveraging CLIP, produced lower VLM S, suggesting discontinuous motions.
 - AnySkill, an RL-based exploration method for unseen texts, showed suboptimal performance (CLIP S: 20.04 ± 0.92, VLM S: 0.95 ± 0.17), attributed to RL instability and a reward function that evaluates immediate states rather than full sequences.
 - In terms of physical plausibility, Re²MoGen (full) significantly reduced Float (2.46 ± 1.05 mm) and Pene (21.70 ± 2.70 mm) compared to other methods, confirming the effectiveness of RL post-training.
- **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 2):** Visual examples showed Re²MoGen generating more accurate and complete motions for complex descriptions like "Hold left leg while standing with right one" and "Raise T pose, step forward and lower both knees," where baselines often failed to capture the full semantic intent or sequence.

#### 4.3. Impact of MCTS and Dynamic Temporal Matching (Q2)

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of MCTS and dynamic temporal matching.
- **Quantitative Ablation (Table 2):**
 - **Without MCTS (w/o MCTS):** Performance dropped in both CLIP S (22.57 ± 0.41) and VLM S (1.96 ± 0.26). This indicated that restricting the LLM to a single planning attempt limited its exploration of the solution space, leading to less consistent plans.
 - **Without Dynamic Temporal Matching (w/o L_temporal):** Performance also decreased (CLIP S: 23.16 ± 0.54, VLM S: 1.57 ± 0.11). This demonstrated that rigidly aligning generated motion with keyframes without temporal flexibility resulted in unnatural sequences.
These results underscore that MCTS enhances LLM reasoning for better text-consistent plans, and dynamic temporal matching is crucial for flexible and natural motion completion.

#### 4.4. Impact of Physics-aware Refinement (Q3)

The RL post-training with physics-aware rewards significantly improved motion physical plausibility.
- **Quantitative Evaluation in IsaacGym (Table 3):**
 - Comparing "Ours (w/o RL)" with "Ours (full)", the full model showed reduced error across all tracking metrics (E_mpbpe, E_mpjpe, E_mpbve, E_mpbae, E_mpjve). For instance, E_mpbpe decreased from 68.20 ± 17.25 to 51.21 ± 12.22.
 - This improvement in tracking accuracy indicated that the refined motions mitigated physical implausibilities, making them more amenable to imitation by trained policies.
- **Real-world Deployment:** Generated motions, refined by the physics-aware stage, were successfully transferred to the MuJoCo platform (sim-to-sim transfer, Figure 3) and deployed on physical humanoid robots (Figure 4), demonstrating the practical viability and real-world potential of the method.

#### 4.5. Additional Experiments

- **SnapMotion Dataset:** Re²MoGen outperformed MLD, MotionGPT, and MoMask on the SnapMotion dataset, reinforcing its generalization capability.
- **Impact of Different LLMs:** LLMs with "deep thinking capabilities" (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-Plus) demonstrated superior performance compared to those without, suggesting the importance of advanced reasoning for keyframe planning.
- **Smoothing Parameter γ:** The method showed robustness across a range of `γ` values for soft-DTW, with `γ = 0.1` selected as the optimal choice.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Re²MoGen framework represents an advancement in open-vocabulary text-to-motion (T2M) generation by effectively integrating Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning with physics-aware refinement. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Generalization in T2M:** The primary contribution is addressing the fundamental challenge of generating motions for unseen, open-vocabulary text descriptions. By moving beyond models constrained to their training data distributions, Re²MoGen broadens the applicability of T2M systems significantly. This generalization capability is crucial for creating more versatile and user-friendly motion generation tools.
- **Improved Semantic Consistency:** The MCTS-enhanced LLM reasoning stage enables the framework to better interpret complex and nuanced textual instructions, leading to motions that are more semantically aligned with the user's intent. This addresses a common failing in previous methods where generated motions might capture isolated elements but miss the overall narrative or sequence of actions described.
- **Guaranteed Physical Plausibility:** The integration of a physics-aware reinforcement learning (RL) refinement stage is a critical component. It effectively mitigates physical implausibilities (such as foot sliding, floating, and ground penetration) that can arise from purely semantic-driven planning or generative models. This ensures that the synthesized motions appear natural and realistic, a prerequisite for many practical applications.
- **Novel Hybrid Approach:** Re²MoGen introduces a novel multi-stage pipeline that synergistically combines symbolic planning (LLM reasoning), learned motion priors (full-body pose optimization and MLD finetuning), and physics-based control (RL refinement). This hybrid approach capitalizes on the strengths of each component, demonstrating an effective strategy for complex generative tasks.
- **Practical Applicability:** The successful deployment of generated motions on both simulated (MuJoCo) and real-world robotic platforms (G1 robot) indicates the method's practical viability. This has significant implications for:
 - **Animation and Game Development:** Generating a wider range of character animations from natural language prompts, reducing manual labor and increasing creative flexibility.
 - **Humanoid Robotics:** Providing a more intuitive way to program complex and varied behaviors for humanoid robots from high-level textual commands, moving towards more generalist and adaptable robots.
 - **Virtual Reality and Metaverse Applications:** Populating virtual environments with diverse and realistic human behaviors on demand.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future research in LLM-driven embodied AI, particularly in bridging the gap between high-level linguistic instructions and low-level physical execution for human and robotic agents. It highlights the potential of using LLMs not just for abstract reasoning but also for generating precise spatial and temporal plans when properly augmented and refined.

In summary, Re²MoGen offers a robust solution to a long-standing challenge in motion generation, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable systems that can understand and enact human-like movements from a broad spectrum of linguistic inputs.
