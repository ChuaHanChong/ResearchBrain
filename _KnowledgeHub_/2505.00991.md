# DexCtrl: Towards Sim-to-Real Dexterity with Adaptive Controller Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel adaptive controller learning framework enables successful sim-to-real transfer of dexterous manipulation policies by jointly predicting actions and controller parameters based on historical trajectories, demonstrating improved performance on in-hand object rotation and flipping tasks using a LEAP robotic hand while reducing the need for manual parameter tuning.

## Problem

- Transferring policies from simulation to real robots is challenging due to discrepancies in low-level controller dynamics
- Manual tuning of controller parameters is labor-intensive and task-specific while domain randomization can increase training difficulty
- The mismatch between simulated and real robot controller behavior has been relatively overlooked in prior sim-to-real research, despite being a major contributor to the transfer gap

## Method

- DexCtrl framework that learns to predict both actions and controller parameters adaptively based on historical information
- Two-module architecture using self-attention for action prediction and cross-attention for controller parameter prediction
- Training an oracle policy in simulation and distilling into a student policy for real-world deployment

## Results

- Outperformed manual tuning baseline in simulation and achieved successful transfer to real robot without additional training
- Demonstrated robust performance across objects with varying physical parameters
- Showed significantly better performance than baseline without controller adaptation, especially in real-world experiments

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mismatch in robot controller behavior is a critical but often overlooked factor in the sim-to-real gap
> - Adaptive controller parameter prediction is crucial for robust real-world performance
> - Controller parameters systematically adapt to object properties (e.g., increased stiffness for heavier objects)

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in robotics: transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world robotic systems, particularly in the domain of dexterous manipulation. This is a significant area of research due to the complexity of real-world environments, the difficulty of accurately modeling robot dynamics and interactions, and the need for robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured settings.

**Broader Research Landscape:**

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A major focus in robotics, with various approaches including domain randomization, system identification, and policy adaptation. This work falls into the policy adaptation category, specifically focusing on adapting controller parameters.
- **Dexterous Manipulation:** A challenging subfield of robotics that aims to enable robots to perform intricate tasks requiring fine motor skills and precise control. This paper contributes to this field by proposing a method to improve the robustness and adaptability of dexterous manipulation policies.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Robotics:** The use of reinforcement learning (RL) to train robotic policies has become increasingly popular. This work leverages RL to train an oracle policy in simulation and then distills this policy into a student policy that can be deployed in the real world.
- **Adaptive Control:** The paper draws upon principles of adaptive control, where controller parameters are adjusted in real-time to compensate for uncertainties and disturbances. While adaptive control has been explored in other robotics applications, this work specifically applies it to the problem of sim-to-real transfer in dexterous manipulation.

This research builds upon existing work in these areas by specifically addressing the "controller gap" between simulation and the real world. It argues that discrepancies in low-level controller dynamics are a significant factor hindering sim-to-real transfer, and proposes a novel method to mitigate this issue.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of this research are:

- **Identify the mismatch of robot controllers as a critical factor in the sim-to-real gap.** The authors argue that this aspect has been relatively overlooked in previous work.
- **Propose a novel method (DexCtrl) to adaptively adjust controller parameters based on historical information.** This aims to narrow the sim-to-real gap without extensive manual tuning or excessive randomization.
- **Design a simple and elegant framework to jointly obtain actions and controller parameters based on historical information.** This is intended to offer better adaptivity to force variation.
- **Demonstrate that the method can significantly outperform baselines in both simulation and the real world.** They aim to validate the effectiveness of DexCtrl through experiments on two different dexterous manipulation tasks.

The motivation behind this work stems from the limitations of existing sim-to-real transfer approaches. Manual tuning of controller parameters is labor-intensive and task-specific, while randomization can increase training difficulty. The authors argue for a more principled and automatic approach to address the controller gap.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research can be broken down into the following key steps:

1. **Oracle Policy Training:** A model-free Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning algorithm is used to train an oracle policy in simulation. This policy learns to output both actions and controller parameters simultaneously. The reward function is designed to encourage desired task behaviors, such as object rotation and contact with fingertips.

2. **Data Collection:** The trained oracle policy is used to collect a dataset of state-action-controller parameter tuples in simulation. This dataset captures the behavior of the policy under diverse object physical parameters.

3. **Student Policy Distillation:** The collected dataset is used to train a student policy. The student policy is divided into two separate modules:
 - **Action Prediction Module:** Predicts desired joint actions based on historical joint trajectories and controller parameters.
 - **Control Parameter Prediction Module:** Predicts controller parameters based on historical joint trajectories, controller parameters, and the predicted action.
 Self-attention is used for action prediction, and cross-attention is used to learn the relationships between current actions and historical inputs for controller parameter prediction.

4. **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The trained student policy is deployed on a real-world robotic system (LEAP hand) without further training. The controller parameters are linearly mapped from simulation values to real-world values.

5. **Evaluation:** The performance of the method is evaluated on two dexterous manipulation tasks: in-hand object rotation and flipping. The performance is compared against two baselines: manual tuning and a variant of DexCtrl without PD (i.e., without adaptive controller parameter prediction). Metrics used for evaluation include rotation reward/radians, time to fail, object linear velocity, and torque penalty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of this research are:

- **DexCtrl improves upon the original oracle performance in simulation.** The method achieves significantly better performance than the manual tuning baseline in terms of rotation reward and time to fail, even without any controller gap.
- **DexCtrl narrows the sim-to-real gap.** The method significantly outperforms the baselines in real-world experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in transferring policies from simulation to the real world.
- **The adaptive controller parameter prediction is crucial for real-world performance.** The performance gap between DexCtrl and the "Ours w/o PD" baseline is much larger in the real world than in simulation, highlighting the importance of adaptively adjusting controller parameters to compensate for real-world uncertainties.
- **DexCtrl performs well across objects with varying physical parameters.** The method demonstrates robust performance on objects with different masses and friction coefficients.
- **The learned controller parameters adapt systematically to object properties.** The learned stiffness values increase with object mass, indicating that the controller is learning to adjust the contact forces based on the object's physical characteristics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are:

- **Addresses a critical challenge in robotics:** By addressing the controller gap in sim-to-real transfer, this work contributes to making it easier to deploy robotic policies trained in simulation on real-world systems.
- **Reduces the need for manual tuning and randomization:** The adaptive controller learning approach proposed in this paper can potentially reduce the amount of human effort required to transfer policies from simulation to the real world.
- **Improves the robustness and adaptability of dexterous manipulation policies:** By learning to adjust controller parameters in real-time, the method can improve the robustness and adaptability of dexterous manipulation policies to variations in object properties and environmental conditions.
- **Potential for broader applications:** The approach could be extended to other robotic tasks and systems where controller dynamics play a significant role in performance.
- **Enables more complex and realistic robotic applications:** By improving sim-to-real transfer, this work can contribute to the development of more complex and realistic robotic applications, such as in-hand object manipulation, assembly, and tool use.

In conclusion, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of robotics by addressing the sim-to-real gap in dexterous manipulation through adaptive controller learning. The proposed method, DexCtrl, demonstrates improved performance and robustness in both simulation and real-world experiments, offering a promising approach to bridging the gap between simulated and real-world robotic systems.
