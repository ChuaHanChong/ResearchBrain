# PrimitiveVLA: Learning Reusable Motion Primitives for Efficient and Generalizable Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PrimitiveVLA, developed by researchers from the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences and others, introduces a "Primitive-Centric Disassemble & Assemble" paradigm for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This framework learns and composes reusable motion primitives, demonstrating up to 6-fold improvements in zero-shot generalization and achieving high performance with 50% of the training data on various robotic manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limitations in data efficiency and generalization, struggling to adapt pre-trained models to new downstream domains.
- The prevalent "Direct Instruction-to-Control Mapping" paradigm leads models to memorize monolithic, task-specific trajectories, rather than reusable underlying motion patterns.
- Knowledge transfer across tasks is inefficient, requiring extensive new demonstrations for even slightly varied tasks or novel object-skill combinations.

## Method

- A "Primitive-Centric Disassemble & Assemble" paradigm is proposed, which defines 11 reusable motion primitives and leverages an automated pipeline to extract primitive-level supervision from task-level demonstrations.
- An inference phase uses a VLM-based primitive planner to sequence primitives for novel tasks and an LLM-generated switch module to manage robust closed-loop execution and transitions between primitives.
- A Multimodal Canonical Representation (MCR) unifies multimodal inputs by mapping task-specific instructions to canonical primitive instructions and using object-centric masked observations, decoupling invariant physical motions from task contexts.

## Results

- PrimitiveVLA achieved superior or competitive performance with full data, improving OpenVLA by **9.2%** and OpenVLA-OFT by **5.0%** on Libero-90, and matched full-data performance with only **50%** of the training data.
- It demonstrated substantial zero-shot generalization, boosting OpenVLA's novel task success rate by 6-fold (to **45.50%**) and increasing π0.5's long-horizon task success from **30.50%** to **80.25%**.
- In real-world evaluations on a UR5e robot, PrimitiveVLA achieved a **90%** average success rate on in-distribution tasks and demonstrated 60-**70%** success on zero-shot compositional tasks where baseline methods failed (**0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Multimodal Canonical Representation (MCR) is the primary driver for improving out-of-domain (OOD) transferability by standardizing inputs and mitigating contextual interference during learning.
> - Primitive Disassembly is critical for enhancing execution stability and robust generalization in long-horizon tasks by enabling the VLA model to learn distinct, physically grounded motion segments.
> - Decoupling invariant physical motions from specific task contexts through canonical representation and primitive learning significantly improves data efficiency and the reusability of learned behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen a rise in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, driven by the convergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) and robotic control. Models such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and Octo, trained on large-scale datasets like Open X-Embodiment and DROID, demonstrate capabilities in open-world manipulation. These models aim to establish "foundation models" for embodied AI by projecting visual observations, instructions, and actions into a unified latent space.

However, a recurring limitation in existing VLA models is their struggle with data efficiency and generalization, particularly when adapting pre-trained models to downstream domains via fine-tuning. Benchmarks like AGNOSTOS reveal brittle performance and difficulties in handling novel object-skill combinations. The paper identifies a fundamental bottleneck in the prevailing "Direct Instruction-to-Control Mapping" paradigm, where models are trained to map high-level instructions directly to low-level control. This approach often leads to models memorizing monolithic, task-specific trajectories and scene-specific visual patterns, rather than learning reusable underlying motion patterns. Consequently, knowledge transfer across tasks is inefficient, requiring extensive new demonstrations for each new task. While some contemporary work, such as $\pi_{0.7}$, introduces task decomposition at inference, its fine-tuning process still adheres to this direct mapping, limiting data reuse.

In parallel, hierarchical robot learning has explored decomposing tasks into modular skills, employing methods like options, Dynamical Movement Primitives (DMPs), unsupervised skill discovery using continuous latent models (e.g., SPiRL, OPAL), or vector quantization (e.g., BeT, VQ-BeT). However, these often lack explicit semantic supervision, resulting in statistical representations that are not directly interpretable or reusable in the context of VLA fine-tuning. LLM-based planners (e.g., SayCan, Code as Policies) address semantic aspects but function primarily as high-level planners, separate from low-level control. Other decomposition efforts (e.g., Pivot-R, Manipulate Anything, StARe VLA) face limitations in generating precise, action-aligned segmentation or use decomposition primarily for reinforcement learning rewards.

PrimitiveVLA positions itself by proposing a "Primitive-Centric Disassemble & Assemble" paradigm as an alternative to the "Direct Instruction-to-Control Mapping." It aims to bridge the gap between high-level reasoning and low-level control by enabling VLA models to learn and compose semantically interpretable and physically grounded motion primitives. This approach seeks to enhance data efficiency and generalization by decoupling invariant physical motions from specific task contexts during training and then reassembling these learned primitives to solve novel tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address the data inefficiency and limited generalization capabilities observed in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly during their fine-tuning and deployment phases. The paper contends that these issues stem from the prevalent "Direct Instruction-to-Control Mapping" paradigm, which compels models to learn and memorize entire, monolithic task trajectories rather than focusing on foundational, reusable motion patterns, referred to as "primitives."

The motivation for proposing the Primitive-Centric Disassemble & Assemble paradigm is multi-faceted:

1. **Enhance Data Efficiency:** In the traditional paradigm, each new task, even if it shares underlying motion patterns (e.g., "opening a cabinet" vs. "opening a microwave" both involve a "pull" motion), requires learning a distinct, often expensive, new demonstration. By disassembling tasks into a finite set of reusable primitives, the model can learn shared motion patterns more efficiently. Training on segmented primitives as independent samples, with re-represented inputs, aims to decouple physical motion from specific task contexts. This re-regulation of data is intended to shift the VLA's learning focus towards mastering invariant motion patterns, thereby increasing the utility of each demonstration.

2. **Improve Generalization Capabilities:** The prevailing direct mapping approach can lead to models overfitting to scene-specific visual patterns rather than abstracting transferable physical motions. This results in brittle performance and poor generalization to unseen or long-horizon tasks. By mastering a library of robust, task-agnostic primitives (e.g., Grasp, Pull, Push), the model is expected to achieve compositional generalization, flexibly assembling these learned behaviors to tackle novel, unseen tasks without requiring new task-specific demonstrations.

To realize this Primitive-Centric paradigm, the authors identified three specific challenges that motivated their methodological design:

1. **Disassembling during Fine-tuning (Label Scarcity):** Existing public datasets primarily offer task-level instructions, lacking fine-grained primitive labels. Manual annotation of these labels is labor-intensive and expensive. This motivated the development of an **automated pipeline** for primitive disassembly, leveraging existing VLM and LLM capabilities to derive primitive sequences and boundaries without human intervention.

2. **Assembling during Inference (Control Complexity):** Effectively combining discrete primitives into a coherent, multi-stage task during real-time inference requires a robust mechanism for managing the execution flow and precisely triggering transitions between primitives in closed-loop environments. This motivated the design of an **assembly mechanism** comprising a VLM-based planner for sequencing primitives and an LLM-generated switch module for managing the precise, rule-based switching logic.

3. **Maintaining Invariance across Tasks (Contextual Interference):** For primitives to be truly reusable, they must remain consistent across diverse tasks. However, task-specific visual or linguistic details can interfere with primitive learning, or conversely, over-unification might strip away task-relevant information, rendering primitives unusable. This motivated the introduction of a **Multimodal Canonical Representation (MCR)** to standardize inputs, unifying instructions to align motion patterns while using object-centric masks to preserve essential task context.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond monolithic trajectory learning to a structured, compositional learning approach based on reusable primitives, thereby addressing the limitations of data inefficiency and generalization in VLA models, and enabling more scalable embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PrimitiveVLA proposes a Primitive-Centric Disassemble & Assemble paradigm, structured into two main phases—Fine-tuning Disassembly and Inference Assembly—unified by a Multimodal Canonical Representation (MCR). The framework is designed to be model-agnostic, allowing various VLA backbones to be integrated.

#### 3.1. Composable Primitives

The framework defines a library $\mathcal{C}$ of 11 reusable primitives, categorized as Spatial Transport (Grasp, Place, Lift, Move), Contact & Interaction (Push, Pull, Insert, Press), and Orientation (Twist, Tilt, Rotate). Each primitive has a kinematic definition (e.g., Grasp: Approaches, seizes target, and performs preliminary lift). This standardized library serves as the shared action space for both fine-tuning and inference.

#### 3.2. Fine-tuning Phase: Primitive Disassembly

To generate primitive-level supervision from task-level demonstrations, an automated pipeline disassembles monolithic trajectories ($\tau$) into primitive-aligned samples.

- **Primitive Sequence Reasoning (Section 4.1.1):** A VLM ($f_{VLM}$) identifies the high-level semantic primitive sequence $S = [p_1, p_2..., p_k]$ for a given task. This is achieved by inputting the task instruction ($l$), an example RGB sequence ($V_{\tau}$), and the primitive library ($\mathcal{C}$) into the VLM. The resulting (instruction, sequence) pairs are stored in a Disassembly Library $D$, providing semantic priors for subsequent stages.

- **State-Based Boundary Segmentation (Section 4.1.2):** An LLM generates Python code ($\phi_i$) that defines termination criteria for each primitive $p_i \in \mathcal{C}$ based on its physical definition. For a primitive starting at $t_{start}$, the endpoint $t_{end}$ is determined by evaluating proprioceptive states ($s_{t-k:t+k}$) within a local window, ensuring deterministic physical grounding. For instance, a "Grasp" primitive might terminate when the gripper width remains constant, and the end-effector height begins to increase. This rule-based approach provides precise, action-aligned segmentation.

- **Learning with MCR:** Before fine-tuning, all disassembled segments undergo transformation via the Multimodal Canonical Representation (MCR). This process converts task-specific instructions and raw observations into canonical primitive instructions ($c_i$) and object-centric masked observations ($\tilde{o}_i$). This decouples invariant physical motions from task-specific contexts, aligning similar motion patterns across tasks into a unified representation for architecture-agnostic multi-task fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. Inference Phase: Primitive Assembly

This phase orchestrates the execution of learned primitives to solve complex tasks.

- **Primitive Planner (Section 4.2.1):** For a new test task ($l_{test}$), a VLM is used to generate the planned primitive sequence ($S_{plan}$). This planning process is retrieval-augmented: the top-3 semantically similar (instruction, sequence) pairs are retrieved from the Disassembly Library $D$ and provided as in-context priors to the VLM, along with $l_{test}$, initial observation ($o_0$), and primitive definitions ($\mathcal{C}$). This constrains the VLM to generate reasonable and executable sequences.

- **Primitive Switch (Section 4.2.2):** An LLM generates Python code ($f_{switch}$) to manage the closed-loop execution flow, mirroring the segmentation criteria ($\phi_i$) but adapted for online monitoring. This code evaluates statistical trends ($s_{stat}$) from a sliding history window ($H_t$) of proprioceptive and action data. A transition from primitive $p_i$ to $p_{i+1}$ is triggered when $f_{switch}$ evaluates to true. The system operates with a dual-threaded architecture, allowing the VLA model to output continuous actions while the monitoring thread concurrently evaluates switch triggers, ensuring reactive and robust task progression. Live observations and the current planned primitive are continuously processed by MCR to maintain consistency.

#### 3.4. Multimodal Canonical Representation (MCR)

MCR serves as a bridge, standardizing multimodal inputs to mitigate contextual interference and ensure primitive reusability.

- **Semantic Unification:** Task-specific instructions are mapped to canonical primitive instructions ($c_i$). For example, "grasp the black bowl" becomes "grasp the object with green mask." This constrains the language space, enabling the model to associate policies with shared primitive identities.

- **Visual Compatibility:** Raw visual observations ($o_i$) are transformed into masked observations ($\tilde{o}_i = o_i \otimes M_i$). Object-centric masks ($M_i$) are generated by segmenting target objects using SAM (Segment Anything Model) and continuously tracked across the trajectory using Cutie. These masks serve as the primary carrier of task-specific context, providing consistent visual grounding for the VLA despite simplified text inputs.

By combining these components, PrimitiveVLA aims to enable VLA models to learn universal motion patterns, facilitating both data-efficient fine-tuning and generalizable, robust inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated PrimitiveVLA's effectiveness in simulation and real-world settings across three core research questions, using OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and $\pi_{0.5}$ as VLA backbones.

#### 4.1. RQ1: Performance & Efficiency

- **Superiority under Full-Data Regime:** PrimitiveVLA generally enhanced or maintained competitive performance against baselines when trained on full datasets. On the Libero-90 benchmark, PrimitiveVLA improved OpenVLA performance by 9.2% and OpenVLA-OFT by 5.0%. Across smaller-scale Libero benchmarks (Object, Spatial, Goal), it yielded substantial improvements for OpenVLA (+5.6%) and OpenVLA-OFT. On the complex RLBench suite, PrimitiveVLA improved the average success rate by 7.0%. For the state-of-the-art $\pi_{0.5}$, PrimitiveVLA maintained comparable performance.

- **Matching Full-Data Performance with 50% Data:** A key finding was PrimitiveVLA's ability to achieve high performance with significantly reduced training data. OpenVLA-based PrimitiveVLA, trained with 50% of the data, achieved an 80.30% success rate, outperforming the 100%-data OpenVLA baseline (78.70%). For OpenVLA-OFT, the 50%-data variant even surpassed its 100%-data baseline on Libero-90 by 2.4%. These results indicate that the paradigm enables VLA models to learn shared motion patterns efficiently, allowing high performance even in data-constrained scenarios.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Cross-Task Generalization

- **Generalization to Unseen Tasks (Libero-90-Novel):** Standard VLAs showed limited zero-shot generalization on novel tasks. OpenVLA achieved 7.38%, OpenVLA-OFT 13.50%, and $\pi_{0.5}$ 56.00%. PrimitiveVLA substantially improved these scores, reaching 45.50% (a 6-fold improvement for OpenVLA), 71.00% for OpenVLA-OFT, and 75.50% for $\pi_{0.5}$. This suggests that disassembling tasks into reusable primitives facilitates flexible assembly for novel tasks, reducing task-level overfitting.

- **Generalization to Long-Horizon Tasks (Libero-Long):** On long-horizon tasks, where standard baselines typically failed (success rates below 5%), PrimitiveVLA delivered consistent improvements. It boosted $\pi_{0.5}$'s success rate from 30.50% to 80.25%. This result supports the claim that the assembly-based method provides robust sequential generalization for complex tasks.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Mechanism Verification (Ablation Studies)

Ablation studies decoupled the contributions of Primitive Disassembly and Multimodal Canonical Representation (MCR).

- **MCR as Primary Driver for OOD Transfer:** MCR was identified as the main component for improving out-of-domain (OOD) transfer. For OpenVLA-OFT, MCR alone increased Libero-90-Novel success from 13.50% (baseline) to 60.00%.

- **Primitive Disassembly for Long-Horizon Stability:** Primitive Disassembly proved critical for stability in long-horizon tasks. For OpenVLA-OFT, using only Primitive Disassembly boosted Libero-Long success from 3.75% (baseline) to 52.30%.

These findings collectively suggest that MCR enhances environmental robustness for novel tasks, while Primitive Disassembly ensures the execution stability necessary for complex, multi-stage operations.

#### 4.4. Real-World Evaluation

PrimitiveVLA was deployed on a UR5e robot with a $\pi_{0.5}$ backbone, evaluating 11 tasks across In-Distribution (ID), Task Generalization, and Compositional Generalization settings.

- **In-Distribution (ID) Performance:** The method achieved a 90% average success rate on ID tasks (T1–T6), outperforming the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline (70.0%), demonstrating robustness against physical variance.

- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** PrimitiveVLA showed significant zero-shot generalization: boosting success on unseen objects (T9) from 10% to 80% and demonstrating 60% and 70% success on compositional tasks (T10 and T11, respectively), where the baseline achieved 0%. These results indicate that reusable primitives enable zero-shot assembly in novel, long-horizon real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PrimitiveVLA framework addresses critical challenges in the current landscape of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically their limitations in data efficiency and generalization. By introducing a "Primitive-Centric Disassemble & Assemble" paradigm, the research proposes a fundamental shift from learning monolithic, task-specific trajectories to mastering a set of reusable physical primitives.

The significance of this work lies in several key areas:

1. **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** The demonstrated ability of PrimitiveVLA to achieve comparable or superior performance with significantly less data (e.g., 50% of training data outperforming 100% data baselines) suggests a pathway towards more economical training of generalist robotic policies. This could alleviate the demand for vast, expensive datasets for fine-tuning VLA models, making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible.

2. **Improved Generalization:** The substantial gains in zero-shot generalization to unseen and long-horizon tasks indicate that learning reusable primitives facilitates compositional generalization. This allows robots to perform novel tasks by assembling familiar motion patterns, rather than requiring re-training for every new task. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, open-world environments where encountering novel situations is common.

3. **Robust and Model-Agnostic Framework:** PrimitiveVLA provides a generalizable framework that can be integrated with various VLA backbones (demonstrated with OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and $\pi_{0.5}$). This versatility means the paradigm can potentially benefit a wide range of existing and future VLA models, enhancing their capabilities without requiring specific architectural changes.

4. **Bridging High-level Reasoning and Low-level Control:** The methodology effectively leverages the symbolic reasoning power of LLMs/VLMs for task decomposition and planning, combined with precise, rule-based control for primitive execution and switching. The Multimodal Canonical Representation (MCR) further unifies these components, mitigating contextual interference and ensuring the invariance and reusability of primitives.

5. **Real-World Applicability:** Validation in real-world robotic experiments confirms the practical transferability of the proposed paradigm. The improvements in both in-distribution robustness and zero-shot compositional generalization in physical settings underscore the potential for PrimitiveVLA to make robots more capable and autonomous in practical applications.

The potential impact extends to accelerating the development of scalable embodied AI. By enabling VLA models to learn more abstract, reusable skills, PrimitiveVLA could lead to robots that are more adaptable, require fewer demonstrations for new tasks, and can tackle complex, multi-stage operations with greater reliability. While the current primitive set relies on a pre-defined taxonomy, the research identifies future directions such as unsupervised primitive discovery to dynamically expand the action space and end-to-end differentiable planning to further optimize the synergy between high-level reasoning and low-level control. This work thus represents a step towards robots that can learn and generalize more efficiently from human demonstrations.
