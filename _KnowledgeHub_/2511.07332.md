# Grounding Computer Use Agents on Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mila, ServiceNow Research, and several universities developed GROUNDCUA, a large-scale, human-annotated dataset for desktop UI grounding, along with GROUNDNEXT vision-language models. These models achieved state-of-the-art accuracy across five benchmarks with significantly less training data than prior methods, demonstrating the efficacy of high-quality data for robust computer-use agents.

## Problem

- Computer-use agents (CUAs) face a critical bottleneck in accurately grounding natural language instructions to visual elements within complex desktop user interfaces.
- Existing datasets for desktop UI grounding often lack the high quality, dense annotations, and real-world complexity necessary for robust agent training, relying on automated or synthetic generation.
- Desktop environments present unique challenges due to diverse layouts, high-resolution displays, numerous visually similar elements, and user-specific content.

## Method

- Developed GROUNDCUA, a large-scale dataset featuring 56K ==screenshots== and over 3.56 million human-verified annotations from expert demonstrations across 87 open-source desktop applications.
- Generated 700K image-instruction pairs for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and 10K for reinforcement learning (RL) using a multimodal LLM to create diverse instruction types (direct, functional, spatial).
- Trained GROUNDNEXT vision-language models, based on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct (3B and 7B), using a two-stage approach combining SFT with RL post-training, employing a custom discrete reward function.

## Results

- GROUNDNEXT models established state-of-the-art accuracy across five challenging benchmarks (e.g., ScreenSpotPro, OSWorld-G), outperforming previous best models by over 10 average percentage points despite being trained on significantly less data (**700K** vs. **9M** samples).
- The **3B** parameter GROUNDNEXT model achieved an impressive **50.6%** on the OSWorld-Verified agentic benchmark, outperforming many larger open-source models and proprietary APIs.
- The models demonstrated substantial improvements in desktop UI grounding, especially for icon recognition, and showed strong cross-platform generalization to mobile and web interfaces, despite being trained exclusively on desktop data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Superior data quality, derived from human experts, proves more effective for UI grounding than relying solely on larger volumes of less precise data.
> - Reinforcement learning consistently refines model performance but yields smaller gains when initial supervised fine-tuning is conducted with high-quality data, suggesting SFT captures most of the grounding capabilities.
> - Models trained exclusively on diverse desktop application data can exhibit strong generalization capabilities to cross-platform user interfaces, including mobile and web environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of computer-use agents (CUAs) that can operate software on behalf of users has gained traction with advancements in multimodal large language models (LLMs). A critical component for the success of these agents is "grounding," which refers to the accurate connection of natural language instructions to specific on-screen elements (e.g., buttons, text fields) for interaction. Without precise grounding, an agent's planned actions cannot be executed effectively, leading to task failures.

Desktop environments present particular challenges for grounding compared to web or mobile interfaces. Desktop applications often feature higher resolutions, denser layouts with many visually similar elements, and custom user-specific content. Existing research has addressed grounding through various approaches:
- **Large-scale datasets for web and mobile interactions:** These are abundant, but their applicability to the complexities of desktop UIs is limited.
- **Automated dataset collection for desktop:** Approaches like OS-ATLAS and AriaUI leverage accessibility-tree traversal. However, accessibility signals can be incomplete or inconsistent, resulting in missing or imprecise element labels.
- **Synthetic desktop interface generation:** Datasets like JEDI achieve scale through synthetic means, but these often simplify real desktop complexities and may not fully represent genuine user interfaces.
- **Supervised fine-tuned (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) models:** Various models such as CogAgent, ShowUI, Ferret-UI, GUI-Actor, and InfiGUI-G1 have been developed to improve interaction capabilities and map language to UI elements. These models often require substantial training data, sometimes encountering challenges with data efficiency in complex desktop environments.

This work addresses the identified gap by introducing a large-scale, human-annotated desktop grounding dataset, GROUNDCUA, which aims to provide high-quality, densely labeled data capturing the diversity and complexity of real-world desktop applications. By doing so, it contributes to the foundation for training more robust and data-efficient grounding models, as demonstrated by the accompanying GROUNDNEXT model family, which achieves competitive performance with substantially less training data than prior efforts. The paper positions itself as a step towards reliable general-purpose computer-use agents by focusing on the quality and density of desktop-specific grounding data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the reliability and capability of computer-use agents (CUAs) by improving their ability to accurately "ground" natural language instructions to specific on-screen elements within complex desktop environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and limitations in the existing landscape of CUA development and UI grounding:

1. **Criticality of Accurate Grounding:** For CUAs to successfully automate tasks and make digital tools more accessible, they must not only plan the correct sequence of steps but also precisely identify and interact with the intended UI elements. Inaccurate grounding can lead to errors that compound and cause tasks to fail.
2. **Complexity and Diversity of Desktop Environments:** Desktop applications are characterized by high-resolution displays, dense layouts, and numerous visually similar elements, making precise localization difficult. Furthermore, they contain user-specific content (e.g., documents, spreadsheets) that introduces variability not typically seen during model training.
3. **Limitations of Existing Desktop Grounding Datasets:**
 - **Data Scarcity and Quality:** While large datasets exist for web and mobile interfaces, high-quality resources specifically for desktop environments are limited.
 - **Incompleteness of Automated Data:** Methods that rely on automated accessibility-tree traversal often yield incomplete or inconsistent element labels, leading to less precise annotations.
 - **Simplified Synthetic Data:** Datasets generated synthetically may not fully capture the visual complexity and real-world intricacies of actual desktop UIs.
 - **Lack of Fine-Grained Detail:** Existing datasets frequently underrepresent small, icon-only controls or dense UI elements that are common in professional desktop software, focusing instead on larger, more prominent components.
4. **Inefficiency of Current Training Approaches:** Many existing grounding models require vast amounts of training data (millions of samples), which can be computationally intensive and may not always translate to robust performance if the data quality is inconsistent.
5. **Need for Robust, Application-Specific Strategies:** Desktop environments often involve families of similar applications (e.g., different office suites). A comprehensive dataset needs to enable agents to learn application-specific grounding strategies to handle these nuances.
6. **Desire to Support Open Research:** The authors aim to contribute to the research community by releasing a high-quality, openly accessible dataset and trained models, fostering further innovation in the field.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:
- **Introduce GROUNDCUA:** Create a large-scale, human-annotated desktop grounding dataset derived from expert human demonstrations, ensuring high-quality, dense, and fine-grained annotations across a diverse range of desktop applications.
- **Develop GROUNDNEXT Models:** Construct a family of vision-language models capable of accurately mapping natural language instructions to target UI elements, leveraging the unique properties of GROUNDCUA.
- **Demonstrate Data Efficiency:** Show that training with high-quality, expert-driven data from GROUNDCUA can achieve state-of-the-art results with significantly fewer training samples compared to prior works.
- **Evaluate SFT and RL Roles:** Systematically analyze the contributions of supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning in enhancing grounding performance.
- **Assess Generalization:** Evaluate the models' performance not only on desktop-specific tasks but also on cross-platform (mobile and web) benchmarks to understand their generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves two primary components: the creation of the GROUNDCUA dataset and the development and training of the GROUNDNEXT models.

#### 3.1. GROUNDCUA Dataset Creation

The GROUNDCUA dataset is a large-scale, human-annotated desktop grounding dataset built from expert human demonstrations.

- **Data Collection Pipeline:**
 - **Application Selection:** 87 open-source applications across 12 diverse categories (e.g., Productivity, Graphics and Design, Development, Scientific) were chosen. This focus on open-source software with permissive licenses facilitates public release and ensures broad applicability by mirroring functionalities of popular closed-source software.
 - **Task Design and Execution:** Annotators, described as trained experts, designed and executed everyday computer-use tasks (e.g., drafting a document, editing a spreadsheet) within these applications. Over 10,000 task demonstrations were recorded. This approach aimed to capture natural interaction trajectories and realistic UI states.
 - **Dense Annotation of Screenshots:** Keyframes were extracted from the recorded demonstrations, specifically capturing the UI state immediately before a user action. Annotators labeled *every visible element* in these keyframes with:
 - **Bounding Boxes:** Precise pixel-level localization of UI elements.
 - **Textual Labels:** Element names, displayed text for shorter strings, or concise summaries for longer text segments (e.g., source code). OCR text (using PaddleOCR) was also extracted for long segments.
 - **High-Level Categories:** Approximately 50% of elements were assigned to one of eight categories (e.g., Input Element, Button, Visual Elements, Menu, Information Display).
 - **Scale and Characteristics:** The dataset comprises 56,000 screenshots and over 3.56 million human-verified annotations. Screenshots have a mean resolution of 2.03 megapixels, ranging from 0.39 to 7 megapixels. Each screenshot contains an average of 64 annotations, with bounding boxes covering a small average area (0.13% of the image), indicating fine-grained element representation.

- **Instruction-Tuning Data Construction:** From the dense annotations, a pipeline was designed to generate diverse instruction-tuning data. This involved deduplicating elements (using text matching and perceptual image similarity) to create approximately 900,000 unique elements. A multimodal LLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) was then prompted with ground-truth bounding boxes, application names, element labels, and surrounding context to generate three primary types of instructions:
 - **Direct Instructions:** Explicitly refer to an element's attributes (e.g., color, shape), position, and surrounding context. This category includes instructions generated from textual elements (matching OCR with labels), visual elements (icon captions), and general templates.
 - **Functional Instructions:** Describe an element by its intended action or purpose (e.g., "Open a new tab"). These primarily target buttons and menus.
 - **Spatial Instructions:** Locate an element by its position relative to another anchor element (e.g., "Click the element to the left of ‘Files’").
 - **Dataset Split:** A subset of 700,000 image-instruction pairs was curated for supervised fine-tuning (SFT), with a mix of 50% direct, 35% functional, and 15% spatial instructions. An additional 10,000 unseen samples were reserved for reinforcement learning (RL) post-training.

#### 3.2. GROUNDNEXT Models Training

The GROUNDNEXT series of vision-language models (3B and 7B scales) were developed using a two-stage training approach.

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct was used as the base model, with both its vision encoder and language model components being finetuned.

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - Models were trained on the 700,000 curated instruction-tuning datapoints from GROUNDCUA.
 - Training utilized LlamaFactory with a learning rate of 3e-6, cosine decay, and a warmup ratio of 0.05.
 - Models were trained for two epochs on a single node equipped with 8 H100 GPUs, using a global batch size of 128.

- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) Post-training:**
 - RL was applied to further refine the SFT-trained models. 10,000 new, previously unseen elements from GROUNDCUA were selected for this stage.
 - **Policy Optimization:** The Relative Leave-One-Out (RLOO) method was employed. This method compares the reward of each rollout to the average reward of other samples within the same group, obviating the need for a separate critic model. The group size was set to n=8.
 - **Reward Function:** A customized discrete reward function was designed based on the normalized signed distance between the predicted 2D point and the ground-truth bounding box. This function assigned rewards ranging from -1.0 (for predictions far outside the box) to +1.0 (for predictions deep inside the box), with intermediate values for "near misses" or points close to the boundary. This granular reward scheme was motivated by the observation of many "near-miss" errors in SFT models.
 - RL training was conducted for one epoch on a single H100 node (8 GPUs), with a batch size of 64.

#### 3.3. Evaluation

Model performance was assessed based on accuracy (predicted point falls within the ground-truth bounding box) across various benchmarks:

- **Desktop Benchmarks:** ScreenSpotPro, OSWorld-G, and UI-Vision (considered in-domain due to platform overlap).
- **Cross-Platform Benchmarks:** MMBench-GUI (L2) and ScreenSpot-v2, which include mobile and web interfaces in addition to desktop.
- **Agentic Performance:** Evaluated in a multi-step task setting on the OSWorld-Verified benchmark, using OpenAI o3 as the planner to generate grounding commands that GROUNDNEXT executes.

Baselines included a range of SFT-only and RL-tuned models from prior work, as well as proprietary APIs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the GROUNDCUA dataset and the performance of the GROUNDNEXT models.

#### 4.1. GROUNDCUA Dataset Characteristics

- **Scale and Density:** GROUNDCUA is presented as the largest expert-annotated dataset for desktop grounding, comprising 56,000 screenshots with over 3.56 million human-verified UI element annotations. Each screenshot averages 64 annotations, which is more than three times denser than some comparable desktop datasets.
- **Resolution and Element Size:** The dataset features high-resolution images (ranging from 0.39 to 7 megapixels, with a mean of 2.03 MP), capable of capturing fine-grained desktop elements. The average bounding box area is small (0.13% of the image), indicating a focus on detailed elements like icons and small controls, which are often challenging for automated collection methods.
- **Application Diversity:** It covers 87 open-source applications across 12 categories, providing a broad range of real-world usage scenarios.

#### 4.2. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Results:** The GROUNDNEXT family of models, trained primarily with SFT on GROUNDCUA, achieved state-of-the-art average performance across five challenging benchmarks (ScreenSpotPro, OSWorld-G, MMBench-GUI, ScreenSpot-v2, UI-Vision) for both 3B and 7B model scales.
 - GROUNDNEXT-3B (SFT) recorded an average accuracy of 66.4%, surpassing the next best 3B SFT baseline (GUI-Actor-3B) by over 12 percentage points.
 - GROUNDNEXT-7B (SFT) achieved an average accuracy of 69.2%, outperforming the next best 7B SFT baseline (JEDI-7B) by over 13 percentage points.
- **Data Efficiency:** A notable finding is that GROUNDNEXT achieved these results using significantly less SFT training data (700K instructions) compared to prior state-of-the-art models (e.g., JEDI, which utilized 9M datapoints). This suggests that the high quality and dense annotations of GROUNDCUA contribute more effectively to performance than sheer data volume.
- **Ablation Study on SFT Sources:** When the same base model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) was trained on 100K samples from various datasets (Aguvis, UGround, OS-Atlas, JEDI, and GROUNDCUA), GROUNDCUA yielded significantly higher SFT average performance, demonstrating its quality.
- **Scaling Behavior:** Performance showed consistent gains as the SFT dataset size increased from 100K to 700K samples, particularly on in-domain benchmarks like UI-Vision, indicating that the dataset provides useful signal across this range.

#### 4.3. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Post-training Results

- **Consistent but Modest Improvements:** RL post-training on top of the SFT models provided consistent but modest gains.
 - GROUNDNEXT-3B (RL) improved its average accuracy from 66.4% to 68.4%.
 - GROUNDNEXT-7B (RL) improved its average accuracy from 69.2% to 70.5%.
- **Interaction with SFT Strength:** The paper noted that models pre-trained more effectively with GROUNDCUA during SFT exhibited smaller marginal gains from RL. This suggests that high-quality SFT already captures a significant portion of the model's potential, leaving less room for drastic improvements from RL in the current setup.
- **RL Data Effectiveness:** Despite modest overall gains on the strong SFT baselines, an ablation study showed that RL training with 10K samples from GROUNDCUA provided substantial gains (an average improvement of 9.8%) over a Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline, indicating the effectiveness of GROUNDCUA data for RL fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Agentic Performance

- **Competitive Utility:** When evaluated in an agentic setting on the OSWorld-Verified benchmark, using OpenAI o3 as a planner, GROUNDNEXT-3B (RL) achieved an overall score of 50.6%.
- **Outperformance of Larger Models:** This performance enabled the 3B model to outperform several larger open-source models (e.g., OpenCUA-72B at 46.1%) and some proprietary APIs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-Flash at 41.6% and Claude-4-Sonnet at 41.4%).
- **Comparable to Larger Peers:** Notably, GROUNDNEXT-3B's performance was comparable to the much larger JEDI-7B (51.0% overall), demonstrating its practical utility for resource-constrained systems.

#### 4.5. Generalization and Error Analysis

- **Desktop Domain Strength:** GROUNDNEXT models showed the largest performance gains on desktop-specific benchmarks (UI-Vision, OSWorld-G, ScreenSpotPro), consistent with GROUNDCUA's focus.
- **Cross-Platform Generalization:** Despite being trained exclusively on desktop data, GROUNDNEXT-7B (RL) performed competitively on mobile (89.2%) and web (81.9%) components of MMBench-GUI, indicating a degree of cross-domain generalization.
- **Icon Recognition:** Significant improvements were observed in icon recognition across various categories (e.g., 10.7% average improvement on SSPro for icons), which is attributed to the dense and diverse icon coverage in GROUNDCUA.
- **Error Categories:** Common error types included limited domain knowledge for web/mobile UI patterns, localization precision (where the model identifies the correct region but misses pixel-perfect accuracy), application-specific semantics (struggling with niche tools in unseen software), and limitations in complex spatial reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer-use agents and multimodal AI through several significant aspects:

1. **High-Quality Desktop Grounding Dataset:** The introduction of GROUNDCUA addresses a critical gap in high-quality, human-annotated datasets for desktop environments. Its scale, density, fine-grained annotations, and broad application diversity provide a robust resource for developing more capable grounding models. This dataset can serve as a new standard for benchmarking and training desktop UI agents.

2. **Validation of Data Quality over Quantity:** The performance of GROUNDNEXT models, achieving state-of-the-art results with significantly less training data than prior approaches, highlights the principle that the quality and curation of data can be more impactful than sheer volume. This has implications for reducing computational resources and training time, making advanced model development more efficient and accessible.

3. **Advancement in General-Purpose CUAs:** By improving grounding capabilities in complex desktop settings, this work lays a stronger foundation for building general-purpose computer-use agents that can reliably operate across a wide array of software. The ability to accurately interpret and act upon diverse UI elements is fundamental for task automation and enhancing user accessibility.

4. **Efficient and Practical Models:** The GROUNDNEXT models, particularly the 3B variant, demonstrate competitive performance against much larger models and even some proprietary APIs. This efficiency is important for real-world deployments where inference speed and resource constraints are key considerations, potentially enabling wider adoption of agentic systems.

5. **Insights into SFT and RL:** The detailed analysis of SFT and RL roles, including the observation that strong SFT baselines may lead to modest marginal gains from RL in certain configurations, provides valuable insights for future multi-stage training strategies in multimodal models. The dataset's effectiveness for RL fine-tuning is also demonstrated.

6. **Promotion of Open Science:** The commitment to releasing both the GROUNDCUA dataset and the trained GROUNDNEXT models fosters open research and development in the community. This allows other researchers to build upon these contributions, accelerate innovation, and explore new directions.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The limitations identified, such as the need for improved cross-domain generalization and more sophisticated RL reward signals, point to clear avenues for future research. The rich metadata within GROUNDCUA also facilitates studies in continual learning and adaptation, which are crucial for agents operating in evolving software environments.

In summary, this paper advances the state of desktop UI grounding by providing a comprehensive, expert-driven dataset and demonstrating that models trained on such data can achieve high performance and efficiency, offering a practical path toward more reliable and adaptable computer-use agents.
