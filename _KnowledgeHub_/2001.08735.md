# Cross-Domain Few-Shot Classification via Learned Feature-Wise Transformation

> [!summary] Summary
> A method designed for cross-domain few-shot classification enhances the generalization of metric-based models by incorporating a Feature-Wise Transformation (FWT) layer. Optimized using a learning-to-learn algorithm, this approach modulates feature distributions during training, resulting in improved accuracy on novel categories from unseen domains.

## Problem

- Metric-based few-shot classification models exhibit poor generalization to novel categories from unseen domains due to significant feature distribution shifts.
- Conventional domain adaptation requires target domain data during training, which is not feasible for many few-shot scenarios with scarce data.
- Existing domain generalization methods focus on known categories in new domains, failing to address the generalization to novel categories in unseen domains.

## Method

- A **Feature-Wise Transformation (FWT)** layer is integrated into the feature encoder, applying ==channel-wise affine transformations== with sampled scaling and bias terms to modulate feature distributions.
- A learning-to-learn (**LTL-FWT**) ==meta-learning algorithm== is devised to automatically optimize the FWT layer's hyper-parameters, such as the standard deviations for sampling scaling and bias terms.
- LTL-FWT samples distinct pseudo-seen and pseudo-unseen domains in each iteration to train the model with FWT-augmented features and update FWT parameters based on generalization performance on the pseudo-unseen domain.

## Results

- The FWT layer, even with manually tuned parameters, consistently improved few-shot classification accuracy for MatchingNet, RelationNet, and GNN models on CUB, Cars, Places, and Plantae unseen domains.
- The LTL-FWT optimized models demonstrated superior generalization, outperforming both baselines and models with fixed FWT parameters across all unseen domains in both **1-shot** and **5-shot** settings.
- T-SNE visualizations confirmed that FWT, particularly with LTL-FWT, reduced inter-domain feature discrepancies, contributing to enhanced cross-domain classification performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Actively modulating feature distributions using learnable transformations during training improves the robustness of metric-based few-shot classifiers to domain shifts.
> - Automated meta-learning for transformation parameters is more effective than manual tuning, yielding superior generalization due to the complexity of the hyper-parameter space.
> - The proposed feature-wise transformation can be broadly applied across various metric-based few-shot classification models, consistently enhancing their cross-domain performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Few-shot classification (FSC) is a subfield of machine learning that focuses on enabling models to recognize novel categories when provided with only a limited number of labeled examples per class. Within FSC, metric-based meta-learning methods have gained attention for their simplicity and effectiveness. These methods operate by learning a feature encoder that embeds images into a feature space and a metric function that computes similarity between query images and support (labeled) examples to predict categories.

Despite their success in scenarios where training and testing data originate from the same domain (e.g., all images from mini-ImageNet), a recognized limitation of existing metric-based FSC approaches is their inadequate generalization to unseen domains. This domain shift problem arises when the feature distributions of images from novel categories in a new domain differ significantly from those encountered during training. This issue is particularly relevant in practical applications where collecting extensive datasets for rare categories across diverse domains can be resource-intensive or infeasible.

Prior research has addressed domain shift through various techniques. Unsupervised domain adaptation methods aim to reduce distribution discrepancies between a source and a target domain by utilizing abundant unlabeled images from the target domain during training. However, this assumption of readily available target domain data is often not met in few-shot scenarios, especially for rare classes. Domain generalization methods, in contrast, seek to learn models that generalize to unseen domains without direct access to their data during training. Nevertheless, existing domain generalization approaches primarily focus on recognizing instances from the same categories present during training, rather than novel categories as required in few-shot classification.

This work positions itself at the intersection of few-shot classification and domain generalization, specifically addressing the challenge of cross-domain few-shot classification for metric-based methods. It seeks to bridge the gap in generalization ability to novel categories in unseen domains without requiring target domain data during the training phase. The paper acknowledges contemporary efforts addressing similar challenges, such as Triantafillou et al. (2020).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of metric-based few-shot classification models when applied to novel categories sourced from unseen domains.

The motivation for this objective stems from several observations and challenges:
1. **Domain Shift Impact on Metric-Based Methods:** Metric-based few-shot classification models learn feature embeddings and a metric function based on data from seen domains. When confronted with tasks from unseen domains, the feature distributions can diverge significantly. This discrepancy can cause the learned metric function to overfit to the feature characteristics of the training domains, leading to a substantial degradation in performance on new, out-of-domain categories. Figure 1 in the paper visually illustrates this problem, showing how feature distributions from unseen domains (CUB, Cars) differ from the training domain (mini-ImageNet).
2. **Practical Imperative for Generalization:** The ability to generalize to unseen domains is crucial for the real-world applicability of few-shot learning. It is often impractical or prohibitively expensive to collect large, labeled datasets for every potential novel category across all possible domains (e.g., distinguishing rare bird species in varied geographical locations). A model capable of adapting to novel domains with only a few examples is therefore highly desirable.
3. **Limitations of Existing Domain Adaptation/Generalization:** As discussed in the broader landscape section, existing domain adaptation techniques typically assume access to unlabeled data from the target domain during training, which is not aligned with the few-shot classification problem in an unseen domain. Domain generalization methods, while not requiring target domain data during training, generally focus on classifying *known* categories in new domains, rather than *novel* categories in new domains, as is the case in few-shot classification.
4. **Need for Feature Distribution Robustness:** The core hypothesis motivating this work is that by making the feature encoder capable of producing a more diverse range of feature distributions during training, the subsequent metric function will become more robust and less susceptible to the specific characteristics of any single training domain. This increased diversity is intended to simulate the variations encountered in different unseen domains, thereby improving generalization.
5. **Automating Hyper-parameter Tuning:** Modulating feature distributions effectively typically involves hyper-parameters. Manual tuning of these parameters can be complex, time-consuming, and may not yield universally optimal settings across different metric-based frameworks or domain combinations. This motivated the development of an automated learning mechanism to optimize these transformation parameters.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a novel mechanism within the feature encoder that actively simulates domain shift during training, coupled with a meta-learning approach to optimize this mechanism, thereby enabling metric-based few-shot classifiers to generalize more effectively to novel categories in arbitrary unseen domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology addresses cross-domain few-shot classification by introducing a feature-wise transformation layer and an accompanying learning-to-learn algorithm for its optimization.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of Few-Shot Classification and Problem Setting

The paper frames few-shot classification as an N-way N-shot problem. During training, a task T is constructed by sampling $N_w$ categories, each with $N_s$ labeled support examples ($X_s, Y_s$) and $N_q$ unlabeled query examples ($X_q, Y_q$). Metric-based methods typically comprise a feature encoder $E$ and a metric function $M$. The encoder extracts features from both support and query images. The metric function then uses these features to predict query image categories: $\hat{Y_q} = M(Y_s, E(X_s), E(X_q))$. The training objective minimizes a classification loss $L_{cls}$ on the query set.

The problem setting is domain generalization for few-shot classification. The model is trained using $N$ seen domains ($\mathcal{T}_1^{seen}, \dots, \mathcal{T}_N^{seen}$), with the goal of generalizing to an entirely unseen domain $\mathcal{T}^{unseen}$ without access to its data during training.

#### 3.2. Feature-Wise Transformation Layer (FWT)

To counter the issue of domain shift, the authors propose integrating a Feature-Wise Transformation (FWT) layer into the feature encoder $E$. The FWT layer is designed to modulate intermediate feature activations using affine transformations, thereby simulating diverse feature distributions during the training phase. This simulation aims to make the metric function $M$ more robust to variations encountered in unseen domains.

The FWT layer is typically inserted after batch normalization layers within the feature encoder. For an intermediate feature activation map $z$ of dimension $C \times H \times W$, the FWT layer applies channel-wise scaling $\gamma$ and bias $\beta$ terms. These terms are not fixed values but are sampled from Gaussian distributions:
$\gamma \sim \mathcal{N}(1, \text{softplus}(\theta_\gamma))$
$\beta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \text{softplus}(\theta_\beta))$
where $\theta_\gamma \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times 1 \times 1}$ and $\theta_\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times 1 \times 1}$ are hyper-parameters representing the standard deviations of these Gaussian distributions. The `softplus` function ensures that the standard deviations are positive.

The modulated activation $\hat{z}$ is then computed as:
$\hat{z}_{c,h,w} = \gamma_c \times z_{c,h,w} + \beta_c$
where $c$ indexes the feature channel. FWT layers can be inserted at multiple levels within the feature encoder to enhance the diversity of generated features.

#### 3.3. Learning the Feature-Wise Transformation Layers (LTL-FWT)

Empirically determining optimal hyper-parameters $\theta_f = \{\theta_\gamma, \theta_\beta\}$ for the FWT layers across different settings (various metric-based frameworks, diverse seen domains) is challenging due to the high-dimensional search space (e.g., 1920 dimensions in their experimental setup). To address this, a learning-to-learn (meta-learning) algorithm is developed to optimize $\theta_f$.

The LTL-FWT algorithm operates as follows in each training iteration:
1. **Domain Sampling:** Two non-overlapping domains are randomly sampled from the set of available seen domains: a pseudo-seen domain $\mathcal{T}^{ps}$ and a pseudo-unseen domain $\mathcal{T}^{pu}$.
2. **Inner Loop (Model Update):** A pseudo-seen task $T^{ps}$ is sampled from $\mathcal{T}^{ps}$. The metric-based model, including its feature encoder $E_{\theta_e}$ and metric function $M_{\theta_m}$, *with the FWT layers (parameterized by $\theta_f$) integrated*, is updated using the classification loss on $T^{ps}$. This step essentially trains the model on data that has undergone feature-wise transformations:
 $(\theta_e^{t+1}, \theta_m^{t+1}) = (\theta_e^t, \theta_m^t) - \alpha \nabla_{\theta_e^t, \theta_m^t} L_{cls}(Y_q^{ps}, M_{\theta_m^t}(Y_s^{ps}, E_{\theta_e^t, \theta_f^t}(X_s^{ps}), E_{\theta_e^t, \theta_f^t}(X_q^{ps})))$
 where $\alpha$ is the learning rate.
3. **Outer Loop (FWT Hyper-parameter Update):** After updating the model parameters, a pseudo-unseen task $T^{pu}$ is sampled from $\mathcal{T}^{pu}$. The generalization ability of the *updated model* (using $\theta_e^{t+1}, \theta_m^{t+1}$), *but with the FWT layers removed*, is evaluated by computing the classification loss on $T^{pu}$:
 $L^{pu} = L_{cls}(Y_q^{pu}, M_{\theta_m^{t+1}}(Y_s^{pu}, E_{\theta_e^{t+1}}(X_s^{pu}), E_{\theta_e^{t+1}}(X_q^{pu})))$
 This loss $L^{pu}$ reflects how well the model, trained with FWT-augmented features, generalizes to a new domain *without* the transformations at test time. The hyper-parameters $\theta_f$ of the FWT layers are then optimized to minimize this $L^{pu}$, thereby encouraging the FWT layers to generate feature distributions that improve generalization to unseen domains:
 $\theta_f^{t+1} = \theta_f^t - \alpha \nabla_{\theta_f^t} L^{pu}$

Both the metric-based model parameters and the FWT hyper-parameters are jointly optimized within this nested training procedure. A pre-trained ResNet-10 encoder on mini-ImageNet training categories is used as the backbone. L2 regularization is applied to $\theta_f$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed feature-wise transformation (FWT) layer and its learning-to-learn (LTL-FWT) optimization scheme across various metric-based few-shot classification models and datasets.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Five datasets were used: mini-ImageNet (as a primary seen domain), CUB, Cars, Places, and Plantae (as unseen domains).
- **Metric-Based Baselines:** MatchingNet, RelationNet, and Graph Neural Networks (GNN) were chosen to demonstrate the method's applicability.
- **Feature Encoder:** ResNet-10 architecture.
- **Pre-training:** The feature encoder was pre-trained by minimizing standard cross-entropy loss on the 64 training categories of mini-ImageNet.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** 5-way 1-shot and 5-way 5-shot classification accuracy, averaged over 1,000 trials.
- **Experimental Settings:**
 1. **Manual FWT Tuning:** Train models on mini-ImageNet, evaluate on the four other datasets, using empirically determined FWT hyper-parameters ($\theta_\gamma = 0.3, \theta_\beta = 0.5$).
 2. **LTL-FWT Optimization:** Utilize a leave-one-out setting where one dataset from CUB, Cars, Places, Plantae serves as the unseen domain, and mini-ImageNet plus the remaining three datasets constitute the seen domains for LTL-FWT training.

#### 4.2. Results with Manual Parameter Tuning (Table 1)

- **Consistent Improvements:** The FWT layer, even with manually chosen hyper-parameters, consistently improved the few-shot classification performance of all three metric-based models (MatchingNet, RelationNet, GNN) on the four unseen domains (CUB, Cars, Places, Plantae) in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings.
- **Performance on Seen Domain:** A marginal performance improvement was also observed on the seen mini-ImageNet domain, attributed to slight feature distribution discrepancies between its training and testing sets.
- **Baseline Strength:** The GNN model, when augmented with FWT, demonstrated competitive performance compared to other advanced methods, suggesting the efficacy of the transformation.

#### 4.3. Results with Learning-to-Learn (LTL-FWT) Optimization (Table 2)

- **Superior Generalization:** The models optimized with the proposed LTL-FWT scheme (denoted as LFT) consistently outperformed both the baseline models and models trained with fixed, empirically determined FWT parameters (denoted as FT). This held true across all unseen domains and both 1-shot and 5-shot scenarios.
- **Importance of Learning:** This result underscores the effectiveness of the learning-to-learn algorithm in dynamically optimizing the FWT hyper-parameters to better capture and simulate the diverse feature variations across different domains, thereby enhancing generalization.
- **Complexity of Hyper-parameter Tuning:** The paper notes that the LTL approach avoids the computationally expensive and meticulous manual tuning process required to find effective hyper-parameters for a multi-dimensional search space (e.g., 1920 dimensions).

#### 4.4. Visualizations (Figures 3 & 4)

- **T-SNE Visualization (Figure 3):** T-SNE plots of image features from different domains (CUB, Cars, Places, Plantae, mini-ImageNet) showed that the original feature encoder produced distinct clusters for each domain. With the FWT layers, the inter-domain feature distances decreased, indicating better alignment. The LTL-FWT further reduced this gap, suggesting a more effective simulation of feature distributions.
- **Learned FWT Parameter Visualization (Figure 4):** Analysis of the learned `softplus(θγ)` and `softplus(θβ)` values revealed patterns: scaling terms (`softplus(θγ)`) tended to be smaller in deeper layers, while bias terms (`softplus(θβ)`) showed less correlation with layer depth. Crucially, the distributions of these parameters varied across different metric-based classification methods, reinforcing the need for a learned, rather than manually tuned, optimization scheme.

#### 4.5. Additional Experimental Results (Appendix)

- **Ablation on Pre-trained Encoder (Table 4):** Pre-training the feature encoder on the mini-ImageNet training categories was shown to significantly improve few-shot classification performance across all metric-based models and domains, a common practice in modern FSC.
- **Generalization to Variable Ways (Table 5):** The LTL-FWT method maintained its performance benefits even when the number of ways ($N_w$) in the testing phase differed from training (e.g., training with 5-way, testing with 2-way, 10-way, or 20-way).
- **Sensitivity to FWT Parameters (Table 6):** An experiment using less optimal fixed FWT hyper-parameters (`θγ = 1, θβ = 1` for FT*) demonstrated degraded performance, sometimes even worse than the baseline without FWT. This further highlights the difficulty of manual tuning and the advantage of the LTL-FWT algorithm.
- **LTL with Single Domain (Table 7):** When the LTL-FWT was trained using only a single seen domain (mini-ImageNet, splitting it into pseudo-seen and pseudo-unseen tasks), the performance improvement was not significant compared to using multiple distinct seen domains. This suggests that the presence of genuine domain diversity during the meta-training process is important for effective optimization of the FWT layers.
- **Comparison to State-of-the-Art (Tables 8 & 9):**
 - On the mini-ImageNet dataset (seen domain), metric-based models augmented with FWT (even with pre-determined parameters) achieved competitive accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods like MetaOptNet-SVM-trainval.
 - In the cross-domain setting, the GNN framework with FWT (pre-determined parameters) performed favorably against MetaOptNet-SVM-trainval, which also exhibited performance degradation under domain shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work offers a significant contribution to the field of few-shot classification, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of generalization across diverse and unseen domains.

**Addressing a Core Limitation:** The primary significance lies in providing a robust mechanism to mitigate domain shift, a known weakness of metric-based few-shot classification methods. By explicitly simulating feature distribution variations during training, the approach directly tackles the problem of models overfitting to specific domain characteristics.

**Method-Agnostic Augmentation:** The proposed feature-wise transformation (FWT) layer is designed to be largely method-agnostic. Its successful integration and consistent performance improvements across three distinct metric-based models (MatchingNet, RelationNet, GNN) suggest its broad applicability to a wider range of existing and future metric-based few-shot learning architectures. This modularity can facilitate its adoption by other researchers.

**Automated Optimization via Meta-Learning:** The development of a learning-to-learn (LTL-FWT) algorithm to optimize the FWT layer's hyper-parameters is an important advancement. It moves beyond tedious and often suboptimal manual hyper-parameter tuning, making the method more practical and scalable. This meta-learning strategy ensures that the transformation layers are optimally configured to promote generalization to diverse unseen domains.

**Enhanced Practicality and Robustness:** The demonstrated ability to improve generalization to unseen domains without requiring data from these domains during training significantly enhances the practicality of few-shot learning systems. This is particularly valuable in real-world scenarios such as medical imaging, ecological monitoring (e.g., rare species identification), or industrial inspection, where data for novel categories in new environments is scarce. The improved robustness against domain shifts allows for more reliable deployment of few-shot classifiers.

**Foundation for Future Research:** The paper provides detailed methodology, extensive experimental validation, and publicly available code and datasets. This contributes a strong foundation for future research in cross-domain few-shot learning, encouraging further exploration of feature transformation techniques and meta-learning strategies for domain generalization. It also highlights the importance of considering domain shift as a primary evaluation criterion for few-shot learning algorithms.
