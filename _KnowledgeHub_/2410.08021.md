# OneRef: Unified One-tower Expression Grounding and Segmentation with Mask Referring Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> OneRef introduces a minimalist, one-tower Transformer for unified visual grounding and segmentation, leveraging a novel Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM) pre-training paradigm to achieve state-of-the-art performance with significantly reduced computational complexity. The framework demonstrates superior accuracy across referring expression comprehension and segmentation tasks, while being substantially more parameter- and inference-efficient than prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing visual grounding and referring segmentation methods rely on complex architectures with separate vision and language encoders, bulky fusion modules, and many parameters.
- Generic Mask Vision Language Modeling (MVLM) pre-training often fails to capture the intricate, fine-grained referential relationships critical for regression-like referring tasks.
- Current grounding and segmentation models frequently depend on special grounding tokens or multiple query anchors, adding architectural complexity and potentially disrupting pre-trained backbones.

## Method

- ==OneRef== employs a unified, modality-shared ==one-tower Transformer== to integrate visual and linguistic feature spaces from the outset, simplifying the overall architecture.
- Introduced ==Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM)==, comprising Referring-aware Mask Image Modeling (MIM) and Referring-aware Mask Language Modeling (MLM), to explicitly pre-train for referential understanding.
- Implemented a ==referring-aware dynamic image masking strategy== and reconstructed visual/semantic target-relation scores to embed precise spatial and semantic referential information directly into backbone features.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art performance across Visual Grounding (REC/PG) and Referring Expression Segmentation (RES) benchmarks, outperforming larger models like Grounding-DINO and LION.
- Demonstrated significant computational efficiency, utilizing **34.9%** fewer FLOPs than Grounding-DINO, **25.2%** fewer than MDETR, and up to **10x** faster inference speed.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of MRefM components, with combined elements yielding a **6.21%** performance gain on RefCOCOg-test, and the referring-aware dynamic masking outperforming fixed masking strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling fine-grained referential relationships during pre-training through MRefM allows a unified one-tower Transformer to achieve high performance with a simplified architecture.
> - A minimalist design with lightweight task heads can outperform complex multi-encoder/decoder models when the backbone's features are inherently rich in referring information.
> - The dynamic masking strategy, which assigns higher mask ratios to referred regions, is crucial for effectively embedding specific referential understanding into the visual features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing, specifically addressing the challenging tasks of **Visual Grounding (VG)**, **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)**, **Phrase Grounding (PG)**, and **Referring Expression Segmentation (RES/RIS)**. These tasks require models to understand a natural language query and accurately locate or segment the described object or region within an image.

Historically, VG and referring tasks have evolved significantly. Early approaches often relied on separate encoding streams for vision and language, followed by complex interaction and fusion modules. With the advent of powerful Transformer architectures like BERT for language and ViT for vision, the field shifted towards pre-training and fine-tuning paradigms. Current state-of-the-art (SoTA) methods typically fall into three architectural categories:
1. **Two modality encoders + cross-modal fusion encoder:** Employing separate vision and language backbones, then fusing their features through an additional Transformer encoder (e.g., TransVG).
2. **Adding a decoder:** Extending the above with an additional decoder for more refined predictions (e.g., MDETR).
3. **Direct regression with language-guided visual features:** Relying on language to guide visual feature processing for direct regression (e.g., LAVT, TransVG++).

A common limitation across these methods, despite their strong performance, is their reliance on *modality-dependent encoders* and *bulky fusion/interaction mechanisms*. This often results in a large number of parameters, intricate processes, and a struggle to seamlessly integrate modalities into a truly unified feature space.

More recently, there has been a growing interest in **unified, modality-shared multimodal frameworks**. Models like YORO, ONE-PEACE, and particularly BEiT-3 have explored architectures where a single Transformer encoder processes tokens from multiple modalities. BEiT-3, built upon a multi-way Transformer and Mixture-of-Expert (MoE) heads, effectively unifies vision and language in a shared feature space. However, existing unified models, including BEiT-3, primarily leverage generic Mask Vision Language Modeling (MVLM) for pre-training. This generic masking, while good for general representation learning, has been found insufficient for capturing the *nuanced referential relationships* critical for tasks like REC and RES, which are inherently regression-like and demand fine-grained cross-modal understanding.

OneRef directly tackles this critical gap. It builds upon the efficiency and shared representation strength of models like BEiT-3 but innovates on the pre-training paradigm. By introducing **Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM)**, OneRef seeks to imbue the unified one-tower Transformer with explicit referential understanding during pre-training. This allows OneRef to simplify the downstream task heads, moving away from complex fusion Transformers, early interaction modules, and special grounding tokens, thereby pushing the boundary towards more minimalist, efficient, and equally (if not more) effective multimodal models for referring tasks. The work positions itself as a significant step towards truly unified and referentially intelligent vision-language models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of OneRef is to develop a novel, highly concise, and unified framework for visual grounding and referring segmentation that significantly simplifies the architectural complexity of existing methods while achieving state-of-the-art performance. The authors are motivated by several critical limitations observed in the current research landscape:

1. **Over-reliance on complex architectures:** Existing methods for visual grounding and referring segmentation typically employ separate vision and language encoders, followed by bulky Transformer-based fusion encoders or decoders. This architectural complexity leads to a high number of parameters and intricate interaction mechanisms. The authors aim to explore simpler, modality-shared frameworks that can unify vision and language within a single feature space.
2. **Inefficient cross-modal interaction:** Many approaches introduce a variety of carefully designed early-stage interaction technologies (e.g., adapters, cross-modal bridges) to facilitate fine-grained alignment between modality-dependent encoders. This adds to the model's complexity without necessarily yielding optimal feature unification. OneRef seeks to obviate the necessity for such elaborate interaction modules.
3. **Limitations of generic Mask Visual Language Modeling (MVLM):** While MVLM has proven effective for general multimodal pre-training (as seen in models like BEiT-3), it typically involves alternating unimodal Mask Image Modeling (MIM) and Mask Language Modeling (MLM). This generic masking paradigm often fails to capture the intricate and fine-grained *referential relationship* between image-text pairs, creating a significant gap when applying these models to regression-like referring tasks.
4. **Dependence on special grounding tokens/anchors:** Many current grounding and segmentation models embed specific `[Region]` tokens or rely on multiple query anchors to regress results. These elements can disrupt pre-trained backbones or depend on specific decoder structures, adding complexity and potentially hindering effective knowledge transfer. The authors aim to discard these special tokens/anchors by embedding referential information directly into the visual tokens through pre-training.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives for OneRef are:
- To propose **OneRef**, a minimalist referring framework built on a **modality-shared one-tower Transformer** that unifies visual and linguistic feature spaces.
- To introduce a novel **Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM)** paradigm, encompassing Referring-aware Mask Image Modeling (Referring MIM) and Referring-aware Mask Language Modeling (Referring MLM), to explicitly model the referential relationship.
- To develop a **referring-aware dynamic image masking strategy** within MRefM that focuses reconstruction on the referred region.
- To enable **direct regression of referring results** using lightweight task heads, eliminating the need for complex fusion en/decoders, modality interaction modules, and special grounding tokens.
- To achieve **state-of-the-art (SoTA) performance** on both visual grounding (REC/PG) and referring segmentation (RES) tasks across multiple datasets, thereby providing valuable insights for future research in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OneRef's methodology is built upon a **modality-shared one-tower Transformer** (specifically, a BEiT-3 model) and a novel **Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM)** pre-training paradigm. The approach aims to unify visual and linguistic features from the outset, enabling simpler downstream task heads.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Leveraging BEiT-3 as a Foundation

OneRef starts with BEiT-3, a model that utilizes a multi-way Transformer with Mixture-of-Expert (MoE) heads to create a shared feature space for vision and language. BEiT-3's pre-training involves generic Mask Image Modeling (MIM) and Mask Language Modeling (MLM), where masked tokens are reconstructed based on their unimodal context. OneRef recognizes the strength of this unified architecture but identifies the need for a more *referring-aware* pre-training strategy.

#### 3.2. Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM) Paradigm

The core innovation lies in MRefM, which enhances the referential capability of the BEiT-3 backbone. MRefM consists of two interconnected components:

- **Referring-aware Mask Image Modeling (Referring MIM):**
 - **Cross-modal Reconstruction:** Unlike vanilla MIM, Referring MIM reconstructs masked visual tokens (`z_i`) using visual tokens that have undergone a dot product operation with an aggregated text token (`e_SEP`). This forces the visual reconstruction to be implicitly guided by linguistic information.
 - **Visual Target-Relation Score Reconstruction:** Critically, Referring MIM also reconstructs a **visual target-relation score** (`s_vt`) for *all* visual patches. This score, predicted by an MLP, comprises four masks (x-mask, y-mask, w-mask, h-mask) representing the normalized horizontal/vertical distance from each patch center to the referred region's center, and the patch's proportion of the referred region's width/height. This mechanism directly embeds spatial referential information into visual features during pre-training.
 - **Referring-aware Dynamic Image Masking Strategy:** To further emphasize referential understanding, a novel masking strategy is introduced. Instead of fixed-ratio random or block-wise masking, this dynamic strategy:
 - Masks a relatively *low ratio* (β) of patches in the general image context.
 - Masks a relatively *high ratio* (γ) of patches *within the referred region* (including a small margin).
 - The overall mask ratio (α) dynamically adjusts per sample. This ensures the model pays particular attention to reconstructing and understanding the referred object and its immediate surroundings.

- **Referring-aware Mask Language Modeling (Referring MLM):**
 - **Cross-modal Reconstruction:** Similar to Referring MIM, this module reconstructs masked text tokens (`t_i`) using text tokens that have undergone a dot product operation with an aggregated visual token (`e_CLS`). This guides language reconstruction with visual context.
 - **Semantic Target-Relation Score Reconstruction:** Referring MLM also reconstructs a **semantic target-relation score** (`s_st`) for all text tokens. This score represents the correlation between each text token and the referred image region, obtained from a teacher BEiT-3 model (pre-tuned with image-text contrastive learning). This score is predicted using a 3-layer MLP and softmax, minimizing KL divergence with the teacher's score. This explicitly teaches the model how language tokens relate to the visual target.
 - **(Implicit) Referring-aware text masking:** The authors mention preferentially masking referential subjects in the text, guided by NLP parsing, though this doesn't lead to a significant performance gain.

#### 3.3. Referring-based Grounding and Segmentation Transfer

After MRefM pre-training, the model is fine-tuned for downstream tasks using simple, lightweight heads:

- **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC):**
 - A softmax-normalized similarity mask (`M_sim`) is computed by taking the cosine similarity between visual tokens and the aggregated language token (`e_SEP`).
 - This similarity mask is then used to weight and sum the visual tokens (after an MLP projection).
 - A 3-layer MLP then directly regresses the bounding box coordinates (`B_hat = (x_c, y_c, w_r, h_r)`).
 - **Loss Function:** Combines standard bounding box losses (smooth L1, GIoU) with a novel **box mask constraint loss** (Focal loss + Dice loss) applied to the `M_sim` mask, treating it as a coarse-grained downsampling box mask. This helps align the cross-modal similarity with the ground truth box.

- **Referring Expression Segmentation (RES):**
 - Visual tokens are up-sampled via a 3-layer deconvolutional network.
 - Cosine similarity between these up-sampled visual tokens and the aggregated language token yields a similarity mask.
 - A 1-layer bilinear interpolation converts this to the final high-resolution segmentation mask (`M_seg_hat`).
 - **Loss Function:** Standard segmentation losses (Focal loss + Dice loss).

By integrating MRefM, OneRef ensures that the visual tokens inherently contain rich referring information, allowing for direct regression without complex fusion mechanisms or special tokens, thus achieving a truly unified and minimalist framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate OneRef's superior performance and efficiency across various referring tasks and settings.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and Phrase Grounding (PG):**
 - OneRef consistently surpasses existing SoTA methods, including highly competitive models like HiVG, Grounding-DINO, OFA, and even much larger Grounding Multimodal Large Language Models (GMLLMs) like LION.
 - In the single-dataset fine-tuning setting, OneRef-B (base model) outperforms HiVG-B by significant margins (e.g., 6.15% on RefCOCO+ testB, 4.73% on RefCOCOg test).
 - In the dataset-mixed intermediate pre-training setting, OneRef-B also shows notable improvements over HiVG (e.g., 2.79% on RefCOCO+ testB, 2.63% on RefCOCOg test), Grounding-DINO (up to 4.76% on RefCOCO+ testB), and even surpasses LION (up to 3.76% on RefCOCO testA), which is 20-60 times larger in parameters.
 - The large-sized model, OneRef-L, consistently exhibits strong scalability, achieving further substantial performance gains.
- **Referring Expression Segmentation (RES):**
 - OneRef-B outperforms SoTA self-supervised methods like RISCLIP-B by 2.65% (RefCOCO testB) and 4.77% (RefCOCO+ testB) and traditional approaches like VG-LAW by even larger margins (e.g., 7.31% on RefCOCO+ testB).
 - In the dataset-mixed pre-training setting, OneRef-B surpasses RISCLIP-B by 4.53% (RefCOCO testB) and 8.21% (RefCOCO+ testB).
 - Similar to REC, OneRef-L demonstrates strong scalability and performance enhancements for RES.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM) Effectiveness:** Ablations confirm that each component of MRefM contributes positively to performance. Referring MIM, Referring MLM, and the referring-aware dynamic image masking strategy individually yield improvements (e.g., on RefCOCOg-test, 3.70% for Referring MIM, 2.16% for Referring MLM, and 1.05% for dynamic masking, with a total gain of 6.21%), validating the efficacy of the proposed paradigm.
- **Referring-aware Dynamic Masking Strategy:** The dynamic masking strategy, which assigns higher mask ratios to referred regions (e.g., β=0.35 for context, γ=0.75 for referred region), is shown to be superior to fixed-ratio random or block-wise masking.
- **Referring-based Task Heads:** The lightweight, direct regression task heads (for REC and RES) are highly effective, demonstrating that the one-tower structure and MRefM pre-training successfully embed referring information directly into the backbone features. The box mask loss in the REC head provides an additional 1.50% performance gain.

#### 4.3. Generality and Efficiency

- **Generality of MRefM:**
 - MRefM demonstrates competitive performance even in an unsupervised pre-training setting, indicating its broad applicability.
 - When applied to existing frameworks like TransVG (DETR backbone) and CLIP-VG (CLIP backbone), MRefM consistently yields an approximate 2.0% performance gain, confirming its generality beyond the OneRef architecture.
- **Generality of Task Heads:** The proposed lightweight REC and RES task heads also show generality, improving performance when integrated into other frameworks like TransVG++ and LAVT.
- **Computational Efficiency:** OneRef showcases remarkable efficiency. It utilizes significantly fewer parameters (e.g., its fusion/head module has only 1.7M parameters compared to 20M in other methods), requires lower FLOPs (e.g., 34.9% of Grounding-DINO, 25.2% of MDETR), and achieves substantially faster inference speeds (e.g., 10x faster than Grounding-DINO and TransVG++), all while achieving superior accuracy.

In summary, OneRef not only establishes new state-of-the-art results across various referring tasks but also does so with a much simpler, more parameter-efficient, and computationally faster architecture, validating its core hypothesis about the benefits of a unified feature space and referentially-aware pre-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OneRef paper presents a significant advancement in the field of visual grounding and referring segmentation, offering several important contributions and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Theoretical and Architectural Significance

- **Paradigm Shift in Mask Modeling:** The introduction of **Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM)** is a pioneering effort to explicitly model fine-grained referential relationships within the mask pre-training paradigm. This moves beyond generic Mask Vision Language Modeling (MVLM) and addresses a crucial gap in how current multimodal models learn referential understanding. By reconstructing modality-specific and cross-modal referring content, MRefM fundamentally changes how models acquire referential intelligence.
- **Architectural Simplification and Efficiency:** OneRef successfully demonstrates that high performance in complex multimodal tasks can be achieved with a **minimalist, one-tower, modality-shared Transformer architecture**. This challenges the prevailing trend of building increasingly bulky models with separate encoders, complex fusion layers, and bespoke interaction modules. By unifying feature spaces and eliminating special grounding tokens, OneRef paves the way for more elegant, efficient, and scalable model designs. This has significant implications for deploying advanced vision-language models in resource-constrained environments.
- **Deepening Cross-Modal Alignment:** The proposed visual and semantic target-relation scores, coupled with the referring-aware dynamic masking strategy, provide novel mechanisms for robust and fine-grained cross-modal alignment. This explicit learning of spatial and semantic relationships between visual patches and linguistic tokens during pre-training is a powerful approach to multimodal understanding.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact and Future Directions

- **State-of-the-Art Performance with Efficiency:** Achieving new SoTA results across multiple challenging datasets for REC, PG, and RES tasks, while simultaneously being significantly more parameter- and computationally-efficient, highlights OneRef's practical utility. It offers a more accessible and environmentally friendly pathway to deploy high-performing referring systems.
- **Potential for Large-Scale Unsupervised Pre-training:** The successful demonstration of competitive performance with unsupervised MRefM pre-training is a critical insight. It suggests a promising direction for future research, where large-scale web-scraped image-text data (even without explicit bounding box annotations) could be leveraged to further enhance referential understanding, potentially overcoming the data scarcity for fine-grained referring tasks.
- **Generality and Plug-and-Play Potential:** The generality studies, showing MRefM's ability to improve other existing frameworks and the reusability of its lightweight task heads, underscore its modularity. This suggests MRefM could serve as a plug-and-play component to enhance referential capabilities in various multimodal architectures.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** OneRef provides valuable insights for the broader multimodal AI community, encouraging researchers to rethink architectural complexity and explore more direct and unified ways to imbue models with specific cross-modal reasoning capabilities, rather than relying on increasingly large and specialized components.

#### 5.3. Broader Societal Impact (as acknowledged by authors)

- **Positive Impacts:** OneRef's strong semantic comprehension capabilities and efficiency can facilitate applications that require precise object localization from natural language, such as advanced human-computer interaction, robotic manipulation, improved accessibility tools for visually impaired individuals, and more intuitive image/video editing.
- **Negative Impacts and Concerns:** The power of OneRef also carries risks. Its ability to accurately ground and segment objects based on arbitrary textual queries could be misused in surveillance, enabling targeted detection or tracking of individuals based on descriptive attributes, which raises significant privacy concerns. Such capabilities could also be exploited to create biased detectors or facilitate the spread of misinformation through manipulated content. As the technology becomes more accessible, it is crucial for researchers and developers to consider and implement safeguards against such malicious or unintended uses.
