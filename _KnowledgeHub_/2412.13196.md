# ExBody2: Advanced Expressive Humanoid Whole-Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> ExBody2 introduces a framework for expressive humanoid whole-body control, employing automated data curation and a generalist-specialist policy architecture to enable robust imitation of diverse human motions on a Unitree G1 robot. This approach achieves superior tracking accuracy for both upper and lower body movements compared to prior methods in both simulation and real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Humanoid whole-body control methods often struggle to achieve natural, dynamic, and truly expressive motions, frequently relying on analytical approaches that lack adaptability.
- A significant 'dynamic and kinematic gap' exists between human motion data and robot capabilities, making direct imitation challenging as many human motions are infeasible or destabilizing for robots.
- Prior reinforcement learning approaches for humanoid imitation face limitations in the diversity of motions they can handle, their expressiveness, or their robustness in real-world environments.

## Method

- An automated data curation pipeline intelligently filters human motion capture data based on lower-body feasibility while preserving upper-body diversity, balancing expressive potential with robotic stability.
- A generalist-specialist policy architecture trains a generalist policy for broad motion coverage then fine-tunes specialist policies for precision on complex motions, using a teacher-student reinforcement learning framework for robust sim-to-real transfer.
- A decoupled motion-velocity control strategy guides overall movement through velocity-based global tracking while focusing on local keypoint tracking in the robot's frame, enhancing robustness and expressiveness.

## Results

- The ExBody2 generalist policy consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in simulation across all metrics, showing improved linear velocity, keybody position, and joint position accuracy.
- ExBody2 achieves higher tracking accuracy for both upper and lower body segments in real-world deployments on a Unitree G1 robot, validating the effectiveness of the sim-to-real transfer and policy robustness.
- Finetuned specialist policies consistently outperform both generalist and scratch-trained policies on task-specific motions, with the performance gap increasing for more difficult movements, and demonstrate superior generalization to out-of-distribution motion patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "Feasibility–Diversity Principle" guides effective training, where optimal performance is achieved by balancing the diversity of human motion data with its feasibility for robot execution, identified by an automated curation process.
> - A generalist policy provides a robust prior for broad motion coverage, and fine-tuning it to create specialist policies significantly improves precision and generalization for specific, complex tasks compared to training from scratch.
> - Decoupling local keypoint tracking from global velocity-based tracking enhances control robustness, allowing the policy to prioritize expressive motion reproduction while maintaining overall stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has long pursued the ambitious goal of enabling robots to perform human-like motions within human environments. This endeavor is fraught with challenges due to the high degrees of freedom, complex dynamics, and stringent balance requirements of bipedal systems. "ExBody2" positions itself at the forefront of addressing these challenges, particularly in the realm of *expressive whole-body control* and *motion imitation* for real-world humanoid robots.

Historically, humanoid whole-body control has relied on analytical, model-based approaches (e.g., using Zero Moment Point (ZMP) or optimization-based control). While effective for stable locomotion, these methods often struggle with achieving natural, dynamic, and truly expressive motions, often requiring precise system identification and intensive online computation. More recently, significant advancements have been made through reinforcement learning (RL) and sim-to-real transfer techniques. These data-driven approaches train policies in simulation, leveraging vast amounts of interaction data, and then transfer them to physical robots, often showing superior robustness and the ability to learn complex, dynamic skills.

"ExBody2" directly builds upon these RL-based advancements. It belongs to a growing subset of RL research that integrates human motion capture (MoCap) data to guide robot policy training, aiming for more lifelike and natural robot behaviors. Previous works in this vein, such as ExBody, H2O, and OmniH2O, have shown promising results in imitation, but often face limitations in the sheer diversity of motions they can handle, their expressiveness, or their robustness to real-world complexities. A common hurdle is the "dynamic and kinematic gap" between human demonstrations and robot capabilities, meaning that many human motions are simply infeasible for robots or introduce instability.

The key contribution of ExBody2 to this landscape is its systematic approach to bridge this gap. Unlike prior methods that might rely on manual data filtering or less robust tracking strategies, ExBody2 introduces an *automated data curation* pipeline that intelligently selects feasible yet diverse human motion data. This addresses a critical bottleneck: how to effectively learn from abundant human data without being hampered by its inherent infeasibility for robotic platforms. Furthermore, the *generalist-specialist policy* architecture allows for both broad motion coverage and high-precision execution of specific tasks, moving beyond single, less adaptable policies. The *decoupled motion-velocity control strategy* also refines how robots track motions, shifting from potentially fragile global keypoint tracking to a more robust local frame and velocity-guided approach, which is crucial for dynamic and non-stationary behaviors.

In essence, ExBody2 advances the state-of-the-art by making humanoid robots not just walk or balance, but *express* themselves through a wider, more dynamic, and robust repertoire of full-body movements, directly addressing the limitations of prior art in handling data feasibility, motion diversity, and control robustness for real-world deployment. It also integrates a teacher-student framework, a common and effective strategy for robust sim-to-real transfer, further solidifying its position within the contemporary robot learning paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "ExBody2" research is to significantly enhance the capabilities of real-world humanoid robots, enabling them to perform a wide array of expressive and dynamic whole-body motions while rigorously maintaining stability and robustness. The core motivation stems from the inherent "dynamic and kinematic gap" between human biological structures and mechanical robots, which makes direct imitation challenging and often necessitates a trade-off between motion expressiveness and system stability.

The paper articulates several key objectives that contribute to this overarching goal:

- **Bridging the Expressiveness-Stability Gap:** The primary motivation is to overcome the long-standing challenge of achieving human-level expressiveness and versatility in humanoid robots without compromising their stability and control. This involves allowing robots to imitate complex human whole-body motions robustly.
- **Developing a Comprehensive Whole-Body Control Framework:** The authors aim to introduce "ExBody2," a novel framework designed to generate whole-body tracking controllers capable of executing graceful, human-like full-body motions. This framework is conceived to be highly adaptable and precise.
- **Automated Data Curation for Feasibility and Diversity:** A critical objective is to develop a method for automatically curating human motion capture datasets. The motivation here is to overcome the limitation that raw human motion data often contains movements beyond a robot's physical limits, which can degrade training performance. The goal is to intelligently filter out infeasible lower-body motions while preserving the diversity of upper-body expressiveness, ensuring an optimal balance for learning.
- **Enhancing Precision for Targeted Motions:** While a generalist policy can cover a broad range of motions, the authors aim to achieve even higher fidelity for specific, complex motion groups. The motivation is to develop "specialist policies" that are fine-tuned for particular behaviors, leveraging learned priors from a generalist policy rather than training from scratch, thereby improving efficiency and performance.
- **Robust and Decoupled Motion Control:** A significant objective is to refine the motion tracking strategy itself. Previous methods often struggle with cumulative errors or global keypoint drift. The motivation is to decouple global velocity tracking from local keypoint tracking and to transition from global keypoint tracking to a more robust velocity-based global tracking, allowing for expressive motion reproduction while maintaining stability.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Given that complex policies are typically trained in simulation, a core objective is to ensure seamless and robust transfer to the real world. This is motivated by the need for real-world deployment without requiring privileged information unavailable on physical robots, leading to the adoption of a teacher-student framework.

In summary, the motivation behind ExBody2 is to push the boundaries of humanoid capabilities beyond mere locomotion to genuinely expressive and dynamic whole-body behaviors, addressing core technical limitations in data utilization, policy design, and real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ExBody2 proposes a sophisticated sim-to-real framework built upon three key innovations: an intelligent data-driven generalist-specialist training pipeline, an automated data curation strategy, and a decoupled motion-velocity control approach, all integrated within a teacher-student reinforcement learning paradigm.

**A. Data-driven Generalist-Specialist Training Pipeline**

The core of ExBody2’s training strategy is its Generalist–Specialist pipeline, guided by the "Feasibility–Diversity Principle." This principle dictates that a robust motion dataset must contain sufficient diversity (especially in the upper body for expressiveness) while maintaining lower-body feasibility to prevent unachievable or destabilizing motions.

1. **Motion Retargeting:** Raw human motion data is first retargeted to the robot's specific morphology (Unitree G1). This step ensures kinematic compatibility before policy training.
2. **Generalist Policy with Automated Data Curation:**
 - An initial "base policy" ($\pi_0$) is trained on a comprehensive, unfiltered motion dataset ($D_{CMU}$). This dataset is diverse but contains many motions infeasible for the robot.
 - The base policy's lower-body tracking accuracy is evaluated for each motion sequence, yielding an error metric $e(s)$. This metric is a weighted sum of mean keybody position error ($E_{key}(s)$) and mean joint-angle tracking error ($E_{dof}(s)$) for the lower body, prioritizing stability.
 - Motion sequences are ranked by their error $e(s)$. An empirical distribution $P(e)$ is derived.
 - An optimal error threshold ($\tau^*$) is identified by systematically evaluating policies trained on subsets ($D_\tau$) filtered by different $\tau$ values. The goal is to maximize performance across the *full* dataset $D$. This process ensures a balance between retaining diverse (especially upper-body) motions and filtering out infeasible (lower-body) ones. The paper identifies $\tau=0.15$ as an optimal threshold.
 - A "generalist policy" ($\pi_{\tau^*}$) is then trained on the automatically curated dataset $D_{\tau^*}$. This policy is designed for broad motion coverage and robustness.

3. **Specialist Policy Finetuning for Targeted Motions:**
 - Once the generalist policy $\pi_{\tau^*}$ is trained, it serves as a warm start for training "specialist policies."
 - For specific, high-precision tasks (e.g., dancing, walking, kung fu), the generalist policy is fine-tuned on smaller, task-specific datasets (e.g., $D_{dancing}$).
 - This approach leverages the generalist's broad priors, making fine-tuning more efficient and enabling adaptation to challenging motions that might be difficult to learn from scratch. It also inherits robustness from exposure to a wider range of conditions.

**B. Policy Objective and Architecture (Teacher-Student Framework)**

ExBody2 employs a two-stage teacher-student training procedure within the IsaacGym simulation environment for efficient parallel training.

1. **Teacher Policy Training:**
 - An "oracle teacher policy" ($\hat{\pi}$) is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a standard reinforcement learning algorithm.
 - The teacher policy has access to "privileged information" unavailable in the real world. This includes ground-truth root velocity, accurate body link positions, and physical properties (e.g., friction coefficients, motor strength). This privileged information significantly boosts sample efficiency and enables the teacher to achieve high performance.
 - The input to the teacher policy includes privileged observations ($P_t$), proprioceptive states ($O_t$), and motion tracking targets ($G_t$).
 - The output action ($\hat{a}_t$) consists of target joint positions for Proportional-Derivative (PD) controllers.
 - The reward function is meticulously designed to encourage tracking (linear velocity, orientation, keypoints, joint positions) and includes regularization terms for stability and sim-to-real transfer (e.g., penalties for joint limits, acceleration, feet air time, stumble events).

2. **Student Policy Training:**
 - A "student policy" ($\pi$) is trained to mimic the teacher's behavior but *without* access to privileged information.
 - The student policy infers necessary information from a longer sequence of historical proprioceptive observations ($O_{t-H:t}$) and the motion tracking target ($G_t$). A history length of 10 is found to be optimal.
 - Training uses a DAgger-style distillation approach: the student policy rolls out in the simulation, and for each visited state, the teacher policy provides the "oracle action" as a supervision signal. The student policy is then refined by minimizing an MSE loss between its actions and the teacher's actions. This iterative process ensures the student learns to perform effectively in real-world observable conditions.

**C. Decoupled Motion-Velocity Control Strategy**

To enhance tracking robustness and expressiveness, ExBody2 introduces a decoupled control strategy for motion tracking:

- **Local Keypoint Tracking:** Unlike previous methods (e.g., H2O, OmniH2O) that relied on global keypoint positions, ExBody2 converts keypoints into the robot's *local frame*. This prevents cumulative errors that can arise from global keypoint drift.
- **Velocity-Based Global Tracking:** Instead of strictly following global keypoint positions, overall movement is guided by *velocity-based tracking*. This allows for slight positional deviations (global drift) during training to enable more challenging keypoint motions, with periodic corrections to the robot's current coordinate frame. During real-world deployment, strict local keypoint tracking is employed, emphasizing expressive motion reproduction even if it means small positional shifts. This decoupling allows the policy to prioritize dynamic expressiveness while maintaining overall stability through velocity control.

The system is deployed and validated on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, leveraging LCM (Lightweight Communications and Marshalling) for communication and a 50Hz inference frequency for the control policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in "ExBody2" rigorously evaluate the proposed framework against state-of-the-art baselines, demonstrating significant improvements in humanoid control.

**Q1. Generalist Policy Performance:**
- **Simulation Performance:** The ExBody2 generalist policy, particularly with automated data filtering, consistently outperforms all compared baselines (Exbody, Exbody†, OmniH2O*) in simulation across all measured metrics: mean linear velocity error ($E_{vel}$), mean per keybody position error (MPKPE for whole, upper, and lower body), and mean per joint position error (MPJPE for whole, upper, and lower body). The improvement in lower-body accuracy is particularly notable, leading to enhanced overall stability and, consequently, more precise upper-body control.
- **Real-World Deployment:** Experiments on the Unitree G1 robot, using a representative subset of motions, mirror the simulation results. ExBody2 achieves higher tracking accuracy for both upper and lower body segments in the real world. This demonstrates the effectiveness of the sim-to-real transfer and the robustness of the trained policies to real-world disturbances.

**Q2. Impact of Automatic Data Curation:**
- The ablation study on dataset filtering thresholds (using subsets of CMU data: $D_{50}$, $D_{250}$, and $D_{CMU}$) empirically validates the "Feasibility–Diversity Principle."
- **Low Thresholds ($D_{50}$):** Policies trained on heavily filtered, simple datasets perform well on in-distribution tasks but exhibit poor generalization to more complex or diverse motions due to a lack of learned variability.
- **High Thresholds (Full $D_{CMU}$):** Policies trained on highly diverse but unfiltered datasets (including infeasible motions) struggle. The inclusion of noisy or unachievable motions degrades training effectiveness and leads to inconsistent behavior and reduced accuracy.
- **Moderate Thresholds (e.g., $D_{\tau=0.15}$ / similar to $D_{250}$):** Policies trained on datasets with an intermediate filtering threshold (balancing feasibility and diversity) achieve the best overall performance. The optimal threshold ($D_{\tau=0.15}$) retains sufficient variability for generalization while effectively removing detrimental, unstable motions, resulting in the lowest overall tracking error across diverse tasks. This confirms the value of the automated data curation process.

**Q3. Specialist Policy Finetuning:**
- **Performance Across Difficulty Levels:** Finetuned specialist policies consistently outperform both the generalist policy and policies trained from scratch on task-specific datasets ($D_{easy}$, $D_{moderate}$, $D_{hard}$). The performance gap between specialist and scratch-trained policies widens with increasing motion difficulty, highlighting the significant advantage of leveraging a strong pretrained generalist prior.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** On the $D_{ACCAD}$ (OOD) dataset, the finetuned specialist policy significantly outperforms both the generalist and scratch policies, demonstrating its superior adaptability and generalization capabilities to previously unseen motion patterns.
- **Qualitative Results (Cha-Cha Dance):** A detailed case study of the Cha-Cha dance visually confirms that the specialist policy achieves substantially lower tracking errors compared to generalist and scratch-trained policies, effectively capturing fine-grained motion details while maintaining stability.
- **Efficiency:** Finetuning a generalist policy is also shown to be more computationally efficient than training specialist policies from scratch.

**Ablation Studies (Appendix):**
- **History Length:** The student policy performs best with a history length of 10 observations. Too short a history limits its ability to infer privileged information, while excessively long histories increase the difficulty of fitting the policy without proportional gains in performance.
- **Teacher-Student Distillation:** Removing the DAgger-style distillation significantly degrades the student policy's performance. This highlights the crucial role of the teacher policy in providing robust supervision, particularly for learning accurate velocity tracking for dynamic motions.

In conclusion, the findings strongly support the effectiveness of ExBody2's integrated approach. The automated data curation ensures a high-quality training dataset, the generalist policy provides broad capabilities, and specialist fine-tuning delivers task-specific precision, all facilitated by a robust sim-to-real teacher-student framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ExBody2 represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in terms of expressive, dynamic, and stable whole-body control for real-world deployments. Its innovations have several key significances and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Humanoid Expressiveness and Realism:** By enabling robots to accurately and robustly replicate a diverse range of human motions, including nuanced gestures and dynamic full-body actions like dancing or punching, ExBody2 contributes to making humanoids more lifelike and engaging. This is crucial for applications requiring natural human-robot interaction, such as caregiving, education, or entertainment.
- **Improved Robustness and Stability in Dynamic Environments:** The meticulous data curation, decoupled control strategy, and teacher-student framework contribute to highly robust policies. This means robots can perform complex actions not just in controlled lab settings but also in real-world environments with inherent uncertainties and disturbances, increasing their practical utility and safety.
- **Practicality of Learning from Human Data:** The automated data curation method is a major practical contribution. It addresses the fundamental challenge of effectively utilizing large, noisy human motion datasets, reducing the significant manual effort previously required to filter out infeasible movements. This makes it easier and more efficient to train new expressive skills.
- **Versatile and Adaptable Control:** The generalist-specialist paradigm offers a powerful approach to robotic control. The generalist policy provides broad foundational capabilities, while specialist policies can be precisely tailored for high-stakes or highly specific tasks. This adaptability is vital for humanoids designed for diverse applications, allowing them to switch between modes of operation with high fidelity.
- **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The robust teacher-student distillation process, combined with careful observation space design (e.g., using historical observations to infer privileged information), further refines sim-to-real transfer techniques. This is a perpetual challenge in robotics, and ExBody2's successful deployment on the Unitree G1 demonstrates a concrete step forward in reliable transfer from high-fidelity simulations to physical hardware.
- **Broader Applications:** Beyond general locomotion, the ability to perform precise and expressive motions opens doors for humanoids in various domains:
 - **Entertainment:** Highly dynamic dance performances or complex martial arts demonstrations.
 - **Assistance and Care:** More natural interaction and assistance with tasks requiring human-like movements.
 - **Research and Development:** Provides a robust baseline and framework for future research in areas like advanced manipulation, dexterous object interaction, and learning from varied human demonstrations (e.g., RGB video input, motion synthesis, as explored in the appendix).

While the paper acknowledges a limitation in seamlessly recombining specialist policies for dynamic transitions between diverse motion types, addressing this in future work would further solidify the framework's versatility. Overall, ExBody2 significantly advances the state of expressive humanoid whole-body control, bringing us closer to a future where robots can move and interact with the fluidity and nuance traditionally associated only with humans.
