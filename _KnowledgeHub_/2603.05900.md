# Reference-guided Policy Optimization for Molecular Optimization via LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Reference-guided Policy Optimization (RePO) trains Large Language Models for instruction-based molecular optimization by combining reward-driven exploration with answer-level reference molecule guidance, effectively addressing sparse supervision. The method consistently outperformed SFT and GRPO baselines, achieving up to 13.0% absolute improvement over the base model on property-based tasks and demonstrating improved reasoning quality.

## Problem

- Instruction-based molecular optimization datasets typically provide only a final optimized molecule, lacking detailed, step-by-step reasoning trajectories for LLM training.
- Existing LLM training paradigms like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on answers often lead to 'collapsed reasoning,' while Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) struggles with sparse learning signals and conservative exploration.
- Molecular optimization inherently involves balancing competing objectives, such as enhancing a target property while maintaining structural similarity, which current LLM methods struggle to effectively manage.

## Method

- RePO (Reference-guided Policy Optimization) integrates an ==RLVR-style== exploration term with a distinct answer-level reference guidance term and KL regularization.
- A key design choice is 'gradient masking,' which applies reference guidance only to the answer tokens, preventing token-level imitation of reasoning and preserving diverse exploration paths.
- A composite scalar reward function combines structural similarity (Tanimoto coefficient) and a binary property improvement metric to effectively guide optimization towards both integrity and target enhancement.

## Results

- RePO consistently outperformed SFT and GRPO baselines, achieving up to **13.0%** absolute improvement over the base model on property-based tasks and up to **8.3%** in SR × Sim on structure-based tasks.
- The method demonstrated robust generalization to unseen instruction styles and out-of-distribution tasks, while also proving robust to significant corruption in reference-query alignments.
- Qualitative and quantitative analyses confirmed that RePO generates higher quality, chemically valid reasoning processes and molecular modifications, and benefits from larger backbone models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding LLMs at the answer level with reference molecules, while allowing reward-driven exploration for reasoning, effectively overcomes the supervision mismatch in complex scientific tasks.
> - Applying gradient masking, which limits reference guidance to the final answer tokens, is crucial for maintaining effective exploration and preventing the model from learning spurious reasoning patterns.
> - The approach successfully balances the LLM's need to explore the chemical space for novel solutions with the exploitation of readily available high-quality reference molecules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in reasoning-intensive tasks, benefiting from supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). These paradigms enable LLMs to perform multi-step deliberation before generating responses, addressing complex problems across various domains. However, the application of these techniques to scientific tasks, particularly in chemistry, remains an area requiring further investigation.

Molecular optimization is a specialized scientific domain that involves modifying chemical structures to improve specific properties while adhering to structural similarity constraints with the original molecule. This task often presents competing objectives: significant property enhancement typically requires non-trivial structural alterations, which can reduce similarity to the original molecule and potentially compromise chemical validity. Traditional molecular design approaches have faced limitations in synthesizing molecules with precisely tailored properties and often exhibit poor generalization to novel objectives or complex constraint combinations.

Recent advancements in LLMs have motivated their application in molecular design, leveraging their ability to follow natural language instructions. Prior work suggests LLMs can capture knowledge of molecular properties, yet they frequently encounter difficulties when confronted with chemistry-specific constraints such as validity preservation or property satisfaction. Existing LLM-based molecular optimization methods often embed LLMs within black-box pipelines (e.g., evolutionary search with LLM-proposed edits, fine-tuning LLMs as scoring oracles, or coupling LLMs with external chemistry tools). These approaches do not directly train the LLM as an end-to-end optimizer that jointly generates reasoning and molecular edits.

This paper addresses a specific challenge within this landscape: the supervision mismatch encountered when applying conventional SFT and RLVR recipes to instruction-based molecular optimization. Datasets for this task typically provide only a single optimized reference molecule without detailed step-by-step reasoning trajectories. The authors identify that existing methods either struggle with exploration in the constrained chemical space or fail to adequately exploit available reference supervision without collapsing the reasoning process. This research aims to develop an LLM-based optimization approach that overcomes these limitations, enabling more effective and robust molecular optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective strategy for training Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform instruction-based molecular optimization. This task requires LLMs to modify an input molecule to enhance a target property while simultaneously maintaining a high degree of structural similarity to the original molecule. The authors identify substantial limitations in existing LLM training paradigms, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), when applied to this specific domain, which serves as the core motivation for their proposed approach.

The detailed motivations stem from a "supervision mismatch" and inherent challenges within molecular optimization:

1. **Absence of Detailed Reasoning Trajectories**: Unlike many reasoning tasks where LLMs benefit from step-by-step supervision, instruction-based molecular optimization datasets typically provide only a single optimized reference molecule. There is no explicit trajectory or sequence of edits that leads from the initial molecule to the optimized one. This lack of intermediate supervision limits the effectiveness of conventional SFT and RLVR.

2. **Limitations of Answer-Only SFT**: The authors observe that when SFT is applied using only the final reference molecule as supervision, the model often "collapses reasoning." This means it learns to produce deterministic, short, answer-only outputs, suppressing the multi-step reasoning capabilities essential for exploring the complex chemical space. Such models demonstrate weak control over the crucial similarity-property trade-off.

3. **Challenges of Sparse Feedback in RLVR**: When RLVR (specifically GRPO) is applied directly or initialized from an SFT model:
 - **Conservative Optimization**: Directly training with RL from a base model often leads to highly conservative edits. The model generates molecules that maintain high similarity to the input but achieve only marginal property improvements.
 - **Sparse Learning Signals**: In the vast chemical space, samples that simultaneously achieve significant property improvement and satisfy similarity constraints are rare. This rarity results in sparse learning signals, making it difficult for RL to effectively push the policy beyond local, conservative modifications.
 - **Failure to Recover Reasoning**: If RLVR is initialized from an SFT-trained model, it often fails to recover multi-step reasoning, instead preserving the short, answer-only response style learned during SFT. This limits exploration and subsequent property improvement.

4. **Competing Objectives**: Molecular optimization inherently involves competing objectives: improving a target property often requires substantial structural changes (e.g., adding functional groups), which can simultaneously reduce structural similarity and potentially compromise chemical validity. Existing methods struggle to balance this trade-off effectively.

To address these limitations, the authors aim to develop an approach that achieves two key desiderata:
- **Effective Exploration**: Enable the LLM to explore the constrained chemical space efficiently to discover new molecules with desired properties.
- **Answer-Level Anchoring**: Provide a mechanism to exploit the available reference molecules at the answer level, offering guidance without imposing rigid, token-level imitation of an optimization process.

The overarching objective is to introduce an optimization approach, Reference-guided Policy Optimization (RePO), that learns from reference molecules *without requiring trajectory data*. This approach is designed to encourage exploration of new molecules while balancing the exploitation of reference molecules, thereby enhancing both optimization performance and the interpretability of the LLM's reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Reference-guided Policy Optimization (RePO), is designed to address the supervision mismatch in instruction-based molecular optimization by combining reward-driven exploration with answer-level reference guidance.

**Core Objective Formulation:**
For each query `q = (x, m0)` (textual instruction `x` and input molecule `m0`), RePO samples `G` responses ` {oi}Gi=1 ` from the old policy `πold(· | q)`. Each response `oi` is partitioned into reasoning tokens `ti` and a final optimized molecule `ˆmi`. A scalar reward `r(oi, q)` is computed based on `ˆmi` and `m0`. The token-wise group-relative advantage `ˆAi,k` is calculated as in traditional RLVR. The RePO objective `JRePO(πθ)` is a composite function combining three terms:

1. **Exploration Term (RLVR-style)**: This term is derived from GRPO, aiming to increase the likelihood of tokens that lead to higher-reward candidate molecules and decrease the likelihood of those leading to lower-reward ones. It applies updates to *all* tokens (`oi,k`) within a generated response (`oi`). This term uses a clipped ratio `ρi,k` (ratio of new to old policy probabilities) to stabilize training, multiplied by `ˆAi,k`. Its purpose is to promote exploration in the constrained chemical space.

2. **Answer-Level Reference Guidance Term**: This term directly increases the likelihood of the reference molecule `mref` (provided in the dataset) conditioned on the *model's sampled reasoning prefix* `ti` from `oi`. Crucially, this guidance is applied only to the *answer tokens* (`mref`) and *not* to the intermediate reasoning tokens (`ti`). A hyperparameter `β` controls the strength of this guidance. This term mitigates reward sparsity and stabilizes training by providing an anchor to known good solutions.

3. **KL Regularization Term**: A KL divergence term, `D_KL(πθ || πref)`, is included to stabilize training and prevent the policy from deviating too far from a reference policy (typically the initial model or a fixed baseline). The K3 estimator is used for calculating this regularization.

**Key Design Choices:**

- **Reference Molecules (`mref`)**: For each query, the dataset provides a single `mref` (a known optimized molecule). RePO uses `mref` solely as an *answer-level anchor* within the guidance term, meaning it guides the final output without dictating the intermediate reasoning steps. RDKit validity checks are applied to `mref`. The key distinction is that `mref` does not offer "reasoning supervision"; trajectory learning remains reward-driven on model-sampled responses.

- **Reward Design (`r(m, m0)`)**: The scalar reward `r` is composed of two parts:
 - `r_struct(m, m0)`: Structural similarity, measured by Tanimoto similarity between the molecular fingerprints (FP(m)) of the candidate molecule `m` and the input molecule `m0`. This term encourages maintaining structural integrity.
 - `r_prop(m, m0)`: Target property improvement, defined as a binary reward. It is 1 if the target property `F(m)` meets the instruction (e.g., `F(m) ≥ F(m0)` for increasing a property, or `F(m) ≤ F(m0)` for decreasing), and 0 otherwise. The paper notes that this binary reward generally provides more stable gains than a continuous version.

- **Training Procedure**:
 1. For a given query `q`, the current policy `πold` samples `G` responses `oi = [ti; ˆmi]`.
 2. The final molecule `ˆmi` from each response is parsed, and its validity and properties are evaluated to compute `r(oi, q)`.
 3. The group-relative advantages `ˆAi,k` are computed.
 4. The RePO objective function (Equation 4) is optimized:
 - The **exploration term** applies updates to *all tokens* in `oi` based on their advantage.
 - The **reference guidance term** increases the likelihood of `mref` by backpropagating gradients *only through the answer tokens*, using `ti` as context. This is crucial for avoiding token-level imitation of reasoning and preserving diverse exploration paths. This is termed "gradient masking."
 - The **KL regularization term** is also applied.

**Architectural and Experimental Setup:**
- **Base Model**: Qwen-2.5-3B Instruct (with additional experiments on Qwen-2.5-7B Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct).
- **Datasets**: TOMG-Bench (single-objective structure-based and property-based tasks) and MuMOInstruct (multi-objective, seen and unseen instruction styles).
- **Baselines**: Base Model, Distill-SFT, SFT (final-answer-only), GRPO (RLVR from base model), GRPO (SFT-init) (RLVR from SFT model). Domain-specific optimizers (Bio-T5, Mol-T5) and multi-round evolutionary methods (Graph-GA, REINVENT, MOLLEO) were also used for comparison.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Success Rate (SR), Similarity (Tanimoto coefficient), and their product (SR × Similarity) to capture the balance between property improvement and structural preservation.
- **Implementation Details**: Llama-Factory for SFT, TRL library for RL. Specific hyperparameters for learning rate, batch size, epochs, and generation settings are provided.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness and robustness of RePO in instruction-based molecular optimization.

#### 4.1. Supervision Mismatch Analysis (Section 3)

- **GRPO (from base model) struggles**: Tends to generate conservative edits, maintaining high similarity but achieving limited property improvement (low Success Rate). This suggests sparse learning signals and updates concentrated in narrow local regimes.
- **SFT (answer-only) produces short outputs**: Fine-tuning with only reference molecules results in short, answer-only outputs, suppressing multi-step reasoning. While achieving moderate success rates, it yields lower similarity than RL methods, indicating insufficient control over the similarity-property trade-off.
- **GRPO (SFT-init) does not recover reasoning**: Starting GRPO from an SFT-initialized model largely preserves the short-response style, failing to recover multi-step reasoning. This configuration achieves the lowest success rate, inheriting SFT's limited exploration without gaining effective RL-driven property improvement.
- **Conclusion**: These observations highlight the need for an approach that provides both directional, answer-level reference guidance (to enhance learning signals) and trajectory-level RL updates (to preserve diverse exploration without token-level imitation).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance (Section 5.2)

- **Single-objective tasks (TOMG-Bench)**: RePO consistently outperforms SFT and GRPO baselines, achieving superior or competitive performance across structure-based and property-based optimization tasks.
 - For structure-based tasks (AddComponent, SubComponent), RePO shows improvements of up to 8.3% and 4% respectively (in SR × Sim) over the next best method.
 - For property-based tasks (QED, LogP, MR), RePO achieves up to 13.0% absolute improvement over the base model.
 - GRPO without SFT initialization performs notably worse on structure-based tasks, indicating challenges with unconstrained exploration in a vast chemical space.
- **Multi-objective tasks (MuMOInstruct)**: RePO demonstrates an improved ability to balance competing objectives (e.g., BDP, BPQ tasks), achieving up to 4% improvements over baselines.
- **Generalization to unseen instruction styles**: RePO maintains its performance advantage even when confronted with novel instruction formats in MuMOInstruct, indicating robust generalization capabilities.
- **Comparison with other optimizers**: RePO is competitive with or outperforms domain-specific LLM optimizers (Bio-T5, Mol-T5) and multi-round evolutionary methods (Graph-GA, REINVENT, MOLLEO), despite using a smaller, open backbone and single-round optimization.

#### 4.3. Mechanism and Robustness Analyses (Section 5.3)

- **Gradient Masking is Crucial**: Ablation studies confirm that applying gradient masking (limiting reference guidance to answer tokens only) is essential for RePO's performance. Without it, answer-level supervision can inappropriately reinforce intermediate reasoning, leading to spurious patterns and underperformance. Masking also improves optimization stability, leading to more consistent reward increases during training.
- **Robustness to Reference Corruption**: RePO exhibits graceful degradation when a significant portion (up to 50%) of query-reference alignments are corrupted, remaining competitive with or superior to baselines.
- **Improved Property-Gain Distributions**: RePO shifts the distribution of property gains towards larger positive values compared to the base model and GRPO, indicating more widespread and substantial optimization outcomes across candidate molecules.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Quality**: An LLM-as-a-judge evaluation (calibrated to expert chemists) indicates that RePO achieves the highest reasoning-quality score among tested methods.
- **Scalability to Larger Backbones**: RePO benefits from larger backbone models (e.g., Qwen-2.5-7B Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), yielding further performance improvements.
- **Prompting-Only Baselines are Insufficient**: Zero-shot and few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting methods do not close the performance gap and often underperform RePO, suggesting that end-to-end policy optimization with reference guidance is more effective than prompt-only reasoning elicitation in this domain.
- **Binary Reward Function Stability**: A binary property reward (`r_prop`) yields more stable gains than a continuous version, suggesting that a discretized success signal is easier to optimize under similarity constraints.
- **Sensitivity to Guidance Weight (`β`)**: Performance improves with increasing `β` (guidance strength) but saturates, indicating that excessively strong guidance can limit exploration.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization**: RePO generalizes better than its counterparts to unseen structure-editing tasks when trained on property optimization, demonstrating transferability of its learned strategy across instruction types.

#### 4.4. Case Studies (Section 5.4)

- **Chemically-Validated Reasoning**: Qualitative examples illustrate RePO's ability to generate chemically sound reasoning processes and effective modifications (e.g., substituting Br with Cl to lower MR). In contrast, GRPO exhibits invalid chemical expressions, incorrect analysis, and chemically implausible modifications. This highlights RePO's capacity for coherent reasoning that aligns with chemical principles.
- **Inference-Scaling Properties**: RePO's best-of-k success rate and the similarity of trials show marked improvements as the number of sampling trials (k) increases, indicating its ability to leverage increased computational budgets at inference time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Reference-guided Policy Optimization (RePO) as a novel and effective approach for enhancing Large Language Model (LLM) performance in instruction-based molecular optimization. Its significance and potential impact span both the methodological advancement of LLM training and practical applications in scientific discovery.

#### 5.1. Methodological Advancements

- **Addresses a Critical Supervision Mismatch**: RePO directly confronts the challenge of sparse, answer-only supervision in complex scientific tasks, a common limitation when applying standard SFT and RLVR. By coupling reward-driven exploration with answer-level reference guidance without requiring intermediate trajectory data, RePO provides a solution for learning in environments with limited expert demonstrations.
- **Balanced Exploration and Exploitation**: The design of RePO, particularly the selective application of gradient masking, allows the model to explore diverse reasoning paths while anchoring its final outputs to high-quality reference molecules. This balance prevents the policy collapse often seen with pure SFT and overcomes the conservative, sparse-reward limitations of unguided RL.
- **Improved Interpretability of LLM Reasoning**: By guiding the model towards chemically sound reasoning, RePO enables the generation of more coherent and interpretable explanations for molecular modifications, which is crucial for domain experts in scientific fields.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact on Molecular Optimization

- **Accelerated Molecule Discovery**: RePO's ability to consistently outperform existing baselines across single- and multi-objective molecular optimization tasks, and to generalize to unseen instruction styles, suggests a potential to significantly accelerate the discovery and design of novel molecules.
- **Enhanced Optimization Effectiveness**: The method improves the balance across competing objectives (e.g., simultaneously increasing a property while maintaining structural similarity), which is a key challenge in real-world drug discovery and materials science.
- **Robustness and Generalization**: RePO's demonstrated robustness to noisy data (reference corruption) and its ability to generalize to out-of-distribution tasks make it a more reliable tool for practical applications, reducing the need for extensive task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Leveraging Existing Data**: The approach effectively utilizes readily available "reference" data (optimized molecules) without demanding costly, manually curated step-by-step reasoning trajectories, making it highly practical for domains with large outcome-oriented datasets.

#### 5.3. Broader Implications for Scientific AI

- **Template for Other Scientific Reasoning Tasks**: The core principles of RePO—learning from sparse, verifiable outcomes while promoting structured exploration—are generalizable. The authors propose future work in areas like retrosynthesis planning and drug-drug interaction prediction, which similarly involve vast search spaces, domain-specific multi-step reasoning, and verifiable (but not explicitly traced) solutions.
- **Integration with Domain Knowledge**: RePO provides a principled method for incorporating expert domain knowledge (through reference molecules) into LLM training, bridging the gap between general-purpose language understanding and specialized scientific problem-solving.
- **Foundation for Trustworthy AI in Science**: By producing more chemically sound reasoning and transparent justifications for molecular modifications, RePO contributes to building more trustworthy AI systems for scientific research, where explainability and validity are paramount.

In conclusion, RePO represents a significant step towards enabling LLMs to serve as more effective and reliable tools for complex scientific optimization, with direct applicability to drug discovery, materials engineering, and other chemistry-intensive fields.
