# Embodied Long Horizon Manipulation with Closed-loop Code Generation and Incremental Few-shot Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> DAHLIA is a dual-tunnel LLM framework for long-horizon robot manipulation that fully discards pretrained low-level policies, generating executable code plans via a planner with chain-of-thought incremental-difficulty few-shot examples, closed-loop-corrected by a VLM reporter giving structured RGB-D feedback; achieves state-of-the-art success rates on 30+ seen/unseen tasks across LoHoRavens, CALVIN, Franka Kitchen, and real-world settings.

## Problem

- LLM-as-planner methods pair with pretrained low-level controllers and assume perfect execution from them, an unrealistic assumption under real-world noise or suboptimal behaviors.
- Code-generation-only frameworks mostly use open-loop control, leaving closed-loop feedback for recovery or replanning under partial observability largely underexplored and rarely open-sourced.
- Prior in-context learning for code generation relies on randomly selected examples without structured ordering, failing to improve generalization to unseen long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- ==Planner tunnel==: a main ==LLM== planner plus auxiliary co-planner ==LMPs== generates executable code plans over skill-primitive APIs, offloading object/position parsing to reduce cumulative planning errors.
- ==Chain-of-thought incremental-difficulty few-shot prompting== arranges in-context examples in a curriculum of increasing complexity, guiding the LLM to extract reasoning patterns rather than surface-mimic.
- ==Reporter tunnel==: a ==VLM== evaluates paired before/after RGB-D observations per loop and returns structured feedback (object, location, target, action) enabling ==temporally-abstracted replanning== without per-step inference.

## Results

- On original LoHoRavens tasks DAHLIA reaches **100%** success rate in five of ten tasks, outperforming CaP (up to **86%** on simple tasks but far lower on complex ones) by up to **12 percentage points**.
- On ten newly generated unseen tasks, closed-loop feedback over the planner-only variant gives gains such as **+24%** on G2, **+12%** on G7, and **+8%** on G3 and G5.
- Scalability tests show **99.60%** overall success on CALVIN (vs. **98.00%** planner-only) and **98.86%** on Franka Kitchen (vs. **96.00%**), with gains up to **+8%** on precision tasks like bottom burner.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discarding pretrained low-level controllers in favor of direct LLM code generation removes a major source of cumulative execution error in long-horizon tasks.
> - Structuring few-shot examples by progressive difficulty with chain-of-thought reasoning generalizes better to unseen tasks than randomly ordered examples of equal count.
> - Temporal abstraction, evaluating and correcting after a full primitive sequence rather than per-step, cuts inference noise while still enabling failure recovery under partial observability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied long-horizon manipulation, where robots interpret complex human commands and perform multi-step tasks in real-world environments, represents a frontier in AI and robotics. This paper, titled DAHLIA (Data-Agnostic Hierarchical Closed-loop Framework for Long-horizon Incremental In-context Adaptation), positions itself at the nexus of several evolving research paradigms, aiming to address critical limitations within each.

Traditionally, robotic manipulation has seen two main lines of work:

1. **Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs):** These models directly map language instructions and visual observations to robotic actions, often leveraging large-scale vision-language models (VLMs). While promising for their end-to-end nature, VLAs like RT-2 and Open-VLA demand massive amounts of pretraining and fine-tuning data, which is prohibitively costly and time-consuming to collect in diverse robotic domains. This "data-hungry" nature severely limits their generalization to novel, unseen scenarios.

2. **Hierarchical Frameworks (LLMs as Planners + Pretrained Low-level Policies):** This approach uses LLMs for high-level task decomposition (planning subtasks in natural language) and then relies on pretrained low-level controllers (often trained via reinforcement learning, e.g., CLIPort) for executing these subtasks. Examples include SayCan, Inner Monologue, DoReMi, and LoHoRavens. A significant drawback here is the inherent assumption of perfect execution from these low-level policies. In real-world environments characterized by noise, perturbations, or suboptimal policy design, this assumption quickly breaks down, leading to cumulative errors and poor robustness in unseen situations. While these methods have explored feedback mechanisms to some extent, their reliance on imperfect low-level policies remains a bottleneck.

A more recent and rapidly growing direction is **Motion Planning with Code Generation**, where LLMs directly generate executable code to control robots, bypassing the need for extensive data collection for low-level policies. Notable works like Code-as-Policy (CaP) exemplify this shift. However, current code-generation approaches often face two key limitations:
 - **Open-loop Control:** Most methods operate in an open-loop fashion, meaning they lack a robust feedback mechanism to evaluate execution outcomes and recover from errors or partial observability. While closed-loop feedback has been studied in language-conditioned, fine-tuned policy approaches, its systematic adoption in code-generation-only frameworks is underexplored.
 - **Ineffective Few-shot Learning:** Many existing code-generation methods rely on randomly selected in-context examples for few-shot learning. This approach often proves insufficient for complex task generalization, as it fails to guide the LLM's reasoning process effectively, particularly for unseen tasks or those requiring complex spatial and contextual understanding.

DAHLIA directly addresses these gaps by completely discarding reliance on pretrained low-level policies. Instead, it leverages LLMs for direct code generation within an innovative **closed-loop dual-tunnel framework**. Its key contributions to the landscape include:
- **Novel Closed-loop for Code Generation:** It introduces a sophisticated closed-loop feedback mechanism into a code-generation-only pipeline, enhancing reliability, task recovery, and adaptability under partial observability – a crucial advancement for real-world deployment.
- **Structured Few-Shot Adaptation:** It moves beyond random examples by employing **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-guided few-shot learning with incrementally structured examples**. This "curriculum learning" approach teaches the LLM underlying reasoning patterns and task logic, significantly boosting generalization to complex and unseen scenarios.
- **Temporal Abstraction:** By executing entire sequences of primitives per loop before receiving inter-loop feedback, DAHLIA reduces per-step inference, minimizes computational overhead, and limits error accumulation, a common issue in long-horizon tasks.

In essence, DAHLIA represents a significant step towards creating data-agnostic, highly generalizable, and robust robotic manipulation systems. It synthesizes the strengths of LLM reasoning with a practical, closed-loop control paradigm, charting a promising path for future embodied AI research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the DAHLIA framework are driven by the need to overcome fundamental limitations observed in prior approaches to embodied long-horizon manipulation. The core motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Eliminate Reliance on Data-Intensive Pretrained Policies:** Existing learning-based approaches, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, require massive, costly, and task-specific datasets for pretraining and fine-tuning. This data dependency severely limits their scalability and generalization to novel environments and tasks. DAHLIA aims to fully discard these pretrained low-level policies, shifting towards a data-agnostic paradigm where LLMs directly generate executable code.

2. **Address the "Perfect Execution" Assumption in Hierarchical Frameworks:** Hierarchical methods that use LLMs for high-level planning and pretrained policies for low-level execution often assume these low-level policies will execute flawlessly. In real-world noisy and dynamic environments, this assumption is unrealistic. Suboptimal policy behaviors or unexpected perturbations lead to errors that accumulate over long-horizon tasks, hindering overall success. DAHLIA seeks to mitigate this by having the LLM directly control actions through code, offering potentially higher precision and adaptability.

3. **Introduce Robust Closed-Loop Feedback to Code Generation:** While some feedback mechanisms exist in language-conditioned approaches, code-generation-only frameworks have largely operated in an open-loop manner. This lack of feedback prevents robots from detecting and recovering from execution errors, handling partial observability (e.g., occluded objects), or replanning effectively. A key objective is to integrate a robust, closed-loop feedback system into an LLM-based code-generation framework to enhance reliability and recovery capabilities.

4. **Improve Generalization to Unseen Tasks through Structured In-Context Learning:** Previous code-generation methods often employ few-shot learning with randomly selected examples, which proves insufficient for complex task generalization. The motivation here is to develop a more effective in-context learning strategy that enables LLMs to understand underlying task intent and generalize robustly. This involves using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting combined with incrementally structured examples, mirroring a human-like curriculum learning approach.

5. **Reduce Computational Overhead and Error Accumulation:** Per-step inference, common in many hierarchical approaches, introduces noise and accumulates errors, especially in long-horizon tasks. DAHLIA aims to reduce this by enabling the agent to execute an entire sequence of primitives within a loop before receiving feedback, leveraging the concept of temporal abstraction to improve efficiency and robustness.

In essence, DAHLIA is motivated by the aspiration to build robotic systems that can perform complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in diverse, unstructured environments with minimal reliance on task-specific training data, while being robust to errors and capable of intelligent self-correction through sophisticated reasoning and feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DAHLIA's methodology is centered on a novel **dual-tunnel closed-loop framework** that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) for direct code generation, completely discarding the need for pretrained low-level policies. The framework is designed for **data-agnostic, long-horizon manipulation** through an iterative planning and reporting cycle.

**A. Prompting Strategy**

The framework begins by providing the LLM with comprehensive prior knowledge through carefully constructed prompts. This ensures the LLM understands its role and can generate directly executable Python code plans using a predefined library of skill primitives (APIs). The prompting includes:

1. **Available APIs:** A list of fundamental motion primitives and scene sensing functionalities (e.g., `get_obj_names()`, `get_obj_pos(obj)`, `put_first_on_second(obj1, obj2)`). The LLM is instructed on their functionality, arguments, and output formats. This forms the foundational "action space" for the LLM.
2. **Coordinate System:** Explicit instructions on the 3D Cartesian coordinate system and relative directions (e.g., "front: x+", "top: z+") to aid the LLM in spatial reasoning.
3. **General Rules:** Detailed text comments outlining the LLM's role (e.g., "You are writing Python code for robot manipulation"), directing it to refer to the APIs and coordinates, and guiding task execution best practices.
4. **Examples with Incremental Difficulty and Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** This is a critical innovation. Instead of randomly selected few-shot examples, DAHLIA provides a structured curriculum of examples, progressively increasing in complexity and functionality. Each example is accompanied by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which guides the LLM to break down tasks step-by-step and understand the underlying logic rather than merely mimicking surface patterns. This enhances the LLM's ability to generalize to novel and complex tasks by extracting robust reasoning strategies.

**B. Dual-Tunnel Framework: Planner and Reporter**

The core of DAHLIA is a closed-loop system comprising two main components: the Planner and the Reporter.

1. **Planner Tunnel (Code Generation for Action):**
 - **Main Planner (LLM):** An LLM (GPT-4o-mini in this work) acts as the primary planner. It interprets human instructions and, using the provided APIs and learned reasoning patterns, generates high-level executable Python code plans. This direct code generation eliminates the need for any pretrained low-level policies, reducing cumulative errors often associated with their imperfect execution.
 - **Co-Planners (Language Model Programs - LMPs):** To handle complex natural language descriptions and detailed scene information, the main planner offloads specific operations to auxiliary LLMs, termed co-planners or Language Model Programs (LMPs). These LMPs perform specialized functions like `parse_obj_name(dsc, ctxt)` (filtering object names from context), `parse_position(dsc)` (converting language descriptions to coordinates), `parse_function(dsc)` (implementing new APIs), and `parse_completion(dsc, ctxt)` (evaluating task completion). This modular design allows the main planner to focus on overarching task schemes while delegating fine-grained information processing.
 - **Temporal Abstraction:** Unlike methods that infer actions at every step, the planner leverages temporal abstraction. It generates a sequence of motion primitives that are executed in their entirety within a single loop before awaiting feedback. This significantly reduces computational cost, minimizes noise from frequent inference, and limits error accumulation over long-horizon tasks.

2. **Reporter Tunnel (VLM-based Feedback):**
 - **VLM-based Reporter:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM, also GPT-4o-mini in this work) functions as the reporter. Its role is to evaluate the task state *after* each execution loop, not at every step. It takes the original task goal and paired RGB-D observations (before and after the execution of the current code plan) as input.
 - **Structured Contextual Feedback:** The reporter uses the `parse_completion` LMP to assess task success. If the task is not yet complete or has deviated from the goal, it provides highly structured, context-aware feedback to the planner. This feedback includes: (1) a binary success signal, and (2) a detailed scene description with four key elements: the `[object]` to manipulate, its `[location]`, the `[target]` location, and the required `[action]`. For example, "A bread at (x,y,z) in the basket is not yet put into the bowl."
 - **Closed-Loop Iteration:** This structured feedback is crucial. It enables the planner to efficiently revise its plan, recover from misalignment, adapt to partial observability (e.g., detecting newly exposed objects), and replan. The process continues in an iterative closed loop until the task goal is confirmed as completed.

**C. Real-World Perception:** For real-world demonstrations, DAHLIA integrates open-world foundation models like Grounded SAM2 to acquire object identities and positions from sensor data (RGB-D camera). This allows the framework to operate in novel, unstructured environments without requiring task-specific object detection training.

In summary, DAHLIA's methodology combines advanced LLM capabilities for code generation and reasoning with a meticulously designed closed-loop feedback system and incremental few-shot learning. This holistic approach aims to achieve unprecedented generalization, robustness, and efficiency in embodied long-horizon manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across diverse benchmarks and real-world scenarios robustly demonstrate DAHLIA's superior performance, generalization capabilities, and adaptability in long-horizon manipulation tasks.

**A. LoHoRavens Benchmark (Seen and Unseen Tasks):**
- **Original Tasks (Seen):** DAHLIA consistently achieved state-of-the-art success rates (SR) on the 10 original LoHoRavens tasks (A-J). It recorded a perfect 100% SR in five of these tasks. Crucially, the closed-loop DAHLIA significantly outperformed its planner-only variant (DAHLIA GPT-4o-mini P ), demonstrating performance gains of up to 12% in some tasks due to the effectiveness of structured feedback in reducing error accumulation and enabling iterative re-planning. Baselines, including language-generation-based methods with CLIPort (e.g., GPT-4o-mini P + GPT-4o-mini R → CLIPort) and the open-loop Code-as-Policy (CaP), showed substantially lower average SRs (below 32% for the best CLIPort baseline), highlighting the limitations of pretrained low-level policies and unstructured few-shot learning. CaP's performance degraded on complex tasks due to its use of randomly ordered in-context examples.
- **Generated Tasks (Unseen):** For the 10 newly generated, more complex, and unseen tasks (G1-G10) in LoHoRavens, DAHLIA maintained its leading performance. It consistently achieved the highest SRs, further validating its robust generalization. The impact of closed-loop feedback was even more pronounced here, with improvements of +24% in task G2, +8% in G3, +8% in G5, and +12% in G7 compared to the planner-only setup. Baselines struggled immensely in these unseen scenarios, with oracle CLIPort failing entirely on most and other baselines showing minimal adaptation due to their reliance on low-level policies not fine-tuned for such novel tasks or CaP's overfitting to original examples.

**B. Scalability Across Embodiments (CALVIN and Franka Kitchen):**
- **CALVIN and Franka Kitchen Benchmarks:** DAHLIA demonstrated strong scalability and generalization across different robot embodiments (Franka arm vs. UR5e) and task setups (articulated object manipulation vs. tabletop). The dual-tunnel DAHLIA consistently outperformed its planner-only variant, reinforcing the critical role of closed-loop feedback in improving execution reliability.
- **High Success Rates:** DAHLIA achieved 100% success in many tasks, including "lift block," "open drawer," "open slide door" (CALVIN), and "microwave," "kettle," "light," "slider cabinet" (Franka Kitchen).
- **Complex Dexterous Tasks:** In tasks requiring precise coordination and fine dexterous manipulation, such as "rotate block" (+4%) and "turn on switch" (+4%) in CALVIN, and "top burner" (+6%) and "bottom burner" (+8%) in Franka Kitchen, the closed-loop feedback enabled DAHLIA to significantly surpass its planner-only counterpart. This highlighted the feedback's ability to detect and correct subtle execution errors (e.g., contact pose or rotation direction), which are crucial for success in such delicate operations.

**C. Real-World Performance:**
- **Diverse Long-Horizon Tasks:** DAHLIA was successfully demonstrated on four challenging real-world tabletop manipulation tasks: (1) picking all fruits and placing them on a plate (with distractors), (2) stacking a red head pyramid, (3) making coffee, and (4) retrieving items from a basket, including an initially occluded bread, while avoiding obstacles.
- **Robust Error Recovery and Partial Observability Handling:** The framework successfully completed the first three tasks in a single loop. In the fourth task, the system initially missed the occluded bread. However, upon its exposure, the VLM-based reporter detected it, issued a re-planning request with structured feedback ("A bread at (x,y,z) in the basket is not yet put into the bowl."), allowing the planner to update its plan and successfully complete the task. This unequivocally demonstrated DAHLIA's robust closed-loop feedback mechanism and adaptability to partial observability and unexpected real-world events.
- **Real-World Perception:** The successful integration of Grounded SAM2 for object identification and localization further showcased the framework's capability to operate in open-world, unstructured real-world scenes.

In summary, DAHLIA has established new state-of-the-art performance across a wide array of simulated and real-world long-horizon manipulation tasks. Its success is attributed to the innovative combination of Chain-of-Thought-guided incremental few-shot learning for robust planning, direct LLM-based code generation, and a highly effective structured closed-loop feedback mechanism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DAHLIA framework presents a significant advancement in embodied artificial intelligence and robotics, with profound implications for the development of more capable, adaptable, and autonomous robotic systems.

**A. Advancing Embodied AI Paradigms:**
- **Data-Agnostic Robotics:** By entirely discarding the need for large, task-specific datasets and pretrained low-level policies, DAHLIA champions a data-agnostic paradigm. This is a fundamental shift from the data-hungry nature of traditional learning-based methods (VLAs), making robotic skill acquisition and deployment far more scalable and accessible, especially for tasks where expert data is scarce or impossible to collect.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The combination of Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-guided few-shot learning with incrementally structured examples significantly boosts the LLM's ability to generalize reasoning patterns rather than merely memorizing examples. This, coupled with the structured closed-loop feedback, provides unprecedented robustness against environmental perturbations, execution errors, and partial observability, enabling reliable performance in both seen and challenging unseen scenarios.

**B. Practical Impact on Robotic Deployment:**
- **Rapid Task Development:** The ability to generate executable code directly from natural language instructions, without extensive training or fine-tuning, dramatically simplifies and accelerates the process of programming robots for new tasks. This opens avenues for non-expert users to program robots using natural language, making robotics more approachable for various industries and home environments.
- **Increased Autonomy and Fault Tolerance:** The closed-loop feedback mechanism allows robots to monitor their progress, detect failures or deviations, and autonomously recover through replanning. This self-correction capability is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unstructured real-world settings where unforeseen circumstances are common, leading to more autonomous and reliable operations.
- **Scalability Across Embodiments:** Demonstrations across LoHoRavens (UR5e), CALVIN (Franka), Franka Kitchen (Franka), and real-world Franka robot setups showcase the framework's inherent scalability and adaptability to different robot platforms and complex task domains, from simple pick-and-place to articulated object manipulation.

**C. Contribution to LLM-in-Robotics Research:**
- **Pioneering Closed-Loop Code Generation:** DAHLIA establishes a new benchmark for incorporating sophisticated closed-loop feedback mechanisms into LLM-based code-generation frameworks, an area that was previously underexplored. This demonstrates the immense potential of integrating LLM reasoning with perception and action loops for truly intelligent robotic control.
- **Effective In-Context Learning Strategies:** The paper highlights the importance of carefully designed in-context learning strategies, particularly the use of incremental difficulty and CoT prompting. This offers valuable insights for future research on how to best harness LLMs' reasoning capabilities in robotics and other complex domains.
- **Bridging High-Level Intent and Low-Level Control:** By directly generating code plans, DAHLIA effectively bridges the gap between abstract human intentions (natural language instructions) and precise low-level robot control (API calls), bypassing the intermediate and often failure-prone steps of policy learning.

**D. Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge future work in adapting to temporally non-stationary environments, improving spatial grounding in ambiguous or occluded scenes (despite current advances in real-world perception), and tighter integration with vision-language models for even more accurate open-world perception and manipulation. These future directions indicate that while DAHLIA represents a significant leap, there is still ample scope for further research to unlock the full potential of LLM-driven embodied intelligence.

In conclusion, DAHLIA stands as a landmark contribution, offering a robust, generalizable, and data-agnostic framework for long-horizon robotic manipulation. Its innovations in closed-loop code generation and structured in-context learning pave the way for a new generation of intelligent robots capable of performing complex tasks with unprecedented autonomy and reliability in diverse real-world environments. The commitment to open-sourcing the code further ensures its impact on accelerating future research in the field.
