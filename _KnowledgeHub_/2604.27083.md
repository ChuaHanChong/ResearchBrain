# Co-Evolving Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Co-Evolving Policy Distillation (CoPD) introduces a framework that enables parallel learning branches of large language models to co-evolve as both teachers and students, effectively consolidating multiple distinct capabilities. This method achieves an average accuracy of 57.71% on text and image reasoning and 58.12% on text, image, and video reasoning, surpassing conventional mixed-data training and static distillation techniques.

## Problem

- Training large language models with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) on mixed datasets often leads to "capability divergence," where optimizing for one skill degrades another due to conflicting gradients.
- Traditional two-stage On-Policy Distillation (OPD), which first trains specialized experts and then distills them, suffers from poor knowledge absorption efficiency due to significant behavioral pattern differences between converged teachers and the student.
- Existing post-training paradigms struggle to efficiently and effectively consolidate diverse capabilities into a single unified model without sacrificing individual domain performance.

## Method

- **CoPD** unifies capability exploration and consolidation by letting multiple ==parallel learning branches== simultaneously evolve as both teachers and students.
- Alternates between a ==Branch-Specific RLVR Phase==, where each branch deepens expertise, and a ==Mutual On-Policy Distillation Phase==, where branches exchange knowledge.
- Actively manages ==behavioral distance== between co-evolving teacher and student models for effective knowledge absorption.

## Results

- **CoPD** outperformed baselines: **57.71%** average on text+image reasoning and **58.12%** on text+image+video reasoning.
- Maintained high top-k token overlap (**>0.90**) and low symmetric KL divergence between branches.
- Ablations: bidirectional distillation and final parameter merging are crucial; optimal **S_RL:S_OPD ratio = 1.5:1**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective On-Policy Distillation (OPD) is critically dependent on the behavioral consistency (top-k token overlap) between teacher and student models; higher overlap correlates with better knowledge absorption.
> - Interleaving capability-specific exploration with mutual, continuous distillation throughout training maintains an optimal knowledge gap, allowing for efficient knowledge transfer while avoiding gradient conflicts.
> - By continuously managing behavioral consistency, CoPD transforms potential cross-domain performance trade-offs into mutual gains, leading to a unified model that can outperform individual specialized experts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses challenges within the post-training paradigms for large language models, specifically focusing on integrating multiple specialized capabilities into a single unified model. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has become a prominent method for post-training, demonstrating success across domains such as visual reasoning, text reasoning, and video understanding. However, directly applying RLVR to data encompassing diverse capabilities often results in a "capability trade-off" or "capability divergence," where optimizing for one skill may degrade another due to conflicting gradients.

To mitigate this, the field has largely adopted a two-stage pipeline: first, multiple specialized "expert" models are trained in isolation, each mastering a specific capability using RLVR. Subsequently, these experts are consolidated into a unified model through on-policy distillation (OPD), or Multi-teacher OPD (MOPD) when multiple teachers are involved. This approach separates capability-specific training from consolidation, thereby circumventing direct gradient conflicts.

This research identifies a limitation within this prevalent two-stage OPD pipeline. It posits that by the time distillation commences, the independently trained and converged experts (teachers) have diverged significantly in their behavioral patterns from the student model (typically the base model or a nascent policy). This behavioral gap, measured by the consistency of token-level predictions, reduces the efficiency with which the student can absorb the teacher's knowledge. The work proposes a method to bridge this gap, aiming to maintain behavioral consistency while still allowing for the acquisition of diverse expertise, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of capability consolidation. It positions itself as an advancement over both mixed-data RLVR, which suffers from divergence, and static OPD, which suffers from absorption inefficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for consolidating multiple distinct expert capabilities into a single, unified model more effectively than current paradigms. The motivation stems from two identified limitations in existing post-training approaches:

1. **Capability Divergence in Mixed-Data RLVR**: When a single model is trained using RLVR on a mixed dataset encompassing various capabilities (e.g., text, image, video reasoning), a trade-off often occurs. Distinct capabilities may require different optimization directions, leading to gradient conflicts and hindering simultaneous improvement across all domains. This results in a "seesaw effect" where gains in one area might be offset by losses in another. The paper formalizes this as a "capability divergence cost" (Φ).

2. **Poor Absorption in Static On-Policy Distillation (OPD)**: The conventional two-stage approach involves first training specialized experts independently to convergence, and then using these fixed, converged experts as teachers for distillation into a student model. The paper hypothesizes and empirically investigates that effective knowledge transfer via OPD relies on the teacher and student exhibiting similar behavioral patterns along on-policy trajectories. When experts are trained to convergence in isolation, their behaviors diverge significantly from a shared base. This large behavioral gap, as measured by low top-k token overlap, makes the teacher's supervision signal difficult for the student to absorb effectively, leading to suboptimal knowledge transfer. The paper formalizes this as low absorption efficiency, η(O_low).

Motivated by these limitations, the research aims to propose a paradigm that simultaneously:
- Avoids the gradient conflicts and capability divergence inherent in mixed-data RLVR by maintaining separate, capability-specific optimization processes.
- Ensures high absorption efficiency during distillation by actively managing the behavioral distance between teacher and student, keeping it within an "absorbable range."

The key insight is that teacher and student models should "co-evolve," with distillation occurring continuously throughout the training process rather than as a post-hoc step after expert convergence. This approach seeks to unify capability exploration (specialization) and consolidation (integration) into a single, dynamic process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Co-Evolving Policy Distillation (CoPD), unifies capability exploration and consolidation by enabling parallel training branches to co-evolve as both teachers and students. The methodology is grounded in a unified analysis of existing paradigms, a behavioral consistency hypothesis, and empirical validation through a pilot study.

**Unified Analysis and Behavioral Hypothesis:**
The paper first formalizes the utility (U_P) of different paradigms as a function of the total optimization signal (X) and an additional loss (b_P). Mixed-data RLVR is characterized by full signal delivery but incurs a capability divergence cost (Φ). Static OPD avoids this divergence but suffers from low absorption efficiency (η(O_low)) due to significant behavioral pattern gaps. CoPD aims to achieve high absorption efficiency (η(O_mod)) while avoiding divergence.

The core of CoPD is built upon the **Behavioral Consistency Hypothesis**: an OPD signal is more easily absorbed when teacher and student exhibit similar behavioral patterns. This is operationalized by defining **top-k token overlap (O_k)** as a measurable indicator.

**Pilot Study:**
Two experiments were conducted to validate the hypothesis:
1. **OPD gain vs. Teacher-Student Overlap**: Fixing a teacher and varying student behavioral overlap (via sampling temperature), the study showed a strong positive correlation (r=0.89, R^2=0.79) between post-OPD gain and top-k token overlap. This supports that η(O_k) rises with O_k when teacher and student differ, but also suggests that if O_k becomes too high (teacher and student are indistinguishable), the supervisory signal loses informativeness.
2. **Standard RLVR's Effect on Overlap**: Training independent experts via standard RLVR showed a monotonic decrease in top-k overlap and an increase in symmetric KL divergence between each expert and the shared base model (a proxy for the student). This demonstrated that static OPD, applied after expert convergence, operates in a regime of low behavioral overlap, consistent with the hypothesized low absorption efficiency.

These findings motivated CoPD's design to: (i) interleave distillation during expert training, (ii) allow teachers to continuously evolve with students, and (iii) use capability-specific training to maintain an informative knowledge gap.

**Co-Evolving Policy Distillation (CoPD) Framework:**
CoPD maintains multiple parallel learning branches, each associated with a distinct capability dataset. The training alternates between two phases:

1. **Branch-Specific RLVR Phase (Phase I)**: In this phase, each branch `k` independently performs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on its own capability data `D_k`. This process, leveraging verifiable rewards `r_k`, deepens the branch's specific expertise and, by specializing, naturally opens up a knowledge gap between the branches. This divergence ensures that subsequent distillation phases have substantive, new knowledge to transfer. The update rule is based on the GRPO objective, maximizing a clipped surrogate objective with a KL regularization term.

2. **Mutual On-Policy Distillation (OPD) Phase (Phase II)**: Following the RLVR phase, each branch `k` serves as a student and simultaneously as a teacher for the other branches. For each branch `k`, it generates on-policy rollouts on the dataset of another branch `j` (`D_j`). Branch `j` (the teacher) then provides token-level supervision to branch `k` (the student) by minimizing the KL divergence between their respective token distributions along the generated trajectories. This process is mutual and bidirectional, meaning all branches continuously exchange newly acquired knowledge. This phase "closes" the behavioral distance, ensuring that the knowledge transfer occurs when branches are sufficiently aligned to absorb information effectively. During this phase, branch-specific RLVR also continues, combining both objectives for an interleaved update.

**Alternating Training Procedure:**
CoPD organizes these two phases into `N` alternating cycles. Hyperparameters `S_RL` (RLVR steps) and `S_OPD` (OPD steps) control the rhythm, balancing exploration (divergence) and consolidation (convergence). The final unified model is obtained by simple parameter merging across the co-evolved branches, leveraging their continuous coupling to ensure parameter alignment.

**Scalability to Multiple Branches:**
While presented with two branches for clarity, CoPD scales to `K > 2` branches. For multi-branch mutual OPD, a "hub-and-spoke" topology is adopted to avoid full pairwise distillation. One designated branch acts as a shared hub, exchanging mutual OPD with each "spoke" branch. For instance, in a three-branch setting involving text, image, and video, the text reasoning branch might serve as the hub due to its foundational role in vision-language models.

**Implementation Details:**
Experiments utilize the Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct model. Training data includes Polaris-Dataset-53K (text), MMFineReason-123K (image), and a filtered collection from OneThinker, VideoChat-R1, and Video-R1 for video. Evaluation benchmarks cover multiple reasoning tasks for each modality. Baselines include single-domain experts, Mixed RLVR, and Static OPD (including MOPD for multiple teachers), with total training steps carefully matched for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CoPD across various reasoning benchmarks demonstrated its effectiveness in consolidating capabilities.

**Co-Evolution on Text and Image Reasoning (Two-Branch Setting):**
- **Performance:** CoPD achieved an overall average accuracy of 57.71%, outperforming all baselines. Specifically, CoPD showed 56.97% average accuracy on image reasoning tasks and 58.76% on text reasoning tasks.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Mixed RLVR:** This baseline exhibited capability divergence, weakening text reasoning performance compared to the dedicated Text-Expert, confirming the analytical prediction of inter-capability interference.
 - **Static OPD (V→T / T→V):** While improving over Mixed RLVR by separating expert training, static OPD only partially transferred teacher knowledge. For instance, the Text-Expert's strong text capability (57.89%) was reduced to 56.09% in the distilled model. This supports the hypothesis that a substantial portion of knowledge is not absorbed due to behavioral mismatch between converged experts and the student.
 - **CoPD's Advantage:** CoPD demonstrated simultaneous improvement in both image and text reasoning, surpassing the performance of individual domain-specific experts. This suggests that CoPD can convert cross-domain trade-offs into mutual gains.

**Scaling to Text, Image, and Video Reasoning (Three-Branch Setting):**
- **Performance:** CoPD maintained its superior performance, achieving an overall average accuracy of 58.12%, again outperforming all baselines including MOPD. It achieved 57.12% on image, 58.63% on text, and 59.21% on video reasoning.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **MOPD:** In the three-branch setting, MOPD underperformed the Video-Expert on video reasoning (58.32% vs. 58.75%), indicating challenges in absorbing knowledge from multiple, potentially divergent, static teachers.
 - **Mixed RLVR:** While achieving a relatively high video average, it came at the cost of text reasoning (55.39%), reinforcing the observed capability divergence pattern.
 - **CoPD's Advantage:** CoPD successfully consolidated all three capabilities without sacrificing individual domain performance, demonstrating its generalizability beyond pairwise capability transfer.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Bidirectional Distillation:** Removing either image-to-text (I-OPD) or text-to-image (T-OPD) distillation from CoPD resulted in performance degradation across respective domains and overall. This confirms that mutual, bidirectional OPD is necessary for effective knowledge transfer and consolidation.
- **Parameter Merging:** Even without the final parameter merging step, individual branches trained with CoPD already achieved overall performance comparable to, or better than, static OPD variants. This indicates that the co-evolutionary process itself produces well-rounded, capable branches, with merging further consolidating their complementary strengths for optimal results.

**Behavioral Pattern Consistency During Training:**
- **Top-k Token Overlap and Symmetric KL:** Analysis of training dynamics showed that in the static OPD baseline, top-k token overlap between branches monotonically decreased, and symmetric KL divergence increased by an order of magnitude. This empirically confirmed the low absorption efficiency of static OPD. In contrast, CoPD successfully maintained top-k overlap above 0.90, with temporary dips during RLVR phases followed by recovery during mutual OPD phases. Symmetric KL remained consistently low. These metrics validate CoPD's design in actively managing and maintaining a moderate, absorbable behavioral overlap between co-evolving branches.

**Effect of S_RL/S_OPD Ratio:**
- Experiments on varying the ratio of RLVR exploration steps (`S_RL`) to OPD consolidation steps (`S_OPD`) showed that CoPD consistently outperformed static OPD across all tested ratios. The optimal ratio was found to be 1.5:1, suggesting that a balanced approach, allowing sufficient branch-specific exploration to generate new knowledge while also ensuring regular, efficient knowledge transfer, is crucial for maximizing performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Co-Evolving Policy Distillation (CoPD) framework introduces a paradigm for post-training large models that addresses limitations in existing approaches for consolidating multiple capabilities. Its primary significance lies in providing a method that effectively integrates diverse expertise into a single model without incurring the capability trade-offs observed in mixed-data training or the knowledge absorption inefficiencies found in static, two-stage distillation pipelines.

**Key Impacts:**
- **Enhanced Capability Consolidation:** CoPD demonstrates the ability to create "all-in-one" models that not only combine text, image, and video reasoning but also *outperform* individual domain-specific experts. This suggests a shift from mere integration to synergistic enhancement, where cross-domain interactions lead to mutual gains rather than compromises. This could lead to more robust and versatile foundation models capable of handling complex, multi-modal reasoning tasks.
- **Addressing Behavioral Mismatch in Distillation:** By highlighting and experimentally validating the critical role of teacher-student behavioral proximity for effective knowledge transfer, CoPD offers a principled solution. Its approach of interleaving specialized exploration with mutual, continuous distillation directly tackles the problem of behavioral divergence, making the learning process more efficient and effective. This insight may inform future distillation strategies across various model architectures and tasks.
- **Novel Training Scaling Paradigm:** The concept of "model parallel training" proposed by CoPD, where multiple models co-evolve and mutually teach each other, presents a promising direction for scaling model capabilities. Instead of relying on ever-larger single models or static ensembles, this approach suggests a dynamic, collaborative learning environment that could unlock new avenues for developing more intelligent and adaptive AI systems. This could be particularly impactful for resource-constrained scenarios or for specialized AI development where knowledge transfer is crucial.
- **Contribution to Self-Taught RLVR:** CoPD is positioned as the third installment in a research series on "Self-Taught RLVR." By exploring "parallel selves mutually teaching each other," it contributes to a broader understanding of how large language models can learn and evolve autonomously. This series explores various dimensions of self-improvement, and CoPD specifically highlights the benefits of collaborative evolution among parallel instantiations of a model.

Overall, CoPD offers a novel and effective method for creating unified, multi-capable models, potentially inspiring new designs for large model training and fostering advancements in general-purpose AI development.
