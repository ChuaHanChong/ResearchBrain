# COMAL: A Convergent Meta-Algorithm for Aligning LLMs with General Preferences

> [!summary] Summary
> A meta-algorithm called COMAL enables robust Large Language Model alignment by achieving provable last-iterate convergence to the exact Nash equilibrium of the general preference alignment game. This method demonstrated win rates above 60.2% against other algorithms on Llama-3-8B and maintained general model capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing LLM alignment methods (e.g., RLHF, DPO) rely on the Bradley-Terry model, which oversimplifies and fails to capture the complexity and intransitivity of general human preferences.
- Current game-theoretic approaches for LLM alignment often suffer from instability, divergence, or only converge to average iterates or equilibria of regularized/modified games, lacking strong last-iterate guarantees for the true problem.
- Practical deployment of LLMs requires algorithms with provable last-iterate convergence to the true Nash equilibrium, which most existing game-theoretic methods do not provide.

## Method

- **COMAL** is a novel meta-algorithm inspired by the conceptual prox-method, iteratively solving a KL-regularized two-player ==zero-sum alignment game== at each step.
- It dynamically updates a 'reference policy' and adaptively adjusts KL regularization strength, which guides the policy towards the exact Nash equilibrium of the unregularized general preference game.
- The iterative subproblems within COMAL can be solved using various existing preference optimization algorithms, such as DPO, IPO, or INPO, as 'prox operators'.

## Results

- COMAL-aligned Llama-3-**8B**-Instruct models achieved win rates exceeding **60.2%** against all compared baselines, and Qwen2.5-**7B** models surpassed **56.9%** win rates, in controlled evaluations with a general preference oracle.
- The algorithm demonstrated superior training stability and consistent performance progression over many iterations, avoiding the performance degradation or plateaus seen in iterative DPO, IPO, and INPO.
- Models trained with COMAL retained comparable general capabilities on standard academic benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MMLU) to base models, and showed strong generalizability on GPT-4 based evaluations like AlpacaEval.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - COMAL is the first algorithm to provide provable last-iterate convergence to the exact Nash equilibrium of the unregularized general preference alignment game, even when subproblems are solved approximately.
> - The dynamic updating of the reference policy and adaptive regularization are critical for achieving stable and continuous performance improvement, overcoming the instability and performance degradation observed in other iterative alignment methods.
> - Framing LLM alignment as a convergent meta-algorithm allows for principled integration of existing preference optimization techniques while achieving stronger theoretical guarantees for handling complex, general human preferences.
