# Exploring Diffusion Transformer Designs via Grafting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University, in collaboration with Liquid AI and Google DeepMind, developed "grafting," a two-stage method for computationally efficient architectural editing of pretrained Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). This approach allows for exploring and materializing diverse new architectures—including swapping operators and restructuring model depth to width—using less than 2% of the original pretraining compute, while maintaining high generative quality and achieving speedups like 1.43x for high-resolution text-to-image models.

## Problem

- The prohibitively high computational and time costs of training large generative AI models (like Diffusion Transformers) from scratch severely limit the exploration of new architectural designs.
- There is a lack of systematic understanding of effective model architectures due to the economic barriers of evaluating design choices, hindering innovation in foundation models.
- Existing state-of-the-art generative models, particularly those relying on attention mechanisms, face efficiency bottlenecks (e.g., quadratic scaling) at high resolutions, necessitating more efficient architectural components.

## Method

- Introduces "==grafting==," a two-stage method for architecturally editing pretrained Diffusion Transformers, addressing the high cost of training models from scratch.
- Stage 1, Activation Distillation, initializes new operators by training them to match the activations of existing pretrained operators using minimal data (e.g., 8k samples).
- Stage 2, Lightweight Finetuning, then refines the entire grafted model using a small subset of the original training data (e.g., 10-25%) to mitigate cumulative errors and recover performance.

## Results

- The grafting procedure consistently recovered near-baseline performance (e.g., FID 2.49 for MHA, 2.54 for MLP) for DiT-XL/2 using less than **2%** of the original pretraining compute budget.
- Hybrid architectures created via grafting, replacing MHA and MLP operators with efficient alternatives, achieved strong quality-efficiency tradeoffs, with FID scores ranging from 2.38 to 2.64 on ImageNet-**1K**.
- For high-resolution text-to-image generation, grafting MHA operators in PixArt-Σ achieved a **1.43x** speedup with a minimal quality drop (<**2%** GenEval score), demonstrating practical deployment utility.
- A novel case study demonstrated converting DiT-XL/2's depth to width via grafting resulted in a 14-layer model (FID 2.77) that outperformed deeper DiT variants and pruning baselines trained from scratch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Architectural innovation in large generative models can be achieved through computationally efficient editing of pretrained models, analogous to building new software on existing code.
> - Empirical analysis reveals significant locality in many Multi-Head Attention (MHA) layers within Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), suggesting local operators are effective and efficient replacements.
> - Grafting enables fundamental architectural restructuring, such as transforming model depth into width, which can lead to improved quality compared to training shallower models from scratch and better leverage modern hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of generative AI, model architecture design, and efficient machine learning, a rapidly evolving and critical area in contemporary AI. The authors explicitly state that "model architecture design plays a central role in machine learning, alongside data, algorithms, compute, and benchmarks."

In the era of "foundation models" – large, pre-trained models like Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) – the cost of training new architectures from scratch has become prohibitively high. This economic barrier severely limits the pace of architectural innovation and understanding of "what works and what doesn't" in complex model designs. Current practices often involve costly trial-and-error pretraining or rely on methods like Neural Architecture Search (NAS), which also typically require extensive training cycles.

The paper directly addresses this challenge by proposing "architectural editing of pretrained models" as an alternative to "training from scratch." This concept is inspired by software development, where new features are built on existing code rather than rewriting entire systems. This positions "grafting" as a novel paradigm for architectural exploration, distinct from traditional NAS or manual design-and-train loops.

Specifically, the work focuses on Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), which have become state-of-the-art for image and video generation. While prior work has explored architectural modifications for diffusion models (e.g., using linear attention variants like DiffuSSM, DiS, Zigma, DiM, DIG, or incorporating Mixture-of-Experts like DiT-MoE), these efforts still largely involve training from scratch or extensive distillation processes. Similarly, in the broader transformer landscape, efforts like linearizing LLMs (e.g., LOLCATS, Mamba in Llama, Transformers to SSMs) have focused on replacing specific operators, primarily attention. Some work, like FFN Fusion, explored parallelizing LLM blocks.

"Grafting" distinguishes itself by offering a more *general and comprehensive* approach to architectural editing. It goes beyond single-operator replacement by enabling modifications of multiple operator types (MHA and MLP), exploring diverse alternatives (convolutions, linear attention, variable MLPs), and even *restructuring* architectures (e.g., converting sequential blocks to parallel ones). This holistic view and the emphasis on *small compute budgets* for architectural exploration represent a significant contribution to making advanced architecture research more accessible and efficient. The paper provides a systematic "testbed" for this exploration, offering a methodological framework for future research in architectural editing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **enable the systematic study and development of new model architectures for generative models, specifically Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), without incurring the prohibitive costs of training models from scratch.**

The core motivations driving this objective are:
1. **High Cost of Architectural Exploration:** Designing effective model architectures, which involves crucial decisions about operator types (e.g., attention, convolution) and configurations (e.g., depth, width), currently requires expensive and time-consuming pretraining for evaluation. This severely limits the depth and breadth of architectural investigations, especially in the context of large "foundation models."
2. **Lack of Insight into Architecture Design:** Due to the aforementioned cost, obtaining clear insights into "what works and what doesn't" in model architecture remains challenging. Researchers often rely on incremental modifications or intuition.
3. **Leveraging Pretrained Models:** Inspired by how new software is built on existing codebases, the authors question whether pretrained models can serve as "scaffolds" for studying new architectures, allowing for iterative improvements and experiments under constrained compute budgets.
4. **Addressing Specific Generative Model Challenges:** The focus on DiTs highlights the need for efficient architectures in high-stakes generative tasks like image and video generation, where quadratic scaling of attention can be a bottleneck, especially at high resolutions.
5. **Desire for Efficiency:** The paper specifically aims to construct "efficient hybrid designs" by replacing existing operators with more efficient alternatives (e.g., linear attention, local convolutions) to reduce computational costs (FLOPs) while preserving or minimally impacting model quality. It also explores replacements that increase FLOPs to broaden the design space.
6. **Enabling Architectural Restructuring:** Beyond simple operator replacement, a key motivation is to explore more fundamental architectural changes, such as trading model depth for width, to leverage modern hardware capabilities that favor parallelism.

In essence, the authors seek to democratize architectural research by providing a computationally efficient framework for exploring and validating novel designs, thereby accelerating progress in the field of generative AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology is **grafting**, a two-stage approach for architectural editing of pretrained Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). The overall approach is designed to materialize new architectures under small compute budgets, addressing the high cost of training models from scratch.

#### 3.1. The Grafting Procedure (Two-Stage Approach)

Grafting focuses on *operator replacement* as the primary strategy, viewing other architectural edits (adding, removing) as special cases.
- **Stage 1: Activation Distillation (Operator Initialization):**
 - **Objective:** To initialize a *new* operator (g_l_theta) so that its functionality approximates that of an *original* pretrained operator (f_l_phi) at a specific layer `l`.
 - **Mechanism:** This is framed as a regression task. The new operator is trained to predict the activations of the pretrained operator's output given its input. The loss function is a regression objective (L2, L1, or Huber loss), which minimizes the difference between the new operator's output and the pretrained operator's output.
 - **Data Requirement:** This stage requires a small amount of data (as few as 8k samples), significantly less than full model pretraining.
 - **Parallelization:** Individual operators can be distilled in parallel, making this stage computationally efficient.
- **Stage 2: Lightweight Finetuning (Error Mitigation):**
 - **Objective:** To mitigate the cumulative error propagation that occurs when multiple new operators are integrated into the computational graph. Initialized operators, while good individually, might not perfectly align in an end-to-end system.
 - **Mechanism:** The entire grafted model undergoes a lightweight end-to-end finetuning process using the standard diffusion model objective (Equation 1).
 - **Data Requirement:** This stage uses a limited subset of the original training data (e.g., 10-25%), which is still a substantial reduction compared to full pretraining.

#### 3.2. Self-Grafting Baseline

Before experimenting with new architectural designs, the authors establish a "self-grafting" baseline. This involves replacing existing MHA and MLP operators in DiT-XL/2 with *identical* randomly initialized counterparts. This crucial control experiment serves three purposes:
1. **Procedure Validation:** To assess the effectiveness of the grafting procedure itself, demonstrating its ability to recover performance even when structural identity is maintained but weights are re-initialized.
2. **Performance Baseline:** To provide a reference point for comparing the performance of grafted *new* architectures against the procedure's inherent recovery capability.
3. **Factor Analysis:** To study the impact of factors like data scale for finetuning and the choice of regression objectives (L1, L2, Huber) on recovery performance.

#### 3.3. Activation Behavior and Locality Analysis

To inform the selection of replacement operators, the authors perform an empirical analysis of DiT-XL/2's internal activations:
- **Activation Variance:** They analyze the range and distribution of activation values for MHA and MLP operators across all layers. They find large variances, especially in deeper layers, which guides the selection of robust regression objectives (e.g., L1 for MHA, L2 for MLP due to parameter count differences).
- **Attention Locality (Band-k Metric):** For MHA, a key analysis involves quantifying the "locality" of attention. They define a metric L_k (Equation 3) that measures the proportion of attention mass within a specified band size `k`. This analysis reveals that many MHA layers in DiT-XL/2 predominantly model local interactions, motivating the exploration of local operators (like convolutions or sliding window attention) as efficient replacements.

#### 3.4. Testbed Design and New Operators

A structured testbed based on DiT-XL/2 is constructed to systematically evaluate grafting schemes along four design axes:
1. **Operator Type to Replace:** Multi-Head Attention (MHA) or Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), as they account for most FLOPs.
2. **Replacement Operator Type:**
 - **For MHA:** Existing efficient operators like Hyena-SE (gated convolution), Sliding Window Attention (SWA), and Mamba-2 (linear attention).
 - **New Contributions:** Introduction of **Hyena-X** and **Hyena-Y**, novel efficient gated convolution operators designed as drop-in replacements for MHA, inspired by locality analysis.
 - **For MLP:** Variable expansion ratio MLPs (r=3, 6) and a convolutional variant (Hyena-X adapted for channels).
3. **Layer Selection Strategy:** "Full" (replace all operators) or "Interleaved" (replace in a repeating pattern), inspired by striped transformer designs.
4. **Replacement Ratio:** Percentage of operators replaced (e.g., 50%, 75%, 100%).

#### 3.5. Experiment Setup and Compute Budget

The experimental setup is standardized across hybrid experiments:
- **Stage 1:** 8K ImageNet-1K samples, 200 epochs, 64 batch size, 1e-4 LR. Each operator takes <30 mins on a single H100 GPU.
- **Stage 2:** 10% of ImageNet-1K training data (128k samples), 50K steps, 256 batch size, 1e-4 LR. Experiments typically complete in <10 hours on 8xH100 GPUs, staying within <2% of DiT-XL/2's pretraining compute.
- **Text-to-Image:** For PixArt-Σ, a small synthetic dataset (12k image-text pairs) and LoRA-based finetuning are used to manage high memory demands.

This rigorous methodology allows for systematic exploration of architectural modifications, with a strong emphasis on computational efficiency throughout the process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across different experimental settings, demonstrating the effectiveness and versatility of the grafting approach:

#### 4.1. Validation of Grafting: Self-Grafting Baseline

- **High-Quality Initialization (Stage 1):** The choice of regression objective significantly impacts performance. For MHA, L1 loss yielded the best FID (2.51), while for MLPs, L2 loss performed best (2.33). This highlights the need for tailored, activation-aware strategies.
- **Near-Baseline Performance with Limited Data (Stage 2):** Full self-grafting (replacing all 28 MHA or MLP operators with randomly initialized *identical* ones) followed by lightweight finetuning with only 10% of the training data achieved near-baseline performance. For MHA, FID improved from >200 to 2.49, and for MLP, from >300 to 2.54 (DiT-XL/2 baseline FID: 2.27). This strongly validates the grafting procedure's ability to recover quality under modest data and compute budgets.

#### 4.2. Hybrid Architectures for Class-Conditional Image Generation (ImageNet-1K)

- **MHA Locality and Effective Replacements:** The analysis showed that 15 out of 28 MHA layers in DiT-XL/2 exhibit significant locality (over 50% attention mass within a band of k=32). This motivated using local operators.
- **Strong Quality-Efficiency Tradeoffs:** Grafting MHA operators with efficient alternatives (Hyena-SE, Hyena-X/Y, SWA, Mamba-2) yielded good quality. Several interleaved hybrid designs achieved FID scores between 2.38 and 2.64, very close to the DiT-XL/2 baseline of 2.27.
- **Effectiveness of Interleaved Grafting:** At 50% interleaved replacement, operators with smaller receptive fields (SWA, Hyena-X/Y with K=4 or w=4) achieved FID scores within 0.5 of the baseline, aligning with the locality analysis. Interleaved replacement generally outperformed other heuristics (e.g., top-local, low-local, deep layers).
- **MLP Grafting Robustness:** Replacing MLP operators with variable expansion ratios (r=3, r=6) was highly effective, even at 100% replacement. The r=6 variant achieved FID=2.42, very close to the baseline and even outperforming the r=3 variant (FID=2.66). This suggests MLP width is a robust dimension for architectural editing.
- **Compute Efficiency:** All these experiments were conducted using less than 2% of the pretraining compute budget, demonstrating significant resource savings.

#### 4.3. Grafting for High-Resolution Text-to-Image Generation (PixArt-Σ)

- **Scalability to Real-World Settings:** Grafting was successfully applied to PixArt-Σ, a high-resolution (2048x2048) text-to-image model, which presents challenges due to long sequence lengths (16,384 tokens), multimodal conditioning, and lack of extensive training data.
- **Meaningful Speedup with Minimal Quality Drop:** By replacing MHA operators (which account for over 62% of latency) with Hyena-X, the grafted PixArt-Σ achieved a **1.43x speedup** in wall-clock time. The GenEval score (a text-to-image alignment metric) saw a minimal drop from 49.75 to 47.78 (<2%). This highlights grafting's practical utility for deploying efficient generative models.
- **Data Efficiency:** This achievement was realized using a small, uncurated synthetic dataset of only 12k image-text pairs for grafting.

#### 4.4. Case Study: Converting Model Depth to Width via Grafting

- **Architectural Restructuring Feasibility:** This novel case study demonstrated that grafting can enable fundamental architectural restructuring. DiT-XL/2 was rewired by parallelizing every pair of sequential transformer blocks, effectively reducing its depth by half (28 layers to 14 layers), with a modest 6% increase in parameters.
- **Improved Quality for Reduced Depth:** The 14-layer grafted model achieved an FID of 2.77. This significantly **outperformed other DiT variants of comparable or even greater depth trained from scratch** (e.g., DiT-L/2 with 24 layers, FID 3.73; U-ViT-L with 21 layers, FID 3.44). It also surpassed pruning baselines like TinyDiT-D14 (FID 3.92) and TinyDiT-D14 w/ MKD (FID 2.86).
- **Leveraging Parallelism:** This demonstrates a practical method to trade model depth for width, leveraging modern GPU architectures that favor parallel computation, leading to potential speedups.

In summary, the main findings consistently underscore that grafting is a highly effective, computationally lightweight, and versatile approach for exploring and materializing a wide range of new Diffusion Transformer architectures, from operator replacement for efficiency to fundamental architectural restructuring, all while preserving high generative quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Impact and Significance

The "grafting" methodology presented in this paper carries significant implications for the future of AI model development, particularly in the domain of large foundation models.

1. **Democratization of Architectural Research:** The most impactful contribution of grafting is its ability to **drastically reduce the computational and time costs associated with exploring new model architectures.** By enabling architectural investigations with less than 2% of pretraining compute and in hours rather than weeks or months, grafting makes advanced architectural research accessible to a much broader community, including smaller research groups and practitioners with limited resources. This could accelerate the pace of innovation in model design.

2. **New Paradigm for Architecture Design:** Grafting shifts the paradigm from "designing and training from scratch" to "architectural editing of pretrained models." This is analogous to the software engineering principle of building on existing code, which is far more efficient than rewriting from first principles. This new methodology provides a powerful tool for iterating on and refining existing high-performing models.

3. **Enhanced Model Efficiency and Deployment:** The paper demonstrates concrete efficiency gains, such as the 1.43x speedup for PixArt-Σ in high-resolution text-to-image generation, with minimal quality degradation. This has direct implications for deploying generative AI models in real-world applications where latency and computational cost are critical factors (e.g., interactive image editing, real-time video generation). The ability to swap out inefficient components for leaner alternatives makes these models more practical and environmentally friendly.

4. **Enabling Fundamental Architectural Restructuring:** The case study on converting model depth to width is a groundbreaking demonstration. It shows that grafting can facilitate not just component-level replacements but also fundamental structural changes at the block level. This capability allows researchers to adapt architectures to specific hardware constraints (e.g., favoring parallelism on modern GPUs) or to explore novel structural hypotheses (e.g., is width truly more effective than depth beyond a certain point for diffusion models?) in a computationally feasible manner.

5. **Accelerating Adaptation to New Modalities and Contexts:** The authors suggest applications such as adapting models from low-resolution to high-resolution, extending capabilities from short-form to long-form video, or improving user experience in interactive applications. Grafting offers a direct path to extend the utility of existing pretrained models to new, more demanding scenarios without retraining them from scratch for each specific use case.

6. **Improved Understanding of Model Mechanics:** The activation behavior and attention locality analyses, which inform the grafting choices, contribute to a deeper understanding of how DiTs function internally. This mechanistic insight can guide not only grafting decisions but also future "from scratch" architectural designs.

In conclusion, "Grafting" provides a practical, efficient, and versatile framework for architectural innovation in the era of large foundation models. By lowering the barriers to experimentation, it stands to significantly impact how new, more efficient, and specialized generative AI models are conceived, developed, and deployed.

#### 5.2. Limitations

The authors are transparent about the limitations of their current work and outline several promising avenues for future research:

- **Scope of Architectural Editing:** The current work primarily focuses on replacing self-attention and MLP operators in Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). Other architectural components, such as normalization layers and activation functions, are not explored but represent potential areas for future architectural editing.
- **Model Family Specificity:** The experiments are largely confined to DiTs. Generalizing the grafting approach to other model families, such as autoregressive language models, is an open question and a direction for future research.
- **VAE Components:** The diffusion models used are latent diffusion models, meaning they rely on a separate Variational Autoencoder (VAE). Grafting components within these VAEs remains an unexplored area.
- **Synthetic Data Quality:** For the PixArt-Σ text-to-image grafting, synthetic data was used due to the lack of publicly available training data. The authors acknowledge that this synthetic data can be of low quality and may propagate artifacts and biases into the grafted models, impacting overall quality.
- **Performance Relative to Training from Scratch:** A crucial open question is whether architectures that perform well under grafting would also perform well if trained from scratch. Grafting optimizes for adapting a *pretrained* model, which might not always align with optimal performance from a full training run. This is a common challenge for transfer learning and adaptation methods.
- **Dependency on Pretrained Models:** Grafting inherently requires access to a high-quality pretrained model as a starting point. It is not a method for *de novo* architecture discovery without any prior knowledge.

#### 5.3. Applications and Future Work

- **Efficiency in Diverse Applications:** The authors envision grafting being valuable in various efficiency-critical applications. This includes adapting models from low-resolution to high-resolution settings, extending capabilities from short-form video understanding/generation to long-form video, or improving user experience in interactive applications like image editing where even small speedups are highly valued.
- **Broader Architectural Exploration:** Future work could explore a wider range of operator types for replacement, including different forms of convolutions, attention mechanisms, or novel architectural primitives.
- **Generalization to Other Modalities/Tasks:** Applying grafting to other generative tasks (e.g., audio, 3D generation) or even discriminative tasks with large foundation models could be explored.
- **Advanced Grafting Strategies:** Investigating more sophisticated layer selection strategies beyond full or interleaved patterns, potentially guided by automated methods or more fine-grained architectural analysis, could yield further improvements.
- **Optimized Implementations:** For the depth-to-width conversion, the authors note that speedup values might vary with batch sizes due to parameter count. Future work will focus on hardware-aware and optimized implementations to achieve consistent speedup across a wider range of scenarios.
- **Addressing Synthetic Data Quality:** Improving synthetic data generation and curation techniques for grafting, perhaps through more robust filtering or active learning, could mitigate the observed quality degradation in setups like PixArt-Σ.
- **Theoretical Understanding:** Deeper theoretical analysis of why grafting works, particularly the interaction between activation distillation and lightweight finetuning, could lead to even more effective and robust methods.

The paper concludes by expressing hope that their testbed, insights, and results will encourage the community to actively explore new architecture designs using this efficient paradigm. The public release of code and models further supports this goal.
