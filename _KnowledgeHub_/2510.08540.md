# MM-HELIX: Boosting Multimodal Long-Chain Reflective Reasoning with Holistic Platform and Adaptive Hybrid Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The MM-HELIX framework evaluates and enhances multimodal large language models' long-chain reflective reasoning through a novel benchmark and an adaptive hybrid policy optimization strategy. This approach achieved an 18.6% accuracy improvement on its own benchmark and a 5.7% gain in generalization across other mathematical and logic tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) critically lack the capacity for long-chain reflective reasoning, which involves iterative trial, error, and correction.
- Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily focus on text-only or puzzle-like challenges, failing to adequately assess end-to-end reflective reasoning in visually-rich, multimodal contexts.
- Standard MLLM training paradigms like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often lead to catastrophic forgetting, while on-policy reinforcement learning struggles with sparse rewards in complex, multi-step tasks.

## Method

- Developed **MM-HELIX**, a novel ==multimodal benchmark== comprising 42 challenging tasks across Algorithms, Graphs, Puzzles, and Games, requiring visual observation, rule comprehension, and sequential reflective thought.
- Created MM-HELIX-100K, a large-scale instruction-tuning dataset of 100,000 high-quality reasoning trajectories with explicit reflection, using a Step-Elicited Response Generation (SERG) pipeline.
- Introduced Adaptive Hybrid Policy Optimization (AHPO), a training algorithm that dynamically integrates off-policy expert guidance from MM-HELIX-100K with on-policy exploration to learn and generalize reflective reasoning skills.

## Results

- Evaluations on MM-HELIX revealed that 23 leading MLLMs struggle with reflective reasoning, with GPT-5 achieving **58.1%** and the best open-source model reaching only **33.3%** accuracy.
- AHPO boosted the Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** baseline model's accuracy by +**18.6%** on the MM-HELIX benchmark, outperforming larger state-of-the-art models and achieving **24.9%**.
- AHPO demonstrated robust generalization, achieving an average performance gain of +**5.7%** across various out-of-domain mathematics and logic tasks (e.g., MathVision, LogicVista), while SFT alone resulted in a -**6.6%** degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leading MLLMs, including GPT-5, exhibit a profound deficit in long-chain reflective reasoning, with top proprietary models achieving only up to 58.1% accuracy on MM-HELIX and open-source models performing substantially worse.
> - A significant modality gap exists, where performance on text-only problem representations is dramatically higher than on multimodal equivalents, indicating challenges in integrating and reasoning over visual and linguistic inputs.
> - Dynamically adapting the influence of offline expert data based on a model's real-time performance, as implemented in AHPO, is crucial for effectively cultivating complex, transferable reflective reasoning skills without experiencing catastrophic forgetting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "MM-HELIX" addresses a critical and increasingly recognized limitation within the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): their capacity for **long-chain reflective reasoning** in complex, interactive, and visually rich environments.

**Current MLLM Capabilities and Limitations:** MLLMs have demonstrated impressive progress across various tasks, from fundamental perception (e.g., recognition) to more sophisticated reasoning (e.g., mathematics). However, the authors argue that most existing MLLMs are designed for single-pass output generation, inherently lacking mechanisms for self-correction, iterative refinement, or backtracking—a cornerstone of human intelligence when solving intricate problems. This gap prevents them from effectively tackling "complex real-world problems" that demand sustained, multi-step thought processes.

**Critique of Existing Benchmarks:** The paper positions itself in contrast to prior work that has explored elements of long-chain reasoning. It specifically mentions benchmarks like Enigmata, VGRP-Bench, and Code2Logic. While these works contribute to reasoning evaluation, the authors contend they primarily focus on text-only problems or puzzle-like challenges constrained to multiple-choice or fill-in-the-blank formats. This focus, according to the authors, fails to adequately assess the *end-to-end reflective reasoning capabilities* of MLLMs in rich multimodal contexts. The "MM-HELIX" benchmark is explicitly designed to fill this void by demanding iterative thinking and backtracking within visually-grounded, multi-step tasks.

**Advancements in Reasoning and Learning Paradigms:** The work builds upon recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) that have incorporated reflective and multi-step thinking strategies (e.g., Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT)). However, it extends these concepts to the multimodal domain, which introduces significant challenges in processing and interpreting visual information iteratively. Furthermore, the paper delves into advanced reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, critiquing the limitations of standard paradigms like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) (which can lead to catastrophic forgetting) and on-policy RL (which struggles with sparse rewards in complex tasks). It aims to provide a more robust and adaptive learning strategy, Adaptive Hybrid Policy Optimization (AHPO), that dynamically combines offline expert guidance with online exploration, differentiating it from other RL methods like LUFFY, GRPO, and DAPO.

In essence, this research pushes the frontier of MLLM capabilities beyond superficial reasoning to encompass deeper, more human-like cognitive processes involving iterative reflection, self-correction, and generalization across diverse multimodal challenges. It provides both a diagnostic tool (MM-HELIX benchmark) and a prescriptive solution (SERG data generation and AHPO training strategy) to advance this crucial area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the MM-HELIX project stems from a fundamental observation: while Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) excel in many reasoning tasks, they critically lack the capacity for **long-chain reflective reasoning**—an iterative process involving trial, error, and correction that is essential for human-like intelligence and tackling complex real-world problems. The authors explicitly state that this capacity remains "largely underexplored" and "underevaluated."

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Empirically Investigate and Evaluate MLLM Reflective Capabilities:** To conduct a thorough investigation into the current state of MLLMs regarding their ability to perform long-chain reflective reasoning. This involves identifying whether existing models can successfully engage in iterative thinking and backtracking within complex tasks.
2. **Construct a Comprehensive Multimodal Benchmark (MM-HELIX):** To develop a novel and challenging benchmark specifically designed to assess these reflective capabilities in an end-to-end manner. This benchmark aims to overcome the limitations of existing evaluations by incorporating multimodal inputs, long chains of reasoning, and requiring genuine reflection and backtracking.
3. **Develop High-Quality Training Data for Reflective Reasoning:** Recognizing the significant performance deficits observed in current MLLMs, an objective is to generate large-scale, high-quality post-training data (MM-HELIX-100K) that embodies reflective reasoning traces, suitable for instruction-tuning.
4. **Propose an Effective Training Strategy (AHPO) to Boost Reflection:** To devise a novel and robust training algorithm, Adaptive Hybrid Policy Optimization (AHPO), that can effectively learn from the generated reflective data while overcoming challenges inherent in standard reinforcement learning, such as sparse rewards and catastrophic forgetting.
5. **Demonstrate Generalization of Enhanced Reflective Skills:** A crucial objective is to show that the reflective reasoning capabilities cultivated through their proposed methodology are not only effective on the in-domain MM-HELIX tasks but can also successfully generalize to broader, out-of-domain reasoning tasks, such as general mathematics and logic problems.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between current MLLM reasoning abilities and the human capacity for adaptive, iterative problem-solving. The objectives collectively aim to diagnose this deficiency, provide appropriate evaluation tools, and offer a practical solution for instilling and generalizing advanced reflective reasoning in MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MM-HELIX framework addresses the challenge of multimodal reflective reasoning through a three-pronged approach: a novel benchmark, a data generation pipeline, and an adaptive training strategy.

#### 3.1. MM-HELIX: Benchmarking Multimodal Reflective End-to-End Reasoning

The authors developed MM-HELIX, a benchmark grounded in four core principles: **Multimodal, Long-Chain Reasoning, Reflection, and End-to-End**.

- **Task Curation:** It comprises 42 challenging tasks curated from public web resources and academic datasets, categorized into four domains: **Algorithms, Graphs, Puzzles, and Games**. Each task requires visual observation, rule comprehension, and sequential thought involving reflection and backtracking.
- **Procedural Generation Framework:** To ensure scalability, diversity, and controlled difficulty, a framework with three components was built:
 - **Instance Generator:** Programmatically creates multimodal questions (Question Description, Visual Input, Initial State) with tunable parameters for varying difficulty.
 - **Deterministic Solver:** Analyzes initial states, determines solvability, and produces ground-truth solutions.
 - **Automated Verifier:** Evaluates model outputs. For simple answers, it uses exact-match; for complex, multi-step solutions, it simulates the proposed actions from the Initial State to confirm validity and serves as a reward oracle for reinforcement learning.
- **Hierarchical Difficulty System:** Task difficulty is controlled by adjusting task-specific parameters, primarily the number of reasoning steps required. This creates five distinct difficulty levels (Level 1: very easy to Level 5: very hard), allowing for fine-grained capability assessment.
- **Dataset Size:** The benchmark consists of 1,260 unique instances, balanced across all 42 tasks and 5 difficulty levels (30 instances per task, 6 per difficulty level).

#### 3.2. MM-HELIX-100K: Guiding Multimodal Reflective Reasoning

To address the performance gap observed in MLLMs, the authors created MM-HELIX-100K, a large-scale instruction-tuning dataset of 100,000 high-quality reasoning trajectories with explicit reflection.

- **Step-Elicited Response Generation (SERG) Pipeline:** This hybrid and efficient data generation pipeline was developed to overcome the inefficiency and low quality of conventional LLM-based CoT generation.
 - **Rule-Based CoT Constructor:** Programmatically generates a "skeletal" reasoning path for 150,000 problem instances. This involves embedding anchors (critical intermediate states/calculations) and connecting them with template-based natural language.
 - **LLM-based Enhancement:** A powerful LLM (Qwen3-235B) refines these skeletal traces into more natural, comprehensive, and human-like reasoning processes, explicitly incorporating reflective steps.
 - **Automated Verifier:** Ensures the integrity of the final dataset by validating each generated trajectory's answer, filtering out errors introduced during the LLM enhancement phase.

#### 3.3. AHPO: Adaptive Hybrid Algorithm for Generalizing Reflection

To effectively train models and overcome the limitations of standard RL (sparse rewards) and SFT (catastrophic forgetting), the authors introduce Adaptive Hybrid Policy Optimization (AHPO).

- **Unified Training Framework:** AHPO integrates off-policy (expert data) and on-policy (exploration) learning into a single stage, rather than a sequential pipeline.
- **Adaptive Mechanism:** A key feature is an adaptive coefficient (ξ) that dynamically modulates the influence of the offline expert data based on the model's real-time performance.
 - When the policy's on-policy success rate is below a predefined threshold (ˆR), ξ is set to 1, providing dense guidance from expert data (negative log-likelihood loss on expert data).
 - As the policy improves and consistently achieves high rewards (success rate above ˆR), ξ is set to 0, deactivating off-policy supervision and encouraging the policy to refine its skills through pure on-policy exploration (clipped policy gradient objective, similar to GRPO).
- **Objective Function:** The AHPO loss function is a combination of the off-policy loss and the on-policy GRPO-style objective, weighted by ξ. The KL divergence term from original GRPO is omitted to reduce constraints and computational overhead.
- **Expert Data:** High-quality CoT trajectories from the MM-HELIX-100K dataset serve as the off-policy expert data.
- **Benefits:** This adaptive strategy aims to leverage expert knowledge during initial, struggling phases while allowing for independent exploration once proficiency is achieved, preventing over-reliance on the expert distribution and fostering generalization.

This comprehensive methodological approach ensures that MM-HELIX not only accurately evaluates reflective reasoning but also provides effective tools and strategies for training MLLMs to acquire and generalize these complex cognitive abilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results, demonstrating both the current limitations of MLLMs in reflective reasoning and the effectiveness of the proposed MM-HELIX framework and AHPO training strategy.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Results on MM-HELIX Benchmark

- **Profound Deficit in Reflective Reasoning:** An extensive evaluation of 23 leading MLLMs (proprietary and open-source) on MM-HELIX revealed a significant performance gap. Even the most advanced proprietary model, GPT-5, achieved only 58.1% accuracy, with no other model surpassing 50%. Open-source models performed even worse, with the best, Intern-S1-241B, reaching only 33.3%. This confirms that current MLLMs struggle profoundly with complex, long-chain iterative and reflective tasks.
- **Impact of "Thinking" Capabilities:** Models explicitly designed with reflective reasoning or "thinking" capabilities generally achieved higher scores than their non-reflective counterparts, underscoring the importance of this targeted capability. For example, InternVL-3-78B, strong on general benchmarks, scored a mere 9.9% on MM-HELIX, highlighting the benchmark's ability to isolate this specific skill.
- **Task Type Performance Variation:** MLLMs showed the highest proficiency in "Algorithm" tasks, which often involve structured mathematical computation. Performance was moderate on "Graph" and "Puzzle" tasks, while the weakest results were observed in "Game" tasks. This indicates that MLLMs are better at executing defined calculations but lack robustness in adhering to complex rules and performing iterative state-tracking required by game environments.
- **Significant Modality Gap:** Presenting problems in a text-only format dramatically improved performance. GPT-5's accuracy, for instance, surged from 58.1% on multimodal tasks to 84.5% on text-only equivalents. This highlights a persistent challenge in integrating and reasoning over visual and linguistic inputs.

#### 4.2. Main Results of AHPO

- **Superior In-Domain Performance:** When applied to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline model, AHPO achieved an accuracy of 24.9% on the MM-HELIX benchmark, representing a substantial **+18.6% improvement** over the baseline. This performance even surpassed that of significantly larger, state-of-the-art models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B and GLM-4.5V-106B.
- **Remarkable Generalization:** AHPO demonstrated strong generalization of learned reflective reasoning capabilities. When trained on a mixed dataset (MM-HELIX-100K and general mathematics RL dataset MMK12, which lacks explicit CoT traces), the model achieved an average performance gain of **+5.7%** across various general mathematics and logic tasks (MathVision, MathVerse-V, LogicVista, WeMath). This validates that AHPO fosters intrinsic reasoning skills that are transferable to new domains.
- **Limitations of Baselines:**
 - **GRPO (RL-only):** Showed negligible improvement on both task sets due to sparse reward signals.
 - **SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning):** While boosting in-domain performance (23.8%), it led to "catastrophic forgetting" and a substantial performance degradation (-6.6%) on general reasoning tasks.
 - **SFT+GRPO (Sequential):** Failed to recover the generalization deficit, indicating poor transferability of fine-tuned reflective skills.
 - **LUFFY (Hybrid):** Showed minor gains but was significantly less effective than AHPO in both performance and generalization.

#### 4.3. Comparison of Generation Pipeline (SERG)

- **Efficiency:** The Step-Elicited Response Generation (SERG) pipeline significantly reduced data generation time by **90%** compared to direct rollouts from a powerful LLM (Qwen3-235B) while producing more concise and structured reasoning traces.
- **Quality:** A model fine-tuned on SERG-generated data outperformed a model trained on purely rule-based CoT data by **4.9%**, confirming the higher quality and utility of SERG-generated training data.

These results collectively highlight that MM-HELIX effectively diagnoses current MLLM shortcomings, and the combination of the SERG pipeline for data generation and AHPO for adaptive training provides a robust solution for cultivating and generalizing reflective reasoning skills in MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MM-HELIX research makes several significant contributions and has the potential for substantial impact on the development and application of Multimodal Large Language Models.

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in MLLM Capabilities:** The most immediate significance is the identification and comprehensive evaluation of MLLMs' "profound deficit" in long-chain reflective reasoning. This highlights a crucial area where current models fall short of human-like intelligence, particularly for complex problem-solving. By pinpointing this limitation, the work directs future research efforts toward truly enabling advanced cognitive abilities in AI.
2. **Establishing a Robust Evaluation Benchmark:** MM-HELIX provides a meticulously designed, multimodal, long-chain, reflective, and end-to-end benchmark. This benchmark is invaluable for systematically assessing MLLMs, allowing researchers to measure progress, identify weaknesses, and compare models more effectively in this challenging domain. Its hierarchical difficulty system further enables fine-grained analysis of model capabilities.
3. **Providing High-Quality, Scalable Training Data:** The development of the MM-HELIX-100K dataset via the Step-Elicited Response Generation (SERG) pipeline offers a scalable and efficient method for creating high-quality, reflective Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces. This dataset is a critical resource for instruction-tuning MLLMs, overcoming the scarcity of such complex reasoning data. The efficiency and quality of SERG are themselves significant methodological advancements.
4. **Introducing an Innovative and Effective Training Strategy:** Adaptive Hybrid Policy Optimization (AHPO) is a novel contribution that effectively tackles common challenges in MLLM training—sparse rewards in complex RL environments and catastrophic forgetting from SFT. By dynamically integrating offline expert guidance with on-policy exploration, AHPO provides a robust paradigm for learning complex, transferable skills. Its demonstrated superior performance and generalization capacity suggest it could become a foundational method for training MLLMs on intricate tasks.
5. **Demonstrating Transferability of Reflective Skills:** The finding that enhanced reflective reasoning abilities generalize to diverse out-of-domain tasks (mathematics and logic) is particularly impactful. This suggests that the skills acquired are not merely memorized solutions but represent a deeper, intrinsic understanding of iterative problem-solving, paving the way for more versatile and truly intelligent MLLM agents.
6. **Paving the Way for More Capable AI:** By boosting MLLMs' capacity for reflection and self-correction, this work moves closer to developing AI systems that can independently reason through and solve complex, real-world problems that require iterative thinking, planning, and adaptation. This has broad implications for applications in scientific discovery, complex decision-making, autonomous systems, and advanced human-AI collaboration, where iterative refinement is often indispensable.

In conclusion, MM-HELIX provides a comprehensive solution for advancing multimodal reflective reasoning in MLLMs, offering both the tools to diagnose current limitations and effective strategies to overcome them. Its impact is likely to be profound, shaping the next generation of more intelligent and adaptable AI systems.
