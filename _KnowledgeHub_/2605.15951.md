# From Failure to Feedback: Group Revision Unlocks Hard Cases in Object-Level Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> A group-revision optimization paradigm enables Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to learn from initially failed object-level grounding attempts by generating dense feedback through a revision and consolidation process. This approach achieved average improvements of 2.16% on reasoning segmentation, 2.22% on referring segmentation, and 4.27% on object counting tasks, alongside a 1.0 point increase in Referring Expression Comprehension Acc@0.5.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) often struggle with precise object-level grounding, limiting their applicability in tasks requiring fine-grained spatial localization.
- Current Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO) methods suffer from sparse reward signals, failing to provide useful feedback for challenging cases where all initial attempts are unsuccessful.
- Adapting process reward models (PRMs) for visual object grounding is difficult due to challenges in reliably linking intermediate reasoning steps to concrete objects for feedback.

## Method

- Introduces a ==group-revision== optimization paradigm that prompts LVLMs to revise initial failed responses to complex queries.
- Employs a consolidation process that quantifies improvement between initial and revised predictions using an alignment cost to generate a dense shaping signal.
- Integrates the shaping signal into the reward function and utilizes it for post-scaling advantages in Group Relative Policy Optimisation, guiding the model to learn from corrected failures.

## Results

- Achieved an average of +**2.16%** on reasoning segmentation, +**2.22%** on referring segmentation, and +**4.27%** on object counting benchmarks over prior GRPO-based models.
- Attained an overall Acc@**0.5** of **85.80%** in Referring Expression Comprehension, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art by 1.0 point.
- Demonstrated consistent gains on general VQA benchmarks (e.g., +**0.55%** on SeedBenchV2+, +**1.25%** average on MMU-pro) and reduced hallucinations in Chain-of-Thought reasoning, indicating improved overall understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Initial failures in object grounding can be transformed into valuable learning opportunities by prompting model revision and quantifying improvements.
> - Generating dense feedback by comparing the quality of initial and revised responses provides more effective training signals than sparse, outcome-level rewards.
> - Amplifying the learning signal for revised responses that demonstrate significant improvement allows the model to better internalize the steps leading to successful grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in multimodal reasoning and general image understanding, ranging from scene summarization to content-based retrieval. However, existing literature indicates that these models often exhibit limitations in precisely grounding answers to specific objects within an image. This limitation is observed in tasks requiring both reasoning and object-level grounding, such as embodied navigation, robotic manipulation, and clinical imaging, where current LVLMs may not deliver reliable performance. This challenge is partly attributed to models failing to adequately exploit object-level grounding signals compared to general visual reasoning.

A promising direction to address this grounding gap involves fine-tuning LVLMs using object-level reward signals through Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO). Prior GRPO-based methods assign rewards at the response level, evaluating a group of candidate responses for their accuracy in localizing target objects. While these methods have shown progress in predicting bounding boxes or generating points for segmentation, they encounter a significant limitation: in challenging scenarios where all candidate responses within a sampled group fail to meet the reward criterion, the policy receives minimal or no training signal. This results in "hard cases" remaining unlearned, as the sparse reward design only evaluates the final output, providing no feedback for intermediate decisions.

This issue is analogous to limitations observed in domains like code generation and mathematical reasoning, where process reward models (PRMs) have been developed to provide step-wise feedback. However, directly applying PRMs to visual object grounding is complex due to the difficulty in reliably linking intermediate reasoning steps (Chain-of-Thought, CoT) to concrete objects in an image for feedback generation. This creates a "chicken-and-egg dilemma" where accurate intermediate guidance is needed for informative rewards, which in turn are essential for learning reasoning.

The presented work addresses this specific limitation by proposing a mechanism to recover learning signals from these "hard cases" where initial attempts fail. Instead of discarding failed attempts, the approach treats them as opportunities for revision, drawing inspiration from reward shaping techniques to provide denser and more informative feedback. This positions the work as an advancement within the reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) paradigm, particularly for visual grounding tasks, aiming to enhance object-level perception and reasoning capabilities in LVLMs beyond the limitations of conventional sparse reward GRPO.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the object-level grounding capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), particularly in challenging scenarios where existing reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning methods struggle due to sparse reward signals.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed limitations:
- **Insufficient Object-Level Grounding:** While LVLMs demonstrate strong general image understanding and multimodal reasoning, they frequently fail to precisely ground answers to specific objects, struggling with tasks requiring fine-grained spatial localization. This hampers their applicability in domains like embodied navigation, robotic manipulation, and clinical imaging that demand both reasoning and precise object interaction.
- **Sparse Reward in GRPO:** Current GRPO-based fine-tuning methods, which assign rewards at the entire response level, suffer from sparse learning signals. When all candidate responses in a sampled group fail to meet the reward criterion for a challenging query (e.g., box IoU < 0.5), the policy receives no useful feedback, leaving these "hard cases" unlearned. This constitutes a fundamental failure mode in the optimization process.
- **Difficulty of Process Reward Models (PRMs) in Visual Grounding:** While PRMs offer step-wise feedback in linguistic tasks, their application to visual object grounding is challenging. It is non-trivial to align each intermediate reasoning sentence in a model’s Chain-of-Thought (CoT) with a concrete object in the image to provide actionable learning signals. This "chicken-and-egg dilemma" necessitates an alternative approach to generate richer feedback.
- **Leveraging Failed Attempts as Clues:** The authors postulate that a failed attempt does not have to be an endpoint but can serve as a valuable clue. By prompting the model to revise its initial incorrect responses, it can be guided to attend to previously overlooked visual cues or reinterpret the scene. This revision process can transform a previously unrewarded, hard case into one that yields a successful outcome, thereby recovering a training signal.
- **Need for Credit Assignment in Revisions:** Simply generating better revised responses is insufficient if the model cannot attribute credit to the specific improvements that led to the success. Without a consolidation process to quantify and internalize these improvements, the model treats each outcome as isolated, missing the opportunity to learn *why* it succeeded after an initial failure.

Therefore, the core objective is to introduce a group-revision optimization paradigm that recovers and amplifies learning signals from hard cases. This paradigm aims to enable LVLMs not just to retry, but to learn effectively from their initial failures by quantifying improvements and using these as informative feedback for policy optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a "group-revision optimisation paradigm" designed to enhance learning from challenging cases in object-level grounding. It extends the Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO) framework by incorporating a revision and consolidation process. The approach is structured into three main phases: Response Sampling, Consolidation Process, and Reward & Advantage calculation for Policy Optimization.

#### 3.1. Response Sampling

The process begins by sampling an initial response for a given image-question pair $(x, q)$.
1. **Initial Response:** A single response, $o^{(1)} = (t^{(1)}, \hat{b}^{(1)}, \hat{p}^{(1)})$, is sampled from the behavior policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot | x, q)$. This response includes a reasoning text $t^{(1)}$, predicted bounding boxes $\hat{b}^{(1)}$, and points $\hat{p}^{(1)}$. This output forms the "initial shaping set" $s^{(1)}_{shape} = \{\hat{b}^{(1)}, \hat{p}^{(1)}, y\}$, where $y$ represents the ground truth. If the initial response is invalid, the system reverts to the standard GRPO update.
2. **Revision Prompt Generation:** The original question $q$ is updated into a dialogue-style revised query $q^{(2)} = U(q, o^{(1)})$. This is achieved by appending a fixed revision prompt template that incorporates cues from the initial reasoning text $t^{(1)}$ and spatial signals $(\hat{b}^{(1)}, \hat{p}^{(1)})$. The revision prompt is designed to guide the model to rethink or revise its previous predictions.
3. **Group of Revised Responses:** Conditioned on the revised query $q^{(2)}$ and the initial response $o^{(1)}$, a group of $G$ revised responses, $\{o^{(2)}_i\}_{i=1}^G$, is sampled from the behavior policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot | x, q^{(2)}, o^{(1)})$. Each $o^{(2)}_i = (t^{(2)}_i, \hat{b}^{(2)}_i, \hat{p}^{(2)}_i)$ serves as an alternative grounding hypothesis, aiming to resolve ambiguities or improve localization in challenging scenes. Each candidate forms a "per-candidate revision shaping set" $s^{(2)}_{shape,i} = \{o^{(2)}_i, y\}$.

#### 3.2. Consolidation Process

This phase quantifies the improvement achieved by the revised responses over the initial attempt, generating an informative shaping signal.
1. **Hungarian Matching and Alignment Cost:** For each shaping set $s^{(r)}_{shape,i}$ (where $r=1$ for the initial response, $r=2$ for revised candidates), Hungarian matching is performed between the predicted objects (bounding boxes and points) and the ground truth objects. If the number of predicted objects is less than ground truth objects, dummy predictions with unit cost are padded to penalize unmatched false negatives.
2. **Potential Function (Alignment Cost):** A potential function $\Phi(s^{(r)}_{shape,i})$ is defined as the average pairwise cost $e^{(r)}_{m,n}$ between matched predicted objects and ground truth objects:
 $\Phi(s^{(r)}_{shape,i}) := \frac{1}{|A^{(r)}_i|} \sum_{(m,n) \in A^{(r)}_i} e^{(r)}_{m,n}$
 The pairwise cost $e^{(r)}_{m,n}$ is computed as:
 $e^{(r)}_{m,n} = \frac{1}{3} \left[ (1 - \text{IoU}(\hat{b}^{(r)}_m, b_n)) + f_{L1}(\hat{b}^{(r)}_m, b_n) + f_{L1}(\hat{p}^{(r)}_m, p_n) \right]$
 Here, $f_{L1}$ is the L1 distance normalized by image scale for both boxes and points. A lower cost indicates better grounding.
3. **Shaping Signal Generation:** The shaping signal $\Delta\phi_i$ quantifies the relative improvement of a revised response $o^{(2)}_i$ over the initial response $o^{(1)}$:
 $\Delta\phi_i = \max \left( 0, \frac{\Phi(s^{(1)}_{shape}) - \Phi(s^{(2)}_{shape,i})}{\Phi(s^{(1)}_{shape})} \right)$
 A positive $\Delta\phi_i$ signifies improvement. The denominator provides a per-sample reference scale, ensuring the signal measures relative improvement and preventing it from vanishing when the initial response is already good.

#### 3.3. Reward and Advantage

The shaping signal is utilized to refine both the reward calculation and advantage scaling in the policy optimization process.
1. **Overall Reward:** The reward $r_i$ for each revised candidate $o^{(2)}_i$ combines standard GRPO reward components with the shaping signal:
 $r_i = R_{format}(o^{(2)}_i) + R_{acc}(o^{(2)}_i, y) + \omega \Delta\phi_i$
 $R_{format}$ checks for adherence to output structure, $R_{acc}$ measures spatial alignment accuracy (e.g., IoU), and $\omega$ is a weight controlling the influence of the shaping signal.
2. **Post-Scaled Advantage:** Following GRPO, a z-score advantage $A_i = (r_i - \mu)/\sigma$ is computed, where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the group. This advantage is then post-scaled using the shaping signal $\Delta\phi_i$:
 $\hat{A}_i = (1 + \Delta\phi_i) A_i$
 This scaling amplifies the influence of candidates that exhibit larger improvements, without altering the preference ranking established by the reward $r_i$.

#### 3.4. Optimization Objective

The policy model $\pi_\theta$ is optimized using a modified GRPO objective function. This objective maximizes the likelihood of higher-reward outputs while regularizing the policy towards a fixed reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ using a KL term. The key modification is the use of the post-scaled advantage $\hat{A}_i$, which incorporates the shaping signal, to guide the updates more effectively.

#### 3.5. Inference

During inference, given an image-question pair, a single response $o = (t, \hat{b}, \hat{p})$ is sampled from the current policy $\pi_\theta$. The number of predicted objects $M$ is used for counting. Predicted boxes $\{\hat{b}_m\}^M_{m=1}$ are used for object localization, and box-point pairs $\{(\hat{b}_m, \hat{p}_m)\}^M_{m=1}$ are used with SAM2 for segmentation.

The method is built upon Qwen2.5VL-7B as the base LVLM and SAM2 for segmentation. Training is conducted using the VeRL framework with the vLLM engine, supporting data and tensor parallelism.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed Group Revision method across multiple visual perception tasks, demonstrating consistent improvements over prior GRPO-based approaches.

#### 4.1. Segmentation and Counting

- **Reasoning Segmentation (ReasonSeg):** In the single-object setup, the method achieves 61.11 gIoU on ReasonSeg (test), showing an improvement of 7.78 points over Seg-R1. The multi-object setup obtains 66.73 gIoU on ReasonSeg (test).
- **Referring Segmentation (RefCOCO and variants):** In the single-object setting, performance on RefCOCOg (testA) reaches 76.72 cIoU and RefCOCO+ (testB) reaches 62.92 cIoU. The multi-object setup achieves 71.08 cIoU on RefCOCOg (testA) and 59.34 cIoU on RefCOCO+ (testB).
- **Object Counting (CountBench, PixMoCount):** The multi-object setup achieves an average accuracy of 79.97% across PixMoCount and CountBench, representing a 5.64% improvement over VisionReasoner.
- Overall, the method demonstrates robust gains, including an average of +2.16% on reasoning segmentation, +2.22% on referring segmentation, and +4.27% on object counting benchmarks compared to prior GRPO-based models, indicating improved object grounding and localization.

#### 4.2. Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)

- The multi-object model achieves an overall Acc@0.5 of 85.80%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art VisionReasoner by 1.0 points.
- On ReasonG (val and test, boxes converted from ReasonSeg), the single-object setup yields improvements of 4.58% and 1.81% over Seg-Zero, respectively.
- The paper notes that while the method may show slightly lower performance on certain RefCOCO+ splits compared to VisionReasoner, this is attributed to VisionReasoner's tendency to produce more false-positive boxes, which can inflate Acc@0.5 but is inconsistent with lower segmentation quality.

#### 4.3. General VQA

- The multi-object variant exhibits consistent gains over the base Qwen2.5VL-7B model on diverse VQA benchmarks.
- It achieves +0.55% on SeedBenchV2+ and an average of +1.25% on MMU-pro. These results suggest that improved object-level grounding contributes positively to overall image-level understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Contribution of Revision and Consolidation:** Both revision sampling and consolidation processes provide consistent gains. Revision sampling alone improves ReasonSeg (val) from 62.54% (baseline) to 64.97% and RefCOCOg (val) from 70.84% to 70.92%. Consolidation further boosts these to 66.99% and 73.84% respectively, and significantly improves PixMoCount (val) from 73.20% to 75.89%.
- **Reward Weight (ω):** An $\omega=5$ (for the shaping signal in the reward function) generally achieves the best overall results across segmentation and REC tasks, though values of 3 and 7 show slight advantages on specific splits.
- **Advantage Post-Scaling:** Advantage post-scaling (Eq. 8) consistently provides gains across all evaluated datasets, with improvements of 0.53% and 1.71% on ReasonSeg, and 1.86% and 0.97% on PixMoCount. This indicates that scaling strengthens gradient updates during consolidation.
- **Consolidation Signal Distribution (∆ϕ PDF):** The probability density function of ∆ϕ shows that in the single-object setting, group-wise mean improvements are mild (0-0.2), while the maximum improvements often fall between 0.8 and 1.0. This suggests that while average improvements are steady, there are strong revision candidates that can be effectively up-weighted. The distribution implies that initial responses are generally competitive and not intentionally poor, reducing the risk of reward hacking.
- **Consolidation Components (Box vs. Point shaping):** Removing either box or point shaping signals from the alignment cost (Eq. 5) leads to a decrease in performance compared to using both, confirming the importance of both components.

#### 4.5. Reasoning Process Evaluation

- An evaluation of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning using an LLM-as-a-Judge protocol (GPT-5) on ReasonSeg shows that the multi-object model produces fewer hallucinations compared to VisionReasoner, specifically -52 cases of attribute mismatch and -22 cases of vague claims. This indicates that optimizing on hard cases improves the model's CoT accuracy.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results and Reward Curves

- Qualitative comparisons demonstrate that the method produces more coherent reasoning and accurate spatial grounding than the baseline Qwen2.5VL-7B model on tasks like reasoning segmentation and counting.
- Reward curves over training steps show that group revision responses consistently achieve higher rewards compared to standard GRPO (without the ∆ϕ term), indicating effective transformation of revision gains into stronger learning signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel group-revision optimization paradigm that addresses a critical limitation in fine-tuning Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for object-level grounding: the sparse reward problem in challenging scenarios. By moving "from failure to feedback," the method extracts valuable learning signals from initially unsuccessful attempts, which would otherwise be ignored by conventional Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO) approaches.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Learning from Hard Cases:** The core contribution is the ability to recover and leverage training signals from complex or ambiguous cases where LVLMs initially struggle. This direct targeting of "hard cases" leads to more robust model performance in challenging visual grounding tasks.
- **Denser and More Informative Feedback:** The proposed consolidation process, inspired by reward shaping, quantifies the improvements achieved through revision. This generates a finer-grained shaping signal that is used to both refine the reward calculation and modulate advantage values during optimization. This provides the policy with specific guidance on which revisions led to more accurate outcomes, fostering more effective learning than sparse, outcome-level rewards.
- **Improved Object-Level Grounding:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across diverse tasks, including reasoning segmentation, referring segmentation, and object counting, highlight the method's effectiveness in improving the precision of object localization and mask generation. This is crucial for applications demanding fine-grained visual perception.
- **Generalizability and Robustness:** The method's ability to achieve gains on various datasets and tasks, including zero-shot generalization to counting and improvements in general VQA benchmarks, suggests its potential to enhance the overall understanding and reasoning capabilities of LVLMs, not just their grounding precision. The reduced hallucination rates in CoT reasoning further support this.
- **Addressing the "Chicken-and-Egg" Dilemma:** By not requiring explicit step-wise alignment between intermediate linguistic reasoning and visual objects (a challenge for process reward models in vision), the method offers a practical solution to provide dense feedback by comparing initial and revised spatial outputs.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, particularly for real-world applications where precise object-level understanding is paramount:
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Improved object grounding can enable more accurate object manipulation, navigation, and interaction for robots, leading to more reliable and capable autonomous systems.
- **Medical Imaging:** Enhanced precision in localizing specific anatomical structures or lesions could contribute to more accurate diagnostics and treatment planning in clinical settings.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** More reliable object-level understanding can lead to more intuitive and effective visual question answering systems and human-AI collaboration tools.
- **Further Advancements in LVLMs:** The paradigm provides a blueprint for developing more resilient and capable LVLMs that can learn effectively from their errors, paving the way for future models that are less prone to hallucination and more adept at fine-grained reasoning.

In summary, this work offers a principled and effective method to improve the object-level grounding capabilities of LVLMs by transforming learning failures into informative feedback, thereby unlocking training signals from previously overlooked hard cases.
