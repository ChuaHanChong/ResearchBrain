# Learning Whole-Body Human-Humanoid Interaction from Human-Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Huang et al. (Sun Yat-sen University) introduce a framework for humanoid robots to learn whole-body human-humanoid interaction from human-human demonstrations, featuring Physics-Aware Interaction Retargeting (PAIR) for data generation and a Decoupled Spatio-Temporal Action Reasoner (D-STAR) for policy learning. The approach achieved an average 75.4% success rate across diverse interactive tasks in simulation and demonstrated successful real-robot deployment.

## Problem

- Scarcity of high-quality Human-Humanoid Interaction (HHoI) data due to collection challenges, despite abundant Human-Human Interaction (HHI) data.
- Existing motion retargeting methods from HHI to HHoI fail to preserve crucial physical contacts due to morphological differences.
- Current HHoI policies primarily mimic observed trajectories, lacking the synchronized timing and precise spatial targeting required for responsive, collaborative behaviors in high-Degrees-of-Freedom humanoids.

## Method

- **Physics-Aware Interaction Retargeting (PAIR)**: A two-stage optimization pipeline that converts HHI data into physically consistent HHoI clips by explicitly preserving contact semantics and relative geometry.
- **Decoupled Spatio-Temporal Action Reasoner (D-STAR)**: A hierarchical policy architecture that disentangles "when to act" (temporal reasoning via Phase Attention) from "where to act" (spatial reasoning via Multi-Scale Spatial module).
- Integration of a whole-body controller (WBC) with D-STAR to translate high-level action targets into safe, physically plausible motor commands for humanoid execution.

## Results

- PAIR achieved a contact F1-score of 0.841 (a 67.5% relative gain over state-of-the-art baselines) and best kinematic similarity (JPE 0.174m), while producing smoother motions.
- D-STAR attained a 75.4% average success rate across six diverse interaction tasks, outperforming strong architectural baselines by 11.1 percentage points, and demonstrated robustness to human partner scale and speed variations.
- The entire framework was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, enabling synchronized whole-body interactions like Hug, Handshake, and High-Five based on text commands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving holistic contact semantics through an explicit loss function and a two-stage optimization strategy is crucial for successfully retargeting human-human interactions to humanoids.
> - Decoupling temporal and spatial reasoning in an interactive policy leads to a more robust understanding of interactive intent, enabling genuinely synchronized and collaborative behaviors.
> - The combination of a high-level action reasoner and a low-level whole-body controller provides an effective pipeline for learning intelligent interactive policies and deploying them on real robots while ensuring physical plausibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of humanoid robots into human-centric environments requires them to master Human-Humanoid Interaction (HHoI). This field is at the frontier of robotics, extending beyond foundational capabilities like locomotion and manipulation to enable dynamic, intuitive, and safe physical collaboration. The broader research landscape for HHoI faces two primary challenges: **data acquisition** and **interaction policy design**.

**Data Acquisition:** Traditional methods for obtaining HHoI data, such as teleoperation, are high-fidelity but suffer from high costs, slow collection rates, safety concerns, and limited diversity. A scalable alternative is to leverage abundant Human-Human Interaction (HHI) data and retarget it to humanoids. However, existing motion retargeting methods often prioritize kinematic similarity or style, failing to preserve crucial physical contacts (e.g., handshakes) due to morphological differences between humans and humanoids. This paper directly addresses this gap by proposing **Physics-Aware Interaction Retargeting (PAIR)**, which focuses on preserving contact semantics and physical consistency. This differentiates it from prior works like classical IK/trajectory optimization or learning-based retargeting that often neglect physical contacts crucial for interaction. It also stands apart from graphics-focused retargeting that may lack robotics-grade constraints.

**Interaction Policy Design:** Controlling high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) humanoids for dynamic HHoI demands real-time management of stability, collision avoidance, and context-aware, responsive actions. Current policies, often developed for single-agent tasks or non-interactive settings, struggle to interpret human intent or proactively engage. They tend to merely mimic trajectories, lacking a true understanding of relational geometry or timing necessary for natural interaction. This work aims to overcome this limitation with **Decoupled Spatio-Temporal Action Reasoner (D-STAR)**. Unlike end-to-end learning methods from teleoperation (e.g., XBG) or open-loop social forecasting (e.g., ECHO), D-STAR introduces a hierarchical policy that disentangles the "when to act" (temporal reasoning) from the "where to act" (spatial reasoning). This contrasts with unified policies that conflate these challenges, offering a novel approach to learning responsive, synchronized whole-body collaboration from HHI demonstrations. It aims to move beyond simple imitation learning which often leads to "average" motions lacking interactive understanding.

By tackling both the data generation bottleneck and the policy design complexity, this research offers a comprehensive pipeline that bridges the gap between abundant HHI data and robust HHoI capabilities, placing it at the forefront of developing truly interactive humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **enable humanoid robots to physically interact with humans in a natural, responsive, and safe manner, leveraging existing human-human interaction data.** This ambitious goal is broken down into two core motivations and corresponding objectives:

1. **Overcome Data Scarcity for Human-Humanoid Interaction (HHoI):**
 - **Motivation:** High-quality HHoI data is scarce due to the challenges of real-world collection (cost, safety, diversity). While abundant Human-Human Interaction (HHI) data exists, standard motion retargeting methods fail to convert it into physically consistent HHoI data, particularly by breaking essential physical contacts crucial for interaction.
 - **Objective:** To develop a novel, contact-centric motion retargeting pipeline, **PAIR (Physics-Aware Interaction Retargeting)**, capable of converting large-scale HHI datasets into physically consistent HHoI clips. This pipeline must preserve contact semantics and relative geometry despite morphological differences between humans and humanoids.

2. **Develop Interactive Policies that Go Beyond Trajectory Mimicry:**
 - **Motivation:** Even with high-fidelity HHoI data, conventional imitation learning policies tend to merely mimic observed trajectories. They lack a deeper interactive understanding, struggling with synchronized timing and precise spatial targeting, which are critical for responsive collaboration.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a hierarchical policy architecture, **D-STAR (Decoupled Spatio-Temporal Action Reasoner)**, that explicitly disentangles "when to act" (temporal intent) from "where to act" (spatial selection). This decoupling is intended to foster robust temporal phasing and precise spatial coordination, leading to genuinely synchronized whole-body behaviors rather than simple replication.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a complete, effective pipeline for learning complex whole-body human-humanoid interactions by simultaneously solving the challenges of data generation and intelligent policy design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a two-pronged solution addressing both data scarcity and policy complexity: PAIR for data generation and D-STAR for policy learning.

#### 3.1. PAIR: Physics-Aware Interaction Retargeting

The PAIR pipeline converts HHI sequences into physically consistent HHoI clips by explicitly preserving contact semantics across morphological differences. It operates in two stages:

1. **Objective Function Formulation:** PAIR frames retargeting as an optimization problem to find an optimal robot motion (M_R) and a minimally adjusted partner motion (M'_H_p). The objective function, L_retarget, is a weighted sum of four components:
 - **Kinematic Similarity Loss (L_kin):** Penalizes differences between the robot's joint positions and those of a morphologically reshaped source human skeleton (Reshaped(H_s)). While a baseline, the paper notes its isolated application leads to contact-breaking failures.
 - **Interaction-Contact Preservation Loss (L_con):** The core innovation. It enforces consistency between pairwise-distance matrices of keypoints (e.g., hands, head, shoulders) for the original HHI and the optimized HHoI. This ensures holistic contact maintenance (e.g., hand-hand) and appropriate social distances beyond brittle pointwise penalties.
 - **Human Motion Fidelity Loss (L_hum):** Prevents unnatural distortions of the human partner's motion by penalizing deviations from the original trajectory, allowing only necessary, selective upper-body adaptations.
 - **Physical Plausibility Regularizers (L_reg):** Includes a Temporal Coherence Loss (L_temp) for smoothness and a Pose Regularization Loss (L_pose) to keep joint angles within comfortable ranges.

2. **Two-Stage Optimization Strategy:** To navigate the complex, non-convex optimization landscape and avoid poor local minima (like "near-miss" interactions), a coarse-to-fine approach is used:
 - **Stage 1: Global Kinematic Initialization:** Optimizes the full objective with a moderate weight for L_con. This establishes a globally consistent and kinematically plausible motion, guiding the solver to a good starting region.
 - **Stage 2: Contact and Stability Refinement:** Uses the Stage 1 output as a warm start and significantly increases the weight of L_con. This aggressively refines subtle contact misalignments and enforces physical stability, converting "near-misses" into robust physical connections. Joint angles are clamped to hardware limits during optimization, and post-processing includes Gaussian smoothing.

#### 3.2. D-STAR: Decoupled Spatio-Temporal Action Reasoner

D-STAR is a hierarchical policy designed to overcome the limitations of simple trajectory mimicry by disentangling temporal and spatial reasoning.

1. **Observations and Temporal Encoder:** The policy takes a history of observation frames, concatenating humanoid proprioceptive state and human SMPL joint positions. A **Long-Short Temporal Encoder (LSTE)** uses two parallel Transformer encoders: a long-term encoder over the full 12-frame history for contextual understanding and a short-term encoder over the most recent 6 frames for fine-grained coordination. Their concatenated output forms a shared temporal feature.

2. **Decoupled Interaction Modules:**
 - **Phase Attention (PA):** Addresses "when to act." It infers the current interaction phase (Preparation, Act, Follow-up) using a phase classifier. Phase-specialized self-attention blocks process the temporal feature, with outputs weighted by predicted phase probabilities, yielding a phase-conditioned temporal feature (f_Phase_t). This provides a temporal gate for decision-making.
 - **Multi-Scale Spatial module (MSS):** Addresses "where to act." It encodes the human-robot geometric relationship using multi-scale features (absolute positions, pairwise distances, relative orientations of key joints) across near (0-0.3m), mid (0.3-0.8m), and far (0.8-3.0m) fields. Dedicated encoders map these signals to a spatial feature (f_MSS_t), capturing coarse-to-fine spatial context.

3. **Hierarchical Action Generation:** The decoupled features (f_Phase_t and f_MSS_t), along with the shared temporal context and a language command embedding, are concatenated to form a global conditioning vector. A **diffusion-based planning head** (trained with a DDIM scheduler) then generates a high-level reference action target (36-D, including 29 joint targets and root motion). This target is then fed to a pre-trained **Whole-Body Controller (WBC)** (WBC-Sim for simulation, HOMIE-based for real robot) which ensures safe, physically plausible execution by converting high-level targets into real-time motor commands while maintaining balance and respecting joint limits. The policy operates at a lower rate (5 Hz) than the controller (50 Hz), with anchors densified and fused via bilinear interpolation.

4. **Training Objectives:** The high-level policy is trained end-to-end via supervised learning on the HHoI dataset, optimizing a composite loss (L_total). This includes a primary action prediction loss (weighted MSE on root translation, orientation, and joint angles), a phase supervision loss (cross-entropy for classification and KL divergence for transition consistency), and auxiliary geometric losses (human-facing orientation, keypoint positioning, spatial consistency). The low-level WBC-Sim is trained via privileged-to-noisy distillation, with an oracle teacher trained on clean observations and a student distilled on noisy observations with history, using a pure action-matching loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive validation through simulations and real-robot deployments, confirming the effectiveness of both PAIR and D-STAR.

#### 4.1. Validation of Physics-Aware Interaction Retargeting (PAIR)

- **Quantitative Performance:** PAIR significantly outperforms existing retargeting methods.
 - **Contact Preservation:** It achieved a contact F1-score of 0.841 (at 0.35m threshold, micro-averaged), marking a 67.5% relative gain over the state-of-the-art learning baseline ImitationNet and 22.2% over the best non-interactive baseline (Simple MSE). This confirms PAIR's ability to preserve essential physical contacts where naive methods fail.
 - **Kinematic Similarity:** PAIR also achieved the best kinematic similarity (JPE 0.174m), indicating that contact preservation does not come at the cost of overall motion style.
 - **Motion Quality:** It produced markedly smoother motion, with a 69% jerk reduction compared to Simple MSE, and maintained physical plausibility metrics (LargeAngle, AngleStd) at competitive levels.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing Human Adaptation or the contact loss (L_con) led to a drop in contact F1-score, highlighting their importance for physical precision.
 - Crucially, collapsing the two-stage optimization into a single stage significantly degraded performance (F1 to 0.788), affirming that the coarse-to-fine strategy is essential for navigating the complex energy landscape and avoiding kinematically plausible yet physically incorrect "near-miss" minima.

#### 4.2. Validation of the HHoI Policy (D-STAR)

- **Task Success Rates:** D-STAR achieved a state-of-the-art **75.4% average success rate** across six diverse interactive tasks (Hug, High-Five, Handshake, Wave, Bend, Fly-Kiss).
 - It significantly surpassed strong architectural baselines: Transformer Policy (64.3%) and TCN Policy (49.2%).
 - The margin of improvement (11.1 points over Transformer) suggests the gains stem from decoupled spatio-temporal reasoning, not just backbone choice.
 - The advantage was most pronounced in contact-rich tasks like Handshake, where success nearly doubled (61.3% vs. 32.3% for Transformer).
 - Naive Mimicry (BC only) failed completely (0% success), underscoring the necessity of a low-level controller and true interactive understanding beyond trajectory replay.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing the Phase Attention (PA) module degraded performance on temporally sensitive interactions (e.g., High-Five), while removing the Multi-Scale Spatial (MSS) module harmed spatially precise ones (e.g., Handshake).
 - This confirmed that both PA and MSS are essential and complementary, validating the decoupled reasoning architecture.
- **Robustness to Spatio-Temporal Variations:** D-STAR demonstrated strong generalization capabilities.
 - When evaluated under combined variations in human partner body scale (0.8x-1.2x) and motion speed (0.8x-1.2x), the policy maintained a performance plateau (peaking at 75.4%) and degraded gracefully, retaining 62.7% success even at the most extreme variations.
 - The policy showed an "embodiment-aware asymmetry," being more resilient to smaller partners than larger ones, adapting to the Unitree G1 humanoid's kinematic affordances rather than simply imitating unattainable human-human behaviors. This provides quantitative evidence of learning a truly generalizable interaction model.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Deployment

- **Practical Executability:** The framework was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot using a standard HOMIE-based controller.
- **Qualitative Results:** The robot executed tasks like Hug, Handshake, and High-Five (illustrated in Figure 1) effectively based on text commands, demonstrating synchronized whole-body interactions.
- **Fine-tuning for Deployment:** A single, task-agnostic fine-tuning procedure was applied to reduce sim-to-real gaps in pre-/post-interaction phases, ensuring stable transitions without altering the core "Act" phase. This shows the adaptability of the learned policy to real-world constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of Human-Humanoid Interaction by systematically addressing two fundamental challenges: data generation and intelligent policy design.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Bridging the Data Gap with PAIR:** The development of PAIR is a critical contribution, providing a principled and effective method to convert abundant HHI data into high-quality, physically consistent HHoI data. This scalable data generation pipeline unlocks a vast resource that was previously underutilized due to the limitations of naive retargeting. It enables the training of HHoI policies without the prohibitive costs and risks of extensive real-robot data collection, accelerating research and development in this domain. The explicit focus on "contact semantics" is a vital distinction from prior art, making the generated data genuinely suitable for interactive tasks.

2. **Advancing Interactive Intelligence with D-STAR:** D-STAR's decoupled spatio-temporal reasoning represents a novel paradigm for learning interactive control. By disentangling "when to act" (Phase Attention) from "where to act" (Multi-Scale Spatial module), the policy achieves a deeper understanding of interactive intent, moving beyond simple trajectory mimicry. This allows for responsive, synchronized, and collaborative behaviors, which are essential for natural human-robot interaction. The robustness demonstrated against variations in human partners (scale, speed) highlights its potential for generalizable and adaptive interaction in unstructured environments.

3. **Complete and Validated Pipeline:** The paper presents a fully integrated pipeline, from data acquisition and processing to policy learning and real-robot deployment. The rigorous validation, including extensive simulations, ablation studies, and real-world qualitative demonstrations, provides strong evidence for the framework's effectiveness and practical viability.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** This work paves the way for humanoids that can genuinely collaborate with humans, understanding and participating in complex physical and social interactions. This has profound implications for applications in logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, and daily assistance, where seamless and intuitive interaction is paramount.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The PAIR dataset generation method and the D-STAR policy architecture provide robust baselines and novel approaches that can inspire further research in data-efficient HHoI learning, multi-modal human-robot communication, and adaptable control strategies for complex social tasks.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By leveraging readily available HHI data, the framework significantly reduces the cost and time associated with developing interactive humanoid capabilities, democratizing access to advanced robotics research.
- **Safer Interactions:** The emphasis on physical consistency and plausible motion, combined with the whole-body controller, inherently contributes to safer human-robot interactions by ensuring stable movements and appropriate contact management.

In conclusion, this research challenges the conventional wisdom of rote mimicry in robot learning, advocating for a shift towards true collaborative intelligence. It provides both the tools and the conceptual framework to achieve more natural, robust, and meaningful human-humanoid interactions, bringing us closer to a future where robots are integrated seamlessly into human society.
