# Zero-Shot 3D Question Answering via Hierarchical View-to-Token Transportation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shenzhen University developed KeyVT, a hierarchical framework that enables 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform zero-shot 3D question answering. The framework enhances VLM performance by efficiently selecting spatially consistent views and representative tokens from 3D scenes, providing a compact and information-rich input context.

## Problem

- 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face limitations in processing extensive 3D scene information from multiple 2D views due to strict input token budgets and context quality issues.
- Existing methods for adapting 2D VLMs to 3D scenes often struggle with inefficient view selection, frequently overlooking crucial spatial relationships or introducing semantic redundancy that wastes input capacity.
- Current token compression techniques can be sensitive to feature distributions, potentially failing to capture diverse and essential 3D details across selected views effectively.

## Method

- KeyV: A geometry-aware key-view selection component that identifies spatially consistent and task-relevant views by combining camera-based spatial distance with semantic relevance scoring across segmented sub-scenes.
- KeyT: An Optimal Transport (OT)-guided key token selection component that compresses redundant information across selected key views by formulating the problem as minimizing Sinkhorn distance to find a compact set of representative tokens.
- The KeyVT framework hierarchically integrates KeyV and KeyT, first selecting an optimal set of views and then compressing tokens within those views, to provide an optimized and compact input context for 2D VLMs.

## Results

- KeyVT consistently outperformed existing tuning-free baselines across ScanQA, SQA3D, and VSI-Bench datasets, achieving higher CIDEr and EM-1 scores with various VLM backbones (e.g., LLaVAVideo-7B yielded **100.7** CIDEr and **57.9** EM-1 on ScanQA).
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of KeyV's geometry-aware design and KeyT's OT-based compression, showing robustness to camera noise and superior performance compared to alternative compression methods.
- The framework demonstrated performance comparable to, and in some instances exceeding, training-based 3D QA approaches, while maintaining a tuning-free, resource-efficient, and generalizable solution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining geometry-aware view selection with semantic relevance is crucial for providing 2D VLMs with a comprehensive and spatially coherent understanding of 3D scenes.
> - Optimal Transport offers a robust method for compressing visual tokens, effectively identifying representative information and reducing redundancy across multiple views without significant loss of diversity.
> - A hierarchical approach that first selects relevant views and then compresses tokens within them allows 2D VLMs to leverage their capabilities for 3D understanding efficiently and without requiring expensive 3D-specific training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned within the field of 3D scene understanding, specifically addressing the challenge of enabling 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to process and reason about 3D environments. Recent advancements in 2D VLMs, such as GPT-4o and Qwen3-VL, trained on vast datasets of 2D images and text, have demonstrated strong capabilities in multimodal tasks. Inspired by these developments, a growing area of research involves adapting these powerful 2D VLMs to the 3D domain by sampling multiple 2D views from 3D point clouds. This approach is seen as a promising alternative to developing dedicated 3D language models (3D-LLMs), which typically require large volumes of high-quality, annotated 3D-text data that remain scarce.

However, leveraging 2D VLMs for 3D understanding introduces its own set of challenges. A primary concern is the "input context quality" and the "limited input budget" of VLMs. 3D scenes can generate an extensive number of 2D views, far exceeding the token limits that VLMs can process. Initial attempts using uniform sampling of views often introduce noise or irrelevant information. Subsequent key-view selection methods, while improving input quality, often prioritize query-view relevance, potentially overlooking critical evidence not explicitly mentioned in the question, or fail to account for spatial relationships crucial in 3D environments. Furthermore, even selected "key views" can contain significant redundancy, as visually and semantically similar views often share overlapping regions. This redundancy consumes valuable input capacity without necessarily adding distinct information.

The current work aims to address these limitations by proposing a hierarchical framework that efficiently selects and compresses information from 3D scenes. It distinguishes itself from prior research, which primarily focuses on view-level selection or token compression within individual video frames, by offering a two-stage approach that first selects spatially and semantically relevant views and then compresses redundant tokens across these selected views. This positions the work as an advancement in making 2D VLMs more effective and efficient for zero-shot 3D scene understanding tasks, without incurring the high training costs or data requirements associated with native 3D-LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to improve the quality and efficiency of input context provided to 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for zero-shot 3D question answering. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations stemming from the limitations of current approaches:

1. **Enhancing Task-Relevant and Spatially Consistent View Selection**: Current methods for adapting 2D VLMs to 3D scenes often struggle with selecting optimal input views. While semantic similarity-based retrieval is common, it may overlook crucial spatial relationships and contextual information not explicitly present in the query. For effective 3D understanding, it is essential to capture not only main objects but also their surrounding environments and spatial configurations. The motivation is to move beyond mere query-view relevance by incorporating geometric parameters to identify views that are both semantically relevant to the question and spatially consistent, thereby providing a more comprehensive and coherent representation of the 3D scene.

2. **Addressing Input Budget Constraints and Redundancy**: Vision-Language Models have inherent limitations on the number of input tokens they can process. This constraint necessitates efficient data representation. Existing key-view selection methods often feed a fixed, small number of views into VLMs, but these views frequently contain redundant semantic information and overlapping regions. This redundancy wastes valuable input capacity that could otherwise be used to incorporate additional key views or more fine-grained details. The motivation is to develop a mechanism that can effectively identify and eliminate this redundancy at a finer granularity (token level) across multiple views, thereby "freeing up input capacity" and allowing VLMs to access richer, non-redundant 3D details within the predefined budget.

3. **Developing Robust Token Compression**: Traditional token compression techniques, particularly clustering-based models, can be sensitive to feature distributions and may not adequately capture diverse tokens across selected views. If a clustering algorithm fails to represent diverse features, the compressed tokens might not accurately reflect the full range of information in the original views. The motivation is to introduce a more principled and robust method for identifying representative tokens that can summarize essential 3D details in the feature space without losing diversity. This aims to overcome the limitations of existing compression methods by providing a comprehensive coverage of view features, even in cases of densely populated or varied feature sets.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose KeyVT, a hierarchical framework that first selects "Key Views" using geometry-aware and task-relevant criteria, and then compresses "Key Tokens" from these views using an Optimal Transport-guided approach. This dual-level selection and compression strategy is designed to provide VLMs with an optimized, information-rich, and compact input context for robust 3D scene understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces KeyVT, a hierarchical framework comprising two main components: KeyV for geometry-aware key-view selection and KeyT for Optimal Transport (OT)-guided key token selection. This methodology aims to efficiently provide 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with task-relevant and spatially consistent 3D scene information within strict input budget constraints.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is defined as generating an answer $A$ for a question $Q$ given a 3D scene represented by a set of 2D views $M = \{V_1, V_2..., V_{|M|}\}$. Each view $V_i$ is accompanied by its camera parameters (position and orientation). Since the total token count of all views ($|M|_t$) significantly exceeds the VLM's input budget ($S$), a selection function $f(M, Q, S)$ is required to extract an optimal input context $I$. The output is $A = VLM(I, Q)$. Existing methods often rely on semantic similarity for view selection, but KeyVT seeks to augment this by integrating spatial information and addressing token-level redundancy.

#### 3.2. KeyV: Geometry-Aware Key-View Selection

This component is designed to identify spatially consistent and task-relevant views from the entire 3D scene.

- **View Distance Metric**: To capture spatial relationships, a view distance $D(V_0, V_i)$ is computed between an initial reference view $V_0$ and any other view $V_i$. This metric combines the Euclidean distance between camera centers $C_0$ and $C_i$ (derived from camera orientation $R$ and position $t$) and the angular distance $\theta(R_0, R_i)$ between their camera orientations. This approach estimates the spatial trajectory, enabling a structural partitioning of the 3D scene.
- **Sub-scene Partitioning**: The 3D scene is segmented into a sequence of spatially consistent sub-scenes, $\{L_1, L_2..., L_{|L|}\}$, by applying a window split over the computed view distances $D$. A predefined window size $\delta$ is used to group views that are spatially close.
- **Sub-scene Relevance Scoring**: To assess the importance of each sub-scene $L_l$ relative to the question $Q$, a relevance score $r_l$ is calculated. This involves using a pre-trained multimodal encoder (specifically, BLIP2's cross-modality encoder) to determine the semantic similarity $O_{l,i}$ between each view $V_i \in L_l$ and the question. The sub-scene's relevance $r_l$ is then determined by combining the maximum and mean of these individual view-question scores ($\max(O_l) + \text{mean}(O_l)$). This design aims to identify sub-scenes that are either globally relevant or contain highly salient elements.
- **Adaptive View Allocation**: The total number of allowed key views $K$ is adaptively distributed among the sub-scenes. For each sub-scene $L_l$, the number of views to be sampled, $N_l$, is calculated by weighting its relevance score $r_l$ by the square root of its size $|L_l|$ and then proportionally allocating views from $K$. This balances the importance of relevant sub-scenes with their diversity-capturing potential.
- **Key View Selection**: Within each sub-scene $L_l$, the $N_l$ most relevant views are selected by taking the top-K views based on their individual semantic similarity scores $O_l$. These selected views from all sub-scenes are then concatenated in their original temporal order to form the final set of key views $M^*$.

#### 3.3. KeyT: OT-Guided Key Token Selection

This component aims to compress redundant information present across the selected key views by selecting a compact set of representative tokens.

- **Token Representation**: From the selected key views $M^*$, a set of $N$ token embeddings $P = \{e_1, e_2..., e_N\}$ is extracted. The objective is to create a smaller set of $M$ virtual tokens $Q = \{c_1, c_2..., c_M\}$, where $M < N$, which retains maximal semantic information.
- **Optimal Transport Formulation**: The problem of finding optimal virtual tokens is framed as an Optimal Transport (OT) problem. $P$ and $Q$ are conceptualized as discrete probability distributions in the embedding space, with uniform importance weights. The goal is to minimize the OT distance between these two distributions, which quantifies the minimum "cost" to transform one distribution into the other.
- **Sinkhorn Distance and Optimization**: To make the optimization tractable for large numbers of tokens, the entropy-regularized Sinkhorn distance is utilized. The cost matrix $C$ between original tokens $e_n$ and virtual tokens $c_m$ is defined by their cosine distance ($1 - \cos(e_n, c_m)$). The virtual tokens $Q$ are then iteratively optimized via a gradient-based method (Adam optimizer) to minimize this Sinkhorn distance over a small number of steps (10-15 iterations).
- **Grounding Virtual Tokens**: Since VLMs require real image patch tokens, the learned virtual tokens $Q$ are mapped back to actual patch tokens. For each virtual token $c_m$, a specific number of real patch tokens (e.g., $S/M$ tokens) are selected from the original set $P$. This selection is guided by the transport plan $T$ (an outcome of the OT optimization), which indicates the transport probability from each original token $e_n$ to each virtual token $c_m$. The real patch tokens with the highest transport probabilities to a given virtual token are chosen.
- **Final Input Context**: The combined set of these selected real patch tokens forms the ultimate input context $I$ that is fed into the VLM.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The cross-modality encoder from a pre-trained BLIP2-itm-ViT-G model is used for semantic relevance scoring. For datasets lacking camera parameters, FastVGGT is employed for estimation. The window size $\delta$ for sub-scene partitioning is set to 1. The OT-based compression uses an Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-2 for 10-15 iterations. Experiments are conducted using LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, and LLaVA-Video as VLM backbones.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of KeyVT was conducted across three 3D question answering benchmarks: ScanQA, SQA3D, and VSI-Bench, using various 2D VLM backbones.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **Overall Performance**: KeyVT consistently demonstrated performance improvements over existing tuning-free methods across all evaluated VLM backbones (LLaVA-OneVision-7B, LLaVAVideo-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B). On ScanQA and SQA3D (Table 1), KeyVT achieved the highest scores in most metrics, particularly CIDEr and EM-1. For instance, with the LLaVAVideo-7B backbone, KeyVT yielded a CIDEr score of 100.7 and an EM-1 of 57.9, surpassing AKS (99.0 CIDEr, 57.3 EM-1) and other compression methods (DivPrune: 99.1 CIDEr, 55.8 EM-1; FLoC: 99.4 CIDEr, 57.2 EM-1) for 8 equivalent input frames.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: The study found that 2D-based VLMs, when integrated with KeyVT, generally outperformed dedicated 3D-LLMs by a considerable margin. KeyVT also exhibited performance comparable to, and in some cases exceeding, training-based approaches like cdViews and Video-3D, especially under similar input constraints. This suggests that the hierarchical selection framework effectively provides rich 3D context to VLMs without the need for extensive training.
- **VSI-Bench Results**: On the VSI-Bench dataset (Table 2), designed to test fine-grained spatial reasoning, KeyVT achieved the highest average performance among open-source models across all VLM backbones. While some proprietary models scored higher, KeyVT outperformed other tuning-free baselines like AKS, FLoC, DivPrune, and See&Trek. For example, with the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, KeyVT attained an average accuracy of 37.0, exceeding See&Trek's 29.0.
- **Small-Scale Models**: KeyVT maintained its superior performance even when applied to smaller VLM backbones (LLAVA-ONEVISION-0.5B, QWEN2.5-VL-3B) on the VSI-Bench (Table 14), indicating its scalability and effectiveness under resource constraints.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **KeyV Effectiveness**: Comparisons of KeyV (geometry-aware view selection) with AKS (temporal keyframe sampling) showed that KeyV consistently outperformed AKS across all three datasets when selecting 16 frames without token compression (Table 3). This highlights the benefit of incorporating geometric information for 3D scene understanding.
- **KeyV Component Analysis**: An ablation of KeyV's components (Table 5) revealed that removing any part, particularly the geometry-aware design, led to a performance decrease, confirming the contribution of each module.
- **Robustness to Camera Noise**: KeyV demonstrated robustness to injected noise in camera parameters (Table 4). Performance remained strong even with 5% noise, indicating tolerance to geometry perturbations.
- **Hyperparameters of KeyV**: The optimal window size $\delta$ for sub-scene partitioning was identified as 1.0 (Table 10). Combining maximum and mean semantic relevance scores for sub-scene selection proved more effective than using either metric alone (Table 11), balancing saliency and contextual coverage.
- **KeyT Effectiveness**: KeyT showed strong token compression capabilities across various compression ratios (75%, 50%, 25%) and VLM backbones (Tables 7 and 15). It consistently outperformed DivPrune and FLoC in maintaining performance, validating the OT-based approach for identifying diverse and representative tokens.
- **Integration with Key View Selectors**: Integrating KeyT with other key view selection methods (Uniform, Retrieval, AKS, KeyV) consistently boosted performance (Table 6), demonstrating its plug-and-play capability. The combination of KeyV and KeyT achieved optimal results.
- **Hyperparameters of KeyT**: KeyT's optimization process was robust to variations in iteration count (10-15 iterations) and learning rate (1e-3 to 1e-2) (Tables 12 and 13).

#### 4.3. Qualitative Visualizations

Visualizations (Figures 3, 4, 5) demonstrated that key views selected by KeyV typically formed spatially consistent and task-relevant sub-scenes. Key tokens, when projected back to 3D, captured principal geometric and semantic structures while effectively removing redundant regions. T-SNE plots confirmed that selected key tokens broadly covered the distribution of original view tokens, indicating preserved diversity. Failure cases (Figure 6) sometimes occurred in dense clutter or with ambiguous spatial relations, where token selection struggled to strictly isolate target objects.

#### 4.4. Complexity Analysis

Inference time analysis (Table 8) showed that KeyV's view selection time (1302.44 ms) was comparable to AKS (1300.79 ms). KeyT's token selection time (1387 ms) was more efficient than FLoC (1684 ms) but slower than DivPrune (548 ms), indicating a favorable trade-off between efficiency and performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The KeyVT framework offers a notable contribution to the field of 3D scene understanding by addressing critical challenges in integrating 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with 3D data. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Enhanced 3D Understanding for 2D VLMs**: KeyVT provides a method for effectively bridging the gap between the rich geometric information of 3D scenes and the input limitations of powerful pre-trained 2D VLMs. By hierarchically selecting both spatially consistent views and representative tokens, the framework enables VLMs to access more relevant and compact 3D context, leading to improved spatial reasoning and overall 3D understanding capabilities in a zero-shot setting.

2. **Resource Efficiency and Accessibility**: A key impact of KeyVT is its data- and tuning-free nature. It operates by preprocessing existing 2D views from 3D scenes without requiring large-scale, costly 3D-text paired datasets for training or extensive fine-tuning of the VLM itself. This significantly lowers the computational and data barriers for researchers and practitioners, promoting more resource-efficient and accessible deployment of 3D-aware AI systems, especially for those with limited computational resources.

3. **Generalizability and Robustness**: The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across multiple VLM backbones and diverse 3D benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, VSI-Bench) highlight the generalizability and robustness of KeyVT. Its ability to achieve performance comparable to, or sometimes exceeding, training-based approaches, and its tolerance to noise in camera parameters, suggest a robust solution for a wide range of 3D reasoning tasks in varying environments.

4. **Broad Applicability**: The improved 3D perception facilitated by KeyVT has potential implications for various real-world applications. These include advancements in robotics for more intelligent navigation and interaction in complex environments, enhanced capabilities for embodied AI systems, more immersive and contextually aware augmented reality applications, and refined indoor navigation and assistive technologies.

While the authors note limited potential risks, as KeyVT primarily functions as a preprocessing and token-selection mechanism, they encourage further exploration of robustness and fairness in real-world deployments. Overall, this work represents a practical and effective step toward making advanced 3D understanding capabilities more broadly available and efficient, by strategically optimizing how existing powerful VLMs consume 3D information.
