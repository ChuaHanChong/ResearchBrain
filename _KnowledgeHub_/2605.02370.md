# Robust Adaptive Predictive Control for Hook-Based Aerial Transportation Between Moving Platforms

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the HUN-REN Institute for Computer Science and Control and ETH Zurich developed a robust adaptive model predictive control (RAMPC) system for autonomous hook-based payload transportation between moving platforms. This system, leveraging a MuJoCo digital twin and online parameter estimation, achieved a 100% success rate in simulations across diverse scenarios and demonstrated consistent performance in real-world flight experiments.

## Problem

- Autonomous pick-and-place operations between two moving platforms using a passive hook-based gripper remained largely unaddressed.
- The coupled dynamics of aerial manipulators with payloads are highly nonlinear, involve abrupt configurational changes, and include complex contact interactions difficult to model precisely.
- Uncertainties, particularly in payload mass, significantly influence system dynamics, requiring robust control strategies to maintain performance and safety.

## Method

- A MuJoCo-based digital twin models the complex, reconfigurable dynamics of the hook-based transportation system directly within the NMPC framework.
- A Robust Adaptive NMPC (RAMPC) integrates Zero-Order Robust Optimization (zoRO) for robust constraint satisfaction with an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) for online parameter estimation, particularly payload mass.
- An optimization-based feasibility analysis computes admissible grasping and placement time windows to certify constraint satisfaction and provide quantitative operational timing guarantees.

## Results

- The RAMPC achieved a **100%** success rate across 800 simulated scenarios with up to 
yu00b150% payload mass deviation, significantly outperforming a nominal MPC which showed a **39%** success rate at **50%** deviation.
- Feasibility analysis determined worst-case admissible grasping windows (e.g., 4.3s minimum start, 3.4s minimum duration) and placement times (e.g., 10.28s longest transport), certifying operation under varied conditions.
- Real-world flight experiments validated the method, showing that pick-up and drop-off times remained within computed bounds and the EKF accurately estimated payload mass online, confirming practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly using a high-fidelity physics simulator (MuJoCo) as the predictive model simplifies the handling of complex, phase-dependent dynamics and reconfigurations within an NMPC framework.
> - The combined approach of robust optimization (zoRO) and online parameter estimation (EKF) enables adaptive conservatism in the NMPC, effectively managing uncertainties like varying payload mass while ensuring robust constraint satisfaction and real-time feasibility.
> - Pre-calculating admissible time windows through optimization-based feasibility analysis provides crucial operational guarantees, enhancing the safety and reliability of dynamic pick-and-place tasks.
