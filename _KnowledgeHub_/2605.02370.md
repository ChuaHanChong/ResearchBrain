# Robust Adaptive Predictive Control for Hook-Based Aerial Transportation Between Moving Platforms

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the HUN-REN Institute for Computer Science and Control and ETH Zurich developed a robust adaptive model predictive control (RAMPC) system for autonomous hook-based payload transportation between moving platforms. This system, leveraging a MuJoCo digital twin and online parameter estimation, achieved a 100% success rate in simulations across diverse scenarios and demonstrated consistent performance in real-world flight experiments.

## Problem

- Autonomous pick-and-place operations between two moving platforms using a passive hook-based gripper remained largely unaddressed.
- The coupled dynamics of aerial manipulators with payloads are highly nonlinear, involve abrupt configurational changes, and include complex contact interactions difficult to model precisely.
- Uncertainties, particularly in payload mass, significantly influence system dynamics, requiring robust control strategies to maintain performance and safety.

## Method

- A MuJoCo-based digital twin models the complex, reconfigurable dynamics of the hook-based transportation system directly within the NMPC framework.
- A Robust Adaptive NMPC (RAMPC) integrates Zero-Order Robust Optimization (zoRO) for robust constraint satisfaction with an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) for online parameter estimation, particularly payload mass.
- An optimization-based feasibility analysis computes admissible grasping and placement time windows to certify constraint satisfaction and provide quantitative operational timing guarantees.

## Results

- The RAMPC achieved a **100%** success rate across 800 simulated scenarios with up to ±50% payload mass deviation, significantly outperforming a nominal MPC which showed a **39%** success rate at **50%** deviation.
- Feasibility analysis determined worst-case admissible grasping windows (e.g., 4.3s minimum start, 3.4s minimum duration) and placement times (e.g., 10.28s longest transport), certifying operation under varied conditions.
- Real-world flight experiments validated the method, showing that pick-up and drop-off times remained within computed bounds and the EKF accurately estimated payload mass online, confirming practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly using a high-fidelity physics simulator (MuJoCo) as the predictive model simplifies the handling of complex, phase-dependent dynamics and reconfigurations within an NMPC framework.
> - The combined approach of robust optimization (zoRO) and online parameter estimation (EKF) enables adaptive conservatism in the NMPC, effectively managing uncertainties like varying payload mass while ensuring robust constraint satisfaction and real-time feasibility.
> - Pre-calculating admissible time windows through optimization-based feasibility analysis provides crucial operational guarantees, enhancing the safety and reliability of dynamic pick-and-place tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), particularly quadrotors, are increasingly employed for complex tasks involving robotic manipulation and physical interaction with the environment, such as infrastructure maintenance or package delivery. Within this domain, autonomous pick-and-place operations are a significant area of research. Current approaches for aerial pick-and-place predominantly fall into two categories: unmanned aerial manipulators (UAMs) and cable-suspended transportation systems.

UAMs, which integrate robotic extensions onto the quadcopter body, offer full actuation and versatility for reliable autonomous pick-and-place. However, their notable weight and power consumption characteristics can limit flight time and maneuverability. Conversely, cable-suspended systems have demonstrated agility and precision, with recent advancements moving from geometric control to nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC), often augmented with hybrid, adaptive, or learning-based extensions.

Despite these advancements, existing cable-based systems generally offer partial solutions to fully autonomous pick-and-place. For instance, magnetic grippers have been used for static payload pick-up but typically require external mechanisms for drop-off. Some research has addressed autonomous placement onto moving platforms, but in these cases, the payload is often pre-attached, bypassing the grasping problem. Consequently, the challenge of fully autonomous pick-and-place between two moving platforms using a passive gripper has largely remained unaddressed.

To bridge the gap between the autonomous grasping capabilities of UAMs and the agility and energy efficiency of cable suspension, a hook-equipped aerial manipulator has been previously introduced for static pick-and-place and extended to dynamic grasping from a moving platform. This platform features a lightweight, passive hook mounted on a rigid pole, connected to the quadrotor via an unactuated two-degrees-of-freedom (2 DoF) joint. While prior work demonstrated autonomous grasping and transportation with this setup, dynamic pick-and-place between two moving platforms persisted as a challenge. This paper seeks to advance the capabilities in this specific area, focusing on overcoming the complexities of dynamic pick-and-place with contact interactions, system configuration changes, and payload mass uncertainties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel model predictive control (MPC) approach for autonomous pick-and-place of a payload between two moving ground platforms using a quadrotor equipped with a hook-based manipulator. This involves the quadrotor autonomously approaching and grasping a payload (with a hook) from a moving platform, transporting it to a target location, and placing it onto another moving platform by detaching its hook. These actions must occur within prescribed grasping and placement time windows, while adhering to state and input constraints, and effectively managing uncertain payload mass.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in this complex scenario:
1. **Complex Dynamics:** The coupled dynamics of the aerial manipulator and its payload are highly nonlinear and involve contact interactions that are difficult to model accurately using traditional analytical methods.
2. **Configurational Changes:** The system operates across multiple distinct configurations (e.g., without payload, with payload attached), leading to abrupt changes in dynamic behavior that a control strategy must accommodate.
3. **Uncertainty Management:** Variations in payload mass directly influence the system's dynamics, necessitating a control design that can robustly handle such parametric uncertainties.
4. **Performance Requirements:** Achieving high performance (i.e., robust constraint satisfaction, precise operation, and computational efficiency) demands accurate dynamic models and an advanced control approach that leverages these models effectively.

To address these challenges, the paper proposes an NMPC-based solution with four specific contributions:
1. **Digital Twin for Predictive Modeling:** To accurately and easily capture the complex dynamics, a MuJoCo-based digital twin of the hook-based transportation system is utilized directly as the predictive model within the NMPC framework. This approach simplifies modeling and reconfiguration compared to analytical derivations.
2. **Robust Adaptive NMPC:** To address uncertainties from sources such as aerodynamics, contact dynamics, and variable payload mass, a robust adaptive NMPC algorithm is developed. This algorithm integrates zero-order robust optimization (zoRO) for uncertainty propagation with an extended Kalman filter (EKF) for online parameter estimation. This aims to ensure robust constraint satisfaction, maintain high performance, and improve computational efficiency.
3. **Optimization-Based Feasibility Analysis:** An approach is proposed to compute admissible pick-and-place time windows. This analysis aims to certify constraint satisfaction across a defined set of scenarios, providing quantitative timing and safety guarantees for the operation.
4. **Experimental Validation:** The effectiveness of the proposed method is to be demonstrated through extensive simulation studies and real-world flight experiments in dynamic pick-and-place scenarios between moving ground platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology integrates a high-fidelity digital twin with a robust adaptive nonlinear model predictive control (RAMPC) framework, complemented by an optimization-based feasibility analysis.

**Digital Twin Modeling (Section II):**
Instead of analytical derivations, the system dynamics are modeled using MuJoCo, a physics simulator. This approach is chosen for its simplicity in modeling, accuracy (considering geometry and inertia of all components), and reconfigurability. The model represents a quadcopter with a hook-based gripper and its payload. A key simplification involves smoothly approximating the contact dynamics between the drone's hook and the payload by augmenting the manipulator model with a 2 DoF revolute joint. The payload mass is treated as a parameter, set to its actual value when attached and to zero otherwise, to represent dynamics across different task phases. The resulting model is a 10-DoF system, described by a 20-dimensional discrete-time state vector, and includes control inputs for collective thrust and torque. Full state information is assumed to be available for the controller.

**MPC Design for Dynamic Pick-and-Place (Section III):**
The core control strategy is a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) designed to simultaneously generate reference trajectories and control inputs. The pick-and-place task is decomposed into five distinct motion phases with specific transition conditions:
1. **Approach:** Quadcopter approaches the payload until a grasping time window opens.
2. **Pick up:** Hook attaches to the payload within the grasping window.
3. **Transport:** Payload is moved towards the target platform until a placement time window opens.
4. **Place:** Payload is placed onto the target platform within the placement window.
5. **Unhook:** Hook detaches from the payload, completing the task.

An Optimal Control Problem (OCP) is formulated, minimizing a sum of stage costs (penalizing output deviations, velocity, and control inputs) subject to discrete-time dynamics, initial conditions, and state/input bounds, as well as nonlinear state constraints. The predictive model in the OCP dynamically switches based on the phase, accounting for the payload mass. Cost functions and constraints are specifically tailored for each phase:
- **Phase 1 (Approach):** Penalizes deviations in hook position and orientation relative to the payload hook, while a constraint ensures the hook stays outside a "forbidden" sphere around the payload.
- **Phase 2 (Pick up):** Similar cost as Phase 1 to guide the hook to the payload, with no additional nonlinear constraints.
- **Phase 3 (Transport):** Penalizes deviations in payload position and yaw angle relative to the drop-off platform, with an adaptive reference height (`z_safe,k`) to ensure the payload is transported above the platform. A constraint prevents premature drop-off.
- **Phase 4 (Place):** Similar cost and reference as Phase 3, but the premature drop-off constraint is deactivated to allow placement.
- **Phase 5 (Unhook):** Penalizes deviations in hook position and orientation relative to a setpoint away from the drop-off platform.

**Robust Adaptive MPC (RAMPC) (Section IV):**
To handle model uncertainties and disturbances (e.g., aerodynamics, neglected contact dynamics, uncertain payload mass), a robust adaptive MPC is proposed.
- **Uncertain Dynamics:** The predictive model is extended to include a vector of uncertain parameters ($\theta$) and an external disturbance term ($w_k$). Parametric uncertainty is modeled as an ellipsoid $E(\bar{\theta}, W_{\theta})$, and disturbances as $E(0, W_w)$.
- **Zero-Order Robust Optimization (zoRO):** This technique is used to ensure robust constraint satisfaction. It approximates the invariant set of the nonlinear system with ellipsoids and decouples constraint tightening from optimization for computational efficiency. The robust OCP minimizes the nominal cost while propagating ellipsoidal uncertainty sets through linearized dynamics and applying "backoff terms" to tighten constraints, thereby ensuring all possible trajectories within the uncertainty set satisfy the original constraints. To maintain real-time feasibility, an approximation recomputes these backoff terms iteratively by linearizing around the current nominal trajectory and propagating uncertainties.
- **Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) for Parameter Estimation:** An EKF is integrated for online estimation of uncertain parameters, particularly the payload mass. It models the parameter evolution as a random walk and uses system measurements to update the parameter estimate and its covariance. The EKF assumes process and measurement noise are normally distributed, and it uses a conversion based on the chi-squared distribution to match the ellipsoidal uncertainty bounds from zoRO with the EKF's covariance.
- **Integration of EKF Covariance and Uncertainty Bounds:** The EKF's estimated parameter covariance ($P_k$) is systematically integrated into the zoRO framework. The uncertainty bound $W_{\theta,k}$ used in zoRO's ellipsoidal parameter uncertainty is updated proportionally to $P_k$ at each EKF update, linking the estimated parameter uncertainty to the robustness margin. This allows for adaptive conservatism, reducing it as parameter estimates become more accurate.

**Admissible Time Windows and Feasibility Analysis (Section V):**
An optimization-based approach is used to determine admissible grasping and placement time windows ($[T_g, \bar{T}_g]$ and $[T_p, \bar{T}_p]$). This analysis aims to certify the feasibility and safety of the RAMPC over a bounded set of admissible scenarios ($\mathcal{H}$ for grasping, $\mathcal{M}$ for placement), characterized by parameters like initial quadcopter position, and moving platform trajectories and speeds, and payload mass.
- **Grasping Window:** An algorithm computes the smallest feasible start time ($T_g^*$) and smallest required window duration ($\bar{T}_g - T_g$) that ensure successful grasping for all scenarios in $\mathcal{H}$. This involves iteratively testing candidate windows, running closed-loop simulations, and computing the worst-case grasping time $T_g^*$ and worst-case constraint violation using Bayesian Optimization (BO) to efficiently explore the scenario space.
- **Placement Window:** An analogous procedure determines the smallest admissible placement end time ($T_p^*$) by transporting the payload from the pick-up configuration to the drop-off platform for all scenarios in $\mathcal{M}$. BO is again used to find the worst-case drop-off time and constraint violation. The analysis verifies that the RAMPC remains feasible and satisfies all constraints for all scenarios within the computed windows.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Simulation Study (Section VI):**
The proposed methodology was evaluated in a MuJoCo simulation environment featuring a 7 × 5 × 3 m flight space, F1Tenth ground vehicles with custom trailers, and paperclip-shaped trajectories for the moving platforms. The MPC employed the `acados` SQP-RTI solver on an Intel i9 CPU, with a sampling time of 0.05s and a control frequency of 50Hz. A deliberate model mismatch was introduced by using a smoothened hook–payload interaction in the predictive model for NMPC, while the main simulation loop used MuJoCo's internal contact model, testing the controller's robustness.

- **Admissible Time Windows:**
 - For grasping, varying initial quadcopter position, moving platform start point, and speed, the computed worst-case admissible grasping time window yielded $T_g^* = 4.3$s and $\bar{T}_g^* = 7.7$s. This implies a minimum start time of 4.3s and a minimum duration of 3.4s are required for successful grasping from any initial position on the flight space boundary.
 - For placement, considering similar variations plus payload mass ($m_L \in [0.05, 0.2]$kg), the worst-case drop-off time was $T_p^* = 10.28$s. This represents the longest time needed to transport and place the payload under the given assumptions. The feasibility analysis demonstrated that pick-and-place is consistently accomplished within these bounds.
- **Comparison of Nominal and RAMPC:**
 - Across 800 simulated scenarios with payload mass deviations ranging from 0% to ±50%, the **RAMPC achieved a 100% success rate**.
 - In contrast, the nominal MPC (without robustness or adaptation) exhibited a 92% success rate even with 0% mass deviation, which progressively decreased to 39% at 50% mass deviation. This highlights the effectiveness of the robust adaptive components in maintaining feasibility and success under uncertainty.
 - The average cost of RAMPC was approximately 25% higher than the nominal controller, a trade-off attributed to the conservativeness introduced by zoRO's uncertainty propagation.
 - Computation times were found to be feasible for real-time operation, with RAMPC requiring only an additional 1.5ms on average and in the worst-case compared to the nominal MPC, maintaining a control frequency of 50Hz.
- **Representative Scenarios:**
 - Three dedicated scenarios with varied initial positions, platform speeds, time windows, and payload masses were tested. The EKF, initialized with a nominal mass of 0.05kg, demonstrated rapid convergence to the actual payload mass value in all simulations, remaining precise throughout the transportation phase. Pick-up and drop-off times for these scenarios fell within their respective bounds.

**Flight Experiments (Section VII):**
Real-world flight experiments were conducted using a Crazyflie Bolt flight controller for low-level control (500Hz PD controller) and an Optitrack system for high-precision pose information. The three representative scenarios from the simulation study were replicated.

- The actual pick-up and drop-off times in flight experiments were observed to be within their respective bounds, consistent with simulation results.
- The EKF's online payload mass estimation in flight experiments showed convergence, albeit slightly slower (~1s) than in simulations. However, the estimation consistently remained within the ±3σ confidence interval and accurately approximated the actual payload mass after convergence.
- Qualitative observations and recorded video footage demonstrated the system’s ability to successfully execute all motion phases, including tracking the pick-up platform, attaching the hooks, tracking the drop-off platform, and detaching the hooks, with consistent performance across the tested payload mass variations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a advancement in the field of autonomous aerial manipulation, particularly for dynamic pick-and-place tasks. The systematic integration of a MuJoCo-based digital twin with a robust adaptive nonlinear MPC (RAMPC) framework addresses several critical challenges that limited previous approaches.

The primary significance lies in the demonstrated ability to achieve **robust constraint satisfaction and high performance** under significant model uncertainties, such as variable payload mass and unmodeled contact dynamics. The use of zero-order robust optimization (zoRO) ensures that the system remains within safe operating limits, while the extended Kalman filter (EKF) adaptively refines parameter estimates, reducing conservatism and improving tracking precision. This combination offers a practical solution for real-world scenarios where precise system parameters may not be fully known or may vary.

The **optimization-based feasibility analysis** for admissible time windows is a notable contribution, providing quantitative safety guarantees and operational timing insights crucial for deploying such systems in logistics, manufacturing, or disaster relief. By systematically determining the boundaries within which the system can reliably operate, it enhances the safety and predictability of complex aerial manipulation tasks.

The **validation through both extensive simulations and real-world flight experiments** strengthens the credibility of the proposed method. The consistent performance observed across varied scenarios and payload mass deviations, especially the 100% success rate in simulations under challenging conditions, indicates the robustness and practical applicability of RAMPC. The real-world experiments further confirm the approach's effectiveness in a physical setting, despite the inherent complexities of real-world dynamics.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for several application areas:
- **Automated Logistics:** Enables fully autonomous, dynamic package delivery and material handling in warehouses or urban environments, even between moving vehicles.
- **Infrastructure Maintenance:** Facilitates tasks like repairing high-voltage lines or wind turbine blades, where precise manipulation from a moving platform or while interacting with moving components is required.
- **Disaster Response:** Could support rapid deployment and transfer of supplies in dynamic and uncertain environments.

Future work outlined, such as fully onboard deployment using vision-based sensing and extension to outdoor settings, indicates a clear path toward broader and more independent application of these aerial manipulation capabilities. By reducing reliance on external sensing infrastructure, the technology could become more versatile and cost-effective.
