# Franca: Nested Matryoshka Clustering for Scalable Visual Representation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Franca introduces the first fully open-source vision foundation model, providing all code, data, and trained weights, and demonstrates performance that matches or exceeds proprietary state-of-the-art models like DINOv2. The model achieves this through innovations in multi-granular representation learning via nested Matryoshka clustering and a post-pretraining technique called RASA for disentangling spatial biases.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) often rely on proprietary datasets and withheld training details, hindering reproducibility, accessibility, and scientific progress.
- Traditional clustering-based self-supervised learning methods struggle with semantic ambiguity and fixed granularity, limiting their ability to capture diverse levels of detail in visual data.
- Vision Transformers (ViTs) can develop unintended spatial biases, entangling patch position with semantic content, which reduces generalization for dense prediction tasks.

## Method

- Developed **Franca**, a fully open-source framework (code, data, and weights) for ==Vision Foundation Models==, trained exclusively on publicly available datasets like ImageNet-21K and LAION-COCO.
- Introduced ==Matryoshka Representations== through a ==multi-head clustering projector== that operates on progressively truncated subspaces of ==ViT== embeddings, inducing a coarse-to-fine hierarchy of semantic abstraction.
- Implemented **RASA** (==Removal of Absolute Spatial Attributes==), a lightweight post-pretraining method that iteratively removes linearly predictable positional biases from dense representations with zero inference overhead.

## Results

- **Franca** matches or surpasses the performance of proprietary SOTA models (DINOv2, CLIP, SigLIPv2) across classification, robustness, various segmentation benchmarks (e.g., **+**5%** mIoU** over DINOv2-L on Pascal VOC), and 3D understanding tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Matryoshka representations, high-resolution fine-tuning, and RASA each contribute cumulative performance gains across tasks.
- The framework achieves strong performance with significantly fewer computational resources (e.g., Franca-G trains in **~20K GPU-hours**, an order of magnitude less than MetaCLIP for similar scale), enhancing accessibility for researchers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving state-of-the-art VFM performance with full transparency and open-source resources is feasible, democratizing advanced vision research.
> - Multi-granular, hierarchical representations learned via nested Matryoshka clustering enhance feature flexibility and efficiency for tasks requiring varying levels of detail, without increasing model size.
> - Explicitly disentangling spatial attributes from semantic content in dense representations significantly improves generalization and robustness for downstream tasks, especially dense prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the field of self-supervised learning (SSL) for Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), which aims to train highly generalizable visual representations using abundant image-only data. The authors identify a gap in the current landscape: while SSL has produced powerful VFMs, many state-of-the-art models, such as DINOv2, SEER, billion-scale MAE, and SigLIP 2, are trained on proprietary datasets and often restrict access to their training code or intermediate weights. This practice impedes reproducibility, accessibility, and scientific progress.

Franca seeks to address this by developing a fully open-source VFM (data, code, weights) that performs comparably to or exceeds these proprietary counterparts. This initiative aligns with efforts by projects like OpenCLIP and Web-SSL, which have also aimed to provide more transparent and accessible foundation models, primarily in the vision-language domain. Franca extends this transparency to vision-only foundation models.

Technically, the work builds upon clustering-based SSL methods, particularly drawing inspiration from DINOv2, which uses optimal-transport clustering (Sinkhorn-Knopp) for pseudo-label assignment. The authors note that existing clustering approaches often struggle with the inherent ambiguity of semantic categories and rely on very large codebooks or produce fixed-size embeddings that lack flexibility for various downstream tasks or computational constraints. Franca introduces Matryoshka representations to address this by enabling multi-granular, hierarchical clustering.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the problem of spatial biases in vision transformer (ViT) models, where patch features can inadvertently encode positional information rather than purely semantic content. This issue, which can hinder generalization, has been explored in prior work on invariant representations and disentanglement. Franca proposes a post-training disentanglement strategy to mitigate this. By focusing on openness, multi-granular representation, and spatial disentanglement, Franca aims to advance the transparency, efficiency, and robustness of SSL for visual representation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and release Franca, a Vision Foundation Model that is fully open-source across its code, data, and weights. The authors explicitly state their goal for Franca to match, and in many cases surpass, the performance of existing state-of-the-art proprietary models, including DINOv2, CLIP, and SigLIP 2. This objective is driven by several motivations:

Firstly, a significant motivation stems from the observed limitations in the transparency and accessibility of leading VFM research. Many high-performing models depend on proprietary datasets and do not release their full training code or intermediate checkpoints. This lack of openness creates substantial barriers to reproducibility, independent verification, and the broader scientific community's ability to analyze and build upon these models. Franca aims to establish a new standard for transparent VFM research by providing a completely open framework, including the release of intermediate checkpoints to allow insight into training dynamics and emergent properties.

Secondly, the research targets critical technical limitations within existing self-supervised learning clustering methods. Current approaches, such as those relying on optimal-transport clustering for pseudo-label assignment, often overlook the inherent ambiguity in cluster semantics. For instance, a "vehicle" category could be semantically grouped in various ways (by manufacturer, color, or model year), and existing methods tend to address this by simply increasing the size of codebooks, which can be inefficient and inflexible. The authors are motivated to overcome this by developing a more sophisticated clustering mechanism that can capture a hierarchy of semantic abstractions without increasing the model's overall parameter count.

Thirdly, the work addresses the issue of positional biases in dense representations generated by ViT models. These models, due to their fixed patch layouts and positional embeddings, can entangle spatial location with semantic content. This entanglement can lead to features that are less generalizable, particularly when objects appear in unexpected or atypical spatial contexts. The motivation is to develop a lightweight and effective method to explicitly remove these positional biases, thereby creating cleaner feature spaces that emphasize purely semantic information. This is expected to improve performance on tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding, such as dense segmentation and in-context learning.

Finally, a broader motivation is to demonstrate that high-quality visual representations can be learned efficiently and scalably. The authors aim to achieve strong performance at modest model sizes without relying on teacher supervision or distillation from much larger models, which can be computationally intensive and limit accessibility for researchers with fewer resources. By combining full openness with high performance and efficiency, Franca endeavors to make advanced VFM research more democratic and facilitate broader innovation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Franca is designed as a scalable open-source self-supervised learning framework, primarily building upon the DINOv2 architecture and pretrained on large public image datasets. Its methodology incorporates three key innovations: Matryoshka Representations, CyclicMask, and RASA.

**Matryoshka Representations for Efficient Multi-Granular Learning:**
This component addresses the limitation of fixed-size embeddings in standard SSL models. Instead of producing a single-resolution feature, Franca adopts Matryoshka representations, which involve nesting progressively truncated subspaces of a high-dimensional embedding.
The core idea is as follows: The Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder generates feature embeddings $Z \in \mathbb{R}^d$. These embeddings are then "sliced" into progressively smaller subsets of dimensions, such as $d$, $d/2$, $d/4$, $d/8$, and $d/16$. A crucial innovation in Franca's approach is that, unlike standard Matryoshka applications, it attaches a *dedicated projection head and a corresponding clustering objective* to each of these feature subspaces. Each head is designed with a proportionally smaller number of prototypes as the feature dimension decreases, encouraging a coarse-to-fine hierarchy of semantic abstraction. A total loss function is computed as the sum of cross-entropy losses from each of these multi-granularity clustering heads. This approach aims to allow different slices to specialize in capturing distinct prototypes and semantic granularities, from global semantics in coarse heads to local structures in fine heads, without increasing the base model's size.

**CyclicMask for Balancing Spatial Distribution of Visible Patches:**
This strategy is integrated into the masked image modeling (MIM) component of Franca. Traditional MIM masking techniques, such as random or block masking, can result in fragmented visible regions or inadvertently bias the model towards learning features at specific spatial locations. CyclicMask addresses this by circularly shifting the masked patches. As depicted in the methodology, this process involves defining a mask region (e.g., a block) and then applying a circular shift to it across spatial axes. This prevents the model from consistently focusing on the same spatial locations, thereby encouraging it to learn more robust and semantically driven features by breaking simple spatial continuity during training.

**RASA (Removal of Absolute Spatial Attributes):**
RASA is a lightweight post-pretraining technique designed to disentangle spatial information from semantic content in the patch embeddings produced by ViT models. It operates on a frozen backbone after initial pretraining. The procedure is iterative:
1. **Position Prediction:** In each iteration, a linear prediction head is optimized to predict the normalized 2D coordinates of patches ($y_i$) from their corresponding embeddings ($Z_i$). This is done by minimizing a mean squared error loss.
2. **Basis Extraction:** The row vectors of the learned linear projection matrix are orthonormalized using Gram-Schmidt to form basis vectors ($u_r, u_c$) that span a "positional subspace."
3. **Orthogonal Projection and Subtraction:** The component of each patch feature vector that lies within this identified positional subspace is then removed. This is achieved by projecting the feature vector onto the subspace and subtracting this projection from the original vector, yielding a refined embedding $Z^{(t+1)}_i$ that is less correlated with positional information.
This iterative refinement process is typically run for a small number of iterations (e.g., 8-9) until the positional prediction loss saturates. Crucially, due to its linear construction, the entire RASA transformation can be represented as a single matrix multiplication, which can be integrated into the final layer weights of the ViT backbone. This design ensures zero inference overhead, making it practical for deployment.

**Training Setup:**
Franca is pretrained using ViT-B, ViT-L, and ViT-G encoders (patch size 14, no registers). Pretraining is done from scratch for 625K iterations at 224x224 resolution. ViT-B uses ImageNet-21K (13.1M images), while ViT-L and ViT-G use LAION-600M (600M images) — a public, image-only subset of ReLAION-2B. DINOv2-style multi-crop augmentations are employed. High-resolution fine-tuning is performed for ViT-B and ViT-L at 364x364 and 518x518 resolutions on a mix of ImageNet-1K, ADE20K, COCO, KITTI, and VOC datasets. RASA post-training is applied specifically on Pascal VOC.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents Franca as a fully open-source Vision Foundation Model that demonstrates competitive, and often superior, performance compared to state-of-the-art proprietary models across various visual tasks.

**Openness and Performance:**
Franca achieves its primary objective of being fully open-source (code, data, weights) while matching or surpassing the performance of models like DINOv2, CLIP, and SigLIP 2. The release of intermediate checkpoints also enhances transparency.

**Effectiveness of Technical Innovations:**
Ablation studies (Figure 2) demonstrate that each of Franca's main components—Matryoshka representations, high-resolution fine-tuning (HRFT), and RASA—contributes to consistent performance gains. Matryoshka representations yield notable improvements, particularly for dense prediction tasks.

**Matryoshka Representations:**
- **Hierarchical Learning:** PCA visualizations (Figure 3) illustrate that Franca, even when features are truncated to smaller dimensions (e.g., dim/16), retains coherent part-level semantic structures. This contrasts with DINOv2, which tends to lose semantic alignment under similar compression.
- **Feature Compression:** k-NN classification accuracy on ImageNet-v2 (Figure 4, Table 9) shows Franca consistently outperforming DINOv2 across various embedding slice levels. Franca maintains higher performance even under significant feature compression (e.g., dim/64), indicating that its Matryoshka-style training enables more effective hierarchical encoding where higher-level semantics are preserved in lower-dimensional subspaces.

**RASA (Removal of Absolute Spatial Attributes):**
- **Positional Debiasing:** An analysis of spatial entropy per cluster (Figure 6) reveals that DINOv2 clusters exhibit low spatial entropy, suggesting a strong positional bias in its features. RASA post-training increases this spatial entropy, indicating a reduction in location-driven feature assignments.
- **Performance Gains:** RASA provides consistent, albeit modest, gains across several downstream tasks (Table 2, Table 13). Ablations on dataset size, learning rate, and training epochs for the RASA head indicate its efficiency and robustness, requiring minimal data (around 10k images) and few iterations (approximately 8) to achieve optimal debiasing without significant architectural or computational overhead.

**Dense Tasks:**
Franca demonstrates strong capabilities across a range of dense prediction tasks:
- **In-Context Learning and Linear Segmentation (Table 3a):** Franca consistently achieves high mIoU scores on Pascal VOC and ADE20K. For instance, Franca-L/14 surpasses DINOv2-L (distilled from DINOv2-G and trained on LVD-142M) by up to +5% mIoU on Pascal VOC and +1% on ADE20K. Notably, Franca-G achieves comparable performance despite not having seen these datasets during its specific finetuning stage. SigLIP 2 showed comparatively poor performance on segmentation, suggesting limitations in spatial localization.
- **Video Object Segmentation (Table 3b):** Franca achieves the best results on the DAVIS benchmark, indicating the learning of temporally stable features.
- **Unsupervised Object Discovery (Table 3b):** On TokenCut, Franca-B and Franca-L achieve higher CorLoc scores than DINOv2 variants, suggesting the production of more spatially coherent features.
- **PCA of Patch Features (Figure 8) and Self-Attention Maps (Figure 11):** Qualitative visualizations show Franca producing dense, semantically coherent color segments aligned with object boundaries, with consistent coloring for similar parts across instances. DINOv2 features, in contrast, appear fragmented. Franca's self-attention maps also show improved object localization compared to DINOv2, even with registers.

**Classification and Robustness:**
- **Image Classification (Table 4):** Franca performs comparably to DINOv2 on ImageNet-1K using k-NN and linear probing, even though Franca's ViT-B and ViT-L models are not distilled from a larger teacher (unlike DINOv2's official versions). Franca-G matches the performance of Web-SSL-7B with significantly fewer parameters.
- **Robustness (Table 4):** Franca-G shows strong robustness on ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, and Sketch datasets, performing comparably to DINOv2-G and outperforming OpenCLIP-G.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection (Figure 9):** Franca consistently outperforms DINOv2 on the OpenOOD benchmark, demonstrating robust scaling for OOD detection.

**3D Awareness:**
- **Gaussian Splatting (Table 5):** Franca achieves the best performance in geometry-related metrics within the Feat2GS framework, suggesting strong 3D geometric awareness.
- **Keypoint Matching and Depth Estimation (Table 10):** Franca demonstrates strong performance on SPair-71k for keypoint correspondence, outperforming DINOv2 (trained on IN-21K). It also achieves comparable results to distilled DINOv2 on NYUv2 for monocular depth estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Franca represents a notable contribution to the field of visual representation learning, primarily through its emphasis on openness, its technical innovations, and its demonstrated performance.

**Promotion of Open Science:** The most direct significance of Franca is its commitment to full transparency by releasing code, data (using publicly available datasets like LAION-600M), and model weights, including intermediate checkpoints. This directly addresses the existing "significant barrier to reproducibility, accessibility, and scientific progress" that the authors identify in the VFM landscape. By providing a completely transparent and verifiable framework, Franca lowers the entry barrier for researchers, facilitates independent verification of results, and enables the community to build upon its foundations without intellectual property or access restrictions. This could foster a more collaborative and accelerated research environment.

**Advancing Self-Supervised Learning Techniques:** Franca introduces two key technical innovations that enhance SSL:
- **Nested Matryoshka Clustering:** This approach provides a method for learning hierarchical, multi-granular representations efficiently. By enabling features to be accessed at different levels of semantic abstraction and compression, it offers flexibility for diverse downstream tasks and computational constraints. This contributes to the development of more adaptable and memory-efficient visual features, which is critical for real-world applications.
- **RASA (Removal of Absolute Spatial Attributes):** This lightweight post-pretraining technique effectively disentangles positional biases from semantic content in vision transformer features. Cleaner, spatially invariant representations are valuable for improving the robustness and generalization of models, particularly in dense prediction tasks where reliance on spurious spatial cues can be detrimental. The fact that RASA can be applied post-training with zero inference overhead makes it a practical and impactful addition to the VFM toolkit.

**Challenging Proprietary Model Dominance:** Franca demonstrates that a fully open-source VFM, trained on publicly available data, can achieve performance that matches or surpasses state-of-the-art proprietary models (such as DINOv2 and SigLIP 2) across a broad spectrum of tasks, including image classification, diverse segmentation benchmarks, robustness evaluations, out-of-distribution detection, and 3D understanding. This finding suggests that proprietary datasets and closed development pipelines are not prerequisites for achieving leading performance, potentially shifting the paradigm towards more open and community-driven VFM development.

**Accessibility and Scalability:** By achieving high performance at modest model sizes and without relying on distillation from much larger teacher models, Franca offers a more accessible and scalable solution for researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources. This can democratize access to powerful visual foundation models, enabling a wider range of institutions and individuals to contribute to and benefit from VFM advancements.

**Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly points to the relevance of Franca's technical contributions, particularly RASA, for emerging frameworks like DINOv3, which still face challenges with positional bias. This indicates that Franca's innovations have foundational value and can be integrated into future advanced SSL models, suggesting a path for continued impact on the field.
