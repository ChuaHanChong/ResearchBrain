# DSPv2: Improved Dense Policy for Effective and Generalizable Whole-body Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong, Astribot, and Tsinghua University developed DSPv2, an improved dense policy for whole-body mobile manipulation, demonstrating superior performance with 80% pick success and 60% place success, and 100% deliver success in real-world tasks. The architecture achieves robust generalization to unseen objects and environments by effectively fusing 2D semantic and 3D spatial features and using coherent, bidirectional autoregressive action generation.

## Problem

- Imitation learning struggles with complex, high-degree-of-freedom whole-body mobile manipulation for household robots in diverse, unconstrained environments.
- Existing whole-body mobile manipulation solutions often inadequately utilize multi-view observations, leading to degraded task accuracy and limited policy-level generalization.
- Coupled dynamics of whole-body systems frequently cause error amplification and motion incoordination, especially with diffusion-based action generation that lacks effective inter-component conditioning.

## Method

- **DSPv2** introduces a policy architecture that employs a ==Sparse 3D Encoder== for voxel-level spatial features from point clouds and a ==LoRA-tuned DINOv2== backbone for multi-view 2D semantic features from RGB images.
- A ==Q-former mechanism== aligns the sparse 3D spatial features with the dense 2D semantic features, creating a comprehensive, geometry-aware visual representation robust to observation shifts.
- A ==Dense Head== extends the Dense Policy paradigm with ==bidirectional autoregressive generation==, enabling precise and coherent actions for the 25-DoF whole-body system by allowing inter-component conditioning and mitigating error propagation.

## Results

- DSPv2 achieved superior real-world manipulation performance, including **80%** pick and **60%** place success in 'Pick and Place', and **100%** success in 'Deliver' tasks, outperforming baseline methods by substantial margins (e.g., WB-WIMA at **50%** pick and **25%** place, **45%** deliver).
- The policy demonstrated robust generalization across various spatial and semantic understanding tests (e.g., changes in table height, object shape, lighting, color, and scene), maintaining higher success rates with smaller performance drops compared to other approaches.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the 2D-3D feature fusion and the Dense Head's bidirectional autoregressive generation are critical components, contributing to improved task performance and enhanced generalization compared to alternative vision encoder fine-tuning strategies or action generation heads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning sparse 3D spatial features with dense multi-view 2D semantic features through a Q-former significantly enhances environmental perception and task execution accuracy for mobile manipulators.
> - Combining a voxel-level 3D encoder with a LoRA-tuned vision foundation model provides robust policy-level generalization capabilities for whole-body mobile manipulation, even with limited task-specific data.
> - Bidirectional autoregressive action generation (Dense Policy) effectively mitigates error amplification and motion incoordination in high-DoF whole-body systems by allowing components to condition on other components' planned actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on DSPv2 addresses a recognized gap in the field of robotic manipulation, specifically concerning whole-body mobile manipulation. While imitation learning has demonstrated capabilities in single or dual-arm manipulation, its application to complex, high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) whole-body household robots remains challenging. Existing imitation learning approaches often focus on constrained, tabletop scenarios, limiting their applicability to the diverse and expansive environments expected of mobile manipulators.

Current whole-body mobile manipulation solutions, such as WB-WIMA, typically rely on processing merged multi-view point clouds, often with sparse sampling, and generating actions via diffusion models. This approach has shown limitations in fully utilizing multi-view observations, which can degrade task execution accuracy, and has not effectively addressed policy-level generalization. Furthermore, the coupled dynamics of whole-body systems frequently lead to issues like error amplification and motion incoordination, particularly with diffusion-based action generation where components cannot effectively condition on the fully denoised states of other components.

DSPv2 builds upon recent advancements in 3D policies that employ sparse convolutions for voxel-level feature extraction, aiming to balance information preservation and feature robustness. It also extends the concept of autoregressive action generation, specifically the Dense Policy paradigm, which has shown promise in mitigating error accumulation compared to diffusion models. The paper positions itself by proposing a novel architecture that integrates effective observation processing, robust generalization through 2D-3D feature fusion, and coherent action generation for whole-body mobile manipulation, aiming to overcome the limitations of current methods and facilitate the deployment of such robots in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing DSPv2 are:

- **Effective Processing of Complex Observations:** Whole-body mobile manipulation robots operate in broad and varied environments, necessitating comprehensive scene awareness often achieved through multiple cameras. The complexity and diverse distribution of multi-view observations pose a significant challenge for policy learning. Existing methods that rely on sparse sampling of 3D vision backbones often limit the utilization of rich multi-view data, thereby degrading task execution accuracy. DSPv2 aims to develop an effective encoding scheme that fully leverages multi-view observations, specifically by aligning 3D spatial features with 2D semantic features to achieve a more comprehensive perception of the environment.

- **Achieving Robust Generalization:** A critical hurdle for practical applicability of robotic policies is their ability to generalize to unseen objects, environments, and conditions not present in the training data. Current whole-body mobile manipulation policies largely lack robust generalization capabilities. This work seeks to propose a generalization approach for whole-body downstream policies that demonstrates strong generalization even with limited task-specific data, by incorporating lightweight vision foundation models and a specialized feature fusion mechanism.

- **Generating Coherent Whole-Body Actions:** Whole-body household robots possess a high number of degrees of freedom (DoF) and require the coordination of multiple components (dual-arm manipulation, whole-body motion, chassis navigation). The coupled dynamics of these systems make them prone to error amplification and motion incoordination, where minor misalignments can rapidly undermine the coherence of the entire motion. Traditional diffusion models, when applied to whole-body systems, can exacerbate this issue by failing to enable adequate conditioning between component actions during denoising. DSPv2 is motivated to extend the Dense Policy paradigm to this domain, aiming to generate precise and coherent actions by enabling inter-component coordination and mitigating error propagation.

In summary, the motivation stems from the significant difficulties in adapting imitation learning for whole-body mobile manipulation, driven by the robot's morphological complexity, the expansive and diverse task environments, and the inherent challenges in processing observations, generalizing skills, and generating coordinated, error-resilient actions. DSPv2 seeks to address these issues through a novel policy architecture that prioritizes effective perception, robust generalization, and coherent action generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DSPv2 is a novel policy architecture designed for whole-body mobile manipulation, incorporating an effective observation encoding, a feature alignment mechanism, and a dense action generation head. The robot platform used is a 25-DoF system, including a 3-DoF chassis, a 4-DoF torso, dual 7-DoF arms, a 2-DoF head, and 2 grippers. Actions are defined as future pose commands for each component relative to the base over a horizon of *T* timesteps.

**A. Observation Encoding**
The policy processes three main types of observations at each timestep *t*: RGB images (*I*t), an uncolored point cloud (*P*t), and the current robot state (*S*t).

- **3D Encoding:** An uncolored point cloud from the head camera is first projected into the base frame. A Sparse 3D Encoder, based on a sparse convolutional network, extracts voxel-level spatial features (*F*P) from this point cloud. This process generates a sparse representation, with fewer than 300 tokens. Each feature token is augmented with a positional embedding (*Pos*P) derived from its spatial location using a sine-cosine function.

- **2D Encoding:** RGB images from multiple camera views (head, torso, dual wrists) are encoded using a DINOv2-base backbone. To adapt the foundation model for the specific task while retaining its general semantic representations for generalization, it is fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation). This encoder generates patch-level semantic feature maps (*Fv1-vnI*) from *n* views, which are then augmented with learnable positional embeddings (*Pos*I) to encode the spatial location of each patch.

- **Robot State Encoding:** The current robot state (*S*t) comprises the poses of its components, excluding the chassis's pose. This exclusion is intended to encourage the policy to rely on environmental context for navigation rather than its own trajectory history, potentially improving learning. During training, remaining pose inputs are randomly masked with a 30% probability to prevent overfitting. An MLP then encodes this state information into feature space (*F*S).

**B. Feature Aligning**
To fuse the distinct 3D spatial and 2D semantic features, a Q-former architecture is employed. This module is designed to align sparse 3D tokens with dense 2D features.
- **Query Generation:** A set of 300 learnable query tokens (*Qlearn*) are initialized. These tokens are combined with the spatial positional embeddings (*Pos*P) from the 3D encoding to create spatially-aware queries (*Q*).
- **Cross-Attention Mechanism:** These spatially-aware queries (*Q*) then perform cross-attention with the multi-view 2D feature maps (*Fv1-vnI*) and their corresponding positional embeddings (*Pos*I), which serve as keys and values. This process allows positional information to flow from the 3D voxel grid to the 2D patch grids, effectively indexing and retrieving semantic features relevant to the spatial voxels.
- **Feature Fusion:** The output of this cross-attention operation, representing the retrieved semantic features, is added to the original sparse spatial features (*F*P) to form the final fused visual representation (*F*V). This decoupled approach aims to allow independent processing of spatial features and color features (learned by the foundation model), while the fusion provides geometry-aware, multi-view features.

**C. Dense Generation**
The fused observation features (*F*V*) and robot state features (*F*S*) are fed into a Dense Head, which implements the Dense Policy paradigm.
- **Bidirectional Autoregressive Generation:** The Dense Head performs coarse-to-fine autoregressive generation of the future action sequence (*A*t:t+T). It employs a bidirectional attention mechanism across the temporal dimension. This allows actions for any given robot component to be conditioned not only on past observations and states but also on the already-determined actions of other components and keyframes within the predicted trajectory.
- **Error Mitigation:** This bidirectional autoregressive approach is designed to mitigate error amplification and incoordination that can arise in whole-body systems, particularly in contrast to diffusion models where components might be conditioned on noised values. By observing raw actions and leveraging temporal dependencies, the Dense Policy aims to improve inter-component coordination and achieve precise and robust action prediction. The logarithmic inference complexity of Dense Policy also reduces inference latency compared to hierarchical diffusion structures.

The overall process aims to predict the future action sequence *A*t:t+T by maximizing the conditional probability *P(A|O)* over a dataset of expert demonstrations, following a standard behavior cloning framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research involved extensive real-world experiments on the Astribot-S1 robot, evaluating DSPv2 against baselines across five manipulation tasks and five generalization tests. Each task was evaluated over 20 trials, with 100 expert demonstrations collected per task.

**A. Effective Manipulation (Table I)**
DSPv2 demonstrated superior performance on the majority of evaluation tasks compared to baselines (WB-WIMA, DP3, and DP).

- **Pick and Place:** DSPv2 achieved 80% pick success and 60% place success, significantly outperforming WB-WIMA (50% pick, 25% place), DP3 (40% pick, 10% place), and DP (30% pick, 20% place). The authors attribute this to DSPv2's comprehensive environmental perception, allowing precise navigation and consistent grasping of the basket's edge.
- **Deliver:** DSPv2 achieved 100% success, surpassing WB-WIMA (45%), DP3 (10%), and DP (55%). This task, requiring grasping a bottle from a low table, highlights DSPv2's ability to coordinate chassis, torso, and arm movements effectively.
- **Sort:** DSPv2 achieved 90% success in Stack-I and 25% in Stack-II, while achieving 100% overall success. Other methods showed lower performance in Stack-I and particularly low in Stack-II. DSPv2's fine-grained feature representation enabled high-precision alignment required for stacking.
- **Bowling:** DSPv2 achieved 80% grasp success and 50% hit success, leading to 90% overall success, matching WB-WIMA's overall success but showing better grasping. The coordinated release phase further highlighted DSPv2's motion coherence.
- **Cart Pushing:** DSPv2 achieved 90% success, matching WB-WIMA and DP. Its superior spatial perception contributed to effective obstacle avoidance during navigation.

The enhanced performance is primarily attributed to three factors: effective environmental perception through 2D-3D feature fusion, precise action generation resulting from finer-grained features, and coherent whole-body motion due to the bidirectional autoregressive nature of the Dense Head.

**B. Generalization Ability (Table II)**
DSPv2 exhibited robust generalization across five challenging tests, maintaining leading performance with minimal performance drops compared to the original task settings.

- **Spatial Understanding (Spatial Arrangement, Object Shape):** When table height or object shapes were altered, DSPv2 maintained higher success rates with smaller drops (e.g., Pick and Place Place: 60% → 20% for DSPv2 vs. 25% → 0% for WB-WIMA). This is attributed to DSPv2's voxel-level 3D processing and the Q-former's ability to bind voxels to multi-view semantic features, making its representation robust to point cloud shifts. A limitation was noted for Sort Stack-II, where all methods, including DSPv2, failed for actions significantly outside the expert distribution.
- **Semantic Understanding (Light, Object Color, Scene):** When ambient lighting, object colors, or scene backgrounds were changed, DSPv2 showed less degradation (e.g., Deliver Pick: 80% → 60% for DSPv2 vs. 50% → 30% for WB-WIMA; Cart Pushing Success: 90% → 80% for DSPv2 vs. 90% → 60% for WB-WIMA). This is due to the decoupled processing of color-agnostic 3D point clouds and the use of a LoRA-tuned DINOv2 for generalizable 2D semantic features, which minimizes degradation from visual domain shifts.

**C. Ablation Studies**

- **Vision Encoder (Figure 4):** An ablation on DINOv2 fine-tuning strategies for the 2D vision foundation model showed that LoRA fine-tuning offered the best balance. In the original Cart Pushing task, both full fine-tuning and LoRA achieved 90% success, while freezing DINOv2 resulted in 70%. In the scene generalization test, LoRA-tuned DINOv2 maintained 80% success, outperforming full fine-tuning (60% success, due to loss of semantic priors) and frozen DINOv2 (60% success, due to insufficient task-level information and destabilized 3D-2D binding).
- **Action Head (Tables III and IV):** Replacing DSPv2's Dense Head with a diffusion head demonstrated that while the encoder itself is effective (outperforming baselines), the Dense Head provided superior performance in both original tasks and generalization tests. For instance, in Pick and Place Place, DSPv2 (Dense Head) achieved 60% (original) and 20% (generalized), compared to DSPv2 w. Diff Head's 30% (original) and 0% (generalized). This supports the argument that the Dense Policy's ability to leverage keyframe actions from preceding levels and provide mutual "visibility" among robot components enhances coherence and mitigates error propagation, especially under observational distribution shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DSPv2 represents an advancement in the field of whole-body mobile manipulation, addressing several critical challenges that hinder the deployment of such robots in complex, real-world environments.

**A. Significance**

- **Effective Observation Utilization:** The proposed 2D-3D feature fusion method provides a comprehensive understanding of complex environments. By aligning sparse 3D spatial features with dense multi-view 2D semantic features via a Q-former, DSPv2 more effectively leverages diverse observational information than prior approaches, leading to improved perception and task execution accuracy.
- **Robust Generalization:** DSPv2 introduces a policy-level generalization approach for whole-body mobile manipulation. Its architecture, particularly the combination of a voxel-level 3D encoder and a LoRA-tuned vision foundation model, enables robustness to variations in lighting, object appearance, spatial arrangement, and entire scene layouts, even with limited task-specific data. This capability is crucial for reducing the need for extensive retraining in varied deployment scenarios.
- **Coherent Whole-Body Actions:** The extension of the Dense Policy paradigm to whole-body systems addresses the long-standing problem of error amplification and motion incoordination. By employing a bidirectional autoregressive generation mechanism, DSPv2 allows different robot components to condition their actions on the planned actions of others within the trajectory, leading to more coherent and precise whole-body motions. This contributes to more reliable task completion, especially in complex, coordinated maneuvers.

**B. Potential Impact**

- **Advancing Household Robotics:** The ability of DSPv2 to achieve effective and generalizable whole-body mobile manipulation can accelerate the development and deployment of household robots. Such robots, capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured domestic environments, hold significant market potential and real-world applicability.
- **Improved Robotic Skill Transfer:** By demonstrating strong generalization with limited task-specific data, DSPv2 could facilitate the transfer of learned skills to novel situations without requiring prohibitive amounts of new demonstrations, making robot programming more efficient.
- **Foundation for Future Generalist Policies:** The methodology proposed in DSPv2, particularly its robust visual encoding and coherent action generation, could serve as a foundational component for developing more generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for whole-body mobile manipulators. This includes tackling more complex, long-horizon, and multi-stage tasks from language instructions, contingent on the availability of large-scale datasets.

**C. Limitations**
The authors acknowledge several limitations. DSPv2 is not yet capable of solving tasks requiring highly constrained or high-frequency actions, common in real-world scenarios, suggesting future work could explore incorporating high-frequency modalities like tactile sensing. Additionally, like many imitation learning algorithms, DSPv2 struggles to generalize to action modes not represented in the expert demonstrations, indicating a need for action heads with stronger reasoning capabilities. Finally, the development of a general VLA model for whole-body mobile manipulation remains a significant challenge, contingent on the emergence of relevant large-scale datasets.
