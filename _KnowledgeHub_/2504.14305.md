# Adversarial Locomotion and Motion Imitation for Humanoid Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ALMI framework employs adversarial learning to enable robust whole-body control in humanoid robots, achieving stable locomotion for the lower body and precise motion imitation for the upper body. This approach facilitated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the Unitree H1-2 robot and produced the ALMI-X dataset to support language-guided foundation models.

## Problem

- Traditional methods struggle to balance robust lower-body locomotion with expressive upper-body motion imitation, often treating the whole body as a unified entity.
- Learning a single, monolithic whole-body control policy for high-Degrees-of-Freedom humanoids is computationally demanding and prone to real-world instability due to complex interactions.
- Existing approaches often do not explicitly account for adversarial interactions where movements of one body part can destabilize the other, compromising overall control and balance.

## Method

- ALMI frames policy learning as a two-player zero-sum Markov game, separately optimizing robust locomotion for the lower body and precise motion tracking for the upper body.
- A simplified adversarial formulation uses adaptive curriculum mechanisms to sample adversarial commands or motions, making the training computationally efficient and practical.
- The framework generates ALMI-X, a large-scale, high-quality, language-annotated dataset of whole-body control trajectories to facilitate the development of humanoid foundation models.

## Results

- ALMI policies consistently outperformed baseline methods in locomotion command tracking, upper-body motion tracking, and overall stability across various task difficulties.
- The approach achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the Unitree H1-2 robot, demonstrating robust omnidirectional locomotion and precise upper-body motion tracking in real-world scenarios.
- Preliminary training of a Transformer-based foundation model on a subset of ALMI-X successfully generated robust, language-guided whole-body behaviors in simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling upper and lower body control with adversarial interaction forces each policy to learn high robustness against the other's destabilizing movements.
> - A curriculum-driven approach to adversarial command/motion sampling is crucial for stable training convergence and practical efficiency in complex robotic systems.
> - The generation of an executable, language-annotated whole-body motion dataset is essential for training robust, language-guided foundation models for humanoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within robotics and artificial intelligence: humanoid locomotion, motion imitation, multi-agent reinforcement learning, and foundation models for robotics.

**Humanoid Locomotion and Whole-Body Control:** Historically, humanoid locomotion has relied on traditional control methods like trajectory planning, Zero-Moment Point (ZMP)-based gait synthesis, and model-predictive control (MPC). While these methods have achieved notable successes, they often struggle with complex, dynamic environments and rich contact scenarios. The advent of learning-based approaches, particularly Reinforcement Learning (RL), has significantly advanced the field, enabling humanoids to learn agile skills such as walking on challenging terrains, standing up, jumping, and even parkour. However, a common limitation of much prior RL work in locomotion is its primary focus on the lower body, often treating the upper body as a fixed load or merely as a means to generate counter-movements for balance. This paper challenges this paradigm by explicitly addressing the distinct and interactive roles of the upper and lower body.

**Humanoid Motion Imitation:** Parallel to locomotion research, motion imitation has aimed to teach robots human-like behaviors from motion capture (mocap) datasets. These methods typically involve motion re-targeting and RL-based optimization to achieve both fine-grained motion mimicry and postural stability. The core challenge here is the inherent trade-off: aggressive motion tracking can destabilize the robot, while excessive focus on stability might compromise motion expressiveness. Existing solutions often involve intricate reward engineering, decomposed control strategies, or residual learning. ALMI distinguishes itself by focusing on robust locomotion for the lower body and precise upper-body motion tracking, acknowledging that a robust base is fundamental for expressive upper-body movements in real-world scenarios. It explicitly departs from methods that try to simultaneously imitate both upper and lower body for overall expressiveness, arguing that such approaches often neglect real-world stability.

**Adversarial Training in Robotics:** The concept of adversarial training, widely known from Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), is applied here in a novel multi-agent control context. While other works have explored separate control policies for the upper and lower body, ALMI introduces an *adversarial* interaction. This adversarial dynamic, where one body policy attempts to destabilize the other to force it to learn robustness, is crucial. It ensures that the policies converge to a *coordinated* and resilient whole-body behavior, moving beyond simple independent optimization which might not lead to robust interaction.

**Foundation Models for Robotics:** This area is burgeoning, driven by the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models. Most existing foundation models for robotics primarily target manipulation tasks on fixed or wheeled platforms, often relying on massive human-collected datasets. For humanoids, notable efforts like UH-1 have attempted text-to-pose mapping, but often struggle with producing executable low-level actions on real robots due to a disconnect between high-level commands and low-level dynamics. ALMI-X, the dataset introduced in this paper, makes a significant contribution by providing a large-scale, high-quality, language-annotated dataset specifically for *whole-body control* of humanoids, which involves a much higher degree of freedom and complexity than most manipulation tasks. It aims to bridge the gap towards creating truly generalist, language-guided humanoid control policies that are robust and executable in the real world.

In summary, this work pushes the boundaries of humanoid control by proposing a structured, adversarial learning framework that explicitly accounts for the symbiotic but distinct roles of the upper and lower body. It offers a practical solution to the long-standing challenge of balancing stability and expressiveness in humanoid movements, culminating in the creation of a valuable dataset for the next generation of humanoid foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the persistent challenges in achieving human-like whole-body coordination in humanoid robots, particularly in dynamic and real-world environments. The authors identify several critical limitations of conventional approaches:

1. **Neglect of Distinct Body Roles:** Traditional methods often attempt to mimic whole-body motions as a unified entity, failing to acknowledge the specialized functions of the lower body (robust locomotion, stability) and the upper body (precise, expressive motion imitation). This oversight leads to suboptimal and uncoordinated control.
2. **Computational Intractability and Training Complexity:** Learning a single, monolithic whole-body control policy for a high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) humanoid robot is computationally demanding. It necessitates extremely complex reward structures and often results in prohibitively expensive and slow training processes.
3. **Convergence Issues and Real-World Instability:** Due to the complexity and physical constraints of robots, directly optimizing for whole-body motion tracking often leads to policies that prioritize precise imitation over fundamental stability. This can cause the robot to frequently lose balance and fall during real-world execution, hindering deployment and usability. Furthermore, human movements can exceed a robot's physical capabilities, making direct imitation challenging for RL policies to converge effectively.
4. **Adversarial Interactions:** The movements of one body part inevitably influence the stability and control of the other. For instance, dynamic upper-body motions can destabilize the lower body, and an unstable lower body can compromise the precision of upper-body tasks. Current methods often do not explicitly account for these critical interactions in a way that promotes mutual robustness.

To address these significant issues, the **key objectives** of the ALMI framework are:

- **To propose a novel adversarial training framework (ALMI)** that separately learns robust locomotion for the lower body and precise motion imitation for the upper body.
- **To enable the lower-body policy** to develop strong, robust locomotion capabilities, allowing it to follow various velocity commands effectively even when subjected to significant adversarial disturbances from the upper body. This ensures stability despite upper-body movements that might otherwise disrupt balance.
- **To enable the upper-body policy** to achieve accurate and expressive motion tracking, even when the lower body is executing rapid or unstable velocity-based movements that would typically introduce disturbances.
- **To facilitate coordinated whole-body control** through an iterative adversarial update process, ensuring that the separately learned policies can work together harmoniously and robustly.
- **To demonstrate the extensibility of this coordinated control** to complex loco-manipulation tasks, leveraging teleoperation systems for enhanced interaction.
- **To create and release a large-scale, high-quality whole-body motion control dataset (ALMI-X)**, featuring episodic trajectories with linguistic descriptions, to support the development of foundation models for humanoid control.
- **To rigorously validate the effectiveness and robustness** of the ALMI method and its learned policies in both high-fidelity simulation and on a full-size humanoid robot (Unitree H1-2).

By tackling these challenges, ALMI aims to move beyond partial solutions and provide a more comprehensive, robust, and practical framework for advanced humanoid robot control, paving the way for more versatile and reliable real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Adversarial Locomotion and Motion Imitation (ALMI)** framework introduces a novel approach to humanoid whole-body control by decomposing the problem into an adversarial interaction between the upper and lower body policies.

#### 3.1. The Adversarial Learning Framework

ALMI frames policy learning as a two-player zero-sum Markov game. This allows for the separate optimization of policies for the lower body ($\pi_l$) and upper body ($\pi_u$) while ensuring they learn to interact robustly.

- **Problem Formulation for learning $\pi_l$ (Lower Body):**
 - The lower body is considered the "agent," aiming to maximize a command-following reward ($r_l$).
 - The upper body acts as the "adversary," attempting to minimize $r_l$ by introducing disturbances.
 - The objective for $\pi_l$ is to find a policy that maximizes its value function $V^l(\pi_l, \pi_u)$ even under adversarial upper-body actions, leading to robust locomotion. This is formalized as a `max-min` problem: $max_{\pi_l} min_{\pi_u} V^l(\pi_l, \pi_u)$.

- **Problem Formulation for learning $\pi_u$ (Upper Body):**
 - Conversely, the upper body is the "agent," aiming to maximize a motion-tracking reward ($r_u$).
 - The lower body becomes the "adversary," trying to minimize $r_u$ by causing instability.
 - The objective for $\pi_u$ is to find a policy that maximizes its motion tracking ability despite lower-body disturbances. This is also a `max-min` problem: $max_{\pi_u} min_{\pi_l} V^u(\pi_l, \pi_u)$.
- **Convergence Guarantee:** The authors provide a theoretical foundation (Theorem 3.1 and Appendix A) demonstrating that under independent RL optimization for both players with a two-timescale learning rate and $\epsilon$-greedy exploration, this adversarial process is guaranteed to converge to an $\epsilon$-approximate Nash equilibrium.

#### 3.2. Simplified Formulation for Practicality

Directly solving the `max-min` problems by updating both agent and adversary *policy parameters* in an inner-outer loop can be computationally prohibitive. To address this, ALMI introduces a simplified, more efficient approach:

- Instead of training a full adversarial policy in the inner loop, the adversary is "simulated" by sampling *adversarial commands/motions* from a carefully designed curriculum.
- When learning $\pi_l$: The upper-body policy $\pi_u$ is kept fixed (from the previous iteration), but it's conditioned on "adversarial motions" ($g_u^{adv}$) sampled from a curriculum to generate disruptive actions.
- When learning $\pi_u$: The lower-body policy $\pi_l$ is kept fixed, but it's conditioned on "adversarial velocity commands" ($c_l^{adv}$) sampled from a curriculum to generate destabilizing lower-body movements.
- This transformation shifts the inner-loop optimization from a complex parameter space search to a more manageable command space search, making the algorithm practically efficient.

#### 3.3. Robust Locomotion for the Lower Body

- **Reward Design:** The lower-body policy ($\pi_l$) is trained with a comprehensive reward function (Table 1) comprising penalty terms (e.g., DoF limits, falling), regularization terms (e.g., minimal linear/angular velocity in Z-axis, orientation, torque, base height), and task-specific rewards (e.g., linear/angular velocity command tracking).
- **Dual Curriculum Mechanism for Adversarial Motion Sampling:** This is crucial for gradually increasing the difficulty of disturbances.
 1. A basic $\pi_l$ is initially trained without upper-body intervention.
 2. Upper-body motions from the AMASS dataset are re-targeted to the robot.
 3. Motions are scored and sorted by their "difficulty" (survival length when applied to the basic $\pi_l$). This creates a motion list $M$.
 4. A "motion scale" $\alpha_s \in$ is introduced to scale the amplitude of target upper-body joint positions, dynamically increasing disturbance intensity.
 5. A "window of motions" from $M$ (controlled by $\alpha_d$) and $\alpha_s$ are adaptively adjusted based on the current policy's mean survival length. This ensures that the lower body learns to resist increasingly severe upper-body perturbations, preventing early training collapse due to overly aggressive disturbances.

#### 3.4. Motion Tracking for the Upper Body

- **Motion Preprocessing:** Human motions from the AMASS dataset are re-targeted to the humanoid robot and low-pass filtered for smoothness.
- **Reward Design:** The upper-body policy ($\pi_u$) is trained to maximize motion tracking reward (Table 2), which includes penalties, regularization terms, and a primary task reward for tracking upper DoF joint positions.
- **Curriculum for Adversarial Lower-Body Commands:** A simpler curriculum mechanism is used here. The range of lower-body velocity commands ([c_l_min, c_l_max]) is adjusted based on the upper-body's motion tracking error. If tracking is good, the velocity command range expands, introducing more significant lower-body instability; otherwise, it contracts.

#### 3.5. ALMI-X Dataset and Foundation Model

- **Dataset Construction:** After ALMI's iterative adversarial training converges, the final $\pi_l$ and $\pi_u$ policies are used to generate a large-scale, high-quality dataset, ALMI-X, in MuJoCo simulations. This dataset pairs diverse upper-body motions (from an augmented AMASS subset) with omnidirectional lower-body commands (11 directions, 4 difficulty levels). Each trajectory is annotated with linguistic descriptions (e.g., "moving backward slowly and wave the left hand"). The dataset records robot states, actions, and privileged information.
- **Foundation Model Training (Preliminary):** A Transformer decoder architecture is used to train a preliminary whole-body foundation model. This model takes text commands (processed by CLIP) and historical state-action pairs as input and autoregressively predicts the next action. This closed-loop approach aims for robust and adaptive control, distinguishing it from open-loop sequence prediction methods.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

- **Robot:** Full-sized Unitree H1-2 robot (27 DoFs, policy controls 21 DoFs).
- **Simulation:** Isaac Gym, 4,096 parallel environments for efficient training.
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an asymmetric actor-critic architecture and Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensively applied to dynamics (friction, mass, CoM, control delay) and external perturbations (pushes, varied terrain) to enhance sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer robustness.
- **Training Rounds:** Three rounds of adversarial iterations, total training duration approximately 17 hours.

This comprehensive methodology, combining theoretical grounding in Markov games with practical simplifications, adaptive curricula, and a focus on high-quality data generation, forms the backbone of ALMI's ability to achieve robust and coordinated humanoid control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation (Isaac Gym with Unitree H1-2 and G1 robots) and on the real Unitree H1-2 robot validate ALMI's effectiveness across multiple dimensions.

#### 4.1. Tracking Precision, Stability, and Robustness (Q1 & Q2)

- **Superior Performance against Baselines:** ALMI consistently outperforms baseline methods such as Exbody and ALMI (whole-body, single policy) in terms of both locomotion command tracking (linear velocity error $E_{vel}$, angular velocity error $E_{ang}$) and upper-body motion tracking (joint position error $E_{upper\_jpe}$, keypoint error $E_{upper\_kpe}$). This superiority is maintained across increasing difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard), which involve higher velocities, more challenging terrains, and external pushes.
- **Enhanced Stability and Survival:** ALMI demonstrates significantly higher survival rates and better stability (lower projected gravity error $E_g$) compared to baselines. This indicates that the adversarial training successfully enables the lower-body policy to maintain balance and execute robust movements even under substantial disturbances from the upper body. The single-policy approaches struggle with the coordination, leading to higher action rates and worse stability.
- **Generalization to Other Platforms:** ALMI's approach successfully generalizes to the Unitree G1 robot, outperforming other imitation-based whole-body control methods like OmniH2O and Exbody2. This suggests that the adversarial framework provides a general mechanism for learning robust humanoid control, rather than being specific to one robot model.

#### 4.2. Benefits of Adversarial Iterative Training and Arm Curriculum (Q3)

- **Impact of Iterative Adversarial Training:** Ablation studies comparing policies from different adversarial training iterations (lower-1, lower-2, lower-3) show clear improvements. While differences are minimal on easy tasks, `lower-3` (from the final iteration) significantly outperforms `lower-1` and `lower-2` on medium and hard tasks across all metrics. This confirms that the iterative refinement process, where policies adapt to progressively larger disturbances from each other, is crucial for achieving high robustness and coordinated behavior.
- **Critical Role of Arm Curriculum:** The ablation study `ALMI (w/o curriculum)` demonstrates the vital importance of the dual curriculum mechanism. Without gradually introducing upper-body motions from easy to hard, the lower-body policy struggles to cope with disturbances, leading to substantially worse performance (higher velocity/angular errors, higher $E_g$, lower survival rate) on challenging tasks. This highlights that uncontrolled adversarial training (e.g., random, full-scale motions) can destabilize the learning process, while a structured curriculum ensures efficient and stable robustness acquisition.
- **Validation of Simplified Adversarial Approach:** Further experiments in Appendix E demonstrate that attempting to directly train an adversary or a policy with multiple adversarial goals (without the simplified curriculum-based command sampling) is prone to training collapse or yields policies with poor robustness. This underscores the practical efficacy and necessity of ALMI's simplified, curriculum-driven adversarial approach.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments (Q4)

- **Successful Sim-to-Real Transfer:** ALMI policies are deployed directly on the Unitree H1-2 robot's onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX, achieving successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without additional fine-tuning. This attests to the robustness imparted by extensive domain randomization during training.
- **Robust Loco-Motion and Precise Motion Tracking:** The real robot demonstrates robust omnidirectional locomotion capabilities via joystick control. Concurrently, it accurately tracks various upper-body motions (e.g., salute, wave, shake hand, punch) while standing or moving (Figures 3 and 8 in Appendix), exhibiting comparable performance to simulations.
- **Complex Loco-Manipulation:** When integrated with teleoperation systems (e.g., VR devices for human hand pose capture), the ALMI policies enable the robot to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks, such as precisely swinging a tennis racket or carrying a 5kg box and vacuuming the floor while walking (Figures 9 and 10 in Appendix). This showcases the framework's practical utility for real-world applications.

#### 4.4. Foundation Model Preliminary Results

- **Proof-of-Concept for Language-Guided Control:** A Transformer-based foundation model trained on a *subset* of the ALMI-X dataset (wave-related motions) successfully learns to generate robust, language-guided whole-body behaviors (e.g., "go forward slowly and wave left") in MuJoCo simulations. This is a significant preliminary step towards purely offline, language-conditioned control of full-sized humanoids.
- **Closed-Loop vs. Open-Loop Control:** Closed-loop control with short input sequences (CL-20sl) exhibits superior lower-body command following and overall locomotion stability. In contrast, open-loop control or closed-loop with very long sequences (CL-400sl) tends to compromise locomotion stability, despite potentially better upper-body motion compliance for the latter.
- **Scaling Challenges:** Performance degrades when the foundation model is trained on the *complete* ALMI-X dataset, indicating that current model architectures may have limitations in handling the high diversity of the full dataset. This highlights an area for future research but confirms ALMI-X as a valuable resource for further development.

In essence, the findings unequivocally demonstrate that ALMI provides a robust and effective solution for humanoid whole-body control, translating well from simulation to the real world and providing a foundational dataset for future generalist robotic intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Adversarial Locomotion and Motion Imitation for Humanoid Policy Learning" makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the field of humanoid robotics and broader AI research.

#### 5.1. Novel and Robust Control Paradigm

- **Decoupled yet Coordinated Learning:** ALMI's core significance lies in its innovative adversarial training framework that elegantly decouples the learning objectives of the upper and lower body while enforcing robust coordination. By treating the other body part as an "adversary," each policy is compelled to learn highly robust behaviors, addressing the fundamental challenge of balancing stability and expressiveness.
- **Enhanced Real-World Deployability:** The framework directly tackles the problem of robot instability and falls, a critical bottleneck for deploying humanoids outside controlled lab environments. By prioritizing robust locomotion for the lower body and enabling precise upper-body movements even under stress, ALMI significantly improves the reliability and safety of humanoid operation. This is evident in the successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the Unitree H1-2 robot, demonstrating practical readiness.

#### 5.2. Practical Algorithm for Complex Systems

- **Computational Efficiency:** The simplified adversarial formulation, which optimizes in the command space using curricula rather than the policy parameter space, makes the approach computationally feasible. This is a crucial practical advantage for high-DoF systems like humanoids, avoiding the often-prohibitive costs of full multi-agent policy optimization.
- **Adaptive Curriculum Learning:** The dual curriculum mechanisms for both upper and lower body policies are vital for stable training. They ensure a progressive increase in adversarial difficulty, allowing the policies to gradually build robustness without collapsing early, a common issue in complex RL setups.

#### 5.3. Foundational Dataset for Generalist Robotics

- **ALMI-X Dataset:** The creation and release of the ALMI-X dataset is a major contribution. It is the *first large-scale, high-quality, language-annotated dataset specifically for whole-body control of humanoids*. This addresses a critical gap in the resources available for training foundation models in humanoid robotics.
- **High Executability:** Unlike some prior datasets that might contain unexecutable or unsafe trajectories, ALMI-X is generated by robust, learned policies, ensuring that the collected data represents behaviors that are feasible and stable for real robots. This "high executability" is paramount for effective supervised learning of generalist policies.

#### 5.4. Stepping Stone for Humanoid Foundation Models

- **Language-Guided Control:** The preliminary work on training a Transformer-based foundation model from ALMI-X demonstrates the feasibility of language-guided whole-body humanoid control. This represents a significant step towards more intuitive and high-level command interfaces for humanoids, moving beyond manually programmed or trajectory-based control.
- **Autonomous and Adaptive Behaviors:** As foundation models mature with more diverse and larger datasets, they promise to unlock a new era of autonomous humanoid robots that can interpret complex natural language commands and adapt their whole-body movements to various tasks and environments without explicit pre-programming for every scenario.

#### 5.5. Broader Societal Impact

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** More capable and robust humanoids can revolutionize applications in logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, and hazardous environment exploration. Their ability to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks under teleoperation, as demonstrated, holds immense potential for telepresence robotics and assistive technologies.
- **Research Acceleration:** By open-sourcing the ALMI-X dataset and the underlying framework, the authors are fostering collaborative research, enabling the broader scientific community to accelerate advancements in humanoid intelligence and control.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that ALMI, while robust for mobile manipulation, may still be suboptimal for highly dynamic whole-body tasks like dancing, suggesting future work on unified task-oriented rewards. The foundation model, while promising, also requires significant enhancement in data efficiency and architectural advancements to handle the full diversity of ALMI-X effectively. Addressing these limitations will further solidify ALMI's impact and propel humanoid robotics toward even more sophisticated capabilities.

In conclusion, ALMI represents a significant leap forward in humanoid robot control, offering a principled, robust, and practical framework. Its contributions in adversarial learning and the creation of a foundational dataset are poised to have a transformative impact on the development of more intelligent, versatile, and safely deployable humanoid robots.
