# GP3: A 3D Geometry-Aware Policy with Multi-View Images for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GP3, a policy developed by DAMO Academy, Alibaba Group, and Tongji University, provides 3D geometry-aware robotic manipulation using only multi-view RGB images, eliminating the need for depth sensors. This approach, leveraging a fine-tuned 3D reconstruction model and a novel language-guided attention mechanism, achieves state-of-the-art performance across simulated and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies often rely on specialized depth sensors, which are expensive, prone to noise, and limit practical deployment.
- Existing methods that infer 3D representations from RGB images frequently struggle with generalization across diverse environments or lack the fine-grained detail needed for precise manipulation.
- 2D vision-based policies, while advanced, inherently lack the precise 3D spatial reasoning crucial for complex object interaction tasks.

## Method

- GP3 introduces RoboVGGT, a geometry-aware spatial encoder built upon a pre-trained 3D reconstruction model (VGGT) and fine-tuned on a curated, multi-domain robotics dataset with camera and depth supervision.
- A novel Global Attention-based Feature-wise Linear Modulation (G-FiLM) mechanism dynamically guides the policy's attention using language instructions, effectively suppressing irrelevant multi-view noise.
- The policy head predicts continuous actions from the fused spatial features and language embeddings, utilizing Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Results

- GP3 achieved 86.7% overall success on MetaWorld and 78.7% on RLBench with multi-view RGB, outperforming prior implicit 3D methods like SPA by 9.8% and 26.7% respectively, and explicit 3D policies by even wider margins.
- Successfully transferred to real-world Mobile ALOHA, demonstrating superior success rates over Diffusion Policy and SPA on household tasks and robust 3D perception by distinguishing real 3D objects from 2D images.
- Ablation studies confirmed that fine-tuning RoboVGGT significantly improved success rates (e.g., from 56.0% to 70.9% for single-view), and G-FiLM consistently outperformed conventional FiLM, especially with multiple views (e.g., 83.9% vs 80.3% with 4 views).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust, fine-grained 3D spatial reasoning for robotic manipulation can be effectively achieved end-to-end from standard multi-view RGB inputs, removing dependency on depth sensors.
> - Targeted fine-tuning of large-scale pre-trained 3D reconstruction models on robot-centric data significantly enhances their performance and generalization for manipulation tasks.
> - Language-guided modulation of global attention (G-FiLM) in multi-view inputs is critical for filtering redundant information and focusing on task-relevant features, improving task success.
