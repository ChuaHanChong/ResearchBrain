# GP3: A 3D Geometry-Aware Policy with Multi-View Images for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GP3, a policy developed by DAMO Academy, Alibaba Group, and Tongji University, provides 3D geometry-aware robotic manipulation using only multi-view RGB images, eliminating the need for depth sensors. This approach, leveraging a fine-tuned 3D reconstruction model and a novel language-guided attention mechanism, achieves state-of-the-art performance across simulated and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies often rely on specialized depth sensors, which are expensive, prone to noise, and limit practical deployment.
- Existing methods that infer 3D representations from RGB images frequently struggle with generalization across diverse environments or lack the fine-grained detail needed for precise manipulation.
- 2D vision-based policies, while advanced, inherently lack the precise 3D spatial reasoning crucial for complex object interaction tasks.

## Method

- GP3 introduces RoboVGGT, a geometry-aware spatial encoder built upon a pre-trained 3D reconstruction model (VGGT) and fine-tuned on a curated, multi-domain robotics dataset with camera and depth supervision.
- A novel Global Attention-based Feature-wise Linear Modulation (G-FiLM) mechanism dynamically guides the policy's attention using language instructions, effectively suppressing irrelevant multi-view noise.
- The policy head predicts continuous actions from the fused spatial features and language embeddings, utilizing Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Results

- GP3 achieved 86.7% overall success on MetaWorld and 78.7% on RLBench with multi-view RGB, outperforming prior implicit 3D methods like SPA by 9.8% and 26.7% respectively, and explicit 3D policies by even wider margins.
- Successfully transferred to real-world Mobile ALOHA, demonstrating superior success rates over Diffusion Policy and SPA on household tasks and robust 3D perception by distinguishing real 3D objects from 2D images.
- Ablation studies confirmed that fine-tuning RoboVGGT significantly improved success rates (e.g., from 56.0% to 70.9% for single-view), and G-FiLM consistently outperformed conventional FiLM, especially with multiple views (e.g., 83.9% vs 80.3% with 4 views).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust, fine-grained 3D spatial reasoning for robotic manipulation can be effectively achieved end-to-end from standard multi-view RGB inputs, removing dependency on depth sensors.
> - Targeted fine-tuning of large-scale pre-trained 3D reconstruction models on robot-centric data significantly enhances their performance and generalization for manipulation tasks.
> - Language-guided modulation of global attention (G-FiLM) in multi-view inputs is critical for filtering redundant information and focusing on task-relevant features, improving task success.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation relies fundamentally on a precise understanding of 3D scene geometry. The broader research landscape in this area is characterized by efforts to integrate 3D information into visuomotor policies, with various approaches attempting to balance accuracy, generalization, and hardware dependency. GP3 positions itself as a critical advancement that bridges significant gaps in current methodologies.

Historically, robotic manipulation policies relied on explicit 3D information, often obtained from specialized hardware. The "Related Work" section categorizes these approaches:
- **Explicit Geometry-Based Approaches:** These methods directly encode 3D data like point clouds, voxels, or Gaussian splats, utilizing sensors such as depth cameras. Examples include frameworks like 3DP, 3DA, LIFT3D, RVT, PerACT, and VoxAct. While effective for fine-grained spatial reasoning, they suffer from several drawbacks: high computational cost, dependency on expensive and potentially unreliable depth sensors, susceptibility to occlusions and sensor noise, and often limited real-world generalization beyond curated benchmarks.
- **Implicit Geometry-Based Approaches from RGB:** To overcome depth sensor dependency, some works infer implicit 3D representations from standard RGB images, sometimes augmented with predicted depth. Examples include RoboHorizon, 3D-MVP, and SPA. While promising, these often struggle with generalization, producing spatial representations that are not robust enough for diverse, unseen environments, and may lack the fine-grained detail required for precision manipulation tasks. SPA, a prior state-of-the-art in this category, produces volumetric features that predominantly support coarse-grained reasoning.
- **2D Vision-Based Policies:** Many modern approaches learn control policies directly from visual (RGB) observations, leveraging large-scale datasets and architectures like Vision Transformers (ViTs), as seen in R3M, VC-1, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like RT-1 and RT-2. While improving task generalization and unifying perception with language, these 2D-based methods frequently lack the precise spatial reasoning necessary for intricate manipulation tasks such as assembly or insertion.
- **Transformer-Based 3D Vision Methods:** A parallel development involves transformer-based foundation models (e.g., DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R, Fast3R, VGGT) that reconstruct dense 3D scenes from raw multi-view RGB, even with unknown camera parameters. VGGT, in particular, achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in local 3D mapping for general scenes.

GP3 directly addresses the limitations of both explicit and implicit 3D methods by proposing a **geometry-aware policy that leverages robust multi-view spatial reasoning solely from RGB inputs.** It uniquely combines the power of large-scale pre-trained 3D reconstruction models (specifically VGGT) with targeted fine-tuning for robotic tasks and a novel attention mechanism (G-FiLM) to integrate language guidance effectively. This approach aims to bridge the gap between general scene reconstruction and fine-grained robotic manipulation, delivering high performance without specialized depth hardware, thereby offering a practical and highly generalizable solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind GP3 stems from the critical need for robots to possess a precise understanding of 3D scene geometry for effective manipulation. While multi-view observations are a natural way to acquire such geometry, existing methods face significant challenges:
- **Dependency on Specialized Hardware:** Many effective 3D-aware methods rely on depth sensors (e.g., for point clouds), which can be expensive, prone to noise, or simply unavailable in real-world scenarios. This limits their practical deployment.
- **Generalization Issues with Implicit 3D:** Approaches that infer 3D representations from standard RGB images often struggle to generalize across diverse, unseen environments, producing spatial representations that are not robust enough for the demands of robotic tasks. Furthermore, prior implicit 3D methods may only support coarse-grained spatial reasoning, which is insufficient for precision-critical manipulation.
- **Limitations of 2D Visuomotor Policies:** While 2D-based vision policies have advanced significantly, they inherently lack the precise 3D spatial relationships needed for complex interaction with objects.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of the GP3 research are:
1. **Develop a 3D geometry-aware robotic manipulation policy (GP3) that leverages multi-view RGB input.** The primary goal is to achieve robust spatial reasoning without requiring explicit depth data or specialized depth sensors.
2. **Infer dense spatial features and a compact, expressive 3D scene representation from multi-view RGB observations.** This representation should be tailored specifically for robotic manipulation tasks.
3. **Enhance the spatial encoder for robotic tasks:** Adapt a large-scale pre-trained 3D reconstruction model (VGGT) to excel in object-centric, small-workspace environments relevant to robotics, ensuring high accuracy at practical resolutions and strong generalization across diverse robotic scenarios.
4. **Introduce an efficient attention mechanism for multi-view, language-guided control (G-FiLM).** This mechanism aims to effectively integrate language instructions to dynamically guide the policy's attention, focusing on task-relevant spatial features from multi-view inputs while suppressing irrelevant noise, particularly when increasing the number of views might otherwise degrade performance.
5. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance on simulated and real-world benchmarks** while demonstrating effective transferability to real robots with minimal fine-tuning and without depth sensors or pre-mapped environments.
6. **Establish a practical, sensor-agnostic, lightweight, scalable, and highly generalizable solution** for geometry-aware robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GP3 framework operates on a two-stage training pipeline, centered around a novel spatial encoder and an efficient attention mechanism.

#### 3.1. Geometry-Aware Spatial Encoder: RoboVGGT

- **Foundation:** GP3 builds its geometry-aware spatial encoder, named **RoboVGGT**, upon VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a), a state-of-the-art transformer-based model for 3D reconstruction from multi-view RGB images, known for its strong generalization capabilities. Unlike methods relying on explicit 3D priors, VGGT inherently learns inter-frame correspondences to reconstruct scenes.
- **Adaptation for Robotics:** The original VGGT is designed for high-resolution inputs and general scene reconstruction, making it computationally intensive and not directly optimized for robot-centric, small-workspace environments. To address this, RoboVGGT undergoes **targeted fine-tuning** at a practical resolution of 224x224 pixels.
- **Curated Robotics Dataset:** This fine-tuning is performed on a newly curated, multi-domain dataset specifically for robotics. This dataset combines:
 - **Simulated data:** 150K frames from popular benchmarks like RLBench (James et al., 2020), MetaWorld (Yu et al., 2020), and RoboTwin (Mu et al., 2025). This diverse simulation data covers various robot configurations and manipulation tasks.
 - **Real-world task data:** 20K real-world frames collected from daily manipulation tasks on the Mobile ALOHA platform.
- **Geometric Supervision:** During fine-tuning, RoboVGGT is supervised using a multi-task camera and depth loss:
 - **Camera Loss ($L_{camera}$):** A Huber loss that supervises the predicted camera parameters against ground-truth parameters, ensuring accurate pose estimation.
 - **Depth Loss ($L_{depth}$):** Integrates aleatoric-uncertainty weighting with gradient-based depth supervision, using a predicted per-pixel uncertainty map. This helps in robust depth estimation even in uncertain regions.
- **Outcome:** This fine-tuning process enables RoboVGGT to robustly generalize and reconstruct robotic manipulation scenes, including the robotic arm itself, with significantly improved quality compared to the original VGGT, especially for unseen tasks and in real-world settings.

#### 3.2. Global Attention-based Feature-wise Linear Modulation (G-FiLM)

- **Motivation:** While multi-view inputs are beneficial, simply increasing the number of views can paradoxically degrade performance. This is due to the introduction of redundant or task-irrelevant information, which can dilute the model's focus.
- **Addressing Redundancy:** GP3 introduces G-FiLM to dynamically guide the policy's attention and effectively suppress irrelevant noise.
- **Mechanism:** Inspired by FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation), G-FiLM applies modulation **exclusively to the global attention layers** within the transformer architecture. The transformer integrates both global (capturing cross-perspective associations) and frame-wise (local feature extraction) attention. G-FiLM selectively modulates the visual features ($F$) in global attention layers using scaling ($\gamma$) and shifting ($\beta$) vectors projected from language embeddings.
- **Language Integration:** This formulation allows language instructions to semantically modulate attention patterns, enabling the model to focus on task-relevant spatial features from the multi-view input. This selective modulation maintains computational efficiency while enhancing task success by filtering out distracting information.

#### 3.3. Action Training

- **Policy Head:** The spatial features extracted by RoboVGGT, fused with language instructions via G-FiLM, are fed into a lightweight policy head to predict continuous actions.
- **Action Representation:** The action space is tailored to the robot:
 - **Simulated tasks:** End-effector pose.
 - **Real-world ALOHA system:** Joint-space configuration.
- **Action Loss:** A Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is used to supervise the predicted actions against ground-truth actions.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (Hu et al., 2022) is employed for efficient fine-tuning of the frozen pre-trained VGGT encoder weights throughout all training stages, including action training. This allows only a small number of additional parameters (A and B matrices) to be learned, significantly reducing computational overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GP3 demonstrates significant advancements in geometry-aware robotic manipulation, achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks and real-world applications.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (MetaWorld and RLBench)

- **Superior Performance:** GP3 consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across two widely used robotic manipulation benchmarks: MetaWorld and RLBench. This includes 2D vision-based methods (CLIP, R3M, VC-1), explicit 3D policies (DP3, Lift3D), and prior implicit 3D representation methods (SPA).
- **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - **MetaWorld:** GP3 (multi-view RGB) achieves an overall success rate of 86.7%, surpassing the best prior implicit 3D representation (SPA) by 9.8% and the best explicit 3D policy (Lift3D) by 16.9%.
 - **RLBench:** GP3 attains 78.7% success with multi-view RGB, outperforming SPA by 26.7% and Lift3D by 25.4%.
- **Single-View Efficacy (GP3-S):** Even with single-view input, GP3-S demonstrates strong performance (MetaWorld: 75.7%, RLBench: 70.0%), surpassing many multi-view and explicit 3D baselines. This highlights the effectiveness of GP3's learned implicit 3D representation.
- **Multi-Task Generalization:** The approach also achieves state-of-the-art performance in multi-task settings on both MetaWorld (83.1%) and RLBench (83.3%), further demonstrating its robust generalization capabilities (Appendix B).

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments (Mobile ALOHA Platform)

- **Effective Transferability:** GP3 successfully transfers to real-world robots (Mobile ALOHA) with minimal fine-tuning, demonstrating its practical viability without relying on depth sensors or pre-mapped environments.
- **State-of-the-Art Real-World Performance:** GP3 with multi-view input achieves superior success rates on four common household-style tasks (Catching desktop paper ball, Grasping cloth, Clearing items, Retrieving coffee), outperforming Diffusion Policy and SPA. For example, it achieved 18/20 successes for "Task 1" compared to 5/20 for Diffusion Policy and 7/20 for SPA.
- **Robust 3D Perception Validation:** A crucial comparative experiment validated GP3's superior 3D understanding. When presented with a printed flat paper ball (visually identical to a real crumpled one from a single frontal view), other methods (Diffusion Policy, SPA multi-view, and even GP3-S single-view) were deceived and attempted to grasp the flat image. In contrast, GP3 with multi-view input correctly identified the object as flat and did not attempt to grasp it, showcasing its robust spatial awareness.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (MetaWorld Subset)

- **Impact of Fine-tuning (RoboVGGT):** Fine-tuning the spatial encoder on robot-centric data significantly improves performance. For single-view inputs, the mean success rate increased from 56.0% (baseline) to 70.9% (w/ FT). Similar improvements were observed for multi-view configurations.
- **Multi-view Challenges:** Without specific handling mechanisms, simply increasing input views can degrade performance (e.g., baseline 2-views 66.1% to 4-views 61.8%; w/ FT 2-views 78.4% to 4-views 77.3%). This validates the necessity for the proposed G-FiLM.
- **Effectiveness of Conventional FiLM:** Integrating conventional FiLM (w/ FT+F) showed positive impact, improving success rates (e.g., 4-views 77.3% to 80.3%).
- **Superiority of G-FiLM:** The proposed G-FiLM (w/ FT+GF) consistently outperformed conventional FiLM, especially with multiple views (e.g., 4-views 80.3% to 83.9%). This confirms that G-FiLM's selective modulation of global attention, guided by language, more effectively handles noise and redundancy in multi-view inputs and optimally fuses geometric and linguistic features.

#### 4.4. Point Map Estimation

- **Improved Geometry Model:** RoboVGGT significantly improves the accuracy (Acc.), completeness (Comp.), and overall Chamfer distance (Overall) of predicted point clouds on unseen RoboTwin tasks compared to the original VGGT. For example, RoboVGGT achieved an "Overall" Chamfer distance of 0.807, a substantial improvement over Original VGGT's 2.840.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of GP3 represents a significant step forward in robotic manipulation, offering a highly practical and impactful solution.

### 6. Significance

- **Sensor Agnostic and Cost-Effective:** GP3 fundamentally shifts the paradigm by achieving robust 3D spatial reasoning for manipulation solely from multi-view RGB inputs. This eliminates the dependency on expensive and often problematic depth sensors, making advanced robotic manipulation more accessible and cost-effective.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** By fine-tuning a powerful 3D reconstruction model (VGGT) on a diverse robotics dataset and integrating a sophisticated attention mechanism (G-FiLM), GP3 demonstrates exceptional generalization capabilities across unseen tasks and environments in both simulation and the real world. Its ability to distinguish a real 3D object from a 2D image of it exemplifies its robust perception.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Consistently outperforming current state-of-the-art 2D-based, explicit 3D-based, and implicit 3D-based methods on challenging benchmarks solidifies GP3's position as a leading approach.
- **Lightweight and Scalable:** The use of efficient fine-tuning techniques like LoRA and adaptation to practical resolutions (224x224) makes GP3 a lightweight solution, facilitating easier deployment and scalability.
- **End-to-End 3D Reasoning from RGB:** The work establishes that robust fine-grained 3D spatial reasoning crucial for manipulation can be effectively achieved end-to-end from standard RGB inputs, simplifying the perceptual pipeline.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Democratization of Advanced Robotics:** By reducing the hardware requirements, GP3 can enable wider adoption of intelligent robotic manipulation in various domains, including household robotics, small and medium-sized enterprises, logistics, and field robotics, where specialized sensors might be impractical or unavailable.
- **Improved Real-World Deployment:** The demonstrated effectiveness in real-world scenarios with minimal fine-tuning suggests that GP3 can lead to more robust and reliable robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured and dynamic environments, less susceptible to sensor failures or environmental ambiguities.
- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** GP3 lays crucial groundwork for developing more sophisticated embodied AI agents. Its robust, geometry-aware visual perception from RGB can serve as a foundational component for tackling more complex, long-horizon, and dynamic manipulation tasks, as well as enabling richer human-robot interaction through better spatial understanding.
- **Advancement in Vision-Language Models for Robotics:** The effective integration of language guidance through G-FiLM further strengthens the potential for developing truly intelligent robots that can understand and execute commands based on natural language, bridging the gap between high-level human instructions and precise robotic actions.

In conclusion, GP3 presents a compelling solution to a long-standing challenge in robotics, offering a practical, high-performing, and broadly applicable framework for visually guided manipulation.
