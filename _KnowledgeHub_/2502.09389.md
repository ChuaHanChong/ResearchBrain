# S$^2$-Diffusion: Generalizing from Instance-level to Category-level Skills in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, S²-Diffusion, integrates spatial prediction and semantic segmentation from pre-trained vision models into a visuomotor diffusion policy for robot manipulation. This approach enables robots to generalize from instance-level demonstrations to category-level skills, achieving robust performance on unseen objects and variations using only a single RGB camera.

## Problem

- Robot imitation learning policies often overfit to specific object instances, actions, or environments, leading to a lack of generalization to task variations or different objects within the same category.
- This limitation necessitates extensive and costly data collection or retraining for each new scenario, hindering real-world deployment.
- Existing solutions like 3D vision methods are often complex, while large VLA models may struggle with complex, fine-grained manipulation tasks.

## Method

- S²-Diffusion leverages a conditional ==Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)== as the policy learning framework.
- It replaces raw RGB inputs with a spatial-semantic representation derived from Grounded-SAM2 (for zero-shot semantic segmentation via text prompts) and DepthAnythingV2 (for relative depth estimation) using a single RGB camera.
- The fused semantic mask and depth map are processed through a ResNet18 encoder, and this visual feature, along with robot proprioceptive state, forms the observation for the diffusion policy.

## Results

- S²-Diffusion achieved superior success rates (**0.75**-**0.88**) across various RoboCasa simulation tasks and performed comparably to multi-view RGB-D methods on SAPIEN tasks, maintaining **0.90** success on unseen instances.
- In real-world whiteboard-wiping, the method achieved 89-**100%** success on unseen scribble colors, significantly outperforming a baseline RGB-only Diffusion Policy which dropped to 0-**44%**.
- For real-world bowl-to-bowl scooping, S²-Diffusion maintained approximately **80%** success on unseen granular materials (e.g., from rice to various cereals) by adapting the semantic prompt, whereas the baseline policy failed entirely.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating open-vocabulary semantic understanding and robust spatial perception (depth estimation) from large foundation models significantly enhances the generalization capabilities of visuomotor policies.
> - A combined spatial-semantic representation allows the robot to learn the functional aspects of a skill by abstracting away task-irrelevant visual details, such as object color or background changes.
> - Diffusion policies, when provided with such an enriched and generalized observation space, can effectively learn category-level skills from limited instance-level demonstrations.
