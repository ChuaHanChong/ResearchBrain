# S$^2$-Diffusion: Generalizing from Instance-level to Category-level Skills in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, S²-Diffusion, integrates spatial prediction and semantic segmentation from pre-trained vision models into a visuomotor diffusion policy for robot manipulation. This approach enables robots to generalize from instance-level demonstrations to category-level skills, achieving robust performance on unseen objects and variations using only a single RGB camera.

## Problem

- Robot imitation learning policies often overfit to specific object instances, actions, or environments, leading to a lack of generalization to task variations or different objects within the same category.
- This limitation necessitates extensive and costly data collection or retraining for each new scenario, hindering real-world deployment.
- Existing solutions like 3D vision methods are often complex, while large VLA models may struggle with complex, fine-grained manipulation tasks.

## Method

- S²-Diffusion leverages a conditional ==Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)== as the policy learning framework.
- It replaces raw RGB inputs with a spatial-semantic representation derived from Grounded-SAM2 (for zero-shot semantic segmentation via text prompts) and DepthAnythingV2 (for relative depth estimation) using a single RGB camera.
- The fused semantic mask and depth map are processed through a ResNet18 encoder, and this visual feature, along with robot proprioceptive state, forms the observation for the diffusion policy.

## Results

- S²-Diffusion achieved superior success rates (**0.75**-**0.88**) across various RoboCasa simulation tasks and performed comparably to multi-view RGB-D methods on SAPIEN tasks, maintaining **0.90** success on unseen instances.
- In real-world whiteboard-wiping, the method achieved 89-**100%** success on unseen scribble colors, significantly outperforming a baseline RGB-only Diffusion Policy which dropped to 0-**44%**.
- For real-world bowl-to-bowl scooping, S²-Diffusion maintained approximately **80%** success on unseen granular materials (e.g., from rice to various cereals) by adapting the semantic prompt, whereas the baseline policy failed entirely.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating open-vocabulary semantic understanding and robust spatial perception (depth estimation) from large foundation models significantly enhances the generalization capabilities of visuomotor policies.
> - A combined spatial-semantic representation allows the robot to learn the functional aspects of a skill by abstracting away task-irrelevant visual details, such as object color or background changes.
> - Diffusion policies, when provided with such an enriched and generalized observation space, can effectively learn category-level skills from limited instance-level demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has made progress in learning complex tasks through imitation learning (IL), which involves robots learning skills from human demonstrations. However, a persistent challenge in IL is the lack of generalization. Policies trained on specific instances of objects, actions, or environments often fail when confronted with variations of the same task or object category. For example, a robot trained to scoop rice may not successfully scoop cereal, even though both are granular materials and the underlying "scooping" skill is functionally similar. This limitation necessitates extensive and costly data collection, fine-tuning, or retraining for each new scenario, hindering the deployment of robots in diverse real-world settings.

Current approaches to address generalization include:
- **3D vision-based methods:** These methods enhance generalization by providing richer spatial information but typically require multi-view RGB-D setups or scene scanning, which can be complex and expensive to implement in practical scenarios.
- **Large pre-trained Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models:** Models like RT-X and Octo demonstrate broad generalization for simple skills such as pick-and-place across various environments and objects. However, they often struggle with more complex, fine-grained, non-prehensile manipulation tasks like scooping or specific object manipulation (e.g., mug-flipping, opening a bottle).
- **Recent imitation learning approaches:** These methods focus on learning challenging manipulation tasks for specific instances but frequently rely on raw perceptual features, which limits their transferability and integration with semantic knowledge in complex scenarios. Diffusion policies, a type of imitation learning, have shown promise in learning from demonstrations but are also susceptible to overfitting to instance-specific visual features.

This research addresses the gap between instance-level skill learning and category-level generalization, particularly for complex manipulation tasks, by integrating open-vocabulary semantic understanding and spatial perception into a visuomotor diffusion policy. It aims to achieve robust generalization using only a single RGB camera, thereby offering a more practical and efficient solution compared to multi-view 3D systems or computationally intensive large VLA models for fine-grained tasks. The work builds upon advances in vision foundation models (e.g., Grounded-SAM2 for semantic segmentation and DepthAnythingV2 for depth estimation) and diffusion policies for robot control, applying these to create policies that are invariant to task-irrelevant visual changes and can transfer skills across instances of the same category.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the limitations of existing imitation learning methods in generalizing beyond the specific instances encountered during training. While humans can easily transfer a skill like "scooping" from rice to cereals, robots often fail when presented with subtle variations (e.g., different colored scribbles on a whiteboard or different types of granular materials). This instance-level overfitting necessitates extensive data collection and retraining, posing a significant hurdle for practical robot deployment.

The key objectives of this research are threefold:

1. **Endow Visuomotor Diffusion Policies with Spatial-Semantic Understanding:** The central objective is to integrate spatial prediction and semantic segmentation features from large pre-trained models into visuomotor diffusion policies. This aims to enable these policies to generalize from expert demonstrations on a single instance of a task to its broader category-level skill. The goal is for the robot to learn the functional aspects of a skill, such as "wiping" or "scooping," rather than specific visual features associated with a particular object or material.

2. **Introduce an Efficient Spatial-Semantic Information Representation:** The authors aim to develop an efficient and practical method for representing spatial-semantic information. This involves combining a promptable semantic module (Grounded-SAM2) with a depth estimation network (DepthAnythingV2) using only a single RGB camera and the robot's proprioceptive information. This approach seeks to provide a rich, object-aware representation that is robust to task-irrelevant factors (e.g., background, object textures) while avoiding the need for complex multi-view 3D setups or dedicated depth sensors, making it suitable for real-time applications and mobile robotics.

3. **Demonstrate Generalizable and Effective Robotic Manipulation Policies:** The research seeks to validate the proposed method through extensive experiments on a diverse set of robot manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world. This objective involves comparing the proposed S²-Diffusion policy against various baselines to demonstrate its ability to:
 - Achieve high performance on challenging manipulation tasks.
 - Generalize from instance-level training to category-level execution, particularly on unseen instances within the same category.
 - Exhibit invariance to changes in category-irrelevant factors.
 - Maintain performance even with common challenges like transparent objects where depth sensors might struggle.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to advance the field of robot manipulation towards more adaptable and robust robotic systems capable of performing a wider range of tasks in varied environments with less training data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named **S²-Diffusion (Spatial-Semantic Diffusion policy)**, integrates spatial prediction with semantic segmentation features from large pre-trained models to achieve generalization from instance-level to category-level skills in robot manipulation. The approach leverages a single RGB camera feed and robot proprioceptive information.

The methodology consists of three main components:

**A. Diffusion for Robot Skill Learning:**
The core policy learning framework is a **conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)**. This model learns an imitation policy $\pi_\theta(a|o)$ by processing a dataset of expert demonstrations.
- **Action Prediction:** Instead of directly predicting actions, the DDPM learns to iteratively denoise a randomly sampled Gaussian noise, gradually transforming it into a noise-free action sequence $(a_K, a_{K-1}..., a_0)$. Each denoising step uses a neural network $\epsilon_\theta$ conditioned on the noisy action $a_k$, the observation $o$, and the current step $k$.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a mean squared error (MSE) loss. It samples a ground-truth action $a_0$ from the demonstration dataset, adds Gaussian noise $\epsilon_k$ for a random step $k$ to create a noisy action $a_k$, and then trains the policy $\pi_\theta$ to predict the original clean action $a_0$ from $a_k$, $k$, and the observation. The Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) scheduler is used for noise scheduling.

**B. Open-Vocabulary Spatial-Semantic Representation:**
To enable generalization, S²-Diffusion replaces raw RGB inputs with a custom spatial-semantic representation. This representation is constructed by combining features from two pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs):
- **Semantic Segmentation with Grounded-SAM2:** The Grounded-SAM2 model is used for zero-shot semantic segmentation. Given an input RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., "Rice. Bowl."), it generates a set of semantic masks. These masks are then combined using a pixel-wise maximum pooling operation across all segmentation masks to create a single fused semantic mask $z_f$. This process allows the system to focus on task-relevant objects identified by the prompt.
- **Depth Map Estimation with DepthAnythingV2:** The DepthAnythingV2 model (a 335M-parameter model) is employed to estimate relative depth maps $z_d$ from the same input RGB image. To ensure consistency across diverse scenes and improve robustness, these depth maps are normalized during both training and evaluation.
- **Spatial-Semantic Feature Fusion:** The resulting semantic feature vector ($z_f$) and spatial depth map ($z_d$) are concatenated along their channel dimensions to form the comprehensive spatial-semantic representation $z = z_f \oplus z_d$. This fused representation captures both *what* the objects are (semantics) and *where* they are in 3D space (spatial).
- **Observation Processing:** This combined spatial-semantic representation $z$ is then passed through a ResNet18 encoder to produce a visual feature vector $f_v$. This $f_v$, along with the robot's proprioceptive state $q$, forms the complete observation $o = (f_v, q)$ fed into the diffusion policy.

**C. Learning Semantic Diffusion Policy:**
The CNN-based diffusion policy architecture is adapted as the decision-making backbone. The training process involves:
1. **Data Collection:** Gathering expert trajectories, each consisting of observations (raw image, robot state) and corresponding robot actions.
2. **Representation Generation:** For each observed raw image, generating the spatial-semantic representation $f_v$ using Grounded-SAM2 and DepthAnythingV2, conditioned on a semantic query (text prompt).
3. **Action Denoising Training:** The policy $\pi_\theta$ is trained to predict clean actions $a_0$ from noisy actions $a_k$, current step $k$, visual feature $f_v$, and robot proprioceptive state $q$, minimizing the MSE loss as formulated in Equation 4.

This methodology aims to provide a robust, semantic-aware, and spatially informed policy that can interpret novel scenes and objects within a learned category, enabling zero-shot generalization from instance-level demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of S²-Diffusion was conducted through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, focusing on performance, category-level generalization, and the contributions of its semantic and spatial components.

**A. Simulation Experiments**

- **Setup:** Experiments were performed on six single-stage tasks from RoboCasa (ServeMug, CloseDoor, TurnOnMicrowave, TurnOffFaucet, MoveSoda, TurnOnStove) and two tasks in SAPIEN (InsertPencil, HangMug). Each RoboCasa task used 50 expert demonstrations, while SAPIEN tasks involved both seen and unseen object instances. RoboCasa provides inherent variations in object color and background.
- **Baselines:** The method was compared against BC-RNN, BC-Transformer, Diffusion Policy (RGB-only), S²-BC-Transformer (BC-Transformer with spatial-semantic data), and GenDP (a multi-view RGB-D method, for SAPIEN only).
- **RoboCasa Results (Table I):**
 - S²-Diffusion consistently achieved the highest success rates across all six RoboCasa tasks, with success rates ranging from 0.75 to 0.88.
 - Traditional imitation learning policies (BC-RNN, BC-Transformer, Diffusion Policy) showed relatively poor performance, especially on tasks like ServeMug and MoveSoda, with success rates often below 0.3. This was attributed to the high diversity of scenes and instance-level variations in the RoboCasa dataset, which caused RGB-only policies to be sensitive to object color and background changes.
 - S²-BC-Transformer, integrating spatial-semantic data into a BC-Transformer, outperformed its RGB-only counterpart and demonstrated competitive results on some tasks (e.g., 0.77 for CloseDoor), highlighting the value of the spatial-semantic representation. However, S²-Diffusion generally exhibited more robust performance.
- **SAPIEN Results (Table II):**
 - S²-Diffusion, using a single RGB camera, performed comparably to GenDP, a multi-view RGB-D method, on both "InsertPencil" and "HangMug" tasks.
 - Critically, on unseen instances of "InsertPencil" and "HangMug", S²-Diffusion maintained high success rates (0.90 for both), demonstrating effective generalization from instance-level training to novel instances within the same category.

**B. Real-World Experiments**

- **Setup:** Two real-world category tasks were evaluated: whiteboard-wiping and bowl-to-bowl scooping.
 - **Whiteboard-wiping:** 40 demonstrations for red-whiteboard-wiping. Tested on seen (red) and unseen instances (black, green scribbles).
 - **Bowl-to-bowl scooping:** 60 demonstrations for rice-bowl-to-bowl scooping. Tested on seen (rice) and unseen instances (choco-cereal, hearts-cereal, mixed-cereal).
 - A 7-DOF Franka Panda robot with an end-effector mounted RGB camera and task-specific tools was used. Language prompts for semantic observations were "handwriting. sponge." and "rice. bowl." (changed to "cereal. bowl." for cereal tasks).
- **Baselines and Ablations:** Comparison against RGB-only Diffusion Policy. Ablation study for scooping: Semantic-Diffusion (semantic only) and Spatial-Diffusion (depth only).
- **Whiteboard-Wiping Results (Fig. 5a):**
 - The baseline Diffusion Policy achieved 100% success on the seen (red-whiteboard-wiping) instance. However, its performance deteriorated significantly on unseen instances: 44% for black-whiteboard-wiping and 0% for green-whiteboard-wiping. This indicated a strong overfitting to instance-specific visual features.
 - S²-Diffusion maintained consistent high performance across all instances: 100% for red, 89% for black, and 89% for green. This demonstrated its ability to learn a category-level skill invariant to scribble color.
- **Bowl-to-Bowl Scooping Results (Fig. 5b):**
 - For the in-distribution rice-bowl-to-bowl scooping, S²-Diffusion achieved 100% success, while the baseline had 80%.
 - When transferring to unseen cereal scooping tasks (choco-cereal, hearts-cereal, mixed-cereal), the baseline Diffusion Policy failed entirely (0% success rate).
 - S²-Diffusion maintained high success rates (approximately 80% across all cereal instances) by adapting the semantic prompt to "cereal. bowl.". This confirmed its capability for category-level generalization to novel granular materials.

**C. Ablation Evaluation (Fig. 7)**

- **Setup:** Evaluated Semantic-Diffusion and Spatial-Diffusion on rice and mixed-cereal scooping tasks.
- **Results:**
 - The full S²-Diffusion model, integrating both modalities, achieved the highest success rates (100% on seen, ~80% on unseen).
 - **Semantic-Diffusion (semantic only):** Performance dropped to 40% (seen) and 50% (novel), indicating that semantic information alone is insufficient for robust 3D manipulation.
 - **Spatial-Diffusion (depth only):** Performance was better than semantic-only but still lower than the full model (70% on seen, 70% on novel). This emphasized the critical role of spatial information.
 - **Generalization to Transparent Objects:** S²-Diffusion showed only a small reduction in success rate for closing a transparent container, outperforming a baseline DP trained directly with a depth camera, which failed the task. This highlighted S²-Diffusion's robustness in scenarios challenging for conventional depth sensors.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that S²-Diffusion effectively addresses the generalization challenge in imitation learning, achieving superior performance over baselines by leveraging a rich spatial-semantic representation derived from readily available RGB input, without requiring specialized hardware like multi-view 3D sensors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents S²-Diffusion as a method to enhance the generalization capabilities of robot manipulation policies, moving beyond instance-specific learning to category-level skill transfer. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Generalization Gap in Imitation Learning:** The primary impact lies in addressing a long-standing challenge in imitation learning: the inability of learned skills to transfer effectively across different instances of the same task or object category. By demonstrating consistent performance on unseen instances (e.g., wiping different colored scribbles, scooping various granular materials) where traditional policies fail, S²-Diffusion provides a concrete step towards more robust and adaptable robotic systems. This reduces the burden of extensive, labor-intensive data collection and retraining for every minor variation in the environment.

- **Practicality and Efficiency with Single RGB Input:** A notable contribution is the ability to achieve robust category-level generalization using only a single RGB camera and the robot's proprioception. This eliminates the need for complex and costly multi-view 3D setups, depth sensors, or extensive scene scanning, making S²-Diffusion highly practical for real-world deployment. Its efficiency and reduced hardware requirements make it particularly attractive for applications where simplicity and cost-effectiveness are crucial, such as in consumer robotics, mobile manipulation, or environments with limited sensor budgets.

- **Leveraging Foundation Models for Robotics:** The work demonstrates an effective integration of recent advances in vision foundation models (Grounded-SAM2 for semantic segmentation and DepthAnythingV2 for depth estimation) into a robotic control framework. By using these large pre-trained models to create a rich spatial-semantic observation space, the policy can abstract away task-irrelevant visual details, focusing instead on functional aspects. This approach showcases a pathway for robotics to benefit from the generalization power embedded in large-scale visual-linguistic pre-training.

- **Robustness to Visual Variability:** S²-Diffusion exhibits invariance to changes in category-irrelevant factors like background textures and object appearances. The ability to handle transparent objects, a known challenge for many depth sensors, further underscores its robustness and applicability in common domestic or industrial settings where such objects are prevalent.

- **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction and Task Specification:** The open-vocabulary nature of the semantic module, driven by natural language prompts, simplifies task specification and allows for more intuitive human-robot interaction. Users can describe the target object or material, and the robot can adapt its behavior based on this semantic understanding, fostering more flexible and user-friendly robotic systems.

While the current work generalizes within a category (e.g., granular materials for scooping), it acknowledges limitations in transferring skills across functionally distinct categories (e.g., scooping granular material vs. frozen ice cream). Addressing these nuanced skill ontologies and potentially fine-tuning VLMs jointly with the policy network represent avenues for future research, which could further broaden the impact of this approach. Overall, S²-Diffusion marks progress towards robots that can learn and apply skills with a level of flexibility and adaptability more akin to human capabilities.
