# MWM: Mobile World Models for Action-Conditioned Consistent Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> MWM (Mobile World Models) improves visual planning for robot navigation by training a world model to generate action-conditioned future observations with enhanced consistency and faster inference. The system achieves a 4x speedup in rollout time while improving multi-step rollout quality and leads to better goal-image navigation performance in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing world models for embodied navigation often produce visually plausible but 'action-conditioned inconsistent' rollouts, where imagined trajectories deviate from true physical paths, undermining Model Predictive Control (MPC).
- Diffusion models, while good for visual generation, typically have high inference latency (many denoising steps) which is impractical for real-time MPC on robots, and current acceleration methods do not explicitly preserve action-conditioned consistency.

## Method

- A two-stage ==Structure-First, Consistency-Refine== training pipeline is introduced, pretraining a ==diffusion model== for scene structure and then post-training to mitigate error accumulation and improve action-conditioned consistency.
- ==Inference-Consistent State Distillation (ICSD)== is designed to enable efficient, few-step diffusion inference while explicitly preserving consistency, by bridging the gap between truncated training states and inference-time denoising endpoints.
- **MWM** integrates with ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== using the ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)== to search for optimal action sequences, evaluated by a terminal-frame perceptual objective against a goal image.

## Results

- **MWM** (with 5 DDIM steps) demonstrates superior action-conditioned consistency on the SCAND dataset, achieving lower **LPIPS (0.495)** and **DreamSim (0.337)** at 16s horizon compared to NWM (25 DDIM steps, LPIPS **0.569**, DreamSim **0.373**).
- It achieves a **4x speedup** in average rollout time (**2.3s** vs. **9.6s** for NWM) on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU, while maintaining or improving visual fidelity (lower FID scores).
- On real-world goal-image navigation tasks with the AIRBOT MMK2 platform, MWM achieves a higher Success Rate of **0.30** and lower Navigation Error of **2.16**, outperforming baselines like NoMaD (SR **0.08**, NE **2.88**) and NWM (SR **0.20**, NE **3.18**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing world model training into distinct structural learning and consistency refinement stages effectively balances visual fidelity with multi-step autoregressive accuracy.
> - Explicitly training for action-conditioned consistency using self-forcing and perceptual losses significantly reduces error accumulation in multi-step rollouts.
> - Introducing an 'inference-consistent state' during distillation helps preserve sequence consistency when accelerating diffusion models, addressing the mismatch between training and efficient inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI), specifically focusing on robot navigation through the application of world models. World models have emerged as a promising framework in Embodied AI and model-based reinforcement learning, enabling agents to plan in an imagined future observation space rather than relying solely on reactive, end-to-end action policies.

Existing approaches to learning-based visual navigation often employ end-to-end policies, such as those seen in GNM and ViNT, or diffusion-based policies like NoMaD. While these methods have advanced robot navigation, they typically do not explicitly model the future visual consequences of candidate actions. This limitation makes them less suitable for environments requiring lookahead planning, which is a key advantage of world model-based systems.

World model-based embodied navigation, exemplified by systems like Navigation World Models (NWM) and UniSim, has demonstrated the ability to generate egocentric future observations conditioned on robot actions, supporting model predictive control (MPC) in imagined visual space. However, a critical limitation in these existing world models is identified: their predictions, while often visually realistic, frequently lack "action-conditioned consistency." This refers to a scenario where the predicted visual rollout may appear plausible frame-by-frame but deviates significantly from the true trajectory that the same action sequence would induce in the real world. Such a mismatch undermines MPC, as planning quality depends on the fidelity of imagined rollouts reflecting real execution outcomes.

Furthermore, the work addresses challenges in the deployment of diffusion probabilistic models, which are a dominant paradigm for high-fidelity visual generation and are widely used in robot learning. Standard diffusion models typically require a large number of sequential denoising steps, leading to high inference latency that is impractical for real-time MPC on robot hardware. While methods like DDIM, DPM-Solver, and consistency distillation aim to accelerate sampling, they primarily focus on preserving *distributional fidelity* at the *individual frame level*. They do not explicitly maintain *action-conditioned rollout consistency* under accelerated sampling. This limitation is linked to the problem of autoregressive error accumulation, where models trained on ground-truth past frames perform poorly when conditioning on their own predictions at test time due to a distribution shift. Previous work such as Scheduled Sampling and DAgger addressed similar discrepancies in sequence models, and Self-Forcing applied this concept to video generation. In the context of robotic world models, this issue is particularly consequential as planning accuracy directly depends on whether the predicted terminal state accurately corresponds to the robot’s true physical location.

MWM attempts to bridge these gaps by developing a world model that simultaneously improves action-conditioned consistency for robust planning and enables efficient, consistency-preserving few-step diffusion inference for practical deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of MWM stem from two identified challenges in existing world model-based navigation systems:

**Challenge 1: Insufficient Action-Conditioned Consistency in Predicted Rollouts**

- **Motivation:** While current world models can generate visually plausible future observations for embodied navigation, these predictions often exhibit a lack of action-conditioned consistency. This means that a sequence of predicted frames, though individually realistic, may not accurately represent the real-world trajectory that would result from the same sequence of actions. This deviation, or "error accumulation," becomes problematic in multi-step rollouts, as small prediction errors at each step compound over time, leading the imagined trajectory to diverge significantly from the true physical path. Such inconsistencies degrade the effectiveness of Model Predictive Control (MPC), where planning relies on selecting actions based on the fidelity of these imagined futures. If the model's predictions do not reliably align with real-world outcomes, MPC may recommend actions that appear optimal in the simulated space but lead to erroneous robot behavior when executed.
- **Objective:** To explicitly reduce the discrepancy between model-predicted rollouts and actual observations. The goal is to develop a training strategy that can maintain high-fidelity image generation while substantially mitigating error accumulation throughout action-conditioned rollouts, ensuring that autoregressive predictions remain well-aligned with real observations over multiple steps.

**Challenge 2: Lack of Consistency Preservation during Efficient Diffusion Inference**

- **Motivation:** For real-world robot deployment, responsive planning and control necessitate fast inference, which typically requires performing few-step diffusion distillation. However, existing diffusion distillation methods primarily focus on achieving distributional similarity between the distilled model and the teacher model at the individual frame level. This distribution-level alignment does not explicitly ensure the preservation of action-conditioned consistency during the multi-step rollout process. Consequently, a mismatch can arise between the training-time objectives and the inference-time requirements, potentially weakening the rollout consistency vital for reliable planning.
- **Objective:** To design a few-step distillation method that explicitly preserves action-conditioned consistency, rather than just distributional fidelity. The motivation is to ensure that the distilled model maintains the reliability of its rollouts with respect to the true trajectories, which is crucial for MPC-based world models, where downstream planning depends on this consistency.

**Overall Goal:** The overarching objective is to propose MWM, a mobile world model that effectively addresses both challenges by enhancing action-conditioned consistency in visual planning and enabling efficient, consistency-preserving few-step diffusion inference, thereby improving both prediction quality and the practical utility of imagined rollouts for downstream navigation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MWM framework introduces a two-stage training pipeline and a specialized distillation mechanism to enhance action-conditioned consistency and inference efficiency.

**A. Two-Stage Training Pipeline: "Structure-First, Consistency-Refine"**

This paradigm separates the learning process into two distinct stages:

1. **Stage I: Structure Pretraining:**
 - **Purpose:** This initial stage focuses on learning fine-grained scene structure and illumination-dependent appearance, providing a robust predictive prior and a strong initialization for subsequent training.
 - **Approach:** MWM is pretrained as an action-conditioned diffusion model using a teacher-forcing setup. In this setup, the model learns to denoise a noised ground-truth next state representation `s_τ+1^(t)` given the current state `s_τ`, action `a_τ`, and diffusion timestep `t`.
 - **Technical Details:** The denoising function `F_θ` predicts a clean target `s_hat_τ+1`. The optimization uses a simple L2 reconstruction loss (`L_simple`) between `s_τ+1` and `s_hat_τ+1`. Action `a_τ` and timestep `t` are encoded using sin-cos features and injected into each CDiT block via AdaLN (Adaptive Layer Normalization). The current state `s_τ` attends to a memory of past states via cross-attention. This stage uses a CDiT-XL backbone.

2. **Stage II: Action-Conditioned Consistency (ACC) Post-training:**
 - **Purpose:** This stage refines the model's autoregressive rollout behavior, aiming to mitigate error accumulation and improve the alignment of action-conditioned observations with ground-truth trajectories, particularly under self-conditioned contexts. It also reduces the train-test mismatch observed after teacher-forcing pretraining.
 - **Approach:** The model continues training on the same dataset but is exposed to its *own predictions* as context, emulating the self-conditioned context encountered during test time. This follows the "self-forcing" principle.
 - **Technical Details:** For a rollout of length N, frames are generated autoregressively. A random truncation denoising step `k` (from a subset of timesteps `t_1..., t_T'`) is chosen. The reverse diffusion process runs from `t_T'` down to `t_k`. Crucially, gradient computation is only enabled for producing the final prediction at the truncation step `k`, denoted as `s_hat_τ^(0)`.
 - **Loss Function:** An observation-level consistency loss, `L_CSF`, is used. This is a multi-frame averaged perceptual loss based on LPIPS, comparing the self-generated frames `s_hat_τ^(0)` with the corresponding ground-truth observations `s_τ`.
 - **Parameter Update Strategy:** To preserve the high-fidelity image quality learned in Stage I, the CDiT backbone is *frozen*. Only the lightweight AdaLN modulation layers (responsible for injecting action conditioning) are optimized. This targeted training improves multi-step observation alignment without degrading structural and appearance details.

**B. Inference-Consistent State Distillation (ICSD)**

- **Purpose:** ICSD is designed to enable efficient, low-step diffusion self-distillation *within* the ACC post-training stage, ensuring that the consistency objective is preserved during accelerated inference. It addresses the issue of over-smoothed or blurred intermediate states that arise from truncated or skip-step denoising during training, which can mismatch the final inference-time endpoint.
- **Approach:** ICSD introduces an "inference-consistent state" `s_IC_τ` to explicitly bridge the discrepancy between the truncated training-time state and the inference-time denoising endpoint.
- **Technical Details:** The diffusion timestep `t` is injected into the model through the same AdaLN modulation network as the action condition. Therefore, optimizing AdaLN in Stage II also implicitly refines how timestep information is integrated, facilitating few-step diffusion.
 - When an estimate `s_hat_τ^(k)->(0)` is obtained by truncating the reverse process at step `k`, it can be blurry. ICSD uses a deterministic DDIM update (with η = 0) to compute `s_IC_τ`. Specifically, the autoregressive context `s_IC_<τ` (previous inference-consistent states) is used when predicting the current frame `s_hat_τ^(0)` at truncation step `t_k`. This aligns the context with a state closer to a fully denoised output, reducing the truncation-induced train-test mismatch and improving multi-step rollout consistency under few-step sampling.

**C. Planning with MWM**

- **Mechanism:** MWM integrates into a planning framework using Model Predictive Control (MPC), optimizing action sequences within the world-model's imagined rollout space.
- **Search Method:** The Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is employed for searching optimal action sequences, consistent with prior work like NWM.
- **Trajectory Evaluation:** A terminal-frame perceptual objective is used to score candidate trajectories. This involves decoding the rolled-out terminal latent state `s_T` back to pixels and computing its LPIPS similarity against a goal image `x_∗`. The objective `S(s_T, x_∗) = -LPIPS(Dec(s_T), x_∗)` aims to minimize the perceptual distance to the goal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MWM encompassed both benchmark datasets and real-world robot tasks, demonstrating improvements across visual fidelity, trajectory accuracy, planning success, and inference efficiency.

**A. Action-Conditioned Consistency (ACC)**
- **Metric:** LPIPS and DreamSim, measuring perceptual similarity between predicted and ground-truth observations.
- **Results (Table I):** MWM, even with only 5 DDIM denoising steps, consistently achieved lower LPIPS and DreamSim values across rollout horizons (1s, 2s, 4s, 8s, 16s) compared to NWM using both 25 and 5 DDIM steps. For instance, at 16s horizon, MWM (DDIM 5) recorded LPIPS of 0.495 and DreamSim of 0.337, outperforming NWM (DDIM 25) which had LPIPS of 0.569 and DreamSim of 0.373. This indicates superior action-conditioned observation consistency with ground-truth trajectories on the SCAND dataset. The results demonstrate that MWM's post-training enables significant acceleration (80% reduction in denoising steps) while improving multi-step rollout quality beyond slower baselines.

**B. Generation Quality and Inference Efficiency**
- **Metrics:** FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) for visual fidelity and average rollout time for efficiency.
- **Results (Table II & III):**
 - **FID:** MWM (DDIM 5) achieved lower FID scores than NWM (DDIM 25) across all horizons (e.g., 93.12 vs. 93.63 at 16s horizon), indicating better overall visual fidelity at the distribution level. NWM (DDIM 5) exhibited a substantial degradation in generation quality, with significantly higher FID scores.
 - **Inference Efficiency:** MWM (DDIM 5) achieved an average rollout time of 2.3 seconds per rollout on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU, offering at least a 4x speedup compared to NWM (DDIM 25) which took 9.6 seconds. This efficiency was achieved while maintaining or improving generation quality.

**C. Navigation Performance**
- **Metrics:** Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) for trajectory accuracy.
- **Results (Table IV):** MWM achieved the lowest ATE (1.14) and RPE (0.302) on SCAND under CEM-based goal-image planning. This performance surpassed various baselines, including GNM, NoMaD, and different configurations of NWM, positioning MWM as a leading method for navigation performance in this evaluation.

**D. Qualitative Results**
- **SCAND Dataset (Figure 3):** Predicted frames from MWM exhibited strong action-conditioned consistency with the ground-truth frames. The visual rollouts were temporally coherent and aligned well with the executed actions across multiple steps.
- **Real-World Evaluation (Figure 4):** MWM produced planned rollouts whose optimal trajectories demonstrated improved consistency with the robot's real observations compared to NWM. This suggests a reduction in multi-step error accumulation, which contributes to enhanced planning quality in real environments.

**E. Ablation Studies**
- **Effect of ACC Loss Design (Table V):** Perceptual LPIPS-based loss for ACC consistently outperformed pixel-wise L1 and L2 losses across FID, DreamSim, and LPIPS metrics. This finding supports the use of perceptual feature space for consistency learning.
- **Effect of Training Paradigm (Table VI):** The two-stage "Structure training + ACC training" paradigm yielded the best overall performance on MMK2-RealNav (lowest FID, DreamSim, LPIPS). Training with only ACC resulted in the worst performance, while structure-only training was intermediate. This validates the sequential "structure-first, consistency-refine" design.
- **Effect of Inference-Consistent State (Table VII):** Using the inference-consistent context `s_IC_<τ` (ICSD) in ACC training consistently led to better performance (lower FID, DreamSim, LPIPS) compared to using `s_hat_<τ^(0)` as context. This confirms ICSD's role in mitigating mismatches arising from intermediate states during few-step distillation.

**F. Real-World Evaluation**
- **Platform:** AIRBOT Mobile Manipulation Kit 2 (MMK2) mobile manipulation platform.
- **Task:** Goal-image navigation in diverse indoor university building environments.
- **Quantitative Results (Table VIII):** MWM achieved a higher Success Rate (SR) of 0.30 and a lower Navigation Error (NE) of 2.16, outperforming NoMaD (SR 0.08, NE 2.88) and NWM (SR 0.20, NE 3.18). This demonstrates MWM's improved reliability in real-world conditions with sensing and actuation noise.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 1, 4):** Real-world demonstrations and comparative visualizations further supported MWM's ability to generate action-conditioned future trajectories that align more closely with observed robot movements, leading to successful image-goal navigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MWM framework presents several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the fields of embodied AI and robotics:

- **Enhanced Reliability for Model Predictive Control:** By explicitly addressing the issue of action-conditioned consistency in world model predictions, MWM provides a more reliable foundation for Model Predictive Control (MPC). The improved alignment between imagined rollouts and real-world trajectories is critical for effective planning, leading to higher success rates and reduced errors in navigation tasks. This directly impacts the trustworthiness and applicability of world model-based planning in real robot systems.

- **Practical Efficiency for Real-World Deployment:** MWM successfully integrates efficient few-step diffusion inference while *improving* prediction quality and consistency. The demonstrated 4x speedup over previous methods, without sacrificing performance, makes diffusion-based world models significantly more viable for real-time robotic applications where low latency is a crucial requirement for responsive control and continuous replanning.

- **Novel Training Paradigm:** The proposed two-stage "structure-first, consistency-refine" training pipeline, coupled with the Inference-Consistent State Distillation (ICSD) mechanism, offers a novel approach to training robust and efficient world models. This paradigm effectively balances the learning of detailed environmental structures with the refinement of autoregressive consistency, providing a blueprint that could be adapted for other embodied AI tasks requiring sequential prediction.

- **Advancement in Diffusion Model Distillation:** The work contributes to the broader understanding and application of diffusion models by demonstrating how to perform distillation not just for distributional fidelity but specifically for preserving sequential, action-conditioned consistency. ICSD addresses a particular challenge in making few-step diffusion robust for autoregressive tasks, extending the utility of diffusion acceleration techniques.

- **Foundation for Closed-Loop Control:** MWM establishes itself as an effective interface between visual prediction and navigation control. The paper explicitly identifies future work towards extending MWM to real-time closed-loop navigation, where the model would continuously update its imagined rollouts and replan based on new observations. This represents a significant step towards enabling robots to operate more autonomously and adaptively in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments.

In summary, MWM advances the state of the art in embodied navigation by overcoming critical limitations of existing world models, offering a path towards more consistent, efficient, and reliable robotic agents.
