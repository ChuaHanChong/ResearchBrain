# TouchAnything: A Dataset and Framework for Bimanual Tactile Estimation from Egocentric Video

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoTouch, a multi-view egocentric dataset with dense bimanual tactile supervision, along with the TouchAnything framework, addresses the challenge of inferring tactile pressure maps from visual input. This approach, which leverages complementary wrist-mounted camera views, improves Contact IoU by 5.0% and Volumetric IoU by 6.1% on seen objects compared to egocentric-only methods.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric vision datasets for embodied intelligence lack tactile information, which is crucial for understanding physical interaction dynamics.
- Scalable collection of tactile data is hindered by expensive and cumbersome specialized hardware, while current vision-to-touch prediction methods are limited by single-view capture or restricted interaction contexts.
- Egocentric camera views frequently suffer from occlusion of hand-object contact regions, making accurate tactile inference challenging.

## Method

- Introduction of EgoTouch, a large-scale, multi-modal dataset comprising 20 hours of synchronized multi-view egocentric video (head-mounted and wrist-mounted), bimanual 3D hand pose, and dense tactile pressure maps.
- Development of the TouchAnything framework, a multi-view vision-to-touch prediction model featuring a shared visual encoder, cross-view fusion module, temporal modeling, and pose-vision fusion for inferring bimanual pressure maps.
- Implementation of a view dropout training strategy to enable robust tactile prediction even when certain camera views are unavailable during inference, fostering adaptability to varying sensor configurations.

## Results

- The TouchAnything framework, utilizing full multi-view input, achieved improved tactile prediction on seen objects with a 5.0% relative increase in Contact IoU (from 0.4792 to 0.5030) and a 6.1% relative increase in Volumetric IoU (from 0.4311 to 0.4575) compared to egocentric-only baselines.
- Generalization to unseen objects also showed improvements, with Contact IoU increasing by 2.3% and Volumetric IoU by 2.9%, demonstrating the method's effectiveness beyond specific trained instances.
- View dropout during training was critical for robust performance, reducing the performance drop between all-view and ego-only inference from -27.20% (without dropout) to -5.78% (with dropout) in Volumetric IoU, highlighting its role in handling missing input views.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complementary wrist-mounted camera views are crucial for accurately inferring tactile information, particularly for localizing contact and estimating pressure magnitude, by resolving occlusions inherent in head-mounted egocentric views.
> - A view dropout training strategy is essential for developing models that can flexibly adapt to different subsets of available camera inputs, ensuring robust performance whether using full multi-view or egocentric-only inputs.
> - Scalable, multi-modal datasets like EgoTouch are vital for training physically grounded embodied AI models, as performance in vision-to-touch prediction consistently improves with increased data volume.
