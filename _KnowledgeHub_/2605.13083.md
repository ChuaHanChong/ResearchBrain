# TouchAnything: A Dataset and Framework for Bimanual Tactile Estimation from Egocentric Video

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoTouch, a multi-view egocentric dataset with dense bimanual tactile supervision, along with the TouchAnything framework, addresses the challenge of inferring tactile pressure maps from visual input. This approach, which leverages complementary wrist-mounted camera views, improves Contact IoU by 5.0% and Volumetric IoU by 6.1% on seen objects compared to egocentric-only methods.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric vision datasets for embodied intelligence lack tactile information, which is crucial for understanding physical interaction dynamics.
- Scalable collection of tactile data is hindered by expensive and cumbersome specialized hardware, while current vision-to-touch prediction methods are limited by single-view capture or restricted interaction contexts.
- Egocentric camera views frequently suffer from occlusion of hand-object contact regions, making accurate tactile inference challenging.

## Method

- Introduction of EgoTouch, a large-scale, multi-modal dataset comprising 20 hours of synchronized multi-view egocentric video (head-mounted and wrist-mounted), bimanual 3D hand pose, and dense tactile pressure maps.
- Development of the TouchAnything framework, a multi-view vision-to-touch prediction model featuring a shared visual encoder, cross-view fusion module, temporal modeling, and pose-vision fusion for inferring bimanual pressure maps.
- Implementation of a view dropout training strategy to enable robust tactile prediction even when certain camera views are unavailable during inference, fostering adaptability to varying sensor configurations.

## Results

- The TouchAnything framework, utilizing full multi-view input, achieved improved tactile prediction on seen objects with a 5.0% relative increase in Contact IoU (from 0.4792 to 0.5030) and a 6.1% relative increase in Volumetric IoU (from 0.4311 to 0.4575) compared to egocentric-only baselines.
- Generalization to unseen objects also showed improvements, with Contact IoU increasing by 2.3% and Volumetric IoU by 2.9%, demonstrating the method's effectiveness beyond specific trained instances.
- View dropout during training was critical for robust performance, reducing the performance drop between all-view and ego-only inference from -27.20% (without dropout) to -5.78% (with dropout) in Volumetric IoU, highlighting its role in handling missing input views.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complementary wrist-mounted camera views are crucial for accurately inferring tactile information, particularly for localizing contact and estimating pressure magnitude, by resolving occlusions inherent in head-mounted egocentric views.
> - A view dropout training strategy is essential for developing models that can flexibly adapt to different subsets of available camera inputs, ensuring robust performance whether using full multi-view or egocentric-only inputs.
> - Scalable, multi-modal datasets like EgoTouch are vital for training physically grounded embodied AI models, as performance in vision-to-touch prediction consistently improves with increased data volume.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work situates itself within the field of embodied intelligence, specifically focusing on the intersection of egocentric vision and tactile sensing for understanding human-environment interactions. Egocentric video datasets have become a primary resource for embodied intelligence due to their scalability and ability to capture natural human behaviors. However, a limitation of most existing egocentric datasets is the absence of tactile information, which provides direct physical cues regarding contact, force, and pressure. Without these signals, computational models may develop physically ungrounded representations of interaction dynamics.

Current approaches to tactile sensing involve either specialized hardware, which is often expensive and challenging to deploy at scale, or vision-to-touch prediction methods. Existing vision-to-touch datasets and models are often limited by relying on single-view capture, focusing on narrow interaction contexts (e.g., hand-surface contact, single-finger presses), or lacking bimanual interaction data. A significant challenge in egocentric vision-to-touch prediction is the frequent occlusion of hand-object contact regions by the hands themselves or the manipulated objects when observed from a head-mounted camera. This occlusion introduces ambiguity, making it difficult to infer accurate tactile signals solely from egocentric visual input.

Prior egocentric datasets, such as Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS, EgoDex, EgoPressure, and OpenTouch, have advanced activity understanding and hand-object interaction. However, they typically either lack dense tactile annotations, do not provide synchronized multi-view video from contact-aware perspectives, or are restricted to single-hand interactions. Multi-view learning has been explored for geometric reconstruction or high-level action recognition, but existing multi-view datasets and methods generally do not offer direct observation of the hand-object contact interface, particularly on palmar surfaces.

This research addresses these gaps by introducing a dataset, EgoTouch, that combines multi-view egocentric video (including wrist-mounted cameras for contact-region observation), bimanual 3D hand pose, and dense real tactile pressure maps. It also proposes a framework, TouchAnything, designed to leverage these complementary visual inputs to infer tactile feedback, thereby providing a foundation for scalable tactile supervision and physically grounded embodied learning in complex, bimanual manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the recognized importance of tactile sensing for embodied intelligence and the limitations in its current availability and scalability within large-scale egocentric datasets. The researchers identify a fundamental bottleneck: the contrast between the abundance of visual data and the scarcity of tactile supervision.

The key motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Necessity of Tactile Sensing for Embodied Intelligence:** Tactile signals are critical for models to develop a deep understanding of physical interaction dynamics. They provide direct cues regarding contact, force, and pressure, which are not explicitly available from visual observations alone. Without tactile feedback, embodied models may struggle to learn physically grounded representations required for successful manipulation in the real world.
- **Scalability Challenges of Tactile Hardware:** Deploying high-quality tactile sensors at a large scale is currently expensive, intrusive, and cumbersome. This hardware dependency limits the widespread collection of tactile data, creating a data gap despite the advancements in scalable visual data collection.
- **Limitations of Existing Vision-to-Touch Approaches and Datasets:**
 - **Data Scarcity:** Existing datasets for vision-to-touch prediction are often too small or focus on restricted interaction settings, such as single-view capture, simple hand-surface contact, or single-finger pressing. These limitations hinder the study of tactile prediction in diverse, realistic, and complex bimanual hand-object interactions.
 - **Occlusion in Egocentric Views:** A significant challenge in egocentric scenarios is the frequent occlusion of hand-object contact regions. The head-mounted egocentric camera often cannot directly observe the palmar surfaces where contact occurs, leading to substantial ambiguity in inferring tactile signals. This problem cannot be resolved by model capacity alone but requires additional viewpoints.
- **Demand for Physically Grounded Representations:** The ability to infer tactile feedback directly from visual observations would enable scalable tactile supervision for large-scale egocentric data. This, in turn, could support interaction-aware embodied learning, allowing models to develop a more profound understanding of the physical world.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Introduce EgoTouch:** To create and release a large-scale, multi-view egocentric dataset with dense tactile supervision for bimanual hand-object interaction. This dataset aims to overcome the limitations of existing datasets by providing synchronized head-mounted and wrist-mounted RGB videos, bimanual 3D hand pose, and continuous pressure maps across diverse tasks and environments, offering complementary visual evidence for learning contact-aware interactions.
2. **Establish a Multi-View Vision-to-Touch Benchmark:** To define evaluation protocols on EgoTouch for tactile prediction under different camera-view configurations and for generalization to unseen objects, enabling systematic analysis of how complementary wrist views affect tactile prediction performance.
3. **Propose TouchAnything Framework:** To develop a baseline multi-view vision-to-touch prediction framework that leverages cross-view fusion and a view dropout training strategy. This framework is designed to support flexible inference with varying subsets of available camera views (egocentric-only or multi-view inputs) and to demonstrate improved tactile prediction performance when additional contact-aware views are available.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work consists of two main components: the EgoTouch dataset and the TouchAnything prediction framework.

#### 3.1. EgoTouch Dataset

The EgoTouch dataset is a large-scale multi-modal collection designed for bimanual hand-object interaction, offering dense tactile supervision.
- **Scale and Diversity:** It comprises 20 hours of synchronized data, 1,891 episodes, and 208 manipulation tasks, covering over 1,000 objects in varied indoor and outdoor environments. This equates to approximately 2.1 million frames.
- **Data Collection Setup:** A synchronized wearable capture system records multiple modalities at 30 Hz:
 - **Multi-view RGB Videos:** A head-mounted camera provides a global egocentric view (640x480 resolution). Two wrist-mounted fisheye cameras (640x480) offer close-up observations of hand-object contact regions.
 - **Bimanual 3D Hand Pose:** Rokoko motion-capture gloves record 42 three-dimensional joints per hand (wrist, finger, fingertip).
 - **Dense Tactile Pressure Maps:** Custom pressure-sensing gloves, each with a 16x16 tactile array on the palm, record bilateral raw pressure. These are then normalized and remapped to canonical 21x21 hand-shaped grids.
 - **6-DoF Localization:** HTC Vive Trackers are mounted on the head and wrists for global pose tracking.
- **Synchronization Strategy:** All data streams are synchronized using software-based time-stamping, ensuring frame-level alignment across modalities.
- **Data Modalities and Preprocessing:** Each frame is organized with synchronized RGB videos, 3D hand pose, tactile pressure maps, and tracker poses. The raw 256-channel tactile data is remapped to a 21x21 hand-shaped grid, with the right hand horizontally mirrored for consistent canonical representation. Preprocessing includes optional baseline pressure subtraction, interpolation for broken sensor columns, and separate normalization for pressure and bending-related sensors.
- **Task Taxonomy:** The 208 tasks are categorized into five environment-based groups: Home, Workbench, Office, Retail, and Outdoor, capturing a range of real-world interactions.
- **Data Storage:** Data is converted into HDF5 files for efficient loading, storing RGB frames, pose data, and pressure grids with metadata.

#### 3.2. TouchAnything Framework

TouchAnything is a baseline multi-view vision-to-touch prediction model designed to infer dense bimanual pressure maps from visual observations and hand pose, specifically addressing occlusion and supporting flexible inference under varying view availability.
- **Problem Formulation:** Given a video clip of `T` frames from a subset of available views `V` (egocentric, left wrist, right wrist) and bimanual 3D hand pose sequence `P`, the goal is to predict bilateral tactile maps `M_hat` (2x21x21) for each timestep.
- **Architecture:**
 - **Shared Visual Encoder with View Embeddings:** A frozen DINOv2-Base (ViT-B/14) backbone processes each input view independently. Learnable view embeddings are added to the extracted patch tokens to distinguish between camera identities (egocentric, left wrist, right wrist), promoting parameter efficiency and flexibility.
 - **Cross-View Fusion Module:** A lightweight cross-view transformer processes summary tokens (obtained via global average pooling) from each view, allowing views to exchange complementary information. A gated mechanism then dynamically weighs the contribution of each view for a unified visual representation.
 - **Temporal Modeling:** A windowed temporal transformer processes the fused visual features to capture interaction dynamics across time.
 - **Pose Encoder and Pose-Vision Fusion:** Bimanual hand pose is encoded into joint-level features by a transformer-based encoder. These joint features are then fused with the temporal visual features via cross-attention, enabling spatially grounded reasoning by allowing each joint to attend to relevant visual regions.
 - **Joint-Level Tactile Decoder:** The fused joint features are separated into left and right hand groups. Each group is independently decoded into a 21x21 pressure map using a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) followed by a sigmoid activation function, normalizing outputs to.
- **View Dropout Training Strategy:** During training, the egocentric view is always retained, while each wrist view is randomly dropped with a probability `p=0.3`. This strategy forces the model to learn robust representations that can operate effectively with different subsets of views at inference time, including egocentric-only, single-wrist, or full multi-view inputs, without requiring architectural changes.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a weighted regression loss (`L`). This loss combines:
 - Mean Squared Error (`L_MSE`) and L1 loss (`L_L1`) for pixel-wise pressure reconstruction.
 - Total Variation loss (`L_TV`) to encourage spatial smoothness in the predicted pressure maps.
 - **Contact-aware weighting:** To address the sparsity of tactile signals (many zero-pressure pixels), contact regions (pixels with pressure > 0.1) are assigned a higher loss weight (3.0) to prevent the model from collapsing to trivial all-zero predictions.
- **Optimization:** The model is optimized using AdamW, with a cosine learning-rate schedule including warmup epochs. The DINOv2 visual encoder is kept frozen.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset Split:** The EgoTouch dataset is split into 80% training, 10% validation, and 10% testing at the episode level, with the test set further subdivided into seen and unseen object categories to assess generalization.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Performance is evaluated using Temporal Accuracy (binary contact onset/termination), Contact IoU (spatial and temporal accuracy of binary contact), Volumetric IoU (incorporates pressure magnitude), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) over normalized pressure values.
- **Ablation Studies:** Experiments include comparing different camera configurations (ego-only, ego + single wrist, ego + both wrists) and studying the effect of view dropout and varying amounts of training data (25%, 50%, 75%, 100% of the dataset).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the EgoTouch dataset using the TouchAnything framework yielded several key findings regarding the efficacy of multi-view tactile prediction and the design choices.

#### 4.1. Impact of Wrist-Mounted Views

The results demonstrate that incorporating wrist-mounted views consistently improves tactile prediction performance compared to using only the egocentric view.
- **Overall Performance Gains:** On seen objects, the full multi-view setting (Ego + wL + wR) improved Contact IoU by 5.0% relative (from 0.4792 to 0.5030) and Volumetric IoU by 6.1% relative (from 0.4311 to 0.4575) compared to the Ego-only baseline. A decrease in MAE was also observed, indicating more accurate pressure magnitude estimation.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** Similar positive trends were observed on unseen objects, though the relative improvements were smaller (e.g., Contact IoU improved by 2.3% and Volumetric IoU by 2.9%). This indicates that wrist views provide benefits even when generalizing to novel object instances.
- **Specific Task Scenarios:** Improvements were consistent across all five environment categories (Home, Workbench, Office, Retail, Outdoor), suggesting the broad applicability of complementary views.
- **Nature of Improvement:** Wrist views primarily enhanced the localization of contact and the estimation of pressure magnitude (Contact IoU, Volumetric IoU, MAE). Temporal Accuracy, which assesses the timing of contact presence, showed more modest and varied changes, suggesting that determining *whether* contact occurs is less dependent on wrist views than *where* and *how strongly* it occurs.
- **Single vs. Dual Wrist Views:** A single wrist view was found to capture a substantial portion of the complementary evidence, likely due to the broad coverage of fisheye cameras, which can sometimes observe the opposite hand. The addition of the second wrist view provided further gains, primarily under scenarios of stronger bimanual occlusion.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of View Dropout:** Training the model without the view dropout strategy led to a significant performance degradation when wrist views were unavailable at inference time. For instance, the relative Volumetric IoU drop (∆V) between all-view and ego-only inference was -27.20% without view dropout. In contrast, with view dropout enabled, this drop was reduced to -5.78%. This indicates that view dropout is critical for developing a model that is robust to missing input views and can generalize effectively to partial-view inputs while maintaining strong performance in full multi-view settings. The strategy encourages the model to learn complementary representations across views rather than relying on a fixed camera configuration.
- **Performance Scales with Data:** An analysis of performance across varying amounts of training data (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) showed consistent improvements in Contact IoU, Volumetric IoU, and MAE as more data was utilized. The observed gains did not saturate at higher data regimes, suggesting that the vision-to-touch prediction task remains data-hungry and could further benefit from even larger and more diverse datasets.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results and Failure Cases

- **Occlusion Resolution:** Qualitative examples demonstrated that egocentric-only predictions often suffered from missing contact regions due to occlusion. Multi-view predictions, enabled by wrist-mounted cameras directly observing the contact interface, successfully recovered both the location and intensity of these previously occluded contacts, affirming the value of complementary viewpoints.
- **Failure Modes:** A notable failure case involved incorrect contact prediction when there was low visual contrast between the hand and the object (e.g., a black glove interacting with black shorts). In such instances, the model misinterprets visual proximity or overlap as actual contact, suggesting a reliance on appearance-based cues that can be misled by color similarity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the fields of embodied intelligence and robotic manipulation by addressing a fundamental challenge: the integration of tactile information, which is critical for understanding physical interaction, with scalable visual data.

The **EgoTouch dataset** represents a significant contribution as it is the first large-scale, multi-view egocentric dataset to include dense bimanual tactile supervision, synchronized with 3D hand pose and diverse manipulation tasks across varied environments. This dataset provides a novel resource for researchers, enabling the study of physically grounded interactions in complex, real-world settings where previous datasets were limited by either the absence of tactile data, lack of multiple contact-aware viewpoints, or restriction to single-hand interactions.

The **TouchAnything framework** establishes a baseline for multi-view vision-to-touch prediction. Its design, particularly the use of cross-view fusion and a view dropout training strategy, demonstrates a robust approach to inferring tactile feedback from visual input. The empirical results show that contact-aware wrist-mounted views significantly improve prediction accuracy, especially for contact localization and pressure estimation under common egocentric occlusion.

The **significance and potential impact** of this work include:

- **Enabling Scalable Tactile Supervision:** By demonstrating the feasibility of inferring tactile feedback from visual observations, this work offers a path toward scalable tactile supervision. This could reduce the reliance on expensive and cumbersome physical tactile sensors in many applications, broadening the accessibility of tactile information for embodied systems.
- **Advancing Physically Grounded Embodied Learning:** The dataset and framework facilitate the development of models that can learn more physically grounded and interaction-aware representations. This is crucial for robots to understand and execute complex manipulation tasks, predict grasp stability, and develop more sophisticated world models that incorporate physical interaction dynamics.
- **Improved Robustness in Complex Interactions:** The multi-view approach, particularly the strategic use of wrist-mounted cameras, offers a method to overcome occlusion challenges inherent in egocentric vision. This enhances the robustness of tactile prediction in dynamic and varied human-object interactions, making future embodied agents more capable in unstructured environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** EgoTouch and TouchAnything establish a benchmark that can stimulate further research in several directions:
 - **Contact-aware manipulation:** Developing robot policies that leverage inferred tactile signals for finer control and adaptive manipulation strategies.
 - **Grasp stability prediction:** Using vision-inferred touch to anticipate and react to changes in grasp stability.
 - **Affordance learning:** Understanding the functional properties of objects based on how they are touched and manipulated.
 - **Tactile-enhanced world models:** Building richer, physically aware representations of the environment.
 - **Robot policy learning from human demonstrations:** Transferring human manipulation skills to robots, augmented with inferred tactile expertise.
- **Methodological Innovation:** The view dropout strategy is a practical contribution for training neural networks to operate flexibly with varying sensor configurations, a common challenge in real-world deployments where sensor availability can fluctuate.

While limitations such as glove-specific appearance bias and the data-hungry nature of the task are acknowledged, the work lays a foundation for future advancements in making tactile perception more accessible and impactful for embodied intelligence.
