# Synthesis of Model Predictive Control and Reinforcement Learning: Survey and Classification

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey establishes a systematic classification of methods synthesizing Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), categorizing approaches based on MPC's role as an expert actor, within a deployed policy, or as a critic. It details their complementary strengths, theoretical foundations, and practical implementation challenges, offering a structured roadmap for interdisciplinary research.

## Problem

- MPC and RL, despite solving similar control problems, possess orthogonal strengths and weaknesses, making their direct integration complex without a unifying framework.
- MPC traditionally requires accurate mathematical models and can be computationally expensive, while RL often lacks safety guarantees, constraint satisfaction, and sample efficiency.
- The burgeoning field of MPC-RL combinations lacked a systematic classification and detailed comparative analysis, hindering interdisciplinary understanding and targeted research.

## Method

- The authors systematically review, synthesize, and classify existing literature on MPC-RL combinations into a novel framework centered around the ==actor-critic RL paradigm==.
- They provide a detailed comparative analysis of MPC and RL, highlighting their distinct characteristics, shared principles, and inherent advantages and disadvantages.
- The work identifies and defines ==four architectural archetypes== for integrating neural networks with MPC, such as Integrated, Hierarchical, Parallel, and Parameterized MPC, to support the classification.

## Results

- The survey produced a novel, actor-critic-centric classification scheme for MPC-RL combinations, categorizing approaches into MPC as an expert actor, within the deployed policy, or as a critic, detailed across **nine** sub-categories.
- **Four** distinct architectural archetypes for integrating neural networks with MPC (Integrated, Hierarchical, Parallel, Parameterized) were identified, providing a meta-framework for structural understanding.
- Key practical challenges such as the need for differentiable optimization solvers and the computational overhead of repeated optimization were detailed, alongside emerging software tools designed to bridge the gap between ML frameworks and MPC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MPC and RL possess fundamentally complementary strengths, with MPC excelling in constraint handling and stability and RL in data-driven adaptation and complex environments, justifying their synergistic combination.
> - MPC can be systematically integrated into the actor-critic reinforcement learning framework in three primary roles: as an expert actor, as an integral component of the deployed policy, or as a critic for value estimation.
> - Theoretical analysis reveals that appropriately formulated Economic NMPC can approximate optimal value functions and policies for discounted Markov Decision Processes, providing a strong basis for their integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a critical and rapidly expanding area within control engineering and artificial intelligence. Historically, these two fields developed largely in parallel, originating from distinct scientific communities with differing paradigms and requirements for solving Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). MPC, rooted in optimization-based control engineering, traditionally relies on explicit mathematical models derived from first principles, offering robust stability, constraint satisfaction, and interpretability. RL, conversely, emerged from machine learning, focusing on data-driven policy optimization, often without explicit environment models, excelling in complex, high-dimensional, or poorly understood environments.

This survey paper positions itself at the nexus of these two powerful methodologies, addressing the increasing recognition that their advantages are "nearly complementary" and "orthogonal." While MPC struggles with model uncertainty, high computational demands for complex models, and adaptation to changing environments, RL often faces challenges with sample efficiency, safety guarantees, constraint satisfaction, and explainability. The core premise driving combination research, and thus this survey, is that a synergistic approach can mitigate the individual weaknesses of each method while leveraging their strengths.

The paper extensively references existing works in its "Related Work" section (I.A), distinguishing its contribution from prior surveys:
- **Conceptual comparisons:** Works like provided conceptual links between MPC and RL, particularly for linear systems. This survey expands on this by offering a systematic classification of *combination algorithms*.
- **RL from a control perspective:** contextualized RL for control systems, briefly mentioning MPC. This paper specifically targets the *synthesis* of the two.
- **Safe learning in robotics:** surveyed methods for safe learning, touching upon MPC's role. This survey delves deeper into how MPC specifically contributes to safety and constraint handling within RL frameworks.
- **Machine learning within MPC:** Surveys such as and focused on general machine learning techniques applied to MPC (e.g., learning models or terminal costs). This work *specifically* examines the deep integration and interaction between MPC and *Reinforcement Learning*, extending beyond general ML applications to address how RL's learning paradigms directly inform or are informed by MPC structures.

Crucially, the authors note that their work can be seen as "complementary" to previous efforts like (which compares RL and MPC from an approximate dynamic programming perspective) and (focused on general ML in MPC). This survey's unique contribution lies in its comprehensive classification system centered around the versatile "actor-critic RL approach," systematically mapping how MPC can function as an expert actor, as part of a deployed policy, or as a critic. By doing so, it provides a much-needed structured overview that helps organize the growing body of complex ideas at this interdisciplinary frontier, illuminating the underlying principles and practical implications of various hybrid MPC-RL algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research paper are clearly articulated in its abstract and introduction:

1. **Illuminate Differences, Similarities, and Fundamentals:** To thoroughly analyze the distinct characteristics, shared principles, and foundational elements that enable the combination of MPC and RL algorithms. This involves detailing their historical development, core mechanisms, and inherent advantages and disadvantages (Section V).
2. **Categorize Existing Work:** To propose a systematic and comprehensive classification scheme for the diverse array of existing MPC-RL combination methods. This classification aims to organize the rapidly growing literature, providing a structured framework for understanding how these two control paradigms are integrated (Section VI).
3. **Focus on Actor-Critic RL:** To specifically examine how MPC can be leveraged within the "versatile actor-critic RL approach" as a central framework for categorization.
4. **Improve Closed-Loop Performance:** To investigate how the online optimization capabilities of MPC can be utilized to enhance the overall closed-loop performance of a learned policy, especially concerning aspects like safety, stability, and efficiency.

The motivation for undertaking this survey stems from several critical observations within the research landscape:

- **Orthogonal Benefits:** Both MPC and RL are successful control techniques for MDPs, yet they possess "nearly complementary advantages." MPC excels in leveraging models, incorporating constraints, and providing theoretical guarantees for stability and feasibility, but can be computationally expensive and sensitive to model inaccuracies. RL, while powerful in learning from data in complex environments, often struggles with safety, interpretability, and sample efficiency. The desire to combine these orthogonal strengths to overcome individual weaknesses is a significant driving force.
- **Increasing Research Interest:** The recognition of these complementary benefits has led to a "significant" and "large and growing set of complex ideas leveraging MPC and RL." There is a clear need to systematically organize and analyze this burgeoning field.
- **Bridging Communities:** The authors acknowledge that MPC and RL emerged from "diverse communities and different requirements," leading to distinct methodologies. This survey is motivated by the need to bridge this gap, foster interdisciplinary understanding, and facilitate the development of more robust, efficient, and safe control solutions.
- **Practical Applications:** Both MPC and RL are widely used in practical applications (robotics, process control, energy systems, autonomous driving). The motivation includes synthesizing methods that can achieve better performance in these real-world scenarios by combining model-based reasoning with data-driven adaptation.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a definitive guide to the "complex ideas" at the intersection of MPC and RL, offering clarity and a roadmap for future research by systematizing current knowledge and highlighting key challenges and opportunities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey and classification paper, the methodology primarily involves a systematic review, synthesis, and categorization of existing literature. The authors adopt a structured approach to achieve their objectives:

1. **Foundational Background (Sections II-IV):** The paper begins by establishing a common theoretical ground.
 - **Problem Setting (Section II):** It introduces the Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework as the overarching problem domain that both MPC and RL aim to solve, defining key concepts like state space, action space, transition model, stage cost, policies, and value functions.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (Section III):** It provides a concise overview of RL's theoretical underpinnings, including dynamic programming, temporal difference (TD) methods (e.g., SARSA, Q-learning), and policy gradient (PG) methods (stochastic and deterministic). It then details influential deep RL algorithms such as Deep Q-Networks (DQN), Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).
 - **Model Predictive Control (Section IV):** It offers a detailed exposition of MPC, covering problem formulations (nominal, constrained NLP), key components (dynamics model, objective, terminal cost, constraints), and various considerations (uncertainty-aware MPC, problem classification, suboptimality, control theory). It also surveys numerical methods for solving MPC problems, distinguishing between sampling-based and derivative-based approaches.

2. **Comparative Analysis (Section V):** After laying the groundwork, the paper provides a dedicated comparison of MPC and RL, highlighting their practical and conceptual differences across several properties: state space representation, model requirements (smoothness, differentiability), handling of intrinsic stochasticity and model mismatch, stability guarantees, constraint handling, online/offline computation time, adaptability, generalization, and practical performance. This comparison serves to underscore their "orthogonal benefits" and the rationale for their combination.

3. **Classification Framework (Section VI):** This is the core methodological contribution. The authors propose a novel, systematic classification of combination approaches based on where MPC is integrated within the actor-critic framework of RL (Fig. 2). The three main categories are:
 - MPC as an expert actor.
 - MPC within the deployed policy (further refined into MDP aligned learning, closed-loop learning, and pre/postprocessing).
 - MPC as part of the critic.

4. **Detailed Literature Review (Sections VII-IX):** Each category of the proposed classification forms a dedicated section where relevant literature is thoroughly reviewed and presented in structured tables. These tables summarize the application domain, whether real-world experiments (RWE) were performed, the specific MPC and RL algorithms used, and other pertinent details (e.g., imitation learning vs. guided policy search, learning objectives, parameterization architectures, differentiation aspects).

5. **Architectures of Parameterized MPC (Section VI.A):** To support the classification, the paper introduces a conceptual taxonomy for how Neural Networks (NNs) or other function approximators can interact with an MPC optimization layer: Integrated, Hierarchical, Parallel, and Parameterized architectures. This allows for a finer-grained understanding of how parameters are learned and gradients are propagated.

6. **Theoretical Considerations (Section X):** The methodology extends to a theoretical analysis of the connection between MPC and RL, particularly focusing on the equivalence of optimality criteria for MDPs and MPC, and how MPC can approximate optimal value functions and policies. It discusses extensions and challenges in this theoretical alignment.

7. **Software and Implementation Aspects (Section XI):** Finally, the paper addresses practical implementation challenges, such as differentiating through optimization layers and computational efficiency, and points to existing and emerging software tools that facilitate MPC-RL integration.

The overall approach is comprehensive and hierarchical, moving from fundamental concepts to detailed classification, practical examples, theoretical underpinnings, and implementation considerations, all aimed at providing a structured and actionable overview of the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core contribution of this survey paper is its comprehensive synthesis and novel classification of the rapidly evolving field of MPC-RL combinations. The main findings and results can be summarized as follows:

1. **Comprehensive Comparison of MPC and RL:** The survey thoroughly details the complementary strengths and weaknesses of MPC and RL across practical properties (Section V, Table II). MPC excels in model reliance, constraint handling, and stability guarantees, while RL is strong in adaptability, generalization (within training distribution), and performance in complex, data-rich environments. This analysis forms the fundamental motivation for combination strategies.
2. **Novel Classification Framework:** The paper proposes a systematic, actor-critic-centric classification scheme for MPC-RL combinations (Section VI, Fig. 2):
 - **MPC as an Expert Actor (Section VII):** MPC can serve as a knowledgeable guide for RL agents.
 - **Imitation Learning (IL) from MPC (VII.A):** Trained NNs learn to mimic MPC behavior, aiming for faster inference times while retaining performance and safety characteristics.
 - **Guided Policy Search (GPS) using MPC (VII.B):** MPC trajectories are used to improve the exploration efficiency and quality during RL training, guiding the learned policy to better solutions.
 - **MPC within the Deployed Policy (Section VIII):** This is the most prevalent and intricate category, where MPC is an integral part of the online control policy.
 - **MDP Aligned Learning (VIII.A):** The MPC's internal model, terminal value function, or cost function parameters are learned to better approximate the true MDP structure. This maintains interpretability and potential guarantees.
 - **Closed-Loop Learning (VIII.B):** An MPC is used as a differentiable optimization layer within an RL actor, and its parameters are learned end-to-end to optimize closed-loop performance directly. This includes methods where gradients are backpropagated through the MPC solver or where the MPC is treated as part of the environment, with RL optimizing its parameters.
 - **MPC for Pre/Postprocessing (VIII.C):** MPC is employed in the deployed policy, *after* RL training, either as a reference generator (preprocessing) or, more commonly, as a safety filter (postprocessing) to ensure constraint satisfaction and robustness of an RL-trained action.
 - **MPC as a Critic (Section IX):** MPC's ability to compute value or action-value functions is utilized to provide training signals for an RL actor.
 - **MPC as an Expert Critic (IX.A):** A fixed, well-designed MPC provides Q-values to guide the learning of an RL policy, especially beneficial when explicit cost functions are complex.
 - **MPC as a Learnable Critic (IX.B):** The MPC parameters within the critic are learned to more accurately approximate the value function of the current policy.
 - **MPC as a Learnable Actor-Critic (IX.C):** MPC structures are used for both the actor (policy) and the critic (value function), enabling a harmonized learning process.

3. **Parameterized MPC Architectures (Section VI.A):** The paper identifies and defines four architectural archetypes for integrating NNs with MPC: Integrated (NN within optimization problem), Hierarchical (NN provides parameters to MPC), Parallel (NN and MPC operate concurrently), and Parameterized (constant learned parameters for MPC). This provides a valuable meta-framework for understanding the structural aspects of combinations.

4. **Theoretical Foundations (Section X):** The survey highlights significant theoretical advancements, particularly the fundamental equivalence result (Theorem 10.1,) demonstrating that appropriately modified Economic NMPC (ENMPC) can capture the optimal value functions and policies of discounted MDPs, even with model mismatches. This provides a strong theoretical justification for closed-loop optimal learning. Extensions to various MPC types (Real-Time Iteration, Mixed-Integer, Scenario-based) are also discussed.

5. **Practical Challenges and Software Solutions (Section XI):** Key implementation challenges are identified, including the need for differentiable optimization solvers, handling "solver states" in the MDP, computational overhead from repeated optimization, and hardware utilization (CPU vs. GPU). The survey points to emerging software tools like `l4casadi`, `l4acados`, and `leap-c` that aim to bridge the gap between machine learning frameworks and fast MPC solvers.

In essence, the paper delivers a structured, in-depth analysis of MPC-RL combinations, presenting a logical framework for understanding their various forms, the underlying theoretical principles, and the practical considerations for their deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey and classification paper carries significant implications for both the academic and industrial landscapes of control engineering and artificial intelligence. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Structuring a Rapidly Evolving Field:** The most immediate and profound impact is providing a much-needed **systematic framework and common language** for MPC-RL combinations. As an interdisciplinary field, it often suffers from fragmented research and inconsistent terminology. The proposed actor-critic-centric classification (MPC as expert actor, within deployed policy, or as critic) offers researchers a clear map, enabling better understanding, comparison, and categorization of diverse algorithms. This will streamline literature reviews, identify research gaps, and foster more coherent development.
2. **Driving Interdisciplinary Research:** By thoroughly explicating the fundamentals of both MPC and RL (Sections III and IV) and then systematically dissecting their points of intersection, the paper acts as a **crucial bridge between the control systems and machine learning communities**. It educates practitioners and researchers from one domain about the intricacies of the other, encouraging deeper collaboration and cross-pollination of ideas.
3. **Accelerating Algorithm Development:** The detailed discussion of various combination architectures (Integrated, Hierarchical, Parallel, Parameterized MPC) and learning paradigms (MDP-aligned vs. closed-loop optimal) will **guide the design of novel hybrid algorithms**. Researchers can more easily pinpoint which aspects of MPC to parameterize, which learning signals to use, and how to structure the overall control system for optimal performance, safety, and efficiency.
4. **Addressing Critical Real-World Challenges:** The motivation for combining MPC and RL directly addresses fundamental limitations of each field when applied to complex, safety-critical systems:
 - **Safety and Constraints:** MPC's inherent ability to handle constraints and offer theoretical guarantees can compensate for RL's historical struggles with safety, particularly through mechanisms like safety filters or robust MPC within the policy. This is paramount for applications like autonomous driving, robotics, and aerospace.
 - **Model Learning and Adaptability:** RL's data-driven model learning capabilities can overcome MPC's reliance on accurate, first-principles models, making control systems more robust to uncertainties and adaptable to changing environments.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Strategies like imitation learning from MPC (replacing slow optimization with fast NNs) or using MPC for warm-starting optimization can significantly improve the online computational efficiency of control.
5. **Highlighting Future Research Directions:** Beyond synthesizing existing work, the survey explicitly points to **open challenges and promising avenues for future research**. These include developing more efficient differentiable optimization layers, improving the handling of "solver states" in RL, better leveraging GPU capabilities for MPC, and exploring the shift from MDP-aligned learning to purely closed-loop optimal learning. The emphasis on the theoretical equivalence between MPC and MDP optimality criteria (Section X) also lays groundwork for principled design choices.
6. **Informing Software Tool Development:** By detailing implementation challenges and showcasing emerging software tools (`l4casadi`, `l4acados`, `leap-c`), the paper influences the **development of integrated platforms** that seamlessly combine machine learning frameworks with high-performance numerical optimization solvers. This is critical for translating theoretical advancements into practical, deployable systems.

In conclusion, this paper is not merely a summary; it's a **strategic guide** for a burgeoning field. By providing clarity, structure, theoretical grounding, and practical insights, it is poised to significantly accelerate research and development in MPC-RL synthesis, leading to more intelligent, robust, and safe autonomous systems across numerous domains. The authors' optimistic outlook on the "prosperous" future of this combination seems well-justified by the comprehensive analysis presented.
