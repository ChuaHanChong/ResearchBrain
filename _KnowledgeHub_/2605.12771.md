# Adaptive Smooth Tchebycheff Attention for Multi-Objective Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Indiana University–Bloomington researchers developed PASTA (Policy-optimization via Adaptive Smooth Tchebycheff Attention), an algorithm that dynamically adjusts the smoothness of Tchebycheff scalarization to robustly discover Pareto-optimal policies in multi-objective reinforcement learning, particularly in non-convex regions. The approach achieved a 45.5% Hypervolume improvement over baselines in a real-world robotic stealth visual search task.

## Problem

- Traditional Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) often uses linear scalarization, which fails to discover Pareto-optimal policies within non-convex regions of trade-off surfaces.
- Tchebycheff scalarization can theoretically find all Pareto-optimal solutions but introduces severe gradient instability in deep reinforcement learning due to its non-differentiable `max` operator.
- Existing Smooth Tchebycheff (STCH) methods rely on a fixed smoothness parameter (μ), which creates a dilemma between approximation accuracy (small μ leading to vanishing gradients) and gradient stability (large μ behaving like linear scalarization), struggling to adapt to non-stationary RL.

## Method

- **PASTA** integrates an Adaptive Smooth Tchebycheff (ASTCH) scalarization framework into a Proximal ==Policy Optimization== (PPO)-based deep ==reinforcement learning== algorithm.
- It dynamically adjusts the STCH smoothness parameter (μ) using a closed-loop controller that reacts to real-time inter-objective gradient interference, quantified by a conflict ratio (κ).
- The framework employs conflict-aware gradient projection (PCGrad) to mitigate destructive gradient interference and an attention-aligned branched critic architecture to maintain consistent objective prioritization.

## Results

- The PASTA algorithm achieved a **45.5%** Hypervolume improvement over the strongest baseline and a **100%** Win Rate in a real-world robotic stealth visual search task.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that conflict-aware gradient projection (PCGrad), the adaptive smoothness controller, and the attention-aligned branched critic architecture were critical for performance.
- PASTA consistently outperformed baselines in multi-objective MuJoCo environments and real-world quadrotor tasks, including achieving a **91.2%** task success rate in a quadrotor Frogger-like environment, and exhibited robustness to variations in its core hyperparameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adjusting the Smooth Tchebycheff (STCH) parameter (μ) based on real-time gradient conflict enables robust discovery of non-convex Pareto-optimal policies while maintaining optimization stability.
> - The conflict ratio derived from gradient projection (PCGrad) serves as an effective feedback signal for adaptively controlling the scalarization's smoothness.
> - Aligning the critic's optimization with the actor's scalarization, via attention-weighted loss in a branched critic, is crucial for consistent and effective multi-objective policy improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses challenges within Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL), particularly for robotic applications involving deep reinforcement learning (RL). Traditional RL often aggregates multiple performance metrics into a single scalar reward, which can obscure inherent trade-offs between conflicting objectives such as speed, energy efficiency, safety, or task completion. MORL extends the Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework to handle vector-valued rewards, with the goal of identifying Pareto-optimal policies—policies where no objective can be improved without degrading another.

A common approach in MORL is scalarization, which converts the multi-objective problem into a single-objective problem (SOP). Linear Scalarization (LS) is computationally efficient but is theoretically limited to finding solutions only on the convex hull of the Pareto front. This limitation is significant in robotics, where complex dynamics and sparse rewards frequently lead to non-convex trade-off surfaces, meaning LS may fail to discover optimal policies in concave regions.

To overcome the geometric limitations of LS, Tchebycheff (TCH) scalarization is employed in Multi-Objective Optimization (MOO). TCH minimizes the maximum weighted deviation from an ideal "utopia" point and is theoretically capable of recovering all Pareto-optimal solutions regardless of the front's convexity. However, TCH relies on a non-differentiable `max` operator, which introduces significant gradient instability and variance in deep RL settings that depend on backpropagation.

More recently, Smooth Tchebycheff (STCH) scalarization was introduced as a differentiable approximation of TCH, replacing the `max` operator with a Log-Sum-Exp function controlled by a smoothness parameter, μ. While STCH aims to provide smoother gradients, it presents a trade-off: as μ approaches zero, STCH approximates true Tchebycheff accurately but can lead to vanishing gradients and feature starvation for non-dominant objectives. Conversely, a large μ causes STCH to behave more like a linear scalarization, losing its ability to recover non-convex solutions. Furthermore, applying STCH to RL is challenging due to parameter sensitivity; a fixed μ struggles to adapt to the non-stationary nature of RL experience, where the optimization landscape continuously shifts.

This work addresses this tension by proposing an adaptive STCH framework. It aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical completeness of Tchebycheff scalarization and the practical stability requirements of deep RL, especially in non-convex multi-objective robotic control tasks. The research contributes to the field by offering a dynamic mechanism to modulate the scalarization's curvature, aiming to enable robust discovery of non-convex Pareto-optimal policies without manual tuning of the smoothness parameter.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a multi-objective reinforcement learning algorithm that can robustly discover Pareto-optimal policies in complex robotic domains, particularly when the Pareto front exhibits non-convex regions. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations of existing MORL approaches:

- **Inability of Linear Scalarization to handle Non-convexity:** Traditional linear scalarization methods are theoretically incapable of discovering solutions located within the concave regions of a non-convex Pareto front. Many real-world robotic tasks involve objectives with inherently non-linear and conflicting trade-offs, making it critical to access these non-convex optimal solutions. Failure to do so can result in suboptimal or unsafe robot behaviors.
- **Optimization Instability of Tchebycheff Scalarization in Deep RL:** While classical Tchebycheff scalarization can theoretically access all Pareto-optimal solutions, its reliance on a non-differentiable `max` operator leads to severe gradient variance and instability when used with deep reinforcement learning algorithms. This instability hinders effective policy optimization and can prevent convergence.
- **Parameter Sensitivity and Trade-off in Fixed Smooth Tchebycheff:** The Smooth Tchebycheff (STCH) method addresses the differentiability issue by approximating the `max` operator with a smooth function, controlled by a parameter μ. However, choosing a fixed μ involves a fundamental trade-off: a small μ provides high approximation accuracy (closer to true Tchebycheff) but risks vanishing gradients and feature starvation for non-dominant objectives. A large μ ensures smoother gradients but makes STCH behave more like linear scalarization, losing the ability to find non-convex solutions. Moreover, the optimal μ often changes throughout the non-stationary learning process in RL, making manual tuning difficult and suboptimal.

To address these motivations, the authors aim to:
- Develop a framework that dynamically modulates the smoothness parameter (μ) of STCH, adapting it in real-time to the evolving optimization landscape.
- Introduce a conflict-driven controller that regulates this smoothness based on observed inter-objective gradient interference, allowing the agent to anneal towards precise non-convex scalarization when objectives are aligned, and revert to smoother approximations when destructive gradient conflicts arise.
- Integrate this adaptive scalarization into a policy-gradient deep RL algorithm, ensuring consistency in objective prioritization for both policy and value function updates.
- Validate the proposed approach on challenging multi-objective robotic tasks that exhibit non-convex trade-offs, demonstrating its ability to robustly discover Pareto-optimal policies in regions inaccessible to linear baselines and unstable for static non-linear methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named PASTA (Policy-optimization via Adaptive Smooth Tchebycheff Attention), integrates an Adaptive Smooth Tchebycheff (ASTCH) scalarization framework into an on-policy policy-gradient method, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The core of PASTA lies in dynamically adjusting the smoothness parameter (μ) of the STCH function based on real-time gradient interference.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The paper first defines Multi-Objective Optimization Problems (MOPs), Pareto Optimality, and common solution methods like Linear Scalarization (LS) and Tchebycheff (TCH) scalarization, highlighting their respective advantages and limitations for gradient-based optimization. It then introduces Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) as Multi-Objective Markov Decision Processes (MOMDPs) and reviews policy optimization algorithms, including Policy Gradient (PG), Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which serves as the backbone for PASTA.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Smooth Tchebycheff (ASTCH) Framework

- **Smooth Tchebycheff Scalarization:** The paper adopts the maximization form of STCH (Eq. 10) to scalarize normalized returns (¯r) based on user-defined preference weights (w) and a dynamically set utopia point (z\*). The smoothness parameter μ dictates the curvature of the optimization landscape, with μ → 0 approaching hard Tchebycheff and μ → ∞ approaching linear scalarization.
- **Smooth Tchebycheff Attention:** The analytical derivative of the STCH scalarization (Eq. 11) naturally generates objective-specific attention weights, δ. These weights prioritize objectives with larger weighted deviations from the utopia point. To mitigate gradient starvation for non-limiting objectives, a Maintenance Rate ρ is introduced (Eq. 12), ensuring each objective receives at least ρ/m of the gradient signal, promoting feature retention and adaptability when trade-offs shift.
- **Attention-Aligned Branched Critic:** To ensure consistent objective prioritization, a Branched Critic architecture is employed. This architecture features a shared feature extractor followed by independent value heads for each objective. The critic's loss (Eq. 13) is weighted by the STCH attention coefficients (η), focusing learning capacity on objectives that most contribute to the scalarized advantage and are bottlenecks for policy improvement.
- **Vectorized Advantage:** Unlike standard approaches that scalarize returns early, PASTA maintains a vector of advantages for each objective, computed using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) and normalized per-objective (Eq. 14). This preserves information about gradient conflict, which is crucial for subsequent gradient projection.
- **Conflict-Aware Gradient Projection (PCGrad):** A modified variant of PCGrad is integrated to address destructive gradient interference. It operates on individual objective gradients (g_i) for the policy loss (L_CLIP). For conflicting gradient pairs (g_i^T g_j < 0), g_i is projected onto the normal plane of g_j (Eq. 15), ensuring all updates contribute positively to each objective without destructive interference. This process also yields a Conflict Ratio κ (Eq. 16), which quantifies the fraction of conflicting gradient pairs.

#### 3.3. Conflict-Driven Adaptive Smoothness Controller

The key innovation for dynamic adjustment of μ is a closed-loop controller that uses the Conflict Ratio κ_t as a real-time feedback signal.

- **Base Decay Schedule:** A linear annealing schedule (μ_base(t), Eq. 17) gradually reduces μ from an initial stable value to a precise terminal value over training steps. This provides a default pressure towards sharper Tchebycheff approximation.
- **Conflict-Driven Braking:** If the observed conflict ratio (κ_t) exceeds a predefined threshold (τ), a boost factor (β_boost, Eq. 18) is calculated, proportional to the excess conflict. This factor is used to interpolate the target smoothness (μ\*_t, Eq. 19) between the base schedule and a maximum stability value (μ_max), effectively "braking" the annealing and making the scalarization more linear-like to stabilize gradients.
- **Inertial Smoothing:** An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) (Eq. 20) is applied to the controller's output (μ\*_t) to prevent rapid oscillations in μ, maintaining stability.

The overall controller dynamics create an "elastic" optimization landscape: μ sharpens for precision, but momentarily relaxes to smooth gradients when conflicts emerge, allowing convergence to non-convex Pareto fronts (Algorithm 3 in Appendix D).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The PASTA algorithm was evaluated across a diverse set of continuous multi-objective environments, including a real-world robotic stealth visual search task, quadrotor navigation among dynamic obstacles, and high-dimensional locomotion tasks (MuJoCo benchmarks). The performance was assessed using metrics such as Hypervolume (HV), Target Dominance Rate (Win Rate), Objective Dominance Rate, and Dolan-Mor´e Performance Profiles (DMP) AUC. The same hyperparameters were used for PASTA across all experiments, suggesting its robustness.

#### 4.1. Evaluation in Stealth Visual Search (Real-World UGV)

The task required a ground robot to balance visual object detection, stealth (minimizing exposure), and exploration speed.
- **Superior Performance:** PASTA achieved a Hypervolume of 0.291 (45.5% improvement over the strongest baseline, STCH μ=10.0), a 100% Win Rate, and a 58.3% Objective Dominance Rate.
- **Baseline Behavior:** Fixed STCH methods demonstrated a transition from Tchebycheff-like behavior (low μ, similar to TCH, e.g., μ=0.01 with 0.125 HV) to linear-like behavior (high μ, similar to Linear Scalarization, e.g., μ=10.0 with 0.200 HV). Linear Scalarization achieved 0.199 HV. This empirically validated the theoretical spectrum of STCH.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation analyses confirmed the contribution of each PASTA component:
- **PCGrad:** Removing PCGrad significantly degraded most metrics (e.g., HV dropped from 0.291 to 0.187), confirming the importance of mitigating destructive gradient interference.
- **Controller:** The decay schedule was critical; removing it ("No Decay") caused the most performance degradation. While "No Conflict" (relying solely on decay) achieved comparable HV (0.295 vs. 0.291), the full PASTA maintained a higher Win Rate, indicating the conflict signal contributes to robustness.
- **Critic Architecture:** Branched critic architectures (separate value heads for each objective) significantly outperformed shared architectures (0.277 vs. 0.249 average HV). Furthermore, the attention-weighted loss for the branched critic (PASTA) improved HV by an additional 10.2% and boosted DMP AUC to 0.957, indicating that aligning critic optimization with actor scalarization is crucial.
- **Fixed Smoothness vs. Adaptive:** While a specific fixed μ (μ=5.0) could sometimes match PASTA's average performance, detailed results showed its success was inconsistent across different preference vectors, with poor performance in some cases. This highlighted PASTA's ability to automate the optimal smoothness search robustly.

#### 4.3. Sensitivity Analysis

PASTA exhibited robustness to variations in its core hyperparameters:
- **Conflict Ratio (κ):** Values just below the midpoint of the tested range were generally sufficient, with larger values being viable but suboptimal.
- **Moving Average Factor (λ):** A low value (λ = 0.05) for inertial smoothing was preferred, suggesting that a stable, slowly-updating memory of gradient conflict is more effective than highly reactive signals.
- **Maintenance Rate (ρ):** The controller was robust to a wide range of ρ values (0.15 to 0.8), but a minimum threshold was essential to prevent performance degradation, potentially due to vanishing gradients without it.
- **Utopia Point (z\*):** PASTA was robust to the exact selection of the utopia point; all tested z\* values outperformed baselines, and minor differences in performance were observed.

#### 4.4. Evaluation on MO-Gym (MuJoCo Environments) and Quadrotor Flights

PASTA consistently achieved better overall performance (higher Hypervolume) compared to baselines in multi-objective MuJoCo environments (HalfCheetah, Humanoid, Ant, Walker2D) and real-world quadrotor tasks (Frogger, Formation). In the quadrotor formation task, PASTA effectively prioritized higher-preference objectives while maintaining formation integrity, even if it meant slightly missing the goal compared to baselines that prioritized the goal bonus at the expense of formation. Real-world experiments with quadrotors demonstrated a 91.2% task success rate in a Frogger-like environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable contribution to multi-objective reinforcement learning by addressing a fundamental tension between optimization stability and the geometric precision required to recover non-convex Pareto-optimal solutions. The Adaptive Smooth Tchebycheff (ASTCH) framework, particularly its conflict-driven controller, offers a mechanism to dynamically adjust the scalarization's smoothness, thereby enabling more robust policy optimization in complex environments.

The development of PASTA demonstrates the potential for autonomous agents to navigate and resolve intricate, conflicting requirements in unstructured, real-world robotic scenarios. Its application and validation across diverse platforms—including a ground robot performing stealth visual search and quadrotors in dynamic obstacle avoidance and formation flight tasks—underscore its practical applicability. By automating the tuning of the smoothness parameter and integrating conflict-aware gradient projection, the method reduces the burden of manual hyperparameter tuning that often plagues MORL approaches, while aiming to prevent gradient starvation and catastrophic forgetting.

The ability to consistently discover solutions on non-convex Pareto fronts has significant implications for robotic systems that must balance disparate, often competing, objectives like efficiency, safety, and task completion. This could lead to more nuanced and adaptable robot behaviors in domains such as environmental monitoring, search-and-rescue, or complex logistical operations, where trade-offs cannot be adequately represented by linear scalarizations.

The identified limitations regarding the interdependence of conflict and maintenance rate hyperparameters suggest avenues for future research into more unified feedback mechanisms. Furthermore, extending this work to preference-conditioned policies, rather than preference-specific learning, could enhance its utility in robotic applications where mission priorities might change dynamically during operation. Overall, this research advances the state-of-the-art in MORL for robotics by providing a more stable, adaptive, and precise framework for multi-objective policy optimization.
