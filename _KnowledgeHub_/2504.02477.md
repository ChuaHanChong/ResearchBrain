# Multimodal Fusion and Vision-Language Models: A Survey for Robot Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and University of Chinese Academy of Sciences comprehensively reviews multimodal fusion and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robot vision. The work details architectural designs and applications across tasks like 3D object detection, navigation, SLAM, and manipulation, highlighting how integrated approaches enhance robotic perception and outlining future research directions.

## Problem

- Traditional robot vision systems, relying on unimodal sensory inputs like RGB cameras, face inherent limitations such as occlusions, varying lighting, and insufficient semantic information in complex real-world environments.
- Achieving robust perception, intelligent reasoning, and efficient decision-making for autonomous robots is challenging with single-modality data, leading to perceptual failures.
- Integrating diverse heterogeneous data types efficiently, ensuring real-time performance on resource-constrained robotic platforms, and adapting large Vision-Language Models to specific robotic tasks remain significant challenges.

## Method

- The paper conducts a systematic and task-oriented survey, categorizing multimodal fusion techniques into encoder-decoder frameworks, attention-based architectures, and ==Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)==.
- It meticulously analyzes the application of these fusion strategies and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) across critical robot vision tasks, including 3D object detection, embodied navigation, SLAM, and robot manipulation.
- The survey details the evolution of VLMs, covering pre-training strategies, cross-modal alignment, Transformer variants, and multimodal instruction following, alongside an in-depth analysis of relevant datasets and performance benchmarks.

## Results

- Multimodal fusion, especially LiDAR-Camera integration with advanced Transformer-based architectures (e.g., MV2DFusion), consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance in 3D object detection, improving accuracy and robustness.
- Vision-Language Models significantly improve embodied navigation through instruction following and dialogue-based paradigms, enhancing success rates and path efficiency by leveraging environmental understanding and structured memory.
- The integration of visual, linguistic, and tactile feedback in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) enables robust perception-to-action transitions and precise robot manipulation in complex scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal fusion and Vision-Language Models are indispensable for advancing robot vision, consistently outperforming unimodal systems by enhancing perceptual robustness, semantic expressiveness, and high-level reasoning capabilities.
> - Cross-modal alignment strategies are paramount for determining the performance ceiling of a robot's perception system, necessitating further research to bridge fundamental modal differences and semantic granularity mismatches.
> - Developing lightweight, multi-stage adaptation mechanisms for large-scale Vision-Language Models and task-oriented, online fusion strategies is crucial for practical, real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot vision is rapidly evolving, driven by the increasing demand for autonomous systems capable of operating robustly and intelligently in complex, unstructured real-world environments. Traditionally, robot vision has relied on unimodal sensory inputs, primarily RGB cameras. However, these unimodal approaches are inherently limited by challenges such as occlusions, varying lighting conditions, sparse textures, and insufficient semantic information.

This survey paper critically positions itself within the burgeoning intersection of multimodal fusion and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as a solution to these traditional limitations. Multimodal fusion, which integrates complementary information from various sensors (e.g., vision, language, depth, LiDAR, tactile data), has emerged as a crucial paradigm for enhancing a robot's perception, reasoning, and decision-making capabilities. The work acknowledges the foundational role of traditional deep learning-based multimodal fusion techniques.

Crucially, the paper highlights the *rapid rise* of VLMs, often powered by Large Language Models (LLMs), as a transformative force in multimodal fusion. VLMs, with their ability to align visual and linguistic information, offer unprecedented potential for zero-shot understanding, instruction following, and natural language interaction, shifting robot vision systems from passive perception to proactive intelligence. This integration is vital for developing embodied AI agents that can not only perceive but also semantically understand and interact with their environment using natural language.

Compared to existing surveys, the authors identify several gaps that their work addresses:
- **Broader Task Coverage:** Unlike many surveys that focus on specific traditional tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation, 3D detection), this paper extends its scope to critical robotic tasks such as Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), embodied navigation, and robot manipulation. This comprehensive coverage reflects the growing complexity of real-world robotic applications.
- **Deeper VLM Analysis:** While other works may explore VLM architectures, this survey delves into their practical deployment in robotic systems, including pre-training strategies, cross-modal alignment mechanisms, and architectural evolution.
- **Emerging Research Directions:** The survey highlights often-overlooked areas like cross-modal self-supervised learning and lightweight fusion architectures, which are crucial for real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.

By systematically integrating traditional multimodal fusion methods with emerging VLM paradigms, the paper serves as a timely and comprehensive reference. It not only reviews the state-of-the-art but also proactively charts future research directions, bridging the gap between theoretical advancements in multimodal AI and their practical deployment in advanced robotic systems. It aligns with the global trend towards more general-purpose, intelligent, and interactive embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is to address the inherent limitations of unimodal approaches in robot vision, which struggle to achieve robust perception and intelligent decision-making in complex, real-world environments. Factors such as occlusions, lighting variations, sparse textures, and insufficient semantic information often lead to perceptual failures when relying on a single sensor modality.

To overcome these challenges, the authors highlight multimodal fusion as a critical enabler for enhancing the perception, reasoning, and decision-making capabilities of robot vision systems. The integration of diverse sensory inputs—including vision, language, depth, LiDAR, and tactile data—allows robots to leverage complementary information, leading to more robust scene understanding and improved task generalization.

A significant motivation stems from the rapid advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models, particularly those based on large language models (LLMs), possess powerful cross-modal alignment and generalization abilities, opening new avenues for zero-shot understanding, natural language instruction following, and sophisticated visual question answering. This marks a pivotal shift towards more proactive, semantically intelligent robotic systems capable of natural human-robot interaction.

However, despite these promising prospects, several practical challenges persist, which further motivate this comprehensive review:
1. **Efficient Heterogeneous Data Integration:** A core issue remains in efficiently integrating diverse data types from different modalities, encompassing modality alignment, unified feature representation, and spatiotemporal synchronization.
2. **Real-time Performance and Resource Efficiency:** Robotic systems demand stringent real-time performance and resource efficiency, necessitating a delicate balance between computational cost and model accuracy in fusion architecture design.
3. **VLM Adaptability to Specific Robotic Tasks:** While pretrained VLMs exhibit strong general capabilities, their adaptability to unique robotic tasks, especially in scenarios with limited annotated data or dynamic environments, remains a significant hurdle.

Based on these motivations and challenges, the key objectives of this survey paper are clearly defined:

- **Systematic Integration and Comparative Analysis:** To systematically review and integrate traditional multimodal fusion strategies with emerging vision-language models, conducting a comparative analysis of their architectural designs, functional characteristics, and applicable tasks to reveal their connections, complementary strengths, and integration potential.
- **Extended Task Scope:** To extend the analysis beyond basic semantic segmentation and object detection to cover emerging and critical robotic application scenarios such as multimodal SLAM, robot manipulation, and embodied navigation, demonstrating the potential of multimodal fusion and VLMs for complex reasoning and long-duration task decision-making.
- **Highlighting Multimodal Advantages:** To summarize the key advantages of multimodal systems over unimodal approaches, including enhanced perceptual robustness, semantic expressiveness, cross-modal alignment, and high-level reasoning, thereby emphasizing their practical value in dynamic, ambiguous, or partially observable environments.
- **In-depth Dataset Analysis:** To provide an in-depth analysis of mainstream multimodal datasets used for robotics tasks, detailing their modal combinations, covered tasks, applicable scenarios, and limitations, offering a reference for future benchmarking and model evaluation.
- **Identifying Challenges and Future Directions:** To identify the key challenges in multimodal fusion, such as cross-modal alignment techniques, efficient training strategies, and real-time performance optimization, and based on these, propose forward-looking research directions to advance the field towards more intelligent, generalizable, and deployable robotic vision systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This survey adopts a **task-oriented perspective** to systematically review the applications and advancements of multimodal fusion methods and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robot vision. The methodology involves a structured analysis across various dimensions:

1. **Categorization of Multimodal Fusion Techniques:** For semantic scene understanding tasks, the paper categorizes fusion approaches into three main architectural paradigms:
 - **Encoder-Decoder Frameworks:** Analyzes classic deep learning architectures that extract features from different modalities through encoders and fuse them in a decoder to produce final outputs. The evolution from basic structures to those incorporating attention mechanisms is discussed.
 - **Attention-based Architectures:** Explores the role of attention mechanisms (self-attention, cross-modal attention) in adaptively weighting and capturing long-range dependencies between cross-modal features, enhancing information association and generalization. The computational complexity of standard attention is also highlighted as a challenge.
 - **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs):** Examines how GNNs model multimodal data by constructing graph structures, enabling effective extraction and fusion of high-level semantic representations through message passing and feature aggregation.

2. **Application-Specific Analysis:** The survey meticulously analyzes the architectural characteristics and practical implementations of these fusion strategies across key robot vision tasks:
 - **3D Object Detection:** Discusses sensor fusion (LiDAR-Camera, Radar-Camera, LiDAR-Radar), distinguishing fusion granularity (ROI-wise, voxel-wise, point-wise) and methods (attention-agnostic vs. attention-based). It traces the evolution from early concatenation methods to modern Transformer-based approaches.
 - **Navigation and Localization:** Covers embodied navigation (goal-oriented, instruction-following, dialogue-based) and visual localization, emphasizing the role of multimodal inputs, topological maps, and long-horizon planning.
 - **Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM):** Reviews LiDAR-based, vision-based, and multimodal fusion SLAM systems, highlighting the integration of cameras, LiDAR, IMU, and GPS, and the evolution towards deeply coupled, semantic SLAM frameworks.
 - **Robot Manipulation:** Examines Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and tactile-based manipulation, demonstrating how multimodal fusion enables precise action planning and execution through integration of visual, linguistic, and tactile feedback.

3. **Evolution of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** A significant part of the methodology is dedicated to charting the technological evolution of VLMs. This includes:
 - **Pre-training Across Modalities:** Discussing the importance of large-scale multimodal pre-training for learning deep correlations between vision and language.
 - **Cross-Modal Alignment and Representation Learning:** Analyzing techniques like contrastive learning, self-supervised learning (e.g., CLIP, DINO), and cross-modal generative models for mapping different modalities into a shared embedding space.
 - **Transformer Variants and Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs):** Tracing the impact of Transformer architecture from NLP (BERT, GPT) to CV (ViT, Swin Transformer) and its scaling into LVLMs (GPT-4, Gemini), including efficient variants and Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures.
 - **Multimodal Instruction Following and Learning:** Highlighting the role of instruction tuning (SFT, RLHF, multimodal adaptation) in enhancing VLM generalization and adaptability to diverse robotic tasks.
 - **Context-Aware Multimodal Learning:** Examining approaches that integrate contextual information to improve representation power and prediction accuracy.
 - **Lightweight and Real-Time Models:** Discussing strategies for designing efficient models for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.

4. **Dataset and Benchmark Analysis:** The authors conduct an in-depth analysis of commonly used multimodal datasets for robot vision (e.g., nuScenes, SemanticKITTI, Matterport3D, ScanQA, R2R, DROID, RT-1). This includes evaluating their applicability, modal combinations, covered tasks, and inherent challenges in real-world robotic scenarios.

5. **Performance Evaluation:** A comparative summary of state-of-the-art multimodal methods is presented for 3D object detection, embodied navigation, and 3D visual question answering, utilizing standard evaluation metrics (mAP, NDS, SR, SPL, CIDEr, BLEU-4, etc.).

6. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** Based on the comprehensive review and comparative analysis, the paper identifies key research challenges (low-quality data, heterogeneity, efficient training/inference, dataset scarcity) and proposes forward-looking research directions (spatial intelligence, ethical deployment, embodied cognition, and brain-inspired design).

Overall, the methodology is comprehensive and systematic, blending a task-oriented review of traditional fusion techniques with a detailed exploration of the rapidly advancing field of VLMs, underpinned by a critical analysis of datasets and performance benchmarks. This approach provides a holistic understanding of the current state and future trajectory of multimodal perception and interaction in robot vision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey provides a comprehensive overview and analysis, yielding several key findings across multimodal fusion techniques, Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and their applications in robot vision:

#### 4.1. Advancements in Multimodal Fusion for Robot Vision Tasks

- **Semantic Scene Understanding:** Traditional methods leverage encoder-decoder frameworks, attention mechanisms, and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to integrate diverse modalities (RGB, depth, LiDAR, audio). Attention mechanisms, in particular, prove effective in capturing long-range dependencies and dynamic weighting, though they face computational complexity challenges. GNNs excel at modeling relational structures within scenes.
- **3D Object Detection:** LiDAR-Camera fusion is a predominant approach, combining LiDAR's precise geometry with camera's rich semantics. The evolution has moved from early non-attention-based fusion (e.g., MV3D, AVOD) to advanced Transformer-based architectures (e.g., TransFusion, BEVFusion, MV2DFusion) that enable more adaptive and context-aware feature integration. Radar-Camera fusion offers robustness in adverse weather and motion detection, complementing LiDAR-Camera systems.
- **Navigation and Localization:** Multimodal inputs are crucial for embodied navigation, which has progressed from goal-oriented to instruction-following and dialogue-based paradigms. Topological maps and long-horizon planning are increasingly vital for generalization in complex environments. Visual localization benefits from deep learning-based methods integrating RGB, depth, and IMU data, with hybrid frameworks combining classical geometry and neural networks showing promise.
- **Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM):** Multimodal SLAM systems have evolved from loosely coupled to deeply integrated frameworks (e.g., LVI-SAM, LIC-Fusion), combining LiDAR, vision, and IMU data to enhance accuracy and robustness, especially in challenging environments. Semantic SLAM, which leverages higher-level understanding, is an emerging direction.
- **Robot Manipulation:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) demonstrate robust perception-to-action transitions by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and action planning, enabling precise task execution. Tactile-based manipulation complements vision by providing local feedback for grasp generation, planning, stability assessment, and dynamic adjustment (e.g., FusionNet-A, Sparsh).

#### 4.2. Evolution and Capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs)

- **Pre-training and Alignment:** Large-scale cross-modal pre-training (e.g., CLIP, ALIGN) is fundamental for VLMs to learn deep correlations between vision and language in a shared embedding space, reducing the reliance on extensive labeled data.
- **Architectural Advancements:** The Transformer architecture has become central, first in NLP (BERT, GPT), then adapted for vision (ViT, Swin Transformer), and finally for multimodal tasks through VLMs. Techniques like Mixture of Experts (MoE) enhance scalability and computational efficiency for ultra-large models (e.g., GPT-4, DeepSeek-MoE).
- **Instruction Following and Reasoning:** Instruction tuning (Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback) significantly boosts the generalization and adaptability of VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, PaLM-E) across various tasks, enabling flexible responses to natural language instructions and complex reasoning.
- **Context-Aware Learning:** Emerging approaches focus on incorporating contextual information to improve representational power and predictive accuracy, enhancing adaptability to diverse data distributions.
- **Lightweight and Real-time Models:** The necessity for deployment on edge devices drives research into efficient architectures (e.g., MobileViT, TinyBERT) and dynamic fusion strategies.

#### 4.3. Dataset Landscape and Performance Benchmarks

- **Diverse Datasets:** A wide array of multimodal datasets exists, covering indoor (Matterport3D, ScanNet) and outdoor (nuScenes, Waymo) environments, and tasks ranging from scene understanding to manipulation.
- **Performance Trends:** Multimodal fusion consistently outperforms unimodal approaches in tasks like 3D object detection (e.g., MV2DFusion achieving state-of-the-art NDS on nuScenes). In embodied navigation, structured memory, environmental adaptation, and VLM pre-training significantly improve success rates and path efficiency (e.g., BEVBert, SUSA). For 3D visual question answering, VLMs demonstrate strong spatial reasoning and language generation capabilities (e.g., BridgeQA leading on ScanQA).
- **Dataset Limitations:** Despite diversity, existing datasets often suffer from limitations in scale, high annotation noise, insufficient semantic diversity, cultural/environmental biases, and lack of real-world reproducibility, hindering true generalization for robotic systems.

#### 4.4. Identified Key Research Findings (from the Conclusion)

1. **Cross-modal alignment strategies** are paramount, directly influencing the performance ceiling of a robot's perception system. Current limitations stem from fundamental modal differences and mismatches in semantic granularity.
2. **Lightweight, multi-stage adaptation mechanisms** are essential for the practical deployment of large-scale vision-language models on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
3. **Task-oriented and online adaptation fusion strategies** are becoming a crucial focus, enabling VLMs to dynamically adjust perception and decision-making pathways in response to high-frequency, highly variable target conditions.

In essence, the survey confirms that multimodal fusion and VLMs are indispensable for advancing robot vision, enabling capabilities that far exceed unimodal systems. However, it also highlights the critical need to address challenges related to data quality, model efficiency, and generalization to unlock their full potential in real-world robotic deployments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant implications for the advancement of robot vision and the broader field of artificial intelligence, offering both theoretical insights and practical guidance.

#### 5.1. Comprehensive Reference and Knowledge Consolidation

The most immediate significance is its role as a detailed, systematic, and up-to-date review of a rapidly evolving domain. By synthesizing a vast body of literature on multimodal fusion and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robot vision, it provides an invaluable resource for researchers, practitioners, and students. It consolidates scattered knowledge, categorizes diverse methodologies, and highlights key advancements, saving considerable effort for those entering or operating within the field. Its comparative analysis of traditional fusion methods with emerging VLM paradigms offers a unique perspective on their complementary strengths and integration potential.

#### 5.2. Driving Innovation in Robot Vision and Embodied AI

The work underscores the critical importance of multimodal perception for developing robust, intelligent, and generalizable robotic systems. By demonstrating how the fusion of vision, language, depth, LiDAR, and tactile data overcomes the inherent limitations of unimodal approaches, the survey advocates for designing more capable robots. The deep dive into tasks like semantic scene understanding, 3D object detection, embodied navigation, SLAM, and robot manipulation provides concrete examples of how multimodal fusion and VLMs can enhance capabilities for tasks ranging from precise grasping to complex human-robot interaction in unknown environments. This directly contributes to the realization of more autonomous and adaptive embodied AI.

#### 5.3. Guiding Future Research Directions

A major impact of this survey lies in its forward-looking discussion, which identifies key challenges and proposes concrete future research directions. These include:
- **Spatial Intelligence:** Emphasizing the need for models to develop robust 3D geometric perception, memory, reasoning, and prediction, including structured spatial memory and topological map embeddings. This pushes beyond simple recognition to higher-level cognitive abilities for robots.
- **Ethical Deployment:** Addressing crucial concerns related to model safety, robustness, interpretability, and human oversight. The proposals for adversarial evaluation benchmarks, causal reasoning, human-in-the-loop trust modeling, and ethical instruction tuning are vital for ensuring responsible AI development and deployment in safety-critical applications.
- **Embodied Cognition and Brain-Inspired Design:** Advocating for interdisciplinary collaboration with cognitive science and neuroscience to build more generalizable and adaptive robotic systems, drawing inspiration from biological learning mechanisms like memory consolidation and value-driven decision-making. This suggests a paradigm shift towards more intrinsically intelligent and plausible AI architectures.

By pinpointing these challenges and opportunities, the survey effectively sets a research agenda for the coming years, directing efforts towards fundamental breakthroughs in areas like cross-modal alignment, efficient real-time deployment, and robust domain adaptation.

#### 5.4. Practical Applications and Economic Value

The advancements highlighted in the survey have immense practical implications across various industries:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Enhanced 3D object detection and SLAM capabilities lead to safer and more reliable self-driving cars, even in adverse weather or complex urban environments.
- **Service Robotics:** Improved embodied navigation and manipulation enable robots to perform more complex tasks in homes, hospitals, and public spaces, fostering more natural human-robot interaction.
- **Industrial Automation:** Precise robot manipulation and scene understanding can optimize manufacturing processes and logistical operations.
- **Exploration and Surveillance:** Robots capable of robust localization and semantic understanding can be deployed in hazardous or inaccessible environments for mapping, inspection, and monitoring.

Ultimately, by contributing to the development of more intelligent, generalizable, and deployable robotic vision systems, this research directly contributes to significant economic value and societal benefit, fostering innovation and addressing complex real-world problems. The emphasis on ethical deployment also ensures that these technological advancements are developed and integrated responsibly.
