# MuGen: Multi-Skill Generative Locomotion Controller for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> MuGen is a generative locomotion controller for humanoid robots that learns diverse, physics-consistent skills directly from human motion data using a VQ-VAE and teacher-student learning. This framework enables robust tracking of complex movements and autonomous generation of novel behaviors, demonstrating improved performance over continuous latent space methods and successful real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing deep reinforcement learning methods for humanoid locomotion require extensive task-specific reward shaping, limiting controller generalizability across diverse tasks.
- Current imitation learning often uses decoupled motion generation and tracking, failing to capture the underlying structure of the robot's action space or ensuring dynamic reliability.
- Dynamically unreliable trajectories can result from separate motion generation and robot control, leading to untrackable kinematic motions and suboptimal robot performance.

## Method

- A data-driven framework uses a Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) to learn discrete, dynamics-feasible skill embeddings from human motion data via a differentiable ==world model==.
- A teacher policy, trained with a model-based reinforcement learning approach, encodes motions into a shared VQ codebook, and a decoder generates actions consistent with physical constraints.
- A student policy is distilled from the teacher using a DAgger-style strategy with a shared VQ codebook, ensuring robust real-time control and sim-to-real transfer with limited observations.

## Results

- The system robustly tracked diverse human locomotion styles (e.g., walking, dancing) in simulation and demonstrated accurate tracking of short segments in real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 robot.
- MuGen successfully generated novel, coherent behaviors like transitions between walking, running, and turning autonomously by sampling from its learned skill latent space.
- Ablation studies showed the VQ-based approach achieved superior generalization (Survival Rate of **0.9535** for unseen motions) and robustness compared to continuous latent space (VAE) and non-generative (MLP) baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a discrete, vector-quantized latent space for motion skills enables effective capture and reuse of motion primitives, enhancing policy robustness and exploration stability.
> - Integrating motion generation and control within a unified, physics-aware framework ensures dynamic reliability of learned and generated motions, facilitating robust tracking of unseen movements.
> - Policy distillation using a shared codebook and progressive scheduling allows for efficient transfer of complex skills from a privileged teacher to a deployable student policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robot locomotion has witnessed advancements through deep reinforcement learning (RL), enabling complex maneuvers such as running, jumping, and acrobatics. However, a persistent challenge in these RL-based approaches is the requirement for meticulous, task-specific reward shaping and extensive tuning, which often results in controllers that lack generalizability across diverse tasks.

Imitation learning (IL) has emerged as a promising alternative, leveraging the morphological similarities between humanoids and humans, alongside the growing availability of human motion data. Existing IL methods commonly involve training policies to track reference kinematic trajectories. A separate motion generation module might create these trajectories based on task goals, which are then fed to a tracking policy. This paradigm, however, presents two primary limitations:
1. **Ineffective Action Space Structure Capture:** Naive tracking policies often do not effectively capture the structure of the action space under the robot's kinematic and dynamic constraints. They may struggle to extract distinctive motion patterns from large datasets or reuse them for different tasks.
2. **Dynamic Reliability Discrepancy:** The decoupled nature of motion generation and robot control can lead to dynamically unreliable generated trajectories. Even kinematically plausible motions might be untrackable by the robot, resulting in suboptimal performance or failures.

"MuGen" addresses these limitations by proposing a generative control system learned directly from a large dataset of diverse human locomotion performances. This approach aims to bridge the gap between kinematic references and robot actions by learning a structured latent space that is consistent with the robot's physical constraints. By integrating motion generation and control within a unified framework, MuGen seeks to achieve robust tracking of unseen motions and the reliable generation of novel movements, thereby reducing the reliance on intricate reward engineering and facilitating direct sim-to-real transfer. The work aligns with recent trends in physics-based motion control, which employ generative models and skill embeddings to condense large motion datasets into reusable skill sets. It specifically builds upon VAE-based approaches by introducing a vector-quantized variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE) for learning a discrete, dynamics-feasible motion latent space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a data-driven framework, named MuGen, capable of learning and deploying multi-skill locomotion for humanoid robots. This framework aims to enable robots to perform expressive, human-like motions guided by example motion sequences, addressing the inherent limitations of conventional RL and imitation learning methodologies.

The motivations for developing MuGen are multi-faceted:

- **Overcoming Reward Shaping Challenges:** Traditional deep RL for humanoid locomotion frequently necessitates extensive and precise reward function design and algorithm tuning. This process is labor-intensive, often task-specific, and hinders the generalizability of learned policies. MuGen seeks to alleviate this burden by learning control strategies directly from human motion data, implicitly embedding desired behaviors.
- **Capturing and Reusing Motion Patterns:** Existing motion tracking policies often treat reference trajectories as raw inputs without effectively extracting the underlying structure of the robot's action space or the distinctive patterns within large motion datasets. MuGen aims to learn a structured latent space for motion skills and actions that inherently captures these patterns, allowing for their reuse in various downstream tasks and enhancing the robot's expressive capabilities.
- **Ensuring Dynamic Reliability:** A significant limitation of separating motion generation from robot control is the potential for generating kinematically plausible but dynamically unexecutable trajectories. This can lead to instability or failure in robot execution. MuGen is motivated to integrate the learning of skill embeddings and actions directly within a physical simulation environment. This integration ensures that the learned skill representations are consistent with the robot’s kinematic and dynamic constraints, thereby guaranteeing the dynamic reliability of both tracked and generated motions.
- **Robust Tracking and Novel Motion Generation:** The goal is to develop a controller that can not only robustly track previously unseen human motions but also generate new, coherent motions autonomously by leveraging its learned latent skill space. This requires a system that can generalize beyond its training data and synthesize diverse behaviors within the robot's capabilities.
- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Given the difficulties associated with transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world robots (the sim-to-real gap), MuGen aims to develop a deployable student policy that is robust to real-world conditions. This includes using strategies like policy distillation and domain randomization during training to ensure practical applicability.

In essence, MuGen is motivated by the need for a more efficient, robust, and versatile framework for humanoid locomotion control that leverages the richness of human motion data to produce physically consistent, expressive, and generalizable robot behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MuGen employs a data-driven framework built upon a teacher-student learning architecture, augmented by a model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) approach and Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAEs).

**A. Problem Formulation**
The task is framed as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), where the objective is to find a policy $\pi$ that maximizes the expected discounted cumulative reward. Unlike model-free RL, MuGen uses a learned, differentiable world model $W(s' | s, a)$ to approximate the true environment dynamics. This allows for efficient gradient computation via backpropagation through predicted state trajectories, enabling stable, end-to-end gradient-based optimization of the control policy.

**B. Dynamics Model**
A differentiable world model serves as a state predictor, taking the current robot state $s_t$ and the teacher policy's action $a_t$ to predict the next state $s_{t+1}$. The world model is optimized by minimizing a reconstruction loss, $L_W$, which penalizes deviations between simulated next states $s^{sim}_{t+1}$ and the world model's predictions $s^W_{t+1}$. The world model has access to privileged information to enhance prediction accuracy and is jointly optimized with the teacher policy through alternating updates. During distillation, the world model is frozen.

**C. Skill Embeddings**
The teacher policy comprises an encoder $E$ and a decoder $D$. The encoder maps states $s$ and reference motion $m$ to continuous embeddings $z = E(s, m)$. These embeddings are then quantized via a VQ bottleneck to the nearest entry in a trainable codebook, resulting in $\hat{z} = B(z)$. The decoder then outputs actions $a \sim D(\cdot | \hat{z}, s)$, conditioned on the quantized code and current state. This discrete latent space promotes the reuse of motion primitives and stabilizes exploration. Since the simulation environment is not fully differentiable, the world model $W$ is used to predict the resulting next state $s^W_{t+1}$ for gradient backpropagation.

The teacher policy and codebook are jointly optimized using an objective function $L_T$. This loss includes:
- $L_{rec}$: A reconstruction loss enforcing consistency between predicted next states from the world model and reference motion states.
- $L_{VQ}$: A regularization term for the latent space, using stop-gradient operators to ensure that the embeddings are committed to the codebook entries.
- $L_{reg}$: Two regularization terms applied to the decoded action to encourage smoothness and prevent extreme actions.
The codebook update employs an exponential moving average (EMA) strategy for dictionary item updates and is only updated during the teacher training phase.

**D. Policy Distillation**
A teacher-student framework is employed to distill the learned skills into a deployable student policy. The student policy learns from the teacher without privileged information, operating in a distinct, limited observation space. A key aspect is the shared VQ codebook $B$ between teacher and student, which facilitates the transfer of high-quality dynamics and motion skills. The student policy's objective function $L_S$ combines:
- $L_{BC}$: A standard behavior cloning loss that minimizes the discrepancy between student and teacher actions.
- $L_{align}$: A loss that encourages the student encoder $E_S$ to produce embeddings compatible with the teacher’s frozen codebook, enabling feature-level knowledge transfer.

**E. Training Schedule**
The training process is structured into three stages:
1. **Pretraining:** The teacher policy and world model are jointly trained through alternating updates. The teacher explores the motion space and builds the VQ codebook. The student policy is not trained during this unstable phase.
2. **Warming Up:** All modules (world model, teacher, student) are optimized, but only the teacher policy is used for rolling out trajectories. This allows the student to warm up with a stable imitation target.
3. **Distillation:** The teacher policy and codebook are frozen. The student policy gradually takes over robot control. A novel DAgger-style distillation strategy is used, where in each rollout trajectory, the control policy at each step is randomly chosen between the teacher and student. The probability of selecting the teacher policy linearly anneals to zero. This progressive scheduling mitigates abrupt distribution shifts and maintains teacher oversight during early student training.

The framework is implemented using the NVIDIA IsaacGym simulator, incorporating domain randomization (varying simulation parameters, adding observation noise, random weight loads) to improve sim-to-real transfer robustness. The LaFAN1 motion dataset, retargeted for the Unitree G1 robot, is used for training and testing. Observation spaces are distinguished into global (privileged information for teacher/world model) and local (proprioception for student).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding MuGen's performance in simulation and real-world deployment, as well as insights from ablation studies.

**A. Simulation Results**
MuGen's student policy demonstrated proficiency in tracking various motions using only deployable observations.
- **Diverse Motion Tracking:** The system successfully tracked different locomotion styles, including manually crafted long walking motions and complex motions like dancing, running, and crouching walks from the LaFAN1 dataset. Even when confronted with motions significantly different from the training data, such as a dance, the policy maintained balance. Targeted training on a specific dance clip allowed the robot to perform the dance accurately. These results suggest MuGen's ability to generalize to diverse locomotion tasks and achieve precise tracking.
- **Generative Ability:** A post-training experiment evaluated MuGen's capacity for autonomous motion generation. A "prior encoder" ($\hat{E}$) was trained to predict discrete codebook indices solely from proprioceptive input (state). This encoder, combined with the frozen codebook and student decoder, allowed the robot to generate smooth and varied behaviors, including transitions between walking, running, crouching, and turning, without any reference motion. This outcome indicates the expressiveness and robustness of the learned skill space for real-time generative control.

**B. Ablation Study**
Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of key architectural components. Models were trained on a small subset of the LaFAN1 dataset (200 seconds of walking motions) and tested on both seen and unseen walking motions. Performance was evaluated using Survival Rate (SR), Mean Joint Rotation Error (MJRE), and Mean linear Velocity Error (MVE) across 1,024 simulation environments with varying domain randomization.

- **Discrete Skill Embeddings vs. Baselines:**
 - MuGen (VQ-based with history) achieved superior performance with higher SR (0.8820 seen, 0.9535 unseen) and lower MJRE/MVE on both seen and unseen motions, demonstrating better generalization and resilience to disturbances.
 - A vanilla MLP policy performed well on training data but showed a significant performance drop on unseen data, indicating overfitting.
 - A VAE-based policy (continuous latent space) showed lower SR and higher errors compared to the VQ-based approach, suggesting that discrete skill embeddings enhance policy robustness.
- **Temporal Context (History Observation):** Incorporating a moderate number of historical frames (e.g., #HS=5) for the student policy significantly improved tracking accuracy and survival rate. However, using excessive history frames (#HT/#HS = 5/5 or 2/10) led to performance degradation, potentially due to noise or exceeding the network's representational capacity.
- **Smooth Transition Strategy:** Disabling the smooth transition mechanism (abrupt switch from teacher to student) resulted in an unstable training process and a substantial drop in final policy performance, underscoring the importance of the progressive scheduling strategy in policy distillation.

**C. Real-World Deployment**
MuGen was deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot (35 kg, 1.32 m height, 23 actuated DOFs).
- The policy executed at 30 Hz, providing target joint positions to a low-level PD controller running at 500 Hz.
- Specific motions were selected for safety, and linear interpolation was applied at the beginning of motion sequences to avoid destabilizing impulsive forces due to differences between the robot's default pose and the reference motion's start.
- Qualitative results (Figure 1 e-f) demonstrated the robot's ability to track short segments of straight and crouching walks from the motion dataset in the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MuGen represents a contribution to the field of humanoid locomotion control by introducing a generative framework that leverages data-driven learning. Its primary significance lies in the development of a VQ-VAE-based skill embedding architecture capable of distilling diverse locomotion skills from unstructured human motion data. This approach generates a deployable student policy that can execute robust real-time motion control.

The potential impact of MuGen is observed in several areas:

- **Reduced Engineering Effort:** By learning from human motion data and employing a model-based reinforcement learning approach, MuGen mitigates the need for labor-intensive, handcrafted reward functions. This can accelerate the development of complex robotic behaviors and make policy design more accessible.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The use of discrete skill embeddings, as evidenced by the ablation studies, contributes to policies that exhibit improved generalization to unseen motions and increased robustness against external perturbations and domain randomization. This is critical for deploying robots in varied and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Autonomous Motion Generation:** The framework's ability to generate novel, human-like behaviors without explicit reference trajectories, through sampling from a learned latent skill space, opens avenues for autonomous and adaptive humanoid robot control. This could enable robots to respond creatively to new situations or interact more naturally with humans.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** While limitations remain, the methodological considerations, such as domain randomization and a structured teacher-student distillation process, contribute to developing policies that are more amenable to real-world deployment on physical humanoid robots.

However, the paper also identifies several limitations that delineate areas for future work:

- **Difficulty with Static Poses:** The current generative policy struggles to maintain stable standing poses, often exhibiting oscillatory motions. This is attributed to the VQ codebook being predominantly trained on motion-rich sequences, leading to underrepresentation or ambiguity for static poses. Addressing this could involve enriching the training data with static poses or incorporating specific regularization for stillness.
- **World Model Accuracy:** The reliance on a simple MLP for the differentiable world model, which does not explicitly account for environmental interactions, caps the performance of the teacher policy. Improvements in world model fidelity through structured modeling or uncertainty estimation could lead to more precise and robust control.
- **Sim-to-Real Adaptation:** The current deployment strategy relies primarily on domain randomization. A more robust and scalable approach would involve fine-tuning the world model using real-world robot rollouts and subsequently re-distilling the student policy, aligning the simulated and real dynamics more closely.

Future directions include replacing MoCap with vision-based or onboard pose estimation, enhancing the world model, scaling the skill codebook for greater behavioral diversity, and integrating natural language interfaces for prompt-driven humanoid motion generation. These advancements could further enhance the versatility and autonomy of humanoid robots in dynamic environments.
