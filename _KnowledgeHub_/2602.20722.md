# Buffer Matters: Unleashing the Power of Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning in Large Language Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Batch Adaptation Policy Optimization (BAPO) introduces an off-policy reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) framework that significantly improves data efficiency and stability in large language model post-training for reasoning tasks. The framework demonstrated an average 12.5% accuracy improvement over baselines and successfully resolved 31% of problems that base models initially failed, utilizing a dynamic batch construction mechanism.

## Problem

- Existing on-policy RLVR methods for LLM post-training suffer from "experience waste," discarding valuable data after a single use.
- Reward homogeneity in on-policy batches leads to advantage estimation collapse and gradient vanishing, especially for very easy or difficult samples, hindering learning on challenging problems.
- Naive application of off-policy RL to LLM fine-tuning can introduce instability, entropy collapse, and performance degradation due to policy distribution discrepancies.

## Method

- **BAPO** introduces an adaptive training ==batch construction== mechanism that combines filtered online samples, re-evaluated historically difficult samples, and reused high-quality historical samples from replay buffers.
- It uses a delayed rollout policy for generating online samples, which are then filtered to exclude those with zero reward variance, preventing gradient vanishing and ensuring training stability.
- A difficulty-aware experience replay mechanism periodically re-evaluates difficult prompts with the current policy and dynamically adjusts quality thresholds for reusing high-quality trajectories.

## Results

- BAPO achieved an average of **12.5%** accuracy improvement over baselines across mathematics, planning, and visual reasoning tasks.
- The framework successfully resolved **31%** of problems that base models initially failed (0/8 accuracy) after 3 epochs, compared to **19%** for GRPO.
- BAPO demonstrated comparable or superior performance with approximately 2.5 times fewer rollouts than advanced on-policy methods like DAPO, indicating improved computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly addressing reward homogeneity in sample selection, through filtering and re-evaluation of difficult samples, is crucial for effective learning and stable advantage estimation in LLM reasoning tasks.
> - Systematically integrating stale off-policy experience while maintaining policy consistency through mechanisms like delayed rollouts and FIFO buffers can yield substantial data efficiency gains without sacrificing stability.
> - The core principles of dynamic batch adaptation are robust to hyperparameter variations and generalizable across different LLM backbones and even other actor-critic algorithms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM) development has seen significant advancements through post-training techniques, particularly Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). A recent evolution, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), replaces human-annotated feedback with deterministic verification functions, offering a potentially more efficient and reliable training paradigm. On-policy RL optimization methods, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO), and Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO, have demonstrated capabilities in LLM post-training for tasks like mathematical reasoning and code generation. These methods are characterized by their "use-once-and-discard" approach to sampled experience, meaning that data generated from a policy is typically used for a single update step before being discarded.

However, existing on-policy RLVR frameworks face limitations, including experience waste and reward homogeneity. Experience waste occurs because each rollout group is consumed only once, leading to the discarding of valuable training data. Reward homogeneity arises when samples exhibit identical rewards within a group, causing advantage estimation (crucial for policy improvement guarantees) to collapse, particularly for very easy or very difficult samples. This results in minimal effective gradient contributions and hinders learning efficiency on challenging problems. Figure 1 in the paper illustrates this by showing that models post-trained with GRPO still struggle with difficult samples, especially those with zero accuracy in the initial rollout group.

Off-policy reinforcement learning, in contrast, offers a solution to increase sample efficiency and diversity by reusing historical experiences. While established in traditional RL, direct application of off-policy schemes to LLM post-training can introduce instability, entropy collapse, and performance degradation. Previous off-policy RLVR efforts, such as AReaL, ARPO, DOTS, RePO, ReMix, ReLIFT, and Kimi K1.5, have explored various strategies for asynchronous generation, fixed rollout policies, or buffer-based sample management. However, these methods often overlook policy stability, as samples from different training steps may exhibit varying policy distributions, contributing noise to learning, or may cause the model to overly focus on existing reasoning paths, suppressing exploration.

This work, Batch Adaptation Policy Optimization (BAPO), positions itself within this landscape by developing an off-policy RLVR framework designed to mitigate the identified limitations of both on-policy and existing off-policy approaches. It aims to systematically explore the utility of stale off-policy experience while maintaining training stability and improving data efficiency in LLM post-training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance data efficiency and learning effectiveness in Large Language Model (LLM) post-training, particularly for complex reasoning tasks, by developing a novel off-policy Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in traditional on-policy RLVR frameworks:

- **Experience Waste:** On-policy methods typically follow a "use-once-and-discard" principle for generated samples. This means that data collected during a policy rollout is used for a single gradient update and then discarded, even if it contains valuable information. This leads to inefficient utilization of computational resources and generated experience, especially when dealing with the high cost of LLM inference.
- **Reward Homogeneity and Gradient Vanishing on Difficult Samples:** Existing on-policy frameworks, such as GRPO, estimate advantages based on the relative diversity of rewards within a batch. When samples within a group have identical rewards (e.g., all correct or all incorrect), the estimated advantage can collapse, leading to negligible policy improvement. This issue is particularly pronounced for samples at the extremes of difficulty—those that are either consistently easy (all correct) or consistently difficult (all wrong). The paper highlights that models post-trained with GRPO struggle to improve on difficult samples that initially have zero accuracy. Such samples provide minimal benefit for post-training policy improvement because the advantage estimation mechanism becomes ineffective.
- **Instability of Naive Off-policy Approaches:** While off-policy methods offer a solution to experience waste by reusing historical data, their direct application to LLM post-training can introduce new problems. Reusing samples generated by older policies can lead to policy distribution discrepancies, causing training instability, entropy collapse (where the policy becomes too narrow), and ultimately performance degradation. This is a critical concern in the sensitive context of LLM fine-tuning.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines specific objectives:

- **Develop a Difficulty-Aware Experience Replay Mechanism:** This mechanism aims to leverage historical data effectively without introducing instability. It involves actively re-evaluating historically difficult prompts to drive exploration and directly reusing high-quality trajectories based on dynamic quality thresholds. This moves beyond simple mixing of buffer data and online data.
- **Provide Theoretical Guarantees for Stability and Improvement:** The research aims to establish a theoretical foundation, demonstrating that the proposed adaptive batch construction mechanism can mitigate the homogeneous reward issue and maintain a lower bound guarantee for policy improvement through adaptive batch construction and KL-constrained updates.
- **Validate Performance Across Diverse Reasoning Tasks and LLM Architectures:** The framework, named Batch Adaptation Policy Optimization (BAPO), is intended to be integrated into multiple reasoning tasks (mathematics, planning, visual reasoning) and with different LLM backbones. The objective is to demonstrate that BAPO achieves better convergence and yields greater improvements, particularly on difficult samples, compared to existing on-policy and off-policy RLVR frameworks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Batch Adaptation Policy Optimization (BAPO) framework is an off-policy RLVR approach designed to improve data efficiency and stability during LLM post-training. It integrates multiple off-policy strategies into an on-policy RLVR structure, primarily through an adaptive training batch construction mechanism.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

BAPO operates in two main phases:
- **Off-policy Rollout:** This phase involves generating responses using a policy that is not necessarily the most current training policy. The rollout policy `α` is synchronized with the trainer's policy `π_θ_t` every `v` training steps, where `v > 0` represents a delay. This delay helps stabilize the policy discrepancies of buffer samples. Responses generated by `α` for input prompts `x` form the online batch `X_on`, along with their log probabilities `α(y|x)` and rewards `r(x,y)`.
- **Off-policy Training:** This phase involves constructing the training batch from a combination of filtered online samples and historical samples stored in replay buffers, and subsequently using this batch to update the LLM policy `π_θ`.

#### 3.2. Formal Definitions

The training objective `L_α(π_θ)` is formulated as a combination of contributions from fresh online samples and historical buffer samples, regularized by a KL divergence term:
`L_α(π_θ) = E_{(x,y)~α} [ρ_α(θ) · Â(x,y)] + E_{(x,y)~B} [ρ_α_B(θ) · Â(x,y)] - β · D_KL(π_θ || α)`
Here, `ρ_α` and `ρ_α_B` are importance sampling ratios, `Â(x,y)` is the estimated advantage, `B` is the replay buffer, and `D_KL` is the Kullback-Leibler divergence to constrain policy deviation. The buffer `B` stores tuples containing prompt IDs, sets of prompts, generated responses, corresponding rewards, and the rollout policy probabilities `α_B(y_i,j|x_i)`.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Training Batch Construction

The core of BAPO is the adaptive construction of the training batch for each step using a filter function `I(x)` that decomposes data selection into three categories:

1. **Filtered Fresh Samples (X1):** These are online samples from the current rollout that are filtered to exclude those with zero reward variance. Specifically, `X1` retains samples where the group mean reward `μ_α,r(x)` falls within `[1/G, (G-1)/G]`, where `G` is the group size (number of responses per prompt). This filtering prevents gradient vanishing and contributes to training stability by ensuring that only samples with non-homogeneous rewards are included from the fresh rollout.
2. **Improved Historical Difficult Samples (X2):** This component addresses historically difficult samples that the current policy might have learned to solve. Periodically (every `m` training steps), responses for samples previously identified as difficult (stored in `B_bad` where `μ_α_B,r(x) ≤ c1`) are re-generated using the current policy `π_θ_t`. `X2` then comprises those re-generated samples that now show improved performance, specifically where `c1 < μ_π_θ_t,r(x) < 1`. A First-In-First-Out (FIFO) mechanism manages the `B_bad` buffer, which is limited in capacity to the training batch size.
3. **Reused Historical High-quality Samples (X3):** To ensure adequately sized training batches and leverage valuable past experiences, BAPO maintains a FIFO auxiliary buffer `B_high`. This buffer stores high-quality trajectories from the three most recent steps, defined by group mean rewards `μ_α_B,r(x)` within a range `[c2, c3]`. `X3` is then randomly sampled from `B_high` to fill any remaining capacity in the training batch after `X1` and `X2` have been selected. To enable progressive learning of harder tasks, the thresholds `c2` and `c3` are dynamically adjusted via a linear mapping based on the global average performance `r_tot`.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical guarantee for BAPO's training stability, building upon existing policy improvement lower bounds. Theorem 3.2 establishes that, under certain assumptions regarding bounded rewards and sufficiently small Total Variation (TV) distance constraints between policies, the expected policy improvement over filtered samples satisfies a lower bound. The theorem highlights:
- **Bounded Stability:** The constants `K1`, `K2`, and `K3` (which scale the penalty for policy divergence in the lower bound) are finite positive values, indicating that the training process remains numerically stable and theoretically bounded. These constants are derived from the minimum reward variances within each filtered sample set (`X1`, `X2`, `X3`).
- **Off-policy Tolerance:** The stability of trust-region methods (which limit policy update magnitudes) helps keep the divergence between the current and delayed rollout policies bounded. The FIFO mechanisms for `B_bad` and `B_high` also ensure that only samples from recent policies are retained, contributing to policy consistency within the training batch.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

BAPO's effectiveness was evaluated across three distinct reasoning tasks:
- **Mathematics:** Using DeepSeek R1 Distilled 1.5B and Qwen3 8B as backbones, trained on DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset, and evaluated on AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, Minerva Math, and OlympiadBench.
- **Planning:** Using Qwen2.5 Math 1.5B and 7B as backbones, trained on a subset of Countdown-34, and evaluated on Countdown-3to4 (CD-34) and Countdown-4 (CD-4).
- **Visual Geometry:** Using Qwen2.5 VL 3B and 7B as backbones, trained on Geometry3K, and evaluated on Geo-3K val and Geo-3K test splits.

Baselines included:
- **On-policy:** GRPO, DAPO, MoPPS.
- **Off-policy:** GRPO (v=5) (with delayed rollout), RePO, Remix-GRPO.
All experiments were conducted on 8 A100 GPUs with identical parameters where applicable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate BAPO's performance across various reasoning tasks and LLM backbones, as well as providing insights into its underlying mechanisms and robustness.

#### 4.1. Main Performance Results

BAPO consistently demonstrated improved performance and stability compared to baseline methods.
- **Overall Accuracy Improvement:** BAPO achieved an average of 12.5% accuracy improvement over baselines across mathematics, planning, and visual reasoning tasks (Table 1).
- **Convergence Stability:** Training curves (Figure 4) show that BAPO exhibits smoother convergence and higher reward bounds compared to GRPO, which often displayed significant oscillations and instability, especially in mathematical tasks with high variance in problem difficulty.
- **Efficiency Compared to DAPO:** While DAPO, another advanced on-policy method, sometimes approached BAPO's performance in certain metrics, it required approximately 2.5 times more rollouts (Figure 9) to do so. BAPO achieved comparable or superior performance with significantly fewer rollouts, indicating a more favorable trade-off between performance and computational cost (Table 2).
- **Resolution of Difficult Problems:** A notable finding is BAPO's ability to resolve challenging problems. It successfully improved 31% of problems that base models initially failed to solve (0/8 accuracy) after 3 epochs, compared to 19% for GRPO (Figure 7).

#### 4.2. Mechanism Analysis

To understand the factors contributing to BAPO's success, specific experiments were conducted.
- **Minimalist Verification:** A "Mini-test" experiment with Qwen3 8B on mathematics, using a hyperparameter-free version of BAPO's batch construction logic (strict zero-advantage filtering for X1, replaying historical all-wrong for X2, and reusing 50% accurate samples for X3), still showed a clear advantage over GRPO (Figure 5). This suggests that the structural components of BAPO (X2 and X3) are primary drivers of performance, rather than specific hyperparameter tuning.
- **Component Efficacy (Ablation Studies):** Ablation studies (Table 1b and Figure 6, Column 2) confirmed the importance of both `X2` (re-evaluated difficult samples) and `X3` (reused high-quality samples). Removing `X2` led to a substantial performance drop (approximately 21%), emphasizing the value of explicitly targeting difficult samples that show improvement.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** BAPO demonstrated robustness to its key hyperparameters. Performance remained stable within reasonable ranges for rollout delay `v` and re-rollout frequency `m` (Figure 6, Column 1). While adaptive difficulty thresholds yielded optimal convergence, fixed ranges for `c2` and `c3` still significantly outperformed baselines (Figure 6, Column 3), suggesting that the presence of diverse historical data is more critical than the exact values of these thresholds.
- **Online Filter Mechanism:** An analysis of online filtering strategies (Appendix A.3, Figure 10) showed that a "Range Filter" (retaining samples with group mean rewards in `[1/G, (G-1)/G]`) achieved the highest asymptotic performance, outperforming "Gaussian Filter" and "Uniform Filter." The uniform filter, which randomly selects samples regardless of quality, led to severe instability and performance collapse, indicating the crucial role of quality filtering in the training batch.

#### 4.3. Detailed Analysis

- **Dynamic Sample Distribution:** Figure 8 illustrates that BAPO's assembled training batch size dynamically fluctuates below the maximum configured capacity. This reduction in the backward propagation load helps offset the computational overhead introduced by periodic re-evaluation and log-probability re-computation.
- **Training Speed and Efficiency:** Table 2 shows that BAPO maintains training speeds comparable to GRPO while requiring significantly fewer rollouts than DAPO. This indicates a superior trade-off between convergence performance and computational cost.
- **Generalizability:** Appendix A.9 and Figure 16 demonstrate that the Batch Adaptation paradigm can be generalized to other actor-critic algorithms like PPO. A BA-PPO variant achieved a +5.5 performance gain on the AIME 2024 benchmark compared to standard PPO, suggesting the core dynamic batch construction principle is algorithm-agnostic.
- **Stability of Historical High-Quality Samples:** An analysis of accuracy migration (Figure 15) demonstrated that high-quality samples stored in the buffer largely retain their quality or improve over time, with negligible degradation. This validates the consistency and utility of `X3` (reused historical high-quality samples) as a data source.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Batch Adaptation Policy Optimization (BAPO), an off-policy Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework that addresses key limitations in the post-training of Large Language Models (LLMs). The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved Data Efficiency:** BAPO directly tackles the "experience waste" problem inherent in traditional on-policy RLVR methods. By strategically reusing high-quality historical samples and re-evaluating difficult ones, it makes more effective use of costly LLM rollouts. This translates to reduced computational resources and time required for LLM post-training, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible.
- **Enhanced Learning on Difficult Samples:** The framework specifically targets the issue of "reward homogeneity" that causes gradient signals to vanish for very easy or very difficult problems. By actively re-evaluating and selecting historically difficult samples that show improvement, BAPO facilitates learning on challenging problems that on-policy methods often fail to resolve. The reported success in resolving a significant percentage of previously unsolvable problems indicates a notable step towards more robust and capable reasoning LLMs.
- **Increased Training Stability:** Naive application of off-policy techniques can lead to training instability. BAPO addresses this by appropriately delaying the rollout policy, filtering online samples for non-homogeneous rewards, and carefully managing the replay buffer to maintain policy consistency. The theoretical guarantees for policy improvement lower bounds and the empirical observation of smoother convergence curves underscore its contribution to stable LLM post-training.
- **Principled Integration of Off-Policy Learning:** This work provides a principled methodology for integrating off-policy experience into RLVR, offering a blueprint for future research in this area. It demonstrates how to leverage stale data while mitigating the associated risks of policy divergence and performance degradation.
- **Generalizability:** The demonstration that the Batch Adaptation paradigm can be extended to other actor-critic algorithms like PPO (BA-PPO) suggests the core mechanism is not limited to GRPO-style RLVR. This broadens the potential impact, indicating that the approach could serve as a general enhancement for various LLM reinforcement learning algorithms.
- **Advancement in LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** By enabling more efficient and effective learning from verifiable rewards, BAPO has the potential to contribute to the development of LLMs with stronger and more reliable reasoning abilities across diverse domains such as mathematics, planning, and visual geometry.

In conclusion, BAPO represents a step in addressing critical challenges in LLM post-training. Its methodology for dynamic, difficulty-aware batch construction, supported by theoretical analysis and extensive empirical validation, offers a pathway for developing more data-efficient, stable, and capable reasoning LLMs. Future work adapting BAPO to complex architectures like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) or agentic RL frameworks could further expand its impact.
