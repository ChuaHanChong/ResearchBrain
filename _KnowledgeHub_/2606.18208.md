# Looped World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LoopWM introduces the first looped transformer architecture for world modeling, leveraging iterative latent depth to achieve stable long-horizon simulations with high parameter efficiency. The model demonstrates superior predictive accuracy and reduced computational cost on benchmarks like ScienceWorld and AlfWorld, outperforming models up to 100 times larger.

## Problem

- Existing world models struggle with compounding prediction errors during long-horizon simulations, leading to rapid degradation of trajectory quality.
- Increasing model depth to mitigate prediction errors results in a proportional increase in parameter count and inference cost, hindering real-time deployment.
- Prior research on looped architectures has been exclusively applied and evaluated in language modeling contexts, leaving world modeling unexplored.

## Method

- Introduces Looped World Models (**LoopWM**), which applies ==looped transformer architectures== for environment simulation and dynamics prediction.
- Develops a parameter-shared recurrent transformer block combined with spectrally-constrained residual dynamics to ensure stable state transitions over arbitrary rollout lengths.
- Implements adaptive early exit mechanisms for inference and deferred decoding to enable uninterrupted latent reasoning across multi-step action sequences.

## Results

- LoopWM (approx. 1 billion parameters) outperformed Claude-Opus-4-6-Max (over 100 times larger) on ScienceWorld, achieving **68.4%** Exact Match and **85.3%** Token F1 (vs. **47.2%** and **72.8%**, respectively).
- On the AlfWorld dataset, LoopWM achieved the highest BLEU score (**71.6%**) among four compared models, and ranked second in Exact Match and Token F1 scores.
- Deferred Decoding led to relative improvements across metrics, with an average **+113.8%** EM improvement at Step 5 on ScienceWorld tasks compared to baselines, indicating enhanced latent reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative latent depth can serve as a novel scaling axis for world models, orthogonal to traditional dimensions like model size and training data volume.
> - Reusing parameters through a looped architecture achieves the predictive quality of larger networks with significantly fewer parameters, leading to compounding computational savings.
> - Spectrally-constrained residual dynamics provide formal guarantees for the numerical stability of latent state evolution, essential for reliable long-horizon predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models (WMs) are a foundational component in sample-efficient reinforcement learning and embodied artificial intelligence, enabling agents to predict environmental evolution based on actions. Early work established the recurrent state-space model (RSSM) as a core architecture, notably in PlaNet and the subsequent Dreamer series, which demonstrated learning latent dynamics from pixels and planning in latent space. Recent advancements have explored transformer-based WMs, such as IRIS, TransDreamer, ∆-IRIS, DIAMOND, and EMERALD, leveraging transformers' representational capabilities. Concurrently, large-scale video generation models like Sora and Genie have been presented as general-purpose world simulators. These developments have expanded WM applications into fields like autonomous driving, embodied AI, and video generation.

Despite this progress, a challenge persists in achieving faithful long-horizon simulations. Such simulations typically require deep or iterative computation, which leads to two issues in conventional fixed-depth architectures. Firstly, prediction errors compound over extended rollouts, causing rapid degradation of trajectory quality. Secondly, increasing model depth to mitigate these errors often leads to a proportional increase in parameter count and inference cost, hindering real-time deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

In a parallel domain, looped transformer architectures have emerged as a method to reuse a shared set of transformer blocks recurrently on the same latent representation. Concepts like the Universal Transformer, ALBERT, and MoEUT introduced weight-sharing and adaptive halting mechanisms. Theoretical work on looped transformers has indicated their capacity to simulate iterative algorithms with constant parameter counts and achieve parameter efficiency comparable to standard transformers in language tasks. Recent practical implementations, such as Ouro, recurrent-depth models, and Hyperloop Transformers, have scaled these architectures to language models, demonstrating efficiency and improved generalization. These models are also connected to depth-continuous and implicit-layer models like Neural ODEs and Deep Equilibrium Models.

This paper addresses the identified gap where, to the authors' knowledge, all prior research on looped architectures has been exclusively applied and evaluated in language modeling contexts. The concept of Looped World Models (LoopWM) has not been explored previously. This work positions itself by proposing the application of looped transformers to world modeling to address the tension between simulation fidelity and computational cost, introducing iterative latent depth as a novel scaling axis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a fundamental tension identified in existing world models: the requirement for deep computational processes to achieve faithful long-horizon simulations versus the inherent drawbacks of such deep models, namely high deployment costs and susceptibility to compounding errors during prediction rollouts. Conventional fixed-depth architectures allocate a uniform amount of computation to every transition, irrespective of its complexity, which can lead to rapid degradation of prediction quality over extended horizons and makes real-time deployment expensive.

The authors propose that looped transformer architectures offer a suitable solution by introducing an explicit iterative refinement mechanism while reusing parameters across computational depth. This approach is motivated by several factors:

- **Structural Alignment:** Environment dynamics frequently involve the repeated application of governing laws. This suggests that modeling a single-step transition through the repeated application of a shared latent update operator within an inner loop could conceptually align the model's computation with the iterative nature of physical processes. The inner loop refines a latent transition estimate rather than representing physical time directly.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** Long-horizon world model rollouts necessitate executing the dynamics model many times in sequence. A looped architecture that achieves the predictive quality of a larger network with a fraction of the parameters can yield compounding computational savings across each rollout step, making deployment more feasible.
- **Adaptive Computation:** Looped models possess an adaptive-depth property, allowing them to allocate varying amounts of computation based on the complexity of each transition. For instance, complex events like collisions might require more iterations for refinement, while simpler dynamics like free flight could require fewer. This capability aims to optimize computational resources relative to a fixed-depth baseline.
- **Numerical Stability:** To ensure that latent states remain bounded and do not numerically explode over many iterative applications, especially critical for long-horizon world modeling, explicit mechanisms for numerical stability are required.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are to:

- Introduce Looped World Models (LoopWM) as the first looped transformer architectures for environment simulation and dynamics prediction.
- Develop a parameter-shared recurrent transformer block combined with spectrally-constrained residual dynamics to enable stable state transitions over arbitrary rollout lengths.
- Demonstrate that LoopWM can achieve competitive or superior predictive accuracy compared to existing world model architectures while utilizing significantly fewer parameters.
- Show that LoopWM can maintain stable rollouts over substantially longer horizons.
- Implement and validate test-time adaptive computation within LoopWM, allowing the model to automatically adjust its computational depth to match the complexity of individual prediction steps.
- Establish iterative latent depth as a new scaling axis for world models, distinct from and orthogonal to conventional scaling axes such as model size and training data volume.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Looped World Models (LoopWM) architecture integrates iterative computation from looped transformers with action-conditioned state prediction for world modeling. The design principles emphasize structural alignment with iterative physical dynamics, provable stability of latent state transitions, and adaptive computational depth.

The overall architecture comprises four modules:

1. **Observation Encoder (Eϕ):** A convolutional or vision-transformer-based encoder maps raw observations `ok` to a latent embedding `ek`.
2. **Action Embedder (Aψ):** Actions `ak` are projected into the same latent space to produce an action embedding `uk`.
3. **Looped Dynamics Core (Lθ):** This is the central component, responsible for predicting the next latent state `hk`. It takes the previous latent state `hk-1`, the current observation embedding `ek`, and the action embedding `uk`, and processes them through `T` iterations of a parameter-shared transformer block with spectrally-constrained residual dynamics.
4. **Prediction Heads (Dξ):** Lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) decode the latent state `hk` into a reconstructed observation `ˆok+1` (or its latent target), a predicted reward `ˆrk`, and a predicted continuation flag `ˆck`.

The Looped Dynamics Core is further structured into three blocks:

- **Prelude (P):** A stack of `LP` transformer layers processes the concatenated input `[hk-1; ek; uk]` to generate a conditioning signal `e`. Layer normalization is applied to `e` to manage input magnitude.
- **Recurrent Block (R):** This block consists of `LR` transformer layers whose parameters are shared across `T` iterative loops. The hidden state `h(t)` is updated iteratively. The update rule is formulated as `h(t+1) = Ā h(t) + B̄ e + R̄(h(t), e)`, where `Ā` is the state-retention matrix, `B̄` is the input-injection matrix for the conditioning signal `e`, and `R̄` encompasses the nonlinear transformer operations (multi-head attention and feed-forward layers).
- **Spectral Stability Constraint:** To prevent the latent state from diverging over numerous iterations, a spectral norm constraint is applied to `Ā`. This is achieved by parameterizing `A` as a learnable negative diagonal matrix `diag(-exp(a))` and discretizing it via a zero-order hold: `Ā = exp(∆ · A)`, where `∆` is a learnable positive diagonal matrix. This construction ensures that all eigenvalues of `Ā` are strictly between 0 and 1, making `Ā` contractive and guaranteeing bounded residual dynamics.
- **Coda (C):** A final stack of `LC` transformer layers, with separate, non-shared parameters, processes the terminal hidden state `h(T)` from the recurrent block to produce the final latent state `hk`.

A key architectural feature is **Cross-Timestep State Propagation**, where the terminal hidden state `h(T)` from environment step `k` serves as the initial `h(0)` for the recurrent block at step `k+1`, facilitating a dual-loop structure: an inner loop for latent refinement and an outer loop for temporal propagation.

**Training Objective:**

- **Variable-Depth Training:** The number of inner loops `T` is sampled from a Poisson distribution with a learnable mean `μrec` at each training step, allowing for varied computational depths.
- **World Model Loss (Lwm):** This is a combined loss function encompassing observation prediction loss, reward prediction loss, and continuation prediction loss, averaged over `K` sequence lengths. Backpropagation through the loop iterations is truncated to `⌈μrec/2⌉` steps to manage memory.
- **Entropy-Regularized Adaptive Depth (Lent):** When an adaptive early-exit mechanism is used, an entropy regularization term is added to prevent the exit gate from converging to trivial behaviors (always exiting or never exiting).

**Adaptive Early Exit for Inference:**
A lightweight exit gate, implemented as a single-layer MLP with a sigmoid activation, is used. At each loop iteration `t`, if the output `g(t)` exceeds a learned threshold `τ`, the inner loop terminates, and `h(t)` becomes the final hidden state. This mechanism is designed to reduce inference FLOPs for simpler transitions and allows for test-time compute scaling by increasing the maximum loop count `Tmax`.

**Deferred Decoding (DD):**
This modification eliminates intermediate observation decoding during multi-step rollouts, aiming to improve reasoning quality and efficiency by allowing the latent state to focus on action-relevant structure rather than per-step visual reconstruction.

- **Formulation:** Instead of applying prediction heads `Dξ` at every environment step `k`, the model performs `K` latent transitions using only the `Lcoreθ` and then decodes `hK` only at the final step `K`.
- **Training Objective for DD:**
 - **Terminal Prediction Loss (Lterminal):** Supervision is provided at the final step `K` for observation, reward, and continuation predictions.
 - **Latent Trajectory Regularizer:**
 - **Latent Consistency Loss (Lconsist):** A lightweight projection `gω` aligns intermediate latent states `hk` with stop-gradient encoded ground-truth observations `sg(Eϕ(ok))`.
 - **Spectral Contraction Budget:** A penalty term enforces a soft upper bound on the cumulative difference between latent states over `K` outer steps.
- **Curriculum Over Deferral Horizon K:** Training starts with `K=1` and gradually increases `K` to stabilize learning.
- **Inference Modes:** DD supports a "Planning mode" that evaluates only the terminal state `hK` for candidate action sequences, reducing decoder invocations. A "Monitoring mode" allows for intermediate low-dimensional state summaries using `gω` without full decoding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents empirical results across two distinct datasets: ScienceWorld and AlfWorld, along with human evaluation for a danmaku generation task.

**On the ScienceWorld Dataset:**
The evaluation involved a world modeling task where consecutive five actions were fed to the models to obtain final scores on world modeling. Key metrics included Exact Match (EM), Token F1, BLEU-4, and Entity score.

- **Comparison with Claude-Opus-4-6-Max:** LoopWM, with approximately 1 billion parameters, demonstrated superior performance compared to the larger Claude-Opus-4-6-Max. LoopWM achieved an overall EM of 68.4%, Token F1 of 85.3%, BLEU-4 of 80.7%, and Entity score of 83.9%. In contrast, Claude-Opus-4-6-Max obtained an overall EM of 47.2%, Token F1 of 72.8%, BLEU-4 of 64.4%, and Entity score of 72.3%. In specific tasks, LoopWM showed substantial improvements, such as increasing the score on the "Lifespan" task from 0% to 100%. The authors note LoopWM's parameter efficiency, being over 100 times smaller than closed-source API models like Claude-Opus-4-6-Max.
- **Comparison with other baselines (Qwen-3.5-flash and Gemini-3-flash-preview-thinking):** LoopWM consistently outperformed Qwen-3.5-flash and Gemini-3-flash-preview-thinking across most metrics, which are noted to be smaller models.

**On the AlfWorld Dataset:**
For the AlfWorld dataset, LoopWM exhibited promising overall results, maintaining its compact size of around 1 billion parameters.

- LoopWM achieved the highest BLEU score (71.6%) among the four compared models (LoopWM, Claude-Opus-4-6-Max, Qwen-3.5-flash, Gemini-3-flash-preview-thinking).
- It ranked second in terms of EM and Token F1 scores.
- An error analysis indicated that entity scores were relatively low for LoopWM across most action categories, suggesting an area for future improvement.

**Deep Analysis on Deferred Decoding (DD):**
The analysis of deferred decoding across various tables indicated its utility, with its benefits increasing as rollout horizons accumulated. Tables detailing the effect of deferred decoding on ScienceWorld, comparing LoopWM against Gemini-3-flash-preview-thinking and Qwen-3.5-flash, showed relative improvements across EM, F1, BLEU, and Entity scores. For instance, on average over all tasks, deferred decoding led to an EM improvement of +113.8% at Step 5 when compared to Gemini-3-flash-preview-thinking (Table 5). This suggests that allowing the latent state to refine over multiple steps without intermediate decoding is beneficial.

**Human Evaluation on Danmaku Generation:**
The paper includes results from human evaluations on a danmaku generation task, presented in figures.
- LoopWM demonstrated a notable relative increase in online performance compared to a baseline (Qwen3.7-max), as shown in Figure 2.
- Human evaluation metrics, including Appropriateness, Informativeness, Engagingness, and Human-likeness, showed LoopWM scoring higher than a "Baseline VLM" (Figure 3). For example, LoopWM scored 91 on Appropriateness compared to 56 for the Baseline VLM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces Looped World Models (LoopWM), representing a significant step in addressing the fundamental tension between the need for deep computation in long-horizon simulations and the associated high deployment costs and compounding errors in conventional world models.

The primary significance of LoopWM lies in its architectural innovation: it is the first application of looped transformer architectures to world modeling. By iteratively refining latent environment states through a parameter-shared transformer block, LoopWM structurally mirrors the recurrent nature of physical systems. This design inherently contributes to several potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Parameter Efficiency:** LoopWM achieves up to 100x parameter efficiency compared to conventional approaches without compromising predictive quality. This efficiency can lead to substantial computational savings during long-horizon rollouts, which involve executing the dynamics model numerous times. Such efficiency is critical for deploying capable world models on resource-constrained platforms.
- **Improved Rollout Stability:** The integration of spectrally-constrained residual dynamics provides formal guarantees for the stability of latent state evolution across arbitrary rollout lengths. This addresses a critical challenge in world modeling: the accumulation of small prediction errors that can rapidly degrade trajectory fidelity over time. The provably stable dynamics ensure that latent states remain bounded, fostering more reliable and longer-term simulations.
- **Adaptive Computational Allocation:** LoopWM's adaptive computation mechanism allows the model to dynamically scale its effective depth based on the complexity of each prediction step. This means that computationally demanding transitions (e.g., multi-body collisions) receive more refinement iterations, while simpler dynamics (e.g., free flight) require fewer, optimizing average inference cost.
- **Introduction of a New Scaling Axis:** The research establishes "iterative latent depth" as a novel scaling axis for world simulation. This axis is orthogonal to traditional scaling dimensions like model size and training data volume, potentially opening new avenues for developing more capable, efficient, and stable world models.
- **Refined Latent Reasoning with Deferred Decoding:** The deferred decoding mechanism allows the model to perform uninterrupted latent reasoning across multi-step action sequences, deferring observation-space computation to the terminal step. This encourages the latent trajectory to encode temporally extended, action-relevant structure, rather than being continuously constrained by pixel-level reconstruction, leading to improved reasoning quality and computational savings in planning.

Beyond the specific results demonstrated on ScienceWorld and AlfWorld datasets and danmaku generation, the principles established by LoopWM suggest broader applicability, including to continuous visual environments. The work's current scope, while providing evidence for the core architectural thesis, indicates potential for future research to further elaborate on the decomposition of gains, conduct more extensive empirical validation, provide a more explicit positioning analysis relative to other world model families (RSSM, autoregressive video token, diffusion-based), and characterize more complete scaling laws across diverse task and compute ranges. This perspective on iterative latent depth could significantly influence the future direction of research in world modeling.
