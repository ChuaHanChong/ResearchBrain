# Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA: From Vision-Language-Action Models to a Real-World Robot Learning Stack

> [!summary] Summary
> HyVLA-0.5, a comprehensive robot learning stack from Tencent Robotics X and Tencent Hy Team, unifies high-fidelity data collection, VLM-based training, preference-driven policy refinement, and real-world deployment for generalist robots. The system achieved 90.9% success on clean simulated tasks and demonstrated effective cross-embodiment transfer and robust policy improvement via reward-free post-training on real robots.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack a holistic 'robot learning stack' for robustness across data collection, training, and real-world deployment, hindering generalist robot emergence.
- Existing data acquisition methods (teleoperation, UMI) often provide unnatural interfaces, lack haptic feedback, or yield coarse action labels, while struggling with precise cross-embodiment transfer.
- Achieving high dexterity with standard imitation learning and deploying policies at high frequencies within a closed visual loop on diverse real hardware remain significant challenges.

## Method

- A custom Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) with optical motion-capture collects the Hy-UMI-10K dataset of over 10,000 hours of high-fidelity human demonstrations, providing sub-millimeter precision action labels and force signals.
- The **HyVLA-0.5** model extends an embodied 4-billion parameter Vision-Language Model (VLM) with a ==flow-matching action expert==, compact memory encoder, and delta-chunk action representation for continuous, high-frequency control.
- FlowPRO, a critic-free, reward-free Proximalized Preference Optimization algorithm, refines policies using real-robot intervention-and-rollback data, and a deployment stack ensures asynchronous, smooth, and embodiment-agnostic real-world execution.

## Results

- HyVLA-**0.5** achieved a **90.9%** success rate on Clean tasks and **90.1%** on Randomized tasks in the RoboTwin **2.0** benchmark, outperforming all contemporary VLA systems.
- Large-scale UMI pre-training consistently improved real-world task performance and enabled successful cross-embodiment transfer to morphologically different robots (e.g., JAKA K1, Astribot S1) without target-robot teleoperation.
- FlowPRO post-training, utilizing real-robot interventions, significantly boosted success rates on long-horizon bimanual tasks (e.g., Bottle: **99%** SR), outperforming DAgger and π0.6* and demonstrating rapid policy refinement from failure cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A holistic 'robot learning stack' approach, co-designing data collection, model architecture, policy refinement, and deployment, is crucial for developing robust, deployable generalist robots.
> - High-fidelity human demonstrations with sub-millimeter precision and rich force signals, acquired via a custom UMI and optical motion-capture, provide essential priors for learning precise manipulation and enabling cross-embodiment transfer.
> - Reward-free, preference-based offline reinforcement learning, like FlowPRO, effectively leverages real-robot failure cases to achieve significant policy robustness and last-mile dexterity without explicit reward engineering.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements have led to a rapid proliferation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, shifting focus toward the development of embodied foundation models. However, the paper posits that a truly generalist robot cannot emerge from a single model in isolation, but rather requires a comprehensive robot learning stack that maintains robustness across data collection, training, and real-world deployment.

The current landscape of VLA research faces several system-level challenges:
- **Data Acquisition**: Traditional teleoperation methods often force operators to adapt to unnatural robot workspaces, lack haptic feedback, and are unsuitable for delicate manipulation. While human demonstrations or existing Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) frameworks alleviate data scarcity, they introduce limitations such as coarse action labels from raw human data or cumbersome handheld devices that fail to capture fingertip-level force transmission in earlier UMI rigs. Furthermore, bridging the cross-embodiment gap involves not only kinematics adaptation but also addressing disparities in human and robot motion spaces, control dynamics, and egocentric versus robot-mounted camera perceptions.
- **Model Architecture and Training**: Early VLA approaches frequently utilized autoregressive modeling over discretized action tokens, which inherently limited execution speed and control precision. While more recent frameworks, such as those coupling VLMs with flow-matching action experts, predict continuous actions, their foundational visual backbones are often general-purpose and not specifically engineered for the dense spatiotemporal reasoning required for physical interaction. Additionally, standard imitation learning struggles to achieve high dexterity, and existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for continuous control often rely on brittle reward models or value networks.
- **Deployment Constraints**: Even a well-trained policy is of limited practical use if it cannot be deployed at high frequencies within a closed visual loop on real hardware, a constraint that is often not treated as a primary design consideration.

The HyVLA-0.5 work aims to address these limitations by presenting an end-to-end system that encompasses the entire robot learning stack, from custom data collection hardware to production-ready deployment. This contrasts with approaches that focus solely on VLA model advancements by integrating data, modeling, policy refinement, and deployment into a unified pipeline. The paper benchmarks against contemporary VLA systems like π0, π0.5, ABot-M0, LingBot-VLA, starVLA, Motus, JoyAI-RA, and Qwen-VLA, indicating its position among advanced generalist robot control models. It builds upon previous embodied VLM backbones such as Hy-Embodied-0.5, expanding its scale and introducing new capabilities. In terms of post-training, it diverges from reward- or value-based RL and prior preference-based RL methods like Flow-DPO by proposing a novel, reward-free approach. The deployment strategy also presents an alternative to existing asynchronous inference and action-chunk smoothing techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the development of deployable generalist robots by moving beyond isolated VLA model improvements. The authors emphasize that achieving generalist robot capabilities requires a holistic "robot learning stack" where data, training, adaptation, and execution layers are co-designed with real-hardware constraints in mind.

The motivation for this comprehensive approach stems from several specific challenges identified in the current state of VLA research and robotic deployment:
- **Improving Data Quality and Relevance**: Existing data collection methods, such as traditional teleoperation, are often unnatural for human operators, lack haptic feedback, and preclude delicate manipulation. While UMI systems offer improvements, current implementations may lack fingertip-level force transmission or rely on visual SLAM, which can introduce pose jitters and track losses. The objective is to collect high-fidelity human demonstrations that provide rich behavioral diversity and precise action labels, directly addressing the shortcomings of coarser human data or less accurate UMI systems.
- **Enhancing Model Design for Robotic Control**: General-purpose VLM backbones, while capable of semantic reasoning, are not always optimized for the dense spatiotemporal understanding critical for physical robot interaction. The motivation is to engineer a VLM backbone specifically for embodied control, enabling stronger spatial priors and faster convergence during training. Additionally, traditional discrete action spaces limit control precision and execution speed, necessitating a shift to continuous action prediction.
- **Facilitating Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: A significant challenge is enabling policies learned from human or one robot morphology to transfer effectively to morphologically different robots. This involves addressing the "embodiment gap" (human vs. robot motion), "control gap" (different dynamics and actuation), and "perception gap" (human egocentric vs. robot views). The objective is to develop representations and training strategies that decouple policy learning from embodiment-specific kinematics, making it more transferable.
- **Achieving Last-Mile Dexterity and Robustness**: Standard imitation learning often falls short in achieving the high dexterity and robustness required for complex, real-world manipulation tasks. Existing reinforcement learning methods, while powerful, often depend on brittle reward models that are difficult to design for contact-rich tasks. The motivation is to develop a policy refinement mechanism that can effectively leverage failure cases to improve long-tail manipulation robustness without requiring explicit reward engineering.
- **Enabling High-Frequency Real-World Deployment**: Even well-trained policies can be ineffective if they cannot operate at high frequencies within a closed visual loop on real hardware. This requires addressing issues like inference latency, command dispatch, and ensuring smooth, continuous motion. The objective is to create a deployment stack that facilitates real-time control, smooth action execution, and adaptation to heterogeneous robot platforms.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a "unified pipeline that jointly tackles data, model, policy refinement, and deployment" to overcome these integrated bottlenecks and accelerate the emergence of deployable generalist robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HyVLA-0.5 is presented as an end-to-end system structured around five core components: data collection, model design, supervised training, reinforcement learning post-training, and real-world deployment.

#### 3.1. Data Collection: Hy-UMI-10K Dataset

The system relies on Hy-UMI-10K, a proprietary dataset of over 10,000 hours of human demonstrations collected using a custom-designed Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) device.
- **Custom UMI Device**: The device features fingertip-attached grippers that allow direct actuation and provide haptic feedback to the operator. It incorporates a head-mounted camera for egocentric views and 6-dimensional force/torque sensors at the gripper tips for capturing tactile information.
- **High-Fidelity Tracking**: Unlike conventional UMI systems that use visual SLAM for pose estimation, HyVLA-0.5 employs an external optical motion-capture system. This system tracks each 6-DoF gripper trajectory with sub-millimeter precision within a globally consistent world frame, providing high-quality action labels.
- **Data Composition**: The dataset comprises over 1 million episodes across 70 distinct tasks, organized into six scene-based task families (e.g., Laundry Room, Kitchen, Dexterous/Tool-use). The collected trajectories are designed to be reusable for downstream adaptation and post-training.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

HyVLA-0.5 extends the Hy-Embodied-0.5 backbone, a 4-billion parameter Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Vision-Language Model (VLM) pre-trained on embodied corpora. The architecture integrates three main components:
- **Hy-Embodied-0.5 Backbone**: This VLM employs a MoT architecture with modality-adaptive computation and native-resolution image encoding (Hy-ViT 2.0). It processes multi-view RGB images, natural language instructions, and proprioceptive robot states. Auxiliary co-training tasks (VQA, 2D/3D grounding) are used to maintain and enrich the VLM's semantic and spatial representations via a next-token prediction objective.
- **Action Expert with Dual-Tower Flow Matching**: Instead of discrete tokens, a dedicated action expert models continuous action distributions using conditional flow matching. It operates as a separate tower, consuming projected robot states and noisy action tokens. The VLM and action expert interact through shared self-attention. A block-wise causal attention mechanism ensures bidirectional attention within token blocks (visual, textual, state, noisy action) but causal attention across them, minimizing distribution shift. The flow-matching objective (L_fm) trains the expert to regress a velocity field that transports noise to ground-truth actions, enabling continuous, high-frequency action prediction.
- **Compact Memory Encoder with Temporal-Spatial Attention**: To process multi-frame, multi-view visual history (K frames from n cameras) efficiently, the image encoder is extended into a video encoder. It aggregates visual context through factorized temporal-spatial attention, inserting a temporal pass every few layers. This design applies causal attention across frames (present and past) and bidirectional spatial attention within each frame, avoiding the O(n^2 K^2) computational cost of joint space-time attention. The encoder is parameter-free, reusing existing ViT block projections, and compresses the temporal dimension such that only current-frame tokens are passed to the VLM, maintaining a constant visual token count.
- **Delta-Chunk Action Representation**: The policy predicts an entire "action chunk" of future actions (horizon H) per inference cycle. Both proprioceptive states and actions are formulated in the end-effector frame (EEF). Actions are "delta-chunks," representing incremental motion relative to the current EEF pose, decoupling policy learning from robot-specific joint kinematics and reducing the optimization search space.

#### 3.3. Pre-training and Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)

The training process occurs in stages:
- **Pre-training**: The HyVLA-0.5 model is initialized from the Hy-Embodied-0.5-MoT checkpoint and pre-trained on the full 10,000-hour Hy-UMI-10K corpus. During this stage, K=1 (no historical frames) is used for efficiency. The model ingests three camera views and predicts an action chunk of H=50 at 10 Hz for 200,000 steps.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)**: The UMI pre-trained checkpoint is then fine-tuned on task-specific demonstrations with K=6 (current frame plus five historical frames). This stage includes two tracks:
 - **Track-A (Intra-embodiment Adaptation)**: Fine-tuning on demonstrations collected via teleoperation on the same robot platform used for evaluation (e.g., Dobot X-Trainer).
 - **Track-B (UMI-only Cross-embodiment Transfer)**: Fine-tuning solely on task-specific UMI demonstrations, followed by deployment on morphologically different robots without target-robot teleoperation (e.g., JAKA K1, Astribot S1, Unitree G1).

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning Post-Training: FlowPRO

HyVLA-0.5 incorporates FlowPRO, a critic-free, reward-free Proximalized Preference Optimization (PRO)-based offline RL algorithm for real-robot post-training.
- **Intervention-and-Rollback Data Collection**: During policy rollouts, an operator intervenes upon detecting erroneous actions. The system rewinds to an earlier state, records the failed segment as a negative trajectory, and captures the operator's corrective demonstration from that same state as a positive trajectory. This creates paired success/failure trajectories.
- **Smooth Interpolation**: To generate dense, per-state preference tuples (s, a_w, a_l), where a_w is the preferred action and a_l is the dispreferred action, a smooth interpolation procedure synthesizes missing counterpart actions for states belonging to only one trajectory.
- **RPRO Loss**: The FlowPRO algorithm utilizes an RPRO loss, which combines a flow-matching-aware preference optimization term (L_PRO) with a supervised term (L_SFT). The L_PRO objective pulls the current policy's action toward the preferred action (a_w) and pushes it away from the dispreferred action (a_l), using a flow-matching log-likelihood proxy as an implicit reward. A symmetric proximal regularizer in L_PRO prevents reward hacking. A contrastive gradient cancellation property allows safe mixing of SFT-style demonstrations. The process is iterative, with the policy from the previous round serving as a frozen reference.

#### 3.5. Real-World Deployment

The deployment stack addresses three runtime aspects:
- **Embodiment-Agnostic Platform Mapping**: The delta-chunk representation keeps the policy's action interface independent of specific robot kinematics. Relative SE(3) predictions are composed with the initial end-effector pose to derive absolute world-frame targets. Inverse kinematics (IK) is then solved on the target robot to produce joint commands. For humanoids (e.g., Astribot S1), a deterministic heuristic infers a fixed chassis frame and floating torso pose from predicted gripper targets. Pre-deployment hygiene protocols (reachability verification, IK feasibility checks) are applied to ensure task envelope alignment.
- **Asynchronous Execution**: To mitigate the latency of VLA policy inference compared to the robot's servo loop, an asynchronous producer-consumer runtime is used. The inference thread updates a thread-safe action buffer with smoothed action sequences, while the execution thread continuously pops commands at the control frequency.
- **Latency-Aware Bézier Chunk Stitching**: This component addresses motion discontinuities and delayed chunk integration. A cubic Bézier segment is used to form a C1-continuous connector between the robot's current state and the policy's predicted action chunk. The Bézier curve's control points are set to align with the historical motion direction and the predicted future chunk direction, ensuring smooth transitions without additional learned controllers. Position is smoothed in R3, orientation using SLERP, and gripper commands are linearly interpolated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HyVLA-0.5 encompasses simulated and real-world tasks, along with an assessment of the FlowPRO post-training algorithm.

#### 4.1. Simulated Tasks (RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark)

HyVLA-0.5 demonstrated competitive performance on the 50-task RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, evaluated under both Clean and Randomized settings.
- **Overall Performance**: HyVLA-0.5 achieved a success rate of 90.9% on Clean tasks and 90.1% on Randomized tasks, outperforming all benchmarked contemporary VLA systems, including strong competitors like JoyAI-RA (90.5% Clean, 89.3% Randomized), starVLA (88.2% Clean, 88.3% Randomized), and π0.5 (82.7% Clean, 76.8% Randomized).
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - Removing the compact memory encoder resulted in a performance decrease from 90.9% to 88.8% (Clean) and 90.1% to 88.6% (Randomized).
 - Further removing the large-scale UMI pre-training stage led to an additional drop, with scores decreasing to 88.1% (Clean) and 87.9% (Randomized).
These ablations indicate that both UMI pre-training and the compact memory encoder contribute to performance gains in simulation, even though the egocentric real-world UMI corpus is visually distinct from the synthetic RoboTwin 2.0 environment.

#### 4.2. Real-World Tasks

Real-world evaluations were conducted on bimanual manipulation tasks across multiple robot platforms, organized into two deployment tracks, and a force-modality validation task.
- **Track A: Intra-Embodiment Fine-Tuning (Dobot X-Trainer)**:
 - Tasks: Insert Bottles, Fold and Store Glasses, Set the Table, Zip Up the Pen Case.
 - Effect of UMI Pre-training: Pre-training on Hy-UMI-10K consistently improved performance on precision-critical tasks. For instance, in "Fold and Store Glasses" and "Zip Up the Pen Case," the pre-trained policy demonstrated increased accuracy at decisive sub-steps requiring sub-centimeter positioning and stable bimanual force coupling, leading to higher end-to-end success rates compared to policies without UMI pre-training. This suggests that the high-precision UMI pre-training sharpens the action distribution at critical manipulation bottlenecks.
- **Track B: Cross-Embodiment Transfer (JAKA K1, Astribot S1)**:
 - Tasks: Put Away the Accessory (JAKA K1), Clean Up the Table (Astribot S1).
 - Effect of UMI Pre-training: When post-training was conducted solely on task-specific UMI demonstrations (without any target-robot teleoperation), HyVLA-0.5 achieved significantly higher success rates than baselines (π0 and π0.5). This finding supports the claim that large-scale, high-fidelity UMI pre-training endows the model with embodiment-agnostic action priors, facilitating deployment to morphologically unseen robots even with small UMI fine-tuning datasets.
- **Force-Modality Validation (Unitree G1)**:
 - Task: A force-discrimination task where the robot had to sequentially grasp two boxes of differing masses and place the lighter one into a basket.
 - Result: HyVLA-0.5 reliably selected the lighter box across trials. This confirms that the force signals recorded by the custom UMI workstation provide actionable non-visual cues for downstream policy learning, enabling force-aware manipulation.

#### 4.3. Real-World Reinforcement Learning Post-Training (FlowPRO on Dobot X-Trainer)

FlowPRO was evaluated on four long-horizon bimanual tasks: Bottle, Cap, USB, and Zip.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: FlowPRO consistently outperformed DAgger (positive-only dataset aggregation) and π0.6* (advantage-conditioned regression using preference data).
 - **Versus DAgger**: FlowPRO leveraged negative trajectories via a contrastive loss, yielding consistent performance gains across all tasks (e.g., Bottle: 99% SR vs. 93% SR for DAgger, with shorter completion times). The contrastive signal from dispreferred actions helped the policy avoid failure modes.
 - **Versus π0.6***: On identical preference data, FlowPRO achieved higher success rates and shorter completion times (e.g., Bottle: 99% SR, 16s CT vs. 95% SR, 24s CT for π0.6*). This suggests that FlowPRO's direct injection of the preference signal into the action-generation loss is more effective than conditioning tokens, which can be diluted by other VLM context.
- **Iterative Improvement**: Success rates consistently improved across the three iterative rounds of post-training, demonstrating FlowPRO's effectiveness in turning failure cases into rapid policy improvements towards near-ceiling success rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HyVLA-0.5 research marks a notable advancement toward the realization of deployable generalist robots by presenting a complete and integrated robot learning stack. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Holistic Approach to Generalist Robotics**: The work shifts the paradigm from optimizing isolated VLA models to co-designing an entire pipeline—from data collection to deployment—around real-world hardware constraints. This integrated "robot learning stack" approach is critical for developing truly generalist robots that are robust and functional across diverse tasks and environments.
- **High-Fidelity, Reusable Data**: The development and utilization of the Hy-UMI-10K dataset, with its sub-millimeter precision and egocentric views collected via an optical motion-capture system, represent a significant step in providing high-quality, reusable human demonstrations. This high-fidelity data is shown to be crucial for learning precise manipulation priors and enables effective cross-embodiment transfer, addressing a long-standing challenge in robot learning.
- **Enhanced Model Capabilities for Embodied AI**: The extension of the Hy-Embodied-0.5 backbone with a flow-matching action expert and a compact memory encoder, coupled with a delta-chunk action representation, provides a robust foundation for continuous, high-frequency, and embodiment-agnostic control. This design enables the policy to leverage temporal context efficiently and decouple learning from specific robot kinematics, improving generalization.
- **Effective Policy Refinement from Failures**: The introduction of FlowPRO, a critic-free and reward-free Proximalized Preference Optimization algorithm, offers a practical method for improving policy robustness from real-robot failure cases. By directly converting teleoperated interventions into optimization signals, FlowPRO provides an efficient iteration loop for achieving last-mile dexterity without the complexities of reward modeling or extensive online exploration.
- **Robust Real-World Deployment**: The asynchronous inference pipeline, combined with latency-aware cubic Bézier chunk stitching, addresses critical deployment challenges. These components enable high-frequency, smooth, and C1-continuous control on real hardware, facilitating the transition of learned policies from simulation or human demonstrations to diverse physical robot platforms, including humanoids.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer Validation**: The empirical demonstration of UMI-only cross-embodiment transfer (Track B) to morphologically unseen robots (JAKA K1, Astribot S1) without target-robot teleoperation highlights the transferability of learned priors. This capability significantly reduces the data collection burden for deploying robots in new environments.
- **Actionable Force Sensing**: The validation of force-modality integration demonstrates the potential for more nuanced, contact-rich manipulation. The UMI workstation's ability to capture tactile signals allows for learning force-aware policies, opening avenues for tasks requiring delicate force control.

The release of a 2,000-hour UMI subset underscores the commitment to open science and fosters community research into the marginal value of data precision, visual augmentation strategies, and execution efficiency. The work collectively contributes to building more capable, adaptable, and deployable generalist robots, paving the way for advanced embodied intelligence applications in real-world scenarios.
