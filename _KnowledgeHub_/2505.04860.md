# D-CODA: Diffusion for Coordinated Dual-Arm Data Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Southern California developed D-CODA, an offline diffusion-based data augmentation framework for eye-in-hand bimanual imitation learning. It synthesizes novel, consistent wrist-camera images and kinematically feasible, coordinated joint-space action labels, improving downstream policy performance in both simulated and real-world bimanual tasks.

## Problem

- Bimanual robotic manipulation tasks have high degrees of freedom and require precise coordination, making them difficult to learn from limited data.
- Collecting diverse, high-quality demonstration data for bimanual systems is costly and labor-intensive.
- Existing data augmentation techniques primarily focus on single-arm manipulation or lack integrated action label supervision suitable for bimanual coordination and consistency across arms.

## Method

- **D-CODA** employs a ==conditional diffusion model== that synthesizes novel, ==viewpoint-consistent wrist-camera images== for both arms, conditioned on source images and camera pose transformations.
- A novel ==camera perturbation sampling== strategy generates constraint-enforced actions, using ==SAM2== for task decomposition (contactless vs. contact-rich states) and ==Dual Annealing== for optimization in contact-rich scenarios.
- Corresponding ==joint-space action labels== are generated using an ==inverse kinematics solver==, with augmented data strategically replacing original states to introduce diversity and mitigate compounding errors.

## Results

- D-CODA outperformed baseline methods (Fine-tuned VISTA, Bimanual DMD, ACT w/o augmentation) on **4 out of 5** simulated bimanual tasks, achieving **77.3%** success on Coordinated Lift Ball (vs. **61.3%** for VISTA) and **44.0%** on Coordinated Lift Tray Easy (vs. **2.6%** for VISTA).
- In real-world experiments across three coordinated tasks (Lift Ball, Lift Drawer, Push Block), D-CODA consistently showed improved success rates, for example achieving **14/20** successes on Lift Drawer compared to **0/20** for Fine-tuned VISTA and **7/20** for ACT without augmentation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the VQ-GAN encoder's training data and the constrained optimization for camera perturbation sampling are crucial for generating high-quality images and feasible, coordinated bimanual actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating relative camera pose information into the diffusion model's cross-attention layers enhances feature representation and improves view consistency across both arms.
> - Decomposing tasks into 'contactless' and 'contact-rich' states allows for tailored perturbation strategies, where applying identical perturbations to both arms in contact-rich states ensures coordinated action generation.
> - Utilizing constrained optimization with inverse kinematics checks guarantees kinematically feasible and functionally coordinated bimanual action labels, preventing invalid or uncoordinated movements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual robotic manipulation is a critical area of robotics, enabling complex tasks that are difficult or impossible for single-arm systems. These tasks often involve high degrees of freedom and require precise coordination between two robotic arms. Recent advancements have highlighted the benefits of eye-in-hand imitation learning, where wrist-mounted cameras provide task-relevant visual information and simplify perception. However, a fundamental challenge in applying learning-based methods to bimanual manipulation is the substantial data requirement for effective generalization. Collecting diverse, high-quality demonstrations for such systems is often costly and labor-intensive.

Traditional data augmentation techniques, widely used in computer vision and visual reinforcement learning, have primarily focused on single-arm robotic manipulation. Extending these methods to bimanual settings introduces several complexities: generating viewpoint-consistent observations across two independent yet coordinated arms, and concurrently producing action labels that are both valid and feasible for bimanual coordination. Previous efforts in visual augmentation for robotics, such as RoVi-Aug and VISTA, have explored novel view generation but often lack integrated action label supervision. Approaches like Diffusion Meets DAgger (DMD) have successfully augmented single-arm eye-in-hand images with action labels using diffusion models, but their direct extension to bimanual tasks remains a challenge due to the complexities of inter-arm coordination.

Complementary approaches, like Dataset Aggregation (DAgger), aim to increase data coverage through supervisor-provided corrective labels but necessitate online environment interactions and the availability of a supervisor, which are not always practical. Other trajectory-level augmentation methods, such as MimicGen, SkillMimicGen, and DexMimicGen, generate full demonstration trajectories but often rely on access to simulation or environment interaction during data generation, limiting their offline applicability.

D-CODA positions itself within this landscape by addressing the specific gap of offline data augmentation for eye-in-hand bimanual imitation learning. It builds upon the foundation of diffusion models for novel view synthesis and action labeling, adapting and extending it to the bimanual domain by incorporating mechanisms to ensure viewpoint consistency across both arms and, crucially, to generate constrained, coordinated action labels. This work aims to provide a scalable solution to the data scarcity problem in bimanual manipulation without requiring online interactions or access to a simulator during data generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable method for offline data augmentation specifically tailored for eye-in-hand bimanual imitation learning. This method, named D-CODA (Diffusion for COordinated Dual-arm Data Augmentation), aims to synthesize novel, diverse, and viewpoint-consistent wrist-camera images for both robotic arms while simultaneously generating corresponding joint-space action labels that are valid and feasible for coordinated bimanual tasks.

The motivation for D-CODA arises from several critical challenges in bimanual robotic manipulation:
- **High-Dimensionality and Coordination Complexity:** Bimanual tasks intrinsically involve a high number of degrees of freedom and demand tight coordination between the two arms. This complexity makes learning effective manipulation policies difficult, especially when relying on limited empirical data.
- **Data Scarcity and Collection Costs:** Learning-based robotic systems, including those for bimanual manipulation, necessitate large and diverse datasets for effective generalization. However, collecting a sufficient quantity of expert demonstrations for bimanual tasks is notably costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive in real-world environments.
- **Limitations of Existing Data Augmentation:** While data augmentation is a proven technique to mitigate data scarcity, prior work has largely focused on single-arm manipulation or visual augmentation without explicit action label generation suitable for bimanual coordination. Extending these approaches to dual-arm systems requires addressing the unique challenges of maintaining consistency across two camera views and ensuring that augmented actions lead to valid and coordinated bimanual behaviors.
- **Need for Offline Augmentation:** Many existing data generation methods for robotics require online interaction with the environment or access to a simulator, which can be computationally expensive or impractical in real-world scenarios. The motivation is to provide an entirely offline solution that can leverage existing demonstration datasets to synthesize new training examples without further interaction or simulation.

By addressing these points, D-CODA seeks to enable more efficient and effective training of bimanual manipulation policies, reducing the reliance on extensive human demonstrations and facilitating greater generalization to novel viewpoints and out-of-distribution states.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

D-CODA is presented as a diffusion-based framework designed for offline data augmentation in eye-in-hand bimanual imitation learning. The methodology consists of a diffusion model for novel-view synthesis, a camera perturbation sampling strategy for coordinated actions, and a procedure for action labeling and dataset construction.

#### 3.1. Diffusion Model for Novel-View Synthesis

The core of D-CODA's image generation component is a conditional diffusion model, an adaptation of previous work by Zhang et al., modified to handle two arms. This model functions as an iterative denoiser, denoted ϵϕ.
- **Input and Output:** The model takes as input two source wrist-camera images (I_l_a and I_r_a) from the left and right arms, respectively, along with their corresponding camera pose transformations (Δp_l and Δp_r). Its objective is to synthesize novel and consistent target wrist-camera images (˜I_l_b and ˜I_r_b).
- **Latent Space Operation:** To manage the high dimensionality of pixel space, both source and target images are first processed through a VQ-GAN autoencoder (V). The diffusion process then operates on the latent representations ({z_l_a, z_l_b, z_r_a, z_r_b}), predicting noise terms (ˆϵ_l and ˆϵ_r) that were added to the target latent vectors (z_l_b,t and z_r_b,t).
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained to minimize the L2 norm between the predicted noise and the actual noise added to the target latents: L = ||ϵ_l - ˆϵ_l||² + ||ϵ_r - ˆϵ_r||².
- **Architecture and Conditioning:** The diffusion model employs a U-Net architecture, incorporating convolution, cross-attention, and self-attention layers. Crucially, camera pose transformations (Δp_l, (Δp_l)⁻¹, Δp_r, (Δp_r)⁻¹) are injected into the cross-attention layers. This conditioning mechanism integrates relative camera pose information between the source and target views, enhancing the quality of feature representations and improving view consistency.
- **Training Data:** The model is trained using a dataset of expert demonstrations (D). Input pairs ({I_l_a, I_l_b, I_r_a, I_r_b}) are constructed by randomly sampling images from a robot trajectory, and the pose transformation (Δp) is computed as the matrix product of the inverse of camera pose 'a' and camera pose 'b'.

#### 3.2. Camera Perturbation Sampling

A key aspect of D-CODA is its novel camera pose sampling procedure, designed to generate constraint-enforced actions that ensure validity and coordination for bimanual tasks.
- **Task Decomposition:** The method utilizes SAM2, a Large Vision Model, to segment gripper masks and track them, thereby decomposing bimanual manipulation tasks into "contactless" (free-space) and "contact-rich" states. Contact detection is performed using depth images (if available) with z-score filtering and depth thresholding, or by inferring contact using the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) when grippers are fully visible in the wrist-camera view.
- **Contactless States:** For states where gripper-to-object contact is not detected, camera perturbations are generated by uniformly sampling random directions for each arm. These perturbations fall within predefined ranges of translation magnitudes and rotations.
- **Contact-Rich States:** When contact is detected, the method formulates camera perturbation sampling as a constrained optimization problem. The central insight is to apply identical perturbations to both arms to ensure coordinated behavior.
 - **Optimization Algorithm:** Dual Annealing, a global optimization algorithm capable of handling constraints, is employed with early stopping.
 - **Decision Variables:** The decision variables (c_trans) represent the normalized translation coordinates (in [-1, 1]) for the camera perturbations.
 - **Cost Function:** The cost function penalizes perturbations that are too small and end-effector poses that are either too close to the table or too close to the other end-effector.
 - **Constraints:** The optimization is subject to constraints on the range of c_trans, minimum distances to the table (d_table) and the other end-effector (d_eff), and critically, a check for the feasibility of the perturbed end-effector poses using an inverse kinematics (IK) solver (Levenvenberg-Marquardt method). This ensures that the generated poses are kinematically reachable.
 - The transformation matrix (T) for the camera perturbation is constructed using the lowest-cost c_trans and an identity rotation matrix.

#### 3.3. Action Labeling and Dataset Construction

Following image synthesis and pose perturbation, corresponding action labels are generated.
- **Perturbed End-Effector Poses:** Perturbed end-effector poses are computed through a series of matrix multiplications involving the camera perturbation transformation (T), the original camera pose (C), and the original end-effector pose (E): C · T · (C)⁻¹ · E.
- **Joint-Space Action Labels:** Since the imitation learning algorithm operates in joint space, an LM inverse kinematics solver is used to convert these perturbed end-effector poses into target joint positions (˜a_t = (˜a_l_t, ˜a_r_t)).
- **Validation and Augmentation:** If the resulting joint configuration is kinematically invalid, the augmentation for that specific state is discarded. Otherwise, the augmented (out-of-distribution) state replaces the original state every 'k' timesteps. This strategic replacement helps to introduce diversity while maintaining a balance with in-distribution data, mitigating the compounding error issue often observed in behavior cloning policies.
- **Final Dataset:** The original dataset (D) is combined with the newly generated augmented dataset (˜D) to form D = D ∪ ˜D, which is then used to train the bimanual manipulation policy (πθ).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

D-CODA was evaluated across a range of simulated and real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

Experiments were conducted on 5 bimanual tasks adapted from PerAct2, including Coordinated Lift Ball, Coordinated Lift Tray Easy, Coordinated Push Box Easy, Dual Push Buttons, and Bimanual Straighten Rope. The evaluation involved 2250 simulation trials. The Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policy was used as the downstream manipulation policy for all methods.

- **Performance Comparison:** D-CODA outperformed Fine-tuned VISTA, Bimanual DMD (an adapted single-arm DMD for bimanual tasks), ACT (more data), and ACT (w/o augment.) on 4 out of 5 tasks.
 - For instance, D-CODA achieved 77.3% success on Coordinated Lift Ball compared to 61.3% for Fine-tuned VISTA and 50.7% for Bimanual DMD.
 - On Coordinated Lift Tray Easy, D-CODA reached 44.0% success (using 2 cameras) versus 2.6% for Fine-tuned VISTA (3 cameras) and 13.3% for Bimanual DMD.
 - D-CODA also showed improved performance on non-coordinated tasks like Dual Push Buttons (53.3%) and Bimanual Straighten Rope (30.7%).
- **Specific Task Performance:**
 - **Coordinated Push Box Easy:** VISTA achieved a higher success rate (76.0%) than D-CODA (58.7%). This was attributed to wrist-camera views offering poor visibility of the entire scene for this task, suggesting that third-person views (used by VISTA) were more informative. However, D-CODA still demonstrated a 20% improvement over the ACT baseline without augmentation.
- **Qualitative Observations:**
 - Both D-CODA and Bimanual DMD, by generating out-of-distribution states, enabled policies to recover from execution failures (e.g., grippers sliding off a box and repositioning).
 - Methods utilizing an overhead camera (like Fine-tuned VISTA) sometimes showed worse performance on high-precision tasks (e.g., Coordinated Lift Tray and Dual Push Buttons) compared to ACT without the overhead camera, suggesting a potential limitation related to the downstream ACT policy's design or how it prioritizes views.
 - Learning from more data (ACT (more data)) did not consistently improve policy performance across all tasks, indicating that data augmentation can offer distinct benefits beyond simply increasing demonstration volume.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

D-CODA was tested on 3 real-world coordinated tasks: Lift Ball, Lift Drawer, and Push Block, using two CB2 UR5 6-DOF robot arms. A total of 300 real-world trials were conducted (20 rollouts per task for each method).

- **Performance Comparison:** D-CODA consistently outperformed baselines, including Fine-tuned VISTA, ACT (w/o augment.), and π0-FAST (w/o augment.).
 - On Lift Ball, D-CODA (ACT downstream) achieved 17/20 successes compared to 12/20 for Fine-tuned VISTA and 15/20 for ACT (w/o augment.). When using π0-FAST, D-CODA achieved 12/20 successes compared to 2/20 for the baseline.
 - For Lift Drawer, D-CODA (ACT downstream) succeeded in 14/20 trials, significantly higher than 0/20 for Fine-tuned VISTA and 7/20 for ACT (w/o augment.).
 - On Push Block, D-CODA performed equally well as Fine-tuned VISTA, achieving 20/20 successes, while ACT (w/o augment.) achieved 15/20.
- **Qualitative Observations:**
 - **Lift Ball:** D-CODA reduced instances of robot arm freezing and enabled more reliable lifting by positioning arms beneath the ball, a strategy not frequently observed in baselines. Failures sometimes occurred due to large action values from the policy, potentially linked to the discontinuous nature of action tokens from augmented states.
 - **Push Block:** D-CODA helped the robot arms get stuck less often when the block was positioned further away.
 - **Lift Drawer:** D-CODA enabled the robot to reach the sides of the drawer more frequently, an intermediate subgoal. Fine-tuned VISTA performed poorly on this task, possibly due to its reliance on third-person views which may overshadow the critical wrist-camera views for precise manipulation.
 - Overall, both ACT and π0-FAST policies showed improved performance with D-CODA augmentation, with ACT exhibiting greater reliability with limited training data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (from Appendix C)

Ablation experiments in simulation highlighted the importance of specific D-CODA components:
- **VQ-GAN Encoder:** Replacing the VQ-GAN encoder with one trained on a different dataset (Open Images instead of RealEstate10K) resulted in lower performance (53.3% on Coordinated Lift Ball vs. 73.3% for D-CODA), accompanied by more image artifacts.
- **Constrained Optimization:** D-CODA without constrained optimization for camera perturbation sampling (i.e., purely random sampling) showed reduced performance (57.3% on Coordinated Lift Ball). This confirms that constraint-enforced sampling is crucial for generating feasible and coordinated bimanual actions, preventing issues like the ball falling due to uncoordinated gripper movements during lifting (as illustrated in Figure 9).

#### 4.4. Generalization Experiment (from Appendix D)

Evaluation of the diffusion model's generalization capabilities to unseen objects and tasks indicated that the model performs best when trained directly on the target dataset (73.3% success in "Train from Scratch"). However, it still achieved reasonable performance in a few-shot setting (60.0% with 10 demonstrations from the target task) and showed degraded performance in a zero-shot setting (44.0%). Image quality correlated with the amount of target demonstration data used for training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The D-CODA framework represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for bimanual systems. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity in Bimanual Manipulation:** D-CODA directly addresses the critical challenge of data scarcity for bimanual tasks. By providing an offline, scalable method for synthesizing diverse and realistic visual data paired with coordinated action labels, it reduces the prohibitive costs and labor associated with collecting large volumes of human demonstrations. This can accelerate the development and deployment of bimanual robotic capabilities.
- **Novelty in Coordinated Dual-Arm Augmentation:** The paper introduces a novel integrated approach that not only generates viewpoint-consistent wrist-camera images for both arms but also simultaneously ensures that the corresponding action labels are valid and conducive to coordinated bimanual manipulation. This goes beyond prior work that largely focused on single-arm systems or lacked robust action labeling for bimanual contexts.
- **Constraint-Enforced Action Generation:** The methodology's inclusion of a perception-based task decomposition (contactless vs. contact-rich states) and subsequent constrained optimization for generating action labels in contact-rich scenarios is a key innovation. This ensures that augmented data supports kinematically feasible and functionally coordinated movements, which is essential for complex bimanual tasks.
- **Improved Policy Generalization and Robustness:** The experimental results demonstrate that D-CODA consistently improves the performance of downstream imitation learning policies (ACT and π0-FAST) in both simulation and real-world settings. This indicates enhanced generalization capabilities and increased robustness to varied initial conditions and potential execution errors, as evidenced by the policies' ability to recover from failures.
- **Facilitating Real-World Applications:** The offline nature of D-CODA makes it highly practical for real-world robotics development, where online interaction can be expensive or hazardous. By enabling effective policy training with more diverse data from existing demonstrations, it can lead to more capable and adaptable bimanual robots in various domains, such as manufacturing, assembly, healthcare, and household tasks.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for future exploration in data augmentation for complex multi-robot or multi-limb systems. It could inspire further research into more sophisticated methods for joint visual and action label synthesis, advanced contact modeling for augmentation, and strategies for handling discontinuities in action spaces introduced by augmented data.

Overall, D-CODA offers a practical and effective solution to a long-standing challenge in bimanual robot learning, paving the way for more generalizable and robust robotic manipulation systems.
