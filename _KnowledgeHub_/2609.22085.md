# SeeQ: Training Generalist Value Functions for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SeeQ is a generalist Q-function built on a 3B PaliGemma vision-language model. It learns values for the currently active subtask rather than the whole task, which shortens temporal-difference credit assignment. It first decodes the active subtask in text, then scores action chunks conditioned on it. Used for best-of-8 steering of a pi0.5 policy, it raises average real-world bimanual success from 35.4% to 66.7%.

## Problem

- Temporal-difference learning becomes unreliable over long horizons, with Q-estimation error growing nearly exponentially, exactly where multi-stage and retry-heavy manipulation tasks most need value guidance.
- Value models trained on Monte Carlo returns or observed progress avoid bootstrapping but tie targets to the behavior policy and its coverage, so they cannot improve much beyond the data collector.
- Prior robot value functions use small networks with little or no pretraining, so they lack the broad priors of generalist policies and need task-specific demonstrations before deployment.

## Method

- ==Subtask-level TD objective==: a sparse reward fires when the active language subtask terminates, and bootstrapping stops at subtask boundaries, turning long-horizon value learning into a chain of short-horizon problems.
- Explicit subtask elicitation: the Q-function autoregressively decodes the active subtask in text under a ==next-token loss== weighted 0.1, then predicts the scalar value conditioned on that decoded subtask.
- ==Best-of-N TD backup== (==TD-BoN==): the bootstrap action chunk is the highest-valued of 8 base-policy samples, matching test-time steering, with the backbone pretrained on roughly 40k RoboCOIN bimanual episodes before per-task finetuning.

## Results

- Best-of-8 steering of pi0.5 raised success from **10 to 22/24** on shirt-hang, **10 to 15/24** on lid-sealing, **9 to 17/24** on grocery-packing, and **5 to 10/24** on LEGO-disassembly.
- On shirt-hang and grocery-packing, task-level Monte Carlo (**9 and 10/24**), task-level TD (**12 and 9/24**) and subtask-level SARSA (**13 and 10/24**) barely beat the base policy, while SeeQ reached **22 and 17/24**.
- Shirt-hang ablations: skipping robot pretraining drops SeeQ to **8/24**, a task-specific ResNet-50 critic reaches **16/24**, removing subtask prediction gives **8/24**, and prediction without conditioning gives **15/24**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The prediction horizon is a first-class design choice: cutting credit assignment at subtask boundaries makes TD learning workable on minute-long real-world tasks where task-level TD and Monte Carlo fail.
> - Making a VLM critic state its intermediate goal in language before scoring acts as a structural regularizer, aligning value resets with subtask transitions and reducing noisy estimates near boundaries.
> - VLM initialization alone can hurt value learning: without diverse robot-action pretraining, the critic overfits to images, barely uses actions, and steers worse than the unsteered base policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Shirt-hang is a precision-heavy sequence. A bimanual robot must remove a hanger, guide it through both sleeves, and return it to the rack. In a long task like this, a small mistake can derail everything that follows. SeeQ changes what the robot's critic is asked to judge. Instead of estimating whether the whole task will eventually succeed, it estimates whether a proposed action advances the step the robot is doing now. In real-robot evaluations, steering a policy this way raised whole-task success.

### 2. A shorter judgment for each step

The authors trace the difficulty to two related failures of imitation policies: errors compound over time, and expert demonstrations contain little recovery behavior. The practical bottleneck is assigning credit across a long sequence from sparse task-level success signals. SeeQ shortens that prediction target while retaining temporal-difference (TD) learning and action ranking.

The critic learns a Q-value, a score estimating expected discounted reward after an action chunk. SeeQ's training labels divide trajectories into spans. The reward for an active span is 1 when that subtask ends and 0 otherwise. The critic bootstraps with TD within the span, but does not bootstrap across a subtask boundary.

At deployment, a 3B PaliGemma vision-language model first generates a natural-language description of the active subtask from the observation and task instruction. It then scores candidate action chunks conditioned on that predicted subtask. During training, annotations supervise subtask generation, and the value head uses the annotated subtask. Deployment needs no external annotations or separate subtask predictor.

SeeQ is pretrained on RoboCOIN's bimanual subset, which has 131 tasks and approximately 40,000 episodes. It is then fine-tuned on each target task. The base policy proposes eight chunks, SeeQ ranks them, and the robot executes the highest-scoring one.

The authors' pipeline. Observations, actions, binary rewards, and subtask labels feed SeeQ pretraining, and downstream task data is used for fine-tuning. At test time, policy-sampled action chunks are scored by Q-values and the highest-scored candidate is chosen.

### 3. What the comparisons test

The strongest measured evidence is real-robot task success. The authors report the mean across four equally sized, 24-trial evaluations rising from 35.4% for the base policies to 66.7% with SeeQ steering (1.88×). On shirt-hang, success rises from 10/24 to 22/24. This is a whole-task success metric comparing the same fine-tuned π₀.₅ policy without and with SeeQ. The shirt-hang data combines autonomous policy behavior with human corrections and recoveries.

The baselines use the same backbone, general robot pretraining, and target-task data. They differ in return target, backup action, and explicit subtask prediction. The key skeptical comparison is task-level TD, which uses the same PaliGemma backbone, RoboCOIN pretraining, target-task data, and best-of-8 backup. On shirt-hang, SeeQ reaches 22/24 successes versus 12/24 for task-level TD. That supports the subtask formulation as a package. It is not an isolated test of subtask prediction, because the task-level baseline also lacks SeeQ's explicit subtask prediction and associated loss. Task-level Monte Carlo and TD offer little improvement over the base policy. Subtask-level SARSA, which backs up using the dataset's next action, gives a more modest improvement.

A separate ablation, limited to shirt-hang, isolates the subtask pieces. The full SeeQ variant reaches 22/24. It reaches 15/24 when the model predicts the subtask but does not condition value estimation on it. Without subtask prediction, the variant gets 8/24, below the 10/24 base policy.

### 4. Local progress, whole tasks

SeeQ does not make shirt-hanging a short task. It replaces one distant success judgment with judgments about the current step, then uses those scores to steer a policy through the full sequence. These results are for four tasks after task-specific fine-tuning, with SeeQ choosing among action chunks proposed by a fixed policy. They are not zero-shot transfer or a new policy learned directly from SeeQ. The comparisons support the combined method but do not establish that horizon reduction alone explains the gains. Local subtask progress can also conflict with full-task success, and a mistaken predicted subtask can misdirect the critic.
