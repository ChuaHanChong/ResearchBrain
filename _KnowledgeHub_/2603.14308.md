# Load-Aware Locomotion Control for Humanoid Robots in Industrial Transportation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huazhong University of Science and Technology developed a load-aware locomotion framework for humanoid robots that enables stable and robust load-carrying and manipulation in industrial settings. The framework integrates a decoupled reinforcement learning policy for lower-body control with a perception-driven kinematic controller for the upper body, utilizing height-conditioned offsets, kinematics-based references, and a history-based state estimator to manage dynamic coupling and partial observability. It successfully demonstrated real-world box-handling tasks on a Tiangong 2.0 Pro humanoid robot, transferring policies directly from simulation.

## Problem

- Achieving stable and robust locomotion for humanoid robots performing industrial load-carrying and manipulation tasks under varying payloads and complex upper-body movements.
- Managing strong dynamic coupling between the upper and lower body, shifts in the robot's center of mass, and time-varying disturbances induced by manipulation.
- Addressing partial observability where payload properties are unknown or change during operation, posing challenges for both model-based and existing reinforcement learning approaches.

## Results

- The proposed method, using a height-conditioned joint-space offset and kinematics-based reference, achieved the fastest and most stable training convergence among compared ablation variants.
- The history-based state estimator demonstrated accurate real-time tracking of base linear velocities (**MSE ~10⁻³**) and base height (**MSE ~10⁻⁶**) in simulation.
- Policies trained exclusively in simulation successfully enabled a Tiangong 2.0 Pro humanoid robot to perform stable load-carrying (**5 kg** box) and manipulation tasks in real-world industrial environments without any fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating kinematics-based locomotion references and height-conditioned joint offsets as structured motion priors drastically improves RL policy convergence speed and stability.
> - Conditioning the lower-body policy on base height via an inverse kinematics offset allows the policy to learn more refined and regular residual corrections, rather than absolute joint targets.
> - A history-based state estimator effectively mitigates partial observability by accurately inferring base states and implicitly learning a compact latent representation of dynamic disturbances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are increasingly being developed for industrial applications, particularly in load-carrying transportation tasks such as material transfer and box handling. A significant challenge in these scenarios is achieving stable and robust locomotion when the robot is subjected to varying payloads and complex upper-body manipulation motions. This is due to dynamic coupling between the upper and lower body, shifts in the center of mass, and time-varying disturbances, compounded by partial observability of environmental and payload properties.

Traditionally, load-carrying locomotion has been addressed using model-based whole-body control and optimization frameworks. While these methods can account for manipulation-induced dynamics by explicitly modeling payload effects, their performance is sensitive to modeling accuracy, contact estimation, and payload identification, often requiring specific tuning. In dynamic industrial settings where payload properties may be unknown or change during operation, purely model-based approaches face limitations in maintaining robust performance.

Recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) have shown promise for humanoid locomotion due to their inherent tolerance to modeling errors and contact uncertainties. Within the RL paradigm for humanoid loco-manipulation, two main approaches have emerged. The first involves decoupled control, where RL generates lower-body locomotion, and upper-body motions are handled by kinematic or optimization-based controllers. Prior work in this area often simplifies payload effects by treating the load as a static mass or struggles to allow the lower-body policy to adapt online to manipulation-induced payload variations. Many decoupled approaches also lack precise feedback mechanisms for autonomous squatting and lifting height regulation, often relying on teleoperation for such tasks.

The second paradigm directly trains whole-body motions using RL, aiming to implicitly capture inter-limb coordination. While these methods demonstrate versatility, they frequently do not explicitly model or provide structured mechanisms for handling payload variability or regulating posture and height under changing load conditions, thereby limiting robustness in industrial environments.

This paper positions its work as addressing the limitations of existing approaches by focusing on both dynamic upper–lower body coupling and partial observability within a unified locomotion framework. It adopts a decoupled yet tightly coordinated architecture, employing RL exclusively for lower-body locomotion while leveraging a perception-driven kinematic controller for upper-body manipulation. Distinct from prior methods, it introduces a kinematics-based locomotion reference conditioned on commanded velocity and base height to provide structured motion priors and a history-based state estimator to infer base states and learn a compact latent representation of disturbances, enhancing robustness to varying payloads and manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a load-aware locomotion control framework that enables humanoid robots to perform stable and robust load-carrying transportation tasks in industrial environments. This objective is motivated by several challenges inherent in such tasks:

1. **Dynamic Coupling:** Industrial box-handling tasks involve tight coordination between locomotion and upper-body manipulation. As the robot squats, lifts, carries, and walks, the payload's mass, location, and distribution can change significantly. These variations induce strong dynamic coupling between the upper and lower body, leading to time-varying disturbances, shifts in the robot's center of mass, and changes in ground reaction forces. Maintaining balance and stability under these dynamically changing conditions is a complex control problem.
2. **Partial Observability:** In realistic industrial settings, payload properties (e.g., mass, exact location) may be unknown, vary across tasks, or change during operation. Furthermore, manipulation-induced disturbances are often difficult to directly measure or model. Traditional model-based approaches struggle with these uncertainties, as their performance is highly sensitive to modeling accuracy and payload identification. Learning-based methods face the challenge of inferring these unobserved dynamics from limited sensory inputs.
3. **Limitations of Existing RL Approaches:**
 - **Decoupled RL:** While simpler, many decoupled RL strategies for loco-manipulation either treat the payload as a static mass, overlooking the dynamic effects of arm movements, or lack mechanisms for the lower-body policy to adapt online to manipulation-induced disturbances. They also often struggle with precise autonomous base height regulation during tasks like squatting and lifting.
 - **Whole-body RL:** Although implicitly capturing inter-limb coordination, current whole-body RL approaches rarely explicitly model or robustly handle payload variability and posture/height regulation under changing load conditions, limiting their robustness in diverse industrial scenarios.
4. **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A consistent challenge in robot learning is bridging the gap between policies trained in simulation and their performance in the real world. Policies need to be robust to real-world uncertainties, including contact variability, hardware imperfections, and unmodeled dynamics, without extensive real-world fine-tuning.

To address these motivations, the paper aims to:
- Develop a decoupled yet coordinated loco-manipulation architecture where a reinforcement learning policy specifically controls lower-body locomotion, while upper-body manipulation is handled by a perception-driven kinematic controller.
- Integrate a kinematics-based locomotion reference and a height-conditioned joint-space offset to guide the lower-body RL policy, promoting efficient learning, accurate height regulation, and smoother ground contact.
- Design a history-based state estimation scheme to explicitly infer crucial base states (linear velocity, height) and implicitly capture load- and manipulation-induced disturbances through a compact latent representation, thereby mitigating partial observability.
- Enable robust sim-to-real transfer of the learned policy to a full-size humanoid robot in industrial box-handling tasks without fine-tuning, leveraging domain randomization and an upper-body curriculum during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named "load-aware locomotion framework," is based on a decoupled yet tightly coordinated loco-manipulation architecture. Lower-body locomotion is governed by a reinforcement learning (RL) policy, while upper-body manipulation is executed through a perception-driven kinematic controller. The framework is trained entirely in simulation and demonstrated on a full-size humanoid robot without fine-tuning.

**A. Decoupled Yet Coordinated Architecture:**
The system receives a high-level locomotion command `c_t = [v*x, v*y, ω*z, z*b]` specifying desired planar linear velocity, yaw rate, and commanded base height. The lower-body RL policy outputs residual joint-space position targets for the 12 lower-limb joints, which are then tracked by joint-level PD controllers. Upper-body motions, involving 14 joints, are generated independently via inverse kinematics (IK) from target poses `T_box` obtained through real-time object pose estimation. Although the locomotion policy does not directly control arm joints, upper-body states are included in its observation space, allowing it to implicitly react to manipulation-induced disturbances and payload-dependent dynamics.

**B. Height-Conditioned Offset Control with Kinematic Reference:**
To enhance training stability, base height tracking, and contact smoothness, the framework employs a height-conditioned offset control augmented with a kinematics-based locomotion reference.
- **Height-Conditioned Joint Offset:** The commanded base height `z*b` is mapped to a nominal lower-body joint configuration `q_off_leg(t)` using inverse kinematics. This offset provides a physically consistent vertical posture and varies continuously with the height command, but does not encode stepping or gait patterns.
- **Residual Actions:** The RL policy outputs residual joint-space actions `Δq_leg(t)`, which are added to `q_off_leg(t)` to form the final joint position commands `q_cmd_leg(t)`. This approach allows the policy to focus on learning local corrections around a nominal posture rather than absolute joint targets across varying height conditions.
- **Kinematics-Based Locomotion Reference:** A reference trajectory `q_ref_leg(t)` is generated from commanded planar velocity, yaw rate, and base height. This involves defining a normalized gait phase `φ_t` and generating foot-end trajectories for each leg. During the swing phase, vertical foot motion is parameterized by a cubic polynomial, with peak swing clearance modulated for smooth ground contact. During the stance phase, foot height is consistent with `z*b`. These reference joint trajectories are *not* directly tracked by the controller during deployment; instead, they are used *only* as auxiliary reward terms during training to guide exploration and improve convergence.

**C. History-Based State Estimation:**
To address partial observability arising from unmodeled payload dynamics and manipulation-induced disturbances, a history-based state estimator is introduced.
- **Observation Stacking:** A fixed-length window of raw proprioceptive observations `O_t = [o_raw_(t-T+1)..., o_raw_t]` (e.g., IMU orientation, angular velocity, joint positions/velocities) is stacked and fed into a lightweight neural encoder.
- **State Prediction:** The encoder predicts the base linear velocity `v_b,t`, base height `z_b,t`, and a compact, `ℓ_2`-normalized latent representation `z_t` that captures residual dynamic variations.
- **Policy Integration:** The inferred states `[v_b,t, z_b,t, z_t]` are incorporated into the locomotion policy's observation space, enabling the controller to adapt to unobserved dynamics.
- **Training Supervision:** During training, the estimator is supervised using privileged simulation information. Regression losses (`L_vel`, `L_height`) are applied to the predicted base linear velocity and height against ground truth. An additional prototype-based swap prediction objective (`L_aux`) encourages informative latent representations. During deployment, the estimator operates solely on onboard sensory inputs.

**D. End-to-End Deep Reinforcement Learning:**
The lower-body locomotion controller is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in an end-to-end manner.
- **Reward Design:** The total reward `r_t = Σ μ_i r_i` is a weighted sum of individual reward terms, designed to balance command tracking (linear/angular velocity, base height), dynamic stability (orientation stabilization, base acceleration), contact quality (consistency, slip, stumble, force), motion regularity (joint imitation, action smoothness), and energy efficiency (torque/joint velocity/acceleration penalties). Exponentially shaped rewards are used. Kinematics-based reference terms (joint imitation, foot height tracking) are active only during training.
- **Upper-Body Curriculum:** To manage the impact of upper-body disturbances during training, a curriculum strategy is employed. Random upper-body joint motions are applied as disturbances with an intensity controlled by a scalar ratio `ρ ∈`, which increases progressively as the policy learns stable locomotion. This allows the policy to first acquire basic locomotion skills before adapting to stronger whole-body perturbations.
- **Domain Randomization:** To facilitate sim-to-real transfer, extensive domain randomization is applied during training. This includes varying ground friction and restitution, applying external pushes, perturbing robot inertial parameters (base/hand mass offset, CoM offset, link mass scaling), randomizing joint properties (position offset, friction, armature), and control parameters (PD factors, torque output scaling, action/observation latency). This encourages the policy to learn robust feedback strategies rather than simulator-specific dynamics.

**E. Upper-Body Control and Deployment:**
In real-world deployment, upper-body manipulation is performed by a perception-driven kinematic control pipeline, independent of the locomotion policy.
- **Object Perception:** A head-mounted depth camera provides inputs for a multi-object detection and 6D pose estimation system. A YOLO-based segmentation network identifies box instances, and a FoundationPose-based estimator determines the 6D pose of a selected target box.
- **Manipulation Execution:** Based on the estimated 6D pose, a predefined grasp or pre-grasp pose is generated. Inverse kinematics computes the corresponding arm joint trajectories, which are then executed by position-based controllers for grasping, lifting, and placement. This loop runs concurrently with the learned locomotion controller, allowing the locomotion policy to adapt to the resulting dynamic effects through its observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from simulation and real-world experiments, evaluating the proposed framework on a full-size humanoid robot, Tiangong 2.0 Pro.

**A. Effect of Kinematic Reference with Residual Control:**
An ablation study compared three locomotion controllers, differing in their joint offset and use of kinematic references, while maintaining consistent policy architecture, training, and domain randomization.
- **Baseline (Fixed Offset w/o Reference):** Used a fixed nominal joint configuration and no kinematics-based reference. This approach exhibited slow convergence, large reward fluctuations, and difficulty in satisfying base-height tracking.
- **Ours Fixed Offset:** Augmented the baseline with kinematics-based reference trajectories used as auxiliary reward terms during training, but kept the joint offset fixed. This showed improved reward growth and reduced variance compared to the baseline, indicating better guidance for early-stage learning.
- **Ours (Proposed Method):** Replaced the fixed offset with a height-conditioned joint-space offset computed via inverse kinematics, in addition to using kinematics-based reference rewards. This method achieved the fastest and most stable convergence, with the longest and most consistent episode lengths (Fig. 3). This indicates that providing a physically plausible nominal configuration allows the policy to focus on learning residual corrections, enhancing sample efficiency and robustness.
- **Action Distribution Analysis:** The baseline controller exhibited wide and irregular action distributions, reflecting reliance on policy outputs for global posture and height compensation. The "Ours Fixed Offset" method showed slight regularization, but the spread remained comparable. In contrast, the "Ours" method produced more structured, joint-dependent action distributions concentrated around specific operating regions with reduced bias and improved symmetry (Fig. 4). This suggests that the height-conditioned IK offset effectively absorbs posture and height variations, allowing the policy to learn more refined, regular, and coordinated residual corrections.

**B. History-Based State Estimation Analysis:**
The performance of the history-based state estimator was evaluated in simulation against ground-truth data.
- **Accurate Tracking:** The estimator's predictions for base linear velocities (v_x, v_y) closely tracked ground-truth signals in both magnitude and phase, even under dynamically varying velocity commands. The mean squared errors (MSE) were low (2.15 × 10^-3 for v_x and 8.86 × 10^-4 for v_y) (Fig. 5a, 5b), indicating accurate reconstruction of planar motion.
- **High Precision Height Estimation:** Base height estimation achieved an MSE on the order of 10^-6 (Fig. 5c), demonstrating high precision and near-perfect alignment with ground truth. This level of accuracy is critical for height-conditioned locomotion and squatting performance.
- **Compensation for Partial Observability:** The results indicate that the history-based estimator effectively compensates for partial observability by providing stable, low-variance state feedback for policy execution under dynamic payload conditions, without observable drift or delayed convergence.

**C. Real-World Loco-Manipulation Experiments:**
The proposed framework was deployed on the Tiangong 2.0 Pro humanoid robot in a reconstructed industrial factory environment for box transportation tasks. Policies were trained entirely in simulation and transferred to the real robot without fine-tuning.
- **Stable Loco-Manipulation:** The robot consistently maintained stable walking, smooth foot-ground contact, and accurate height regulation across different task phases, despite the decoupled control architecture and the dynamic nature of the tasks.
- **Industrial Transportation Scenarios:**
 - **Without Depalletizing:** The robot successfully transitioned from unloaded walking to load-carrying locomotion with a 5 kg box, squatted to a commanded height, placed the box onto a rack while maintaining a crouched posture, and then stood up (Fig. 6a-c).
 - **With Depalletizing:** The robot performed a full depalletizing pipeline, including box removal from a stacked pallet, walking while carrying the 5 kg payload, placing the box at a designated location, and subsequently resuming unloaded locomotion (Fig. 6d-f).
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The success of these real-world experiments without fine-tuning validates the effectiveness of the kinematics-based reference with height-conditioned residual control and history-based state estimation in enabling robust sim-to-real transfer for humanoid loco-manipulation in realistic industrial environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, particularly for industrial applications involving load-carrying and manipulation. The proposed load-aware locomotion framework addresses several critical challenges that have traditionally limited the robustness and autonomy of humanoids in dynamic industrial settings.

**Key areas of significance include:**

- **Robustness to Dynamic Coupling and Partial Observability:** By explicitly addressing dynamic coupling through observation design and mitigating partial observability via a history-based state estimator, the framework enhances the robot's ability to maintain stability and execute tasks under varying and unknown payload conditions and upper-body motions. This is crucial for real-world industrial deployment where payload properties are often inconsistent or dynamic.
- **Improved Learning Efficiency and Performance:** The integration of a kinematics-based locomotion reference and a height-conditioned joint-space offset with residual reinforcement learning demonstrates a more structured approach to policy learning. This design choice leads to faster training convergence, reduced variance in learning, and more stable and accurate height regulation. By providing motion priors, it allows the RL policy to focus on learning fine-grained corrections rather than discovering basic movement patterns from scratch, thereby improving sample efficiency and overall control quality.
- **Successful Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The ability to train policies entirely in simulation and deploy them on a real full-size humanoid robot without fine-tuning is a notable achievement. This significantly reduces the time, cost, and safety concerns associated with real-world robot training. The extensive domain randomization and upper-body curriculum strategies employed are key enablers for this robust transfer.
- **Practical Applicability in Industrial Settings:** The framework's validation through real-world box-handling tasks in a factory-like environment demonstrates its practical utility. Humanoid robots equipped with this control framework could autonomously perform a range of industrial transportation tasks, such as material handling, depalletizing, and workstation-to-workstation delivery, under realistic and challenging conditions. This has direct implications for enhancing automation and flexibility in manufacturing and logistics.
- **Decoupled yet Coordinated Control Paradigm:** The approach offers a pragmatic solution by decoupling complex manipulation planning from core locomotion control, while still maintaining dynamic awareness through careful observation design. This modularity simplifies controller design and debugging, allowing specialized components (e.g., vision-based manipulation) to be developed independently while ensuring overall system stability.

The potential impact of this work extends to the broader development of intelligent humanoid systems capable of operating in unstructured and dynamic human environments. By laying a foundation for robust load-aware locomotion, it opens avenues for humanoids to undertake more complex and physically demanding tasks beyond simple walking, further bridging the gap between research prototypes and practical industrial applications. Future research directions, such as adaptive reference generation and tighter whole-body integration, suggest continued advancements towards even greater versatility and autonomy.
