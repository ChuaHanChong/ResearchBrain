# EgoMI: Learning Active Vision and Whole-Body Manipulation from Egocentric Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called EgoMI enables robots to learn complex active vision and whole-body manipulation skills directly from egocentric human demonstrations, achieving robust zero-shot transfer to real robots. The system, which captures synchronized head and hand movements and incorporates a spatial memory mechanism, demonstrated superior performance in visual search and memory-intensive tasks compared to policies lacking active head control or memory.

## Problem

- The 'embodiment gap' in active perception, where robots struggle to mimic human-like dynamic head and eye movements for visual search and occlusion resolution from egocentric demonstrations.
- Context loss in robot policies due to rapid egocentric viewpoint changes, making it difficult for standard approaches to maintain awareness of objects temporarily out of view.
- Challenges in achieving scalable and data-efficient robot skill transfer from human demonstrations without extensive domain adaptation or robot-specific data collection.

## Method

- Developed the EgoMI system to capture synchronized egocentric human head and hand trajectories, along with proprioception and camera data, compatible with robotic execution.
- Employed a semi-humanoid robot with a fully actuated camera head to enable faithful whole-body motion retargeting from human demonstrations.
- Integrated Spatial-Aware Robust Keyframe Selection (SPARKS), a training-free spatial memory mechanism, to selectively incorporate relevant past visual context during dynamic head movements.

## Results

- In 'Tabletop Search,' the active head policy (29D) succeeded in 36/40 trials, outperforming the wrist camera-only policy (29/40) and the fixed head-cam policy (2/20).
- For 'Shelf Search,' the active head policy achieved 35/40 successful points, while policies without active head control achieved 0/40.
- The SPARKS-augmented policy successfully completed 31/40 memory tasks, showing improved performance over the single-timestep policy (21/40) by leveraging spatial memory for object recall.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active head actuation and corresponding egocentric observations are essential for robots to effectively perform visual search and manipulation across wide workspaces.
> - Merely providing head-camera input is insufficient; active control over head movements is necessary for precise object interaction and placement accuracy.
> - A spatial memory mechanism is critical for maintaining visual context during rapid head movements, enabling policies to reason about temporarily occluded or off-screen objects.
