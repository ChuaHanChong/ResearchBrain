# ReForce: Learning Force-aware Retargeting for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> REFORCE turns kinematically retargeted human demonstrations into dexterous-hand robot actions that reproduce intended contact. A learned residual force tracker, trained on large-scale simulated hand-object trajectories, adapts joint commands online using fingertip force feedback, needing no digital twin, and lowers force-tracking error and missing-contact failures on real contact-rich tasks.

## Problem

- Kinematic-only retargeting aligns human joint positions to robot hands but ignores contact dynamics, producing coarse motion matches that fail on force-sensitive tasks.
- Force-aware retargeting via reinforcement learning or sampling in simulation needs a digital twin, an accurate simulation-ready object model unavailable for arbitrary objects.
- Prior force-aware methods optimize each trajectory against a recorded dataset offline, so they cannot run during online teleoperation, the main source of quality demonstration data.

## Method

- General ==force tracker==: a per-finger ==MLP== policy predicting bounded joint-command residuals from measured state, upstream motion-force reference, and target-measurement error, trained on large-scale simulated hand-object interaction data.
- Post-hoc motion-and-contact targets averaged over a future window supervise context while the action label stays the demonstrated one-step joint update, exposing the policy to contact deviations via structured data.
- ==ACT==-based ==action-chunking== reference policy with separate motion and force prediction branches conditioned on joint history and task phase, composed with **REFORCE** via ==temporal ensembling== for deployment.

## Results

- On real paper-cup grasping replay, REFORCE achieves lowest force-tracking error versus replay and admittance baselines, **0.247N** vs **0.309N** and **0.280N** for side grasp, **0.474N** vs **0.736N** and **0.619N** for top grasp.
- With learned references on paper-cup grasping, REFORCE reaches **70%** force-safe success, **0.124N** tracking error, zero severe missing-contact failures, and **2.61** mean active fingers versus **0.17** for reference policy alone.
- Simulation ablation over **14,605** held-out episodes shows adding pose reference cuts tracking error **32-35%** over state-force-target input; combined augmentation mixture reaches lowest error **0.0379N**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Force-aware residual correction generalizes across arbitrary objects without a digital twin, since the tracker learns from randomized simulated interactions rather than per-object optimization.
> - Low measured contact force is ambiguous, it can mean safe execution or missing contact, so contact-engagement metrics like active-finger count matter alongside force-tracking error.
> - Structured recovery augmentations, such as stalling references and drifting poses toward low-force states, teach a controller to handle deployment-time contact deviations better than naive noise alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

**Challenges in Dexterous Manipulation Retargeting**
Human demonstrations represent a substantial source of data for learning dexterous manipulation skills in robotics. However, translating these demonstrations into effective robot actions presents a significant challenge due to the "embodiment gap." This gap arises from the inherent differences in morphology, actuation, sensing, and compliance between human and robotic hands. Most conventional retargeting algorithms primarily focus on kinematic mapping, aligning human joint positions with their robot counterparts. This approach often overlooks critical aspects such as geometry and contact dynamics, leading to robot motions that are kinematically similar but functionally coarse. Such kinematic retargeting often serves as a preliminary step, requiring further refinement through robot-specific data collection, reinforcement learning, or direct human teleoperation to bridge the performance gap.

**Limitations of Existing Force-Aware Approaches**
Manipulation is fundamentally governed by force, which dictates how a hand interacts with an object, determines grasp stability, and prevents outcomes like crushing or slipping. Recognizing this, recent research has explored force-aware retargeting methods. These approaches typically involve tracking human-object demonstrations and then employing techniques such as reinforcement learning or simulation-based sampling to identify robot trajectories that reproduce the observed contact forces. While these methods represent an advancement, they are characterized by two primary limitations:
1. **Dependency on Digital Twins:** They often necessitate a "digital twin," meaning a precise, simulation-ready model of the object, coupled with accurate hand and object pose information. Such detailed models are frequently unavailable for arbitrary objects, restricting the generalizability of these methods.
2. **Offline Optimization:** These methods typically optimize trajectories against pre-recorded datasets, precluding their use in online teleoperation scenarios. Online teleoperation is a critical mode for generating high-quality robotic manipulation data and for human-in-the-loop control, rendering the offline constraint a significant practical drawback.

**Positioning of REFORCE**
REFORCE is introduced as a novel force-aware retargeting method designed to overcome the limitations of prior approaches. It aims to transform human motion and force demonstrations into robot actions that replicate the intended contact, without requiring a digital twin and while supporting online adaptation. The work builds upon existing research in learning from human demonstration by focusing on the adaptation of human-guided trajectories when robot-side fingertip force deviates from the intended contact. It distinguishes itself from purely kinematic dexterous retargeting by explicitly studying how a robot hand should adapt a human-guided motion and force reference under real-time contact feedback. Furthermore, while drawing inspiration from learning-based tactile manipulation, REFORCE frames this fast local feedback as a learned retargeting controller that can be integrated with multiple sources of human-guided trajectories, moving beyond hand-designed force-to-motion mappings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable force-aware retargeting method for dexterous manipulation. This method aims to enable robots to reproduce the intended contact from human demonstrations, even for force-sensitive tasks, by adaptively responding to real-time force feedback.

The motivation for REFORCE stems from several critical challenges and limitations identified in the broader field of dexterous manipulation:

- **Addressing the Embodiment Gap in Contact-Rich Tasks:** Traditional kinematic retargeting methods are insufficient for contact-rich manipulation because they fail to account for the dynamic interactions and forces that dictate task success (e.g., maintaining a grasp without crushing an object). The primary motivation is to bridge this embodiment gap by making retargeting force-aware, directly addressing how the robot hand interacts physically with the object.

- **Overcoming Prior Limitations of Force-Aware Methods:**
 - **Eliminating Digital Twin Dependence:** Current force-aware retargeting often requires a precise simulation model of the object and accurate pose tracking, which are impractical for arbitrary, unknown objects. REFORCE seeks to remove this dependency, broadening the applicability of force-aware manipulation.
 - **Enabling Online Teleoperation:** Previous force-aware methods are typically designed for offline trajectory optimization. A key motivation for REFORCE is to enable real-time, online force-aware teleoperation, allowing for immediate adaptation during human-guided control and facilitating the collection of high-quality interaction data.

- **Developing a General Force Tracking Paradigm:** The authors aim to establish a novel retargeting paradigm termed "general force tracking." This paradigm involves adapting kinematically retargeted actions using online force feedback, thereby creating a closed-loop system that is responsive and adaptive.

- **Facilitating Zero-Shot Transfer to Force-Sensitive Tasks:** The goal is for REFORCE to enable zero-shot transfer of skills learned from human demonstrations to tasks requiring precise force control, such as grasping delicate objects like paper cups or operating tools like tongs.

To achieve these objectives, the research outlines three specific contributions:
1. The proposal of the general force tracking paradigm, which adapts kinematic retargeting using online force feedback and requires no digital twin.
2. The creation of a large-scale simulated dataset of hand-object trajectories, paired with joint positions and contact forces, used to train a single, general force tracker.
3. Empirical evaluation in both simulation and real-world hardware, demonstrating improved force tracking capabilities in both online teleoperation and offline reference settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The REFORCE methodology integrates a closed-loop force-aware retargeting controller with a mechanism for generating motion and force references from human demonstrations.

**Problem Formulation**
The REFORCE policy functions as a closed-loop controller that adapts an incoming motion-and-contact reference using the robot's current hand state and real-time fingertip force feedback. At each control step `t`, the system observes the hand's joint configuration (`q_obs_t`) and measured fingertip normal forces (`F_t`). An upstream source provides a reference motion-and-contact state (`s_star_t`), comprising a target joint configuration (`q_star_t`) and a target contact force (`F_star_t`). An error signal (`e_t`) is computed based on the difference between the observed and target states for both joint positions and forces. All input quantities are standardized, with forces undergoing a log-transformation prior to standardization to accommodate their concentrated distribution near zero and long positive tail. The REFORCE policy, parameterized by `theta`, then predicts a bounded joint-command update (`delta_q_cmd_t`), which is accumulated and clipped to the hardware's joint limits to produce the next commanded joint configuration (`q_cmd_t+1`). The system is designed for a 12-DoF hand, with `D_F = 5` corresponding to the normal forces on the thumb, index, middle, ring, and pinky fingers.

**Force-Aware Policy Learning**
The REFORCE policy is trained through behavior cloning on a large-scale dataset of simulated hand-object interaction trajectories. These trajectories include variations in hand configurations, motion, and object contact properties.
- **Post-hoc Targets:** For training, motion (`q_star_t`) and force (`F_star_t`) targets are defined as the average of future observed states and forces over a target window of `K_t` steps. These future-window targets provide contextual information about a nearby motion-and-contact state that is expected to be reached.
- **Supervised Action:** The policy is supervised to predict the one-step joint update observed in the demonstration, `delta_q_demo_t = q_t+1 - q_t`.
- **Behavior-Cloning Objective:** The policy is trained using a mean-squared error loss (`L_ReForce`). This loss is first averaged over the joints within each finger, then equally averaged across all five finger groups.
- **Training Data Robustness:** The simulated training data are designed to expose the controller to a variety of contact deviations, including variations in contact timing, force response, missing contact, and residual force, preparing it for real-world deployment challenges. Observation perturbations such as Gaussian joint noise, Gaussian force noise, and per-finger force dropout are applied.

**Learning from Human Demonstration and Composition**
REFORCE is designed to operate with human-guided references generated either through online teleoperation or an offline learned policy.
- **Human Demonstration Retargeting:** For generating reference trajectories, human hand motion is retargeted to robot-hand joint configurations (`q_ref_t`). Simultaneously, calibrated tactile measurements from human fingertip FSR sensors provide corresponding per-finger force references (`F_ref_t`). These form a paired reference trajectory `(q_ref_t, F_ref_t)`.
- **Action-Chunking Policy (ACT-style):** For scenarios where offline references are desired, an ACT-style policy is trained. This policy predicts a short chunk of future robot-space motion and force references. Its inputs include a history of robot joint configurations (`Q_t`) and a task phase (`phi_t`). Crucially, this ACT policy does not receive real-time fingertip force feedback (`F_t`) as an input; its predicted force chunk (`hat_F_ref`) represents a phase-dependent contact intent learned from the demonstrations.
- **Composition with REFORCE:** During deployment, whether through teleoperation or an ACT-style policy, the generated `q_star_t` and `F_star_t` (motion and force references) are fed into REFORCE. REFORCE then uses the robot's *online measured fingertip force* (`F_t`) as feedback to adapt the robot's command, tracking the predicted references while reacting to actual contact conditions.

**Experimental Setup**
The real-world experiments utilized a UFACTORY xArm equipped with a 12-DoF XHand, which provides fingertip tactile sensor data (`F_t`). For human teleoperation, a Quest controller captured wrist pose, a Manus glove captured human hand pose, and five fingertip FSR sensors provided the human force reference (`F_star_t`). Both human FSR and robot tactile measurements were calibrated to Newtons for consistent physical scaling. For training the ACT-style reference policy, demonstrations included wrist tracking via an AprilTag and RealSense L515 camera, alongside glove and FSR data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of REFORCE was conducted in both simulation and real-world environments, focusing on force-sensitive tasks such as paper-cup grasping and tongs manipulation. Performance was assessed against direct replay and admittance control baselines.

**Real-World Force Tracking with Replayed References**
When provided with identical, replayed reference trajectories for paper-cup grasping (both side and top grasps), REFORCE demonstrated superior force-tracking performance compared to both direct replay and admittance control.
- **Paper Cup (Side Grasp):** REFORCE achieved a mean absolute force error of 0.247 N, while direct replay showed 0.309 N and admittance control 0.280 N.
- **Paper Cup (Top Grasp):** REFORCE yielded 0.474 N error, compared to 0.736 N for direct replay and 0.619 N for admittance control.
Qualitatively, direct replay often failed to reproduce intended contact due to lack of reactivity, and admittance control could retain residual force even after the reference decreased. REFORCE exhibited a more balanced approach, effectively establishing contact and releasing force by adapting commands based on measured interaction.

**Real-World Execution with Learned References**
The study also evaluated REFORCE when combined with a learned action-chunking policy for generating motion-and-force references.
- **Paper-Cup Grasping:**
 - REFORCE achieved the highest force-safe success rate (70%), significantly outperforming the reference policy alone (30%) and the reference policy with admittance control (60%). Force-safe success requires task completion, forces below a specific threshold (1.0 N for paper cup), and no severe missing-contact failures.
 - It exhibited the lowest force-tracking error (0.124 N) compared to the reference policy (0.812 N) and admittance (0.683 N).
 - REFORCE recorded no severe missing-contact failures (0/10 trials), a substantial improvement over the reference policy (7/10) and admittance (1/10).
 - It maintained the largest mean number of active fingers (2.61), indicating superior contact engagement.
- **Tongs Manipulation:**
 - For tongs manipulation, admittance control achieved the highest force-safe success (90%) and the lowest force-tracking error (0.419 N). REFORCE showed 54.5% force-safe success and 0.441 N tracking error.
 - REFORCE still demonstrated the largest mean number of active fingers (1.62) and reduced severe missing-contact failures relative to the reference policy (1/11 for REFORCE vs. 5/10 for reference policy alone), though admittance performed better on this specific metric (1/10).
 - While REFORCE improved tracking error over the reference policy, its four over-force trials (out of 11) contributed to a lower force-safe success rate compared to admittance control.

These real-world results indicate that REFORCE consistently improved contact engagement and reduced missing-contact failures. Its advantage in force tracking over admittance control was task-dependent, showing a stronger benefit for paper-cup grasping.

**Simulation Ablation of Training Data and Policy Inputs**
A simulation study investigated the impact of training-data composition and policy input representation on force-tracking performance.
- **Training Data Composition:**
 - The "Base" distribution comprised nominal simulated demonstrations with standard observation perturbations.
 - "Reference-stall augmentation" (20% of samples) partially stalled pose references for force-active fingers, exposing the policy to stale motion references.
 - "Pose-drift augmentation" (10% of samples) constructed synthetic recovery samples from low-force states by perturbing observed joints towards limits.
 - Both structured augmentations individually improved performance. The combination of 70% Base, 20% reference-stall, and 10% pose-drift samples yielded the best overall force-tracking error.
- **Policy Inputs:**
 - Adding the pose reference (`q_star_t`) to the basic state and force target inputs (`q_obs_t, F_t, F_star_t`) reduced force-tracking error by approximately 32–35%.
 - Further including explicit motion and force error features (`q_star_t - q_obs_t, F_star_t - F_t`) provided additional improvements.
 - The lowest overall force-tracking error (0.0379 ± 0.0006 N) was achieved with the combined data augmentation and the richest input representation (state, force target, pose reference, and error features).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

REFORCE represents a notable advancement in the field of dexterous manipulation by introducing a force-aware retargeting method that leverages online fingertip force feedback to adapt human-guided motion and contact references. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation:** By explicitly integrating force feedback, REFORCE facilitates more reliable and precise control in contact-rich manipulation tasks. This capability is critical for achieving delicate interactions, stable grasps, and nuanced object handling that traditional kinematic approaches often fail to deliver. The demonstrated improvements in force tracking and multi-finger contact engagement are direct evidence of this enhanced capability.

- **Bridging the Embodiment Gap More Effectively:** The method offers a more complete solution to the long-standing challenge of the embodiment gap in human-to-robot skill transfer. By considering both motion and force, REFORCE allows robots to reproduce not just the geometric configuration but also the crucial physical interaction intent of human demonstrations, leading to more functional and robust robot behaviors.

- **Increased Practicality and Generalizability:** A key impact is the elimination of the requirement for a "digital twin" (a precise 3D model of the object) in many scenarios. This significantly broadens the applicability of force-aware retargeting to arbitrary and previously unknown objects, moving beyond lab settings to more unstructured real-world environments.

- **Enabling Online Force-Aware Teleoperation:** The support for online force-aware teleoperation is particularly impactful. This capability is crucial for both collecting high-quality human demonstrations—as it allows the human to directly feel and correct robot-object interactions—and for real-time human-in-the-loop control, where immediate force feedback allows for adaptive and precise guidance.

- **Modular and Composable Framework:** REFORCE functions as a force-aware execution layer that can be composed with various upstream reference sources, including direct teleoperation and learned policies. This modularity offers flexibility for future robotic systems, allowing the force-adaptive layer to enhance any kinematically derived or learned trajectory.

**Identified Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations that also suggest avenues for future research:
1. **Limited Reference Policy Adaptability:** The current action-chunking (ACT-style) reference policy relies solely on joint-state history and task phase, lacking the ability to adapt references based on observed task states. Integrating visual observations could significantly enhance the policy's capacity for task-aware motion-and-force reference generation, allowing for more intelligent and context-dependent planning.
2. **Scope of Tactile Feedback:** REFORCE currently utilizes only normal fingertip force. Incorporating richer tactile signals, such as shear force, slip detection, and contact distribution, could provide a more comprehensive understanding of physical interaction. This expanded tactile information has the potential to further improve the robot's dexterity, robustness, and ability to handle complex contact conditions.
