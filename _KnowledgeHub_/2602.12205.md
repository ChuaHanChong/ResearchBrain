# DeepGen 1.0: A Lightweight Unified Multimodal Model for Advancing Image Generation and Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepGen 1.0 introduces a lightweight 5-billion-parameter unified multimodal model for image generation and editing, achieving performance competitive with or superior to much larger models across diverse tasks, including general generation, reasoning, and text rendering. The model integrates an efficient VLM-DiT architecture with a novel Stacked Channel Bridging module and a three-stage data-centric training strategy to enable comprehensive capabilities within a compact parameter budget.

## Problem

- High-performing unified multimodal models for image generation and editing typically require massive parameter scales (often >10B), extensive training data, and significant computational resources, leading to prohibitive costs and large deployment footprints.
- Many existing systems necessitate separate models for generation and editing, further increasing total parameter count and operational complexity.
- Prior lightweight unified models have generally underperformed across diverse tasks, reinforcing the perception that comprehensive multimodal capabilities are intrinsically tied to large model sizes.

## Method

- **DeepGen 1.0** uses a lightweight ==VLM-DiT architecture== comprising a 3B VLM and a 2B DiT for tight integration within a total **5B parameter budget**.
- ==Stacked Channel Bridging (SCB)== is introduced as a lightweight alignment module that integrates features from multiple VLM layers and learnable ==think tokens== to capture fine-grained details and enhance reasoning.
- A ==three-stage data-centric training strategy==: alignment pre-training, joint supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning with ==MR-GRPO== and an auxiliary SFT loss for capability preservation.

## Results

- DeepGen 1.0 matched or surpassed much larger open-source models (Qwen-Image, LongCat-Image, Hunyuan-Image 3.0), achieving **0.87 on GenEval** and **87.90 on DPG-Bench**.
- Outperformed the 80B HunyuanImage 3.0 by **+28%** on WISE (**0.73**) and surpassed closed-source GPT-Image-1 on UniREditBench (**77.5 SFT**).
- Demonstrated strong text rendering: improved Word Accuracy on CVTG-2K from **0.6605 to 0.7533** with RL training, while maintaining high CLIPScore (**0.8278**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-performance unified multimodal image generation and editing are achievable within a significantly compact parameter budget (5B), challenging the conventional scale-performance paradigm.
> - Intelligent architectural designs like Stacked Channel Bridging and learnable 'think tokens' can effectively integrate multi-layer VLM features and facilitate implicit reasoning for fine-grained control.
> - A progressive, data-centric three-stage training strategy, particularly RL with an auxiliary SFT loss, is crucial for developing omni-capabilities, aligning with human preferences, and preventing capability degradation in lightweight models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal AI, particularly for image generation and editing, has seen rapid advancements through the integration of vision-language models (VLMs) with diffusion models. This paradigm aims to achieve semantically accurate generation and precise editing based on complex instructions. Closed-source systems like GPT-Image-1 and Nano Banana, alongside open-source models such as BAGEL, HunyuanImage 3.0, Qwen-Image, and LongCat-Image, have demonstrated the potential of unified models in enhancing generative performance.

However, a predominant characteristic of these high-performing unified models is their substantial parameter scale, often exceeding 10 billion parameters (e.g., Qwen-Image at 27B, HunyuanImage 3.0 at 80B, BAGEL at 14B, Emu3.5 at 34B). This necessitates massive training datasets (billions of samples) and considerable computational resources, leading to prohibitive training costs and large deployment footprints. Furthermore, some systems require separate models for generation and editing, effectively doubling their parameter count and resource demands.

In contrast, existing small-scale unified models have generally exhibited underperformance across diverse tasks, reinforcing a perception that comprehensive multimodal capabilities are inherently tied to large model sizes. DeepGen 1.0 challenges this perception by observing that model performance does not always scale monotonically with parameter count. For instance, Lumina-DiMOO (8B) has shown to surpass BAGEL (14B) on certain benchmarks. This work positions itself as an effort to demonstrate that intelligent architectural design and optimized training strategies can enable a lightweight model to achieve comprehensive capabilities competitive with or superior to much larger counterparts. By focusing on efficiency and performance within a compact parameter budget, DeepGen 1.0 aims to provide an accessible and high-performance alternative, thereby contributing to the democratization of unified multimodal research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind DeepGen 1.0 are rooted in addressing the limitations of current unified multimodal models for image generation and editing.

The core motivation arises from the observation that prevailing high-performing unified models demand massive parameter scales, typically exceeding 10 billion, and necessitate extensive training data and computational resources. This results in prohibitively high training costs and deployment footprints, limiting their accessibility and broader research exploration. For example, models like Qwen-Image-Edit (27B) and HunyuanImage 3.0 (80B) represent significant resource investments. Additionally, many systems require distinct models for generation and editing, further escalating the total parameter count and operational complexity.

While the need for lightweight alternatives is recognized, existing small-scale unified models have historically demonstrated underperformance across various tasks. This has contributed to a prevailing perception that compact models intrinsically lack the capacity for comprehensive multimodal generation and editing tasks.

DeepGen 1.0 is motivated by an alternative perspective: that model performance does not necessarily scale monotonically with size. Empirical evidence from existing benchmarks, such as Lumina-DiMOO (8B) outperforming BAGEL (14B) on DPG-Bench, suggests that larger scale alone does not guarantee stronger performance. This indicates a potential for synergistic architecture design and data-centric training strategies to enable lightweight models to achieve competitive or superior capabilities.

Therefore, the key objective of DeepGen 1.0 is to develop a lightweight unified multimodal model, specifically with a total of 5 billion parameters, that can integrate comprehensive capabilities. These capabilities include general generation, reasoning generation, text rendering, general editing, and reasoning editing, all within a single framework. The model aims to achieve performance competitive with or surpassing much larger existing models, challenging the established notion that massive scaling is the sole path to high-performance multimodal generation. A secondary objective is to open-source the training code, model weights, and datasets to foster an efficient and high-performance alternative, thereby democratizing unified multimodal research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepGen 1.0 employs a VLM-DiT (Vision-Language Model – Diffusion Transformer) architecture designed for tight integration within a compact 5B parameter budget. The methodology involves a specialized architecture and a three-stage, data-centric training strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The model consists of a 3B VLM (Qwen-2.5-VL) as the understanding and reasoning backbone and a 2B DiT (SD3.5-Medium) as the generative backbone. The total parameter count is approximately 5B.

- **Dual-Branch Visual Encoding:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder captures high-level semantics for the VLM, while a VAE encoder extracts compressed latent features for the DiT.
- **Multimodal Conditioning:** Multimodal conditions derived from the VLM, along with reference-image VAE latents, are concatenated with the target image’s noise tokens to form a single DiT input sequence, enabling self-attention over both conditioning and generation signals.

#### 3.2. Stacked Channel Bridging (SCB)

SCB is introduced as a lightweight alignment module to overcome limitations of prior methods (e.g., single final-layer VLM conditioning losing fine-grained details, or deep fusion methods increasing complexity). SCB integrates features from multiple VLM layers while preserving detail and enhancing reasoning. It operates in three steps:

1. **Think Token Injection:** A fixed set of 128 learnable "think tokens" are injected into the VLM input sequence. These tokens interact with textual and visual inputs across all VLM layers via self-attention, functioning as an implicit chain of thought to summarize hidden representations and extract knowledge.
2. **Layer Selection:** Six hidden states are selected uniformly across the low, mid, and high levels of the VLM. This strategy aims to capture a balance of varying-granularity visual features, semantics, and the reasoning information embedded in the think token positions, rather than relying on a single layer which can be prone to specific biases or detail loss.
3. **Feature Fusion:** The selected multi-layer hidden states, now encoding both multimodal features and think token representations, are stacked along the channel dimension. This concatenated feature tensor is then projected to match the DiT input width using a lightweight two-layer MLP. Finally, these aligned features are fed into a Transformer-encoder-based connector for deep information fusion, producing a robust conditional input for the DiT.

#### 3.3. Data-Centric Training Strategy

The training process is structured into three progressive stages, emphasizing simplicity and data efficiency, using approximately 50 million samples in total.

1. **Stage 1: Alignment Pre-training:**
 - **Objective:** To establish alignment between the VLM and the DiT.
 - **Trainable Components:** Only the SCB connector and the 128 learnable think tokens are optimized, while all other model parameters are frozen.
 - **Data:** Large-scale general text-to-image pairs and image editing triplets (e.g., text-to-image-2M, LAION-Aesthetic-6M, NHR-Edit, GPT-Image-Edit). The stage utilized approximately 35M generation samples and 6.6M editing samples.
 - **Duration:** 200,000 iterations.

2. **Stage 2: Joint Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** To strengthen instruction-following capabilities and image synthesis quality, including visual fidelity, semantic alignment, and knowledge-aware reasoning, fostering omni-capabilities.
 - **Trainable Components:** The entire model is unfrozen, with the DiT optimized directly and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) applied to the VLM to mitigate potential degradation of multimodal comprehension during joint optimization.
 - **Data:** A diverse, high-quality mixture of tasks including general generation and editing, reasoning-based generation and editing, and text rendering (e.g., BLIP-3o, ShareGPT-4o-Image, UniReason-T2I, UniReason-Edit, and text rendering datasets). This stage utilized approximately 11M generation samples, 6.6M editing samples, 150K reasoning generation, 100K reasoning editing, and 560K text rendering samples.
 - **Duration:** 400,000 iterations.

3. **Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning (RL) with MR-GRPO:**
 - **Objective:** To further align the model with human preferences and improve generation quality, while preventing capability degradation.
 - **Framework:** MR-GRPO (Mixture of Reward-Group Relative Policy Optimization), a variant of Pref-GRPO adapted for flow matching models.
 - **Key Enhancements:**
 - **Noise-Preserving Stochastic Sampling:** Adopted to produce cleaner samples and more accurate reward signals by ensuring noise levels are consistent with the flow matching scheduler.
 - **Decoupled Advantage Normalization:** Implemented to better preserve the granularity of multiple reward signals by normalizing each reward independently within a group before aggregation.
 - **Mixture of Reward Functions:** Three complementary reward functions are used: (1) a VLM-based pairwise preference reward for image-text alignment and visual quality, (2) an OCR reward for text rendering accuracy, and (3) a CLIP similarity score for overall semantic consistency. Reward weights are adjusted based on prompt categories.
 - **Auxiliary Supervised Diffusion Loss (L_SFT):** Introduced alongside KL regularization. This loss is computed on the high-quality SFT dataset and continuously anchors the model to its supervised fine-tuning distribution. This is critical for preventing capability degradation (e.g., in reasoning tasks) during prolonged RL training, as KL regularization alone was found insufficient. The total objective is a weighted sum of the GRPO loss and the auxiliary SFT loss.
 - **Data:** RL training prompts from general text-to-image and text-rendering categories, with auxiliary SFT data from an independent curated corpus.
 - **Duration:** 1,500 steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DeepGen 1.0, despite its lightweight 5B parameter count, demonstrates performance competitive with or superior to much larger unified multimodal models across a diverse set of generation and editing benchmarks. The model consistently ranks among the top performers in open-source comparisons and, in several instances, surpasses even closed-source systems.

#### 4.1. Performance of General Generation and Editing

- **General Generation:**
 - On **GenEval**, DeepGen 1.0 achieved a score of 0.87, matching leading models such as Qwen-Image and LongCat-Image while utilizing significantly fewer parameters.
 - On **DPG-Bench**, it attained 87.90, ranking second among all evaluated models and demonstrating robust long-horizon instruction following.
 - On the comprehensive **UniGenBench**, DeepGen 1.0 scored 75.74, placing second and outperforming numerous larger open-source baselines including LongCat-Image, Z-Image-Turbo, and Hunyuan-Image 3.0.
- **General Editing:**
 - Across **ImgEdit** and **GEdit-EN**, DeepGen 1.0 ranked third after RL training, outperforming strong unified baselines such as Mammoth2, BAGEL, and OmniGen2. Its performance approached that of larger, specialized editing models like Qwen-Image-Edit and LongCat-Image-Edit.
- **Impact of RL:** Reinforcement Learning consistently yielded further performance gains, enhancing both general capabilities and text rendering performance, as shown by the UniGenBench evaluation curves during RL training.

#### 4.2. Performance of Reasoning-based Generation and Editing

DeepGen 1.0 exhibited advanced reasoning capabilities despite its compact size.

- **Reasoning-based Generation:**
 - On **WISE**, DeepGen 1.0 achieved an overall score of 0.73, making it the best-performing open-source model. It notably outperformed the 80B HunyuanImage 3.0 by a 28% margin (0.73 vs. 0.57) and surpassed other strong baselines like BAGEL (which uses explicit CoT). Improvements were consistent across various knowledge domains including cultural, temporal, spatial, and natural scientific reasoning.
 - On **T2I-CoREBench**, the model scored 46.5, ranking among the top open-source models and matching or slightly surpassing substantially larger baselines such as Qwen-Image and Hunyuan-Image 3.0 across diverse reasoning types.
- **Reasoning-based Editing:**
 - On **RISE**, DeepGen 1.0 achieved a leading overall score of 13.3 with SFT, demonstrating robust world-knowledge-grounded editing.
 - On **UniREditBench**, it attained 77.5 (SFT) and 75.7 (RL), significantly outperforming other open-source baselines and exceeding the closed-source GPT-Image-1 overall.

#### 4.3. Performance of Text Rendering

DeepGen 1.0 demonstrated strong text-rendering capabilities.

- **CVTG-2K:** RL training significantly improved Word Accuracy from 0.6605 to 0.7533, indicating enhanced character-level correctness. Simultaneously, DeepGen 1.0 maintained the highest CLIPScore (0.8278) among open-source models, suggesting that textual fidelity improvements did not compromise overall semantic alignment.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Architecture Design:**
 - Removing **Stacked Channel Bridging (SCB)** consistently degraded performance across all benchmarks (e.g., DPGBench -1.5, GEdit -0.37, WISE -0.02, RISE -0.7), confirming its effectiveness in aggregating multi-layer VLM features.
 - Removing **Think Tokens** resulted in the most pronounced regression on reasoning-intensive benchmarks (WISE -0.04, RISE -1.6), indicating their role as an implicit reasoning buffer.
 - Disabling **VLM activation** during fine-tuning also harmed performance (e.g., GenEval -0.01, GEdit -0.19, WISE -0.01, RISE -0.4), suggesting modest VLM fine-tuning improves task alignment.
- **RL Settings:**
 - The **auxiliary SFT loss** was found critical for maintaining generation quality; its removal led to performance degradation after approximately 300 steps and lower text rendering scores.
 - Removing **KL regularization** resulted in lower overall scores and suggested that unconstrained policy updates could lead to forgetting.
 - The **reward-wise advantage normalization** proved essential for stable multi-reward optimization; its absence caused performance to lag after approximately 600 steps, particularly affecting text generation scores.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepGen 1.0 contributes to the field of multimodal AI by demonstrating that high-performance unified image generation and editing are achievable within a significantly compact parameter budget. The model's key significance and potential impacts are:

- **Challenging the Scale-Performance Paradigm:** DeepGen 1.0 directly challenges the prevalent notion that comprehensive multimodal capabilities necessitate massive parameter scales and extensive computational resources. By achieving competitive or superior performance to models 3× to 16× its size, it provides empirical evidence that intelligent architectural design (SCB) and progressive training strategies can circumvent the need for prohibitive scaling.
- **Democratization of Unified Multimodal Research:** The model's lightweight nature (5B parameters) significantly reduces the computational burden for training and deployment. This allows researchers and developers with access to more modest hardware to engage in advanced multimodal AI research, fostering broader participation and innovation. The open-sourcing of the model weights, training and evaluation code, and key datasets further facilitates this democratization.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Sustainability:** By delivering high performance with fewer parameters and requiring less training data (approximately 50M samples compared to billions for larger models), DeepGen 1.0 offers a more resource-efficient and environmentally sustainable approach to developing advanced AI systems. This could lead to a broader adoption of multimodal AI in real-world applications where computational resources are constrained.
- **Comprehensive Task Integration:** The model successfully unifies general generation, reasoning-intensive generation, text rendering, general editing, and reasoning-based editing into a single framework. This comprehensive capability in a compact model could simplify development pipelines and enable more versatile AI applications.
- **Advancements in Reasoning and Fine-Grained Control:** The introduction of Stacked Channel Bridging (SCB) and learnable "think tokens" provides a robust mechanism for deep semantic understanding, reasoning, and fine-grained control, which are critical for complex instruction following in generation and editing tasks. Its leading performance in reasoning benchmarks highlights a practical approach to integrating world knowledge into generative models.
