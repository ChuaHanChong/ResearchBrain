# rStar2-Agent: Agentic Reasoning Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research introduces rStar2-Agent, an agentic reasoning model that enables large language models to "think smarter" by autonomously interacting with Python coding tools. The 14B parameter model achieves state-of-the-art mathematical reasoning on AIME24, surpassing much larger models while utilizing significantly less computational resources and producing more concise responses.

## Problem

- Existing large language model (LLM) reasoning, primarily relying on extended Chain-of-Thought (CoT), often fails to self-correct effectively for complex problems requiring precise calculations or creative shifts in reasoning.
- Integrating external tools and developing agentic capabilities in LLMs has been hindered by "environment noise" from tool execution errors and the substantial infrastructure demands of large-scale agentic reinforcement learning.
- Current outcome-only reward systems in agentic RL do not effectively penalize inefficient intermediate behaviors, potentially leading to lengthy and low-quality reasoning trajectories.

## Method

- Introduces rStar2-Agent, an ==agentic paradigm== that trains LLMs to autonomously interact with Python coding tools in a multi-turn, interactive process.
- Develops ==GRPO-RoC== (Group Relative Policy Optimization with Resampling on Correct), an agentic RL algorithm that mitigates environment noise and promotes high-quality reasoning by selectively sampling successful trajectories.
- Designs a custom, large-scale RL infrastructure featuring a reliable, ==high-throughput code execution environment== and a dynamic, load-balanced rollout scheduler to manage computational demands and asynchronous tool calls.
- Employs an efficient ==multi-stage training recipe==, starting with lightweight instruction-following SFT, followed by three incremental RL stages on a curated math dataset.

## Results

- The rStar2-Agent-14B model achieved **80.6%** accuracy on AIME24, outperforming models like OpenAI o3-mini (**79.6%**) and DeepSeek-R1 (671B, **79.8%**).
- This performance was attained with a **14B** parameter model using only **510** RL training steps over one week on **64** MI300X GPUs, demonstrating superior computational efficiency compared to larger models.
- The model produced significantly more concise responses, averaging **9339.7** tokens on AIME24, and exhibited strong generalization, improving GPQA-Diamond accuracy from **42.1%** to **60.9%** despite math-only agentic RL training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agentic reinforcement learning, when coupled with external tools and robust feedback mechanisms, can dramatically improve an LLM's reasoning abilities, even starting from a model with minimal explicit reasoning pre-training.
> - Careful management of "environment noise" through asymmetric trajectory sampling (GRPO-RoC) is crucial for stable and efficient agentic RL, enabling the model to learn from high-quality reasoning paths rather than distracting errors.
> - A specialized, high-throughput infrastructure with isolated tool execution and dynamic load balancing is essential for scaling agentic RL and effectively managing the complexity of multi-turn interactions and concurrent tool calls.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses the evolving landscape of large language model (LLM) reasoning, marking a significant step beyond current paradigms. Historically, advancements in complex reasoning have largely been driven by "test-time scaling," primarily through extending Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. Models like OpenAI's o-series, DeepSeek-R1, and Gemini-2.5 have demonstrated that encouraging models to "think longer" by generating more intermediate steps, often optimized through large-scale reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), can yield substantial performance gains.

However, the authors identify a fundamental limitation of this "thinking longer" approach: for highly complex problems prone to subtle errors or requiring creative shifts in reasoning, internal self-reflection alone often proves insufficient. Models may fail to detect mistakes or self-correct effectively if their initial reasoning path is flawed. This paper positions itself as a critical evolution, aiming to enable models to "think smarter" by developing advanced cognitive abilities that autonomously leverage external tools, validate intermediate steps, and adapt reasoning based on environmental feedback. This shift represents a move from purely introspective reasoning to interactive, agentic reasoning, where the LLM acts as an intelligent agent within a dedicated environment.

The choice of Python coding tools and an interpreter as the primary environment is strategic. Python's versatility, combined with libraries like Numpy, Scipy, and SymPy, significantly broadens the model's action space, allowing for precise calculation, exploration of alternative solutions, and robust verification of intermediate results – capabilities that complement or surpass internal CoT.

The work also tackles the practical challenges that have hindered the widespread adoption and scaling of agentic reinforcement learning. Previous attempts to integrate tools often suffer from "environment noise" (e.g., syntactically incorrect code, logical errors leading to non-task-related error messages) and the inherent inefficiencies of large-scale RL infrastructure. rStar2-Agent directly confronts these issues with novel algorithmic and infrastructural solutions, positioning it as a leading effort in making agentic RL a viable and scalable approach for enhancing LLM reasoning. By achieving frontier-level performance with a significantly smaller model (14B) compared to much larger counterparts (e.g., 671B DeepSeek-R1), this research also contributes to the broader goal of developing more efficient and accessible high-performance AI models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable large language models to "think smarter" rather than merely "thinking longer"** when tackling complex reasoning problems, particularly in mathematics. This involves moving beyond sequential Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to an agentic paradigm where models can autonomously interact with external tools and environments.

The core motivations driving this objective are:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Long CoT:** Existing methods primarily focus on generating longer, more detailed CoT sequences. While effective to a degree, this approach struggles with problems requiring precise calculations, external validation, or significant shifts in reasoning strategy, where internal self-correction often fails.
- **Developing Advanced Cognitive Abilities:** The authors aim to cultivate human-like problem-solving behaviors in LLMs, such as:
 - Knowing *when* to invoke the right tools (e.g., Python for precise calculations).
 - Generating logically correct and functional code.
 - Reflecting critically on tool execution feedback to guide subsequent reasoning steps.
- **Leveraging Verifiable External Environments:** Python coding tools and interpreters offer a valuable, verifiable environment. They expand the model's action space and provide objective feedback signals (e.g., code output, error messages) that can steer the model toward more robust and accurate reasoning paths, effectively complementing internal reflection.
- **Addressing Scalability Challenges of Agentic RL:** Effectively scaling agentic reinforcement learning presents significant hurdles:
 - **Environment Noise:** Tool errors (syntax, logic, timeouts) generate feedback that can be distracting or misleading, causing models to waste tokens on debugging rather than progressing reasoning. Current outcome-only reward systems often fail to penalize these intermediate errors.
 - **Infrastructure Demands:** Agentic rollouts involve numerous concurrent tool calls and multi-turn interactions, demanding a reliable, high-throughput, and isolated execution environment, as well as efficient handling of imbalanced computational loads across GPUs.
- **Achieving Frontier Performance with Minimal Compute:** A secondary but significant motivation is to demonstrate that advanced reasoning capabilities can be instilled in relatively smaller models (14B) with considerably less computational cost and training time compared to training much larger models or relying solely on extensive CoT prompting.

To achieve these objectives, the paper introduces three key innovations:
1. An efficient and reliable large-scale RL infrastructure.
2. GRPO-RoC, an agentic RL algorithm designed to handle environment noise.
3. An efficient multi-stage agent training recipe.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The rStar2-Agent approach is built upon a sophisticated blend of agentic reinforcement learning, a custom-designed infrastructure, and an optimized training recipe.

#### 3.1. Agentic Reinforcement Learning Methodology

The core of rStar2-Agent's methodology lies in training LLMs to natively and adaptively use Python coding tools within a dedicated execution environment.

- **Multi-turn Rollout:** Unlike traditional RL rollouts that generate a complete trajectory in one go, rStar2-Agent employs a multi-turn, interactive process. The model generates a reasoning step, potentially invokes a tool, receives feedback from the environment, and then uses this feedback to inform its next reasoning turn. This iterative process continues until a final answer is produced or a maximum number of turns is reached. This design mimics human problem-solving, where intermediate steps are verified and refined.
- **Tool Call Format:** A standardized, structured JSON format is used for tool invocation (`<tool_call>{"name": ..., "arguments": {"code": ..., "input": ...}}</tool_call>`). This approach ensures parsing clarity, extends to diverse tools, and aligns with common LLM API protocols, offering better extensibility than markdown-style code blocks.
- **Environment Feedback:** The system meticulously processes tool calls. If parsing fails, an error message is returned. Otherwise, the code is executed in an isolated environment, yielding one of four responses: successful execution with/without output, execution error, or timeout. All feedback is wrapped in `<tool_response>` tags and fed back to the model, allowing it to learn from both successes and failures.
- **Prompt Template:** A specific prompt template guides the model, instructing it to enclose reasoning in `<reason>` tags, the final answer in `<answer>` tags (with `\boxed{}` for extraction), and explicitly detailing available tools and their structured JSON call format.

#### 3.2. End-to-End Agentic Reinforcement Learning (GRPO-RoC)

The chosen RL algorithm, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), is adapted to address the unique challenges of agentic learning, resulting in **GRPO-RoC (Group Relative Policy Optimization with Resampling on Correct)**.

- **Preliminary: GRPO:** GRPO optimizes the policy by sampling a group of rollout trajectories for each question and using their relative rewards (normalized by the group's mean and standard deviation) to compute advantages. This relative reward structure helps stabilize training. The authors make key modifications to GRPO: removing the KL divergence penalty (to encourage exploration of novel tool-augmented reasoning), adopting Clip-Higher (relaxing the importance sampling ratio's upper bound), and eliminating the entropy loss term (to prevent training instability).
- **Challenges in Agentic RL:** The paper identifies two main challenges:
 - **Inherent Environment Noises:** Code generation inevitably leads to errors (syntactic, logical, timeouts), generating noisy feedback that can distract the model from reasoning.
 - **Outcome-only Reward Limitations:** While effective against reward hacking, binary outcome-only rewards (1 for correct final answer, 0 otherwise) do not penalize undesirable *intermediate* behaviors, such as inefficient tool usage or repeated errors, if the final answer is correct. This can lead to lengthy, low-quality trajectories.
- **GRPO-RoC: Resample on Correct (RoC) Strategy:** To address these challenges while retaining outcome-only rewards, RoC introduces an asymmetric sampling strategy:
 - **Oversampling and Downsampling:** Instead of `G` rollouts, `2G` rollouts are initially generated.
 - **Negative Samples (Preserving Diversity):** For incorrect rollouts, a subset (e.g., half) is uniformly sampled to retain diverse failure modes as informative negative signals.
 - **Positive Samples (Filtering and Prioritizing Quality):** For correct rollouts, a subset (e.g., half) is sampled, but with a preference for higher-quality traces. Each correct trajectory is scored based on:
 - **Tool Call Error Ratio (`p_err`):** Proportion of failed tool calls (errors, timeouts). Trajectories without tool calls are assigned `p_err = 0.5` to encourage tool use.
 - **Format Violation Penalty (`p_format`):** Penalizes issues like missing or multiple `<answer>` tags, or `<reason>` blocks after `<answer>`.
 - The total penalty `p_total = p_err + p_format` is used to sample positive rollouts inversely proportionally to `p_total`, prioritizing cleaner, more efficient successes. This ensures the model learns from high-quality demonstrations of tool use and reasoning.

#### 3.3. Large-Scale Agentic RL Infrastructure

To support the massive computational demands of agentic RL, a custom infrastructure is built on VERL v0.2 and SGLang.

- **Reliable High-Throughput Code Environment:**
 - **Isolation and Distribution:** A dedicated environment service, distributed across CPU cores of the GPU cluster, isolates tool execution from the main training process, preventing stalls and security risks from unpredictable LLM-generated code.
 - **Concurrent Tool Calls:** A centralized task queue with send workers groups up to 64 tool calls into batches, dispatched to worker nodes. Each worker node has a task scheduler and a pool of execution workers to handle calls dynamically. This architecture enables handling up to 45K concurrent tool calls per step with low latency (0.3 seconds average).
 - **Answer Correctness Verification Offloading:** CPU-intensive answer verification is offloaded to this service, running asynchronously to prevent blocking GPU rollouts.
- **Load-Balanced Rollout Scheduler:**
 - **Addressing Static Allocation Issues:** Traditional static allocation leads to imbalanced GPU workloads, idle time (waiting for longest rollouts or synchronous tool calls), and KV cache overflows (requiring recomputation).
 - **Dynamic Allocation:** The proposed scheduler dynamically allocates rollout requests based on the *available KV cache capacity* of each GPU. GPUs execute assigned rollouts independently. Tool calls are dispatched *asynchronously* to the environment service immediately upon generation. As a GPU frees up KV cache space, the scheduler assigns new requests in real-time, significantly improving GPU utilization and rollout efficiency.

#### 3.4. Training Recipe

The training recipe focuses on achieving frontier performance with minimal compute for the Qwen3-14B-base model.

- **Non-Reasoning Cold Start for Instruction Following:** Unlike prior work that includes heavy reasoning data in SFT, rStar2-Agent begins with a lightweight SFT stage focused *only* on general instruction-following, JSON formatting, and basic coding tool usage. This prevents SFT overfitting, keeps initial responses short, and allows RL to primarily cultivate reasoning from the base model's pre-trained capabilities.
- **RL Data Curation:** A dataset of 42K high-quality math problems is curated from DAPO, Art of Problem Solving (AoPS) forums, and Project Euler. A critical rule is **integer-only answers** to ensure reliable, unambiguous verification (as algebraic equivalence checking is difficult and prone to errors).
- **Multi-Stage RL Training:**
 - **Stage 1 (Concise Training at 8K Response Length):** Trains on the full 42K dataset with an 8K token limit. This shorter length, combined with GRPO-RoC, encourages efficient reasoning, as the model learns to self-adjust and remain concise despite temporary high clipping ratios.
 - **Stage 2 (Extending to 12K Response Length):** When performance plateaus at 8K, the maximum length is increased to 12K, allowing for further reasoning exploration and performance gains.
 - **Stage 3 (Focused Training on Difficult Problems):** The latest policy is used to filter out problems already solved perfectly, yielding 17.3K harder problems. Training on this focused subset (with reset optimizer states) pushes the model to frontier performance.
 - This multi-stage approach, with incrementally increasing length and difficulty, proves highly efficient, completing 510 RL steps on 64 MI300X GPUs in one week.

- **Unsuccessful Attempts and Lessons:**
 - **Overlong Filtering:** Discarding truncated rollouts without negative reward (as suggested by DAPO) was found to be detrimental, leading to increased overlong outputs. Penalizing truncation provides a useful signal for conciseness.
 - **N-gram Repetition Detection:** Finer-grained scoring of intermediate repetitions in correct rollouts proved ineffective and harmful. It's difficult to distinguish undesirable repetition from legitimate reasoning (e.g., verifying results with similar tool calls but different inputs). This reinforced the decision for a minimal, answer-only outcome reward and addressing quality via RoC.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The rStar2-Agent-14B model demonstrates remarkable performance, efficiency, and generalization capabilities, achieving frontier-level reasoning with a significantly smaller model size and lower computational cost.

#### 4.1. Competitive Mathematical Reasoning

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** rStar2-Agent-14B achieves competitive mathematical reasoning performance, rivaling or surpassing much larger frontier LLMs. On AIME24, it scores 80.6% accuracy, outperforming OpenAI o3-mini (79.6%), DeepSeek-R1 (671B, 79.8%), and Claude Opus 4.0 (76.0%). It also achieves strong results on AIME25 (69.8%) and HMMT25 (52.7%), demonstrating consistent strength across challenging benchmarks.
- **Efficiency in Training:** The model reaches these frontier-level scores in only 510 RL training steps over one week, utilizing 64 MI300X GPUs. This highlights an exceptionally cost-effective training approach compared to large-scale efforts that involve extensive data and compute budgets for larger models.
- **Effectiveness of Agentic RL without Heavy Reasoning SFT:** rStar2-Agent-14B starts with a non-reasoning SFT, primarily for tool formatting and instruction following, and no explicit reasoning-specific SFT. Despite this, GRPO-RoC boosts its performance from near-zero (3.3% on AIME24) to 80.6%, demonstrating the power of agentic RL as a standalone driver of advanced reasoning, even outperforming models like DeepSeek-R1-Zero (671B, 71.0%) that also lack reasoning SFT.
- **Per-RL Stage Improvement:** Performance consistently improves across the three RL stages: Stage 1 (8K length) yields substantial gains (3.3% to 72.1% on AIME24), Stage 2 (12K length) further boosts it to 77.0%, and Stage 3 (focused on harder problems) brings it to the final 80.6%.

#### 4.2. Smarter Reasoning with Fewer Tokens

- **Conciseness and Efficiency:** rStar2-Agent-14B not only achieves high reasoning accuracy but does so with significantly shorter responses. On AIME24, its average response length is 9339.7 tokens, compared to DeepSeek-R1-Zero (14246.8), QWQ-32B (11868.4), and Qwen3-14B (14747.6). This indicates that the model has learned to use coding tools more intelligently and efficiently, leading to more concise and effective reasoning trajectories.

#### 4.3. Strong Generalization Performance

- **Cross-Domain Transfer:** Despite being trained solely on math data for agentic RL, rStar2-Agent-14B demonstrates strong generalization capabilities. On GPQA-Diamond (science reasoning), it improves accuracy from 42.1% (SFT baseline) to 60.9%, surpassing DeepSeek-V3 (59.1%) by 1.8%. This suggests that the agentic reasoning patterns learned from mathematics effectively transfer to other complex reasoning domains.
- **Tool-Use and Alignment:** The model maintains competitive performance on agentic tool-use tasks (BFCL v3) and general alignment benchmarks (IFEval, Arena-Hard), indicating that math-only agentic RL enhances reasoning without negatively impacting other general capabilities.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study and Discussions

- **Superiority of GRPO-RoC:** Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of GRPO-RoC. Compared to a vanilla GRPO with Tool baseline, GRPO-RoC consistently achieves higher reasoning accuracy on AIME24 and AIME25 and significantly reduces the average response length. This validates the RoC strategy's ability to filter out low-quality, noisy trajectories and reinforce higher-quality positive reasoning. The vanilla GRPO with Tool itself outperforms CoT-only methods (like DAPO), reinforcing the value of tool integration.
- **Upper Limit of RL-Improved Reasoning:** The study notes that continued RL training beyond a certain point (around step 510 in Stage 3) for the 14B model led to policy and reward signal collapse. This observation suggests that RL might primarily optimize for the base model's inherent reasoning capacity rather than extending it indefinitely. The rStar2-Agent approach successfully and efficiently reaches this reasoning ceiling.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Agentic Reasoning Behaviors (High-Entropy Tokens)

- **Forking Tokens:** Analysis of high-entropy tokens reveals "forking tokens" (e.g., "But before," "double-check") which signal uncertainty and trigger self-reflection, exploration, and verification steps. Agentic RL preserves these crucial behaviors.
- **Reflection Tokens on Tool Call Responses:** Uniquely to agentic reasoning, the model generates sequences of high-entropy "reflection tokens" immediately after receiving feedback from the code environment. These tokens are used to analyze, interpret, diagnose errors, explore alternative solutions, and refine reasoning based on external tool outputs. This behavior demonstrates a more advanced, adaptive cognitive capability akin to human-like response to environmental interaction. Interestingly, the Python code itself tends to be low-entropy, likely due to extensive pre-training on code.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The rStar2-Agent research represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model reasoning, offering a potent and efficient pathway to developing highly capable AI agents. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift to "Smarter Thinking":** This work fundamentally shifts the focus from simply generating longer chains of thought to cultivating "smarter", agentic reasoning. By enabling models to autonomously leverage external tools and learn from environmental feedback, rStar2-Agent unlocks a new dimension of problem-solving capability, moving beyond the inherent limitations of internal reflection. This approach promises more robust, verifiable, and adaptive reasoning across complex tasks.
- **Efficiency in Achieving Frontier Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art math reasoning performance with a 14B model (rStar2-Agent-14B) that rivals or surpasses 671B models like DeepSeek-R1 is a major breakthrough. This efficiency, coupled with minimal compute (510 RL steps on 64 MI300X GPUs in one week), demonstrates a highly cost-effective paradigm for developing high-performance reasoning models. This could democratize access to advanced AI capabilities, making them achievable for research groups and organizations with more constrained resources.
- **Robust Agentic Reinforcement Learning:** The introduction of GRPO-RoC is a critical algorithmic innovation. By effectively handling "environment noise" from coding tools and refining outcome-only rewards through intelligent trajectory resampling, it provides a stable and efficient method for training agents in interactive environments. This addresses a core challenge that has previously hampered the scalability and reliability of agentic RL.
- **Scalable and Reliable Infrastructure:** The custom-built, high-throughput agentic RL infrastructure, featuring an isolated code environment and a dynamic load-balanced rollout scheduler, solves significant engineering bottlenecks. Its ability to handle massive concurrent tool calls and highly imbalanced multi-turn rollouts efficiently is crucial for scaling future agentic AI research and applications.
- **Demonstrated Generalization:** The strong generalization performance of rStar2-Agent-14B to scientific reasoning and agentic tool-use tasks, despite being trained solely on mathematical reasoning, suggests that the "smarter" reasoning patterns learned are highly transferable. This indicates that agentic RL, even in specific domains, can foster fundamental cognitive improvements that benefit a wide array of applications.
- **Novel Insights into Cognitive Behaviors:** The analysis of high-entropy tokens revealing "reflection tokens on tool call responses" offers new insights into how LLMs learn to interact with environments. This discovery of environment-driven adaptive reasoning is a significant contribution to understanding the internal mechanisms of agentic AI.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The public release of code, datasets, and training recipes ("https://github.com/microsoft/rStar") is a commendable step. It fosters transparency, reproducibility, and accelerates further research and development in agentic reasoning, allowing the broader AI community to build upon these innovations.

In conclusion, rStar2-Agent not only pushes the boundaries of LLM reasoning performance but also provides a comprehensive, efficient, and robust framework for developing the next generation of intelligent agents that can "think smarter" through dynamic interaction with their environment.
