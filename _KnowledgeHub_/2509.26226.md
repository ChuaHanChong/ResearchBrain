# Thinking-Free Policy Initialization Makes Distilled Reasoning Models More Effective and Efficient Reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> Thinking-Free Policy Initialization (TFPI) introduces a lightweight intermediate training stage that significantly enhances the effectiveness and efficiency of distilled reasoning models. It achieves superior performance on reasoning benchmarks with substantially reduced computational costs and improved token efficiency by leveraging a simple "ThinkingFree" operation during policy initialization.

## Problem

- Training Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) is computationally expensive due to the need for extremely long context lengths during rollouts.
- Existing solutions, such as multi-stage RLVR or algorithmic improvements, often lead to performance degradation or still require substantial resources, failing to fully address scalability issues.
- Achieving token-efficient reasoning typically requires complex reward shaping or architectural changes, often at the expense of accuracy or through intricate engineering.

## Method

- Introduces the "**ThinkingFree**" operation, which adapts input queries by appending a specific token (e.g., ) to explicitly omit intermediate thinking content during model rollouts.
- Proposes Thinking-Free Policy Initialization (TFPI) as an intermediate training stage for SFT-distilled LRMs, modifying the RLVR objective to utilize these thinking-free queries during rollouts.
- Employs a multi-stage training strategy within TFPI, progressively increasing maximum output lengths (e.g., 2K  4K  8K) to efficiently learn at lower costs before potential full-length RLVR.

## Results

- TFPI alone consistently delivered substantial accuracy improvements over SFT-distilled models (e.g., DS-**1.5B** average accuracy from **22.0%** to **29.2%**) and outperformed standard RLVR ("Direct RL") with matched compute.
- Using TFPI as an initialization for subsequent RLVR ("TFPI + RL") achieved a higher performance ceiling (e.g., Qwen3-**4B** overall accuracy to **65.7%** vs. **62.0%** for Direct RL) with significantly reduced compute costs (e.g., **1.5K** H800 GPU hours vs. **8K** for Polaris-**4B**-Preview).
- Models trained with TFPI demonstrated over **70%** reduction in inference output tokens and achieved an optimal performance-efficiency trade-off among various RL-based token control methods, without specialized rewards or complex designs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying the "ThinkingFree" operation during RL training, even with very short context lengths, surprisingly improves the model's slow-thinking (reasoning) capabilities without typical performance degradation.
> - TFPI training encourages a broader and more diverse exploration of the model's parameter space, with its parameter update directions progressively aligning with direct RL, contributing to the generation of more capable LRMs.
> - Learned verification behaviors from thinking-free training successfully generalize to the original slow-thinking mode, indicating efficient knowledge transfer and robustness of the approach.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the development of powerful large language models (LLMs): the immense computational cost associated with training sophisticated reasoning capabilities. Specifically, it focuses on Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR), a highly effective approach for training LLMs to generate complex Chains-of-Thought (CoT) and solve challenging reasoning tasks. These RLVR-trained models are often referred to as "long-CoT LLMs," "slow-thinking LLMs," or "large reasoning models (LRMs)."

The existing landscape for training LRMs faces a significant bottleneck: RLVR necessitates extremely long context lengths during training rollouts, leading to substantial computational costs. Prior efforts to mitigate this problem include:
- **Multi-stage RLVR:** This strategy involves starting with relatively short contexts and gradually increasing the training length (e.g., Luo et al., 2025b; An et al., 2025). However, the paper highlights its limitations, noting that "multistage training might cause irreversible performance degradation" and still demands "significant computational resources."
- **Algorithmic improvements to GRPO:** Variants of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), such as DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) and Dr GRPO (Liu et al., 2025b), aim to improve efficiency or reduce length bias within the RLVR framework.
- **Efficient Reasoning Techniques:** A broader category of research focuses on making LLMs reason more efficiently. These methods can be prompt-based, supervised fine-tuning (SFT)-based, or other RL-related designs. RL-based approaches in this category often involve specialized length-based reward shaping, integrating "fast and slow thinking," or employing thinking budget control mechanisms (e.g., Aggarwal & Welleck, 2025; Fang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025a). These often trade off accuracy for efficiency and require complex modifications to the training process.

"Thinking-Free Policy Initialization (TFPI)" positions itself as a novel and "orthogonal" approach to these existing strategies. Instead of modifying the core RLVR algorithm or introducing complex reward shaping, TFPI proposes a *simple yet effective intermediate stage* between long CoT distillation (SFT-distilled LRMs) and standard RLVR. Its key differentiation is that it naturally leads to more token-efficient models without specialized rewards or complex training designs, while simultaneously accelerating RL convergence and achieving a higher performance ceiling. It challenges the assumption that efficiency must come at the cost of accuracy or through intricate engineering. By focusing on a "ThinkingFree" operation during policy initialization, this work carves out a new path for developing highly capable and computationally economical LRMs, directly addressing the scalability issues that hinder broader adoption of advanced reasoning models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind TFPI stems from the observed inefficiency and high computational demands of training powerful Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) using Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR). While RLVR has proven exceptionally effective in endowing LLMs with spontaneous Chain-of-Thought (CoT) capabilities and strong performance on complex tasks, its reliance on extremely long context lengths during training rollouts translates into prohibitively high computational costs. Existing solutions, such as multi-stage training, offer only partial mitigation, often leading to performance degradation and still requiring substantial resources.

Against this backdrop, the authors set forth several key objectives:

1. **Reduce Computational Costs:** The most immediate motivation is to drastically lower the computational burden associated with RLVR training, particularly during the rollout stage, which is dominated by the generation of long CoT responses. This involves finding a method that can reduce token usage without sacrificing the model's reasoning capabilities.
2. **Accelerate RL Convergence:** To make the training process more efficient, an objective is to develop a method that speeds up the convergence of RLVR algorithms, allowing LRMs to reach high performance levels with fewer training steps and less overall compute.
3. **Achieve a Higher Performance Ceiling:** Beyond just accelerating training, the goal is to enable LRMs to attain a superior maximum performance level, pushing the boundaries of what these models can achieve on challenging reasoning tasks.
4. **Enhance Token Efficiency Naturally:** The authors aim to produce reasoning models that are inherently more token-efficient during inference, meaning they can achieve high accuracy with shorter, more concise outputs. Crucially, this efficiency should be achieved "without specialized rewards or complex training designs," distinguishing it from many existing "efficient reasoning" methods that often trade accuracy for brevity or require intricate reward engineering.
5. **Bridge CoT Distillation and RLVR:** The work seeks to provide a more effective and efficient initialization point for standard RLVR, building upon the strengths of SFT-distilled LRMs while overcoming their inherent inefficiencies for RL training. By providing a "Thinking-Free Policy Initialization," the authors aim to better prepare models for subsequent, computationally intensive RL stages.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to make the development of highly capable, "slow-thinking" LRMs more accessible, scalable, and practical by tackling the core efficiency challenges of RLVR training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of TFPI is built upon a simple yet profound observation regarding the "ThinkingFree" operation and its unexpected benefits for both inference and training.

#### 3.1. The ThinkingFree Operation

The core of the methodology begins with defining the "ThinkingFree" operation. For SFT-distilled LRMs, queries typically follow a "thinking mode" template (e.g., Template 1 in the paper) that encourages step-by-step reasoning (Chain-of-Thought, CoT). The ThinkingFree operation transforms an input query `x` into `x' = ThinkingFree(x)` by explicitly instructing the model to omit the thinking content via a direct `</think>` append in the chat template (e.g., Template 2).

- **Inference Efficiency:** Initial experiments confirmed that applying ThinkingFree during inference substantially reduces token consumption (over 70% reduction) for models like DeepSeek-Distilled-Qwen-1.5B (DS-1.5B) and Qwen3-4B, across both SFT-distilled long-CoT models and fast-slow fusion models.
- **Surprising Training Benefits:** The pivotal insight came from applying this ThinkingFree operation not just at inference, but during the *rollout stage of RLVR training*. Counter-intuitively, RL training with ThinkingFree-adapted inputs, even with very short training context lengths (e.g., 4K tokens), not only reduced token consumption but also *improved* the model's performance when evaluated in the original slow-thinking mode. This stands in stark contrast to standard RLVR with short contexts, which typically leads to substantial performance degradation. This finding laid the groundwork for TFPI.

#### 3.2. ThinkingFree Policy Initialization (TFPI)

Inspired by these observations, TFPI is formally introduced as a dedicated, lightweight intermediate stage before standard RLVR for SFT-distilled LRMs.

- **Modified Objective:** During the TFPI stage, the RLVR objective `J_RLVR(θ)` is modified to `J_TFPI(θ) = E_x~D [J_RLVR(θ, x')]`, where `x' = ThinkingFree(x)`. This means that during rollouts, the model generates `G` responses `y_i` conditioned on the thinking-free query `x'`.
- **Reward Calculation:** The importance ratios and advantages are calculated based on these thinking-free rollouts. Importantly, the reward `r(x', y_i)` is equivalent to `r(x, y_i)` because the ThinkingFree operation only alters the output format, not the underlying problem or its ground truth.
- **Base RLVR Algorithm:** For fair comparison, DAPO (Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization), a variant of GRPO, is chosen as the underlying RLVR algorithm for all experiments, both in the TFPI stage and the subsequent standard RLVR stage. DAPO's features include token-level normalization, dynamic sampling, and a clip-higher mechanism.
- **Multi-stage Training within TFPI:** TFPI itself employs a multi-stage training strategy, starting with short maximum output lengths and progressively increasing them (e.g., 2K → 4K → 8K for smaller models, 4K → 8K → 16K for larger models). This graduated approach allows for efficient learning at lower costs before potentially moving to full-length RLVR.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Experiments are conducted on various model scales: DS-1.5B, Qwen3-4B, and DS-7B.
- **Training Data:** Polaris-53K (math-specific data) is used as the training dataset for consistency.
- **Baselines:** TFPI is compared against "Direct RLVR" (standard RLVR from an SFT-distilled LRM with matched total compute), "TFPI + RL" (TFPI as an initialization followed by standard RLVR), and several other competitive LRMs and efficient reasoning baselines (e.g., Polaris, DeepScaleR, AutoThink, L1Max) for comprehensive evaluation.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** A diverse set of benchmarks is used, covering in-domain math reasoning (AIME24/25, BeyondAIME), out-of-domain multi-task reasoning (GPQA-Diamond), code generation (LiveCodeBench), and instruction following (IFEval). Models are typically evaluated in "thinking mode" for core performance and in "thinking-free mode" for efficiency analysis.

#### 3.4. Analysis Techniques

To understand *why* TFPI works, the authors conduct two levels of analysis:
- **Behavioral Level:** They track the "verification ratio" (number of verification steps / total steps) in generated responses across different TFPI stages for both thinking-free and thinking modes. This helps determine if learned behaviors generalize.
- **Parameter Level:** Using PCA (Principal Component Analysis) visualizations of model parameters and cosine similarity of parameter updates, they analyze how TFPI training traverses the parameter space compared to direct RL.

This comprehensive methodology, from identifying a simple yet powerful operation to rigorous experimental validation and in-depth analysis, underscores the robustness of the TFPI approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling findings that underscore the effectiveness and efficiency of Thinking-Free Policy Initialization (TFPI).

1. **ThinkingFree Substantially Reduces Inference Cost:** The initial meta-experiment clearly demonstrated that applying the "ThinkingFree" operation during inference reduces the number of output tokens by over 70% for SFT-distilled models like DS-1.5B and Qwen3-4B. This shows its immediate utility for token-efficient deployment.

2. **ThinkingFree Training Enhances Slow-Thinking Capabilities:** Surprisingly, training with ThinkingFree-adapted inputs significantly improved the model's slow-thinking capabilities (accuracy), even when trained with very short context lengths (e.g., 4K tokens) and evaluated in the original thinking mode. For Qwen-3-4B, this led to an approximately 2% accuracy increase on AIME25 while reducing output tokens by 20%, contrasting sharply with a >40% performance drop observed with standard RLVR using similarly short contexts. This finding is critical as it indicates that TFPI enables effective training under low-cost settings without performance compromise.

3. **TFPI Enhances Slow-Thinking and Outperforms Direct RL:**
 - **Accuracy Gains:** Across all models (DS-1.5B, Qwen3-4B, DS-7B), TFPI consistently delivered substantial accuracy improvements over the initial SFT-distilled models, even with small response lengths during its stages. For instance, DS-1.5B's overall average accuracy increased from 22.0% to 29.2% after three TFPI stages.
 - **Faster and Larger Improvements:** Compared to "Direct RL" (standard RLVR with matched total training compute), TFPI achieved faster convergence and superior final performance across nearly all benchmarks. Qwen3-4B, for example, reached 63.8% overall accuracy with TFPI stage 3 versus 60.2% for Direct RL (+3.6%).
 - **Generalizability:** Despite being trained exclusively on mathematics data (Polaris-53K), TFPI demonstrated notable improvements on out-of-domain tasks such as GPQA, LiveCodeBench, and IFEval, indicating a degree of robust transfer learning.

4. **TFPI as a Foundation for Higher-Performing and More Efficient RLVR:**
 - **Elevated Performance Ceiling:** When TFPI was used as an initialization phase followed by a standard RLVR stage ("TFPI + RL"), models consistently achieved higher accuracies than "Direct RL" under the same total compute budget. Qwen3-4B, for instance, saw its overall accuracy rise to 65.7% with TFPI+RL, compared to 62.0% for Direct RL. This suggests TFPI provides a stronger starting point for subsequent RL training.
 - **Dramatic Compute Reduction:** TFPI + RL proved to be an exceptionally efficient strategy. For example, a Qwen3-4B model trained with TFPI+RL achieved superior performance compared to Polaris-4B-Preview, while requiring approximately 1.5K H800 GPU hours versus Polaris's 8K H800 GPU hours. The total compute cost of TFPI's three stages was less than 20% of standard 32K-context RL training. Remarkably, even TFPI alone (without a subsequent RL stage) allowed a 4B model to reach 89.0% accuracy on AIME24, outperforming a 235B model (Qwen3-235B-Thinking) in math reasoning and code generation.

5. **TFPI Improves Token Efficiency with Optimal Trade-off:**
 - **Simultaneous Improvement:** TFPI training steadily improved both accuracy and token efficiency. For DS-1.5B, thinking-free accuracy on AIME24 rose from 29.6% to 37.5%, while average output length remained significantly shorter (5.3K vs. 16.7K tokens for the initial thinking mode).
 - **Pareto Frontier Performance:** TFPI achieved the best performance-efficiency trade-off among various RL-based token control methods, often with competitive or lower token usage and higher accuracy, all "without specialized rewards or training designs." This positions TFPI as a superior alternative to methods relying on complex length reward shaping.

6. **Behavioral and Parameter-Level Insights:**
 - **Behavioral Generalization:** The analysis showed that learned verification behaviors during thinking-free TFPI training generalized to the slow-thinking mode. Verification ratios showed similar trends, suggesting knowledge transfer.
 - **Parameter Space Exploration:** PCA visualization indicated that TFPI training traverses a larger and more diverse region of the parameter space, with its parameter update directions progressively aligning with those of "Direct RL." This broader exploration is hypothesized to contribute to TFPI's superior LRM generation.
 - **Reasoning Pattern Preservation & Rollout Speed-up:** TFPI preserved the core slow-thinking reasoning pattern (stable answer token length) and significantly reduced the average output tokens during the rollout stage of subsequent long-CoT RL training (e.g., from over 9K to approximately 6K), thereby speeding up the overall training process.

In summary, the main findings establish TFPI as a highly effective, efficient, and broadly applicable method for training advanced reasoning LLMs, offering significant advantages in terms of performance, cost reduction, and token efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Thinking-Free Policy Initialization (TFPI) holds substantial significance and promises a wide-ranging impact across the field of large language models and artificial intelligence research.

1. **Democratization of Advanced Reasoning Models:** The most immediate and profound impact of TFPI is its ability to drastically reduce the computational cost of training high-performing Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). By cutting down GPU hours significantly (e.g., 1.5K H800 hours for TFPI+RL vs. 8K for Polaris-4B-Preview for comparable or superior performance), TFPI makes the development and fine-tuning of advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible. This could enable smaller research labs, academic institutions, and even individual researchers with limited resources to contribute to the LRM landscape, fostering greater innovation and competition.

2. **Pushing the Performance Frontier:** TFPI does not merely offer efficiency; it simultaneously accelerates RL convergence and helps achieve a *higher performance ceiling* for LRMs. This means that models trained with TFPI can attain state-of-the-art results (as demonstrated by a 4B model outperforming a 235B model in certain math and code benchmarks with TFPI alone). This dual benefit of increased efficiency and enhanced performance is critical for advancing the capabilities of AI in complex problem-solving.

3. **A New Paradigm for Token-Efficient Reasoning:** TFPI challenges existing approaches to "efficient reasoning" that often rely on complex reward shaping, architectural modifications, or trade-offs between accuracy and token length. By naturally yielding models that are both highly accurate and token-efficient without specialized rewards or intricate designs, TFPI offers a simpler, more robust alternative. This is vital for practical deployment, where minimizing inference costs and latency is paramount. The "thinking-free mode" during inference becomes a straightforward mechanism for activating this efficiency.

4. **Simplified and More Robust Training Pipelines:** By providing a "simple yet effective adaptation" and a strong initialization point for standard RLVR, TFPI simplifies the overall training process for LRMs. Its multi-stage nature, which effectively manages context lengths, appears to mitigate the performance degradation often associated with short-context training in traditional multi-stage RLVR. This robustness and ease of implementation could accelerate the adoption of RLVR techniques.

5. **Insights into Model Learning and Generalization:** The behavioral and parameter-level analyses offer valuable insights into how LLMs learn and generalize reasoning patterns. Understanding that verification behaviors transfer from thinking-free training to slow-thinking inference, and that TFPI encourages a broader exploration of the parameter space, can inform the design of future training strategies and foundational models.

6. **Significant Industry Impact:** For large technology companies like Tencent, which are heavily invested in deploying LLMs at scale, the efficiency gains offered by TFPI are invaluable. Reduced training and inference costs translate directly into lower operational expenses, faster iteration cycles for model improvement, and the ability to serve more complex reasoning tasks to a wider user base. This work reinforces the trend of industry-academia collaborations driving practical advancements in AI.

In conclusion, TFPI represents a significant step forward in making advanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs more attainable, powerful, and practical. Its impact will likely be felt across research, development, and deployment, fostering a new era of efficient and effective large language models.
