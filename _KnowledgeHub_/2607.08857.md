# AgenticFocus: Object-Preserving Mixed Reality Synthesis from Human FPV Video for Dexterous Humanoid Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> AgenticFocus is a mixed-reality pipeline turning ordinary monocular first-person human videos into humanoid training demonstrations. A vision-language model picks the task object, SAM2 tracking plus object-protected inpainting erases the human while keeping object geometry, full-hand pose is retargeted to a Unitree G1 with BrainCo hands in a camera-relative frame, and layered MuJoCo compositing preserves contact depth. It reaches SPARC -5.18 versus -5.56 and -6.05 for baselines.

## Problem

- Teleoperation, mocap gloves and wearable rigs are costly and embodiment-specific, so dexterous humanoid demonstration data does not scale across platforms or tasks.
- Prior video-editing transfer degrades exactly at hand-object contact regions: actor removal and inpainting corrupt occluded object geometry, boundaries and local depth cues policies need.
- Existing pipelines assume stereo or scene-specific capture, or collapse human hands to gripper abstractions, and rarely emit embodiment-consistent paired actions and states.

## Method

- ==Object-centric restoration==: a ==vision-language model== selects the task object, ==SAM2== tracks it, and inpainting is masked to hand-minus-object pixels, then a clean-frame object template is reinserted.
- ==Full-hand camera-relative retargeting==: WiLoR/HaMeR hand reconstruction feeds ==MuJoCo inverse kinematics== through a scaled camera-axis transform with offset, plus exponential-moving-average joint smoothing.
- ==Layered mixed-reality compositing== (**AgenticFocus**): separate articulated-hand and near-contact thumb render passes with the restored object template layered between them, giving plausible occlusion for wrap-around grasps.

## Results

- Mean wrist SPARC over **75** validation episodes is **-5.18** (95% CI -5.38 to -4.98), versus **-5.56** for Masquerade and **-6.05** for Do as I Do.
- Smoothness magnitude improves roughly **7%** over Masquerade and about **14%** over Do as I Do, with the rolling episode-wise curve staying least negative throughout.
- Mean 3D position error against ground-truth trajectories on EPIC-KITCHENS and internal **30 FPS** clips is lowest for this pipeline, with **95%** confidence intervals across episodes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Protecting the manipulated object during actor removal beats erase-then-inpaint: contact geometry is the signal, not the background, so mask subtraction preserves it.
> - Explicit articulated rendering avoids diffusion hallucination artifacts, trading generative realism for deterministic, temporally stable observations that pair cleanly with actions.
> - Evaluation stops at trajectory fidelity and smoothness proxies; no downstream policy training is shown, so transfer to control performance stays unverified.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "AgenticFocus" addresses a fundamental challenge within the field of Physical AI, specifically in the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies and visuomotor architectures for dexterous humanoid robots. The broader landscape of robotics research increasingly emphasizes the need for large volumes of high-quality, robot-usable visual and action data to train complex manipulation policies.

Current methods for acquiring such data for dexterous humanoids face several limitations:
- **Teleoperation:** This approach is resource-intensive and expensive.
- **Wearable capture systems:** These systems can be cumbersome and affect the naturalness of demonstrations.
- **Embodiment-specific data collection:** This method does not generalize well across different robotic platforms, limiting scalability.

In contrast, human egocentric (first-person-view, FPV) video is an abundant source of diverse object interactions in natural environments. Its internet-scale availability makes it a potentially valuable, yet largely untapped, resource for humanoid manipulation learning.

However, converting human FPV video into a robot-trainable signal involves bridging three key gaps:
1. **Viewpoint Gap:** Human videos are captured from a human-centric perspective (head or chest), whereas robot observations are defined in a robot-centered frame.
2. **Interaction-Region Gap:** During dexterous manipulation, human hands frequently occlude manipulated objects, especially at crucial contact points. Conventional methods for actor removal or inpainting often fail in these regions, corrupting the visual information vital for control.
3. **Action Grounding Gap:** Synthesized robot-like observations are frequently not paired with embodiment-consistent actions and states suitable for direct policy learning.

Existing research has begun to address aspects of this conversion. Projects like DexCap focus on high-quality dexterous supervision using specialized motion-capture hardware. EgoDex and EgoEngine explore richer egocentric sensing setups for humanoid learning. Cross-embodiment video editing techniques such as Masquerade, EgoEngine, and related methods have demonstrated the transformation of human demonstrations into robot-like observations. However, these approaches often rely on specialized capture hardware, stereo sensing, or scene-specific reconstructions. Some simplify human interactions to gripper-level abstractions or degrade object geometry under heavy occlusion. Consequently, a robust pathway from ordinary monocular FPV human video to the focused visual-action supervision required by multi-fingered humanoid policies remains an open challenge.

AgenticFocus positions itself within this landscape by specifically targeting the conversion of *ordinary monocular FPV human video* into *robot-trainable demonstrations* for dexterous humanoids, without requiring specialized capture hardware. It formulates this human-to-humanoid transfer as a Mixed Reality synthesis problem, aiming to preserve the physical interaction structure and task semantics while replacing the human embodiment with a robotic one, thereby producing supervision in a format directly usable by reactive control policies in modern agentic robot systems. This approach seeks to provide a scalable and accessible data source, mitigating a central data-conversion bottleneck in Physical AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a structured Mixed Reality synthesis pipeline, named AgenticFocus, capable of converting ordinary human First-Person-View (FPV) videos into robot-trainable demonstrations tailored for dexterous humanoids. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and broken down into specific technical contributions:

**Motivation:**
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The fundamental motivation is to overcome the scarcity of high-quality, robot-usable visual and action data for training dexterous humanoid manipulation policies. Traditional methods for data collection are resource-intensive, cumbersome, or lack scalability.
- **Leveraging Abundant Human Video:** Human egocentric video represents a vast, largely untapped source of diverse manipulation demonstrations. The project aims to unlock this resource by bridging the critical gaps that prevent its direct use in robotic learning.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Methods:** Current cross-embodiment transfer methods often require specialized hardware, rely on stereo setups, simplify complex dexterous interactions, or struggle with object occlusion, leading to degraded visual fidelity. AgenticFocus seeks to provide a solution that avoids these dependencies and limitations, specifically for ordinary monocular FPV video.
- **Supporting Agentic Robot Systems:** Modern robot architectures often separate high-level reasoning from low-level control. Reactive policies require focused representations of task-relevant objects, local contact geometry, and embodiment-consistent motion. The pipeline aims to generate supervision data in this specific format.
- **Increasing Accessibility:** By removing the need for specialized capture hardware, the project aims to lower the barrier for data collection, making it more accessible for robot learning research and educational purposes.

**Key Objectives (as Threefold Contributions):**
1. **Develop a Focused Mixed Reality Synthesis Pipeline:** The first objective is to introduce a pipeline that combines task-relevant object selection, robust object restoration under occlusion, and humanoid mixed-reality compositing. This pipeline aims to convert ordinary human FPV video into robot-trainable demonstrations that are visually focused on the interaction and suitable for policy learning.
2. **Propose Full-Hand Cross-Embodiment Retargeting:** A second objective is to reconstruct and transfer full-hand human motion to a multi-fingered robotic embodiment. This involves using a camera-relative alignment formulation to avoid collapsing complex dexterous demonstrations into simplified gripper-level interactions, thereby preserving the richness of human manipulation.
3. **Design a Layered, Object-Preserving Compositing Strategy:** The third objective is to create a compositing method for contact-rich manipulation that accurately preserves the manipulated object's geometry and plausible depth relationships near contact points. This strategy is intended to produce synchronized visual and action/state data that accurately reflects the interaction, making it suitable for training reactive humanoid visuomotor policies.

In essence, AgenticFocus is motivated by the need for a practical, scalable, and hardware-agnostic method to transform readily available human FPV video into high-fidelity, robot-ready supervision data for advanced dexterous humanoid learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AgenticFocus implements a multi-stage Mixed Reality synthesis pipeline designed to convert ordinary human FPV manipulation videos into synchronized humanoid training demonstrations. The core principle guiding this methodology is to preserve the semantics of the scene at the object level while transferring the embodiment at the manipulator level. The pipeline consists of three main stages: Object-Centric Restoration, Full-Hand Humanoid Retargeting, and Layered Mixed-Reality Compositing.

#### 3.1. Overview

The process begins with an input egocentric video. The pipeline first identifies and tracks the task-relevant interaction object. Subsequently, it removes the human actor from the scene while meticulously preserving the object's geometry, even under occlusion. In parallel, it reconstructs the full-hand human motion and retargets it to a humanoid embodiment within a camera-relative frame. The final output is then synthesized by compositing the restored scene with the retargeted robot using a layered rendering approach. This modular decomposition avoids reliance on end-to-end generative translation, aiming to produce deterministic, robot-trainable visual-action pairs.

#### 3.2. Object-Centric Restoration

This initial stage focuses on isolating and preparing the manipulated object from the original human video.
1. **Object Identification and Tracking:** A vision-language model is employed to select the task-relevant object within the scene. Following this, a SAM-style (Segment Anything Model) segmentation model tracks the mask of this object across all frames of the video. This step generates per-frame object masks, RGBA object crops, and debugging videos for mask verification.
2. **Occlusion Handling and Inpainting:** A significant challenge arises from human hand occlusion, which often results in incomplete object masks containing only visible fragments rather than the full object geometry. To address this, an object-preserving inpainting mask is constructed. Let $M_{hand}(t)$ represent the human hand or arm mask at frame $t$, and $M_{obj}(t)$ denote the target-object mask. The inpainting region $M_{inpaint}(t)$ is defined as $M_{hand}(t) \cap \neg M_{obj}(t)$. This formulation ensures that only actor pixels *outside* the protected object region are removed. SAM2 is utilized for object and arm tracking, and the resulting inpainting masks are fed into E2FGVI-HQ for background reconstruction after actor removal.
3. **Stable Object Template Restoration:** To further enhance stability and visual fidelity, especially in contact-rich interactions, background inpainting alone is deemed insufficient. Residual hand artifacts, partial object remnants, or boundary flicker can persist near the interaction area. Therefore, a stable object template is restored from a clean frame of the video. This template provides consistent object appearance and geometry, while the per-frame object masks are used to track its spatial placement over time. This separation aims to reduce ghosting, suppress hand-induced boundary artifacts, and preserve the full object geometry even when the source object is heavily occluded by the human hand.

#### 3.3. Full-Hand Humanoid Retargeting

This stage runs in parallel to object restoration, focusing on transferring human hand motion to the robotic embodiment.
1. **Human Hand Pose Estimation:** Human hand pose is estimated frame-by-frame using a 3D hand reconstruction model. The implementation specifically recovers finger and wrist motion using WiLoR/HaMeR-style modules.
2. **Inverse Kinematics Mapping:** The recovered 3D hand topology (wrist and finger joint angles) is then mapped to the specific target robot, which, in this implementation, consists of Unitree G1 arms and BrainCo dexterous hands. This mapping is performed through inverse kinematics within the MuJoCo physics engine.
3. **Camera-Relative Retargeting Formulation:** A critical challenge is the discrepancy between the human demonstrator's egocentric camera frame and the robot's embodiment-specific viewpoint. To bridge this, the retargeting is formulated in a camera-relative coordinate system. A 3D point $P_{human}$ in the human camera frame is transformed into the robot’s virtual camera frame $P_{robot}$ using the equation: $P_{robot} = \beta R_{cam} P_{human} + T_{offset}$. Here, $R_{cam}$ is the camera-axis transformation, $\beta$ is a workspace scaling factor, and $T_{offset}$ represents the position of the virtual robot camera relative to its torso frame. This formulation decouples the demonstration from its original recording geometry, allowing retargeting to be solved in an embodiment-consistent frame.
4. **Motion Smoothing:** To improve temporal stability, the resulting wrist and finger trajectories are smoothed. An exponential moving average is applied to the robot joint configuration $q_t$: $q_t = \alpha \hat{q}_t + (1 - \alpha)q_{t-1}$, where $\hat{q}_t$ is the raw inverse kinematics (IK) estimate and $\alpha$ is the smoothing coefficient. Additionally, fixed wrist-orientation corrections are applied to compensate for the mounting geometry of the robot hands relative to the recovered human palm orientation.
5. **Output:** This stage produces a synchronized action-state representation, encompassing arm trajectories, finger commands, and camera-relative robot states.

#### 3.4. Layered Mixed-Reality Compositing

The final stage integrates the retargeted humanoid with the cleaned scene.
1. **Addressing Depth Plausibility:** A simple 2D overlay of the robot hand onto the scene is insufficient for dexterous interactions. Such an approach would either render the robot hand entirely in front (leading to implausible contact) or entirely behind the object (losing grasp structure), particularly problematic for fingers wrapping around small objects.
2. **Layered Rendering Strategy:** To preserve local depth cues at the interaction region, robot rendering is separated into two passes: a full articulated-hand pass and a near-contact thumb pass. The restored object template is then composited *around* these renders. This allows the object to occlude appropriate finger regions while ensuring that visible thumb contact, crucial for grasping, is preserved where required. This layered approach aims to produce more plausible interaction structures, especially for small objects and wrap-around grasps.
3. **Rendering Environment:** Rendering is performed in a headless MuJoCo environment, utilizing the Unitree G1 and BrainCo hand meshes derived from the robot description. This explicit, articulated model-based rendering approach ensures visual stability and prevents hallucination artifacts typically associated with image synthesis methods.

The output of AgenticFocus is a paired dataset: a focused visual sequence where human manipulators are replaced by humanoid hands interacting with real task objects, alongside synchronized robot actions and states, which are suitable for downstream policy learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AgenticFocus focused on two primary aspects of human-to-humanoid motion retargeting: spatial accuracy against ground-truth trajectories and temporal smoothness of the retargeted humanoid wrist motion.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

Evaluation sequences were constructed from egocentric human videos, including clips from the EPIC-KITCHENS dataset and internally processed demonstrations. All videos were standardized to 30 frames per second (FPS). For each video, the AgenticFocus pipeline generated two synchronized outputs:
1. A rendered mixed-reality video, where human hands were replaced by Unitree G1 and BrainCo hands, while the target object remained within the real scene.
2. A structural action-state record, stored in .npz/.json format, containing arm trajectories, finger commands, and camera-relative robot states.

AgenticFocus was compared against two cross-embodiment baselines: Masquerade and Do as I Do. Prior to comparison, all trajectories were temporally resampled to a common normalized timeline and expressed in the same coordinate frame to ensure consistency.

#### 4.2. Trajectory Accuracy

- **Metric:** The mean 3D position error was computed between the ground-truth trajectory ($p_t$) and the reconstructed trajectory ($\hat{p}_t$) produced by each method. The error was quantified as $e_t = 100 ||\hat{p}_t - p_t||_2$, with the factor of 100 converting meters to centimeters. The final error was averaged across all frames and evaluation clips, with error bars representing the 95% confidence interval across episodes.
- **Results:** AgenticFocus demonstrated the lowest reconstruction error among the compared methods. This outcome suggests a closer agreement with the ground-truth trajectory compared to the baselines. The paper indicates that the camera-relative alignment and full-hand retargeting strategies employed by AgenticFocus contribute to a reduction in spatial mismatch during the human-to-robot transfer process.

#### 4.3. Retargeting Smoothness

- **Metric:** Temporal smoothness was quantified using the Spectral Arc Length (SPARC) metric, which is derived from the humanoid wrist speed profile. SPARC values are inherently negative, with less negative values indicating smoother and more temporally stable motion. The metric is computed based on the Fourier magnitude spectrum of the wrist speed signal.
- **Results:**
 - Episode-wise SPARC analysis across 75 validation episodes showed that AgenticFocus consistently maintained the least negative SPARC values, indicating smoother and more coherent retargeted wrist motion than both Masquerade and Do as I Do.
 - The mean SPARC values, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over 75 episodes, were:
 - AgenticFocus: -5.18 (95% CI [-5.38, -4.98])
 - Masquerade: -5.56 (95% CI [-5.84, -5.31])
 - Do as I Do: -6.05 (95% CI [-6.42, -5.72])
 - Quantitatively, AgenticFocus achieved approximately a 7% reduction in SPARC magnitude relative to Masquerade and roughly a 14% reduction relative to Do as I Do. These results suggest that the camera-relative retargeting formulation and the dedicated trajectory smoothing techniques implemented in AgenticFocus substantially enhance the temporal stability and quality of the robot-ready humanoid wrist motion.

In summary, the experimental results indicate that AgenticFocus provides demonstrably improved spatial accuracy and temporal smoothness in retargeted humanoid motions when compared to established cross-embodiment baselines, suggesting that its methodological innovations are effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of AgenticFocus carries several implications for the fields of robotics, artificial intelligence, and human-robot interaction.

1. **Lowering the Data Barrier for Humanoid Manipulation:** A primary significance is the reduction of the data acquisition burden for training dexterous humanoid manipulation policies. By effectively converting readily available, ordinary human FPV videos into robot-trainable demonstrations, AgenticFocus offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative to labor-intensive teleoperation or specialized hardware-based data collection. This could accelerate the development of robotic capabilities in complex manipulation tasks.
2. **Enabling New Applications:** The ability to generate high-fidelity, robot-ready supervision data from everyday human activities has the potential to impact various domains. These include household and everyday-object handling, where human demonstrations are abundant, as well as assistive and service robotics, and applications in healthcare or eldercare support tasks, where complex, human-like dexterity is often required.
3. **Increased Accessibility for Research and Education:** The pipeline's independence from specialized motion-capture hardware, stereo rigs, or scene-specific digital twins makes data collection more accessible. This democratizes access to high-quality manipulation demonstrations, which could foster broader participation in robot learning research and education, potentially leading to faster innovation in the field.
4. **Addressing a Core Bottleneck in Physical AI:** AgenticFocus directly addresses a central data-conversion bottleneck in Physical AI. By transforming abundant egocentric human video into embodiment-consistent visual-action supervision, it offers a practical solution to a challenge that has limited the scalability of learning for embodied intelligent systems. This contributes to bridging the gap between human capabilities and robot performance in dexterous tasks.
5. **Provision of Synchronized, Focused Supervision:** The output of AgenticFocus is not merely an edited video but a synchronized representation that pairs focused visual observations with robot actions and camera-relative states. This structured data format is directly suitable for downstream visuomotor policy learning, enabling robots to learn reactive control policies that are grounded in real-world human interactions while being consistent with their own embodiment.
6. **Advancement in Mixed Reality and Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The methodological contributions in object-preserving mixed reality synthesis, camera-relative full-hand retargeting, and layered compositing represent advancements in cross-embodiment data conversion. These techniques improve the fidelity and plausibility of synthesized robot demonstrations, particularly in contact-rich manipulation scenarios.

In conclusion, AgenticFocus offers a practical, robust, and accessible pathway for leveraging the vast resource of human FPV videos to train dexterous humanoid robots. Its potential impact lies in accelerating research, broadening application possibilities, and lowering entry barriers within the domain of embodied AI and robotics.
