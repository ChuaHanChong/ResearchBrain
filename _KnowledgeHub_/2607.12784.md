# Directional Constraints for Efficient Exploration in Safe Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> An enhancement to the ATACOM safety layer, ATACOM-Directional Constraints (ATACOM-DC), improves exploration efficiency in safe reinforcement learning by selectively applying constraints based on the action's direction relative to boundaries. The modification enables faster learning convergence and superior task performance in simulated robotic control tasks while consistently upholding safety guarantees.

## Problem

- Existing Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL) methods, including the original ATACOM framework, often restrict exploration excessively due to conservative constraint enforcement, hindering learning speed and potentially suboptimal task performance.
- The original ATACOM framework symmetrically morphs actions near constraint boundaries, suppressing movement both towards and away from constraints, which can trap agents in conservative regions.
- Achieving an efficient balance between maintaining strict safety guarantees and allowing sufficient exploration for fast learning in constrained RL remains a fundamental challenge.

## Method

- Introduces **ATACOM-Directional Constraints (ATACOM-DC)**, an extension of the **ATACOM** framework that selectively applies ==directional constraint enforcement== based on the direction of the agent's proposed action.
- The method only scales down actions that would move the robot towards a ==constraint boundary==, while actions moving away from a constraint are left largely unaffected, enabling more permissive exploration.
- For multiple constraints, it dynamically identifies and 'deactivates' constraints where the proposed action moves away from the boundary, constructing a modified ==Jacobian== and ==basis for safe action computation== only on the active constraints.

## Results

- ATACOM-DC consistently demonstrated **faster learning convergence** and achieved **higher final task performance** compared to the vanilla ATACOM across Kuka iiwa air hockey, planar air hockey, and quadrotor navigation tasks.
- The method maintained safety guarantees with comparable or reduced constraint violations to vanilla ATACOM, achieving **near-zero** long-term violations in tested environments.
- An ablation study showed ATACOM-DC offers a **Pareto-superior** performance-safety trade-off for varying safety margins (**beta** parameter) and safety threshold settings (**delta** parameter) compared to its non-directional counterparts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymmetrically applying safety constraints, by allowing unhindered movement away from boundaries, can significantly improve exploration efficiency without compromising fundamental safety guarantees.
> - The conventional symmetric morphing of actions in safety layers unnecessarily restricts the agent's search space, leading to slower learning and potentially suboptimal policies.
> - Refining safety layers to differentiate between 'dangerous' (approaching constraint) and 'safe' (moving away from constraint) action directions enables a better Pareto-optimal trade-off between safety and learning performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has significantly advanced the capability to learn complex robotic skills, often leveraging simulation-based training with techniques like Domain Randomization to improve robustness for real-world deployment. However, direct deployment in open-ended, real-world environments necessitates strong safety guarantees to prevent behaviors that could damage equipment, the environment, or harm individuals. Uncontrolled policy behaviors in physical systems can have serious consequences, contrasting with the benign nature of errors in simulation.

This need for safety has led to the development of Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL) methods and Safe Exploration (SafeExp) approaches. SafeRL typically frames the problem within Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs), aiming to limit cumulative constraint costs, often through safety filters or constrained optimization techniques like Lagrangian-based methods. SafeExp, prevalent in robotics and control, focuses on ensuring step-by-step safety by adhering to state constraints, frequently utilizing theoretical frameworks such as Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), Lyapunov stability, Reachability Analysis, and Model Predictive Control (MPC).

A fundamental challenge across both SafeRL and SafeExp is that learning under explicit safety constraints is often more difficult than unconstrained RL. Constraints can restrict the agent's exploration space, slow down the learning process, and potentially lead to suboptimal task performance because the agent must solve a more complex optimization problem. This issue is compounded when constraint functions are unknown and must be estimated from data, introducing further approximation errors and limitations on exploration.

The ATACOM (Acting on the TAngent Space of the COnstraint Manifold) framework is a contemporary approach designed to enforce safety constraints with low computational overhead, particularly in SafeExp settings. It has demonstrated effectiveness in maintaining safety during real-world fine-tuning of RL policies. However, a known limitation of the original ATACOM framework is its tendency to hamper exploration. By symmetrically morphing actions near constraint boundaries, it suppresses action components orthogonal to the constraint, which, while preventing violations, also unintentionally inhibits actions that would move the agent *away* from the constraint. This symmetric suppression can make it challenging for the agent to escape conservative regions of the state space, thus slowing down learning and making it less efficient, especially during initial training phases.

The presented work, ATACOM-Directional Constraints (ATACOM-DC), addresses this specific limitation within the existing ATACOM framework. It aims to improve the efficiency of exploration while maintaining safety, thereby enhancing the safety-performance trade-off in constrained RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency of exploration in Safe Reinforcement Learning settings, specifically by improving upon the existing ATACOM safety layer. The central motivation stems from the observation that while ATACOM effectively ensures safety by projecting actions onto a safe tangent space, this mechanism also imposes an exploration penalty. The original ATACOM approach morphs actions symmetrically, meaning it restricts movement both towards *and away* from constraint boundaries, thus forcing the agent to operate more conservatively than necessary. This conservative behavior can significantly reduce learning speed and hinder the achievement of optimal task performance, particularly in early training epochs or in environments with multiple restrictive constraints.

To address this issue, the paper proposes a modification to the ATACOM framework by introducing the concept of "Directional Constraints." The key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Improve the Safety-Performance Trade-off:** The overarching goal is to enable faster and more efficient learning while maintaining, or even enhancing, the safety guarantees of the original ATACOM framework. This aims to overcome the inherent compromise between safety and optimal task performance that often characterizes constrained RL problems.
2. **Facilitate Unrestricted Movement Away from Constraints:** The core motivation for directional constraints is to selectively apply the safety layer. Specifically, the objective is to morph an agent's intended action *only* when that action would move the system closer to a constraint boundary. Conversely, if an action would move the system away from a constraint, the safety layer should allow that action to pass largely unmodified. This asymmetrical application is designed to promote safe exploration by allowing the agent to quickly "escape" potentially unsafe regions and explore safer parts of the state space without undue restriction.
3. **Reduce Learning Time and Achieve Better Final Performance:** By enabling more effective exploration, the authors hypothesize that agents using ATACOM-DC will learn optimal policies more quickly and potentially achieve higher final task performance compared to the vanilla ATACOM, which might limit the search space excessively.
4. **Systematically Analyze Parameter Sensitivity:** Beyond introducing the new method, a specific objective is to rigorously analyze how key parameters of both ATACOM and the proposed ATACOM-DC affect the delicate balance between safety and performance. This analysis aims to provide insight into the robustness and tunability of the improved framework.

In essence, the motivation is to make safety layers "smarter" by differentiating between "dangerous" actions (approaching constraints) and "safe" actions (moving away from constraints), thereby leveraging the benefits of safety guarantees without unduly sacrificing exploration efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, ATACOM Directional Constraints (ATACOM-DC), extends the ATACOM framework by introducing a mechanism that selectively applies constraint enforcement based on the direction of the agent's proposed action relative to constraint boundaries.

**A. ATACOM Framework (Preliminaries)**
The ATACOM framework operates on control-affine dynamical systems (`ṡ = f(s) + G(s)u_s`) and assumes analytically defined constraints `k(s) ≤ 0`. It ensures safety by transforming the constrained problem into an unconstrained one on a constraint manifold. This is achieved by augmenting the state with slack variables `μ` to form `c(s, μ) = k(s) + μ = 0`. The control input `[u_s; u_μ]` is then designed to lie within the tangent space of this manifold. The safe control law incorporates a drift compensation term, a contraction term to drive the system back towards the manifold, and a tangential term that allows the RL agent's policy output `u` to operate within the safe space. Slack variables evolve with dynamics `μ_i = α_i(μ_i)u_μ,i`, where `α_i(μ_i) = exp(βμ_i) - 1`, and `β` is a parameter balancing safety and performance.

**B. Distributional ATACOM (D-ATACOM)**
D-ATACOM is an extension that removes the requirement for an analytical constraint function. Instead, it learns a Feasibility Value Function (FVF) to estimate expected constraint violations, utilizing distributional RL and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) to handle uncertainty. It also employs a dynamic threshold `δ` to mitigate constraint function fitting errors and enable meaningful exploration during initial training.

**C. Key Idea: Directional Constraints**
The core innovation of ATACOM-DC is to address the symmetric action morphing of vanilla ATACOM. Instead of always suppressing motion orthogonal to a constraint, ATACOM-DC only scales down actions that would move the robot *towards* a constraint boundary. Actions that move the robot *away* from a constraint are left largely unaffected.

To implement this, the method evaluates the effect of a sampled action `u_s` on each constraint `k_i(s)` by calculating the constraint derivative `ċ_i(s) = ψ_i(s) + J_i_k(s)G(s)u_s`.
- If `ċ_i > 0`, the constraint value is increasing, indicating the action is moving towards the constraint. In this case, the constraint is considered "active" for morphing.
- If `ċ_i ≤ 0`, the constraint value is decreasing or stable, indicating the action is moving away from or parallel to the constraint. In this case, the constraint is effectively "deactivated" for the morphing process, allowing the action to pass.

**D. Handling Multiple Constraints**
Extending directional constraints to multiple constraints involves leveraging the geometric properties of the problem. The method relies on two assumptions:
1. **Linearity of Constraint Derivatives:** All constraint derivatives are linear with respect to `u_s`.
2. **Drift Compensation:** It is assumed that the system drift can always be compensated, meaning `ψ(s) + J_u(s, μ) [u_s; u_μ] = 0` is solvable. This implies that the number of active constraints (those with zero slack) should not exceed the available action degrees of freedom.
3. **Non-inverting Null-Space Basis:** The null-space basis `B_u` (used for projecting the RL action) is assumed not to invert the sign of the actual system's actions, which is typically true when using smooth basis algorithms.

Under these assumptions, after compensating for the system drift, the "tangential" action sampled from the RL policy effectively defines a quadrant in the action space. Only constraints whose derivatives indicate movement towards the boundary within this quadrant are considered for action morphing. Constraints for which the action pushes away are disregarded.

**E. ATACOM-DC Algorithm (Algorithm 1)**
The complete algorithm proceeds as follows:
1. **Determine Slack Variables:** `μ` is calculated based on current constraint violations `k(s)`.
2. **Compute Jacobians and Drift:** Relevant Jacobians (`J_G`, `J_u`) and the constraint drift `ψ(s)` are computed.
3. **Compute Constraint Derivatives:** The derivatives `ċ(s,u)` are calculated for all constraints, assuming the drift is compensated by the ATACOM controller.
4. **Disable Constraints:** For constraints where `ċ(s,u) < 0` (action moves away), their corresponding entries in the `A(μ)` matrix (which relates to slack dynamics) are conceptually removed or, in practice, set to a sufficiently large constant `μ_η` to effectively deactivate them from the morphing calculation.
5. **Compute Directional Jacobian and Basis:** A modified Jacobian `J_u_dir(s, μ)` is constructed using only the active (non-disabled) constraints. A smooth basis `B_u` is computed based on this `J_u_dir`.
6. **Compute Safe Action:** The final safe action `u_s` is computed using the ATACOM control law (Eq. 2), but now utilizing the modified `J_u_dir` and `B_u`, ensuring drift compensation and tangential movement only with respect to the active constraints.

This approach retains the theoretical safety guarantees of the original ATACOM under ideal conditions (perfect model and constraints) because it only alters behavior when the proposed action is already "safe" (moving away from a constraint). For computational efficiency in batched environments, "disabling" a constraint is implemented by setting its `A_ii` entry to a large constant instead of explicitly removing rows, maintaining consistent matrix dimensions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ATACOM-DC was conducted across three simulated robotic control tasks: Kuka iiwa air hockey, planar air hockey (controlled in velocity and acceleration), and quadrotor navigation. The proposed method was compared against an unconstrained Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) baseline, the original ATACOM framework, and the Distributional ATACOM (D-ATACOM). Each experiment was repeated over 15 independent random seeds. Task performance metrics (e.g., success rate, puck velocity, distance to target) and safety metrics (episodic cost, defined as cumulative `max(k(s_t), 0)`) were recorded.

**A. Performance against Unconstrained Methods (SAC)**
- **Kuka iiwa air hockey:** The unconstrained SAC algorithm was unable to learn in this complex, high-dimensional task without explicit safety constraint information, thus no results were reported for this baseline.
- **Quadrotor and Planar Air Hockey (Acceleration):** In these tasks, the final policy learned by SAC often violated constraints, albeit achieving high task performance. This suggests that the optimal unconstrained policy for these tasks might inherently involve unsafe maneuvers.
- **ATACOM-DC:** In contrast, ATACOM-DC consistently maintained safety throughout the entire training process in both scenarios. It achieved final task performance comparable to or sometimes exceeding SAC, with learning curves that were generally better or equivalent, indicating its ability to learn effectively without compromising safety.

**B. Impact of Directional Constraints (ATACOM-DC vs. Vanilla ATACOM)**
- Across all evaluated tasks (Kuka iiwa air hockey, planar air hockey, quadrotor), ATACOM-DC demonstrated consistent advantages over the vanilla ATACOM algorithm.
- **Faster Learning and Improved Performance:** ATACOM-DC led to significantly faster learning convergence and generally achieved higher final task performance. This was particularly evident in the Kuka iiwa air hockey task (Figure 5), where the less restrictive exploration allowed for the learning of faster and more precise policies, resulting in higher success rates and puck velocities.
- **Safety Guarantees:** Constraint violations were generally reduced or remained comparable to vanilla ATACOM. Although initial epochs might show slightly higher violations due to less restrictive exploration, this behavior allowed the agent to learn to move away from unsafe areas more effectively, leading to lower long-term constraint violations. All approaches ultimately achieved very low, near-zero constraint violation costs. The minor violations observed in Kuka air hockey at higher speeds were attributed to model inaccuracies not accounted for in the low-level controller.

**C. D-ATACOM Improvements (D-ATACOM-DC vs. D-ATACOM)**
- **Planar Air Hockey (Velocity):** D-ATACOM-DC, when integrated with D-ATACOM, showed comparable safety performance to the vanilla D-ATACOM but provided a boost in overall learning performance.
- **Planar Air Hockey (Acceleration):** No clear statistical improvement in either performance or safety was observed for D-ATACOM-DC in this scenario.
- **Direct Constraint Learning:** The research explored learning constraints directly instead of relying on the Feasibility Value Function (FVF) within D-ATACOM. When combining D-ATACOM-DC with direct constraint learning and fixing the dynamic threshold `δ` (removing automatic tuning and initial warm-up trajectories), a notable performance boost was observed in terms of learning speed and safety. This "D-ATACOM-DC-constraint" variant outperformed other approaches, largely due to reduced early unsafe exploration and faster convergence without the overhead of temporal difference learning for FVF.
- **Sensitivity to δ Parameter:** An analysis of the `δ` parameter (which controls the safety margin) revealed that D-ATACOM-DC consistently yielded higher success rates and lower episodic costs across various `δ` values compared to D-ATACOM. This suggests that D-ATACOM-DC offers a Pareto-optimal advantage in the safety-performance trade-off for different levels of desired safety margins.

**D. Beta Analysis (Performance-Safety Trade-off)**
- An ablation study on the `β` parameter (which defines the slack dynamics and thus the safety margin) showed that ATACOM-DC enabled effective operation even when a larger safety margin was required (i.e., higher `β` values), without degrading performance. This indicates that ATACOM-DC improves the fundamental performance-safety trade-off, presenting a Pareto-superior behavior compared to vanilla ATACOM in the tested experimental settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ATACOM-DC represents a methodological advancement in Safe Reinforcement Learning, specifically addressing the trade-off between ensuring safety and facilitating efficient exploration. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Improved Exploration Efficiency in Safe RL:** The primary contribution of ATACOM-DC is its ability to selectively apply safety constraints. By distinguishing between actions that move towards a constraint boundary (where enforcement is critical) and those that move away (where it is not), the method allows for a more permissive and thus more efficient exploration strategy. This directly mitigates a known limitation of prior safety layers, including the original ATACOM, which can be overly conservative.

2. **Enhanced Learning Speed and Task Performance:** Experimental results consistently demonstrate that ATACOM-DC leads to faster learning convergence and, in many cases, superior final task performance compared to the vanilla ATACOM. This accelerated learning and improved policy quality are crucial for deploying RL in real-world robotics, where training time and peak performance are key considerations.

3. **Maintained or Improved Safety Guarantees:** Crucially, these performance gains do not come at the expense of safety. ATACOM-DC maintains, and in some settings, even improves, the long-term safety of the system by enabling the agent to quickly learn how to move into safer regions of the state space. The method's theoretical underpinnings ensure that its safety guarantees are preserved under ideal model and constraint conditions, as it only modifies behavior for actions already deemed safe.

4. **Robustness and Better Trade-offs:** The analysis of parameters like `β` and `δ` indicates that ATACOM-DC exhibits greater robustness and offers a more favorable performance-safety trade-off. It allows for effective operation even with larger safety margins, and for various safety threshold settings, it achieves better performance with lower costs (Pareto-dominance). This provides practitioners with more flexibility in tuning safety parameters without severely compromising learning outcomes.

5. **Simplification of Learning Architectures:** The finding that direct constraint learning, when combined with ATACOM-DC and a fixed safety threshold, can outperform methods relying on Feasibility Value Functions (FVFs) and dynamic threshold tuning, is significant. This suggests that for certain system types (e.g., first-order systems), the complexity of temporal difference learning for FVFs might be unnecessary, leading to faster and more accurate safety learning.

6. **Broader Applicability in Robotics:** The demonstrated effectiveness across diverse robotic control tasks (manipulation, navigation) implies that ATACOM-DC can be broadly applicable to various safety-critical robotic systems. By making safe exploration more tractable, it reduces barriers to deploying sophisticated RL policies on physical robots, potentially enabling more autonomous and capable robotic systems in industries ranging from manufacturing to logistics and service robotics.

In conclusion, ATACOM-DC significantly contributes to the field of Safe Reinforcement Learning by refining how safety constraints are enforced, leading to more efficient, faster, and often better-performing learning agents without sacrificing safety. This work paves the way for more practical and robust real-world applications of deep reinforcement learning in safety-critical domains. Future work aims to integrate these advancements with cutting-edge off-policy actor-critic algorithms to facilitate rapid learning from scratch in highly complex real-world environments.
