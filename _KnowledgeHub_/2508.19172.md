# From Tabula Rasa to Emergent Abilities: Discovering Robot Skills via Real-World Unsupervised Quality-Diversity

> [!summary] Summary
> The URSA framework enables a Unitree A1 quadruped robot to autonomously discover diverse, high-performing locomotion skills directly in the real world, without human supervision, by combining Quality-Diversity algorithms with a world model and safety constraints. This system successfully demonstrates enhanced adaptability to physical damage and achieves greater behavioral diversity than prior methods.

## Problem

- Traditional Quality-Diversity methods for robot skill discovery require manually defined behavior descriptors or skill spaces, limiting their applicability to complex or novel scenarios.
- Applying advanced skill discovery directly to physical robots is challenging due to limited data efficiency, safety concerns, and the lack of reliable reset mechanisms.
- Existing robot learning methods often rely on carefully tuned heuristics, reducing autonomy and generalizability for real-world deployment.

## Method

- **URSA** (Unsupervised Real-world Skill Acquisition) proposes a novel framework extending ==QDAC== with a learned latent skill space via a ==VAE==, eliminating the need for manual skill definition.
- It integrates ==safety-aware optimization== with a ==min-max Lagrangian formulation== and efficient skill sampling using a ==Gaussian KDE== over a dynamic repertoire, preventing unsafe behaviors and promoting diverse exploration.
- Leveraging the ==DayDreamer== world model architecture, **URSA** performs asynchronous real-world data collection and imagination-based training, enabling sample-efficient learning directly on physical hardware.

## Results

- URSA discovered a wide variety of distinct locomotion patterns, achieving **four times greater coverage** in the discretized joint angle space compared to DayDreamer and DOMiNiC.
- In real-world damage adaptation experiments, URSA combined with ITE outperformed DayDreamer in most scenarios, demonstrating efficient recovery from physical damage (e.g., recovering from back-left leg damage in **8 trials**).
- The system successfully learned a repertoire that spanned a broad range of target forward and angular velocities in a heuristic-based setting, confirming its ability to discover controllable skills for real-world, target-driven control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining unsupervised latent skill space learning with Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms allows for the emergent discovery of diverse robot behaviors without human-specified features.
> - Integrating a model-based reinforcement learning backbone (like DayDreamer) with QD principles enables sample-efficient and safe skill acquisition directly on physical robots, overcoming significant real-world learning constraints.
> - Explicit safety constraints and adaptive skill repertoire management are crucial for practical, continuous, and robust unsupervised skill discovery in real-world robotic systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of **autonomous skill discovery, Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms, reinforcement learning (RL), and real-world robotics**. The broader goal in this landscape is to equip robots with the animal-like ability to autonomously acquire diverse behaviors, enabling them to operate effectively in dynamic, unpredictable environments beyond narrow, pre-defined tasks.

Historically, specialized robots have excelled in specific domains (e.g., PCB insertion, legged locomotion) but lack generalizability. A key challenge for general-purpose robots is adapting to unforeseen circumstances, such as physical damage or varied terrains. Quality-Diversity algorithms emerged as a promising approach to generate diverse and high-performing behavioral repertoires, but they traditionally suffered from requiring manually defined "behavior descriptors" or "skill spaces" and carefully tuned heuristics, limiting their applicability to novel or complex scenarios. Recent advancements in unsupervised skill discovery have sought to overcome the need for hand-crafted descriptors by learning these representations directly from data.

However, a significant hurdle remains: applying these sophisticated skill discovery methods directly to **physical robots in the real world**. Real-world robot learning is inherently challenging due to constraints such as limited data efficiency (physical interactions are time-consuming and costly), safety concerns (unsafe behaviors can damage hardware), and the lack of reliable reset mechanisms typical in simulation. Most existing unsupervised QD methods either rely heavily on simulation, which then necessitates robust but often difficult sim-to-real transfer, or are too sample-inefficient for practical real-world deployment (e.g., traditional evolutionary algorithms). While model-based RL techniques like DayDreamer, SERL, and "A Walk in the Park" have made strides in sample efficiency for real-world learning, they often focus on single-skill acquisition or require specific reward functions. Other methods like off-DADS address real-world skill discovery but do not consider extrinsic rewards.

This work, **Unsupervised Real-world Skill Acquisition (URSA)**, directly addresses these critical gaps. It builds upon the Quality-Diversity Actor-Critic (QDAC) framework developed by the same authors and integrates it with the sample-efficient DayDreamer world model. By doing so, URSA aims to create a framework that can *autonomously discover diverse, high-performing skills directly on physical hardware*, without explicit supervision, while simultaneously ensuring safety and maintaining data efficiency. It represents a significant step towards enabling continuous, unsupervised, and safe skill learning for truly autonomous and adaptive robots, moving beyond the reliance on simulation or predefined objectives.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that animals exhibit remarkable adaptability and a wide range of movement patterns developed through autonomous interaction with their environment. The authors aim to imbue robots with a similar capacity: the ability to learn diverse behaviors through self-directed experience, which is crucial for their deployment in dynamic and unpredictable real-world settings. While specialized robots are effective in their narrow domains, they lack the versatility needed for general-purpose applications. A robot equipped with a diverse skill set, as demonstrated by prior work in damage adaptation and varied navigation, can adapt to novel tasks and conditions.

However, existing approaches to skill discovery face several limitations that URSA seeks to overcome:
1. **Manual Definition of Skill Spaces:** Traditional Quality-Diversity (QD) methods, while effective for diversity, typically require human-defined behavior descriptors or skill spaces, which are difficult to define for complex, emergent behaviors.
2. **Real-World Applicability Challenges:** Learning directly on physical hardware is severely constrained by safety requirements (preventing damage to the robot) and data efficiency (physical interactions are costly and time-consuming). Many advanced QD and unsupervised RL methods remain largely confined to simulation due to these practical barriers.
3. **Heuristic Tuning:** Existing methods often rely on carefully tuned heuristics, which further limits their autonomy and generalizability.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of this paper are explicitly articulated through its contributions:

- **Propose URSA:** To introduce a novel framework for unsupervised Quality-Diversity that extends the existing QDAC algorithm. This extension includes a learned latent skill space (eliminating manual definition), safety-aware optimization (addressing real-world safety concerns), and efficient sampling strategies (improving data efficiency).
- **Demonstrate Real-World Learning:** To show that URSA can successfully learn diverse locomotion behaviors directly on a physical robot (specifically, a Unitree A1 quadruped) in the real world. This objective emphasizes the capability of the system to operate without reliance on simulation or complex sim-to-real transfer techniques, utilizing imagination-based training with world models.
- **Enable Downstream Task Adaptation:** To prove the utility of the learned skill repertoire by demonstrating its effectiveness in downstream tasks, such as adapting to physical damage. This objective highlights the practical value of diverse skill sets for enhancing robot resilience and autonomy.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to move towards a paradigm where robots can continuously discover and master diverse, high-performing skills with minimal human intervention, thereby becoming more autonomous, adaptable, and resilient robotic systems suitable for varied real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Unsupervised Real-world Skill Acquisition (URSA)** framework is designed to autonomously discover diverse robot skills directly on physical hardware, addressing the limitations of prior work. It builds upon two foundational components: the Quality-Diversity Actor-Critic (QDAC) algorithm for multi-skill learning and the DayDreamer world model for sample-efficient real-world learning.

**Problem Formulation:**
URSA models the environment as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and defines a "skill" `z` as the expected feature vector `E_π[ϕ(s, a)]` under a policy's stationary distribution. The core objective is twofold: (1) maximize the volume of the "achieved skill space" (behavioral diversity) and (2) ensure that each discovered skill is executed with a high expected return (performance). To manage this complex objective, URSA introduces a surrogate probability distribution `q(·)` over the skill space and aims to learn a skill-conditioned policy `π_z` that maximizes return while matching the sampled skill `z`, subject to safety and skill-matching constraints.

**Key Extensions and Components of URSA:**

1. **Unsupervised Skill Space Learning:**
 - Instead of manually defining behavior descriptors, URSA learns a compact, latent skill representation `ϕ(·)` from raw state observations.
 - This is achieved using a **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**, which encodes zeroth-order kinematics (e.g., joint angles, body height) into a low-dimensional latent space. The VAE is trained on the diverse set of states collected within the learned skill repertoire `R`. This allows for the discovery of meaningful skills without human-provided features.

2. **Safety-Aware Optimization:**
 - To prevent unsafe behaviors (e.g., falls) in real-world settings, URSA incorporates **safety constraints** into its optimization problem.
 - A user-provided cost function `c(s)` defines safe states `S_safe`. A cost critic `C(s, z)` is trained to predict future costs, and the policy is optimized to ensure `C(s, z) ≤ 0`.
 - This constrained optimization is solved using a **min-max Lagrangian formulation**, where Lagrange multipliers (`λ_1`, `λ_2`) dynamically balance reward maximization, skill tracking, and safety. Clipped multipliers improve stability during training.

3. **Efficient Skill Sampling from the Reachable Space:**
 - URSA maintains a **repertoire `R`** of discovered skills, which are features `ϕ_t` of safe states.
 - An adaptive skill distribution `q(·)` is modeled as a **Gaussian Kernel Density Estimator (KDE)** fitted on `R`. This non-parametric approach allows flexible representation of the reachable skill space.
 - To maximize diversity and promote uniform exploration, URSA continuously updates `R` by replacing existing skills with newly discovered safe features that increase the estimated entropy of `q(·)`. This is done by adding a new skill and removing the skill from the repertoire (including the new one) that has the smallest nearest-neighbor distance.
 - A **dynamic constraint threshold `δ`** (for `QDAC`'s skill-matching constraint) is adaptively tuned. It scales with the repertoire's density and the target number of distinct skills `N_z`, ensuring that each learned skill is sufficiently distinct.

4. **Real-World Efficiency with DayDreamer Backbone:**
 - URSA leverages the **DayDreamer world model** architecture, enabling efficient learning directly on hardware.
 - This involves **asynchronous data collection and training**:
 - **Actor Thread (Data Collection - Algorithm 1):** Samples skills from the KDE-based `q(·)`, interacts with the real robot, collects real-world experience, and adds transitions to a replay buffer `D`. If the robot's state is safe, the extracted feature `ϕ_t` is committed to the repertoire `R`.
 - **Learner Thread (Training - Algorithm 2):** Uses the collected data to train a world model `W`. Crucially, it then generates **imagined rollouts** within this world model, conditioned on sampled skills, to efficiently update the value function `V`, successor features `ψ`, cost critic `C`, policy `π`, and the VAE encoder `ϕ` (if unsupervised). This imagination-based training significantly reduces the need for costly real-world interactions.

In essence, URSA provides a comprehensive framework that combines QD principles for diverse, high-performing skill acquisition with model-based RL for real-world data efficiency, all while incorporating unsupervised feature learning and explicit safety constraints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluate URSA on a Unitree A1 quadruped robot in both PyBullet simulation and real-world environments, comparing its performance against two baselines: DayDreamer (a single-skill learner) and DOMiNiC (a multi-skill learner with near-optimality constraints, set to 8 skills). The evaluation focuses on three key questions (RQ1-RQ3).

#### 4.1. RQ1: Unsupervised Discovery of Diverse and High-Performing Locomotion Skills

- **Experiment:** URSA was trained to acquire forward movement skills in an unsupervised setting, where `ϕ_t` (the skill feature) was a 2-dimensional latent vector encoded by a VAE from joint angles and torso height. The reward encouraged forward velocity and stability, with a hand-defined safe state for upright posture.
- **Results:** URSA successfully discovered a wide variety of distinct locomotion patterns, evident from the diverse distribution of average joint angles across different leg combinations (Figure 3) and illustrative examples of gaits (Figure 1). Quantitatively, URSA achieved **four times greater coverage** in the discretized joint angle space compared to DayDreamer and DOMiNiC, demonstrating significantly more behavioral diversity. DayDreamer converged on a single optimal strategy, while DOMiNiC, though learning multiple skills, exhibited less joint-level diversity due to its strong near-optimality constraint.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Downstream Use of Skills for Damage Adaptation

- **Experiment:** The utility of URSA's learned repertoire was tested in adapting to various physical damage scenarios, including single joint failures and full leg failures, in both simulation and the real world. For adaptation, URSA was combined with Iterative Trial and Error (ITE), which uses Bayesian optimization to select the best skill from the repertoire with few trials.
- **Simulation (Figure 4):** URSA + ITE outperformed both DOMiNiC and DayDreamer in scenarios involving upper-leg joint damage. However, for more severe damage (e.g., full leg failures), DOMiNiC's near-optimality constraint proved more beneficial. The upper bound of URSA (executing all skills and taking the best) consistently showed strong performance. A complementary cumulative distribution function (CCDF) analysis (Figure 7) revealed that URSA and URSA + ITE exhibited less performance degradation overall across the damage scenarios, indicating greater robustness.
- **Real-World (Figure 5):** URSA + ITE outperformed DayDreamer in all real-world damage scenarios except one (back-right leg failure). URSA demonstrated reduced overall performance degradation under damage, effectively leveraging its diverse skill repertoire. The ITE mechanism allowed URSA to progressively improve its total return within a few iterations (e.g., recovering from back-left leg damage in 8 trials), highlighting its efficient adaptation. The training process showed consistent improvement in average rewards, indicating a repertoire of high-performing behaviors. In contrast, DayDreamer, optimized for a single task, experienced significant performance drops when front legs were damaged. Interestingly, URSA also achieved higher returns in the undamaged setting, suggesting that behavioral diversity can serve as a stepping stone to discovering high-performing strategies.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Heuristic-Based Skill Discovery for Velocity Tracking

- **Experiment:** To assess the controllability of discovered skills, URSA was tested in a heuristic-based setting where `ϕ(s_t)` was explicitly defined as forward and angular velocities (`[v_x,t, ω_z,t]`). The reward promoted stable posture and smooth movement.
- **Results (Figure 6):** URSA successfully learned a repertoire that spanned a broad range of target forward and angular velocities. The system was capable of real-world, target-driven control. However, tracking performance degraded as target velocities deviated significantly from zero, particularly for forward velocity. This was attributed to the fact that some target velocities, while instantaneously achievable, could not be consistently sustained throughout the entire duration of a skill. This result confirms URSA's ability to discover controllable skills even with heuristic-defined features.

In summary, URSA successfully demonstrates unsupervised discovery of diverse and high-performing locomotion skills, effectively leverages this repertoire for real-world damage adaptation, and shows capability for target-driven control using heuristic features. Its performance indicates a significant step forward in autonomous and adaptable robot learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of **Unsupervised Real-world Skill Acquisition (URSA)** represents a significant advancement in robot learning, particularly for autonomous and adaptable robotic systems. The work's primary significance lies in its ability to bridge critical gaps in the existing research landscape, offering a practical framework for robots to learn diverse, high-performing skills directly on physical hardware without explicit human supervision.

The **key contributions and their implications** are manifold:

1. **Overcoming the Need for Manual Skill Definition:** By learning latent skill representations through a VAE, URSA liberates robots from the laborious and often limiting requirement of human-crafted behavior descriptors. This "tabula rasa" approach enables the discovery of novel and unexpected behaviors, potentially leading to more creative and robust solutions than what human designers might conceive. This is crucial for equipping robots to handle truly unknown environments.

2. **Enabling Safe and Data-Efficient Real-World Learning:** The integration of DayDreamer's imagination-based training with explicit safety constraints is a game-changer for real-world robot deployment. It drastically reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming physical interactions, making skill discovery practical and affordable. The safety-aware optimization ensures that exploration does not lead to hardware damage, a critical concern that often limits real-world experimentation. This framework fosters continuous learning directly on the robot, moving beyond the sim-to-real gap.

3. **Enhanced Adaptability and Resilience:** The demonstrated capability of URSA's learned repertoire to facilitate adaptation to physical damage is a direct and impactful benefit. Robots equipped with such diverse skill sets can autonomously recover from unexpected failures, extending their operational lifespan and reducing the need for costly human intervention or repair. This moves robots closer to the adaptability seen in biological systems, which is essential for deployments in unstructured and unpredictable environments like disaster zones or extraterrestrial exploration.

4. **Foundation for General-Purpose Robotics:** By enabling robots to autonomously discover a wide repertoire of behaviors, URSA lays a crucial foundation for the development of truly general-purpose robots. Such robots would not be limited to pre-programmed tasks but could dynamically learn and adapt to changing tasks and conditions, exhibiting emergent intelligence and versatile problem-solving capabilities.

5. **A New Paradigm for Continuous Skill Discovery:** URSA establishes a framework for continuous skill discovery, where robots can progressively expand and refine their behavioral repertoires throughout their operational lifetime. This contrasts with episodic learning paradigms that typically rely on resets and restart from scratch. The dynamic management of the skill repertoire, guided by entropy maximization and adaptive thresholds, ensures ongoing exploration and improvement.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the reliance on specific input state variables for the VAE or the lack of fully reset-free operation, these also highlight fruitful avenues for future research that, if addressed, will further amplify URSA's impact. The work represents a significant stride toward a future where robots are not just task-specific tools but autonomous, adaptive, and resilient agents capable of continuously learning and evolving in the real world.
