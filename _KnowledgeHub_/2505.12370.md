# Enhancing Visual Grounding for GUI Agents via Self-Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SE-GUI is a reinforcement learning framework that enhances visual grounding for GUI agents using a self-evolutionary mechanism and dense point rewards. It achieves 47.3% accuracy on the ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark, outperforming larger models while utilizing only 3,018 high-quality training samples.

## Problem

- Existing GUI agents trained with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) struggle with data efficiency and generalization, particularly in complex, high-resolution environments.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for GUI grounding face sparse reward issues, where binary success/failure signals impede effective early-stage training.
- The quality of initial training data for GUI tasks is often low due to noisy annotations and weak alignment, negatively impacting model learning.

## Method

- A rigorous three-fold ==data curation== process filters 300,000 samples down to 3,018 high-quality "seed" examples (**SE-GUI**-3k) for initial training.
- A dense point reward mechanism, combined with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), provides continuous feedback during RL training, allowing learning from imprecise predictions.
- A self-evolutionary reinforcement fine-tuning (SE-RFT) loop iteratively refines the model by using its own attention maps to filter low-quality training signals.

## Results

- SE-GUI-**7B** achieved **47.3%** accuracy on the ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art UI-TARS-**72B** (**38.1%**) by **24.2%** while using significantly fewer parameters and less training data.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization across other benchmarks, achieving **88.2%** on ScreenSpot and **90.3%** on ScreenSpot-v2, and outperforming baselines like GPT-4o and UGround-V1 in zero-shot agent evaluation tasks on AndroidControl and OmniACT.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of the components, showing the dense point reward alone led to a **4.21%** improvement, and the self-evolutionary process consistently improved performance across iterative stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, meticulously curated seed data, even in small quantities, significantly improves the effectiveness of reinforcement learning for GUI agents.
> - Dense, continuous reward signals based on prediction proximity are crucial for guiding models to achieve precise localization in complex visual environments, overcoming the limitations of sparse rewards.
> - A self-evolutionary loop, where the model leverages its own attention maps to filter training data, enables continuous performance improvement and enhances the quality of learning signals.
