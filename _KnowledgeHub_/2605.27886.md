# Tabero: Learning Gentle Manipulation with Closed-Loop Force Feedback from Vision, Touch, and Language

> [!summary] Summary
> Nvidia and Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) researchers introduced Tabero, a framework that provides a scalable vision-tactile-language dataset and a VTLA model suite for gentle, language-conditioned robotic manipulation. It integrates closed-loop force feedback to enable robots to modulate interaction forces based on linguistic cues.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack tactile feedback, preventing them from performing force-sensitive tasks requiring nuanced physical interaction.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality datasets that align vision, tactile, and language modalities with corresponding robot actions, hindering VTLA model development.
- Current evaluation benchmarks for robotic manipulation primarily rely on task success rates, overlooking critical aspects of physical interaction quality like applied forces or gentleness.

## Method

- The **Tabero** benchmark provides a ==data-efficient pipeline== for generating diverse vision-tactile-language datasets by repurposing open-source robot trajectories through ==high-fidelity tactile simulation==.
- Tabero introduces a ==multidimensional evaluation protocol== that assesses task success alongside ==process-aware metrics== for physical interaction quality (e.g., Maximum Transient Grip Force, Average Applied Force).
- **Tabero-VTLA** proposes a novel architecture featuring a ==decoupled force-position command interface==, executed by a fixed ==hybrid controller==, to enable real-time, force-aware manipulation.

## Results

- Equipping the robot with a tactile sensor-integrated gripper led to a measurable drop in task success rates, validating the inherent challenges of gentle manipulation.
- Tabero-VTLA models with force-field tactile injection and explicit force supervision achieved firm task success rates of **0.86** and gentle success rates of **0.52**, significantly reducing average grip forces to **3.7 N** for gentle tasks.
- The model demonstrated an ability to adjust grip force based on linguistic adverbs (e.g., 'firmly' vs. 'gently') and generalized to some out-of-domain terms, showing zero-shot compositional understanding of force-related language.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating rich tactile representations (like marker motion fields) with explicit force supervision is crucial for enabling effective, gentleness-aware manipulation policies.
> - A decoupled hybrid controller, which independently manages grip force and applied force, is essential for accurate real-time force tracking and compliant physical interaction.
> - VTLA models can learn to semantically interpret linguistic adverbs related to force, allowing for intuitive and flexible control over interaction intensity.
