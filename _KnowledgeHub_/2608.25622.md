# Plans You Can Check: Verifier-Grounded Learning of an Open-Weight Planner for Executable Video-Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> RefineCut trains a compact open-weight 8B planner that turns a brief, clip pool, and music metadata into an executable video-editing timeline through structured JSON patches. A deterministic verifier checks each patch against an explicit constraint ledger. Multi-teacher traces are replayed through the verifier to keep only verifier-best repairs, then the student self-improves with preference training. Video-Editing Score rises from 0.620 to 0.924, matching or exceeding frontier teachers.

## Problem

- Existing video generation and instruction-editing models work at the pixel or single-shot level and produce no clip-level plan over a real footage pool, leaving the editing decision layer unaddressed.
- Workflow systems for mashup and dubbing wrap a frozen prompted frontier model in a hand-built scaffold, so the planning policy is never optimized against hard constraints and stays locked to closed models.
- Editing has no single ground-truth repair, so imitating a teacher's first-choice branch yields noisy labels, and planner training lacks checkable supervision against explicit briefs and constraints.

## Method

- **RefinePatch** formulation: the planner edits a ==typed timeline== through RFC 6902-style ==JSON patches==, and a ==deterministic verifier== applies each patch and recomputes a seven-family ==constraint ledger== instead of using an LLM judge.
- ==Verifier-replayed trajectory distillation==: canonicalizes traces from three frontier teachers, replays every candidate branch through the verifier, and keeps ==verifier-best branches== as SFT targets and ==mixed-granularity preference pairs==.
- **RefineCut-Evo**: the student samples four repairs per state, scores them with verifier plus a seven-part rubric, blends the two scores with weight 0.65 on the verifier, and trains ==DPO== on ==high-margin pairs==. Inference runs a ==closed verifier loop== of at most three steps.

## Results

- On RefineCut-Bench Common-100, Video-Editing Score climbs from **0.620** for raw imitation to **0.858** with verified SFT and **0.924** with RefineCut-Evo. Hard-constraint pass rises **0.670** to **0.820** and convergence **0.800** to **0.950**.
- Verified SFT beats raw imitation using a training set **three times smaller** (**3,317** vs **9,690** examples). Even verifier-best teacher branches satisfy the full ledger in at most **3%** of single steps.
- Evo beats GPT-5.4 by **0.030** VES and Qwen3-Max by **0.150**, and statistically ties DeepSeek-V4-Pro. Verified SFT lifts Llama-3.1-8B by **0.153** and GLM-4-9B by **0.079**, while raw imitation hurts both.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Where outputs are checkable, replaying candidate branches through a deterministic verifier turns noisy multi-teacher traces into reliable supervision, and filtering by execution beats adding more raw imitation data.
> - In self-improvement, choosing which preference pairs to train on matters more than how finely each is scored: dropping the high-margin filter cost 0.027, and verifier-only scoring already captured most of the gain.
> - Explicit constraint ledgers make an open-ended creative task learnable by a small model. Caution: the score comes from the same verifier used in training, so it is in-protocol, not independent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of AI-assisted content creation has seen advancements in generating and transforming visual content, particularly with text-to-video and image-to-video models focusing on pixel generation (e.g., Zhuang et al., 2024; Long et al., 2024) and instruction-editing frameworks modifying content within single shots (e.g., Yoon et al., 2025). However, these approaches often operate at a lower level of abstraction or sidestep the complex decision-making involved in practical video editing.

Real-world video editing encompasses a "decision layer" where an editor must select, trim, order clips, apply transitions, and align cuts to music, all while adhering to various constraints (e.g., target duration, clip inclusion/exclusion, pacing, music synchronization). Existing workflow-oriented systems (e.g., Li et al., 2026a; Zhang et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2024) address this decision layer by wrapping a prompted, frozen frontier model within a hand-built scaffold. While these systems orchestrate complex tasks, their planning policy remains implicit, cannot be directly optimized against hard constraints, and is often tied to closed-source models. This limits their adaptability and interpretability.

RefineCut positions itself within this landscape by focusing explicitly on this constrained "planning layer" of video editing. It proposes to train a compact, open-weight planner that learns these editing decisions directly, rather than relying on implicit prompting of large, static models. This approach leverages the key insight that the outputs of the editing decision layer, unlike open-ended pixel generation, can be formally checked and verified against explicit specifications. This verifiability provides a pathway for supervised learning and self-improvement, drawing parallels with work in verifiable domains like mathematical reasoning and code generation (e.g., Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024).

The work builds upon advancements in tool-executable planner learning (e.g., Zeng et al., 2024; Song et al., 2024a) and self-improvement techniques like EvoLM (Li et al., 2026b) and Rubric-Grounded RL (Bhattarai et al., 2026). However, RefineCut differentiates itself by using a deterministic editing verifier as the primary training signal, rather than a general LLM judge or human feedback, for its core learning process. By releasing an open-weight planner and a dedicated benchmark (RefineCut-Bench), the research aims to establish a new paradigm for verifiable, planning-level AI in video editing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind RefineCut stems from a perceived gap in current AI-assisted video editing systems. The authors identify that practical video editing goes beyond merely generating pixels or altering content within a single shot. Instead, it involves intricate decision-making to transform a brief, a pool of clips, and optional music metadata into an executable timeline, all while satisfying explicit and often hard constraints. This constitutes a "decision layer" or "constrained planning problem."

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the Implicit Nature of Current Planning:** Existing workflow-oriented systems often manage the decision layer by prompting large, frozen frontier models. In these systems, the planning logic is embedded implicitly within the prompt scaffolding, making the planning policy unoptimizable against specific constraints and unadaptable. A central objective of RefineCut is to make this planning explicit and learnable.

2. **Develop an Open-Weight, Compact Planner:** There is a lack of compact, open-weight planners capable of learning complex editing decisions and sitting in front of any rendering backend. The motivation is to create such a planner, making the technology more accessible, transparent, and amenable to community-driven improvements, in contrast to reliance on expensive, closed-source models.

3. **Leverage Output Verifiability:** A core insight is that the outputs of the editing decision layer, specifically an "executable edit plan," are inherently checkable. Unlike open-ended pixel generation, whether an edit plan meets explicit requirements (e.g., duration, clip inclusion) can be deterministically verified. This property motivates the formalization of the problem as "executable video-editing planning."

4. **Formalize Executable Video-Editing Planning:** The work aims to formally define the task. This involves providing the planner with a natural-language brief, a real clip pool with metadata, optional music metadata, an initial timeline state, and, crucially, an *explicit constraint ledger*. The planner's output is a `RefinePatch`, an RFC 6902-style JSON Patch, which modifies a typed timeline state.

5. **Develop a Deterministic Verifier:** A key objective is to create a deterministic verifier that can apply each `RefinePatch` and recompute the ledger's satisfaction status. This verifier serves as a reliable, machine-checkable mechanism to validate editing decisions against explicit requirements, departing from reliance on learned LLM judges.

6. **Overcome the "No Single Ground-Truth Repair" Challenge:** Video editing often allows for multiple valid solutions for a given brief. Directly imitating noisy teacher traces from frontier models, which may disagree or offer suboptimal first choices, can lead to inconsistent learning. The motivation is to develop a learning mechanism that can canonicalize and arbitrate these noisy traces by replaying them through the deterministic verifier, thereby converting them into consistently graded supervision signals.

7. **Enable Self-Improvement:** The framework aims to allow the student planner to improve beyond initial distillation. This involves a mechanism where the student can generate its own repair candidates, score them against the verifier and a task-specific rubric, and learn from high-margin preference pairs to continuously refine its policy.

8. **Create a Dedicated Benchmark:** To support the research and enable reproducible evaluation, a detailed benchmark, RefineCut-Bench, is developed and released. This benchmark integrates real clip/music metadata, explicit constraint ledgers, and multi-teacher trajectories, along with a deterministic verifier-based evaluation protocol.

In summary, the core objective is to move beyond implicit, black-box approaches to video editing by establishing a principled, verifiable, and learnable framework for the decision layer, enabling the creation of compact, open-weight AI planners competitive with or superior to larger frontier models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RefineCut is structured as a two-stage learning framework, followed by a closed-loop runtime deployment. The methodology centers on the principle that video editing decisions can be learned and verified using a deterministic verifier.

#### 3.1. Task Definition and Components

- **Task Instance:** A task `x` is defined as a tuple `(b, C, M, s₀, L)`, comprising a natural-language brief (`b`), a real clip pool (`C`) with captions and metadata, optional music metadata (`M`), an initial timeline state (`s₀`), and an explicit constraint ledger (`L`).
- **Planner Output:** The planner (`π_θ`) emits a `RefinePatch` (`p_t`), an RFC 6902-style JSON Patch, over a typed timeline state.
- **Deterministic Verifier:** A deterministic verifier applies `p_t` to update the state (`s_{t+1} = Apply(s_t, p_t)`) and recomputes ledger satisfaction (`L_{t+1} = Verify(s_{t+1})`).
- **Objective:** To reach a ledger-satisfying state within a small repair budget (max T=3 steps).
- **Inputs and Outputs:**
 - **Clip Pool and Metadata:** Clips include textual captions and visual metadata (scene, motion intensity, camera duration) generated by an upstream vision-language captioner. Music metadata (beat times, energy) is extracted by a beat-tracking pipeline.
 - **Editing State (`s_t`):** Holds the selected clip sequence, segment durations, transition styles, music-sync parameters, and placeholder slots for generative content.
 - **RefinePatch:** A structured, machine-applicable patch with operations (add, remove, replace), rationale against the ledger, repair operator label, free-text thought, and confidence.
 - **Constraint Ledger (`L`):** Composed of explicit entries (`item_id, type, spec, satisfied, evidence`), covering seven constraint families: duration, transition, music sync, clip inclusion/exclusion, repeat limit, and pacing. Hard constraints have deterministic pass/fail tests, while softer entries earn graded credit. The verifier solely updates the `satisfied` field.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Principles

Evaluation proceeds in three layers:
1. **Execution Gate:** A prerequisite checking schema validity, patch applicability (PASR), and timeline validity. Failure at this layer renders a plan non-executable.
2. **Planning Quality:** Evaluated by the deterministic verifier based on metrics such as:
 - **Final Constraint Satisfaction Rate (FCSR):** Terminal mean per-entry pass rate.
 - **HardPass:** Binary indicator if every hard constraint holds.
 - **TargetedRepair:** Fraction of named ledger items repaired by a patch.
 - **FinalReqClipRecall (ReqCR):** Recall over required clip IDs.
 - **DurationPass@2S (Dur):** Binary indicator for target duration within a 2-second tolerance.
 - **NoRegression:** No previously satisfied ledger item becomes unsatisfied.
 - **Converged@3 (Cvg):** Final plan achieves FCSR ≥ 0.8 within 3 steps.
 - **Video-Editing Score (VES):** An aggregate weighted sum of component metrics, designed to be an in-protocol measure.
3. **Human-rendered Quality:** Assessed after rendering, covering instruction fit, content relevance, temporal coherence, shot continuity, pacing, audio-visual rhythm, and overall quality, by human annotators.

#### 3.3. RefineCut-Bench

The paper introduces RefineCut-Bench, a benchmark designed for executable-planning:
- **Assets:** 7,971 captioned clips, 23,913 caption-anchored frames, 499 music tracks.
- **Tasks:** 3,578 canonical tasks across three families (multi-clip composition, targeted insertion/replacement, generative composition) with 15 subtypes. Tasks are split into train/dev/test, including a `canonical-clean` test subset.
- **Multi-Teacher Trajectories:** Provides refinement rollouts from three frontier API teachers (GPT-5.4, Qwen3-Max, DeepSeek-V4-Pro) for every training task. Each step includes multiple candidate `RefinePatch` branches, with an associated first-choice flag and confidence. These traces are acknowledged as noisy, as teachers can disagree, and even their best choices might not fully resolve constraints.

#### 3.4. Two-Stage Method: Verifier-Replayed Distillation and Verifier-Centered Self-Improvement

The RefineCut framework comprises two offline learning stages and one runtime loop.

**Stage 1: Verifier-Replayed Teacher Bootstrap**
- **Canonical Trajectory Normalization:** Raw teacher outputs, which can be heterogeneous, are rewritten into canonical patch trajectories. JSON Pointer paths are mapped to a canonical namespace, and clip references are validated against task-local aliases.
- **Verifier Replay and Branch Arbitration:** For each canonicalized step, up to four candidate branches (with distinct `repair_operators` and clip selections) are replayed through the deterministic verifier. The verifier validates and applies each patch, recomputes the ledger, and scores it based on six signals: constraint-satisfaction change, targeted repair, required-clip recall, patch applicability, no-regression, and locality. The `verifier-best` branch advances the state, and score gaps drive preference pairing.
- **Verifier-Replayed Distillation:**
 - **Verified SFT:** The `verifier-best` branch is retained as `p*` if it canonicalizes, applies, repairs at least one violated ledger entry, doesn't regress satisfied constraints, and uses legitimate clip aliases. These patches are used for standard next-token cross-entropy fine-tuning (producing the `Verified` planner).
 - **Mixed-Granularity Preference:** Combines step-level (verifier-best vs. low-scoring branches) and trajectory-level pairs (ranked by terminal verifier score). The `Verified` checkpoint is further fine-tuned using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023), resulting in the `Mixed-Pref` planner.

**Stage 2: RefineCut-Evo (Verifier-Centered Student Self-Improvement)**
- **Candidate Generation:** From the `Mixed-Pref` planner, K=4 `RefinePatches` are sampled for each training state. These are parsed, replayed, and filtered for schema/apply failures.
- **Joint Scoring:** Each valid candidate `c` receives a joint score `S(c) = λV(c) + (1-λ)R(c)`, where `V(c)` is the deterministic verifier score and `R(c)` is a score from a fixed, task-specific editing rubric (ER1-ER7, covering criteria like intent, ledger satisfaction, clip grounding, timeline coherence, duration/pacing, music/beat alignment, and edit economy). `λ` is set to 0.65.
- **Preference Pair Construction:** For each state, `c+` (highest-scoring candidate) is chosen, and `c-` is a hard negative (a lower-scoring candidate with a score margin `τ` below `c+`).
- **Training:** RefineCut-Evo starts from the `Mixed-Pref` checkpoint and trains with DPO on these high-margin pairs, shifting supervision from external teachers to verifier-grounded self-improvement.

**Closed-Loop Deployment:**
- At inference, the trained RefineCut-Evo planner operates in a closed loop with the verifier. It reads the current state and violated ledger entries, emits one `RefinePatch`, which the verifier validates, applies, and uses to recompute the ledger.
- This loop repeats for a maximum of T=3 steps, after which the final state is dispatched to a downstream editing toolchain. No teacher calls are made during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments address five research questions (RQ1-RQ5), evaluate robustness, perform failure analysis, and include a blind rendered-preview evaluation.

- **RQ1: Can an Open-Weight Planner Learn Executable Editing Planning?**
 - An open-weight planner (Qwen3-8B-Instruct) demonstrates learning. Initial verifier-replayed SFT (`Raw` to `Verified`) significantly improves the Video-Editing Score (VES) from 0.620 to 0.858.
 - The self-improvement stage (`RefineCut-Evo`) further increases VES from 0.864 (`Mixed-Pref`) to 0.924. This gain is concentrated in "all-or-nothing" checks crucial for a finished plan, such as HardPass (0.670→0.820), DurationPass@2S (0.830→0.980), and Converged@3 (0.800→0.950).

- **RQ2: Why is Verifier Replay Needed?**
 - Teacher traces are inherently noisy; even verifier-best branches from frontier models (GPT-5.4, Qwen3-Max, DeepSeek-V4-Pro) rarely satisfy the entire ledger in one step (HardPass ≤ 0.03 in replay diagnostics).
 - Direct imitation of teacher-selected branches yields a VES of 0.620. In contrast, training on `verifier-best` branches (a smaller, cleaner dataset) yields 0.858 VES. This indicates that verifier replay, by filtering out non-executable or low-quality branches, is crucial for effective distillation, not merely more data.

- **RQ3: Can RefineCut-Evo Self-Improve Beyond Mixed-Pref?**
 - RefineCut-Evo successfully self-improves, showing a paired gain of +0.059 VES (95% CI [0.028, 0.092]) over `Mixed-Pref`.
 - This improvement is characterized as targeted repair: Evo improves 22 out of 100 test tasks, degrades only 5, and leaves 73 untouched. It notably reduces duration mismatches, the dominant failure under `Mixed-Pref`, from 16 tasks to 1, increasing "OK plans" from 74 to 91.

- **RQ4: What Drives RefineCut-Evo?**
 - Verifier-scored self-improvement is the primary driver. DPO training with verifier scores alone achieves 0.909 VES.
 - The rubric-structured margins, which refine the verifier score, contribute an additional 0.015 VES, specifically improving duration (0.940→0.980) and convergence (0.900→0.950).
 - The selection scheme for preference pairs is also important: keeping only high-margin pairs outperforms using every pair (costing 0.027 VES) or alternative selection schemes like UCPO-lite (costing 0.044 VES).

- **RQ5: Does Verifier Replay Transfer Across Backbones, and Does RefineCut Compete with Frontier Policies?**
 - **Cross-backbone transfer:** The benefits of verifier-replayed SFT transfer to other 8B-9B models, Llama-3.1-8B and GLM-4-9B. Raw imitation is either flat (Qwen) or harmful (Llama: -0.070, GLM: -0.035), while verified SFT consistently improves performance (+0.153 VES for Llama, +0.079 for GLM), boosting required-clip recall to high levels (0.98, 0.92, 0.99).
 - **Competitiveness with Frontier Policies:** In the identical closed loop, RefineCut-Evo (0.924 VES) matches or exceeds its frontier teachers: it outperforms GPT-5.4 (0.893 VES) and Qwen3-Max (0.773 VES), statistically ties DeepSeek-V4-Pro (0.936 VES), and matches the best Converged@3. This is achieved at a local latency of 11.7 s/task, competitive with or faster than API calls to frontier models.
 - **Baseline Comparisons:** Direct prompting of the untrained Qwen3-8B achieves 0.502 VES. Adding verifier feedback improves it to 0.592. Even R=4 verifier reranking reaches only 0.700 VES, and an oracle selecting the best visited state achieves 0.712 VES, demonstrating that learning from the verifier's signal is more effective than search.

- **Robustness and Failure Analysis:**
 - The performance gains are robust across the `canonical-clean` test subset (0.917 VES for Evo vs. 0.859 for Mixed-Pref) and transfer to 50 human-written, free-form briefs (0.902 VES for Evo vs. 0.848 for Mixed-Pref).
 - RefineCut-Evo achieves improvements with fewer patches (1.76 vs. 2.72 for baseline) and operations (3.77 vs. 5.95) per task, indicating more selective and efficient editing.
 - Semantic content (clip captions) is crucial; removing or shuffling it significantly degrades performance.
 - Blind API judges and human-rendered preview preferences generally align with the verifier-based VES, confirming the perceived quality improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on RefineCut introduces several contributions that have implications for AI-assisted content creation and broader machine learning research.

- **Novel Formalization of Video Editing:** RefineCut formally defines video editing as an "executable planning problem." This reframes the task from mere pixel generation or content manipulation to a structured decision-making process constrained by an explicit ledger and operating on a typed timeline. This explicit formulation provides a clear framework for verifiable AI in a complex creative domain.

- **Pioneering Verifier-Grounded Learning:** The research demonstrates that a deterministic verifier can effectively serve as the primary training signal for a creative task when an executable specification is available. This approach reduces reliance on human labels or subjective learned judges, offering a scalable and objective method for generating supervision. This paradigm has the potential to be applied to other domains where outputs can be formally checked.

- **Development of an Open-Weight, Compact Planner:** RefineCut successfully trains a compact 8B-parameter open-weight planner that exhibits performance competitive with or superior to larger, closed-source frontier models in a closed-loop setting. This is significant for fostering transparency, accessibility, and community-driven development in AI-assisted video editing, potentially reducing the prohibitive computational and API costs associated with frontier models.

- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** The two-stage learning framework, combining verifier-replayed distillation with verifier-centered self-improvement, yields substantial performance gains. The final RefineCut-Evo planner demonstrates high constraint satisfaction, robust plan execution, and improved convergence, effectively addressing the complexities of real-world editing constraints. The robustness checks against canonical-clean data and human-written briefs indicate practical applicability.

- **Public Release of Resources:** The release of the RefineCut-Bench benchmark and the accompanying code provides a valuable public resource for the research community. This enables reproducible research, facilitates comparative studies, and accelerates further innovation in verifiable, planning-level AI for video editing.

- **Broader Implications for AI Training:** The success of RefineCut suggests a generalizable approach for training AI models in domains where creative tasks can be broken down into verifiable, discrete steps. This could extend to other structured creative processes, such as interactive design, musical composition, or architectural planning, where formal rules and constraints are prevalent.

In summary, RefineCut represents a shift towards explicit, verifiable, and learnable AI in video editing, offering a robust and accessible solution for automated creative tasks while also contributing a new paradigm for AI model training grounded in deterministic verification.
