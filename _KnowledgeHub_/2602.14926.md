# MAC-AMP: A Closed-Loop Multi-Agent Collaboration System for Multi-Objective Antimicrobial Peptide Design

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Western University developed MAC-AMP, a closed-loop multi-agent collaboration system that autonomously designs antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) optimized for multiple properties simultaneously. The system consistently outperformed baseline generative models in antibacterial activity, toxicity, and structural reliability, and demonstrated broad-spectrum activity in generated AMPs while being transferable to other scientific design tasks.

## Problem

- The global health crisis of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) creates an urgent need for novel antimicrobial agents.
- Existing AI models for antimicrobial peptide (AMP) design often focus on single-objective optimization, leading to peptides with undesirable properties like high toxicity or poor stability.
- Current LLM-based multi-agent collaboration systems are typically open-loop, requiring human intervention, and produce natural language outputs that lack actionable training signals for stable reinforcement learning.

## Method

- **MAC-AMP** integrates a Property Prediction Module, an ==AI-Simulated Peer Review== Module, an RL Refinement Module, and a Peptide Generation Module into a closed-loop system.
- An AI-Simulated Peer Review Module utilizes multiple LLM-based agents (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, Perplexity) to evaluate AMP candidates in a committee-style deliberation, generating structured consensus and scores.
- The RL Refinement Module dynamically converts the multi-agent consensus and evaluations into an optimized, machine-actionable reward function, which is then used by a GPT-2 peptide generator with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for adaptive sequence generation.

## Results

- MAC-AMP consistently generated AMPs with superior antibacterial activity, lower toxicity, and higher structural reliability compared to baseline generative models and real-world AMPs across multiple bacterial targets.
- Generated AMPs demonstrated strong broad-spectrum generalization, with **40%** of E. coli-designed peptides showing activity against major ESKAPE pathogens, and exhibited canonical amphipathic motifs.
- The system produced novel AMP sequences with an average of **27%** similarity to training data, indicating effective exploration of biophysically plausible chemical space, and achieved structural stability verified by Molecular Dynamics simulations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-agent deliberation, mimicking academic peer review, can effectively translate qualitative expert evaluations into quantifiable, structured consensus suitable for robust multi-objective optimization.
> - A closed-loop system that adaptively refines reinforcement learning reward functions based on real-time multi-agent feedback can achieve stable and balanced optimization across conflicting molecular properties.
> - The core multi-agent framework is highly generalizable and transferable, demonstrated by its successful application to a table-to-text generation task, indicating broad utility beyond molecular design.
