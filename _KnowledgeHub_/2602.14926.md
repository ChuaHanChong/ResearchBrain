# MAC-AMP: A Closed-Loop Multi-Agent Collaboration System for Multi-Objective Antimicrobial Peptide Design

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Western University developed MAC-AMP, a closed-loop multi-agent collaboration system that autonomously designs antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) optimized for multiple properties simultaneously. The system consistently outperformed baseline generative models in antibacterial activity, toxicity, and structural reliability, and demonstrated broad-spectrum activity in generated AMPs while being transferable to other scientific design tasks.

## Problem

- The global health crisis of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) creates an urgent need for novel antimicrobial agents.
- Existing AI models for antimicrobial peptide (AMP) design often focus on single-objective optimization, leading to peptides with undesirable properties like high toxicity or poor stability.
- Current LLM-based multi-agent collaboration systems are typically open-loop, requiring human intervention, and produce natural language outputs that lack actionable training signals for stable reinforcement learning.

## Method

- **MAC-AMP** integrates a Property Prediction Module, an ==AI-Simulated Peer Review== Module, an RL Refinement Module, and a Peptide Generation Module into a closed-loop system.
- An AI-Simulated Peer Review Module utilizes multiple LLM-based agents (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, Perplexity) to evaluate AMP candidates in a committee-style deliberation, generating structured consensus and scores.
- The RL Refinement Module dynamically converts the multi-agent consensus and evaluations into an optimized, machine-actionable reward function, which is then used by a GPT-2 peptide generator with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for adaptive sequence generation.

## Results

- MAC-AMP consistently generated AMPs with superior antibacterial activity, lower toxicity, and higher structural reliability compared to baseline generative models and real-world AMPs across multiple bacterial targets.
- Generated AMPs demonstrated strong broad-spectrum generalization, with **40%** of E. coli-designed peptides showing activity against major ESKAPE pathogens, and exhibited canonical amphipathic motifs.
- The system produced novel AMP sequences with an average of **27%** similarity to training data, indicating effective exploration of biophysically plausible chemical space, and achieved structural stability verified by Molecular Dynamics simulations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-agent deliberation, mimicking academic peer review, can effectively translate qualitative expert evaluations into quantifiable, structured consensus suitable for robust multi-objective optimization.
> - A closed-loop system that adaptively refines reinforcement learning reward functions based on real-time multi-agent feedback can achieve stable and balanced optimization across conflicting molecular properties.
> - The core multi-agent framework is highly generalizable and transferable, demonstrated by its successful application to a table-to-text generation task, indicating broad utility beyond molecular design.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in MAC-AMP is situated at the critical confluence of several rapidly advancing research fields: the global health crisis of antimicrobial resistance (AMR), the search for novel antimicrobial agents, and the application of cutting-edge artificial intelligence, especially Large Language Models (LLMs) and multi-agent systems, to scientific discovery and design.

**Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) and Antimicrobial Peptides (AMPs):** The paper prominently highlights AMR as a "critical challenge" causing millions of deaths annually, with projections of even more dire consequences by 2050. This establishes the urgent societal need for new therapeutics. Antimicrobial Peptides (AMPs) are presented as a promising solution due to their broad-spectrum activity, diverse mechanisms, and higher resistance barriers compared to traditional antibiotics. However, AMP development faces bottlenecks, including toxicity, stability issues, and manufacturability limitations. MAC-AMP directly addresses these challenges by aiming to design AMPs that optimize multiple properties simultaneously.

**AI in AMP Design:** The paper acknowledges the growing role of AI in AMP discovery, moving beyond simple retrieval and screening to generation and optimization. However, it identifies significant limitations in existing AI-driven AMP design models:
- **Single-objective optimization:** Most models prioritize only antibacterial activity, leading to peptides with undesirable properties like high toxicity or poor synthesizability.
- **Unstable multi-objective optimization:** Models attempting multi-objective optimization often use static weighting or thresholding, leading to instability, reward hacking, or diversity collapse.
- **Lack of actionable feedback:** Outputs are often scattered scores or natural language, making it difficult to convert them into clear, reproducible learning signals for stable reinforcement learning (RL).

**LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems:** The paper contextualizes its approach within the rapidly evolving landscape of LLM-based multi-agent collaboration (MAC) systems. These systems are gaining traction for solving complex tasks through division of labor, communication, and collaboration. While current MAC systems show promise in complex problem-solving (e.g., Virtual Lab for nanobody design, OSDA Agent for material design, CAMEL, AutoGen for general coordination), they typically suffer from two key limitations that MAC-AMP aims to overcome:
- **Open-loop nature:** Many existing MAC systems are open-loop, often requiring human-in-the-loop operation, and rely on trial-and-error prompting rather than principled closed-loop learning.
- **Non-executable outputs:** Their outputs are primarily natural language narratives or heterogeneous scores, lacking direct conversion into reproducible training signals for model optimization.

MAC-AMP distinguishes itself by specifically addressing the "closed-loop" and "actionable training signal" gaps within the LLM-based multi-agent system paradigm, applying this innovative architecture to the high-stakes problem of multi-objective AMP design. It also integrates ideas from LLM-enhanced Reinforcement Learning, particularly automated reward design (like Eureka), but does so within a multi-agent consensus framework. By combining these elements, MAC-AMP proposes a novel and robust solution that pushes the boundaries of AI-driven drug discovery and general scientific design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of MAC-AMP is to establish a **closed-loop multi-agent collaboration (MAC) system for multi-objective antimicrobial peptide (AMP) design**. This overarching goal is driven by several specific aims and motivations:

- **Combat Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR):** The most fundamental motivation is to address the global health crisis of AMR by accelerating the discovery and design of effective AMPs.
- **Overcome limitations of existing AI models for AMP design:** Current AI models often struggle to balance multiple critical properties (activity, toxicity, novelty, stability). MAC-AMP aims to provide a more robust and stable approach to multi-objective optimization.
- **Bridge the gap between multi-agent consensus and actionable training signals:** A key objective is to convert the nuanced, multi-criteria evaluations from LLM-based agents into concrete, machine-actionable reward functions suitable for stable reinforcement learning.
- **Develop a fully autonomous system:** MAC-AMP seeks to be a self-improving system that requires only an initial task description and example dataset, minimizing human intervention in the iterative design process.
- **Introduce explainability and auditability:** Unlike many "black-box" AI models, MAC-AMP aims to provide transparency through structured logs and decision tracking, allowing for error localization and systematic correction.
- **Ensure cross-domain transferability:** A critical design objective is to create a generalizable framework that can be adapted to other complex scientific design tasks beyond AMPs, demonstrating the broad applicability of the multi-agent collaboration paradigm.
- **Balance conflicting biological constraints:** The system is motivated to inherently support the simultaneous optimization of diverse properties such as antibacterial activity, structural stability, toxicity, and AMP-likeness, which are often conflicting in peptide design.

In essence, MAC-AMP is motivated by the need for a sophisticated, intelligent, and transparent AI system capable of navigating the intricate landscape of drug design, offering a powerful tool to generate optimized therapeutic candidates that meet real-world clinical requirements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MAC-AMP introduces a novel, closed-loop multi-agent collaboration (MAC) system designed to autonomously generate novel antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) optimized for multiple molecular properties. The framework operates through six interconnected modules, beginning with user input (target bacterium name and an example dataset of AMPs with MIC values) and concluding with optimized AMP outputs.

The core methodology revolves around four main architectural modules:

1. **Property Prediction Module:**
 - **Function:** Evaluates generated AMP candidates for various molecular properties.
 - **Tools:**
 - **Target-specific MIC predictor:** A custom LLM-based regressor (adapted from BERT AmPEP60, fine-tuning ProtBERT) trained on user-provided data to predict antibacterial activity against a specific bacterial target. Outputs an "antibacterial activity score" (S_a).
 - **Macrel 1.5:** Predicts AMP likelihood (S_b).
 - **ToxinPred 3.0:** Predicts toxicity scores (V_a).
 - **OmegaFold v1:** Predicts structural reliability via pLDDT scores (V_b).
 - **ProtParam (Biopython):** Provides physicochemical summaries (V_c).
 - **Foldseek 10:** Quantifies similarity to a set of high-performing example AMPs (structural templates) (V_d).
 - **Outputs:** Two categories: explicit reward signals (S_a, S_b) and auxiliary evidence (V_a, V_b, V_c, V_d) to constrain generation. This module underwent extensive validation, including ablation studies, substitution analyses, and Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations to confirm OmegaFold's reliability.

2. **AI-Simulated Peer Review Module:**
 - **Function:** A multi-agent system that reviews AMP candidates in a committee-style deliberation, inspired by academic peer review.
 - **Components:**
 - **Reviewer Agents:** Three independent LLM-based agents (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, Perplexity), each with distinct background knowledge (e.g., GPT-5 as a safety/discriminability gatekeeper, Perplexity as a potency driver, Gemini 2.5 mediating structural reliability/potency). They evaluate AMPs across four task-specific dimensions: Efficiency (EFF), Safety (SAFE), Developmental Sequence Structure (DevStruct), and Originality (Orig).
 - **Weighted Lexicon Subtables:** Each dimension is associated with a weighted lexicon of "Tags" (ID(State, Weight)) to quantify free-text comments. Reviewers provide comments, self-annotate with tags, and assign confidence scores.
 - **Area Chair Agent:** Aggregates reviewer outputs, drafts meta-comments, resolves semantic conflicts in tags, computes mean reviewer scores, estimates divergence penalties, and applies these to obtain final dimension-level meta scores (S_c) and a four-line meta-review text (T).
 - **Key Aspect:** Emphasizes converting qualitative expert review into quantifiable, structured consensus suitable for downstream optimization.

3. **RL Refinement Module:**
 - **Function:** Converts the evaluations and consensus from the previous modules into trainable optimization signals, dynamically shaping and adapting the reward function for the peptide generator.
 - **Process:**
 - **Candidate Reward Design:** Starts with cold-starting reward design using example AMPs.
 - **Expert Agents:** A Computer Science (CS)-based Reward Design agent (focused on observable signals) and a Biomedical-Based Reward Alignment agent (analyzing meta-review texts and integrating domain knowledge) collaboratively refine the reward function.
 - **Sandbox Simulation:** Candidate reward functions are filtered by a rule-based validator, then used to run short simulated training within a cloned Peptide Generation module (sandbox).
 - **RL Reward Decision Agent:** Selects the best reward function via Pareto optimization based on sandbox logs. This "inner loop" iterates multiple times for refinement.
 - **Output:** An optimized, machine-actionable reward function for the Peptide Generation module.

4. **Peptide Generation Module:**
 - **Function:** Generates novel AMP sequences guided by the adaptively optimized reward function.
 - **Generator:** A GPT-2 auto-regressive model (inherited from AMP-Designer architecture) augmented with a trainable soft prompt to inject domain knowledge.
 - **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** The selected reward function is compiled into a PPO objective, which guides the generator's training. PPO minimizes a total loss combining policy, value regression, and entropy regularization terms.
 - **Stage-Based Adaptive Optimization:** The RL module aggregates evaluation logs at the end of each "stage" (e.g., 15 epochs) and adaptively redesigns the reward, allowing the reward function to co-evolve with real-time feedback and multi-agent consensus. This process is repeated across multiple stages (e.g., early, mid, late, with changing optimization foci).

**Structural Design of Multi-Agent Collaboration (Appendix C):** The underlying MAC framework is built on schema-driven prompting, formalizing roles (Title, Expertise, Goal, Role), operating contracts (Input/Output formats, Workflow, Startup Command), and knowledge injection (via preparatory meetings with human experts). This ensures agents consistently fulfill their roles and communicate effectively, enhancing generality and transferability.

**Closed-Loop Mechanism & Explainability:** The system is explicitly designed as closed-loop, where generated AMPs are evaluated, reviewed, and this feedback directly updates the reward function for subsequent generation. Transparency is maintained through structured system logs tracking every step, enabling "stepwise explainability and auditability."

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate MAC-AMP's superior performance across multiple objectives in AMP design and validate its architectural novelties.

#### 4.1. Target-Specific AMP Testing

- MAC-AMP consistently outperformed baseline generative models (AMP-Designer, BroadAMP-GPT, PepGAN, Diff-AMP) and even real-world top-K AMPs across three single-task bacterial targets: *Escherichia coli* (E. coli), *Staphylococcus aureus* (S. aureus), and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (P. aeruginosa).
- It achieved the **best scores** in **antibacterial activity**, **toxicity**, and **structural reliability**.
- While BroadAMP-GPT showed a slight edge in "AMP Likelihood," MAC-AMP maintained comparable AMP-likeness while significantly improving other critical properties, demonstrating effective multi-objective optimization without sacrificing discriminability. This highlights MAC-AMP's ability to produce AMPs that better align with real-world research and development needs by balancing conflicting goals.

#### 4.2. Broad-Spectrum Activity Testing

- E. coli-designed AMPs generated by MAC-AMP demonstrated the **strongest generalization** when evaluated against other bacterial strains (S. aureus, P. aeruginosa, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Enterococcus faecium*).
- Excellent generalization was observed in other Gram-negative species (P. aeruginosa, K. pneumoniae), indicating that learned physicochemical patterns transfer well due to shared outer membrane architecture.
- Surprisingly strong generalization was also observed for *E. faecium* (Gram-positive), suggesting that broad-spectrum efficacy is achievable across both Gram-negative and Gram-positive species, even if species-specific factors might modulate activity levels (as seen with slightly reduced activity against S. aureus).
- Independent validation using APEX 1.1 confirmed that a high proportion (40%) of MAC-AMP generated E. coli AMPs exhibited broad-spectrum activity across major ESKAPE pathogens. Motif analysis of these broad-spectrum AMPs identified canonical amphipathic structures (e.g., KFLKGA, WLLGKW) with alternating cationic and hydrophobic residues, consistent with membrane-disrupting mechanisms.

#### 4.3. Novelty and Structural Reliability

- **Novelty:** MAC-AMP generated AMPs exhibited consistently low sequence similarity (average ~27%, max 84.6%) to the training dataset, indicating high sequence-level novelty. Internal similarity among generated AMPs was also low, demonstrating diversity.
- **Chemical Space Exploration:** UMAP projections showed that MAC-AMP candidates cluster within or along the edges of the density wells defined by real-world AMPs, rather than being scattered randomly. This indicates that the model learns AMP-like features while exploring novel, underrepresented, but biophysically plausible regions of the sequence space.
- **Structural Stability:** Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations on a subset of generated AMPs confirmed general structural stability (mean RMSD around 2-4 Å), validating the use of OmegaFold pLDDT as a reliable proxy for guiding AMP design.
- **Biophysical Properties:** Comparative analysis of 2,000 generated AMPs with baselines and real-world AMPs showed that MAC-AMP produces peptides with similar, but often enhanced, distributions for key properties like cationic charge, hydrophobicity, and amphipathicity, favoring membrane-active designs.

#### 4.4. Ablation and Substitution Studies

- **Property Prediction Module:** Ablation studies confirmed that removing any of the multi-objective predictors (toxicity, structural reliability, AMP likelihood) led to performance degradation in the dropped objective, demonstrating that all components are necessary for balanced multi-objective optimization, despite marginal computational cost savings.
- **AI-Simulated Peer Review Module:** This module was crucial. Removing any Reviewer agent or all of them led to significant deterioration in balanced multi-objective outcomes, highlighting the distinct and complementary roles of GPT-5 (safety/discriminability), Perplexity (potency), and Gemini 2.5 (structural reliability/potency synergy). The agents collectively achieve a balanced optimization.
- **RL Refinement Module:** Both the RL Reward Decision agent and Adaptive Optimization were indispensable. Removing them led to safety degradation (without decision agent) or overexploitation and property drops (without adaptive optimization), confirming that stage-wise updates and intelligent reward selection are critical for stable multi-objective optimization and regularization.
- **Agent Models & Lexicon Weights:** Substitution analyses confirmed that the chosen API-based LLMs (GPT-5, Perplexity, Gemini 2.5) provided the most favorable balance of performance, despite slightly higher computational costs compared to locally deployed smaller models. The agent-determined lexicon weights also proved reliable and efficient.

#### 4.5. Cross-Domain Transferability (Table-to-Text Generation)

- MAC-AMP's framework successfully transferred to an English table-to-text generation task (ToTTo dataset).
- With minimal domain-specific adaptations (redefining objectives, replacing molecular predictors with text evaluation metrics), the T5-based MAC-AMP framework outperformed a T5 baseline in BLEU, PARENT, and BLEURT scores, particularly for non-overlap (unseen) tables. This powerfully demonstrates the **generality and transferability** of MAC-AMP's closed-loop multi-agent collaboration and adaptive reward design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MAC-AMP system represents a significant advancement in both the field of AI-driven drug discovery and the broader application of multi-agent LLM systems to complex scientific problems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Revolutionizing Antimicrobial Peptide Design:** MAC-AMP directly addresses the critical need for novel antimicrobial agents to combat the global health crisis of AMR. By robustly optimizing multiple, often conflicting, molecular properties (activity, toxicity, structural reliability, novelty, AMP-likeness), it moves beyond simplistic single-objective designs. This capability is crucial for accelerating the pipeline from *in silico* design to viable therapeutic candidates. The generated AMPs show promise for broad-spectrum activity, which is a highly desirable trait for new antibiotics.

2. **Pioneering Closed-Loop Multi-Agent Systems for Scientific Design:** The paper introduces the first fully autonomous, closed-loop multi-agent system specifically designed for molecular discovery. This architecture is a paradigm shift from traditional open-loop systems or single-model approaches. By converting complex, multi-criteria evaluations from simulated peer review into executable reinforcement learning reward signals, MAC-AMP establishes a powerful, self-improving feedback loop for continuous design and optimization. This model is not just a tool; it's a new way of doing scientific research with AI.

3. **Enhancing Explainability and Auditability in AI-Driven Discovery:** A major advantage of MAC-AMP is its "stepwise explainability and auditability." Through transparent logs, replay traces, and consensus-aware decision tracking across all agents, the system overcomes the "black-box" limitations prevalent in many AI models. This transparency is vital in scientific and medical contexts, allowing researchers to understand *why* certain designs are proposed, localize errors, and systematically improve the process, fostering trust and facilitating regulatory pathways.

4. **Establishing a Principled Foundation for Next-Generation Molecular Design:** MAC-AMP's core framework, with its role-based agent profiles, schema-driven prompts, and knowledge injection mechanisms, is highly generalizable. Its successful cross-domain transferability to a table-to-text generation task highlights its potential as a domain-agnostic framework for a wide array of scientific and engineering design problems. This indicates that the principles of dynamic multi-agent consensus, translated into adaptive reward functions, could be applied to design novel proteins, materials, chemicals, or even experimental protocols.

5. **Advancing Reinforcement Learning and Automated Reward Design:** The integration of LLM-based agents for automated reward design within a multi-agent consensus framework pushes the boundaries of RL from AI feedback. The adaptive optimization strategy, which allows the reward function to co-evolve with feedback, prevents reward hacking and maintains stable multi-objective progress, offering valuable insights for future RL applications.

In conclusion, MAC-AMP sets a new benchmark for generative AMP design, not only by outperforming existing models in critical molecular properties but also by providing a scalable, interpretable, and transferable framework that could significantly accelerate discovery in molecular sciences and beyond. It represents a crucial step towards more autonomous, intelligent, and trustworthy AI systems in scientific research.
