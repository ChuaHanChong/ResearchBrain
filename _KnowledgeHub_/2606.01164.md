# Towards Interactive Video World Modeling: Frontiers, Challenges, Benchmarks, and Future Trends

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey systematically mapping interactive video world modeling: models that recurrently generate future observations conditioned on interaction history, user actions, and auxiliary conditions. It organizes recent trends (specialist to generalist, static to self-evolving multi-agent states, single- to multi-sensory interfaces), analyzes three core bottlenecks (action controllability, long-horizon memory, real-time responsiveness), and compares benchmarks across open-world exploration, game engines, autonomous driving, and embodied AI.

## Problem

- Prior video generation models use one-shot, non-causal text or image conditioning, giving only coarse control and no multi-round, frame-level user intervention.
- Existing world models suffer compounding errors and temporal drift over long rollouts, with fixed-length history windows failing to preserve long-horizon spatial and object consistency.
- Real-time interactivity is under-addressed: increasing history context for coherence conflicts with action-following responsiveness, and few works jointly optimize both under compute constraints.

## Method

- Taxonomizes four ==camera-action injection== manners (==token concatenation==, ==feature scaling/shifting==, ==camera-controlled rendering/simulation==, ==matrix transformation==) and separately covers text-instruction injection via ==cross-attention==.
- Organizes long-horizon consistency solutions into ==history-as-context==, memory construction (==implicit video-token memory== vs ==explicit 3D/4D geometric memory==), ==explicit 3D reconstruction==, and ==noise-augmentation==/==Forcing-based training== schemes (Diffusion/Self/Context/Geometry Forcing).
- Compiles a comparative benchmark and metrics framework across four application domains (open-world exploration, game engines, embodied AI manipulation/policy learning, autonomous driving), plus an efficiency taxonomy of ==distillation==, ==caching==, and ==parallel-decoding== acceleration methods.

## Results

- On WorldArena manipulation benchmark, action-following scores range widely (**0.00 to 9.92**) across compared world models, with Veo3.1 and Wan2.6 leading instruction-following (**97.14** and **89.96**) but weaker dynamic degree.
- On RoboTwin 2.0 and Libero, world-action policies substantially outperform baseline VLA models, with Fast-WAM and LingBot-VA reaching **~91.8%** and **92.2%** average success on RoboTwin 2.0 versus **62.2%** for pi-0.
- Game-engine world models show clear scaling trends: newer methods (Matrix-Game 3.0, Yume-1.5) reach up to **40-60 FPS** at 720p-1080p with smaller model sizes (**5B**) than earlier **13-17B** models running at lower speeds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interactivity is defined by closed-loop, multi-round, frame-level conditioning on history, actions, and auxiliary context, distinguishing world models fundamentally from one-shot video generators.
> - Memory design trades off geometric consistency (explicit 3D/4D storage) against dynamic-handling flexibility (implicit latent tokens), motivating hybrid memory architectures as a general design pattern.
> - Coherence and responsiveness are fundamentally in tension under fixed compute, so Forcing-based training schemes that bridge train-test mismatch generalize as a key lever across domains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses the rapidly evolving field of world modeling, particularly focusing on its interactive aspects. The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements in Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC) and multi-modal large language models (LLMs), which have propelled world models into prominence for applications in game engines, embodied AI, and autonomous driving.

Traditional world models aim to learn the underlying dynamics of environments and generate future predictions based on observations and actions. However, these models often suffered from limited interactivity. The current work positions itself at the forefront of "interactive world modeling," a paradigm that explicitly incorporates user actions into the world state transition process. This approach is noted for enhancing controllability over world evolutions, enabling users to actively traverse, manipulate, navigate, and personalize generated environments.

The authors identify a gap in the existing literature: despite extensive developments and the emergence of industrial and academic milestones (e.g., Google's Genie series, Meta's WorldGen, NVIDIA's Lyra 2.0), a comprehensive review systematically covering the latest research trends, technical developments, evaluation benchmarks, and future directions within interactive world modeling was lacking. This paper aims to fill this void by providing such a systematic overview, thereby serving as a foundational resource for the research community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to provide a systematic and comprehensive review of interactive world modeling. This encompasses summarizing recent research trends, detailing technical developments, comparing evaluation benchmarks, and proposing future potential directions in the field.

The motivation for undertaking this survey stems from several key observations and existing challenges:
- **Growing Importance of World Models:** The rapid progress in AIGC and multi-modal LLMs has intensified research interest in world models. These models are fundamental for understanding environmental dynamics, predicting future states, and supporting applications in autonomous driving, embodied AI, and game engines.
- **Centrality of Interactivity:** Interactivity is recognized as a core property, mirroring how humans learn and comprehend the world through continuous interaction. For machine intelligence, enabling higher-level, frame- or region-based interventions within world models enhances cognitive and reasoning abilities.
- **Emergence of Interactive World Models:** Recent advancements have empowered world models with interactivity, transforming passive video generation into active intervention. This allows users to actively manipulate and explore generated environments, leading to more immersive experiences. Examples such as Genie, WorldGen, and Lyra 2.0 demonstrate significant progress in this area.
- **Persistent Challenges:** Despite considerable progress, interactive world modeling continues to face substantial technical hurdles. These include maintaining long-horizon consistency during prolonged interactions, ensuring real-time responsiveness to user actions, developing effective and generalizable methods for action injection, and facilitating efficient memory retrieval.
- **Lack of a Comprehensive Review:** The authors identify a critical gap in the existing literature: the absence of a unified survey that thoroughly consolidates recent advancements, outlines unresolved challenges, compares commonly adopted benchmarks, and discusses future research trends specifically for interactive world modeling. This paper is motivated to address this need, offering a structured and holistic perspective on the field.

By addressing these points, the paper aims to provide a clearer understanding of the current state of interactive world modeling and to guide future research efforts toward overcoming its remaining limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper functions as a comprehensive survey and review of the interactive world modeling domain. Its methodology involves structuring the vast body of literature into logical categories to facilitate understanding and analysis. The authors' approach can be summarized as follows:

- **Establishing Preliminaries:** The survey begins by defining core concepts, differentiating between traditional world models and interactive video world models. It outlines the specific characteristics of interactive models, emphasizing multi-round fine-grained controllability, long-horizon consistent world transition, and real-time interaction feedback. It also categorizes diverse interaction interfaces (visual, text, audio, physical, and others) and reviews advanced generative model backbones commonly used (VAEs, GANs, Diffusion Models, Autoregressive Models).
- **Summarizing Research Trends:** The authors categorize recent research trends along three dimensions:
 - **Applicability:** Tracing the evolution from specialist models tailored for single applications (e.g., specific game engines) to generalist models capable of generalizing across diverse domains by leveraging large-scale pre-trained backbones and mixed datasets.
 - **World State Representation:** Documenting the shift from static, single-agent explorable environments to dynamic, self-evolving, multi-agent worlds, including the challenge of modeling "out-of-sight dynamics."
 - **Interactive Modality:** Examining the expansion from single-sensory inputs (e.g., visual or text) to multi-sensory interfaces (e.g., audio, physical force, structured data in autonomous driving).
- **Delving into Key Technical Challenges:** The core of the paper systematically analyzes three major technical bottlenecks:
 - **User Action Controllability:** Differentiating between one-shot and multi-shot fine-grained control, and detailing various action injection manners for camera pose/trajectory (concatenation, scaling/shifting, camera-controlled rendering, matrix transformation) and text instructions (cross-attention with LLMs).
 - **Long-Horizon Interactions and Memory:** Exploring strategies to maintain spatio-temporal consistency over extended durations and mitigate compounding errors. This includes using history frames as conditions, developing explicit and implicit memory mechanisms (e.g., token-level memory banks, 3D geometric storage), employing explicit 3D reconstruction, and utilizing noise augmentation and forcing-based training paradigms in autoregressive diffusion models.
 - **Action-Following Responsiveness for Real-Time Interactivity:** Addressing the inherent conflict between maintaining coherence and achieving real-time responsiveness. Solutions discussed include model distillation in diffusion models, cache-based acceleration, and other efficiency optimization techniques (e.g., parallel decoding, sampling quantization).
- **Benchmarking and Comparison:** A significant portion of the paper is dedicated to summarizing and comparing commonly used datasets, benchmarks, and metrics across four key application domains: interactive open-world exploration, immersive game engines, embodied AI, and interactive simulators for autonomous driving. This section provides quantitative and qualitative comparisons of representative methods.
- **Identifying Future Directions:** The survey concludes by analyzing unresolved challenges and proposing promising future research directions. These include improving large-scale action-labeled data acquisition, enhancing counterfactual reasoning and real-world alignment, achieving extreme long-horizon consistency, integrating physical awareness, and developing more accessible interfaces with direct manipulation.
- **Resource Provision:** The authors supplement their review by providing a publicly available repository (https://github.com/liujiuming123/Awesome-Interactive-World-Model), indicating an ongoing effort to curate and update relevant research.

This structured approach allows for a detailed and systematic examination of the interactive world modeling landscape, synthesizing information from a large volume of research papers and organizing it into a coherent framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive overview of interactive world modeling, revealing key trends, technical advancements, and persistent challenges.

**Research Trends (Section 3):**
- **Applicability:** The field is transitioning from specialized world models, designed for specific applications like game engines, to generalist models. These generalist models (e.g., UniSim, AdaWorld, Astra, GAIA-1) leverage large-scale pre-trained video backbones and diverse datasets to achieve broader applicability across domains such as open-world exploration, robotics, and autonomous driving.
- **World State Representation:** There is a clear progression from models generating static, single-agent environments to those capable of dynamic, self-evolving, multi-agent worlds. Early models focused on explorable but temporally static scenes (e.g., WonderWorld), while recent advancements incorporate dynamic entities (e.g., WorldCanvas, NeoVerse, VerseCrafter) and address "out-of-sight dynamics" to maintain consistent world states for unobserved regions (e.g., LiveWorld, HM-World). Furthermore, models are increasingly supporting multi-agent interactions, modeling feature exchange and cooperation (e.g., Solaris, MultiWorld, Combo).
- **Interactive Modality:** Interactive world models are expanding beyond single-sensory inputs (e.g., visual and text) to integrate multi-sensory interfaces. This includes audio guidance (SonoWorld), physical-informed actions like force and gravity (RealWonder, PerpetualWonder), and structured interaction interfaces specific to autonomous driving (e.g., ego velocity, road semantics, multi-camera observations).

**User Action Controllability (Section 4):**
- **Distinction from General Video Generation:** A core finding is the clear differentiation between traditional one-shot video generation, which offers coarse-grained control, and interactive world models, which support multi-round, fine-grained, frame-level, or even region- and object-level control. This enables continuous user intervention and progressive refinement of generated content.
- **Action Injection Mechanisms:** The paper details various methods for injecting user actions:
 - **Camera Pose/Trajectory:** Methods include concatenating camera inputs with visual tokens (Genie, iVideoGPT), scaling and shifting visual tokens (GameGen-X, LingBot-World), camera-controlled rendering or simulation (Vmem, LiveWorld), and matrix transformations for panoramic views (IaaW, GenEx).
 - **Text Instructions:** Leveraging pre-trained LLMs (e.g., T5) allows for flexible control over semantic attributes, styles, and object manipulation, typically integrated via cross-attention.
- **Challenges in Controllability:** Fine-grained action injection can negatively impact long-duration consistency and poses efficiency challenges for real-time feedback.

**Long-Horizon Interactions and Memory (Section 5):**
- **Strategies for Consistency:** The survey highlights several approaches to overcome the long-standing bottleneck of maintaining spatio-temporal consistency and mitigating compounding errors in autoregressive generation:
 - **History Frames as Condition:** Basic strategy of using recent past frames as conditions (UniSim, Matrix-Game series), but limited by fixed temporal windows.
 - **Memory Construction:**
 - *Implicit Memory:* Storing latent video tokens, often augmented with state cues like spatial location, viewpoint, and timestamp (WorldMem, RELIC, HY-WorldPlay). These typically use retrieval mechanisms to enhance coherence.
 - *Explicit 3D Geometric Storage:* Storing and updating explicit 3D representations such as Surfels, point clouds, or 3D Gaussian Splatting (Vmem, DeepVerse, Spmem). This provides better geometric awareness but can be complex to update. Hybrid memory approaches (MosaicMem) are also emerging.
 - **Explicit 3D Reconstruction:** Directly reconstructing 3D states (Wonder series, WorldGen, HY-World series) to inherently maintain structural consistency across views and time.
 - **Noise Augmentation and Forcing-based Training:** Techniques like dynamic noising (Oasis) and various "Forcing" strategies (Diffusion Forcing, Self-Forcing, Geometry Forcing, Context Forcing) are employed to reduce exposure bias during autoregressive training and improve long-term consistency.

**Action-Following Responsiveness for Real-Time Interactivity (Section 6):**
- **Coherence-Responsiveness Conflict:** A critical finding is the inherent trade-off between ensuring long-term coherence (which often benefits from longer history context) and achieving immediate responsiveness to user actions. Techniques like injecting random noise into conditioning frames (Astra) or context forcing (HY-WorldPlay) are explored to balance this.
- **Efficiency Optimization:** To enable real-time feedback crucial for interactive applications:
 - **Model Distillation:** Distilling knowledge from larger teacher networks to smaller, faster student networks for fewer-step inference (GameNGen, HY-GameCraft, Matrix-Game 3.0).
 - **Cache-based Acceleration:** Reusing intermediate features in transformer backbones (Yume, HY-World 1.0, RELIC) to reduce computational load.
 - **Other Optimizations:** Parallel decoding, key-frame reconstruction, sampling quantization, and model pruning.

**Benchmarks and Method Comparisons (Section 7):**
- The paper provides a structured comparison of datasets and evaluation benchmarks across four key application fields:
 - **Interactive Open-World Exploration:** Benchmarks are evolving from focusing solely on visual quality (TC-Bench, EvalCrafter) to comprehensive assessments of interactive capabilities, camera controllability, 3D consistency, and memory mechanisms (WorldScore, OmniWorldBench, MIND, iWorldBench). Newer methods demonstrate improved CLIP-based metrics and aesthetic scores.
 - **Immersive Game Engines:** Benchmarks often involve data from 2D games, AAA games, Minecraft, or Unreal Engine. Evaluations focus on action space, resolution, speed, interaction duration, memory, and model size. Advancements show higher resolution, faster FPS (e.g., Matrix-Game 3.0 at 40 FPS), and longer duration generation with memory.
 - **Embodied AI:** High-quality interaction data is expensive. World models are used to augment training data (EMMA, GigaWorld-0) or as direct policy learners (Motus, Cosmos Policy, LingBot-VA), often outperforming traditional VLA models in task success rates on benchmarks like RobotWin 2.0 and Libero.
 - **Interactive Simulators for Autonomous Driving:** Evaluation frameworks assess controllable future generation. This includes scene-level controllable simulators (DriveGAN, DriveDreamer), joint video-action generation models (DrivingGPT, Vega), and factorized video–trajectory rollout paradigms (DrivingWorld, Epona). Metrics evaluate distributional similarity (FVD, FTD), visual quality, temporal consistency, and trajectory alignment (ADE, DTW).

Overall, the findings indicate significant progress in interactive world modeling, particularly in enhancing controllability, extending temporal consistency through memory, and improving real-time performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant implications for the field of interactive world modeling and its related disciplines. Its potential impact can be delineated as follows:

- **Consolidating Knowledge and Guiding Research:** By systematically reviewing the current state of interactive world modeling, the paper consolidates a fragmented body of literature. This provides a unified understanding of the field's advancements, current trends, and technical methodologies. For new researchers, it serves as an invaluable entry point, while for experienced practitioners, it offers a structured overview and a clear identification of critical areas for future exploration.
- **Highlighting Key Technical Bottlenecks:** The detailed analysis of challenges such as action-conditioned controllability, long-horizon consistency, and real-time responsiveness provides a roadmap for future technical development. By explicitly articulating these issues and current approaches, the paper helps researchers prioritize efforts and develop more effective solutions.
- **Standardizing Evaluation and Benchmarking:** The comprehensive comparison of datasets and metrics across diverse application domains (open-world exploration, game engines, embodied AI, autonomous driving) is crucial. This facilitates more consistent and comparable evaluations of new models, accelerating progress by enabling researchers to assess performance against established standards and identify areas requiring more robust benchmarks.
- **Fostering Cross-Disciplinary Innovation:** The survey connects advancements in large language models, diffusion models, and 3D reconstruction with the demands of interactive environments. This interdisciplinary perspective can inspire novel solutions that leverage insights from various AI subfields to address complex problems in interactive world modeling.
- **Accelerating Real-World Applications:** The focus on interactive capabilities and real-time performance directly supports the development of more practical and deployable AI systems.
 - **Immersive Gaming:** Enables the creation of more dynamic, personalized, and responsive game worlds, enhancing user experience.
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Facilitates advanced robot policy learning, data generation for manipulation tasks, and the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents that can operate in complex, interactive environments.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Improves the realism and controllability of driving simulators, allowing for safer and more robust testing of autonomous vehicles, especially in complex, safety-critical scenarios that demand counterfactual reasoning and physics awareness.
- **Shaping Future Directions:** The final section, which outlines future challenges—such as large-scale action-labeled data acquisition, counterfactual reasoning, extreme long-horizon consistency, physical awareness, and accessible direct manipulation interfaces—lays out a strategic agenda for the field. This forward-looking perspective can drive the next generation of breakthroughs, ensuring that interactive world models evolve towards greater reliability, generalization, and user-centric design.
- **Community Resource:** The provision of a public repository for curated lists of research papers further enhances the paper's impact by offering a dynamic, community-driven resource that can continuously track and disseminate progress in the field.

In summary, this paper contributes significantly by synthesizing the current state of interactive world modeling, clarifying its challenges, and charting a course for its future, thereby poised to influence both academic research and practical applications across multiple domains.
