# LatentLens: Revealing Highly Interpretable Visual Tokens in LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mila and affiliated universities introduce LATENTLENS, a training-free method to interpret visual tokens within vision-language models (VLMs) by comparing them to contextualized text representations from an LLM. This approach reveals that visual tokens are consistently interpretable across all layers and align with the semantic richness found in the LLM's intermediate processing layers.

## Problem

- Understanding how large language models (LLMs) integrate visual information when adapted into vision-language models (VLMs) remains a challenge.
- Previous training-free interpretability methods (e.g., EmbeddingLens, LogitLens) often found visual tokens to be largely uninterpretable, particularly in early layers.
- Existing methods were limited by relying on the LLM's fixed vocabulary and static input/output embedding matrices, potentially failing to capture nuanced, contextualized semantics.

## Method

- Developed ==LATENTLENS==, a training-free interpretability technique that compares a VLM's visual token representations to a corpus of contextualized text representations generated by the same LLM across multiple intermediate layers.
- Utilized an ==LLM-based judge==, validated against human annotations, to quantitatively assess the interpretability of visual token descriptions across various VLM architectures.
- Constructed a large reference corpus of ==2.99 million Visual Genome captions==, processed by the LLM, to provide rich, sentence-level descriptions for visual tokens.

## Results

- LATENTLENS showed an average of **72%** of visual tokens were interpretable, outperforming EmbeddingLens (approx. **30%**) and LogitLens (approx. **23%**) across models and layers.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed LATENTLENS provides semantically richer, full-word or sentence-level interpretations that accurately describe visual content, unlike the subword or unrenderable outputs from baselines.
- The method successfully generalized to an off-the-shelf VLM (Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct), maintaining high interpretability (**49-70%**) and exhibiting a similar Mid-Layer Leap pattern.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual tokens are highly interpretable across all LLM layers, with LATENTLENS revealing an average of 72% interpretability, significantly higher than baseline methods.
> - A "Mid-Layer Leap" phenomenon demonstrates that visual tokens, even from early layers, align most strongly with contextualized text representations from the LLM's intermediate layers (e.g., layers 8-16).
> - Visual tokens undergo less internal transformation within the LLM compared to text tokens, suggesting they arrive in a pre-contextualized state compatible with the LLM's semantic processing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically addressing the interpretability of visual information processed by Large Language Models (LLMs). A common approach to transform a pre-trained LLM into a VLM involves mapping visual representations from a vision encoder into the LLM's embedding space, often through a simple linear transformation or a shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). This empirical success raises questions regarding the LLM's capacity to readily process visual tokens and the nature of the information encoded within these representations.

Prior research has explored various hypotheses for this adaptability, including the idea of LLMs as "universal computation engines" capable of processing arbitrary modalities, their implicit learning of world models through natural language training, or a potential convergence of separately trained vision and language representations to a shared structure. However, these hypotheses do not fully explain how visual representations are integrated into the LLM's internal representation space or whether these visual tokens are semantically interpretable.

Existing interpretability methods for VLMs, such as EmbeddingLens (which compares visual tokens to the LLM's static embedding matrix) and LogitLens (which projects latent representations to the unembedding space), have yielded inconclusive results regarding the interpretability of visual tokens. These methods often suggest that visual tokens are rarely interpretable, especially at the input level, and their reliability can vary across models and layers. Training-based approaches, such as sparse autoencoders or supervised probing, have also been applied but have not definitively resolved the question of visual token interpretability in LLMs.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing LATENTLENS, a novel training-free interpretability method. It proposes that a more appropriate comparison for visual token representations is a large pool of contextualized text representations from intermediate LLM layers, rather than static input or output embedding matrices. By providing fine-grained, sentence-level descriptions, LATENTLENS aims to offer a more comprehensive understanding of what visual tokens encode, thereby contributing new evidence on the alignment between vision and language representations and challenging previous conclusions about visual token interpretability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate LATENTLENS, a novel interpretability method designed to reveal the semantic content encoded within visual token representations throughout the processing layers of a VLM.

The specific goals guiding this objective include:
- **Method Development:** To introduce LATENTLENS as a training-free approach that maps latent VLM representations to detailed natural language descriptions. This involves constructing a large reference corpus of contextualized text embeddings from various LLM layers for comparison.
- **Empirical Evaluation:** To systematically evaluate the interpretability of visual tokens across a diverse set of VLM architectures and layers. This includes comparing LATENTLENS against established training-free methods like EmbeddingLens and LogitLens.
- **Quantitative Demonstration:** To provide quantitative evidence that LATENTLENS consistently yields higher interpretability scores for visual tokens, addressing the limitations and inconclusive findings of previous methods.
- **Alignment Characterization:** To investigate the nature of alignment between visual and language representations within the LLM, specifically identifying which layers of the LLM's text processing pipeline correspond most closely to visual token representations at different stages.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** To present qualitative examples illustrating the semantic richness and accuracy of the descriptions generated by LATENTLENS, highlighting its advantages over token-level interpretations.
- **Generalizability Assessment:** To validate the robustness and applicability of LATENTLENS across a range of controlled VLM setups and publicly available, off-the-shelf VLMs, as well as under various ablation conditions.

The motivation for this work stems from several fundamental questions and challenges in the VLM domain:
- **Ease of VLM Adaptation:** The observation that LLMs can be readily adapted to process non-linguistic inputs, such as visual tokens, with minimal architectural changes (e.g., a shallow MLP connector) is intriguing. Understanding the underlying mechanisms that enable this "plug-and-play" capability is a core driver.
- **Inconclusive Prior Interpretability:** Existing interpretability methods have largely provided limited or ambiguous insights into whether visual tokens are truly "language-like" or semantically meaningful within the LLM's internal representations. This necessitates the development of more effective tools.
- **Bridging the Modality Gap:** The research is motivated by the broader goal of understanding the relationship between vision and language representations, exploring whether and how these distinct modalities converge or align within neural networks. Insights into this alignment can inform theories about implicit world models in LLMs or the "Platonic Representation Hypothesis."
- **Advancing Model Understanding and Control:** A deeper understanding of what visual tokens encode at each layer of an LLM can contribute to demystifying the internal workings of foundation models. This knowledge is considered a necessary step towards developing more controllable, predictable, and potentially safer AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around LATENTLENS, a training-free approach for interpreting latent representations in VLMs, which is designed to overcome limitations identified in existing methods.

#### 3.1. Conceptual Foundation: Unified Lens Framework and its Limitations

The paper first unifies existing training-free interpretability methods, EmbeddingLens and LogitLens, under a common framework. Both methods map a latent representation `h^(ℓ)_i` to a natural language description by:
1. Computing a similarity score between `h^(ℓ)_i` and a set of candidate reference vectors `r_j`.
2. Selecting the top-k most similar `r_j`.
3. Returning their corresponding descriptions `d_j`.
The primary limitation of these traditional "lenses" is that their description set `C` is restricted to the LLM's sub-word vocabulary `V`, and they compare latent representations exclusively to the static input embedding matrix or the output unembedding matrix, irrespective of the LLM layer `ℓ`. This restricts the granularity of descriptions and may not reflect the evolving semantic content of representations through deeper layers.

#### 3.2. LATENTLENS: Core Mechanism and Data Generation

LATENTLENS addresses these limitations by proposing that visual token representations should be compared against *contextualized text representations* obtained from intermediate layers of the LLM. The method operates as follows:
- **Reference Corpus Construction:** A large corpus of sentences (e.g., 2.99 million Visual Genome captions) is selected. Each sentence `d_j` from this corpus is passed through the target LLM.
- **Contextualized Embedding Pool:** For every token `t` within each sentence `d_j`, its contextualized representation `r^(ℓ)_(j,t)` is extracted from a selection of intermediate LLM layers (e.g., layers 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 24, L-2, L-1). Up to 20 contextual embeddings per vocabulary token per layer are stored, using float8 compression to manage storage (approx. 26 GB for 8 layers).
- **Similarity Search:** To interpret a visual token representation `h^(ℓ')_i` at a specific layer `ℓ'`, its cosine similarity is computed against *all* stored contextualized text representations `r^(ℓ)_(j,t)` in the pool, allowing for cross-layer comparisons (i.e., `ℓ` and `ℓ'` are not necessarily equal).
- **Description Generation:** The top-k nearest neighbor contextualized text representations are identified. Their corresponding *full sentences* from the original corpus `C` are then returned as descriptions of the visual token. This provides richer, sentence-level interpretations compared to sub-word tokens.

#### 3.3. Interpretability Evaluation

- **Automated LLM Judge:** To quantify interpretability, a GPT-5-based judge is employed. Given an image patch (visual token region highlighted in red, with 8 surrounding visual tokens) and the top-5 candidate descriptions from any method (LATENTLENS, EmbeddingLens, or LogitLens), the judge classifies each description as:
 - **Concrete:** Directly visible in the cropped region (e.g., objects, colors, text).
 - **Abstract:** Conceptually related to the region but not literally present (e.g., emotions, activities, qualities).
 - **Global:** Present elsewhere in the full image but not in the highlighted region.
- **Interpretability Threshold:** A visual token is considered "interpretable" if at least one of its top-5 descriptions is classified as interpretable (concrete, abstract, or global).
- **Validation:** The LLM judge's classifications were validated against human annotations (1,020 instances), demonstrating substantial agreement (Cohen's κ = 0.68). For a fair comparison with baseline methods, LATENTLENS descriptions provided to the judge were limited to the full words corresponding to the top-5 contextualized tokens, rather than full sentences.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup (Controlled and Off-the-Shelf)

- **Controlled Setup:** Nine VLM configurations were created by combining three LLMs (OLMo-7B, Qwen2-7B, LLaMA3-8B) with three pre-trained vision encoders (CLIP-ViT-L/14-336, DINOv2-L-336, SigLIP-so400M-patch14-384). A 3-layer MLP served as the projection function (`proj`), mapping visual features into the LLM's embedding space. The LLM and vision encoder weights remained frozen during training. Models were trained using the Molmo framework on the PixMo-Cap dataset (image-caption pairs) for 12,000 steps.
- **Captioning Performance:** The quality of captions generated by these models was assessed using DCScore, a GPT-4o-based judge, yielding an average score of 6.0/10.
- **Off-the-Shelf VLMs:** To evaluate generalization, LATENTLENS was also applied to six publicly available VLMs: Molmo-7B-D, Molmo-72B, LLaVA-1.5-7B, LLaVA-NeXT-34B, Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, and Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on the OLMo+CLIP-ViT model to assess the robustness of LATENTLENS findings to various training parameters:
- **Random Seed:** Investigating the impact of different connector weight initializations and data sampling.
- **Connector Expressivity:** Comparing a linear projection to the 3-layer MLP.
- **Caption Detail:** Training with single-sentence captions versus the multi-sentence PixMo-Cap dataset.
- **LLM Frozen Status:** Evaluating interpretability when the LLM weights are unfrozen and allowed to adapt during training.
- **Task Type:** Training on a spatial prediction task ("Where is [OBJECT]?") instead of image captioning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the interpretability and alignment of visual tokens in LLMs.

#### 4.1. Quantified Interpretability of Visual Tokens

- **LATENTLENS Outperformance:** LATENTLENS consistently demonstrated higher interpretability of visual tokens across all 15 VLM configurations (9 controlled, 6 off-the-shelf) and all tested layers, compared to EmbeddingLens and LogitLens.
- **Average Interpretability:** Across all models and layers studied, LATENTLENS classified 68% of visual tokens as interpretable. In contrast, EmbeddingLens achieved 32%, and LogitLens 24%.
- **Layer-wise Consistency:** LATENTLENS showed relatively consistent interpretability scores (60–85%) from the input layer through to the final layers, with minor variations across models. This contrasts with LogitLens, which exhibited low interpretability at early layers (<25%) and improved only at later layers (60–80% for some models at final layers), aligning with its known limitations closer to the model's output. EmbeddingLens showed variable interpretability across models.
- **DINOv2 Performance:** Visual tokens from DINOv2-based models, which lack explicit linguistic supervision during pre-training, consistently exhibited high interpretability with all three lenses, suggesting an inherent structural alignment.
- **Robustness to Ablations:** Ablation studies (Section D) indicated that LATENTLENS interpretability remained robust under various training conditions, including different random seeds, using a linear instead of a 3-layer MLP connector, and training on shorter captions. However, interpretability significantly decreased (by ~30%) when the VLM was trained on a non-linguistic spatial prediction task, emphasizing the role of language-based objectives for the observed alignment. Unfreezing the LLM during training led to a slight increase in interpretability (+5.5%).

#### 4.2. The Mid-Layer Leap Phenomenon

- **Cross-Layer Alignment:** A novel finding, termed the "Mid-Layer Leap," revealed that visual token representations from early LLM layers (including the input layer) exhibit the highest cosine similarity with contextualized text representations originating from *middle layers* of the LLM (e.g., layers 8-16), rather than text representations from the same early layers.
- **Semantic Alignment:** This phenomenon suggests that the learned projection into the LLM's embedding space aligns visual tokens with more semantically abstract or contextualized language representations, rather than raw lexical or word-level embeddings found in earlier layers.
- **Token Drift Analysis (Section H):** Further analysis indicated that visual token representations undergo minimal transformation as they propagate through the LLM (maintaining high similarity, often above 0.8, to their input representation through mid-layers). In contrast, text tokens quickly diverge from their input representations, reflecting contextualization. This minimal drift of visual tokens implies they are "pre-contextualized" upon entering the LLM, thus naturally aligning with the more processed, mid-layer text representations.

#### 4.3. Generalization and Robustness

- **Off-the-Shelf Models:** LATENTLENS successfully generalized to six diverse off-the-shelf VLMs, consistently achieving the highest interpretability scores across all of them. The Mid-Layer Leap phenomenon was also observed in these models (Section I), confirming its generality beyond the controlled experimental setup.
- **Corpus Size Sensitivity:** Interpretability was largely robust to the size of the contextual embedding corpus. A 1% subsample of the full corpus yielded interpretability scores only slightly lower (67.3%) than the full corpus (71.6%) (Section 4.5), indicating efficiency gains are possible without significant loss of interpretability.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis of Descriptions

- **Rich and Accurate Descriptions:** Qualitatively, LATENTLENS produced semantically more meaningful and accurate descriptions compared to LogitLens, which often yielded unrenderable tokens, subwords, punctuation, or unrelated non-English tokens. LATENTLENS descriptions, especially when using full sentence contexts (Section K), provided richer spatial and material information.
- **Rendered Text Interpretation:** The method effectively interpreted rendered text within images, accurately identifying words like "couch," "tomato," and "cafe" in visual patches.

#### 4.5. Fine-grained Interpretation Characteristics (Section J)

- **Interpretation Types:** A breakdown of interpretation types revealed that "concrete" interpretations (directly visible entities, colors, textures) predominated (70-75% on average), with "abstract" and "global" interpretations each accounting for 11-15%. These proportions remained stable across layers in frozen LLMs.
- **Parts-of-Speech:** Nouns constituted the most common part-of-speech among nearest neighbors (45-50%), followed by proper nouns (10-20%) and verbs (10-15%). This distribution was consistent across layers, reflecting the visual input's focus on objects and entities.
- **Visual Attributes:** Color words were the most frequent visual attributes (5-6% in early layers, declining to ~3% in later layers), while shape and texture words were rare (<1%). This suggests that raw color information is more prominent in early visual token representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the understanding of how vision and language interact within large multimodal models, with implications for both fundamental AI research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Scientific Contributions

- **Revising Interpretability of Visual Tokens:** The introduction of LATENTLENS fundamentally challenges previous conclusions that visual tokens are rarely interpretable in LLMs. By leveraging contextualized embeddings, the study demonstrates that a significant majority (68%) of visual tokens are consistently interpretable across diverse VLM architectures and layers. This highlights the critical importance of appropriate interpretability methodology.
- **Explaining VLM Adaptability:** The findings offer new insights into why LLMs can be so readily adapted for multimodal processing with minimal architectural changes. The observed "Mid-Layer Leap"—where early visual tokens align with semantically richer, intermediate language representations—suggests that the projection layer effectively transforms visual features into a form already compatible with the LLM's learned semantic space, rather than just its superficial lexical input. This mechanism helps explain the efficiency of connecting separately trained vision and language models.
- **Advancing Vision-Language Alignment Theories:** The work provides empirical evidence that supports the notion of a robust alignment between vision and language representations within neural systems. It contributes to ongoing discussions about the "Platonic Representation Hypothesis" or structural similarities between modalities, suggesting that the underlying representational spaces are more isomorphic than previously indicated.
- **Novel Interpretability Tool:** LATENTLENS offers a training-free, fine-grained, and layer-agnostic interpretability method that generates semantically rich, sentence-level descriptions. Its availability as a pip-package and interactive demo facilitates adoption by the broader research community.

#### 5.2. Practical Implications and Future Work

- **Mitigating VLM Hallucinations:** By providing a clearer understanding of what specific visual tokens encode, LATENTLENS could be instrumental in diagnosing and potentially mitigating VLM hallucinations, a critical challenge in current multimodal AI systems.
- **Causal Ablations and Model Control:** The ability to identify interpretable visual tokens opens avenues for causal ablation studies, where the impact of semantically meaningful versus non-interpretable tokens on downstream task performance can be rigorously investigated. This could lead to more controllable and predictable VLM behavior.
- **Broader Applicability:** The LATENTLENS methodology may extend beyond VLMs to interpret other non-linguistic tokens, such as soft prompts, latent "thinking" tokens in LLMs, or speech representations, offering a unified approach to understanding diverse inputs to foundation models.
- **Dynamic Corpora and Native Multimodal Models:** Future work could explore using dynamically generated corpora for contextual embeddings, adapting LATENTLENS to natively multimodal models (not just those with frozen LLM backbones), or applying it to video processing.

#### 5.3. Acknowledged Limitations

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Storage Requirements:** LATENTLENS necessitates pre-computing and storing a large corpus of contextual embeddings, incurring storage overhead compared to methods relying solely on model weights.
- **Corpus Dependency:** Interpretability judgments are influenced by the choice and content of the reference corpus (Visual Genome), which may introduce biases (e.g., noun dominance) or limit applicability to domains not well-represented.
- **Generalizability to Diverse Architectures:** While tested on 15 VLM configurations, the findings might not fully generalize to VLM architectures that significantly differ from transformer-based models with MLP connectors.
- **Subjectivity of Interpretation:** Despite validation with human annotations, interpretability judgments retain an inherent degree of subjectivity, and the binary classification of "interpretable/non-interpretable" may oversimplify the nuances of interpretation quality.
- **Lack of Causal Link to Downstream Behavior:** The study focuses on *what* tokens encode, but does not explicitly investigate *how* these LATENTLENS interpretations causally affect the downstream behavior or outputs of the VLM.
