# A Closed-Loop Multi-Agent Framework for Robust Multi-Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A closed-loop multi-agent framework enables robust multi-robot manipulation in physical environments by integrating LLM-based semantic planning with real-time verification and hierarchical failure recovery. This framework demonstrated superior success rates and robustness against disturbances across various manipulation tasks, including complex multi-robot collaborations.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based single-robot systems lack mechanisms for coordinating multiple robots or handling tasks that span distributed environments.
- Current multi-robot LLM systems primarily focus on high-level task planning and often assume idealized low-level manipulation, leading to failures when encountering real-world uncertainties during execution.
- There is a gap between robust physical grounding capabilities seen in single-robot manipulation and the collaborative planning capabilities of multi-robot systems, particularly regarding dynamic execution uncertainties.

## Method

- A hierarchical closed-loop LLM-based multi-agent framework is proposed, orchestrated by a **Planning Agent**, a **Manipulation Agent** (per robot), and a **Verification Agent** (per robot).
- The Planning Agent provides high-level semantic reasoning for ==dependency-aware task decomposition== and ==capability-based allocation== to heterogeneous robots.
- Manipulation Agents ground sub-tasks into physical actions using ==VLM-based visual perception== and ==tool-augmented action primitives==, while Verification Agents monitor execution outcomes and trigger ==hierarchical error recovery==.

## Results

- The framework achieved superior success rates and task completion percentages across dual-arm tabletop and multi-robot collaboration tasks, outperforming learning-based (OpenVLA-OFT, 0) and learning-free (ReKep) baselines (e.g., **85%** completion for "Block Stacking" vs. **35-75%** for baselines).
- It demonstrated robust performance under external disturbances (action-level, sub-task level, global level), maintaining an average **63%** success rate in desktop tasks, significantly higher than baselines (**7-20%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of the visual perception tool, Verification Agent, and short-term memory for performance, with keypoint selection identified as the primary failure bottleneck, accounting for **60.4%** of observed failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a closed-loop agentic design with real-time monitoring and hierarchical failure recovery is essential for grounding abstract LLM plans in uncertain physical multi-robot environments.
> - Combining high-level LLM reasoning for task planning with VLM-based visual perception and adaptive tool invocation for fine-grained physical action parameters enhances precision and versatility.
> - Memory mechanisms, including short-term interaction history and a long-term experience pool, improve the efficiency, consistency, and robustness of LLM-robot interactions by resolving ambiguities and caching validated actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotics has emerged as a significant development, offering capabilities in semantic understanding and logical reasoning for complex tasks. Within this landscape, existing LLM-based approaches broadly fall into two categories.

The first category focuses on single-robot manipulation. These methods leverage LLMs to generate manipulation policies, code, grasping guidance, or constraints, often achieving robust performance in contact-rich tasks. However, their scope is typically limited to individual workspaces and they generally lack mechanisms for coordinating multiple robots or handling tasks that span distributed environments.

The second category addresses multi-robot systems, primarily focusing on high-level task allocation, symbolic planning, and inter-robot communication. These frameworks utilize LLMs for decomposing instructions into multi-stage plans or coordinating actions among heterogeneous robots. While effective at the planning level, these approaches frequently treat low-level manipulation as an idealized primitive, assuming deterministic success. They often validate plans prior to execution and do not typically incorporate dynamic verification or feedback mechanisms during actual physical execution. This oversight can lead to system failures when real-world uncertainties, such as grasp failures, environmental disturbances, or workspace constraints, are encountered.

This research aims to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It seeks to combine the robust physical grounding capabilities seen in single-robot manipulation with the collaborative planning capabilities of multi-robot systems. The proposed framework distinguishes itself by introducing a closed-loop, agentic approach that explicitly addresses execution uncertainties through real-time monitoring and hierarchical failure recovery, moving beyond the open-loop execution typical of many existing multi-robot LLM-based systems. It attempts to provide a system that can adapt from single-robot operations to multi-robot collaborative tasks while maintaining robustness against real-world disturbances.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work is to overcome the limitations of current LLM-based multi-robot systems, specifically their struggle to robustly ground high-level reasoning in physical execution amidst real-world uncertainties. While multi-robot systems offer inherent advantages like parallelism, redundancy, and expanded capabilities for long-horizon tasks, and LLMs provide powerful reasoning for task decomposition, their effective integration remains a challenge.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Robust Multi-Agent Framework:** To propose a hierarchical closed-loop LLM-based framework that enables multi-robot systems to perform complex manipulation tasks reliably in physical environments. This framework is designed to move from static execution pipelines to an iterative agentic process.
2. **Integrate Semantic Planning with Physical Grounding:** To create a system where high-level semantic instructions from LLMs are effectively translated into fine-grained physical actions for robots. This involves ensuring that planning considers both the logical dependencies of tasks and the specific capabilities of heterogeneous robots.
3. **Ensure Self-Correction and Adaptability:** To implement a robust feedback mechanism that allows the system to perceive environmental feedback, identify execution errors, and adaptively recover from failures. This includes handling various types of uncertainties such as grasp failures, contact variability, external disturbances, and workspace constraints.
4. **Achieve Versatile Manipulation Capabilities:** To demonstrate that the framework can perform a diverse range of manipulation skills, from precise pick-and-place operations to articulated object interactions, and can scale from single-robot tabletop setups to complex cross-workspace collaborations involving multiple, heterogeneous robots.
5. **Enable Hierarchical Recovery:** To design a multi-level recovery mechanism that can diagnose the cause of errors and trigger appropriate re-planning strategies, either locally for execution-level failures or globally for capability constraints requiring inter-robot collaboration.

Ultimately, the research aims to ensure the reliable deployment of multi-robot systems for complex manipulation tasks by empowering them with active reasoning, action, and reflection capabilities in dynamic, uncertain real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework is a closed-loop multi-agent system designed for robust multi-robot manipulation, integrating semantic planning, physical manipulation, and verification into an iterative cycle. It takes natural language instructions and coarse scene descriptions as input and is coordinated by three specialized agents: the Planning Agent, the Manipulation Agent, and the Verification Agent.

**A. System Overview**
The framework operates as a collaborative agentic workflow. The Planning Agent provides high-level reasoning and task decomposition. The Manipulation Agent translates sub-tasks into real-world actions for each robot. The Verification Agent assesses action feasibility and execution outcomes, closing the loop by providing feedback for hierarchical re-planning.

**B. Planning Agent: Semantic Reasoning and Task Allocation**
The Planning Agent acts as the semantic reasoning core, interpreting user instructions and generating a high-level task graph.
- **Dependency-Aware Task Decomposition:** It decomposes the global objective into a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of logical sub-tasks, identifying sequential and parallel operations. This includes a "parallel flag" for concurrent execution by heterogeneous robots.
- **Capability-Based Allocation & Interactive Refinement:** It assigns sub-tasks to robots based on their capabilities (e.g., mobile vs. fixed base, dual-arm vs. single-arm). For ambiguous instructions or insufficient scene information, it can send a `further_perception` flag to query the Manipulation Agent for fine-grained observations, then refines the task graph.

**C. Manipulation Agent: Physical Action Grounding**
Each robot is equipped with a Manipulation Agent, responsible for translating logical sub-tasks into direct robot control. It uses adaptive tool invocation and re-perceives the environment between sub-operations.
- **Role-Based Operation Resolution:** It breaks down sub-tasks into fine-grained actions using VLM-based reasoning. It categorizes entities into active/passive roles and determines semantic part descriptions (e.g., "handle"), also evaluating if dual-arm actions are needed.
- **Tool-Augmented Visual Perception:** A visual grounding pipeline converts semantic roles into action parameters.
 - **Object Detection & Segmentation:** A VLM-based detector identifies bounding boxes, followed by Segment Anything Model (SAM) for precise pixel-level masks.
 - **Affordance-Aware Keypoint Selection:** A visual prompting tool localizes optimal 3D interaction points (e.g., upper part of a cup handle), projecting 2D keypoints into 3D using depth information. For dual-arm grasping, a geometric heuristic identifies symmetric key points.
- **Action Primitives Generation with Adaptive Tool Invocation:** The agent synthesizes operations by querying an action primitive library (Table I), adaptively invoking specialized tools.
 - **Grasp Pose Generation:** For grasping, it triggers AnyGrasp to generate 6-DoF grasp poses, matching them against 3D semantic keypoints to minimize spatial alignment error. For small, articulated, or bimanual objects, it uses geometric heuristics.
 - **Rotation Tool:** For tasks requiring specific object orientation, a VLM-guided tool projects the end-effector frame onto the object's center and performs spatial reasoning to predict the necessary rotation axis, direction, and angle.
- **Memory-Augmented Reasoning:**
 - **Short-Term Memory:** Maintains an interaction history to resolve ambiguous sub-task instructions and aid verification.
 - **Long-Term Memory (Experience Pool):** Records validated sub-task decompositions and action primitives as templates. If an incoming sub-task matches a template signature, it uses the cached actions directly, reducing VLM API calls and accelerating execution.

**D. Verification Agent: Local Monitoring and Hierarchical Recovery**
Each Manipulation Agent is paired with a Verification Agent to ensure reliability by monitoring task states and triggering recovery.
- **Discrete Visual Outcome Validation:** It employs a visual feedback tool to semantically assess each sub-operation by analyzing pre- and post-action images relative to instructions, generating a binary success prediction.
- **Hierarchical Error Recovery:**
 - **Local Recovery:** For execution failures where the task is feasible (e.g., grasp slip), the agent initiates a local recovery loop without disrupting the global plan. It constructs a corrective sequence based on interaction history and visual states, dispatched to the Manipulation Agent.
 - **Global Recovery:** If an action is identified as physically infeasible (e.g., out of workspace), the Verification Agent sends failure feedback to the Planning Agent, triggering a global recovery loop. The Planning Agent then generates a new task graph, potentially re-allocating the sub-task to another robot.

The system utilizes Qwen3-Max for the Planning Agent and Qwen3-VL-Plus for Manipulation and Verification Agents, accessed via Alibaba Cloud DashScope API.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework was evaluated through extensive real-world experiments on six tasks, categorized into dual-arm tabletop tasks and multi-robot collaboration tasks, demonstrating versatility, robustness, and adaptability.

**A. Dual-arm Table Top Tasks Performance (Block Stacking, Table Organization, Object Stowing)** The framework was compared against learning-based baselines (OpenVLA-OFT, π0) and a learning-free LLM-based method (ReKep).
- **Versatility:** The proposed method achieved superior success rates and task completion percentages across all three tasks (Table II). For "Block Stacking," it reached 14/20 successful trials (85% completion) compared to baselines ranging from 1/20 (35%) to 9/20 (75%). "Table Organization" saw 11/20 successes (76%), and "Object Stowing" 14/20 successes (78%), outperforming baselines significantly.
- **Analysis of Baselines:** Learning-based methods struggled with contact-rich tasks due to lack of precise geometric grounding, leading to collisions and undetected failures in long-horizon tasks. ReKep failed completely in "Object Stowing" due to limitations in defining key points for articulated objects and produced sub-optimal solutions in "Block Stacking."
- **Framework Strengths:** The framework's tool-augmented perception provided the necessary precision for stacking, diverse action primitives offered versatility, and the Verification Agent ensured closed-loop robustness by detecting and recovering from errors.

**B. Robustness to External Disturbances** The framework was tested under three types of disturbances: action-level (object displacement), sub-task level (environment reset), and global level (object out of fixed-base robot's workspace).
- **Superior Robustness:** Under these perturbations, the proposed method maintained a robust average success rate of 63% across desktop tasks (Table III), a slight decrease from undisturbed conditions. Learning-based baselines showed significantly lower average success rates (OpenVLA-OFT: 7%, π0: 20%) due to the absence of closed-loop feedback and inability to recover from complex disturbances.
- **Validation of Verification Loop:** The results underscore the necessity of the verification loop for maintaining performance in dynamic, uncertain environments.

**C. Verification Agent Analysis**
The Verification Agent's performance was analyzed using Failure Identification Rate (ID), Re-planning Success Rate (Re-Plan), Physical Recovery Success Rate (Phy-Rec), and Final Task Success Rate (SR).
- **High Failure Identification:** The agent demonstrated strong failure identification capabilities, with ID rates around 90–100% across tasks (Table IV), indicating effective VLM-based outcome assessment.
- **Effective Recovery:** In single-robot tasks, the Re-plan Success Rate was consistently 100%, though physical recovery varied depending on the reversibility of the state (e.g., 80% Final SR for "Object Stowing" vs. 50% for "Block Stacking" due to irreversible block collapse).
- **Global Recovery Validation:** For multi-robot tasks like "Basket Shelving," the system successfully identified workspace-exceeding errors and performed global re-planning with 100% success, re-allocating the task to a mobile robot.

**D. Ablation Study**
An ablation study assessed the contribution of key components: visual perception tool, Verification Agent, short-term memory, and long-term memory.
- **Critical Role of Visual Perception:** Removing the object-specific keypoint selection tool led to a catastrophic performance drop (Table V, Fig. 4), highlighting its essential role in bridging high-level plans to physical manipulation.
- **Importance of Verification Agent:** Ablation of the Verification Agent resulted in significant performance decline, especially in long-horizon tasks, confirming that closed-loop error detection and recovery are crucial for complex multi-robot tasks.
- **Value of Short-Term Memory:** Removing short-term memory negatively impacted sequential manipulation, as historical context was vital for resolving ambiguous sub-task instructions and generating logically consistent sub-operations.
- **Long-Term Memory for Efficiency/Stability:** Removing the experience pool caused only a slight decline in performance, demonstrating that while it enhances efficiency and stability by avoiding VLM variance, the core Manipulation Agent is robust enough not to strictly rely on memorization.

**E. System Error Breakdown**
An analysis of 48 failure cases from the full model experiments revealed the dominant failure modes (Fig. 5).
- **Keypoint Selection Bottleneck:** Keypoint selection accounted for 60.4% of failures, primarily due to VLMs struggling to localize keypoints precisely according to semantic constraints.
- **Action Primitives Generation:** Failures in generating action primitives accounted for 16.7%, often related to kinematic solution issues or incorrect VLM reasoning about keypoint relations.
- **Minor Failures:** Other failure types, such as Verification Agent errors (10.4%) and grasp failures (8.3%), constituted smaller percentages. Irrecoverable physical constraints (e.g., collisions, noisy sensor data) also contributed to a minor portion of failures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that advances the field of multi-robot manipulation by effectively integrating high-level language reasoning with robust low-level physical execution in real-world environments. Its significance lies in several aspects:

1. **Bridging the Reality Gap:** The closed-loop, agentic design directly addresses the long-standing challenge of grounding abstract LLM plans in the uncertainties of the physical world. By moving beyond idealized primitives, the framework allows multi-robot systems to actively detect and recover from execution failures, a critical capability for real-world deployment.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** The hierarchical recovery mechanism, distinguishing between local execution errors and global feasibility constraints, contributes to a more robust system. This enables continuous operation and task completion even in the presence of external disturbances, enhancing the reliability of multi-robot systems in dynamic settings.
3. **Versatility and Adaptability:** The framework's ability to handle diverse manipulation tasks (from precision pick-and-place to articulated object interactions) and scale from single-robot tabletop setups to complex multi-robot, cross-workspace collaborations demonstrates its versatility and adaptability, making it applicable to a wider range of scenarios.
4. **Practical Tool Integration:** The adaptive tool invocation within the Manipulation Agent, leveraging specialized modules for visual perception, keypoint selection, and grasp generation, represents a practical approach to achieving fine-grained control and grounding semantic instructions. The memory mechanisms further enhance efficiency and consistency.
5. **Foundation for Future Autonomous Systems:** This work lays a foundation for more autonomous and intelligent multi-robot systems that can operate with less human intervention. The explicit modeling of uncertainty and recovery mechanisms moves closer to truly intelligent robotic agents capable of self-diagnosis and self-correction.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations. The current reliance on a predefined library of action primitives restricts the system's ability to handle more complex, novel manipulation tasks. Additionally, the system's efficiency is constrained by the execution speeds of heterogeneous robots, with a simple dependency check rather than dynamic scheduling to optimize global efficiency.

Future work will focus on integrating learning-based primitive discovery to expand manipulation capabilities and exploring concurrent task allocation methods to minimize idle time and enhance the overall efficiency of multi-robot collaboration. Addressing the identified bottleneck in keypoint selection by improving VLM-based localization precision is also an area for further development.
