# LLaVA-SP: Enhancing Visual Representation with Visual Spatial Tokens for MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaVA-SP, developed by researchers from Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications and Beihang University, enhances multimodal large language models by introducing a novel projector that efficiently captures visual spatial information. The method adds only six spatial visual tokens, enabling significant improvements across 10 out of 11 multimodal benchmarks, including visual grounding and hallucination mitigation, while maintaining inference efficiency comparable to LLaVA-1.5.

## Problem

- Mainstream MLLMs using CLIP-ViT vision encoders struggle to capture fine-grained perceptual details and suffer from disrupted spatial relationships due to ViT's patch flattening.
- Existing methods to improve visual understanding often lead to a significant increase in visual token counts, resulting in substantially higher training and inference costs.
- Deficiencies in visual representation contribute to issues like visual hallucinations and poor performance in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning or object localization.

## Method

- LLaVA-SP enhances the standard MLLM architecture (CLIP-ViT + Projector + LLM) by introducing a novel Projector design with two components: a ==Spatial Feature Extractor (SFE)== and a ==Detail Feature Integrator (DFI)==.
- The SFE uses ==convolutional kernels== to generate a small set of visual spatial tokens (six) by either progressively cropping or adaptively pooling multi-scale 2D features from the ViT encoder's output.
- The DFI employs a cross-attention mechanism to integrate fine-grained details from a broader feature map into these spatial tokens, enriching their information content without increasing their count.

## Results

- LLaVA-SP outperforms LLaVA-1.5 on **10 out of 11** multimodal benchmarks (e.g., **+5.9%** on VizWiz, **+2.5%** on TextVQA), achieving a **1.5-1.6%** average normalized score improvement.
- The model maintains nearly identical inference latency compared to LLaVA-1.5 (approx. **20.5 tokens/s** vs. **20.76 tokens/s**), adding only six spatial visual tokens.
- LLaVA-SP-Cropping improved visual grounding on RefCOCO and demonstrated better performance in spatial relation, object localization, and physical relation tasks, while both variants showed improved scores on hallucination mitigation benchmarks (POPE, MMVP).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Re-introducing 2D spatial relationships and fine-grained details via a specialized projector can significantly enhance MLLM visual understanding without substantially increasing token count or inference latency.
> - Convolutional kernels are more effective than transformer blocks for extracting spatial information within the projector, while the optimal number of spatial tokens is small (e.g., six).
> - The Detail Feature Integrator (DFI) effectively enhances 'cropping'-based spatial features but can negatively impact 'pooling'-based features, highlighting different mechanisms for detail integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has rapidly evolved, demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and generating content across visual and linguistic modalities. The core challenge in MLLMs lies in effective modality alignment—bridging the semantic gap between visual and textual representations. Current mainstream MLLMs, exemplified by the LLaVA series, typically employ a vision encoder (often CLIP-ViT) connected to a large language model (LLM) via a simple projector (e.g., an MLP).

However, the paper identifies critical limitations of this prevalent architecture, particularly concerning the CLIP-ViT vision encoder:
1. **Fine-grained Detail Understanding:** CLIP-ViT, pre-trained on noisy image-text pair datasets via contrastive learning, struggles to capture intricate perceptual details.
2. **Spatial Relationship Disruption:** ViT's inherent mechanism of flattening 2D image patches into a 1D sequence disrupts the intrinsic spatial relationships between adjacent patches. While adept at global feature extraction, it falters in modeling local relationships, leading to a weaker visual representation for detailed understanding.

The broader research landscape for visual-enhanced MLLMs has explored several strategies to mitigate these issues:
- **Increased Image Resolution:** Approaches like LLaVA-1.5 (336 resolution) and InternVL-1.5 (dynamic 1024 resolution) aim to provide more raw visual information.
- **More Powerful Vision Encoders:** InternVL employed a larger InternViT model, and SPHINX combined multiple vision encoders (CLIP-ViT, ConvNext, DINOv2-ViT).
- **Multiple Visual Features:** Monkey fed different image blocks in parallel, and Mini-Gemini simultaneously input low and high-resolution images.
- **Projector Improvements:** Honeybee designed a CNN-based Q-former, and DeCo used adaptive average pooling to reduce visual tokens.

A significant drawback of many of these existing methods is the **increase in visual token counts**, which directly leads to substantially higher training and inference costs. This paper explicitly positions itself against this trend, posing the central question: "Can we fully leverage the capabilities of the vision encoder to enhance visual feature representation without significantly increasing the number of visual tokens?"

LLaVA-SP carves out its niche by focusing on an **efficient enhancement of the existing vision encoder's output** without resorting to massive token increases. It builds upon the successful LLaVA-1.5 architecture but proposes a novel, specialized projector. Unlike end-to-end MLLMs (e.g., Fuyu-8B, EVE) that forgo pre-trained vision encoders, LLaVA-SP aims to unlock the "potential of vision encoder to enhance visual representation" within the established framework, ensuring compatibility with widely adopted pre-trained visual backbones while addressing their known weaknesses in spatial and fine-grained understanding. This makes LLaVA-SP a valuable contribution for improving the efficiency and detail-oriented capabilities of a broad category of MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind LLaVA-SP stems from the identified shortcomings of current MLLM architectures, particularly their reliance on CLIP-ViT for visual encoding. While CLIP-ViT excels at capturing global image features and performs well in general understanding tasks, its design inherently limits its ability to model local relationships between adjacent patches and understand fine-grained perceptual details. This weakness in visual representation directly impedes the detailed understanding capabilities of MLLMs, leading to issues like visual hallucinations or an inability to answer precise questions about an image's content.

The paper sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

1. **Enhance Visual Representation without Significant Token Increase:** The core objective is to improve the depth and granularity of visual features provided to the LLM, specifically by enriching the spatial information, but critically, without incurring the prohibitive computational costs associated with generating a large number of additional visual tokens. The proposed solution aims to add *only six spatial visual tokens* to the existing visual tokens.
2. **Restore Lost Spatial Information:** To explicitly re-introduce the 2D spatial relationships that are disrupted when ViT flattens image patches into a 1D sequence. This is crucial for improving the model's ability to reason about object locations, relationships, and fine-grained details.
3. **Improve Detailed Understanding:** By providing richer visual representations, the model aims to enhance the MLLM's ability to understand subtle visual cues, perform accurate visual grounding, and mitigate common problems like visual hallucinations, which often arise from a superficial understanding of visual content.
4. **Offer Task-Specific Adaptability:** To develop a flexible solution that can be tailored to different types of visual understanding tasks. This leads to the proposal of two distinct model variants: one focused on detailed features (cropping) and another on global semantics (pooling).
5. **Maintain Inference Efficiency:** A practical objective is to achieve performance improvements while maintaining inference latency comparable to existing state-of-the-art models like LLaVA-1.5, making the solution viable for real-world applications.

In essence, LLaVA-SP is motivated by the need to bridge the gap between global visual understanding and fine-grained spatial reasoning in MLLMs, offering an elegant and computationally efficient solution that leverages the inherent capabilities of convolutional operations to capture missing spatial context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LLaVA-SP maintains the fundamental three-part architecture common in MLLMs: a Vision Encoder, a Projector, and a Large Language Model (LLM). Its innovation primarily resides within the **Projector** component.

#### 3.1. Vision Encoder

The paper utilizes the pre-trained **CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 model** as its vision encoder. This choice aligns with the established practice in the LLaVA series, providing a strong baseline for global image feature extraction. The vision encoder processes an input image \(X_v\) to produce ViT patch features, denoted as \(Z_p\).

#### 3.2. Large Language Model (LLM)

**Vicuna-1.5** (7B parameters) is selected as the LLM. The visual tokens generated by the projector are concatenated with the language instruction tokens (\(H_q\)) and fed sequentially into the LLM for autoregressive training, following the standard LLaVA paradigm.

#### 3.3. Novel Projector Design

The core of LLaVA-SP's methodology lies in its novel Projector, which is composed of two primary sub-components: the **Spatial Feature Extractor (SFE)** and the **Detail Feature Integrator (DFI)**, along with two parallel MLPs for final token transformation.

##### 3.3.1. Spatial Feature Extractor (SFE)

The SFE is designed to capture the 2D spatial relationships that are lost when ViT flattens image patches. Its design adheres to two principles: obtaining multi-scale features that preserve 2D structure and using convolutional kernels for feature extraction. The SFE generates only six additional visual spatial tokens.

To obtain multi-scale features, two distinct operations are proposed:

- **LLaVA-SP-Cropping:**
 - **Motivation:** Emphasize detailed regional features.
 - **Mechanism:** The \(Z_p\) (ViT patch features) are first reshaped back to their original 2D shape (24x24 for CLIP-ViT-L/14-336). The SFE then progressively crops the feature map inward with a stride of 2, starting from the full 24x24 map and continuing until the central region (e.g., 4x4). This generates a sequence of multi-scale features (e.g., 24x24, 20x20..., 4x4).
 - **Spatial Ordering:** These features are then conceptually arranged in a "from central region to global" order, ensuring that finer details from smaller regions are prioritized.
- **LLaVA-SP-Pooling:**
 - **Motivation:** Capture global semantics and abstract information.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of cropping, this variant uses adaptive average pooling layers to generate multi-scale features from the 2D reshaped \(Z_p\). This simulates human visual perception, moving from abstract (smaller feature maps) to specific (larger feature maps).
 - **Spatial Ordering:** These features are arranged in an "from abstract to specific" order.

For both methods, after obtaining these multi-scale 2D feature maps, **convolutional kernels** of varying sizes (k=4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24) are applied to each to capture visual spatial tokens. These 1x1xC output tokens (one per scale) are then concatenated to form \(Z_{s-small}\) (6 tokens of dimension 1x1xC), which represents the primary visual spatial features.

##### 3.3.2. Detail Feature Integrator (DFI)

The DFI's purpose is to further enrich the visual spatial features (\(Z_{s-small}\)) by integrating fine-grained details, crucially without increasing the number of tokens. It addresses the trade-off where large convolutional kernels might miss finer details.

- **Mechanism:** DFI employs a cross-attention mechanism.
 - \(Z_{s-small}\) (the six spatial tokens) serve as the **query (Q)**.
 - \(Z_{s-big}\) (a larger feature map extracted using a smaller convolutional kernel, e.g., 16x16, from the full 24x24 ViT patch features) serves as the **key (K)** and **value (V)**.
 - Through cross-attention, fine-grained information from \(Z_{s-big}\) is mined and injected into \(Z_{s-small}\).
- **Output:** The attention-weighted features are then concatenated (channel-wise) with the original \(Z_{s-small}\) to form the final enhanced visual spatial features, \(Z_{vs}\) (6 tokens of dimension 1x1x2C).

Finally, two parallel MLPs transform \(Z_{vs}\) into visual spatial tokens \(H_{vs}\) and the original \(Z_p\) into visual patch tokens \(H_{vp}\). These \(H_{vs}\) and \(H_{vp}\) tokens are then concatenated with language tokens \(H_q\) and fed into the LLM.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

LLaVA-SP adopts a two-stage training strategy similar to LLaVA-1.5:
1. **Pre-training Stage:** The LLM is frozen, and only the Projector (SFE and DFI components) is trained to align visual and language representations.
2. **Instruction Tuning Stage:** Both the LLM and the Projector are fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) to enhance the model's ability to follow human instructions.

The training datasets include 558K pre-training data (LAION, Conceptual Captions, SBU Captions) and 665K instruction-following data (LLaVA Synthetic Data).

This comprehensive methodology allows LLaVA-SP to enhance the visual understanding of MLLMs by cleverly re-introducing spatial information and fine-grained details through a specialized projector, all while maintaining computational efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation of LLaVA-SP across various multimodal benchmarks demonstrates significant improvements and validates its design principles.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Efficiency

- **Benchmark Superiority (Table 1):** LLaVA-SP (both Cropping and Pooling variants), fine-tuned with LoRA, consistently outperforms the fully trained LLaVA-1.5 model on 10 out of 11 multimodal benchmarks. Notable improvements include +5.9% on VizWiz, +2.5% on TextVQA, +2.1% on SQA-IMG, and +1.3% on MM-Vet, MMBench, and LLaVA-Bench. The average normalized score (AvgN) across 11 benchmarks showed improvements of 1.5% and 1.6% for Cropping and Pooling, respectively, over the fully trained LLaVA-1.5.
- **Inference Speed (Table 2):** Despite the performance gains, LLaVA-SP maintains nearly identical inference latency compared to LLaVA-1.5 (20.51 and 20.28 tokens/s vs. 20.76 tokens/s). This is a critical achievement, demonstrating that the enhanced visual representation does not come at the cost of practical efficiency, especially when compared to methods that increase visual token counts (e.g., Qwen-VL with more tokens has lower throughput).

#### 4.2. Spatial Feature Extractor (SFE) Analysis

- **Effectiveness:** Ablation studies (Table 3) confirm the SFE's overall effectiveness, with both Cropping and Pooling methods leading to improvements over the LLaVA-1.5 baseline.
- **Convolution vs. Transformer Blocks (Table 3):** Convolutional kernels for SFE consistently outperform transformer blocks. This validates the design choice, attributing convolution's superiority to its inherent strength in extracting spatial information from images. Attempts with multi-layer CNNs or very small-scale kernels resulted in training instability, suggesting the chosen kernel sizes and single-layer application are optimal.
- **Optimal Number of Spatial Tokens (Table 4):** Experiments on LLaVA-SP-Cropping show that six visual spatial tokens provide the best balance of performance and efficiency. While 12 tokens showed comparable performance, they doubled parameters and slowed inference. The "crop step by step" method for obtaining these tokens, which considers adjacent features in all directions, proved more effective than a simple "sliding window" approach (Table 5), further validating the human visual perception-inspired design.

#### 4.3. Detail Feature Integrator (DFI) Analysis

- **Impact of Z_s-big Size (Table 6):** For both Cropping and Pooling, a Z_s-big feature map size of 5x5 (corresponding to a 16x16 convolutional kernel) yielded the best performance improvement for DFI. This suggests that a broader receptive field in \(Z_{s-big}\) provides more beneficial fine-grained details for integration.
- **Differential Impact on Cropping vs. Pooling (Table 6 & Figure 5):** DFI enhances LLaVA-SP-Cropping but negatively affects LLaVA-SP-Pooling in most individual benchmarks (though Pooling's overall AvgN might still improve slightly due to strong performance on a few benchmarks). This crucial finding is attributed to the pooling operation's tendency to abstract original features, making it difficult for the attention mechanism in DFI to discern significant fine-grained details. Visualizations of attention maps (Figure 5) support this, showing a more concentrated, less uniform attention distribution for Pooling on non-semantic (Gaussian noise) images, indicating a less effective feature fusion.

#### 4.4. Enhanced Visual Understanding Capabilities

- **Visual Spatial Understanding (Table 8):** LLaVA-SP-Cropping achieved the best AvgN score (46.5) across spatial relation, object localization, and physical relation tasks (MME, MMBench, SEED-IMG), outperforming other specialized methods like Honeybee and DeCo. This confirms its effectiveness in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning.
- **Visual Grounding (Table 9):** Both LLaVA-SP variants significantly enhance visual grounding capabilities on the RefCOCO benchmark, with LLaVA-SP-Cropping achieving the highest scores. This further underscores its strength in fine-grained local image understanding, crucial for identifying and localizing specific objects.
- **Hallucination Mitigation (Table 10):** Both LLaVA-SP-Cropping and LLaVA-SP-Pooling show improved scores on POPE and MMVP benchmarks, indicating their ability to alleviate the hallucination problem. This suggests a better distinction of subtle visual differences, addressing the "CLIP-Blind problem."

#### 4.5. Generalization (Table 7)

- The LLaVA-SP method demonstrates strong generalization across different vision encoders (SigLIP-L/16) and MLLM frameworks (InternVL-2.0). Applying SP to these models resulted in consistent performance improvements, suggesting that the approach is complementary and can be effectively integrated with other powerful visual backbones and MLLM designs. This indicates that the limitations in spatial representation within CLIP (or similar ViT encoders) persist even with stronger models, and LLaVA-SP offers a generalized solution to address this.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that LLaVA-SP successfully achieves its objectives: significantly enhancing MLLM visual understanding and mitigating hallucination, all while maintaining high inference efficiency by intelligently leveraging a minimal number of additional spatial tokens. The dual variants offer flexibility for tasks requiring either fine-grained or global understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LLaVA-SP represents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal large language models, particularly in its elegant and efficient approach to enhancing visual representation. Its potential impact spans both academic research and practical applications:

1. **Efficiency-Performance Paradigm Shift:** The most profound impact of LLaVA-SP is its demonstration that substantial improvements in MLLM visual understanding can be achieved without the conventional trade-off of increased computational costs and inference latency. By adding only six spatial visual tokens, the method provides a powerful alternative to strategies that rely on higher resolutions or more visual tokens. This efficiency is critical for deploying MLLMs in real-time applications and resource-constrained environments.

2. **Addressing Core Vision Encoder Limitations:** LLaVA-SP directly tackles a fundamental weakness of widely adopted vision encoders like CLIP-ViT: their struggle with local spatial relationships and fine-grained details due to patch flattening. By re-introducing and enriching this spatial context through a novel projector, the work unlocks capabilities that were previously underdeveloped, leading to more robust and accurate visual understanding.

3. **Improved MLLM Reliability:** The demonstrated ability to mitigate visual hallucinations is highly significant. Hallucinations are a persistent and challenging problem in generative AI, eroding user trust. LLaVA-SP's enhanced visual grounding and ability to discern subtle visual differences contribute directly to more faithful and reliable MLLM outputs, making these models more trustworthy for critical applications.

4. **Versatile Visual Understanding:** The introduction of two distinct variants, LLaVA-SP-Cropping (for detailed understanding) and LLaVA-SP-Pooling (for global semantics), offers practical versatility. Researchers and developers can choose the variant best suited for their specific task requirements, whether it's precise object localization, fine-grained reasoning, or holistic scene comprehension. This modularity enhances the adaptability of MLLMs.

5. **Broad Applicability and Generalizability:** The successful generalization of LLaVA-SP to different vision encoders (SigLIP) and MLLM frameworks (InternVL-2.0) highlights its potential as a complementary enhancement. This suggests that the proposed projector design is not tightly coupled to a specific base model but can improve the visual understanding capabilities across a wide range of existing and future MLLM architectures that utilize patch-based vision encoders. This indicates that the core problem it solves (spatial information loss in ViT) is widespread.

6. **Future Research Directions:** LLaVA-SP opens new avenues for research in MLLM projector design. It encourages further exploration into how visual information can be effectively distilled, structured, and presented to LLMs with minimal overhead. Future work might focus on even more sophisticated spatial feature extraction techniques, dynamic adaptation between cropping and pooling strategies based on input image characteristics, or optimizing the projector's parameters for even greater efficiency. The paper also points to the potential of scaling the method to larger LLMs, which could unlock even more impressive multimodal reasoning capabilities.

In conclusion, LLaVA-SP offers an intelligent, efficient, and broadly applicable solution to enhance the visual representation within MLLMs. By focusing on the quality of visual tokens rather than simply their quantity, it marks an important step towards building more capable, reliable, and computationally practical multimodal AI systems.
