# CoFineLLM: Conformal Finetuning of LLMs for Language-Instructed Robot Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> CoFineLLM is a conformal-prediction-aware finetuning framework for LLM robot planners. It simulates conformalization inside training mini-batches, periodically recalibrating the confidence threshold, and adds a conformal regularizer to cross-entropy that penalizes non-singleton prediction sets containing the ground-truth action. On BabyAI-Text planning with Gemma3-1B plus LoRA, it cuts help rate 23.67% and raises intervention-free verification 21.05% over uncertainty-agnostic finetuning while preserving 95% coverage.

## Problem

- Conformal prediction is applied only post hoc to uncertainty-agnostic LLM planners, so prediction sets stay large and human help is requested at prohibitively many steps.
- Existing conformal training methods target single-step classifiers, not sequential multi-step decision-making where correctness must hold across a whole action plan.
- Prior conformal losses saturate when a pretrained model is overconfident yet wrong, giving near-zero gradients that halt learning for models far from convergence.

## Method

- **Conformal finetuning** loop simulates calibration inside training: every K epochs the threshold is recomputed from a calibration set using a worst-case-over-horizon ==nonconformity score==.
- Composite loss adds a ==conformal regularizer== to cross-entropy, penalizing the strongest rival action's margin above threshold only when the ground-truth action already lies in the set.
- Optimization uses ==LoRA== adapters on a frozen backbone with phase-based ==curriculum learning== that introduces harder mission categories while retaining simpler ones against forgetting.

## Results

- At **95%** target coverage, set size falls to **1.109** and help rate to **9.7%** versus **1.138** and **12.7%** for uncertainty-agnostic finetuning; verification rises **41.2%** to **49.8%**.
- The ConfTr-style uncertainty-informed baseline fails entirely, stuck at **100%** help rate and set size **6** (full action space), confirming the vanishing-gradient diagnosis.
- Generalization holds at untrained coverage levels (help rate down **72.45%** at **85%**, **7.36%** at **96%**) and on out-of-distribution hardware scenarios (help rate down **48.67%** over 80 missions).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gating an efficiency penalty on the prediction already being correct avoids objective conflict early in training, keeping accuracy and set-size goals from fighting each other.
> - Pure set-size losses assume a near-converged model; combining them with cross-entropy is what keeps gradients alive for overconfident-but-wrong pretrained models.
> - Calibration-time uncertainty shaping transfers: a model finetuned at one coverage level keeps its efficiency gains at other levels and under distribution shift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical and rapidly evolving intersection of artificial intelligence and robotics: **language-instructed robot planning**. The broader landscape for this work is characterized by several key developments and challenges:

Firstly, **the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) as high-level planners for robots** has been a transformative trend. LLMs' impressive natural language understanding and generation capabilities have enabled robots to interpret complex human instructions and generate sequences of actions to accomplish high-level missions (Ahn et al., 2022; Singh et al., 2023; Shah et al., 2023). This paradigm shift offers a more intuitive interface for human-robot interaction compared to traditional formal task specification languages.

Secondly, despite their promise, **LLM-based planners suffer from significant reliability challenges**. They are prone to "hallucinations," generating overconfident but incorrect outputs, especially in long-horizon or safety-critical tasks. This lack of robustness severely limits their autonomous deployment in real-world scenarios. Addressing this uncertainty and improving the trustworthiness of LLM outputs is a major area of active research.

Thirdly, **Conformal Prediction (CP)** has emerged as a powerful, distribution-free statistical tool for quantifying uncertainty and providing valid coverage guarantees for machine learning models. Recent work has applied CP *post-hoc* to LLM outputs, particularly for multiple-choice question-answering (MCQA) tasks and subsequent robot planning (Ren et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025b). By generating prediction sets that are guaranteed to contain the correct action with a user-defined probability, CP offers a mechanism to ensure plan correctness. When a prediction set is a singleton, the robot executes the action; otherwise, it requests human help.

However, a significant limitation of existing CP applications is that **LLMs are typically trained in an uncertainty-agnostic manner**. Applying CP only at test time, without the model being aware of its uncertainty during training, often leads to inefficient prediction sets that are unnecessarily large. This results in frequent human interventions, negating some of the benefits of autonomous operation and increasing the "help rate" to prohibitively high levels.

Finally, while **conformal training methods** have been proposed for neural network classifiers (e.g., Stutz ets al., 2022), these approaches often focus on single-step classification tasks and may not directly translate to the complexities of sequential decision-making or the unique characteristics of LLMs. As highlighted by the authors, some existing conformal training losses can be sensitive to the initial model quality and prone to vanishing gradients when applied to LLMs that are far from convergence and exhibit overconfident incorrect predictions.

CoFineLLM directly addresses this critical gap by introducing the **first CP-aware fine-tuning framework for LLM-based planners in sequential decision-making tasks.** It integrates CP into the training loop, enabling LLMs to learn to produce more efficient and smaller prediction sets while still maintaining coverage guarantees. By doing so, this work aims to significantly reduce human intervention rates, thereby pushing the frontier of reliable and autonomously deployable LLM-driven robotic systems. It represents an evolution from post-hoc uncertainty quantification to integrated uncertainty-aware learning for LLMs in interactive, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for CoFineLLM stems from the inherent tension between the impressive capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in interpreting natural language instructions for robot planning and their critical shortcomings in reliability and efficiency. While LLMs excel at generating action sequences for complex tasks, their propensity for "hallucinations" – producing overconfident yet incorrect outputs – poses a significant barrier to their practical deployment in autonomous systems.

Existing solutions, particularly those leveraging Conformal Prediction (CP), have provided a crucial safeguard by guaranteeing the correctness of a robot's plan with a user-defined probability. However, these methods typically apply CP as a post-hoc analysis at test time. This approach overlooks the fact that LLMs are fundamentally trained in an "uncertainty-agnostic" manner. Consequently, while the probabilistic correctness is assured, the LLMs often generate excessively large prediction sets, especially at higher confidence levels, leading to frequent and undesirable human interventions. This high "help rate" undermines the promise of autonomous operation, transforming what should be a robust planner into a system that constantly demands human oversight.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of CoFineLLM are:

1. **To introduce the first uncertainty-aware fine-tuning framework for LLM-based planners:** This involves integrating Conformal Prediction directly into the LLM's training process, moving beyond post-hoc application.
2. **To explicitly reduce prediction-set sizes and, consequently, the help rate:** The primary goal is to optimize the LLM's outputs such that, at test time, the prediction sets generated by CP are as small as possible, ideally singletons, thereby minimizing the need for human assistance, while maintaining the user-defined coverage guarantee.
3. **To preserve the probabilistic coverage guarantees of Conformal Prediction:** Crucially, the fine-tuning process must not compromise the theoretical assurance that the correct action is contained within the prediction set with the specified confidence level.
4. **To develop a novel loss function suitable for sequential decision-making with LLMs:** The proposed loss function must effectively balance task accuracy with prediction set efficiency, and overcome limitations of prior conformal training methods, such as vanishing gradients, especially when fine-tuning large, pre-trained LLMs that might initially be far from optimal.
5. **To demonstrate robustness to varying test-time coverage thresholds and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios:** The fine-tuned model should generalize well, maintaining its benefits even when the desired confidence level or the operational environment differs from those encountered during training.

In essence, CoFineLLM aims to teach LLMs not just *what* the correct action is, but also *how confident* they should be, in a way that minimizes ambiguity and maximizes autonomy for robot planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoFineLLM introduces a novel, Conformal Prediction (CP)-aware fine-tuning framework for Large Language Models (LLMs) specifically designed for sequential, language-instructed robot planning. The core innovation lies in integrating CP principles directly into the LLM's training objective and process, rather than applying them solely at test time.

The overall approach can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation as Sequential MCQA

The problem of language-instructed robot planning is framed as a sequence of Multiple-Choice Question-Answering (MCQA) tasks. At each time step `t`, the LLM `g` is presented with a "question" (`ℓ(t)`) – a textual description of the robot's current scenario, past actions, and the natural language mission (`ϕ`). The "choices" are the finite set of available robot actions `A`. The LLM outputs confidence scores `g(a|ℓ(t))` for each action.

#### 3.2. Conformal Prediction for Plan Correctness

Following established CP-based planning methods (Ren et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025b), a prediction set `C(t)` is constructed at each step `t`. This set includes all actions `a` for which `g(a|ℓ(t))` exceeds a dynamically computed threshold `δ`. If `C(t)` is a singleton, that action is executed. Otherwise, human intervention is requested. This process ensures that the entire generated plan `τ` is correct with a user-specified probability `1-α`.

#### 3.3. Conformal Threshold (`δ`) Computation (Algorithm 1)

The threshold `δ` is crucial for constructing prediction sets. It is derived from a calibration dataset `M_cal` consisting of `D` independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) mission scenarios with ground-truth plans.
- **Nonconformity Score (NCS):** For each calibration scenario `ξ_i`, the NCS `¯r_i` is defined as `1 - min_{t} g(τ_{i,cal}(t) | ℓ_{i,cal}(t))`. This quantifies the worst-case "error" (or rather, lack of confidence in the correct action) of the LLM across all steps of a mission.
- **Threshold Calculation:** The `(1-α)` empirical quantile of these `¯r_i` values yields `ˆq`, from which `δ = 1 - ˆq` is computed. This `δ` is then used to form prediction sets.

#### 3.4. CoFineLLM: Conformal Fine-Tuning (Algorithm 2)

The core of CoFineLLM is its fine-tuning process, which explicitly integrates the CP mechanism:
- **Model Initialization:** The fine-tuning starts with a pre-trained LLM (Gemma3-1B). Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used to fine-tune, adapting small adapter layers while keeping the backbone frozen, which is efficient for large models.
- **Dynamic Threshold Update:** Unlike prior work, the threshold `δ` is not static. Since the LLM's parameters (and thus its confidence scores) evolve during training, `δ` is **periodically re-computed** (every `K` epochs) using Algorithm 1 and the current model parameters on the calibration dataset `M_cal`. This simulates the test-time conformalization process within the training loop.
- **Novel Loss Function `L(θ, δ)`:** The fine-tuning objective minimizes a compound loss function:
 `L(θ, δ) = L_CE(θ) + λ L_CP(θ, δ)`
 - **Cross-Entropy Term (`L_CE(θ)`):** This standard term encourages the model to assign higher probabilities to the ground-truth actions, improving the LLM's base task accuracy.
 - **Conformal Prediction Term (`L_CP(θ, δ)`):** This novel regularizer aims to minimize the size of prediction sets. Crucially, it only introduces penalties **when the ground-truth action `y` is already contained within the prediction set AND the set is non-singleton.**
 `L_CP(θ, δ) = (1/|B|) Σ_{(ℓ,y)∈B} ψ(p_y, δ) [p_max - δ]₊`
 - `p_y = g(y | ℓ)`: Confidence score for the correct action.
 - `p_max = max_{a≠y} g(a | ℓ)`: Confidence score for the "strongest rival" action (highest confidence among incorrect actions).
 - `ψ(p_y, δ) = 1(p_y ≥ δ)`: An indicator function, 1 if the correct action's confidence meets the threshold, 0 otherwise.
 - `[p_max - δ]₊`: ReLU function, penalizing only when `p_max ≥ δ`, meaning the strongest rival action is also included in the prediction set, making it non-singleton.
- **Rationale for `L_CP` Design:** This conditional penalization is key. It ensures that the model first focuses on correctness (getting `y` into the prediction set) via `L_CE`. Only once `y` is reliably included does `L_CP` then push the model to refine its scores to shrink prediction set size by increasing `p_y` relative to `p_max` or decreasing `p_max` below `δ`. This design mitigates the vanishing gradient problem observed in prior conformal training methods, which might penalize large sets even when the model is incorrect, leading to unstable learning for LLMs.
- **Differentiability:** The non-differentiable indicator `ψ(p_y, δ)` is approximated using a smooth sigmoid function `σ(T(p_y - δ))` to enable gradient-based optimization.
- **Curriculum Learning:** A phase-based curriculum learning strategy is employed to gradually introduce more complex mission categories during training, enhancing stability and preventing catastrophic forgetting.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Environment:** BabyAI-Text environment (Carta et al., 2023), a language-conditioned grid-world simulator, supporting tasks like GoTo, PickUp, PickUpThenGoTo, and PutNext.
- **Action Space:** A discrete set of 6 primitive robot actions.
- **Prompt Engineering:** Detailed prompts are constructed including system description, environment state, task description, response format, action history, and step index.
- **Datasets:** Separate training, calibration, and validation datasets are sampled from an in-distribution `D`, and an out-of-distribution `D'` for robustness testing.

By dynamically updating the CP threshold during training and using a carefully designed loss function that prioritizes correctness before optimizing for prediction set efficiency, CoFineLLM enables the LLM to become "conformal-aware" and inherently reduce unnecessary human interventions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CoFineLLM demonstrates consistent and significant improvements over baseline methods, validating its effectiveness in achieving lower human intervention rates while preserving coverage guarantees.

#### 4.1. Comparative Evaluation in In-Distribution Scenarios (Table 1)

- **Pre-trained Model Performance:** Before fine-tuning, the Gemma3-1B model performed poorly, requiring 100% human help to achieve 95% coverage, indicating overconfident but wrong predictions.
- **CoFineLLM vs. Baselines:** At a target coverage level of `1-α = 0.95` (both during finetuning and validation), CoFineLLM (with `λ=0.1`) significantly outperformed both the Uncertainty-Agnostic (UA) baseline (optimizing only cross-entropy) and the Uncertainty-Informed (UI) baseline (using ConfTr loss).
 - **Help Rate:** CoFineLLM reduced the help rate by **23.67%** (from 12.7% to 9.7%) compared to the UA baseline.
 - **Verification Rate:** CoFineLLM increased the verification rate (missions completed without help) by **21.05%** (from 41.2% to 49.8%) relative to the UA baseline.
 - **Set Size:** CoFineLLM achieved a smaller average prediction set size (1.109 vs. 1.138 for UA).
 - **Coverage Rate:** All fine-tuned models, including CoFineLLM and UA, maintained coverage rates very close to the desired `1-α = 0.95`, demonstrating CP's effectiveness in upholding guarantees.
- **Failure of UI Baseline:** The UI baseline (ConfTr loss) failed to learn any useful patterns, remaining at a 100% help rate with an average prediction set size of 6 (the size of the entire action space). This validates the authors' hypothesis about the vanishing gradient issue when applying such losses to overconfident and incorrect pre-trained LLMs. CoFineLLM's loss function, by combining cross-entropy and conditionally penalizing only when the correct action is in the set, successfully avoids this pitfall.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies: Generalization to Unseen Test-time Coverage Thresholds (Table 2)

- The fine-tuned CoFineLLM model (trained with `1-α=0.95` and `λ=0.1`) was evaluated on unseen test-time coverage targets (`1-α ∈ {0.85, 0.90, 0.96}`).
- CoFineLLM consistently outperformed the UA baseline across all tested coverage levels.
 - At `1-α=0.85`, help rate decreased by **72.45%** (from 3.7% to 1.0%), and verification rate increased by **20.87%** (from 76.3% to 92.2%).
 - Even at a stricter `1-α=0.96`, CoFineLLM reduced help rate by **7.36%** and increased verification rate by **1.1%**.
- These results highlight the strong generalization capability of CoFineLLM's uncertainty calibration, where the benefits persist even when the deployment confidence requirement differs from training.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies: Sensitivity to Parameter `λ` (Table 3)

- The `λ` parameter, controlling the trade-off between task accuracy and CP efficiency, was varied (`0.1, 0.3, 0.5`) at `1-α=0.95`.
- All `λ` values showed improvements over the UA baseline.
- A `λ=0.1` yielded the strongest improvements in help rate (-23.67%) and verification rate (+21.05%). `λ=0.3` also provided significant gains.
- This demonstrates that CoFineLLM is robust to the choice of `λ`, consistently improving prediction-set size and help rates, suggesting that even a small regularization term is effective.

#### 4.4. Hardware Experiments & Comparative Evaluations in OOD Mission Scenarios (Table 4 & Fig. 2)

- The fine-tuned CoFineLLM model was tested on out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where validation tasks (`D'`) involved different environments and objects (e.g., traffic cones, fire hydrants, cars in a 3x5 grid world) than the training data (`D`).
- **Robustness:** Even with a threshold `δ` calibrated on in-distribution data, CoFineLLM demonstrated substantial improvements in OOD settings:
 - Help rate dropped by **48.67%** (from 11.7% to 6%) compared to the UA baseline.
 - Verification rate increased by **21.70%** (from 58.9% to 71.7%).
- Crucially, both methods preserved the `1-α` coverage level despite the distribution shift, demonstrating the inherent robustness of CP's guarantees.
- **Hardware Demonstration:** The success was further validated on a physical TurtleBot3 Waffle-Pi robot in a miniature urban environment, showcasing the practical applicability and sim-to-real transferability of the method for tasks like "put the fire hydrant next to the red car."

In summary, CoFineLLM successfully addresses the limitations of uncertainty-agnostic LLM fine-tuning and post-hoc CP. It leads to more efficient and trustworthy LLM-based robot planners that require significantly less human intervention, generalize well across confidence levels, and perform robustly in novel, out-of-distribution environments, marking a crucial step towards reliable autonomous robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of CoFineLLM marks a significant advancement in the field of language-instructed robot planning and, more broadly, in the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) in safety-critical autonomous systems. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

#### 5.1. Enhancing Trustworthiness and Reliability of LLM-based Robotics

CoFineLLM directly tackles one of the most pressing challenges of using LLMs in robotics: their inherent unreliability and tendency to hallucinate. By making LLMs "uncertainty-aware" during training, the framework enables them to generate plans with verifiable probabilistic guarantees, ensuring that the robot's actions are correct with a user-defined confidence level. This fosters greater trust in LLM-driven autonomous agents, which is paramount for deployment in real-world, safety-critical applications where failures can have severe consequences.

#### 5.2. Reducing Human Intervention for Increased Autonomy

A primary benefit demonstrated by CoFineLLM is the substantial reduction in the "help rate" – the frequency with which a robot needs to query a human for disambiguation. By optimizing LLMs to produce smaller, more precise prediction sets, the method allows robots to execute more steps autonomously. This translates to more efficient robot operation, reduced operational costs, and the ability to deploy robots in environments where continuous human oversight is impractical or impossible. This is a critical step toward truly autonomous and scalable robotic systems.

#### 5.3. Advancing Conformal Prediction Integration into Deep Learning

The paper makes a notable methodological contribution by proposing the first CP-aware fine-tuning framework for LLMs in *sequential decision-making tasks*. It addresses the limitations of previous conformal training methods (like ConfTr), which struggled with large, overconfident, and initially incorrect LLMs by providing a more stable and effective loss function. This robust integration of CP into the training loop of foundation models opens new avenues for developing inherently trustworthy AI, not just in robotics but potentially across various domains requiring calibrated uncertainty.

#### 5.4. Bridging the Gap Between Research and Real-World Deployment

The validation of CoFineLLM through hardware experiments in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios is particularly impactful. Demonstrating that the method retains its benefits even when the robot operates in novel environments with unfamiliar objects, and on a real physical platform, significantly strengthens the case for its practical applicability. This moves the research beyond purely simulated results, highlighting its potential to tackle the complexities and unpredictability of real-world robotic tasks.

#### 5.5. Broader Applicability and Future Research Directions

While developed for language-instructed robot planning, the core principles of CoFineLLM's conformal fine-tuning framework could be generalized to other sequential decision-making problems or other LLM applications where calibrated uncertainty and reduced human intervention are critical. This could include complex control systems, medical diagnostics with LLMs, or any domain where LLM outputs require high reliability and confidence quantification. The framework encourages further research into how to embed uncertainty awareness deeper into the training of large foundation models, beyond just post-hoc application.

In conclusion, CoFineLLM represents a pivotal development for robotics and AI. By enabling LLMs to learn not only *what* to do but also *how confident* they are in their decisions, and by doing so efficiently within the training process, it paves the way for a new generation of LLM-powered robots that are more reliable, autonomous, and capable of operating effectively in complex, uncertain real-world environments with minimal human supervision.
