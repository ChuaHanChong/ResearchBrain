# Flow Matching Imitation Learning for Multi-Support Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical control architecture is presented that utilizes Flow Matching imitation learning to enable the Talos humanoid robot to perform complex multi-support manipulation tasks. This system learns multi-modal whole-body behaviors, including dynamic contact placement and switching, demonstrating robust execution autonomously and through shared autonomy on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots typically compartmentalize locomotion and manipulation, preventing them from leveraging their entire body for stability, extended reach, and complex multi-contact tasks.
- Model-based approaches for multi-contact tasks require precise environment models and struggle with non-linear dynamics and the computational demands for real-time, reactive behaviors.
- Traditional imitation learning methods fail to capture multi-modal distributions in expert demonstrations, often producing averaged, unfeasible actions for tasks with multiple valid strategies.

## Method

- A hierarchical control architecture combines a high-level ==Flow Matching== policy for learning multi-modal behaviors with a low-level model-based whole-body controller (SEIKO) for robust execution and contact force management.
- Flow Matching, a generative model, learns continuous flow vector fields to transform a simple distribution into the complex, multi-modal expert trajectory distribution, enabling deterministic and faster inference compared to Diffusion models.
- A shared autonomy mode allows a human operator to command primary manipulation while the learned policy intelligently assists by autonomously managing support contact placements and switches, aiding in out-of-distribution tasks.

## Results

- In simulation, Flow Matching achieved 99-**100%** success in multi-modal contact placement, significantly outperforming supervised learning (**52%** success) and demonstrating slightly better out-of-distribution generalization than Diffusion models.
- The Talos humanoid robot successfully executed autonomous multi-support manipulation tasks, such as closing dishwasher drawers and pushing boxes, by dynamically adding and removing support contacts.
- Assistive shared autonomy enabled the robot to perform out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., pushing a novel box shape) that purely autonomous policies failed, by intelligently automating support contact placement for human operators.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Flow Matching effectively captures and samples from multi-modal distributions in robotic tasks, yielding superior performance over traditional supervised learning and competitive results with Diffusion models.
> - The deterministic nature and faster inference of Flow Matching (35ms) compared to Diffusion models (178ms for DDPM) make it particularly suitable for real-time, reactive robotic control.
> - A robust hierarchical integration of generative imitation learning with a model-based whole-body controller is essential for translating complex learned behaviors into safe, feasible, and stable actions on full-size humanoid robots.
