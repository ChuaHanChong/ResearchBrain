# COOPER: A Unified Model for Cooperative Perception and Reasoning in Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> COOPER, developed by researchers from Chinese Academy of Sciences and Baidu Inc., introduces a unified multimodal large language model that intrinsically generates auxiliary visual cues, such as depth and segmentation maps, and adaptively integrates them with textual reasoning. The model achieves an average 6.91% improvement in spatial reasoning and a 4.47% gain on general multimodal benchmarks, surpassing several larger and proprietary models on specific spatial tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limited 3D-aware reasoning, struggling to infer geometry, depth, and spatial relationships from 2D images.
- Existing MLLM approaches typically treat perception enhancement (e.g., using auxiliary modalities) and reasoning enhancement (e.g., textual Chain-of-Thought) as separate processes, hindering their interdependent benefits.
- Current unified MLLMs lack the ability to internally generate non-RGB auxiliary modalities and adaptively interleave such generation with textual reasoning during problem-solving.

## Method

- COOPER extends a unified MLLM to intrinsically generate ==depth estimation and semantic segmentation maps== by representing these non-RGB auxiliary modalities as RGB-compatible pseudo-images.
- An ==adaptive interleaved reasoning framework== is implemented using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to teach basic dynamic behavior and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for refinement.
- A novel ==Cooperative Perception–Reasoning (CPR) reward==, incorporating an exploration-guided component, optimizes the model's adaptive scheduling of visual assistance during problem-solving.

## Results

- COOPER demonstrates an average **6.91%** improvement in spatial reasoning tasks and a **4.47%** improvement on general multimodal benchmarks compared to its base model.
- The model surpasses proprietary GPT models and the 38B-parameter open-source InternVL3.5-38B on fine-grained spatial perception benchmarks like Q-SpatialBench.
- Ablation studies show that learning auxiliary modality generation (Stage 1) alone provides a **7.92%** gain on Q-SpatialBench, indicating internalized spatial knowledge, and the exploration-guided reward (`r_e`) component effectively refines adaptive reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying intrinsic perception (generating auxiliary spatial cues) and adaptive reasoning within a single MLLM leads to superior spatial intelligence compared to approaches that treat these capabilities in isolation.
> - Learning to generate auxiliary visual modalities inherently strengthens an MLLM's internal spatial understanding and knowledge, even before explicit reasoning supervision.
> - An adaptive 'Multimodal Chain-of-Thought,' where the model dynamically decides when to generate and incorporate visual information with textual reasoning, mirrors human-like cognitive processes and enhances problem-solving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of artificial intelligence, specifically within the domain of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): **Visual Spatial Reasoning (VSR)**. VSR involves enabling models to perceive, understand, and reason about object properties and spatial relationships in 3D space based on visual inputs, a capability fundamental for achieving human-level intelligence in vision-language models and for applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and AR/VR.

The current research landscape in MLLMs, despite significant advancements, struggles with robust 3D-aware reasoning. Existing approaches generally fall into two categories, which the authors argue are often treated in isolation:

1. **Perception Enhancement:** These methods augment standard RGB inputs with auxiliary modalities such as depth maps, semantic segmentation, or 3D point clouds. The goal is to enrich low- and mid-level visual features, thereby improving the extraction of geometric cues. While effective at the perception level, these approaches typically yield only marginal gains in high-level spatial reasoning and complex decision-making, as the enhanced perception is not fully integrated with adaptive reasoning.
2. **Reasoning Enhancement:** This line of work focuses on strengthening the model's ability to decompose and analyze complex problems, often through textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. Techniques include training on specialized spatial Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets with annotated reasoning trajectories and employing reinforcement learning to refine reasoning policies. However, these methods primarily perform spatial reasoning from raw 2D images without explicit 3D grounding, leading to brittle and inconsistent spatial understanding.

The authors identify a crucial gap: "perception and reasoning constitute two interdependent pillars of spatial intelligence" but are typically improved independently. Without precise geometric perception, high-level planning is prone to error, and without robust reasoning, even accurate perception struggles to support complex tasks.

COOPER directly addresses this gap by proposing a **unified MLLM that cooperatively unifies perception and reasoning**. This work leverages the emerging capabilities of unified MLLMs—single models capable of both multimodal understanding and generation (e.g., image-to-text, text-to-image)—to bridge the divide. Previous unified MLLMs primarily focused on photorealistic RGB image generation and text, lacking the intrinsic ability to generate non-RGB auxiliary modalities (like depth or segmentation) or to adaptively interleave such generation with textual reasoning. COOPER's innovation lies in equipping a unified MLLM with the ability to *internally generate* these informative spatial cues and then *adaptively decide* when to generate and use them as part of a "Multimodal Chain-of-Thought" for enhanced spatial intelligence. This positions COOPER at the forefront of efforts to create more holistic and human-like AI systems capable of complex visual-spatial cognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind COOPER is to overcome the limitations of current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in visual spatial reasoning by fostering a cooperative relationship between perception and reasoning capabilities within a single, unified model. The paper explicitly states its core question: "Can a single model unify perception and reasoning in a cooperative way to achieve stronger spatial intelligence?"

From this overarching question, the key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

1. **Enhance 3D-aware Reasoning in MLLMs:** MLLMs, predominantly trained on 2D image-text pairs, exhibit limited 3D awareness. They struggle to infer geometry, depth, and object boundaries from raw pixels, leading to significant performance gaps compared to human spatial understanding. COOPER aims to directly address this deficiency.
2. **Unify Perception and Reasoning within a Single Model:** Recognizing the interdependence of accurate geometric perception and robust high-level reasoning, the authors are motivated to move beyond approaches that treat these two aspects in isolation. The goal is to build a unified MLLM that can intrinsically perform both, allowing for a more integrated and powerful approach to spatial intelligence.
3. **Enable Intrinsic Auxiliary Modality Generation:** Current perception enhancement methods often rely on external, fixed pipelines to provide auxiliary modalities. A key objective for COOPER is to empower the unified MLLM to *generate its own* non-RGB auxiliary modalities (e.g., depth maps, segmentation masks) internally. This is challenging because unified MLLMs are typically optimized for photorealistic RGB outputs.
4. **Achieve Adaptive, Interleaved Spatial Reasoning:** Beyond merely generating auxiliary modalities, COOPER aims to enable the model to *adaptively schedule* when to engage in perception (i.e., generate auxiliary visual cues) and when to perform textual reasoning during problem-solving. Existing MLLMs lack such flexible control mechanisms, following predefined generation pipelines. This adaptive interleaving is crucial for developing a dynamic "Multimodal Chain-of-Thought" that mirrors human problem-solving, where visual and cognitive processes are tightly coupled.
5. **Develop a Robust Training Methodology:** To achieve the above objectives, the authors are motivated to design a fine-grained training pipeline. This pipeline must first equip the MLLM with auxiliary modality generation capabilities and then optimize it for adaptive, interleaved reasoning, balancing exploration and exploitation in its decision-making process.
6. **Empirical Validation of a New Paradigm:** The authors aim to empirically demonstrate that this novel paradigm—where an MLLM intrinsically generates and exploits auxiliary visual signals for spatial reasoning—can lead to significant performance gains on spatial intelligence benchmarks while maintaining or improving general multimodal capabilities.

In essence, the motivation is to build a more intelligent, autonomous MLLM that not only "sees" better by generating its own spatial cues but also "thinks" more effectively by adaptively integrating these cues into a dynamic reasoning process, thereby moving closer to human-level spatial cognition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

COOPER's methodology revolves around extending a unified Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to intrinsically generate auxiliary visual modalities and adaptively interleave these generative perception steps with textual reasoning. The approach is structured into two core stages built upon the **BAGEL** framework, a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture employing Rectified Flow for visual generation.

#### 3.1. Auxiliary Modality Generation (Stage 1)

This stage equips BAGEL with the ability to generate non-RGB auxiliary modalities (depth estimation and semantic segmentation) without altering its fundamental architecture or training objective, which is designed for RGB image generation.

- **Representing Auxiliary Modalities in RGB Space:**
 - **Segmentation:** Single-channel segmentation masks are converted into RGB pseudo-images by assigning distinct RGB colors to different instances. This makes them compatible with BAGEL's existing RGB-based image generation pipeline.
 - **Depth Estimation:** Ground-truth depth maps (single-channel) are replicated across three channels to form an RGB-like input. An affine transformation is then applied to scale their values into the `[-1, 1]` range, aligning with the VAE's expected numerical input for latent space operations. During inference, the channel-wise mean of the VAE decoder's three-channel output yields the final depth prediction.
- **Training and Inference:** The model is jointly trained on aggregated open-source datasets (HyperSim and Virtual KITTI for depth, ADE20K for segmentation) using the original flow matching loss. Crucially, during both training and inference, task-specific prompts (e.g., `<depth-estimation>...</depth-estimation>`) are used as conditioning signals to dynamically select the appropriate decoder path for generating depth or segmentation maps.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Interleaved Reasoning (Stage 2)

Building on the model's newfound ability to generate auxiliary modalities, this stage focuses on enabling adaptive, interleaved reasoning using a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework.

- **Data Construction:**
 - **Seed Data Collection:** The SAT VQA dataset (for spatial reasoning) and TACO dataset (for general QA) are used as initial data.
 - **Initial Evaluation:** The base BAGEL model is evaluated on this data in two settings: raw input (`acc_raw`) and augmented with ground-truth depth/segmentation maps (`acc_aux`). This helps identify the impact of auxiliary modalities.
 - **Data Filtering and Partitioning:** Samples with trivial (`acc_raw` = 1) or extremely hard (`acc_raw` = 0) answers are discarded to ensure meaningful learning signals for RL. The remaining data is categorized based on the "accuracy gain" (`acc_aux - acc_raw`) relative to a threshold (λ = 0.375), indicating whether auxiliary modalities provide a positive, negative, or negligible ("boundary") effect. Boundary examples are subsampled and split for SFT and RL.
 - **SFT Data Generation (GPT-4o Curated):** GPT-4o acts as an agent to construct interleaved vision-language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data. Crucially, GPT-4o uses the **Stage 1 trained BAGEL as callable tools** for depth estimation and segmentation. This grounds GPT-4o's "reflections" in the actual capabilities of the model being trained, and examples with incorrect final answers are filtered out, ensuring high-quality SFT data.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The model undergoes SFT on the GPT-4o curated interleaved CoT data using a cross-entropy objective. Importantly, only the *textual reasoning and answer tokens* within the CoT are optimized. The visual content, being self-generated, is not optimized during SFT to prevent introducing noise or target drift. This teaches the model basic dynamic activation and reasoning behaviors.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with CPR Reward:** To refine the SFT-trained model's adaptive reasoning and generalization, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed with a novel **Cooperative Perception–Reasoning (CPR) reward**. The CPR reward is a composite of three components:
 - `r_a` (Answer Reward): A binary reward (1.0 for correct, 0.0 for incorrect) based on the final answer.
 - `r_f` (Format Reward): Rewards adherence to the interleaved CoT format (e.g., `<think>...</think><depth-estimation>...</depth-estimation><think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`).
 - `r_e` (Exploration-Guided Reward): This critical component uses the *offline visual-gain labels* (positive, negative, boundary from data construction) and a threshold `σ` to guide the model's use of visual assistance. It positively rewards visual assistance on samples where it historically provided a positive gain, but only if the proportion of visual assistance generated (`u(O)`) is below `σ`. Conversely, it penalizes visual assistance on samples where it provided a negative gain, but only if `u(O)` is above `σ`. Boundary cases receive no exploration reward. This mechanism prevents both the overuse and undersuppression of visual assistance, promoting an adaptive and balanced policy.

The combination of converting auxiliary modalities into an RGB-compatible format, using a high-quality GPT-4o-curated dataset, and designing a nuanced CPR reward with GRPO enables COOPER to develop an intrinsic, adaptive, and cooperative approach to spatial perception and reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

COOPER demonstrates significant advancements in spatial intelligence for MLLMs, validated through extensive experiments on both spatial reasoning and general multimodal benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Improvements

- **Significant Spatial Reasoning Enhancement:** COOPER achieves an average improvement of **6.91%** in spatial reasoning performance compared to its base model, BAGEL.
 - Specifically, on the Q-SpatialBench (focused on distance and size estimation), COOPER impressively **surpasses proprietary GPT models** and the 38B open-source InternVL3.5-38B, demonstrating its strong quantitative fine-grained perception and estimation capabilities.
 - On the comprehensive SIBench spatial reasoning benchmark, COOPER reaches a performance level comparable to GPT-4o, a leading proprietary model.
- **Maintained (and Improved) General Multimodal Capability:** While specializing in spatial reasoning, COOPER also yields an average improvement of **4.47%** on general multimodal benchmarks (MMBench and MM-Vet) compared to the base BAGEL model. This indicates that enhancing specialized spatial intelligence does not come at the cost of general multimodal capabilities; rather, it provides a modest overall gain.
- **Superiority of Interleaved Reasoning:** The results clearly show that COOPER, with its interleaved vision-language reasoning, offers a higher performance ceiling than baselines focusing solely on perception enhancement (BAGEL-PE) or purely textual reasoning enhancement (BAGEL-RE). COOPER achieves an average 4.59% improvement on spatial tasks over BAGEL-PE and an average 1.3% improvement on general tasks over BAGEL-RE. This validates the core hypothesis that unifying perception and reasoning cooperatively is more effective.

#### 4.2. Variant Analysis (Ablation Studies)

Ablation studies provide insights into the contribution of each training stage:

- **Impact of Auxiliary Modality Generation (Stage 1):** Even a variant trained *only* for auxiliary modality generation (without reasoning supervision, denoted "+ Stage1") achieves a substantial **7.92% gain on Q-SpatialBench** (distance and size estimation) and about 4.63% improvement on general benchmarks. This crucial finding suggests that **learning to generate auxiliary modalities helps the model internalize spatial knowledge and strengthens spatial understanding**, even before explicit reasoning training.
- **Role of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** For a relatively weaker base model like BAGEL, SFT plays a crucial role, providing a substantial average improvement of **5.86%**. This establishes a strong foundation for adaptive reasoning behavior.
- **Contribution of Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL, applied after SFT, further refines the model's capabilities, adding an additional **6.68% gain**. This indicates that while SFT teaches basic patterns, RL is essential for enhancing adaptive reasoning and generalization.
- **Effectiveness of the Exploration-Guided Reward (`r_e`):** The `r_e` component of the CPR reward consistently improves performance across benchmarks compared to RL with only format and outcome rewards. This confirms that `r_e` effectively guides the model in deciding *when and how strongly* to invoke visual assistance, leading to better capability selection and scheduling.

#### 4.3. Reasoning and Auxiliary Modality Analysis

- **Adaptive Reasoning Patterns:** COOPER adaptively selects its reasoning mode based on the task type:
 - For **Relative Distance (RD)** tasks, it frequently generates depth maps.
 - For **Situational Question Answering (SQA)** tasks, it often highlights target objects in segmentation maps.
 - For **Geometric Reasoning (GR)** tasks, it primarily relies on pure textual reasoning, as auxiliary visual modalities offer minimal benefit.
 - Qualitative examples visually demonstrate COOPER's process: analyzing the task, selecting an appropriate auxiliary modality, generating it, and then synthesizing information from the original image and the generated modality for the final answer.
- **High-Quality Auxiliary Modality Generation:** COOPER's generated depth and segmentation maps are found to be comparable to, and in some aspects, even superior to specialized models.
 - **Segmentation:** Qualitatively, COOPER often produces finer boundaries and more distinguishable colors than ground truth, demonstrating detailed object recognition.
 - **Depth Estimation:** COOPER generates sharper depth maps with clearer boundaries than the specialized Marigold model on the out-of-domain NYUv2 dataset, and quantitatively, its performance (AbsRel, δ1) is on par with Marigold. This showcases that its internal generative capabilities are highly competitive.

In summary, COOPER not only achieves significant quantitative improvements in spatial reasoning and understanding but also demonstrates a robust, adaptive, and intrinsically capable approach to leveraging generated visual cues, positioning it as a leading model in the pursuit of comprehensive spatial intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

COOPER presents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and visual AI, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **A Novel Paradigm for Spatial Intelligence:** COOPER introduces and empirically validates a new paradigm that unifies perception and reasoning within a single MLLM through cooperative and adaptive interleaved processing. This is a fundamental shift from traditional approaches that treat these capabilities in isolation or rely on external tools. By demonstrating that a model can *internally generate and exploit* auxiliary spatial modalities, COOPER lays the groundwork for more holistic and autonomous AI systems.

2. **Enhanced 3D-Aware Visual Reasoning:** The work directly addresses a critical weakness of current MLLMs: their limited 3D awareness. By enabling intrinsic generation of depth and segmentation maps and adaptively integrating them into reasoning, COOPER significantly boosts MLLMs' capabilities in fine-grained perception, distance and size estimation, and complex spatial relationship understanding. This moves AI systems closer to human-level comprehension of the 3D world from 2D images.

3. **Broadened Applications in Embodied AI:** The enhanced spatial intelligence provided by COOPER has immediate and profound implications for various embodied AI applications.
 - **Robotics:** Robots require precise 3D understanding for navigation, object manipulation, and interaction with their environment. COOPER's ability to generate and reason with depth and segmentation maps can lead to more robust and adaptive robotic control.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Accurate perception of distances, object boundaries, and spatial relationships is paramount for safe and effective autonomous vehicles. COOPER's methods can contribute to more reliable environmental understanding and decision-making.
 - **AR/VR:** Creating immersive and interactive augmented/virtual reality experiences necessitates precise 3D reconstruction and spatial awareness. COOPER could enable more sophisticated scene understanding and content generation in these domains.

4. **Advancement of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** COOPER elevates the concept of Multimodal CoT by making visual information an intrinsic and adaptive part of the reasoning chain, rather than relying on fixed inputs or static visual aids. The model dynamically decides *what* visual information to generate and *when* to incorporate it, mimicking a more human-like cognitive process of "thinking with images."

5. **Methodological Contributions:**
 - **Auxiliary Modality Integration:** The ingenious technique of converting non-RGB auxiliary modalities into RGB pseudo-images allows existing unified MLLMs (optimized for RGB generation) to learn these specialized perception tasks without architectural changes.
 - **Adaptive Learning Framework:** The SFT+RL pipeline, coupled with the novel Cooperative Perception–Reasoning (CPR) reward, provides a robust framework for training MLLMs to dynamically select and interleave perception and reasoning actions. The exploration-guided reward (`r_e`) is particularly significant for balancing exploitation of known useful modalities with exploration of novel reasoning paths.
 - **Internalized Spatial Knowledge:** The finding that merely learning to *generate* auxiliary modalities leads to substantial improvements in understanding tasks highlights the power of "learning by doing" for MLLMs, suggesting that generative tasks can intrinsically imbue models with deeper representational knowledge.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper opens up exciting avenues for future research, including:
 - Developing joint text-image GRPO to optimize both textual reasoning and visual generation simultaneously.
 - Scaling COOPER to efficient long-video spatial reasoning, which is crucial for real-world dynamic environments.
 - Enriching the range of auxiliary modalities (e.g., 3D point clouds, optical flow) to provide even more comprehensive spatial cues for reasoning.

In conclusion, COOPER represents a pivotal step towards building truly intelligent MLLMs that possess a more comprehensive and adaptive understanding of our 3D world. Its cooperative unification of perception and reasoning capabilities offers a robust foundation for next-generation AI systems, particularly those operating in physically embodied or spatially aware environments.
