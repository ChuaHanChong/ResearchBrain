# AI & Human Co-Improvement for Safer Co-Superintelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> FAIR at Meta researchers Jason Weston and Jakob Foerster propose a "co-improving AI" paradigm, advocating for human-AI collaboration in research to achieve "co-superintelligence." This approach aims to accelerate AI development while enhancing safety, transparency, and alignment, presenting it as a superior alternative to purely autonomous self-improving AI.

## Problem

- The prevailing goal of creating autonomous self-improving AI systems poses significant risks, including potential misalignment and misuse, making robust safety and steerability challenging.
- Relying solely on autonomous AI for self-improvement may not be the fastest or most efficient path to superintelligence, given current AI limitations and the complexity of paradigm shifts in research.
- Existing human-AI collaboration is often a byproduct of general AI capability rather than a targeted development of collaborative research skills.

## Method

- Develop AI systems specifically designed for deep collaboration with human researchers across all stages of AI development, shifting from autonomous ==self-improvement== to ==co-improvement==.
- Establish a comprehensive conceptual framework outlining **12** specific ==co-improvement goals== for human-AI teamwork, ranging from ==problem identification== and ==method innovation== to ==safety co-development==.
- Propose the creation of new ==benchmarks== and ==training methods== tailored to measure and enhance AI's ==collaborative research skills==, fostering a ==symbiotic relationship==.

## Results

- The paper concludes that co-improvement is a superior and safer strategic path for developing superintelligence compared to a purely autonomous approach.
- A detailed conceptual framework was established, outlining specific collaborative activities (e.g., joint experiment design, safety co-development) to guide future human-AI research efforts.
- The proposed paradigm envisions a future 'co-superintelligence' that can accelerate scientific discovery across all domains and effectively address complex global societal problems through collective human-AI intelligence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-improvement leverages complementary strengths of humans (creativity, values) and AI (computational power, pattern recognition) to accelerate scientific discovery and paradigm shifts more effectively than either working alone.
> - Continuous human involvement throughout the AI development cycle, through co-improvement, offers a proactive and iterative approach to ensuring safety, alignment, and steerability.
> - Targeted development of collaborative AI, supported by specific benchmarks and training, can lead to a human-centric superintelligence that augments and empowers humanity rather than replacing it.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper positions itself directly within, and as a critical response to, one of the most exciting and contentious areas of contemporary AI research: the pursuit of **Self-Improving AI**. Since its inception, a core goal of AI has been to create systems that can improve themselves, evolving from optimizing fixed parameters (like weights in neural networks) to dynamically adapting architectures, generating training data, evaluating performance, and even rewriting their own code. The paper highlights this historical trajectory, noting the significant performance gains from scaling models and the current push towards autonomous AI research agents (e.g.,). This ambition is shared by many frontier AI labs, with the ultimate vision often being the achievement of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) or Superintelligence (SI).

However, the authors immediately juxtapose this dominant quest with its inherent **dangers**. The paper explicitly references concerns around "misuse through to misalignment", a topic that has gained immense urgency within the AI safety and alignment communities. Many researchers and organizations, including OpenAI (cited in the paper), are actively working on frameworks to mitigate the catastrophic risks associated with highly capable, autonomously self-improving AI systems that might not align with human values or goals.

The paper then carves out a distinct alternative: **"Co-Improving AI."** While acknowledging that some current AI systems already offer forms of human-AI collaboration (e.g., code assistance, writing assistance), the authors argue that these are often *byproducts* of general capability improvement rather than *targeted development* of collaborative research skills. They differentiate their proposal from general "human-centered AI" and "cooperative AI" by specifically focusing on *collaborative AI research* as the pathway to superintelligence. They also contrast their vision with "AI co-scientist" systems that exist for specific domains like biomedicine, emphasizing their focus on *AI research itself*.

By advocating for co-improvement, Weston and Foerster are essentially proposing a **paradigm shift** in the strategic objective of frontier AI development. Instead of minimizing human intervention as quickly as possible, they argue for maximizing it, particularly in the critical phase of developing the AI itself. This is a direct counter-position to some of the more radical visions of autonomous AI development and the associated existential risk narratives, such as those that foresee humans playing a diminished or non-existent role in a superintelligent future. The paper thereby engages directly with the philosophical and practical debates surrounding the future trajectory of AI, aligning with those who prioritize human oversight and augmentation over full automation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this paper is to **reframe the primary goal of AI research from achieving autonomous "self-improving AI" to fostering "co-improving AI."** The authors advocate for building AI systems specifically designed to collaborate with human researchers to accelerate AI research itself, with the ultimate aim of achieving **"co-superintelligence"**—a state where both humans and AIs reach superintelligent capabilities through symbiosis.

This core objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Accelerated Progress:** The authors contend that co-improvement is a *faster* path to superintelligence than purely autonomous self-improvement. They argue that by leveraging the complementary strengths of humans (creativity, intuition, value alignment) and AI (computational power, pattern recognition, data processing), the research process—especially the discovery of "paradigm shifts" in AI methods and data—can be significantly accelerated. This synergy can overcome the limitations of either humans or AIs working in isolation.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Alignment:** A paramount motivation is to achieve superintelligence *more safely*. The paper explicitly highlights the "fraught with danger" nature of endowing AIs with autonomous self-improvement capabilities without appropriate guidance, citing risks of misuse and misalignment. By keeping humans actively "in the loop" throughout the AI's development and improvement cycle, co-improvement offers continuous opportunities to steer the research towards human-centric goals, co-develop safety mechanisms, and ensure value alignment. This proactive integration of safety is presented as superior to attempting to impose alignment after a fully autonomous superintelligence has emerged.

3. **Increased Transparency and Steerability:** Human involvement in the co-improvement process inherently leads to greater transparency in AI development. Researchers can better understand how AI systems are evolving and making decisions, allowing for more informed interventions and guidance, thereby increasing the steerability of the superintelligence trajectory.

4. **Human Augmentation, Not Replacement:** The paper is motivated by a vision where AI augments and enables humans across all societal domains, rather than pursuing full automation that displaces human decision-making. This aligns with a more optimistic and human-centric view of AI's future role, where superintelligence empowers humanity to solve complex global problems, rather than dictating solutions.

5. **Addressing Current AI Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that current AI is "not mature enough yet to fully self-improve and is susceptible to misalignment." This recognition serves as a practical motivation for co-improvement, suggesting it as a pragmatic, interim, and ultimately superior strategy while AI capabilities continue to advance.

In essence, the paper seeks to shift the focus from merely building smarter machines to building *smarter partnerships*, where the very process of AI development becomes a collaborative human-AI endeavor aimed at collective human-beneficial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a **position paper**, this work does not present empirical results from novel experiments or algorithms. Instead, its "methodology" is conceptual, prescriptive, and argumentative, aimed at defining a new strategic direction for AI research. The approach can be broken down into several key elements:

1. **Definition and Distinction:** The paper begins by clearly defining "self-improving AI" based on its historical and current interpretations (Table 2), outlining how the search for improvement has expanded from parameters to data, objectives, architecture, and ultimately, overall code. It then explicitly defines and distinguishes "co-improvement" as a collaborative, bidirectional process where humans and AI work together, each improving the other's abilities and understanding. This clear distinction is crucial for establishing the proposed alternative.

2. **Argumentation for Superiority:** The core of the paper's approach involves constructing a robust argument for why co-improvement is a better goal than purely autonomous self-improvement. This is achieved by:
- Highlighting the inherent dangers and risks (misuse, misalignment) of autonomous self-improvement.
- Presenting the advantages of co-improvement in terms of faster progress, enhanced safety, increased transparency, and human augmentation.
- Drawing parallels to existing, albeit often untargeted, forms of human-AI collaboration (e.g., coding assistants) to demonstrate feasibility.

3. **Conceptual Framework for Collaboration:** A key part of the "how do we do it?" is the detailed conceptual framework presented in Table 1, which outlines specific "co-improvement goals across major AI development activities." This framework serves as a practical guide for researchers, identifying concrete areas where human-AI collaboration can be fostered:
- **Collaborative Problem Identification:** Jointly defining research goals and identifying failures.
- **Benchmark Creation & Problem Evaluation:** Co-designing evaluation metrics and refining benchmarks.
- **Method Innovation & Idea Generation:** Brainstorming solutions and new architectures.
- **Joint Experiment Design:** Co-designing experimental protocols and ablations.
- **Collaborative Execution:** Co-producing and running multi-step workflows.
- **Evaluation & Error Analysis:** Jointly analyzing performance and providing feedback.
- **Safety & Alignment Co-development:** Crucially, co-developing methods, values, and constitutions for safe AI.
- **Systems & Infrastructure Co-design:** Jointly architecting pipelines and optimizations.
- **Integrating into Real-World Systems:** Converting research into practical applications.
- **Scientific Communication:** Jointly drafting documentation and write-ups.
- **Collective Intelligence & Group Research:** Facilitating multi-human and AI collaboration.
- **Bidirectional Co-improvement:** The overarching aim to increase intelligence in both humans and AI.

4. **Prescriptive Research Directions:** The paper suggests a practical "methodology" for implementing co-improvement:
- **Benchmarking:** Developing new benchmarks specifically to measure AI's research collaboration skills.
- **Training Data and Methods:** Constructing training data and methods tailored to improve performance on these collaboration benchmarks. This mirrors how other AI capabilities (e.g., coding, reasoning) have been advanced through targeted development.

5. **Contrasting with Related Positions:** The authors strengthen their argument by explicitly relating their position to existing works (human-centered AI, cooperative AI, scalable oversight) and, more importantly, by contrasting it sharply with opposing views that advocate for minimal human involvement or see a diminished role for humanity in a superintelligent future. This contextualization helps solidify the unique contribution and ethical stance of the paper.

In summary, the paper's methodology is one of **principled advocacy**, using structured arguments and a detailed conceptual framework to persuade the AI research community to adopt a more collaborative and human-integrated approach to superintelligence development.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a position paper, "Self-Improving AI & Human Co-Improvement for Safer Co-Superintelligence" does not present empirical "findings" or "results" in the traditional sense of experimental outcomes. Instead, its "findings" are the **conclusions derived from its conceptual analysis and argumentation**, which serve as the core tenets of its proposed paradigm shift.

The main conclusions, or "findings," are:

1. **Autonomous Self-Improvement is Suboptimal:** The paper "finds" that the current dominant goal of developing autonomous self-improving AI is misguided. This is based on two primary reasons: it is neither the fastest nor the safest path to achieving superintelligence. The inherent risks of misalignment, misuse, and lack of human steerability are deemed too significant to ignore, and the computational challenges too vast for purely autonomous discovery.

2. **Co-Improvement is the Superior Path:** The central "finding" is that a human-AI collaborative approach, termed "co-improvement," offers a superior alternative. This collaborative symbiosis is presented as a means to achieve "co-superintelligence"—a state of collective intelligence far exceeding individual human or current AI capabilities.

3. **Benefits of Co-Improvement are Multifaceted:** The paper identifies several critical advantages that co-improvement offers:
- **Accelerated Progress:** By combining human ingenuity with AI's processing power, co-improvement can expedite the discovery of fundamental "paradigm shifts" in AI, leading to faster overall progress.
- **Enhanced Safety and Alignment:** Human involvement throughout the research loop provides continuous guidance, allowing for the proactive co-development of safety measures, values, and constitutions, thereby mitigating misalignment risks more effectively than retrospective efforts.
- **Increased Transparency and Steerability:** Direct collaboration ensures that the development of superintelligent AI remains transparent and amenable to human guidance and intervention, preventing the emergence of opaque, uncontrollable systems.
- **Human-Centric Outcomes:** Co-improvement inherently aims to augment human abilities and knowledge, ensuring that the development of advanced AI serves humanity's best interests and helps solve societal problems, rather than replacing or dominating human decision-making.

4. **Feasibility through Targeted Development:** The paper implicitly "finds" that fostering collaborative AI research skills is feasible. It argues that, much like other AI capabilities (e.g., coding), these skills can be developed through targeted efforts: creating specific benchmarks for research collaboration, generating appropriate training data, and refining methods to improve AI performance on these benchmarks. The detailed framework in Table 1 provides concrete areas for such targeted development.

5. **Potential for Broader Societal Impact:** Beyond AI research itself, the paper "finds" that co-improvement can eventually extend to all forms of research and critical societal issues. This paradigm suggests a future where superintelligent AI helps humanity collectively synthesize consensus, structure debates, and find actionable solutions to global challenges, thereby increasing collective human knowledge and well-being.

In summary, the paper's main "findings" are a compelling set of arguments and a comprehensive conceptual framework advocating for a strategic redirection of AI research towards deeply integrated human-AI collaboration as the most effective and safest route to advanced intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of "Self-Improving AI & Human Co-Improvement for Safer Co-Superintelligence" are substantial, particularly given its authors' standing and institutional backing at FAIR/Meta. This paper is not merely an academic exercise; it represents a **strategic proposal** from within the heart of frontier AI development.

1. **Reshaping the Narrative and Research Agenda:** The paper directly challenges the increasingly dominant narrative of purely autonomous AI self-improvement. By presenting a well-reasoned alternative, it could significantly influence the research agenda of AI labs, potentially shifting investment and talent towards developing collaborative AI agents rather than solely autonomous ones. This reorientation could lead to the emergence of new sub-fields focused specifically on human-AI research collaboration.

2. **Proactive AI Safety and Alignment:** One of the most critical impacts lies in its approach to AI safety. Instead of treating alignment as a post-deployment challenge or a separate research track, co-improvement integrates safety and alignment directly into the *development process itself*. By advocating for humans and AIs to "co-develop methods as well as values, and constitutions" (Table 1), the paper proposes a proactive, iterative, and inherently collaborative solution to the misalignment problem. This could lead to more robustly aligned and inherently safer superintelligent systems, as humans would continuously guide the AI's moral and ethical development alongside its capabilities.

3. **Acceleration of Scientific Discovery:** The envisioned "co-superintelligence" could dramatically accelerate scientific discovery, not just in AI but across all disciplines. By creating AI systems that are super-human at tasks like theoretical analysis, experimental design, and error analysis, humanity could solve problems currently beyond its reach, from developing provably safe AI to tackling grand challenges in science, medicine, and engineering. This synergistic approach could unlock unprecedented rates of innovation.

4. **Human Augmentation and Empowerment:** The paper's vision profoundly impacts the future of human agency. It promotes a future where AI acts as a powerful augmenter of human intellect and capabilities, rather than a replacement. This perspective offers an optimistic counterpoint to dystopian views of AI, fostering a future where humans are empowered to achieve more, rather than being marginalized or controlled. It underscores the potential for AI to enhance collective human intelligence and decision-making for complex societal problems.

5. **Call for New Benchmarks and Training Paradigms:** The concrete call to action—developing new benchmarks and training methods for AI research collaboration skills—is a direct impact on the practical research front. This could spur the creation of new datasets, evaluation metrics, and model architectures specifically designed to foster human-AI teamwork, leading to novel advancements in areas like human-computer interaction, explainable AI, and multi-modal reasoning.

6. **Influence on Policy and Openness Debates:** By touching upon "managed openness" and discussing societal harms and benefits, the paper also contributes to the ongoing policy debates around AI development. Its stance—that co-improvement can advance collective scientific knowledge while considering harms—adds a nuanced perspective to discussions about regulating frontier AI and the trade-offs between open science and responsible deployment.

In conclusion, Weston and Foerster's paper is a significant contribution that offers a compelling and timely alternative to the prevailing self-improvement paradigm in AI. By advocating for deeply integrated human-AI collaboration, it outlines a path that promises not only faster scientific progress but also a safer, more transparent, and human-centric future for artificial superintelligence. Its impact could be to fundamentally reshape how the AI community conceives, develops, and aligns the most advanced intelligent systems.
