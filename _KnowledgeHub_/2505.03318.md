# Unified Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Reward Model through Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University, Shanghai AI Lab, and Tencent's Hunyuan developed UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK, the first unified multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reward model that integrates multi-dimensional, step-by-step reasoning. The model consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art reward models across image and video understanding and generation tasks, providing enhanced accuracy and interpretability.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal reward models often lack interpretability and provide shallow justifications, leading to inaccuracies in complex visual scenarios.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reward data, making it challenging to train models with explicit reasoning via traditional supervised methods.
- Visual-language models (VLMs) possess latent reasoning capabilities, but current training methodologies struggle to effectively elicit and refine these for reward modeling.

## Method

- A three-stage ==exploration-driven reinforcement fine-tuning pipeline== (==Cold Start==, ==Rejection Sampling==, ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==) is introduced to activate and refine CoT reasoning.
- The model leverages a small amount of GPT-4o distilled CoT data for an initial =='cold start'== to learn reasoning formats, followed by ==rejection sampling== and ==GRPO== to generalize and enhance reasoning for challenging cases.
- A novel ==multidimensional CoT reward scoring strategy== is embedded, prompting the model to evaluate candidates along multiple task-relevant dimensions, ensuring alignment between reasoning steps and final judgments.

## Results

- UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK consistently outperforms state-of-the-art multimodal reward models, including strong baselines like GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro, across image and video understanding and generation benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirm that each stage of the training pipeline, particularly the GRPO reinforcement fine-tuning, contributes substantial performance gains, with GRPO yielding the most significant improvements for challenging samples.
- The methodology demonstrates robustness by achieving consistent improvements when trained on different base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B), showcasing its generalizability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit long Chains-of-Thought substantially enhances the reliability and robustness of multimodal reward models across diverse visual tasks.
> - Training with explicit CoT reasoning also improves the model's accuracy in providing direct, implicit reward signals, demonstrating a deeper internalization of logical thinking.
> - An exploration-driven reinforcement fine-tuning approach, specifically GRPO, effectively elicits and refines complex CoT reasoning even with limited initial CoT data by focusing on challenging samples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of multimodal artificial intelligence, reward modeling, and advanced reasoning techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Its significance can be understood by considering several interconnected trends:

- **Rise of Multimodal Generative AI:** Recent years have seen an explosion in the capabilities of multimodal models, capable of generating high-quality images, videos, and complex textual descriptions from various inputs. However, aligning these generative outputs with nuanced human preferences remains a significant challenge.
- **Importance of Reward Models (RMs) in RLHF:** Reward Models are a cornerstone of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), a paradigm that has been instrumental in fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) and, increasingly, multimodal models. RMs provide scalable feedback signals to guide model training, moving beyond simple accuracy metrics to capture subjective human judgments.
- **Limitations of Traditional RMs:** Existing multimodal RMs, while effective, often operate as black boxes, providing direct preference scores or rankings without transparent explanations. Their reasoning processes are typically shallow, making them prone to inaccuracies in complex scenarios and difficult for users to interpret or trust. This lack of interpretability and robustness is a critical bottleneck for deploying these models in sensitive applications.
- **Impact of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** CoT has revolutionized LLMs by enabling them to break down complex problems into intermediate, interpretable steps. This step-by-step reasoning significantly improves the reliability, robustness, and interpretability of LLM outputs. However, extending sophisticated CoT reasoning to the multimodal domain, particularly for reward modeling, is an underexplored area.
- **Unified Multimodal Frameworks:** The field is moving towards unified models capable of handling diverse modalities and tasks. The base model, UnifiedReward (Wang et al., 2025d), is an example of this trend, demonstrating the benefits of joint learning across image/video generation and understanding. UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK extends this unification by adding a robust, interpretable reasoning layer.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning:** While RL has been widely applied to enhance LLM reasoning (e.g., Deepseek-R1-Zero [Guo et et al., 2025]), its application to specifically elicit and refine *CoT reasoning in multimodal reward models* is novel. This work fills a critical gap by adapting powerful RL techniques like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to the multimodal reward domain.

In essence, this work pushes the boundaries of multimodal reward modeling by integrating advanced reasoning capabilities, addressing the crucial need for transparency, accuracy, and robustness in aligning generative AI with human preferences across a wide range of visual tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK, the first unified multimodal CoT-based reward model**, capable of multi-dimensional, step-by-step long-chain reasoning for both visual understanding and generation reward tasks. This overarching goal is driven by two core hypotheses and a significant practical challenge:

**Core Hypotheses:**
1. **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness via Explicit CoT:** The authors posit that integrating explicit long Chains-of-Thought into the reward reasoning process will substantially enhance the reliability and robustness of multimodal reward models. This addresses the limitations of current RMs that offer only direct responses or shallow justifications, which can be inaccurate in complex visual scenarios.
2. **Improved Implicit Reasoning through CoT Internalization:** They hypothesize that once a model internalizes the ability for explicit CoT reasoning, its accuracy in providing *direct* reward signals (even without explicit reasoning traces) will also improve due to enhanced implicit logical thinking capabilities. This suggests that the training process not only teaches explicit reasoning but also fosters a deeper, underlying understanding.

**Motivation (Addressing Key Challenges):**
- **Scarcity of CoT Reward Data:** A major practical hurdle for training CoT-based RMs using traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods is the extreme scarcity of large-scale, high-quality CoT reward data. Manual annotation of such detailed reasoning traces is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- **Eliciting Latent Reasoning Capabilities:** The authors argue that visual-language models (VLMs) inherently possess a prior knowledge of complex reasoning, but they often lack an effective strategy to elicit and incentivize this capability within the context of reward modeling. The motivation is to devise a training methodology that effectively "activates" and refines this latent potential with limited data.
- **Interpretability and Trust:** By enabling RMs to provide detailed, step-by-step justifications, the research aims to significantly improve the interpretability of reward signals. This transparency is crucial for users to understand *why* a particular output is preferred, thereby increasing trust in AI-driven evaluation and generation systems.
- **Unified and Generalizable Evaluation:** Building upon their prior work, the team is motivated to create a single, unified reward model that can assess diverse visual tasks (image/video generation and understanding), demonstrating that a CoT approach can effectively generalize across these different modalities and task types.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond "black-box" reward prediction to an "interpretable and robust" reasoning process, thereby offering more trustworthy and human-aligned feedback for the next generation of multimodal AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK introduces an innovative exploration-driven reinforcement fine-tuning approach to activate and refine the multi-dimensional, step-by-step long-chain reasoning capabilities of a VLM for unified multimodal reward tasks. The methodology is structured in a three-stage pipeline, complemented by a novel multidimensional CoT reward scoring strategy.

**Base Model:** The foundation of UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK is **UnifiedReward** [Wang et al., 2025d], a pre-trained multimodal reward model known for its ability to assess both image/video generation and understanding tasks. This provides a strong starting point with extensive prior knowledge in visual perception and generation.

**Three-Stage Training Pipeline:**

1. **Stage 1: Cold Start: Learning CoT Reward Format**
 - **Objective:** To teach the model the desired format and structure of long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, as VLMs may lack a suitable articulation format for reward modeling despite inherent reasoning potential.
 - **Process:**
 - A small amount (5,000 samples) of high-quality image generation preference data is collected.
 - This data is fed to **GPT-4o** [Hurst et al., 2024], a powerful general-purpose AI, to distill detailed long-chain reasoning processes and corresponding final answers.
 - Only those reasoning trajectories whose final answers align with the ground-truth (GT) labels are retained, forming the `ImageGen-CoT-Reward-5K` dataset.
 - The base model undergoes supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on this distilled data using a cross-entropy loss, enabling it to learn the explicit `<think>` and `<answer>` tag structure and the flow of CoT reasoning.

2. **Stage 2: Rejection Sampling: Unified CoT Reward Generalization Fine-tuning**
 - **Objective:** To expand the model’s CoT reasoning capabilities to a wider range of visual understanding and generation tasks, leveraging its prior knowledge and generalization abilities. This stage also pre-filters easier cases before more computationally intensive reinforcement learning.
 - **Process:**
 - Large-scale unified multimodal preference datasets are prepared, covering diverse tasks (e.g., image/video generation and understanding).
 - The cold-started model is prompted to generate CoT reasoning outputs for these datasets.
 - A "rejection sampling" mechanism is applied: only reasoning samples whose final answers consistently match the ground-truth labels are retained. Incorrectly reasoned samples are discarded or set aside for the next stage.
 - SFT is then performed on this filtered, correct reasoning data. This reinforces accurate reasoning patterns and enhances the model's generalization across diverse visual domains.

3. **Stage 3: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): Unified CoT Reward Reinforcement Fine-tuning**
 - **Objective:** To further enhance the model's CoT reasoning abilities, particularly for challenging cases that were mispredicted during rejection sampling. GRPO encourages exploration of diverse reasoning paths.
 - **Process:**
 - The incorrectly predicted samples from the rejection sampling stage are used.
 - The current policy model ($\pi_\theta$) generates $N$ distinct candidate responses (CoT reasoning + final answer) for each input.
 - **Verifiable Rewards:** Two types of rule-based rewards are defined to guide learning:
 - **Format Reward ($R_{fmt}$):** Rewards 1 if the response correctly follows the `<think>` and `<answer>` tag structure, else 0. This ensures structural consistency.
 - **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):** Rewards 1 if the final answer within the `<answer>` tag exactly matches the ground truth, else 0. This ensures factual correctness.
 - The overall reward $R = R_{fmt} + R_{acc}$.
 - **GRPO Algorithm:**
 - The $N$ responses are evaluated using the verifiable rewards to obtain scores {$R^{(1)}..., R^{(N)}$}.
 - These scores are normalized to compute advantage estimates ($\hat{A}^{(i)}$), quantifying the relative quality of each response within its group.
 - GRPO uses a clipped likelihood ratio ($r^{(i)}$) and a KL divergence penalty to a reference model ($\pi_{ref}$) to stabilize training and prevent policy divergence.
 - The objective function optimizes for responses with higher advantage, encouraging the model to explore and refine reasoning paths that lead to accurate and robust solutions. This is a crucial distinction from SFT, which only imitates predefined answers.

**Multidimensional CoT Reward Scoring Strategy:**
To address the common issue of inconsistency between a model's reasoning steps and its final decision, a multidimensional scoring strategy is embedded within the CoT process itself.
- The model is prompted to evaluate each candidate (image, video, or textual response) along *multiple task-relevant dimensions* (e.g., semantic consistency, aesthetics, authenticity for generation; semantic accuracy, factual correctness, clarity for understanding).
- Scores from each dimension are aggregated to derive the final decision. This explicit, structured evaluation forces alignment between the intermediate reasoning steps and the ultimate judgment.
- This design implicitly ensures the correctness of the reasoning process, even when data filtering (cold-start, rejection sampling) and direct supervision (GRPO) primarily rely on the final answer matching the ground truth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the substantial effectiveness and superiority of UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK across a wide array of multimodal reward tasks. The key findings are:

- **Superior Performance Across All Tasks:** UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art multimodal reward models and strong baselines (e.g., Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4o, LLaVA-Critic, PickScore, HPSv2, VisionReward, VideoScore, LiFT-Critic, VideoReward, and the base UnifiedReward model) in both visual understanding (image and video) and generation (image and video) assessment benchmarks.
 - **Image Understanding:** Achieves significantly higher "Overall Accuracy" and "Macro Accuracy" on VLRewardBench across general, hallucination, and reasoning aspects (e.g., 73.8 Overall Accuracy for Ours vs. 67.5 for UnifiedReward).
 - **Image and Video Generation:** Demonstrates superior performance on GenAI-Bench and VideoGen-RewardBench, particularly in "diff" metrics which exclude tied pairs (e.g., 72.5 for Ours Image Generation diff vs. 70.9 for UnifiedReward; 82.3 for Ours Video Generation diff vs. 77.2 for UnifiedReward).
 - **Qualitative Results:** Figures 1, 3, and 4 provide compelling qualitative examples showcasing the model's ability to perform multi-dimensional, long-chain CoT reasoning, leading to accurate preference judgments in complex scenarios.

- **Significant Gains from CoT Reasoning:** The direct comparison with the base UnifiedReward model clearly shows that integrating multi-dimensional and multi-step CoT reasoning yields substantial performance improvements across all evaluation tasks. This strongly validates the first hypothesis that explicit CoT enhances reliability and robustness.

- **Strong Implicit Reasoning Capabilities:** A remarkable finding is that UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK, after mastering explicit CoT reasoning, also exhibits robust implicit reasoning capabilities. Even when prompted to provide *direct reward outputs without explicit CoT traces*, it consistently surpasses existing baselines across all vision reward tasks. This validates the second hypothesis, indicating a deep internalization of the reasoning process, leading to improved direct decision-making.

- **Effectiveness of Each Training Stage (Ablation Studies):**
 - **Cold Start:** While learning the CoT format, the model initially shows limited improvement in predictive accuracy.
 - **Rejection Sampling:** Introduces clear performance gains by reinforcing correct reasoning patterns from large-scale unified preference data.
 - **GRPO:** Yields the most substantial performance gains, specifically by focusing on previously challenging or mispredicted samples, allowing the model to explore and converge on more accurate reasoning solutions. This highlights the complementary nature and importance of each stage in the pipeline.

- **Necessity of CoT for GRPO's Efficacy:** An ablation study comparing GRPO *with* and *without* CoT reasoning demonstrates that merely optimizing reward predictions based on final answers (without explicit CoT) yields only marginal improvements over the baseline. This underscores that the power of the GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning in this work stems from explicitly modeling and refining the *reasoning process*, not just the final output.

- **Robustness Across Backbones:** The methodology demonstrates robustness, achieving consistent improvements when trained on different base models, such as Qwen2.5-VL-7B, showcasing its generalizability beyond a single architecture.

In summary, the results convincingly establish UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK as a state-of-the-art multimodal reward model that not only delivers superior accuracy but also provides interpretable reasoning, a crucial step towards more transparent and trustworthy AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK marks a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, with profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Interpretability and Trust:** By enabling multimodal reward models to articulate their judgments through multi-dimensional, step-by-step CoT reasoning, this work fundamentally addresses the "black box" problem prevalent in many AI systems. Users can now understand *why* an AI prefers one image or video over another, fostering greater transparency, trust, and alignment with human intent in critical applications like content generation, safety filtering, and quality control.

- **More Reliable and Robust Reward Signals:** The demonstrated improvements in accuracy and robustness through CoT reasoning mean that downstream generative models, when aligned with UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK, will receive higher-quality feedback. This is expected to lead to the creation of more desirable, higher-fidelity, and human-preferred multimodal content.

- **Advanced Alignment for Generative AI:** UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK provides a superior reward signal for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) pipelines for vision models. This more sophisticated feedback mechanism can guide generative AI towards intricate preferences, subtle stylistic nuances, and complex semantic consistency, pushing the boundaries of what multimodal generative models can achieve.

- **Efficient Training with Limited CoT Data:** The proposed exploration-driven reinforcement fine-tuning approach, particularly the cold start and GRPO stages, effectively addresses the challenge of scarce high-quality CoT data. This makes it practical to infuse complex reasoning capabilities into models without requiring massive, expensive human annotations for every task.

- **Unified and Generalizable Multimodal AI:** Building on the UnifiedReward framework, UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK further solidifies the concept of a single, powerful reward model capable of evaluating diverse tasks across images and videos for both generation and understanding. This unification simplifies the development and deployment of multimodal AI evaluation systems.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens new avenues for research in several areas:
 - **Reasoning in Multimodal Models:** It encourages further exploration into how multimodal models can perform even deeper and more complex reasoning, potentially incorporating common sense, causal inference, and domain-specific knowledge.
 - **Active Learning and Data Efficiency:** The methodology's efficiency in eliciting reasoning from limited data could inspire new active learning strategies for collecting and curating multimodal preference datasets.
 - **Ethical AI and Bias Detection:** With increased interpretability, reward models like UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK could potentially be leveraged to identify and mitigate biases in generative models by tracing the reasoning path that leads to biased outputs. However, as noted in the limitations, they could also reinforce biases if not handled carefully with fairness-aware training strategies.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that incorporating explicit long CoT reasoning inevitably increases inference time. However, the finding that the model can leverage *implicit* reasoning for enhanced accuracy without explicit traces mitigates this concern for real-time applications. Future work could focus on techniques to further optimize inference speed while retaining reasoning depth. Additionally, while RL amplifies existing potential, the authors suggest that scaling up high-quality CoT supervision remains a promising direction to fundamentally extend a model's capabilities. Addressing the ethical implications, particularly the potential for reinforcing harmful biases, will also be crucial as these powerful reward models become more prevalent.

In conclusion, UNIFIEDREWARD-THINK represents a significant step towards creating more intelligent, interpretable, and human-aligned multimodal AI systems, promising to unlock new levels of performance and trustworthiness in generative models.
