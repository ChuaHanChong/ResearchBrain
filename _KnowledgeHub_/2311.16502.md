# MMMU: A Massive Multi-discipline Multimodal Understanding and Reasoning Benchmark for Expert AGI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Waterloo, The Ohio State University, and collaborators present MMMU, a new benchmark designed to evaluate Large Multimodal Models on expert-level, multi-discipline understanding and reasoning. The benchmark, featuring over 11,000 college-level questions with diverse image types, reveals that even leading models like GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro significantly trail human experts, struggling with domain-specific visual perception, deep knowledge, and complex reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal benchmarks primarily test basic perception or commonsense, lacking the depth required for evaluating expert-level understanding in diverse academic disciplines.
- Current AGI benchmarks are predominantly text-based, failing to assess multimodal reasoning capabilities crucial for human intelligence and expert performance.
- There was no comprehensive benchmark to evaluate Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) on college-level problems requiring deep subject knowledge and complex, deliberate reasoning across multiple disciplines.

## Method

- The paper introduces **MMMU**, a benchmark of ==11,550 college-level multimodal questions== spanning 6 disciplines, 30 subjects, and 183 subfields.
- The dataset features a high prevalence and ==heterogeneity of image types== (30 distinct types), with questions often requiring ==interleaved text and image understanding==.
- A rigorous ==three-stage data curation process== involving over 50 university student annotators and subsequent quality control steps ensured the comprehensiveness, relevance, and difficulty of the questions.

## Results

- MMMU poses substantial challenges to state-of-the-art LMMs; for example, GPT-4o achieved **69.1%** accuracy, while human experts scored between **76.2%** and **88.6%** on the validation set, indicating a notable performance gap.
- Models struggled significantly with specific image types (e.g., geometric shapes, music sheets, chemical structures) and 'Hard' difficulty questions, revealing limitations in generalization and complex problem-solving.
- Error analysis on GPT-4V showed common failures stemming from perceptual misinterpretations (**35%**), lack of specialized domain knowledge (**29%**), and failures in logical or scientific reasoning (**26%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expert-level AGI evaluation necessitates benchmarks that mirror human expertise by combining multi-discipline knowledge with complex multimodal understanding and reasoning.
> - Current multimodal models require true joint interpretation of visual and textual information, as simple image-to-text conversion (via OCR or captioning) is insufficient for solving challenging, expert-level problems.
> - LMMs face limitations in handling highly heterogeneous and domain-specific visual inputs, acquiring deep domain knowledge, and performing multi-step deliberate reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of MMMU is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: large language models (LLMs), large multimodal models (LMMs), and the ambitious pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

**Current State of LLMs and LMMs:** The past few years have witnessed unprecedented advancements in LLMs, which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in text understanding and generation. This has spurred discussions about the feasibility and implications of AGI. Concurrently, LMMs have emerged, extending the capabilities of LLMs to encompass visual information, allowing them to process and reason over both text and images. These models, exemplified by systems like CLIP, BLIP-2, Flamingo, LLaVA, and the proprietary GPT-4V(ision) and Gemini, have shown impressive performance on existing multimodal benchmarks.

**Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Despite the progress, the authors identify a critical gap in the current evaluation landscape for LMMs. Existing benchmarks, while valuable, primarily focus on "commonsense/daily knowledge" or "relatively basic perception abilities."
- **Text-only AGI Benchmarks:** Benchmarks like MMLU (Measuring Massive Multitask Language Understanding) and AGIEval assess LLMs on text-based questions across various subjects, including college-level content. However, they inherently omit the crucial multimodal aspect of human intelligence.
- **Existing Multimodal Benchmarks:** Datasets such as VQA, OK-VQA, MSCOCO, GQA, SEED-Bench, MMBench, and MM-Vet have pushed multimodal research forward. Yet, they predominantly test fundamental perception and shallow reasoning without requiring deep, domain-specific knowledge. Even ScienceQA, which covers diverse disciplines, largely consists of elementary to middle school level questions, thus lacking the "depth" required for expert evaluation. MathVista is a step towards complex reasoning in a visual context but is limited to the mathematical domain.

**The "Expert AGI" Context:** The paper explicitly frames MMMU within the taxonomy of AGI proposed by Morris et al., specifically targeting "Level 3, or Expert AGI." This level implies AI systems performing at "at least 90th percentile of skilled adults" across a broad range of tasks, signifying a "substitution threshold for machine intelligence in lieu of human labor." To monitor progress towards this milestone, the authors argue for benchmarks that reflect the capabilities of skilled adults, i.e., college-level problems that demand both broad discipline knowledge and deep reasoning.

**MMMU's Contribution to the Landscape:** MMMU directly addresses the identified gap by providing a benchmark that:
1. **Combines Breadth and Depth:** It is "comprehensive" (spanning 6 disciplines, 30 subjects, 183 subfields) and requires "expert-level perception and reasoning rooted in deep subject knowledge."
2. **Handles Heterogeneous Multimodality:** It includes "30 highly heterogeneous image types" and "interleaved text and images," pushing beyond simpler visual question answering tasks.
3. **Focuses on Deliberate Reasoning:** Unlike benchmarks that might be solvable with surface-level pattern matching or commonsense, MMMU problems often necessitate applying complex domain-specific principles (e.g., Fourier Transform, Equilibrium Theory) and multi-step logical deduction.

By setting a higher bar for LMMs, MMMU aims to "stimulate the community to build next-generation multimodal foundation models towards expert artificial general intelligence." It serves as a crucial diagnostic tool, revealing the current limitations of even the most advanced LMMs and guiding future research directions. Its concurrent introduction with GAIA, another benchmark for general AI assistants, underscores a growing recognition within the AI community of the need for more rigorous and comprehensive evaluation paradigms to truly assess the breadth and depth of emerging AI capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **MMMU: a new benchmark designed to evaluate multimodal models on massive multi-discipline tasks demanding college-level subject knowledge and deliberate reasoning.**

The motivations underpinning this objective are multifaceted and deeply rooted in the current trajectory of artificial intelligence research, particularly concerning the concept of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):

- **Monitoring Progress Towards Expert AGI:** The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) and large multimodal models (LMMs) have intensified discussions around AGI. The authors subscribe to the leveled taxonomy for AGI proposed by Morris et al., which defines "Expert AGI" (Level 3) as systems performing at or above the 90th percentile of skilled adults across a broad range of tasks. To effectively monitor and measure progress towards this critical milestone, there is an urgent need for operationalizable definitions and challenging benchmarks that reflect expert-level human capabilities. College-level exams, which are designed to evaluate skilled adults specializing in diverse disciplines, serve as a natural, well-established standard for this purpose.

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:**
 - **Lack of Multimodality in Expert Benchmarks:** While benchmarks like MMLU and AGIEval utilize college-level questions, they are predominantly text-based. Human experts, however, routinely solve complex problems that integrate information from both text and images. Current LMMs, despite their multimodal capabilities, lack comprehensive evaluation tailored to this expert-level multimodal reasoning.
 - **Insufficient Depth in Existing Multimodal Benchmarks:** Most existing multimodal benchmarks (e.g., VQA-v2, GQA, MMBench) tend to focus on commonsense knowledge, basic perception, or elementary-to-middle school level reasoning (e.g., ScienceQA). They do not adequately challenge models with the intricacies of domain-specific knowledge and complex, deliberate reasoning required in professional and academic settings.
 - **Limited Diversity in Image Types:** Current benchmarks often feature a narrower range of image formats, predominantly natural scenes, which do not fully capture the variety of specialized visual data encountered in diverse academic disciplines (e.g., scientific diagrams, medical images, architectural blueprints, musical notation, chemical structures).

- **Pushing the Boundaries of LMM Capabilities:** MMMU is explicitly designed to pose "significant challenges" to state-of-the-art LMMs. By introducing highly difficult, expert-level problems, the benchmark aims to:
 - **Test Advanced Perception:** Require LMMs to interpret a wide array of heterogeneous image types, from photographs and paintings to highly structured diagrams, tables, and domain-specific visual representations (e.g., sheet music, chemical structures).
 - **Demand Deep Subject Knowledge:** Force models to go beyond surface-level understanding and recall specialized, college-level domain expertise.
 - **Require Complex Reasoning:** Assess models' ability to perform multi-step, deliberate reasoning processes, often involving the application of specific theories, formulas, and logical deductions, rather than simple pattern matching or retrieval.
 - **Handle Interleaved Text and Images:** Challenge models to jointly understand and integrate information from both textual and visual modalities when they are intricately interwoven within a single problem.

In essence, the motivation behind MMMU is to bridge the gap between the burgeoning capabilities of LMMs and the sophisticated requirements for expert-level AGI, providing a rigorous and comprehensive tool to measure progress and identify critical areas for future model development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in creating and evaluating the MMMU benchmark is meticulous, focusing on large-scale data curation, rigorous quality control, and comprehensive model assessment.

#### 3.1. The MMMU Dataset

- **Scale and Scope:** MMMU comprises 11,550 carefully collected multimodal questions. These questions span six core disciplines: Art & Design, Business, Science, Health & Medicine, Humanities & Social Science, and Tech & Engineering. Within these disciplines, the questions cover 30 diverse subjects and 183 subfields.
- **Question Types:** The benchmark includes both multiple-choice questions (94.03%) and open-ended questions (5.97%). A subset of questions (17.62%) also provides explanations, valuable for potential future research in explainable AI.
- **Image Integration:** A defining characteristic is the prevalence and heterogeneity of images: 97.52% of questions include images, with varying placements (beginning, middle, end) and 7.39% containing multiple images. It features 30 highly heterogeneous image types, including diagrams, tables, plots, charts, photographs, chemical structures, paintings, medical images, sheet music, geometric figures, pathology images, microscopic images, and comics.
- **Difficulty:** Questions are categorized into three difficulty levels: Easy (28%), Medium (45%), and Hard (27%), ensuring a challenging distribution.

#### 3.2. Data Curation Process

The data collection and curation involved a three-stage process to ensure comprehensiveness, relevance, and quality:
1. **Subject Selection:** The process began by identifying common university majors, prioritizing subjects where visual inputs are integral to conveying valuable information. This led to the selection of 30 subjects across six disciplines, intentionally excluding subjects like law and linguistics where multimodal problems are less common.
2. **Question Collection:** Over 50 university students, including co-authors, specializing in the chosen majors, were recruited as annotators. They collected multimodal questions from diverse sources, including major textbooks, online educational resources, and lecture materials. Crucially, annotators were instructed to:
 - Adhere strictly to copyright and licensing regulations.
 - Prioritize questions without "immediately available answers" (e.g., answers in separate documents or at the end of textbooks) to mitigate data contamination concerns for foundation models.
 - Create new questions based on their expertise where necessary, ensuring novelty and addressing potential gaps.
3. **Data Quality Control:** A rigorous two-step cleaning process was implemented:
 - **Duplicate Detection:** Lexical overlap and source URL similarity checks were performed to identify and eliminate potential duplicate problems.
 - **Format and Typo Checking:** Co-authors manually reviewed problems for adherence to a standardized format and correction of any typos.
 - **Difficulty Categorization:** Authors categorized problems into four difficulty levels (very easy, easy, medium, hard). Approximately 10% of "very easy" problems were excluded to maintain the benchmark's challenging nature.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Methodology

- **Evaluation Metric:** Micro-averaged accuracy is used as the primary evaluation metric.
- **Answer Extraction:** For both multiple-choice and open-ended questions, systematic, rule-based evaluation pipelines using robust regular expressions were developed to extract key phrases, numbers, and conclusions from model responses, mitigating the influence of intermediate generations.
- **Zero-Shot Setting:** All model evaluations are conducted in a zero-shot setting, assessing models' inherent capabilities without fine-tuning or in-context demonstrations on the MMMU dataset. Prompt engineering was performed on the validation set for models without default prompts.
- **Baselines:** A wide array of models were evaluated:
 - **Large Multimodal Models (LMMs):** 28 open-source LMMs (e.g., Kosmos2, LLaMA-Adapter2, BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, OpenFlamingo, CogVLM, Fuyu, Qwen-VL, Otter, MiniGPT-4, mPLUG-Owl2, Yi-VL, InternVL, VILA, SenseChat-Vision) and leading proprietary LMMs (GPT-4V(ision), Gemini 1.0 Ultra, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3 Opus, GPT-4o). For each family, the latest, largest, and best-performing checkpoint was selected.
 - **Text-only LLMs:** Capable LLMs like GPT-4 Text, Llama2-7B, FLAN-T5-XXL, and Vicuna-13B were included. To assess the impact of visual pre-processing, these LLMs were also augmented with external image-to-text tools like MMOCR (Optical Character Recognition) or LLaVA-1.5-generated captions.
 - **Human Experts:** 90 college senior students (3 per subject, covering all 30 subjects) completed the 900-question validation set. They were permitted to use textbooks but prohibited from internet searches, providing a realistic upper bound for human performance in a controlled setting.
- **Dataset Splits:** The 11.5K questions are divided into a few-shot development set (5 questions per subject), a validation set (~900 questions for hyperparameter tuning), and a large test set (~10,500 questions for final evaluation).

This comprehensive methodology ensures that MMMU is a robust, challenging, and fair benchmark for evaluating advanced multimodal understanding and reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of numerous LMMs and LLMs on the MMMU benchmark yielded several significant findings, underscoring the benchmark's challenging nature and the current limitations of AI models in expert-level multimodal understanding.

- **MMMU Poses Significant Challenges:**
 - Even the most advanced proprietary models struggled to achieve high accuracy. GPT-4V(ision) attained an accuracy of 55.7%, while Gemini 1.0 Ultra achieved 59.4% (or Gemini 1.5 Pro 62.2% and GPT-4o 69.1% in later versions as per Table 4 in appendix), leaving substantial room for improvement.
 - In contrast, human experts achieved a validation accuracy ranging from 76.2% (worst) to 88.6% (best), demonstrating a considerable gap between human expert performance and current AI capabilities. This validates MMMU as a rigorous benchmark for Expert AGI.

- **Pronounced Disparity Between Proprietary and Open-Source LMMs:**
 - At the time of the paper's initial submission, leading open-source models like BLIP2-FLAN-T5-XXL and LLaVA-1.5 achieved accuracies of approximately 34%. This indicated a significant performance gap compared to proprietary models like GPT-4V.
 - However, the updated results in Appendix Table 4 show rapid progress in open-source models, with LLaVA-1.6-34B reaching 44.7% and InternVL-Chat-V1.2 achieving 46.2%, narrowing the gap but still trailing the top proprietary models.

- **Limited Benefit from OCR and Captioning for Text-Only LLMs:**
 - Augmenting text-only LLMs with external OCR or generated image captions (from LLaVA-1.5) did not yield a notable improvement in performance. This crucial finding suggests that MMMU problems cannot be effectively solved by simply converting images to text. Instead, they necessitate a deeper, joint interpretation and integration of visual and textual information, where models must truly "understand" the interplay between modalities.

- **Varying Performance Across Disciplines:**
 - Models exhibited relatively higher performance in disciplines where visual data is generally less complex or more "natural," such as Art & Design and Humanities & Social Science.
 - Conversely, performance was significantly lower in fields like Business, Science, Health & Medicine, and Tech & Engineering. These disciplines often feature intricate diagrams, technical schematics, or complex visual data (e.g., medical scans, chemical structures) that require highly specialized perception and deeper, domain-specific reasoning.

- **Struggles with Heterogeneous Image Types and Difficulty Levels:**
 - Analysis by image type revealed that all models performed relatively strongly on common categories like Photos and Paintings, likely due to their prevalence in training data.
 - However, models obtained very low scores on less common or highly specialized image types, such as Geometric shapes, Music sheets, and Chemical structures, often performing near random chance. This indicates poor generalization to novel or highly domain-specific visual formats.
 - Regarding difficulty, GPT-4V showed a clear advantage in "Easy" and "Medium" categories. However, for "Hard" questions, the performance gap between GPT-4V and open-source models substantially narrowed, with all models exhibiting much lower accuracy. This suggests that even the most advanced LMMs struggle significantly with truly expert-level reasoning and complex problem-solving.

#### 4.1. Error Analysis (on GPT-4V errors)

A detailed analysis of 150 randomly sampled errors from GPT-4V revealed key areas of model weakness:
- **Perceptual Errors (35%):** These errors involve misinterpreting visual information. Examples include basic mistakes like misjudging spatial relationships (e.g., "left to right, top to bottom" sequences as in Figure 6) or domain-specific misinterpretations (e.g., failing to recognize symbolic elements like national flags in design contexts, Figure 15). A notable observation was GPT-4V's bias towards prioritizing textual information over visual cues, sometimes leading to incorrect conclusions when text and image conveyed conflicting or nuanced information (e.g., interpreting a cartoon about the US as "Imperialist" instead of "Savior," Figure 67).
- **Lack of Knowledge (29%):** Models frequently failed when problems required specialized, domain-specific knowledge that was not adequately encoded or retrievable. This includes instances where models correctly identified visual elements but could not interpret their meaning within a specific domain (e.g., double circles in a DFA not being recognized as "accept states," Figure 83), or where flawed reasoning stemmed directly from missing factual knowledge (e.g., medical diagnoses, Figure 54).
- **Reasoning Errors (26%):** Even when models correctly perceived information and recalled relevant knowledge, they often failed to apply logical, mathematical, or domain-specific reasoning effectively to derive the correct solution. This includes neglecting critical steps in calculations (Figure 45), drawing illogical conclusions from correctly understood premises (e.g., biological processes, Figure 33), or making incorrect sequential deductions (Figure 89).

Other minor error categories included textual understanding errors (6%), rejection to answer (3%), annotation errors (2%), and answer extraction errors (1%).

These findings collectively highlight that current LMMs, while impressive, are far from achieving expert-level performance. They struggle with the intricate interplay of multimodal information, lack comprehensive domain knowledge, and exhibit limitations in complex, multi-step reasoning, providing clear targets for future research and model development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MMMU carries profound significance for the advancement of artificial intelligence, particularly in the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

- **A New Standard for Expert AGI Evaluation:** MMMU fills a critical void in the benchmarking landscape. By demanding college-level, multi-discipline, and multimodal understanding and reasoning, it establishes a new, higher bar for evaluating the "depth" and "breadth" of LMM capabilities. It moves beyond superficial understanding and common sense, requiring models to demonstrate skills akin to human experts in specialized fields. This directly contributes to operationalizing the "Expert AGI" (Level 3) milestone as defined by Morris et al., providing a concrete measure of progress.

- **Diagnostic Tool for Current Model Limitations:** The benchmark serves as a powerful diagnostic tool, clearly exposing the current shortcomings of even the most sophisticated LMMs, including proprietary leaders like GPT-4V and Gemini. The detailed error analysis (perceptual, lack of knowledge, reasoning) provides invaluable insights for researchers. It pinpoints specific areas where models fail, such as:
 - **Robust Multimodal Perception:** Struggling with interpreting highly heterogeneous and domain-specific image types (e.g., chemical structures, sheet music, technical diagrams).
 - **Deep Domain Knowledge Integration:** The inability to effectively recall and apply specialized, expert-level knowledge in complex problem-solving.
 - **Advanced Deliberate Reasoning:** Difficulties in performing multi-step logical, mathematical, or scientific deductions, even when intermediate information is correctly extracted.
 - **Seamless Multimodal Fusion:** The limited improvement from OCR/captioning highlights that LMMs still struggle with truly integrating text and vision in a holistic manner, rather than treating them as separate streams.

- **Driving Next-Generation Model Development:** By presenting these clear challenges, MMMU is poised to "stimulate the community to build next-generation multimodal foundation models." It provides a clear target for innovation in model architecture, training methodologies, and data curation strategies. Future LMMs will need to be more adept at:
 - Learning from and integrating diverse, structured visual representations.
 - Acquiring and leveraging vast amounts of domain-specific knowledge.
 - Developing more robust and flexible reasoning capabilities that mirror human cognitive processes in complex scenarios.
 - Reducing biases, such as the observed preference for textual over visual information.

- **Necessity for AGI, Not Sufficiency:** The authors cautiously clarify that while strong performance on MMMU should be a *necessary* criterion for an Expert AGI system, it is not *sufficient*. AGI encompasses more than just solving college exam problems, including aspects like adaptability, creativity, and common sense in real-world, dynamic environments. Nevertheless, demonstrating broad and deep subject knowledge and expert-level understanding through a benchmark like MMMU is an indispensable step.

In conclusion, MMMU is a timely and significant contribution to the field of AI. It provides a much-needed, high-standard benchmark that mirrors the complexity of human expert knowledge and reasoning in a multimodal context. By doing so, it offers a crucial compass for guiding the development and evaluation of LMMs on the path toward realizing the ambitious goals of Artificial General Intelligence.
