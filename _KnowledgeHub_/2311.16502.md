# MMMU: A Massive Multi-discipline Multimodal Understanding and Reasoning Benchmark for Expert AGI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Waterloo, The Ohio State University, and collaborators present MMMU, a new benchmark designed to evaluate Large Multimodal Models on expert-level, multi-discipline understanding and reasoning. The benchmark, featuring over 11,000 college-level questions with diverse image types, reveals that even leading models like GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro significantly trail human experts, struggling with domain-specific visual perception, deep knowledge, and complex reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal benchmarks primarily test basic perception or commonsense, lacking the depth required for evaluating expert-level understanding in diverse academic disciplines.
- Current AGI benchmarks are predominantly text-based, failing to assess multimodal reasoning capabilities crucial for human intelligence and expert performance.
- There was no comprehensive benchmark to evaluate Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) on college-level problems requiring deep subject knowledge and complex, deliberate reasoning across multiple disciplines.

## Method

- The paper introduces **MMMU**, a benchmark of ==11,550 college-level multimodal questions== spanning 6 disciplines, 30 subjects, and 183 subfields.
- The dataset features a high prevalence and ==heterogeneity of image types== (30 distinct types), with questions often requiring ==interleaved text and image understanding==.
- A rigorous ==three-stage data curation process== involving over 50 university student annotators and subsequent quality control steps ensured the comprehensiveness, relevance, and difficulty of the questions.

## Results

- MMMU poses substantial challenges to state-of-the-art LMMs; for example, GPT-4o achieved **69.1%** accuracy, while human experts scored between **76.2%** and **88.6%** on the validation set, indicating a notable performance gap.
- Models struggled significantly with specific image types (e.g., geometric shapes, music sheets, chemical structures) and 'Hard' difficulty questions, revealing limitations in generalization and complex problem-solving.
- Error analysis on GPT-4V showed common failures stemming from perceptual misinterpretations (**35%**), lack of specialized domain knowledge (**29%**), and failures in logical or scientific reasoning (**26%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expert-level AGI evaluation necessitates benchmarks that mirror human expertise by combining multi-discipline knowledge with complex multimodal understanding and reasoning.
> - Current multimodal models require true joint interpretation of visual and textual information, as simple image-to-text conversion (via OCR or captioning) is insufficient for solving challenging, expert-level problems.
> - LMMs face limitations in handling highly heterogeneous and domain-specific visual inputs, acquiring deep domain knowledge, and performing multi-step deliberate reasoning.
