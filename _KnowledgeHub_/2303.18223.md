# A Survey of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey by Zhao et al. synthesizes the rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), outlining foundational techniques across pre-training, adaptation, utilization, and evaluation. The review identifies key resources and challenges, providing a structured understanding of their capabilities and future research directions.

## Problem

- The rapid and impactful advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly with models like ChatGPT, created a pressing need for a consolidated and up-to-date understanding of the field.
- The emergence of novel 'emergent abilities' (e.g., in-context learning) in LLMs, not present in smaller pre-trained language models (PLMs), necessitated a dedicated, systematic review beyond existing PLM literature.
- A lack of a unified resource that integrates the evolving research and engineering challenges, mainstream techniques, and available resources specifically for LLMs, complicated navigation for researchers and practitioners.

## Method

- The survey systematically reviews and synthesizes the extensive literature on LLMs, categorizing findings into four core aspects: ==pre-training==, ==adaptation tuning== (==instruction tuning==, ==RLHF==), ==utilization strategies== (==in-context learning==, ==chain-of-thought prompting==), and ==capacity evaluation==.
- It contextualizes LLMs by tracing the evolution of language models (SLM → NLM → PLM → LLM) and detailing the technical advancements of key ==decoder-only Transformer== models like the **GPT** series, highlighting the shift in AI development paradigms.
- The work compiles a comprehensive list of publicly available resources, including model checkpoints, pre-training corpora, fine-tuning datasets, and open-source libraries, to support practical LLM development.

## Results

- Instruction tuning significantly enhances LLM performance across various tasks and improves instruction following, enabling even smaller models to outperform larger, untuned counterparts.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting effectively boosts LLM performance on complex reasoning tasks by encouraging intermediate thinking steps, with further improvements from structured reasoning and verification methods.
- Despite strong capabilities, LLMs consistently face challenges like hallucination, knowledge recency, and reasoning inconsistency, necessitating continued research into mitigation strategies such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and robust alignment techniques.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models exhibit non-linear 'emergent abilities' like in-context learning and Chain-of-Thought reasoning upon reaching certain scales, fundamentally changing their problem-solving capabilities.
> - The quality and scale of pre-training data, alongside optimized architectures (predominantly decoder-only Transformers) and advanced distributed training techniques, are paramount for developing capable LLMs.
> - Post-training adaptation, including instruction tuning and alignment tuning (e.g., RLHF), is critical for enhancing LLMs' ability to follow instructions, generalize, and align with human values for safe and effective deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on language intelligence by machines has evolved significantly since the Turing Test. Language modeling, a core approach, progressed from statistical models to neural models, and then to pre-trained language models (PLMs) based on the Transformer architecture. PLMs, pre-trained on large corpora, demonstrated strong capabilities across various Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks.

This survey addresses the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs), defined as PLMs of significant size (tens to hundreds of billions of parameters). These LLMs exhibit "emergent abilities" (e.g., in-context learning) not present in smaller models, marking a distinct phase in language model development. The advent of models like ChatGPT and GPT-4 has further accelerated research and societal interest in LLMs, shifting the paradigm of AI development and utilization.

While previous surveys have covered PLMs or specific aspects of LLMs, this work aims to provide an up-to-date, detailed, and comprehensive review specifically focused on the recent advances of LLMs. It differentiates LLMs from earlier PLMs by emphasizing their emergent abilities, the shift towards prompting as an interface, and the convergence of research and engineering challenges in their development. The survey seeks to consolidate mainstream techniques, key findings, and available resources, discussing their background, pre-training, adaptation, utilization, and capacity evaluation, as well as future directions. It positions itself as a resource for both researchers and engineers navigating the rapid evolution of LLM technology.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a detailed and comprehensive review of the recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs). This includes introducing their background, key findings, and mainstream techniques across four major aspects: pre-training, adaptation tuning, utilization, and capacity evaluation. Additionally, the authors aim to summarize available resources for LLM development and discuss remaining challenges and future directions.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors:

- **Rapid Technical Progress and Impact:** The field of LLMs has witnessed rapid advancements, particularly with the public release of models like ChatGPT and GPT-4. These models have garnered widespread attention and are making a substantial impact across the entire AI community, leading to discussions about Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and revolutionizing AI development paradigms in fields like NLP, Information Retrieval (IR), and Computer Vision (CV).
- **Emergent Abilities:** LLMs demonstrate surprising emergent abilities (e.g., in-context learning, instruction following, step-by-step reasoning) that are not observed in smaller PLMs. These abilities are crucial for performance on complex tasks, making LLMs unprecedentedly powerful. Understanding and documenting these capabilities is a core motivation.
- **Shifting Development and Utilization Paradigms:** The way AI algorithms are developed and used is changing. LLMs are often accessed via prompting interfaces (e.g., APIs), requiring users to understand their operational mechanisms and format tasks accordingly. This shift necessitates a systematic review of utilization strategies.
- **Blurred Lines Between Research and Engineering:** Training LLMs demands extensive practical experience in large-scale data processing and distributed parallel training, merging research and engineering challenges. A comprehensive survey can guide practitioners in navigating these complexities.
- **Lack of Systematic Review for LLMs:** Existing literature often surveyed PLMs or specific facets of LLMs. There was a need for a unified and systematic review that specifically focuses on the unique characteristics and advancements of LLMs, providing a consolidated reference for the field.
- **Unexplored Underlying Principles and Challenges:** Despite progress, several fundamental questions about LLMs remain unanswered. These include the mysterious emergence of abilities, the difficulty and cost of training capable LLMs (especially with proprietary details), and the challenge of aligning LLMs with human values to prevent harmful outputs. Addressing these issues, and providing a foundational understanding, is a strong motivation for this survey.

Ultimately, the authors aim to equip both researchers and engineers with an up-to-date understanding and resources to contribute to the research and development of LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is a comprehensive survey, and its methodology centers on systematically reviewing and synthesizing existing literature on Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors categorize and analyze various aspects of LLMs, providing an organized overview of their development, capabilities, and challenges.

The approach is structured as follows:

1. **Contextualization and Historical Overview:**
 - The survey begins by tracing the evolution of language models from statistical (SLM) to neural (NLM), then to pre-trained (PLM), and finally to Large Language Models (LLM). This establishes the historical trajectory and the context for LLMs' emergence.
 - A dedicated subsection details the technical evolution of GPT-series models, illustrating how key advancements (decoder-only architecture, scaling, code training, human alignment) led to powerful models like ChatGPT and GPT-4.

2. **Core Aspects of LLMs:** The main body of the survey is organized around four fundamental aspects of LLMs:
 - **Pre-training:** This section elaborates on the foundational stage of LLM development.
 - **Data Collection and Preparation:** Discusses various data sources (web pages, books, academic data, code, mixed data), emphasizing data quality (filtering, deduplication, privacy reduction) and strategic data scheduling (data mixture, curriculum learning).
 - **Architecture:** Reviews mainstream Transformer-based architectures (encoder-decoder, causal decoder, prefix decoder) and emergent architectures (e.g., Mamba). It also details common configurations like normalization methods/positions, activation functions, and position embeddings (absolute, relative, rotary, ALiBi), as well as attention mechanisms (full, sparse, multi-query, FlashAttention, PagedAttention).
 - **Model Training:** Covers optimization settings (batch training, learning rate schedules, optimizers like Adam/Adafactor, stability techniques like weight decay and gradient clipping) and scalable training techniques (3D parallelism: data, pipeline, tensor parallelism; mixed precision training: FP16, BF16).
 - **Post-training (Adaptation Tuning):** This section focuses on refining pre-trained LLMs.
 - **Instruction Tuning:** Explains how models are fine-tuned on natural language instructions. It covers methods for constructing formatted instances (from NLP datasets, daily chat, synthetic data), key factors for dataset quality (scaling, format, quality improvement, selection), and tuning strategies (balancing data, combining with pre-training, multi-stage tuning).
 - **Alignment Tuning:** Details methods to align LLMs with human values (helpfulness, honesty, harmlessness). This includes human feedback collection (ranking, question-based, rule-based) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) via a three-step process (supervised fine-tuning, reward model training, RL fine-tuning). Alternatives like direct preference optimization (DPO) and process-supervised RLHF are also discussed.
 - **Parameter-Efficient Model Adaptation (PEFT):** Explores techniques to reduce computational costs during fine-tuning, including adapter tuning, prefix tuning, prompt tuning, and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).
 - **Utilization:** This section describes how LLMs are used for various tasks.
 - **Prompting:** Covers prompt creation (key ingredients like task description, input data, context, style; design principles) and prompt optimization (discrete vs. continuous prompts, gradient-based, RL-based, LLM-based approaches).
 - **In-Context Learning (ICL):** Explains the ICL formulation and its dependence on demonstration design (selection, format, order). It also discusses the underlying mechanisms (task recognition, task learning).
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Details the basic CoT approach, improved strategies (sampling-based, verification-based), and reasoning structure extensions (tree-, graph-structured). It also examines when and why CoT works.
 - **Planning:** Introduces an overall framework (task planner, plan executor, environment) for complex task solving, detailing plan generation (text-based, code-based), feedback acquisition (internal, external), and plan refinement (reasoning, backtracking, memorization).
 - **Capacity and Evaluation:** This section assesses LLM capabilities.
 - **Basic Abilities:** Covers language generation (language modeling, conditional text generation, code synthesis), knowledge utilization (closed-book QA, open-book QA, knowledge completion), and complex reasoning (knowledge, symbolic, mathematical reasoning). Major issues like hallucination and knowledge recency are discussed.
 - **Advanced Abilities:** Explores human alignment, interaction with external environments, and tool manipulation.
 - **Benchmarks and Evaluation Approaches:** Presents comprehensive benchmarks (MMLU, BIG-bench, HELM) and evaluation methodologies for different types of LLMs (base, fine-tuned, specialized) using benchmark-based, human-based, and model-based approaches.

3. **Advanced Topics and Future Directions:**
 - The survey delves into specialized topics like long context modeling, LLM-empowered agents, training/inference optimization, model compression (quantization, distillation, pruning), retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), hallucination analysis, and complex reasoning (long CoT).

4. **Empirical Analysis:** The authors perform their own empirical studies to illustrate key concepts, such as:
 - Evaluating the impact of different instruction datasets and improvement strategies on LLaMA models (7B and 13B) for chat and QA settings.
 - Demonstrating the effect of various prompt designs on ChatGPT across diverse tasks.
 - Analyzing the inference performance of quantized LLaMA models.

5. **Resource Compilation:** The paper integrates references to publicly available model checkpoints (or APIs), common corpora for pre-training, datasets for fine-tuning, and open-source libraries, accompanied by a GitHub project.

The overall approach is descriptive, analytical, and synthesizes a vast body of recent work, supplemented by focused empirical investigations to support the discussions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey identifies and synthesizes numerous key findings and results across the development, adaptation, utilization, and evaluation of Large Language Models (LLMs):

#### 4.1. Emergence and Scaling

- **Emergent Abilities:** LLMs, when scaled beyond a certain parameter size (e.g., tens of billions), exhibit "emergent abilities" such as in-context learning, instruction following, and step-by-step reasoning, which are not observed in smaller models. These abilities appear non-linearly, with performance rising sharply past a critical scale.
- **Scaling Laws:** Empirical scaling laws (e.g., Kaplan et al., Chinchilla) demonstrate that model performance (measured by language modeling loss) predictably improves with increased model size, dataset size, and computational resources. The Chinchilla scaling law, in particular, suggests a more balanced allocation of compute to both model and data size than previously thought.
- **Predictable Scaling:** Despite emergent abilities, some LLM capabilities (e.g., coding) can be reliably predicted using scaling laws from smaller models, aiding in large-scale model training.

#### 4.2. Pre-training Foundations

- **Data is Critical:** The scale, diversity, and quality of pre-training data are paramount. Filtering noisy, redundant, or toxic data significantly improves model capacity. Specialized data (multilingual, scientific, code) enhances specific abilities.
- **Data Scheduling:** Optimal data mixtures and curricula (e.g., multi-stage training with general then skill-specific data) are crucial for effective pre-training and specialized capability development.
- **Architecture Dominance:** The decoder-only Transformer architecture, exemplified by the GPT series, has become the de facto standard due to its parallelizability and emergent in-context learning capabilities. Variants like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) and new architectures like Mamba (SSM-based) are explored for efficiency.
- **Optimization:** Training billion-parameter models requires advanced distributed training techniques (data, pipeline, and tensor parallelism, often combined as 3D parallelism), mixed-precision training (BF16 offers better accuracy than FP16), and stability tricks (dynamic batch size, specific learning rate schedules, gradient clipping, restarting from checkpoints).

#### 4.3. Post-training Adaptation

- **Instruction Tuning (SFT):** Fine-tuning LLMs on diverse, human-written, or synthetically generated instructions significantly enhances their ability to follow instructions and generalize to unseen tasks. It can enable smaller models to outperform larger, untuned ones. Quality and diversity of instruction data are more important than sheer quantity.
- **Alignment Tuning (RLHF):** Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is a key technique for aligning LLMs with human values (helpful, honest, harmless), crucial for safe deployment. It involves supervised fine-tuning, training a reward model from human preferences, and optimizing the LLM using RL algorithms (e.g., PPO). Alternatives like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) simplify this by reframing reward learning as a supervised problem.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):** Methods like LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) are essential for adapting LLMs to new tasks with minimal computational cost, by only updating a small fraction of parameters. These methods often achieve performance comparable to full fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Utilization Strategies

- **Prompt Engineering:** Careful design of prompts, including clear task descriptions, contextual information, few-shot demonstrations, and specific styles (e.g., role-playing), dramatically influences LLM performance. Automatic prompt optimization methods are under active research.
- **In-Context Learning (ICL):** LLMs can learn new tasks from demonstrations within the prompt itself, without weight updates. This is attributed to both "task recognition" (leveraging pre-trained knowledge) and "task learning" (implicitly performing optimization steps).
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Incorporating intermediate reasoning steps in prompts significantly boosts performance on complex reasoning tasks (arithmetic, commonsense, symbolic). Variants explore tree- or graph-structured reasoning paths and employ sampling or verification for robustness.
- **Planning:** LLMs can act as task planners, decomposing complex problems into sub-tasks and generating execution plans (text- or code-based). These plans can be refined iteratively using internal (self-reflection) or external (tool outputs) feedback.

#### 4.5. Capacity and Evaluation Insights

- **Strong Basic Abilities:** LLMs demonstrate robust capabilities in language generation (modeling, conditional text generation, code synthesis), knowledge utilization (QA, fact completion), and complex reasoning.
- **Persistent Issues:** Despite advancements, LLMs face challenges such as:
 - **Hallucination:** Generating factually incorrect or unverified information. This is a major concern, linked to data quality, training methods, and decoding. Detection and mitigation strategies (alignment, RAG, decoding adjustments) are crucial.
 - **Knowledge Recency:** Difficulty with up-to-date information due to fixed training data. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a primary solution.
 - **Reasoning Inconsistency:** Producing correct answers through invalid steps, or vice-versa. Addressed by process-based feedback, ensemble methods, and code-based reasoning.
 - **Numerical Computation:** Limited accuracy with large numbers or complex arithmetic. Augmented with external tools (calculators) or specialized tokenization (digit-based).
- **Advanced Capabilities:** LLMs show promise in human alignment, interacting with simulated environments (embodied AI), and manipulating external tools (plugins, APIs).
- **Evaluation Landscape:** Diverse benchmarks (MMLU, BIG-bench, HELM) and evaluation approaches (automatic, human-based, model-based using other LLMs as judges) are used. Each has limitations, including data contamination, subjectivity, and biases (position, verbosity, self-enhancement).
- **Empirical Studies:** The authors' experiments confirm that instruction tuning generally improves LLM performance across tasks, with certain types of instructions (e.g., daily chat) being more effective for specific use cases. Quantization (e.g., 4-bit) can significantly reduce memory footprint with minimal performance loss, especially for emergent abilities.

#### 4.6. Advanced Topics

- **Long Context Modeling:** Techniques like positional interpolation and attention sinks enable LLMs to handle input sequences far exceeding their original training context length, crucial for document processing tasks.
- **LLM-Empowered Agents:** LLMs form the core of autonomous agents with memory, planning, and execution components, allowing complex task-solving in single or multi-agent settings, though they face computational and robustness challenges.
- **Inference Optimization & Compression:** System-level optimizations (FlashAttention, PagedAttention, batch management) and algorithmic approaches (speculative decoding, cascade inference, early exit) are vital for speeding up LLM inference. Quantization (INT8, INT4) significantly reduces memory footprint for deployment, often with minimal impact on performance.
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Integrates external knowledge retrieval into the generation process to improve factual accuracy, address knowledge recency, and mitigate hallucination. It involves optimizing retrieval, prompt construction, and iterative refinement.
- **Complex Reasoning (Long CoT):** Represents a "slow thinking" mode for LLMs, generating extended, multi-step thought processes (often with self-correction and exploration) before providing a final answer. This is fostered through distillation from advanced models, search-based data synthesis, or multi-agent collaboration, and trained via specialized instruction tuning or scaled reinforcement learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey highlights the profound significance and potential impact of Large Language Models (LLMs) across various dimensions:

#### 5.1. Transformative Impact on AI Research and Development

- **Paradigm Shift:** LLMs are driving a fundamental shift in AI, moving towards general-purpose, task-agnostic problem solvers capable of addressing a wide array of challenges through natural language interaction. This alters traditional NLP research paradigms that focused on specialized models for individual tasks.
- **Emergence of Novel Capabilities:** The discovery of emergent abilities in LLMs (e.g., in-context learning, CoT reasoning) suggests that scaling itself can unlock qualitatively new intelligence, prompting deeper investigation into the underlying mechanisms of large neural networks.
- **Blurring of Disciplines:** The development of LLMs is increasingly integrating elements of system engineering, data science, and cognitive science, requiring interdisciplinary approaches and collaboration.
- **New Research Frontiers:** LLMs open new avenues for research in areas such as efficient model training and inference, novel architectural designs (e.g., SSMs), advanced prompting and planning strategies, multimodal AI, and robust knowledge integration (e.g., KG-enhanced LLMs, RAG).

#### 5.2. Revolutionizing Real-World Applications

- **Enhanced Human-AI Interaction:** Models like ChatGPT demonstrate sophisticated conversational abilities, potentially redefining how humans interact with information and technology (e.g., conversational search engines like New Bing).
- **Cross-Domain Problem Solving:** LLMs can serve as powerful assistants across diverse specialized domains, significantly impacting fields such as:
 - **Healthcare:** Aiding in medical information extraction, advice consultation, mental health analysis, and report simplification (e.g., Med-PaLM achieving expert-level performance on medical exams).
 - **Education:** Acting as writing/reading assistants, performing well on standardized tests, and offering personalized learning support.
 - **Law:** Assisting in legal document analysis, judgment prediction, and document writing (e.g., GPT-4's performance on simulated bar exams).
 - **Finance:** Supporting tasks like numerical claim detection, sentiment analysis, and financial reasoning (e.g., BloombergGPT).
 - **Scientific Research:** Facilitating literature reviews, hypothesis generation, data analysis, and scientific writing, potentially accelerating scientific discovery.
- **Development of Autonomous Agents:** LLM-empowered agents, with capabilities for memory, planning, and execution, hold the potential to autonomously solve complex, long-horizon tasks in various environments (e.g., web browsing, robotics, gaming).
- **Improved AI Systems:** LLMs can enhance existing AI systems by providing better data (e.g., for IR relevance judgments, recommendation simulators), acting as sophisticated evaluators for other models, or augmenting traditional models with their extensive knowledge.

#### 5.3. Challenges and Responsible Development

- **Safety and Alignment:** The prevalence of hallucination, bias, and the potential for generating harmful or toxic content necessitate robust alignment techniques (like RLHF) and safety protocols (red teaming). This is critical for building trustworthy AI systems.
- **Computational and Economic Barriers:** The immense computational resources and costs required for training, fine-tuning, and deploying LLMs pose significant barriers, emphasizing the need for efficiency optimizations (e.g., FlashAttention, quantization, PEFT) to democratize access.
- **Ethical Considerations:** Concerns regarding data privacy, misinformation, plagiarism, and equitable access must be addressed as LLM applications become more widespread.
- **Understanding "Intelligence":** The capabilities of LLMs challenge current understandings of intelligence and learning, pushing researchers to explore new theoretical frameworks and interpretability methods.

In summary, LLMs represent a significant leap in AI capabilities, poised to reshape technological landscapes and human interaction with machines. Their full potential, however, is coupled with critical challenges that underscore the importance of ongoing research into their fundamental principles, robust engineering, and ethical deployment. The survey itself contributes to this by consolidating knowledge and identifying key areas for future exploration.
