# Generative Artificial Intelligence in Robotic Manipulation: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive overview of how generative artificial intelligence models address critical challenges in robotic manipulation, detailing their applications in alleviating data scarcity, enabling complex task planning, and enhancing multi-modal reasoning across various operational layers. It highlights their utility in synthesizing diverse data, decomposing tasks with LLMs, and generating robust grasp and trajectory policies.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation faces challenges with insufficient data and inefficient data acquisition for data-driven learning methods like RL and IL.
- Complex, long-horizon tasks require advanced planning capabilities that traditional deterministic models struggle with due to uncertainties and dynamic interactions.
- Robots need robust multi-modality reasoning to capture diverse valid actions from a single observed state, which is often limited in deterministic policy learning.

## Method

- The survey systematically reviews recent advancements of various generative AI models (GANs, VAEs, ==Diffusion Models==, etc.) applied to robotic manipulation.
- It introduces a novel three-layered hierarchical framework—Foundation, Intermediate, and Policy Layers—to categorize and understand the role of generative AI.
- The approach details how generative models alleviate data dependency, enable complex task decomposition and planning, and facilitate multi-modal reasoning for robust policy learning.

## Results

- Generative models, particularly diffusion models and VLMs, have revolutionized data and reward generation, synthesizing diverse and realistic scenarios, demonstrations, and supervision signals.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) enable advanced intermediate layer functionalities, including complex task decomposition, code generation for robot control, and enhanced visual/state representations for planning.
- In the policy layer, generative approaches (VAE-based, diffusion models, large pre-trained VLA models) facilitate robust, multi-modal grasp generation and highly precise, generalizable trajectory generation for diverse robotic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative AI models are crucial for overcoming long-standing bottlenecks in robotic manipulation by forming an integrated ecosystem, enabling more autonomous and versatile robotic systems.
> - These models significantly enhance generalization and adaptability by learning rich, multi-modal representations from vast datasets, improving robot performance in unseen objects and environments.
> - Generative AI facilitates bridging the sim-to-real gap and improves human-robot collaboration by enabling more intuitive interaction and programming methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a cornerstone of intelligent autonomy, enabling machines to physically interact with and alter their environments across diverse applications, from manufacturing to assistive care. Historically, this field has grappled with significant challenges, including the scarcity and inefficiency of data acquisition, the complexity of planning for long-horizon and multi-step tasks, and the necessity for multi-modal reasoning to ensure robust performance across varied environments.

In recent years, the broader research landscape in robotics has witnessed a significant shift towards data-driven approaches, leveraging large-scale datasets and advanced machine learning techniques to enhance perception, adaptation, and interaction. Within this paradigm, the emergence of generative artificial intelligence (AI) models, such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), diffusion models, probabilistic flow models, and autoregressive models, has been particularly transformative. These models have demonstrated extraordinary capabilities in synthesizing high-quality data, understanding complex scenes, performing intricate reasoning, and synthesizing effective policies.

This survey positions itself by identifying a critical gap in the existing literature. While several surveys have broadly explored foundational models in robotics or the general progression towards artificial general intelligence, none have specifically focused on the *application of generative models to address the key challenges inherent in robotic manipulation*. By providing a "unified yet specific perspective," this work aims to fill this void. It systematically examines how generative models can directly tackle the bottlenecks of data insufficiency, complex task planning, and multi-modality reasoning that have historically hampered robotic manipulation. It frames these contributions within a novel hierarchical structure—Foundation, Intermediate, and Policy Layers—demonstrating the pervasive and interconnected roles of generative AI in advancing manipulation capabilities. This structured approach allows for a deeper understanding of how generative AI is not just an incremental improvement but a fundamental paradigm shift enabling more autonomous, efficient, and versatile robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is to thoroughly review the recent advancements of generative learning models in robotic manipulation, highlighting their potential to address long-standing and critical bottlenecks in the field. The authors pinpoint three major challenges that necessitate the adoption of generative AI:

1. **Insufficient Data and Inefficient Data Acquisition:** Data-driven methods like Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL) are notoriously data-hungry. Collecting high-quality, task-specific data for robotic manipulation is time-consuming, expensive, and difficult to scale in real-world environments. Generative models, such as Stable Diffusion and large-scale pre-trained language models, can synthesize diverse and high-quality synthetic data (e.g., images, videos, annotations) and reward signals, significantly alleviating this data dependency and providing scalable data generation pipelines.
2. **Long-Horizon and Complex Task Planning:** Multi-step assembly operations, object rearrangement in cluttered environments, and human-robot collaboration require robots to plan and execute extended sequences of interdependent actions. Traditional deterministic models struggle with the inherent uncertainties and dynamic interactions of such tasks, often lacking the ability to decompose complex goals or understand causal relationships. Generative models offer solutions by enabling task decomposition into manageable sub-goals (e.g., via Chain-of-Thought reasoning, language generation, code generation) and facilitating the development of world models for predicting future states and learning dynamics.
3. **Multi-modality Reasoning Ability for Robust Policy Learning Performance:** In manipulation, a single observed state can correspond to multiple valid actions or outcomes (e.g., grasping a mug by the handle or body, depending on the subsequent task). Deterministic models often fail to capture this inherent multi-modality, limiting adaptability. Generative models excel at modeling multi-modal distributions, enabling the generation of diverse and physically plausible grasp poses and motion trajectories, thus enhancing the robot's ability to perform precise and complex manipulation tasks.

Therefore, the key objective of this survey is to provide a comprehensive, structured, and insightful review of how these generative model paradigms are being applied across various aspects of robotic manipulation to overcome these challenges. The authors aim to organize existing literature into a coherent framework that elucidates the "transformative potential" of generative AI in this domain, thereby guiding future research directions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this survey is characterized by its comprehensive scope and structured, hierarchical categorization of generative AI applications in robotic manipulation.

**Generative Model Paradigms Covered:**
The survey begins by providing a foundational overview of several key generative model paradigms, explaining their core principles and relevance to robotic manipulation. These include:
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs):** Used for synthesizing diverse and high-quality data by training a generator against a discriminator.
- **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs):** Employed for learning probabilistic mappings to a lower-dimensional latent space, enabling the generation of new data by sampling from this learned space, useful for smooth trajectory and adaptable grasp planning.
- **Diffusion Models (DDPM, DDIM):** Leveraging a process of gradual noise corruption and subsequent denoising, these models are adept at generating diverse and realistic outputs, crucial for tasks requiring adaptability and precision.
- **Probabilistic Flow Models:** Learn invertible transformations between a simple prior distribution and complex data distribution, allowing for efficient likelihood estimation and exact inference, valuable for anomaly detection and consistent data generation.
- **Autoregressive Models:** Generate data token by token, conditioning each step on previous ones, which has seen immense success in large language models and is being extended to vision-language-action models for sequential task decomposition and action generation.

**Hierarchical Application Categorization:**
The core of the survey's approach is its novel three-layered hierarchical framework, designed to systematically understand the role of generative models in robotic manipulation:

1. **Foundation Layer:** This layer focuses on generating essential resources that form the backbone for model training and evaluation.
 - **Data Generation:** Explores methods for creating synthetic data to augment limited datasets, including simulation-based generation (PyBullet, MuJoCo), direct generation via generative models (e.g., LLM/diffusion for scenario/demonstration generation like RoboGen, MimicGen), and data augmentation using generative models (e.g., diffusion for texture/shape editing, VLMs for semantic augmentation).
 - **Reward Generation:** Discusses how generative models provide crucial feedback for policy optimization. This includes providing supervision signals (e.g., using VLMs like CLIP, Flamingo to relabel demonstrations or generate success indicators) and offering policy scores (e.g., VLMs as online reward functions for model predictive control, aligning rankings in inverse reinforcement learning).

2. **Intermediate Layer:** This layer bridges high-level task planning and low-level policy execution by generating structured, interpretable representations.
 - **Natural Language Generation:** Covers the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) for task decomposition (breaking down complex tasks into sub-goals), physically-grounded language generation (ensuring executable plans by considering real-world constraints), and language generation with external memory (leveraging knowledge bases for enhanced planning).
 - **Code Generation:** Explores translating natural language instructions into executable robot control programs through direct code generation, decomposition-based code generation (breaking down tasks before code generation), and constraint-based code generation (incorporating physical and spatial limitations).
 - **Visual Generation:** Details how generative models create realistic visual cues to interpret and interact with environments, including video generation (for visualizing action sequences), image generation (for intermediate subgoals), and 3D generation (for detailed spatial and semantic representations like NeRFs and Gaussian Splatting).
 - **State Generation:** Focuses on creating meaningful and compact representations of environment or task dynamics, encompassing modeling observations (transforming raw sensory inputs into structured latent representations) and modeling dynamics (predicting state transitions and understanding action dependencies for long-horizon planning).

3. **Policy Layer:** This layer directly addresses the core robotic manipulation problem by translating insights from lower layers into actionable control strategies.
 - **Grasp Generation:** Reviews methods for finding target end-effector poses for grasping, distinguishing between deterministic and generative approaches. It highlights VAE-based methods (for diverse and plausible grasps), diffusion-based methods (for modeling multimodal action distributions), and other generative models (GANs, probabilistic flows for adaptive domain transfer and pre-grasp pose generation).
 - **Trajectory Generation:** Explores how generative models produce consistent and stable action sequences for robotic manipulation. Categories include sampling-based generation (e.g., Diffusion Policy, ACT for learning visuomotor policies), large pre-trained model-based generation (e.g., Vision-Language-Action models like RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA leveraging extensive pre-training), and hybrid-mode generation (combining multiple techniques for robustness and versatility).

By systematically detailing applications within these layers, the survey effectively maps the evolving landscape of generative AI in robotic manipulation, offering a clear framework for understanding current capabilities and identifying future research avenues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive review presented in the survey highlights the pervasive and transformative impact of generative AI across all layers of robotic manipulation, addressing the field's most pressing challenges.

**Overall Efficacy of Generative Models:**
The central finding is that generative models (GANs, VAEs, Diffusion, Probabilistic Flows, Autoregressive models) have become indispensable tools for overcoming long-standing bottlenecks in robotic manipulation. They have significantly alleviated data scarcity, enabled more sophisticated long-horizon planning, and improved multi-modality reasoning in policy learning. Tasks previously considered "formidably difficult," such as operating deformable materials or executing intricate long-horizon sequences, have now been demonstrated successfully thanks to these models.

**Key Contributions Across Layers:**

- **Foundation Layer (Data and Reward Generation):**
 - **Data Generation:** Generative models have revolutionized data creation, moving beyond traditional simulations to synthesize highly diverse and realistic scenarios and demonstrations (e.g., GRUtopia, RoboGen, MimicGen). Diffusion models and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are pivotal for semantic data augmentation, modifying textures, shapes, and backgrounds, although ensuring physical accuracy remains a challenge.
 - **Reward Generation:** VLMs have emerged as powerful tools for generating supervision signals and policy scores, densifying sparse rewards in reinforcement learning (e.g., HER with Flamingo) and providing human-level reward design (e.g., EUREKA). They can also assist in online policy selection for model predictive control, despite challenges in grounding domain-general models to specific tasks.

- **Intermediate Layer (Task Understanding and Representation):**
 - **Natural Language Generation:** Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate remarkable capabilities in decomposing complex, long-horizon tasks into executable sub-goals (e.g., SayCan, Inner Monologue). Progress is being made in physically grounding these language plans to ensure executability and in integrating external memory for enhanced reasoning.
 - **Code Generation:** LLMs and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly used to translate natural language instructions directly into executable robot control programs (e.g., Code as Policies, RoboScript). Decomposition-based and constraint-based methods enhance reliability and ensure adherence to physical limitations.
 - **Visual Generation:** Advances in visual generative models (video, image, and 3D generation) allow robots to predict and simulate complex tasks visually (e.g., UniPi, SuSIE, ManiGaussian). This enables better scene understanding and planning by generating future states or detailed 3D representations, complementing language and code generation.
 - **State Generation:** Generative models are effective for learning compact and meaningful latent representations of environment observations and dynamics, facilitating long-horizon planning. Approaches like Dreamer use latent imagination for planning, while self-supervised pre-training (e.g., on human videos) captures transition dependencies and provides valuable state representations for downstream tasks.

- **Policy Layer (Actionable Control Strategies):**
 - **Grasp Generation:** Generative models excel at addressing the multi-modal nature of grasping. VAE-based methods (e.g., GraspNet, Contact-GraspNet) learn compact latent spaces for diverse grasp pose generation. Diffusion models (e.g., SE(3)-DiF, DexDiffuser) are highly effective in modeling complex, multi-modal grasp pose distributions, including for multi-fingered and dexterous hands, often leveraging point cloud inputs. Other generative models like GANs and probabilistic flows also contribute to robust grasp synthesis and domain adaptation.
 - **Trajectory Generation:** This area has seen significant advancements, with a clear trend towards large, pre-trained models.
 - **Sampling-based:** Diffusion Policy and ALOHA's ACT algorithm demonstrate robust learning of visuomotor policies and high-precision bimanual manipulation by modeling multimodal action distributions.
 - **Large Pre-trained Model-based:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA) leverage extensive pre-training on diverse datasets to generate actions conditioned on multi-modal inputs, showing superior generalization.
 - **Hybrid-mode:** Frameworks like RDT and π₀ combine different generative techniques and pre-trained knowledge to achieve generalist robot control, integrating semantic understanding with continuous action generation for high-frequency control across varied tasks and robots.

In essence, the survey concludes that generative AI is not just a collection of disparate tools but forms an integrated ecosystem that significantly enhances robotic manipulation capabilities, from fundamental data infrastructure to high-level reasoning and precise physical control. The ability of these models to learn complex, multi-modal distributions and generalize from vast datasets is the core enabler for current and future advancements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of generative artificial intelligence in robotic manipulation, as articulated by this survey, are profound and far-reaching. The work identifies several critical areas where generative AI is not just incrementally improving but fundamentally transforming the capabilities of robotic systems:

1. **Enabling Autonomous and Capable Robotic Systems:** By addressing challenges like data scarcity, complex task planning, and multi-modality reasoning, generative models pave the way for robots that are more autonomous, efficient, and versatile. This means robots can operate with less human intervention, learn faster, adapt to novel situations, and perform tasks that were previously too complex or unpredictable for automation. This impact will be felt across industries, from advanced manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and domestic assistance.

2. **Bridging the Reality Gap:** A persistent challenge in robotics is the "sim-to-real gap," where policies trained in simulation fail to generalize effectively to the real world due to discrepancies in physics, sensing, and dynamics. Generative models offer powerful tools to mitigate this. Through realistic data generation, physics-augmented training, and physics-constrained generation, they can create synthetic environments and data that more closely mimic real-world conditions, thereby improving the transferability of learned policies. Enhanced physical law awareness in generative models is crucial for robust performance in unstructured and dynamic real-world settings.

3. **Advancing Generalization and Adaptability:** Generative models, especially large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and multi-modal transformers, are pushing the boundaries of generalization. By learning rich, multi-modal representations from vast datasets, robots can better understand and adapt to unseen objects, environments, and task variations. The ability to ground domain-general models to domain-specific constraints (e.g., through task-centric adaptation like Robotic-CLIP) ensures that general knowledge can be effectively applied to specific manipulation scenarios, leading to more robust and adaptable robot behaviors.

4. **Facilitating Human-Robot Collaboration:** The advancements in natural language and code generation enable more intuitive and accessible ways for humans to interact with and program robots. Non-experts can specify complex tasks using natural language, allowing generative AI to handle the translation into low-level commands. This lowers the barrier to entry for robotics and opens up new possibilities for collaborative environments where robots can understand and respond to human instructions more effectively.

5. **Setting Future Research Agenda:** The survey not only synthesizes current knowledge but also critically outlines pressing challenges and promising future directions. These include:
 - **Grounding Domain-General Models:** Developing hybrid explicit-implicit representation learning and task-centric adaptation to align domain-general AI knowledge (e.g., from web data) with the specific, often subtle, physical and interaction dynamics required for robotic manipulation. This involves overcoming semantic-physical misalignment and data-induced physical biases.
 - **Unified Benchmarking:** The call for standardized tasks, datasets, and evaluation metrics is vital for accelerating progress, ensuring fair comparisons, and promoting reproducibility across the research community. This will help prevent fragmented efforts and foster a more cumulative science of robotic learning.
 - **Enhanced Physical Law Awareness:** Integrating a deeper understanding of physics into generative models through techniques like physics-augmented training, physics-constrained generation, and reinforcement learning with physical feedback. This is essential for robust operation in real-world scenarios where physical feasibility and safety are paramount.

In conclusion, this survey signifies that generative AI is not merely a tool but a foundational enabler for the next generation of robotic manipulation systems. Its potential impact lies in creating robots that are not only capable of performing complex tasks but can also learn, adapt, and operate intelligently and safely in the dynamic and unstructured environments of the real world, fundamentally reshaping automation and human-robot interaction.
