# Advancing vision-language models in front-end development via data synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KE Holdings Inc. and Alibaba Group developed a self-reflective data synthesis pipeline to generate large-scale, high-quality multimodal training data for front-end development, and introduced Flame, a Vision-Language Model for React code generation. The Flame models, trained on this synthetic data, achieved a pass@1 score of up to 52.6% on a new React benchmark, significantly outperforming existing state-of-the-art models including GPT-4o (4.9%).

## Problem

- Current Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to generate industry-standard, modular, and dynamic code for modern front-end (FE) frameworks like React.
- Natural language alone is often insufficient or ambiguous for articulating complex UI designs, modular architectures, and state management required for interactive user interfaces.
- A critical bottleneck exists due to the scarcity of high-quality, diverse image-text-code training data that accurately reflects real-world FE development practices.

## Method

- Developed a ==self-reflective agentic workflow== for automated multimodal data generation, specifically tailored to produce image-code-description triplets for React components.
- Introduced three distinct data synthesis strategies—==Evolution-Based==, ==Waterfall-Model-Based==, and ==Additive Development==—to expand the volume and diversity of the training dataset.
- Trained "Flame," a VLM utilizing a ==SigLIP vision encoder== and DeepSeek Coder LLM, on the synthesized data using a three-stage training strategy.

## Results

- Flame models, trained on the synthesized datasets, achieved a pass@1 score of up to **52.6%** on the `Flame-React-Eval` benchmark, outperforming GPT-4o (**4.9%**) and other state-of-the-art VLMs.
- The data synthesis pipeline successfully generated large-scale, diverse multimodal datasets (e.g., 164,860 to 175,485 instances per method), effectively addressing the data scarcity problem for specialized front-end VLMs.
- Structured data synthesis methods like Waterfall-Model-Based and Additive Development produced Flame models with higher pass@k scores (51.6-**52.6%** pass@1) compared to the Evolution-Based method (**43.8%** pass@1).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training Vision-Language Models to interpret image layouts as an intermediate step ("Interpretation Before Coding" or 3-stage training) significantly enhances subsequent code generation performance.
> - Structured data synthesis methods like Waterfall-Model-Based and Additive Development are more effective than purely evolution-based approaches for generating high-quality, coherent training data for complex front-end code.
> - Carefully designed benchmarks, such as `Flame-React-Eval`, are crucial for evaluating the practical utility of generated front-end code, requiring checks for compilation, rendering, and visual similarity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of automatic code generation has experienced "astonishing progress" with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs), as evidenced by commercial successes like GitHub Copilot. However, this paper identifies critical limitations, particularly in the context of front-end (FE) development.

- **Evolution of Code Generation:** Initially, LLMs focused on generating code from natural language instructions. While effective for backend logic or isolated functions, they struggle with the visual and interactive complexities inherent in modern FE development. The paper notes that natural language alone is often insufficient or cumbersome to articulate precise UI designs, modular architectures, state management, and declarative rendering paradigms.
- **Limitations of Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for FE:** The paper critically assesses state-of-the-art VLMs like GPT-4o, Design2Code, and DeclarUI. While these models can generate basic components or static code from design images, they largely fail to produce "industry-standard" code for modern FE frameworks like React. Their outputs often lack modularity, reusability, dynamic behavior, and sophisticated state management—features essential for scalable and interactive user interfaces. Many existing approaches rely on multi-stage pipelines or intermediate representations, which can introduce complexity, reduce efficiency, and limit generalization across diverse UI frameworks.
- **The Data Scarcity Problem:** A fundamental challenge identified in the paper is the scarcity of high-quality, diverse image-text data explicitly linking visual designs to functional, modern FE code. Traditional datasets are either too simplistic, too static, or not aligned with the intricacies of frameworks like React.
- **This Work's Contribution to the Landscape:** This research directly addresses these gaps by proposing a novel, end-to-end approach to FE code generation. Instead of refining existing VLM architectures or relying on limited datasets, the authors tackle the data scarcity problem head-on through a sophisticated **data synthesis pipeline**. By synthesizing large volumes of high-quality, multimodal (image-code-description) training data tailored for React, they aim to train a VLM, named Flame, capable of generating modern, modular, and dynamic FE code directly from design images. This positions the work as a significant step towards bridging the gap between design mockups and executable, production-grade front-end code, thereby advancing the practical utility of VLMs in software engineering. The focus on React, a widely adopted framework, underscores the practical relevance and potential impact on industry practices.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the recognized shortcomings of current large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) in the domain of modern front-end (FE) development.

**Motivation:**

1. **Complexity of Modern FE Development:** Modern FE frameworks like React introduce complexities such as modular architectures, declarative rendering, state management, and component reusability. Describing these multifaceted aspects accurately using natural language alone is time-intensive, prone to ambiguity, and insufficient for conveying intricate UI designs.
2. **Limitations of Current VLMs:** Existing VLMs, despite their advancements in general image-to-text tasks, generate static, monolithic, and non-modular code unsuitable for contemporary FE workflows. They often fail to capture dynamic behaviors or adhere to best practices for scalability and maintainability.
3. **Scarcity of High-Quality Training Data:** A major bottleneck for developing robust VLMs for FE code generation is the lack of large-scale, high-fidelity image-text datasets that accurately reflect the diverse characteristics of real-world FE development, including visual inputs linked to functional, modular code.

**Key Objectives (as outlined in the paper's contributions):**

1. **Develop a Self-Reflective Agentic Workflow for Data Synthesis:** To overcome data scarcity, the primary objective is to create an automated pipeline capable of synthesizing high-quality, large-scale, and diverse image-text training data. This workflow extracts self-contained code snippets from real-world projects, renders their visual outputs, and generates detailed descriptions linking design elements to functional code.
2. **Introduce Novel Data Synthesis Strategies:** Within the workflow, three distinct methods are proposed to expand the dataset's scope and utility:
 - **Evolution-Based Synthesis:** To enable scalable and diverse dataset expansion by generating varied code instances.
 - **Waterfall-Model-Based Synthesis:** To create logically coherent code derived from simulated system requirements, mimicking traditional software development lifecycles.
 - **Additive Development Synthesis:** To iteratively increase the complexity of human-authored components, ensuring realism and practical relevance.
3. **Build a Large Vision-Language Model (Flame) for FE Code Generation:** The project aims to develop "Flame" (Front-end language assistant with multimodal expertise), a VLM specifically designed to translate design concepts (images) directly into executable, framework-compliant front-end code (React in this demonstration).
4. **Explore Optimal Training Practices for FE VLMs:** Through ablation studies, the research seeks to identify effective training strategies, particularly the impact of an intermediate image interpretation stage, for enhancing VLM performance in this domain.
5. **Construct a Benchmark for React Code Generation:** To enable rigorous and unbiased evaluation, a new benchmark dataset, `Flame-React-Eval`, is created. This dataset assesses syntactic precision, functional correctness, and visual consistency of generated React code.
6. **Open Source Resources:** A crucial objective is to publicly release the data synthesis pipeline, synthesized training data, the evaluation dataset, and the Flame models to foster further research and development in the community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work revolves around an innovative **self-reflective agentic workflow for multimodal data generation**, specifically tailored for front-end (FE) React development. This pipeline is designed to overcome the scarcity of high-quality training data, a significant bottleneck for VLMs in this domain.

**A. Automated Multimodal Data Generation Pipeline:**
The pipeline consists of three primary stages, illustrated in Fig. 1:

1. **Self-Contained Code Snippet Generation:**
 - **Data Collection:** Identifies React repositories from GitHub using APIs, filters by keywords, star count (>10), and blacklists boilerplate projects.
 - **Component Identification:** Employs advanced regular expressions and an agent-based validation mechanism to extract React components (hooks, class-based, functional). Components are parsed to remove irrelevant code and ensure pairing with dependencies and styles.
 - **Code Snippet Generation:** Transforms extracted components into fully self-contained units by:
 - **Integrating Dependencies:** Replacing import statements with inline code to embed all necessary dependencies.
 - **Validation and Correction:** Applying rule-based validation and agent-assisted correction for syntactic/functional consistency, fixing formatting, missing references, etc.
 - **Simulating Real-World Inputs:** Using an iterative, agent-driven approach to generate realistic mock data and placeholder functions for props and state, allowing components to operate independently.

2. **Code Data Synthesis (to expand dataset volume and diversity):**
 - **Evolution-Based Synthesis:** Inspired by Evol-Instruct, this method uses LLMs to generate diverse variations of existing code snippets. It operates in two directions:
 - **In-Breadth Evolution:** Expands functionality, modifies architectural structures, and refines visual styles (e.g., dark mode, layout changes, new features like sorting/filtering).
 - **In-Depth Evolution:** Deepens technical complexity (e.g., advanced state management with `useReducer`, performance optimizations, accessibility enhancements).
 - **Waterfall-Model-Based Synthesis:** Adopts a structured, sequential software development model. Starting with an inferred system/application from a code snippet, it progresses through:
 - Requirement Analysis -> UI and Architectural Design -> System Design -> Stepwise Coding.
 - Each stage is executed by an LLM, ensuring logically coherent and interconnected code components, reflecting real-world system complexities.
 - **Additive Development Synthesis:** Focuses on realism by building iteratively upon human-authored code snippets. It follows a streamlined waterfall model, incorporating the given snippet into prompts at each step to develop a stepwise plan that retains the original style and context. This method emphasizes incremental refinement (e.g., enhancing a button with state, accessibility, or API integration).

3. **Rendering:**
 - **Automatic Rendering Pipeline:** Self-contained snippets are integrated into a boilerplate React application within a controlled environment. Automated scripts handle server configuration, dependency installation, and routing to render components and capture visual outputs (screenshots).
 - **Error Handling and Self-reflective Correction:** A reflective agent analyzes logs for server errors, dependency issues, or rendering failures. It suggests refined dependency versions, configurations, or code modifications, and retries the process iteratively, minimizing manual intervention.

4. **Layout Description Generation:**
 - Visual representations (rendered screenshots) are augmented with detailed textual layout descriptions. These descriptions analyze structural hierarchy, spatial alignment, and interactions among UI elements, creating a rich multimodal dataset (component code, styling code, layout description, screenshot).

**B. Modeling and Training (Flame):**

- **Model Architecture:** Flame adopts a mainstream VLM design, comprising:
 - A **vision encoder**: SigLIP SO400M/14@384, chosen for its strong preliminary performance.
 - A **vision-to-text token connector**: Implemented with a 2-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP).
 - A **large language model (LLM)**: DeepSeek Coder models, selected for their exceptional performance among open-source code models.
- **Three-Stage Training Strategy:**
 1. **Connector Warm-up:** The vision-to-text connector is initially warmed up using public datasets (BLIP558K and LLaVA Single-Image3.2M).
 2. **Vision Encoder + Connector Training:** The vision encoder and connector are trained using the synthesized dataset to learn to describe the layout of an input image. The LLM remains frozen here.
 3. **Full Model Instruction-Tuning:** The entire model (vision encoder, connector, LLM) is instruction-tuned on the synthesized dataset to generate code from input images. The AnyRes method is adopted to handle high-resolution images.
- **Training Resources:** All training was performed on 24 NVIDIA H800 GPUs.

**C. Benchmarking and Evaluation:**

- **Benchmark Dataset (`Flame-React-Eval`):** A manually curated dataset of 80 test cases, independently designed, to evaluate React code generation. Each test case includes rendered screenshots, structured layout descriptions, and fully functional React code (with styling). It's designed to capture diverse aspects like component hierarchies, layout, and dynamic behaviors.
- **Evaluation Metric (`pass@k` for FE):** The traditional `pass@k` metric is adapted for FE:
 1. Generated code successfully compiles.
 2. Renders a non-error image.
 3. The rendered image visually matches the reference image. Visual similarity is assessed using cosine similarity (>0.9 threshold) between DINOv2-base embeddings of the rendered and reference images, aligning with human perception.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against state-of-the-art proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5-Flash) and open-source VLMs (InternVL2.5 78B, LLaVA-Qwen2-72B-OV, LLaVA-Qwen2-7B-OV).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings regarding data synthesis, model performance, and training strategies for front-end code generation.

1. **Effectiveness of Data Synthesis:**
 - The proposed data synthesis pipeline successfully generated substantial and diverse datasets: 164,860 instances for Evolution-Based, 175,485 for Waterfall-Model-Based, and 69,458 for Additive-Development. This demonstrates the pipeline's capability to address the data scarcity challenge.

2. **Superior Performance of Flame Models:**
 - **Outperforming SOTA Baselines:** Flame models, specifically trained on the synthesized datasets, significantly outperformed all tested state-of-the-art proprietary (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5-Flash) and open-source (LLaVA-Qwen2, InternVL2.5) vision-language models for React code generation. For example, Flame-Waterfall-7B achieved a pass@1 of 52.6%, whereas GPT-4o-2024-08-06 only managed 4.9%. This highlights Flame's specialized capabilities for modern front-end development.
 - **Impact of Synthesis Methods:** Flame models trained on Waterfall-Model-Based (52.6% pass@1) and Additive-Development (51.6% pass@1) synthetic data achieved higher pass@k scores compared to the Evolution-Based (43.8% pass@1) synthetic data. This suggests that structured data synthesis methods, which align more closely with real-world coding patterns and logical progression, produce more coherent and contextually meaningful training data.
 - **Trade-offs in Synthesis:** While Waterfall-Model-Based synthesis generates larger datasets with minimal reliance on seed data, Additive Development, despite producing fewer data points, can achieve similar performance by iteratively building on human-authored code, showcasing a trade-off between volume and directed complexity.

3. **Insights from Ablation Studies on Training Strategies:**
 - **Benefits of 3-Stage Training:** The 3-Stage training strategy (which includes an intermediate stage where the vision encoder and connector learn to describe image layouts before full model instruction-tuning) consistently outperformed the 2-Stage strategy. This indicates that explicitly training the model to interpret images enhances its overall code generation performance.
 - **"Interpretation Before Coding" (IC) Recipe:** Training with a data mixing recipe where half the tasks require generating an image layout description before code (IC) generally improved Flame models' code generation performance compared to direct "Code Only" (C) generation. This further supports the hypothesis that strengthening image interpretation capabilities boosts VLM performance in this domain.
 - **Fine-tuning Off-the-Shelf Models:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of an existing VLM like LLaVA-Qwen2-7B-OV on the synthesized datasets yielded performance comparable to Flame models. However, Flame, being purpose-built, required significantly less training data (3.3 million vs. approximately 6 million for LLaVA-Qwen2-7B-OV). Interestingly, off-the-shelf models showed less sensitivity to the IC vs. C data mixing strategies, possibly due to their pre-existing visual-instruction-tuning.

4. **Impact of Explicit Interpretation Before Coding:**
 - Models specifically tuned with an "interpretation-first" approach (generating a layout description before code) showed comparable pass@1 scores but significantly higher pass@3 and pass@5 scores (e.g., Flame-Evo-Itp pass@5 of 75.8% vs. Flame-Evo-7B pass@5 of 69.1%). This suggests that prompting for an explicit intermediate interpretation encourages a more structured, step-by-step reasoning process, leading to a more effective exploration of the solution space.
 - This approach particularly improved performance for the Evolution-Based dataset, which initially showed weaker results, implying that interpretation-first can mitigate limitations of lower-quality training data. However, it did not perform well for the smaller Additive-Dev dataset, possibly due to insufficient data for effective learning of image interpretation.

5. **Multi-Image Input Capabilities:**
 - The Waterfall-Model-Based and Additive Development synthesis approaches proved well-suited for creating training data for multi-image input scenarios, which mimic iterative design refinement.
 - A Flame model trained on a 603K-entry multi-image dataset achieved respectable pass@k scores (52.2% pass@1, 61.8% pass@3, 64.8% pass@5), demonstrating the pipeline's applicability to generating updated code from evolving design mockups and previous implementations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of automated front-end (FE) development, specifically for modern, component-based frameworks like React. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Design-Development Gap:** The most immediate impact is the potential to dramatically accelerate the translation of visual design mockups into functional, production-ready FE code. By generating modular, dynamic, and framework-compliant React code directly from images, Flame could significantly reduce the manual effort, time, and potential for error in the traditional UI development workflow. This enhances designer-developer collaboration and allows for faster iteration cycles.

2. **Solving the Data Scarcity Problem for Specialized VLMs:** The core innovation of this paper lies in its comprehensive data synthesis pipeline. By demonstrating highly effective methods (Evolution-Based, Waterfall-Model-Based, Additive Development) for generating vast quantities of high-quality, multimodal image-text-code data, the research provides a scalable solution to a critical bottleneck in VLM development for highly specialized domains like FE. This methodology could inspire similar data generation strategies for other complex code generation tasks where real-world datasets are limited.

3. **Advancing VLM Capabilities for Complex Code Generation:** Flame significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art general-purpose VLMs (including powerful proprietary models like GPT-4o) in the specific task of React FE code generation. This highlights the importance of domain-specific training data and strategies. The findings that an intermediate image interpretation stage and "interpretation before coding" prompt recipes enhance performance suggest new avenues for improving VLM reasoning and code generation quality, analogous to Chain-of-Thought prompting in LLMs.

4. **Promoting Industry-Standard Practices:** Unlike previous VLMs that often generated static or monolithic code, Flame is trained to produce modular, reusable, and dynamic components consistent with React's best practices. This ensures that the generated code is not only functional but also maintainable, scalable, and adaptable to evolving requirements in professional development environments. The multi-image input capability further supports realistic iterative development workflows.

5. **Establishing a New Benchmark:** The introduction of `Flame-React-Eval` provides the first publicly available benchmark for evaluating vision-language models in modern FE development with declarative frameworks like React. This standardized evaluation framework, which considers compilation, rendering, and visual fidelity (via DINOv2-based cosine similarity), is crucial for fostering comparative research and tracking progress in this specialized field.

6. **Fostering Open Science and Community Development:** The commitment to open-sourcing the data synthesis pipeline, synthesized datasets, evaluation benchmark, and Flame models is highly impactful. This move lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers, enabling them to build upon this work, explore new synthesis methods, experiment with different VLM architectures, and expand support to other FE frameworks (e.g., Vue, Angular), thus accelerating innovation across the broader community.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its significant contributions, the paper acknowledges limitations that offer clear avenues for future research:
- **Layout Description Verification:** The current pipeline lacks rigorous verification of layout descriptions, which could be improved for higher data quality.
- **Single-Turn Interaction:** Flame is currently limited to single-turn interactions. Extending it to multi-turn conversations would enhance its utility in real-world, interactive development scenarios.
- **Framework Scope:** The current work focuses solely on React. Expanding the data synthesis and model capabilities to other popular FE frameworks like Vue and Angular would greatly increase its versatility and applicability.

In conclusion, this research marks a substantial leap forward in automated front-end development, demonstrating that with carefully synthesized, domain-specific data and optimized training strategies, VLMs can generate high-quality, industry-relevant code directly from visual designs. The open-source nature of this work positions it as a foundational contribution for future advancements in AI-driven software engineering.
