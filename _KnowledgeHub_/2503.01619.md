# Advancing vision-language models in front-end development via data synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KE Holdings Inc. and Alibaba Group developed a self-reflective data synthesis pipeline to generate large-scale, high-quality multimodal training data for front-end development, and introduced Flame, a Vision-Language Model for React code generation. The Flame models, trained on this synthetic data, achieved a pass@1 score of up to 52.6% on a new React benchmark, significantly outperforming existing state-of-the-art models including GPT-4o (4.9%).

## Problem

- Current Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to generate industry-standard, modular, and dynamic code for modern front-end (FE) frameworks like React.
- Natural language alone is often insufficient or ambiguous for articulating complex UI designs, modular architectures, and state management required for interactive user interfaces.
- A critical bottleneck exists due to the scarcity of high-quality, diverse image-text-code training data that accurately reflects real-world FE development practices.

## Method

- Developed a ==self-reflective agentic workflow== for automated multimodal data generation, specifically tailored to produce image-code-description triplets for React components.
- Introduced three distinct data synthesis strategies—==Evolution-Based==, ==Waterfall-Model-Based==, and ==Additive Development==—to expand the volume and diversity of the training dataset.
- Trained "Flame," a VLM utilizing a ==SigLIP vision encoder== and DeepSeek Coder LLM, on the synthesized data using a three-stage training strategy.

## Results

- Flame models, trained on the synthesized datasets, achieved a pass@1 score of up to **52.6%** on the `Flame-React-Eval` benchmark, outperforming GPT-4o (**4.9%**) and other state-of-the-art VLMs.
- The data synthesis pipeline successfully generated large-scale, diverse multimodal datasets (e.g., 164,860 to 175,485 instances per method), effectively addressing the data scarcity problem for specialized front-end VLMs.
- Structured data synthesis methods like Waterfall-Model-Based and Additive Development produced Flame models with higher pass@k scores (51.6-**52.6%** pass@1) compared to the Evolution-Based method (**43.8%** pass@1).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training Vision-Language Models to interpret image layouts as an intermediate step ("Interpretation Before Coding" or 3-stage training) significantly enhances subsequent code generation performance.
> - Structured data synthesis methods like Waterfall-Model-Based and Additive Development are more effective than purely evolution-based approaches for generating high-quality, coherent training data for complex front-end code.
> - Carefully designed benchmarks, such as `Flame-React-Eval`, are crucial for evaluating the practical utility of generated front-end code, requiring checks for compilation, rendering, and visual similarity.
