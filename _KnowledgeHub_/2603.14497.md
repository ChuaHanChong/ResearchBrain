reason about high-level scene contexts and accurately predict the dynamics of their surrounding environment. Vision- Language Models (VLMs) have recently emerged as promising tools for decision-making and scene understanding, offering strong capabilities in contextual reasoning. However, their limited spatial comprehension constrains their effectiveness as end-to-end driving models. World Models (WM) internalize environmental dynamics to predict future scene evolution. Recently explored as ego-motion predictors and foundation models for autonomous driving, they represent a promising direction for addressing key challenges in the field, particularly enhancing generalization while maintaining dynamic prediction. To leverage the complementary strengths of context-based decision making and prediction, we propose WorldVLM: A hybrid architecture that unifies VLMs and WMs. In our design, the high-level VLM generates behavior commands to guide the driving WM, enabling interpretable and context-aware actions. We evaluate conditioning strategies and provide insights into the hybrid design challenges.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offer strong contextual reasoning but struggle with precise ego-trajectory prediction due to their reliance on 2D image data.
- World Models (WMs) excel at predicting environmental dynamics with physical realism but lack high-level semantic reasoning for robust behavioral planning.
- Autonomous driving systems require both sophisticated contextual understanding and physically consistent trajectory generation, which neither VLMs nor WMs fully address individually.
- WorldVLM introduces a two-stage hybrid architecture where a high-level VLM generates abstract, interpretable behavior commands, including textual justifications and a 2D steering-velocity vector.
- These VLM-generated behavior commands condition an existing World Model (LAW) by injecting the behavior vector into its waypoint and WM transformer decoders.
- The conditioned World Model then forecasts future scene latent features, from which the ego-trajectory is extracted, aligning predictions with the VLM's high-level semantic intent.
- WorldVLM achieved trajectory prediction L2 errors (e.g., 0.31m at 1s, 1.03m at 3s) comparable to the baseline LAW model, with slightly higher long-horizon collision rates (0.48% at 3s).
- Qualitative analysis suggested WorldVLM promotes more cautious behavior, maintaining larger safety margins around vulnerable road users and exhibiting more conservative lane usage.
- Fine-tuning improved the VLM's reasoning quality, showing a 24% increase in BERTScore (0.67) and substantial gains in ROUGE recall (0.47) and BLEU-1 precision (0.36) compared to a zero-shot baseline.
- The integration of VLM-generated explicit justification–action reasoning enhances the interpretability of autonomous driving decisions, providing transparency for critical safety applications.
- Continuous motion vectors derived from VLM outputs are highly effective in conditioning World Models for trajectory prediction, outperforming discrete action signals.
- Concatenating behavior vectors into the World Model's transformer decoder reduces collision rates and improves reconstruction quality by preventing behavior-agnostic future predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems require robust models capable of high-level scene understanding, accurate prediction of environmental dynamics, and effective decision-making in complex and dynamic urban environments. The current research landscape in autonomous driving has seen the emergence of two distinct yet promising paradigms: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and World Models (WMs).

Vision-Language Models leverage their training on internet-scale multimodal datasets to provide strong contextual reasoning, natural language proficiency, and inherent explainability, making them suitable for high-level decision-making and human interaction. However, existing VLM-based approaches typically rely on 2D image data, which fundamentally limits their spatial reasoning capabilities, making them less reliable for precise ego-trajectory prediction due to challenges like causal confusion and covariate shift. Efforts to mitigate these limitations include incorporating multi-modal sensor data and Bird's Eye View (BEV) representations.

Conversely, World Models excel at learning and internalizing environmental dynamics to generate realistic future scenarios and predict scene evolution. They are increasingly explored as efficient simulators or as foundation models for autonomous driving, capable of generating physically consistent future frames or latent-space dynamics that support ego-motion prediction. Models like LAW prioritize spatio-temporal consistency and realistic dynamics. While WMs offer physical reliability and improved generalization, they generally lack the intrinsic reasoning and high-level semantic decision-making capabilities that are crucial for navigating ambiguous or safety-critical situations.

This work addresses a recognized gap in the autonomous driving research landscape by proposing a hybrid architecture that integrates the complementary strengths of VLMs and WMs. It aims to combine the contextual reasoning and interpretable decision-making of VLMs with the physically grounded trajectory prediction capabilities of WMs. Similar integrations have been explored in robotics for task decomposition, but this paper specifically focuses on their combined application for ego-trajectory planning in the open-world dynamics of autonomous driving. By doing so, WorldVLM seeks to enhance robustness, safety, and explainability in autonomous driving policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind WorldVLM is to address the inherent limitations of both Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and World Models (WMs) when applied individually to autonomous driving. VLMs demonstrate strong capabilities in high-level contextual reasoning and decision-making, offering potential for explainability and human interaction through natural language outputs. However, their reliance on 2D image pre-training restricts their spatial understanding and accuracy in precise ego-trajectory prediction, leading to potential issues such as causal confusion and covariate shift. Conversely, World Models excel at learning complex environmental dynamics and predicting future scene evolution with physical realism, which is critical for generating accurate trajectories. However, WMs inherently lack the high-level semantic reasoning and decision-making abilities necessary for robust behavioral planning in diverse and complex driving scenarios.

To overcome these individual shortcomings, the key objectives of the WorldVLM project are:

1. **Develop a Hybrid Architecture:** To design and implement a conceptual framework, WorldVLM, that unifies a high-level VLM with a trajectory-predictive WM. The aim is for the VLM to generate interpretable behavior commands that guide the WM, thereby integrating context-aware decision-making with physically reliable trajectory forecasting.
2. **Enable Semantically Guided Trajectory Generation:** To demonstrate that VLM-generated behavioral commands can effectively condition a WM to produce ego-trajectories that are not only dynamically sound but also aligned with high-level semantic intent, leading to safer and more contextually appropriate actions.
3. **Enhance Interpretability:** To allow the VLM to generate explicit justification–action reasoning traces alongside behavior commands. This provides transparency into the model’s intentions and decision-making process, which is critical for building trustworthy autonomous systems.
4. **Investigate Conditioning Strategies:** To evaluate different strategies for effectively transferring high-level behavioral guidance from the VLM to the latent-space WM, providing insights into the challenges and optimal practices for such hybrid designs.
5. **Contribute to Research Infrastructure:** To extend the nuScenes dataset with a novel justification–action annotation schema and release the model checkpoints and source code. This aims to promote reproducibility and facilitate further research in language-guided planning for autonomous driving.

In essence, the motivation is to leverage the complementary strengths of reasoning (VLM) and prediction (WM) to create an autonomous driving system that is both intelligent in its decision-making and reliable in its execution, addressing the critical needs for safety, robustness, and explainability in real-world driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WorldVLM framework is a modular, two-stage architecture designed to combine high-level vision-language reasoning with low-level world model trajectory prediction.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

WorldVLM operates by first using a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to analyze a front-view image of the current scene and generate abstract, interpretable behavior commands along with a textual justification. These behavior commands then condition an off-the-shelf World Model (WM) that takes visual latent features from surrounding images to predict future scene latent features, from which the ego-trajectory is extracted.

#### 3.2. Behavior-Planning VLM

The VLM serves as the high-level behavior planner.
- **Input:** It receives a single front-view image of the current timestep, textual navigation instructions (e.g., "turn left," "go straight") from nuScenes, and the ego-vehicle's current speed. These inputs are fed to the VLM following a prompt that describes the task and the desired output schema.
- **Output Schema:** The VLM is prompted to generate a structured JSON output with three fields:
 1. **Justification:** A free-text explanation of *why* a particular action is safe and appropriate.
 2. **Action:** A free-text natural language description of *what* the ego-vehicle should do next.
 3. **Action Token:** A selection from predefined lateral actions ({Left Turn, Right Turn, Straight, Follow Lane, Lanechange to Left, Lanechange to Right}) paired with longitudinal actions ({Stop, Accelerate, Slow Down, Maintain}).
- **Behavior Command Generation:** The VLM's generated text output is followed by a structured behavior vector. This 2D steering-velocity vector is derived by a three-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) behavior head, with ReLU activations and dropout (0.5), which maps aggregated final-layer language model hidden states to the control output. The hidden states used can be either from a fixed subset of generated output tokens (e.g., the first 16 positions) or from dedicated special behavior tokens appended to the VLM output sequence.
- **Behavior Target Derivation:** The ground-truth behavior target (normalized velocity `v` and normalized steering angle `α`) is computed from the first and last points of the 3-second future trajectory. The velocity is derived from the total displacement length, normalized by a constant (30). The steering angle is obtained from the `atan2` of the displacement components, with a shift to align the forward direction with `α ≈ 0`.
- **Loss Function:** The VLM is trained using a combined objective:
 - `ℓ_behavior`: Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted and ground-truth normalized steering angle and velocity.
 - `ℓ_text`: Standard token-level negative log-likelihood (cross-entropy) for next-token prediction, guiding the generation of justification and action text.
 - The total loss is `ℓ = ℓ_behavior + ℓ_text`.

#### 3.3. Trajectory-Predicting World Model

The World Model component leverages LAW, an existing latent action-aware predictive model, for forecasting ego-centric waypoints and future visual features from multi-view image representations.
- **LAW Components:** LAW typically extracts spatial view features via a transformer-based spatial decoder, predicts waypoints using a waypoint transformer decoder on learned queries, and forecasts next-step latent features using a WM transformer decoder.
- **Behavior Conditioning:** The VLM's generated behavior vector (zero-padded to a fixed embedding size of 8) is injected into LAW at two points (as shown in Figure 3):
 1. It is concatenated with the learned waypoint queries and the spatial view features *before* the waypoint transformer decoder, guiding the waypoint prediction.
 2. The predicted waypoints are then flattened and concatenated with the spatial features and the behavior vector *before* being processed by an action-aware MLP and the WM transformer decoder, influencing the prediction of future latent features. This ensures that the WM's predictions align with the VLM's reasoned behavioral intent.
- **Trajectory Extraction:** The ego-trajectory is then extracted directly from these conditioned latent predictions.

#### 3.4. Dataset and Training

- **Dataset:** The nuScenes dataset is extended with custom justification–action annotations. These are generated by combining scene-level instructions from doScenes and frame-level VQA data from DriveLM. Frame-level prompts from DriveLM are fed to GPT-OSS-120B to enforce JSON output with justification, action, and action-enum fields, creating a reasoning format for explainable driving. The ground truth annotations are generated for the nuScenes train split, split 80% for training and 20% for validation.
- **Training Stages:** The overall framework is trained in two stages:
 1. **VLM Training:** The VLM is fine-tuned to generate reasoning and structured behavioral instructions using LLaVA-Qwen1.5-0.5B and LLaVA-Qwen2-1.5B models. Training is performed for four epochs with a batch size of 3 across two GPUs, using a learning rate initialized at 1e-6 with 100 warm-up steps and 0.06 weight decay.
 2. **WM Training:** The LAW World Model is then trained with the VLM's outputs (behavior commands) as conditioning. The published vision encoder checkpoint of LAW is leveraged for resource-efficient decoder training. Training uses 4 NVIDIA A40 GPUs, assuming the configurations described in the original LAW paper.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

- **Trajectory Prediction:** Open-loop evaluation uses nuScenes evaluation metrics: L2 error in meters (for 1s, 2s, 3s horizons) and collision rate in percentage.
- **Reasoning Quality:** The quality of the VLM's justification and action outputs is evaluated using standard NLP metrics:
 - **BLEU:** Measures n-gram precision against ground truth (BLEU-1, BLEU-2, BLEU-3).
 - **ROUGE:** Complements BLEU with recall (ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L).
 - **BERTScore:** Assesses semantic similarity by comparing token embeddings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to demonstrate the feasibility and benefits of the WorldVLM conceptual framework, using accessible, off-the-shelf models for its VLM and WM components.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Reasoning:** The fine-tuned VLM (LLaVA-Qwen1.5) was observed to generate contextually appropriate justifications and actions (Figure 5). Examples include correctly identifying pedestrians at an intersection and justifying a stop, and proposing a safe steering maneuver around a construction vehicle. The authors note that the explicit observation-action linkage allows tracing the visual evidence for decisions, even when misclassifications (e.g., traffic light color) lead to partially incorrect justifications.
- **Open-Loop Trajectories:** Qualitative comparisons between WorldVLM and the unconditioned LAW baseline (Figure 6) suggest that WorldVLM exhibits more cautious behavior in interactive scenarios. This includes maintaining larger safety margins around vulnerable road users (VRUs) and other obstacles, yielding appropriately, and utilizing available lanes more conservatively.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Combined Framework (Table I):** When conditioned with VLM-generated motion vectors, WorldVLM's trajectory prediction accuracy (L2 error) was comparable to the baseline LAW model and a zero-padded variant across 1s, 2s, and 3s horizons (e.g., 0.31m, 0.62m, 1.03m for WorldVLM vs. 0.31m, 0.61m, 1.02m for baseline LAW at 1s, 2s, 3s respectively). However, WorldVLM showed slightly higher long-horizon collision rates (e.g., 0.48% at 3s vs. 0.44% for baseline). The authors interpret this as WorldVLM promoting risk-aware, conservative behavior in interactive situations, which may trade off with aggregate collision metrics. The zero-padded "No Behavior" variant also increased collision rates compared to baseline, indicating that the VLM conditioning, while slightly increasing long-horizon collisions, mitigated some of the negative effects of the zero-padding.
- **Reasoning Quality (Table II):** Fine-tuning significantly improved the VLM's ability to generate relevant justifications and actions. Compared to a zero-shot baseline, fine-tuned Qwen1.5-0.5B showed a 24% improvement in BERTScore (0.67 vs. 0.54), indicating stronger semantic alignment. It also substantially improved ROUGE recall (0.47 vs. 0.09) and BLEU precision (0.36 for BLEU-1 vs. 0.04), demonstrating better word choice and phrase matching. The specific method of using the first 16 tokens or 5 specialized behavior tokens for the behavior head showed no significant difference in reasoning metrics. The larger Qwen2-1.5B model surprisingly underperformed the smaller Qwen1.5-0.5B, which the authors attributed to potential under-optimization during training.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Conditioning Architecture (Table III):** Ablations using ground-truth angle and speed Motion Vectors for WM prediction showed that concatenating the behavior vector into the WM Transformer Decoder reduced collision rates. While L2 error remained similar (improving baseline by 73% at the 3s horizon compared to baseline LAW with ground-truth motion vectors), this indicates that behavior concatenation is effective in helping to disambiguate commands and avoid behavior-agnostic future predictions, thus enhancing reconstruction quality.
- **Conditioning Types (Table IV):** Different conditioning signals were evaluated using ground truth data on a smaller split:
 - **Motion Vector:** Continuous motion vectors (angle and speed) without navigation instructions achieved the best L2 error (0.28m at 3s) and lowest collision rates (0.10% at 3s). Ablating to angle-only conditioning harmed performance, while speed-only retained most benefits. This suggests that precise, low-noise continuous signals directly describing the target waypoint are most effective.
 - **Discrete Actions/Goals:** Discrete ego-motion binning (e.g., goal-speed bins) yielded lower L2 errors than acceleration commands, and goal signals were better at preventing long-horizon crashes, while action signals reduced short-horizon collisions.
 - **Navigation Instructions:** For reasoning-derived discrete actions, adding navigation instructions slightly improved L2 and significantly reduced collisions, indicating benefits from added semantic context for coarse labels. However, for fine-grained motion vectors, navigation instructions could introduce noise.
 - Overall, goal-oriented signals were found to excel in L2 error and long-horizon collision avoidance, while action commands better reduced short-horizon crashes. Fine-grained commands boosted open-loop performance with accurate inputs.
- **Action Token Representation (Table V):** Evaluating different VLM token representations for input to the behavior head, it was found that using the "First 16" tokens of the VLM's output sequence yielded the lowest Mean Absolute Error for predicting both steering angle (0.0135) and speed (0.1788). Dedicated behavior tokens showed good performance but were slightly higher. Using last-layer tokens or too few (e.g., First 8) tokens degraded performance. The VLM model size (0.5B vs. 1.5B) did not significantly impact these results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WorldVLM framework presents a notable contribution to autonomous driving research by proposing a hybrid architecture that integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with World Models (WMs). This approach aims to leverage the complementary strengths of high-level semantic reasoning and low-level dynamic prediction, addressing key challenges in the field.

**Key Significance:**

- **Feasibility of Hybrid Architectures:** The work demonstrates the conceptual feasibility of combining VLMs and WMs for autonomous driving, specifically by using VLM-derived high-level behavioral commands to condition a trajectory-predictive WM. This establishes a precedent for integrating different AI paradigms to tackle complex tasks.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** A core contribution is the VLM's ability to generate explicit justification–action reasoning traces. This feature offers transparency into the model's decision-making process, which is critical for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. Understanding "why" a vehicle acts in a certain way can facilitate debugging, build trust, and enable human oversight, potentially making autonomous systems more acceptable to the public and regulators.
- **Context-Aware and Safer Trajectories (Qualitative):** While quantitative metrics show L2 parity with a baseline and slightly increased long-horizon collision rates in the combined framework, qualitative analysis suggests that WorldVLM promotes more cautious and interaction-aware behaviors, particularly around vulnerable road users. This indicates a potential for improved safety in complex, interactive scenarios where explicit reasoning can guide conservative planning.
- **Design Guidelines for Multimodal Trajectory Prediction:** The comprehensive ablation studies provide valuable insights into effective strategies for conditioning WMs with VLM outputs. Findings regarding the superiority of continuous motion vectors, the benefits of specific conditioning types, and optimal token representations offer practical guidance for future research and development of similar hybrid architectures.
- **Research Infrastructure:** The release of the extended nuScenes dataset with justification–action annotations, along with model checkpoints and source code, contributes significantly to the research community. This resource can foster further exploration and benchmarking of language-guided planning and explainable AI in autonomous driving.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Towards More Robust Systems:** By combining semantic understanding with physical prediction, WorldVLM could contribute to developing autonomous driving systems that are more robust to novel or ambiguous scenarios, where a VLM's general world knowledge can compensate for the WM's purely dynamic understanding.
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** The explicit reasoning capabilities could pave the way for more intuitive and effective human-AI interaction in autonomous vehicles, allowing passengers or remote operators to understand and potentially influence the vehicle's behavior through natural language.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as single-frame processing, narrow field of view, and the need for closed-loop evaluation and human-annotated reasoning traces, lay out clear directions for future work. The proposed strategy of integrating the VLM more deeply into the WM's latent representations and decoupling inference rates could lead to more performant and efficient systems.
- **Advancement in Generative AI for Robotics:** Beyond autonomous driving, the principles demonstrated by WorldVLM could be applicable to other robotic systems requiring both high-level task planning and fine-grained motor control, especially in environments demanding both interpretability and physical realism.

In summary, WorldVLM represents a step towards creating autonomous driving systems that are not only capable of executing complex maneuvers but also of reasoning about them in a human-interpretable manner, thereby advancing the field towards more trustworthy and reliable intelligent agents.
