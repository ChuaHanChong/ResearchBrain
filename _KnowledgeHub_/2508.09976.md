# Masquerade: Learning from In-the-wild Human Videos using Data-Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University introduce Masquerade, a framework that processes large-scale, uncurated human videos to create "robotized" demonstrations. This method explicitly closes the visual embodiment gap and employs a co-training strategy, resulting in up to 5-6 times higher zero-shot generalization success rates on novel environments for complex bimanual robot tasks compared to previous baselines.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation research faces a critical data scarcity issue, limiting the training of robust and generalizable policies.
- Abundant 'in-the-wild' human videos offer rich manipulation examples but are difficult to use due to the visual embodiment gap between humans and robots, and the absence of precise robot-action labels.
- Prior methods struggle to fully leverage the vast scale of uncurated human video data due to these explicit visual and labeling discrepancies.

## Method

- A data-editing pipeline transforms raw human videos into "robotized" demonstrations by estimating human hand poses, inpainting human body parts, and overlaying a virtual bimanual robot tracking the inferred actions.
- A vision encoder, pre-trained on these visually-aligned "robotized" human videos, is subsequently co-trained with a diffusion-based policy head on a small set of real robot demonstrations.
- During co-training, an auxiliary 2D keypoint regression loss on edited human videos is continuously optimized alongside the imitation learning loss on real robot data to maintain human knowledge and facilitate robustness.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **74%** across three bimanual kitchen tasks in out-of-distribution scenes, demonstrating a 5-6 times improvement (62 percentage points higher) over state-of-the-art baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the indispensable role of both the robot overlay in data-editing and the continuous co-training strategy, with their removal causing substantial performance drops.
- Performance on the Stack Pots task monotonically improved with increasing amounts of edited human video data (e.g., from **2%** to **68%** when scaling to **100%** of available data), indicating effective data scaling benefits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly closing the visual embodiment gap through data-editing, even with imperfect overlays, is crucial for effectively transferring knowledge from human videos to robot policies.
> - A continuous co-training strategy that leverages both edited human videos and real robot demonstrations is essential for the vision encoder to retain diverse representations and achieve strong out-of-distribution generalization.
> - In-the-wild human videos, when coupled with a robust data-editing and co-training methodology, can serve as a scalable and effective solution to the data scarcity problem in robot manipulation.
