# Safe Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation via Combined Model and Learning-based Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories (MERL) developed a hybrid control framework for legged mobile manipulators, combining a model-based admittance controller with a reinforcement learning policy for whole-body loco-manipulation. The system achieves safe and compliant interaction by explicitly tracking 6-DoF forces with F/T sensors and utilizing a Reference Governor for safety guarantees, demonstrating end-effector velocity tracking MSEs below 0.005 m²/s² for linear and 0.029 rad²/s² for angular motion during human-robot collaboration.

## Problem

- Existing loco-manipulation methods often decouple locomotion and manipulation, leading to a lack of coordinated dynamics and suboptimal performance.
- Learning-based whole-body control approaches typically lack formal safety guarantees, crucial for human-robot interaction and operation in cluttered environments.
- Accurate base velocity estimation in legged robots is challenging due to inherent oscillatory noise, impacting controller performance and stability.

## Method

- A combined model-based admittance controller manages the manipulator arm for compliant 6-DoF force tracking using F/T sensors, integrating with a Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy for base locomotion.
- A neural network-enhanced Kalman filter improves the robustness and accuracy of base linear and angular velocity estimation, crucial for stable control.
- A Reference Governor (RG) provides formal safety guarantees for the arm by preventing constraint violations (position, velocity, wrench, kinematics) through online reference modification based on pre-computed admissible sets.

## Results

- The robot successfully performed force-tracking loco-manipulation, responding compliantly to external wrenches during a human-robot collaboration task.
- End-effector velocity tracking achieved a Mean Squared Error (MSE) of ≤0.005 m2/s2≤ 0.005 \, m^2/s^2≤0.005m2/s2 for linear components and ≤0.029 rad2/s2≤ 0.029 \, rad^2/s^2≤0.029rad2/s2 for angular components.
- The Reference Governor effectively enforced safety, preventing constraint violations and maintaining recursive feasibility within defined position, velocity, and kinematic limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating model-based control for arm precision and safety with learning-based control for base adaptability can achieve tightly coordinated and robust whole-body loco-manipulation.
> - Explicitly regulating 6-DoF contact forces using F/T sensors enables compliant human-robot interaction and precise manipulation in dynamic environments.
> - Enhancing state estimation for legged robots with a neural network-augmented Kalman filter significantly improves overall system stability and robustness by mitigating the impact of oscillatory noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged loco-manipulation focuses on enabling robots with legs and manipulator arms to interact with their environment beyond the constraints of a fixed base. This capability expands the operational range and task repertoire of robots, encompassing applications from navigating complex terrain to human-robot collaboration. Current research in this domain broadly categorizes approaches into several groups:

- **Decoupled Control:** Many earlier or simplified systems treat locomotion and manipulation as separate modules. These methods often utilize existing robot APIs for base locomotion while focusing on arm control, leading to a lack of coordinated dynamics and potentially suboptimal performance.
- **Model-Based Whole-Body Control:** These approaches explicitly consider the coupled dynamics of the entire robot. They often involve solving large-scale optimization problems, such as inverse dynamics or Model Predictive Control (MPC), to achieve stable motion and adhere to reference trajectories. While offering formal guarantees and accuracy, these methods can be computationally intensive, often requiring simplifications of robot dynamics which may limit performance in complex, real-world scenarios.
- **Learning-Based Whole-Body Control:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) has gained traction for its ability to manage complex dynamics and generate agile behaviors in contact-rich tasks. These methods can range from hierarchical policies (training separate policies for legs and arms) to unified policies (training a single policy for the entire robot). While learning-based approaches can be highly adaptable and generalizable, they typically lack formal safety guarantees, require extensive data, and often handle contact forces implicitly rather than explicitly. The unpredictable nature of RL outputs presents challenges for safety quantification.
- **Combined Model and Learning-Based Control:** To leverage the strengths of both paradigms, hybrid approaches integrate model-based components for aspects requiring precision and safety (e.g., manipulation) with learning-based components for adaptability and complex dynamics (e.g., locomotion). A common challenge in these hybrid methods is achieving tight coordination between the modular controllers, as independent operation can lead to error propagation and non-smooth behavior.
- **Force-Based Control for Loco-Manipulation:** While many loco-manipulation works prioritize tracking end-effector poses or base velocities, the explicit regulation of contact forces remains an active area. Force control is critical for compliant behavior, enabling kinesthetic teaching, safe human-robot interaction, preventing damage, and optimizing body posture for improved force manipulability. Existing force-centric methods often rely on impedance control without direct Force/Torque (F/T) sensor measurements, estimating forces using simpler models (e.g., spring-damper), or focus on specific downstream tasks without providing comprehensive safety guarantees. Simulators often struggle to accurately model complex contact forces, underscoring the importance of real-world F/T sensor data.

This work addresses the existing gaps by proposing a combined model and learning-based approach that focuses on explicit force tracking using F/T sensors, integrating formal safety guarantees for the manipulator arm, and improving system robustness through enhanced state estimation, aiming for a tightly coordinated whole-body control scheme.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a whole-body controller that enables safe and compliant loco-manipulation for legged robots with manipulator arms, specifically in scenarios involving physical interaction.

The detailed objectives are:

- **Achieve Coordinated Loco-Manipulation:** To enable simultaneous control of both the legged base and the manipulator arm, ensuring they work in concert to achieve desired tasks.
- **Implement Compliant Behavior via Explicit Force Tracking:** To allow the robot to react compliantly to external wrenches applied at the end-effector by explicitly tracking forces and torques using Force/Torque (F/T) sensors mounted on the wrist.
- **Guarantee Safety for the Manipulator Arm:** To incorporate formal safety guarantees into the controller design for the arm, particularly crucial for human-robot collaboration and contact-rich environments.
- **Enhance Robustness through Accurate State Estimation:** To improve the stability and accuracy of the combined controller by developing a robust method for estimating the robot's base linear and angular velocities, which are essential inputs for the control system.
- **Validate the Approach in Simulation and Hardware:** To empirically demonstrate the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed methods on a physical legged robot platform.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges and limitations observed in the current state of loco-manipulation research:

- **Complexity of Whole-Body Systems:** Modeling and controlling legged robots with arms is inherently complex. Existing model-based approaches often incur high computational costs or rely on simplified dynamics, while learning-based methods demand significant data and often lack interpretability or safety assurances.
- **Limitations of Decoupled Control:** Many previous methods decouple locomotion and manipulation, which limits their ability to coordinate actions and fully leverage the robot's capabilities in dynamic or interactive tasks.
- **Implicit vs. Explicit Force Control:** Most learning-based methods manage contact forces implicitly, which is insufficient for tasks requiring precise interaction, such as kinesthetic teaching or co-manipulation with humans. The absence of explicit force regulation can lead to unsafe interactions or damage.
- **Absence of Formal Safety Guarantees:** A significant limitation of purely learning-based approaches is the lack of formal safety guarantees. For applications involving human interaction or operation in cluttered environments, predictable and safe behavior is paramount.
- **Challenges in State Estimation:** Legged locomotion introduces oscillatory noise, which can significantly degrade the accuracy of base velocity estimation. Inaccurate state estimates directly impact controller performance and stability, particularly for model-based control components.
- **Importance of F/T Sensors:** Many approaches estimate contact forces rather than directly measuring them. The paper argues that direct F/T sensing is critical for accuracy and safety, as simulators struggle to model complex real-world contact dynamics.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to advance the capabilities of legged mobile manipulators, making them more suitable for collaborative, contact-rich tasks in diverse environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates a model-based admittance controller for the manipulator arm with a Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy for legged locomotion, augmented by a neural network-enhanced Kalman filter for state estimation and a Reference Governor (RG) for safety guarantees.

**A. Whole-Body Admittance Control (for the Arm)**

The manipulator arm is treated as a fast, low-inertia actuator for rapid local corrections. An admittance controller is used to map external wrenches, sensed by a wrist-mounted 6-DoF Force/Torque (F/T) sensor, into desired end-effector linear ($\mathbf{v}^w_{ee}$) and angular ($\boldsymbol{\omega}^w_{ee}$) velocities.
1. **Desired Velocity Calculation:** Equation (1) defines the relationship between an external wrench ($\mathbf{W}$), a reference wrench ($\mathbf{W}'$), desired and current end-effector poses ($\mathbf{x}'^w_{ee}$, $\mathbf{x}^w_{ee}$), and the desired end-effector velocities, using stiffness ($\mathbf{K}$) and damping ($\mathbf{D}$) coefficients. This formulation allows for compliant behavior.
2. **F/T Sensor Integration:** Sensor data from the wrist-mounted F/T sensor are transformed into the world frame and combined with a gravitational wrench ($\mathbf{W}_G$) to compute the input for the admittance controller (Equation 2).
3. **Kinematic Conversion:** The desired end-effector velocities are expressed in the world frame. To derive the required arm joint velocities, these are decomposed into contributions from the robot base velocity ($\mathbf{v}^w_b$, $\boldsymbol{\omega}^w_b$) and the arm's relative velocity with respect to the base ($\mathbf{v}^b_{ee}$, $\boldsymbol{\omega}^b_{ee}$) (Equation 3).
4. **Joint Velocity Command:** The relative end-effector velocities are then mapped to arm joint velocities ($\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{arm}$) using the damped pseudo-inverse of the arm's Jacobian matrix ($\mathbf{J}^\#$) (Equations 4, 5).

**B. Reinforcement Learning Policy for Base Motion**

The robot base acts as a slower, high-inertia body, responsible for repositioning to sustain motion and extend the workspace. An RL policy, adapted from the MuJoCo Playground PPO setup, controls the robot's leg joint positions.
1. **Objective:** The primary objective of the RL policy is to track the desired end-effector velocity ($\mathbf{v}^w_{ee}$, $\boldsymbol{\omega}^w_{ee}$) output by the model-based admittance controller.
2. **Observation Space:** The policy's observations include gravity in the body frame, leg joint positions and velocities, linear and angular velocity of the base, the previous action, and crucially, the desired end-effector velocity from the admittance controller.
3. **Action Space:** The policy outputs scaled leg joint position commands.
4. **Reward Function:** A weighted sum of reward terms guides the learning process:
 - **Velocity Tracking:** Rewards for minimizing errors in tracking desired end-effector linear and angular velocities.
 - **Energy Saving:** Penalties for high joint torques, action rates, and overall energy consumption.
 - **Behavior Shaping:** Rewards/penalties for maintaining proper pose, preventing standstill when motion is desired, controlling vertical and angular base velocities, promoting appropriate foot airtime, swing clearance, swing height, and penalizing foot slip.
5. **Sim-to-Real Robustness:** During RL training, the ground-truth base velocities from the simulator are replaced with noisy estimates. This noise is derived from the state covariance matrix ($\mathbf{P}_t$) of the Neural Kalman Filter (described below), ensuring the policy learns robustness to realistic state estimation uncertainties encountered in hardware.

**C. Velocity Estimation and Uncertainty (Neural Kalman Filter)**

To address the challenges of base velocity estimation due to inherent oscillatory noise in legged locomotion, a Kalman filter enhanced with a neural network is employed.
1. **System Dynamics:** The Kalman filter's prediction step incorporates a system dynamics model `Dyn(.)` (Equation 18) that accounts for angular velocity, ground reaction forces, and gravity.
2. **Neural Network for Error Correction:** A simple two-layer feedforward neural network (`NN_theta`) is trained offline using real hardware motion capture data to estimate the difference ($\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t$) between the dynamic model's prediction and the ground truth. This `$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t$` is treated as process noise covariance ($\mathbf{Q}_t = \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t^\top$).
3. **Enhanced Prediction:** The network's output is added to the model's state prediction, refining `$\hat{\mathbf{x}}$` (Equation 10) and updating the state covariance matrix (`$\mathbf{P}_{t+1|t}$`) (Equation 12).
4. **Measurement Model:** The update step of the Kalman filter uses IMU measurements for angular velocity and estimates linear velocity from the feet in contact with the ground (Equation 19).
5. **Noise Injection:** The state covariance matrix `$\mathbf{P}_t$` from the Kalman filter is utilized to inject realistic zero-mean Gaussian noise into the RL observations during training, bridging the sim-to-real gap.
6. **Smoothness:** A low-pass filter is applied to the Kalman filter's output for improved smoothness (Equation 21).

**D. Safety Guarantees using Reference Governor (RG)**

A Reference Governor (RG) is integrated to provide formal safety guarantees for the arm's admittance controller by preventing constraint violations.
1. **Maximal Output Admissible Set (MOAS):** The RG operates by asymptotically adjusting the reference input based on a pre-computed Maximal Output Admissible Set (`$\mathcal{O}_\infty$`) (Equation 22). This set defines all initial states and reference inputs for which the system can operate indefinitely without violating user-defined constraints on position, velocity, wrench, and kinematics.
2. **Offline Computation:** The `$\mathcal{O}_\infty$` is computed offline through an iterative process that simulates system trajectories for various combinations of initial states and reference inputs. Any trajectory that violates constraints is discarded. The MOAS is computed independently for relevant axes and dimensions (e.g., x, y, z for position, velocity, force, and gripper normal axis for torque).
3. **Online Feasibility Checking:** For real-time deployment, discrete samples of `$\mathcal{O}_\infty$` are stored in a KD-Tree, allowing for rapid queries. At each time step, the RG checks if the current state and desired reference (`$\mathbf{o}_q$`) are within `$\mathcal{O}_\infty$` (Equation 23).
4. **Reference Modification:** If `$\mathbf{o}_q$` is not in `$\mathcal{O}_\infty$` or is deemed infeasible, the RG modifies the desired reference to the closest admissible state (`$\mathbf{o}^*$`) within the set, ensuring that the system is steered back into a safe operating region.

**E. Hardware Implementation**

The framework is validated on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot equipped with a D1 6-DoF servo arm and a BOTA F/T sensor at the wrist (Figure 6). The RL locomotion controller runs at 50 Hz, the arm admittance controller at 40 Hz, and the state estimator at 500 Hz. The RG's online component involves fast queries to the pre-computed MOAS.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of the proposed framework involved both simulation and hardware experiments using the Unitree Go2 quadruped robot. The primary demonstration involved a human-robot collaboration task where the robot and a human jointly moved a wooden plank, requiring compliant behavior and coordinated loco-manipulation.

- **Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation Performance:** The robot successfully demonstrated force-tracking loco-manipulation. It responded to push/pull wrenches applied by the human on the end-effector by generating appropriate linear and angular velocities, allowing it to follow the human's motion while carrying a load. This was achieved by setting the reference wrench in the Reference Governor (RG) input state to zero and applying orientation constraints, but no position reference for the end-effector's x, y, and z axes.

- **End-Effector Velocity Tracking:**
 - The end-effector exhibited accurate tracking of commanded linear and angular velocities, which were derived from the admittance controller's interpretation of interaction forces.
 - Mean Squared Error (MSE) values for linear components were reported as $\leq 0.005 \, \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$, and for angular components as $\leq 0.029 \, \text{rad}^2/\text{s}^2$ across all x, y, and z axes. These values indicate a reasonable level of tracking accuracy for a dynamic, interactive task.
 - Analysis of velocity tracking plots (Figure 4) revealed larger errors and delays in angular velocity tracking, particularly for the z-axis between 15.0 s and 20.0 s, and for x and y angular velocities. These discrepancies are attributed to potential factors such as actuator and sensor errors, external disturbances, and the inherent difficulty of simultaneously tracking all 6 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) and the associated trade-offs in admittance controller gain tuning.

- **Safety Guarantees via Reference Governor:**
 - The Reference Governor (RG) effectively enforced safety and constraint satisfaction. An example trajectory (Figure 5) illustrated that the RG prevented the controller from overshooting position constraint limits, especially when the reference position changed rapidly (e.g., at 5.0 s and 7.5 s).
 - The RG successfully modified the desired reference, ensuring that the trajectory remained within the Maximal Output Admissible Set (MOAS) volume. This demonstrated recursive feasibility and compliance with position, velocity, and kinematic constraints throughout the task.
 - The controller also demonstrated the ability to reduce force disturbances back to zero, correcting for them through appropriate velocity commands.

- **Velocity Estimation and Uncertainty (Neural Kalman Filter):**
 - The Neural Kalman Filter provided improved estimates of the robot's base linear and angular velocities.
 - Quantitative metrics (Table II) indicated relatively low estimation errors compared to motion capture ground truth. Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for angular velocities ranged from 0.0009 rad/s ($\omega_z$) to 0.0031 rad/s ($\omega_x$), and for linear velocities from 0.0202 m/s ($v_z$) to 0.0385 m/s ($v_x$).
 - The improved state estimation is stated to contribute to the stability and accuracy of the overall force controller.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for whole-body loco-manipulation that integrates model-based control principles with learning-based adaptability, addressing several critical challenges in robotics. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood in several key areas:

- **Enhanced Safety in Human-Robot Interaction:** The integration of a Reference Governor (RG) for the manipulator arm provides formal safety guarantees. This is a substantial contribution, especially for compliant tasks where robots directly interact with humans or delicate environments. The ability to mathematically ensure that the robot's actions remain within predefined safety boundaries, even under external disturbances or rapid reference changes, is critical for real-world deployment in collaborative settings.
- **Improved Compliance and Adaptability:** By explicitly tracking 6-DoF forces and torques using wrist-mounted F/T sensors, the system enables compliant interaction. This capability is fundamental for tasks such as kinesthetic teaching, cooperative lifting, navigating cluttered spaces, and avoiding damage during incidental contact. The robot's ability to react naturally to external wrenches expands its utility in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Robustness through Advanced State Estimation:** The development and integration of a neural network-enhanced Kalman filter significantly improve the accuracy and robustness of base velocity estimation for legged robots. Given the inherent oscillatory nature of legged locomotion, reliable state estimation is crucial for the performance and stability of any whole-body controller. This contribution enhances the overall system's ability to operate effectively in complex, noisy real-world conditions.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The methodology of incorporating the Kalman filter's state covariance into the Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy's observation space during training is a practical strategy for improving sim-to-real transfer. By exposing the learning agent to realistic estimation uncertainties, the resulting policy is more robust and less susceptible to the discrepancies between simulated and physical environments.
- **Advancement in Whole-Body Control:** The combined model and learning-based approach offers a balanced strategy, leveraging the strengths of both paradigms. The model-based admittance controller provides precision and safety for the arm, while the learning-based policy offers adaptability and agility for the base locomotion. This hybrid architecture represents a promising direction for achieving complex, dynamic behaviors in mobile manipulators.
- **Broader Applications:** The demonstrated capabilities have potential implications for a wide range of applications, including:
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Co-working environments where robots assist humans with tasks requiring physical interaction, such as assembly, material handling, or assistance robotics.
 - **Search and Rescue:** Robots navigating uneven terrain and manipulating objects to assist in disaster zones.
 - **Logistics and Manufacturing:** Mobile manipulation tasks in warehouses or factories that require flexibility and interaction with dynamic elements.
 - **Exploration and Inspection:** Robots capable of both traversing challenging landscapes and interacting with objects for data collection or maintenance.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as improving simultaneous tracking across all DoF, training the Neural Kalman filter on varied object loads, and incorporating vision, suggests further enhancements that could broaden the applicability and performance of this framework.
