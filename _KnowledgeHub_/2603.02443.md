# Safe Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation via Combined Model and Learning-based Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories (MERL) developed a hybrid control framework for legged mobile manipulators, combining a model-based admittance controller with a reinforcement learning policy for whole-body loco-manipulation. The system achieves safe and compliant interaction by explicitly tracking 6-DoF forces with F/T sensors and utilizing a Reference Governor for safety guarantees, demonstrating end-effector velocity tracking MSEs below 0.005 m²/s² for linear and 0.029 rad²/s² for angular motion during human-robot collaboration.

## Problem

- Existing loco-manipulation methods often decouple locomotion and manipulation, leading to a lack of coordinated dynamics and suboptimal performance.
- Learning-based whole-body control approaches typically lack formal safety guarantees, crucial for human-robot interaction and operation in cluttered environments.
- Accurate base velocity estimation in legged robots is challenging due to inherent oscillatory noise, impacting controller performance and stability.

## Method

- A combined model-based admittance controller manages the manipulator arm for compliant 6-DoF force tracking using F/T sensors, integrating with a Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy for base locomotion.
- A neural network-enhanced Kalman filter improves the robustness and accuracy of base linear and angular velocity estimation, crucial for stable control.
- A Reference Governor (RG) provides formal safety guarantees for the arm by preventing constraint violations (position, velocity, wrench, kinematics) through online reference modification based on pre-computed admissible sets.

## Results

- The robot successfully performed force-tracking loco-manipulation, responding compliantly to external wrenches during a human-robot collaboration task.
- End-effector velocity tracking achieved a Mean Squared Error (MSE) of ≤0.005 m2/s2\leq 0.005 \, \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2≤0.005m2/s2 for linear components and ≤0.029 rad2/s2\leq 0.029 \, \text{rad}^2/\text{s}^2≤0.029rad2/s2 for angular components.
- The Reference Governor effectively enforced safety, preventing constraint violations and maintaining recursive feasibility within defined position, velocity, and kinematic limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating model-based control for arm precision and safety with learning-based control for base adaptability can achieve tightly coordinated and robust whole-body loco-manipulation.
> - Explicitly regulating 6-DoF contact forces using F/T sensors enables compliant human-robot interaction and precise manipulation in dynamic environments.
> - Enhancing state estimation for legged robots with a neural network-augmented Kalman filter significantly improves overall system stability and robustness by mitigating the impact of oscillatory noise.
