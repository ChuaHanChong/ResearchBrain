# Co-rewarding: Stable Self-supervised RL for Eliciting Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Co-rewarding framework introduces a self-supervised reinforcement learning approach for Large Language Models that stabilizes training by enforcing "reasoning invariance" through complementary supervision. This method mitigates training collapse observed in prior self-rewarding techniques, leading to consistent performance improvements, such as average relative gains of +4.42% and +12.90% over baselines on mathematical benchmarks, and occasionally matching or exceeding ground-truth trained models.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for enhancing LLM reasoning relies on costly, human-annotated ground-truth labels, which creates a significant scalability bottleneck.
- Existing label-free self-rewarding methods for LLMs frequently suffer from "training collapse," where initial performance gains degrade due to "reward hacking."
- Reward hacking occurs when internally generated reward signals, based on a single view (e.g., entropy, majority voting), lead models to exploit superficial patterns for inflated rewards without genuine reasoning improvement, thus eroding exploration and diversity.

## Method

- **Co-rewarding**, a ==self-supervised RL== framework, is introduced to achieve training stability by enforcing "reasoning invariance" through complementary supervision from multiple perspectives.
- Co-rewarding-I (Data-Side) uses "analogy-invariance," deriving cross-referenced pseudo-labels from semantically analogous rephrased questions.
- Co-rewarding-II (Model-Side) employs "temporal invariance" by decoupling the reward signal from the online policy, utilizing a slowly updated Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher model to generate stable pseudo-labels.

## Results

- Co-rewarding consistently achieved superior performance and enhanced training stability, preventing the "training collapse" observed in prior self-rewarding baselines.
- Co-rewarding-I and Co-rewarding-II demonstrated average relative gains of +**4.42%** and +**12.90%** over best baselines on mathematical benchmarks, with Co-rewarding-III further boosting performance.
- The framework matched or surpassed models trained with ground-truth labels in some settings (e.g., **94.01%** Pass@1 on GSM8K for Co-rewarding-II with Qwen3-**8B**-Base), and showed cross-domain generalization while preserving general instruction-following and multi-task capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training collapse in self-rewarding LLM RL is driven by reward hacking due to single-view, self-consistent feedback loops.
> - Enforcing "reasoning invariance" through complementary supervision from alternative data views or stable temporal references effectively mitigates reward hacking and stabilizes self-supervised RL training.
> - Decoupling the reward generation from the immediate online policy via a slowly evolving teacher model or by cross-referencing analogous inputs raises the bar for trivial rewards, fostering genuine reasoning improvements.
