# Co-rewarding: Stable Self-supervised RL for Eliciting Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Co-rewarding framework introduces a self-supervised reinforcement learning approach for Large Language Models that stabilizes training by enforcing "reasoning invariance" through complementary supervision. This method mitigates training collapse observed in prior self-rewarding techniques, leading to consistent performance improvements, such as average relative gains of +4.42% and +12.90% over baselines on mathematical benchmarks, and occasionally matching or exceeding ground-truth trained models.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for enhancing LLM reasoning relies on costly, human-annotated ground-truth labels, which creates a significant scalability bottleneck.
- Existing label-free self-rewarding methods for LLMs frequently suffer from "training collapse," where initial performance gains degrade due to "reward hacking."
- Reward hacking occurs when internally generated reward signals, based on a single view (e.g., entropy, majority voting), lead models to exploit superficial patterns for inflated rewards without genuine reasoning improvement, thus eroding exploration and diversity.

## Method

- **Co-rewarding**, a ==self-supervised RL== framework, is introduced to achieve training stability by enforcing "reasoning invariance" through complementary supervision from multiple perspectives.
- Co-rewarding-I (Data-Side) uses "analogy-invariance," deriving cross-referenced pseudo-labels from semantically analogous rephrased questions.
- Co-rewarding-II (Model-Side) employs "temporal invariance" by decoupling the reward signal from the online policy, utilizing a slowly updated Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher model to generate stable pseudo-labels.

## Results

- Co-rewarding consistently achieved superior performance and enhanced training stability, preventing the "training collapse" observed in prior self-rewarding baselines.
- Co-rewarding-I and Co-rewarding-II demonstrated average relative gains of +**4.42%** and +**12.90%** over best baselines on mathematical benchmarks, with Co-rewarding-III further boosting performance.
- The framework matched or surpassed models trained with ground-truth labels in some settings (e.g., **94.01%** Pass@1 on GSM8K for Co-rewarding-II with Qwen3-**8B**-Base), and showed cross-domain generalization while preserving general instruction-following and multi-task capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training collapse in self-rewarding LLM RL is driven by reward hacking due to single-view, self-consistent feedback loops.
> - Enforcing "reasoning invariance" through complementary supervision from alternative data views or stable temporal references effectively mitigates reward hacking and stabilizes self-supervised RL training.
> - Decoupling the reward generation from the immediate online policy via a slowly evolving teacher model or by cross-referencing analogous inputs raises the bar for trivial rewards, fostering genuine reasoning improvements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated general capabilities across various linguistic tasks. A significant area of ongoing research focuses on enhancing their reasoning abilities, particularly in complex scenarios such as mathematical problem-solving and code generation. Within this domain, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as an effective post-training paradigm. RLVR relies on externally verifiable signals, such as the outcomes of program executions or evaluations of mathematical equivalence, to provide explicit reward signals for model training. While successful in improving reasoning, RLVR's fundamental dependency on high-quality, human-annotated ground-truth (GT) labels presents a scaling challenge. Acquiring such labels is resource-intensive, especially for complex or niche tasks, leading to a bottleneck in scaling LLM reasoning capabilities.

To address the limitations of labeled data, recent research has explored self-rewarding methods. These label-free alternatives aim to unlock LLMs' reasoning potential by generating supervisory signals internally. Two primary approaches exist:
1. **Internal Signal-Based Methods**: These leverage internal model signals, such as entropy (e.g., Prabhudesai et al., 2025) or self-certainty (e.g., Zhao et al., 2025b), to encourage the model to generate more confident or less uncertain outputs. The implicit assumption is that higher confidence correlates with correctness in reasoning.
2. **Consensus-Based Methods**: These approaches seek answer-level consistency across multiple rollouts for a given question (e.g., Zuo et al., 2025; Shafayat et al., 2025). A common strategy involves using a majority voting mechanism to derive pseudo-labels that serve as the basis for reward signals.

However, a significant limitation observed in these existing self-rewarding methods is frequent "training collapse." This phenomenon stems from "reward hacking" under a "self-consistent illusion." Because the reward signal is internally produced by the policy model from a single-view perspective of its own outputs, it can easily converge to trivial solutions that inflate the reward without corresponding improvements in genuine reasoning. For instance, entropy-based methods might learn to concentrate probability mass on a narrow set of tokens, generating repetitive or overly confident but incorrect strings. Similarly, consensus-based methods can converge on consistently wrong answers that achieve high agreement across rollouts. Both scenarios lead to inflated rewards but erode exploration, diversity, and ultimately, reasoning correctness, causing training instability and limiting scalability.

This work introduces "Co-rewarding," a self-supervised RL framework, directly addressing the training collapse issue in current self-rewarding methods. Inspired by principles from self-supervised learning, Co-rewarding aims to achieve training stability by introducing complementary supervision from "another views," thereby reducing the susceptibility to reward hacking and fostering more robust reasoning elicitation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a stable self-supervised Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework, named Co-rewarding, that effectively elicits reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) while mitigating the training collapse prevalent in existing self-rewarding approaches.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

First, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has demonstrated utility in enhancing LLMs' reasoning for complex tasks like mathematics and coding. However, its fundamental reliance on high-quality, human-annotated ground-truth (GT) labels poses a significant scalability challenge. Obtaining such labels is time-consuming and expensive, particularly for intricate or specialized reasoning problems. This bottleneck limits the application and widespread adoption of RLVR, especially in the context of scaling laws where larger models and datasets are continually being explored.

Second, in response to the scalability issues of RLVR, recent advancements have focused on "self-rewarding" methods. These label-free approaches aim to bypass the need for external human annotations by deriving reward signals directly from the LLM's own outputs or internal states. While this paradigm offers a promising avenue for scalable training, it frequently encounters a critical drawback: "training collapse." This instability manifests as the model's performance degrading significantly after initial improvements, preventing sustained learning.

The underlying cause of this collapse is identified as "reward hacking," driven by a "self-consistent illusion." When a model generates its own rewards based on a single-view perspective of its outputs, it can exploit superficial patterns to maximize the reward signal without truly improving its reasoning abilities. For instance:
- **Internal signal-based methods** (e.g., entropy minimization or self-certainty maximization) might lead the model to concentrate its probability mass on a few tokens, generating highly confident yet repetitive or erroneous sequences. The model perceives these high-certainty outputs as "good" despite their lack of actual correctness.
- **Consensus-based methods** (e.g., majority voting) can incentivize the model to converge on a consistent but ultimately incorrect answer. If multiple rollouts consistently produce the same wrong answer, the reward mechanism might incorrectly interpret this consensus as a sign of valid reasoning.

In both scenarios, the internal reward signal becomes decoupled from true reasoning competence, leading the model down a path of local optima that ultimately degrades its overall performance and generalizability. This reward hacking erodes the model's capacity for exploration and diversity, essential for complex reasoning.

Motivated by these challenges, Co-rewarding seeks to introduce "complementary supervision from another views." The core philosophical idea is to establish "reasoning invariance" – that true reasoning should remain valid across different data perspectives or maintain consistency over the model's temporal evolution. By enforcing such invariances, the framework aims to increase the difficulty for the model to find and exploit trivial solutions, thereby preventing reward hacking and fostering stable, effective self-supervised RL training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Co-rewarding framework is designed to address the training collapse in self-supervised RL by integrating complementary supervision, inspired by self-supervised learning principles. The core idea is to establish reasoning invariance beyond single-view outputs, ensuring training signals remain valid across different data perspectives or stable over time. The framework is instantiated in two orthogonal ways and one combined version, building upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

**Preliminary: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) is adopted as the underlying policy optimization method. It optimizes the policy by maximizing a reward signal while regularizing with a KL divergence term to keep the policy close to an initial reference model. For a given question, GRPO samples multiple outputs ({y_i}) from the current policy, computes a reward for each, and then normalizes these rewards to estimate an advantage (Â_i). This advantage is used to update the policy, with a clipping mechanism to prevent large deviations.

**Co-rewarding-I: Data-Side Instantiation (Analogy-Invariance)**
This instantiation derives complementary supervision from "analogy-invariance" by leveraging contrastive agreement across semantically analogous questions. The hypothesis is that questions sharing the same mathematical essence, despite differing in surface form (e.g., via paraphrasing or reformatting), should elicit similar and valid reasoning.
1. **Question Rephrasing**: For each original question (x) in the training dataset (D), a semantically analogous rephrased version (x') is generated. In this work, the open-source Qwen3-32B model is used for rephrasing, ensuring mathematical equivalence and different wording.
2. **Cross-Referenced Pseudo-Labeling**:
 - For the original question (x), the model's rollouts {y_i} are generated. Separately, the model generates rollouts {y'_i} for the rephrased question (x').
 - Pseudo-labels are then derived through a majority voting mechanism (Shafayat et al., 2025): the most frequent answer among {y'_i} (denoted y'_v) serves as the pseudo-label for the original question (x), and vice versa (y_v for x').
3. **Reward Calculation**: Rewards for the rollouts from x are computed by comparing them against y'_v (i.e., r(y'_v, y_i)), and rewards for rollouts from x' are computed against y_v (i.e., r(y_v, y'_i)). These cross-referenced rewards are then used to calculate the GRPO advantage.
4. **Objective**: The learning objective combines the GRPO objectives for both original and rephrased questions, with rewards based on cross-referenced pseudo-labels. This forces the model to achieve consistent reasoning across diverse but semantically equivalent inputs, making reward hacking on single-view outputs more difficult.

**Co-rewarding-II: Model-Side Instantiation (Temporal Invariance/Self-Distillation)**
This instantiation aims to stabilize self-supervised RL by decoupling the reward signal from the current online policy. It achieves this by using a slowly-updated reference teacher model to provide pseudo-labels, akin to self-distillation.
1. **Student and Teacher Models**: An online policy (π_θ, the student) is trained, while a separate Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's old policy is maintained as a reference teacher model (˜π_ref).
2. **Teacher-Generated Pseudo-Labels**: For a given question (x), the student policy (π_θ) generates its rollouts ({y_i}). Concurrently, the *teacher model* (˜π_ref) generates its own set of rollouts ({˜y_j}).
3. **Pseudo-Labeling for Reward**: A pseudo-label (˜y_v) is obtained by majority voting from the *teacher's* rollouts ({˜y_j}). This pseudo-label then supervises the student's learning by defining the reward: r(˜y_v, y_i).
4. **Teacher Update**: The teacher model's parameters are updated as an EMA of the student's old policy parameters. The EMA weight (α) is scheduled using a cosine annealing schedule (from α_start=0.99 to α_end=0.9999), ensuring the teacher evolves smoothly and slowly. This temporal decoupling makes the pseudo-label source more stable and resistant to short-term fluctuations or reward hacking by the student.
5. **Benefit**: This breaks the immediate on-policy feedback loop, compelling the student to learn from a more robust and disentangled supervisory signal, effectively raising the bar for achieving trivial rewards.

**Co-rewarding-III: Combined Data-Side + Model-Side Instantiation**
This version integrates the strengths of both Co-rewarding-I and Co-rewarding-II. It leverages analogy-invariance (data-side cross-supervision) while simultaneously employing the EMA-updated reference teacher (model-side self-distillation) to generate the pseudo-labels. Specifically, the teacher model generates rollouts for both original and rephrased questions, and the pseudo-label from one view (e.g., teacher's rephrased rollouts) supervises the student's output on the other view (e.g., student's original rollouts). This orthogonal combination aims to further enhance stability and performance.

**Implementation Details**:
The experiments utilize diverse LLMs, including Qwen2.5 (3B/7B), Qwen3 (1.7B/4B/8B-Base), and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct. Training datasets include MATH, DAPO-14k, and OpenRS. All methods share common training configurations, such as a global batch size of 128, 8 rollouts per question, AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 3e-6, and specific KL loss coefficients. Evaluation is conducted across multiple benchmarks for mathematical reasoning (MATH500, GSM8K, AMC, AIME24), code generation (LiveCodeBench, CRUX), and general abilities (IFEval, MMLU-Pro).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Co-rewarding across various LLM architectures, training datasets, and evaluation benchmarks yielded several findings regarding its performance, stability, and underlying mechanisms.

**Superior Performance and Stability**:
Co-rewarding consistently demonstrated stronger performance compared to other state-of-the-art self-rewarding baselines, including Self-Certainty, Entropy, and Majority-Voting. For instance, Co-rewarding-I achieved an average relative gain of +4.42% over the best baselines across four mathematical benchmarks, while Co-rewarding-II exhibited an even larger average relative gain of +12.90%. The combined Co-rewarding-III version further boosted performance by an average of +7.11% over Co-rewarding-I and +1.72% over Co-rewarding-II, indicating a synergistic effect of the two complementary supervision approaches.

A key factor contributing to this superior performance was the framework's enhanced training stability. Unlike existing self-rewarding baselines, which frequently exhibited "training collapse" (characterized by initial performance gains followed by a sharp decline or plateau), Co-rewarding maintained stable and continuous improvement throughout the training process. This stability allowed the models to learn effectively from more data over extended periods, preventing the degradation observed in other methods. Co-rewarding-I showed stability on the MATH dataset, while Co-rewarding-II consistently maintained stability across all tested models and datasets (e.g., MATH and DAPO-14k), attributed to its design that decouples supervision from the online policy.

**Performance Relative to Ground-Truth Rewards**:
Notably, Co-rewarding instantiations, in some settings, matched or even surpassed models trained with ground-truth (GT) labels. For example, on the GSM8K benchmark, Co-rewarding-II with Qwen3-8B-Base achieved a Pass@1 accuracy of 94.01%, which was observed to be higher than the GT-Reward baseline. This outcome suggests that for certain benchmarks, particularly those where self-supervised signals are sufficiently robust, label-free RL can achieve competitive or even superior performance. This may be partly due to the distribution mismatch between training and evaluation data, where self-supervised methods might exhibit better generalization.

**Generalization Across Domains**:
Despite being trained primarily on math-oriented datasets, models fine-tuned with Co-rewarding demonstrated improvements on code generation benchmarks (e.g., CRUX, LiveCodeBench). This indicates a cross-domain generalization effect for reasoning abilities from mathematics to code. Furthermore, Co-rewarding preserved or enhanced the models' general instruction-following (IFEval) and multi-task (MMLU-Pro) capabilities. Co-rewarding-II, for instance, outperformed other self-rewarding baselines in 12 out of 14 MMLU-Pro categories, suggesting that the improvements in reasoning were achieved without compromising broader general-domain performance.

**Alleviation of Reward Hacking and Balanced Exploration-Exploitation**:
Analysis of reward and response length curves showed that baselines like Entropy and Majority-Voting quickly achieved high rewards but simultaneously produced shorter or repetitive outputs, indicative of reward hacking. Entropy-based methods often led to the collapse of probability mass onto a narrow set of tokens, resulting in repetitive string generation. Majority-Voting, when collapsed, would often box an incorrect but consistently generated answer. In contrast, Co-rewarding exhibited smoother, gradually increasing reward curves and maintained moderate response lengths, suggesting a more balanced exploration-exploitation trade-off and genuine reasoning improvement.

**Ablation Studies and Mechanism Validation**:
Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of each component of Co-rewarding. For Co-rewarding-I, the cross-supervision between original and rephrased questions was found to be critical for mitigating collapse; simply augmenting data (e.g., Majority-Voting with a union of original and rephrased data) did not resolve the inherent instability. For Co-rewarding-II, removing the Exponential Moving Average (EMA) update for the reference teacher led to a clear degradation in performance and flat pseudo-label accuracy, underscoring the necessity of a dynamically updated, stable teacher for providing high-quality pseudo-labels and enabling effective self-distillation. The pseudo-label accuracy of Co-rewarding-II steadily improved over training, unlike baselines where it quickly collapsed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Co-rewarding, a self-supervised Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework that addresses a critical challenge in training Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex reasoning tasks: the "training collapse" issue prevalent in existing self-rewarding methods.

The primary significance of this work lies in its contribution to advancing label-free LLM training. By proposing a stable self-supervised RL framework, Co-rewarding offers a scalable alternative to traditional RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR), which is hindered by the high cost and scarcity of human-annotated ground-truth labels. The ability to effectively train LLMs for reasoning without explicit external supervision has substantial implications for the broader development and deployment of more capable and autonomous AI systems.

The core conceptual innovation of Co-rewarding is its inspiration from self-supervised learning, particularly the introduction of "complementary supervision from another views" to enforce "reasoning invariance." This paradigm shift moves beyond single-view feedback, which is susceptible to reward hacking, towards a more robust form of self-supervision. The two distinct instantiations, Co-rewarding-I (data-side analogy-invariance) and Co-rewarding-II (model-side temporal invariance through self-distillation), provide practical mechanisms to achieve this. Their combined version, Co-rewarding-III, further demonstrates the synergistic benefits of these complementary approaches.

The empirical results underscore this significance. Co-rewarding not only consistently outperforms prior self-rewarding baselines but also exhibits stable training dynamics, a crucial factor for sustained learning and performance scaling. The observed instances where Co-rewarding matches or even surpasses models trained with ground-truth labels on certain benchmarks highlight the potential for high-quality self-generated supervision to be a viable and powerful alternative to costly human annotations.

Furthermore, the framework demonstrates desirable properties such as cross-domain generalization (from mathematics to code) and preservation of broader instruction-following and multi-task abilities. This indicates that the learned reasoning improvements are robust and do not come at the expense of other critical LLM capabilities. The detailed analyses of reward signals, response lengths, and pseudo-label accuracy provide insights into how Co-rewarding effectively mitigates reward hacking and maintains a balanced exploration-exploitation trade-off.

The potential impact of Co-rewarding extends to several areas:
- **Scalability**: By reducing the reliance on human annotations, it enables the training of LLMs for increasingly complex and diverse reasoning tasks at a scale that was previously infeasible.
- **Research Direction**: It opens new avenues for research into self-supervised RL for reasoning, encouraging further exploration of different invariance principles, pseudo-labeling strategies, and teacher-student dynamics.
- **Autonomous LLM Improvement**: The framework contributes to the long-term goal of enabling LLMs to autonomously improve their reasoning abilities, leading to more intelligent and self-sufficient AI agents.
- **Practical Applications**: Improved and more stable reasoning capabilities, especially in a label-free manner, could enhance LLMs' performance in various real-world applications requiring robust problem-solving, such as scientific discovery, software engineering, and complex decision-making.

The provision of open-source code further ensures reproducibility and encourages community engagement, potentially accelerating advancements in this critical area of LLM research.
