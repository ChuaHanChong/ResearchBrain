# Act Only When It Pays: Efficient Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning via Selective Rollouts

> [!summary] Summary
> GRESO efficiently trains large language models for reasoning by selectively filtering out uninformative prompts before costly rollouts, achieving comparable accuracy to state-of-the-art methods while reducing total training time by up to 2.0 times and rollouts by up to 3.35 times. This method addresses the major computational bottleneck in RL-based LLM fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLM reasoning relies on extensive and computationally expensive 'rollouts' to generate training data.
- A large proportion of prompts become 'uninformative' (zero-variance) during training, wasting significant computational resources during rollouts.
- Existing online data selection methods filter uninformative prompts only *after* costly rollouts, leading to substantial wasted computation and limiting efficient scaling.

## Method

- Introduces **GRESO** (GRPO with Efficient ==Selective Rollout==), an online, lightweight algorithm for pre-rollout filtering of uninformative prompts.
- Utilizes probabilistic pre-rollout filtering based on a prompt's historical reward dynamics and consecutive zero-variance occurrences.
- Employs a self-adjustable base exploration probability and an Adaptive Sampling Batch Size (ABS) to dynamically optimize sampling and maintain effective training batch ratios.

## Results

- Achieved accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art Dynamic Sampling across various models and math reasoning benchmarks.
- Reduced the total training time by up to **2.0** times and rollout generation time by up to **2.4** times.
- Required up to **3.35** times fewer rollouts compared to Dynamic Sampling, significantly improving computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Zero-variance prompts, which provide no learning signal, significantly increase in proportion (up to 80%) during later stages of RL training.
> - Prompt value exhibits strong temporal consistency; prompts uninformative in one epoch are highly likely (over 90%) to remain uninformative in subsequent epochs, enabling pre-rollout prediction.
> - Balancing exploitation (skipping uninformative prompts) with exploration (revisiting some previously uninformative prompts) is crucial for sustained performance.
