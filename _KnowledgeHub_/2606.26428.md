# Play2Perfect: What Matters in Dexterous Play Pretraining for Precise Assembly?

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage framework, Play2Perfect, enables multi-fingered robots to efficiently learn and execute precise, contact-rich assembly tasks by combining task-agnostic play pretraining with sparse-reward reinforcement learning finetuning. This approach achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and demonstrated significant sample efficiency gains, solving tasks up to 33 times faster than training from scratch while achieving high success rates on tight-clearance assembly.

## Problem

- Dexterous multi-fingered robots struggle with precise, contact-rich assembly due to high degrees of freedom and the challenge of collecting high-quality data for imitation learning.
- Reinforcement learning for these tasks faces the sparse reward problem, requiring complex hand-engineered rewards or simplified environments to achieve success.
- Prior solutions often require task-specific hardware, environmental modifications, or extensive human demonstrations, limiting generalizability and scalability.

## Method

- A two-stage **Play2Perfect** framework is proposed, starting with dexterous play pretraining in simulation to learn general, task-agnostic manipulation skills.
- The pretraining utilizes a goal-conditioned policy, diverse object geometries and properties, 6D pose control objectives, randomized goal trajectories, and precise goal tolerances.
- The pretrained policy is then finetuned using sparse rewards on specific assembly tasks defined by CAD models, with domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- Play2Perfect solved all evaluated assembly tasks within 2-5 hours of RL training, achieving near-perfect success rates on complex tasks where scratch policies failed after 24 hours.
- Policies demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks, achieving up to 9/10 success on 2mm clearance tight-insertion and 7/10 on multi-part beam assembly and screwing tasks.
- Learned policies exhibited robust behaviors such as local search, corrective motions under contact, and in-hand reorientation in real-world environments, completing tasks in short average times (**6.4** to **15.6** seconds).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task-agnostic play pretraining significantly improves sample efficiency for precise assembly, enabling policies to learn 33x faster than from scratch and providing greater robustness to perturbations.
> - Specific design choices in play pretraining are critical for transferability: 6D pose control, diverse goal trajectories, and precise goal tolerances (e.g., 1 cm) are essential for learning fine motor skills.
> - Zero-shot play policies are insufficient for precise assembly; finetuning with sparse assembly rewards is necessary for achieving high precision and robust contact-rich interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly with multi-fingered hands, presents a dual challenge of achieving high speed and dexterity while performing complex tasks like precise assembly. Historically, these tasks have remained difficult for conventional robotic methods due to their contact-rich nature and the sparsity of reward signals in reinforcement learning (RL) settings.

Prior research in dexterous manipulation has largely bifurcated into imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL). Imitation learning approaches often rely on demonstrations, typically collected through human teleoperation or motion capture. However, the contact-rich requirements of precise assembly make high-quality data collection challenging due to the inherent embodiment gap between human operators and robots, as well as limitations in tactile feedback. Consequently, IL has often focused on less contact-intensive tasks like pick-and-place.

Reinforcement learning offers an alternative, with advancements in dexterous skills such as grasping and in-hand reorientation, predominantly in free space. Extending RL to contact-rich assembly typically necessitates dense, hand-engineered reward functions or highly structured environments with specialized grippers, tool attachments, or fixtures. While these methods have demonstrated success, they often require task-specific hardware modifications or extensive manual reward shaping, limiting their generalizability and scalability. The reliance on parallel-jaw grippers in some approaches also restricts the speed and dexterity achievable with multi-fingered hands.

The concept of "learning from play" has emerged as a promising direction, where robots learn general manipulation skills through unsupervised or self-supervised exploration in diverse environments. Previous works have explored learning task-agnostic controllers from play, sometimes combined with teleoperation or demonstrations for generalization. However, these "play-only" policies often struggle with the precision and contact-rich demands of assembly when deployed zero-shot.

This work, Play2Perfect, integrates into this landscape by proposing a two-stage framework: task-agnostic play pretraining followed by sparse-reward RL finetuning. It distinguishes itself by systematically investigating which aspects of "play" specifically contribute to solving precise, contact-rich assembly tasks, rather than assuming zero-shot transfer. The approach aims to leverage the benefits of dexterous hands without relying on teleoperation for data collection or task-specific hardware engineering. It positions play not as an end in itself for zero-shot deployment, but as a mechanism to acquire a generalizable, reusable manipulation prior that can be rapidly specialized for downstream tasks, addressing the sample efficiency and brittleness challenges of training precise assembly policies from scratch. This contrasts with other pretraining efforts like Vision Language Action (VLA) models, which often rely on large datasets typically gathered with simpler parallel-jaw grippers, or human videos, which may lack crucial contact information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable multi-fingered robots to execute precise, contact-rich assembly tasks efficiently and robustly, without necessitating extensive task-specific engineering of hardware or reward functions.

The motivation stems from several core challenges inherent in current robotic assembly:
1. **Complexity of Dexterous Control:** Multi-fingered hands possess many degrees of freedom, offering high dexterity but making their control for complex tasks challenging, especially when engaging in intricate contacts required for assembly.
2. **Contact-Rich Nature of Assembly:** Assembly tasks frequently involve sustained and precise physical contact between parts. This characteristic makes data collection for imitation learning difficult due to the embodiment gap and lack of rich tactile feedback in teleoperation.
3. **Sparse Reward Problem in Reinforcement Learning:** Precise assembly tasks are often defined by a final successful configuration, resulting in sparse reward signals. Training RL agents from scratch in such environments is intractable, as the agent must discover fundamental skills like grasping, in-hand reorientation, alignment, and contact-rich insertion before receiving any positive feedback.
4. **Limitations of Prior Solutions:** Previous methods have addressed these challenges by simplifying the problem through:
 - **Environment Modification:** Using custom fixtures to constrain parts and simplify manipulation.
 - **Robot Modification:** Employing specialized grippers or tool attachments to simplify the control problem.
 - **Dense Reward Shaping:** Designing complex, task-specific dense reward functions to guide RL exploration.
 - **Demonstrations/Warm-starting:** Relying on human demonstrations or teleoperation for initial policy acquisition.
 These solutions, while effective, often require significant manual effort per task, limit robot versatility, or introduce reliance on external data that may be difficult to acquire for contact-rich scenarios.

The central hypothesis motivating this work is that "before a robot can perfect precise assembly, it must first learn to play." The authors posit that an intuitive learning progression involves acquiring broad manipulation capabilities in a task-agnostic manner, which can then be refined for specialized tasks. This "play" phase is intended to foster reusable manipulation priors, including robust grasping, agile in-hand reorientation, and precise 6D pose control.

A key objective is to systematically investigate and identify the specific design choices within this play pretraining process that are most influential for successful transfer to precise assembly tasks. This includes exploring the impact of:
- **Object Diversity:** The range of objects manipulated during play.
- **Training Objective:** The type of manipulation skills (e.g., translation, rotation, 6D pose) targeted during play.
- **Trajectory Diversity:** The variability of goal sequences encountered during play.
- **Goal Precision:** The accuracy requirements for reaching goals during play.

By addressing these questions, the research aims to develop a framework that significantly improves the sample efficiency and robustness of learning for dexterous assembly, offering a generalizable approach that reduces the need for task-specific engineering.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Play2Perfect framework comprises two main stages: dexterous play pretraining and sparse-reward reinforcement learning finetuning for assembly. The robot system consists of a 22-DoF Sharpa five-fingered hand mounted on a 7-DoF KUKA iiwa 14 arm.

#### 3.1. Dexterous Play Pretraining

The initial stage focuses on training a general, task-agnostic dexterous manipulation policy in simulation.
- **Goal-Conditioned Policy:** A policy $\pi_\theta(s_t, o_t, g_t, \phi)$ is trained, where $s_t$ is robot proprioception, $o_t$ is the current object pose, $g_t$ is the target object pose, and $\phi$ encodes object geometry (3D bounding-box dimensions). The policy controls both the arm and hand.
- **Object Diversity:** To acquire broad competence, the system procedurally generates diverse primitive objects (cuboids and cylinders) whose dimensions fit within the robot hand. Object densities and additional masses are randomized to vary center of mass and inertia, promoting object-control strategies independent of specific geometry or mass distribution.
- **Training Objective:** The robot is trained to manipulate objects through sequences of 6D goal poses. The first goal involves grasping and lifting the object from a table, while subsequent goals require in-hand control of object pose without drops. The reward function combines:
 - `r_smooth`: Penalizes joint velocities for smooth control.
 - `r_grasp`: Encourages grasping and lifting the object above the table.
 - `r_goal`: Rewards reaching the current 6D object goal, incorporating a large sparse success bonus when the keypoint-based 6D pose distance $d(o_t, g_t)$ falls below a threshold $\epsilon$. Default $\epsilon=1$ cm. This keypoint-based distance jointly captures translation and rotation error.
- **Trajectory Diversity:** Goal sequences are randomized in each episode. The first goal is broadly sampled within the robot's workspace, while subsequent goals are sampled near the previous one with significant rotations. This design encourages the development of in-hand manipulation rather than simple arm movements with fixed grasps.
- **Goal Precision:** The default success threshold $\epsilon$ for goal reaching is 1 cm, requiring accurate object pose control.
- **RL Algorithm:** Split and Aggregate Policy Gradients (SAPG) is utilized, a population-based PPO variant, for its exploration capabilities in parallel environments. An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is used, where the critic has access to privileged, noise-free state information during training.
- **Domain Randomization:** To facilitate sim-to-real transfer, pretraining incorporates domain randomization by modeling action latency, proprioceptive observation delays, and noise in current and goal object poses, as well as randomizing object geometry and physics.

#### 3.2. RL Finetuning on Assembly Tasks

After pretraining, the general manipulation policy is specialized for specific assembly tasks.
- **Assembly Construction from CAD:** Each assembly task is defined by a CAD design. A sequence of assembly steps is generated using "assembly-by-disassembly" by reversing a feasible part removal order. Each step defines an RL environment with randomized part and fixture poses. Contact-critical regions (e.g., holes) are represented using high-resolution signed distance fields (SDFs) for accurate contact dynamics, while other geometries use convex decomposition.
- **Sparse Assembly Rewards:** Finetuning exclusively uses sparse success rewards derived from the CAD-specified part configurations. For each part-fixture pair, the desired relative transform is known. The final assembled pose is a primary goal. For contact-rich tasks like insertion or screwing, a small sequence of sparse intermediate contact goals is derived by reversing the disassembly motion (e.g., a pre-insertion pose, or poses along a screw thread). The policy receives a bonus only upon reaching these CAD-derived goals. No explicit dense rewards for approach, grasping, lifting, alignment, or contact are provided during finetuning.
- **RL Algorithm and Domain Randomization:** The same SAPG algorithm and hyperparameters are used for finetuning. Domain randomization is also applied, including noise on current and goal part poses, fixture yaw, and external forces/torques on the manipulated part.

#### 3.3. Training Details and Sim-to-Real Transfer

- **Computational Resources:** Training for both stages occurs in Isaac Sim on NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs, with physics running at 120 Hz and policy at 60 Hz. Play pretraining uses 24,576 parallel environments for 7 days, while finetuning uses 12,228 environments for 1 day, reflecting higher memory demands for contact-rich simulations.
- **Real-World Deployment:**
 - **Object Pose Tracking:** FoundationPose is used for real-time 6D pose tracking of manipulated parts and fixtures, leveraging the CAD models.
 - **Control Loop:** The finetuned policy operates in a closed-loop at 60 Hz, receiving robot proprioception, current object pose, goal pose, and a grasp bounding box (derived from CAD). It outputs joint position commands for the arm and hand.
 - **No Real-World Finetuning:** All policies are transferred zero-shot from simulation to reality.
- **Tasks:** Evaluation is conducted on three tasks:
 1. **Tight-Insertion:** Inserting a T-shaped peg into holes with varying contact clearances.
 2. **Assemble-Beam:** A multi-part beam assembly task.
 3. **Screw-Leg:** Screwing a furniture leg into a table fixture.
 For Assemble-Beam and Screw-Leg, parts are scaled 3x from benchmarks to be suitable for dexterous hands and reliable visual tracking.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success Rate (part reaches final goal within 1 cm tolerance) and Completion Time (full task duration) are reported for 500 simulation rollouts (randomized initial poses) and 10 real-world rollouts (fixed fixture, randomized part poses).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across several experimental comparisons, including the efficacy of play pretraining, the impact of its design choices, the necessity of finetuning, and real-world transfer capabilities.

#### 4.1. Efficacy of Play Pretraining vs. Training from Scratch

- **Sample Efficiency:** Play2Perfect policies solved all evaluated assembly tasks within approximately 2 to 5 hours of wall-clock RL training. In contrast, training policies from scratch (without pretraining), whether using only sparse rewards or even with extensive hand-engineered dense, multi-stage rewards (for grasping, lifting, and waypoint tracking), failed to achieve any successful rollouts after 24 hours of training on complex tasks.
- **Performance on Simplified Tasks:** On a simplified fixtured insertion task, where grasping was easier, a scratch policy with dense rewards eventually achieved near-perfect success, but required over 100 hours of training. Play2Perfect achieved the same success rate in only 4 hours, representing a 33x speed-up.
- **Robustness:** The scratch policy learned a brittle strategy, balancing the peg with only the thumb, which failed under external force perturbations (success dropped to ~20% with 10N perturbation). Play2Perfect, due to its pretraining, learned more stable multi-finger grasps and robust recovery strategies, maintaining over 75% success even under the largest perturbations.

#### 4.2. Impact of Play Pretraining Design Choices

A systematic ablation study on key play pretraining components revealed their influence on downstream assembly finetuning:
- **Object Diversity:** Increasing the number of diverse objects during pretraining (from 10 to 100 to 1000) generally improved transfer speed and final performance, though with diminishing returns between 100 and 1000 objects. This suggests that a moderately diverse object set is sufficient.
- **Training Objective:** 6D pose control was identified as critical.
 - `Translation-only` pretraining, which rewarded only positional accuracy, failed to transfer effectively because it did not necessitate learning in-hand reorientation.
 - `Rotation-only` pretraining, focusing purely on orientation control after an initial grasp, transferred well but was slightly slower than the full 6D objective, likely due to less practice coupling reorientation with translational motion.
 - The default `6D Pose` objective, which jointly rewards both translation and rotation, proved most effective and consistent across tasks.
- **Trajectory Diversity:** Pretraining with `random goal trajectories` resulted in the fastest learning for downstream tasks. Fixed sets of 10 or 100 trajectories performed similarly but were slower than online randomized trajectories, indicating that broad coverage of goal-pose transitions is beneficial.
- **Goal Precision:** `Precise goals` (1 cm tolerance) during pretraining led to faster and more successful finetuning. A looser 10 cm threshold failed to transfer, as coarse goal reaching did not require the fine object-pose control needed for assembly. A 5 cm threshold learned more slowly than the 1 cm threshold, reinforcing that precise play induces priors better matched to tight-clearance assembly.

#### 4.3. Necessity of RL Finetuning for Precise Assembly

- **Zero-Shot Play Limitations:** The `Play-only` policy (pretrained but not finetuned on assembly tasks) could only solve the loosest insertion settings. In simulation, it achieved 75% success at 40 mm clearance but dropped to nearly 0% by 4 mm.
- **Finetuning for Precision:** Play2Perfect, with assembly finetuning, maintained high success even as clearance tightened, achieving 95% at 4 mm, 92% at 1 mm, and 80% at 0.2 mm (tighter than its training distribution).
- **Qualitative Behavior:** Play-only policies tended to move directly towards the goal and treated contact as a disturbance. In contrast, finetuned Play2Perfect policies learned to perform local searches near the hole, make corrective motions under contact, and commit to insertion once alignment was achieved. Real-world results showed a similar trend: Play2Perfect achieved 60% success at 0.5 mm clearance where Play-only completely failed.

#### 4.4. Sim-to-Real Transfer Capabilities

- **Zero-Shot Transfer Success:** Play2Perfect policies demonstrated zero-shot transfer to real-world assembly tasks across all three benchmarks.
 - **Tight-Insertion:** Achieved 10/10 success at 10 mm clearance, 9/10 at 2 mm, and 6/10 at 0.5 mm.
 - **Assemble-Beam:** Achieved 8/10 success on Step 1 and 7/10 on Step 2.
 - **Screw-Leg:** Achieved 7/10 success on insertion and 5/10 on full screwing.
- **Execution Efficiency:** Successful real-world trials were completed in short average times, ranging from 6.4 to 15.6 seconds, including grasping, transport, and final assembly.
- **Learned Behaviors:** Policies exhibited closed-loop recovery behaviors such as local search, corrective motions under contact, and regrasping after accidental drops, without requiring scripted recovery controllers. In screwing tasks, the policy could spin the leg directly within the hand, demonstrating efficient in-hand manipulation.
- **Failure Modes:** Real-world failures primarily stemmed from:
 - **Perception Errors:** Fast part motion, hand-object occlusion, and visual symmetries (e.g., in the rectangular screw leg) could degrade 6D pose estimation.
 - **Sim-to-Real Mismatch in Contact Dynamics:** Minor differences in fixture rigidity or compliance in the real world compared to simulation could cause policies to struggle during final contact-rich interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Play2Perfect framework contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a method for addressing the challenges of precise, contact-rich assembly with dexterous multi-fingered hands.

- **Addressing Dexterous Assembly Challenges:** This work demonstrates a pathway to enable robots with human-like hands to perform complex assembly tasks, which have historically been difficult due to sparse rewards and the intricacies of contact-rich manipulation. It provides a method that does not rely on task-specific hardware, environment fixtures, or extensive teleoperation for data collection.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Robustness:** By leveraging task-agnostic play pretraining, the framework significantly enhances the sample efficiency of downstream RL finetuning (demonstrating a 33x speed-up). The resulting policies also exhibit greater robustness to external perturbations compared to policies trained from scratch, suggesting that the diverse experiences during play contribute to more stable manipulation strategies.
- **Generalizable Prior for Manipulation:** The concept of a reusable, task-agnostic "play prior" represents a step towards more general robotic intelligence. This prior, encompassing grasping, in-hand reorientation, and 6D pose control, can be quickly adapted to a variety of new assembly tasks defined purely by CAD designs and sparse rewards, reducing the engineering effort per task.
- **Insights into Pretraining Design:** The systematic ablation study provides empirical evidence for key design considerations in creating effective dexterous manipulation priors. The findings highlight the importance of 6D pose control objectives, diverse goal trajectories, and precise goal tolerances during pretraining to induce the necessary fine motor skills for contact-rich assembly.
- **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstration of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex, contact-rich tasks underscores the effectiveness of the proposed methodology and domain randomization strategies, bridging the gap between simulated learning and real-world deployment.
- **Potential for Autonomous Systems:** Play2Perfect moves towards more autonomous assembly systems by reducing reliance on human intervention or pre-programmed routines for fundamental manipulation skills. This could have implications for various applications, from industrial manufacturing to complex object handling in unstructured environments.

Future research could build upon this foundation by integrating capabilities such as task sequencing, active part selection, scene memory, and more sophisticated recovery strategies to realize fully autonomous assembly pipelines. Furthermore, incorporating diverse visual or tactile observations could enhance perception and scene awareness, addressing current limitations in real-world deployment.
