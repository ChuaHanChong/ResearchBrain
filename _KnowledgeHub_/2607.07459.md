# EmbodiedGen V2: An Agentic, Simulation-Ready 3D World Engine for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Horizon Robotics and WuwenAI developed EmbodiedGen V2, a 3D world engine that generates executable, physically plausible, and interactive simulation environments for embodied AI. This system automates the creation of sim-ready 3D assets, scene composition, affordance labeling, and supports natural language-driven world editing. Policies trained in these generated environments achieved an 88.9% real-world success rate for cube stacking and improved sim-to-real transfer from 21.7% to 75.0% for mobile manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing generative 3D models prioritize visual fidelity, resulting in outputs that require substantial manual adaptation to be usable in physics simulations for embodied AI.
- The manual process of assembling 3D assets into policy-ready task environments is unscalable, hindering the efficiency of closed-loop learning for embodied agents.
- A lack of unified, simulation-ready representations prevents consistent deployment and portability of generated environments across various simulators and embodied AI frameworks.

## Method

- **EmbodiedGen V2** establishes a unified ==sim-ready representation== that couples metric geometry, physical validity, interaction semantics, and cross-simulator portability.
- It integrates modular pipelines for generating sim-ready 3D assets from text/image, ==affordance autolabeling==, composing task-driven interactive worlds, and synthesizing large-scale multi-room scenes.
- The system introduces **Vibe Coding**, an ==LLM-based agent== for stateful, natural-language-driven editing of simulation worlds, enabling iterative refinement while preserving physical feasibility.

## Results

- The sim-ready asset pipeline achieved a **96.5%** human acceptance rate and **98.6%** collision success, demonstrating high quality and efficiency in generating physics-compatible assets.
- The affordance autolabeling pipeline improved the end-to-end affordance pass rate by **19 percentage points**, with the full configuration being the most efficient at **94.0 seconds** per asset.
- Policies trained using EmbodiedGen V2-generated environments showed improved online trainability, increasing simulation success from **9.7%** to **79.8%**, and achieved enhanced real-robot transfer for tasks like mobile manipulation (from **21.7%** to **75.0%** success) and cube stacking (from **43.1%** to **88.9%** success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming visually plausible 3D content into 'sim-ready' assets requires robust pipelines for geometry refinement (mesh fixing, convex decomposition), physical property recovery (scale, mass, friction), and cross-format export to ensure executability in physics engines.
> - Augmenting 3D assets with part-level affordances via functional segmentation, VLM-based semantic annotation, and physics-validated grasp generation is crucial for enabling meaningful agent-object interaction.
> - A hierarchical, task-conditioned approach for scene generation, coupled with natural language interfaces for editing, allows for scalable and intuitive authoring of diverse, complex environments for embodied AI training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative 3D models has experienced rapid advancements, producing visually plausible objects and scenes. Concurrently, embodied artificial intelligence (AI) and robotics research requires increasingly sophisticated and diverse simulated environments for training, evaluating, and deploying policies. Prior work has addressed individual aspects of this challenge, such as generating 3D assets, creating scene layouts, annotating affordances, or enabling natural-language-driven editing. However, a significant gap exists in unifying these capabilities into a coherent, simulation-ready infrastructure. Existing 3D generative pipelines often prioritize visual fidelity, resulting in outputs that require substantial manual adaptation to be usable in physics simulations. This manual process limits the scalability and efficiency of closed-loop learning for embodied agents.

EmbodiedGen V2 addresses this gap by integrating diverse generative 3D capabilities into a unified "sim-ready" 3D world engine. It extends beyond generating static 3D content to producing executable task environments where objects possess physical properties, layouts adhere to task and navigation constraints, and worlds are portable across simulators, editable, and suitable for closed-loop training and evaluation. This work positions generative 3D world building as essential simulation infrastructure for embodied AI, moving beyond individual component advancements to a holistic system designed for policy development workflows. It builds upon EmbodiedGen V1 by expanding from a generative content toolkit to a comprehensive 3D world engine, focusing on complete executable environments rather than isolated generated artifacts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of EmbodiedGen V2 is to develop a generative 3D world engine capable of producing executable, simulation-ready environments for embodied intelligence tasks. This objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Bridging the Gap between Visual 3D Generation and Embodied AI Requirements:** While generative 3D models can create visually appealing content, embodied policy learning demands environments where objects have physical properties, interaction affordances, and where layouts respect task and navigation constraints. The motivation is to move beyond visualization-level 3D usability to simulation-level usability, where generated assets are directly executable in physics engines without manual post-processing.
- **Enabling Scalable Closed-Loop Learning:** The current manual process of assembling 3D assets into policy-ready task environments restricts the scalability of training and evaluation for embodied agents. A key motivation is to automate and streamline this process, allowing for the generation of diverse environments at scale to support large-scale data generation for reinforcement learning (RL) and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Ensuring Unified Representation and Cross-Simulator Portability:** Different simulators and embodied AI frameworks often use disparate asset formats and physics representations. A core motivation is to develop a unified sim-ready representation that preserves geometry, physics, affordances, and task semantics, ensuring consistency and portability across mainstream simulators (e.g., Genesis, Isaac Sim, Isaac Gym, SAPIEN, Bullet, MuJoCo).
- **Supporting Comprehensive Embodied Tasks:** Embodied agents require environments that facilitate various complex tasks, including manipulation, navigation, and mobile manipulation. This necessitates the generation of not only individual objects but also large-scale multi-room scenes with explicit room topology, traversable openings, and individually addressable furniture.
- **Facilitating Natural Language-Driven World Authoring:** Manual 3D world creation and editing are time-consuming and require specialized skills. A significant motivation is to enable intuitive, stateful editing of 3D worlds through natural language, allowing users to converse with an AI agent to generate, compose, and refine simulation environments while enforcing physical validity.
- **Validating Environments through Policy Learning:** Beyond static quality metrics, the ultimate test of generated environments is their utility for embodied policy training. The motivation includes demonstrating that environments generated by EmbodiedGen V2 can effectively support online RL, leading to improved policy performance in simulation and successful transfer to real-world robots.

In essence, EmbodiedGen V2 aims to serve as a foundational infrastructure that connects generative 3D world generation to the core workflows of embodied policy development, addressing the "data-supply problem" for robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EmbodiedGen V2 proposes a comprehensive architecture centered around a unified sim-ready representation that couples metric geometry, physical validity, interaction semantics, and cross-simulator portability. This representation provides a common interface across the entire generation and editing pipeline, progressively built through several modular components.

#### 3.1. Sim-Ready 3D Asset Generation

The pipeline transforms open-ended text or image inputs into simulation-compatible 3D object assets. It consists of five stages:
1. **Input Preparation:** Text prompts are converted to images using pluggable text-to-image models (e.g., SD3.5, Kolors). Image inputs (unoccluded or partially occluded) are processed by foreground segmentation models (e.g., Rembg, SAM) to extract the target object. A VLM validates semantic correctness and geometric completeness.
2. **3D Generation & Quality Check:** The foreground image feeds into a pluggable image-to-3D model (e.g., TRELLIS, SAM3D, Hunyuan3D), producing 3D Gaussian representations and meshes. Geometric integrity is evaluated, and failures trigger retries.
3. **Geometry Refinement & Texture Baking:** Meshes undergo topological repair and simplification. Multi-view back-projection bakes Gaussian appearance into explicit texture maps. Convex decomposition (using CoACD) is applied to create compact convex collision bodies.
4. **Physical Property Recovery:** A VLM infers real-world scale, mass, and friction coefficients from multi-view renderings and object categories, which are used for metric rescaling and stored as metadata.
5. **Simulation Asset Packaging & Cross-Format Export:** Geometry, appearance, and physical information are assembled into a unified URDF intermediate representation, then automatically converted to simulator-specific formats (e.g., XML for MuJoCo, USD for Isaac Sim) for consistent deployment across platforms. The pipeline also supports deformable body simulation. For partially occluded inputs, 3D-Fixer is used for in-place completion.

#### 3.2. Affordance Autolabeling Pipeline

This pipeline augments sim-ready 3D assets with part-level interaction semantics:
1. **Functional Part Segmentation:** P3-SAM decomposes the mesh into functional part regions at a face-level. Geometry-consistent post-processing and VLM-guided part merging refine these segments to align with functional object structures.
2. **Part-wise Semantic Annotation:** A VLM (GPT-5.4) interprets aligned RGB and part-mask renderings to infer structured attributes for each part, including name, graspability, task-conditioned grasp scenarios, functional labels, and semantic descriptions. A VLM-based checker verifies and revises annotations.
3. **Grasp Generation and Physical Validation:** GraspGen proposes 6-DoF grasp candidates, which are associated with contacted mesh parts and filtered through physics-based execution tests in SAPIEN to ensure stability.

#### 3.3. Task-Driven Interactive Worlds Generation

This module maps natural-language task descriptions to fully instantiated 3D worlds:
1. **Scene Graph Generation:** An LLM decomposes the task into semantic categories (ROBOT, BACKGROUND, CONTEXT, MANIPULATED_OBJS, DISTRACTOR_OBJS). A second LLM organizes these into a shallow rooted Scene Graph with explicit spatial relations (ON, INSIDE, FLOOR, IN) and generates per-object visual descriptions.
2. **Asset Acquisition:** Assets and backgrounds are sourced through either online generation (using the sim-ready asset pipeline) or retrieval from an offline database, balancing diversity and efficiency. All acquired assets conform to the sim-ready contract.
3. **BFS Spatial Placement:** The Scene Graph is instantiated in breadth-first order. A relation-specific sampler proposes child object positions on or within parent geometry, subject to support, collision, and robot reachability constraints. Objects are then settled under gravity in SAPIEN to resolve penetrations and exported as a standardized layout configuration.

#### 3.4. Large-Scale Scenes Generation

This module synthesizes multi-room, navigable simulation-ready indoor scenes:
1. **Task-Conditioned Routing:** A VLM maps task descriptions to discrete controls: room scope (local vs. whole-house) and scene complexity (e.g., Minimalist, Simple, Medium, Detail).
2. **Hierarchical Scene Solving:** A procedural indoor generation framework is reshaped into a simulation-oriented solver. It places furniture in coarse-to-fine order (skeleton, mid-scale, clutter) under floor-plan and cross-room traversability constraints, with complexity tiers controlling density. The generator suppresses non-physics-compatible or purely decorative geometry.
3. **Simulator-Agnostic Canonicalization:** House-level geometry is decomposed into individually loadable furniture and architectural units. CoACD is batch-applied for convex collision proxies. Scene-level canonicalization aligns the house centroid to the world origin. The scene is exported via URDF and reused by the unified format converter.

#### 3.5. Vibe Coding for Stateful Sim-Ready 3D World Editing

This component enables iterative, natural-language-driven editing of simulation-ready 3D worlds:
1. **Architecture:** It comprises an LLM-based agent for dialogue understanding and skill selection, self-contained skills (backed by the generation and solving modules), and a harness that maintains an evolving simulation-ready world state S_t = (G_t, A_t, P_t, H_t) (Scene Graph, assets, poses, history).
2. **Skill Suite and Runtime Contract:** Skills are organized around asset grounding, world composition, stateful editing (spatial-computing), and execution validation (sim-runner). Each skill declares its usage, arguments, outputs, and failure modes.
3. **Agent–Skill Editing Loop:** An iterative Parse–Ground–Invoke–Commit loop processes user instructions. The agent parses the intent, grounds references to the world state, invokes the relevant skill, and if successful, commits a bounded state update while preserving physical feasibility. Failed calls return diagnostics without altering the state.
4. **Instance Grounding and Spatial Editing:** The `Ground` function resolves linguistic references to specific instances in the scene graph. The `spatial-computing` skill uses the offline placement solver to perform collision-aware spatial edits on a room-partitioned floorplan, ensuring every committed edit maintains consistency across simulators.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EmbodiedGen V2 focuses on the quality of its generated outputs, the efficiency of its pipelines, and its utility for downstream embodied AI tasks.

#### 4.1. Sim-Ready Pipeline Quality Evaluation

An ablation study on 200 held-out assets assessed the impact of key components:
- **Human Acceptance and Collision Success:** The full pipeline achieved 96.5% human acceptance (assets judged sim-ready) and 98.6% collision success (successful top-down grasp-and-lift trials).
- **Quality Checker:** Removing the quality checker reduced human acceptance by 5.5 points (to 91.0%) due to uncorrected defects, with minimal time savings.
- **Mesh Fixing:** Omitting mesh fixing drastically increased per-asset runtime by approximately 8× (from 2.6 ± 0.4 min to 21.3 ± 22.8 min) and visual mesh size (from 1.43 MB to 51.63 MB) due to topological defects hindering downstream processing.
- **Convex Decomposition:** Without convex decomposition, collision success decreased from 98.6% to 96.5%, as simulators operated on non-convex visual meshes, potentially leading to unstable contact and grasp failures. The collision mesh size also increased from 0.29 MB to 1.45 MB.
These results indicate that the three components – quality checker, mesh fixing, and convex decomposition – collectively address perceptual quality, deployment efficiency, and contact reliability, enabling high-quality sim-ready asset generation.

#### 4.2. Affordance Autolabeling Pipeline Quality Evaluation

An ablation study on 200 sim-ready assets analyzed the affordance pipeline:
- **Segmentation Pass Rate:** Baseline P3-SAM segmentation achieved 47.0% pass rate. Adding geometry-consistent post-processing improved this to 56.5%. Further integrating VLM-guided part merging increased the rate to 69.5%, demonstrating improved alignment with functional object structures.
- **Semantic Validity Rate:** The VLM-based semantic annotation maintained high validity rates (98.9% to 99.3%) across all settings.
- **Grasp Coverage Rate:** The conditional grasp coverage rate, measuring at least one validated grasp, improved from 66.7% (baseline) to 74.6% with post-processing, and remained competitive at 72.5% with VLM merging.
- **Affordance Pass Rate (End-to-End):** The full configuration achieved a 50.0% end-to-end affordance pass rate, a 19.0 percentage point improvement over the baseline (31.0%).
- **Efficiency:** The full variant was the most efficient at 94.0 ± 30.2 seconds per asset, indicating that the overhead of post-processing and VLM verification is offset by reduced downstream workload due to more accurate part decompositions.

#### 4.3. Task-Driven Interactive Worlds Generation Evaluation

Evaluation on 150 diverse natural-language manipulation tasks:
- **World Generation Success:** 83.3% of the generated interactive worlds were directly usable for downstream simulation without manual modification.
- **Efficiency:** Fully online sequential generation took 47.7 ± 5.4 minutes per world. Background synthesis was the dominant cost (25.5 ± 3.5 min), while object asset generation took 3.6 ± 1.1 min per instance.
- **Asset Quality Assurance (QA):** Automated checks achieved single-attempt pass rates of 76.2% for semantic appearance, 75.9% for mesh geometry, and 91.0% for cross-modal text-to-3D alignment. The generate–verify–retry mechanism ensured that each valid asset required an average of 1.35 attempts.
- **Failure Analysis:** Remaining failures were primarily attributed to object-scale mismatch, local geometry defects, or imperfect initial spatial placement, highlighting areas for future refinement.

#### 4.4. Downstream Closed-Loop Validation

EmbodiedGen V2-generated environments were validated through their use in online reinforcement learning for robot vision-language-action (VLA) policies, as reported in and:
- **Online Trainability:** Using only EmbodiedGen V2-generated scenes, online RL improved VLA policy simulation success from 9.7% to 79.8%, with average completion time decreasing.
- **Scene-Distribution Scaling:** Scaling the number of generated training scenes from N=1 to N=50 increased out-of-distribution (OOD) success from 53.2% to 77.9%, reducing the ID–OOD gap from 41.1 to 2.6 percentage points.
- **Comparison to Hand-Built Scenes:** A policy trained on hand-built SimplerEnv scenes achieved only 36.0% success on EmbodiedGen V2 scenes, in contrast to 96.7% on its original distribution, indicating the distinctiveness and utility of the generated environments.
- **Real-Robot Transfer:** Policies fine-tuned in generated environments (with domain randomization) transferred to real robots, improving task success from 21.7% to 75.0% and reducing dynamics failures from 66.7% to 18.3% across 12 real-world scenes and 240 trials.
- **Cube Stacking:** Further studies showed that EmbodiedGen V2 scenes supported training for cube stacking, raising real-world success from 43.1% to 88.9% across 144 trials.

These results collectively demonstrate that EmbodiedGen V2's generated environments are not only high-quality and efficiently produced but also effectively support closed-loop policy learning and facilitate sim-to-real transfer for embodied agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EmbodiedGen V2 represents a step in advancing generative 3D world building from content creation to a foundational simulation infrastructure for embodied AI. Its primary significance lies in operationalizing the concept of a "sim-ready" world, where metric scale, physical validity, interaction affordances, stateful editability, and consistent simulator interfaces are inherent properties of generated environments. This unification addresses a critical bottleneck in embodied AI development: the scalable provision of diverse, physically plausible, and task-conditioned training and evaluation environments.

The potential impacts of EmbodiedGen V2 include:

- **Accelerated Embodied Policy Development:** By automating the generation of complex, interactive, and diverse simulation environments, EmbodiedGen V2 can significantly accelerate the training and evaluation cycles for robot learning. This reduces the manual effort traditionally required to create and annotate simulation scenes, allowing researchers and developers to focus on policy design and optimization.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The ability to generate a wide array of varied environments with controllable complexity and task configurations can lead to the development of more generalized and robust embodied policies. This is crucial for real-world deployment where agents encounter unforeseen variations.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The empirical validation demonstrating successful sim-to-real transfer of policies trained on EmbodiedGen V2 scenes indicates its utility in bridging the reality gap. By providing high-fidelity, physically consistent simulated experiences, it facilitates the transfer of learned behaviors to physical robots.
- **Democratization of Embodied AI Research:** Lowering the barrier to entry for creating sophisticated simulation environments, particularly through natural language interfaces like Vibe Coding, can democratize embodied AI research. This enables a broader range of users, including those without extensive 3D modeling or simulation expertise, to design and experiment with embodied tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Generative AI Systems:** EmbodiedGen V2 points towards a new paradigm for 3D generative systems, shifting the metric of success beyond mere visual fidelity to whether generated worlds can support closed-loop embodied behavior. This approach can inform the design of future generative models that prioritize executability and interactivity for agent-centric applications.
- **Development of Richer Task Curricula:** The framework for task-driven world generation allows for the systematic creation of varied task scenarios and large-scale, multi-room environments. This capability is essential for designing complex task curricula that progressively challenge and improve the capabilities of embodied agents, including long-horizon navigation and mobile manipulation.

In conclusion, EmbodiedGen V2 provides a scalable and integrated solution for the environment-generation needs of embodied AI, transforming open-ended intent into executable, physically validated, and editable simulation worlds. This work contributes to making generated 3D worlds a more practical and essential interface between human task specification, robot learning, and real-world deployment.
