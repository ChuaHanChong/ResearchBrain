# Intriguing Properties of Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> This study systematically investigates Vision Transformers' (ViTs) robustness and generalization compared to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), demonstrating ViTs' superior resilience to occlusions and various perturbations, a stronger shape bias, and highly transferable features. It shows ViTs maintaining high accuracy even with 80% image occlusion and achieving state-of-the-art performance in transfer learning using a novel feature ensemble strategy.

## Problem

- Despite impressive performance, Vision Transformers' (ViTs) internal mechanisms, learned representations, and robustness characteristics were less understood compared to well-researched CNNs.
- A lack of comprehensive understanding of how ViTs' content-dependent self-attention differs from CNNs' local convolutions in handling image nuisances, shape vs. texture bias, and feature transferability.
- The necessity to characterize ViTs' robustness and generalizability for deployment in safety-critical applications such as autonomous systems and healthcare.

## Method

- A systematic experimental methodology comparing three ViT families (==ViT==, ==DeiT==, ==T2T==) against CNN baselines (==ResNet50==, ==RegNetY==) across fifteen diverse vision datasets.
- Extensive evaluation of robustness to various image nuisances, including severe occlusions (==PatchDrop==, ==PixelDrop==), domain shifts (==Stylized ImageNet==), spatial permutations, and adversarial/natural corruptions.
- Introduction of a novel **'shape token' distillation** approach to enhance and analyze ViTs' shape bias, alongside an investigation into emergent segmentation capabilities without pixel-level supervision.
- Assessment of off-the-shelf feature generalization and transferability using a proposed **feature ensemble strategy** for generic classification and few-shot learning scenarios.

## Results

- ViTs consistently demonstrated superior robustness to severe occlusions, maintaining up to **60%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet with **80%** random occlusion, while CNNs showed near-zero accuracy.
- Shape-biased ViTs achieved human-level performance in shape recognition on Stylized ImageNet and could perform accurate semantic segmentation on PASCAL-VOC12 without pixel-level supervision.
- ViTs exhibited better robustness against common natural corruptions (lower mCE) and both universal and sample-specific adversarial attacks compared to CNNs.
- A proposed feature ensemble strategy for ViTs achieved state-of-the-art performance in transfer learning across various downstream classification tasks and significantly outperformed CNNs in few-shot learning scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ViTs possess a flexible, dynamic, and content-dependent receptive field due to global self-attention, allowing them to adaptively focus on relevant image regions even under severe occlusions.
> - ViTs exhibit an inherently stronger shape-bias than CNNs, which can be further enhanced to enable emergent semantic segmentation of foreground objects using only class-token attention maps.
> - ViTs' robustness to patch permutation suggests that positional encodings play a limited role in 'recovering' global context, indicating the self-attention mechanism is highly effective at capturing relationships irrespective of strict spatial order.
> - Features from deeper ViT blocks are more discriminative, and an ensemble of these features provides highly generalizable representations that significantly outperform CNN features in transfer and few-shot learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The introduction of Vision Transformers (ViTs) marked a significant paradigm shift in computer vision, moving beyond the long-dominant Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). While ViTs have demonstrated impressive performance across a multitude of vision tasks, their internal mechanisms, learned representations, and robustness characteristics are still less understood compared to the decades of research accumulated on CNNs. This paper precisely addresses this crucial gap in knowledge.

The research directly responds to the growing interest in ViTs and the necessity to understand their learned representations, especially for safety-critical applications such as autonomous systems, robotics, and healthcare, where robustness and generalizability are paramount. It positions itself by asking fundamental questions about how the core differences between self-attention (global, content-dependent interactions) and convolutions (local, content-independent interactions) manifest in terms of model behavior under various "nuisances" like occlusions, domain shifts, and perturbations.

The paper acknowledges concurrent works that also began exploring ViT robustness, indicating a collective research effort within the community to characterize these new models. However, this study distinguishes itself through its *systematic and extensive* experimental setup, encompassing three ViT families across fifteen vision datasets, and its deep dive into specific properties like shape bias, permutation invariance, and the nature of off-the-shelf features.

A significant aspect of its contribution to the landscape is its direct challenge to established understandings about CNNs. For instance, it builds upon the work of Geirhos et al., who showed CNNs are often biased towards texture over shape. This paper investigates whether ViTs exhibit a similar bias or offer a more shape-centric representation, which is highly relevant for achieving human-like visual understanding. By demonstrating ViTs' ability to encode shape and even perform segmentation without pixel-level supervision, it pushes the boundaries of emergent properties in vision models.

Finally, the study extends the concept of analyzing and transferring "off-the-shelf" features, a well-established practice with CNNs, to ViTs. It explores the generalization capabilities of ViT features across diverse downstream tasks and few-shot learning settings, aiming to establish ViTs as the new default for feature extraction, building on their ability to model global context and preserve structural information. In essence, this work aims to provide a comprehensive mechanistic understanding of ViTs' advantages, thereby solidifying their role and guiding future research in computer vision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate and characterize the "intriguing properties" of Vision Transformers (ViTs) in terms of their robustness against various image nuisances and their generalizability to new tasks and domains. The authors are driven by a motivation to deeply understand the fundamental operational differences between ViTs and CNNs and how these differences translate into practical advantages.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Understanding ViT Characteristics for Safety-Critical Applications:** With ViTs gaining rapid adoption, especially in fields like autonomous driving and healthcare, it is crucial to understand if their learned representations are inherently robust and generalizable. This paper aims to provide this understanding, which is vital for deploying these models in real-world, high-stakes scenarios.
- **Investigating the Role of Self-Attention vs. Convolutions:** The core motivation stems from the architectural divergence between content-dependent, global self-attention mechanisms in ViTs and content-independent, local convolutions in CNNs. The authors seek to explore how this difference impacts the models' ability to cope with common image corruptions, occlusions, and distributional shifts.
- **Evaluating Robustness to Nuisances:** A central objective is to quantitatively assess ViTs' resilience to a wide array of image nuisances, including:
 - Severe occlusions (random, foreground, background patches, and pixels).
 - Domain shifts (e.g., stylized images that remove texture cues).
 - Spatial permutations of image patches.
 - Adversarial perturbations (universal adversarial patches and sample-specific attacks like FGSM and PGD).
 - Common natural corruptions (e.g., noise, blur, contrast).
- **Characterizing Shape vs. Texture Bias:** Building on previous findings that CNNs are biased towards texture, a key objective is to determine if ViTs exhibit a stronger shape bias, which is often considered more aligned with human perception and critical for robustness to distributional shifts. This also involves exploring methods to enhance shape bias in ViTs.
- **Exploring Emergent Properties:** The research aims to uncover unexpected benefits or "emerging properties" of ViTs, such as the ability to perform accurate semantic segmentation without explicit pixel-level supervision when trained with a focus on shape.
- **Assessing Generalization and Transferability of Features:** A significant objective is to evaluate the utility of "off-the-shelf" ViT features for downstream tasks. This includes analyzing the discriminative power of features from different model layers/blocks and developing strategies (like feature ensembles) to maximize their transferability for generic classification and few-shot learning scenarios across diverse datasets.
- **Understanding the Dynamic Receptive Field:** The authors seek to demonstrate that ViTs' superior robustness is largely due to their flexible and dynamic receptive fields, which can adapt based on image content to focus on relevant information, unlike the fixed receptive fields of CNNs.
- **Developing Novel Design Choices:** The paper also aims to introduce practical innovations, such as a dedicated "shape token" for architectural modification and a feature ensemble strategy, to enhance ViT capabilities and reveal their potential.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond merely reporting high performance for ViTs and to provide a deeper, systematic, and comparative understanding of their fundamental characteristics, thereby informing both theoretical insights and practical applications in computer vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a systematic and extensive experimental methodology to investigate the properties of Vision Transformers, involving multiple ViT architectures and comparing them rigorously against high-performing CNN baselines across a wide array of visual tasks and conditions.

**Models Under Study:**
The core ViT families investigated include:
- **ViT:** The original Vision Transformer.
- **DeiT:** Data-efficient Image Transformers, which introduced distillation tokens.
- **T2T:** Tokens-to-Token ViT, which aims to improve patch embedding.
For comparison, the primary CNN baseline is **ResNet50**. Additional CNNs like **RegNetY** and other ViT variants like **Swin Transformer** are also included in the appendix for broader comparison. Most models are pre-trained on ImageNet.

**Datasets:**
- **ImageNet (ILSVRC’12):** Used for pre-training models and for validation during robustness experiments.
- **Stylized ImageNet (SIN):** A version of ImageNet with texture cues removed, crucial for shape-bias analysis.
- **Diverse Downstream Datasets:** For transfer learning and few-shot learning, datasets include fine-grained recognition (CUB, Flowers, Aircraft, Fungi), texture classification (DTD), traffic sign recognition (GTSRB), species classification (iNaturalist), and scene recognition (Places365).
- **Meta-Dataset:** A large-scale benchmark designed for few-shot learning across various domains.
- **PASCAL-VOC12:** Used for evaluating automated segmentation.

**Robustness Evaluation Techniques:**

1. **Occlusion Modeling (PatchDrop and PixelDrop):**
 - **PatchDrop:** Image patches are masked (set to zero) based on:
 - **Random PatchDrop:** Randomly selected patches.
 - **Salient (foreground) PatchDrop:** Patches covering top Q% of foreground information (identified via DINO).
 - **Non-salient (background) PatchDrop:** Patches covering lowest Q% of foreground information.
 - **Random PixelDrop:** Individual pixels are randomly masked to decouple occlusion from ViT's grid operation.
 - **Varying Patch Sizes and Offset:** Experiments in the appendix explore robustness to occlusions with different grid sizes and spatial offsets to the masks.
 - **Analysis:** Top-1 accuracy is measured under varying levels of "information loss" (ratio of dropped patches/pixels). Attention maps are visualized, and correlation coefficients between features of original and occluded images are calculated.

2. **Shape vs. Texture Bias Analysis:**
 - **Training without Local Texture:** ViT models (DeiT-Tiny, DeiT-Small) are trained on Stylized ImageNet (SIN) without data augmentations to force reliance on shape. Their "shape-bias" is quantified using the metric from Geirhos et al..
 - **Shape Distillation:** A novel approach is introduced where a dedicated "shape token" is added to the DeiT architecture. This token is then trained via knowledge distillation from a shape-biased CNN (ResNet50-SIN) to specifically encode shape information, while a standard "class token" handles texture. Cosine similarity between class and shape tokens is measured to confirm distinct feature learning.
 - **Automated Segmentation:** The Jaccard Index is used to evaluate the ability of shape-biased ViTs (using class-token attention, similar to DINO) to segment foreground objects on PASCAL-VOC12, even though they were only trained with image-level labels.

3. **Sensitivity to Spatial Structure (Permutation Invariance):**
 - **Patch Shuffling:** Input image patches are randomly shuffled to destroy global structural information.
 - **Analysis:** Top-1 accuracy is measured, and the impact of positional encoding on robustness to permutations is assessed by comparing models with and without it, and with varying patch sizes.

4. **Robustness to Adversarial and Natural Perturbations:**
 - **Common Corruptions:** Models are evaluated on the mean Corruption Error (mCE) across various synthetic corruptions (e.g., noise, blur).
 - **Adversarial Attacks:**
 - **Universal Adversarial Patch Attack:** Unbounded changes to pixel values in a specific region.
 - **Sample-Specific Attacks:** Single-step Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) and multi-step Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) attacks, bounded by an L∞ norm.
 - **Analysis:** Performance (Top-1 accuracy) is compared between ViTs and CNNs, and also between models trained on ImageNet vs. Stylized ImageNet (to observe the shape-bias vs. robustness trade-off).

**Generalization Evaluation Techniques (Off-the-shelf Features):**

1. **Effective Token Identification:** The discriminative ability of the class token from each transformer block is measured by training a linear classifier on its output.
2. **Feature Ensemble Strategy:** Based on the observation that deeper block tokens are more discriminative, an ensemble approach is proposed where class tokens (and optionally averaged patch tokens) from the last few blocks are concatenated to form a richer feature representation.
3. **Transfer Learning:** A linear classifier is trained on top of the extracted ViT features (using the proposed ensemble or single tokens) for various classification tasks across the downstream datasets.
4. **Few-Shot Learning (FSL):** Using Meta-Dataset, ViT features are extracted, and a linear classifier is learned on the support set for each query, following standard FSL protocols.

The methodology is meticulously designed to provide quantitative evidence for ViTs' properties, employing visual analysis (attention maps, segmentation masks) and statistical measures (correlation coefficients, Jaccard Index, mCE) to support the findings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental analysis revealed several intriguing and favorable properties of Vision Transformers, consistently demonstrating their advantages over CNNs in specific aspects of robustness and generalization.

1. **Exceptional Robustness to Occlusions:**
 - ViTs, across all studied families (ViT, DeiT, T2T), exhibit significantly higher robustness to severe occlusions compared to CNNs like ResNet50 and RegNetY. For instance, DeiT-S maintained approximately 60% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet even when 80% of the image content was randomly occluded, a scenario where CNNs showed near-zero accuracy.
 - This robustness extends to occlusions of salient (foreground) and non-salient (background) regions, as well as random pixel masking.
 - Analysis of attention maps showed that deeper ViT layers dynamically adjust their focus to attend to the remaining non-occluded regions, indicating a flexible and content-dependent receptive field.
 - Class tokens from ViTs were found to be highly robust to information loss, maintaining a strong correlation even with severely occluded inputs, unlike ResNet50 features.
 - ViTs also demonstrated the ability to exploit discriminative information regardless of its position, achieving high accuracy even when a rescaled image was placed in different corners of a larger black background, further confirming their dynamic receptive field.

2. **Stronger Shape-Bias and Automated Segmentation:**
 - ViTs pre-trained on ImageNet already showed a higher shape-bias compared to CNNs of similar capacity (e.g., DeiT-S vs. ResNet50).
 - When trained on Stylized ImageNet (SIN) without local texture cues, ViTs achieved human-level performance in shape recognition, significantly outperforming CNNs trained similarly.
 - A novel "shape token" mechanism, introduced via distillation into DeiT, enabled the model to learn distinct shape-related features while simultaneously encoding texture information with its class token. The low cosine similarity between these tokens confirmed their unique roles.
 - A remarkable emergent property was that these shape-biased ViTs could perform accurate semantic segmentation of foreground objects without any pixel-level supervision, simply by leveraging the attention maps of the class token. This capability was comparable to self-supervised methods like DINO.

3. **Permutation Invariance (Limited Role of Positional Encoding):**
 - ViTs demonstrated high permutation invariance to the spatial ordering of image patches. Shuffling patches only moderately affected performance, indicating that ViTs do not heavily rely on positional encoding to "recover" global image context.
 - Performance degradation was observed when patch sizes were varied during training, suggesting that robustness is more tied to the content within patches and the dynamic interaction capabilities of self-attention rather than strict spatial order from positional encodings.

4. **Robustness to Adversarial and Natural Perturbations:**
 - ViTs showed better robustness than CNNs against common natural corruptions (e.g., noise, blur) with lower mean Corruption Error (mCE).
 - They were also more robust to both universal adversarial patch attacks and sample-specific adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD), even with fewer parameters than comparative CNNs.
 - However, a trade-off was observed: training ViTs (and CNNs) to achieve a higher shape-bias (e.g., on SIN) made them more vulnerable to both common corruptions and adversarial attacks, consistent with prior research.

5. **Effective Off-the-Shelf Features for Generalization:**
 - An analysis of block-wise class tokens revealed that deeper blocks within ViT models produce more discriminative features, leading to higher classification accuracy on ImageNet.
 - A proposed feature ensemble strategy, which concatenates class tokens from the last few blocks (e.g., blocks 9-12) of a single pre-trained ViT model (DeiT-S), achieved state-of-the-art transfer learning performance across a wide range of downstream classification tasks (fine-grained, texture, scene recognition).
 - ViT features, especially with the ensemble strategy, significantly outperformed CNN baselines in few-shot learning tasks on diverse datasets like Meta-Dataset. This improvement was particularly noticeable on sketch datasets (QuickDraw), aligning with ViTs' enhanced shape-bias.

In summary, the main findings highlight that ViTs' content-dependent, global attention mechanisms provide them with a flexible and dynamic receptive field, leading to superior robustness to occlusions and perturbations, a stronger shape-bias akin to human vision, and highly transferable features compared to CNNs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "Intriguing Properties of Vision Transformers" have profound significance for the field of computer vision and hold substantial potential impact across various applications and future research directions.

1. **Deeper Understanding of Vision Transformers:** This paper moves beyond simply reporting high performance for ViTs and provides a comprehensive, systematic investigation into *why* they are effective. By dissecting their behavior under various challenging conditions and comparing them against CNNs, the study offers crucial insights into the fundamental advantages of self-attention – particularly its flexible, content-dependent, and dynamic receptive field – over fixed, local convolutions. This deeper understanding is vital for guiding future architectural designs and theoretical analyses of ViTs.

2. **Enhanced Robustness for Safety-Critical Applications:** The demonstrated superior robustness of ViTs to severe occlusions, various perturbations (adversarial and natural), and positional shifts is a game-changer for safety-critical domains. In autonomous driving, robotics, and medical imaging, models often encounter incomplete, corrupted, or unusual inputs. ViTs' ability to maintain high accuracy under such conditions could lead to more reliable and trustworthy AI systems, significantly reducing risks in real-world deployments.

3. **Towards Human-like Vision and Interpretability:** The strong evidence for ViTs' shape-bias, even achieving human-level performance in shape recognition, marks a significant step towards more human-aligned artificial vision. Unlike texture-biased CNNs, models prioritizing shape are often considered more robust to distributional shifts and potentially more interpretable, as shape is a fundamental characteristic for human object recognition. This could foster the development of AI models that reason about visual information in a manner more akin to humans.

4. **Novel Approaches for Weakly Supervised Learning:** The discovery that shape-biased ViTs can automatically perform semantic segmentation without any pixel-level supervision is a highly impactful emergent property. This finding opens new avenues for weakly supervised learning, potentially drastically reducing the laborious and expensive need for pixel-level annotations in segmentation tasks. Future research can build upon this to develop more efficient and scalable annotation-free segmentation methods.

5. **New Baseline for Feature Extraction and Transfer Learning:** The paper establishes a new, highly effective baseline for "off-the-shelf" feature extraction by proposing an ensemble strategy of class tokens from deeper ViT blocks. This approach significantly outperforms CNN features in both generic transfer learning and challenging few-shot learning scenarios across diverse datasets. This suggests that ViTs are poised to replace CNNs as the default choice for feature extractors in many transfer learning applications, leading to higher accuracy and better generalization in new domains.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper explicitly outlines several intriguing future research directions:
 - **Combining Diverse Cues:** How to effectively combine the diverse cues modeled by separate tokens within a single ViT (e.g., shape token and class token) to maximize their complementary benefits.
 - **Integrating with Self-Supervision:** Exploring the integration of shape-bias with self-supervised methods like DINO to further enhance auto-segmentation capabilities and generalize them (e.g., using texture-based local views and shape-based global views during DINO training).
 - **Addressing Data Biases:** Acknowledging the ethical implications of using ImageNet (with its Western bias, stereotypes, and privacy concerns), the authors commit to using newer, ethically curated datasets in future work, promoting responsible AI development.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances our understanding of Vision Transformers, revealing their robust, flexible, and generalizable nature. Its findings have direct implications for enhancing the reliability of AI systems, fostering more human-aligned vision, and streamlining various computer vision tasks through innovative feature extraction and weakly supervised learning paradigms.
