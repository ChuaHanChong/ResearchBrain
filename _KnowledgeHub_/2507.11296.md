# Diffusion-Based Imaginative Coordination for Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Fudan University and King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST) developed a diffusion-based framework that uses future video imagination to implicitly coordinate bimanual robotic arms. This method improved success rates by 24.9% on the ALOHA benchmark and 32.5% in real-world experiments while maintaining a control frequency of 35.8 Hz.

## Problem

- Bimanual manipulation involves high-dimensional action spaces and complex coordination, making it challenging for robots to learn and execute tasks.
- Multimodal behaviors are common in bimanual tasks, where multiple valid action sequences can achieve the same goal, posing difficulties for policy learning.
- Existing video prediction-based methods often face computational inefficiency, hindering their use in real-time robotic control, while other approaches may lack flexibility or rely on task-specific designs.

## Method

- A unified ==transformer-based diffusion model== is proposed to jointly optimize action prediction and ==future visual observation (video) forecasting==, conditioned on current observations.
- A ==multi-frame latent prediction== strategy is used, where future frames are compressed into compact ==video tokens== by a pre-trained tokenizer, reducing computational complexity.
- A ==unidirectional attention mechanism== is implemented within the diffusion model's decoder, allowing future video tokens to attend to past actions during training, but decoupling action prediction from video prediction during inference to enhance efficiency.

## Results

- The method achieved an average success rate of **71.9%** on the ALOHA benchmark and **56.3%** on RoboTwin, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by up to **24.9%** and **14.0%** respectively.
- In real-world bimanual tasks, the approach demonstrated a **32.5%** increase in average success rate over baselines, maintaining a high control frequency of **35.8 Hz**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that video prediction significantly improves task success, particularly for sequentially coordinated tasks, and the multi-frame latent prediction with unidirectional attention is critical for both performance and inference efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging future video prediction as an "imaginative interface" enables implicit coordination between robotic arms, allowing them to anticipate interactions and align objectives for improved task success.
> - Jointly learning to predict both actions and future visual states within a diffusion model provides a robust way to model multimodal behaviors and environmental dynamics in bimanual tasks.
> - Designing a unidirectional attention mechanism effectively mitigates the computational burden of video prediction at inference time, making complex, foresight-enabled policies viable for real-time robotic control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual manipulation is a recognized area of research in robotics, with applications spanning industrial automation, surgical procedures, and household services. Compared to single-arm systems, bimanual robots offer increased dexterity, efficiency, and versatility, which are advantageous for complex, heavy, or precision-oriented tasks. However, this domain presents challenges, primarily due to the high-dimensional action space involved and the intricate coordination required between the two robotic arms. The phenomenon of multimodal behavior, where multiple valid ways exist to complete a task, is also more pronounced in bimanual scenarios.

Prior research has approached these challenges through several avenues. To address multimodality, generative models such as Conditional Variational Autoencoders (CVAE) and diffusion models have been explored to capture diverse behaviors. For coordination, some studies have introduced inductive biases, such as constraining functional roles (e.g., one arm for manipulation, another for stabilization), regulating task execution, or employing auxiliary modules for dynamic action scheduling. While these methods have demonstrated performance in specific contexts, they often exhibit limitations in terms of flexibility, task specificity, or reliance on pre-trained skill primitives.

A parallel line of research involves leveraging video prediction for representation learning and control, particularly in robot learning. Video prediction models are noted for their capacity to capture dynamic and behavioral information. However, their application has largely been concentrated on unimanual manipulation tasks, leaving their potential for enhancing bimanual coordination relatively unexplored. Furthermore, video prediction-based models typically encounter computational efficiency issues, which can impede real-time robotic control.

This research aims to address the limitations of existing bimanual manipulation methods by proposing an approach that leverages video prediction for implicit coordination while simultaneously addressing the computational efficiency concerns. It seeks to integrate future imagination into a visuomotor policy as a mechanism for both arms to anticipate interactions, align objectives, and achieve a coordinated outcome, without requiring intricate, task-specific designs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a bimanual manipulation model that can effectively address the complexities of coordination and high-dimensional action spaces without relying on task-specific designs or pre-defined primitives. The motivation stems from the observation that humans employ foresight and imagination of future scenarios to plan coordinated and precise actions.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Enhancing Bimanual Coordination through Future Imagination**: The central motivation is to explore an implicit coordination method for bimanual manipulation by leveraging the prediction of future visual states (video prediction). The hypothesis is that conventional strategies, which often rely solely on shared current observations, are limited by a lack of foresight into future interactions. By enabling both arms to "imagine" future scenarios, the system can anticipate interactions, align individual arm objectives, and reach a consensus on actions, thereby improving overall coordination. This represents a departure from explicit coordination rules or shared observations.

2. **Developing a Unified Diffusion-Based Framework**: The paper aims to bridge the gap in bimanual manipulation by proposing a novel, unified diffusion-based framework. This framework is designed to jointly optimize both video prediction and action prediction. The intention is to leverage the strengths of diffusion models for multimodal modeling while using video prediction as a mechanism to improve bimanual coordination. This integration seeks to create a shared future imagination space where visual perception, environment dynamics modeling, and action control are co-optimized.

3. **Improving Computational Efficiency**: A known challenge with video prediction-based action models, particularly in robotics, is computational inefficiency. This work aims to mitigate this issue, especially for high-frequency robotic tasks. Two specific strategies are proposed:
 - **Multi-frame Latent Prediction Strategy**: To reduce redundancy and computational load, the framework predicts future states using video tokens compressed by a video tokenizer, rather than directly predicting raw frame pixels or image tokens. This approach is intended to preserve task-relevant features in a more compact latent space.
 - **Unidirectional Attention Mechanism**: To accelerate inference, a unidirectional attention mechanism is introduced. This design ensures that during training, video prediction is conditioned on action, but crucially, action prediction remains independent of video prediction. This independence allows for the omission of video prediction during inference, which is projected to significantly enhance computational efficiency and reduce latency, enabling higher control frequencies essential for real-time robotic execution.

4. **Demonstrating Generalizability and Robustness**: A broader objective is to develop a method that performs robustly across various bimanual manipulation tasks and environments. This includes evaluating performance on diverse simulated benchmarks and validating the approach in real-world robotic settings to confirm its practical applicability and effectiveness beyond idealized simulations.

In summary, the motivation is to imbue bimanual robotic systems with a human-like capacity for foresight to improve coordination, packaged within an efficient, unified learning framework based on diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method frames bimanual manipulation as a conditional generation problem, utilizing a unified transformer-based diffusion model for joint action prediction and video forecasting.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

- **Bimanual Imitation Learning**: The objective is to learn a bimanual policy $\pi$ from expert demonstrations, where each trajectory $\tau$ consists of sequences of observations $(o_t)$, proprioceptive states $(p_t)$, and bimanual actions $(a_t)$. The policy is designed to predict a future action sequence $a_{t:t+N}$ given the current observation $(o_t, p_t)$.
- **Bimanual Manipulation with Video Prediction**: The framework extends this by jointly modeling the prediction of both future action sequences $a_{t:t+N}$ and future visual observations $o_{t+1:t+N+1}$ from the current observation $(o_t, p_t)$. This joint modeling is intended to create a shared "future imagination space."

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The model architecture (depicted in Figure 2) consists of several integrated components:

- **Observation Tokenization and Embedding**:
 - **Visual Input**: A single-view RGB image is processed by a ResNet-18-based 2D image encoder to extract a 2D feature map. This map is then flattened and projected into a sequence of $d$-dimensional visual tokens, augmented with 2D positional encoding.
 - **Proprioceptive State**: The robot's proprioceptive state is mapped to the same latent space via a linear layer.
 - **Combined Observation Representation**: The visual tokens and the proprioceptive embedding are concatenated to form the final observation representation. These tokens are then fed into a Transformer encoder, which generates an observation embedding ($e$) that serves as the conditioning input for the diffusion model.

- **Future Frame Representation**:
 - To enable efficient video prediction, future frames are converted into a compact, tokenized representation.
 - Future frames are uniformly sampled over time to capture sufficient visual variation.
 - These sampled frames are then encoded using a pre-trained Cosmos video tokenizer. This tokenizer compresses the video into latent features, reducing temporal redundancy and computational complexity compared to pixel-level or image-token prediction.
 - The latent features undergo a patchification process, dividing them into non-overlapping patches to yield final tokens. Each future token is enriched with a spatio-temporal position embedding.

- **Conditional Diffusion Modeling**:
 - A conditional diffusion model iteratively refines noisy representations of future actions ($a_k$) and visual tokens ($v_k$), conditioned on the observation embedding ($e$) and the current diffusion step ($k$).
 - Noise is injected into clean action sequences and future frame tokens. These noisy representations are projected into a latent space.
 - A shared Transformer decoder processes these latent embeddings. It uses cross-attention to incorporate information from the observation embedding and the diffusion step encoding.
 - The decoder's output is processed by two separate prediction heads: an action head (linear layer) reconstructs the denoised action sequence, and a prediction head (MLP) recovers the clean future visual tokens.

- **Unidirectional Attention Mechanism**:
 - Within the self-attention layer of the denoising decoder's cross-attention module, a unidirectional attention mechanism is implemented (illustrated in Figure 3).
 - **Action Tokens**: These tokens attend exclusively to themselves, meaning their prediction is not directly influenced by future visual tokens.
 - **Future Frame Tokens**: These tokens attend to both themselves and "historical" (prior) action tokens. This allows the model to capture the causal relationship between actions and their visual outcomes, facilitating the learning of environment dynamics during training.
 - **Inference Advantage**: This design enables video prediction to be skipped entirely during inference, as action generation is decoupled from future visual tokens. This reduces computational overhead, lowers inference latency, and improves control frequency, which are critical for real-time robotic control.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

- **Training Objective**:
 - At each training step, a random timestep $t$ and diffusion step $k$ are sampled. Noise $\epsilon = (\epsilon_a, \epsilon_v)$ is added to the clean action chunk and future visual tokens.
 - The model is trained to predict this added noise.
 - The overall loss function ($L$) is a weighted sum of an $\ell_1$ loss for action reconstruction (for $\epsilon_a$) and a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss for visual token prediction (for $\epsilon_v$). A weighting coefficient ($w$) balances the importance of video prediction.
 - DDIM is used as the noise scheduler with a square cosine schedule, employing 100 diffusion steps.

- **Inference and Execution**:
 - During inference, the video prediction branch is omitted. Only action generation is performed.
 - An initial action sequence is sampled from Gaussian noise ($a_K \sim N(0, I)$).
 - The model iteratively refines these action tokens through the reverse diffusion process.
 - The predicted action chunk is executed in an open-loop manner until completion, after which the next action chunk is generated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed method involved comparisons with several baseline approaches across two simulated benchmarks (ALOHA and RoboTwin) and a real-world setting.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Overall Performance**: The method demonstrated an increase in success rate over the baseline ACT, with a 24.9% increase on ALOHA, an 11.1% increase on RoboTwin, and a 32.5% increase in real-world experiments (Figure 1).

- **ALOHA Benchmark (Table 1)**:
 - Achieved an average success rate of 71.9%, surpassing all baselines (ACT, Diffusion Policy).
 - Outperformed baselines on both scripted and human-collected demonstrations, indicating its capacity to capture multimodal human behaviors. For instance, on "Transfer Cube Scripted," it achieved 95.9% success.

- **RoboTwin Benchmark (Table 2 & 3)**:
 - Recorded an average success rate of 56.3% across 16 bimanual manipulation tasks, representing a 14.0% increase over 3D Diffusion Policy, an 11.4% increase over ACT, and a 5.2% increase over RDT-1B.
 - Showed performance gains in long-horizon coordination tasks (e.g., "block handover," "put apple cabinet") and robustness to visual distractions (e.g., "pick apple messy").
 - Achieved the highest success rate across all three categorized bimanual task types ("Dominant-select," "Sync-bimanual," "Seq-coordinate"), with a notable lead in "Seq-coordinate" tasks (50.8%).

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Future Prediction (Figure 5)**:
 - The full model consistently outperformed the variant without video prediction across all benchmarks, confirming the utility of video prediction in improving task success rates (e.g., +7.3% on ALOHA).
 - Video prediction provided a substantial 6.2% increase in "Seq-Coordinated" tasks, attributed to improved temporal dependencies and alignment of arm actions. A marginal decline of 0.7% was observed in "Sync-Bimanual" tasks.

- **Choice of Future Prediction Strategy (Table 4)**:
 - Predicting multi-frame latent features yielded the highest success rate (71.9%), outperforming next-frame latent prediction (71.3%) and next-frame pixel prediction (68.7%). Latent features generally improved performance compared to raw pixels.

- **Choice of Attention Mechanism (Table 5)**:
 - The proposed unidirectional attention mechanism achieved the highest success rate (71.9%) and lowest inference time (0.03s).
 - Full attention yielded a slightly lower success rate (70.8%) with double the inference time (0.06s). Decoupled attention had similar inference time but lower success (70.1%).
 - Compared to GR-MG, the method achieved higher success and faster inference.

- **Impact of Video Prediction Weight (Table 6)**:
 - A video prediction weight ($\omega$) of 0.2 resulted in the optimal performance (71.9%), suggesting an effective balance between action fitting and video prediction.

- **Impact of Video Prediction Horizon (Table 7)**:
 - Predicting 40 future frames yielded the best results on ALOHA, performing better than predicting 20 or 100 frames.

- **Choice of Chunk Size (Figure 6)**:
 - A moderate chunk size generally resulted in optimal performance. Video prediction consistently improved performance irrespective of the chunk size. Optimal chunk size was also found to be task-dependent.

- **Additional Comparisons (Table 12)**:
 - The method achieved an average success rate of 65.3% on ALOHA human demonstrations, outperforming other transformer-based baselines like InterACT (63.0%) and ARP (59.4%).

- **Multi-task Setting (Table 13)**:
 - When extended to a language-conditioned multi-task policy, the method outperformed RDT-1B with an 8.0% increase in average success rate across three representative RoboTwin tasks.

- **Video Token Type (Table 15)**:
 - Discrete video tokens (DV) from Cosmos Tokenizer led to higher action prediction success (73.7%) compared to continuous video tokens (CV, 70.7%).

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation (Table 8)

- **Performance**: The method consistently achieved the highest average success rate of 60.0% across 4 complex bimanual tasks ("Water Wipe," "Cup Stack," "Can Handover," "Coffee Stir") compared to ACT (27.5%) and Diffusion Policy (7.5%).
- **Control Frequency**: It maintained a control frequency of 35.8 Hz, which was the highest among the tested methods, attributed to the efficiency gains from the unidirectional attention mechanism during inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic bimanual manipulation by introducing a diffusion-based framework that integrates future imagination into visuomotor policy learning. The work addresses several core challenges in bimanual robotics, offering a method that is not reliant on intricate, task-specific designs.

The key significance points include:

- **Implicit Coordination Mechanism**: The introduction of video prediction as an "imaginative interface" for implicit coordination represents a conceptual shift from approaches that depend on explicit rules or shared current observations. By anticipating future states, both robotic arms can align their objectives and actions more effectively, which is particularly beneficial for complex, sequentially coordinated tasks.
- **Unified and Efficient Learning Framework**: The proposed unified diffusion-based framework for jointly optimizing video and action prediction provides a coherent approach to perception, prediction, and control. The multi-frame latent prediction strategy and the unidirectional attention mechanism are instrumental in enhancing computational efficiency, allowing the model to perform real-time robotic control by omitting video prediction during inference. This aspect is critical for practical applications requiring high control frequencies.
- **Demonstrated Generalizability and Robustness**: The consistent performance increases observed across diverse simulated benchmarks (ALOHA and RoboTwin, covering fine-grained manipulation, long-horizon coordination, and robustness to distractions) and real-world experiments indicate the method's robustness and potential for broader application beyond specific task configurations. The strong performance in "Seq-coordinate" tasks highlights its capacity for complex sequential reasoning.
- **Advancement in Visuomotor Policy Learning**: By leveraging the representational power of diffusion models for multimodal behavior capture and integrating predictive foresight, the research advances the capabilities of visuomotor policies for complex robotic tasks.

The potential impact of this work extends to various domains requiring advanced bimanual robotic capabilities, such as:

- **Industrial Automation**: Enabling robots to perform more complex assembly, handling, and manufacturing tasks.
- **Surgical Robotics**: Enhancing precision and coordination in robotic-assisted surgeries.
- **Service Robotics**: Improving the dexterity and autonomy of robots in household and service environments, allowing them to handle a wider array of domestic chores and assistance tasks.

While the method demonstrates notable improvements, the paper also identifies areas for future exploration, such as integrating stronger visual encoders for fine-grained tasks, investigating arm-specific representations for loosely coupled tasks, and optimizing keyframe selection for prediction. These aspects suggest a foundation for continued research to further enhance bimanual manipulation systems.
