# RISE: Reliable Improvement in Self-Evolving Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The RISE framework enables Vision-Language Models to reliably and efficiently improve their multimodal reasoning capabilities using unlabeled images. It achieves this by addressing issues of feedback delay, low-quality self-generated questions, and skill diversity collapse in self-evolving systems, resulting in average performance gains of approximately 4% on prominent VLM benchmarks for both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B models.

## Problem

- Continuous improvement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is constrained by the high cost and scarcity of human-annotated datasets for complex reasoning tasks.
- Prior self-evolving VLM methods exhibited inefficient feedback loops between questioner and solver roles, leading to slow and misaligned capability improvements.
- Self-generated supervision in VLMs often suffered from degradation in question quality (e.g., semantic errors, unanswerable questions) and a narrow diversity of question types (mode collapse).

## Method

- Implemented a fine-grained role alternation strategy, dividing update steps into shorter phases to accelerate feedback and synchronization between the questioner and solver.
- Introduced a quality supervisor, sharing parameters with the current solver, to validate self-generated questions and verify the reliability of pseudo-labels, ensuring high-quality learning signals.
- Employed a skill-aware dynamic balancing mechanism, utilizing skill bonuses to encourage diverse question generation and skill-stratified sampling for balanced solver training.

## Results

- Achieved consistent performance improvements, increasing the weighted average score of Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct by +4.09 and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct by +3.99 across seven diverse VLM benchmarks.
- Successfully maintained high question quality and a balanced skill distribution throughout the self-evolution process, preventing the degradation and mode collapse observed in methods without explicit controls.
- Ablation studies confirmed that fine-grained role alternation enhanced efficiency, while the quality supervisor prevented performance collapse in faster interaction settings, and skill-aware balancing ensured comprehensive skill acquisition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Uncontrolled self-evolving VLMs face issues of question quality degradation and mode collapse in skill distribution, hindering sustained improvement.
> - Fine-grained role alternation significantly enhances the efficiency of self-evolution by shortening feedback loops, but requires robust quality control to prevent the generation of low-quality, exploitative questions.
> - Integrating a quality supervisor, co-located with the solver, effectively maintains the reliability of self-generated questions and pseudo-labels, stabilizing the self-evolution process and allowing efficiency gains to be realized.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated advanced multimodal reasoning and understanding capabilities, primarily through extensive training on large-scale, human-annotated datasets. However, the continuous improvement of these models, particularly for complex reasoning tasks, is increasingly constrained by the high cost and diminishing availability of high-quality human-curated supervision. The manual creation of datasets involving carefully designed questions, answers, and feedback signals for reasoning-intensive multimodal tasks presents a significant scalability challenge.

This constraint has motivated exploration into self-evolving learning paradigms, where models autonomously generate learning signals and improve their capabilities without substantial human annotation. This concept has gained traction in the domain of large language models (LLMs), with frameworks such as R-Zero and Absolute Zero employing a dual-role closed-loop mechanism where a "questioner" proposes tasks and a "solver" attempts to resolve them.

Recent research has begun to transfer this self-evolving paradigm to the VLM domain, with works like VisPlay and MM-Zero demonstrating that multimodal models can benefit from self-generated data. While this transfer offers a promising direction for leveraging abundant unlabeled images, existing VLM self-evolving methods face specific challenges. These include issues related to inefficient role interaction, degradation in the quality of self-generated questions, and a tendency for the types of questions generated to become narrow, limiting the breadth of acquired skills. RISE addresses these specific limitations, aiming to enhance the reliability and efficiency of VLM self-evolution by introducing mechanisms to mitigate these observed problems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reliable self-evolving framework for vision-language models (VLMs) that enables sustained capability improvement using unlabeled images, thereby reducing reliance on costly human-annotated multimodal supervision. The authors identify three major limitations in current VLM self-evolving methods that motivate the proposed solution:

1. **Coarse-Grained Role Alternation and Delayed Feedback:** Existing dual-role self-evolving frameworks for VLMs often optimize the questioner and solver roles in lengthy, separate phases. This coarse-grained alternation means that the feedback between question generation and solver adaptation is significantly delayed. The questioner might adapt to a stale solver policy, and the solver might train on data generated from an out-of-sync questioner. This asynchronous interaction reduces the overall efficiency of the self-evolving loop, hindering the timely coordination and alignment of capabilities between the two roles. The motivation is to shorten this feedback loop to improve efficiency and accelerate capability alignment.

2. **Degradation of Generated Question Quality:** In the multimodal context, generating meaningful, image-grounded questions is inherently more complex than text-based task generation for LLMs. The authors observe that without explicit control, self-generated supervision in VLMs is prone to degeneration. This degradation manifests as:
 - Semantic errors in questions.
 - Pseudo-difficult questions that are weakly grounded in the image or visually unanswerable.
 - Questions that are skill-mismatched or semantically ill-posed.
 Such low-quality questions introduce noise into the training process, weakening the usability of self-generated supervision and potentially harming solver training. The motivation is to implement mechanisms that ensure the validity and reliability of generated questions and their corresponding pseudo-labels.

3. **Collapse of Question Type Diversity (Mode Collapse):** The authors note that during self-evolution, the questioner tends to gravitate towards generating questions belonging to a few "easy-to-reward" categories, such as counting or basic mathematical reasoning. This mode collapse leads to a progressively narrow distribution of generated questions, limiting the range of visual reasoning skills the model can acquire. If the model only trains on a restricted set of skills, its overall capability growth becomes unbalanced, and its ability to generalize to diverse multimodal tasks is constrained. The motivation is to introduce controls that encourage a broad and balanced coverage of skills during question generation.

To address these issues, the research aims to integrate complementary designs: a more frequent role alternation strategy to enhance efficiency, a quality control mechanism for generated supervision, and a diversity control mechanism to maintain skill coverage.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, RISE (Reliable Improvement in Self-Evolving), is a dual-role self-evolving system designed to improve Vision-Language Models from unlabeled images. It employs a single VLM that alternates between two functional roles: a questioner (π_q) and a solver (π_s). The methodology integrates three core components: fine-grained role alternation, a quality supervisor, and skill-aware dynamic balancing.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Pipeline

Given an unlabeled image set, the RISE framework operates in iterative cycles:
1. **Questioner Training:** The questioner generates informative questions based on images, optimized using reward signals derived from the solver's response quality and feedback from a quality supervisor.
2. **Pseudo-Labeled Data Construction:** Based on questions from the updated questioner and corresponding responses from the solver, pseudo-labeled training data is constructed and filtered.
3. **Solver Training:** The solver is trained on this filtered pseudo-labeled data to enhance its visual reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.2. Fine-Grained Role Alternation

Traditional dual-role self-evolving methods use coarse-grained alternation, where the questioner and solver are updated in long, separate phases. This delays feedback and synchronization between the two roles. RISE addresses this by implementing a fine-grained role alternation strategy. Instead of a single long phase of B update steps for each role per round, the same total update budget (B) is divided into `n` shorter phases, each with `b` steps (B = nb). This means that after every `b` update steps, the roles switch, shortening the feedback loop. The questioner receives feedback from a more current solver, and the solver learns from more up-to-date questions, leading to more efficient capability alignment.

#### 3.3. Quality and Diversity Control

##### 3.3.1. Quality Supervisor

To prevent the questioner from generating low-quality, pseudo-difficult questions, RISE introduces a quality supervisor. Instead of training a separate model, the supervisor shares parameters with the current solver (π_sup ≡ π_s). This design avoids additional model costs and enhances internal consistency, as the model that answers questions also assesses their acceptability. The supervisor performs two functions:
- **Question Validity Judgment:** For a generated question (q, k) related to an image (x), the supervisor provides a binary signal `v(x, q, k) ∈ {0, 1}`. This signal indicates if the question is image-grounded, semantically well-formed, answerable from the image, and consistent with its declared skill. This `v` signal is incorporated into the questioner's reward, discouraging invalid questions.
- **Pseudo-Label Reliability Verification:** During pseudo-label data construction, after the solver generates candidate answers and a majority-voted answer `ˆy` is formed, the supervisor verifies its correctness. A binary signal `u(x, q, ˆy) ∈ {0, 1}` is used to confirm the acceptability of the pseudo-label. Both `v` and `u` signals are obtained via prompt-based judgment by the supervisor.

##### 3.3.2. Skill-Aware Dynamic Balancing

To counter the mode collapse phenomenon where the questioner favors certain skills, a skill-aware dynamic balancing mechanism is introduced.
- **Skill Set:** The authors define six skill categories: coarse perception, fine-grained perception, instance reasoning, logical reasoning, math & counting, and science & technology.
- **Skill Bonus:** For each skill `k`, the recent number of generated questions (`n_k`) is tracked. A skill bonus `b_skill(k)` is calculated, providing additional reward only to relatively under-represented skills. This bonus is proportional to `(average_skill_frequency - n_k) / average_skill_frequency`. This encourages the questioner to explore and generate questions from a broader range of skills.
- **Skill-Stratified Sampling:** During the construction of the final pseudo-labeled data for solver training, skill-stratified sampling is applied to ensure approximately equal-sized subsets are drawn from each of the six skill categories, promoting balanced skill coverage in solver training.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

- **Questioner Objective:** The questioner aims to generate questions that are moderately difficult, valid, and diverse.
 - **Difficulty Score:** Computed based on the self-consistency `c(x, q)` of `M` solver responses (how frequently a majority answer appears). The difficulty score `d(x, q)` is `min(c(x, q), 1 - c(x, q))`, favoring questions where the solver's consistency is around 0.5 (neither too easy nor too unstable).
 - **Reward:** The questioner's reward `r_q` combines the difficulty score `d(x, q)`, the validity signal `v(x, q, k)`, and the skill bonus `b_skill(k)`. Invalid formats receive a -1 penalty.
- **Solver Objective:** The solver is trained on filtered pseudo-labeled data.
 - **Pseudo-Label Construction:** For a given question, `M` responses are sampled from the solver, and a majority-voted answer `ˆy` is determined as the pseudo-label.
 - **Filtering:** Pseudo-labeled samples are first pre-filtered based on a self-consistency threshold range `[τ_min, τ_max]` to remove overly easy or highly unstable samples. Subsequently, only samples where `v(x, q, k) = 1` (question valid) and `u(x, q, ˆy) = 1` (pseudo-label verified) are retained.
 - **Training Set:** The filtered samples are then skill-balanced sampled to form the final training set `D_s`.
 - **Reward:** The solver's reward `r_s` is binary: 1 if its generated answer matches the pseudo-label `ˆy`, and -1 for invalid formats.
Both the questioner and solver policies are updated using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning method that normalizes rewards within response groups to compute relative advantages for policy optimization.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted on two VLM backbones: Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B. The self-evolving training utilized an unlabeled image collection from Vision-47K (47,000 diverse images). Evaluation was performed on seven benchmarks covering general visual understanding, chart understanding, and multimodal mathematical reasoning: MMMU, MMVet, RealWorldQA, ChartQA, MathVista, MathVerse, and MathVision. An LLM-as-a-Judge protocol (using Qwen3-Max) was employed to assess answer correctness, handling semantically equivalent answers more effectively than exact string matching.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Self-Evolving Performance

RISE consistently improved the performance of both VLM backbones across different self-evolving steps.
- On Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, the weighted average score increased from 48.17 (base model) to 52.26 after 60 steps, representing a total gain of +4.09.
- On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, the average score improved from 53.38 (base model) to 57.37 after 60 steps, corresponding to a total gain of +3.99.
These gains were observed across diverse evaluation domains, including general visual understanding, chart comprehension, and multimodal mathematical reasoning, with particularly notable improvements on reasoning-intensive benchmarks like MathVerse and MathVision. The results suggest that explicit control over question quality and skill distribution contributes to more reliable and effective supervision throughout the training process. The marginal gains were observed to diminish in later stages, indicating a gradual convergence effect as the solver becomes stronger, consistent with the nature of majority-voted pseudo-labeling.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Prior Self-Evolving Methods

When compared to prior VLM self-evolving methods such as VisPlay, EvoLMM, Vision-Zero, and MM-Zero (considering reported improvement margins over their respective base models), RISE demonstrated comparatively strong improvement margins across different backbones and multiple benchmarks.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Quality and Diversity

Statistical analysis of the intermediate VQA data generated during self-evolution confirmed the degeneration phenomena and the effectiveness of RISE's control mechanisms:
- **Question Quality:** Without the quality supervisor, the rate of valid and correct questions steadily declined, dropping below 10% in later stages, indicating an accumulation of low-quality questions. With the supervisor, question quality remained consistently high, preventing degradation and ensuring more reliable intermediate supervision.
- **Question Diversity:** Without skill-aware dynamic balancing, the skill distribution of generated questions collapsed towards a few high-reward skills, particularly counting or math. This suggested the questioner exploited the difficulty score to favor question types that easily yielded moderately uncertain solver responses. With skill balancing, the skill distribution remained more balanced across categories, effectively mitigating mode collapse. These patterns were consistent across both base models.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on RISE Components

An ablation study on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct highlighted the contribution of each core component:
- **Fine-Grained Role Alternation:** This component consistently improved self-evolving efficiency, leading to stronger performance regimes with fewer update steps compared to coarse-grained alternation, both with and without the quality supervisor.
- **Aggravated Degradation without Supervisor:** Fine-grained alternation alone, without the quality supervisor, led to a sharp drop in average performance in later stages (e.g., from 50.43 to 44.85 at step 60). This indicates that faster interaction without quality control can lead the questioner to exploit rewards by generating low-quality or reward-hacked questions, ultimately harming solver training.
- **Supervisor for Stability:** The quality supervisor effectively stabilized the fine-grained self-evolving process, preventing the severe performance collapse observed in the unsupervised fine-grained setting. It ensured question validity and pseudo-label reliability, allowing the efficiency benefits of fine-grained alternation to be retained without sacrificing training stability.
- **Skill Balancing for Performance:** Adding skill-aware dynamic balancing on top of fine-grained alternation with the supervisor (the full RISE model) yielded the best overall performance, demonstrating that it prevents mode collapse and supports more balanced capability growth across benchmarks.

#### 4.5. Ablation on Role Alternation Granularity

Further ablation on the granularity of role alternation (`b` steps per cycle) showed that moderate fine-grained settings (e.g., `b=5`) achieved the best long-term performance, outperforming both coarse-grained (`b=20`) and overly frequent, fully online-style (`b=1`) alternations. This suggests an optimal trade-off between feedback freshness and training stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RISE framework contributes to the field of vision-language models by addressing critical challenges in self-evolving learning. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Reduced Reliance on Human Annotation:** By enabling VLMs to improve capabilities from unlabeled images using self-generated learning signals, RISE offers a pathway to reduce the dependence on costly and labor-intensive human-annotated multimodal supervision. This has the potential to enhance the scalability and accessibility of VLM development, particularly for reasoning-intensive visual tasks where high-quality annotations are scarce and expensive.

- **Improved VLM Capabilities:** The consistent and sustained performance gains demonstrated across different VLM scales and multiple diverse benchmarks suggest that RISE effectively enhances multimodal understanding and reasoning abilities. The framework shows particular strength in improving performance on reasoning-intensive tasks, which are often the most difficult to annotate.

- **Enhanced Reliability and Efficiency of Self-Evolution:** RISE directly tackles key limitations of existing VLM self-evolving methods. Fine-grained role alternation improves the efficiency of interaction and capability alignment between the questioner and solver. The quality supervisor ensures the validity of generated questions and the reliability of pseudo-labels, preventing training signal degradation. Skill-aware dynamic balancing mitigates mode collapse, promoting broader skill coverage and more balanced model development. These mechanisms make the self-evolution process more robust and stable.

- **Advancement in AI Self-Improvement:** This work contributes to the broader research direction of self-evolving AI, offering practical solutions for multimodal contexts. The findings related to the importance of controlled supervision and reliable interaction dynamics can inform future designs of self-improving agents and foundation models.

- **Potential for Broader Applications:** As VLMs become more capable through self-evolution, they could be applied more effectively in areas requiring robust visual reasoning, such as autonomous systems, advanced image analysis, and intelligent assistants, where generating diverse and high-quality training data has been a bottleneck.

**Limitations and Ethical Considerations:**
Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges several limitations. Self-evolution still exhibits a convergence trend, with diminishing marginal gains in later stages, suggesting that majority-voted pseudo-labeling might have inherent limits as models grow stronger. The behavior of self-evolution can also vary depending on the initial capabilities of the base model. Furthermore, the current alternation frequency relies on manually selected hyperparameters, indicating potential for more adaptive mechanisms.

From a broader impact perspective, self-evolving VLMs, like other advanced AI systems, may amplify existing biases, propagate hallucinations, or perpetuate visual grounding errors present in the base model, as the training signals are partially self-generated. Improved VLM capabilities could also be misused in sensitive applications such as surveillance or generating misleading visual content. While RISE includes quality supervision to mitigate unreliable supervision, these mechanisms do not eliminate all risks. The authors stress that careful evaluation remains necessary before deploying evolved models in real-world scenarios.
