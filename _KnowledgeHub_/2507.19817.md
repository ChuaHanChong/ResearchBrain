# Ag2x2: Robust Agent-Agnostic Visual Representations for Zero-Shot Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Ag2x2 learns coordination-aware visual representations from human videos by encoding both object states and 2D hand positions while masking human appearance, then uses these embeddings to shape RL rewards for zero-shot bimanual robot manipulation, reaching 73.5% success across 13 tasks without demonstrations or engineered rewards.

## Problem

- Agent-agnostic representations like Ag2Manip mask out all human presence, discarding hand-position information essential for coordinating two effectors in bimanual tasks.
- RL methods for bimanual manipulation depend on carefully engineered, task-specific reward functions that rarely generalize across tasks or embodiments.
- Imitation learning for bimanual tasks requires extensive teleoperated or visual demonstrations, making data collection time-consuming and unscalable.

## Method

- ==Coordination-aware visual encoder== extends Ag2Manip's ViT backbone to jointly embed inpainted RGB frames with both hands' 2D coordinates via ==LoRA fine-tuning== and position-token MLPs.
- ==Hand-aware agent-agnostic dataset pipeline== built from EpicKitchens using ==HaMeR==-based hand pose detection, ==ODISE segmentation==, and ==E2FGVI inpainting== to preserve hand positions while removing human appearance.
- ==Tilted reward-shaping== formulation drives ==PPO== policy learning for two free-floating proxy agents by rewarding embedding similarity to a goal image that exceeds the initial-state similarity baseline.

## Results

- Ag2x2 reaches **73.5%** average success across 13 Bi-DexHands and PerAct2 tasks, beating Ag2Manip's **56.4%** and expert-engineered rewards' **63.2%**.
- Ablation removing hand-position tokens (Ag2x2-H) drops performance by **16.2** percentage points to **57.3%**, confirming hand coordination signal drives the gain.
- An imitation policy trained on just **12** Ag2x2-collected rope-straightening trajectories generalizes to unseen initial rope configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving selective agent-specific cues (hand position) within an otherwise agent-agnostic representation can be necessary once a task requires coordinating multiple effectors.
> - Reward shaping via similarity-to-goal embeddings can substitute for both hand-engineered rewards and expert demonstrations, even matching or beating expert-designed rewards on deformable-object tasks.
> - Self-collected autonomous trajectories from a learned policy can bootstrap imitation learning, offering a scalable alternative to human teleoperation for data collection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly bimanual tasks, presents a significant challenge in robotics. Humans routinely perform bimanual tasks requiring precise coordination, such as cooking or tying shoelaces, yet robots struggle with autonomous acquisition of such skills. This difficulty primarily stems from the reliance on human expertise for supervision and the inherent complexity of achieving synchronized control between two robotic effectors.

Current paradigms for bimanual manipulation face scalability issues. Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, exemplified by works like Bi-DexHands, achieve impressive dexterity but necessitate labor-intensive, task-specific reward engineering that often lacks generalization across different tasks or robotic embodiments. Imitation learning (IL) approaches, which learn from teleoperation or visual demonstrations, offer promise but are limited by the high cost and time investment required to collect extensive expert demonstrations for bimanual tasks.

Recent advancements in single-arm manipulation have explored learning from unstructured human videos to guide policy learning, demonstrating that time-contrastive visual representations can be effective. Approaches such as R3M, VIP, and Ag2Manip leverage human-centric data to derive generalizable features. However, directly applying these methods to bimanual tasks reveals limitations. Many either focus exclusively on single-arm scenarios or, like Ag2Manip, mask out human agents entirely to achieve agent-agnosticism. While effective for single-arm tasks by focusing on object movements, this complete removal of human presence eliminates crucial spatial and temporal information about hand coordination, which is fundamental for bimanual tasks such as object transfer or synchronized manipulation of articulated or deformable objects.

The current work addresses this gap by proposing a framework that extends agent-agnostic representation learning to bimanual manipulation. It aims to develop visual representations that capture both object state progression and critical hand motion patterns, thereby preserving coordination information while maintaining agent-agnosticism. This represents a step towards enabling autonomous, zero-shot acquisition of complex bimanual skills without reliance on expert demonstrations or manually engineered rewards, thereby enhancing the scalability and generalization capabilities of robotic systems in manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable computational framework, named Ag2x2, that enables autonomous acquisition of bimanual manipulation skills without requiring expert demonstrations or task-specific reward engineering. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

- **Addressing the Scalability Bottleneck in Bimanual Manipulation:** Existing methods for bimanual control largely depend on human expertise, either through costly and time-consuming data collection for imitation learning or through complex, non-generalizable reward function design for reinforcement learning. This dependency creates a significant barrier to scaling robotic capabilities to a wider range of tasks and environments. Ag2x2 aims to reduce this reliance on human supervision.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Agent-Agnostic Representations for Bimanual Tasks:** Previous work, such as Ag2Manip, successfully demonstrated agent-agnostic learning for single-arm manipulation by masking out human-specific features from videos, thereby facilitating transfer to robots. However, this approach inherently removes all information regarding human hand movements and coordination patterns, which are critical for bimanual tasks. A key motivation is to devise a representation that is still agent-agnostic (transferable to robots) but simultaneously "coordination-aware," capturing essential hand motion and interaction patterns.
- **Jointly Encoding Object States and Hand Motion Patterns:** For bimanual tasks, understanding the progression of object states is necessary but insufficient. The relative positioning, movement, and interaction of both hands are equally crucial for successful task completion. The research is motivated by the need to develop a visual representation that can jointly encode both the changes in the environment (object states) and the dynamic spatial relationships between the manipulating agents (hand motion patterns).
- **Enabling Zero-Shot Skill Acquisition:** The framework seeks to allow robots to acquire new bimanual skills directly from visual goal specifications, without requiring prior demonstrations for the specific task or explicit reward functions. This aims to significantly lower the barrier for deploying robots in novel bimanual scenarios.
- **Promoting Effective Exploration in RL:** Traditional similarity-based rewards in RL can be insufficient for effective exploration, particularly in early training stages where policy behavior is stochastic. The research is motivated to design a reward shaping mechanism that encourages progress by requiring goal states to exceed an initial condition rather than merely seeking incremental improvements.
- **Establishing a Scalable Pipeline for Data Generation:** Beyond direct skill acquisition, the research aims to demonstrate that the skills learned through Ag2x2 can serve as a source of high-quality training data for imitation learning. This would create an efficient pipeline for generating large-scale bimanual manipulation data, further alleviating the need for human-generated demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Ag2x2 framework operates through two main components: coordination-aware visual representation learning from human videos and policy learning via reinforcement learning guided by these representations.

### 4. A. Coordination-Aware Visual Representation Learning

The approach builds upon prior agent-agnostic methods but incorporates hand coordination information.
1. **Input Data Processing:** Human ego-centric videos from datasets like EpicKitchens are processed to create a "hand-aware agent-agnostic" dataset (D_ag+h).
 - Hand pose detection is performed using the HaMeR model with a YOLO-v5 backbone.
 - 3D hand key points are projected to 2D coordinates using estimated camera poses.
 - Each hand's position is computed as the mean of its 21 key points.
 - Agent-agnostic frames are generated by segmenting human regions using ODISE and then inpainting them using E2FGVI, effectively removing human appearance while preserving the context of the environment.
 - The final input to the encoder is a concatenation of the inpainted RGB image and the 2D coordinates of both hands.
2. **Visual Encoder Architecture (F_phi):** A ViT-Large model, pre-trained on ImageNet-21k, is utilized as the visual encoder.
 - **Fine-tuning:** The model is initially fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) on the inpainted images to adapt to the domain while preserving pre-trained knowledge.
 - **Hand Position Integration:** Position tokens are introduced to explicitly incorporate hand coordinate information. Each hand's 2D position is encoded through a two-layer MLP (with ReLU activation and hidden dimensions). These position-encoded tokens are concatenated with the image tokens before being fed into the transformer layers. For occluded or out-of-frame hands, learnable variables are employed.
3. **Training Objective:** The visual encoder is trained using a time-contrastive objective, adapted from previous work. This objective combines:
 - **Temporal Contrastive Learning (L_tcn):** Encourages embeddings of temporally close frames to be similar and embeddings of distant frames to be dissimilar.
 - **Regularization (L_reg):** L1 and L2 norm regularization on the embeddings to ensure feature sparsity and prevent overfitting. This objective is applied to the enhanced dataset D_ag+h, ensuring that the learned embeddings capture both object-centric features and coordination-aware hand positions.

### 5. B. Policy Learning and Reward Shaping

The learned coordination-aware visual representations are then used to guide reinforcement learning for bimanual skill acquisition.
1. **Agent-Agnostic Action Representation:**
 - Instead of directly controlling robot joints, the policy learns coordinated motions and forces for two "free-floating proxy agents," represented as spheres.
 - These proxy trajectories are subsequently converted into robot joint commands using inverse kinematics.
 - Grasping is enabled by identifying feasible graspable poses for target objects using GraspNet. A grasp is considered valid when a proxy agent is within 5cm of a feasible pose.
2. **Policy Learning with Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - A model-free RL algorithm, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), is employed to train the bimanual manipulation policy.
 - The policy (π) maps current proxy states (p_t) and environment states (s_t) to actions (a_t). Actions comprise target positions (a_t^p ∈ R^6 for two 3D positions) and target forces (a_t^f ∈ R^6 for two 3D forces) for the two proxy agents. A PD controller translates these target actions into actual proxy movements.
 - The goal specification (g) includes an image of the desired goal state and the 2D projections of the desired end-effector positions in camera space.
3. **Tilted Reward Function:** To maximize embedding similarity between the current state (o_t) and the goal state (g) while promoting effective exploration, a "tilted" reward function is introduced:
 - `R(o_t, g; φ) = exp((Sp(z_t, z_g) - β) / β) - 1` when `Sp(z_t, z_g) > β`, and 0 otherwise.
 - `β = Sp(z_0, z_g)` represents the initial embedding similarity between the start and goal states.
 - `α > 0` (set to 3.0 based on ablation) is a scaling parameter.
 - This reward structure provides positive rewards only when the current state's similarity to the goal surpasses the *initial* similarity, encouraging the agent to actively improve beyond its starting condition rather than just making incremental progress.

### 6. C. Implementation Details

- Images are resized to 224x224x3. LoRA with rank `r=4` is applied to query and value matrices in attention layers for efficient fine-tuning.
- Encoder training occurs in two phases: visual adaptation (40 hours on 4 A100 GPUs) followed by hand-position integration (20 hours on 8 A100 GPUs).
- RL training uses 68 parallel environments over 200 episodes per task, taking approximately 3 hours per skill on a single NVIDIA 3090 workstation.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Ag2x2 involved 13 diverse bimanual manipulation tasks drawn from the Bi-DexHands and PerAct2 benchmarks, simulated in NVIDIA IsaacGym using two 9-DoF Franka Emika robotic arms. Each task was evaluated under nine configurations (3 initial seeds × 3 camera perspectives).

### 8. A. Overall Performance

- Ag2x2 achieved an average success rate of 73.5% across all 13 tasks.
- This performance surpassed baseline autonomous methods and notably exceeded the success rate of policies trained with expert-engineered rewards (63.2%).

### 9. B. Comparison to Baselines (Table I)

- **Agent-aware representations (R3M, VIP):** Consistently underperformed (15.4% and 26.5% success rates, respectively) due to the inherent visual and kinematic domain gap between human demonstrations and robotic systems.
- **Agent-agnostic representation (Ag2Manip):** Achieved a 56.4% success rate. While effective for single-arm tasks, it struggled with complex bimanual coordination (e.g., box pushing, tray lifting) because it completely masks human hands, losing critical coordination information.
- **LLM-driven reward generation (Eureka):** Demonstrated a 15.4% success rate, performing adequately only on tasks with easily describable goal states (e.g., closing scissors, pressing buttons) but failing in more complex interactions.
- **Expert-designed rewards:** Achieved 63.2% success. While strong on many tasks, this approach failed in scenarios requiring nuanced temporal coordination between hands and objects (e.g., "put cubes into drawer," "lift tray," "sweep dirt").
- **Ag2x2's Consistency:** Ag2x2 exhibited consistent performance across diverse challenges, successfully learning 12 out of 13 tasks (with "put cube into microwave" being the only task below 50% success). It demonstrated particular proficiency in manipulating deformable objects, such as "straighten rope," without specialized rewards or demonstrations.

### 10. C. Failure Case Analysis

Two primary failure patterns were identified:
1. **Object Interference:** In tasks involving containers (e.g., "put cube into drawer," "put cube into microwave"), the visual salience of container movements sometimes overshadowed the object placement and hand motion, leading to suboptimal reward signals and reduced accuracy.
2. **Imperfect Bimanual Coordination:** Occasional failures stemmed from precise coordination issues. For instance, in "close door inward," one hand might close the door prematurely. In "straighten rope," one hand sometimes reached the target while the other missed by a small margin.

### 11. D. Ablation Study (Table I: Ag2x2-H)

An ablation study, "Ag2x2-H" (without hands), was conducted where hand position tokens were excluded from the model during representation learning.
- **Performance Drop:** Removing hand position information resulted in a consistent 16.2% performance decrease across the task suite (73.5% for Ag2x2 vs. 57.3% for Ag2x2-H), underscoring the critical role of this feature for precise bimanual coordination.
- **Exceptional Case:** The "put cube into microwave" task showed improved performance in the ablated model, indicating that in scenarios where container state changes visually dominate, the hand positioning signal might be less impactful or even distracting.
- **Architectural Impact:** Ag2x2-H's performance was similar to Ag2Manip, suggesting that Ag2x2's superior performance primarily stems from the integration of hand position information rather than changes in the visual backbone (e.g., from ResNet to ViT).

### 12. E. Imitation Learning (Figure 4)

Ag2x2 enabled the collection of 12 high-quality demonstration trajectories for rope straightening across various initial configurations. An imitation learning policy trained on these trajectories successfully generalized to novel rope configurations, including those with reversed bending directions. This demonstrates Ag2x2's potential for generating scalable, high-quality data for robotic skill acquisition.

### 13. F. Additional Analysis

- **Trajectory Smoothness (Table II):** Ag2x2 generated trajectories with smoothness comparable to expert-designed rewards and notably smoother than the highest-performing baseline methods, indicating stable end-effector motions.
- **Reward Consistency (Table III):** Ag2x2 showed superior Spearman Rank Correlation between temporal sequence and goal-state similarity compared to baselines, suggesting a more consistent representation of task progress. However, for tasks with discrete visual state changes (e.g., button pressing), correlation metrics showed limitations as they did not align with continuous progress measurement.
- **Proprioception (Table IV):** A controlled experiment indicated that while proprioceptive signals during policy learning offered some benefit, Ag2x2's primary performance advantage originated from the explicit incorporation of hand information during the pre-training phase of representation learning.

### 14. Significance and Potential Impact

Ag2x2 represents a advancement in the field of robotic bimanual manipulation, primarily through its novel approach to learning coordination-aware visual representations from human videos. The demonstrated capabilities carry several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Reduced Reliance on Human Expertise:** A core contribution of Ag2x2 is its ability to enable zero-shot acquisition of bimanual manipulation skills without requiring expert demonstrations or manually engineered reward functions. This directly addresses a major bottleneck in robot learning, as both data collection for imitation learning and reward design for reinforcement learning are time-consuming, costly, and often lead to non-generalizable solutions.
- **Scalable Skill Acquisition Pipeline:** The framework's capacity to autonomously generate high-quality demonstrations, as shown in the imitation learning experiments, opens pathways for a scalable pipeline for robotic skill acquisition. This can significantly accelerate the process of teaching robots new tasks by automating the generation of training data, rather than relying on human teleoperation or programming.
- **Enhanced Generalization to Complex Tasks:** Ag2x2's robust performance across 13 diverse and challenging bimanual tasks, including those involving deformable objects like ropes, suggests improved generalization capabilities compared to existing methods. This is particularly relevant for real-world applications where robots need to interact with a variety of objects and adapt to dynamic environments.
- **Advancement in Coordination-Aware Representations:** The research highlights the effectiveness of visual representations that jointly encode object states and hand motion patterns while maintaining agent-agnosticism. This approach provides a foundation for robots to understand and execute complex coordinated movements, moving beyond object-centric or single-effector perspectives. This direction could be impactful for tasks requiring intricate spatial and temporal synchronization between multiple robot limbs.
- **Broader Applicability for Robotic Systems:** By offering a method for autonomous learning of complex physical interactions, Ag2x2 contributes to the development of more versatile and autonomous robotic systems. Such systems could potentially operate in unstructured environments, performing household chores, industrial assembly, or other tasks that currently require significant human intervention or specialized programming.

Despite its current limitations, such as reliance on static goal images for tasks involving complex dynamics and the modeling of only end-effector positions (limiting applications to simple grippers), Ag2x2 establishes a promising foundation. The methodology of leveraging readily available human videos for pre-training coordination-aware representations offers a viable path towards improving learning efficiency and expanding the capabilities of robotic manipulation, even for more sophisticated, dexterous tasks in the future.
