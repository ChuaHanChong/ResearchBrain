# MLNet: Mutual Learning Network with Neighborhood Invariance for Universal Domain Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> MLNet is a framework for Universal Domain Adaptation that improves feature discriminability and balances known/unknown class identification by integrating neighborhood invariance learning, cross-domain manifold mixup, and a mutual learning consistency constraint. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance across various UniDA benchmarks.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks perform poorly when data distribution shifts between training and deployment domains.
- Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) lacks prior knowledge about source and target label set relationships, making it challenging to learn discriminative features and reliably identify unknown classes.
- Current UniDA methods struggle with scattered feature representations for target samples and accurately distinguishing between visually similar known and truly unknown target categories.

## Method

- **MLNet** enhances the OVANet architecture with three integrated components: ==Neighborhood Invariance Learning==, ==Cross-domain Manifold Mixup==, and ==Mutual Learning via Consistency Constraint==.
- ==Neighborhood Invariance Learning (NIL)== uses a self-adaptive neighbor search and confidence-guided representation learning to reduce intra-domain variations in target features.
- ==Cross-domain Manifold Mixup (CMM)== simulates unknown-class samples by interpolating source and target features, while Mutual Learning (CC) refines closed-set and open-set classifiers to balance known and unknown class identification.

## Results

- MLNet consistently and substantially outperforms the OVANet baseline across all Universal Domain Adaptation settings (CDA, ODA, PDA, OPDA) on Office, Office-Home, and VisDA datasets.
- The method achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy in the challenging OPDA setting, surpassing previous methods by up to **12.6%** on VisDA.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Neighborhood Invariance Learning, Cross-domain Manifold Mixup, and Mutual Learning via Consistency Constraint are all essential for MLNet's robust performance, with significant drops observed when any component is removed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reducing intra-domain variations in target feature space through neighborhood-based learning is crucial for improving feature discriminability in Universal Domain Adaptation.
> - Generating robust "pseudo-unknown" samples via cross-domain feature interpolation effectively helps open-set classifiers learn to reject truly unknown target samples.
> - A consistency constraint between closed-set and open-set classifiers allows for mutual refinement, correcting misidentified known-class samples while preserving the identification of genuine unknown classes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape: Universal Domain Adaptation

**The Challenge of Domain Adaptation:**
Deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success in various domains when trained and tested on data from the same distribution. However, their performance often degrades significantly when applied to novel domains where the data distribution shifts. This phenomenon, known as "domain shift," is a major impediment to the real-world deployment of AI systems, as collecting and labeling vast amounts of data for every possible target domain is often impractical or impossible. Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) aims to mitigate this by transferring knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain.

**Evolution of Domain Adaptation Variants:**
The field of UDA has evolved through several challenging variants, each relaxing assumptions about the relationship between source (C_s) and target (C_t) label sets:
- **Closed-Set Domain Adaptation (CDA):** Assumes that the label sets are identical (C_s = C_t). While promising, this assumption is often invalid in real-world scenarios.
- **Open-Set Domain Adaptation (ODA):** Assumes the target domain may contain classes not present in the source domain (C_s ⊂ C_t), requiring identification of "unknown" target samples.
- **Partial Domain Adaptation (PDA):** Assumes the source domain contains additional classes not present in the target domain (C_t ⊂ C_s), requiring the model to ignore irrelevant source knowledge.
- **Open Partial Domain Adaptation (OPDA):** A more complex scenario where there are shared classes, source-private classes, and target-private (unknown) classes (C_s ∩ C_t ≠ ∅, C_s - C_t ≠ ∅, C_t - C_s ≠ ∅).

**Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA): The Frontier:**
UniDA is the most challenging and practical variant, as it makes no prior assumption about the relationship between C_s and C_t. The objective is to classify target samples into either one of the known classes (from C_s) or an aggregated "unknown" class, without any prior knowledge of the label set relation. This 'agnostic' nature makes UniDA highly relevant for real-world applications where the exact nature of domain shift is unknown.

**Positioning MLNet within UniDA:**
Existing UniDA methods grapple with two primary issues:
1. **Overlooking intra-domain variations in the target domain:** Distribution shifts can lead to scattered and less discriminative feature representations for target samples, even within the same class. This makes it hard to generalize knowledge.
2. **Difficulty in separating between similar known and unknown classes:** Many methods struggle to robustly identify truly unknown samples without misclassifying known target samples as unknown, or vice versa, especially when known and unknown classes are visually similar. Techniques like fixed thresholds or relying solely on source-domain inter-class distances often fall short. OVANet, a strong baseline, trains one-vs-all classifiers but may still produce non-discriminative feature spaces and suffer from misidentification errors.

MLNet directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel framework that integrates three key components. It builds upon the OVANet architecture but significantly enhances its feature learning and decision-making capabilities. By explicitly tackling intra-domain variations and introducing a mutual learning strategy between classifiers, MLNet aims to achieve a more robust and accurate solution for the complex UniDA problem, pushing the state-of-the-art in this critical area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of MLNet stem from the inherent challenges and shortcomings of existing Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) methods.

**1. Addressing Core UniDA Challenges:**
The overarching goal of MLNet is to improve the accuracy and robustness of universal domain adaptation. UniDA is practical because it requires no prior knowledge of the relationship between source and target label sets, but this agnosticism also makes it exceptionally difficult. Existing methods often struggle with two fundamental problems that MLNet explicitly aims to solve:
- **Overlooking intra-domain variations in the target domain:** Distribution shifts between source and target domains can cause target samples belonging to the same class to have varied feature representations, making them less discriminative. This leads to poor generalization from the source-trained model to the unlabeled target data.
- **Difficulty in separating between similar known and unknown classes:** A core challenge in UniDA is to accurately distinguish between known categories (present in the source) and entirely unknown categories (unique to the target). Existing techniques, such as using fixed thresholds or relying on inter-class distances learned solely from the source, often lead to errors where known classes are misidentified as unknown, or vice-versa, especially when categories share visual similarities.

**2. Enhancing Feature Discriminability:**
A key motivation is to learn more "generalizable" and "discriminative" feature representations for target domain samples. The authors posit that if features are not well-structured, particularly with reduced intra-domain variations, even sophisticated classification heads will struggle. They aim to achieve this by leveraging the concept of "neighborhood invariance," where samples within a local neighborhood in the feature space are highly likely to belong to the same category. This would help in creating tighter clusters for known classes and better separation from unknown ones.

**3. Improving Unknown-Class Identification and Known-Class Correction:**
The paper acknowledges that identifying unknown-class samples is crucial but can be overly aggressive, leading to known-class errors. Therefore, a specific motivation is to:
- **Explicitly simulate unknown-class samples:** Current methods for identifying unknown classes might not adequately account for distribution shifts. The authors seek a more effective way to generate "pseudo-unknown" samples that bridge the domain gap and improve the open-set classifier's ability to reject actual unknown target samples.
- **Compensate for misidentified known-class errors:** A strong unknown-class identification mechanism might inadvertently cause some known-class target samples to be classified as unknown. The motivation is to introduce a mechanism that can correct these misidentifications without compromising the ability to detect true unknown samples, thereby balancing the trade-off between known and unknown accuracy. This requires a novel approach to leverage the distinct information provided by closed-set and open-set classifiers.

In essence, MLNet is motivated by the need for a robust, accurate, and practically applicable UniDA solution that simultaneously improves feature quality, unknown-class discovery, and known-class classification by addressing the fundamental challenges of distribution shift and category discrepancy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MLNet proposes a novel framework built upon three interconnected components designed to address the challenges of Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA): Neighborhood Invariance Learning (NIL), Cross-domain Manifold Mixup (CMM), and Mutual Learning via Consistency Constraint (CC). The network architecture is based on the OVANet baseline, comprising a feature extractor (F), a closed-set classifier (C), and K one-vs-all open-set classifiers (O_l).

**1. Network Architecture Overview (Baseline Foundation):**
- **Feature Extractor (F):** Maps input images `x` into an embedding feature `z`.
- **Closed-set Classifier (C):** Predicts probabilities `p_c(l|x)` for `K` known classes.
- **Open-set Classifiers (O_l):** `K` binary classifiers, each `O_l` outputs a positive score `p_o(l|x)` indicating whether `x` belongs to class `l` or is unknown.
- **Decision Rule:** During testing, a target sample `x` is first assigned to the known class `l` with the highest `p_c(l|x)`. Then, `p_o(l|x)` is compared against a threshold (0.5) to decide if `x` is truly known (`l`) or unknown.
The baseline OVANet is trained with a combination of cross-entropy loss for `C`, one-vs-all loss (HNCS) for `O_l` on source data, and open-set entropy minimization (OEM) for `O_l` on target data to encourage confident predictions.

**2. Neighborhood Invariance Learning (NIL):**
This component aims to reduce intra-domain variations in the target domain and learn more generalizable features.
- **Memory Bank (M):** A memory bank stores `l2`-normalized features of all target samples, updated dynamically. Pairwise similarities `s_j,k` between samples are computed as dot products of their features in the memory. These similarities are then Softmax-normalized to estimate `p_jk`, the probability of two samples sharing the same class.
- **Self-adaptive Neighbor Search:** Instead of fixed K-nearest neighbors, which are sensitive to class sample sizes, NIL employs a self-adaptive strategy. The neighborhood `N_j` for a sample `x_t_j` includes all other samples `x_t_k` whose similarity `s_j,k` is greater than a relative ratio `ϵ` of the similarity to `x_t_j`'s nearest neighbor. This approach dynamically adjusts neighborhood size, mitigating class imbalance issues and improving robustness.
- **Confidence-guided Representation Learning:** To enhance robustness, each neighbor `k` in `N_j` is assigned a confidence weight `w_jk` based on the Jaccard distance between their respective neighborhoods (`|N_j ∩ N_k| / |N_j ∪ N_k|`). Neighbors with more overlapping neighborhoods are considered more reliable.
- **NIL Loss (L_nil):** The loss function `L_nil` minimizes `sum(w_jk * log p_jk)` over all neighbors in `N_j`. This maximizes pairwise similarities between a sample and its confident neighbors, enforcing feature invariance and reducing intra-class variance.

**3. Cross-domain Manifold Mixup (CMM):**
CMM is designed to improve the identification of unknown-class samples by simulating them explicitly, overcoming the limitations of source-only mixup or naive entropy minimization.
- **Mixup Strategy:** It randomly samples an interpolation factor `λ` from a Beta distribution (`Beta(α, α)`). A mixup feature `z_m` is then created by interpolating features from a source sample `x_s_i` and a target sample `x_t_j`: `z_m = λF(x_s_i) + (1 - λ)F(x_t_j)`.
- **Unknown-Class Simulation:** The authors theoretically prove that such cross-domain mixup features `z_m` belong to any specific known class with a low probability (unless `x_s_i` and `x_t_j` are from the same shared known class). Consequently, these `z_m` samples effectively act as robust simulations of potential unknown-class samples, bridging the domain gap more smoothly than source-only mixups.
- **CMM Loss (L_cmm):** This loss supervises the open-set classifier `O_yl` (corresponding to the source sample's label `y_s_i`) to predict the mixup feature `z_m` as *unknown*. Specifically, `L_cmm = -log(1 - p_o(y_s_i|z_m))`, which encourages `p_o(y_s_i|z_m)` to be low, making the open-set classifier more adept at rejecting unknown-like samples.

**4. Mutual Learning via Consistency Constraint (CC):**
While CMM enhances unknown-class identification, it can potentially shrink the decision space for known classes, leading to misidentification of known-class samples as unknown. CC addresses this by leveraging the unique relationship between the closed-set and open-set classifiers.
- **Inconsistency Observation:** The authors observe that known-class target samples misidentified as unknown typically have high confidence from the closed-set classifier `p_c(l|x)` but low positive scores from the corresponding open-set classifier `p_o(l|x)`. Conversely, true unknown samples usually have consistently low `p_c(l|x)` and `p_o(l|x)`.
- **Consistency Constraint Loss (L_cc):** The `L_cc` is defined as `sum(p_c(l|x_t_j) * p_o(l|x_t_j))`. Minimizing this loss encourages the open-set positive score `p_o(l|x_t_j)` to increase proportionally to the closed-set confidence `p_c(l|x_t_j)`.
- **Effect:** For misidentified known-class samples with high `p_c(l|x)`, `L_cc` correction significantly increases `p_o(l|x)`, pushing them towards correct known-class classification. For true unknown samples, since both `p_c(l|x)` and `p_o(l|x)` are inherently low, the corrective increase is minimal, preserving their unknown status. This creates a mutual learning effect, optimizing decision boundaries to better separate both known and unknown classes.

**5. Optimization Strategy:**
The overall training objective `L_all` combines the baseline loss `L_base` with the three proposed components: `L_nil`, `L_cmm`, and `L_cc`, weighted by hyperparameters `β1`, `β2`, and `η`. The weights are carefully tuned through experiments and kept consistent across datasets where possible, with a slight adjustment for larger datasets like VisDA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MLNet demonstrates its superior performance across various UniDA settings and benchmarks, significantly outperforming the baseline and achieving state-of-the-art results in most cases.

**1. Experimental Setup:**
- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on three public UniDA benchmarks:
 - **Office (Saenko et al. 2010):** 3 domains, 31 categories, ~4,700 images.
 - **Office-Home (Venkateswara et al. 2017):** 4 domains, 65 categories, ~15,500 images.
 - **VisDA (Peng et al. 2017):** Large-scale, synthetic-to-real, 12 categories, ~200,000 images.
- **Evaluation Protocols:**
 - **CDA & PDA:** Classification accuracy across all target samples.
 - **ODA & OPDA:** H-score (harmonic mean of known-class average accuracy and unknown-class accuracy), which balances the trade-off between identifying known and unknown classes.
- **Implementation Details:** ResNet-50 pre-trained on ImageNet as the feature extractor. SGD optimizer, Nesterov momentum, inverse scheduler. Batch size 36, 50 epochs. NIL loss disabled in the first epoch. Hyperparameters `τ=10.0`, `ϵ=0.875`, `β1=0.5`, `β2=0.1` fixed across datasets, while `η` (consistency constraint weight) was `0.16` by default and `0.32` for VisDA.

**2. Overall Performance Against Baselines and State-of-the-Arts:**
- **Significant Improvement over Baseline (OVANet):** MLNet consistently and substantially outperforms the OVANet baseline across all UniDA settings (CDA, ODA, PDA, OPDA) on all three datasets.
 - On **VisDA**, the average improvements are particularly striking: 17.0% for accuracy (CDA/PDA) and 11.3% for H-score (ODA/OPDA).
 - On **Office** and **Office-Home**, improvements range from 3.7% to 9.2% for accuracy/H-score.

**3. Detailed Performance Across UniDA Settings:**
- **OPDA Setting (Table 1):** MLNet achieves new state-of-the-art results across all datasets in the challenging OPDA setting. It outperforms UniOT (tailored for OPDA) by ~1% on Office and OfficeHome, and a remarkable 12.6% on VisDA.
- **ODA Setting (Table 8 in supplementary):** MLNet yields the best H-score on the Office dataset and competitive results on OfficeHome and VisDA, with significant 2-3% improvements over the baseline.
- **PDA Setting (Table 9 in supplementary):** MLNet sets new state-of-the-art, beating all previous approaches. It shows a large margin of about 5% improvement on OfficeHome and VisDA.
- **CDA Setting (Table 10 in supplementary):** MLNet achieves significant improvements over the baseline (e.g., 14.5% on VisDA) and competitive performance against other state-of-the-arts, often outperforming most except for a few specific strong baselines like GATE.

**4. Ablation Studies and Component Validation (Table 3):**
Ablation studies on the Office dataset confirm the importance of each proposed component:
- **Removing NIL (Ours w/o NIL):** Leads to a severe drop in performance across all metrics and settings, especially known accuracy, highlighting the necessity of reducing intra-domain variations.
- **Removing CMM (Ours w/o CMM):** Significantly degrades unknown accuracy and H-score, affirming CMM's role in unknown-class identification.
- **Removing CC (Ours w/o CC):** Drastically reduces known accuracy and H-score, validating its role in correcting misidentified known-class samples without affecting unknown-class identification.
- **Effect of Confidence-guidance (Ours w/o Conf.):** A slight but consistent decrease in performance when removing confidence weights, showing its contribution to robustness.
- **Effect of Self-adaptive Neighborhood (Ours w/ KNN):** Replacing the self-adaptive search with fixed K-nearest neighbors (K=5) causes severe degradation, particularly when category shift exists, proving the superiority of the self-adaptive approach.
- **Effect of Cross-domain Mixup (Ours w/ SMM):** Using source-domain manifold mixup (SMM) from GATE instead of CMM results in lower performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of cross-domain interpolation.
- **Choices for L_cc (Table 4 in supplementary):** Comparison with other consistency methods (cosine, JS/KL divergence) shows that the proposed `L_cc` is uniquely effective in improving H-score for ODA/OPDA while also boosting CDA/PDA performance. Visualization (Figure 6a,b in supplementary) clearly shows how CC helps resolve inconsistencies for known classes while preserving unknown class separation.

**5. Hyperparameter Robustness:**
Experiments on the Office dataset (Figure 4) demonstrate that MLNet is relatively robust to the choices of key hyperparameters (`β2`, `η`, `ϵ`), with performance varying by typically less than 1% for accuracy and 3% for H-score across a reasonable range of values. The self-adaptive nature of `ϵ` contributes significantly to this robustness.

In summary, MLNet's detailed experimental validation underscores its effectiveness in tackling the complex challenges of UniDA, achieving state-of-the-art performance by judiciously combining novel feature learning, unknown-class simulation, and mutual classifier refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MLNet represents a significant advancement in the field of Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA), offering a robust and high-performing solution to one of the most challenging problems in machine learning generalization. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

**1. Advancing Universal Domain Adaptation:**
- **Bridging the Knowledge Gap:** UniDA addresses the critical real-world scenario where the relationship between source and target data categories is unknown. MLNet’s superior performance in this agnostic setting, especially on challenging benchmarks like VisDA, pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in domain adaptation without prior knowledge. This means AI models can be deployed more readily in new environments without extensive re-labeling or assumptions.
- **Robust Feature Learning:** The Neighborhood Invariance Learning (NIL) component is a crucial contribution. By actively reducing intra-domain variations and learning more discriminative features in the target domain through self-adaptive, confidence-guided neighbor search, MLNet creates a more robust embedding space. This is fundamental for improving classification accuracy in the presence of distribution shifts. The self-adaptive nature of NIL is particularly impactful as it reduces the need for dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning, making the method more practical.
- **Enhanced Unknown-Class Identification:** The Cross-domain Manifold Mixup (CMM) is an innovative approach to simulate unknown classes. By leveraging intermediate feature states between source and target, it effectively smooths the decision boundary for open-set classifiers, leading to more accurate rejection of truly unknown samples. This addresses a major weakness in many UniDA methods that struggle to distinguish between similar known and unknown entities.
- **Balanced Classification:** The Mutual Learning via Consistency Constraint (CC) is a novel mechanism that addresses a common trade-off: aggressive unknown-class identification can lead to known-class misclassification. By mutually refining closed-set and open-set classifiers, MLNet achieves a better balance, improving known-class accuracy without compromising the ability to identify unknowns. This is a subtle yet powerful contribution that ensures holistic performance.

**2. Practical Implications:**
- **Reduced Annotation Burden:** The core benefit of UniDA, and MLNet's enhanced performance in this area, is the drastic reduction in the need for human annotation in new target domains. This translates to significant cost and time savings for deploying AI systems in diverse real-world applications (e.g., autonomous driving, medical imaging, industrial inspection, robotics).
- **Greater Model Generalizability:** MLNet contributes to developing more generalizable AI models that can adapt effectively to unforeseen categories and distribution shifts, making them more resilient and versatile in dynamic environments.
- **Deployment in Open-World Scenarios:** Many real-world applications operate in "open-world" settings where new, unseen categories can emerge. MLNet’s ability to reliably identify unknown classes makes it a valuable tool for such scenarios, allowing systems to flag novel observations for human review or further learning.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper's successful integration of neighborhood invariance, cross-domain mixup, and mutual learning provides a strong framework and baseline for future research in UniDA and related areas of domain generalization and open-set recognition. The detailed ablation studies offer clear insights into the utility of each component, guiding subsequent developments.

In conclusion, MLNet significantly advances the field of Universal Domain Adaptation by addressing critical limitations of prior work. Its innovative methodology for learning discriminative features, simulating unknown classes, and balancing classifier decisions leads to state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks. This makes MLNet a crucial step towards more robust, generalizable, and practically deployable artificial intelligence systems capable of navigating the complexities of real-world data heterogeneity.
