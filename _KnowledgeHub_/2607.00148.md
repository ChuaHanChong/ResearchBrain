# 3D Point World Models: Point Completion Enables More Accurate Dynamics Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Oregon State University developed a 3D Point World Model (3DPWM) that learns dynamics on complete point clouds, derived from partial observations through a point cloud completion module. This approach achieves superior long-horizon rollout accuracy, with Chamfer L1 distance reduced from 0.080 to 0.009 on PickCube, and enables more effective model-based planning in robotic manipulation tasks, improving closed-loop success rates on PickCube from 35% to 85% compared to baselines.

## Problem

- 2D and 2.5D dynamics models struggle with geometric inconsistency and physically implausible predictions, hindering precise robot control.
- Existing 3D world models operating on partial point clouds are highly sensitive to occlusions, leading to compounding errors in long-horizon rollouts.
- Many advanced 3D dynamics models trained on complete simulated geometry face challenges when deployed in the real world where only partial sensor observations are available.

## Method

- The **3DPWM** system integrates a ==point cloud completion== module to reconstruct complete 3D object geometries from single-view partial RGB-D observations.
- An ==action-conditioned 3D dynamics model== (based on ==Point Transformer V3==) is trained to predict future complete point cloud states by forecasting per-point velocities.
- The model is trained with ==multi-step rollout supervision== over a horizon of 15 steps and utilizes ==register tokens== in ==cross-attention== for effective action conditioning.

## Results

- 3DPWM achieved significantly lower Chamfer distances (e.g., **0.009** on PickCube) and higher reward correlations (**0.77-0.86**) across various tasks, indicating superior geometric accuracy in long-horizon rollouts compared to baselines (**0.080-0.085** Chamfer on PickCube).
- It enabled superior closed-loop planning, achieving success rates of **85%** on PickCube, **70%** on StackCube, and **65%** on PickYCB, substantially outperforming partial-observation baselines (e.g., **35%** on PickCube).
- Demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer without fine-tuning, outperforming baselines by up to **40%** (e.g., **60%** vs **20%** on PickApple) in real-world picking tasks due to improved object geometry.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly providing complete 3D geometry, even through a learned completion process, significantly enhances long-horizon dynamics prediction accuracy and geometric consistency compared to operating on partial observations.
> - Training the dynamics model with multi-step rollout supervision is crucial for reducing compounding errors and improving the reliability of predictions over extended time horizons.
> - Register tokens in the action-conditioned cross-attention mechanism effectively decouple action-relevant and action-irrelevant points, leading to more robust action conditioning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Learning predictive models of the world, often referred to as "world models," is a foundational challenge in artificial intelligence, particularly for embodied agents such as robots. These models allow agents to anticipate the consequences of their actions, facilitating planning and autonomous control. Historically, much of this work has focused on learning dynamics from 2D visual observations (e.g., video frames).

Prior 2D video-based dynamics models, including those used for reinforcement learning or large video models trained on internet-scale datasets, often struggle with geometric consistency and can hallucinate unrealistic future states. Furthermore, deploying these models for physical action requires learning complex inverse kinematics (IK) models, which can be ambiguous and difficult to generalize.

To address the limitations of purely 2D approaches, 2.5D models have emerged, jointly predicting geometric properties like depth and surface normals alongside RGB frames. While more grounded in the physical world, these models still process features in a 2D fashion, leading to implicit geometric reasoning and potential multi-view inconsistencies. Both 2D and 2.5D models operating on partial observations are susceptible to compounding errors during long-horizon rollouts, hindering reliable prediction and planning.

More recent research has explored explicit 3D world models that operate directly on 3D scene representations, such as partial point clouds, particles, or Gaussian splats. These approaches inherently offer improved geometric consistency compared to 2D/2.5D methods. However, models based on partial point clouds, while suitable for real-world deployment where complete geometry is often unavailable, suffer from compounding errors in occluded scenes due to the limited view inputs. Conversely, models trained on complete point clouds in simulation often face challenges when transferred to real-world scenarios where full scene geometry is not directly observable.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the gap between simulation-trained 3D dynamics models (which often assume complete geometry) and real-world deployment challenges (where only partial observations are available). It proposes an integrated system that first reconstructs complete 3D geometry from partial sensor data via point cloud completion and then applies a 3D dynamics model to this completed representation. This approach aims to combine the geometric advantages of operating on complete 3D data with the practical necessity of processing partial real-world observations, thereby enabling more accurate and reliable long-horizon predictions for robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a task-agnostic world model that operates entirely in 3D space, providing more accurate and reliable dynamics learning for robotic control. The motivations stem from several limitations observed in existing world modeling approaches:

1. **Geometric Inconsistency and Hallucinations in 2D/2.5D Models:** Traditional video-based dynamics models frequently produce geometrically inconsistent or physically implausible future frames, especially over longer prediction horizons. This makes them less suitable for precise robot control where physical accuracy is critical. Even 2.5D models, which incorporate depth information, process it in a 2D context, leading to implicit geometric reasoning that can result in multi-view inconsistencies and compounding errors from partial observations.
2. **Challenges with Action Inference (Inverse Kinematics):** For 2D-based models, inferring robot actions from generated visual sequences requires learning robust inverse kinematics (IK) models. This is a complex and often ambiguous problem, as the same visual motion can correspond to different underlying physical commands depending on unobserved properties like object mass or friction. Operating in 3D space with explicit geometry offers a more direct pathway for action-conditioned dynamics.
3. **Sensitivity to Occlusions and Compounding Errors in Partial 3D Models:** While emerging 3D dynamics models using partial point clouds improve geometric consistency, they remain highly sensitive to occlusions. When parts of the scene or objects are hidden from the sensor view, the dynamics model must implicitly infer the occluded geometry, which can lead to prediction drift and accumulated errors, particularly during long-horizon rollouts. This makes planning in complex, occluded environments unreliable.
4. **Discrepancy between Simulation and Real-world 3D Data:** Many advanced 3D dynamics models are trained on complete point clouds in simulated environments where full geometry is readily available. However, these models often struggle with real-world deployment because only partial sensor observations are typically available. This necessitates bridging the sim-to-real gap, allowing models trained on complete geometry to function effectively with incomplete real-world data.
5. **Task-Specificity and Lack of Generalization:** Many prior world models are task-specific, requiring fine-tuning or retraining when adapting to new tasks. A key motivation is to develop a more task-agnostic world model that can learn general interaction dynamics and facilitate adaptation to novel, unseen tasks, improving robot versatility.
6. **Need for Accurate Cost Evaluation for Planning:** Effective model-based planning relies on accurate predictions of future states and the ability to evaluate a cost function based on these predicted states. Errors in the dynamics model, especially those related to geometric inconsistencies or occlusions, directly translate to inaccurate cost evaluations, compromising planning performance.

To address these motivations, the authors propose the 3D Point World Model (3DPWM), which emphasizes operating on complete 3D geometry. Their key insight is that providing complete point clouds, even if derived from partial observations through a completion process, offers a superior training regime for dynamics models. This is posited to lead to more reliable long-horizon predictions, more accurate cost function computation for planning, and better adaptation to novel tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 3D Point World Model (3DPWM) system integrates point cloud completion with a learned 3D dynamics model to predict future scene states given robot actions. The system is designed to operate on single-view RGB-D observations in real-world scenarios while benefiting from dynamics training on complete 3D geometry obtained from simulation.

**Problem Formulation:**
The system takes robot proprioception (joint angles $q_t$) and a single-view partial point cloud (from an RGB-D camera) as observation $O_t$ at each time step. The first stage involves completing this partial observation to obtain $O_{t}^{\text{complete}}$. An action $a_t$ is then input to a learned transition function $T: O_{t}^{\text{complete}} \times A \rightarrow O_{t+1}^{\text{complete}}$ that predicts the next complete observation. This learned dynamics model is subsequently used with a planner to generate trajectories and optimize a defined cost objective.

**Obtaining Complete Point Clouds:**
This process occurs in two main steps to construct the full scene point cloud $P_t$:
1. **Robot Point Cloud:** Given the robot's joint angles $q_t$ and its URDF (Unified Robot Description Format), forward kinematics is used to compute the robot's mesh configuration. Points are then sampled from this mesh, specifically focusing on the end-effector as the action space is defined in its frame, to obtain $P_{t}^{\text{robot}}$.
2. **Object Point Clouds:**
 - **Segmentation:** Each visible object in the scene is segmented from the RGB-D image using SAM3 (Segment Anything Model 3), typically with manual prompts to identify target objects.
 - **Partial Point Cloud Extraction:** The depth map corresponding to each segmented object is back-projected into 3D space to recover partial object point clouds.
 - **Completion:** A specialized point cloud completion model (fine-tuned on YCB objects) is applied to these partial object point clouds to produce their completed geometries, $P_{t}^{\text{obj}}$. This completion module is trained with rotation augmentation across all three axes and random jittering to enhance robustness to arbitrary object orientations and sensor noise in real-world scenarios.
3. **Scene Point Cloud:** The final observation $P_t$ is a combination of the robot end-effector points and the completed object point clouds: $P_t = P_{t}^{\text{robot}} \cup (\cup_{\text{obj}} P_{t}^{\text{obj}})$.

**3D Point World Model (Dynamics Model):**
The core of 3DPWM is an action-conditioned dynamics model that predicts the next state of the complete point cloud.
1. **Feature Representation:** For each point in the complete scene point cloud $P_t$, features $F_t$ are constructed. These include the point's current spatial location ($x_t, y_t, z_t$) and its current and previous timestep velocities ($v_t, v_{t-1}$). During training in simulation, point-to-point correspondences are available to compute velocities directly. For real-world deployment, initial velocities are set to zero, and subsequent velocities are tracked or estimated.
2. **Point Cloud Encoder:** The observation representation $(P_t, F_t)$ is fed into a Point Transformer V3 (PTV3) encoder. PTV3 is a state-of-the-art point cloud encoding architecture that processes the point cloud into a point-based latent representation $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$, where $M$ is the number of points after downsampling and $d$ is the feature dimensionality. The PTV3 encoder uses a reduced depth of for its four stages and incorporates relative positional encoding (RPE) in alternating blocks.
3. **Action Conditioning:** Action conditioning is introduced at the bottleneck level:
 - The action $a_t$ is first encoded by a small MLP to produce an action embedding $z_{t}^{\text{act}}$.
 - A cross-attention mechanism is then employed. The point features $z_t$ serve as queries, while the action embedding $z_{t}^{\text{act}}$ and a learnable register token serve as keys and values. This design allows action-relevant points (e.g., robot parts) to attend to action commands, and action-irrelevant points (e.g., static scene objects) to attend to the register token, which acts as a dummy key. The paper reports that using one register token significantly improves performance.
 - Following cross-attention, self-attention layers propagate the effect of the action across all scene points, resulting in $z'_t$.
4. **Point Cloud Decoder and Velocity Prediction:** The $z'_t$ representation is decoded using PTV3's point decoder to recover features at the original point cloud resolution. A lightweight MLP then processes these per-point features to predict per-point velocities $\hat{v}_{t+1}$ for the next timestep.
5. **Next State Prediction:** The next timestep point cloud $\hat{P}_{t+1}$ is obtained by integrating the predicted velocities: $\hat{P}_{t+1} = P_t + \hat{v}_{t+1}$. Long-horizon rollouts are generated by recursively applying this dynamics model, where the predicted next state becomes the input for the subsequent prediction.

**Training Objective:**
The 3DPWM is trained using a Huber loss between the predicted per-point velocities $\hat{v}_{t+h}$ and the ground-truth velocities $v_{t+h}$. Crucially, the model is trained with multi-step rollout supervision over a horizon $H$, rather than single-step prediction. This means the loss is accumulated over an action sequence $a_t..., a_{t+H}$, which is found to significantly reduce compounding errors during rollouts. During simulation data collection, random actions are injected into expert trajectories to generate diverse interaction and collision dynamics, improving model robustness.

**Planning with 3DPWM:**
For control, 3DPWM is integrated into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework using a sampling-based MPPI planner. At each step, the planner rolls out $K$ trajectories using the learned dynamics model and optimizes a predefined cost function. This cost function is defined directly in the complete point cloud observation space, similar to how reward functions are specified in simulators.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research evaluates 3DPWM across several dimensions, including long-horizon rollout accuracy, model-based planning, adaptation to novel tasks, and sim-to-real transfer.

#### 4.1. Accuracy of Long-Horizon Rollouts

- **Setting:** Experiments were conducted on seven datasets spanning parallel gripper (ManiSkill: PickCube, StackCube, PickYCB; MimicGen: MugCleanup) and dexterous hand (DexMimicGen: BoxCleanup, DrawerCleanup) embodiments. Average rollout lengths ranged from 66 to 336 steps.
- **Baselines:** ParticleFormer$^+$ (a PTV3-based ParticleFormer variant trained on partial point clouds with a hybrid Chamfer/Hausdorff loss) and Partial 3DPWM (an ablated 3DPWM without the completion module, also trained on partial point clouds).
- **Metrics:** L1 Chamfer distance between rolled-out and ground-truth point clouds (lower is better), and Pearson correlation (r) between downstream task rewards on rolled-out and ground-truth point clouds (higher is better).
- **Results:**
 - 3DPWM consistently achieved significantly lower Chamfer distances across all tasks, indicating superior geometric accuracy in its long-horizon rollouts. For instance, on PickCube, 3DPWM achieved 0.009 Chamfer L1, compared to 0.080 for ParticleFormer$^+$ and 0.085 for Partial 3DPWM. Similar improvements were observed for other tasks.
 - 3DPWM also demonstrated higher reward correlation, with values ranging from 0.77 to 0.86, substantially outperforming ParticleFormer$^+$ (0.29-0.61) and Partial 3DPWM (0.25-0.57). This indicates that 3DPWM's rollouts are more reliable for evaluating task-specific costs.
 - The comparison between ParticleFormer$^+$ and Partial 3DPWM, which both operate on partial point clouds, showed competitive performance, highlighting the specific benefit of point completion in 3DPWM. The authors attribute this to the explicit completion module, which removes the need for the dynamics model to implicitly reason about occluded geometry.

#### 4.2. Model-based Planning

- **Setting:** MPPI planning was evaluated in both open-loop and closed-loop settings on PickCube, StackCube, and PickYCB tasks. Open-loop planning served as a stress test for long-horizon prediction robustness.
- **Baselines:** ParticleFormer$^+$, Partial 3DPWM, and 3DPWM variants with ground-truth (GT) point completion and learned point completion.
- **Metrics:** Success rate over 20 MPPI runs.
- **Results:**
 - **Closed-loop planning:** 3DPWM with learned completion achieved success rates of 85% (PickCube), 70% (StackCube), and 65% (PickYCB). With GT completion, performance was even higher (90%, 75%, 65%). In contrast, ParticleFormer$^+$ and Partial 3DPWM showed significantly lower success rates (e.g., ParticleFormer$^+$ achieved 35% on PickCube, 20% on StackCube, and 15% on PickYCB), attributed to inaccurate predictions from partial point clouds.
 - **Open-loop planning:** All methods experienced a substantial drop in performance compared to closed-loop. However, 3DPWM with GT completion (45% on PickCube, 35% on StackCube, 30% on PickYCB) and to a lesser extent with learned completion (35% on PickCube, 20% on StackCube, 15% on PickYCB) still achieved non-trivial success. ParticleFormer$^+$ and Partial 3DPWM largely failed (e.g., 5% on PickCube, 0% elsewhere), demonstrating their inability to produce accurate long-horizon rollouts required for open-loop control.

#### 4.3. Adaptation to Unseen Long-Horizon Tasks

- **Setting:** 3DPWM dynamics were trained on Coffee and Mug Cleanup tasks (MimicGen) and evaluated on the long-horizon Coffee Preparation task, which requires composing multiple subtasks.
- **Baseline:** ParticleFormer$^+$.
- **Results:** 3DPWM achieved 45% success on Coffee Preparation, demonstrating its ability to compose individually learned dynamics into extended task sequences. This represents a 15% drop from short-horizon tasks due to compounding errors. ParticleFormer$^+$ failed entirely on Coffee Preparation and performed poorly on the short-horizon tasks (0% success).

#### 4.4. Sim-to-Real Transfer

- **Setting:** Sim-to-real transfer was evaluated on tabletop Pick and Place tasks (PickApple, PickDrill, PlaceApple) using a Franka Panda robot with a real-world RGB-D camera. The PickYCB checkpoint from simulation was deployed without real-world fine-tuning.
- **Baseline:** ParticleFormer$^+$.
- **Metrics:** Success rates over 10 trials.
- **Results:**
 - On PlaceApple, both 3DPWM and ParticleFormer$^+$ achieved comparable success rates (70–80%).
 - On PickApple and PickDrill, 3DPWM substantially outperformed ParticleFormer$^+$ (PickApple: 3DPWM 60%, ParticleFormer$^+$ 20%; PickDrill: 3DPWM 50%, ParticleFormer$^+$ 20%). The improved performance is attributed to the enhanced object geometry provided by point completion.
 - Remaining failures in sim-to-real were primarily due to biased center-of-mass estimation, SAM3 segmentation failures (e.g., occluded objects), and noisy/out-of-distribution inputs to the point completion module.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Register Tokens:** Increasing the number of register tokens from 0 to 1 in the cross-attention mechanism yielded substantial gains in Chamfer distance (e.g., PickCube: 0.013 to 0.009; Mug Cleanup: 0.178 to 0.022). Additional tokens beyond one showed diminishing returns, supporting the hypothesis that a single register token effectively handles action-irrelevant points.
- **Multi-step Supervision Horizon (H):** Longer training horizons consistently improved dynamics modeling accuracy. Increasing H from 3 to 15 steps progressively reduced Chamfer distance across tasks (e.g., PickCube: 0.068 (H=3) to 0.009 (H=15)). The choice of H=15 was used for all main experiments, balancing accuracy with increased training time.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This work presents a 3D Point World Model (3DPWM) that integrates point cloud completion with a learned 3D dynamics model, demonstrating several advancements with potential implications for robotic manipulation.

The primary significance lies in the **improvement of long-horizon rollout accuracy and geometric consistency** by explicitly completing partial 3D observations. By operating on completed geometry, 3DPWM mitigates the challenges of occlusions and implicit geometric reasoning faced by previous 2D, 2.5D, and partial 3D dynamics models. The significantly lower Chamfer distances and higher reward correlations achieved by 3DPWM demonstrate its capacity to maintain accurate scene geometry and dynamics over extended prediction horizons (100-300+ steps), which is critical for complex, sequential robotic tasks.

This enhanced prediction fidelity directly translates to **more effective model-based planning**. The results indicate that 3DPWM enables superior performance in both closed-loop and open-loop planning scenarios, especially when compared to baselines that operate solely on partial point clouds. The ability to perform non-trivial open-loop planning underscores 3DPWM's potential as a robust proxy simulator in real-world environments where ground-truth observations for every step might be unavailable or costly. This could simplify deployment pipelines and reduce the need for constant re-planning.

Furthermore, the research demonstrates 3DPWM's **ability to adapt to novel, unseen long-horizon tasks**. By learning general interaction dynamics from a diverse set of demonstrations (even partially successful ones), the model can compose learned sub-task dynamics to tackle more complex, multi-stage manipulation sequences. This task-agnosticism contributes to the development of more versatile and general-purpose robotic systems, reducing the need for extensive task-specific model retraining.

The successful **sim-to-real transfer** without real-world fine-tuning is another notable impact. By training the dynamics model on complete point clouds in simulation and pairing it with a real-world point cloud completion module, 3DPWM bridges the reality gap. This approach leverages the advantages of high-fidelity simulation data for dynamics learning while enabling deployment on real hardware with partial sensor observations. The observed performance gains in real-world picking tasks suggest a pathway for more robust and capable real-world robotic systems.

From a technical perspective, the inclusion of **register tokens in the action-conditioned cross-attention** and the use of **multi-step rollout supervision** are demonstrated as important design choices. These contribute to better decoupling of action-relevant and action-irrelevant points and improved long-term stability of predictions, respectively.

Despite these advancements, the work also highlights areas for future development. The modular nature of 3DPWM, while offering flexibility, introduces **latency** and susceptibility to **cascading errors** from perception failures (e.g., SAM3 segmentation or point completion issues due to noisy or out-of-distribution real-world inputs). Addressing these limitations, potentially through tighter integration of perception components, training completion models on scene-level occlusions, or optimizing prediction latency with chunked multi-step prediction, could further amplify the impact of 3D point world models in dynamic and unstructured environments. The explicit 3D understanding provided by 3DPWM could enable robots to perform more dexterous and complex manipulations in cluttered and uncertain real-world settings.
