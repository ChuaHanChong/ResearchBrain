# Generative Verifiers: Reward Modeling as Next-Token Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Generative Verifiers (GenRM), developed by Google DeepMind, reframes reward modeling for Large Language Models as a next-token prediction task, enabling models to leverage their generative capabilities for verification. This approach, particularly with Chain-of-Thought reasoning and synthetic rationales, substantially improves performance on complex algorithmic and mathematical reasoning tasks, outperforming traditional discriminative verifiers.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often generate logically or factually incorrect information ("hallucinations"), particularly in complex reasoning tasks like mathematics.
- Traditional discriminative verifiers, used in "Best-of-N" strategies to mitigate hallucinations, fail to fully leverage the generative capabilities (e.g., Chain-of-Thought reasoning, instruction tuning) inherent in pre-trained LLMs.
- Existing methods like "LLM-as-a-Judge" or DPO have limitations in performance or task-specific fine-tuning for robust verification.

## Method

- Introduces ==Generative Verifiers (GenRM)== that reframe ==reward modeling as next-token prediction==, fine-tuned using standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
- Develops ==GenRM-CoT==, which incorporates Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning by training the model to generate an explicit critique before outputting a correctness judgment.
- Utilizes purely synthetic, ==reference-guided rationales== to train GenRM-CoT, reducing reliance on expensive human annotations.

## Results

- GenRM-CoT consistently and substantially outperforms discriminative reward models, DPO, and LLM-as-a-Judge across algorithmic and mathematical reasoning tasks.
- On GSM8K, using a Gemma2-9B GenRM-CoT verifier on Gemini 1.0 Pro solutions, the problem-solving rate increased from **73%** to **93.4%**.
- The approach demonstrates strong easy-to-hard generalization, improving performance on the challenging MATH dataset from **28%** to **44.6%** (Best-of-32) when trained on GSM8K.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Realigning reward modeling with the native next-token prediction objective of LLMs allows verifiers to leverage their full generative capabilities, improving accuracy.
> - Incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning within the verifier, particularly with inference-time majority voting, leads to substantial performance gains and strong generalization to harder problems.
> - High-quality verifiers can be effectively trained using purely synthetic, LLM-generated verification rationales, significantly reducing annotation costs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated extraordinary capabilities across a wide array of tasks, from natural language understanding to complex reasoning. However, a persistent challenge remains: their propensity to generate logically or factually incorrect information, even when appearing confident. This "hallucination" problem is particularly acute in reasoning tasks, such as mathematics or algorithmic problem-solving, where a single erroneous step can invalidate an entire solution.

To mitigate this, the "Best-of-N" strategy has emerged as a widely adopted technique. This involves an LLM generating multiple candidate solutions (N), which are then evaluated and ranked by a separate "verifier" or "reward model," with the highest-scoring solution being selected. The efficacy of Best-of-N is thus directly tied to the accuracy and reliability of the verifier.

Traditionally, LLM-based verifiers have been trained as *discriminative classifiers*. These models are fine-tuned to output a numerical score, typically between 0 and 1, representing the probability of a solution's correctness. While effective to some extent, this discriminative approach fundamentally overlooks the *generative* nature of the underlying LLM. Pre-trained LLMs are built upon the next-token prediction objective, giving them inherent capabilities in text generation, instruction following, and chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. Discriminative verifiers, by reducing the task to a single score, fail to fully leverage these strengths. This limitation means they cannot easily benefit from unified instruction tuning, explicit CoT reasoning during verification, or the ability to scale performance with additional inference-time computation (e.g., by sampling multiple reasoning paths).

Another contemporary approach, "LLM-as-a-Judge," involves simply prompting a pre-trained, general-purpose LLM to evaluate solutions. While this leverages the LLM's generative abilities for critique, it typically underperforms verifiers that have undergone specific training for the task, as it lacks task-specific fine-tuning. Furthermore, preference optimization methods like DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) have aimed to unify policy (generation) and reward modeling, but have shown limitations such as erroneous extrapolation or degradation in learned representations for reasoning tasks.

This paper introduces **Generative Verifiers (GenRM)** as a paradigm shift, positioning verification as an *intrinsic text generation task* using the ubiquitous next-token prediction objective. By doing so, it directly addresses the shortcomings of discriminative verifiers and LLM-as-a-Judge. GenRM aims to bridge the gap between an LLM's core generative capabilities and the specific demands of a verification task. It distinguishes itself from DPO by using a simpler Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) approach for verification, avoiding some of the complexities and potential pitfalls of implicit reward modeling. The work thus contributes to the ongoing effort to make LLMs more reliable and robust in complex reasoning domains, offering a more natural and powerful way to train verifiers that fully harnesses the underlying architecture of modern LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind "Generative Verifiers" stems from the observation that traditional discriminative reward models, while useful, fail to fully exploit the text generation capabilities inherent in pre-trained LLMs. This limitation prevents them from benefiting from several key advantages that LLMs offer, such as seamless integration with instruction tuning, the ability to perform chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, and the utilization of additional test-time compute.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Reframe Reward Modeling as Next-Token Prediction:** The primary objective is to propose and develop Generative Verifiers (GenRM) that train reward models using the fundamental next-token prediction objective. This shifts the paradigm from a classification task to a generation task, where the correctness decision (e.g., "Yes" or "No") is just another token in a sequence.
2. **Leverage LLM-Specific Advantages:** Demonstrate that this generative approach enables the verifier to inherently benefit from:
 - **Unified Instruction Tuning:** Allowing the verifier to be fine-tuned more effectively with instruction-tuned LLMs.
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** Enabling the verifier to explicitly reason step-by-step before making a correctness judgment, thereby detecting subtle errors.
 - **Scalable Inference-Time Compute:** Allowing the verifier to improve accuracy by sampling multiple CoT rationales and using techniques like majority voting, which is not feasible for discriminative RMs.
3. **Comparative Performance Evaluation:** Rigorously compare GenRM against established verification approaches, including standard discriminative reward models, DPO-based verifiers, and LLM-as-a-Judge, across a range of challenging reasoning tasks (algorithmic and mathematical).
4. **Explore Synergy Between Generation and Verification:** Investigate whether unifying the training for solution generation and solution verification within the same LLM, through a combined next-token prediction objective, can lead to mutual benefits and positive transfer between the two tasks.
5. **Assess Scaling Properties:** Evaluate how GenRM's performance scales with increasing model size and with the application of additional inference-time compute, demonstrating its potential for larger, more capable models.
6. **Validate Synthetic Rationales:** Determine the effectiveness of training GenRM-CoT using purely synthetically generated verification rationales (rather than human-written ones), thereby addressing the high cost and difficulty of human annotation, especially for complex reasoning.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to introduce a more potent and naturally aligned framework for building LLM verifiers, ultimately enhancing the reliability and reasoning prowess of LLMs in critical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology lies in reformulating the reward modeling problem from a discriminative classification task to a generative next-token prediction task. The paper introduces **Generative Verifiers (GenRM)**, which are LLMs fine-tuned using the standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) objective.

#### 3.1. GenRM (Direct Verifier)

In its simplest form, GenRM directly predicts the correctness of a solution by predicting either a 'Yes' or 'No' token. The model is fine-tuned using the SFT loss (cross-entropy) on a dataset where each example consists of a problem, a candidate solution, and an instruction (e.g., "Is the answer correct (Yes/No)?"), followed by the target token ('Yes' for correct solutions, 'No' for incorrect ones). During inference, the verifier's score for a solution is derived from the likelihood of the 'Yes' token, i.e., $P_{\theta}(\text{Yes} | \text{problem, solution, instruction})$. This probability naturally reflects the model's confidence.

#### 3.2. GenRM-CoT (Chain-of-Thought Verifier)

Recognizing that complex reasoning often benefits from explicit step-by-step thinking, GenRM-CoT extends the direct verifier by incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. The model is trained to first generate an intermediate verification rationale (critique or reasoning steps) before outputting the final 'Yes' or 'No' correctness token. This is achieved by training on datasets where the target sequence includes both the verification CoT and the correctness token, often preceded by an instruction like "Let's verify step by step."

A significant advantage of GenRM-CoT is its ability to leverage additional **inference-time compute** for improved accuracy. By sampling multiple CoT rationales ($K$) for a given problem-solution pair, the verifier can average the 'Yes' token probabilities across these different reasoning paths (majority voting). This aggregates confidence and mitigates errors from individual reasoning paths, enhancing overall verification accuracy. The paper uses $K=32$ votes by default for GenRM-CoT evaluations.

#### 3.3. Unifying Generation and Verification

GenRM inherently supports unifying solution generation and verification within a single LLM. This is achieved by combining the SFT loss for verification with a standard SFT loss for generating correct solutions. The total loss is $L_{\text{GenRM}}(\theta, D_{\text{verify}}) = L_{\text{SFT}}(\theta, D_{\text{verify}}) + \lambda L_{\text{SFT}}(\theta, D_{\text{correct}})$, where $\lambda$ controls the mixture ratio. This joint training is hypothesized to foster positive transfer, as the model learns both how to generate correct solutions and how to discern correctness.

#### 3.4. Synthetic Verification CoT Rationales

A critical practical aspect is the acquisition of verification rationales for training GenRM-CoT. To circumvent the expense and difficulty of human annotation, especially for complex problems, the authors explore using synthetically generated rationales. They employ a more sophisticated technique than simple self-critique: **reference-guided grading**. During synthetic data generation, a highly capable LLM (Gemini 1.0 Pro) is prompted to generate a verification rationale by comparing a candidate solution to a known *correct reference solution*. This provides a clearer basis for critique. The generated rationales are then filtered based on whether they correctly assess the solution's correctness. Importantly, the reference solution is *only* used during synthetic data generation and not during the actual fine-tuning or inference of the verifier, preventing train/test mismatch.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Baselines

The methodology includes extensive empirical evaluation on:
- **Algorithmic Reasoning:** Last Letter Concatenation and Word Sorting, designed to test length generalization.
- **Math Reasoning:** GSM8K (grade-school math) and its easy-to-hard generalization to the more challenging MATH dataset, as well as MMLU mathematical tasks.

**Baselines for comparison include:**
- **Discriminative RM:** The standard approach fine-tuning an LLM as a binary classifier.
- **LLM-as-a-Judge:** Prompting an off-the-shelf LLM (Gemini 1.0 Pro) to perform verification, also using majority voting ($K=32$).
- **DPO:** A preference optimization method for training verifiers.
- **Self-consistency:** A baseline that selects the most frequent answer among N sampled solutions, without a learned verifier.

**Models & Training:** The verifiers are fine-tuned from Gemma models (2B, 7B, Gemma2-9B). Solution generation and LLM-as-a-Judge use Gemma 2B for algorithmic tasks and Gemini 1.0 Pro for math. Hyperparameters include Adam optimizer, specific learning rates, weight decay, and cosine decay schedules. Evaluation metrics are primarily Best-of-N performance (% problems solved) and RM accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate the superior performance of Generative Verifiers (GenRM) compared to existing methods across various reasoning tasks.

- **Generative Verifiers Outperform Standard Approaches:**
 - **GenRM (Direct):** Performs comparably to or slightly better than discriminative RMs, while significantly outperforming DPO verifiers and LLM-as-a-Judge (Figure D.1).
 - **GenRM-CoT (with Majority Voting):** Shows substantial improvements over the direct GenRM and all other baselines. On algorithmic tasks, GenRM-CoT (using oracle verification CoTs) nearly matches the performance of an *oracle verifier*. On GSM8K, using a Gemma2-9B GenRM-CoT verifier on solutions from Gemini 1.0 Pro, the problem-solving rate jumps from 73% to 93.4%, surpassing reported GPT-4 and Gemini 1.5 Pro performance levels.
 - **Qualitative Advantage:** GenRM-CoT is adept at detecting subtle reasoning errors that often elude discriminative or direct GenRM verifiers (Figures 2, 4, 15), owing to its explicit chain-of-thought verification process.

- **Strong Easy-to-Hard Generalization:**
 - GenRM-CoT, when trained solely on grade-school math problems (GSM8K), exhibits remarkable generalization to significantly harder high-school competition problems in the MATH dataset. It improves performance from 28% to 44.6% (with Best-of-32) and achieves a 6.4x better sample efficiency compared to discriminative verifiers (Figure 5).
 - Similar strong generalization is observed on MMLU abstract algebra, improving from 37.9% to 53.5% (Table 1), with more significant gains on harder MMLU math subtasks (College Mathematics, Abstract Algebra).
 - GenRM-CoT also shows superior performance across all difficulty levels of the MATH dataset and various subject areas like Pre-Algebra, Algebra, and Pre-Calculus (Figure 6).

- **Synergy Between Generation and Verification:**
 - Unified training, where GenRM is jointly trained for both solution generation and verification via next-token prediction, consistently improves verification performance across all tasks (Figure 9). This suggests that learning to generate correct solutions positively influences the ability to verify them.
 - Conversely, incorporating CoT verification data into the generator's training mix also boosts its own solution generation performance, as indicated by improved Best-of-N scores with an oracle verifier (Figure 10). This implies a mutual benefit, deepening the model's understanding of reasoning.

- **Favorable Scaling with Model Size and Inference-Time Compute:**
 - **Inference-Time Compute:** GenRM-CoT's performance scales gracefully with the number of sampled CoT rationales for majority voting. Even with just 2 votes, it surpasses greedy decoding performance. Crucially, finetuned GenRM-CoT consistently outperforms LLM-as-a-Judge (even when LLM-as-a-Judge uses a more capable base model like Gemini 1.0 Pro and the same number of votes) (Figure 11).
 - **Model Size:** Generative verifiers, especially GenRM-CoT, consistently outperform discriminative RMs across different Gemma model sizes (2B, 7B, Gemma2-9B), both in Best-of-N performance and RM accuracy (Figure 12). This demonstrates that the generative verification approach scales effectively with model capacity.

- **Effectiveness of Synthetic Rationales:**
 - The study shows that high-performing CoT verifiers can be trained using purely synthetic, LLM-generated verification rationales, removing the dependency on expensive human annotation.
 - **Quality Matters:** Employing "reference-guided grading" during synthetic rationale generation significantly improves performance (Figure 13).
 - **Quantity Matters:** Using multiple rationales per solution during training also enhances performance, likely due to an ensembling effect that mitigates errors in individual synthetic rationales (Figure 14).

These findings collectively highlight GenRM as a robust and highly effective framework for building LLM verifiers, demonstrating significant advancements in LLM reasoning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Generative Verifiers (GenRM) represents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in the field of Large Language Models, particularly in enhancing their reasoning capabilities and trustworthiness.

**1. Paradigm Shift in Reward Modeling:** GenRM fundamentally redefines reward modeling from a discriminative classification problem to a generative next-token prediction task. This aligns reward modeling with the native architecture and training objective of LLMs, enabling the verifier to fully leverage the model's inherent text generation prowess. This shift could inspire new research directions in how auxiliary tasks are framed and integrated within generative models.

**2. Substantial Performance Gains on Reasoning Tasks:** The demonstrated improvements in Best-of-N performance on complex algorithmic and mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., 73% to 93.4% on GSM8K, significant gains on MATH and MMLU) are substantial. These gains directly translate to more reliable LLM outputs, which is critical for sensitive applications where accuracy is paramount, such as scientific discovery, engineering design, or financial analysis. The ability to nearly match oracle verifier performance on some tasks is particularly noteworthy.

**3. Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** GenRM-CoT's strong easy-to-hard generalization, where a model trained on simpler problems effectively verifies solutions to much harder ones, is highly impactful. This property is crucial for real-world deployment, as it reduces the need for extensive, domain-specific training data for complex problems. The qualitative examples showing GenRM-CoT's ability to pinpoint subtle reasoning errors further underscore its robustness.

**4. Reduced Annotation Burden and Cost:** A major practical implication is the finding that high-quality human-annotated verification rationales are not strictly necessary. The success of training GenRM-CoT with purely synthetic, model-generated rationales (especially with reference-guided grading) has the potential to significantly reduce the cost and time associated with developing high-performing verifiers. This "data efficiency" could democratize access to advanced verification techniques, making them more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited annotation budgets.

**5. Optimal Utilization of LLM Capabilities:** By seamlessly integrating instruction tuning, CoT reasoning, and inference-time compute scaling (via majority voting), GenRM maximizes the utility of modern LLM architectures. This demonstrates a more "natural" way for LLMs to perform verification, tapping into their full potential rather than forcing them into a restrictive classification paradigm.

**6. Unified and More Coherent LLM Training:** The ability to unify solution generation and verification training within a single LLM is a step towards more holistic and integrated AI systems. This mutual positive transfer between generation and verification indicates a deeper, more robust understanding of the problem space, potentially leading to more versatile and capable foundation models.

**7. Future Research Catalyst:** The paper concludes by outlining several promising future directions, suggesting GenRM as a foundational framework. These include applying generative verification to diverse domains (coding, alignment, text-to-image), leveraging advanced techniques like retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and tool use, and integrating GenRM into reinforcement learning pipelines for LLMs. This breadth of potential applications underscores the framework's versatility and its capacity to spur significant follow-up research.

In summary, "Generative Verifiers" proposes a compelling and effective method for improving the reliability of LLMs in reasoning tasks. By realigning reward modeling with the generative core of LLMs, it offers a more powerful, scalable, and data-efficient approach, poised to significantly impact the development and deployment of more trustworthy and intelligent AI systems.
