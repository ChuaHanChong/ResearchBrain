# VLA-RL: Towards Masterful and General Robotic Manipulation with Scalable Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University and Nanyang Technological University researchers develop VLA-RL, a reinforcement learning framework that fine-tunes pre-trained vision-language-action models for robotic manipulation, achieving 4.5% performance improvement over supervised fine-tuning baselines on the LIBERO benchmark and matching commercial model performance through trajectory-level RL formulation combined with a robotic process reward model that provides dense rewards for sparse manipulation tasks, while demonstrating test-time scaling benefits where extended training consistently improves robot performance and exhibiting superior action space coverage compared to behavior cloning approaches.

## Problem

- Imitation learning approaches for robotic manipulation struggle with out-of-distribution generalization when deployed in new environments or tasks
- Sparse reward signals in robotic reinforcement learning make training inefficient and unstable
- Existing robotic foundation models trained purely on offline datasets lack the exploration capabilities needed for robust real-world performance

## Method

- Formulate robotic manipulation as ==multi-modal, multi-turn conversations== enabling ==trajectory-level RL optimization== using existing LLM frameworks
- Train a ==vision-language robotic process reward model== on automatically extracted task segments to provide dense, informative rewards
- Implement systematic improvements including ==curriculum selection strategy==, ==GPU-balanced vectorized environments==, and ==critic warmup== for training stability

## Results

- **VLA-RL** achieves **4.5%** improvement over supervised fine-tuning baseline on LIBERO benchmark manipulation tasks
- Performance matches advanced commercial models like π0-FAST while demonstrating superior action space coverage and robustness
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of each framework component, with the robotic process reward model providing the largest performance gains

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning can successfully improve pre-trained robotic foundation models beyond what supervised fine-tuning achieves
> - Test-time scaling laws appear to apply to robotics, with extended RL training consistently improving performance
> - Dense process rewards are crucial for enabling effective RL training in sparse-reward robotic manipulation tasks

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research falls within the intersection of several active areas of research:

- **Robotic Foundation Models:** There is a growing trend toward developing large, general-purpose models for robotics that can be adapted to a variety of tasks and environments. VLA-RL directly contributes to this area by exploring how to fine-tune existing vision-language-action models for improved performance. The paper acknowledges and builds upon previous work in this area, such as RT-1, RT-2, and OpenVLA.

- **Reinforcement Learning for Robotics:** Reinforcement learning has long been a promising approach to robotics, but it has often been limited by data inefficiency and the need for careful reward engineering. VLA-RL addresses these challenges by using a pre-trained VLA model as a starting point and by developing a robotic process reward model to provide denser and more informative rewards.

- **Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models:** Recent breakthroughs in applying reinforcement learning to large language models have shown remarkable progress in improving reasoning capabilities and aligning models with human preferences. VLA-RL draws inspiration from this work by formulating trajectory-level optimization as a multi-modal, multi-turn conversation, enabling robots to benefit from similar RL frameworks that have proven effective for large language models.

- **Offline vs. Online Learning:** The paper highlights the limitations of purely offline learning approaches (i.e., imitation learning) for robotics, where models trained on limited datasets may struggle to generalize to out-of-distribution scenarios. VLA-RL advocates for an exploration-based approach that leverages online reinforcement learning to improve performance and robustness.

This paper contributes to the ongoing shift from imitation learning to RL-based methods, addressing the out-of-distribution problem that arises when deploying robot learning models in the real world. This work is significant, because it tackles the challenge of achieving generalization by allowing the models to actively explore the environment and adapt their behavior through trial and error, rather than simply mimicking pre-recorded demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the paper are:

- **To develop a scalable reinforcement learning framework (VLA-RL) that can improve the performance of pre-trained vision-language-action models for robotic manipulation.**
- **To address the challenges of sparse rewards in robotic reinforcement learning by developing a robotic process reward model.**
- **To demonstrate the effectiveness of VLA-RL on a challenging set of robotic manipulation tasks in the LIBERO benchmark.**
- **To investigate whether reinforcement learning can lead to test-time scaling benefits in robotics, similar to those observed in large language models.**

The motivation behind this work stems from the limitations of existing imitation learning approaches for robotic manipulation, which often struggle to generalize to new environments and tasks. The authors believe that reinforcement learning can overcome these limitations by allowing robots to actively explore their environment and learn from their own experiences. By fine-tuning large robotic foundation models with trajectory-level RL, the research aims to significantly reduce the search space and enable the model to learn complex motion patterns, making training on general tasks and environments possible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a framework called VLA-RL, which combines a pre-trained vision-language-action model (OpenVLA-7B) with online reinforcement learning. The key components of their approach are:

- **Trajectory-Level RL Formulation:** The authors formulate general robotic manipulation as a multi-modal, multi-turn conversation, where the robot interacts with the environment by generating action token sequences based on visual observations and human instructions. This formulation allows them to leverage existing RL algorithms developed for large language models.

- **Robotic Process Reward Model:** To address the challenge of sparse rewards, the authors train a vision-language model as a robotic process reward model. This model is trained on automatically extracted task segments and pseudo-reward labels to provide denser and more informative rewards to the agent.

- **Systematic Implementation Improvements:** The authors identify several implementation details that improve the stability and efficiency of VLA-RL, including a curriculum selection strategy, GPU-balanced vectorized environments, batch decoding, and critic warmup.

- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** They utilize PPO, a popular policy gradient algorithm, to optimize the VLA policy based on the rewards provided by the environment and the robotic process reward model.

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** The authors evaluate VLA-RL on a set of 40 challenging robotic manipulation tasks in the LIBERO benchmark. This benchmark allows them to compare the performance of VLA-RL against existing imitation learning methods and commercial models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the paper are:

- **VLA-RL significantly improves the performance of the base OpenVLA-7B model on the LIBERO benchmark.** VLA-RL outperforms the supervised fine-tuning baseline by 4.5% and even matches the performance of advanced commercial models such as π0-FAST.

- **VLA-RL benefits from increased test-time optimization, indicating an early spark of inference scaling laws in robotics.** The authors observe that the performance of VLA-RL consistently improves as the model is trained for longer, suggesting that reinforcement learning can lead to significant gains in performance over time.

- **VLA-RL exhibits higher robustness than behavior cloning in terms of the state and action coverage of the training data.** The authors show that the actions generated by VLA-RL are distributed more uniformly across the action space, allowing the model to adapt to a wider variety of states.

- **The proposed techniques and implementation details (robotic process reward model, curriculum selection strategy, critic warmup, etc.) significantly impact VLA-RL's performance.** The authors conduct an ablation study to demonstrate the importance of each component of VLA-RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has several significant implications for the field of robotics:

- **It demonstrates the potential of reinforcement learning for improving the performance of robotic foundation models.** VLA-RL shows that reinforcement learning can overcome the limitations of imitation learning and enable robots to learn more robust and generalizable skills.

- **It provides a practical framework for scaling up reinforcement learning for robotics.** The systematic implementation improvements identified in the paper can help other researchers to train VLA models more efficiently and effectively.

- **It suggests that reinforcement learning can lead to test-time scaling benefits in robotics, similar to those observed in large language models.** This finding could have a significant impact on the way robots are trained and deployed in the future.

- **It advances the development of general-purpose robots that can perform a wide range of tasks in diverse environments.** By improving the generalization and robustness of robotic manipulation skills, VLA-RL contributes to the long-term goal of creating robots that can assist humans in a variety of real-world settings.

The potential impact of this work extends beyond robotics, as the techniques developed in VLA-RL could be applied to other areas where reinforcement learning is used to control complex systems, such as autonomous driving and game playing. This research moves the field closer to achieving truly intelligent and adaptable robotic systems that can learn and improve over time.
