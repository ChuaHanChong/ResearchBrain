# Learning to summarize user information for personalized reinforcement learning from human feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Preference Learning Using Summarization (PLUS) uses a language model to create natural language summaries of user preferences, which subsequently personalize a reward model for reinforcement learning from human feedback. This approach achieved higher prediction accuracy for personalized reward modeling across various benchmarks and enabled personalization of powerful proprietary LLMs.

## Problem

- Conventional Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) assumes uniform user preferences, failing to account for diverse and often conflicting individual needs.
- Existing personalized RLHF methods typically represent user characteristics as opaque, fixed-length embedding vectors, which limits interpretability and the capacity to retain rich textual information.
- Personalizing Large Language Models (LLMs) for heterogeneous user populations in complex, real-world scenarios with sparse data remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **PLUS** develops a novel framework that leverages an LLM as a ==summarizer== to generate concise, natural language summaries of user context and preferences.
- It employs an online co-adaptation mechanism, where the summarizer and a summary-conditioned reward model are jointly trained using a reinforcement learning loop (PPO for summarizer, NLL loss for reward model).
- The summarizer is explicitly optimized to create summaries that maximize the reward model's accuracy in predicting personalized user preferences.

## Results

- PLUS achieved up to **17.7%** higher prediction accuracy over standard baselines like BTL and other personalized methods (VPL, ICL) on complex UltraFeedback datasets.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, maintaining **93.67%** accuracy on out-of-distribution preference topics (Pets OOD) while ICL and VPL performance significantly dropped.
- PLUS-generated summaries improved the accuracy of GPT-4o's preference predictions by **19.5%** and enabled personalized response generation with a **72%** win rate against unpersonalized responses, as evaluated by an oracle reward model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Text-based, learned summaries provide a more interpretable and powerful representation of user preferences compared to opaque vector embeddings, enhancing predictive utility for personalized RLHF.
> - The co-adaptation of the summarizer and reward model is critical; training them jointly allows the summarizer to produce information directly relevant to improving reward model accuracy.
> - Effective user summaries can enable personalization of powerful proprietary LLMs without requiring their direct fine-tuning, demonstrating significant portability and practical value.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The proliferation of large language models (LLMs) and their integration into daily user interactions has underscored the necessity for personalization. While Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has been instrumental in enhancing LLMs' general helpfulness and fluency, conventional RLHF methodologies, such as those employing the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) reward model, typically operate under the assumption of uniform user preferences. This approach models the entire user population with a single reward function, thereby failing to account for the inherent diversity and potential conflicts in individual user preferences.

Existing research has identified this limitation, leading to calls for "pluralistic alignment" techniques to enable LLMs to personalize responses to heterogeneous user needs. Previous attempts to address this include the development of reward models conditioned on user variability, often by learning latent user embeddings (e.g., Variational Preference Learning (VPL)) or group-specific adaptations. However, these methods frequently represent user characteristics as embedding vectors or linear weights. This vector-based representation can compromise both the interpretability of the user model and its capacity to retain rich textual information, potentially leading to performance degradation.

This paper positions itself within this evolving landscape by proposing a novel framework that leverages the text-generation capabilities of LLMs to create interpretable, text-based summaries of user preferences. Unlike prior work that might use pre-trained summarizers or fixed user classifiers, this research introduces an online co-adaptation mechanism, where the user summarization model and the reward model are trained simultaneously. This approach aims to provide a more natural and powerful method for capturing diverse user preferences, improving accuracy, interpretability, and transferability in personalized RLHF, particularly for complex, real-world datasets where explicit user identifiers or clear preference labels may be sparse or unavailable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind this research stems from the growing imperative to personalize LLM responses in an era where billions of users interact with these AI assistants daily across a multitude of tasks, from domain-specific queries to creative content generation. Current RLHF pipelines, largely based on a single reward model, exhibit significant limitations when confronted with the reality of diverse and often conflicting user preferences.

The key motivations are:

- **Addressing the "One-Size-Fits-All" Limitation of Standard RLHF:** The standard BTL model, prevalent in RLHF, learns a reward function based on majority preferences. This design inherently struggles when user preferences diverge, leading to situations where minority preferences are disregarded or the reward model becomes inaccurate for all user groups.
- **Enabling Pluralistic Alignment:** Given the widespread and diverse user base of LLMs, the ability to model and adhere to more than one set of preferences is crucial for developing effective and accurate personalized assistants. This research directly addresses the need for pluralistic alignment to tailor LLMs to individual users' unique requirements.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Latent Vector Representations:** Previous personalized RLHF methods often compress rich user context into fixed-length embedding vectors. This compression can lead to a loss of information and reduced interpretability, which can be a barrier to understanding how an LLM is personalizing its responses and why.
- **Improving Performance in Complex, Real-World Scenarios:** Existing techniques have demonstrated varying degrees of success on controlled benchmarks but have shown limited published results on highly heterogeneous, real-world datasets like PRISM, which feature diverse users, conversation topics, and LLM interactions.
- **Enhancing Transparency and User Control:** The opaque nature of latent embeddings can hinder user trust and control over personalized AI systems. Representing user preferences in natural language offers a more transparent and potentially editable format for users.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:

- **Develop a novel pluralistic alignment algorithm, Preference Learning Using Summarization (PLUS):** This framework utilizes reinforcement learning to jointly learn a text-based summarizer and a reward model.
- **Demonstrate improved reward model accuracy:** The goal is to achieve significant improvements in personalized preference prediction compared to standard BTL and existing personalized RLHF techniques across various benchmark datasets.
- **Ensure robustness to novel user contexts and conversation topics:** The developed framework should effectively generalize to new users with unseen preferences and conversation topics that differ from the training data.
- **Enable personalization of powerful proprietary LLMs without fine-tuning:** A key objective is to show that PLUS-generated summaries can be used to condition responses from strong proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4) for personalization, without requiring additional fine-tuning of these large models.
- **Support learning from flexible, unstructured user contexts:** The methodology should be capable of inferring user preferences from various forms of textual input, including past conversations without explicit preference labels or user-guided instructions, beyond just chosen/rejected response pairs.
- **Provide interpretable user representations:** The generated text-based summaries are intended to offer transparent and human-readable representations of user preferences, facilitating greater understanding and potential user control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Preference Learning Using Summarization (PLUS)**, is designed to learn text-based summaries of user information and use these summaries to personalize the reward model in an RLHF setting. The core approach involves the online co-adaptation of two primary components: a summarizer and a summary-conditioned reward model.

**Framework Overview:**
PLUS leverages an LLM as a summarizer, denoted as **π_θ**, to generate concise natural language representations (summaries, `z`) based on a user's context (`c`). This context can include past conversations, preferences, or other textual information. The generated summary `z` then conditions the **reward model (r_ϕ)**, enabling it to make personalized predictions about which responses a specific user would prefer.

**Co-dependent Training Phases:**
The training of `π_θ` and `r_ϕ` is designed as a co-adaptive loop, formalized in Algorithm 1. This means that neither component is pre-trained independently; instead, they learn iteratively, with each informing the other's optimization.

1. **Training the Reward Model (r_ϕ):**
 - In this phase, the weights of the summarizer `π_θ` are kept fixed.
 - The reward model `r_ϕ` is trained to minimize a negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss, as defined in Equation 3. This loss measures how accurately `r_ϕ` predicts user preferences for pairs of chosen (`s_A`) and rejected (`s_B`) responses, conditioned on summaries (`z`) sampled from the current `π_θ`.
 - The objective is to maximize the likelihood of the preferred response given the user context and summary.

2. **Training the Summarizer (π_θ):**
 - Once `r_ϕ` is updated, its weights are fixed to provide a reward signal for the summarizer.
 - Generating a summary is framed as a reinforcement learning problem. The summarizer `π_θ` is optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to maximize a return signal derived from the reward model's performance (Equation 4).
 - The reward `R_ϕ` for the summarizer is defined as the negative log-likelihood loss of the reward model's prediction at the end of a summary generation trajectory (`t=H`). This means a summary is considered "good" if it helps `r_ϕ` make accurate predictions.
 - PPO is chosen for its robustness in multi-agent RL (MARL) problems, where the reward signal for the summarizer becomes non-stationary due to the continuous updates of the reward model. Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) (Equation 5) is used for token-level advantage estimation, and a clipping mechanism (Equation 6) is applied to maintain training stability.

**Rationale for Learning to Summarize:**
The paper emphasizes that directly prompting an LLM to generate summaries without fine-tuning, or conditioning the reward model on the entire conversation history (In-Context Learning - ICL), often falls short. Untrained summaries may focus on irrelevant details or general stylistic preferences, lacking predictive power for the reward model. ICL, while theoretically capable, can be computationally expensive and sample-inefficient due to long context lengths, especially when conversation topics shift. Learned, concise summaries, in contrast, are designed to extract only the information most pertinent to preference prediction.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, and Llama3.2-3B-Instruct were used for reward models and summarizers, with models from the same family typically paired.
- **Hardware:** Training was conducted on 2-4 x H200 GPUs.
- **Framework:** The co-adaptation pipeline was built upon the OpenRLHF PPO training framework, with adaptations for reward model training from OpenRLHF and baselines (VPL, DPL, BTL) from Poddar et al. (2024).

**Evaluation Domains:**
The methodology was evaluated across diverse datasets:
- **Pets:** A synthetic dataset with a binary preference (dogs vs. cats) to demonstrate basic personalization.
- **Pets OOD (Out-Of-Distribution):** A variant of Pets where test-time user preferences (rabbits vs. birds) differ from training preferences, testing generalization to novel preferences.
- **UltraFeedback-P2 & UltraFeedback-P4:** Domains derived from the UltraFeedback dataset, focusing on preferences over LLM attributes like helpfulness, honesty, truthfulness, and instruction-following, with varying conversation content between train and test sets.
- **PRISM:** A real-world, large-scale, and highly heterogeneous dataset of 1,500 users from 75 countries, engaging in open-ended conversations with 21 LLMs. This dataset is particularly challenging due to its diversity and sparse data for specific preferences.

**Baselines:**
PLUS was compared against several established and state-of-the-art RLHF techniques:
- **Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL):** Standard, non-personalized reward model.
- **Distributional Preference Learning (DPL):** Learns mean and variance of reward distribution to capture overall user variance.
- **Variational Preference Learning (VPL):** Learns a latent vector representation of each user using a VAE.
- **In-Context Learning (ICL):** Uses past conversation examples directly as part of the reward model's prompt.
- **PLUS-untrained:** An ablation where the summarizer is not fine-tuned with RL, using default LLM summaries.
- **Oracle:** A reward model conditioned on true user preferences, serving as an upper bound.

This comprehensive methodological approach emphasizes the learned, text-based nature of user representations and the online co-adaptation of components to maximize predictive utility and interpretability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various benchmarks and a real-world dataset yielded several key findings regarding the performance and characteristics of the PLUS framework.

**1. Advantages on Existing Benchmarks (Table 1):**
- PLUS consistently achieved higher prediction accuracy for personalized reward modeling compared to all baselines, including BTL and other personalized RLHF techniques (DPL, VPL, ICL).
- On the Pets dataset, PLUS demonstrated a 77% improvement over BTL, achieving 99.80% accuracy. Both VPL and ICL also performed strongly on Pets, but their performance was notably lower on more complex domains.
- For the UltraFeedback-P2 and UltraFeedback-P4 domains, which feature more varied conversation content, PLUS showed significant improvements of 10.4–17.7% over BTL, whereas ICL achieved 0.9–8.4% improvements, and VPL’s improvements were marginal (ranging from -2.76% to 4.81%). This indicates that VPL struggles to generalize to nuanced real-world preferences when distilling rich text into fixed-length vectors.
- The ablation study with PLUS-untrained (using a fixed, un-finetuned summarizer) showed improvements over BTL but significantly underperformed the full PLUS model, highlighting the importance of the co-adaptation process for learning effective summaries.

**2. Robustness to New Topics and Users (Pets OOD) (Table 2):**
- PLUS demonstrated strong generalization capabilities when tested on out-of-distribution (OOD) user contexts with entirely new preference topics (rabbits and birds, after training on cats and dogs).
- PLUS maintained an accuracy of 93.67%, while ICL’s performance dropped from 100% (in-distribution) to 72.50%, and VPL’s accuracy fell below 50%.
- This result suggests that PLUS learns a more generalized mechanism for extracting and utilizing user information, making it robust to shifts in conversation content and new user preferences.

**3. Importance of Co-adapting Summarizer and Reward Model (Table 3):**
- The co-adaptation of the summarizer and reward model was critical for performance. Even a smaller co-adapted system (e.g., 0.5B reward model with a 3B summarizer) achieved higher accuracy (69.40% on UF-P2) than scenarios where either the reward model or summarizer was static.
- Using a strong proprietary model like GPT-4o as a summarizer with a trained 3B reward model still underperformed co-trained smaller models (59.30% for UF-P2 vs. 69.40% for co-adapted PLUS), supporting the hypothesis that the quality of a summary is best defined by its utility to the downstream reward model, necessitating joint optimization.

**4. Incorporating Flexible Textual Inputs (Table 4, Appendix A):**
- PLUS demonstrated the ability to infer user preferences from flexible textual inputs beyond explicit preference labels, such as non-preference-based conversation data or user-guided instructions.
- When trained on unstructured user contexts, PLUS outperformed ICL by 9–15% on UltraFeedback datasets.
- With additional user-guided instructions, PLUS showed a 10–16% improvement over ICL, indicating its capacity to leverage diverse forms of user information effectively.

**5. Evaluation in the Wild with PRISM User Data (Table 8, Appendix G):**
- On the challenging real-world PRISM dataset, PLUS achieved 62.9% accuracy on held-out conversations for in-distribution users, outperforming BTL (59.8%), VPL (60.4%), and ICL (60.1%).
- However, overall reward prediction accuracy for all methods, including PLUS, was noted as "somewhat low" for PRISM, indicating the dataset's high heterogeneity and the difficulty of modeling nuanced real user preferences with limited data.
- For out-of-distribution users in PRISM (Table 11), non-personalized models (BTL and DPL) surprisingly outperformed personalized models by simply predicting the majority, highlighting the significant challenge of generalizing personalized models to unseen users in highly diverse, sparse data environments.

**6. Personalization of Strong Proprietary Models with PLUS Summaries (Figure 4, Table 9, Table 10, Appendix E, F):**
- **LLM-as-Judges:** PLUS-generated summaries improved the accuracy of GPT-4o's preference predictions by 19.5% and GPT-4.1's by 2.9% across various conversation types (value-guided, controversial, new users) in an LLM-as-a-judge setup. This was particularly effective for controversial topics.
- **Personalized Response Generation:** When GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 generated responses conditioned on PLUS summaries, they achieved a significantly higher win rate (72% for GPT-4o, 69% for GPT-4.1) against default, unpersonalized responses, as evaluated by an oracle reward model.
- These results demonstrate the portability and effectiveness of PLUS-generated summaries in enabling personalization for powerful proprietary models without requiring their fine-tuning.
- Examples provided in the appendix illustrate how summaries lead to tailored responses in terms of length, reference to specific user values, and factuality.

In summary, the main findings establish PLUS as a robust and performant framework for personalized RLHF, offering significant improvements in accuracy and generalization, particularly in complex and diverse user preference scenarios. The interpretability and portability of its text-based summaries represent a distinct advantage over existing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of the Preference Learning Using Summarization (PLUS) framework carries several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of personalized AI assistants and reinforcement learning from human feedback.

**Addressing a Critical Gap in LLM Personalization:** PLUS directly tackles the fundamental limitation of current RLHF methodologies that assume homogenous user preferences. By enabling personalization through learned text-based summaries, it moves beyond a "one-size-fits-all" approach, which is becoming increasingly untenable given the global and diverse user base of LLMs. This capability is critical for creating AI assistants that are truly aligned with individual user needs and preferences.

**Improved Performance and Robustness:** The empirical results demonstrate that PLUS significantly outperforms standard BTL and several state-of-the-art personalized RLHF techniques in terms of reward model accuracy. Its robustness to shifts in conversation topics and generalization to new users, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios, suggests a more sophisticated and transferable learning mechanism compared to methods relying on latent vector embeddings or in-context learning of entire histories. This robustness is vital for real-world deployment where user interactions are dynamic and unpredictable.

**Enhanced Interpretability and Transparency:** A key strength of PLUS is its generation of human-readable text summaries of user preferences. This contrasts with the opaque numerical embeddings used by many other personalized methods. This interpretability offers several benefits:
- **User Trust and Control:** Users can understand *why* an LLM is responding in a particular way, fostering greater trust. The potential to allow users to review, edit, or remove information from their summaries could provide unprecedented levels of control over their personalized AI experience and address privacy concerns proactively.
- **Developer Insight:** Developers can gain clearer insights into the learned user characteristics, which can aid in debugging, bias detection, and further model development.

**Portability and Practicality with Proprietary Models:** The ability of PLUS-generated summaries to personalize strong proprietary models like GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 without requiring their direct fine-tuning is a substantial practical advantage. This feature simplifies deployment, reduces computational costs associated with fine-tuning large models, and expands the applicability of personalized RLHF to models whose internal architectures may not be directly accessible for modification. This facilitates a more flexible ecosystem for integrating personalization into existing LLM services.

**Flexibility in Handling User Contexts:** PLUS's capacity to infer user preferences from diverse and unstructured textual inputs, including unlabeled conversation data and user-guided instructions, broadens its applicability to real-world scenarios where explicit preference labels (chosen/rejected pairs) might be sparse or non-existent. This flexibility makes the framework more adaptable to various forms of user interaction data.

**Advancing Pluralistic Alignment and Ethical AI:** This work contributes significantly to the research agenda on pluralistic alignment, moving towards AI systems that can respectfully and effectively cater to diverse values and perspectives. By making personalization more transparent and potentially user-editable, PLUS offers a pathway for developing more ethical and accountable AI systems, where user privacy and individual preferences are explicitly considered.

**Future Research Directions:** The challenges observed with generalizing to entirely unseen users in the PRISM dataset, where simpler majority-vote models sometimes outperformed personalized ones, highlight an important area for future research. This suggests a need for larger, more diverse real-world datasets for personalized RLHF to capture the full spectrum of human heterogeneity and improve out-of-distribution generalization for personalized models. Further exploration into the interplay of PPO hyperparameters in the MARL setup for co-adaptation could also yield more stable and efficient training regimes.

Overall, PLUS represents a notable advancement in personalized RLHF, offering a technically robust, interpretable, and practically adaptable solution to a critical challenge in AI alignment. Its impact could extend from more satisfying individual user experiences to fostering more equitable and transparent AI interactions on a societal scale.
