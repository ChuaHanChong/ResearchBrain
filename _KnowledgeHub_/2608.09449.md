# Sekai2: From World Exploration to Interactive World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Sekai2 is a real-world video corpus for interactive world modeling, pairing every clip with an estimated camera trajectory and hierarchical, temporally grounded captions that separate subject motion, environment dynamics, static scene, and camera behavior. A unified data engine curates 128,892 clips (2,826 hours, 113 countries) plus 982 loop-rich panoramic sequences, yielding 649,597 annotated segments.

## Problem

- Large-scale video-text corpora offer broad visual diversity but ship no camera trajectories and no temporally localized captions, so viewpoint change cannot be separated from world change.
- Pose-annotated spatial datasets target reconstruction, view synthesis, or short-range camera control, with clips of seconds rather than the sustained minute-scale trajectories long-horizon world evolution needs.
- Controlled revisits are rare in ordinary web video, leaving little supervision for testing whether world models retain previously observed scenes across long temporal and viewpoint gaps.

## Method

- A manifest-driven data engine: ==OmniShotCut== shot-aware cutting into 120-second clips, then ==RAFT== motion, media-integrity, HUD, and OCR gates, plus a separately curated low-motion recovery branch.
- Camera supervision via a pose-only ==ViPE== deployment: ==GeoCalib== intrinsics, ==Grounding-DINO== plus ==SAM== dynamic masking, ==DROID-SLAM== optimization; panoramic trajectories refined by spherical-ray-verified loop closures inside ==pose-graph optimization==.
- Hierarchical ==Kimi-K2.6== annotation schema factorizing subject motion, environment motion, static scene, and camera description at clip and interval level, with discrete camera_path labels and camera-tagged full and short prompts.

## Results

- Visual quality matches baselines (LAION aesthetic **5.23** versus **5.45**, **5.20**, **5.18**) while motion is far richer: mean optical flow **35.08** versus **25.13**, Max-Running-LPIPS **0.64** versus **0.52**.
- Epipolar validation gives **0.12 px** median Sampson error on inherited clips versus **2.83 px** under a mismatched-pose control; cross-run rotation RPE is **0.103 degrees**, normalized ATE **0.0176**.
- A VLM judge scores **4.28** coverage and **4.01** camera correctness; structured captions beat one-shot captions **60.9%** under the position-biased ordering; turn-versus-straight yaw separation reaches AUC **0.709**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing captions into subject, environment, scene, and camera fields with delimited camera text lets one corpus serve camera-conditioned and camera-free training without re-annotation.
> - Deliberate loop-and-revisit capture is a data design choice, not a byproduct; revisits are what expose whether a model remembers a previously seen scene.
> - Agreement between independently produced poses and captions is a cheap cross-modal audit, but it measures consistency, not accuracy against metric ground truth.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation is progressing from synthesizing short visual clips to developing interactive world models. These models aim to maintain persistent scene states, produce coherent observations over extended durations, and respond to dynamic viewpoint or user controls. This progression places increased demands on training data, requiring not only local appearance and short-term motion modeling but also the ability to preserve world state during observer movement, distinguish environmental changes from viewpoint changes, and maintain spatial consistency over long trajectories.

Existing video datasets provide only partial solutions to these requirements. Large-scale video-text corpora, such as WebVid, Panda-70M, OpenVid-1M, and MiraData, offer extensive visual and semantic diversity but typically lack explicit camera trajectories and temporally aligned descriptions. Conversely, spatial datasets like RealEstate10K, DL3DV-10K, SpatialVID, and OmniWorld provide camera poses, depth, or other spatial supervision, but are largely designed for reconstruction, view synthesis, or short-range camera control, rather than sustained world evolution over minute-scale trajectories. Action-centric datasets and interactive game pipelines, while offering richer control signals, often focus on localized actions or are derived from simulated environments. Consequently, a significant gap exists for real-world, long-horizon video data that comprehensively includes both explicit camera trajectories and temporally grounded semantics.

A further challenge in world modeling involves scenarios where long trajectories revisit previously observed locations. Such revisits are crucial for training models to preserve observed scene structure and maintain long-term spatial memory, rather than solely relying on recent visual context. However, controlled revisit trajectories are uncommon in typical web video, limiting direct supervision for studying long-range revisit consistency.

Sekai made an initial contribution by offering geographically diverse first-person and aerial videos for real-world exploration, along with rich metadata and camera trajectories. Sekai2 builds upon this foundation by repositioning the data emphasis from general world exploration to the specific needs of interactive world modeling. This is achieved through a unified reprocessing and annotation pipeline for both existing Sekai videos and newly collected content, and crucially, by incorporating a dedicated subset of revisit-rich panoramic sequences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Sekai2, a real-world video dataset specifically designed to address critical data limitations for training interactive world models. The motivations stem from the evolving requirements of these models and the shortcomings of existing datasets.

A key motivation is the increasing demand for generative systems that can maintain persistent scene states, produce coherent observations over extended horizons, and respond to changes in viewpoint or user control. Training such models necessitates data that jointly provides temporal continuity, explicit camera-pose supervision, and fine-grained, temporally localized semantics. Current datasets typically offer only a subset of these properties, hindering the development of world models capable of distinguishing environment changes from viewpoint changes, preserving world state during observer motion, and maintaining spatial consistency over long trajectories.

Specifically, the authors identify several motivations:
- **Lack of Joint Supervision**: Existing large-scale video-text datasets provide broad visual and semantic diversity but generally omit camera trajectories and temporally aligned descriptions. Spatial datasets, while offering camera poses, typically focus on short-range interactions or reconstruction rather than minute-scale world evolution. Sekai2 aims to bridge this gap by providing all three elements concurrently in long-form real-world video.
- **Need for Long Temporal Continuity**: Interactive world models benefit from training data that captures sustained evolution rather than isolated events. Sekai2 is designed to feature long video clips, allowing models to learn extended temporal dependencies and state preservation.
- **Importance of Camera-Pose Supervision**: Accurate camera trajectories are essential for enabling camera-controllable synthesis and for disentangling scene dynamics from observer motion. The dataset includes explicit camera trajectories for every clip.
- **Requirement for Temporally Grounded Semantics**: Fine-grained annotations that localize changes in scene content, environmental dynamics, and viewpoint behavior on a shared timeline are crucial for detailed model supervision. Sekai2 introduces a hierarchical annotation schema to provide such detailed descriptions.
- **Addressing Revisit Consistency**: A significant challenge in long-horizon modeling is maintaining spatial memory when an observer revisits previously seen locations from different viewpoints or after a temporal interval. Ordinary web video rarely contains controlled revisit trajectories. Sekai2 directly addresses this by introducing a dedicated subset of panoramic sequences explicitly captured along non-linear routes with loops and revisits, providing essential supervision for learning persistent scene representations and long-term spatial memory.
- **Unified Data Framework**: The project is motivated by the need to re-curate and process diverse video sources (existing Sekai, new web videos, and self-captured panoramas) under a consistent quality control, trajectory processing, and hierarchical annotation pipeline. This ensures that heterogeneous videos are provided with a uniform supervision scheme that aligns scene content, local dynamics, and viewpoint changes over time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction of Sekai2 adheres to four design principles: temporal continuity, world coverage, camera supervision, and temporally grounded semantics. The methodology employs a multi-component data engine that integrates source acquisition, long-clip curation and filtering, camera-pose estimation, and hierarchical semantic annotation, complemented by a robust quality assurance framework.

#### 3.1. Data Acquisition

The source pool for Sekai2 is assembled from three distinct channels:
- **Inherited Sekai data**: Over 5,000 hours of first-person and aerial videos from the original Sekai collection are reprocessed. These videos undergo new screening and annotation criteria, though their existing per-frame intrinsics are reused.
- **Newly collected YouTube videos**: An initial crawl of 5,199 YouTube source videos (5,089 after deduplication against Sekai) provides 173,303 candidate clips, totaling approximately 3,957 hours. These cover various motion types, including walking tours, driving, rail travel, drone footage, and FPV.
- **Revisit-rich panoramic captures**: Approximately 160 hours of 1,283 panoramic videos are self-captured using Insta360 cameras across diverse real-world environments (urban streets, residential, campuses, parks, indoor spaces). These sequences are deliberately captured along non-linear routes with loops and repeated visits to previously observed locations to provide long-range trajectory structures for studying revisit consistency.

#### 3.2. Long-Clip Curation and Filtering

To ensure data quality and relevance for world modeling, a multi-stage filtering pipeline is applied:
- **Temporal Segmentation**: OmniShotCut detects hard cuts and gradual transitions, removing a 6-second buffer around internal boundaries to avoid discontinuous state changes. Continuous perspective shots are partitioned into clips of at most 120 seconds, discarding residual segments shorter than 10 seconds. Panoramic videos are retained as complete sequences to preserve their long-range loop and revisit structures.
- **Filtering Pipeline**: A manifest-driven pipeline applies various quality gates:
 - **Optical Flow**: Clips with too much static content (r_static ≥ 0.5) are rejected by a strict motion gate.
 - **Media Integrity**: Checks for minimum resolution (720p), valid frame rates (24-65 fps), supported codecs, severe exposure failures, and black borders.
 - **HUD and OCR Detection**: Identifies and removes clips with persistent UI elements, subtitles, watermarks, or large on-screen text using optical flow, `Grounding DINO`, `SAM` and `EasyOCR`.
 - **Low-Motion Recovery**: Clips rejected only by the strict motion gate but passing all other checks are curated into a separate low-motion branch, contributing 6% of the final footage duration, to ensure a balanced dataset.
 - **Independent Gates**: Camera-pose quality and semantic-annotation validity are evaluated as independent gates.

#### 3.3. Camera-Pose Annotation

Every retained clip is paired with a camera-pose trajectory:
- **Scalable Pose Estimation**: ViPE is used to estimate camera intrinsics and per-frame extrinsics. GeoCalib initializes intrinsics, and dynamic foregrounds (detected by Grounding DINO + SAM) are masked before DROID-SLAM optimization to improve reconstruction stability. A monocular depth prior is used for regularization.
- **Outputs**: Each trajectory file includes frame indices and camera-to-world transformations, along with estimated intrinsics.
- **Panoramic Trajectory Refinement**: For long panoramic sequences prone to accumulated drift, loop-closure candidates between temporally distant keyframes are retrieved using ORB features and geometrically verified via spherical ray alignment and SVD. Verified closures are incorporated into pose-graph optimization (PGO) as camera-center constraints, refining 81.9% of panoramic trajectories.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical Semantic Annotation

Annotations are generated by the Kimi-K2.6 vision-language model, providing fine-grained supervision at two levels:
- **Clip-Level Global Descriptions**: Structured attributes (e.g., geographic context, scene type, environmental conditions, camera properties) are provided. Free-form descriptions are factorized into `subject_motion`, `environment_motion`, `static_scene`, and `camera_description` to reduce semantic entanglement. Two training-oriented prompts are derived: `full_prompt` (integrates all information, with camera details inside `<camera>...</camera>` tags) and `short_prompt` (compact, scene-centric, without camera-specific details).
- **Interval-Level Local Descriptions**: Temporal boundaries are determined by salient changes in scene content, illumination, surface type, or dominant camera behavior. Each interval includes the same structured fields as the global annotation, plus a discrete `camera_path` label (e.g., `straight`, `turn_left`, `stationary`, `mixed`). This results in 649,597 temporally grounded segments.
- **Prompt Design**: The annotation prompt explicitly instructs the VLM on factorization, camera-tagging, and length constraints for derived prompts (Appendix B.5, Fig. 12).
- **Quality Assurance**: Post-annotation validation checks for schema compliance, content validity, temporal coverage, prompt formatting, and duplication. A cross-modal audit compares pose-derived camera motion with `camera_path` labels to diagnose grounding consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Sekai2 encompasses its scale, diversity, visual quality, temporal dynamics, camera trajectory accuracy, semantic annotation quality, and cross-modal grounding.

#### 4.1. Dataset Scale, Composition, and Temporal Extent

- **Overall Scale**: Sekai2 contains 128,892 clips, totaling 2,826 hours of video. These clips originate from 10,428 source videos across 113 countries or regions.
- **Source Composition**: The dataset is composed of 47,699 inherited Sekai clips (795 hours), 80,211 newly collected YouTube clips (1,912 hours), and 982 self-captured panoramic sequences (119 hours).
- **Temporal Continuity**: A defining characteristic is sustained observation. Under a 120-second decomposition, 43,594 segments reach the full two-minute duration, accounting for 51.4% of all footage. Segments of at least one minute cover 92.3% of the total duration, with an average video unit duration of 78.9 seconds.

#### 4.2. Environment and Scene Diversity

- **Attribute Coverage**: The dataset includes 113 countries/regions, 18 camera motion types, 7 lighting conditions, 6 times of day, 7 weather types, and 15 scene types.
- **Scene Distribution**: Urban streets constitute 60.9% of scenes, followed by parks (11.1%), markets (7.0%), mountains (3.7%), indoor spaces (3.4%), and forests (3.1%).
- **Environmental Conditions**: 35.3% of clips are recorded in the evening or at night, 41.5% under non-clear weather, and 34.0% under artificial or mixed lighting.
- **Attribute Independence**: Analysis of normalized mutual information (NMI) between clip-level attributes shows a mean NMI of 0.13, indicating largely independent variation across most properties. Stronger dependencies are observed within viewpoint-related attributes (e.g., camera motion, perspective, video style) and illumination-related attributes (e.g., time of day, lighting).

#### 4.3. Camera-Trajectory Diversity

- **Complementary Profiles**: The three data sources exhibit distinct trajectory profiles. Newly collected YouTube clips have greater median path length (123 vs. 68 ViPE units for Sekai) and cumulative rotation (270° vs. 184°). Panoramic sequences show the broadest spatial coverage (median path length 452 units, bird’s-eye-view extent 174 units) with lower cumulative rotation (92°), consistent with omnidirectional capture. Median camera speed is similar across sources (1.10–1.22 units/s).

#### 4.4. Caption Density and Lexical Structure

- **Annotation Volume**: The caption corpus contains approximately 135 million words across all annotation fields, with 649,597 temporally grounded segments (average 5.04 segments per clip, median 15-second duration).
- **Field-Wise Lengths**: Clip-level descriptions average 19 words for `subject_motion`, 26 for `environment_motion`, 32 for `static_scene`, 27 for `camera_description`, 18 for `short_prompt`, and 97 for `full_prompt`.
- **Semantic Division**: Word cloud analysis demonstrates a clear semantic division, with each factorized description emphasizing complementary aspects (e.g., agents/actions for `subject_motion`, persistent geometry for `static_scene`).
- **Vocabulary Growth and Uniqueness**: The vocabulary grows continuously (Heaps’ law exponent β ≈ 0.43), and annotations show high uniqueness (99.86% for segment-level short prompts), suggesting diverse content rather than template reuse.

#### 4.5. Visual Quality and Temporal Dynamics

- **Aesthetic Quality**: Sekai2 maintains comparable aesthetic quality to other representative video corpora (e.g., MiraData, RealEstate10K, SpatialVID), achieving an average LAION aesthetic score of 5.23, which is close to the other datasets.
- **Temporal Variation**: Sekai2 exhibits substantially stronger local temporal variation, with a mean optical-flow magnitude of 35.08 (compared to 19.40–25.13 for others) and a Max-Running-LPIPS score of 0.64 (compared to 0.46–0.52).

#### 4.6. Camera-Trajectory Quality

- **Validity and Smoothness**: Across all 128,892 released clips, the median valid-pose ratio is 1.0, with near-zero translation-jump rates and low rotational jerk, indicating numerical completeness and local smoothness.
- **Epipolar Consistency**: A ground-truth-free epipolar test shows low median Sampson errors (0.12–0.43 px). A mismatched-pose negative control results in significantly higher errors (median 2.83 px), confirming the geometric consistency of the released poses with the video frames.
- **Cross-Run Geometric Consistency**: Comparison with DROID-SLAM reconstructions under matched intrinsics demonstrates strong consistency: median rotation RPE of 0.103° (1s) and 0.414° (5s), median translation-direction errors of 1.88° (1s) and 1.48° (5s), and normalized ATE of 0.0176. Reconstruction success rate reaches 99.5%. Consistency is strongest for straight trajectories and decreases for curved and winding motions. Static trajectories present challenges for translation-direction metrics due to poor conditioning.

#### 4.7. Caption Quality

- **Judge-Based Assessment**: A VLM judge rated the annotations highly for coverage (4.28), scene correctness (4.31), motion correctness (4.14), and camera correctness (4.01) on a 1–5 scale.
- **Informative Content**: In pairwise comparisons against conventional one-shot captions (generated by the same VLM), Sekai2’s structured annotations were preferred in 60.9% and judged more informative in 68.4% of conservative-order trials.
- **Complementary Semantics**: Analysis of vocabulary overlap (Jaccard index for top-150 words) among the six clip-level annotation components indicates substantial differentiation: a mean Jaccard overlap of 0.24, with 51% of words unique to a single view.

#### 4.8. Cross-Modal Grounding

- **Pose-Caption Agreement**: A consistent correspondence is observed between pose-derived camera yaw changes and the `camera_path` labels. Measured rotation increases monotonically with the semantic labels: `stationary` (median 1.3°), `straight` (8.5°), `mixed` (20.6°), and `turn` (27.9°). The labels distinguish turning from non-turning segments with an AUC of 0.709. Within-clip comparisons further confirm this ordering, with `turn` segments showing stronger rotation than `straight` in 71.8% of clips.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Sekai2 represents an advancement in real-world video data for generative AI by jointly providing properties that are currently scarce in existing datasets. Its significance and potential impact span several areas within video generation and interactive world modeling.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Data Gap

The dataset directly addresses the identified gap in training data for interactive world models by offering long-horizon real-world videos that simultaneously provide:
- **Long-form temporal continuity**: Capturing sustained evolution and interaction.
- **Explicit camera trajectories**: Essential for camera-controllable synthesis and distinguishing scene changes from viewpoint changes.
- **Fine-grained, temporally grounded hierarchical semantic annotations**: Offering detailed, factorized descriptions of subject motion, environmental dynamics, static scene content, and camera behavior, aligned with specific temporal intervals.
- **Revisit structures**: The dedicated panoramic subset with loops and revisits provides direct supervision for learning persistent scene representations and long-term spatial memory, which are critical for overcoming "scene forgetting" in world models.

#### 5.2. Enabling Advanced Model Training and Development

Sekai2 is designed to facilitate the training of next-generation interactive world models. Its comprehensive nature supports:
- **Long-Horizon Video Generation**: Models can be trained to produce coherent and consistent video sequences over extended durations, maintaining world state across complex trajectories.
- **Camera-Controllable Synthesis**: The explicit camera trajectories allow for the development of generative models that can synthesize video output conditioned on specified camera movements and viewpoints.
- **Interactive World Model Pre-training**: The combination of real-world visual data, precise camera control signals, and detailed semantic understanding provides a rich environment for pre-training agents and systems that operate in persistent, dynamic virtual worlds.

#### 5.3. Facilitating Research on Core Challenges

The dataset provides a resource for investigating specific challenges in world modeling:
- **Viewpoint Evolution**: Researchers can better understand and model how scenes evolve visually as an observer moves through an environment.
- **Long-Range Spatial Consistency**: The data supports the development of models capable of maintaining coherent spatial understanding over extended, non-linear paths.
- **Revisit Consistency**: The inclusion of deliberate revisits allows for focused research into how world models remember and reconcile observations of the same location from different perspectives or at different times, a known area of difficulty for current models.

#### 5.4. Reproducible and Auditable Data Foundation

The detailed methodology for data acquisition, rigorous filtering, automated pose estimation with refinement, and hierarchical VLM-based annotation, coupled with extensive quality assurance and a reproducible data engine, contributes to a reliable and auditable data resource for the research community. This transparency supports further research and benchmarking in areas like camera calibration, trajectory uncertainty, and VLM annotation refinement.

In summary, Sekai2 is positioned as a foundational real-world resource that integrates previously disparate forms of supervision into a unified framework. It offers specific properties to drive advancements in long-horizon video generation, camera-controllable synthesis, and the development of interactive world models with enhanced spatial memory and revisit consistency.
