# FASTer: Toward Efficient Autoregressive Vision Language Action Modeling via Neural Action Tokenization

> [!summary] Summary
> FASTER is a unified framework featuring a learnable action tokenizer (FASTerVQ) and an efficient autoregressive policy (FASTerVLA) for robotic manipulation. It achieves state-of-the-art performance with a 97.9% success rate on Libero and significantly reduces inference latency to 112ms, enabling efficient real-time control for complex tasks.

## Problem

- Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation struggle with efficient and accurate discretization of continuous robot actions into discrete tokens.
- Existing action tokenization methods face a trade-off between high compression efficiency and robust reconstruction quality of fine-grained motion details.
- Current approaches often fail to model the coupled two-dimensional structure (action and temporal dimensions) of robot action sequences, limiting generalization and leading to high inference latency.

## Method

- **FASTer** introduces **FASTerVQ**, a learnable ==action tokenizer== that uses an ==Action Patchifier== for 2D partitioning and a ==Transformer-based Residual Vector Quantization (TAAE)== architecture to encode actions into compact, high-fidelity tokens.
- It develops **FASTerVLA**, an ==autoregressive== policy that integrates with VLM backbones and incorporates a lightweight ==action expert== for efficient decoding of discrete tokens.
- A ==Block-wise Autoregression (BAR)== decoding strategy is employed, predicting entire blocks of action tokens in parallel within a single forward pass, significantly reducing inference passes and latency.

## Results

- The FASTER framework achieved a new state-of-the-art success rate of **97.9%** on the Libero benchmark, outperforming prior diffusion-based and autoregressive methods across multiple simulated and real-world tasks.
- It demonstrated superior out-of-distribution generalization, exhibiting the lowest relative performance drop (**29%**) on VLABench and achieving an **87.9%** success rate on real-world Simpler-Bridge tasks.
- Block-wise Autoregression (BAR) reduced FASTerVLA's inference time to **112ms** on LIBERO, a substantial improvement over baselines, and achieved competitive runtimes (**~230ms**) for high-dimensional whole-body control, enabling practical real-time operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learnable action tokenizer leveraging residual vector quantization and 2D action patchification can effectively balance high compression efficiency with robust reconstruction fidelity for continuous robot actions.
> - Decomposing the action prediction into block-wise autoregression dramatically improves inference efficiency for VLA models without sacrificing accuracy, making real-time control feasible.
> - A superior neural action tokenizer can generalize across diverse robot embodiments and action types, providing a transferable action prior that enhances the performance and adaptability of various VLM backbones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has recently seen a paradigm shift with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to create generalist robot policies capable of understanding and executing complex tasks based on multimodal inputs. These models typically fall into two main categories: diffusion-based and autoregressive.

Diffusion-based VLA models, while demonstrating superior precision in certain manipulation tasks, often struggle to effectively leverage the rich contextual information provided by visual and linguistic cues. In contrast, autoregressive VLA models, despite historically being slower, have shown increasing promise in instruction-following, scene generalization, and transferring common-sense knowledge. Their architectural similarity to highly successful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suggests significant potential for future advancements, particularly in integrating high-level reasoning with low-level control.

A pivotal challenge for autoregressive VLA models lies in the efficient and accurate discretization of continuous robot action sequences into "action tokens." The quality of this tokenization directly influences overall model performance, a principle well-established in other sequence modeling domains like Large Language Models (LLMs) and Speech-LLMs. However, current tokenization methods for robotics face several limitations:
1. **Compression Efficiency:** They often generate a large number of tokens, leading to slow and inefficient inference.
2. **Reconstruction Quality:** Achieving high compression frequently comes at the cost of fidelity, losing crucial fine-grained motion details.
3. **2D Structural Modeling:** Action sequences possess both an action dimension (distinct physical semantics) and a temporal dimension (redundancy). Jointly modeling these coupled structures is crucial for an optimal efficiency-accuracy trade-off.
4. **Flexibility:** Many existing tokenizers lack generalization across different robot backbones, tasks, and embodiments.

The authors highlight that current tokenization methods fail to comprehensively satisfy these requirements. For instance, some methods under-compress, others over-fragment, and many lack robust reconstruction fidelity, leading to action vocabularies difficult for autoregressive VLAs to model effectively. This directly impacts inference efficiency, with some approaches requiring hundreds of tokens for short motions, leading to significant delays.

FASTER directly addresses these fundamental challenges. By introducing a novel, learnable action tokenizer (FASTerVQ) and an efficient autoregressive policy built upon it (FASTerVLA), this work aims to bridge the performance and efficiency gap between autoregressive and diffusion-based VLAs. It leverages insights from VQ tokenization across other modalities (e.g., audio codecs, image/video generation) and block-wise generation techniques to achieve a more compact, structured, and functionally faithful action representation, ultimately making autoregressive VLA models faster, more stable, and more generalizable for real-world robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a unified framework for efficient and generalizable robot learning using autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The authors are motivated by the inherent strengths of autoregressive VLAs, such as their strong language grounding and generalization capabilities, but recognize their critical limitations, particularly concerning action tokenization and inference efficiency.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcome the Action Tokenization Bottleneck:** The primary motivation is to address the "pivotal challenge" of action tokenization in autoregressive VLA models. This involves discretizing continuous robot action sequences into discrete tokens in a manner that balances information retention with computational efficiency.
2. **Achieve High Compression Efficiency and Robust Reconstruction:** The authors aim to develop an action tokenizer that generates a minimal number of tokens for long action sequences (high compression) while simultaneously guaranteeing high reconstruction fidelity, even for fine-grained motion details. This directly tackles the observed trade-off between these two crucial aspects in existing methods.
3. **Model 2D Structural Dependencies:** A specific objective is to design a tokenizer that can effectively account for the two-dimensional nature of robot actions – both the physical action dimensions (e.g., end-effector position, gripper state) and the temporal dimension, which often exhibits significant redundancy. Jointly modeling these coupled structures is essential for achieving an optimal balance between efficiency and accuracy.
4. **Ensure Flexibility and Generalizability:** The tokenizer must be universally applicable across different VLM backbones, robotic tasks, and embodiments without requiring extensive re-engineering or retraining. This is a measure of its true generalization ability, moving towards a "generalist" robot policy.
5. **Improve Inference Efficiency of Autoregressive VLAs:** Current autoregressive VLAs are significantly slower than their diffusion-based counterparts. A core motivation is to drastically reduce inference latency, bringing the control frequency into a practically usable regime for real-time robot operation.
6. **Enhance Task Performance and Multimodal Alignment:** Beyond efficiency, the goal is to improve overall task performance, achieve stronger multimodal alignment between language instructions and continuous control, and demonstrate consistent state-of-the-art results across diverse tasks and robot platforms.
7. **Bridge the Performance Gap with Diffusion Models:** By addressing the efficiency and fidelity issues, the authors aim for autoregressive VLA models to increasingly rival and even surpass diffusion-based approaches in task success, while retaining their inherent advantages in instruction following and generalization.

In essence, the authors seek to build a robust, efficient, and generalizable foundation for autoregressive VLA models, believing that a superior action tokenization scheme is the key to unlocking their full potential in generalist robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FASTER is presented as a unified framework consisting of two complementary components: FASTerVQ (a learnable action tokenizer) and FASTerVLA (an autoregressive policy built upon it).

**Problem Formulation:**
The framework aims to learn a VLA policy that maps multimodal observations (RGB image $I_t$, proprioceptive state $s_t$, language instruction $l$) to an action sequence $A_{t:t+H}$. Instead of directly predicting continuous actions, FASTER discretizes each action chunk into a sequence of tokens $(z_1..., z_N)$ using FASTerVQ. The FASTerVLA policy then autoregressively generates these discrete codes, which are subsequently decoded back into continuous actions. The core challenge addressed is the computational bottleneck caused by long token sequences and variable token lengths in whole-body control.

#### 3.1. FASTerVQ: The Action Tokenizer

FASTerVQ comprises an **Action Patchifier** and a **Transformer-based Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) Tokenizer (TAAE)**.

- **Action Patchifier:** This initial step processes the raw action sequence $A_{t:t+H} = (a_t..., a_{t+H-1})$. It performs a two-dimensional partitioning:
 - **Temporal Dimension:** Uniformly divides the sequence into $m$ groups of length $h$.
 - **Action Dimension:** Non-uniformly partitions the action vector $a_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$ into $n$ groups based on physical characteristics (e.g., end-effector position, orientation, gripper state).
 This process yields a structured tensor, which is then flattened into patches $a^P_{t:t+H}$. This patchification mitigates data distribution imbalance across action dimensions and handles temporal redundancy, effectively increasing information density per token.

- **Residual VQ Action Tokenizer (TAAE):** Inspired by RVQ and transformer codecs, TAAE is a hybrid encoder-decoder architecture.
 - **Encoder ($\phi_{enc}$):** Downsamples the action patch $a^P_{t:t+H}$ into a latent embedding $z \in \mathbb{R}^{C_h \times C_a}$. It combines the adaptive global receptive field of a transformer with the local modeling and downsampling capacity of convolutions.
 - **Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ):** The latent embedding $z$ is then quantized using $N_c$ quantization levels. This process decomposes $z$ into residuals: $r_1 = z$, and $r_{i+1} = r_i - Q_i(r_i)$, where $Q_i$ is the $i$-th quantizer. Each $Q_i$ selects the nearest codebook entry from its codebook. The quantized latent embedding is $z_q = \sum_{i=1}^{N_c} Q_i(r_i)$. The selected codebook indices form a discrete code tensor $C$, which serves as the action tokens. This RVQ structure imparts a coarse-to-fine representation, capturing low-frequency components early and refining high-frequency residuals later.
 - **Decoder ($\phi_{dec}$):** Reconstructs the action patch $\hat{a}^P_{t:t+H}$ from $z_q$ using a straight-through estimator (STE) for gradient flow.

- **Training Objective:** FASTerVQ is trained to minimize a combined loss:
 - **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rec}$):** An $\ell_1$ loss is applied to both the temporal action signal $a^P_{t:t+H}$ and its Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT). The $\ell_1$ loss is chosen for its robustness to noise in real-world robot data, and the DCT component helps capture overall trends.
 - **Commitment Loss ($L_{commit}$):** Regularizes the encoder's output to stay close to the chosen codebook vectors.
 RVQ codebooks are updated using an exponential moving average (EMA) with reinitialization for "dead codes."

#### 3.2. FASTerVLA: The Autoregressive Policy

FASTerVLA builds upon the tokenized action space created by FASTerVQ.

- **Architecture:** It largely follows the standard VLM structure:
 - **Vision Tower:** Encodes visual inputs.
 - **Projection Layer:** Maps visual features to the model dimension.
 - **Transformer-based Language Backbone:** Processes all multimodal inputs.
 - **Action Embeddings & Proprioception Encoding:** The VLM's embedding table is extended to include slots for action codes. Proprioceptive states are discretized and tokenized as text.
 - **Positional Encoding & Spacing Augmentation:** Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) are used. To prevent position overfitting, "spacing augmentation" perturbs the relative offset between action tokens during training, making the model more robust. A fixed spacing is used during inference.
 - **Lightweight Action Expert:** A parameter-efficient expert module, architecturally aligned with the VLM backbone, is introduced. This expert decodes action tokens autoregressively from the multimodal context encoded by the main backbone, mitigating interference with pretrained VLM weights.

- **Block-wise Autoregression (BAR):** This is a key innovation for inference efficiency.
 - **Motivation:** Standard autoregressive decoding predicts one token at a time, leading to high latency due to many forward passes. Many action codes exhibit weak coupling across dimensions.
 - **Approach:** Instead of predicting one token, BAR partitions the sequence of action codes $C$ into $J$ contiguous blocks of size $B$. It then predicts an entire block of tokens in a single forward pass.
 - **Loss:** The BAR loss is formulated to predict tokens within a block, conditioned on preceding blocks.
 - **Causal Mask:** The standard causal mask is replaced with a block-wise causal mask that allows intra-block attention, enabling parallel prediction within a block while maintaining autoregressive consistency across blocks.
 - **Control Tokens:** Special tokens, $\langle$BoBlk$\rangle$ and $\langle$EoBlk$\rangle$, manage the switching between block-wise and standard autoregressive prediction, allowing seamless integration.
 - **Decoding Order:** FASTerVLA decodes hierarchically across codebook and temporal horizon dimensions. For each codebook, it decodes tokens along the horizon before moving to the next codebook. This coarse-to-fine order aligns with RVQ's residual nature, improving efficiency and stability.
 - **Efficiency:** BAR reduces the number of forward passes from $N$ (total tokens) to roughly $N/B$ (total blocks), significantly cutting inference latency.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating a sophisticated learnable tokenizer with an efficient block-wise autoregressive policy, aims to deliver high-performance and generalizable robot control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors are extensive, covering both simulated and real-world benchmarks across diverse tasks, embodiments, and VLM backbones.

#### 4.1. FASTerVQ Performance: Compression, Fidelity, and Generalization

- **Superior Compression and Reconstruction Fidelity:** FASTerVQ achieves a significantly better balance between compression ratio and reconstruction accuracy compared to baseline VQ methods (MiniVLA, VQ-VLA, Fast). While existing VQ methods compress well, they often suffer from poor reconstruction accuracy, leading to "biased supervision" for the downstream VLA. FASTerVQ minimizes tokens while preserving fine-grained motion details, maintaining significantly higher compression efficiency, especially for high-frequency action sequences (Figure 5, Figure 6).
- **Valid Reconstruction Rate (VRR):** The introduction of VRR, which measures functional fidelity within a tolerance threshold, highlights FASTerVQ's superiority. It consistently delivers state-of-the-art reconstruction across various error tolerances ($\sigma$), indicating that its reconstructions are physically meaningful and actionable.
- **Data Scaling Behavior:** FASTerVQ demonstrates a clear data-scaling trend. Training larger variants (FASTer(L), FASTer(XL)) with more data consistently improves reconstruction quality, with FASTerVQ-XL achieving near-lossless reconstruction at a physically meaningful tolerance level ($\sigma = 10^{-3}$), even with data budgets comparable to or smaller than Fast+ (Figure 5).
- **Strong Cross-Embodiment and Cross-Action-Type Generalization:** Despite being trained solely on single-arm delta-EEF trajectories, FASTerVQ maintains strong reconstruction performance on unseen tasks, embodiments (e.g., Widow, XArm), and action representations (e.g., Droid joint-velocity, Galaxea Open absolute joint, Agilex delta joint positions) (Figure 8, Figure 13). This suggests that FASTerVQ learns a transferable action prior, capturing common structures across diverse robot platforms.
- **Effective Codebook Utilization:** Unlike some baselines (e.g., Fast) that under-utilize their codebooks, FASTerVQ (with 4096 entries) achieves 100% codebook utilization and higher normalized entropy, indicating more balanced and information-rich token usage. This diverse and evenly activated codebook is crucial for expressiveness, better generalization, and higher task success rates (Table 8, Table 10).

#### 4.2. FASTerVLA Performance: Task Success, Efficiency, and Adaptability

- **State-of-the-Art In-Distribution Performance:** FASTerVLA sets a new state-of-the-art with a 97.9% success rate on the Libero benchmark. It consistently outperforms prior diffusion-based and autoregressive baselines across various simulated (LIBERO, VLABench, GalaxeaManisim) and real-world (xArm, R1Lite single-arm and whole-body control) settings (Table 1, Figure 4). This finding highlights the potential for well-designed autoregressive VLAs to match or exceed diffusion-based approaches.
- **Superior Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** In OOD settings, FASTerVLA achieves the highest overall success rate on VLABench, demonstrating the lowest relative performance drop (29%) compared to its in-distribution baseline (Figure 9). On real-world platforms like Simpler-Bridge, it reaches an 87.9% success rate, significantly outperforming the next best model by 12.9% (Figure 10). This confirms its robustness and generalization capabilities across different tasks and environments.
- **Cross-Backbone Adaptability:** FASTer framework shows strong adaptability across different VLM backbones (Palligemma, Qwen2.5, InternVL3.5). Notably, it boosted InternVL3.5-2B's success rate by 17.3% to 96.65% on Libero, making it the strongest performer overall, demonstrating that FASTerVQ's concise and data-driven representation better aligns with VLM instruction distributions (Figure 7, Table 9). The neural VQ tokenizer is identified as the primary driver of these gains.
- **Significant Inference Efficiency Improvements:** While observation encoding remains the dominant bottleneck (88–127ms), FASTer's action tokenizer is lightweight (2.7–7ms). The **Block-wise Autoregression (BAR)** is crucial for speed. On LIBERO, FASTerVLA achieves 112ms inference time, substantially faster than $\pi_0$ (176ms) and $\pi_0$-FAST (197–556ms). For high-dimensional whole-body control (R1Lite-WBC), FASTerVLA converges to competitive runtimes (~230ms), whereas $\pi_0$-FAST incurs a significantly higher cost (1,100–3,000ms) due to the large number of action tokens (Table 2, Table 5). This brings autoregressive VLAs into a practically usable real-time regime.
- **Ablation Studies Confirm Design Choices:**
 - **FASTerVQ Ablations:** The Transformer Action AutoEncoder (TAAE) architecture outperforms CNN and Transformer variants. A codebook size of 4096 is optimal, preventing codebook collapse, and 2-3 residual layers provide the most stable gains (Table 6).
 - **FASTerVLA Ablations:** The lightweight action expert (AE) consistently enhances success rates, especially with pretraining. Block-wise Autoregression (BAR) substantially improves both accuracy and efficiency, reducing latency by over 2x. The combination of BAR and AE yields the most harmonious balance of precision and speed (Table 7).

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that FASTER, through its innovative action tokenizer and efficient autoregressive policy, sets new benchmarks in both performance and efficiency for VLA models, making autoregressive robot control a more viable and powerful approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FASTER framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) modeling, with substantial potential impact on the development of generalist robot policies.

The core significance of this work lies in its effective resolution of a long-standing trade-off in autoregressive VLA models: the tension between action reconstruction fidelity and inference efficiency. By introducing FASTerVQ, a learnable, codec-inspired Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) module, the authors have developed an action tokenizer that simultaneously achieves high compression, robust reconstruction quality, and strong generalization across diverse robotic embodiments and action spaces. This addresses a critical bottleneck that has limited the practical applicability and performance of autoregressive approaches.

The integration of FASTerVQ with FASTerVLA, an efficient autoregressive policy featuring block-wise decoding and a lightweight action expert, leads to several key impacts:

1. **Enabling Practically Usable Autoregressive VLAs:** The dramatic reduction in inference latency achieved by FASTerVLA, especially through Block-wise Autoregression (BAR), is transformative. Bringing the control frequency into a real-time, practically usable regime for complex tasks, including whole-body control, makes autoregressive VLA models a much more viable option for real-world robotics. This efficiency gain allows for faster decision-making and more responsive robot behavior.
2. **Setting New Performance Benchmarks:** FASTER consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance across a wide array of simulated and real-world manipulation tasks. This demonstrates that, when properly designed, autoregressive VLA models can not only rival but even surpass diffusion-based counterparts, while retaining their inherent advantages in language grounding and common-sense reasoning.
3. **Advancing Generalist Robot Learning:** The robust cross-task, cross-embodiment, and cross-backbone generalization abilities of FASTER are crucial steps towards the goal of truly generalist robot policies. The learnable action prior captured by FASTerVQ allows the framework to adapt to unseen scenarios and robot configurations, reducing the need for extensive retraining.
4. **Modular and Transferable Components:** The design philosophy, particularly with a pretrainable VQ tokenizer that can be reused in downstream tasks without retraining, highlights a modular and transferable approach. This can accelerate research and development in robot learning by providing a plug-and-play component for action discretization.
5. **Strengthening VLM-Robot Integration:** By providing a more efficient and faithful way to translate high-level linguistic instructions from VLMs into low-level continuous robot actions, FASTER significantly strengthens the bridge between advanced language understanding and physical robot control. This is vital for developing robots that can understand and execute complex, human-like instructions.
6. **Guiding Future Research:** This work provides compelling evidence that autoregressive modeling, when coupled with intelligent tokenization and efficient decoding strategies, can be a fast, transferable, and scalable paradigm for multimodal generation in robotics. It opens new avenues for exploring the full potential of large multimodal models in embodied AI.

In conclusion, FASTER not only pushes the boundaries of performance and efficiency in robot manipulation but also re-establishes autoregressive VLA models as a leading approach for building generalist and intelligent robots capable of operating effectively in complex, unstructured environments.
