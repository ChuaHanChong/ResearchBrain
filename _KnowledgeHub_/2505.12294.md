# PartDexTOG: Generating Dexterous Task-Oriented Grasping via Language-driven Part Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Defense Technology developed PartDexTOG, a framework that generates dexterous, task-oriented grasps for robots by integrating language-driven object part analysis with a conditional diffusion model. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on the OakInk-shape dataset, demonstrating superior physical plausibility, stability, and semantic correctness, and robust zero-shot generalization to novel objects and tasks.

## Problem

- Limited progress in generating dexterous task-oriented grasps (TOG) for robots, particularly due to the high degrees of freedom of dexterous hands and insufficient diverse datasets.
- Traditional TOG methods lack robust generalization to novel objects and unseen tasks, often requiring extensive task-specific datasets or pre-defined knowledge graphs.
- Difficulty in acquiring precise part annotations for open-vocabulary objects to guide optimal grasp selection for diverse manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **PartDexTOG** utilizes ==Large Language Models (LLMs)== to perform ==language-driven, multi-scale part analysis==, generating both category-level and part-level grasp descriptions for semantic understanding.
- Employs a ==conditional diffusion model== that synthesizes dexterous grasps for each identified object part, conditioned on aggregated language and geometric features.
- Introduces a ==geometric consistency measure== to select the most plausible grasp-part pairing, thereby avoiding the need for direct supervision of optimal part annotations.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the OakInk-shape dataset, outperforming previous baselines by improving Penetration Volume by **3.58%** and Grasp Displace by **2.87%**.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization capabilities, with superior performance in physical plausibility and stability on **5** novel tasks and **47** objects from **5** unseen categories.
- Ranked highest in a user study across all perception metrics, including semantic consistency (**4.68**) and physical plausibility (**4.50**), and recorded the lowest P-FID (**14.24**) and highest LLM Score (**70.5**) for semantic correctness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively generate multi-scale, language-driven functional descriptions of object parts, providing crucial semantic grounding for dexterous grasping.
> - Aggregating category-level general grasping knowledge with specific part-level geometric and functional descriptions significantly enhances grasp generation robustness and task compliance.
> - A geometric consistency measure can robustly select the most plausible task-oriented grasp and corresponding object part, even in the absence of explicit part annotation supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG). TOG involves selecting an optimal grasp pose on an object that facilitates subsequent manipulation tasks. Existing research in TOG has explored various approaches, including building knowledge graphs, learning from human action videos, and leveraging pre-trained foundation models. While considerable progress has been made, particularly with parallel grippers, the application of TOG to dexterous hands remains an area with ongoing challenges.

Dexterous hands offer enhanced manipulation precision and versatility compared to parallel grippers, enabling more complex tasks. However, directly extending methods designed for parallel grippers to dexterous hands is problematic due to the higher degrees of freedom (DoF) involved, which complicates network design for grasp pose estimation on novel geometries. Furthermore, the broader range of manipulation types achievable with dexterous hands necessitates more comprehensive datasets than currently available for parallel grasping.

The paper integrates two prominent trends in robotics: the increasing use of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the application of part analysis in object interaction. LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in open-world understanding and reasoning, facilitating zero-shot generalization in robotics tasks by interpreting instructions and generating actions based on high-level semantic knowledge. Simultaneously, object part analysis is recognized as fundamental to understanding how objects can be effectively interacted with, with various methods using parts to guide grasp generation or decompose objects for novel object grasping.

This work distinguishes itself by combining these elements to address dexterous TOG, aiming to leverage LLMs' open-world knowledge to perform language-driven part analysis. This approach seeks to provide detailed, part-level functional information about objects, thereby enhancing dexterous grasping and improving generalization capabilities without requiring extensive, task-specific dexterous grasping datasets. It positions itself as a solution to generating versatile and physically plausible dexterous grasps, particularly for novel objects and tasks, by integrating semantic understanding with geometric reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for generating dexterous task-oriented grasps. This entails not only identifying a stable grip but also ensuring that the grasp facilitates a specified manipulation task, given a 3D object and a language-based task instruction.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and opportunities within the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Addressing the Gap in Dexterous Task-Oriented Grasping:** While significant advancements have been made in task-oriented grasping using parallel grippers, less attention has been paid to dexterous hands. Dexterous hands offer superior precision and versatility, enabling robots to perform complex tasks that parallel grippers cannot. However, the high degree of freedom of dexterous hands and the inadequacy of existing datasets for their versatile manipulation pose significant challenges. The research aims to bridge this gap by specifically developing a framework for dexterous TOG.
- **Leveraging Open-World Knowledge Reasoning with LLMs:** Traditional TOG methods often rely on constructing knowledge graphs or learning from extensive, task-specific datasets, which can limit their generality to unseen objects or tasks. The authors observe that Large Language Models (LLMs) possess powerful open-world knowledge reasoning capabilities, enabling zero-shot generalization. By incorporating LLMs, the research is motivated to inject this semantic knowledge into the grasp generation process, particularly for understanding object functionality at a part-level. This is intended to reduce the need for large-scale, explicit grasping datasets and improve robustness.
- **Enhancing Grasp Generation through Part Analysis:** The paper posits that part analysis can provide detailed, functionality-specific information about an object, which is crucial for effective dexterous grasping. Objects are composed of fundamental parts, and understanding these parts, their geometry, and their functional roles can significantly guide where and how to grasp an object for a particular task. The motivation is to use language-driven part analysis to ground the abstract grasping knowledge from LLMs into the specific geometry of object parts.
- **Improving Generality to Novel Objects and Tasks:** A core motivation is to create a system that can generalize effectively to objects with novel geometries and previously unseen manipulation tasks. By analyzing objects at a multi-scale part level and deriving part-dependent grasp descriptions through LLMs, the method aims to learn principles that apply across objects with similar parts, thus facilitating zero-shot generalization. This multi-scale approach is also intended to enable the generation of more versatile grasps for various task requirements.
- **Overcoming Challenges of Optimal Part Annotation:** Annotating the optimal part for grasping for open-vocabulary objects is a complex and often infeasible task for supervised learning. The research is motivated to develop a method that can identify the most plausible part and generate a corresponding grasp without requiring direct supervision for part annotations, instead relying on a measure of geometric consistency to select the best combination.

In summary, the key objective is to generate precise, versatile, and task-compliant dexterous grasps by intelligently leveraging LLMs for language-driven, multi-scale part analysis, thereby enabling robust zero-shot generalization to novel scenarios in robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PartDexTOG method generates dexterous task-oriented grasps by integrating language-driven part analysis with a conditional diffusion model. The approach proceeds in three main stages: grasp description generation, dexterous grasp generation for each part, and selection of the most plausible grasp-part combination.

#### 3.1. Grasp Description Generation

This stage utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) to derive semantic understanding relevant to the grasping task.

- **Category-level Description Generation:** Given an object's category and a manipulation task (e.g., "liftup the bottle"), an LLM (specifically GPT-4o) is prompted to generate a category-level grasp description. This description aims to capture general grasping knowledge for the object category and task, focusing on the part of the object that typically interacts with the hand, its geometric and shape characteristics, and its role in the task. This provides high-level semantic correctness. To enhance robustness, multiple descriptions are generated, and one is randomly selected.
- **Part-level Description Generation:**
 - **Multi-scale Part Label Generation:** An LLM is prompted with the object category to generate a list of potential multi-scale part labels. This prompt considers factors like part visibility, functionality (prioritizing interaction regions), and generality (common parts like "head," "body"). The method uses two segmentation scales for optimal performance in most scenarios.
 - **Part Segmentation:** To ground these abstract part labels to the specific 3D input object, PartSLIP is employed. PartSLIP takes the object's point cloud and the generated multi-scale part labels as input, producing corresponding point sets. Point sets with an insufficient number of points are filtered out, resulting in a set of valid parts. PartSLIP, in conjunction with a pre-trained GLIP model, performs zero-shot segmentation.
 - **Description Generation:** For each valid part identified, an LLM is prompted again to generate a part-level grasp description. This prompt focuses on the geometric and shape characteristics of the part and its functionality in hand-object interaction. Notably, the task type is not included in this prompt, making the part descriptions generalizable across various manipulation tasks.

#### 3.2. Dexterous Grasp Generation

This stage takes the generated descriptions and the object's geometry to synthesize dexterous grasps for each valid part. The approach avoids direct supervision for optimal part annotation.

- **Category-part Language Feature Aggregation:**
 - The category-level and each part-level grasp description are encoded into feature vectors using a pre-trained BERT model.
 - For each part, a cross-attention mechanism is applied between the category-level feature (query) and the corresponding part-level feature (key and value). This aggregates information from both general category knowledge and specific part functionality into a single `F_Cat-Part_n` feature vector for each part `n`. This aggregation is intended to capture correlations between the two levels of description, leading to more informed grasp generation.
- **Conditional Diffusion Model:** A Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) is utilized to generate dexterous grasps.
 - **Input Features:** The point clouds of the entire object and the specific part `n` are processed separately by a PointNet++ network to extract geometric features (`F_Obj-Geo` and `F_Part-Geo_n`). These geometric features are then concatenated with the aggregated language feature (`F_Cat-Part_n`). This combined feature `F_n` acts as the condition for the diffusion model.
 - **Hand Representation:** The MANO dexterous hand model is used, with its parameters `g` (61 dimensions, including pose, shape, and palm coordinates) representing the target grasp.
 - **Training:** During training, Gaussian noise is progressively added to ground-truth hand parameters `g_0` to create `g_t`. The model learns to predict this noise (`ϵ_θ`) based on `g_t`, the time step `t`, and the conditional feature `F_n`, using an L1 loss.
 - **Inference:** Starting from Gaussian noise, the denoising network (implemented as a U-Net architecture) iteratively refines the hand parameters, conditioned on `F_n`, to generate the final dexterous grasp `g_0`.

#### 3.3. Measure of Geometric Consistency

After generating a dexterous grasp for each valid part, a mechanism is required to select the most plausible grasp and its corresponding part.

- **Metric Definition:** A geometric consistency measure `E_n` is computed for each generated grasp. This measure quantifies the contact surface area between the generated hand and the corresponding part, normalized by the number of points in that part's point cloud. A distance threshold (`λ`) is used to determine contact.
- **Selection:** The grasp associated with the part that yields the highest `E_n` value (i.e., the most geometrically consistent hand-part interaction) is selected as the final dexterous task-oriented grasp.

#### 3.4. Implementations

The system is trained for 1000 epochs with an Adam optimizer. OpenAI's GPT-4o model is used for all LLM prompts. PartSLIP uses a pre-trained GLIP model for segmentation. BERT is used for language feature encoding, and PointNet++ for geometric feature extraction. The diffusion model uses 100 steps and a linear noise schedule.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PartDexTOG was evaluated on the OakInk-shape dataset, which is a large-scale dataset for dexterous Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG), encompassing diverse shapes and tasks, including objects from untrained categories in its test set. The evaluation utilized multiple metrics covering physical plausibility, stability, and semantic correctness.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

- **Physical Plausibility and Stability:**
 - **Penetration Volume:** Volume of hand-object intersection (lower is better).
 - **Penetration Depth:** Maximum penetration distance (lower is better).
 - **Grasp Displace:** Object's center of mass displacement under gravity in a physics simulation (lower is better, indicating stability).
 - **Contact Ratio:** Sample-level contact ratio between hand and object (higher is better).
- **Semantic Correctness:**
 - **P-FID (Fréchet Inception Distance):** Measures the similarity between features of predicted and ground-truth hands (lower is better).
 - **LLM Score:** Semantic score generated by GPT-4v (higher is better).
 - **Diversity:** Variance of grasp parameters (higher is better).
 - **Perceptual Scores (User Study):** Volunteers rated grasps (0-5) across four aspects:
 - Semantic Consistency (SC) between task and grasp.
 - Physical Plausibility (PP) of hand posture.
 - Interaction Stability (IS) between hand and object.
 - Correctness of Part selection (CP).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparisons to Baselines

PartDexTOG was compared against state-of-the-art methods, including GrabNet, IntGen, SceneDiffuser, and DexGYSGrasp.

- **Overall Performance:** The method achieved state-of-the-art performance on the OakInk-shape dataset.
 - It demonstrated improvements over the previous state-of-the-art by 3.58% in Penetration Volume, 2.87% in Grasp Displace, and 41.43% in P-FID.
- **Physical Plausibility & Stability:** PartDexTOG outperformed all baselines in Grasp Displace (3.14 cm mean, 3.61 cm variance) and Contact Ratio (99.33%). While IntGen achieved a lower Penetration Depth, the proposed method showed competitive Penetration Volume (4.74 cm³) and a better balance between hand-object penetration and grasp stability.
- **Semantic Correctness:** The method achieved the best performance across semantic metrics:
 - **P-FID:** Lowest at 14.24, indicating high similarity to ground-truth hand configurations.
 - **LLM Score:** Highest at 70.5, suggesting superior task compliance.
 - **Diversity:** Highest at 0.993, demonstrating a greater variety of generated grasps.
 - **Perceptual Scores:** Ranked highest in all four aspects: Semantic Consistency (4.68), Physical Plausibility (4.50), Interaction Stability (4.15), and Correctness of Part selection (4.41).

#### 4.3. Zero-shot Generality

To assess generalization, experiments were conducted on 5 novel tasks and 47 objects from 5 novel categories not seen during training.

- **Performance on Novel Scenarios:** PartDexTOG consistently outperformed all baselines in Penetration Volume and Grasp Displace for both novel tasks and novel categories. For novel tasks, it achieved 5.30 cm³ Penetration Volume and 3.09 cm Grasp Displace. For novel categories, it achieved 16.22 cm³ Penetration Volume and 2.73 cm Grasp Displace. These results suggest that the language-driven part analysis contributes significantly to its open-world generalization capabilities.

#### 4.4. Effects of Part Selection

Visualizations demonstrated that PartDexTOG could adopt different parts to generate plausible grasps for various objects under the same task type ("use"). The method showed robustness even when the part segmentation algorithm encountered failures, implying strong capabilities in part selection. Statistics on task-part correspondence further illustrate this flexibility (e.g., "Liftup" often involved the "Head" part of objects like bottles).

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments quantified the contribution of key components:

- **Category-level Grasp Description:** Removing this component resulted in a significant performance drop across most metrics (e.g., Grasp Displace increased to 4.72 cm, P-FID to 22.95), confirming its role in providing general grasping knowledge.
- **Part-level Grasp Description:** Removing part-level features also led to a decline (e.g., Grasp Displace increased to 3.64 cm, P-FID to 14.98), highlighting its importance for fine-grained, geometry-grounded knowledge.
- **Alternative Grasp Description (Simpler Language):** Using simpler language in prompts degraded performance, indicating the necessity of detailed grasp descriptions.
- **Multi-scale Parts:** Disabling multi-scale part segmentation reduced performance (e.g., P-FID increased to 15.67), validating its contribution to handling shape diversity and task versatility.
- **Cross-attention for Language Feature Aggregation:** Replacing cross-attention with simple concatenation resulted in inferior performance (e.g., Grasp Displace increased to 3.47 cm, P-FID to 14.81), suggesting the effectiveness of cross-attention in feature aggregation.
- **Measure of Geometric Consistency:** Removing this module (and using cosine similarity for selection) caused a notable performance decrease (e.g., Grasp Displace increased to 4.72 cm, P-FID to 16.72), emphasizing its role in selecting the most plausible grasp-part combination.

#### 4.6. Performance under Imperfect Segmentation

The method exhibited robustness to imperfect part segmentation. Even with manually introduced perturbations (random incorrect labels near boundaries, using a more compact GLIP model for PartSLIP), PartDexTOG maintained performance levels that surpassed baselines, demonstrating its resilience to segmentation errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PartDexTOG framework presents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in dexterous task-oriented grasping.

#### 5.1. Significance of the Work

- **Novel Integration of LLMs and Part Analysis for Dexterous Grasping:** The core significance lies in its innovative approach to generate dexterous Task-Oriented Grasps by explicitly combining language-driven part analysis with the open-world knowledge reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models. This addresses a recognized gap in dexterous grasping research.
- **Enhanced Generality and Zero-Shot Capabilities:** By leveraging LLMs for both category-level and multi-scale part-level grasp descriptions, the method significantly improves generality. It demonstrates robust performance on novel object categories and unseen manipulation tasks, a crucial capability for real-world robotic deployment where explicit training data for every scenario is impractical. This open-world understanding reduces the reliance on large-scale, task-specific grasping datasets.
- **Multi-Scale and Task-Compliant Grasp Generation:** The use of multi-scale part segmentation allows the system to consider various functional regions of an object, leading to more versatile and task-appropriate grasps. The method implicitly learns task correspondences through language, rather than relying on predefined semantic representations or extensive labeled data.
- **Geometric Consistency for Plausible Selection:** The proposed measure of geometric consistency between the generated hand and object parts offers a mechanism to select the most plausible grasp without requiring direct supervision for optimal part annotations, simplifying the learning process and enhancing applicability.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving top rankings on the challenging OakInk-shape dataset across multiple physical and semantic metrics, and outperforming existing methods by considerable margins, validates the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed architecture.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability of robots to perform complex, task-oriented dexterous grasps, guided by natural language, could lead to more intuitive and versatile human-robot interaction in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics.
- **Increased Autonomy in Robotics:** Robots equipped with this technology could operate more autonomously in unstructured and dynamic environments by adapting to novel objects and tasks without explicit reprogramming or extensive retraining. This could boost applications in household robotics, medical procedures, and exploration.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework and especially the methodology of generating detailed, multi-level grasp descriptions using LLM prompts could serve as a valuable blueprint for other robotic tasks that require grounding language instructions in physical actions and object understanding.
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** By effectively utilizing the pre-trained knowledge of LLMs, the approach mitigates the need for collecting and annotating massive, task-specific datasets, which is often a bottleneck in robotics research.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The paper acknowledges certain limitations, such as the potential for selecting a more robust part segmentation algorithm. Additionally, the current method focuses on generating a single dexterous hand grasp. Future work could explore integrating pre-trained vision models to further enhance performance, particularly for open-vocabulary object categories. Investigating multi-hand or cooperative grasping scenarios could also extend the impact of this research. While the method demonstrates robustness to imperfect segmentation, further enhancements in this area could strengthen its real-world applicability.
