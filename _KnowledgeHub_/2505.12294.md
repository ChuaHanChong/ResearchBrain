# PartDexTOG: Generating Dexterous Task-Oriented Grasping via Language-driven Part Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Defense Technology developed PartDexTOG, a framework that generates dexterous, task-oriented grasps for robots by integrating language-driven object part analysis with a conditional diffusion model. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on the OakInk-shape dataset, demonstrating superior physical plausibility, stability, and semantic correctness, and robust zero-shot generalization to novel objects and tasks.

## Problem

- Limited progress in generating dexterous task-oriented grasps (TOG) for robots, particularly due to the high degrees of freedom of dexterous hands and insufficient diverse datasets.
- Traditional TOG methods lack robust generalization to novel objects and unseen tasks, often requiring extensive task-specific datasets or pre-defined knowledge graphs.
- Difficulty in acquiring precise part annotations for open-vocabulary objects to guide optimal grasp selection for diverse manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **PartDexTOG** utilizes ==Large Language Models (LLMs)== to perform ==language-driven, multi-scale part analysis==, generating both category-level and part-level grasp descriptions for semantic understanding.
- Employs a ==conditional diffusion model== that synthesizes dexterous grasps for each identified object part, conditioned on aggregated language and geometric features.
- Introduces a ==geometric consistency measure== to select the most plausible grasp-part pairing, thereby avoiding the need for direct supervision of optimal part annotations.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the OakInk-shape dataset, outperforming previous baselines by improving Penetration Volume by **3.58%** and Grasp Displace by **2.87%**.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization capabilities, with superior performance in physical plausibility and stability on **5** novel tasks and **47** objects from **5** unseen categories.
- Ranked highest in a user study across all perception metrics, including semantic consistency (**4.68**) and physical plausibility (**4.50**), and recorded the lowest P-FID (**14.24**) and highest LLM Score (**70.5**) for semantic correctness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively generate multi-scale, language-driven functional descriptions of object parts, providing crucial semantic grounding for dexterous grasping.
> - Aggregating category-level general grasping knowledge with specific part-level geometric and functional descriptions significantly enhances grasp generation robustness and task compliance.
> - A geometric consistency measure can robustly select the most plausible task-oriented grasp and corresponding object part, even in the absence of explicit part annotation supervision.
