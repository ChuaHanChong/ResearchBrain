# Re$^3$Sim: Generating High-Fidelity Simulation Data via 3D-Photorealistic Real-to-Sim for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RE3SIM introduces a system that reconstructs real-world scenes into high-fidelity 3D photorealistic simulations, leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting for backgrounds and precise mesh recovery. This approach enables robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation tasks and facilitates efficient large-scale data generation.

## Problem

- High costs, time demands, and resource intensity associated with collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world training data for robot learning.
- The 'sim-to-real' gap, characterized by geometric and visual discrepancies between simulated and real environments, hindering effective policy generalization.
- Failure of policies trained in simulation to transfer to real robots due to unrealistic visual features and imprecise object geometries, requiring expensive real-world fine-tuning.

## Method

- Developed RE3SIM, a 'real-to-sim-to-real' pipeline that reconstructs real-world scenes into physics-based simulators, focusing on both geometry and visual appearance.
- Implemented a hybrid visual rendering strategy: foreground objects are rendered using ray tracing in the simulator, while the background utilizes photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS).
- Employed advanced 3D reconstruction techniques, including COLMAP/OpenMVS for backgrounds and ARCode for foreground objects, alongside ArUco markers and ICP for accurate sim-to-real alignment.

## Results

- Policies trained on RE3SIM data achieved an average real-world zero-shot success rate exceeding 58% across various manipulation tasks, outperforming baselines and showing comparable performance to real-world trained policies for some tasks.
- The pipeline demonstrated high efficiency, with scene reconstruction taking minimal human effort (51.5s for background, 60.5s per object) and 100 simulation episodes generated in minutes using GPUs.
- Quantitative evaluation showed superior visual fidelity (e.g., SSIM of 0.37 for 3DGS background) compared to other methods, alongside a strong Pearson correlation (0.924) between sim and real-world performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid rendering approach, combining simulator ray tracing with 3DGS, effectively bridges the visual sim-to-real gap, yielding high photorealism and supporting real-time multi-view rendering.
> - Precise 3D geometric reconstruction of both scene backgrounds and individual manipulable objects is essential for realistic physics interaction and successful zero-shot policy transfer.
> - Efficient generation of large-scale expert demonstrations within a high-fidelity simulated environment, augmented with domain randomization, enables policies that generalize robustly to novel and cluttered real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent robotic systems capable of performing complex manipulation tasks in real-world environments is a central challenge in robotics. A critical bottleneck in this field is the acquisition of large-scale, high-quality training data. Traditional methods of collecting real-world data are notoriously expensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive, often requiring skilled operators and specialized hardware. While recent advancements in teleoperation systems and inter-institutional data collection initiatives have made progress, the fundamental issues of scalability and cost persist.

Simulations offer a promising alternative, providing a virtually unlimited source of data at a fraction of the real-world cost. However, a major hurdle for simulation-based training is the "sim-to-real" gap. This gap arises from discrepancies between simulated and real environments, primarily manifesting as geometric mismatches (e.g., imprecise object shapes, simplified scene layouts) and visual differences (e.g., unrealistic textures, lighting, and rendering artifacts). These discrepancies often lead to policies trained in simulation failing to generalize effectively to the real world, necessitating costly fine-tuning with real-world data.

RE3SIM directly addresses these geometric and visual sim-to-real gaps by integrating state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction and neural rendering techniques with physics-based simulators. The paper builds upon significant advancements in several sub-fields:

- **3D Reconstruction:** It leverages established Structure-from-Motion (SfM) techniques like COLMAP and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) algorithms such as OpenMVS for geometric reconstruction, along with specialized tools like ARCode for object mesh recovery.
- **Neural Rendering:** A key innovation in bridging the visual gap comes from the adoption of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Kerbl et al., 2023), a recent explicit representation method known for its photorealistic rendering quality and efficiency, outperforming earlier implicit methods like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) (Mildenhall et al., 2021) in terms of rendering speed for novel views.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer and Robot Learning:** The work falls under the umbrella of "real-to-sim-to-real" pipelines, which aim to reconstruct real environments into simulation, train policies in the enriched simulation, and then deploy them back in the real world. This approach is gaining traction with works like RoboStudio, SplatSim, and RoboGSim, which also explore 3DGS for robotic environments.

RE3SIM distinguishes itself from existing real-to-sim-to-real efforts by focusing on a comprehensive pipeline that reconstructs both geometric and visual aspects with minimal gaps and, critically, validates the learned robot policies through extensive real-world experiments. While some related works focus primarily on rendering quality or specific components, RE3SIM aims for an end-to-end, efficient, and robust system that can generate high-fidelity data suitable for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across diverse manipulation tasks. It seeks to provide a practical solution for scaling up robot learning, especially in the context of developing generalist robot policies that require vast amounts of varied training data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind RE3SIM stems from the pressing need for scalable and cost-effective data generation methods in robotics, to overcome the limitations of real-world data collection and the persistent sim-to-real generalization gap. The authors propose RE3SIM to achieve several key objectives:

- **To Develop a 3D-Photorealistic Real-to-Sim System:** The overarching goal is to create a robust system, RE3SIM, that can faithfully replicate real-world scenarios into a physics-based simulator. This involves accurately capturing both the geometry and visual appearance of the environment.
- **To Bridge the Geometric Sim-to-Real Gap:** A core objective is to overcome the discrepancies in object shapes and scene structures between real and simulated environments. This is addressed through advanced 3D reconstruction techniques that generate realistic and detailed meshes for both backgrounds and foreground objects.
- **To Bridge the Visual Sim-to-Real Gap:** The paper aims to eliminate the visual differences often found in simulations, such as unrealistic lighting, textures, and rendering quality. This is achieved by integrating high-fidelity neural rendering methods, specifically 3D Gaussian Splatting, for photorealistic visual output.
- **To Enable Efficient Expert Data Collection in Simulation:** By creating a high-fidelity simulated environment, the system aims to leverage "privileged information" (e.g., exact object poses) to efficiently generate large volumes of expert demonstrations within the simulator, vastly accelerating the data collection process compared to real-world teleoperation.
- **To Validate Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A crucial objective is to demonstrate that policies trained solely on data generated by RE3SIM can achieve robust zero-shot transfer to real-world robots without any real-world fine-tuning. This directly assesses the effectiveness of the proposed system in minimizing the sim-to-real gap.
- **To Facilitate Large-Scale Data Generation for Robust Policies:** Beyond basic tasks, the system is designed to generate large-scale datasets that can train policies capable of generalizing to more challenging scenarios, including unseen objects, cluttered environments, and varying lighting conditions, thus pushing the limits of generalizable robot manipulation.
- **To Ensure Time and Cost Efficiency:** The methodology is specifically designed to minimize human effort during scene reconstruction and computational cost during data generation, making it a practical and scalable solution for robotics research.

In essence, RE3SIM is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of simulation for robot learning by making simulated data as close to real data as possible, thereby accelerating the development of capable and generalist robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RE3SIM proposes a comprehensive "real-to-sim-to-real" pipeline designed to generate high-fidelity simulation data for robotic manipulation tasks. The system comprises four main components: Mesh Recovery, Hybrid Visual Rendering, Real-World Alignment, and Expert Data Collection. The entire process is integrated with a physics-based simulator, such as NVIDIA Isaac Sim.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The RE3SIM pipeline begins by reconstructing a real-world scene, including backgrounds and foreground objects, but excluding the robot itself, as its 3D assets are usually readily available. This reconstructed scene is then imported into a physics simulator. The key idea is to use separate reconstruction and rendering strategies for backgrounds and foreground objects to achieve optimal fidelity and realism.

#### 3.2. Mesh Recovery

This component focuses on reconstructing accurate 3D geometries (meshes) of the scene and objects, which are crucial for realistic collision detection and physics simulation within the simulator.
- **Background Reconstruction:** For the background, the system first employs Structure-from-Motion (SfM) techniques, specifically COLMAP, to estimate camera poses and generate a sparse point cloud from a set of images or a video. This is followed by Multi-View Stereo (MVS) using OpenMVS to produce a detailed mesh. This mesh is then simplified to create collision bodies, ensuring accuracy for physics calculations while maintaining planar integrity (e.g., tabletops).
- **Foreground Object Reconstruction:** For individual foreground objects, RE3SIM uses ARCode, a commercial solution capable of automatically segmenting and reconstructing object meshes. This balances usability with quality for individual manipulable items.
- **Post-processing:** Reconstructed meshes often have imperfections (voids, sharp edges) from imperfect image quality. To prevent erratic behavior in simulation (e.g., unstable object resting), post-processing steps like void filling and surface smoothing are applied. While acquiring precise physical parameters (mass, friction) from visual data is challenging, default values are used and found to be effective for many manipulation tasks. The meshes are represented in USD (Universal Scene Description) for extensibility within the simulation environment.

#### 3.3. Hybrid Visual Rendering

To address the visual sim-to-real gap, RE3SIM employs a hybrid rendering strategy that combines different techniques for foreground and background elements:
- **Foreground Object Rendering:** Foreground objects are rendered from their reconstructed meshes using ray tracing capabilities within the physics simulator (e.g., Isaac Sim). This provides realistic lighting and shadows for the manipulable items.
- **Background Rendering:** For the background, which constitutes the majority of the visual scene and demands high photorealism, RE3SIM leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). 3DGS offers superior visual quality and rendering efficiency compared to traditional mesh rendering or other neural rendering methods like NeRF.
- **Composition:** A Z-buffer rendering technique is used to accurately composite the foreground objects rendered via ray tracing with the 3DGS-rendered background, based on their ground-truth depth information. This hybrid approach supports multi-view rendering from various camera perspectives, delivering high-fidelity visual observations in real-time (24 FPS for 480p images across two camera views).

#### 3.4. Real-World Alignment

Accurate alignment between the reconstructed simulated scene and the real-world robot's coordinate system is critical for successful sim-to-real transfer.
- **Background Alignment:** ArUco markers placed on the real table are used to align the 3DGS coordinate system with the robot's base coordinate system.
- **Fine Alignment:** Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm is then applied to further optimize this alignment. ICP refines the relative transformation between a partial point cloud obtained from a depth camera (in the real world) and a complete point cloud sampled from the reconstructed mesh (in simulation), minimizing alignment errors.
- **Foreground Object Placement:** For foreground objects, their placement in simulation is defined within a range of possible positions, mimicking realistic task variations.

#### 3.5. Expert Data Collection

Once the high-fidelity simulation environment is established, RE3SIM generates expert demonstrations for robot policy training:
- **Privileged Policy:** A rule-based "privileged policy" (`π_priv`) is used. This policy has access to privileged information, such as the exact 6D poses of target objects, which are unavailable to a real-world vision-based policy. This allows for efficient generation of optimal trajectories.
- **Motion Planning:** The policy computes 6D gripper poses at key steps, and a motion planner (based on RRTConnect) plans collision-free paths in the joint space between these steps.
- **Domain Randomization:** To enhance policy robustness and generalization, domain randomizations are applied during data collection, including varying source/target object placements and robot arm base positions.
- **Reject Sampling:** Failed rollouts (e.g., objects falling, collisions) are filtered out using reject sampling to ensure a high-quality dataset of successful demonstrations.
- **Observation-Action Pairs:** The collected data consists of observation-action pairs (`(o_t, a_t)`), where `o_t` includes hybrid-rendered images and robot proprioception data, mirroring what would be accessible in the real world. This data is then used to train robot policies via imitation learning.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that RE3SIM generates simulation data that is geometrically accurate, visually photorealistic, and well-aligned with the real world, paving the way for effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of RE3SIM focuses on answering four key research questions, demonstrating its capabilities across various manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. High-Quality and Well-Aligned Reconstruction (Q1)

- **Qualitative Evaluation:** Visual comparisons (Figure 3, Appendix E) between real-world images and RE3SIM's hybrid-rendered simulated images demonstrate a high degree of visual fidelity and realism. Both wrist and third-person camera views show strong similarity, indicating accurate reconstruction and alignment.
- **Quantitative Evaluation:** Table 1 quantitatively evaluates the vision gap using PSNR and SSIM metrics. RE3SIM's hybrid rendering (employing 3DGS for the background) significantly outperforms Polycam in both metrics (e.g., SSIM of 0.37 for 3DGS vs. 0.34 for Polycam). While OpenMVS shows a comparable PSNR to 3DGS, its SSIM is notably lower (0.27), primarily due to visible cracks in its reconstructed textures, which introduce visual discrepancies. These results validate RE3SIM's design choice for superior visual rendering.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Performance (Q2)

- **Task Design:** Three tabletop manipulation tasks were used: "pick and drop a bottle into a basket," "place a vegetable on the board," and "stack blocks."
- **Performance:** Policies trained on only 100 episodes of RE3SIM-generated simulation data achieved an average success rate exceeding 58% in real-world zero-shot transfer (Table 2). This significantly outperforms a baseline method (RialTo+IL) and achieves comparable or even slightly better performance than policies trained on 50 episodes of real-world data for certain tasks (e.g., 0.75 vs 0.8 for pick and drop, 0.75 vs 0.6 for place vegetable). For picking a bottle, RE3SIM also showed competitive performance (0.75 success rate) compared to a state-of-the-art grasping method (AnyGrasp+Prim) which achieved 0.9, but AnyGrasp only predicts grasp poses and cannot solve the full task. This strong performance highlights the effectiveness of RE3SIM in minimizing the sim-to-real gap.

#### 4.3. Large-Scale Sim-to-Real for Challenging Tasks (Q3)

- **Complex Task:** A long-horizon task, "clear objects on the table," involving sequential picking of multiple objects, was used to test generalization.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The policy, trained on a large dataset generated from only five types of objects, demonstrated impressive generalization capabilities (Figure 4). It achieved a 0.65 success rate on "unseen" objects (different shapes/colors) and in "cluttered" scenes (0.69 success rate), and showed strong robustness to varying lighting conditions ("darkness" condition achieved 0.65 success rate). This suggests that high-fidelity, large-scale simulated data can build policies that generalize well beyond their training distribution.
- **Data Scaling Effects:** Experiments showed a clear positive correlation between the amount of simulated data and real-world success rates (Figure 5). Increasing data size led to significant performance improvements, demonstrating the scalability benefits of RE3SIM's data generation.

#### 4.4. Pipeline Efficiency (Q4)

- **Human Effort:** Scene reconstruction is highly efficient. Reconstructing the background using video input takes approximately 51.5 seconds of human effort, and reconstructing an object with ARCode takes about 60.5 seconds (Table 3).
- **Data Collection Cost:** Collecting 100 simulation episodes for complex tasks requires only 6.45 to 13.78 minutes using 8 RTX 4090 GPUs (Table 4), demonstrating a massive efficiency gain compared to real-world teleoperation.
- **Real-time Performance:** The system operates at approximately 24 frames per second (FPS) for two 480p camera views (Table 5), ensuring real-time performance and visual fidelity during data generation.

#### 4.5. Sim-Real Consistency

A high Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.924 was found between policy performance in simulation and the real world (Figure 1d). This strong correlation indicates that models performing well in simulation are likely to perform well in reality, further validating the minimal sim-to-real gap achieved by RE3SIM.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies (Appendix)

- **Visual Augmentations:** Appropriate image augmentation techniques (Gaussian Blur, Defocus, ColorJitter, Gaussian Noise) were shown to significantly improve real-world success rates (Table 7), effectively bridging residual sim-to-real visual discrepancies.
- **Data Properties:** Analysis of object location distributions, episode lengths, and gripper angles revealed slight differences between simulated and real-world data, providing insights into potential areas for further improvement (Figure 8, 9).
- **Co-training and Fine-tuning:** Experiments showed that co-training or fine-tuning with real data effectively integrated RE3SIM-generated data, introducing new features without disrupting the training process (Figure 10).

In summary, RE3SIM successfully demonstrates the generation of high-fidelity simulation data that enables robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, generalizes to novel scenarios, scales efficiently, and exhibits strong sim-real consistency, making it a powerful tool for accelerating robot learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RE3SIM represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, offering a highly practical and scalable solution to some of the most persistent challenges. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Radical Reduction in Data Collection Cost and Time:** The most immediate and profound impact of RE3SIM is its ability to drastically reduce the cost and time associated with collecting high-quality robot training data. By enabling efficient generation of large-scale, photorealistic datasets in simulation, RE3SIM liberates researchers and developers from the labor-intensive and expensive process of real-world teleoperation. This democratizes access to data, allowing smaller labs or individual researchers to train sophisticated robot policies previously only feasible for well-funded institutions.
- **Enabling Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By meticulously addressing both geometric and visual sim-to-real gaps through advanced reconstruction and rendering, RE3SIM achieves impressive zero-shot transfer capabilities. This means policies trained purely in simulation can be directly deployed on real robots with high success rates, eliminating the need for costly and time-consuming real-world fine-tuning. This is a crucial step towards more deployable and versatile robotic systems.
- **Accelerating the Development of Generalist Robot Policies:** The ability to generate vast amounts of diverse, high-fidelity data at scale is foundational for training large-scale, generalist robot models. RE3SIM provides the necessary data infrastructure to explore data scaling laws in robotics, similar to those that have driven progress in large language models and vision transformers. This can lead to robots that are more adaptable, robust, and capable of performing a wider array of tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Broad Applicability Across Robotic Domains:** While validated on tabletop manipulation tasks, the underlying principles of RE3SIM – 3D reconstruction, hybrid rendering, and physics-based simulation – are broadly applicable. This methodology can potentially be extended to various robotic applications, including mobile manipulation, industrial automation, and even human-robot interaction, accelerating research and development in these areas.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** RE3SIM establishes a strong foundation for future research in several directions. It highlights the importance of hybrid rendering, the power of neural rendering techniques like 3DGS, and the continued need for sophisticated scene reconstruction. Future work can build upon this to address more complex scenarios and further refine the sim-to-real pipeline.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors candidly acknowledge several limitations, which also point to future research avenues:
- **Rigid Objects Only:** RE3SIM is currently limited to rigid objects and cannot handle articulated, deformable objects, or liquids. Extending the reconstruction and simulation capabilities to these complex object types is a significant challenge.
- **Manual Physics Parameters:** The system relies on manually defined physics parameters (mass, friction), rather than automatic system identification. Integrating robust system identification methods could further enhance realism and reduce manual effort.
- **Rule-Based Data Collection:** The use of rule-based policies for expert data collection becomes increasingly challenging as task complexity grows. Future work could explore learning-based data generation methods (e.g., reinforcement learning, imitation learning from diverse human demonstrations) or more advanced motion planning.

**Impact Statement (from paper):**
The paper's impact statement explicitly notes its goal to "release the human burden of collecting large-scale real-world data, improve efficiency, and push the limit of generalist robot policies with data scaling law." It also responsibly acknowledges the potential for "unemployment issues" arising from the widespread application of such efficient automation technologies.

In conclusion, RE3SIM offers a timely and impactful solution for bridging the sim-to-real gap, promising to significantly accelerate the progress of robot learning by making high-quality, scalable simulation data generation a practical reality. Its contribution moves the field closer to developing truly intelligent and versatile robotic systems for the real world.
