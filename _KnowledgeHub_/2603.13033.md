# ESPIRE: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Spatial Reasoning of Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces ESPIRE, a diagnostic benchmark within a simulated physical environment to evaluate embodied spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It proposes a generative evaluation paradigm unifying 3D localization and execution tasks, which reveals that current VLMs struggle with 3D rotational geometry and precise distance understanding.

## Problem

- Existing VLM evaluation benchmarks for spatial reasoning are static, prone to linguistic biases, and do not reflect active, generative real-world robotic tasks.
- Current generative evaluation methods often neglect or oversimplify the execution phase critical for robotic tasks, limiting comprehensive assessment.
- Real-world evaluations are constrained by hardware and scalability, while existing simulated environments lack systematic task designs for fine-grained diagnosis of spatial reasoning.

## Method

- Developed **ESPIRE**, a novel diagnostic benchmark in a simulated physical world (==Isaac Sim==) to assess embodied spatial reasoning in VLMs.
- Implemented a ==generative evaluation paradigm== that decomposes robotic tasks into ==3D localization== (generating 2D pixel coordinates) and ==6-DoF execution== (predicting goal poses).
- Designed a ==hierarchical task framework== for fine-grained analysis across spatial aspects (attributes, distances, relationships, orientations), reference frames, and reference objects.

## Results

- Proprietary VLMs generally perform strongest, but some open-access models (e.g., Qwen3-VL series) achieve competitive results, occasionally outperforming in specific execution categories.
- 'Place' tasks are consistently more challenging than 'pick' tasks due to stricter acceptance conditions, increased occlusion, and additional constraints of target spaces.
- An ablation study on rotation prediction showed pitch is crucial for 'pick' tasks and roll for 'place' tasks, with execution difficulty increasing as more rotation axes need prediction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs demonstrate significantly better performance in localization (passive understanding) compared to execution (acting-oriented reasoning), indicating a gap in 3D rotational geometry and precise distance comprehension.
> - Providing feedback (reflection) improves localization but degrades execution, suggesting current VLMs lack robust 3D rotation understanding for effective execution reflection.
> - Models exhibit particular weaknesses in reasoning about precise 'distances' and 'orientations', pointing to a fundamental deficiency in grounding 3D rotational geometry.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which aim to integrate visual perception with linguistic understanding. A critical frontier in this area involves enhancing VLMs' spatial cognition, which is fundamental for embodied agents to reason about and interact with the 3D physical world. Such capabilities are essential for real-world applications like robotic navigation and manipulation.

Despite ongoing efforts to improve spatial intelligence in VLMs, the methods for evaluating these models have exhibited limitations. Existing benchmarks predominantly utilize static, multiple-choice Visual Question Answering (VQA) paradigms. While these facilitate automatic evaluation, they are susceptible to linguistic biases due to their reliance on distractors and diverge from the active, generative nature of real-world robotic tasks where agents must act upon instructions. Real-world evaluations, though more reliable, are often constrained by specific hardware, limited scalability, and reproducibility challenges.

More recent approaches have introduced generative evaluation methods, such as pointing tasks, which require models to identify target objects or spaces by generating 2D pixel coordinates. However, these often neglect or oversimplify the subsequent execution phase, which is integral to robotic tasks. Efforts to incorporate execution using simulated environments have also been limited by simplified scenes, a reliance on external tools, or a lack of systematic task design that allows for fine-grained diagnosis of spatial reasoning across various aspects and granularities. This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing these shortcomings, proposing a diagnostic benchmark that is physically grounded, generative, systematic, and aims to bridge the gap between passive spatial understanding and active, embodied reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are motivated by the identified limitations in current VLM evaluation paradigms for spatial reasoning. The authors aim to:

- **Address Evaluation Limitations**: Overcome the shortcomings of existing benchmarks, which are often static (VQA-based), prone to biases, depart from real-world embodied scenarios, and lack systematic diagnostic capabilities for spatial reasoning.
- **Introduce a Diagnostic Benchmark**: Develop ESPIRE (Embodied Spatial Reasoning), a novel benchmark specifically designed for the diagnostic evaluation of embodied spatial reasoning in VLMs. This benchmark is intended to offer a more comprehensive and nuanced assessment of VLM capabilities.
- **Bridge the Sim-to-Real Gap**: Utilize a simulated physical world to ground VLMs, thereby narrowing the discrepancy between how models are evaluated and their performance in real-world deployment scenarios, particularly in robotics.
- **Establish a Generative Evaluation Paradigm**: Implement an evaluation methodology that moves beyond discriminative VQA. This involves decomposing robotic tasks into "localization" (identifying targets) and "execution" (performing actions) and framing both as generative problems (e.g., goal position and pose generation). This approach is intended to extend passive spatial reasoning towards reasoning for action.
- **Enable Fine-Grained Analysis**: Design a systematic framework for tasks that allows for a detailed analysis of VLMs' intrinsic spatial reasoning. This includes examining performance across various spatial aspects (attributes, relationships, distances, orientations), reference objects (oriented/non-oriented, near/distant), and reference frames (relative, intrinsic, absolute), as well as across different granularities (e.g., coarse vs. precise distances or orientations).
- **Quantify Bottlenecks**: Through systematic experimentation, identify specific weaknesses and bottlenecks in current VLMs' spatial reasoning abilities, particularly concerning 3D rotational geometry and precise distance understanding. This diagnostic insight is intended to guide future model development.
- **Facilitate Rapid Model Development**: Provide a scalable and reproducible alternative to real-world evaluations, complementing them by allowing for rapid, iterative improvement of spatially intelligent VLMs.

In essence, the motivation is to create a robust, systematic tool that can rigorously test and diagnose the spatial reasoning capabilities of VLMs, particularly in the context of robotic interaction, and provide actionable insights for their enhancement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ESPIRE benchmark is constructed as a simulated physical world designed to evaluate embodied spatial reasoning in VLMs. The methodology involves a specific evaluation paradigm, systematic task design, a realistic simulation environment, and a scalable task generation process.

#### 3.1. Evaluation Paradigm: Localization and Execution

To adapt VLMs, which are not inherently trained to control physical actions, to robotic tasks, each task is decomposed into two sequential subtasks:

- **Localization**: This subtask requires the VLM to identify a target object or space specified by an instruction within a given scene. It is formulated as a generative pointing task, where the model outputs 2D pixel coordinates on scene images. The performance is measured by **accuracy**, defined as the fraction of correct localizations, determined by comparing the predicted point against a ground-truth segmentation mask. This contrasts with discriminative VQA-style evaluations by directly requiring point generation rather than choice selection.
- **Execution**: Following localization, this subtask involves performing a corresponding action (e.g., pick or place) in the physically grounded environment. Since VLMs cannot directly produce low-level control, execution is simplified as a 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) task, requiring the prediction of a goal pose (position and orientation) in SE(3). Goal position prediction is also framed as a pointing task. Performance is measured by **acceptance rate**, which is the fraction of physically achievable poses as verified by a motion planner (e.g., cuRobo). This grounding ensures that the VLM's generated poses are physically feasible.

Both localization and execution inherently require native spatial reasoning without reliance on external tools, with their shared pointing formulation bridging spatial reasoning for understanding and for acting.

#### 3.2. Systematic Task Design

ESPIRE employs a hierarchical and systematic task design to enable comprehensive assessment and fine-grained analysis of VLMs' spatial reasoning. This design is built around three primary factors:

- **Spatial Aspects (S)**: Tasks are grouped into four classes:
 - **Attributes**: Intrinsic object properties like dimensions (height, width, volume) and states (empty, non-empty, emptiest).
 - **Distances**: Proximity between objects, including relative (nearest, farthest) and precise (e.g., "within 1 meter," "20 centimeters away").
 - **Relationships**: Positional relations (left, right, in front of, behind, above, below), including comparative and superlative forms.
 - **Orientations**: Directional expressions, from coarse (upright, tilted) to fine-grained (clock positions, tilt degrees).
- **Reference Frames (F)**: Following Levinson (2003), three types are considered:
 - **Relative Frames**: Viewer-centered (e.g., "to your left").
 - **Intrinsic Frames**: Object-centered, often based on an object's inherent front face (e.g., "behind the mirror").
 - **Absolute Frames**: Global coordinates (e.g., elevation, cardinal directions), used sparingly in indoor settings.
- **Reference Objects (O)**: Objects used to define spatial relations:
 - **Intrinsic-oriented**: Objects with a clear front face (e.g., a chair, mirror).
 - **Non-oriented**: Objects without a distinct front face (e.g., a jar, ball).
 - **Near vs. Distant**: Categorization by proximity to the robot for distance reasoning analysis.

A specific configuration of these factors, C = (S, F, O), defines a context for spatial reasoning. Tasks are curated within these contexts to examine reasoning across different granularities.

**Instruction Representation**: Each task instruction is represented as a 3-tuple T = (C, A, P), where C is the task specification, A is the execution action (pick/place), and P is the localization instruction. P is further represented as a functional program, composed of atomic functions, that operates on 3D scene graph representations of environment states to yield ground-truth targets. This functional program approach allows for flexible control over task complexity (limited to 3 reasoning hops for primary focus on spatial reasoning) and scalable task generation. A total of 148 spatial-reasoning task types, distributed across 65 instruction families, are curated with 3-4 templates each for linguistic diversity.

#### 3.3. Simulation Environment

ESPIRE is built on Isaac Sim (NVIDIA, 2025), leveraging its realistic physics simulation. Two main task environments are simulated:

- **Tabletop Scene**: For 'pick' tasks, initialized with manipulable books (three sizes, three initial poses), support ornaments, and reference ornaments. Near references appear on the table, while distant references are on the floor. Clutter is varied by changing the number of books.
- **Shelf Scene**: For 'place' tasks, containing similar object types. Supports additional relationships like 'above' and 'below'. Complexity is varied by shelf layouts (panels, grids).

**Environment Representation and Generation**: Each environment is initialized from a random state, represented by a 3D scene graph with annotated objects (sizes, dimensions, poses). Random 3D scene graphs are rendered in Isaac Sim, ensuring physical validity. The Franka robot is initialized in a random pose, equipped with an on-wrist camera (ego-view) and supplemented by fixed-position global cameras (world views). External lights are randomized to enhance realism.

**Reducing Sim-to-Real Visual Gaps**: Instead of complex visual-matching mitigation, the approach focuses on enhancing environmental diversity. This includes using annotated 3D assets with realistic textures, tuning their sizes to real-world counterparts, and randomly assigning textures from real-world materials for background assets. Randomization of lighting and camera poses further contributes to visual realism.

#### 3.4. Task Generation

A simulation task is defined by an environment state and an instruction. 'Pick' and 'place' tasks are generated sequentially. After environment sampling, `pick` tasks are generated first, followed by `place` tasks. For each instruction family, variables are sampled and filtered (e.g., ensuring reference objects are visible). The resulting functional program is executed on the 3D scene graph, and yielded answers are verified for feasible manipulations using a motion planner. A balanced task sampling strategy ensures tasks are uniformly distributed across task families and difficulty levels (categorized into easy, medium, hard based on object count or shelf fullness).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using ESPIRE yielded several findings regarding the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

#### 4.1. Overall Model Performance

- **Proprietary vs. Public Models**: Proprietary VLMs, such as Gemini2.5-Pro, generally demonstrated the strongest performance across most metrics. However, open-access models, particularly the Qwen3-VL series, showed competitive performance, occasionally outperforming Gemini2.5-Pro in specific categories like execution on 'place' tasks.
- **Model Size and Performance**: The study observed that larger model size does not consistently correlate with better performance. For instance, Qwen3-VL-30B (with 3B activated parameters) sometimes surpassed Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3-VL-235B (with 22B activated parameters).
- **Localization Accuracy**: Most models achieved reasonable localization accuracy, with the notable exception of InternVL3-78B, which scored below 30%. This lower performance was hypothesized to be due to its native multimodal pre-training approach being less effective than stage-by-stage alignment learning.
- **Localization vs. Execution Discrepancy**: A general trend was observed where VLMs performed significantly better in localization than in execution. This suggests that models possess a better capacity for passive spatial understanding compared to acting-oriented spatial reasoning, particularly concerning 3D rotational geometry. RoboBrain2.0-7B, despite strong localization, exhibited low execution rates (<20%), indicating a gap between spatial understanding and active manipulation.
- **InternVL3-78B Anomaly**: Despite poor localization, InternVL3-78B surprisingly achieved higher execution rates (>40%), suggesting a decoupled or different learning mechanism for these two phases in this specific model.

#### 4.2. Pick vs. Place Tasks

'Place' tasks were generally found to be more challenging than 'pick' tasks. This is attributed to stricter acceptance conditions in 'place' tasks, which often involve considering additional constraints of the target space (especially if partially occupied) and dealing with increased occlusion as the robot approaches the target. In contrast, 'pick' tasks allow for more flexibility in grasping faces and positional adjustments.

#### 4.3. Impact of Reflection

While reflection (providing feedback on previous failed attempts) improved localization performance across all tested Qwen3-VL models, it paradoxically degraded execution performance. The authors suggest this indicates that current VLMs lack robust 3D rotation understanding, which is crucial for effective reflection in execution. This highlights a need for better fine-tuning data for rotation reasoning.

#### 4.4. Spatial Aspect Performance

Analysis across spatial aspects revealed that all models struggled most with 'distance' reasoning, indicating a deficiency in precise distance understanding. Orientation reasoning also posed a significant challenge in both localization and execution stages, suggesting a fundamental weakness in grounding 3D rotational geometry. Gemini2.5-Pro and RoboBrain2.0-7B exhibited relatively stronger and more consistent performance across spatial aspects, potentially due to specific fine-tuning on related tasks.

#### 4.5. Task Difficulty Analysis

Performance generally decreased as task difficulty increased across both localization and execution for most models. However, some exceptions were noted, such as InternVL3-78B and RoboBrain2.0-7B occasionally performing slightly better on harder 'pick' tasks, and similar inconsistencies in 'place' tasks for InternVL3-78B and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B.

#### 4.6. Prerequisites for Successful Execution

The study found no clear direct correlation between execution success and the initial distance to the target before execution. However, models that performed better in execution, such as InternVL3-78B and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, exhibited different "far-sighted" (48cm average pre-success distance) versus "near-sighted" (38cm average pre-success distance) tendencies, respectively. 'Place' tasks generally involved twice the pre-success distance compared to 'pick' tasks, possibly to mitigate occlusion. Stronger models also tended to require a moderate number of moves (around 2.1 attempts) for successful execution, whereas weaker models often attempted more times before failure.

#### 4.7. Ablation Study of Rotation Prediction

A focused ablation study on rotation prediction (pitch, roll, yaw) with Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B revealed that pitch was a key factor for 'pick' tasks, likely relating to feasible grasping face selection. Roll was more critical for constrained 'place' tasks, possibly due to its role in determining deviation from an upright direction. As expected, execution became more challenging as more rotation axes needed to be predicted, with specific axis combinations (pitch-yaw for pick, yaw-roll for constrained place) having the largest adverse impact. This further emphasized VLMs' struggle with 3D geometric reasoning.

#### 4.8. Human Study on Reference Frames

A human study investigating ambiguous reference frames showed strong human-model agreement when the table or distant objects served as references. However, significant disagreement occurred with near oriented objects (e.g., an alarm clock). In such cases, humans tended to prefer the intrinsic frame of the reference object, while models defaulted to the relative frame. This suggests that current VLMs struggle with object-centric orientation inference, defaulting to a viewer-centric perspective when geometric cues for intrinsic frames are ambiguous or less salient for them.

#### 4.9. Benchmark Efficiency

The efficiency analysis indicated that latency primarily stems from API calls and VLM inference time. For example, a single inference for RoboBrain2.0-7B on an RTX 4090 took an average of 9.25 seconds. Execution involving motion planning and environment updates averaged 18.12 seconds per move request. Reflection settings generally increased evaluation time due to additional API calls and processing steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ESPIRE represents a notable contribution to the evaluation of Vision-Language Models for embodied spatial reasoning, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Novel Diagnostic Benchmark**: ESPIRE is presented as the first simulated physical environment specifically designed for the diagnostic evaluation of spatial cognition in VLMs. Its emphasis on systematic task design across diverse spatial reasoning contexts and granularities enables a comprehensive and fine-grained analysis of model behaviors, which was previously lacking in the research landscape.
- **Bridging Understanding and Action**: By proposing a generative evaluation paradigm that unifies 3D localization (passive spatial understanding) and execution (acting-oriented spatial reasoning), ESPIRE directly addresses the gap between VLM's perceptual abilities and their capacity for physical interaction. This is crucial for the development of truly embodied AI agents.
- **Identification of Key Bottlenecks**: The experimental results and in-depth analysis highlight critical deficiencies in current VLMs, particularly in 3D rotational geometry, precise distance understanding, and object-centric orientation inference. These quantified bottlenecks provide a clear roadmap for future research directions, guiding efforts to enhance the specialized spatial inductive biases required for future VLM architectures.
- **Scalable and Reproducible Evaluation**: ESPIRE offers a scalable and reproducible alternative to real-world robot evaluations, which are often costly, time-consuming, and hardware-dependent. This facilitates rapid, iterative model development and experimentation, accelerating progress in embodied AI.
- **Informing VLA Development**: The benchmark's design, which decouples reasoning (localization) and acting (execution), serves as a "microscope" to pinpoint where spatial reasoning chains break. This provides valuable insights for the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, allowing researchers to improve foundational VLM components before integrating them into end-to-end control policies.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: ESPIRE opens new avenues for research, such as designing long-horizon tasks that demand multi-step spatial reasoning, thereby enabling studies on the dependencies between reasoning steps and the role of memory. Its distinct workspaces for 'pick' and 'place' tasks also make it suitable for extensions to evaluate mobile manipulation, covering a broader range of embodied robotics challenges.

While acknowledged limitations, such as its restriction to indoor scenes, present opportunities for future extensions, ESPIRE provides a robust framework for understanding and advancing the spatial intelligence of VLMs, contributing significantly to the progression of embodied artificial intelligence.
