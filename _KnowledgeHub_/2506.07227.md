# Hallucination at a Glance: Controlled Visual Edits and Fine-Grained Multimodal Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework integrates controlled visual editing with fine-grained multimodal learning to reduce hallucinations in MLLMs. It generates the Micro Edit Dataset (MED) of 50K image-text pairs and a benchmark, using supervised fine-tuning with a feature-level consistency loss, enabling fine-tuned open-source models to match or exceed commercial MLLMs like GPT-4o on fine-grained visual tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently hallucinate, generating factually incorrect responses, especially when confronted with subtle visual details or fine-grained differences.
- A scarcity of suitable training data exists that contains tightly controlled, minimal semantic differences with precisely aligned textual descriptions.
- Existing MLLM learning objectives do not explicitly enforce feature-level stability across small visual perturbations, leading to brittle visual embeddings.

## Method

- Developed the ==Micro Edit Dataset (MED)== through a pipeline involving MLLM-guided data filtering, controlled visual editing with Gemini Flash 2.0, and caption alignment, producing over **50K** high-quality minimally edited image-text pairs.
- Constructed the ==Micro Edit Detection (MED) benchmark==, comprising **165** machine-generated questions and **35** real-world examples, specifically designed to assess fine-grained visual reasoning and difference detection.
- Introduced a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) strategy incorporating a ==feature-level consistency regularization term==, explicitly training MLLMs to align visual feature differences with natural language descriptions of changes.

## Results

- Fine-tuned open-source MLLMs demonstrated significant performance improvements on the MED benchmark; for example, Qwen2.5-VL-7B improved from **40.24%** to **51.61%** accuracy (**+11.37 percentage points**).
- The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieved **51.61%** accuracy on the MED benchmark, comparable to or slightly surpassing leading commercial models like GPT-4o (**51.32%**) and Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro (**51.81%**).
- Generalizable performance gains were observed across various multimodal benchmarks (MMStar, BLINK, POPE, MME), with the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieving the highest average score (**58.52**) among open-source models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLM hallucinations regarding subtle visual changes stem from inadequate fine-grained training data and learning objectives that fail to encourage stable feature representations under minor perturbations.
> - Controlled generation of minimally different image-text pairs with precise semantic descriptions can effectively teach MLLMs to detect and reason about fine-grained visual distinctions.
> - A feature-level consistency loss during fine-tuning promotes more robust visual embeddings and improves the MLLM's ability to ground language in subtle visual cues, directly mitigating hallucination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, demonstrating impressive capabilities across a wide spectrum of vision-language tasks such as visual question answering (VQA), image captioning, and referring expression comprehension. However, a persistent and critical challenge facing MLLMs is their "hallucination" problem—the tendency to generate fluent but factually incorrect or ungrounded responses, especially when confronted with subtle visual details or fine-grained differences. This work directly addresses this significant limitation, positioning itself at the intersection of MLLM robustness, fine-grained visual understanding, and controlled data generation.

Current MLLMs, including state-of-the-art models like GPT-4o, often struggle with detecting minor changes in object presence, count, spatial position, or attributes, which are trivial for human perception. This deficiency renders them brittle for real-world applications demanding high precision, such as robotics, industrial quality control, and medical imaging. The authors attribute this problem to two key factors: the scarcity of suitable training data containing tightly controlled, minimal semantic differences with aligned textual descriptions, and the limitations of existing learning objectives that do not explicitly enforce feature-level stability for small visual perturbations.

This paper builds upon advancements in image editing, particularly diffusion and flow-based models (e.g., Gemini Flash 2.0), to systematically generate synthetic, controlled data. It also leverages the power of existing MLLMs (e.g., Qwen-2.5-VL-72B) in an innovative self-supervisory role for data filtering, caption generation, and alignment. By creating the "Micro Edit Dataset (MED)" and the "Micro Edit Detection benchmark," the authors contribute valuable resources to the community, akin to previous efforts to create more robust MLLM evaluation benchmarks (e.g., MM-Vet, POPE, MMBench) but with a specific focus on fine-grained visual differences. Furthermore, the proposed supervised fine-tuning (SFT) framework with a feature-level consistency loss directly tackles the learning objective limitation, aiming to improve MLLM representations at a fundamental level, rather than just post-hoc filtering of hallucinations. This approach represents a crucial step towards developing more reliable and precisely grounded MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the fine-grained visual understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and, consequently, mitigate their tendency to hallucinate. The authors aim to achieve this by overcoming two primary deficiencies they identify in current MLLM training paradigms:
1. **Lack of Suitable Training Data:** Existing large-scale datasets, often web-crawled, typically lack image pairs with controlled, minimal semantic differences and precisely aligned textual descriptions. This makes it challenging for MLLMs to learn the intricate mapping between subtle visual changes and corresponding linguistic shifts.
2. **Limitations of Learning Objectives:** Current training objectives do not explicitly promote feature-level stability across small visual perturbations. This results in MLLM visual embeddings that are brittle and easily drift under fine-grained edits, leading to misinterpretations and hallucinations.

Motivated by the critical need for MLLMs to operate with high precision in real-world applications (e.g., robotics, quality control, medical imaging) where subtle visual cues are paramount, the authors propose a novel framework. Their goal is to develop an approach that can:
- Systematically generate high-quality, minimally edited image-caption pairs at scale.
- Leverage this specialized data to fine-tune MLLMs, explicitly teaching them to recognize and reason about fine-grained visual distinctions.
- Introduce an alignment objective that encourages stable visual representations under minor edits.
- Provide a robust evaluation benchmark specifically designed to assess sensitivity to subtle visual changes.

Ultimately, the motivation is to make MLLMs more reliable, robust, and capable of precise visual grounding, moving beyond impressive but sometimes superficial performance to achieve genuine fine-grained visual reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a comprehensive framework that intertwines targeted data construction with a novel fine-grained alignment objective to enhance MLLMs' fine-grained visual understanding.

#### 3.1. The Micro Edit Dataset (MED) Construction Pipeline

The core of their approach lies in creating a semantically controlled dataset. The pipeline involves several stages:

- **Data Generation:**
 - **Source Data:** Candidate image-text pairs are sampled from DOCCI and Visual Genome, chosen for their diverse, object-rich images and detailed captions.
 - **MLLM-Guided Data Filtering:** To ensure quality and editability, Qwen-2.5-VL-72B is used to automatically evaluate image quality, caption clarity, and editability. Only samples strictly deemed suitable are retained, ensuring that minimal edits would semantically alter captions.
 - **Controlled Visual Editing:** Gemini Flash 2.0, a state-of-the-art image editing model, is employed to generate minimally modified image pairs. Edit instructions are crafted by Qwen-2.5-VL-72B based on 11 predefined compositional change categories (e.g., attribute, object manipulation, spatial reconfiguration, counting, comparison) known to cause MLLM hallucinations. This ensures precise, localized, and meaningful changes.
 - **Visual Similarity Filtering:** To guarantee subtle differences, CLIP embeddings (clip-vit-base-patch32) are used to calculate cosine similarity between original and edited image pairs. Pairs below a 0.7 similarity threshold are discarded, retaining only those with minimal yet semantically significant visual variations.

- **Caption Alignment and Generation:** A structured four-step pipeline, primarily using Qwen2.5-VL-72B and Qwen3-32B, ensures precise and grounded captions:
 - **Caption Completion for Original Image:** The original caption is revised by an MLLM to be comprehensive and strictly grounded in visible content, given the original image and edit prompt.
 - **Caption Generation for Edited Image:** A new caption is generated for the edited image, reflecting the updated content and guided by the complete original caption and edit type.
 - **Generating Difference Description:** Qwen3-32B (a text-only LLM) compares the original and edited captions to generate a concise description of their key semantic difference, classified into a predefined edit type.
 - **Human Verification and Iterative Refinement:** 1,000 sampled image pairs are manually inspected for caption and difference description accuracy. Prompts are refined, and affected samples reprocessed using Qwen-VL-Max to eliminate errors, enriching vocabulary and clarity. This process yields over 50K high-quality image-text pairs for the Micro Edit Dataset (MED).

#### 3.2. The Micro Edit Detection (MED) Benchmark

From the MED dataset, a dedicated benchmark is constructed to evaluate fine-grained reasoning.
- Image pairs with high semantic similarity (CLIP cosine similarity > 0.95) are selected.
- For each pair, nine rephrased questions are generated, and GPT-4o provides a correct answer and three plausible distractors.
- All question-answer sets are manually verified and categorized into the 11 edit types.
- The final benchmark comprises 165 questions, augmented with 35 real-world minimally different image pairs from the MMVP benchmark (MED-Real Set), to assess generalization capabilities.

#### 3.3. Fine-Grained Multimodal Learning for MLLMs

The authors introduce a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) strategy incorporating a feature-level consistency regularization term.
- **Problem Setup:** The task is formalized as describing differences between two visually similar images (d_X(x1,x2) <= epsilon), aiming to generate a natural language description (Delta_T(t1,t2)). They identify issues with semantic under-specification and a generalization gap between standard MLLM training objectives (minimizing autoregressive captioning and CLIP-style contrastive loss on potentially noisy data) and the specific downstream task of difference detection.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** To address the limitations of noisy datasets and objective mismatch, the proposed SFT approach leverages the curated MED dataset. For each (original image, edited image, difference description) triplet, the model is fine-tuned to minimize an empirical SFT loss. This loss encourages the MLLM to translate the feature-level image difference (I_theta(xi) - I_theta(x_hat_i)) into the generated difference description (S_phi(ti, t_hat_i)). This explicitly trains the model to recognize and describe fine-grained visual changes, promoting deeper visual understanding and architectural awareness of subtle semantic shifts. The fine-tuning updates the MLLM's parameters from a noisy initial state to a refined estimate more attuned to fine-grained differences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates the significant effectiveness of the proposed framework in enhancing MLLMs' fine-grained visual understanding and reducing hallucinations.

#### 4.1. Performance on MED Benchmark

- **Human Performance:** Human annotators achieved an average accuracy of 95.21% on the MED benchmark, setting a high ceiling for model performance.
- **Closed-Source Model Performance:** Even top-tier API-based models like GPT-4.1 (54.26%) and GPT-4o (51.32%) struggled significantly with fine-grained difference detection, particularly in relational tasks such as Action, Comparison, and Negation, highlighting a persistent gap compared to human ability. Claude 3.7 Sonnet performed even lower at 40.44%.
- **Open-Source Model Enhancement:** The supervised fine-tuning method consistently and significantly improved the performance of all tested open-source MLLM backbones:
 - Qwen2-VL-7B improved from 38.48% to 47.55% (+9.07 percentage points).
 - Qwen2.5-VL-7B increased from 40.24% to 51.61% (+11.37 percentage points), showing major gains in Comparison (+21.06), Count (+22.23), and Universality (+14.29).
 - LLaMA-3.2-11B rose from 34.71% to 40.92% (+6.21 percentage points).
 - LLaVA-V1.6-7B improved from 40.44% to 44.04% (+3.60 percentage points).
- **Competitive Performance:** Notably, the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Ours) achieved an accuracy of 51.61%, which is comparable to, and even slightly surpassed, commercial models like GPT-4o (51.32%) and Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro (51.81%), despite being a smaller, open-source model. It also significantly outperformed Claude 3.7 Sonnet (40.44%) and Qwen-VL-Plus (47.36%).
- **Generalization to Real-World Edits:** Evaluation on the MED-Real Set (35 minimally different real-world image pairs) confirmed the method's generalization capabilities. Fine-tuned models consistently outperformed their base versions (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B improved from 68.57% to 74.29%, LLaVA-V1.6-7B from 34.29% to 45.71%).

#### 4.2. Experimental Results on Other Benchmarks

The fine-tuned models also demonstrated consistent and generalizable performance gains on established multimodal benchmarks (MMStar, BLINK, POPE, MME).
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Ours) achieved the highest average score (58.52) and MME perception score (1701.87) among open-source models.
- Notable improvements were observed in Count (+9.17), Visual Correspondence (+4.07), and POPE (+1.23), indicating stronger numeracy, spatial grounding, and resistance to hallucination.
- The POPE score improvement for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Ours) (97.52) and LLaVA-V1.6-7B (Ours) (97.39) highlighted enhanced robustness against hallucinated object predictions. While minor decreases in Fine-grained Perception tasks were occasionally observed (potentially due to increased sensitivity to small edits), the overall improvements outweighed these trade-offs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the contribution of different components of the SFT strategy:
- **Vision Encoder (ViT) Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning the ViT alone provided solid gains, particularly in perception-heavy tasks (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B showed a 1.31 gain in average score and nearly 10 points on MME perception score), emphasizing the importance of refined visual representations.
- **Joint Fine-tuning (ViT and LLM):** Joint fine-tuning of both the vision encoder and language model yielded the best overall performance, demonstrating additional gains (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B showed an additional +1.34 in average score and +7.04 in MME compared to vision-only fine-tuning). This underscores the critical role of aligning the language head with the updated visual features for optimal cross-modal integration and hallucination reduction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in addressing the critical issue of hallucinations and limited fine-grained visual understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models. Its impact can be primarily seen in several key areas:

- **Enhanced MLLM Robustness and Reliability:** By explicitly training models on subtle visual differences and enforcing feature-level consistency, the work directly tackles the brittleness of MLLMs. This leads to more reliable models that can be trusted in precision-sensitive real-world applications such as medical diagnosis, industrial quality control, and robotic perception, where even minor visual misinterpretations can have serious consequences.
- **Advancement in Fine-Grained Visual Reasoning:** The framework pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities beyond coarse-grained understanding. It demonstrates that with carefully curated data and targeted learning objectives, MLLMs can achieve human-like sensitivity to subtle visual changes, an area where state-of-the-art models previously struggled.
- **Valuable Open-Source Resources:** The release of the **Micro Edit Dataset (MED)**, comprising over 50K high-quality, minimally edited image-text pairs across 11 fine-grained edit categories, and the **Micro Edit Detection benchmark** (200 items, including real-world examples), provides invaluable resources for the research community. These datasets enable systematic evaluation and further development of models specifically designed for fine-grained visual tasks, fostering accelerated progress in this challenging domain.
- **Bridging the Gap between Open-Source and Closed-Source MLLMs:** The demonstration that fine-tuned open-source models can rival or even surpass leading commercial closed-source APIs like GPT-4o on fine-grained tasks is highly impactful. It democratizes access to advanced MLLM capabilities and empowers broader research and development within the open-source community, reducing dependency on proprietary models.
- **Novel Training Paradigm:** The introduction of a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) strategy with a feature-level consistency loss offers a new paradigm for MLLM training. This objective explicitly encourages stable visual embeddings under small perturbations, providing a more robust foundation for visual-textual alignment.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that pave the way for future research:
- **Binary Edits:** The current framework focuses on binary edits between image pairs. Future work could explore extending it to multi-step transformations, temporal reasoning, or more complex compositional edits.
- **Computational Cost:** Full fine-tuning of both vision and language components, while effective, can be computationally expensive. Exploring more efficient adaptation methods, such as parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques, could reduce this burden.
- **Unified-Token Architectures:** Applying this approach to unified-token architectures like Chameleon and Emu3 could further enhance its applicability and explore its potential benefits in a more integrated multimodal learning paradigm.

Overall, this paper makes a substantial contribution by providing both a novel methodology and critical resources to address a fundamental weakness in current MLLMs, pushing them closer to reliable and precise visual reasoning for complex real-world challenges.
