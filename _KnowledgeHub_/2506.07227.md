# Hallucination at a Glance: Controlled Visual Edits and Fine-Grained Multimodal Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework integrates controlled visual editing with fine-grained multimodal learning to reduce hallucinations in MLLMs. It generates the Micro Edit Dataset (MED) of 50K image-text pairs and a benchmark, using supervised fine-tuning with a feature-level consistency loss, enabling fine-tuned open-source models to match or exceed commercial MLLMs like GPT-4o on fine-grained visual tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently hallucinate, generating factually incorrect responses, especially when confronted with subtle visual details or fine-grained differences.
- A scarcity of suitable training data exists that contains tightly controlled, minimal semantic differences with precisely aligned textual descriptions.
- Existing MLLM learning objectives do not explicitly enforce feature-level stability across small visual perturbations, leading to brittle visual embeddings.

## Method

- Developed the ==Micro Edit Dataset (MED)== through a pipeline involving MLLM-guided data filtering, controlled visual editing with Gemini Flash 2.0, and caption alignment, producing over **50K** high-quality minimally edited image-text pairs.
- Constructed the ==Micro Edit Detection (MED) benchmark==, comprising **165** machine-generated questions and **35** real-world examples, specifically designed to assess fine-grained visual reasoning and difference detection.
- Introduced a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) strategy incorporating a ==feature-level consistency regularization term==, explicitly training MLLMs to align visual feature differences with natural language descriptions of changes.

## Results

- Fine-tuned open-source MLLMs demonstrated significant performance improvements on the MED benchmark; for example, Qwen2.5-VL-7B improved from **40.24%** to **51.61%** accuracy (**+11.37 percentage points**).
- The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieved **51.61%** accuracy on the MED benchmark, comparable to or slightly surpassing leading commercial models like GPT-4o (**51.32%**) and Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro (**51.81%**).
- Generalizable performance gains were observed across various multimodal benchmarks (MMStar, BLINK, POPE, MME), with the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieving the highest average score (**58.52**) among open-source models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLM hallucinations regarding subtle visual changes stem from inadequate fine-grained training data and learning objectives that fail to encourage stable feature representations under minor perturbations.
> - Controlled generation of minimally different image-text pairs with precise semantic descriptions can effectively teach MLLMs to detect and reason about fine-grained visual distinctions.
> - A feature-level consistency loss during fine-tuning promotes more robust visual embeddings and improves the MLLM's ability to ground language in subtle visual cues, directly mitigating hallucination.
