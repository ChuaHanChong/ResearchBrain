# SpatialViz-Bench: A Cognitively-Grounded Benchmark for Diagnosing Spatial Visualization in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialViz-Bench, a new cognitively-grounded benchmark, evaluates the spatial visualization capabilities of MLLMs through 12 programmatically generated tasks across four sub-abilities. It reveals substantial performance gaps between models and humans, with top proprietary models achieving up to 44.66% accuracy, and identifies fundamental perceptual and spatial transformation errors as dominant failure modes in MLLMs.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM evaluation for spatial reasoning primarily focuses on perception and memorization, largely overlooking the distinct cognitive ability of spatial visualization (mentally manipulating unseen structures).
- Current benchmarks often rely on publicly available problems, leading to data contamination risks and hindering reliable assessment of genuine reasoning.
- Spatial visualization tasks are frequently embedded within broader reasoning categories, obscuring their specific evaluation and limiting diagnostic depth.

## Method

- **SpatialViz-Bench** was developed based on a cognitive framework decomposing ==spatial visualization== into four sub-abilities: ==Mental Rotation==, ==Mental Folding==, ==Visual Penetration==, and ==Mental Animation==.
- The benchmark includes 12 distinct tasks with controlled difficulty levels, programmatically generating 1,180 question-answer pairs to ensure scalability and prevent data contamination.
- It evaluates 27 diverse MLLMs in a ==zero-shot setting== using both direct and ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== prompting, comparing against a human baseline.

## Results

- All MLLMs performed substantially below the human baseline of **82.46%**, with the top-performing MLLM (Gemini-2.5-pro) achieving **44.66%** overall accuracy.
- A statistically significant performance gap of approximately **10%** was observed between proprietary closed-source and leading open-source models.
- Models generally struggled with core 3D visualization tasks like 3D Rotation, Cube Unfolding, and Cube Reconstruction, often performing at near-random accuracy levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit significant limitations in fundamental visuospatial acquisition and transformation, with perceptual and spatial transformation errors accounting for nearly 60% of failures.
> - A "CoT paradox" was observed where Chain-of-Thought prompting benefited top closed-source MLLMs but often degraded the accuracy of leading open-source models on pure-visual spatial tasks.
> - Simply scaling MLLM parameters, as seen between 7B and 72B models, does not substantially resolve core spatial reasoning deficits, indicating a need for targeted architectural or training innovations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The evaluation of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in spatial reasoning has primarily focused on two areas: spatial perception and spatial memorization. Spatial perception benchmarks, such as VSR, What’sUp, SpatialVLM, SpatialMM, and MMRel, assess a model's ability to interpret object-centric relationships, relative distances, sizes, and positions from static visual inputs. Advanced benchmarks like Blink and SpatialRGPT-bench extend this to include multi-view reasoning and world knowledge, but still concentrate on explicitly observable information. Spatial memorization, addressed by video-based benchmarks like VCBench and VSI-bench, evaluates object tracking and understanding of dynamic scenes, often involving egocentric-to-allocentric transformations.

Despite progress in these areas, the existing landscape largely overlooks spatial visualization—the cognitive ability to mentally construct, manipulate, and reason about unseen structures or transformations derived from visible inputs. Current assessments of spatial visualization are often embedded within broader categories like mathematical or logical reasoning in general MLLM benchmarks (e.g., MM-IQ, MARVEL, EMMA, MathVision), which obscures its evaluation as a distinct capability. Furthermore, many existing benchmarks source problems from public domains like IQ tests and competitive exams, which introduces a risk of data contamination where models might perform well due to memorization from pre-training data rather than genuine reasoning. This practice also leads to inconsistent problem formulations and a scarcity of items per sub-skill, increasing the potential for random error and making it difficult to diagnose specific reasoning failures. This research addresses these limitations by introducing a dedicated, cognitively-grounded, and programmatically generated benchmark to specifically assess spatial visualization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce SpatialViz-Bench, a comprehensive and cognitively-grounded multimodal benchmark designed to formally evaluate the spatial visualization capabilities of MLLMs. The motivation for developing this benchmark stems from several critical shortcomings in the existing MLLM evaluation landscape:

- **Underserved Cognitive Ability:** While MLLMs have advanced in general reasoning and tasks involving visible visual information, their ability to infer unseen relationships through mental spatial manipulation (spatial visualization) remains insufficiently evaluated. This capability is crucial for real-world applications in fields such as architectural design, engineering, and medical imaging. The authors argue that spatial visualization, a core human cognitive faculty, needs to be abstracted and assessed directly to isolate it from confounding factors.
- **Data Contamination and Reliability Issues:** Existing evaluations frequently rely on problems sourced from publicly available IQ tests, math competitions, or administrative exams. This approach poses a significant risk of data leakage, where test problems or similar examples may have been included in the vast datasets used for MLLM pre-training. Such contamination compromises the reliability and validity of performance assessments, making it difficult to discern true reasoning abilities from mere memorization. The authors emphasize the need for a procedurally generated, standardized benchmark to ensure fair and continuously reliable evaluations.
- **Obscured Categorization and Narrow Task Coverage:** Spatial visualization tasks are often subsumed under broader categories within existing benchmarks, such as "3D-Geometry" or "Spatial Reasoning," rather than being treated as a distinct and fundamental spatial skill. This categorization hinders targeted research and development efforts focused on improving core spatial abilities. Moreover, specialized datasets that do exist tend to have narrow task coverage, focusing on only one or two sub-skills (e.g., mental rotation or paper folding) rather than offering a comprehensive assessment across the multifaceted nature of spatial visualization.
- **Lack of Scalability and Diagnostic Depth:** Current evaluation methodologies often lack the capacity for scalable task expansion, which is essential for ongoing, rigorous assessment in a rapidly evolving field. Furthermore, the heterogeneous formats of existing problems make it challenging to conduct fine-grained error analysis and distinguish true reasoning failures from misinterpretation of instructions or visual inputs.

To address these issues, SpatialViz-Bench aims to provide a robust framework that is scalable, resistant to data contamination, comprehensive in its coverage of spatial visualization sub-abilities, and designed for detailed diagnostic analysis of MLLM performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of SpatialViz-Bench is grounded in cognitive science and employs a systematic, ability-centric methodology for problem generation and evaluation.

- **Cognitive Framework:** The benchmark is structured around a cognitive framework that decomposes spatial visualization into two phases: observing visible information (requiring spatial perception) and discerning implicit information (requiring iterative spatial visualization and spatial memorization). Building on Thurstone's work on primary mental abilities, the benchmark specifically targets four core sub-abilities:
 - **Mental Rotation:** The ability to mentally represent and rotate objects (2D or 3D) while maintaining their features. Tasks include 2D Rotation, 3D Rotation, and Three-View Projection.
 - **Mental Folding:** The ability to mentally fold 2D patterns into 3D objects or unfold 3D objects into 2D patterns. Tasks include Paper Folding, Cube Unfolding, and Cube Reconstruction.
 - **Visual Penetration:** The ability to imagine the internal structure of an object from its external features or infer properties of partially observed structures. Tasks include Cross-Section, Cube Counting, and Cube Assembly.
 - **Mental Animation:** The ability to mentally visualize the motion and interaction of components within a system, predicting dynamic state changes. Tasks include Arrow Moving, Block Moving, and Mechanical System.

- **Task Design and Dataset Size:** SpatialViz-Bench comprises 12 distinct tasks, with 3 tasks designed for each of the four core sub-abilities. Each task includes 2 to 3 difficulty levels, with each level containing 40 or 50 test cases, resulting in a total of 1,180 question-answer pairs. The majority of problems feature image-based options (818 questions) to emphasize visual reasoning, complemented by text-based or numeric answers to prevent overfitting to a single style.

- **Programmatic Generation:** For 11 of the 12 tasks, a programmatic generation pipeline was developed, integrating Python with FreeCAD (an open-source CAD software) or implemented solely in Python. This approach ensures:
 - **Scalability:** Allows for continuous expansion of the task bank and generation of new, novel test cases.
 - **Data Contamination Prevention:** Dynamically updating the test bank through randomized generation mitigates the risk of overlap with MLLM pre-training data.
 - **Difficulty Control:** Cognitive load parameters (e.g., rotational complexity, number of folds, assembly dimensions) are explicitly used to control task difficulty.
 - **Distractor Generation:** The system systematically generates plausible distractor options, often with explanations, for deeper diagnostic analysis.
 - **Standardization:** Standardized question templates are employed to minimize errors arising from varied instructions and ensure a unified input format.
 The "Three-View Projection" task (Level 1) incorporates fixed DeepCAD models, but novel distractors are programmatically generated. The "Mechanical System" task was manually designed by experts using representative public simulations to ensure physical consistency, as programmatic generation for this task proved technically challenging.

- **Evaluation Setup:**
 - **Models:** A comprehensive set of 27 MLLMs was evaluated, including 8 closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o, o1, Gemini-2.5-pro, Claude-3.5-sonnet, Qwen-VL-max, Doubao-1.5-vision-pro) and 19 open-source models across various parameter scales (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL series, InternVL-3 series, Deepseek-VL2 series, Llama-4 series). A text-only LLM (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) was also included as a baseline.
 - **Setting:** All experiments were conducted in a zero-shot setting.
 - **Prompting Schemes:** Models were evaluated under two prompting schemes:
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Prompts designed to encourage models to output their reasoning process before the final answer.
 - **Direct Answering (Non-CoT):** Prompts that solicited the answer directly, without intermediate reasoning.
 - **Metric:** Model performance was assessed using accuracy, based on a match between predicted and ground-truth answers.
 - **Human Baseline:** A human baseline was established by having 8 graduate students with strong spatial reasoning backgrounds solve a 72-problem subset under controlled conditions (no external aids, unlimited time).

- **Error Analysis:** A diagnostic error analysis was conducted, primarily through manual review by two human annotators, assisted by Gemini-2.5-pro. Errors were classified into six categories: Perceptual, Spatial Transformation, Spatial Memorization, Instruction Following, Methodological, and Calculation & Reasoning. Cohen’s Kappa coefficient (κ = 0.85) indicated strong inter-annotator agreement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MLLMs on SpatialViz-Bench yielded several findings regarding their spatial visualization capabilities:

- **Vision-Dependent and Reasoning-Intensive Nature:** The benchmark problems proved to be highly vision-dependent and reasoning-intensive. A powerful text-only LLM (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) achieved an overall accuracy of 25.86%, which is statistically indistinguishable from the random-chance baseline (25.08%). This quantitatively confirms that visual input is indispensable for problem-solving in this benchmark. The image-based options and multi-step spatial transformations demand complex visual analysis and inference.

- **Significant Performance Gaps and Model Hierarchy:** All evaluated MLLMs performed substantially below the human baseline of 82.46%, underscoring the challenge posed by SpatialViz-Bench. The top-performing MLLM, Gemini-2.5-pro, achieved an overall accuracy of 44.66% (95% CI: [41.85%, 47.51%]), demonstrating capabilities above the random baseline. A statistically significant performance gap (approximately 10%) was observed between proprietary closed-source models and leading open-source models, with the confidence intervals of Gemini-2.5-pro and the top open-source model, LLaMA-4-Scout (34.24%, 95% CI: [31.58%, 36.99%]), showing no overlap. This indicates the benchmark's strong discriminative power.

- **Common Failures in Core 3D Visualization Tasks:** Models generally struggled with core 3D tasks such as 3D Rotation, Cube Unfolding, and Cube Reconstruction, often performing at near-random accuracy levels. This suggests prevalent perceptual and visualization limitations in handling 3D space. While top proprietary models performed well on tasks like Arrow Moving (Gemini-2.5-pro surpassed human performance), many open-source models remained at near-random levels, indicating a deficiency in understanding object-centered motion despite relatively low visual complexity.

- **Difficulty Sensitivity and Collapse in Top-Tier Models:** The intended difficulty gradient, validated against human performance, was only visibly reflected in top-tier models. For example, Gemini-2.5-pro exhibited sensitivity to 7 difficulty gradients across tasks, whereas 10 models showed sensitivity to 1 or fewer gradients. On the 3D Rotation task, only Gemini-2.5-pro and o1 demonstrated a statistically significant performance drop with increased difficulty, confirming that only models with non-random baseline accuracy at lower difficulty levels were capable of showing a statistically significant "difficulty collapse" at higher levels.

- **The "CoT Paradox":** A "CoT paradox" was observed, wherein Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting benefited high-performing closed-source MLLMs (e.g., Claude-3.5-sonnet showed a significantly positive impact, p < 0.05) but paradoxically degraded the accuracy of several leading open-source models (e.g., Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct, Deepseek-VL2-tiny, Internvl2.5-78B, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, Sail-VL-1.6-8B all showed significantly negative impacts, p < 0.05). This degradation was primarily concentrated in "pure-visual" spatial tasks (e.g., 3-View Projection, 3D Rotation), suggesting that for these open-source models, generating explanatory text interferes with native visual-spatial judgment. Top-tier closed-source models, conversely, appeared more resistant to this interference.

- **Robustness to Prompting and Extraction Strategies:** A sensitivity analysis confirmed that the observed CoT degradation was not an artifact of specific prompt engineering or parsing failures. Performance trends remained consistent with alternative CoT prompt templates, and the discrepancy between distinct answer extraction rules was negligible (< 1.2%), indicating a genuine reasoning failure rather than a technical artifact.

- **Dominance of Perceptual and Spatial Transformation Errors:** Statistical error analysis across representative models (Gemini-2.5-pro, o1, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) revealed that Perceptual and Spatial Transformation errors collectively accounted for nearly 60% of all failures. This finding supports the hypothesis that the primary MLLM bottleneck lies in fundamental failures of visual perception and transformation, rather than high-level logical reasoning. Methodological errors constituted the third-largest category (over 23%), indicating suboptimal problem-solving strategies. Conversely, Calculation & Reasoning and Instruction Following errors were infrequent.

- **Limitations of Model Scaling:** Comparing Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-72B, despite a tenfold increase in parameters, their core error patterns remained similar, with Perceptual and Transformation errors persisting as dominant issues. While the 72B model showed improvements in Spatial Memorization and Calculation errors, gains in critical spatial error types were limited. This suggests that simply scaling model size may not resolve fundamental spatial reasoning deficits.

- **Qualitative Analysis of Gemini-2.5-pro:** A qualitative case study of Gemini-2.5-pro indicated that errors occurred at both the visible information perception stage (e.g., 2D color recognition, complex pattern identification, 3D cube position identification) and the unseen spatial relationships reasoning stage (e.g., inferring cube net structures, symmetrical relationships). The model also exhibited pre-training biases, sometimes defaulting to theoretical physics formulas for "Mechanical System" tasks rather than mentally simulating motion, diverging from human intuitive strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SpatialViz-Bench contributes to the field by providing a diagnostic and comprehensive benchmark for spatial visualization, a core cognitive ability that has been previously under-evaluated in MLLMs.

- **Diagnostic Tool for MLLM Capabilities:** The benchmark's structured design, encompassing 12 tasks across 4 sub-abilities and multiple difficulty levels, offers a fine-grained diagnostic tool. It systematically reveals specific strengths and, more significantly, the pervasive weaknesses of current MLLMs in spatial visualization, differentiating between models and isolating specific failure modes (e.g., perceptual, spatial transformation, methodological).
- **Guidance for Future Research and Development:** The findings indicate that the primary limitations of MLLMs in spatial visualization stem from fundamental deficiencies in visuospatial acquisition and transformation, rather than high-level logical reasoning. This insight provides a clear direction for targeted optimizations in training paradigms, suggesting that efforts beyond mere model scaling are required to achieve substantial progress in spatial skills. Innovations in data augmentation, architectural design, or learning algorithms that explicitly address these low-level spatial deficits may be necessary.
- **Mitigation of Data Contamination:** The programmatic generation methodology, employed for 11 out of 12 tasks, establishes a new standard for fair and trustworthy evaluation. By enabling the continuous expansion and dynamic updating of the test bank with novel problems, it effectively prevents data contamination, ensuring that performance reflects genuine reasoning abilities rather than memorization.
- **Advancement of Multimodal Intelligence:** Improving MLLMs' spatial visualization capabilities, as diagnosed by this benchmark, could lead to the development of more capable "internal world models." Such models could efficiently run fast, lightweight internal "what-if" scenarios to predict the outcomes of actions (e.g., how an object changes shape or how gears interact). This is a more efficient alternative to computationally expensive methods like invoking large, diffusion-based video generation models for explicit future state rendering, with potential impacts on fields like robotics, design, and simulation.

In summary, SpatialViz-Bench addresses a significant lacuna in MLLM evaluation by offering a robust, scalable, and cognitively-grounded framework. Its detailed diagnostic analysis offers valuable insights for advancing multimodal artificial intelligence towards more human-like spatial reasoning abilities.
