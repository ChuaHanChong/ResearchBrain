# EvoGymCM: Harnessing Continuous Material Stiffness for Soft Robot Co-Design

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces EvoGymCM, an extension of the EvoGym benchmark, to enable co-design of soft robots with continuous material stiffness alongside morphology and control. It demonstrates that optimizing material properties, either dynamically or statically, improves robot performance by up to 41% in various tasks and facilitates synergistic interactions across design facets.

## Problem

- Automated soft robot design traditionally treats material stiffness as a fixed, discrete attribute, limiting the design space and robot performance.
- Current co-design platforms like EvoGym impose a bottleneck by discretizing the material dimension, restricting precise adaptation to diverse task environments.
- The full physical potential of soft robots is not realized due to the inability to dynamically or statically tailor material stiffness fields.

## Method

- The EvoGym framework is augmented with a ==continuous material stiffness dimension== (S ∈ [0.5, 2]ʷˣᴴ) as a first-class optimizable parameter, creating the **EvoGymCM** benchmark suite.
- Two co-design paradigms are introduced: **EvoGymCM-R** (==Reactive-Material Co-Design==) for programmable materials and **EvoGymCM-I** (==Invariant-Material Co-Design==) for traditional materials with fixed stiffness.
- ==Bi-level optimization== frameworks are developed for each paradigm to jointly optimize morphology, material properties (real-time tuning or static field), and control policies.

## Results

- Reactive-Material Co-Design improved performance by up to **41%** in complex environments (e.g., BridgeWalker-v0 from 2.10 to 2.97) and extreme shape-change tasks (e.g., AreaMaximizer-v0 from 0.62 to 0.74), correlating with environmental complexity.
- Invariant-Material Co-Design accelerated evolutionary efficiency and elevated terminal fitness for locomotion tasks, demonstrating synergistic patterns like low-stiffness central bodies for deformability and high-stiffness bases for stability.
- Continuous material adaptation reshaped the evolutionary landscape, allowing for the stable evolution of morphologies (e.g., slender limbs) that would be unstable or suboptimal under fixed-material paradigms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating continuous material stiffness significantly expands the soft robot design space, enabling finer-grained optimization than with discrete material types.
> - Dynamic (reactive) material tuning allows real-time adaptation to environmental interactions, such as anticipatory stiffening for stability and impact management.
> - Static (invariant) optimization of spatial material distribution reveals critical coupled regularities between material properties and evolved morphology, essential for unlocking full physical capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Automated design has emerged as a significant paradigm in the development of high-performance soft robots, replacing traditional manual approaches. Soft robots, characterized by intrinsic compliance, offer advantages in unstructured environments but also present design challenges due to their infinite degrees of freedom and highly nonlinear dynamics. This complexity has made algorithm-driven optimization within simulation environments a necessary approach to navigate the extensive design space.

The co-design of morphology and control constitutes a foundational paradigm in soft robotics. Early efforts in this area explored co-evolving the morphologies and neural controllers of virtual creatures and later expanded to voxel-based soft robot structures. The introduction of Evolution Gym (EvoGym) provided a standardized, large-scale benchmark for the co-optimization of soft robot morphology and control, facilitating comparative studies across various methods. Subsequent advancements building upon EvoGym have focused on improving efficiency and effectiveness through probabilistic generative models (e.g., variational autoencoders, diffusion models), integrating Large Language Models as heuristic operators, and implementing knowledge transfer and inheritance mechanisms (e.g., curriculum-based approaches, Transformer-based controllers, Lamarckian transfer learning, pre-training). Despite these developments, the material dimension has largely been treated as a fixed, passive attribute rather than a primary design variable in most co-design frameworks. Mainstream platforms, including EvoGym, typically represent materials as a finite set of discrete voxel types with predefined stiffness properties, thereby limiting the design space.

Concurrently, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) provides a framework for evaluating and controlling soft robotic systems, capable of handling high-dimensional, non-linear state spaces. Algorithms such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) are commonly employed to enable robust policy gradient methods for reactive, closed-loop control in compliant structures.

This research addresses a limitation within this landscape by formally integrating continuous material stiffness as a first-class design variable. It extends the EvoGym framework to allow for the optimization of material properties alongside morphology and control, thereby expanding the design space beyond discrete material selections. The work aims to bridge the gap between current co-design methodologies and the potential for materials to be dynamically or statically tailored to specific task requirements, aligning with advancements in programmable and traditional material manufacturing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work is to unlock the full physical potential of soft robots by enabling the precise adaptation of their material stiffness field to diverse task environments. The authors contend that current automated co-design platforms, such as EvoGym, impose a fundamental bottleneck by discretizing the material dimension. This discretization restricts the design space and consequently limits the performance capabilities of soft robots.

The key objectives are as follows:

1. **Elevate Material Stiffness as a First-Class Design Variable**: To transition material stiffness from a passive, fixed attribute to an actively optimizable parameter, treated on par with morphology and control. This involves transforming the design problem from a discrete combinatorial search to a continuous field optimization.
2. **Introduce a Benchmark for Continuous Material Stiffness**: To establish a formal benchmark suite, named EvoGymCM (EvoGym with Continuous Materials), that incorporates continuous material stiffness into the existing EvoGym framework. This suite aims to provide a standardized platform for research in this expanded design space.
3. **Align with Real-World Material Mechanisms**: To develop two distinct settings within EvoGymCM that correspond to practical material properties:
 - **EvoGymCM-R (Reactive)**: Motivated by programmable materials that can dynamically tune their stiffness during environmental interactions.
 - **EvoGymCM-I (Invariant)**: Motivated by traditional materials where the optimized stiffness field remains fixed post-fabrication.
4. **Formulate Co-Design Paradigms for High-Dimensional Coupling**: To develop specific Morphology–Material–Control co-design paradigms capable of addressing the complex, high-dimensional, and non-linear coupling among these three design facets.
 - **Reactive-Material Co-Design**: Designed for EvoGymCM-R, this paradigm focuses on learning real-time stiffness tuning policies to guide programmable materials.
 - **Invariant-Material Co-Design**: Designed for EvoGymCM-I, this paradigm jointly optimizes morphology and fixed material fields to provide quantitative guidance for traditional material fabrication.
5. **Demonstrate Performance Enhancement and Synergy**: To systematically evaluate through experiments that continuous material optimization leads to improved performance and facilitates synergistic interactions across morphology, material, and control.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to overcome existing performance limitations in soft robot design and enable the creation of robots with enhanced adaptability and capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on augmenting the existing EvoGym framework to incorporate continuous material stiffness as an optimizable dimension, resulting in the EvoGymCM benchmark suite. This expanded framework enables the formulation and evaluation of two distinct co-design paradigms tailored to different material properties.

**A. EvoGymCM Benchmark Suite**

1. **Robot Representation**: The conventional voxel-based soft robot representation is augmented to include a continuous material dimension.
 - **Morphology (M)**: A discrete matrix M ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 4}ʷˣᴴ dictates the spatial distribution of voxel types (rigid, soft, horizontal actuator, vertical actuator).
 - **Material Stiffness (S)**: A continuous matrix S ∈ [0.5, 2]ʷˣᴴ is introduced. This dimension acts as a relative scaling field, assigning a continuous multiplier to the baseline stiffness of structural voxels defined in M.
 - **Control (C)**: A learned neural policy that maps real-time state observations to target expansion or contraction ratios for actuators.
2. **Continuous Material Stiffness Dimension**: The backend dynamics, governed by a 2D mass-spring physics engine, are modified. Each voxel is modeled as point masses and elastic springs.
 - The effective spring constant (k_effective) for a spring within a voxel (i, j) is calculated based on its material stiffness factor Sᵢ,ⱼ and the baseline constant k_base.
 - For diagonal springs within a voxel, k_effective = Sᵢ,ⱼ ⋅ k_base.
 - For edge springs shared between adjacent voxels (i, j) and (i', j'), k_effective = ½(Sᵢ,ⱼ + Sᵢ',ⱼ') ⋅ k_base.
3. **EvoGymCM-R: Benchmark for Programmable Materials**: This setting enables dynamic, real-time stiffness tuning.
 - **Underlying Physics Implementation**: The material stiffness field S_t is time-variant. At each simulation step, S_t is passed to the force calculation phase, transiently instantiating effective spring constants.
 - **Augmented Action Space**: The reinforcement learning (RL) action space is augmented. The control policy outputs an action vector a_t = [c_t, s_t], where c_t are motor actuation signals and s_t are continuous stiffness modulation commands for S_t.
4. **EvoGymCM-I: Benchmark for Traditional Materials**: This setting models materials with fixed properties post-fabrication.
 - **Underlying Physics Implementation**: The continuous stiffness field S is time-invariant and optimized offline. It scales baseline spring constants to define k_effective for all occupied voxels during the initialization phase, and these values remain fixed throughout the simulation.

**B. Morphology-Material-Control Co-Design Algorithmic Paradigms**

Two distinct paradigms are proposed to tackle the high-dimensional optimization of the ⟨M, S, C⟩ triad, framed as a bi-level optimization problem.

1. **Reactive-Material Co-Design (for EvoGymCM-R)**:
 - **Objective**: Maximize the expected return by finding an optimal morphology M\* and a policy π_θ\* that jointly controls motor actuation (c_t) and real-time material stiffness (s_t).
 - **Framework**: A nested bi-level optimization.
 - **Outer Loop**: Morphological evolution, where new morphologies (M) are proposed.
 - **Inner Loop (Material-Control Co-Optimization)**: For each morphology, an RL agent is trained to jointly execute motor control and real-time stiffness tuning. This allows the soft robot to dynamically react to environmental interactions.
 - **Outcome**: The resulting policy π_θ\* serves as an adaptive modulation policy for programmable materials.
2. **Invariant-Material Co-Design (for EvoGymCM-I)**:
 - **Objective**: Co-optimize a unified physical body B = ⟨M, S⟩ (where S is a fixed structural parameter) alongside its corresponding control policy π_θ (which outputs only motor actuation commands c_t).
 - **Framework**: Material optimization is elevated to the outer loop, alongside morphology.
 - **Structural Evolution**: The primary search for optimal morphology (M).
 - **Material Local Search**: Applied to top-performing morphologies. Iteratively adds noise to the current material matrix (S) to generate candidates. These candidates are evaluated via zero-shot evaluations using the existing controller. If a candidate yields a higher reward, it replaces the current material matrix.
 - **Fine-Tune Controller**: After the material local search, the inherited control policy is updated for the newly optimized embodiment.
 - **Outcome**: The algorithm outputs the globally optimal physical body B\* = ⟨M\*, S\*⟩ and its dedicated policy π_θ\*. The optimized material stiffness distribution S provides quantitative guidance for physical fabrication.

The experiments utilized six representative tasks categorized into walking, locomotion, and shape change to evaluate the generalizability and impact of these co-design paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments systematically evaluated the integration of a continuous material stiffness dimension across diverse tasks, focusing on performance improvements, synergistic coupling, and influence on morphological evolution.

**A. Evaluation of Reactive-Material Co-Design (Under EvoGymCM-R)**

1. **Material-Control Co-Optimization vs. Control-Only (Fixed Morphology)**:
 - **Performance Improvements**: Performance gains from continuous material dimension were observed to correlate with environmental complexity.
 - Marginal gains in simple tasks like `Walker-v0` (9.46 to 9.55) as flat terrains imposed minimal compliance demands.
 - Substantial improvements in complex environments such as `BridgeWalker-v0` (2.10 to 2.97) and `DownStepper-v0` (3.25 to 3.92), and in extreme shape-change tasks like `AreaMaximizer-v0` (0.62 to 0.74) and `AreaMinimizer-v0` (0.61 to 0.69). This indicated that reactive material tuning enhanced terrain adaptability and deformability.
 - `CaveCrawler-v0` showed negligible improvement (2.02 to 2.03), attributed to a hard morphological bottleneck (narrow gap requiring volume reduction beyond stiffness modulation capabilities).
 - **Reactive Behavioral Analysis**: Visual analysis confirmed dynamic changes in material properties. For instance, "leg" voxels autonomously increased stiffness (darker colors) immediately prior to ground impact, suggesting anticipatory stiffening for stability and impact force management.
 - **Quantitative Analysis of Reactive Synergy**: Real-time signal analysis of motor actuation (c_t) and material stiffness (s_t) showed a strong statistical correlation and frequency alignment. A consistent phase shift was observed, attributed to the physical actuation lag. This synchronization indicated the successful formation of a unified control-material policy for optimizing performance.

2. **Impact of Reactive-Material on Co-Design (vs. Fixed-Material)**:
 - **Performance Upper Bound**: The integration of continuous material tuning primarily elevated the performance upper bound of evolved robots rather than altering the initial efficiency of the morphological search. Both methods shared comparable initial convergence rates due to similar outer-loop exploration strategies.
 - **Task-Specific Enhancement**:
 - Modest performance gains for `Walker-v0` and `BridgeWalker-v0`, as basic walking relies more on overall stride scale.
 - Pronounced fitness ceiling increases for `CaveCrawler-v0` and `DownStepper-v0` due to enhanced deformability for navigating irregular terrains.
 - Significant performance breakthroughs in `AreaMaximizer-v0` and `AreaMinimizer-v0`, indicating that variable stiffness enhanced deformation capabilities.
 - **Evolutionary Landscape Reshaping**: The reactive-material dimension encouraged an evolutionary trend toward structurally narrower morphologies. For example, in `DownStepper-v0`, the algorithm favored a thin "leg" structure. In traditional fixed-material settings, such a limb would be unstable, but reactive stiffness regulation allowed the agent to dynamically stabilize it, transforming a structural liability into an adaptive advantage for irregular environments.

**B. Evaluation of Invariant-Material Co-Design (Under EvoGymCM-I)**

1. **Impact of Optimizable Invariant-Material on Co-Design (vs. Prescribed-Material)**:
 - **Accelerated Efficiency and Elevated Performance**: Integrating the invariant material dimension simultaneously accelerated evolutionary efficiency and elevated the performance upper bound.
 - Substantial improvements in both convergence speed and terminal fitness were observed across walking tasks (`Walker-v0`, `BridgeWalker-v0`) and locomotion tasks (`CaveCrawler-v0`, `DownStepper-v0`). This indicated that optimizing the spatial material distribution enhanced the physical potential of robots.
 - Marginal performance gains for shape-changing tasks (`AreaMaximizer-v0`, `AreaMinimizer-v0`). This was hypothesized to be due to the global spatial field optimization lacking the micro-level precision required for extreme, voxel-specific stiffness tuning needed for these tasks.
 - **Coupled Regularities in Material-Morphology Synergy**: The introduction of the spatial material dimension revealed tightly coupled regularities between material distribution and evolved morphology, rather than altering macroscopic shape drastically.
 - In `BridgeWalker-v0`, two synergistic material patterns emerged: a central body voxel evolved towards lower stiffness to minimize mechanical constraints and enhance deformability, and a high-stiffness base consistently evolved to improve locomotion stability. This demonstrated the importance of optimizing spatial material fields for unlocking full physical capabilities.

Overall, the findings indicate that incorporating continuous material stiffness significantly enhances soft robot performance and design adaptivity, whether through dynamic real-time tuning or static pre-optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a limitation in the automated design of soft robots by formalizing continuous material stiffness as a first-class design variable, alongside morphology and control. Its significance lies in several areas:

1. **Expansion of Design Space**: By moving beyond discrete material representations, the EvoGymCM benchmark significantly expands the design space for soft robots. This allows for finer-grained optimization of physical properties, potentially leading to more specialized and efficient designs than previously achievable.
2. **Alignment with Real-World Materials**: The introduction of two settings, EvoGymCM-R (Reactive) and EvoGymCM-I (Invariant), directly aligns the simulation environment with distinct real-world material mechanisms. This provides a framework for designing robots with dynamically tunable programmable materials or with optimized, fixed-stiffness traditional materials.
3. **Novel Co-Design Paradigms**: The proposed Reactive-Material and Invariant-Material Co-Design paradigms offer structured approaches to tackle the complex, high-dimensional coupling of morphology, material, and control. These methodologies provide a blueprint for integrating material properties into existing evolutionary and reinforcement learning frameworks.
4. **Demonstrated Performance Improvements and Synergy**: The systematic experiments provide empirical evidence that continuous material optimization boosts performance across various tasks. Furthermore, the analysis reveals synergistic interactions, such as dynamic stiffening to prevent collapse or the evolution of specific stiffness patterns (e.g., softened body, stiff base) to enhance performance. This deeper understanding of interplay between material, morphology, and control is valuable for future design efforts.
5. **Shaping Evolutionary Trajectories**: The work demonstrates that incorporating material adaptability can reshape the evolutionary landscape, allowing for the development of morphologies that would otherwise be deemed unstable or suboptimal in fixed-material paradigms (e.g., slender limbs stabilized by reactive stiffness).

The potential impact of this work is substantial:

- **Advanced Soft Robot Capabilities**: It could lead to the design of soft robots with enhanced adaptability, robustness, and performance in complex and unstructured environments, such as those encountered in exploration, manipulation, or medical applications. Robots could dynamically adjust their compliance to navigate obstacles, grasp delicate objects, or withstand impacts more effectively.
- **Guidance for Material Science and Fabrication**: The Invariant-Material Co-Design paradigm, in particular, offers quantitative guidance for material scientists and engineers. The optimized spatial stiffness fields can inform the development of multi-material 3D printing techniques, mixing ratios, and infill densities for fabricating robots with precisely tailored, heterogeneous material properties.
- **Development of Programmable Materials**: The Reactive-Material Co-Design paradigm provides a framework for exploring control strategies for future programmable materials that can dynamically change their properties. This could accelerate research into smart materials capable of real-time adaptation.
- **New Avenues for Research**: The work establishes a new benchmark and methodology, which may stimulate further research into more sample-efficient algorithms for navigating the expanded, high-dimensional design space. It also opens up avenues for investigating the Sim-to-Real gap, aiming to translate these optimally evolved designs and control policies onto physical robotic platforms.

In summary, this research provides a foundational step towards fully integrating material properties into the automated co-design pipeline of soft robots, promising advancements in both robotic capabilities and material engineering.
