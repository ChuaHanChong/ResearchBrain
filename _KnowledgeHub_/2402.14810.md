# GeneOH Diffusion: Towards Generalizable Hand-Object Interaction Denoising via Denoising Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> GeneOH Diffusion denoises erroneous hand-object interaction sequences. It pairs GeneOH, a contact-centric representation encoding canonicalized hand trajectory plus hand-object spatial and temporal relations around generalized contact points, with a canonical diffusion denoiser applied progressively in three stages, where inputs are first diffused into a whitened noise space. Trained only on GRAB, it beats prior refinement methods across four benchmarks with large domain shifts.

## Problem

- Interaction noise is heterogeneous: unnatural hand poses, penetrating hand-object spatial relations, and temporally inconsistent hand-object motion, which single-stage learned refiners cannot clean together.
- Prior refinement models learn pattern-specific noise-to-data mappings, so they overfit training noise and give conceptually wrong output on unseen synthetic or real sensing noise.
- Existing representations omit explicit temporal relations or contact-centric canonicalization, so they align poorly across new objects and interactions and lose high-frequency hand pose detail.

## Method

- **GeneOH** representation: canonicalized hand keypoint trajectory, contact-centric spatial relations, and temporal relations (relative velocities, distances, normal-decomposed encodings) around generalized contact points sampled within 5mm of the hand.
- ==Denoising via diffusion==: a canonical multi-step stochastic denoiser maps a ==whitened noise space== to the data manifold; arbitrary noisy input is first Gaussian-diffused for a tunable number of steps, then cleaned.
- Progressive three-stage refinement, **MotionDiff** then **SpatialDiff** then **TemporalDiff**, each a specialist denoiser over one GeneOH component, with a proof that later stages do not undermine earlier naturalness; ==MANO== fitting recovers the mesh.

## Results

- On GRAB, MPJPE drops to **9.28mm** versus **12.38mm** for the prior refinement baseline, with hand-object motion consistency **0.57** against **20.37**, close to the **0.51** ground-truth value.
- Under unseen Beta-distributed noise, it reaches **9.09mm** MPJPE while the baseline degrades to **24.10mm**; on real HOI4D sensing noise, intersection volume falls to **1.99cm3** versus **4.09cm3**.
- Ablations on HOI4D confirm each part: removing diffusion raises intersection volume to **3.16cm3**, removing contact-centric canonicalization raises penetration depth to **3.57mm** versus **2.15mm** full.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusing an out-of-domain corrupted input into a single whitened noise space before denoising converts pattern-specific refiners into domain-generalizable ones, with one knob trading generalization against input fidelity.
> - Canonicalizing features around the local interaction region rather than whole-object geometry is what buys transfer to unseen objects; it beats generic domain-generalization tricks like style mixing.
> - Splitting heterogeneous noise into representation components denoised in a provably non-destructive order is more tractable than learning one high-dimensional noise-to-data mapping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Hand-object interaction (HOI) data is fundamental to numerous computer vision and robotics applications, from virtual reality experiences to robotic manipulation systems. However, HOI data obtained from tracking algorithms, motion synthesis, or retargeting often contains significant noise and artifacts that compromise its utility. This paper introduces GeneOH Diffusion, a method that addresses the critical challenge of denoising hand-object interactions while maintaining generalizability across diverse scenarios.

The research, conducted by Xueyi Liu and Li Yi from Tsinghua University, Shanghai AI Laboratory, and Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute, tackles a fundamental problem: existing denoising methods struggle to generalize beyond their training distributions, failing when confronted with new objects, hand motions, or unseen noise patterns. This limitation severely restricts their practical applicability in real-world scenarios where noise characteristics can vary dramatically.

### 2. The Challenge of Hand-Object Interaction Denoising

Hand-object interaction denoising presents several interconnected challenges that make it particularly difficult to solve effectively:

**Complex Interaction Noise**: Raw HOI data contains diverse types of errors including unnatural hand poses, incorrect spatial relationships (such as hand-object penetrations), and inconsistent temporal dynamics where hand movements don't align properly with object motion.

**Generalization Across Domains**: Perhaps most critically, existing data-driven methods suffer from poor generalization. They tend to overfit to specific noise patterns encountered during training and fail when presented with different types of noise or interaction scenarios. This "distribution shift" problem severely limits their practical utility.

**Multi-modal Nature of Solutions**: HOI denoising is inherently ill-posed - multiple plausible clean solutions may exist for a single noisy input. For instance, a hand penetrating an object could be resolved through various hand poses or manipulation strategies.

Previous approaches like TOCH and ManipNet have made progress but remain limited by their inability to handle unseen noise distributions and their lack of comprehensive temporal modeling. These methods typically learn direct mappings from noisy to clean data, making them vulnerable to distribution shifts.

### 3. The GeneOH Representation

The core innovation of this work begins with GeneOH (Generalized Contact-centric Hand-Object), a novel representation designed to capture HOI processes in a way that promotes generalization while highlighting interaction errors.

**Generalized Contact Points**: Rather than considering the entire object surface, GeneOH focuses on a sparse set of "generalized contact points" - points on the object surface within 5mm of the hand trajectory. Each contact point is associated with a 6D pose (position and orientation), creating a contact-centric coordinate system.

**Canonicalized Hand Trajectories**: Hand movements, represented by 21 keypoints from models like MANO, are transformed into local coordinate systems defined by the contact points. This canonicalization removes the influence of global object poses and enables alignment across different interactions.

**Spatial Relations Encoding**: For each contact point, the representation captures:
- Relative offsets from contact points to hand keypoints
- Object point normals
- Object point positions

These spatial features are canonicalized using the contact point's 6D pose, effectively highlighting spatial errors like penetrations.

**Temporal Relations Parameterization**: To address temporal inconsistencies, GeneOH explicitly encodes:
- Relative velocities between hand keypoints and object contact points
- Euclidean distances between hand and object points
- Object velocities

Importantly, relative velocities are decomposed into components orthogonal and parallel to object tangent planes, with distance-dependent weighting that emphasizes close hand-object interactions.

This comprehensive representation strategy serves multiple purposes: it faithfully captures interaction dynamics, makes errors more apparent, and crucially, promotes generalization through careful canonicalization that aligns different HOI sequences despite variations in objects and global poses.

### 4. Denoising via Diffusion Strategy

The second major innovation is the "denoising via diffusion" strategy, which adapts diffusion models for the inverse problem of denoising rather than their typical generative applications.

**Canonical Denoiser Concept**: Instead of learning direct mappings from diverse noisy inputs to clean data, the method learns a "canonical denoiser" that operates specifically on a "whitened noise space." This space is constructed by adding Gaussian noise to clean training data at various scales through a controlled diffusion process.

**Two-Step Denoising Process**: When presented with a novel noisy input `x̂`:

1. **Diffusion Step**: The input is first "diffused" by adding Gaussian noise for `t_diff` steps, aligning it with the whitened noise space the canonical denoiser understands.

2. **Denoising Step**: The diffused sample is then iteratively cleaned using the canonical denoiser, which operates as a learned score function similar to diffusion-based generative models.

This strategy provides remarkable robustness to unseen noise patterns by projecting them into a distribution the denoiser was trained to handle. The hyperparameter `t_diff` controls the balance between generalization capability and fidelity to the original input.

**Theoretical Foundation**: The authors provide theoretical justification (detailed in their appendix) showing that this approach maintains the naturalness of intermediate denoising results while progressively improving quality.

### 5. Progressive Multi-Stage Denoising

Recognizing the complexity and heterogeneity of interaction noise, GeneOH Diffusion employs a three-stage progressive denoising strategy that leverages the modularity of the GeneOH representation:

**Stage 1 - MotionDiff**: Focuses on denoising the canonicalized hand trajectory, addressing issues with unnatural hand poses and basic motion artifacts.

**Stage 2 - SpatialDiff**: Refines hand-object spatial relations by cleaning spatial offset information and updating hand trajectories to resolve penetrations and improve spatial consistency.

**Stage 3 - TemporalDiff**: Addresses temporal relations by ensuring hand-object motions are consistent over time, resolving issues where hand movements don't properly align with object dynamics.

Each stage builds upon the previous one while maintaining the quality improvements achieved in earlier stages. The final step involves fitting MANO parameters to the refined hand keypoints to generate realistic 3D hand mesh sequences.

### 6. Experimental Results and Performance

The experimental evaluation demonstrates GeneOH Diffusion's superior performance across multiple challenging benchmarks:

**Benchmark Testing**: The method was evaluated on four diverse datasets:
- GRAB (novel objects)
- GRAB (Beta) (synthetic noise patterns)
- HOI4D (real-world depth sensing noise, articulated objects)
- ARCTIC (dynamic bimanual interactions)

**Quantitative Performance**: GeneOH Diffusion consistently outperformed baseline methods including TOCH and its variants across all metrics (MPJPE, MPVPE, C-IoU, IV, Penetration Depth, Proximity Error, HO Motion Consistency). Notably, when trained only on GRAB data, it generalized remarkably well to the significantly different HOI4D and ARCTIC datasets.

**Stochastic Denoising Capability**: The method can generate multiple plausible solutions for a single noisy input, reflecting the ill-posed nature of the denoising problem and enabling exploration of different manipulation strategies.

**Ablation Studies**: Key findings include:
- Removing contact-centric canonicalization reduces generalization performance
- Replacing the diffusion-based denoiser with standard autoencoders significantly degrades results
- Each progressive denoising stage contributes meaningfully to overall performance

### 7. Applications and Practical Impact

GeneOH Diffusion demonstrates immediate practical value across several domains:

**Video-based Hand Tracking**: Successfully refines noisy hand trajectory estimations from image sequences, serving as an effective post-processing tool for real-world tracking systems.

**Motion Retargeting**: Cleans artifacts from direct motion retargeting, resolving penetrations and improving realism in transferred motions.

**Data Generation Pipeline**: The high-quality denoised HOI data proves beneficial for downstream tasks like grasp synthesis and manipulation synthesis, facilitating creation of training datasets for other learning-based models.

**Virtual and Augmented Reality**: Enables more immersive experiences through realistic hand-object interactions with reduced visual artifacts.

**Robotics Applications**: Provides cleaner training data for dexterous manipulation tasks, potentially leading to more robust robotic behaviors.

### 8. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge several limitations that point toward future research directions:

**Object Pose Assumption**: The current method assumes accurate object pose trajectories. Real-world scenarios often involve noisy object poses as well, suggesting the need for joint hand-object refinement approaches.

**Specific Failure Cases**: The method struggles with extremely wide hand openings for large objects, sudden motion discontinuities, and very thin object geometries where subtle penetrations may persist.

**Computational Considerations**: While not explicitly discussed, the multi-stage diffusion process likely involves computational overhead that may limit real-time applications.

The success of the "denoising via diffusion" strategy suggests its potential applicability to other inverse problems beyond HOI, opening new research avenues in computer vision and machine learning. The work represents a significant step toward robust, generalizable solutions for complex interaction denoising tasks.
