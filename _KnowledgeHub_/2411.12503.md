# ManiSkill-ViTac 2025: Challenge on Manipulation Skill Learning With Vision and Tactile Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> The ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2025 introduces a multi-track competition and benchmark platform to advance contact-rich robotic manipulation by integrating vision and tactile sensing. It offers a high-fidelity simulation and standardized hardware for evaluating tactile-only, vision-tactile fusion, and novel tactile sensor designs, building on the successful participation of 18 teams in its 2024 predecessor.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems relying on vision alone lack robustness and stability in contact-rich scenarios due to insufficient precise contact information.
- A comprehensive and standardized benchmark for fairly comparing multi-modal (vision-tactile) approaches in robotic manipulation has been absent.
- Progress in robotic manipulation is hindered by a disconnect between algorithmic development and innovation in tactile sensor hardware design.

## Method

- The **ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2025** is organized into three tracks: ==Tactile Manipulation==, ==Tactile-Vision Fusion Manipulation==, and ==Sensor Structure Design==, each with specific tasks and evaluation criteria.
- A high-fidelity simulation environment based on ==SAPIEN==, utilizing ==IPC== for robust physics and ==FEM== for tactile sensor deformation, is provided alongside a standardized real-world hardware platform (GelSight Mini, RealSense D415, Robotiq Hand-E).
- Observation spaces encompassing relative motion, ==marker flow==, and visual data, along with defined action spaces and a baseline ==TD3== policy with task-specific reward functions, are supplied to participants.

## Results

- The ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2024 successfully attracted **18 global teams**, validating the challenge format's effectiveness in stimulating research in vision-tactile manipulation.
- A comprehensive and standardized platform for multi-modal manipulation research has been established, featuring a robust simulation environment and consistent real-world hardware.
- The challenge introduces a novel 'Sensor Structure Design' track, explicitly integrating hardware innovation for tactile sensor silicone components into the manipulation challenge framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving robust and precise contact-rich robotic manipulation requires the effective integration of both global visual context and localized tactile feedback.
> - Advancements in tactile manipulation are not solely dependent on algorithmic improvements but also on the physical design and engineering of the tactile sensors themselves.
> - High-fidelity simulation environments with minimal sim-to-real gaps are critical for accelerating research and democratizing access to complex robotic manipulation challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a foundational area of robotics and embodied AI, witnessing rapid advancements largely due to breakthroughs in reinforcement learning, computer vision, and physics simulation. However, a significant gap remains in achieving robust and stable performance in real-world, contact-rich scenarios. This limitation is often attributed to the reliance on vision-only sensing, which struggles to detect precise contact status, especially under occlusion or in environments lacking distinct visual features.

The broader research landscape for robotic manipulation has seen the establishment of numerous vision-based benchmarks and challenges, such as UniTeam, HomeRobot, ARNOLD, and the earlier ManiSkill series. While these platforms have pushed the capabilities of vision-based systems in tasks like object detection, tracking, and grasping, they inherently face limitations when fine-grained contact information is critical.

Tactile sensing has emerged as a crucial complementary modality to address these shortcomings. It excels at detecting subtle contact information, such as slippage, and provides rich data about object properties and interaction forces. However, tactile-only systems suffer from limited spatial awareness, making generalization across diverse objects and environments challenging. The integration of vision (for distal cues and global context) with tactile sensing (for localized contact information) is widely recognized as a promising path toward more capable and robust manipulation.

Despite this growing interest in vision-tactile fusion, the paper highlights a significant gap: the absence of a comprehensive benchmark or open challenge that fairly compares different multi-modal approaches. Existing benchmarks primarily focus on visual input, leaving researchers without a standardized platform to evaluate the true potential of integrating vision and touch. The ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2025 directly addresses this unmet need. By providing a unified platform for researchers to explore and evaluate tactile-vision fusion, it aims to establish a new standard for assessing multi-modal approaches in robotic manipulation, thereby accelerating progress in a critical area of robotics. Furthermore, the novel inclusion of a "Sensor Structure Design" track differentiates this challenge by directly engaging with hardware innovation, acknowledging that the physical properties of tactile sensors are intrinsically linked to manipulation performance. This holistic approach bridges algorithm development with sensor engineering, a unique contribution to the existing landscape of robotics challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2025 is to **push the boundaries of contact-rich manipulation skill learning by leveraging both tactile and visual sensing**. This primary goal is broken down into several specific objectives and motivations:

- **Address Limitations of Vision-Only Systems:** A core motivation is to overcome the inherent fragility and lack of robustness of vision-only manipulation systems in scenarios demanding precise contact information, such as intricate assembly or handling of deformable objects. By integrating tactile data, robots can better understand and react to physical interactions.
- **Promote Tactile-Only Manipulation:** Track 1 aims to develop robust policies for scenarios where visual feedback is unreliable or unavailable (e.g., dark, occluded spaces). This encourages foundational research into how tactile information alone can enable reliable contact-rich strategies, providing crucial insights even for fusion approaches.
- **Advance Tactile-Vision Fusion Manipulation:** Track 2 is motivated by the desire to combine the strengths of both modalities. Tactile data provides localized contact details, while vision offers distal cues and global spatial awareness. The objective is to foster the development of algorithms that effectively integrate these diverse data streams to enhance task execution in more complex scenarios, such as selecting the correct hole from multiple options.
- **Innovate Tactile Sensor Design:** A novel objective of Track 3 is to encourage participants to develop more effective physical designs for the silicone components of tactile sensors. Recognizing that sensor structure heavily influences the quality and quantity of information obtained, this track directly links hardware design to manipulation performance, aiming to stimulate innovation in an often overlooked aspect of robotic perception.
- **Establish Standardized Evaluation:** The challenge seeks to provide a fair and consistent platform for comparing different algorithms. This includes standardized metrics, an identical hardware platform for real-world evaluation, and a closely matched simulation environment to facilitate development and minimize the sim-to-real gap. This standardization is critical for reproducible research and benchmarking progress in the field.
- **Facilitate Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A significant motivation is to enable researchers without extensive hardware access to contribute meaningfully. By offering a high-fidelity simulation environment with a small sim-to-real gap, participants can develop and refine their algorithms in simulation, with the expectation that these solutions will transfer effectively to real robots.
- **Foster Collaboration and Community Growth:** By bringing together experts from tactile sensing, computer vision, and policy learning, the challenge aims to foster interdisciplinary collaboration, accelerate the development of advanced manipulation techniques, and expand the embodied AI community.

In essence, the challenge is motivated by the recognized need for more intelligent and robust robotic manipulation that can truly interact with the physical world, moving beyond the limitations of single-modality sensing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2025 employs a multi-faceted approach centered around a well-defined experimental platform (both simulated and real) and three distinct competition tracks.

**A. Challenge Structure and Tracks:**
The challenge is organized into three independent tracks, each with its own specific focus and evaluation process:
1. **Track 1: Tactile Manipulation:** Focuses on tactile-only manipulation tasks, specifically "Peg Insertion" and "Lock Opening." The goal is to develop robust policies using solely tactile feedback for scenarios where visual information is limited.
2. **Track 2: Tactile-Vision Fusion Manipulation:** This track involves a "Peg Insertion" task where the robot must also visually identify the correct target hole from multiple options. It requires the integration of both tactile and visual data to enhance task execution.
3. **Track 3: Sensor Structure Design:** Participants are challenged to design the physical structure of the tactile sensor's silicone component to optimize performance in a "Peg Insertion" task, similar to Track 1. This track encourages innovation in hardware design.

All tracks involve two stages (Stage 1 and Stage 2 for Tracks 1 and 2, Stage 1 only for Track 3) culminating in winner selection and awards at the ICRA 2025 ViTac Workshop.

**B. Experimental Platform:**
The challenge provides both a standardized real-world hardware platform and a high-fidelity simulation environment designed for close sim-to-real transfer.

- **Hardware Platform:**
 - **Sensing:** Dual GelSight Mini sensors for tactile feedback, an SRI M3813A 6DoF F/T Sensor for force/torque measurements, and an Intel RealSense Depth Camera D415 for visual input.
 - **Manipulation:** A Robotiq Hand-E parallel gripper mounted on a 3-axis translation stage and a rotary stage, providing precise 4-degrees-of-freedom (x, y, z translation, z-axis rotation) movement for manipulation tasks.
 - **Data Acquisition:** Utilizes the Robot Operating System (ROS) for real-time data acquisition from GelSight Mini (marker tracking, contact detection) and Intel RealSense (aligned color and depth images, point clouds derived from depth). The Segment Anything Model (SAM) is employed for segmenting objects of interest (e.g., pegs, holes) from visual data.

- **Simulation Platform:**
 - **Engine:** Based on SAPIEN, a simulated part-based interactive environment, and utilizes Incremental Potential Contact (IPC) for robust and stable physics simulation, especially crucial for contact-rich interactions and large deformations.
 - **Tactile Sensor Simulation:** The elastomer of the GelSight Mini is discretized using the Finite Element Method (FEM). IPC accurately simulates the dynamic and hyperelastic behavior of the elastomer, ensuring intersection-free and inversion-free simulations vital for stable reinforcement learning training. Marker positions are calculated from FEM vertex displacements using barycentric weights and transformed to the camera frame.
 - **Depth Sensor Simulation:** Employs Zhang's active stereo sensor simulation method, mimicking the Intel RealSense D415. It offers three modes: ground truth, rasterization rendering, and ray-tracing rendering (for higher fidelity IR images) to simulate depth map calculation.
 - **Action Simulation:** Robot actions (linear and angular velocities) are applied as Dirichlet boundary conditions to the constrained surfaces of the simulated elastomer. The total action is divided into multiple substeps to maintain simulation stability and accuracy, calculating velocities for each substep based on maximum allowable limits.

**C. Observation and Action Spaces:**

- **Track 1 & 3 (Tactile-only / Sensor Design):**
 - **Observation:** Relative Motion (sensor's offset and rotation from initial pose) and Marker Flow (initial and current marker points on the tactile sensor surface, processed for noise and tracking losses).
 - **Action (Peg Insertion):** 3D action vector [$\delta_x, \delta_y, \delta_\theta$] (displacement in x, y, and rotation around z-axis relative to the object's local frame).
 - **Action (Lock Opening):** 3D action vector [$\delta_x, \delta_y, \delta_z$] (displacement in x, y, z).
 - Actions undergo clipping and conversion to global coordinates, then are divided into substeps for stable simulation.

- **Track 2 (Tactile-Vision Fusion):**
 - **Observation:** Expands on Track 1 with Relative Motion, Marker Flow, and adds visual information: RGB Picture, Depth Picture, and Point Cloud data (from the depth sensor). Participants can choose which visual modalities to use.
 - **Action:** 4D action vector [$\delta_x, \delta_y, \delta_z, \delta_\theta$] (displacements in x, y, z, and rotation around z-axis relative to the peg). Similar action clipping and substep execution logic.

**D. Reference Method (Baseline Policy):**
For policy learning, the challenge provides a reference method based on the Twin-Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) algorithm.
- **Network Architecture:** A Shared Weight Encoder Network processes marker flow from dual tactile sensors to extract k-dimensional feature vectors. These tactile features are combined with relative motion (and visual features in Track 2) as input to an MLP actor network, which outputs the action vector.
- **Critic Network:** Evaluates action Q-values using task-specific ground truth offsets (e.g., between peg and hole, key teeth and pin positions).
- **Reward Functions:** Task-specific reward functions are defined, typically composed of:
 - Decrease in error ($e_{t-1} - e_t$).
 - Constant penalty for each step.
 - Final success reward.
 - Penalty for exceeding allowable error or spatial boundaries.
 - Track 1 Lock Opening also includes a penalty for surface point deviation.
 - Track 2 incorporates a similar structure, with error calculation including z and $\theta$ components.

**E. Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Tracks 1 & 2:** Success is determined by multiple criteria including: an "excessive error check" (peg/key position/orientation within boundaries), a "step limit" (for efficiency), "depth and z-axis translation" (achieving minimum insertion depth), and "surface point consistency" (mean Euclidean distance between current and initial marker points below a threshold, indicating stable contact/alignment).
- **Track 3:** Combines the performance metrics of Track 1 with an evaluation of the innovativeness of the sensor design itself, focusing on both the structural design of the silicone component and the marker distribution. This unique metric encourages creative engineering solutions.

The methodology emphasizes a rigorous, standardized, and multi-modal approach to tackle the complexities of contact-rich manipulation, fostering both algorithmic and hardware innovations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

It is important to clarify that this paper serves as an **introduction and call for participation** for the ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2025, rather than a report detailing novel research findings from the challenge itself. Therefore, the "findings and results" are primarily about the **design, rationale, and anticipated impact of the challenge platform**, as well as the **outcomes of its predecessor**.

The key "results" presented in this paper are:

- **Successful Precedent for Challenge Format:** The paper highlights the success of the ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2024, which attracted 18 teams globally and featured presentations at the ICRA 2024 ViTac Workshop. This demonstrates the viability and appeal of such a challenge format in stimulating research in vision-tactile fusion. The positive reception and active participation in the previous iteration provide empirical evidence for the need and impact of such a benchmarking initiative.
- **Establishment of a Comprehensive Multi-Modal Benchmark:** The paper effectively demonstrates the successful design and setup of a comprehensive platform for vision-tactile manipulation. This includes:
 - **High-Fidelity Simulation Environment:** The detailed description of the SAPIEN-based simulation, incorporating IPC for robust contact physics, FEM for tactile sensor deformation, and advanced depth sensor simulation (Zhang's method with ground truth/rasterization/ray-tracing modes), indicates a significant achievement in creating a realistic and stable virtual testing ground.
 - **Standardized Hardware Platform:** The specification of a consistent real-world setup (GelSight Mini, RealSense D415, Robotiq Hand-E, etc.) ensures that real-world evaluations are fair and comparable across participants.
 - **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer Capabilities:** The explicit emphasis on a "small sim-to-real gap" and the detailed explanation of how actions are handled in both simulation and reality (e.g., action clipping, substeps, consistent coordinate systems) suggests that the underlying systems have been carefully engineered to facilitate practical transfer of learned policies.
- **Definition of Challenging and Relevant Tasks:** The design of tasks like "Peg Insertion" (with tactile-only and vision-tactile variants) and "Lock Opening" reflects a deep understanding of the complexities of contact-rich manipulation. These tasks are chosen to specifically highlight the advantages of multi-modal sensing and present clear evaluation criteria.
- **Introduction of a Novel Sensor Design Track:** The creation of Track 3 is a significant development. It addresses a critical, yet often separated, aspect of robotics: hardware innovation. By integrating sensor structure design directly into a manipulation challenge, the organizers implicitly "find" that progress in tactile manipulation is not solely algorithmic but also heavily dependent on the physical design of the sensors.
- **Development of Baseline Methodologies:** The provision of a reference method based on the TD3 algorithm, along with detailed policy networks and reward functions for each track, serves as a crucial baseline. While not a research finding itself, it demonstrates the feasibility of applying contemporary reinforcement learning techniques to these tasks and provides a starting point for participants, ensuring a level playing field for competition.

In summary, the "results" of this paper are the successful conceptualization, detailed architectural design, and robust implementation plan for a critical new challenge in robotics, building on a proven track record. It lays the groundwork for future research findings that will emerge from participant contributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2025 carries substantial significance and potential impact across both academic research and industrial applications in robotics and embodied AI.

**A. Advancing Robotics Research:**

- **Accelerating Multi-Modal Sensing Integration:** The challenge acts as a powerful catalyst for researchers to develop novel algorithms that effectively integrate vision and tactile data. By providing a standardized platform and diverse tasks, it encourages innovation in sensor fusion, representation learning, and control strategies for contact-rich interactions, pushing beyond the limitations of single-modality systems.
- **Standardization and Benchmarking:** The creation of a unified benchmark with consistent evaluation metrics, spanning both simulation and real-world environments, is crucial for scientific progress. It allows for fair comparison of different approaches, fosters reproducible research, and helps identify the most promising directions for future development in vision-tactile manipulation. This addresses a significant gap identified in the current research landscape.
- **Stimulating Sensor Design Innovation:** The unique "Sensor Structure Design" track is a pioneering step in integrating hardware development directly into a manipulation challenge. This will accelerate the development of improved, task-specific tactile sensor designs, emphasizing the critical interplay between physical sensor properties and algorithmic performance. This could lead to breakthroughs in fundamental sensor technology.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The emphasis on a high-fidelity simulation environment with robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers without access to expensive and complex robotic hardware. This democratization of research resources will broaden participation, accelerate algorithm development cycles, and promote practical applicability of learned policies.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** By explicitly inviting experts from computer vision, tactile sensing, and policy learning, the challenge promotes cross-pollination of ideas and methodologies. This interdisciplinary approach is essential for tackling the complex challenges inherent in embodied AI.

**B. Impact on Industry and Real-World Applications:**

- **Enhanced Robotic Robustness and Dexterity:** Solutions emerging from this challenge will lead to robots capable of performing contact-rich tasks with greater robustness, precision, and stability. This is critical for applications requiring delicate manipulation, precise assembly, or handling of deformable/fragile objects in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and service robotics.
- **Automation in Challenging Environments:** The focus on tactile-only manipulation for occluded or visually ambiguous environments (Track 1) directly benefits industrial scenarios where vision might be impaired, such as in dark factories, underwater inspection, or tasks involving highly reflective surfaces.
- **Faster Deployment of Advanced Capabilities:** By accelerating research and providing validated solutions, the challenge will shorten the timeline for translating cutting-edge manipulation capabilities from laboratories to real-world industrial deployment, making advanced robotics more accessible and effective.
- **Development of Next-Generation Grippers and Tools:** Insights from the sensor design track could directly influence the development of smarter, more capable robotic grippers and manipulation tools that are intrinsically designed to provide rich tactile feedback, improving overall system performance.

**C. Future Directions:**

The challenge also outlines future directions that will amplify its long-term impact, including expanding task complexity to multi-stage assembly and dexterous manipulation, incorporating dynamic object interactions and deformable materials, and further developing transfer learning strategies. These ambitions highlight a roadmap for sustained innovation driven by the ManiSkill-ViTac platform.

In conclusion, the ManiSkill-ViTac Challenge 2025 is more than just a competition; it is a strategically designed scientific endeavor aimed at addressing fundamental limitations in robotic manipulation. By providing a unique, standardized, and multi-modal benchmarking platform, it is poised to significantly advance both the theoretical understanding and practical capabilities of robots interacting with the physical world through both vision and touch.
