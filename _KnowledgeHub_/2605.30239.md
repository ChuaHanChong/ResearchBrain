# CA-World: Multi-Object Counterfactual Alignment for Efficient Interactive-Ready Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> SAM3D-Phys is a training-free pipeline that turns multi-view reconstructions of real scenes into physically simulatable multi-object worlds. PGSR reconstruction plus LaMa inpainting yields a clean background; SAM3D generative 3D priors complete occluded object geometry; render-and-compare pose fitting with physics constraints and mask-guided appearance distillation restore each object in place, raising in-mask PSNR from 23.92 to 30.04 before MPM simulation.

## Problem

- Multi-view reconstruction yields visually accurate scenes, but occlusion and low pixel coverage leave objects with holes and irregular geometry unusable for stable physics simulation.
- Prior physics-reconstruction methods assume simple scenes, planar foreground-background contact, or single objects, and cannot disentangle multiple mutually occluding objects in cluttered real captures.
- Generative 3D models supply shape priors but output objects detached from scene geometry, appearance, and 6DoF placement, so physically meaningful in-scene interaction is lost.

## Method

- Scene stage uses ==PGSR== planar Gaussian splatting with ==SAM 2== segmentation-affinity features, then removes selected objects, inpaints holes with ==LaMa==, and fine-tunes a clean background scene.
- Object-scene spatial alignment runs render-and-compare pose optimization on ==SSIM== and ==MS-SSIM==, initialized from depth-derived pointmaps, plus physics-constrained refinement with ground-contact and object-object non-penetration losses over an LMM-inferred relation graph.
- Mask-based appearance distillation applies a patch-level ==VGG== feature loss inside the overlap of ground-truth and rendered object masks; aligned objects are then simulated by a modified ==MPM== solver.

## Results

- Ablation shows removing appearance distillation drops in-mask PSNR from **30.04** to **23.92** and SSIM from **0.9537** to **0.8693**; spatial alignment lowers Sobel edge error from **1.086** to **1.075**.
- A **12**-participant user study scored motion realism **41** for the method versus **28** for DecoupledGaussian and **16** for Feature Splatting; visual quality scored **42** versus **37** and **15**.
- LMM-as-judge scoring with Gemini 3.1 Pro agreed, giving **43** versus **20** and **10** on motion realism and **33** versus **20** and **15** on visual quality across **six** real scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative shape priors and multi-view reconstruction are complementary: priors fill occluded geometry, while reconstruction supplies metric depth and appearance that anchor generated objects in place.
> - Per-object generation ignores inter-object constraints, so explicit contact and non-penetration losses are required; photometric render-and-compare alignment alone still leaves visible interpenetration.
> - Training-free post-processing on a consumer GPU can make reconstructions simulation-ready, but specular highlights, shadows, and context-poor regions still break appearance consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of 3D scene reconstruction with physics-based simulation for interactive environments is an active area of research with applications in virtual reality, robotic manipulation, interactive video generation, and embodied intelligence. Recent advancements in neural rendering and Gaussian splatting have enabled the reconstruction of visually detailed real-world scenes from multi-view images, supporting tasks such as novel view synthesis and basic scene understanding.

However, a persistent challenge in this field is the reliable enabling of physics-based interaction within these reconstructed scenes, particularly in real-world environments that often contain multiple interacting objects. The primary difficulty stems from the fact that objects reconstructed from real-world observations frequently exhibit incomplete or partial geometry due to occlusions and limited viewpoints. Such geometric inaccuracies, while potentially acceptable for visual rendering, pose significant problems for physics simulation, which typically requires coherent and complete object representations to maintain stability and physical plausibility, especially when multiple objects interact.

Existing approaches attempt to bridge this gap by representing scene elements as simulation particles or incorporating physical priors into reconstruction. However, these methods often address simpler scenes or focus on single-object interactions. In complex, cluttered real-world scenarios with multiple objects and mutual occlusions, reconstruction errors can accumulate, compromising both object geometry and the stability of physical interactions.

Generative 3D models, which encode shape priors learned from large-scale datasets, offer a potential solution for inferring complete object structures from partial observations. Nevertheless, integrating these generated objects back into reconstructed scenes introduces new challenges related to maintaining consistency with the observed scene's geometry, appearance, and spatial configuration. This work positions itself within this landscape by exploring how generative 3D priors can be leveraged to complement scene reconstruction, specifically to address the issue of incomplete object geometry and enable physically consistent multi-object interactive simulation in real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of recovering complete, physically simulatable object geometry from reconstructed real-world scenes, thereby enabling stable physics-based interaction with multiple objects embedded within these scenes.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations and requirements in existing methods:

- **Incomplete Object Geometry from Reconstruction:** Modern multi-view reconstruction techniques, while visually accurate for scene rendering, often produce incomplete or irregular object geometries. This incompleteness is typically caused by occlusions, limited camera viewpoints, or objects occupying only a small number of pixels in an image. Such fragmented or partial representations are unsuitable for physics engines, which demand watertight and coherent geometries for stable and plausible simulation.
- **Challenges in Multi-Object Interaction:** The problem is exacerbated in real-world scenes containing multiple interacting objects. Geometric inaccuracies can propagate and compound through object contacts and interactions during simulation, leading to unstable or physically implausible dynamics, such as objects interpenetrating, floating unnaturally, or exhibiting erratic behavior.
- **Limitations of Existing Simulation-Reconstruction Bridges:** Previous methods that attempt to combine reconstruction and physics simulation often make simplifying assumptions, such as focusing on single-object interactions or scenes with less clutter. These approaches are insufficient for the complexity of real-world multi-object scenarios where mutual occlusions and intricate contacts are common.
- **Underutilization of Generative 3D Priors:** Generative 3D models have demonstrated the capacity to infer plausible object structures from partial observations by leveraging learned shape priors. However, a significant challenge lies in effectively integrating these generated, complete objects back into a reconstructed scene while ensuring consistency in terms of spatial placement, observed geometry, and visual appearance. Without such consistency, the simulated interactions would not be physically meaningful within the context of the original scene.

Therefore, the key objective is to address these limitations by proposing a training-free framework that integrates scene reconstruction with generative 3D priors to:
1. Recover complete 3D object geometry from partial observations.
2. Restore these recovered objects to their scene-consistent states, including accurate spatial pose and visual appearance.
3. Facilitate physically plausible and stable multi-object interactive simulation within the reconstructed real-world environment.
This approach aims to bridge the gap between visually accurate scene reconstruction and the geometric fidelity required for reliable physics simulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SAM3D-Phys framework is a training-free pipeline designed to enable multi-object interactive simulation in reconstructed real-world scenes. It integrates scene reconstruction with generative 3D priors and employs specific alignment strategies to ensure scene consistency. The methodology is structured into four main stages: Scene Reconstruction, Object Extraction, Object-Scene Alignment, and Multi-Object Physical Interaction.

**Preliminaries:**
- **3D Scene Representation:** The framework utilizes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to represent scenes as point clouds. Each Gaussian is defined by its centroid (x), covariance matrix ($\Sigma$), spherical harmonic coefficients (C), and opacity ($\alpha$). This representation allows for novel view rendering through differentiable splatting and $\alpha$-blending.
- **Material Point Method (MPM):** For physical simulation, the Material Point Method is employed. MPM is a hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian approach that represents continuous materials as particles, each carrying state variables like position, velocity, and deformation gradient. These particle attributes are mapped to an auxiliary Eulerian grid, where momentum conservation is enforced, and then mapped back to update particle states, ensuring mass conservation and realistic deformation.
- **Planar-based Gaussian Splatting (PGSR):** To ensure accurate geometry, especially for depth estimation, the method adopts PGSR. PGSR enhances vanilla 3DGS by enforcing a planar constraint on scaling factors, compressing Gaussians into 2D surfels, and calculating exact intersection depths between viewing rays and Gaussian planes. This addresses geometric ambiguity and provides high-fidelity depth maps.

**Framework Stages:**

1. **Scene Reconstruction (Stage A):**
 - **Initial Reconstruction:** The 3D scene is initially reconstructed from multi-view images using PGSR. Segmentation maps, generated by SAM 2, are used to augment each Gaussian with a segmentation-affinity feature, encoding its semantic association.
 - **Object Removal and Inpainting:** After initial reconstruction, user-selected target objects are removed from the scene. The resulting holes in the background are then inpainted using the LaMa model.
 - **Background Refinement:** The scene reconstruction is fine-tuned to obtain a clean and complete background scene without the target objects.

2. **Object Extraction (Stage B):**
 - **Generative Prior Application:** To address the issue of incomplete object geometry due to occlusions or limited observations, the SAM3D generative 3D prior model is leveraged. SAM3D recovers complete 3D geometry for individual objects from their partial observations extracted during the initial scene reconstruction. This process treats multi-object decoupling as a decoupled object-generation problem, ensuring that each object's full shape is inferred.

3. **Object-Scene Alignment (Stage C):**
 - This stage is crucial for ensuring that the recovered, complete objects are spatially and visually consistent with the reconstructed scene. It comprises two complementary strategies:
 - **Physics-Constrained Spatial Alignment (Section 3.3):**
 - **Render-and-Compare Refinement:** This module iteratively optimizes each object's 6DoF pose (translation and rotation). It compares rendered masked object images with corresponding ground-truth observations using SSIM and MS-SSIM as optimization objectives. Metric cues from 3D reconstruction (e.g., depth maps converted to pointmaps) are used to provide accurate 3D initialization for the generative model. Translation and rotation are updated separately.
 - **Physical Constrained Alignment Refinement:** To prevent physically implausible states such as interpenetration (between objects or with the scene background) after independent object processing, a physics-constrained refinement is introduced. A relation graph, inferred by a large multimodal model (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro), describes scene-object and object-object interactions.
 - *Object-Scene Relation Constraint:* Ensures stable contact with the ground plane (no floating or sinking) using a squared hinge loss based on the object's bottom surface points and the ground plane equation.
 - *Object-Object Non-Penetration Constraint:* Prevents interpenetration between objects by enforcing a minimum positive distance between them using a squared hinge loss based on the minimum distance between the surfaces of two objects.
 - **Mask-Based Appearance Distillation (Section 3.4):**
 - **Addressing Appearance Inconsistencies:** Generative 3D models might produce objects with "cartoon-like" artifacts or visual inconsistencies when integrated into a real scene.
 - **Refinement:** This module refines object textures. For each view, it computes the overlap between the ground-truth mask and the rendered object mask. A patch-level VGG feature loss is then applied to the colors within this overlapping region. This leverages 3DGS's property that local supervision can optimize particle attributes over a broader spatial extent, preventing hard boundaries. This process is applied as a post-processing step and is training-free.

4. **Multi-Object Physical Interaction (Stage D):**
 - **Reintegration:** Once aligned and refined, the complete and consistent object representations are reintegrated into the reconstructed scene.
 - **Simulation:** Their dynamic interactions are simulated using a modified Material Point Method (MPM) algorithm. Users can define interactive forces, such as impulse forces or force fields, to manipulate objects. The simulation is designed to run efficiently on consumer-grade GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SAM3D-Phys was conducted on a benchmark comprising both self-captured and web-collected real-world multi-object scenes, including the "bear" and "room" scenes from DecoupledGaussian, and four newly captured scenes (Outdoor, Flower Bed, Billiard Table, Dorm). Spatial alignment was evaluated using edge error (Sobel edge error between rendered and ground-truth images), while appearance alignment was assessed using average PSNR and SSIM within object-mask regions. Baselines for comparison included Feature Splatting and DecoupledGaussian, with O2Recon and Amodal3R excluded due to reported poor performance on real-world data.

**Key Findings:**

- **Decoupled Object Recovery:**
 - SAM3D-Phys demonstrated more accurate and complete object decoupling and restoration compared to baseline methods.
 - Feature Splatting often failed to cleanly separate objects from the background, resulting in fragmented object geometry and disrupted scene structure.
 - DecoupledGaussian could separate foreground from background but was unable to disentangle individual objects within the foreground, for example, distinguishing two slippers as separate entities.
 - The SAM3D-based image-to-3D module facilitated the recovery of complete 3D shapes for multiple objects from various viewpoints, addressing the geometric incompleteness inherent in initial scene reconstruction.

- **Multi-Object Spatial Alignment:**
 - **Render-and-Compare Refinement with Scene Metric:** The iterative render-and-compare optimization, guided by SSIM/MS-SSIM and initialized with depth-map-derived pointmaps, progressively aligned rendered object masks with ground-truth observations. This process led to accurate object initialization and convergence to correct spatial placements within the scene. Quantitative evaluation showed that the spatial alignment module contributed to a reduction in edge error, indicating improved object pose accuracy (Table 1).
 - **Physical Constrained Alignment Refinement:** This module effectively resolved inter-object and object-scene interpenetration. For instance, in an example with two slippers, initial render-and-compare alignment still showed interpenetration, which was subsequently corrected by applying physics-constrained refinement. This ensured physically plausible distances and relations between objects, preventing simulation instability.

- **Object-Scene Appearance Alignment:**
 - The mask-based appearance distillation module significantly improved the visual realism and consistency of the extracted objects. Prior to this module, objects often exhibited "cartoon-like" artifacts. After distillation, textures were refined, resulting in a closer visual fidelity to the original in-scene objects.
 - Quantitative metrics (PSNR and SSIM) demonstrated a substantial improvement when appearance distillation was employed, confirming its contribution to visual quality (Table 1).

- **Interactive Simulation and Applications:**
 - The framework successfully enabled stable, realistic, and physically consistent multi-object interactions within reconstructed real-world scenes, supported by the modified Material Point Method (MPM) solver.
 - The accurate geometry recovery and alignment also facilitated dynamic scene editing, such as the removal or modification of objects, with subsequent physically plausible simulations. This capability supports downstream tasks like interactive video generation.

- **User Study and LMM-as-Judge Evaluation (Supplementary Material):**
 - A user study and an LMM-as-Judge evaluation (using Gemini 3.1 Pro) were conducted to assess motion realism and visual quality of the generated videos.
 - SAM3D-Phys significantly outperformed Feature Splatting and DecoupledGaussian in both metrics. Feature Splatting suffered from segmentation failures, tearing, and blur artifacts, while DecoupledGaussian struggled with disentangling individual foreground objects and was limited by assumptions of planar contact surfaces. The LMM-as-Judge results were reported to align closely with human judgments, further validating the approach's effectiveness.

Overall, the findings indicate that SAM3D-Phys successfully integrates generative 3D priors with scene reconstruction to overcome limitations in object completeness, achieving high fidelity in both spatial and appearance alignment, thereby enabling stable and realistic physics-based interactions in complex multi-object real-world scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, SAM3D-Phys, that addresses a critical limitation in the domain of 3D scene reconstruction and interactive simulation: the recovery of complete, physically simulatable object geometry from partially observed real-world scenes. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

- **Enabling Robust Physics-Based Interaction:** The primary contribution is providing a robust method for enabling reliable physics-based interaction in complex, multi-object real-world scenes. By generating complete object geometries from incomplete observations and ensuring their precise alignment within the reconstructed scene, SAM3D-Phys overcomes a major hurdle that often renders visually accurate reconstructions unsuitable for physical simulation. This capability is fundamental for a wide range of applications requiring objects to behave realistically.
- **Advancement in Generative 3D Prior Integration:** The work demonstrates an effective strategy for integrating generative 3D priors (specifically SAM3D) into a scene reconstruction pipeline. This integration allows for the inference of entire object shapes, even when severely occluded, a capability that traditional reconstruction methods struggle to achieve. The framework's training-free nature makes it efficient and adaptable without requiring extensive retraining for new scenes or objects.
- **Enhanced Scene Consistency:** The introduction of physics-constrained spatial optimization and mask-guided appearance distillation modules is significant. These modules are crucial for re-establishing scene consistency, ensuring that recovered objects are not only geometrically complete but also correctly positioned and visually harmonious with their original environment. This dual alignment process contributes to the overall plausibility and realism of the interactive experience.
- **Broadening Application Scope:** The capabilities demonstrated by SAM3D-Phys have a wide array of potential impacts across various fields:
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Enables more immersive and interactive VR/AR experiences where users can physically interact with virtualized real-world objects in a plausible manner.
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** Provides robots with more accurate and complete 3D models of objects in their environment, which is essential for planning robust and collision-free manipulation tasks in cluttered real-world settings.
 - **Interactive Video Generation and Dynamic Scene Editing:** Facilitates the creation of interactive content by allowing users to manipulate objects within existing videos or reconstructed scenes, leading to new forms of content creation and storytelling.
 - **World Modeling for Embodied Intelligence:** Contributes to the development of more sophisticated world models for embodied AI agents, allowing them to understand and interact with their environment based on physical principles.
- **Practicality and Accessibility:** The framework's ability to run efficiently on consumer-grade hardware (e.g., NVIDIA RTX 4090) enhances its practical applicability and accessibility to researchers and developers without requiring specialized, high-end computing infrastructure.

While the paper acknowledges limitations concerning appearance inconsistencies under varied illumination and reduced performance with limited contextual cues, the core methodology represents a substantial step towards bridging the gap between passive 3D scene reconstruction and active, physics-grounded interaction in complex real-world environments.
