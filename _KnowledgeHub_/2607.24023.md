# Self-Supervised Consistency Enhanced Disentangled Learning for Neural Decoding Generalization in Brain-Machine Interface

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Self-Supervised Consistency enhanced Disentangled Learning (SSCDL) is introduced to improve the long-term generalization of neural decoders in Brain-Machine Interfaces. It learns stable neural representations through a self-supervised consistency mechanism and by disentangling motor parameters, achieving superior decoding accuracy and robustness across different days and tasks without requiring recalibration.

## Problem

- Neural decoding performance in Brain-Machine Interfaces (BMIs) degrades significantly over time due to "neural drift," making decoders trained on one day less effective on subsequent days.
- Existing generalization methods struggle to learn neural representations that are robust and invariant to day-to-day variations using only single-day training data.
- Current decoders often treat kinematic variables (e.g., velocity) as a unified target, neglecting that neural drift may manifest differently across specific motor components like movement direction and speed.

## Method

- The **Self-Supervised Consistency enhanced Disentangled Learning (SSCDL)** framework is proposed, comprising a ==Consistency enhanced Neural Decoder (CND)== and a ==Complementary-Disentangled Generalization (CDG)== mechanism.
- CND utilizes a ==teacher-student architecture== with ==Neural Signal Masking== and a teacher-student consistency loss to learn robust neural representations invariant to input perturbations and cross-day variability.
- CDG explicitly disentangles velocity decoding into overall velocity, movement direction, and speed, training specialized models for each component and then combining their predictions via a complementary ensemble strategy.

## Results

- SSCDL demonstrated superior or highly competitive decoding performance (higher CC and R²) across three NHP datasets, diverse motor tasks, and long temporal spans (up to **three months**), outperforming existing generalization-based methods.
- The framework surpassed the performance of a recalibration-based method (WDGRL) without requiring target-domain unlabeled data, highlighting its efficiency and robustness to neural drift.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of the CND and CDG modules, with their synergistic integration in SSCDL leading to the best overall performance and lower Speed Error (SE) and Angle Error (AE) for fine-grained control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning with consistency regularization and simulated neural signal masking can effectively foster robust, drift-invariant neural representations from single-day training data.
> - Decomposing complex motor parameters into biologically inspired components (direction and speed) and handling them separately improves decoding generalization by addressing parameter-specific neural drift.
> - A complementary ensemble combining predictions from holistic velocity and disentangled direction and speed models yields more accurate and stable neural decoding for long-term BMI applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Brain-Machine Interfaces (BMIs) represent a field focused on establishing direct communication pathways between the brain and external devices. These interfaces hold potential for applications in rehabilitation, human motor augmentation, and human-centered robotics by enabling individuals to control assistive and robotic technologies directly through neural activity. Invasive BMIs (iBMIs), which utilize high-quality neural recordings from motor cortical areas, are particularly significant both as assistive tools and as platforms for neuroscience research into motor control mechanisms.

A critical challenge in the long-term viability and widespread adoption of BMIs is the degradation of decoding performance over time, primarily due to "neural drift." This phenomenon, influenced by factors such as electrode movement, neuronal changes, and synaptic plasticity, renders decoders trained on one day less effective on subsequent days.

Existing solutions to address neural drift broadly fall into two categories:
- **Recalibration-based methods:** These approaches involve retraining decoders periodically using newly collected labeled or unlabeled data. While effective in maintaining performance, they are often impractical for real-world applications due to the time and cost associated with acquiring fresh data. Examples include methods utilizing Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) such as ADAN, UAN, DA-DCF, WDGRL, and SeSA.
- **Generalization-based methods:** These methods aim to achieve cross-day robustness without requiring decoder retraining, making them more suitable for practical, long-term BMI deployment. Prior work in this area includes models like KRSRL and EvoEnsemble, which address short-term neural changes, SABLE and CAPTIVATE, which employ adversarial feature alignment or neuron-based latent synchronization, MRNN, which models long-term nonlinear mappings, and LFDA, which uses data augmentation and contrastive learning.

Despite advancements in generalization-based methods, two main limitations persist. First, it remains challenging to learn neural representations that are stable over long periods using only single-day training data. Second, existing methods often treat motor parameters, such as velocity, as a unified target variable, neglecting the observation that neural drift may manifest differently across specific motor components (e.g., direction and speed). This oversight can lead to suboptimal generalization, as individual neurons exhibit distinct tuning preferences for these disentangled motor parameters.

This paper positions itself within the generalization-based paradigm, specifically addressing the aforementioned limitations. It proposes a framework that seeks to learn robust, drift-invariant neural representations and explicitly accounts for the heterogeneous nature of neural drift across different motor parameters, drawing inspiration from neural preference theory and disentangled representation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust neural decoding framework that can maintain stable performance over extended periods in Brain-Machine Interfaces, particularly in the context of human-centered robotic systems, using only single-day training data. This goal is motivated by the limitations of current approaches to neural decoding generalization.

The specific objectives and underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Learning Robust Neural Representations:** A key challenge in long-term BMI operation is the instability of neural activity, known as neural drift. Current generalization-based methods struggle to extract neural representations that are robust and invariant to these day-to-day variations when trained exclusively on data from a single recording session. The authors are motivated to design a mechanism that can learn such stable representations, thereby mitigating performance degradation without requiring continuous recalibration.
- **Addressing Heterogeneous Neural Drift Across Motor Parameters:** Existing neural decoders often treat kinematic variables, such as velocity, as a monolithic target for prediction. However, neuroscientific evidence indicates that individual neurons and neural populations exhibit distinct tuning preferences for different motor parameters, such as movement direction and speed. Consequently, neural drift may affect these parameters disparately. The motivation here is to move beyond a unified velocity decoding approach and instead recognize and account for the parameter-specific nature of neural drift. This involves exploring whether disentangling these motor components can lead to more effective generalization.
- **Improving Cross-Day Generalization for Fine-Grained Control:** The ultimate aim is to enhance the ability of BMI decoders to generalize effectively across different recording days. Such generalization is crucial for reliable and intuitive long-term interaction with assistive and robotic devices, especially for applications requiring fine-grained motor control. By developing a framework that can learn stable, parameter-specific neural representations, the authors seek to enable more precise and consistent control signals over time.
- **Leveraging Self-Supervised Learning and Neural Preference Theory:** The authors are motivated to integrate recent advancements in self-supervised learning (SSL) to foster the learning of robust representations. SSL techniques have shown promise in learning from unlabeled data or by creating supervisory signals from the data itself. Additionally, the well-established principle of neural tuning preferences for specific motor parameters provides a neuroscientific basis for a disentangled learning approach. The motivation is to combine these computational and neuroscientific insights to build a more effective generalization framework.

In summary, the research aims to overcome the dual challenges of learning robust, drift-invariant neural representations and addressing the parameter-specific nature of neural drift, ultimately enabling long-term, stable, and fine-grained neural decoding for practical BMI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose Self-Supervised Consistency enhanced Disentangled Learning (SSCDL), a neural decoding generalization framework comprising two main innovations: the Consistency enhanced Neural Decoder (CND) and the Complementary-Disentangled Generalization (CDG) mechanism.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Definition

The problem is framed as a single-domain generalization task. A decoder is trained using data from a single source day ($D_s$) and must generalize effectively to unseen target days ($D_t$) without further retraining, thereby addressing performance degradation due to neural drift.

#### 3.2. B. Consistency enhanced Neural Decoder (CND)

CND serves as the backbone model for learning neural representations robust to cross-day variability. It employs a teacher-student architecture based on self-supervised learning principles.

- **Architecture:** The student network ($f_\theta$) and teacher network ($f_{\hat{\theta}}$) share the same architecture, typically a multilayer perceptron (MLP) with ReLU activations and dropout layers, followed by a linear regression head. Initially, their parameters are identical.
- **Student Network Optimization:** The student network is optimized using a combined loss function:
 - **Masked Neural Decoding Loss ($L_{MND}$):** To simulate real-world neural signal perturbations (e.g., electrode instability, channel dropout), a data augmentation strategy called Neural Signal Masking (NSM) is introduced. During training, a subset of neural signal channels (e.g., $c$ channels) in the input ($x_i$) are randomly masked to create a perturbed input ($\hat{x}_i$). The $L_{MND}$ measures the prediction error between the student's output for $\hat{x}_i$ and the ground-truth kinematic variable ($y_i$).
 - **Teacher-Student Consistency Loss ($L_{TSC}$):** This self-supervised constraint encourages the student network's prediction on the perturbed input ($\hat{x}_i$) to be consistent with the teacher network's prediction on the clean (unperturbed) input ($x_i$). This consistency loss aims to make the student network less sensitive to input perturbations and focus on stable neural structures.
 - **Combined Loss ($L_{CND}$):** The total loss for the student network is a weighted sum of $L_{MND}$ and $L_{TSC}$, controlled by a balance weight $\lambda$.
- **Teacher Network Update:** The teacher network's parameters are not updated via gradient descent. Instead, they are updated through an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student network's parameters. This EMA mechanism provides a smoothed and stable reference model, which is beneficial for noisy neural recordings and consistent representation learning. A warm-up threshold is used before EMA begins, where teacher parameters directly mirror student parameters.

#### 3.3. C. Complementary-Disentangled Generalization (CDG)

Motivated by neuroscience studies indicating that different neural populations encode distinct motor parameters (e.g., preferred direction, preferred speed), CDG aims to improve cross-day generalization by explicitly modeling these parameters separately rather than treating velocity as a unified target.

- **Disentangled Learning:** Velocity decoding is decomposed into three complementary components:
 - **Overall Velocity:** Holistic prediction of the 2D or 3D velocity vector.
 - **Direction:** Prediction of the movement direction.
 - **Speed:** Prediction of the movement speed.
 Three separate models are trained, each specializing in one of these components. Parameter-specific difference functions are defined for each component to measure prediction discrepancy:
 - $Z_{direction}$: Uses the cosine similarity (1 - cosine) weighted by the ground-truth speed magnitude to penalize angular differences.
 - $Z_{speed}$: Uses the squared difference between predicted and ground-truth speed magnitudes.
 - $Z_{velocity}$: Uses the squared Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth velocity vectors.
- **Complementary Ensemble:** After training, the predictions from the three specialized models are combined. The final velocity prediction ($\tilde{y}_i$) is computed as an average of two estimates:
 1. Velocity reconstructed from the disentangled direction and speed predictions (i.e., predicted speed magnitude scaled by the unit vector of predicted direction).
 2. The holistic velocity prediction from the dedicated overall velocity model.
 This ensemble approach leverages both the parameter-specific specialization and the holistic decoding to produce more accurate and robust final predictions.

#### 3.4. D. Overall Framework (SSCDL)

SSCDL integrates CND as the backbone for each branch of the CDG mechanism. Three independent CND models are constructed: one for overall velocity, one for direction, and one for speed. Each CND model is trained using its respective parameter-specific loss function ($L_{CND}(Z_{velocity})$, $L_{CND}(Z_{direction})$, $L_{CND}(Z_{speed})$). The branches operate in parallel, maintaining training efficiency. For inference, the teacher networks of these three branches provide predictions, which are then combined using the complementary ensemble strategy to yield the final decoding output.

#### 3.5. E. Experimental Settings

The methodology includes evaluations on three non-human primate (NHP) datasets (Chewie, Mihili, Jango) for center-out reaching (CO), random-target reaching (RT), and isometric wrist movements (ISO). Neural signals are processed (binned, convolved with Gaussian kernel, z-score normalized). Cross-day generalization experiments use the earliest recording day as the source domain and subsequent days (spanning short, medium, and long intervals) as target domains. The CND models are three-layer MLPs. Performance metrics include Correlation Coefficient (CC), Coefficient of Determination (R$^2$), Speed Error (SE), and Angle Error (AE). Comparisons are made against a Vanilla MLP baseline, generalization-based methods (KRSRL, MRNN, LFDA), and a recalibration-based method (WDGRL).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented extensive experimental results to evaluate the performance and robustness of the proposed Self-Supervised Consistency enhanced Disentangled Learning (SSCDL) framework.

#### 4.1. A. Comparison with Other Methods (RQ1)

- **Superior Cross-Day Performance:** SSCDL demonstrated consistently superior or highly competitive decoding performance across three non-human primate (NHP) datasets (Chewie, Mihili, Jango), diverse motor tasks (center-out reaching, random-target reaching, isometric wrist movements), and varying temporal spans (short-term, medium-term, long-term, up to three months). It achieved the highest Correlation Coefficient (CC) and Coefficient of Determination (R$^2$) values in a majority of the tested conditions, indicating strong robustness to neural drift compared to existing generalization-based methods (Vanilla MLP, KRSRL, MRNN, LFDA, CDNG).
- **Outperforming Recalibration-Based Methods:** Notably, SSCDL, a single-domain generalization method, surpassed the performance of WDGRL, a recalibration-based approach that utilizes unlabeled data from the target domain for adaptation. This outcome highlights the effectiveness of SSCDL's ability to learn robust representations from single-day training data, reducing the need for target-domain data.
- **Qualitative Trajectory Stability:** Visualizations of decoded motor trajectories showed that SSCDL produced smooth and accurate movements that closely matched the ground truth. In contrast, the Vanilla MLP baseline generated erratic trajectories, and LFDA, while improved, still exhibited overshooting. The CND backbone alone also yielded stable trajectories, which was further refined by SSCDL. This qualitative evidence suggests improved stability and precision in neural decoding.
- **Fine-grained Accuracy:** The explicit disentanglement of velocity into direction and speed components allowed for a fine-grained evaluation using Speed Error (SE) and Angle Error (AE). SSCDL achieved lower SE and AE values compared to most baseline and generalization methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of the Complementary-Disentangled Generalization (CDG) module in capturing parameter-specific neural population shifts and improving the accuracy of individual motor components.

#### 4.2. B. Ablation Study (RQ2)

Ablation experiments conducted on the Chewie C0–C1 session provided insights into the contributions of individual SSCDL modules:
- **CND's Contribution:** The Consistency enhanced Neural Decoder (CND) was shown to be a robust backbone. The baseline decoder exhibited a significant performance drop on the target domain due to neural drift. The introduction of Neural Signal Masking (NSM), the teacher-student consistency constraint, and the Exponential Moving Average (EMA) update for the teacher network substantially improved cross-day decoding performance, confirming CND's ability to learn stable neural representations.
- **CDG's Contribution:** The Complementary-Disentangled Generalization (CDG) mechanism, even when applied with a baseline decoder, improved performance compared to a single-velocity decoder. This indicates that decomposing velocity into direction, speed, and overall velocity, and training specialized models, enhances generalization by addressing parameter-specific neural drift.
- **Synergistic Effect:** The integration of CND and CDG within SSCDL resulted in the best overall performance, demonstrating a synergistic effect between the two modules. CND contributes stable neural representations, while CDG provides parameter-specific specialization, leading to a more comprehensive framework for long-term neural decoding.

#### 4.3. C. Robustness Analysis (RQ3)

- **Robustness Across Conditions:** SSCDL demonstrated consistent generalization across different neural decoding tasks (CO, ISO, RT), multiple subjects, and long temporal spans (up to three months) without requiring recalibration. This suggests its scalability and suitability for diverse and long-term BMI applications.
- **Robustness to Masked Channels:** An analysis of robustness to neural signal corruption (masked channels) revealed that moderate masking (e.g., 32 channels for Chewie) led to optimal performance, implying that NSM encourages the model to rely on distributed neural information. Performance remained stable even with increased masking but degraded significantly with excessive occlusion (e.g., 64 channels), defining a practical limit for channel loss tolerance.
- **Robustness on Human Dataset:** Further evaluation on a real-world human robotic effector dataset (FALCON benchmark) confirmed SSCDL's robustness and practical applicability. SSCDL consistently outperformed existing methods across multiple target domains in human-robot interaction scenarios.

#### 4.4. D. Computational Cost

Despite employing a three-model ensemble, SSCDL exhibited moderate computational overhead, with inference times and parameter counts comparable to or smaller than existing methods, making it practically feasible for current BMI devices.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Self-Supervised Consistency enhanced Disentangled Learning (SSCDL) presents a significant advancement in the field of Brain-Machine Interfaces (BMIs) by directly addressing a critical limitation: the degradation of decoding performance over time due to neural drift.

The significance and potential impact of this work can be outlined as follows:

- **Enhanced Long-Term BMI Stability:** By providing a framework that enables robust neural decoding generalization using only single-day training data, SSCDL reduces the need for frequent and costly recalibration. This capability is crucial for the long-term viability and practical deployment of iBMIs, allowing users to maintain consistent and reliable control over assistive devices for extended periods without interruption.
- **Improved Decoding Precision and Fine-Grained Control:** The introduction of the Complementary-Disentangled Generalization (CDG) mechanism, which decomposes velocity into direction, speed, and overall velocity components, allows the model to capture parameter-specific neural dynamics. This approach leads to more accurate and fine-grained predictions of motor parameters, as evidenced by lower Speed Error and Angle Error. Such precision is vital for complex tasks in human-centered robotics and advanced assistive applications where subtle movements and nuanced control are required.
- **Robustness to Neural Variability:** The Consistency enhanced Neural Decoder (CND) backbone, with its self-supervised teacher-student consistency constraint and neural signal masking, effectively learns stable neural representations that are invariant to typical neural perturbations. This inherent robustness ensures that the decoder can maintain performance despite variations in neural activity, electrode drift, or partial signal loss, thereby increasing the reliability of BMI systems in real-world conditions.
- **Scalability and Applicability:** The demonstrated robustness of SSCDL across various tasks (reaching, isometric movements), different subjects, and diverse temporal scales (short to long-term) suggests its broad applicability. Its successful evaluation on a real-world human dataset further validates its potential for translation into practical human-robot interaction scenarios. The modular design, allowing parallel training of its components, also contributes to its scalability.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling stable and fine-grained neural decoding, SSCDL facilitates more adaptive and continuous human-robot interaction. This could lead to more intuitive and effective control of prosthetic limbs, exoskeletons, and teleoperation systems, enhancing the quality of life for individuals with motor impairments and expanding human capabilities through augmentation.
- **Neuroscientific Insights:** The work underscores the importance of considering the disentangled nature of neural encoding for motor parameters. By explicitly accounting for direction and speed preferences, the model aligns with neuroscientific observations, suggesting that future BMI designs could benefit from a more biologically informed approach to neural signal processing.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The framework maintains moderate computational overhead despite its multi-component structure, making it a feasible solution for integration into existing BMI hardware and software systems.

While the current offline operation presents a limitation for immediate real-time control, the foundational capabilities established by SSCDL pave the way for future advancements in low-latency online decoding, model optimization, and multimodal integration. Overall, this research contributes significantly to overcoming a critical barrier in BMI technology, moving closer to the realization of highly stable, robust, and intuitive brain-controlled devices for diverse applications.
