# Vad-R1: Towards Video Anomaly Reasoning via Perception-to-Cognition Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Video Anomaly Reasoning (VAR) as a new task, moving beyond mere detection to deep, human-like understanding of anomalous video events through a Perception-to-Cognition Chain-of-Thought (P2C-CoT). Researchers at Sun Yat-sen University developed Vad-R1, an MLLM-based framework that significantly improves both anomaly reasoning and detection, surpassing existing models on the new Vad-Reasoning dataset and VANE benchmark.

## Problem

- Traditional Video Anomaly Detection (VAD) methods can identify when/where an anomaly occurs but lack the ability to understand and explain *why* an event is anomalous or its implications.
- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) applied to VAD offer superficial descriptions, lacking the structured, step-by-step analytical reasoning humans use for anomaly interpretation.
- A scarcity of VAD datasets with structured reasoning annotations limits the development and evaluation of models capable of deep anomaly reasoning.

## Method

- Developed a structured Perception-to-Cognition Chain-of-Thought (P2C-CoT) template to guide MLLMs through a four-step process (Global Perception, Local Perception, Shallow Cognition, Deep Cognition) for comprehensive anomaly analysis.
- Constructed Vad-Reasoning, a new dataset with over 8,000 videos, featuring high-quality step-by-step P2C-CoT annotations for supervised fine-tuning and weak video-level labels for reinforcement learning.
- Proposed Anomaly Verification Augmented GRPO (AVA-GRPO), a novel reinforcement learning algorithm with a self-verification mechanism to effectively train MLLMs for open-ended VAR using weak labels.

## Results

- Vad-R1 achieved state-of-the-art performance on the Vad-Reasoning test set for both anomaly reasoning (e.g., BLEU score improvements of 0.088 to 0.127 over proprietary MLLMs) and anomaly detection (Accuracy, F1, mIoU).
- P2C-CoT prompting alone increased Recall by +0.265 and F1 by +0.133 for a base MLLM, demonstrating the direct benefits of structured reasoning for detection.
- LLM-guided evaluation showed Vad-R1 outperformed proprietary models like GPT-4o in Reasonability, Detail, and Consistency of anomaly explanations, indicating superior semantic quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured, step-by-step reasoning (P2C-CoT) is crucial for enhancing both anomaly detection and deep understanding in MLLMs, outperforming direct answering and random reasoning.
> - A two-stage training strategy combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) is most effective for acquiring and refining complex multimodal reasoning capabilities.
> - The proposed Anomaly Verification Reward within AVA-GRPO enables effective learning from limited weak labels by self-verifying the model's anomaly predictions, pushing beyond simple pattern matching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Video Anomaly Detection (VAD) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities. It addresses a critical gap in the current state of both fields.

**Traditional VAD Methods:** Historically, VAD has focused on identifying "what" or "when" an anomaly occurs. These methods broadly fall into two categories: semi-supervised VAD, which models normal events and flags deviations, and weakly-supervised VAD, which uses video-level labels to learn anomaly patterns. While effective for detection, these methods typically lack the ability to *understand* and *explain* anomalies beyond simple classification or localization. Recent efforts have started to integrate semantic information from vision-language models into VAD, offering rudimentary descriptions, but still falling short of deep contextual understanding.

**Emergence of MLLMs and Reasoning:** Concurrently, the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen a surge in "reasoning" capabilities, often facilitated by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, where models are guided to "think" step-by-step before producing a final answer. This has been further enhanced by reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, as exemplified by projects like DeepSeek-R1, to stimulate more robust reasoning. These advancements have naturally extended to multimodal domains, leading to MLLMs capable of processing and reasoning about both visual and textual information.

**The Identified Gap:** The authors highlight that despite the growing prowess of MLLMs in general reasoning and their nascent integration into VAD, existing MLLM-based VAD methods remain superficial. Current approaches often use MLLMs either as auxiliary modules (providing explanations *after* a detector predicts an anomaly) or for generating simple anomaly descriptions or answering basic questions. They lack the explicit, structured, and deep analytical "thinking" process that humans employ to understand anomalies—contextual understanding, behavior interpretation, and norm violation analysis. This limits their ability to provide comprehensive insights into *why* an event is anomalous or *what its implications are*.

**Introducing Video Anomaly Reasoning (VAR):** To bridge this gap, the paper proposes a new task: Video Anomaly Reasoning (VAR). Unlike traditional VAD that focuses on *detection*, or shallow MLLM-VAD that offers *simple understanding*, VAR aims to empower MLLMs with the ability to perform **structured, step-by-step reasoning** about anomalous events in videos. This new task challenges MLLMs to mimic human cognition, moving from mere perception to deeper analysis, causality, and societal impact. This work thus represents a significant advancement in pushing the boundaries of VAD from mere identification to profound interpretability and explanatory power, aligning with the broader trend of developing more transparent and trustworthy AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to move beyond mere anomaly detection in videos towards a deeper, human-like understanding and explanation of anomalous events. The paper introduces and aims to enable a new task called **Video Anomaly Reasoning (VAR)**.

The key objectives are:

1. **Empower MLLMs with Deep Anomaly Analysis:** To develop an MLLM-based framework that can perform in-depth analysis and understanding of anomalies in videos, going beyond shallow descriptions.
2. **Facilitate Structured, Step-by-Step Reasoning:** To enable MLLMs to explicitly "think before answering" about anomalous events, mimicking the cognitive process humans use to recognize and interpret anomalies. This includes understanding context, interpreting behaviors, and analyzing norm violations.
3. **Address Data Limitations for Reasoning:** To overcome the scarcity of VAD datasets that include structured reasoning annotations, which are crucial for training and evaluating models capable of VAR.
4. **Develop Effective Training Strategies for Open-Ended Reasoning:** To devise methods for effectively training MLLMs to acquire complex, multi-step reasoning capabilities for open-ended VAR tasks, especially given the challenges in defining clear objectives for such a task.

The motivations driving these objectives are multi-faceted:

- **Limitations of Existing VAD:** Current VAD methods, whether traditional or MLLM-based, are primarily focused on *detecting* anomalies. They can identify *that* something abnormal happened, and sometimes *when* and *where*, but they cannot explain *why* it is abnormal, its potential causes, or its consequences. This lack of deeper reasoning limits their utility in critical applications like surveillance and autonomous driving, where understanding the nature and implications of an anomaly is paramount.
- **Untapped Potential of MLLMs:** While MLLMs have demonstrated impressive reasoning capabilities in various domains, this potential remains largely underexplored in the context of video anomaly analysis. The authors believe that MLLMs, with proper guidance and training, can provide the "thinking" and "analytical abilities" missing in current VAD systems.
- **Mimicking Human Cognition:** Humans instinctively perform a multi-stage cognitive process when encountering an anomaly—from initial perception to deeper understanding and causal analysis. The paper is motivated by the desire to instill a similar structured thinking process in MLLMs to achieve more robust and interpretable anomaly understanding.
- **Practical Applications:** Enabling deep anomaly reasoning would significantly enhance the intelligence of surveillance systems, allowing them not only to flag an incident but also to explain its nature, potential risks, and deviations from normal behavior. In autonomous driving, it could lead to better decision-making when encountering unusual events by understanding the context and implications.

In essence, the paper seeks to elevate the task of VAD from a "what and when" problem to a "what, when, where, why, which, and how" problem, making AI systems more capable of sophisticated, human-like anomaly comprehension.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Vad-R1 framework is an end-to-end MLLM-based solution designed for Video Anomaly Reasoning (VAR). Its methodology is built upon three core components: a structured reasoning template, a custom dataset, and a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, integrated into a two-stage training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Perception-to-Cognition Chain-of-Thought (P2C-CoT)

To simulate human anomaly interpretation, the authors propose a structured P2C-CoT, comprising two stages and four steps, concluding with a concise answer:

- **Stage 1: Perception**
 - **Step 1: Global Perception:** The model first observes the entire video, describing the scene, environment, and common objects to establish a comprehensive understanding of "normality."
 - **Step 2: Local Perception:** Building on global understanding, the model then focuses on suspicious video clips, identifying events that deviate, "what" happens, and "when" and "where" the event occurs.
- **Stage 2: Cognition**
 - **Step 3: Shallow Cognition:** The model assesses the abnormality of the identified event, explaining "why" it's anomalous by linking it to relevant visual signals and deviations from the established normal pattern.
 - **Step 4: Deep Cognition:** This step involves higher-level reasoning, analyzing the underlying causes of the anomaly, the violated social expectations or norms, and the potential consequences.
- **Answer:** Finally, the model synthesizes its reasoning into a structured summary that addresses "Which" anomaly category, "What" event occurred, "When" (temporal range) and "Where" (spatial location) it happened, "Why" it's anomalous, and "How" it could cause harm or violate norms (potential influence). For normal videos, the P2C-CoT is simplified to two steps: Perception and Cognition, followed by a streamlined answer.

#### 3.2. Dataset: Vad-Reasoning

Recognizing the lack of suitable datasets for VAR, the authors constructed **Vad-Reasoning**.

- **Video Collection:** Videos were collected from diverse existing VAD datasets (e.g., UCF-Crime, XD-Violence, UBnormal, ECVA) to cover a wide range of real-life scenarios (surveillance, traffic, campus, city). Additional videos were sourced from the internet to expand anomaly category coverage.
- **Anomaly Taxonomy:** A comprehensive taxonomy was defined, categorizing anomalies into "Human Activity Anomaly," "Environments Anomaly," and "Objects Anomaly," each with fine-grained subcategories.
- **Dataset Structure:** Vad-Reasoning contains 8203 training videos and 438 test videos. The training set is split into:
 - **Vad-Reasoning-SFT (1755 videos):** Annotated with high-quality, step-by-step P2C-CoT, crucial for Supervised Fine-Tuning.
 - **Vad-Reasoning-RL (6448 videos):** Provides only video-level weak labels (Abnormal/Normal), designed for Reinforcement Learning to handle scalability and high annotation costs.
- **Annotation Pipeline:** A multi-stage pipeline using proprietary models (Qwen-Max and Qwen-VL-Max) was designed to generate CoT annotations. This involved dense frame descriptions by Qwen-VL-Max, followed by Qwen-Max generating step-by-step CoT guided by specific prompts and strict ethical and quality guidelines (relevance, objectivity, neutrality, non-discrimination, style, conciseness).

#### 3.3. Anomaly Verification Augmented GRPO (AVA-GRPO)

To effectively train models for open-ended VAR with limited annotations, the authors propose AVA-GRPO, an extension of the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

- **Motivation:** Standard GRPO is effective for text-based reasoning, but VAR is multimodal, and the RL stage has only weak video-level labels. Simple accuracy and format rewards are insufficient for evaluating complex reasoning.
- **Anomaly Verification Reward:** The core novelty of AVA-GRPO is a self-verification mechanism:
 - **For "Abnormal" predictions:** The model's predicted temporal range of the anomaly is extracted, and that segment is discarded from the original video. The *trimmed video* (now containing only "normal" segments) is re-fed to the model. If the model then predicts *normal*, a positive reward (0.5) is assigned, reinforcing the initial correct identification of abnormality.
 - **For "Normal" predictions:** To counter "temporal hacking" (models relying on a few frames), if the model predicts *normal*, a random beginning or ending segment is discarded. The *trimmed video* is re-fed. If the model then predicts *abnormal*, a negative reward (-0.2) is assigned, penalizing reliance on insufficient evidence.
- **Other Rewards:** AVA-GRPO also incorporates accuracy reward, format reward (for adherence to output structure), and a length reward (to guide the verbosity of the reasoning process).
- **Objective Function:** The algorithm maximizes the relative advantages of a group of generated completions, regularized by KL-divergence to prevent policy drift from a reference model.

#### 3.4. Training Pipeline

Vad-R1 adopts a two-stage training strategy:

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The base MLLM (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B) is fine-tuned on the Vad-Reasoning-SFT dataset, utilizing its high-quality CoT annotations. This stage equips the model with fundamental anomaly reasoning capabilities and shifts its focus from general multimodal understanding to video anomaly understanding.
- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The model continues training on the larger Vad-Reasoning-RL dataset using the proposed AVA-GRPO algorithm. This stage uses video-level weak labels and the self-verification mechanism to further incentivize and refine the model's reasoning capabilities, moving it beyond pattern-matching to develop more flexible and transferable anomaly reasoning.

This comprehensive approach, combining a novel reasoning paradigm, a specialized dataset, and an advanced RL algorithm, positions Vad-R1 to perform deep and robust video anomaly reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate the effectiveness of Vad-R1, particularly in enhancing anomaly reasoning and detection capabilities. The authors address three key questions through their experiments:

**Q1: Does reasoning improve anomaly detection?**
The results clearly demonstrate that structured reasoning significantly improves anomaly detection.
- **Effectiveness of P2C-CoT:** When base MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3) were prompted to reason according to the proposed P2C-CoT, they showed notable performance gains in both anomaly reasoning (BLEU, METEOR) and anomaly detection (Recall, F1) compared to direct answering. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL saw a +0.265 increase in Recall and +0.133 in F1. Random reasoning, conversely, yielded minimal improvement or even degraded performance, underscoring the importance of a structured CoT.
- **Vad-R1's Performance with CoT:** Vad-R1, when trained with the full P2C-CoT, achieved superior performance compared to training with only the final answer, further confirming the benefits of an explicit reasoning process.

**Q2: How well does Vad-R1 perform in anomaly reasoning and detection?**
Vad-R1 consistently outperformed a wide range of baselines across multiple evaluation scenarios.
- **Vad-Reasoning Dataset Performance:** On the test set of Vad-Reasoning, Vad-R1 achieved state-of-the-art results in both anomaly reasoning (text quality metrics like BLEU-2, METEOR, ROUGE-2) and anomaly detection (Accuracy, F1, mIoU, R@K). Notably, Vad-R1 significantly surpassed existing proprietary reasoning MLLMs (Gemini2.5-Pro, QVQ-Max, o4-mini) in reasoning capability, with BLEU score improvements of 0.088 to 0.127. It also exhibited substantial advantages over other open-source video MLLMs and MLLM-based VAD methods.
- **VANE Benchmark Performance:** Vad-R1 demonstrated superior performance on the VANE benchmark (a video anomaly benchmark for MLLMs) across various anomaly categories, outperforming general video MLLMs and MLLM-based VAD methods.
- **LLM-Guided Evaluation:** Beyond traditional metrics, an LLM-guided evaluation showed Vad-R1 excelling in semantic quality. It achieved the best scores in Reasonability, Detail, and Consistency, even outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o, highlighting its ability to produce logically sound, informative, and factually aligned anomaly explanations.

**Q3: How to acquire the capability of reasoning?**
The two-stage training strategy (SFT followed by RL) proved to be the most effective for acquiring and enhancing reasoning capabilities.
- **Importance of SFT:** Applying SFT alone on the Vad-Reasoning-SFT dataset yielded significant performance improvements, indicating that the structured CoT annotations successfully establish basic anomaly reasoning abilities.
- **Importance of RL:** While direct RL without SFT showed limited improvement, the combination of SFT and RL achieved the best overall performance. The SFT stage provides foundational reasoning, and the subsequent RL stage with AVA-GRPO further refines and enhances this capability, aligning with findings from other reasoning-focused LLM research.
- **Effectiveness of AVA-GRPO:** Ablation studies confirmed that the novel Anomaly Verification Reward and the Length Reward within AVA-GRPO are crucial. AVA-GRPO consistently outperformed the original GRPO, demonstrating that the self-verification mechanism and length control effectively incentivize better reasoning and detection, especially when only weak labels are available.

**Impact of Input Tokens:** Experiments exploring input frames and spatial resolution showed that increasing the number of input frames (from 16 to 32 or 64) generally improved performance. Higher spatial resolution could offer marginal gains for short clips but might lead to redundancy for longer ones, suggesting that increasing temporal context is generally more beneficial than excessively high spatial detail.

**Qualitative Analysis:** Qualitative examples provided in the paper further illustrate Vad-R1's ability to generate accurate, detailed, and logically sound anomaly reasoning, often surpassing proprietary models that either hallucinate or provide partially correct reasoning.

In summary, Vad-R1 sets a new benchmark for video anomaly reasoning and detection, showcasing the power of structured Chain-of-Thought combined with a targeted reinforcement learning approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Vad-R1 framework makes several significant contributions to the fields of video understanding and artificial intelligence, with considerable potential impact:

1. **Introduction of Video Anomaly Reasoning (VAR) as a New Task:** This paper formally defines and tackles Video Anomaly Reasoning, shifting the paradigm from mere anomaly detection to a comprehensive understanding and explanation of anomalous events. This is a crucial step towards developing more intelligent and transparent AI systems that can not only identify issues but also articulate their nature and implications.
2. **Novel Perception-to-Cognition Chain-of-Thought (P2C-CoT):** The design of P2C-CoT is a standout contribution. By explicitly mimicking the human cognitive process of observing, perceiving, and reasoning about anomalies, it provides a structured and intuitive way for MLLMs to generate deep, step-by-step analyses. This CoT framework is interpretable and offers a clear pathway for models to build logical explanations.
3. **Construction of Vad-Reasoning Dataset:** The creation of a dedicated, large-scale dataset, Vad-Reasoning, with its unique split (Vad-Reasoning-SFT with high-quality CoT annotations and Vad-Reasoning-RL with weak labels), directly addresses a critical data scarcity problem. This dataset is invaluable for training and benchmarking future VAR models, enabling progress in an area previously limited by the lack of structured reasoning annotations.
4. **Innovation in Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal Reasoning (AVA-GRPO):** The proposed Anomaly Verification Augmented GRPO (AVA-GRPO) is a significant algorithmic advancement. Its self-verification mechanism intelligently leverages limited video-level weak labels to incentivize and refine anomaly reasoning capabilities. This innovative reward structure is particularly relevant for complex multimodal tasks where dense, explicit reasoning annotations are costly and scarce, making the RL process more efficient and effective.
5. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Vad-R1 consistently outperforms existing open-source and proprietary MLLMs, as well as MLLM-based VAD methods, across multiple metrics for both anomaly reasoning and detection. This empirical success establishes Vad-R1 as a new benchmark in the field and demonstrates the viability and superiority of its approach.
6. **Enhanced Interpretability and Trustworthiness of AI:** By enabling MLLMs to provide detailed, reasoned explanations for anomalies, Vad-R1 contributes to building more interpretable and trustworthy AI systems. Users can understand *why* an anomaly was flagged, which is critical in sensitive applications like surveillance, law enforcement, and autonomous driving.
7. **Broad Practical Impact:** The ability to deeply reason about video anomalies has far-reaching implications:
 - **Intelligent Surveillance:** Beyond alerting, surveillance systems could provide contextual explanations for incidents (e.g., "fighting due to a perceived threat, potentially leading to injuries").
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Vehicles could better understand unusual road events, distinguishing between benign deviations and critical threats, leading to safer and more informed decision-making.
 - **Content Moderation:** Enhanced reasoning could improve the detection and explanation of harmful content in videos.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Future extensions could involve reasoning about abnormalities in medical videos.

**Limitations:**
Despite its significant advancements, the paper acknowledges a key limitation: the **inference speed of Vad-R1**. The multi-step reasoning process, while crucial for deep understanding, inherently introduces additional computational overhead. This could be a constraint for real-time applications where immediate anomaly detection and response are paramount. Future work may need to explore methods for accelerating the reasoning process without sacrificing depth or accuracy.

In conclusion, Vad-R1 represents a pivotal step in pushing video analysis beyond mere detection, fostering a new era of AI systems capable of human-like reasoning and understanding of complex visual events. The methodologies and dataset introduced provide a robust foundation for future research in interpretable and deeply analytical video AI.
