# Skills-Coach: A Self-Evolving Skill Optimizer via Training-Free GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> Skills-Coach is an automated framework for the self-evolution of skills in LLM-based agents, which iteratively refines skill instructions and code using Training-Free Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). It achieved substantial performance improvements on the Skill-X benchmark, raising the average skill score from 0.37 to 0.84 (127.0% relative growth) and the pass rate from 33.59% to 88.02% (54.43 percentage points increase).

## Problem

- The existing skill ecosystem for LLM-based agents is fragmented, with many skills designed for narrow problems, leading to functional gaps for complex tasks.
- This fragmentation results in integration bottlenecks, restricting the scalable deployment of intelligent applications.
- There is a lack of autonomous mechanisms for LLM agents to explore the capability boundaries of their existing skills and proactively expand them without continuous human intervention.

## Method

- **Skills-Coach** introduces an automated framework that uses ==Training-Free Group Relative Policy Optimization== (**GRPO**) for continuous, lightweight optimization of skill instructions and code.
- A ==Diverse Task Generation== Module autonomously creates comprehensive test suites, including standard, advanced, and boundary-probing tasks, to systematically challenge skill capabilities.
- A ==Traceable Evaluation== Module objectively assesses skill performance, provides multi-dimensional scores, and guides data-driven retention decisions in a closed-loop system.

## Results

- Skills-Coach improved the average skill score on the Skill-X benchmark from 0.37 to 0.84, representing a **127.0%** relative growth.
- The overall pass rate for skills increased from **33.59%** to **88.02%**, a gain of 54.43 percentage points.
- Significant performance gains were observed across all skill types and task difficulties, with code-inclusive skills showing a **145.5%** average score increase and advanced tasks improving by **149.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training-Free GRPO enables efficient iterative refinement of skills with significantly reduced computational costs (hours to minutes) and data requirements (thousands to dozens of samples) compared to conventional gradient-based methods.
> - Systematically generating diverse, boundary-probing tasks, including standard, advanced, and edge cases, is critical for identifying and effectively addressing skill limitations.
> - An autonomous, closed-loop system for task generation, optimization, comparative execution, and traceable evaluation allows for continuous self-evolution of skills without human intervention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Skills-Coach" operates within the context of Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents and their increasing reliance on external "skills" or tools to extend their capabilities. LLM-based agents are recognized for their role in intelligent applications across various industries, utilizing skills as modular mechanisms for dynamic capability extension and precise execution of specialized tasks. Examples of such applications range from document generation and data analysis to personal routine automation. The rapid growth of this ecosystem is evidenced by platforms like Clawhub, which hosts over 56,000 skills.

However, the paper identifies a limitation within this expansive skill ecosystem: a significant portion of skills are developed for highly specific, localized problems. This fragmented development often results in an abundance of skills that collectively fail to provide comprehensive functional coverage for complex, multifaceted tasks. Consequently, users encounter functional gaps and integration bottlenecks, hindering the robust and scalable deployment of LLM-based agents to their full potential.

"Skills-Coach" aims to address this fragmentation and improve the utility of existing skills by enabling autonomous self-evolution. This research contributes to the broader effort of enhancing the adaptability, robustness, and generalizability of LLM-based agents, moving beyond static skill sets to dynamically refined and expanded capabilities. It aligns with research directions focused on autonomous agent development, prompt optimization, and the creation of robust evaluation frameworks for agent performance. The introduction of Training-Free Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for skill refinement positions this work at the intersection of reinforcement learning, prompt engineering, and automated code optimization in the LLM domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "Skills-Coach" stems from the identified limitations within the current skill ecosystem for LLM-based agents. The current landscape is characterized by a high volume of skills designed for narrow, specific problems, leading to fragmented coverage and functional gaps when agents attempt complex tasks. This fragmentation restricts the scalability and deployment of intelligent applications.

Motivated by these challenges, the authors posed a central question: "Can an agent autonomously explore the capability boundaries of its existing skills and proactively expand them to achieve skill self-evolution?" To systematically address this overarching question, three critical sub-questions were formulated, outlining the core objectives of the research:

1. **How can boundary-probing tasks be generated automatically?** Exploring the limits of a skill's capabilities requires a comprehensive and challenging test suite. Trivial tasks would not sufficiently probe these boundaries, while unsystematic tasks would yield unrepresentative results. Therefore, an objective was to develop a mechanism for agents to autonomously generate test cases that systematically construct boundary-testing samples through induction and abstraction of existing tasks. This ensures the generated tasks are diverse, reflect actual operational limits, and possess real-world applicability.

2. **How can skills achieve self-evolution?** Beyond merely identifying capability boundaries, the research aimed to develop a method for agents to transcend these limitations by refining the skills themselves. This involves extracting patterns from failed attempts and dynamically updating skill modules (instructions and code). A concurrent objective was to ensure that newly generated or refined skills maintain consistency and complementarity with the existing skill ecosystem, preventing capability degradation or conflicts.

3. **How can skill capabilities be effectively evaluated?** To ensure the correct trajectory of skill evolution, a robust evaluation framework was deemed essential. This framework needed to precisely quantify a skill’s performance across specific dimensions, characterize its generalization boundaries and evolutionary potential, and account for its collaborative efficacy with the agent. The objective was to provide reliable feedback to guide the self-evolution process and support data-driven retention decisions.

In summary, the key objectives were to develop an automated, human-intervention-free framework that can systematically explore skill boundaries, optimize skill instructions and code, and rigorously evaluate improvements, thereby enabling continuous self-evolution of skills within LLM-based agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Skills-Coach is an automated framework designed to achieve skill self-evolution in LLM-based agents through a structured, iterative refinement pipeline. The framework comprises four core modules and supports two execution modes.

#### 3.1. Overview of Skills-Coach

The framework evaluates and optimizes the execution capability and robustness of skills by taking a target skill as input and producing optimized versions and detailed reports. It operates as a closed-loop system, iteratively refining skill capabilities without continuous human intervention.

#### 3.2. Diverse Task Generation Module

This module is responsible for constructing a comprehensive test suite for a target skill.
- **Purpose:** To generate diverse training and test sets that cover standard use cases and challenging edge-case scenarios, ensuring comprehensive coverage and appropriate difficulty for evaluation and optimization.
- **Characteristics of Samples:** Samples are designed to exhibit sufficient diversity, provide realistic boundary representations reflecting actual operational limits, and maintain real-world applicability.
- **Process:**
 - The module parses skill instruction files (e.g., `Skill.md`, `Readme.md`) to extract key information, including functional instructions, supported commands, parameters, I/O formats, constraints, and usage examples.
 - It then categorizes the skill (executable or instruction-based), identifies runnable commands, and analyzes parameter roles and formatting.
 - Regular expressions and structured parsing are employed to delineate the skill’s capability boundaries, encompassing core functionalities, optional features, boundary conditions, and potential failure scenarios.
- **Hierarchical Generation Strategy:** Tasks are synthesized into three types:
 - **Standard tasks:** Cover routine operations (e.g., basic file processing).
 - **Advanced tasks:** Evaluate complex multi-step workflows and anomalous input handling.
 - **Boundary tasks:** Probe operational limits through conditions such as min/max bounds, invalid inputs, and resource constraints.
 - Test set tasks maintain comparable difficulty to training tasks but feature distinct contexts to rigorously assess generalization.
- **Quality Principles:** The module adheres to determinism, strict data isolation (training and test sets), diversity in input types, and objectivity through deterministically verifiable validation criteria (e.g., output file existence, keyword inclusion).
- **Evaluation Dimensions:** Generated samples span eight dimensions: structural integrity, usability, example quality, technical depth, clarity, command coverage, error handling, and advanced scenarios, encompassing 51 discrete evaluation metrics (as detailed in Appendix A).

#### 3.3. Lightweight Optimization Module

This module is the core optimization engine, designed for continuous enhancement of skill instructions and code.
- **Mechanism:** It leverages Training-Free Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which departs from conventional gradient-based parameter optimization. Instead, it utilizes the introspective capabilities of LLMs to refine skills via a multi-driven mechanism.
- **Advantages:** This approach enables efficient iterative refinement, significantly reduces computational costs (from hours to minutes), decreases data requirements (thousands to dozens of samples), mitigates overfitting, and demonstrates generalization and cross-domain transfer capabilities.
- **Optimization Pathways:** Each optimization epoch involves two parallel pathways:
 - **Instruction Optimization Pathway:** Training-Free GRPO generates multiple instruction variants, comparatively scores them, and selects the highest-performing variant as the baseline for subsequent iterations.
 - **Code Optimization Pathway:** This pathway employs a three-tier sequential mechanism:
 - A rule-driven optimizer integrates caching, input validation, and error-handling logic.
 - An LLM-based command optimizer extracts and refines executable instructions.
 - An auto-fixer remediates specific issues (e.g., dependency conflicts, parameter misconfigurations, path errors) based on failure analysis.
 Modifications are executed sequentially by priority, with re-evaluations after each step until convergence or an iteration limit is reached.
- **Differentiated Strategies:** Optimization is tailored to skill categories:
 - **Instruction-only skills:** Focus on content clarity, structural logic, example sufficiency, and description completeness.
 - **Code-inclusive skills:** Extend beyond instruction to code-level improvements, including defect remediation, error-handling augmentation, performance optimization, and overall code quality.

#### 3.4. Comparative Execution Module

This module is responsible for the unbiased execution of skills and systematic recording of results.
- **Purpose:** To establish a controlled, isolated, and reproducible environment for executing both original and optimized skills on identical test tasks, capturing all outputs, side effects, errors, and performance metrics. It refrains from scoring or judgment, focusing solely on logging execution.
- **Operational Mechanisms:**
 - **Environment Checker:** Handles pre-execution dependency validation and automated configuration, verifying system commands and provisioning missing dependencies.
 - **Skill Executor:** Provisions independent temporary workspaces for each task, duplicates environments, executes commands, and captures standard outputs, errors, return codes, and generated files. Post-execution, it compiles a detailed log and clears the temporary space.
- **Isolation Strategy:** Tasks run in exclusive temporary directories; original and optimized skills execute sequentially to eliminate order-based dependencies; temporary directories are purged post-execution.
- **Efficiency and Fault Tolerance:** Includes a parallel execution mode using a thread pool and a Fail-Safe strategy to log exceptions, capture error messages, preserve partial outputs, and proceed to the next task upon failure.
- **Traceability:** Generates structured outputs detailing error types, messages, stack traces, and system state in execution logs, with a summary report including success rate statistics.

#### 3.5. Traceable Evaluation Module

This module objectively assesses performance differences and guides retention decisions.
- **Purpose:** To perform multi-dimensional scoring on execution results, compute normalized metrics, conduct comparative analysis, and render data-driven retention decisions.
- **Core Principles:** Scoring objectivity, criterion consistency, analysis depth, decision rigor, and interpretability.
- **Components:**
 - **Criterion Parser:** Extracts evaluation criteria, scoring scales, and passing thresholds from rule documents.
 - **Task Evaluator:** Scores individual task results criterion-by-criterion, supporting LLM-based deep evaluation and heuristic fallback modes.
 - **Metrics Computer:** Aggregates task-level scores and computes macro-level indicators (pass rate, average score, standard/advanced task scores, error rate).
 - **Decision Engine:** Renders retain-or-discard decisions based on quantitative results and generates detailed justifications.
- **Dual-Mode Strategy:**
 - **Primary Mode:** Leverages LLMs for in-depth assessment across seven dimensions: structural completeness, practicality, example quality, technical depth, clarity, error handling, and comprehensiveness, yielding scores (0–100) with supporting evidence.
 - **Enhanced Heuristic Mode:** Activates if LLM is unavailable or times out, applying multi-dimensional rule-based checks (keyword matching, structural analysis, content statistics) to ensure continued robustness.

#### 3.6. Execution Modes

Skills-Coach supports two configurations:
- **Virtual Mode:** Bypasses actual command execution, estimating task completion by verifying the presence of evaluation-criteria-related keywords within skill instructions and using deterministic random numbers.
- **Real Mode:** Deploys the original or optimized skill in a practical environment, precisely evaluating task fulfillment by analyzing actual output files, execution logs, and error messages. The default setting for experiments and final evaluation is real mode.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Skills-Coach was evaluated using a newly introduced benchmark dataset, Skill-X, and a range of quantitative metrics.

#### 4.1. Skill-X Benchmark

Skill-X is a standardized evaluation benchmark comprising 48 widely used skills. These skills were curated from major developer platforms such as ClawHub, Anthropics, and Vercel Labs, ensuring diversity and practical utility. The dataset categorizes skills into two primary types: 29 instruction-only skills and 19 code-inclusive skills, covering various technology stacks and input modalities. Skill-X aims to evaluate comprehension, orchestration, execution, and optimization capabilities of Skills-Coach under diverse interface specifications and complex task requirements, moving beyond synthetic environments.

#### 4.2. Overall Performance Comparison

Performance was quantified by awarding points for each satisfied condition in a task's execution, with a task "passed" if its score met or exceeded 70% of the maximum possible score. The overall assessment metrics included pass rate, average score, standard task score, and advanced task score.

As summarized in Table 1 and Figure 1, Skills-Coach demonstrated significant performance improvements across all evaluation metrics when comparing original and optimized skills:
- **Average Score:** Increased from 0.37 to 0.84, representing a relative growth of 127.0%.
- **Pass Rate:** Improved from 33.59% to 88.02%, indicating an increase of 54.43 percentage points.
- **Standard Task Score:** Increased from 43.00% to 87.43%, a relative improvement of 103.3%.
- **Advanced Task Score:** Increased from 32.71% to 81.61%, a relative improvement of 149.5%.

These results indicate that Skills-Coach enhances both skill generalization and practical application capabilities through its optimization strategies.

#### 4.3. Performance by Skill Type

The improvements were consistent across different skill types:
- **Instruction-only skills:** Showed an average score increase from 0.388 to 0.839 (+116.2%), and a pass rate increase from 37.93% to 91.38% (+53.45 percentage points).
- **Code-inclusive skills:** Exhibited an average score increase from 0.343 to 0.842 (+145.5%), and a pass rate increase from 26.97% to 82.89% (+55.92 percentage points).
The largest relative improvement in pass rate was observed for code-inclusive skills, suggesting particular effectiveness for tasks requiring complex logical reasoning.

#### 4.4. Performance by Task Difficulty

Skills-Coach also demonstrated performance gains across task difficulties:
- Both standard and advanced tasks showed improved scores.
- Advanced tasks experienced a greater magnitude of improvement than standard tasks, with a 149.5% relative increase in score compared to 103.3% for standard tasks. This highlights the framework's advantage in addressing more challenging problems.

#### 4.5. Further Analyses of Individual Skills

Table 2 provides a systematic evaluation of optimization results for the 48 skills, categorized by improvement level:
- **Exceptional Improvement (≥ +0.5 score increase):** 23 skills fell into this category. Notably, skills like "Browser," "Ontology," "mcp-builder," and "rss-daily-digest" saw their scores leap from 0.0 to 1.0 (a +1.0 improvement) and their pass rates from 0% to 100%. This indicates Skills-Coach's ability to significantly enhance underperforming skills.
- **Significant Improvement (+0.3 to +0.49 score increase):** 11 skills, such as "algorithmic-art" and "multi-search-engine," showed substantial gains.
- **Moderate Improvement (+0.1 to +0.29 score increase):** 7 skills, including "azure-diagnostics" and "pdf," had more modest but still positive improvements.
- **Limited Improvement (< +0.1 score increase):** Only one skill, "react-view-transitions," showed a small gain (from 0.92 to 1.0, +0.08).
- **Perfect Performance (No Improvement Needed):** 5 skills ("admapix," "azure-prepare," "nano-banana-pro," "self-improving-agent," "slack-gif-creator") maintained perfect scores and pass rates (1.0 and 100%) both before and after optimization, indicating they were already optimal.

These analyses suggest that while Skills-Coach can dramatically improve underperforming skills, the marginal benefit of optimization exhibits diminishing returns for skills already operating at high performance levels. This provides quantitative evidence for prioritizing optimization resources toward skills with substantial room for improvement.

#### 4.6. Case Study: Pollyreach

A case study on the "Pollyreach" skill illustrated the task generation and evaluation process (Figure 3 and 4). Basic tasks assessed standard command execution and output generation, while advanced tasks increased complexity by requiring features like same-day delivery and rigorous input validation, producing JSON output. The summary report for Pollyreach showcased comparative improvements between original and optimized skills across metrics like pass rate and average score, segmented by task difficulty, providing multi-dimensional data for iterative optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Skills-Coach represents a development in the domain of LLM-based agents and their skill ecosystems. By addressing the challenge of fragmented and often underperforming skills, this framework offers a mechanism for autonomous skill self-evolution without requiring continuous human intervention.

One significant contribution is the formalization of the skill self-evolution problem and the proposal of an automated solution. This has the potential to reduce the development and maintenance burden on skill developers, allowing them to focus on initial skill creation while the framework handles refinement.

The methodology’s reliance on Training-Free GRPO as an alternative to conventional gradient-based parameter optimization is notable. The paper demonstrates that this approach can achieve competitive results with significantly reduced computational resources and data requirements, shortening training times from hours to minutes and reducing necessary samples from thousands to dozens. This efficiency could make autonomous skill optimization more accessible and scalable.

The empirical validation on Skill-X, a comprehensive benchmark comprising 48 diverse skills, provides evidence of Skills-Coach's effectiveness. The framework consistently yielded substantial performance improvements across various skill categories and task complexities, with particularly pronounced gains observed for code-inclusive skills and advanced tasks. This indicates the framework's capability to enhance both the fundamental functionality and the robustness of skills when dealing with complex or edge-case scenarios.

The potential impact of Skills-Coach extends to several areas:
- **Advancement of LLM-based Agents:** By enabling skills to continuously improve and adapt, Skills-Coach can contribute to the development of more robust, adaptable, and intelligent LLM-based agents. These agents may be better equipped to handle dynamic and complex real-world tasks.
- **Transformation of Skill Ecosystems:** The autonomous optimization capabilities could lead to a more integrated and functional skill ecosystem, moving away from fragmented, localized solutions towards a more comprehensive coverage of intelligent application requirements.
- **Developer Empowerment:** Developers could leverage this framework to automatically identify and rectify limitations in their skills, thereby enhancing the quality and reliability of their contributions to agent platforms.
- **Practical Deployment:** The ability to optimize skills at scale could accelerate the practical deployment of intelligent applications across diverse industrial domains, where agent performance and reliability are critical.

Overall, Skills-Coach contributes to the ongoing research into autonomous agents and meta-learning, offering a practical framework for enhancing the operational capabilities of LLM-based systems.
