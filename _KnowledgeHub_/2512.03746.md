# Thinking with Programming Vision: Towards a Unified View for Thinking with Images

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and ByteDance introduced CodeVision, a "code-as-tool" framework that equips Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to programmatically interact with images. The approach significantly improves MLLM robustness by correcting common image corruptions and enables state-of-the-art multi-tool reasoning through emergent tool use and error recovery.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit surprising brittleness to simple real-world visual perturbations like image rotations and flips, leading to significant performance degradation.
- Existing tool-augmented MLLMs often lack flexibility and scalability, relying on brittle, hand-specified tool interfaces that hinder generalization.
- Current MLLM tool-use paradigms frequently offer only marginal performance gains, raising questions about the genuine necessity and utility of tools for many tasks.

## Method

- The CodeVision framework employs a ==two-stage training process==: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to teach fundamental tool-use syntax and multi-tool sequences, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) for strategic decision-making.
- A =="code-as-tool" paradigm== allows MLLMs to generate code as a universal interface to invoke any image operation, moving beyond fixed tool registries for greater flexibility and scalability.
- A ==dense, multi-component reward function== for RL training guides the agent in achieving correct answers, mastering strategic tool selection (including discovery of emergent tools), and ensuring robust error recovery.

## Results

- CodeVision models consistently and substantially outperform baseline MLLMs on transformed OCRBench and ChartQAPro, achieving an average score of **73.4** on transformed OCRBench with CodeVision-7B, a **+17.4** improvement over its base model.
- The framework establishes a new state-of-the-art on the novel MVToolBench for multi-tool composition, with CodeVision-7B scoring **60.1**, nearly doubling the performance of Gemini2.5-Pro (**32.6**).
- Qualitative results demonstrate CodeVision's emergent capabilities, including the discovery and use of beneficial tools not explicitly trained, efficient chaining of multiple operations, and sophisticated error handling by revising incorrect tool calls based on runtime feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs show significant vulnerability (up to 80% performance drop) to simple image orientation changes, highlighting a fundamental robustness issue that necessitates tool use.
> - A "code-as-tool" approach, combined with a multi-stage training methodology and dense reward, enables emergent behaviors like the discovery of unseen tools, efficient tool chaining, and robust error recovery.
> - Carefully curated SFT data with metadata-conditioned image transformations and a rich RL reward function are crucial for bootstrapping the model and guiding it towards strategic, multi-tool reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the forefront of research into Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically addressing their ability to "think with images" – a paradigm popularized by OpenAI's "o3" initiative (OpenAI, 2025c). The core idea is to move MLLMs beyond merely describing visual inputs to actively manipulating them using external tools to gather evidence and perform complex reasoning.

The broader research landscape for MLLMs is rapidly evolving, with models demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and generating content across modalities. However, a significant challenge remains: equipping these models with robust, flexible, and scalable interactive reasoning abilities. Current approaches to tool-augmented MLLMs suffer from several limitations that "Thinking with Programming Vision" aims to tackle:

- **Necessity and Impact of Tools:** Previous work often focused on a narrow set of tools, primarily "crop" or "zoom," with marginal performance gains (2-5% accuracy). This raised questions about the genuine utility and necessity of tools for many tasks. This paper argues that the *types* of tasks and tools need to be re-evaluated to truly exercise the potential of tool use.
- **Flexibility and Scalability of Tool Integration:** Many existing systems rely on brittle, hand-specified tool names and argument schemas. Renaming a tool or introducing a new one often requires significant retraining, hindering generalization and scalability. This contrasts with the broader trend in LLMs of integrating diverse external capabilities (e.g., search engines, code interpreters, generative models) to overcome inherent limitations, as seen in projects like LLM-I (Guo et al., 2025b) and DeepResearch (Team et al., 2025).
- **Multi-Turn, Multi-Tool Composition:** While some works explore multi-turn settings, they often focus on repetitive use of a single tool (e.g., repeated cropping), rather than the complex, sequential composition of different tools required for real-world problem-solving.

This paper makes a critical observation that even state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit surprising brittleness to simple, real-world visual perturbations like orientation changes (rotations, flips). This overlooked weakness creates a clear imperative for tools that can correct such fundamental issues, thus making tool use not just beneficial but *necessary*. By proposing "CodeVision," the authors draw inspiration from the "code-as-tool" paradigm previously explored in other contexts (e.g., Chen et al., 2023 for numerical reasoning), but extend it rigorously to visual tasks. They aim to unlock specific advantages: emergent tool use, efficient chaining, and robust error recovery, which have not been fully realized in prior image-centric systems limited by fixed tool registries. The work also aligns with the growing trend of employing Reinforcement Learning (RL) (e.g., PPO, GRPO, DAPO) to enhance reasoning and strategic decision-making in both LLMs and MLLMs (e.g., Hong et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025).

In essence, this work positions itself as a foundational step towards building more robust, adaptable, and genuinely intelligent MLLMs that can interact with and reason about visual inputs in a manner akin to a programmer, capable of dynamically invoking a vast array of operations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are directly motivated by the identified limitations and a critical, previously overlooked weakness in existing MLLMs:

**Key Motivation 1: Overlooked Brittleness of State-of-the-Art MLLMs**
The authors demonstrate a striking vulnerability: even leading MLLMs like GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro suffer significant performance degradation (up to 80%) on images subjected to simple, real-world perturbations such as orientation changes (rotations, horizontal/vertical flips) or mirrored selfies. Humans easily correct for these, but current MLLMs struggle to identify and address them, leading to failures in downstream perception and reasoning tasks (e.g., OCR, chart understanding). This reveals a profound lack of robustness and underscores the *necessity* of tools for fundamental image correction, providing a compelling use case where tools are genuinely indispensable.

**Key Motivation 2: Limitations of Current Tool-Augmented MLLM Approaches**
Beyond the brittleness, the paper identifies three prevailing shortcomings in how MLLMs currently use tools:
- **Lack of Tool Necessity:** Many existing tasks and benchmarks for MLLM tool use (e.g., focused on cropping) offer only marginal performance gains, suggesting that the tools are not truly essential for problem-solving.
- **Limited Flexibility and Scalability:** Current methods often rely on manually specified tool names and arguments, making them brittle and difficult to scale. Any change to a tool's interface might require retraining, severely limiting generalization.
- **Insufficient Multi-Turn, Multi-Tool Composition:** Most systems support only simple, single-tool operations or repetitive use of a single tool (like repeated cropping) across turns, rather than the complex, dynamic composition of diverse tools needed for realistic problem-solving.

### 3. Key Objectives of CodeVision

To address these motivations, the authors propose **CodeVision**, a "code-as-tool" framework, with the following key objectives:
1. **Enhance Robustness:** Enable MLLMs to effectively identify and correct real-world image corruptions (like orientation changes) that currently cause significant performance degradation.
2. **Achieve Universal and Flexible Tool Invocation:** Move beyond fixed, pre-registered toolsets by allowing the MLLM to generate code as a universal interface to invoke *any* image operation. This aims to dramatically improve flexibility, scalability, and generalization to unseen tools.
3. **Foster Advanced Multi-Tool Reasoning:** Develop models capable of composing multiple tools strategically across multiple turns, enabling complex problem-solving that reflects real-world requirements.
4. **Promote Emergent Capabilities:** Through principled training, enable emergent behaviors such as the discovery and use of novel tools (not seen in training), efficient chaining of multiple tools within a single execution, and robust error recovery from runtime feedback.
5. **Establish Rigorous Evaluation:** Construct new, challenging benchmarks designed to rigorously evaluate model robustness to orientation changes and sophisticated multi-tool reasoning.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The CodeVision framework employs a sophisticated two-stage training methodology, starting with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL), complemented by novel dataset construction and a dense reward function.

#### 4.1. Two-Stage Training Process

1. **Stage 1: Cold Start with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
 - **Goal:** To bootstrap the model by teaching it the fundamental syntax, patterns, and principles of tool use, including complex multi-tool sequences and error handling. This prevents the agent from floundering in the vast action space of code generation during RL.
 - **Dataset Construction:** A high-quality SFT corpus (approx. 5,000 examples) is meticulously curated:
 - **Diverse Sources:** Aggregates data from handwriting, OCR/VQA, table/chart understanding, and math reasoning datasets to ensure variety.
 - **Task Types and Metadata:** Each sample is annotated with metadata classifying it into `single-tool`, `multi-tool`, `multi-crop`, `error-handling`, or `no-tool` categories. This metadata guides data generation and ensures explicit coverage of challenging scenarios. For `multi-crop`, coarse-to-fine zoom-in with monotonically shrinking windows is enforced to build spatial awareness. For `error-handling`, intentional failures (incorrect tools, runtime errors) are introduced, requiring the model to read error logs and revise code.
 - **Metadata-Conditioned Image Transformations:** Crucially, images are transformed based on metadata (e.g., rotated if `rotate-180` is the required tool). This makes the subsequent tool invocation *necessary* to restore the canonical view, addressing the "necessity of tools" limitation.
 - **SFT Generation with GPT-5:** GPT-5 generates step-by-step reasoning traces and actions (tool calls or answers). These tool calls are executed in a controlled runtime, and outputs are compared against ground truth. Error logs are fed back for `error-handling` examples.
 - **Iterative Multi-Turn Construction:** The context is updated after each step, and GPT-5 continues until the turn budget is reached or a stop action is produced, creating multi-turn trajectories.
 - **Training:** Standard causal language model training is applied. Assistant tokens (reasoning and tool-call specifications) are masked for loss calculation, while user inputs and cached tool returns are context. Tools are not executed online during SFT; their outputs are pre-computed.

2. **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
 - **Goal:** To move beyond imitation, enabling the agent to strategize, make efficient decisions, and generalize robustly.
 - **Dataset:** An augmented RL training dataset (approx. 40,000 items) is created by adding more reasoning-heavy and perception samples. Difficulty filtering is applied to focus on informative instances. Each item is annotated with a `must-use` tool field (e.g., rotate90, crop) and bounding boxes for crop-required items, encouraging the learning of tool invocation conditions.
 - **Reward Function:** A dense, rich, and multi-component reward function is designed to stabilize RL training and guide strategic tool use:
 - **Outcome Reward ($R_{outcome}$):** Includes terminal accuracy on the final answer and a formatting reward for correct reasoning tags (<think>, <answer>).
 - **Strategy Shaping ($R_{strategy}$):** Provides process-level signals:
 - **Must-use Tools ($S_{req}$):** Rewards (1/N budget per tool) for correctly using prerequisite tools. Categorical tools get a one-time bonus; "crop" gets a reward proportional to IoU improvement. A bonus for perfectly matching required tool-use trajectory encourages precision and optimal path learning.
 - **Suggested Tools Bonus:** Rewards the discovery and use of emergent, empirically beneficial tools not in $S_{req}$. A rollout comparison mechanism identifies optional tools that improve success rate, adding $r_{nec}$ as a bonus to successful trajectories using them. An additional per-trajectory bonus is granted for using *any* optional tool and getting the correct answer.
 - **Constraint Penalties ($P_{cost}$):** Counteracts "reward hacking" behaviors:
 - **Turn Limit Penalty:** Penalizes excessive tool-call turns beyond a budget of $|S_{req}| + 1$.
 - **Poor Reasoning Penalty:** Penalizes correct answers derived from irrelevant visual evidence (e.g., crop with < 0.1 IoU).
 - **Inappropriate Tool Use Penalty:** Penalizes using orientation-adjusting tools when `S_req` is None.
 - **RL Algorithm:** GRPO (Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization) is used as the base algorithm.

#### 4.2. Evaluation and Benchmarks

To rigorously evaluate CodeVision, the authors introduce a novel benchmark suite alongside standard ones:
- **Robustness to Orientation Changes:** New benchmarks are created by applying five transformations (rotate 90/180/270 degrees, horizontal/vertical flip) to **OCRBench** (testing perception) and **ChartQAPro** (testing reasoning).
- **Multi-Tool Reasoning:** **MVToolBench** is a newly constructed benchmark for complex, multi-step tool composition. It uses the HierText dataset, filters for extremely small text (requiring crop), generates Q&A without positional cues, and augments images with random rotations/flips (requiring orientation correction + crop).
- **Single-Tool Cropping:** Standard benchmarks like **V***, **HRBench4k**, and **HRBench8k** are used.

#### 4.3. Implementation Details

CodeVision models are built upon various Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL series backbones (7B, 8B, 32B models). Specific hyperparameters are provided for both SFT and RL stages, including learning rates, batch sizes, and reward coefficients.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that CodeVision significantly improves MLLM performance on challenging benchmarks and fosters emergent capabilities.

1. **Validation of MLLM Brittleness:** Table 1 clearly shows that state-of-the-art MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini2.5-Pro, various Qwen and InternVL models) suffer severe performance degradation (up to 80% on some metrics) on OCRBench and ChartQAPro when images are subjected to simple rotations or flips. This empirically confirms the core weakness identified by the authors.

2. **Superior Robustness to Image Corruptions:** CodeVision models consistently and substantially outperform baseline MLLMs on transformed OCRBench and ChartQAPro (Table 1). For instance, CodeVision-7B achieves an average score of 73.4 on transformed OCRBench, a +17.4 improvement over its base model. This indicates CodeVision's robust ability to identify necessary transformations and apply the correct tools to restore images for successful downstream reasoning.

3. **State-of-the-Art Multi-Tool Reasoning:** On the novel MVToolBench, designed to test multi-tool composition, CodeVision establishes a new state-of-the-art (Table 2). CodeVision-7B achieves 60.1, nearly doubling the performance of the next-best model (Gemini2.5-Pro at 32.6). This highlights CodeVision's superior capability in chaining multiple tools to solve complex, multi-step problems.

4. **Effective Reinforcement Learning Strategy:** The RL training curves (Figure 5) show a steady increase in outcome, strategy, and total rewards. This signifies that the dense, multi-component reward function effectively guides the agent to not only achieve correct answers but also master strategic tool selection and application processes.

5. **Emergent Capabilities:**
 - **Emergent Tool Use:** CodeVision demonstrates the ability to discover and utilize beneficial tools that were not explicitly included in the RL training data (Figures 7, 8, 9, 10). The word cloud (Figure 8) showcases a wide variety of such emergent tools (brightness, blur, edge detection). Figure 9 and 10 illustrate examples where the model applies tools like "contrast enhancement," "grayscale conversion," or "sharpness" even though they were not part of the `S_req` set.
 - **Efficient Tool Chaining:** The model can chain multiple operations within a single turn, demonstrating efficiency in its problem-solving (Figures 9 and 10).
 - **Robust Error Recovery:** Qualitative examples (Figure 6) show CodeVision's sophisticated error-handling. The model can initially make an incorrect tool call (e.g., `flip-horizontal`), receive runtime feedback, identify the mistake, and then correctly apply the right tool (e.g., `rotate-90`).

6. **Ablation Study Results:**
 - **Necessity of Dense Reward:** Removing the `strategy reward` or `constraint penalties` significantly degrades performance across benchmarks (Table 3), validating that a simple outcome-based reward is insufficient and that the dense process signals and guardrails are critical for robust and efficient learning, preventing reward hacking (Figure 11 vs. Figure 12).
 - **Importance of Cold Start (SFT):** Training with RL directly from the base model without the SFT cold start fails to achieve meaningful improvement (Figure 16). This confirms that SFT is crucial for bootstrapping the model with fundamental tool-use syntax and providing a stable foundation for subsequent RL refinement.

7. **Case Studies:** While generally successful (Figure 12, 13), the case studies also reveal limitations, particularly in precise localization for cropping (Figure 13 shows suboptimal cropping, Figure 14 shows failure due to slightly inaccurate coordinates), indicating areas for future improvement.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Thinking with Programming Vision" paper makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the field of MLLMs and AI agents:

1. **Addressing a Fundamental MLLM Weakness:** By identifying and effectively mitigating the surprising brittleness of state-of-the-art MLLMs to simple image transformations, CodeVision makes these models significantly more robust and reliable for real-world applications where visual inputs are often imperfect, taken from diverse orientations, or subject to common corruptions. This directly enhances the practical utility of MLLMs.

2. **Pioneering a Truly Flexible and Scalable Tool-Use Paradigm:** The "code-as-tool" framework offers a universal interface for tool invocation, liberating MLLMs from the limitations of fixed, hand-specified tool registries. This dramatically improves scalability, allowing models to leverage an effectively unbounded set of visual operations without requiring extensive re-training for every new tool or interface change. This flexibility is a critical step towards more general-purpose visual AI.

3. **Advancing MLLM Reasoning and Agency:** CodeVision fosters advanced reasoning capabilities such as complex multi-turn, multi-tool composition, strategic planning, efficient tool chaining, and robust error recovery from runtime feedback. These emergent behaviors push MLLMs closer to autonomous AI agents that can actively diagnose visual problems, select appropriate interventions, and adapt their strategies based on outcomes. The framing of visual interaction as a "programming task" is a powerful conceptual shift.

4. **Introducing Rigorous Benchmarking for Complex Tool Use:** The development of new benchmarks like transformed OCRBench/ChartQAPro (for robustness) and MVToolBench (for multi-tool reasoning) provides essential tools for the research community. These benchmarks move beyond simplistic evaluations, creating more challenging and realistic scenarios that necessitate sophisticated tool integration and reasoning, thereby driving future progress in the field.

5. **Blueprint for Future AI Agents:** The methodology, particularly the two-stage SFT-then-RL training with a dense, multi-component reward function, offers a valuable blueprint for building more capable AI agents across various domains. It highlights the importance of guided pre-training combined with strategic reinforcement learning to unlock complex behaviors and overcome the challenges of vast action spaces.

6. **Foundation for Expanding Tool Ecosystems:** The flexibility of the code-as-tool paradigm opens avenues for integrating a much wider variety of tools – from standard Python libraries to custom proprietary functions or even other generative AI models – by simply exposing an API. This could lead to MLLMs that can not only correct images but also enhance them, analyze them with specialized algorithms, or even generate new visual content based on user instructions.

In conclusion, "Thinking with Programming Vision" presents a compelling solution to fundamental challenges in MLLM robustness and tool integration. By reframing visual interaction as a programmatic task, it significantly advances the capabilities of MLLMs, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and practical AI systems that can truly "think with images."
