# Exploring Visual Pre-training for Robot Manipulation: Datasets, Models and Methods

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ByteDance Research systematically investigated visual pre-training strategies for robot manipulation, developing the EgoNet dataset from egocentric videos rich in human-object interactions. They introduce Vi-PRoM, a pre-training scheme that combines contrastive learning with supervised fine-tuning for visual semantics and temporal dynamics, achieving improved performance on simulated and real-world robot manipulation tasks with reduced demonstration data.

## Problem

- Learning visual semantics and sequential action patterns for robot manipulation typically requires extensive and costly in-domain observation-action samples.
- Existing visual pre-training methods for robotics vary significantly in their choice of datasets, models, and methods, lacking a systematic comparison for optimal strategies.
- General visual datasets like ImageNet often lack the dynamic human-object interaction content critical for robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic benchmarking study on visual pre-training components, evaluating different datasets (ImageNet, EgoNet), model architectures (ResNet-34/50/101), and pre-training methods (==Contrastive Learning==, Masked Image Modeling).
- Introduced EgoNet, a new benchmark dataset of approximately 500,000 egocentric video clips, extracted from Ego4D, specifically designed to capture human-object interactions.
- Proposed Vi-PRoM (Visual Pre-training scheme for Robot Manipulation), a two-phase scheme using contrastive self-supervised learning on EgoNet, followed by supervised fine-tuning with auxiliary tasks for visual semantics and temporal dynamics.

## Results

- EgoNet pre-training significantly improved success rates in simulated tasks (**40.5%** in Franka Kitchen, **61.2%** in MetaWorld) compared to ImageNet pre-training.
- Vi-PRoM consistently outperformed baseline methods across simulated environments, achieving **43.8%** success in Franka Kitchen and **63.5%** in MetaWorld using Behavior Cloning, an improvement of **3.3%** and **2.3%** over the best MoCo-v3 baseline.
- Vi-PRoM successfully enabled a real robot to perform four contact-rich kitchen manipulation tasks with only 30 demonstrations per task, demonstrating practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-training with data rich in human-object interactions (EgoNet) is more effective for robot manipulation than using general image datasets (ImageNet).
> - Convolutional ResNet-50 architectures offer a suitable balance of performance and efficiency for robot manipulation tasks, performing comparably to deeper ResNet models.
> - Contrastive learning objectives are more beneficial for learning effective visual representations for robot manipulation than masked image modeling approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot manipulation tasks, particularly those relying on visual observations, present challenges in learning both visual semantics and sequential action patterns. Traditionally, deep neural networks for vision-based robot control are trained end-to-end with in-domain data, a process that typically requires extensive observation-action samples. This requirement can limit the practical application of such methods due to the high cost and complexity of data collection in real-world robotic scenarios.

The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements in visual representation learning through pre-training on large-scale, real-world visual datasets. Pre-trained visual encoders have demonstrated strong generalization abilities across various computer vision tasks, including image segmentation, object detection, and autonomous driving. This success has motivated researchers to explore the application of visual pre-training to robot learning as a strategy to achieve more data-efficient control policies.

Recent work in this area, such as R3M and MVP, has shown the potential benefits of visual pre-training for robot control from pixels. However, these prior efforts often differ in their choices of pre-training data, models, and methods, leading to an open question regarding the optimal strategies for leveraging visual pre-training in robot manipulation. This paper addresses this gap by conducting a systematic investigation into these fundamental aspects. It aims to provide empirical insights and a refined pre-training scheme that can serve as a benchmark for future research in visual pre-training for robot manipulation. The work contributes to the ongoing effort to enable robots to learn complex manipulation skills with reduced reliance on task-specific, large-scale demonstration data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:

1. **Systematic Investigation of Visual Pre-training Strategies:** The paper aims to thoroughly investigate the effects of different visual pre-training strategies on robot manipulation tasks. This investigation focuses on three fundamental perspectives:
 - **Pre-training Datasets:** To determine which types of large-scale visual data are most beneficial for learning representations applicable to robot manipulation.
 - **Model Architectures:** To evaluate how different visual encoder architectures influence performance in downstream robot tasks.
 - **Training Methods (Objectives):** To compare the effectiveness of various self-supervised and supervised pre-training objectives in acquiring relevant visual representations.
 The motivation for this investigation stems from the observed diversity in existing visual pre-training approaches for robotics and the lack of a comprehensive understanding of their comparative effectiveness. A systematic benchmark is needed to guide future research and development in this domain.

2. **Proposal of an Improved Visual Pre-training Scheme:** Based on the empirical findings from the systematic investigation, the paper aims to propose a novel visual pre-training scheme specifically designed for robot manipulation, termed Vi-PRoM (Visual Pre-training scheme for Robot Manipulation). This scheme seeks to combine the strengths of different learning paradigms to acquire comprehensive visual representations.
 The motivation here is to develop a more effective and robust pre-training approach that integrates insights gained from the benchmarking study. This involves leveraging self-supervised learning for extracting underlying patterns from unlabeled data and incorporating supervised learning to foster the perception of visual semantics and temporal dynamics, which are crucial for sequential decision-making in robot tasks.

Additionally, a significant motivation includes the creation of a new dataset, **EgoNet**, designed to serve as a benchmark for pre-training visual models for robot manipulation. This dataset addresses the perceived limitations of general image datasets like ImageNet, which may lack the dynamic human-object interaction content relevant to robotic tasks. By providing a rich dataset of egocentric videos, the authors aim to facilitate the learning of interaction-related knowledge and temporal relationships beneficial for robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research follows a two-stage approach: a systematic benchmarking study of visual pre-training components, followed by the development and evaluation of a novel pre-training scheme, Vi-PRoM, based on the insights gained.

**Benchmarking Setup:**
To evaluate the effectiveness of pre-trained visual encoders, the authors adopt a fixed evaluation pipeline. The visual encoder is pre-trained on various datasets and with different methods, and its parameters are subsequently frozen. A policy network is then trained on robot manipulation tasks using imitation learning (Behavior Cloning) or reinforcement learning (Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO) based on features extracted by the frozen encoder. The performance is measured by the success rate on these tasks.

- **Robot Manipulation Environments:**
 - **Franka Kitchen:** A simulation environment involving complex, long-horizon multi-task manipulations in a kitchen setting.
 - **MetaWorld:** A benchmark for multi-task and meta-reinforcement learning, featuring a variety of manipulation tasks.
 - **Real World:** Experiments are also conducted on a real robot system equipped with a 6-DoF arm, a 2-finger gripper, and an RGB-D camera.

- **Pre-training Datasets:**
 - **ImageNet:** A widely used large-scale image dataset for general object recognition.
 - **EgoNet:** A novel dataset proposed in this paper, constructed from Ego4D. It comprises approximately 500,000 one-second video clips, rich in human-object interactions, collected from egocentric perspectives. The videos are down-sampled to achieve a total of 1.5 million frames, comparable in scale to ImageNet.

- **Model Architectures:**
 - **ResNet-34, ResNet-50, ResNet-101:** Convolutional neural network architectures of varying depths are selected as visual encoders to investigate the impact of model capacity.

- **Pre-training Methods:**
 - **Contrastive Learning:** Implemented using MoCo-v3, which aims to pull together representations of augmented views of the same image while pushing apart representations of different images.
 - **Masked Image Modeling (MIM):** Implemented using MAE (Masked AutoEncoder), which involves reconstructing randomly masked patches of an input image.

**Proposed Approach (Vi-PRoM):**
Based on the observations from the benchmarking study (EgoNet dataset, ResNet-50 architecture, and contrastive learning effectiveness), Vi-PRoM is designed as a cascade visual pre-training scheme.

- **Architecture and Dataset:** Vi-PRoM utilizes a ResNet-50 visual encoder and is pre-trained on the EgoNet dataset.

- **Phase 1: Contrastive Self-supervised Learning:**
 - The visual encoder is initially pre-trained using contrastive learning (MoCo-v3). This phase aims to enable the model to learn universal visual representations and implicitly acquire human-object interaction patterns by distinguishing scenes. The InfoNCE loss is minimized.

- **Phase 2: Supervised Fine-tuning with Auxiliary Tasks:**
 After contrastive pre-training, two additional learning objectives are introduced to further enrich the encoder with visual semantics and temporal dynamics. These objectives do not require manual labeling for EgoNet.
 - **Learning Visual Semantics (L_VS):** A pseudo-label predicting task is employed. A ResNet-101 model pre-trained on ImageNet for classification is used to generate pseudo-labels for the EgoNet video frames. The backbone is then fine-tuned using a cross-entropy loss against these pseudo-labels, with a classification head.
 - **Predicting Temporal Dynamics (L_TD):** A frame order prediction task is designed. Given a sequence of images from a video clip, the images are scrambled, and the model is trained to predict the original order of each image. This is formulated as a classification problem over N possible orders, using a cross-entropy loss with a separate classification head.
 - **Joint Training:** The visual semantics and temporal dynamics losses are combined with a balancing coefficient (λ = 0.33) for joint training (L_total = L_VS + λL_TD).

**Robot Imitation Learning:**
For downstream robot manipulation tasks, the well-trained Vi-PRoM visual encoder is frozen and used to extract features from pixel observations. A simple two-layer perceptron serves as the policy network, trained using Behavior Cloning (BC) or Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) based on expert demonstrations. The demonstration dataset size is typically set to 5 for most experiments, with further analysis on scalability with more demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings derived from the benchmarking study and demonstrates the performance of the proposed Vi-PRoM scheme.

**Benchmarking Observations:**

- **Pre-training Dataset:** EgoNet exhibited superior performance compared to ImageNet for robot manipulation tasks. A ResNet-50 encoder pre-trained with MoCo-v3 on EgoNet achieved success rates of 40.5% in Franka Kitchen and 61.2% in MetaWorld, outperforming the same model pre-trained on ImageNet (31.1% and 54.1%, respectively). This indicates that data rich in human-object interactions and egocentric perspectives are more beneficial for learning robot manipulation representations.
- **Model Architecture:** ResNet-50 performed comparably to ResNet-101 and better than ResNet-34. On EgoNet, ResNet-50 achieved 40.5% in Franka Kitchen and 61.2% in MetaWorld. ResNet-101 showed a marginal improvement in MetaWorld (61.6%) but a slight decrease in Franka Kitchen (40.0%), suggesting diminishing returns beyond ResNet-50 for the evaluated scenarios and data sizes. ResNet-34 lagged significantly (22.6% in Franka Kitchen, 52.4% in MetaWorld).
- **Pre-training Method:** Contrastive learning (MoCo-v3) consistently outperformed Masked Image Modeling (MAE) across both ImageNet and EgoNet datasets. For example, on EgoNet, MoCo-v3 achieved 40.5% and 61.2%, while MAE reached 18.0% and 49.8% in Franka Kitchen and MetaWorld, respectively. This suggests that the visual semantics learned through contrastive learning are more critical for robot manipulation than the structural information learned by masked image modeling.

**Key Conclusions from Benchmarking:**
1. Visual pre-training with human-object interaction data is important for robot manipulation.
2. Convolution-based ResNet-50 is a suitable architecture for retaining visual knowledge.
3. Sequential patterns and semantic information learned by contrastive learning are effective.

**Vi-PRoM Performance:**

- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art (Simulation):** Vi-PRoM consistently outperformed baseline methods across both Franka Kitchen and MetaWorld environments, using both Behavior Cloning (BC) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC):** Vi-PRoM achieved 43.8% in Franka Kitchen and 63.5% in MetaWorld. This represents an improvement of 3.3% and 2.3% over MoCo-v3 (40.5%, 61.2%), and significant gains over R3M (27.4%, 61.7%) and training from scratch (22.3%, 26.5%).
 - **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** Vi-PRoM achieved 39.5% in Franka Kitchen and 46.8% in MetaWorld, similarly outperforming MoCo-v3 (36.8%, 43.6%) and other methods.
 The explicit learning of visual semantics and temporal dynamics in Vi-PRoM contributed to these performance gains.

- **Real Robot Experiments:** Vi-PRoM demonstrated successful performance on a real robot for four kitchen manipulation tasks (opening/closing a door and a drawer). With 30 demonstrations per task, the robot was able to generalize and perform these contact-rich interactions, indicating the practical applicability of the learned representations.

- **Ablation Study on Vi-PRoM Components:** The ablation study confirmed the importance of both visual semantic learning and temporal dynamics learning within Vi-PRoM.
 - Without visual semantic learning, success rates decreased by 3.1% (Franka Kitchen) and 0.9% (MetaWorld).
 - Without temporal dynamics learning, success rates decreased by 0.6% (Franka Kitchen) and 1.5% (MetaWorld).
 The combined absence of these objectives led to a substantial performance degradation, validating their collaborative effectiveness.

- **Model Scalability:** Vi-PRoM exhibited scalability with respect to the amount of demonstration data. As the size of the demonstration dataset increased in both Franka Kitchen and MetaWorld, the success rate of Vi-PRoM showed a steady improvement, suggesting its ability to leverage more data effectively when available.

- **Comparison with Other Pre-trained Models:** Vi-PRoM outperformed a range of other popular pre-trained vision models (e.g., ImageNet Supervised ResNet-50, MDETR, CLIP, MAE, MVP) when used as frozen visual encoders for robot manipulation tasks, further highlighting its specialized effectiveness for this domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robot learning by systematically investigating key components of visual pre-training and proposing an effective scheme. The findings provide empirical guidance for future work:

- **Data-Centric Design:** The identification of human-object interaction data (as exemplified by EgoNet) as a crucial factor for robot manipulation representation learning is a significant insight. This suggests a shift from general image datasets to interaction-rich video data for pre-training. The creation of EgoNet itself serves as a new benchmark resource.
- **Architectural Efficiency:** The finding that ResNet-50 performs comparably to larger ResNet models for robot manipulation suggests that deeper architectures might not always be necessary, potentially allowing for more computationally efficient deployment.
- **Effective Learning Objectives:** The demonstrated effectiveness of contrastive learning over masked image modeling, combined with the benefits of explicitly learning visual semantics and temporal dynamics, offers a refined understanding of what types of representations are most valuable for sequential robot tasks. This guides the design of pre-training objectives for robotics.
- **Improved Data Efficiency for Robot Learning:** The proposed Vi-PRoM scheme, by leveraging these insights, achieves higher performance in robot manipulation tasks compared to existing methods, particularly when learning from limited demonstrations. This improvement in data efficiency has the potential to reduce the extensive data collection requirements for deploying robots in real-world scenarios, making robot learning more accessible and scalable.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment and demonstration of Vi-PRoM on a real robot for contact-rich manipulation tasks validates its practical utility, indicating that the learned representations generalize to physical environments.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the potential for directly training visual encoders on raw video clips to learn temporal dynamics more effectively, and the need for larger pre-training datasets. The observed sim-to-real gap, where models pre-trained on real-world data are evaluated in simulation, also points to the importance of establishing real-world evaluation benchmarks to further facilitate research and ensure robust performance in diverse environments. Overall, this work provides a framework and empirical evidence that can guide the development of more capable and efficient robot manipulation systems.
