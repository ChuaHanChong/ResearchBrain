# ANYmal Parkour: Learning Agile Navigation for Quadrupedal Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich and NVIDIA developed a fully-learned, hierarchical control system that enables the ANYmal D quadrupedal robot to autonomously perform agile navigation in complex, parkour-like environments. The system, trained entirely in simulation, integrates perception, diverse locomotion skills, and high-level navigation planning to achieve speeds up to 2 m/s while dynamically overcoming obstacles like gaps and ledges up to 1 meter in height.

## Problem

- Enabling quadrupedal robots to achieve agile, human-like navigation in complex, obstacle-rich environments like those found in parkour.
- Traditional model predictive control (MPC) methods struggle with highly dynamic, non-periodic motions and varied contacts, while prior learning-based approaches often require expert demonstrations or simplified models.
- Integrating robust perception, diverse locomotion skills, and intelligent real-time planning into a cohesive, autonomous system for unknown, dynamic terrains.

## Method

- A fully-learned ==hierarchical architecture== comprising three interconnected modules: Perception, Locomotion (low-level skills), and Navigation (high-level planning).
- The ==Perception module== uses a ==multi-resolution neural network== to create a compact ==latent tensor== of the 3D environment from sensor data, with ==auto-regressive feedback== for occluded regions.
- Five distinct ==locomotion skills== (walking, jumping, climbing up/down, crouching) are trained via ==deep reinforcement learning== with tailored rewards, ==curriculum learning==, and ==symmetry augmentation==, and a ==navigation policy== (a modified ==PPO== with ==skill selection==) learns to command these skills to reach a global target.

## Results

- The ANYmal D robot autonomously navigates diverse, randomized obstacle courses at speeds up to **2 m/s**, performing complex maneuvers like jumping over **1m** gaps and climbing **1m** obstacles.
- The learned high-level navigation policy achieves a **96.3%** success rate on complex terrains, outperforming manually hard-coded trajectories (**60.9%**) by dynamically selecting skills and adjusting paths.
- The perception module accurately reconstructs 3D terrain from noisy, occluded sensor data, including vertical surfaces and overhangs, and is robust to robot state estimation drift, enabling continuous scene understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fully-learned hierarchical system enables effective integration of perception, diverse low-level locomotion skills, and high-level navigation planning.
> - Massively parallel deep reinforcement learning in simulation, combined with techniques like symmetry augmentation and curriculum learning, facilitates robust sim-to-real transfer.
> - The navigation policy implicitly learns the capabilities and limitations of low-level skills from their roll-outs, allowing it to select appropriate behaviors and adapt paths in real-time based on environmental perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robotics strives to enable robots to traverse complex, unstructured, and dynamic terrains with the agility of humans or animals. This paper directly addresses a significant challenge within this ambition: **agile navigation in highly dynamic, obstacle-rich environments**. It positions itself against several existing paradigms and highlights its unique contributions.

Traditionally, legged robot locomotion has relied on **model predictive control (MPC)**, which can achieve precise foot placement and motion but is often limited by underlying model accuracy, assumptions about contact schedules, and computational complexity, making it less suitable for highly dynamic, non-periodic motions or varied contacts (e.g., using knees or shanks). Furthermore, MPC-based navigation often requires offline computation or a priori knowledge of the environment.

More recently, **deep reinforcement learning (DRL)** has emerged as a powerful alternative for robust and perceptive locomotion, demonstrating impressive feats like running, jumping, and recovery from falls. However, while individual skills have advanced, integrating them into a cohesive navigation system for complex, sequential tasks, especially in novel environments, remains a challenge. Previous hierarchical learning approaches for navigation either relied on human-designed paths and skill selection (e.g., in dog agility competitions) or simplified kinematic models in the inner loop, limiting their applicability to highly agile and dynamic scenarios.

**Perception for legged robots** has also seen evolution, with elevation maps being a common representation. However, these 2.5D maps struggle with overhangs, occlusions, and state estimation drift, which are prevalent in parkour-like environments. While signed distance fields (SDFs) offer full 3D representation, their integration with real-time planning and learning remains complex.

This work distinguishes itself by proposing a **fully-learned, end-to-end hierarchical system** that combines advanced locomotion, navigation, and perception modules. Crucially, it tackles the entire parkour challenge **without expert demonstration, offline computation, or a priori knowledge of the environment**, and critically, it operates in real-time onboard the robot. By achieving this, the paper bridges gaps between isolated skill learning and holistic agile navigation, pushing beyond the limitations of purely model-based methods and addressing the integration challenges faced by previous learning-based approaches. It sets a new benchmark for autonomous agile locomotion and navigation on quadrupedal platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable **agile navigation for quadrupedal robots in challenging scenarios reminiscent of parkour**. The authors aim to push the boundaries of legged robot capabilities by developing a system that can effectively navigate complex, dynamic environments requiring a diverse set of behaviors beyond simple walking.

The core motivations driving this objective include:

- **Matching Human/Animal Agility:** Inspired by free-runners, the paper aims to understand and overcome the limitations in robot perception, actuation, and control pipelines to achieve comparable agility and nimbleness. This pursuit drives advancements across all robotic sub-disciplines.
- **Expanding Application Domains:** By increasing robot capabilities, the work paves the way for new applications, particularly in hazardous and complex terrains. Examples given include search and rescue operations in collapsed buildings or navigation in complex natural environments, where current robot capabilities are often insufficient.
- **Addressing Fundamental Robotics Challenges:** The task of parkour-like navigation inherently exacerbates several common challenges in mobile robotics:
 - **Complex Locomotion:** Requiring highly dynamic, non-periodic motions and varied limb contacts beyond standard gaits.
 - **Robust State Estimation:** Prone to drift due to high-impact forces and varied contacts.
 - **Perception under Occlusion:** Dealing with partial, noisy views and self-occlusions inherent to dynamic movements.
 - **Intelligent Planning:** The need for a planner that understands the robot's kinematic and dynamic capabilities, as well as the limitations of its low-level controllers, to produce feasible trajectories in real-time.
 - **Low Latency:** Critical for fast, dynamic motions to prevent catastrophic failures.

By proposing a fully-learned, hierarchical approach, the authors aim to overcome these challenges, enabling the ANYmal robot to autonomously perceive, plan, and execute complex sequences of agile movements to reach a target location swiftly and efficiently.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a **fully-learned, hierarchical approach** decomposed into three interconnected learning-based modules: Perception, Locomotion, and Navigation. All modules are trained in simulation and successfully transferred to the real-world ANYmal D robot.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Architecture Overview

The system operates with an outer loop (Navigation Module at 5 Hz) controlling an inner loop (Locomotion Module at 50 Hz). The Perception Module runs asynchronously on a dedicated processing unit, providing scene understanding to both other modules.

#### 3.2. Perception Module

- **Goal:** Reconstruct the terrain around the robot from noisy, occluded point cloud measurements (from depth cameras and LiDAR) and produce a compact "latent tensor" representing the scene's belief state.
- **Approach:** A neural network with an encoder-decoder architecture inspired by previous work.
 - **Multi-resolution Scheme:** Consists of two networks operating at different scales:
 - **Coarse-resolution network:** Produces a larger-scale, lower-resolution map (4m range, 12.5 cm voxels) for broader scene understanding for navigation. It incorporates auto-regressive feedback, using previous outputs to accumulate evidence over time and reconstruct occluded elements (e.g., top of a table when crawling underneath).
 - **High-resolution network (Refiner):** Uses features from the coarse network and current measurements to generate a precise, higher-resolution map (2m range, 6.25 cm voxels) crucial for local locomotion decisions.
 - **Input/Output:** Point cloud measurements are converted to a dense voxel grid. The network outputs voxel occupancy probability and centroid positions.
 - **Training:** Unsupervised training from simulated data (2000 trajectories) using binary cross-entropy for occupancy and Euclidean distance for centroids. Data augmentation (perturbing points, adding noise, robot position noise) is crucial for robustness to real-world inaccuracies and drift.
 - **Implementation:** Custom CUDA kernels and NVIDIA Warp for efficient raycasting and voxel grid conversion, enabling parallel training with 4096 robots in Isaac Gym.

#### 3.3. Locomotion Module

- **Goal:** Provide a catalog of specialized low-level locomotion skills that can overcome specific terrains, responding to intermediate commands from the navigation module.
- **Approach:** Five distinct policies, each trained separately using deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for specific skills:
 - **Walking:** For irregular terrains (stairs, slopes, small obstacles).
 - **Jumping:** Over gaps up to 1m.
 - **Climbing Down:** From boxes up to 1m height.
 - **Climbing Up:** Onto boxes up to 1m height.
 - **Crouching:** Under narrow passages (min. 0.4m height).
- **Training Details:**
 - **Position-based commands:** Unlike velocity tracking, the robot must reach a target position and heading within a given time.
 - **Observations:** Proprioceptive state, a 2.5D elevation map (derived from the perception module's output, with added noise for robustness), and intermediate commands.
 - **Rewards & Termination Conditions:** Tailored for each skill to encourage desired behaviors and prevent damage (e.g., high impact force penalty for climbing down).
 - **Curriculum Learning:** Used for jumping, climbing up/down, and crouching to progressively increase obstacle difficulty.
 - **Symmetry Augmentation:** A novel technique applied during training to overcome issues of asymmetric motions by duplicating environment transitions with symmetric variants, boosting policy robustness and natural gaits.
 - **Input Map:** Uses a 2.5D elevation map, which is computationally cheaper for high-frequency control, perturbed to bridge the reality gap.

#### 3.4. Navigation Module

- **Goal:** Guide the robot around the terrain to reach a global target within an allocated time by selecting the appropriate locomotion skill and providing it with local commands (position, heading, time).
- **Approach:** A high-level policy trained with reinforcement learning in a hierarchical setup, where the low-level locomotion policies are frozen.
- **Architecture:** Uses a modified PPO algorithm with a hybrid actor output: a Gaussian distribution for local commands (position, heading, time) and a categorical distribution for skill selection.
- **Observations:** Relative global goal position, remaining global time, robot base velocity/orientation, and the latent tensor from the Perception Module (providing scene understanding).
- **Rewards:** Sparse reward based on reaching the global goal, with penalties for falling or high contact forces.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Progressively increases the distance of global targets to aid convergence.
- **Key Aspect:** The navigation policy learns to make informed decisions by implicitly understanding the capabilities and limitations of each low-level skill based on the perceptive input and the roll-out of the actual low-level policies during training. This allows it to adapt paths (e.g., taking a longer route for a too-high obstacle) and precisely adjust local commands.

#### 3.5. Simulation and Sim-to-Real Transfer

All modules are trained entirely in the Isaac Gym simulator, leveraging its GPU-accelerated physics engine for massively parallel training. The trained neural networks are then directly deployed on the physical ANYmal D robot, demonstrating robust sim-to-real transfer without further fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates significant advancements in agile navigation for quadrupedal robots, validating the effectiveness of their fully-learned hierarchical approach through extensive simulated and real-world experiments.

#### 4.1. Overall System Performance

- **Autonomous Navigation:** The complete pipeline enables the ANYmal D robot to reliably reach target locations across diverse, randomized obstacle arrangements without expert demonstration, offline computation, or prior environmental knowledge.
- **High Agility and Speed:** The robot achieves speeds of up to 2 m/s while navigating challenging terrains, performing fast accelerations and decelerations.
- **Dynamic Decision-Making:** The system makes real-time, context-aware decisions regarding path planning and skill selection.
- **Robustness:** The robot demonstrates recovery from disturbances, such as falls and slippage due to low friction, and can adapt its trajectory when obstacles are dynamically removed.

#### 4.2. Locomotion Skill Performance

- **Specialized and Robust Behaviors:** Each of the five learned skills exhibits highly specialized and effective behaviors:
 - **Jumping:** Approaches gaps sideways, uses three legs for propulsion, one for landing, robustly recovers from missteps.
 - **Climbing Down:** Lowers center of gravity, uses knees as hooks, lands gently on front legs, robust to perceived terrain shifts.
 - **Climbing Up:** Lifts body using front feet, re-positions, balances with hind legs, and can utilize knees for recovery if feet slip.
 - **Crouching:** Lowers base while walking, adapts gait, uses hip motors to lift feet, demonstrating expected behavior for overhangs.
 - **Walking:** Generalizes well to diverse irregular terrains (stairs, slopes, small obstacles), achieving high speeds on flat ground.
- **High Success Rates:** All skills perform successfully up to 90% of their maximum training difficulty, with walking generalizing well beyond. The performance drops sharply near the physical limits of the robot or perception (e.g., crouching in passages narrower than robot height).
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** Low-level skills show an ability to cope with more intricate scenarios than explicitly trained for (e.g., jumping skill adapting to narrow boxes).

#### 4.3. Navigation Policy Behaviors

- **Terrain-Adapted Path Selection:** The high-level policy can select sub-goals and adapt its path based on the instantaneous 3D information from the perception module's latent space, considering obstacle dimensions. For instance, it chooses a longer but safer path for obstacles that are too high to climb directly.
- **Intelligent Skill Switching and Control:** The navigation module smoothly selects the most appropriate locomotion skill for the terrain and effectively commands it (position, heading, time). It learns to exploit unexpected strengths of skills (e.g., using the jumping skill for quick turns in narrow spaces).
- **Superior to Manual Control:** Quantitative comparison against manually hard-coded trajectories shows the learned navigation policy achieving higher success rates, especially in more complex scenarios (e.g., 96.3% vs. 60.9% on one terrain). It also dynamically adjusts targets, leading to higher speeds than fixed manual targets.

#### 4.4. Perception Module Robustness

- **Accurate 3D Reconstruction:** The module effectively processes noisy and occluded point clouds to produce accurate 3D scene layouts, including vertical surfaces and top surfaces of high obstacles, which traditional elevation maps cannot represent.
- **Multi-resolution Effectiveness:** The combination of coarse and high-resolution outputs balances map size with accuracy, providing a broad view while maintaining precision near the robot.
- **Robust to State Estimation Drift:** The network demonstrates the ability to detect and quickly correct for robot position estimate drift, unlike baseline elevation mapping methods that show artifacts from such inaccuracies.
- **Auto-regressive Feedback:** Enables the module to "remember" and reconstruct occluded regions (e.g., the top of a table when the robot is underneath), crucial for continuous scene understanding.

#### 4.5. Real-world Validation

The successful deployment of all simulation-trained modules on the ANYmal D robot under demanding indoor and outdoor conditions underscores the effectiveness of the sim-to-real transfer and the robustness of the learned policies and perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a groundbreaking achievement in quadrupedal robotics, demonstrating a level of agility and autonomous navigation previously unseen in complex, unstructured environments. The significance and potential impact are substantial:

#### 5.1. Achieved Capabilities

- **Unprecedented Agility:** The ANYmal robot exhibits complex, dynamic maneuvers, including climbing, jumping, and crouching, integrated seamlessly into a continuous navigation task. This moves beyond isolated skill demonstrations to truly agile, continuous locomotion.
- **Fully Autonomous and Real-time:** A core contribution is the elimination of expert demonstrations, offline computation, or a priori knowledge of the environment for complex path planning and skill execution. All computations happen onboard the robot in real-time, making it highly practical for dynamic, unknown environments.
- **Holistic Learning-Based Approach:** By integrating learning across perception, low-level control, and high-level navigation, the system transcends the limitations of traditional model-based methods and pre-programmed behaviors. The complexity of the problem is effectively "shifted to the learning stage," yielding compact, efficient networks for deployment.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The training methodology, including extensive randomization, curriculum learning, and clever data augmentation (e.g., symmetry for locomotion, noise for perception), leads to highly robust policies capable of handling real-world noise, disturbances, and even some out-of-distribution scenarios.

#### 5.2. Broader Implications

- **Advancing Legged Robotics:** This work significantly pushes the state of the art in legged robot locomotion and navigation, bringing them closer to the agility and versatility of biological systems.
- **Real-world Applications:** The demonstrated capabilities are directly relevant to challenging real-world applications where traditional wheeled or tracked robots struggle. This includes:
 - **Search and Rescue:** Navigating rubble and complex debris in disaster zones.
 - **Inspection and Exploration:** Traversing industrial sites, construction areas, or natural terrains that are unsafe or inaccessible for humans.
 - **Defense and Security:** Operating in varied and unpredictable combat or surveillance environments.
- **Paradigm Shift in Robot Autonomy:** The success of this fully-learned, hierarchical, sim-to-real approach provides a compelling case for designing complex robotic systems primarily through learning-based methodologies, leveraging the power of high-fidelity simulation and parallel training.

#### 5.3. Current Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive achievements, the paper acknowledges several limitations, which also outline avenues for future research:
- **Scalability to Broader Scenarios:** The current demonstration is within a limited range of structured obstacles. Scaling to highly diverse and truly unstructured environments (e.g., a real collapsed building) would require a wider variety of skills and greater generalization capabilities from all modules.
- **Training Complexity and Interdependencies:** Training eight interdependent neural networks is time-consuming and requires careful tuning. Modifying one module often necessitates retraining others. Future work could explore simultaneous or more integrated training strategies to address this.
- **Navigation Policy Convergence:** The high-level navigation policy's convergence can be slow due to the sparse rewards and vast possibility space. Research into pre-training with expert demonstrations or more sophisticated curriculum learning techniques could accelerate this.
- **Perception Blind Spots/Errors:** While robust, the perception module can still occasionally produce erroneous reconstructions (e.g., hallucinating stairs, inflating obstacle shapes). Improving perception under extreme occlusion and state estimation inaccuracies remains an area for improvement.

In conclusion, "ANYmal Parkour" is a landmark paper that showcases the immense potential of deep reinforcement learning and hierarchical control for enabling truly agile and autonomous navigation in complex environments. It not only delivers unprecedented performance on quadrupedal robots but also provides a robust framework and valuable insights for future development in intelligent robotic systems.
