# Router-R1: Teaching LLMs Multi-Round Routing and Aggregation via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Router-R1, developed by researchers at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, introduces a reinforcement learning framework that trains an LLM to perform multi-round routing and aggregation of other LLMs for complex tasks. This approach enables dynamic coordination of multiple models, yielding improved performance on diverse QA benchmarks, robust generalization to unseen models and data, and controllable trade-offs between accuracy and computational cost.

## Problem

- Existing LLM routing strategies are limited to single-round, one-to-one model selection, failing to address tasks requiring iterative interaction and complementary strengths of multiple LLMs.
- Complex reasoning tasks, such as multi-hop question answering, demand a sequential orchestration of multiple LLMs, which single-shot routing cannot provide.
- Optimizing discrete routing decisions in a multi-round, sequential process is intractable for traditional gradient-based end-to-end training methods.

## Method

- Router-R1 formulates ==multi-LLM routing and aggregation as a sequential decision process==, optimizing an LLM acting as the router using a ==cost-aware reinforcement learning (PPO) framework==.
- The router LLM interleaves internal 'think' actions with external 'route' actions to dynamically select and invoke candidate LLMs, integrating their responses over multiple rounds to refine the answer.
- A ==hierarchical reward function== includes format adherence, final answer correctness (Exact Match), and a novel ==cost reward== to balance task performance with the computational cost of LLM invocations.

## Results

- Router-R1 consistently outperformed over **ten competitive baselines** across **seven diverse QA benchmarks**, achieving average Exact Match scores of **0.416** (Qwen) and **0.409** (Llama).
- It demonstrated robust generalization, maintaining strong performance on **five out-of-domain** QA datasets and effectively incorporating unseen LLM candidates without additional fine-tuning.
- The framework learned controllable performance-cost trade-offs, enabling reduced average LLM invocation cost while maintaining competitive accuracy by prioritizing cheaper models when appropriate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning effectively teaches an LLM to dynamically coordinate other LLMs in a multi-round fashion, enabling complex problem decomposition and iterative answer refinement.
> - Introducing a cost reward allows the router to learn strategies that balance answer quality with the computational expense of querying different LLMs, creating controllable performance-cost trade-offs.
> - An LLM acting as a router can generalize to unseen candidate LLMs without retraining by leveraging simple textual descriptors of their capabilities and costs provided at inference time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid proliferation of diverse large language models has led to the emergence of "LLM routers," which aim to efficiently direct user queries to the most suitable single LLM from a pool of candidates. Prior work in this area, such as HybridLLM, GraphRouter, FrugalGPT, RouterDC, and RouteLLM, has primarily focused on *single-round, one-to-one mapping*. These existing routers typically make a single dispatch decision: given a query, they select one model, collect its output, and stop. While this approach can improve average performance and cost efficiency for many tasks, it fundamentally overlooks the complexity of real-world reasoning.

The paper argues that complex tasks, such as multi-hop question answering or intricate problem-solving, often demand more than a single model's isolated strength. Instead, they require the *complementary strengths* of multiple LLMs, orchestrated through a sequence of interactions. This limitation of conventional single-shot routing creates a significant gap in the current LLM ecosystem. Furthermore, the discrete nature of selecting an LLM at each step of a multi-round process makes traditional gradient-based end-to-end training intractable, a challenge that many prior gradient-based single-shot routers do not fully address in a multi-round context.

Router-R1 positions itself within the broader landscape of "Optimizing LLM Behaviors via Reinforcement Learning." This field has seen significant advancements with techniques like RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), RLAIF (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback), RRHF, and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). These methods use RL to fine-tune LLMs for better alignment with human preferences or specific objectives. More recently, RL has been used to empower LLMs to interact adaptively with external tools, as seen in Search-R1, which trains LLMs to dynamically retrieve information from search engines. Router-R1 builds upon this paradigm by extending it to dynamic interaction with a *pool of other LLMs*.

What differentiates Router-R1 is its novel formulation of multi-LLM routing as a *sequential decision process*, where the router itself is an LLM. Unlike single-shot routers, Router-R1 dynamically interleaves internal reasoning ("think" actions) with external model invocations ("route" actions), gradually refining its understanding and answer. This "LLM-as-router" approach, combined with a cost-aware RL framework, represents a significant step beyond existing methods, enabling richer, more adaptive, and cost-efficient multi-model coordination that was previously unaddressed.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Router-R1 stems from the inherent limitations of existing LLM routing strategies when confronted with complex reasoning tasks. The authors identify a crucial unmet need: how to effectively coordinate multiple LLMs in a multi-round routing and aggregation process to jointly solve tasks that require more than a single, isolated model call.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Multi-Round Routing and Aggregation Framework**: To move beyond single-shot routing and enable an LLM to dynamically select and interact with multiple specialized LLMs over several rounds, building an answer incrementally.
2. **Facilitate Interleaved Reasoning and Routing**: To instantiate the router itself as a capable LLM, leveraging its intrinsic reasoning abilities to interleave internal deliberation ("think" actions) with external model invocation ("route" actions). This allows the router to dynamically assess needs, select appropriate models, and integrate their responses into an evolving context.
3. **Address Non-Differentiability with Reinforcement Learning**: To tackle the challenge of optimizing discrete routing decisions, the framework adopts reinforcement learning, which is well-suited for sequential decision-making in non-differentiable environments.
4. **Optimize Performance-Cost Trade-offs**: To introduce a novel *cost reward* component into the RL framework. This motivates the router to learn strategies that balance answer quality with the computational cost of invoking various LLMs, enabling more resource-aware deployments.
5. **Ensure Robust Generalization**: To design the system such that it can generalize effectively to unseen LLM candidates without requiring retraining. This is achieved by conditioning routing decisions on simple, interpretable model descriptors like pricing, latency, and example performance.

By achieving these objectives, Router-R1 aims to enable LLM systems to tackle more intricate and demanding tasks, fostering a new generation of adaptive and resource-efficient multi-LLM architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Router-R1 formulates the problem of multi-LLM routing and aggregation as a sequential decision process, which is optimized using reinforcement learning. The core of its methodology revolves around an LLM acting as the router, capable of both internal deliberation and external model invocation.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning Formulation

The framework optimizes a policy LLM (denoted `π`) to act as a coordinator. The objective is to maximize an expected reward `E[r_ϕ(x, y) - β log(π(y|x;P) / π_ref(y|x;P))]`, where `x` is the input query, `y` is the generated output (potentially interleaved with external LLM responses), `r_ϕ(x, y)` is the reward function, `P` is the LLM routing pool, and `π_ref` is a reference LLM used for stable training via KL regularization. This general objective allows for various RL algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which is used in Router-R1.

#### 3.2. Reward Curation

A lightweight yet effective rule-based reward function `r_ϕ(x, y)` is designed with a hierarchical structure to provide clear supervision signals:

1. **Format Rewards (`R_format`)**: This is the highest priority reward. It assigns `-1` if the generated output does not conform to a predefined structured format (e.g., incorrect tag usage for `<think>`, `<search>`, `<info>`, `<answer>`) and `0` otherwise. This is crucial for stabilizing training and preventing "reward hacking" or nonsensical outputs.
2. **Final Outcome Rewards (`R_outcome`)**: This component measures the correctness of the final answer using Exact Match (EM) against the ground truth. It directly guides the model towards task success.
3. **Cost Rewards (`R_cost`)**: This novel component penalizes the computational cost incurred by invoking candidate LLMs. It is inversely proportional to the product of a model-dependent cost function `m(P_LLM)` (reflecting pricing tiers) and the number of output tokens `T_out`. The cost reward is normalized using a sliding window approach (Appendix A.1.3) and inverted, such that higher costs lead to lower rewards. This enables Router-R1 to learn performance-cost trade-offs.

The overall reward is defined as `r_ϕ(x, y) = R_format + (1 - α)R_outcome + αR_cost`, where `α` is a hyperparameter controlling the balance between task performance and cost. If `R_format` is -1, other rewards are nullified, enforcing strict format adherence.

#### 3.3. Multi-Round Interaction Training Paradigm

Router-R1 is trained using a structured prompt template (Figure 2, Appendix C) that guides its multi-round interaction:
- **Internal Reasoning (`<think>...</think>`)**: Upon receiving a question, Router-R1 first deliberates internally to assess information needs.
- **External Model Invocation (`<search> Candidate LLM: Query </search>`)**: If more information is required, Router-R1 selects a suitable candidate LLM from a pool and formulates a sub-query. Model descriptors (e.g., parameter size, task specialization, pricing, latency) provided in the prompt act as initial "cold-start priors" (Appendix A.1.1).
- **Information Integration (`<info>...</info>`)**: The response from the invoked LLM is returned and integrated into the Router-R1's evolving context.
- **Iterative Refinement**: This process of thinking, routing, and integrating information can be repeated for multiple rounds (up to 4 steps in experiments), allowing Router-R1 to decompose complex problems and progressively refine its answer.
- **Final Answer (`<answer>...</answer>`)**: Once no further external knowledge is needed, Router-R1 provides its final answer.

An important aspect of this design is its ability to generalize to unseen LLM candidates without retraining. By providing new model descriptions directly in the inference prompt, Router-R1 can dynamically infer their capabilities and integrate them into its routing strategy.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base LLMs**: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct.
- **Training**: PPO algorithm via the `veRL` framework, trained on a joint dataset of 7K samples from Natural Questions (NQ) and 7K from HotpotQA (HpQA).
- **LLM Routing Pool**: A diverse set of six models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Mixtral-8x22B-Instruct, and Gemma-2-27B-Instruct.
- **Evaluation**: Seven QA datasets (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA for general QA; HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle for multi-hop QA), using Exact Match (EM) and F1-Score metrics.

This comprehensive methodology allows Router-R1 to learn sophisticated routing behaviors, adapt to varying task complexities, and manage resource constraints effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis of Router-R1 yielded several key findings demonstrating its superior performance, robustness, and generalization capabilities.

1. **Superior Overall Performance**: Router-R1 consistently outperformed all basic baselines (Direct, CoT, SFT, RAG, Search-R1) and over ten competitive query-based LLM router baselines (e.g., Prompt LLM, Largest LLM, KNN Router, RouterDC, GraphRouter) across seven diverse QA benchmarks. For instance, Router-R1-Qwen achieved an average Exact Match (EM) score of 0.416, and Router-R1-Llama achieved 0.409, setting new state-of-the-art performance for these tasks. This superiority is attributed to its core design of multi-round routing and interleaved reasoning, allowing for adaptive coordination and iterative refinement of answers.
2. **Robust Generalization to Unseen Data**: Despite being trained solely on in-domain samples from NQ and HotpotQA, Router-R1 demonstrated strong performance on five out-of-domain QA datasets (TriviaQA, PopQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle). This indicates that Router-R1 learns transferable routing and reasoning strategies, capable of generalizing effectively across diverse knowledge-intensive tasks.
3. **Controllable Performance-Cost Trade-offs**: The analysis of cost rewards (by varying the `α` coefficient) showed that Router-R1 can dynamically adjust its routing behavior to balance accuracy and computational expense. As `α` increased, the model prioritized cost efficiency, leading to a reduction in average LLM invocation cost while maintaining competitive accuracy. This emergent strategy involved querying smaller, cheaper models first and only escalating to larger, more expensive ones when necessary, thereby creating a Pareto-style frontier of performance vs. cost.
4. **Strong Generalization to Unseen Candidate LLMs**: Router-R1 exhibited a remarkable ability to incorporate and leverage previously unseen LLM candidates (e.g., Palmyra-Creative-122B and LLaMA3-ChatQA-1.5-8B) at inference time without any additional fine-tuning. By simply updating their textual descriptors in the prompt, Router-R1 maintained and even slightly improved its performance, highlighting its descriptor-based reasoning capability to infer and utilize new models effectively.
5. **Adaptive LLM API Call Frequency**: The framework demonstrated adaptive behavior in resource utilization. On multi-hop QA benchmarks, Router-R1 made significantly more average LLM API calls compared to general QA tasks. This suggests that Router-R1 can assess task difficulty and selectively invoke external LLMs only when more complex reasoning or external knowledge is required.
6. **Rapid and Stable Training Convergence**: Router-R1 showed rapid convergence during training, achieving stable performance within approximately 100 steps. The hierarchical reward design, particularly the format rewards, proved critical for this stability, preventing frequent formatting breakdowns and ensuring reliable learning.
7. **Robustness to Model Descriptor Richness and Dynamic Pool Evolution**: Further sensitivity analysis showed that simplifying model descriptors had only a negligible impact on performance, indicating robustness. Moreover, incorporating new candidate LLMs *during* training also resulted in stable or slightly improved performance, further validating its adaptability to evolving model ecosystems.

In summary, Router-R1 delivers state-of-the-art performance across diverse QA tasks by effectively coordinating multiple LLMs. Its ability to generalize to unseen data and models, coupled with its cost-aware routing, underscores its practical utility and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Router-R1 presents a significant advancement in the field of large language model orchestration and coordination, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Complex Task Solving**: By formulating multi-LLM routing and aggregation as a sequential decision process, Router-R1 provides a principled way for LLM systems to tackle inherently complex tasks that demand the complementary strengths and iterative interactions of multiple specialized models. This moves beyond the limitations of single-shot routing, unlocking new capabilities for applications in complex reasoning, multi-hop question answering, and beyond.
2. **Bridging Reasoning and Tool Use**: The innovation of instantiating the router itself as a capable LLM, interleaving "think" (internal deliberation) and "route" (external model invocation) actions, represents a crucial step towards more sophisticated AI agents. It leverages the inherent reasoning capabilities of LLMs to intelligently decide when and how to utilize external tools (in this case, other LLMs), fostering a more adaptive and intelligent form of LLM agentic behavior.
3. **Cost-Efficiency and Resource Management**: The introduction of a novel cost reward mechanism is highly significant for practical deployment. It allows Router-R1 to learn flexible performance-cost trade-offs, making it possible to achieve strong performance while minimizing the use of expensive models. This has direct implications for reducing operational costs and potentially the environmental footprint of large-scale LLM applications.
4. **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability**: Router-R1's ability to generalize to unseen data and, crucially, to *unseen LLM candidates without retraining*, is a major breakthrough. By conditioning on simple model descriptors, it offers unparalleled adaptability to the rapidly evolving LLM ecosystem. This drastically reduces the overhead of integrating new, specialized models into existing routing systems, accelerating innovation and deployment.
5. **Promoting Modular and Composable AI**: Router-R1's architecture encourages the view of LLMs as modular components that can be composed and coordinated. This fosters a future where AI systems are built by orchestrating a diverse array of specialized models, rather than relying on a single monolithic LLM, thereby promoting system flexibility, maintainability, and reusability.
6. **Broader Impacts**: Beyond immediate technical benefits, Router-R1 contributes to more efficient and scalable AI deployments. By optimizing for cost, it can lower financial barriers to using powerful LLMs. Its modular nature and generalization capabilities can accelerate research and development in collaborative AI, allowing researchers and developers to quickly integrate and experiment with new models.

While the current evaluation focuses on QA tasks and uses rule-based rewards, the framework lays a strong foundation. Future work could explore its application to other domains like dialogue, summarization, or code generation, integrate more nuanced learned reward functions, and address inference latency. Nevertheless, Router-R1 represents a substantial leap towards building more intelligent, adaptive, and resource-aware LLM systems.
