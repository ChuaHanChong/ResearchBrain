# Structured World Models from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> SWIM (Structured World Models for Intentionality) learns real-world robot manipulation by pre-training a Dreamer-style latent world model on 50k egocentric human videos in a morphology-invariant affordance action space of grasp and post-grasp image points, then fine-tuning reward-free on under 30 minutes of robot data and planning to goal images with CEM, reaching ~79% average success across six tasks and two robots.

## Problem

- Robot policies trained with engineered rewards or task-specific demonstrations do not scale, transfer poorly to new tasks, and demand expensive real-world interaction data.
- World models for control are bottlenecked by scarce action-annotated robot data, so prior robot dynamics models stay narrow and generalize weakly across tasks.
- Prior human-video methods extract only visual representations, not actionable signals, since low-level human actions cannot cross the human-robot morphology gap.

## Method

- Structured human-centric ==affordance action space== of image-space grasp and post-grasp waypoints plus depth, distilled by an ==affordance model== trained on hand-object detections from egocentric human video.
- Hybrid action parameterization mixing affordance mode (pixel plus depth targets) with Cartesian end-effector delta mode, letting one policy switch between abstract object interaction and fine control.
- Three-phase **SWIM** pipeline: ==Dreamer== world model pre-trained on Epic-Kitchens, reward-free fine-tuning on robot rollouts, then ==CEM planning== over affordance and GMM action proposals toward a goal image.

## Results

- Averaged **~79%** success over six real-world tasks on a Franka arm and a Hello Stretch mobile manipulator, versus **~40%** for the strongest baselines, using under 30 minutes of robot interaction.
- Ablations show structured affordance actions give over **5x** higher success than Cartesian actions and roughly double random pixel actions; removing human-video pre-training collapses joint-task performance.
- Joint multi-task world model beat per-task models (**79%** vs **75%**), and iterative retraining on deployment data pushed some tasks above **90%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choosing an action space that is semantically meaningful and morphology-invariant buys more sample efficiency than more data or bigger policies.
> - Passive human video can supply dynamics, not just representations, once actions are re-expressed in a space both humans and robots share.
> - Pre-trained world models make multi-task joint training a gain rather than interference; without pre-training, sharing data across tasks actively hurts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is actively striving to develop "general-purpose" robots capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured environments, much like humans. However, this goal faces significant hurdles. The current paradigm typically involves training policies with engineered rewards or task-specific demonstrations, which is not scalable, difficult to transfer to new tasks, and expensive due to the need for extensive real-world robot interaction data.

This paper addresses these challenges by strategically integrating several cutting-edge research directions:

- **World Models for Robot Control:** World models, which learn the dynamics of an agent and its environment, have shown promise for planning and sample-efficient learning. However, their application in robotics has been limited by the scarcity of action-annotated robot interaction data. This work aims to overcome this data limitation.
- **Leveraging Large-Scale Passive Data (Human Videos):** Inspired by the success of large-scale datasets in computer vision and natural language processing, researchers are increasingly looking towards the internet's vast collection of human videos. These videos offer rich information about human-world interactions. Previous approaches primarily focused on learning visual representations from these videos (e.g., R3M [Nair et al., 2022], Masked Visual Pre-training [Xiao et al., 2022]), without explicitly extracting *actionable* information suitable for robot control due to the significant "morphology gap" between human hands and robot grippers.
- **Human-to-Robot Transfer Learning:** A key challenge in this area is how to translate human behaviors into robot actions. Low-level joint space commands are morphology-dependent and don't transfer. Approaches that learn visual representations are useful but often lack the explicit behavioral component needed for control.
- **Structured Action Spaces:** To enhance sample efficiency, prior work has explored reducing the search space through skills, options, or visually grounded action spaces (e.g., 2D/3D pixel locations).

The work presented in this paper, **Structured World Models for Intentionality (SWIM)**, makes a crucial advancement by bridging these gaps. It proposes a novel method to extract *actionable, morphology-invariant* information from human videos, specifically **visual affordances** (grasp and post-grasp locations). By pre-training a world model on internet-scale human videos in this structured action space, SWIM allows the robot to build an understanding of useful actions and their effects on the world. This pre-trained model is then efficiently fine-tuned with a *small amount* of reward-free robot interaction data, making real-world deployment feasible.

This approach significantly pushes the boundaries towards **generalist robot agents** that can learn diverse manipulation skills with remarkable sample efficiency. It moves beyond just passive visual understanding from human videos to actively leveraging them for learning **actionable dynamics**, a critical step for developing robots that can operate effectively in complex, unstructured real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable robots to learn complex, general manipulation behaviors directly in the real world with high efficiency, requiring only a minimal amount of real-world interaction data. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations:

- **Building General-Purpose Robots:** The vision is to create a "truly useful home robot" that is general-purpose, capable of performing arbitrary manipulation tasks, and continuously improves with experience. The current paradigm of task-specific learning with engineered rewards or demonstrations is inherently limited and not scalable to the vast array of tasks required for a generalist agent.
- **Leveraging Abundant Human Video Data:** The internet hosts an enormous, untapped resource of human videos showcasing diverse interactions with the world. Humans demonstrate a rich understanding of actions and affordances, as well as how these actions affect the environment. The authors believe that robots must be able to leverage this "internet-scale, human video data" to achieve efficient learning, mirroring the success of large datasets in computer vision and natural language processing.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity in Robot Learning:** Collecting large quantities of robot interaction data in the real world is expensive, cumbersome, and time-consuming. This limits the scale and generality of robot learning systems. The paper aims to mitigate this by relying predominantly on passively collected human data.
- **Overcoming the Morphology Gap for Actionable Learning:** A significant challenge in using human videos for robot control is the morphological difference between humans and robots. Low-level action signals do not transfer. The motivation here is to define a high-level, morphology-invariant action space that captures the "intentions" of human manipulation, making it transferable to robots.
- **Developing Robust World Models:** While world models are powerful for learning dynamics, they require action information. Existing robot world models are typically trained on limited robot data. The motivation is to pre-train world models on abundant human data, enabling them to capture shared environmental dynamics and physics across diverse tasks, thus facilitating more robust and generalizable predictions.

In essence, the research aims to unlock the potential of passive human video data to bootstrap and accelerate robot learning, enabling the creation of versatile, sample-efficient, and continually improving robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, **Structured World Models for Intentionality (SWIM)**, is a three-stage framework designed to enable efficient, general-purpose robot manipulation by leveraging large-scale human videos.

**Core Idea:** Pre-train a world model on human videos using a structured, morphology-invariant action space grounded in visual affordances. This pre-trained model is then fine-tuned with minimal, reward-free robot interaction data and subsequently used for goal-conditioned planning.

### 4. Key Components

- **Visual Affordances as Actions:**
 - **Problem:** Direct transfer of low-level human actions to robots is impossible due to morphological differences.
 - **Solution:** Define a high-level, human-centric "structured action space" based on visual affordances. For manipulation, this means predicting a **grasp location ($p_g$)** and **post-grasp waypoints ($p_{pg}$)** in image space, combined with depth information. This abstracts away low-level control, focusing on object interaction.
 - **Extraction from Human Videos:** The "100 Days of Hands" [Shan et al., 2020] detector is used to identify hand trajectories and object locations in human videos. These are then processed to pinpoint grasp and post-grasp pixel coordinates.
 - **Affordance Model ($G_\phi$):** A neural network model, conditioned on the first frame of a video clip, is trained to distill these human-centric grasp and post-grasp labels into visual affordances.
 - **Transfer to Robot:** For robot execution, the 2D pixel coordinates ($p_t$) are combined with depth information ($d_t$) from a depth camera to project into 3D robot coordinates using hand-eye calibration.
- **Hybrid Action Space:** To combine the benefits of structured affordance actions and fine-grained robot control, SWIM uses a hybrid action space:
 - **Mode 0 (Affordance Mode):** Executes image-space actions (`[p_t, d_t]`, corresponding to grasp/post-grasp locations).
 - **Mode 1 (Cartesian Control Mode):** Executes arbitrary end-effector Cartesian delta actions (`[Δy_t]`).
 - An action `a_t` is defined as `[m_t, θ_t, u_t, Δy_t]`, where `m_t` is the mode, `θ_t` is gripper rotation, `u_t` is the image-space action, and `Δy_t` is the Cartesian action. At any time, only one mode is active.
- **World Model ($W$):** The core dynamics model is based on Dreamer [Hafner et al., 2020], which learns a compact latent state space ($s_t$) from high-dimensional image observations ($x_t$). It consists of an encoder, dynamics model, posterior model, and decoders for image reconstruction and reward prediction. For improved visual encoding, NVAE [Vahdat & Kautz, 2020] is used.

### 5. Three Phases of SWIM

1. **World Model Pre-training on Large-Scale Passive Human Videos (Alg. 1):**
 - **Data:** Epic-Kitchens dataset [Damen et al., 2018], comprising 50k egocentric videos of human manipulation tasks.
 - **Process:** Video clips are processed to obtain grasp ($p_g$) and post-grasp ($p_{pg}$) pixels using the "100 Days of Hands" detector. These are converted into structured actions `a_t` (with `m_t=0`). Since depth and rotation are not available in passive videos, they are randomly sampled.
 - **Training:** Both the affordance model ($G_\phi$) and the world model ($W$) are trained on these sequences, allowing $W$ to learn the dynamics of human-object interactions in the structured action space.
2. **Unsupervised Finetuning with Robot Interaction Data:**
 - **Data Collection:** The robot collects a small amount of "in-domain" data ($R_D$) by executing actions generated by the *visual affordance model G*. Crucially, this step is **reward-free** and **task-unsupervised**, allowing data collection from diverse settings without explicit task objectives. This typically involves around 30 minutes of real-world interaction.
 - **Training:** The pre-trained world model ($W$) is fine-tuned on this collected robot data. This step adapts the general human-interaction dynamics to the specific robot's embodiment and environment.
3. **Robot Deployment to Perform Goal-Conditioned Tasks (Alg. 2):**
 - **Goal Specification:** Tasks are defined by a goal image ($I_g$).
 - **Action Proposal Generation:** Two sets of action proposals are generated:
 - By querying the visual affordance model ($G$) on the current scene.
 - By fitting a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to successful trajectories in the fine-tuning dataset ($R_D$) that reached states similar to $I_g$.
 - **Planning:** The fine-tuned world model ($W$) is used in conjunction with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to plan an optimal sequence of actions. The reward for planning is the cosine distance to the goal image $I_g$ in the world model's learned feature space (or optionally, an external feature space like R3M [Nair et al., 2022]).
 - **Execution:** The optimized action sequence is executed in an open-loop manner on the robot.

### 6. Experimental Setup

- **Robots:** Franka Emika arm (for kitchen-like manipulation: cabinet, veggies, knife) and Hello Stretch RE-1 mobile manipulator (for real-world kitchen tasks: dishwasher, drawer, garbage can).
- **Cameras:** Intel Realsense D415 RGBD cameras.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set of baselines included variations of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and Behavior Cloning (BC) with different action spaces (Cartesian, random pixel, affordance) and pre-training conditions (with/without human video pre-training).

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SWIM across six diverse real-world manipulation tasks and two distinct robot systems yielded several key findings:

- **Superior Performance and Sample Efficiency:** SWIM consistently achieved significantly higher success rates, averaging **~79%** across all tasks, compared to the best baselines, which only reached around 40%. Crucially, this performance was achieved with remarkable sample efficiency, requiring less than **30 minutes of real-world robot interaction data** for fine-tuning.
- **Effectiveness of Joint World Model:** SWIM demonstrated the ability to train a single world model jointly across data from diverse tasks and robots, leading to improved performance compared to models trained individually for each task (SWIM-joint vs. SWIM-single, 79% vs. 75% average success). This highlights the model's capacity to capture shared structure across different manipulation scenarios. In contrast, baselines without human video pre-training showed a significant drop in performance when attempting joint training, emphasizing the crucial role of pre-training for generalization.
- **Critical Role of Human Video Pre-training:** Pre-training the world model on human videos was identified as a critical component. Ablation studies showed a large drop in success when removing human video pre-training (e.g., MBRL-Affordance-joint performed poorly). Visualizations confirmed this, with SWIM's world model producing realistic image reconstructions very early in training (within minutes), while baselines without pre-training yielded pixelated and unrealistic predictions, indicating effective knowledge transfer from human data.
- **Advantage of Structured Affordance Action Space:** The human-centric visual affordance action space proved highly beneficial. Comparing MBRL-single (Cartesian actions) with MBRL-Affordance-single (affordance actions), there was a **>5X increase in success** using structured actions. Even when compared to randomly sampled pixel-space actions, the affordance action space approximately doubled the success rate, confirming that a semantically meaningful action space significantly aids efficient learning and effective data collection.
- **Importance of the World Model:** While Behavior Cloning with affordance actions (BC-Affordance) showed competitive single-task performance, the full SWIM framework, leveraging a world model, outperformed it significantly (over 2X). The world model's ability to utilize multi-task data, incorporate human video pre-training, and enable effective planning was crucial for achieving high overall success and generalization.
- **Continual Improvement:** SWIM demonstrated the capacity for continuous improvement. By iteratively retraining the world model on newly collected data during task deployment, its performance continued to rise, achieving over 90% success in some tasks. This indicates the system's ability to learn and refine its skills over time, an essential characteristic for generalist agents.
- **Reward Model Flexibility:** An ablation showed that using only the world model's internal feature space for goal distance (as a reward signal) was competitive with incorporating an external, pre-trained feature space (R3M), suggesting that the world model learns sufficiently rich and useful internal representations for planning.

In summary, SWIM effectively combines human video pre-training with structured affordance actions and a world model framework to achieve unprecedented sample efficiency and generalization for real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 8. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Structured World Models from Human Videos" holds significant implications for the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Scalable Robot Learning and Generalist Agents:** SWIM provides a critical step towards building truly general-purpose robots that can learn a wide array of manipulation skills and continuously improve. By demonstrating how to effectively leverage vast, passively available human video data, the work offers a scalable path to acquire knowledge that is otherwise prohibitively expensive or time-consuming to obtain through direct robot interaction. This moves robotics closer to the "generalist agent" paradigm seen in other AI fields.
- **Drastically Improved Sample Efficiency:** The ability to learn complex manipulation skills with less than 30 minutes of real-world robot interaction data is a groundbreaking achievement. This level of sample efficiency makes real-world robot deployment and learning significantly more practical, accessible, and less resource-intensive for researchers and practitioners. It effectively tackles one of the biggest bottlenecks in current robot learning systems.
- **Bridging the Human-Robot Morphology Gap:** The introduction and successful application of a structured, human-centric, and morphology-invariant action space (visual affordances) is a key conceptual contribution. It provides a robust mechanism to transfer *actionable* knowledge from human demonstrations (videos) to robots, sidestepping the complexities of low-level, morphology-dependent control. This opens new avenues for richer human-robot interaction and collaboration.
- **Enhanced Generalization Across Tasks and Robots:** By pre-training a world model on diverse human interactions and fine-tuning it on multi-task robot data, SWIM demonstrates remarkable generalization capabilities. The same underlying framework and pre-trained model can be adapted to different robots (Franka arm, Hello Stretch) and varied manipulation tasks with minimal effort, showcasing the power of learning shared dynamics.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for future research in several areas:
 - **Larger-Scale Human Video Utilization:** It encourages the creation and utilization of even larger and more diverse human video datasets, with potentially richer annotation schemes.
 - **Advanced Structured Actions:** Further exploration of different action parameterizations beyond grasp/post-grasp points, to tackle more dynamic or complex manipulation tasks.
 - **Continuous Learning and Adaptation:** The demonstrated ability for continual improvement sets the stage for developing robots that can autonomously learn and adapt throughout their operational lifetime, accumulating experience and becoming more proficient over time.
 - **Embodied AI:** It pushes the boundaries of embodied AI by demonstrating how abstract knowledge from human interaction can be concretely embodied in robotic systems for practical tasks.

While the current work focuses on quasi-static manipulation, its principles are highly transferable. The potential impact spans from making domestic robots viable to accelerating automation in various industries, where rapid task learning and adaptation are crucial. SWIM represents a significant leap forward in making robot learning more practical, scalable, and generalizable by ingeniously harnessing the wealth of human experience.
