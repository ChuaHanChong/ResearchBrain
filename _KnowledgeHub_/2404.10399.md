# FoundationGrasp: Generalizable Task-Oriented Grasping with Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FoundationGrasp integrates open-ended semantic and geometric knowledge from large language and vision-language models into a unified framework for generalizable 6-DoF task-oriented grasp synthesis. The system achieves superior generalization to novel objects and tasks, improving performance by approximately 10% over prior semantic-only methods and demonstrating over 70% success rates in real-robot deployments.

## Problem

- Existing Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG) methods suffer from "closed-set" knowledge, struggling to generalize to novel objects or tasks not explicitly encountered during training.
- Prior data-driven TOG approaches are limited by pre-defined, static knowledge sources, leading to significant performance degradation when faced with novel classes or tasks.
- Grounding the open-ended knowledge from foundation models directly into low-level 6-DoF robotic control for precise grasp synthesis in 3D space remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A three-stage pipeline is developed: Knowledge Generation, Multi-Modal Feature Representation, and Task-Oriented Grasp Evaluation, building a new dataset called LaViA-TaskGrasp.
- Utilizes LLMs (GPT-4) to generate rich semantic and geometric linguistic descriptions for objects and tasks, and VLMs (CLIP) for visual data augmentation with web images.
- Employs a Transformer-based architecture with distinct semantic and geometric branches to evaluate 6-DoF grasp candidates, fusing object point cloud features with multi-modal knowledge from BERT (semantics) and CLIP (geometry and vision).

## Results

- Achieved superior performance on the LaViA-TaskGrasp dataset, consistently outperforming baselines across novel object instances, classes, and tasks, with an approximate 10% mAP improvement over its semantic-only preliminary version, GraspGPT.
- Demonstrated robust real-robot performance with a Kinova Gen3 arm, achieving 71-74% action success rates across held-out settings (instance, class, task) and up to 80% in light clutter for task-oriented grasping.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of both the semantic and geometric knowledge branches, as well as distinct object and task knowledge, for enhancing generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically integrating both open-ended semantic knowledge from LLMs and geometric knowledge from VLMs is crucial for robust generalization in task-oriented grasping, outperforming semantic-only approaches.
> - Foundation models can effectively ground high-level cognitive reasoning into low-level 6-DoF physical robotic actions, bridging the gap between abstract understanding and precise manipulation.
> - The "closed-set" limitation of prior TOG methods can be overcome by leveraging the vast pre-trained knowledge of large foundation models, enabling generalization to novel object instances, classes, and tasks.
