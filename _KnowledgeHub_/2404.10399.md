# FoundationGrasp: Generalizable Task-Oriented Grasping with Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FoundationGrasp integrates open-ended semantic and geometric knowledge from large language and vision-language models into a unified framework for generalizable 6-DoF task-oriented grasp synthesis. The system achieves superior generalization to novel objects and tasks, improving performance by approximately 10% over prior semantic-only methods and demonstrating over 70% success rates in real-robot deployments.

## Problem

- Existing Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG) methods suffer from "closed-set" knowledge, struggling to generalize to novel objects or tasks not explicitly encountered during training.
- Prior data-driven TOG approaches are limited by pre-defined, static knowledge sources, leading to significant performance degradation when faced with novel classes or tasks.
- Grounding the open-ended knowledge from foundation models directly into low-level 6-DoF robotic control for precise grasp synthesis in 3D space remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A three-stage pipeline is developed: Knowledge Generation, Multi-Modal Feature Representation, and Task-Oriented Grasp Evaluation, building a new dataset called LaViA-TaskGrasp.
- Utilizes LLMs (GPT-4) to generate rich semantic and geometric linguistic descriptions for objects and tasks, and VLMs (CLIP) for visual data augmentation with web images.
- Employs a Transformer-based architecture with distinct semantic and geometric branches to evaluate 6-DoF grasp candidates, fusing object point cloud features with multi-modal knowledge from BERT (semantics) and CLIP (geometry and vision).

## Results

- Achieved superior performance on the LaViA-TaskGrasp dataset, consistently outperforming baselines across novel object instances, classes, and tasks, with an approximate 10% mAP improvement over its semantic-only preliminary version, GraspGPT.
- Demonstrated robust real-robot performance with a Kinova Gen3 arm, achieving 71-74% action success rates across held-out settings (instance, class, task) and up to 80% in light clutter for task-oriented grasping.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of both the semantic and geometric knowledge branches, as well as distinct object and task knowledge, for enhancing generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically integrating both open-ended semantic knowledge from LLMs and geometric knowledge from VLMs is crucial for robust generalization in task-oriented grasping, outperforming semantic-only approaches.
> - Foundation models can effectively ground high-level cognitive reasoning into low-level 6-DoF physical robotic actions, bridging the gap between abstract understanding and precise manipulation.
> - The "closed-set" limitation of prior TOG methods can be overcome by leveraging the vast pre-trained knowledge of large foundation models, enabling generalization to novel object instances, classes, and tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of robotic grasping, manipulation, and the burgeoning field of foundation models in artificial intelligence. Historically, robotic grasping research has evolved from **Task-Agnostic Grasping (TAG)**, which prioritizes stable grasps irrespective of subsequent actions, to **Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG)**, where grasp synthesis considers the compatibility with a downstream manipulation task.

Early TAG methods relied on analytical models that required complete object and hand models, severely limiting their generalization. Data-driven TAG methods, utilizing deep learning on sensory data like RGB-D images and point clouds (e.g., Dex-Net 2.0, Contact-GraspNet), improved generalization to novel objects but still ignored task context.

TOG emerged to address this limitation. Initial data-driven TOG methods learned object-task-grasp relationships from limited datasets, but inherently struggled to generalize to novel objects and tasks outside their training distributions due to the "closed-set" nature of their knowledge. More advanced TOG approaches incorporated external prior knowledge, such as semantic knowledge bases (e.g., ConceptNet, TaskGrasp's knowledge graph in GCNGrasp) and geometric properties. While these methods offered some improvements, they still faced a fundamental bottleneck: their knowledge sources were pre-defined and static, preventing effective generalization to elements completely *unseen* during training. The paper explicitly highlights that even state-of-the-art graph-based methods like GCNGrasp suffer significant performance degradation when faced with novel classes or tasks.

The emergence of **Foundation Models** (FMs), such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has revolutionized various AI applications due to their open-ended knowledge and impressive generalization capabilities. In robotics, FMs have been increasingly leveraged for high-level tasks like task planning (e.g., Code as Policies, Voxposer) and scene understanding (e.g., LM-Nav). However, grounding this open-ended knowledge directly into low-level robotic control, particularly for 6-DoF grasp synthesis in 3D space, remains a significant challenge. Some recent works like LERF-TOGO and LAN-Grasp have started using FMs for identifying "where to grasp" (e.g., object parts), but often overlook the "how to grasp" aspect, focusing on 2D or limited 3D applications.

FoundationGrasp positions itself as a critical advancement in this landscape. It directly addresses the "closed-set" limitation of prior TOG methods by being the first to systematically integrate the open-ended semantic and geometric knowledge from LLMs and VLMs into a unified framework for **generalizable 6-DoF task-oriented grasp synthesis in 3D space**. This work extends the group's preliminary work, GraspGPT, which primarily focused on semantic generalization. FoundationGrasp expands this by recognizing and incorporating the crucial role of geometric understanding, enabling robust performance across novel object instances, classes, and tasks. By jointly addressing "where to grasp" and "how to grasp" using a multi-modal foundation model approach, FoundationGrasp aims to bridge the gap between high-level cognitive reasoning and physically grounded robotic actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind FoundationGrasp stems from the inherent limitations of existing Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG) methods. While TOG is a crucial precursor to advanced tool manipulation, current approaches struggle significantly when confronted with novel objects or tasks not explicitly encountered during training. This "closed-set" nature of their knowledge bases renders them impractical for real-world, open-ended environments like homes or dynamic industrial settings, where robots must continuously adapt to unforeseen scenarios. The authors draw an analogy to human cognition, where semantic and geometric reasoning are jointly activated, emphasizing that robots too need rich, open-ended semantic and geometric prior knowledge to understand the complex interplay between objects, tasks, and compatible grasps.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the FoundationGrasp research are multifaceted:

1. **To develop a novel TOG framework, FoundationGrasp, that leverages the open-ended knowledge of modern foundation models (Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models).** The core aim is to move beyond the limitations of closed-set knowledge and enable robots to interpret and act upon novel objects and tasks by tapping into the vast, pre-trained knowledge embedded in these models.
2. **To significantly enhance the generalization capabilities of TOG skills.** Specifically, the research aims to demonstrate that FoundationGrasp can effectively generalize to novel object instances, entirely new object classes, and previously unseen tasks, thereby overcoming the generalization hurdle that plagues prior methods.
3. **To contribute a new multi-modal dataset, LaViA-TaskGrasp.** This dataset augments the existing TaskGrasp benchmark with rich linguistic descriptions (semantic and geometric) and diverse web-based visual data, providing a comprehensive resource for training and evaluating generalizable TOG methods.
4. **To validate the practical effectiveness of FoundationGrasp in real-world robotic applications.** This involves deploying the framework on a physical robotic arm to perform task-oriented grasping and subsequent manipulation experiments, demonstrating its robustness and utility in a real-world context.
5. **To extend and improve upon previous work (GraspGPT).** While GraspGPT initiated the exploration of LLMs for semantic generalization in TOG, FoundationGrasp aims to provide a more comprehensive solution by additionally integrating crucial geometric knowledge, acknowledging that semantic understanding alone is often insufficient for robust physical interaction.

Ultimately, the overarching objective is to advance robotic manipulation towards a higher level of autonomy and intelligence, enabling robots to perform complex tool-use tasks in diverse and unpredictable environments by endowing them with truly generalizable grasping skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FoundationGrasp employs a sophisticated three-stage pipeline to achieve generalizable task-oriented grasping: (1) Knowledge Generation, (2) Multi-Modal Feature Representation, and (3) Task-Oriented Grasp Evaluation.

**1. Knowledge Generation:** This stage is crucial for building the novel Language and Vision Augmented TaskGrasp (LaViA-TaskGrasp) dataset, which serves as the training and evaluation bedrock.
 - **Language Description Generation:** Leveraging an LLM (specifically, OpenAI GPT-4), the framework generates detailed linguistic descriptions for each object class and task. These descriptions are categorized into:
 - *Semantic descriptions:* Object-to-Object (O2O, e.g., similar objects by shape/function), Object-to-Task (O2T, e.g., tasks an object affords), Task-to-Task (T2T, e.g., semantically relevant tasks), and Task-to-Object (T2O, e.g., objects used for a task). These aim to build a rich semantic web.
 - *Geometric descriptions:* Object-to-Part (O2P, e.g., object's constituent parts) and Task-to-Part (T2P, e.g., geometric primitives suitable for a task). These are regularized as bullet points to facilitate grounding to 3D representations.
 - **Language Instruction Generation:** An LLM is also used to augment initial instruction templates (e.g., "Use the [obj] to [task]") to generate a diverse set of over two million language instructions, enhancing robustness to varied human language inputs.
 - **Web-Based Image Retrieval:** To enrich visual data, the system retrieves additional images from the internet for each object instance in the original TaskGrasp dataset. The Google Cloud Vision API is used for initial candidate retrieval, Segment Anything Model (SAM) for background removal, and CLIP's visual encoder for ensuring visual diversity by filtering similar images. This provides varied visual contexts for learning geometric priors.
 - **Grasp Candidate Generation:** For each object, collision-free 6-DoF grasp candidates are generated by a pre-trained task-agnostic grasp sampler, Contact-GraspNet. A subset of these is then selected using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) for evaluation.

**2. Multi-Modal Feature Representation:** This stage transforms the raw multi-modal data into a unified latent feature space.
 - **Object and Grasp Encoder:** A PointNet++ (PN++) architecture processes the concatenated point cloud of the object (X_o) and the six control points representing a candidate grasp (X_g). This encoder captures localized 3D geometric features (˜F_X) while preserving spatial relationships.
 - **Semantic Knowledge Encoder:** A large pre-trained BERT model (frozen during training) encodes the generated semantic description paragraphs (L_sem_c, L_sem_t) and the natural language instruction (L) into their respective latent semantic embeddings (˜F_sem_c, ˜F_sem_t, ˜F_L). This leverages BERT's broad linguistic understanding without fine-tuning.
 - **Geometric Knowledge Encoder:** A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (CLIP, frozen during training) is used to extract both visual features (˜F_I) from the retrieved images and language embeddings (˜F_geo_c, ˜F_geo_t) from the geometric descriptions. CLIP's image-text pre-training allows it to distill relevant geometric concepts from both modalities.

**3. Task-Oriented Grasp Evaluation:** The core of FoundationGrasp, this stage assesses the task compatibility of each candidate grasp using a customized Transformer-based architecture with two specialized branches.
 - **Semantic Branch (TGE_sem):** Designed as a Transformer decoder, this branch takes the language instruction embeddings (˜F_L) and contextualizes them with the object (˜F_sem_c) and task (˜F_sem_t) semantic knowledge. This process, analogous to machine translation, "translates" novel linguistic concepts in the instruction into familiar terms using the provided semantic descriptions, outputting a semantically contextualized language instruction embedding (F_sem_L).
 - **Geometric Branch (TGE_geo):** This branch fuses the multi-modal geometric features (visual features ˜F_I and geometric description embeddings ˜F_geo_c, ˜F_geo_t) with the object-grasp point features (˜F_X). It uses cross-attention layers to integrate geometric descriptions and visual information with the 3D point cloud features, followed by self-attention for rectification. This process yields a geometrically contextualized point embedding (F_geo_X).
 - **Final Score Prediction:** The final task-compatibility score (S) for a given grasp candidate is computed by concatenating the outputs of both branches (F_geo_X and F_sem_L) and passing them through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with a sigmoid activation function.
 - **Loss Function:** The model is trained using a Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss, comparing the predicted score S with ground truth binary labels (1 for task-compatible, 0 for incompatible).

Throughout the process, the foundation models (GPT-4, BERT, CLIP) are kept frozen, preventing catastrophic forgetting and allowing the framework to efficiently leverage their extensive pre-trained knowledge. The modular design ensures that future, more advanced foundation models can be seamlessly integrated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FoundationGrasp demonstrated significant advancements in generalizable task-oriented grasping through extensive perception and real-robot experiments, complemented by detailed ablation studies and a user study.

**Perception Experiments (LaViA-TaskGrasp Dataset):**
- **Superior Generalization:** FoundationGrasp consistently outperformed all baselines (TAG, SGN, SGN+we, GCNGrasp-ow, GCNGrasp-cw) across all three challenging held-out settings: novel object instances, novel object classes, and novel tasks. This conclusively validates its ability to generalize beyond the training set.
- **Limitations of Prior Work:** The experiments clearly showed that GCNGrasp-ow, a state-of-the-art graph-based method, suffered severe performance degradation (up to 42.94% mAP drop in held-out task setting) when encountering novel elements outside its pre-defined knowledge graph. This highlighted the fundamental "closed-set" limitation FoundationGrasp aims to overcome.
- **Impact of Multi-modal Knowledge:** FoundationGrasp, by integrating both semantic and geometric knowledge, achieved an approximate 10% performance improvement over its preliminary version, GraspGPT, which primarily relied on semantic knowledge. This emphasizes the critical synergy between semantic and geometric understanding for robust TOG.
- **Commonsense Reasoning:** Qualitative results indicated that FoundationGrasp exhibits a degree of commonsense reasoning, capable of rejecting grasps when an object is fundamentally incompatible with the intended task (e.g., trying to "pour" with a hammer).

**Real-Robot Experiments (Kinova Gen3 Robotic Arm):**
- **Robust Single-Object TOG:** FoundationGrasp achieved high perception success rates (>80%) and overall action success rates around 71-74% across held-out instance, class, and task settings with 42 novel objects. This confirmed its effectiveness in real-world scenarios, even with partial object observations (single-view camera).
- **Clutter Robustness:** In multi-object task-oriented grasping, FoundationGrasp maintained high success rates (80% and 73.33%) in light and medium clutter, respectively. This demonstrated its ability to operate in more complex scenes by accurately grounding and segmenting target objects using external models like GroundingDINO and SAM. Performance decreased in heavy clutter (53.33%) primarily due to significant occlusions and planning challenges.
- **Facilitating Downstream Manipulation:** Combined with rule-based manipulation skills, FoundationGrasp achieved an average grasping success rate of 78.89% and manipulation success rate of 66.67% across nine household tasks, validating its role as a crucial first step for complex tool manipulation.
- **Identified Failure Modes:** Key challenges in real-robot deployment included unstable grasp candidates from the task-agnostic sampler, motion planning failures, and "false rejection" of valid grasps for objects *too divergent* from the model's implicit knowledge base, suggesting a need for a more representative base knowledge set.
- **Real-Time Performance:** While prompt generation and processing from foundation models added a significant overhead (90.40s) during real-robot inference, the core grasp evaluation was efficient (1.88s for 25 candidates).

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Component Importance:** Both semantic and geometric branches, as well as distinct object and task knowledge, were confirmed to be crucial for generalization, especially for novel classes and tasks.
- **Fusion Strategy:** Cross-attention-based fusion was found to offer a good balance of performance and computational cost, outperforming simpler concatenation and competing closely with more complex attention mechanisms.
- **Foundation Model Robustness:** FoundationGrasp demonstrated robust performance across various choices of LLMs (BERT, RoBERTa, LLaMA 3, T5) for the semantic encoder and VLMs (CLIP, BLIP-2, LLaVa) for the geometric encoder. Notably, vision-only CLIP-V underperformed, underscoring the importance of multi-modal understanding for geometry. GPT-4 slightly outperformed GPT-3.5 for language description generation, but the differences were minor. These findings suggest the modularity of FoundationGrasp allows for seamless integration of future state-of-the-art FMs.

**User Study:**
- Participants generally agreed that the robot exhibited task-oriented grasping, high repeatability, and robustness to arbitrary object poses. Suggestions for improvement included considering object state (e.g., hot pot) and understanding direct part-specific instructions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FoundationGrasp represents a significant stride forward in robotic manipulation, particularly for task-oriented grasping in unstructured, open-world environments. Its core significance lies in overcoming the long-standing "closed-set" limitation that has hindered previous TOG methods, enabling robots to robustly grasp and interact with objects and perform tasks entirely *unseen* during their training.

The **primary impact** is the demonstration of how **open-ended knowledge from foundation models (LLMs and VLMs) can be effectively grounded into low-level robotic control for 6-DoF grasp synthesis**. This establishes a novel paradigm for integrating high-level semantic and geometric reasoning, typically associated with human cognition, directly into physical robotic actions. By doing so, FoundationGrasp enhances the intelligence and adaptability of robots, making them more capable of complex tool manipulation tasks in real-world scenarios.

**Key contributions and their potential impact include:**

1. **Enabling Generalizable TOG:** This work moves the field closer to truly intelligent household and industrial robots that can adapt to the immense diversity of objects and tasks they might encounter. For instance, a robot designed to clean a kitchen could now grasp and use a novel cleaning tool or handle an unfamiliar dish, rather than being limited to a pre-programmed set.
2. **Bridging High-Level AI and Low-Level Robotics:** FoundationGrasp provides a concrete framework for harnessing the vast, implicit knowledge of LLMs and VLMs – which understand concepts, relationships, and even object parts from extensive pre-training – and translating this into precise, physically grounded actions. This is crucial for developing robots that can understand and execute human commands in natural language.
3. **Robustness in Complex Environments:** The validation through real-robot experiments in cluttered scenes and diverse manipulation tasks highlights its practical applicability. The ability to perform well with partial observations (single-view RGB-D) is particularly valuable for real-world deployment where full 3D models are often unavailable.
4. **Multi-Modal Learning Paradigm:** The explicit integration and demonstrated necessity of both semantic and geometric knowledge, as evidenced by the performance gains over semantic-only approaches, sets a new standard for comprehensive object-task-grasp understanding in robotics.
5. **New Dataset for Future Research:** The LaViA-TaskGrasp dataset is a valuable public resource that will facilitate further research and benchmarking in generalizable TOG and multi-modal robotics, accelerating advancements in the field.
6. **Direction for Future Development:** The identified limitations, such as the need for less labor-intensive grasp data acquisition (e.g., via human demonstrations or AIGC), a unified sampling-and-evaluation pipeline, gripper generalization, object state awareness, and force feedback, provide clear and impactful avenues for future research, pushing towards more autonomous, intuitive, and universally applicable robotic systems.

In summary, FoundationGrasp marks a significant step towards more autonomous and intelligent robots by endowing them with truly generalizable task-oriented grasping capabilities, effectively leveraging the power of modern foundation models. This work not only pushes the boundaries of robotic manipulation but also opens up new directions for seamlessly integrating high-level AI reasoning with physical embodiment.
