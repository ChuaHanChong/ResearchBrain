# Trust-Region Adaptive Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces TRAPO, a framework that unifies Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) within each training instance to enhance Large Language Models' (LLMs) complex reasoning abilities. It achieves superior performance across mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks by stabilizing the SFT-RL interaction and adaptively guiding exploration.

## Problem

- The conventional two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline for LLMs is inconsistent and inefficient for complex reasoning tasks.
- Standard SFT's mode-covering property (minimizing forward KL divergence) can suppress exploration, lead to catastrophic forgetting, and destabilize subsequent RL training.
- Fixed expert guidance, such as uniform prefix lengths, is inefficient as it can over-guide easy problems and under-guide challenging ones.

## Method

- TRAPO unifies SFT and RL within each training instance using a =="learn-while-practicing" paradigm==, where expert prefixes are used for SFT and model completions for RL.
- It introduces ==Trust-Region SFT (TrSFT)== that clips the SFT gradient, mitigating the mode-covering property and enabling stable, mode-seeking knowledge internalisation from expert demonstrations.
- ==Micro-Group Sampling== provides adaptive guidance by dynamically selecting expert prefix lengths based on the model's performance on preceding micro-groups, ensuring guidance is provided "only as much as needed."

## Results

- TRAPO achieved an average score of **56.6** across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the SFT-then-RL baseline by **+2.3 points** and pure RL by **+6.2 points**.
- It demonstrated strong generalization to general-domain reasoning, attaining an average score of **68.3** on ARC-c and MMLU-Pro, surpassing all baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that direct SFT-RL combination without TrSFT led to a catastrophic performance collapse of over **18 points**, validating TrSFT's necessity for stable integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard SFT's mode-covering behavior, which assigns probability to regions not supported by expert policies, is a primary cause of instability when combined with RL.
> - Modifying SFT's gradient with a trust-region parameter (TrSFT) effectively transforms the objective towards mode-seeking, making SFT compatible and synergistic with RL.
> - Dynamically adjusting expert guidance based on a model's current performance significantly improves training efficiency and stability compared to static guidance.
