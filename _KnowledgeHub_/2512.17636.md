# Trust-Region Adaptive Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces TRAPO, a framework that unifies Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) within each training instance to enhance Large Language Models' (LLMs) complex reasoning abilities. It achieves superior performance across mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks by stabilizing the SFT-RL interaction and adaptively guiding exploration.

## Problem

- The conventional two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline for LLMs is inconsistent and inefficient for complex reasoning tasks.
- Standard SFT's mode-covering property (minimizing forward KL divergence) can suppress exploration, lead to catastrophic forgetting, and destabilize subsequent RL training.
- Fixed expert guidance, such as uniform prefix lengths, is inefficient as it can over-guide easy problems and under-guide challenging ones.

## Method

- TRAPO unifies SFT and RL within each training instance using a =="learn-while-practicing" paradigm==, where expert prefixes are used for SFT and model completions for RL.
- It introduces ==Trust-Region SFT (TrSFT)== that clips the SFT gradient, mitigating the mode-covering property and enabling stable, mode-seeking knowledge internalisation from expert demonstrations.
- ==Micro-Group Sampling== provides adaptive guidance by dynamically selecting expert prefix lengths based on the model's performance on preceding micro-groups, ensuring guidance is provided "only as much as needed."

## Results

- TRAPO achieved an average score of **56.6** across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the SFT-then-RL baseline by **+2.3 points** and pure RL by **+6.2 points**.
- It demonstrated strong generalization to general-domain reasoning, attaining an average score of **68.3** on ARC-c and MMLU-Pro, surpassing all baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that direct SFT-RL combination without TrSFT led to a catastrophic performance collapse of over **18 points**, validating TrSFT's necessity for stable integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard SFT's mode-covering behavior, which assigns probability to regions not supported by expert policies, is a primary cause of instability when combined with RL.
> - Modifying SFT's gradient with a trust-region parameter (TrSFT) effectively transforms the objective towards mode-seeking, making SFT compatible and synergistic with RL.
> - Dynamically adjusting expert guidance based on a model's current performance significantly improves training efficiency and stability compared to static guidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training, specifically addressing the critical task of enhancing LLMs' complex reasoning abilities. Driven by methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), LLMs have transitioned from basic chatbots to capable reasoners in domains such as mathematical deduction, program synthesis, and multi-step decision-making.

The dominant paradigm in this field has been a two-stage pipeline: first, SFT to imitate expert demonstrations, followed by RL to refine and sharpen skills through trial-and-error. However, the authors identify a fundamental inconsistency and inefficiency in this sequential approach. SFT, which aims for rigid imitation, can suppress exploratory behavior crucial for RL and may lead to catastrophic forgetting of pretraining knowledge. This creates a "fundamental barrier" that hinders the synergistic potential between SFT and RL. The paper states that "a lower SFT loss does not suggest a better starting point for RL training," and "over-training in SFT might push the model out of the region suited for RL."

Recognizing this challenge, the broader research community has begun to explore ways to integrate SFT and RL more effectively. Related works, as highlighted in Section 4, include:
- **Direct Combination of Losses:** SRFT (dynamically adjusting SFT/RL weights based on token entropy), AMFT (meta-learning optimal imitation-exploration balance), and HPT (dynamically switching between SFT/RL based on rollout performance). These approaches typically involve combining the objectives but may not deeply address the underlying incompatibility of SFT's mode-covering behavior.
- **Interleaving SFT within RL:** ReLIFT (collecting poor RL samples for SFT) and LUFFY (treating expert trajectories as offline data in RL groups). These methods attempt to bring SFT and RL closer but often rely on specific strategies for data mixing or objective switching.
- **Prefix-based Guidance:** Prefix-RFT (sampling prefixes as guidance and using entropy for SFT token selection) shares some conceptual similarities with TRAPO's use of expert prefixes.

TRAPO distinguishes itself by pinpointing the "mode-covering property" of standard SFT (which minimizes forward KL divergence) as a root cause of instability when interleaved with RL. It theoretically investigates this challenge and proposes a novel solution (Trust-Region SFT) that effectively shifts the optimization objective towards mode-seeking behavior, making it more compatible with RL. Furthermore, its adaptive prefix-selection mechanism offers a refined approach to guidance, moving beyond "one-size-fits-all" strategies. By addressing the deep-seated incompatibility between SFT and RL at an algorithmic level and offering a principled integration, TRAPO seeks to establish a more robust and effective paradigm for post-training LLMs, particularly for complex reasoning tasks where both expert knowledge and exploratory refinement are essential.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind TRAPO stems from a critical observation about the conventional two-stage post-training pipeline for LLMs, which involves Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL). While both SFT and RL are crucial for enhancing LLMs' complex reasoning abilities, their sequential application presents significant inefficiencies and inconsistencies.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

1. **Address the Inconsistency of Two-Stage SFT-then-RL:** The primary motivation is to overcome the "fundamental barrier" that hinders synergy between SFT and RL in the traditional pipeline. SFT's objective of strictly imitating expert demonstrations (mode-covering property of forward KL divergence) can inadvertently lock the model into rigid behaviors, suppress valuable exploration, and induce catastrophic forgetting of pretraining knowledge. This limits RL's potential for further improvements. The authors pinpoint the disjoint nature of the two-stage training as the root cause, noting that simply reducing SFT loss doesn't guarantee a better starting point for RL; indeed, "over-training in SFT might push the model out of the region suited for RL."

2. **Unify SFT and RL within Each Training Instance:** The main objective is to move away from the disjoint, sequential training to a hybrid framework that interleaves SFT and RL "at the finest grain," meaning within each training instance. This aims to synergistically combine the knowledge distillation benefits of SFT with the self-exploration advantages of RL. Specifically, the goal is to optimize SFT loss on expert prefixes (to internalize expert skills) and RL loss on the model's own completions (to refine via self-exploration).

3. **Stabilize Training by Mitigating SFT's Mode-Covering Property:** A significant challenge in directly combining SFT and RL is SFT's tendency to distribute probability mass even to "void regions" where the expert policy has no support. This "distribution-blending" can lead to degenerate outputs (e.g., repetitions, erroneous decoding) during RL rollouts, making effective exploration difficult. The paper aims to develop a modified SFT objective that ensures stable and robust policy updates when interleaved with RL.

4. **Develop an Adaptive Guidance Mechanism:** The second challenge is determining the optimal amount of expert guidance. A fixed "one-size-fits-all" prefix length is inefficient, as it can over-guide easy problems and under-guide challenging ones. The objective is to design an adaptive mechanism that dynamically allocates expert guidance based on the measured utility or the model's performance, providing "only the minimal guidance necessary" to discover correct solution paths. This scaffolding approach aims to strike a dynamic balance between self-exploration and expert supervision.

In essence, TRAPO's objectives are to create a more efficient, stable, and synergistic LLM post-training paradigm that judiciously leverages expert knowledge while fostering robust self-exploration and skill acquisition, ultimately leading to superior reasoning-enhanced LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TRAPO (Trust-Region Adaptive Policy Optimization) is a hybrid post-training framework designed to unify Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) within each training instance. Its methodology hinges on resolving two key challenges: effectively internalizing expert guidance and adaptively selecting the appropriate amount of guidance.

The core workflow for each prompt in a mini-batch is a "learn-while-practicing" paradigm:
1. An expert prefix (dynamically selected) is provided as context.
2. The target policy rolls out to complete the reasoning from that prefix.
3. A dual update is performed: Trust-Region SFT (TrSFT) loss is applied to the expert prefix for skill internalization, and a standard RL objective (specifically, a variant of GRPO) is applied to the model's self-generated completion.

The two main technical innovations addressing the identified challenges are:

#### 3.1. Trust-Region SFT (TrSFT) for Guidance Internalization

**Motivation:** The paper identifies that a naive combination of standard SFT loss and an RL objective leads to severe performance degradation. This is attributed to standard SFT's mode-covering property, where its negative log-likelihood (NLL) objective (equivalent to minimizing forward Kullback-Leibler divergence) assigns probability to "void regions" not supported by the expert policy. The gradient `1/p_θ_T(y_n|x, y_<n)` in SFT becomes extremely large for low-probability tokens, pushing the target policy into these undesirable regions and hindering effective RL exploration.

**Approach (Clipping the Gradient):** TrSFT modifies the standard SFT gradient by introducing a trust-region parameter `α`. The gradient is calculated as:
`∇_θ L_α_TrSFT = - (1/N) Σ_i Σ_n (1 / max(p_θ_T(y_i_n|x_i, y_i_<n), α)) ∇_θ p_θ_T(y_i_n|x_i, y_i_<n)`

- **Mechanism:** When the target policy's probability for an expert token `p_θ_T` is greater than or equal to `α` (i.e., inside the trust region), the standard SFT gradient weight `1/p_θ_T` is used, allowing aggressive imitation.
- **Intervention:** When `p_θ_T` is less than `α` (outside the trust region), the gradient weight is clipped to a constant `1/α`. This significantly dampens large gradient updates from low-probability tokens.

**Theoretical Properties and Benefits:**
1. **Safe Knowledge Instillation:** TrSFT prioritizes learning from high-overlapping regions (modes favored by both expert and target policies), preventing the model from collapsing into "void regions" due to extreme gradient updates. It ensures that the model internalizes "close" and readily tangible expert wisdom while preserving its existing strengths.
2. **Mode-Seeking Optimization:** Proposition 1 theoretically demonstrates that the optimal solution for TrSFT counters distribution-blending. It prunes low-probability regions in the expert policy and rescales primary modes. This effectively transforms the objective from the mode-covering behavior of forward KL divergence to a mode-seeking behavior akin to reverse KL divergence, forcing the policy to focus on the expert's core skills and facilitating high-return rollouts for RL.

#### 3.2. Micro-Group Sampling for Adaptive Guidance Selection

**Motivation:** A fixed-length expert prefix is inefficient. It over-guides easy problems (stifling exploration) and under-guides difficult ones (leading to failed rollouts). The goal is to provide guidance adaptively, "only as much as needed."

**Approach (Scaffolding):**
- For each training prompt, TRAPO creates `N` ordered micro-groups, `g_i`.
- Each micro-group `g_i` is defined by three hyperparameters: `L_i` (prefix length ratio), `t_i` (return threshold), and `n_i` (sampling budget).
- **Dynamic Allocation:** For micro-group `g_i`, TRAPO first calculates the average return (`pr_i`) from all samples generated in the *preceding* micro-groups for that prompt.
- **Conditional Guidance:**
 - If `pr_i` is less than or equal to `t_i`, expert guidance is injected: a prefix of length `⌊L_i · |y|⌋` (from the expert trajectory `y`) is provided, and `n_i` completions are sampled from the target policy *starting from this prefix*.
 - Otherwise (if `pr_i` is high), no expert prefix is provided, and `n_i` policy rollouts are obtained directly from the target policy without guidance.
- **Progressive Guidance:** `L_1 = 0` (ensuring initial unguided exploration) and `L_N = 1` (allowing full expert guidance when necessary), with `0 = L_1 < L_2 < · · · < L_N = 1`. This ensures incrementally richer guidance is provided only when shorter or no prefixes prove insufficient.

**Integration:** The generated completions (guided or unguided) are then used for standard RL updates (using GRPO in this work), while the expert prefixes provided for guidance are used for TrSFT updates. This dual optimization occurs within each batch, ensuring continuous learning from both imitation and exploration. The overall system is implemented using the GRPO algorithm for RL with modifications for the TrSFT objective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on five mathematical reasoning benchmarks and two general-domain reasoning benchmarks demonstrate the significant advantages of TRAPO over existing post-training methods.

1. **Superior Performance on Mathematical Reasoning:**
 - TRAPO achieved an average score of **56.6** across AIME2024, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, and Olympiad benchmarks, outperforming all baselines.
 - It yielded improvements of **+6.3 points** over standalone SFT and **+6.2 points** over pure RL (GRPO).
 - Crucially, TRAPO surpassed the highly competitive SFT-then-RL baseline by **+2.3 points**, demonstrating the superiority of its integrated framework over the disjoint two-stage approach.
 - It also outperformed other advanced methods combining SFT and RL, such as ReLIFT and LUFFY, indicating its state-of-the-art capability in this domain.

2. **Strong Generalization to General-Domain Reasoning:**
 - On ARC-c and MMLU-Pro, TRAPO attained an average score of **68.3**, also surpassing all baselines.
 - This indicates that TRAPO, despite leveraging external guidance for mathematical reasoning, does not confine the model to rigid patterns. Instead, it fosters stronger generalization abilities, suggesting that the acquired reasoning skills are robust and transferable.

3. **Validation of TrSFT Necessity through Ablation:**
 - A critical finding was that a naive, direct combination of standard SFT loss and RL objectives was **counterproductive**, leading to a catastrophic performance collapse of over **18 points** compared to the pure RL baseline. This strongly validates the initial hypothesis about the incompatibility of standard SFT with RL and highlights the necessity of TrSFT for a stable and synergistic integration.
 - Ablation studies confirmed that micro-group sampling alone improved GRPO, and adding TrSFT loss on top of it further significantly boosted performance, unlike standard SFT loss or LUFFY loss.

4. **Positive Training Dynamics:**
 - Compared to GRPO, TRAPO consistently achieved higher rewards, indicating more effective learning.
 - TRAPO showed a rapid increase in generation length during early training, signifying swift internalization of expert reasoning patterns, while GRPO struggled to produce longer, more complex solutions.
 - TRAPO stabilized at a relatively higher policy entropy, suggesting it maintains flexibility and openness to learning, balancing refinement of high-probability paths with leveraging low-probability expert guidance.

5. **Expansion of Solution Space (Pass@k Analysis):**
 - Pass@k analysis on AIME2024 revealed that TRAPO, similar to SFT-based methods, demonstrates strong performance scaling with larger `k`. This indicates that TRAPO effectively expands the model's underlying solution space and internalizes new problem-solving skills.
 - In contrast, pure RL methods like GRPO primarily refine existing solution trajectories without fundamentally expanding the reasoning capability.

6. **Adaptive Guidance Mechanism Effectiveness:**
 - The dynamics of prefix usage during training showed that unguided trajectories consistently dominated, ensuring self-exploration.
 - The number of expert-guided trajectories steadily decreased as training progressed, confirming that the adaptive mechanism provides guidance only when needed, reducing reliance on external data as the model improves. This demonstrates "smarter guidance rather than more guidance."

7. **Generalizability Across Base Models:**
 - TRAPO's effectiveness was further validated on a general-purpose instruction-tuned model, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, where it still achieved higher average accuracy on mathematical benchmarks compared to baselines.

8. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - While TRAPO has a higher per-step computational cost due to longer generations and sequential micro-group sampling, it achieves stronger reasoning performance under matched wall-clock training-time budgets, performing slightly fewer training steps than baselines. This indicates superior sample efficiency and effective training-time scaling compared to GRPO and LUFFY, and requires less total GPU hours than SFT-then-RL for better results.

9. **Case Study Insights:**
 - A case study on a polynomial factorization problem illustrated that TRAPO combines the best attributes: it incorporates verification and reflective reasoning from expert trajectories while preserving the base model's clear planning structure, avoiding GRPO's computational errors and SFT's rigid, excessive imitation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TRAPO represents a significant advancement in the post-training of large language models for complex reasoning tasks, offering several key areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **A New Paradigm for LLM Post-Training:** TRAPO establishes a novel and robust one-stage training paradigm that unifies SFT and RL at the instance level. By effectively interleaving these two crucial fine-tuning approaches, it moves beyond the inefficiencies and inconsistencies of the traditional two-stage pipeline. This integrated framework offers a more principled way to combine expert knowledge distillation with self-exploration, promising more capable and reliable LLMs for demanding applications.

2. **Addressing a Fundamental Incompatibility:** The paper makes a crucial theoretical and empirical contribution by identifying the mode-covering property of standard SFT (forward KL divergence) as a root cause of instability when combined with RL. The proposed Trust-Region SFT (TrSFT) provides an elegant solution by shifting the optimization objective towards mode-seeking behavior (akin to reverse KL divergence), thereby ensuring stable and synergistic updates. This insight and solution could influence the design of future hybrid training objectives across various domains.

3. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities and Generalization:** TRAPO's consistent outperformance across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks and two general-domain reasoning benchmarks demonstrates its ability to significantly enhance LLMs' complex reasoning skills. The pass@k analysis further reveals that TRAPO not only refines existing solutions but fundamentally expands the model's underlying solution space by effectively internalizing external knowledge. This leads to LLMs that are not only better at specific tasks but also exhibit stronger generalization and adaptability.

4. **Improved Training Efficiency and Stability:** The adaptive prefix-selection mechanism (micro-group sampling) allows for judicious allocation of expert guidance, providing scaffolding only when needed and gradually reducing reliance as the model improves. This "smarter guidance" approach, combined with the stable updates from TrSFT, contributes to improved sample efficiency and overall training stability. Despite potentially higher per-step computational costs, TRAPO achieves better performance within comparable wall-clock training times, indicating a more effective use of computational resources.

5. **Broader Applicability and Impact on AI Development:** While demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, the principles of TRAPO – stable integration of SFT and RL, and adaptive guidance – are generalizable. They could be applied to other complex, multi-step reasoning domains such as scientific problem-solving, code generation, strategic planning, and medical diagnosis. By fostering LLMs with deeper reasoning capacities and better generalization, TRAPO contributes to the broader goal of developing more intelligent, autonomous, and trustworthy AI systems.

6. **Fostering Open Science:** The public release of code and data supports reproducibility and encourages further research and innovation in this area. This allows other researchers to build upon TRAPO's foundations, extending its methodologies or applying them to new challenges.

In summary, TRAPO provides a robust, theoretically grounded, and empirically validated framework that tackles a critical challenge in LLM post-training. Its innovations are poised to accelerate the development of highly capable, reasoning-enhanced language models, with a broad impact on both academic research and practical AI applications.
