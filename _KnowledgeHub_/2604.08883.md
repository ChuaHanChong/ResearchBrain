# HTNav: A Hybrid Navigation Framework with Tiered Structure for Urban Aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> HTNav introduces a hybrid Imitation Learning and Reinforcement Learning framework with a tiered decision-making mechanism and a map representation learning module to enhance urban aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation for UAVs. The framework improves generalization, long-range path planning, and spatial understanding, achieving a 25.49% success rate and 40.3m navigation error on the revised CityNav Test Unseen split, outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods exhibit insufficient generalization to unseen urban environments, leading to reduced success rates.
- Current approaches struggle with suboptimal long-range path planning, accumulating errors during iterative decision-making.
- Limitations exist in adequately understanding complex spatial continuity and relationships from various map information sources, hindering precise navigation.

## Method

- A hybrid ==Imitation Learning== (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework is employed, using IL for initial policy acquisition and RL for subsequent guided exploration and policy optimization.
- A tiered decision-making mechanism, consisting of a MacroPlanner for high-level waypoint generation and a MicroActor for low-level action control, decouples global planning from local execution.
- A dedicated map representation learning module, featuring residual encoders and an SCConv module, efficiently encodes and fuses multi-source map information to enhance spatial reasoning.

## Results

- HTNav achieved a **25.49%** Success Rate (SR) and **40.3m** Navigation Error (NE) on the revised CityNav Test Unseen split, demonstrating more than double the success rate of the MGP* baseline (**9.70%** SR).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the hybrid IL-RL framework, tiered decision mechanism, and map representation learning module each significantly contributed to improved navigation performance.
- The refined CityNav dataset, including approximately 800 corrections and removal of non-navigable trajectories, established a more robust benchmark for future aerial VLN research.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining IL and RL in a staged training approach provides a stable baseline while enabling efficient exploration, thereby improving generalization to novel scenes.
> - Decomposing navigation into macro-level waypoint planning and micro-level action execution effectively mitigates accumulated errors, leading to more rational and robust long-range trajectories.
> - Utilizing residual connections and spatial-channel convolutions in map feature extraction enhances the understanding of spatial semantics and geometric continuity, crucial for accurate urban aerial navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), specifically focusing on its application to Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in urban environments, referred to as aerial VLN. VLN, broadly, involves an agent navigating an environment by interpreting natural language instructions and visual information. Significant advancements have been made in ground-level VLN, with benchmarks such as R2R, R4R, RxR, VLN-CE, REVERIE, Embodied Question Answering (EQA), ALFRED, TouchDown, and Landmark-RxR. These methods have progressively addressed challenges like longer horizons, multilingualism, continuous control, object grounding, and outdoor urban environments.

Aerial VLN represents an extension of this field, capitalizing on the flexibility and reduced terrain constraints of UAVs for applications such as logistics delivery, urban inspection, management, and disaster monitoring. Prior work in aerial VLN includes methods based on datasets like AVDN, CityNav, AerialVLN, OpenUAV, and OpenFLY. Some approaches, like CityNav, FG-AVDN, and FlightGPT, utilize top-down 2D perspectives with satellite imagery. Others, such as AerialVLN, OpenUAV, and OpenFLY, leverage 3D simulation environments like AirSim to provide immersive navigation experiences. Methods like STMR and NavAgent have focused on improving trajectory reliability and small-landmark recognition, respectively. Recent efforts, including GeoNav and SA-GCS, have explored the decomposition of navigation into subgoals, often incorporating vision-language models. The paper also notes a trend of using Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning to enhance generalization in aerial VLN.

Despite these advancements, the paper identifies several challenges in UAV navigation within complex urban environments. These include insufficient generalization to unseen scenes, suboptimal performance in long-range path planning, and limitations in understanding complex spatial continuity. Existing methods often exhibit decreased success rates and increased navigation errors in unseen scenarios, struggle with accumulated errors in long-range navigation, and face difficulties in parsing intricate spatial relationships.

HTNav is presented as a framework designed to address these specific challenges. It aims to improve generalization through a hybrid Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach, enhance long-range planning with a tiered decision-making mechanism, and deepen spatial understanding via a dedicated map representation learning module. By integrating these components, HTNav seeks to advance the autonomous navigation capabilities of UAVs in complex and unfamiliar urban settings beyond current state-of-the-art methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective navigation framework for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) that can interpret natural language instructions and accurately navigate in complex urban environments, particularly in unseen scenarios and over long distances. The framework, named HTNav, aims to enhance navigation precision and robustness.

This objective is motivated by several identified limitations and challenges in existing aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods:

- **Limited Generalization and Environmental Exploration:** Current methods often demonstrate insufficient generalization capabilities when deployed in unseen environments. This results in reduced success rates and an accumulation of navigation errors, indicating that agents struggle to adapt to novel visual and spatial configurations not encountered during training. The research seeks to improve the model's ability to operate effectively beyond its training data distribution.
- **Suboptimal Long-Range Path Planning:** In long-range navigation tasks, existing approaches tend to accumulate errors during iterative decision-making, which can lead to agents losing track of their precise location or the ultimate goal. This often results in navigation failure or highly inefficient paths. A key motivation is to enable UAVs to plan and execute extended trajectories more reliably by mitigating these accumulated errors and avoiding local optima.
- **Inadequate Understanding of Spatial Continuity and Relationships:** Accurate interpretation of complex spatial information and relationships is crucial for precise UAV navigation. Current methods are noted to have limitations in this regard, hindering their ability to parse intricate spatial cues within instructions and environment maps to plan optimal paths from start to destination. The research aims to enhance the system's spatial reasoning capabilities.

To address these motivations, HTNav proposes specific architectural and methodological innovations:
1. **Hybrid IL-RL Paradigm:** To improve generalization and exploration, HTNav integrates Imitation Learning (IL) for initial policy acquisition from expert demonstrations and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for subsequent environmental interaction and policy optimization. This staged approach is intended to provide a stable baseline while enabling discovery of novel, generalized solutions.
2. **Tiered Decision-Making Mechanism:** To improve long-range planning, the framework adopts a tiered architecture that decouples navigation into macro-level path planning (generating intermediate waypoints) and fine-grained micro-level action control. This aims to ensure global path rationality while maintaining reactive local control.
3. **Map Representation Learning Module:** To deepen the understanding of spatial continuity, a dedicated module is designed to efficiently encode and utilize multi-source map information, including landmark data, using residual networks and spatial-channel compression.

By focusing on these specific areas, the research aims to bridge the gap between current aerial VLN capabilities and the requirements for robust, real-world UAV deployment in urban settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HTNav framework is designed to address challenges in urban aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) through a hybrid learning architecture, a tiered decision-making mechanism, and a specialized map representation learning module.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The research utilizes the CityNav task, where a UAV navigates to a target specified by a natural language instruction. At each step, the UAV receives the linguistic goal description, its current pose, and real-time top-down RGB and depth images. It constructs an incremental navigation map from its trajectory and uses a pre-constructed landmark map for spatial referencing when instructions refer to landmarks. Navigation is successful if the UAV stops within 20 meters of the ground-truth target.

#### 3.2. Hybrid Navigation Architecture (IL-RL)

HTNav integrates Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) in a staged training approach to leverage their respective strengths. IL provides rapid acquisition of a baseline policy from expert demonstrations, while RL enables exploration beyond these demonstrations to discover optimal strategies in complex environments.

- **Stage 1: Imitation Learning (IL):**
 - A multi-task goal predictor is trained using expert demonstration trajectories.
 - This model incorporates an explicit value function head, simultaneously predicting target position (ˆg), navigation progress (ˆp), and the state-value function V(s_t). The state-value function estimates the expected discounted future rewards from a given state, guiding the agent to understand the reward structure early.
 - The imitation loss (L_IL) and value function loss (L_V) are both formulated using Mean Squared Error (MSE).
- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) Fine-tuning:**
 - The policy is fine-tuned using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, which employs a clipped surrogate objective function (L_CLIP) to ensure stable policy updates.
 - The PPO value network is initialized with the parameters learned during the IL stage, providing a high-quality value baseline that transforms exploration from unguided to guided, enhancing efficiency and stability.
- **Joint Training:**
 - The total loss function (L_total) is a weighted combination of IL, value function, and RL objectives, where the RL component is active only during the RL fine-tuning stage.
- **Reward Function:**
 - The reward function (r_t) is designed to optimize navigation through four components:
 - **Distance Reward:** Encourages movement towards the target (based on the change in distance).
 - **Direction Reward:** Promotes alignment of the UAV's heading with the target direction.
 - **Goal Reward:** Provided when the agent is within a specified distance threshold of the target.
 - **Step Penalty:** A constant negative reward to discourage inefficient exploration or excessively long paths.
 - The raw reward is clipped to ensure numerical stability during training.

#### 3.3. Tiered Decision Mechanism

To handle long-range navigation effectively, HTNav employs a tiered cognitive architecture that decouples global path planning from local action control.

- **MacroPlanner (High-Level):**
 - Inputs: Navigation map features (m, including landmark information), UAV pose (s_t: position p_t, yaw angle θ_t), and the natural language target description (d).
 - Function: Reasons about these inputs to predict the next informative intermediate waypoint (w_k+1). This decomposition into sub-goals ({w_1..., w_K}) helps avoid local optima in long-range tasks.
 - Trigger: The MacroPlanner is activated to generate a new waypoint once the current sub-goal w_k is achieved (i.e., UAV is within a small threshold ϵ of w_k).
- **MicroActor (Low-Level):**
 - Inputs: Current RGB observation (o_t), UAV pose (s_t), and the current sub-goal (w_k) provided by the MacroPlanner.
 - Function: Selects the optimal action (a*_t) from a discrete action space A = {go up, go down, forward, turn left, turn right, stop}.
 - Role: Executes fine-grained actions to transition the UAV's state (s_t → s_t+1) until the current sub-goal is reached.

This mechanism ensures global path rationality while allowing for adaptive, reactive local control based on real-time observations.

#### 3.4. Map Representation Learning Module

Efficient utilization of map information is crucial for spatial reasoning in aerial VLN. HTNav introduces a dedicated module for this purpose.

- **Inputs:** Explored Area Map, View Area Map, and Landmark Map.
- **Residual Encoder:** Incorporates residual connections into multi-layer convolutional feature extraction. This design mitigates vanishing gradients and preserves fine-grained spatial details and local geometric continuity during feature extraction.
- **SCConv Module:** Following the residual encoder, an SCConv (Spatial-Channel Convolution) module is introduced. It fuses spatial and channel features through a projection-aligned depthwise-pointwise operation. This module jointly models correlations across spatial and channel dimensions, reducing feature redundancy and improving feature utilization compared to traditional convolutions.
- **Output:** The processed map features are fed into a goal prediction head to output the goal location coordinates (x, y). This simplified map design eliminates the need for target-level semantic segmentation (e.g., using GroundingDINO or MobileSAM).

#### 3.5. Dataset Refinement

The researchers performed a manual review and refinement of the CityNav dataset. Approximately 800 corrections were made to address missing landmark annotations, incorrectly extracted landmarks from descriptions, and misspelled landmark names. Additionally, 311 non-navigable trajectories lacking landmark descriptions were removed, resulting in a total of 32,326 trajectories. This refined dataset was used for evaluation to establish a more robust benchmark.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of HTNav was conducted on the CityNav dataset, including both its original and a revised version. Performance was assessed using Navigation Error (NE), Success Rate (SR), Oracle Success Rate (OSR), and Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

HTNav demonstrated performance across all scene levels and task difficulties on both the original and revised CityNav datasets.
- On the **original CityNav dataset (Test Unseen split)**, HTNav achieved an NE of 68.5m, SR of 22.23%, OSR of 41.02%, and SPL of 19.63%. This represents a substantial improvement over the MGP baseline, which showed an NE of 93.8m, SR of 6.38%, OSR of 26.04%, and SPL of 6.08%.
- On the **revised CityNav dataset (Test Unseen split)**, HTNav further improved, achieving an NE of 40.3m, SR of 25.49%, OSR of 48.82%, and SPL of 21.56%. This constitutes more than double the success rate of the revised MGP baseline (SR 9.70%) and outperforms other baselines like FlightGPT* (SR 24.47%) across all metrics.
- Despite these advancements, human performance (NE 9.8m, SR 87.86%, OSR 95.29%, SPL 57.04%) still significantly exceeded that of all automated agents, indicating a notable gap in spatial reasoning and dynamic decision-making capabilities.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Navigation Accuracy (NE):** HTNav consistently achieved lower NE values across all scenarios, indicating that the agent navigated closer to the target locations. For instance, on the revised Test Unseen split, HTNav's NE was 40.3m, which is notably lower than MGP*'s 82.6m and FlightGPT*'s 61.4m.
- **Success Rate (SR) and Oracle Success Rate (OSR):** HTNav consistently recorded the highest SR and OSR. A higher OSR suggests that the agent more frequently approaches the target area during navigation, increasing the probability of successful task completion.
- **Path Efficiency (SPL):** HTNav achieved a higher SPL compared to other baselines, demonstrating improved path efficiency without compromising task success.

#### 4.3. Performance in Different Task Difficulties

On the Test-Unseen Set, HTNav demonstrated robust performance across Easy, Medium, and Hard difficulty levels.
- For **Easy tasks** (revised dataset), HTNav* achieved an NE of 41.1m and SR of 23.62%, compared to MGP's 98.9m and 6.15%.
- For **Medium tasks**, HTNav* recorded an NE of 40.8m and SR of 26.26%, while MGP showed 90.9m and 6.29%.
- For **Hard tasks**, HTNav* achieved an NE of 39.9m and SR of 28.45%, compared to MGP's 90.0m and 6.80%.

The results indicate a significant improvement in success rate for HTNav, particularly in challenging scenarios. The paper notes that shorter instructions in some easy and medium tasks can lead to ambiguity and lower success rates for all methods, including HTNav and MGP.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the revised dataset investigated the contribution of each proposed component:

- **IL-RL Framework:** Adopting the hybrid IL-RL framework significantly improved navigation performance. For example, on the Test-Unseen split, the baseline MGP+IL-RL improved SR from 9.70% (MGP*) to 18.95%.
- **Tiered Structure:** Integrating the tiered decision-making mechanism further reduced NE and improved SPL, validating its effectiveness in task decomposition.
- **Residual Map Encoder and SCConv Module:** Incorporating the residual map encoder and the SCConv module boosted performance across all scenarios, with notable gains in seen environments. The study also suggested that improvements in unseen environments still rely primarily on reinforcement learning. The full HTNav* model (with all components) achieved the best metrics across the board.
- **RL Weight (λ_RL):** An ablation study on the RL weight showed that a λ_RL of 0.20 yielded the best performance (Test-Unseen SR 25.49%). Models without RL (λ_RL=0) performed weakest, and excessively large weights (e.g., 0.25, 0.30) led to degraded performance, indicating potential training instability.
- **Navigation Submaps:** Removing the target and surroundings maps from the original MGP framework preserved strong performance while simplifying the design, justifying HTNav's leaner map design. Conversely, discarding the landmark map caused a significant drop in SR, highlighting its critical importance for navigation.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

Visualizations of successful navigation trajectories demonstrated HTNav's capabilities:
- It exhibited stable performance in long-range navigation, with predicted paths structurally analogous to ground-truth trajectories.
- The system demonstrated precise localization and approach to small targets, such as a specific blue car.
- Predicted trajectories showed close alignment with ground-truth paths, indicating improved navigation efficiency and accuracy in complex environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HTNav framework presents a notable contribution to the field of aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) by directly addressing several key challenges identified in urban UAV navigation.

The proposed **hybrid Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework** is significant because it combines the benefits of learning from expert demonstrations (IL) for robust initial policies with the explorative and adaptive capabilities of RL. This staged training mechanism aims to improve the agent's generalization ability to unseen environments, a critical factor for real-world deployment where UAVs will inevitably encounter novel scenarios. The initialization of the RL value network with IL-learned parameters provides a form of guided exploration, accelerating convergence and enhancing training stability compared to pure RL approaches.

The **tiered decision-making mechanism** (MacroPlanner and MicroActor) is impactful for long-range navigation. By decomposing the complex task into a high-level strategic planning component (generating waypoints) and a low-level reactive action control component, the framework mitigates the issue of accumulated errors common in long-range tasks. This hierarchical approach enables global path rationality while maintaining flexibility for local, real-time environmental perception, leading to more robust and efficient navigation trajectories.

The **map representation learning module**, incorporating residual connections and SCConv, enhances the UAV's understanding of multi-source spatial semantics and geometric relationships. This is crucial for accurate path planning and target localization in complex urban environments. By efficiently encoding and fusing different types of map information, the module supports precise reasoning about spatial continuity in open domains, improving the agent's ability to interpret and utilize contextual spatial cues. The simplification of the map design by reducing the number of submaps while retaining performance also suggests improved computational efficiency and a leaner pipeline.

The **dataset refinement effort** on the CityNav benchmark contributes to the broader research community by establishing a more robust and accurate evaluation standard for aerial VLN. The demonstrated improvements in baseline models when evaluated on the revised dataset underscore the importance of high-quality data for training and evaluation.

Collectively, these innovations in HTNav result in improved navigation precision and robustness for UAVs in complex urban settings, as evidenced by the state-of-the-art performance across key metrics on both original and refined benchmarks. This work suggests a path toward more reliable and autonomous UAV operation for practical applications such as logistics, inspection, and disaster response. The identified gap between agent and human performance also indicates avenues for future research, particularly in developing models that can achieve more human-like spatial reasoning and dynamic decision-making capabilities.
