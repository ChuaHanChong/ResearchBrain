# HTNav: A Hybrid Navigation Framework with Tiered Structure for Urban Aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> HTNav introduces a hybrid Imitation Learning and Reinforcement Learning framework with a tiered decision-making mechanism and a map representation learning module to enhance urban aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation for UAVs. The framework improves generalization, long-range path planning, and spatial understanding, achieving a 25.49% success rate and 40.3m navigation error on the revised CityNav Test Unseen split, outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods exhibit insufficient generalization to unseen urban environments, leading to reduced success rates.
- Current approaches struggle with suboptimal long-range path planning, accumulating errors during iterative decision-making.
- Limitations exist in adequately understanding complex spatial continuity and relationships from various map information sources, hindering precise navigation.

## Method

- A hybrid ==Imitation Learning== (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework is employed, using IL for initial policy acquisition and RL for subsequent guided exploration and policy optimization.
- A tiered decision-making mechanism, consisting of a MacroPlanner for high-level waypoint generation and a MicroActor for low-level action control, decouples global planning from local execution.
- A dedicated map representation learning module, featuring residual encoders and an SCConv module, efficiently encodes and fuses multi-source map information to enhance spatial reasoning.

## Results

- HTNav achieved a **25.49%** Success Rate (SR) and **40.3m** Navigation Error (NE) on the revised CityNav Test Unseen split, demonstrating more than double the success rate of the MGP* baseline (**9.70%** SR).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the hybrid IL-RL framework, tiered decision mechanism, and map representation learning module each significantly contributed to improved navigation performance.
- The refined CityNav dataset, including approximately 800 corrections and removal of non-navigable trajectories, established a more robust benchmark for future aerial VLN research.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining IL and RL in a staged training approach provides a stable baseline while enabling efficient exploration, thereby improving generalization to novel scenes.
> - Decomposing navigation into macro-level waypoint planning and micro-level action execution effectively mitigates accumulated errors, leading to more rational and robust long-range trajectories.
> - Utilizing residual connections and spatial-channel convolutions in map feature extraction enhances the understanding of spatial semantics and geometric continuity, crucial for accurate urban aerial navigation.
