# ViLAM: Distilling Vision-Language Reasoning into Attention Maps for Social Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> ViLAM enables social robot navigation by distilling vision-language reasoning from large models into efficient attention maps for a local motion planner. The system achieved a 28.7% improvement in Fréchet distance, indicating more human-like trajectories, and consistently higher success rates across diverse real-world scenarios compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Traditional robot navigation struggles with social compliance, treating humans as obstacles and leading to unnatural or disruptive behaviors.
- Existing socially aware navigation methods lack high-level semantic reasoning and context-dependent understanding, limiting generalization to novel, dynamic environments.
- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offer sophisticated social reasoning but are computationally too demanding for real-time deployment on resource-constrained mobile robots due to high latency.

## Method

- **ViLAM** distills social intelligence from a large ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== into a lightweight, transformer-based model using offline VLM-annotated data.
- It employs an attention-guided loss function to fine-tune the distilled model, balancing navigation priors from a pretrained vision-action model with socially guided attention from the VLM.
- The distilled attention maps are integrated into a modified Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) local planner, guiding the robot to generate socially compliant and efficient trajectories in real-time.

## Results

- ViLAM achieved the highest success rates across all test scenarios, with increases ranging from **14.2%** to **50%** compared to baselines.
- Robot trajectories generated by ViLAM showed an overall **28.7%** improvement in Fréchet distance, indicating greater similarity to human teleoperated paths and enhanced social compliance.
- The system operates at approximately 20Hz, demonstrating real-time capability by avoiding the high latency of direct VLM inference, while outperforming VLM-based baselines in "Time to Goal" metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex social reasoning from large VLMs can be effectively distilled into computationally efficient attention maps, making it deployable on mobile robots.
> - Fusing navigation-relevant attention from a self-supervised model with socially guided attention from a VLM leads to richer and more robust spatial understanding for navigation.
> - An attention consistency loss, using SSIM as cosine similarity, enables stable learning of socially relevant regions, improving robot behavior in human-centered environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of autonomous robots into human-centered environments necessitates socially compliant navigation capabilities. Traditional robot navigation primarily focuses on geometric path planning and collision avoidance, often treating humans as dynamic or static obstacles. This approach can result in robot behaviors that are perceived as unnatural, disruptive, or socially inappropriate, such as cutting through groups of people or blocking pathways.

To address these limitations, the field of socially aware navigation has explored various strategies, including hand-crafted social rules, imitation learning from human demonstrations, and reinforcement learning policies. While these methods have shown progress, they often require extensive training data, exhibit limited generalization to novel environments, and may lack the high-level semantic reasoning necessary to interpret complex social cues and infer context-appropriate behaviors.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs), such as GPT-4V and Gemini, have demonstrated capabilities in semantic scene understanding, commonsense reasoning, and human behavior prediction. These models can infer implicit social cues and identify regions of interest for navigation. However, a significant challenge for deploying these large models on mobile robotic platforms is their computational demand, which leads to high latency and memory requirements, making real-time inference impractical for safety-critical navigation tasks.

Knowledge distillation has emerged as a technique to transfer knowledge from large, computationally intensive "teacher" models to smaller, more efficient "student" models. Existing distillation methods often focus on output-level predictions. This paper positions ViLAM within this landscape by introducing a novel attention-level distillation approach. It aims to bridge the gap between the sophisticated social reasoning of large VLMs and the real-time computational constraints of robotic platforms, building upon the observation that intermediate attention activations from pretrained vision models can highlight navigationally relevant areas. ViLAM specifically distills VLM-derived social cues into attention maps that can be efficiently used by a local motion planner, thereby enabling socially compliant navigation without continuous, real-time VLM inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable autonomous robots to navigate human-centered environments in a safe, efficient, and socially compliant manner, while operating in real time on resource-constrained platforms.

The motivation for developing ViLAM stems from several key challenges in current robot navigation:

1. **Limitations of Traditional Navigation Approaches:** Conventional navigation algorithms prioritize collision avoidance and geometric efficiency. They often model humans as simple obstacles, failing to account for social expectations and cultural norms. This can lead to robot behaviors that are perceived as awkward or intrusive, such as encroaching on personal space or disrupting pedestrian flow, hindering robot acceptance and effective integration into human environments.
2. **Lack of Semantic and Social Reasoning:** Existing socially aware navigation methods, including rule-based systems, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning, typically struggle with high-level semantic scene understanding and context-dependent social reasoning. They often require extensive, specific datasets or laborious rule engineering, limiting their generalizability to diverse and dynamic real-world scenarios where implicit social cues are prevalent.
3. **Computational Constraints of Advanced Models:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) possess powerful capabilities for semantic understanding, commonsense reasoning, and human behavior prediction, which are highly beneficial for social navigation. However, their architecture, often comprising billions of parameters, demands substantial computational resources (memory and processing power). Directly deploying and performing real-time inference with such models on typical mobile robot hardware is currently impractical due to high latency, which compromises responsiveness in dynamic, safety-critical navigation situations.
4. **Need for Real-Time Efficiency:** Robot navigation in dynamic human environments requires rapid decision-making and immediate responses to environmental changes and human movements. The computational overhead associated with large VLMs makes their direct, online application unsuitable for scenarios demanding low-latency control.

To address these motivations, ViLAM's key objectives are:

- **To distill sophisticated vision-language reasoning** from large VLMs into a lightweight, transformer-based model. This aims to capture the VLM's understanding of social norms and human behavior without incurring its significant computational cost during deployment.
- **To leverage attention maps** as an intermediate representation for knowledge distillation. By aligning attention maps from a pretrained vision-action model with socially guided attention maps derived from a VLM, ViLAM seeks to create enhanced attention representations that implicitly encode social awareness.
- **To generate socially informed spatial cost maps** that highlight navigationally critical regions, including areas to avoid or traverse cautiously, based on distilled social intelligence.
- **To integrate these distilled attention maps** into a real-time local motion planner, enabling the robot to generate smooth, natural, and socially compliant trajectories in dynamic environments.
- **To achieve higher success rates and improved social compliance** in robot navigation, measured by metrics such as successful goal-reaching, time efficiency, and alignment with human-like trajectories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViLAM is designed to distill vision-language reasoning into spatial attention maps for socially compliant robot navigation, leveraging a four-component framework: Data Generation, Distilled Model, Attention-Guided Loss Function, and a Socially Aware Motion Planner.

The overall system architecture involves parallel processing during training. A pretrained vision-action model (VANP) extracts navigation-relevant attention maps. A separate Vision-Language Model (VLM) provides socially guided attention maps through offline annotation. The core of ViLAM is a lightweight transformer-based model that is fine-tuned to align its attention maps with both sources.

#### 3.1. A. Data Generation

The approach begins by constructing a dataset augmented with VLM-derived social supervision. A subset of the SCAND (Socially Compliant Navigation Dataset) is utilized. For each RGB image (I_RGB,t) in the dataset, three potential navigation "frontiers" (left, center, right) are defined. These frontiers are explicitly marked on the image, for instance, by overlaying colored rectangles, before being presented to a large VLM.

A Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting approach is employed to query the VLM (specifically, GPT-4o in the implementation). The prompt provides the image, current social context, and visible pedestrians, asking the VLM to estimate the likelihood of each frontier becoming crowded. Based on these likelihood scores, a socially guided attention map (A_VLM) is generated, annotating I_RGB with cues pertaining to human behavior, movement patterns, and social norms. This offline process creates a VLM-annotated dataset (D_VLM) of approximately 10,000 samples, which subsequently informs the distilled model without requiring real-time VLM inference during robot operation.

#### 3.2. B. Distilled Model

To achieve efficient, socially aware navigation, ViLAM utilizes a lightweight transformer-based model (F_ViLAM) for real-time deployment. This model is initially a copy of a pretrained vision-action model, VANP, which employs a ResNet-50 CNN backbone and a Transformer encoder. VANP is a self-supervised model trained to predict future actions from past visual observations and, as an emergent property, generates attention maps (A_pretrained) highlighting navigation-relevant regions.

For fine-tuning F_ViLAM, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used. LoRA introduces small, trainable low-rank adapters to the original model, allowing efficient parameter updates while keeping the majority of the pretrained weights frozen. This method significantly reduces computational and memory overhead during training. During this phase, F_ViLAM generates its attention map (A_ViLAM) from the last layer of its ResNet-50 backbone. The objective is to optimize F_ViLAM's parameters such that it balances retaining the navigation-aware representations from the frozen F_pretrained (VANP) with incorporating the socially guided attention from A_VLM. Classification (CLS) and learned register tokens are also appended to image embeddings to enrich spatial attention reasoning.

#### 3.3. C. Attention-Guided Loss Function

To integrate social reasoning from VLMs into the distilled model while preserving the navigation priors from the pretrained vision-action model, an attention consistency loss function is introduced. This function guides the fine-tuning process of F_ViLAM.

The total loss function is formulated as:
`L = (1 - λ_VLM) · L_SSIM(A_ViLAM, A_pretrained) + λ_VLM · L_SSIM(A_ViLAM, A_VLM)`

Here, A_ViLAM is the attention map produced by the distilled model, A_pretrained is the attention map from the frozen pretrained model (VANP), and A_VLM is the socially guided attention map provided by the VLM. L_SSIM(A, B) represents the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) Loss, which in this context is defined as the Cosine Similarity between the flattened attention maps A and B. The parameter λ_VLM is a weighting factor that controls the relative influence of the pretrained model's navigation attention versus the VLM's social attention. This cosine similarity-based SSIM loss is designed to promote smoother gradient updates, facilitating stable and effective learning of socially relevant attention regions.

#### 3.4. D. Socially Aware Motion Planner

ViLAM integrates a modified local planner, based on the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA), to translate the distilled attention maps into real-time robot motion. This planner optimizes linear (v) and angular (ω) velocity commands.

The planning process involves:
1. **Admissible Velocity Space:** Determining a set of possible linear and angular velocities (V_s) that the robot can execute.
2. **Feasible Velocities:** Filtering V_s to obtain a reduced set of dynamically feasible and collision-free velocities (V_r).
3. **Objective Function:** To select the optimal control action (v, ω) from V_r, an objective function J(v, ω) is defined:
 `J(v, ω) = β_1 · goal(v, ω) + β_2 · soc(v, ω)`
 - `goal(v, ω)`: A term that encourages the robot's movement towards its designated target.
 - `soc(v, ω)`: A novel social cost function, directly dependent on the distilled attention map A_ViLAM.

The social cost function `soc(v, ω)` is computed by:
1. Extrapolating a short-horizon trajectory `traj_R(v, ω)` in the robot's frame using a unicycle kinematic model for each candidate (v, ω) pair.
2. Projecting this trajectory onto the distilled attention map A_ViLAM using homography projection.
3. Calculating `soc(v, ω)` as the maximum value of A_ViLAM along the projected trajectory:
 `soc(v, ω) = max_(i,j)∈traj_C(v,ω) A_ViLAM(i, j)`
 This means the social cost is highest in regions that the attention map highlights as undesirable (e.g., areas with high pedestrian density or close proximity to humans).

Finally, the optimal control action (v*, ω*) is selected by minimizing the objective function J(v, ω) over the feasible velocity space V_r. This process ensures that the robot's navigation respects social norms while maintaining smooth and adaptive real-time trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ViLAM was evaluated through real-world experiments on a Clearpath Husky robot, equipped with an Intel RealSense D435i camera and an onboard computing system. The model was trained using PyTorch on an Nvidia A6000 GPU and utilized GPT-4o for offline dataset annotation. The system operated at approximately 20Hz during inference, which is a rate suitable for dynamic navigation.

**Comparison Methods:** ViLAM was compared against:
- **Dynamic Window Approach (DWA):** A classical, LiDAR-based local planner primarily focused on obstacle avoidance.
- **CoNVOI:** A VLM-based context-aware navigation method that uses VLMs to generate reference trajectories for a classical planner.
- **VANP:** The self-supervised pretrained vision-action model whose attention maps (A_pretrained) were integrated into ViLAM's planner.

**Evaluation Metrics:** Performance was assessed using three metrics across 10 trials per scenario:
- **Success Rate (↑):** Percentage of trials where the robot successfully reached its goal without freezing or colliding.
- **Time to Goal (↓):** Average time in seconds to reach the goal in successful trials.
- **Fréchet Distance (↓):** A measure of similarity between the robot's trajectory and a human teleoperated trajectory; lower values indicate greater social compliance.

**Test Scenarios:** Four real-world scenarios were used, including outdoor settings with dynamic agents and static obstacles (Scenario 1 & 2) and indoor settings with multiple human agents and varying lighting conditions (Scenario 3 & 4). The distilled model was trained using outdoor trajectories from SCAND.

**Quantitative Results (Table I Summary):**

- **Success Rate:** ViLAM consistently achieved the highest success rates across all test scenarios. It demonstrated 100% success in Scenario 1, 90% in Scenarios 2 and 3, and 80% in Scenario 4. This represents a significant improvement compared to baselines, with increases ranging from 14.2% (e.g., 80% vs 70% for DWA in Scen. 4) to 50% (e.g., 90% vs 40% for VANP in Scen. 3).
- **Time to Goal:** ViLAM generally exhibited competitive or superior "Time to Goal" measurements. Notably, it outperformed CoNVOI in all scenarios (e.g., 12.2s vs 21.4s in Scenario 1) due to its efficient, offline knowledge distillation, which avoids real-time VLM inference latency.
- **Fréchet Distance:** ViLAM consistently achieved the lowest Fréchet Distance across all scenarios (e.g., 0.431 in Scenario 1, 0.732 in Scenario 2, 1.231 in Scenario 3, 1.034 in Scenario 4). This indicates that ViLAM's generated trajectories most closely resembled human teleoperated actions, signifying enhanced social compliance. The paper highlights an overall improvement of 28.7% in Fréchet distance relative to human teleoperated actions.

**Qualitative Analysis (Visualizations and Discussion):**

- **Attention Map Visualization (Fig. 3):** Visual comparisons of attention maps show that ViLAM's distilled attention maps effectively fuse knowledge from both VANP and the VLM. The resulting maps provide improved focus on critical social and navigational regions, correcting ambiguities or missed attention from either source alone. High attention regions (red) accurately highlight areas to be avoided or navigated with caution.
- **Scenario-Specific Performance (Fig. 4):**
 - **Scenario 1:** ViLAM initiated movement earlier and maintained greater social distance from humans compared to DWA and CoNVOI, which often approached humans more closely before adjusting. ViLAM's ability to predict dynamic agent motion prevented unnecessary detours.
 - **Scenario 2:** DWA and CoNVOI struggled with a low curb due to their reliance on LiDAR, leading to collisions. ViLAM accurately identified both the curb and the human, adapting its trajectory to avoid both effectively.
 - **Scenario 3:** In a cluttered indoor environment with multiple humans and obstacles, VANP and DWA frequently collided. ViLAM successfully navigated, demonstrating awareness of human presence and adapting to environmental changes.
 - **Scenario 4:** Under challenging lighting conditions, VANP's perception degraded, and CoNVOI's performance was hindered by VLM query latency. DWA, while avoiding collisions, generated trajectories with high Fréchet distance (uncomfortably close to humans). ViLAM maintained dynamic trajectory adjustments, ensuring both safety and efficiency.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Benefits of VLM:** The integration of VLM annotations was crucial. Using A_VLM during training endowed ViLAM with richer semantic understanding and context-aware reasoning, leading to more informed decision-making than relying solely on VANP's A_pretrained.
- **Benefits of Sequential Images:** Removing historical image sequences from the input negatively impacted navigation performance across all scenarios, particularly in dynamic and variable lighting conditions (Scenarios 3 and 4). Temporal context from image sequences was found to be important for stable navigation and robust feature learning during attention distillation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViLAM presents a methodological contribution to socially aware robot navigation by addressing the challenge of integrating sophisticated vision-language reasoning into real-time, resource-constrained robotic systems.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to **distill complex social intelligence from large Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** into a compact, deployable model. This approach bypasses the computational and latency limitations inherent in direct, online VLM inference, making advanced social reasoning feasible for practical robotic applications. By performing knowledge distillation at the attention map level, ViLAM demonstrates a method for effectively transferring high-level semantic understanding of social norms and human behavior into an intermediate representation that a robot's local planner can utilize.

The **improved navigation performance** observed across diverse real-world scenarios, particularly in terms of higher success rates, reduced time to goal, and significantly lower Fréchet distances (indicating trajectories closer to human teleoperation), underscores ViLAM's practical impact. This suggests that robots employing ViLAM could operate more safely, efficiently, and acceptably in shared human spaces. The system's ability to anticipate human motion, navigate around subtle obstacles like low curbs, and adapt to varying lighting conditions showcases a more robust and adaptable social navigation capability.

The methodology of fusing knowledge from both a pretrained vision-action model (for navigation priors) and a VLM (for social semantics) via an **attention-level distillation loss** offers a novel paradigm for combining disparate sources of knowledge efficiently. This approach could be generalizable to other domains where foundational models hold rich latent knowledge that needs to be deployed on edge devices.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots that navigate more naturally and predictably are likely to be better accepted and trusted by humans, facilitating wider deployment in service, logistics, healthcare, and public spaces.
- **Increased Safety in Shared Environments:** By proactively anticipating human intentions and adhering to social comfort zones, robots can reduce the risk of uncomfortable encounters or collisions, particularly in dynamic and crowded settings.
- **Advancements in Robot Autonomy:** The ability to effectively incorporate high-level, human-centric reasoning without crippling computational demands pushes the boundaries of autonomous robot decision-making in complex social environments.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work opens avenues for exploring multimodal attention distillation (incorporating depth, LiDAR), online adaptation strategies for dynamic social norms, and scaling the approach to long-horizon navigation tasks in highly complex and previously unseen environments.
