# FolDeX: A Physical-World Benchmark for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation of Deformable Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> FolDeX is a real-robot benchmark for long-horizon deformable manipulation, centered on complete garment-folding episodes from basket retrieval through flattening, folding, and stacking. It provides 2,000+ hours of physical data across 20+ tasks and 10+ embodiments, organized along recovery, cross-task, cross-scene, and cross-embodiment reuse axes, plus FoldChallenge, a standardized external evaluation platform scoring success, neatness, and time.

## Problem

- Existing real-robot benchmarks emphasize short-horizon rigid-object manipulation, giving limited coverage of long-horizon deformable tasks where state changes continuously across dependent stages.
- Simulation results degrade on physical robots, and folding appears only as an isolated task inside general-purpose suites rather than a deeply covered task family.
- Real-robot data are expensive, yet whether previously collected recovery, task, scene, and embodiment experience remains reusable under new models is untested.

## Method

- **FolDeX** dataset: real-robot trajectories under a common schema with synchronized RGB from two wrist cameras plus one workspace camera, proprioception, language instruction, and episode metadata.
- Four benchmark tracks with standardized data partitions and controlled comparisons: ==recovery-data utilization==, ==cross-task rigid-to-deformable transfer==, ==cross-scene transfer==, and ==cross-embodiment transfer==.
- **FoldChallenge** platform with held-out garments, controlled initialization, and **FoldScore**, a weighted combination of normalized final-state neatness, task success rate, and completion time.

## Results

- Adding DAgger recovery trajectories to the multi-task RTC baseline raised average success from **80.75%** to **95.00%** and FoldScore from **75.59** to **82.53**, with pants and towel reaching **100%**.
- Training-time real-time chunking lifted multi-task success from **72.50%** to **80.75%**; task-specific policies hit the top success of **82.50%** but a lower FoldScore of **71.69**.
- Joint and continued training across rigid-deformable tasks and across embodiments produced action-space interference and catastrophic forgetting, failing full episodes; scene changes proved far less disruptive.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Binary success hides folding quality: policies with similar success rates diverge sharply on neatness and completion time, so composite metrics are needed.
> - Deployment by-products, human intervention and recovery rollouts, are a high-value training asset rather than disposable, yielding the largest measured gains here.
> - Naive mixing of heterogeneous robot data does not work: action-space interference dominates, and scene shifts are much easier than embodiment or dynamics shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) is progressing towards models that can reliably operate in the physical world, encompassing vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs). These models aim to predict actions or future visual dynamics from observations and robot states. While simulation has enabled rapid development in embodied AI, two fundamental challenges persist:
1. **The sim-to-real gap:** Methods that perform well in simulation often exhibit significant degradation when deployed on physical robots due to discrepancies in visual, physical, and system-level factors like friction, object placement, calibration, latency, and sensing noise.
2. **The rigid-to-deformable gap:** Approaches developed for rigid-object manipulation typically transfer poorly to deformable tasks. Deformable objects (e.g., garments) continuously change shape, self-occlude, entangle, and respond dynamically to contact, requiring different reasoning capabilities than rigid objects. This gap is particularly evident in WAMs, where rich visual imagination does not necessarily translate to stronger deformable manipulation.

Existing real-robot benchmarks, such as RoboChallenge, primarily focus on short-horizon rigid-object manipulation. They offer limited coverage of long-horizon deformable tasks, which are crucial for household robotics involving items like garments. Such tasks demand policies capable of tracking continuously changing states, coordinating contact-rich bimanual actions, maintaining progress across multiple semantic stages, and recovering from accumulating errors throughout an episode. While specialized systems exist for garment manipulation (e.g., smoothing, unfolding, folding) and simulation benchmarks like SoftGym and GarmentLab provide scalable deformable environments, a comprehensive, shared real-robot benchmark for complete, long-horizon deformable manipulation has been absent.

Furthermore, real-robot evaluation is highly sensitive to various factors, including data composition, reset procedures, inference settings, calibration, and communication latency. This sensitivity can lead to irreproducible comparisons, where apparent algorithmic advantages might stem from non-standardized evaluation settings.

This work, FolDeX, addresses these limitations by introducing a physical-world benchmark specifically designed for long-horizon robotic manipulation of deformable objects. It aims to bridge the sim-to-real and rigid-to-deformable gaps in the context of complex, multi-stage tasks like garment folding. It also provides a standardized evaluation platform to ensure fair and reproducible comparisons of policies, while simultaneously studying the efficient reuse of heterogeneous real-robot data, which is an expensive resource.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for FolDeX stems from the challenges in real-world embodied AI, particularly the persistent performance gap between models developed in simulation and their deployment on physical robots, especially for deformable objects. The paper highlights that current benchmarks inadequately address long-horizon deformable manipulation, a critical area for applications such as household robotics.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Address the Sim-to-Real and Rigid-to-Deformable Gaps:** The research is motivated by the observation that policies performing well in simulation or on rigid objects often fail or degrade substantially when applied to real robots or deformable objects. This necessitates direct, standardized evaluation in the physical world for complex deformable tasks.
- **Establish a Benchmark for Long-Horizon Deformable Manipulation:** Current real-robot benchmarks predominantly focus on short-horizon rigid-object tasks, leaving a gap for scenarios involving continuous state changes, multi-stage bimanual interactions, and error recovery over extended episodes. Garment folding serves as a primary example of such a long-horizon deformable task, requiring complex reasoning about object structure and physical state. FolDeX aims to fill this void by providing a dedicated benchmark for this type of challenge.
- **Enable Fair and Reproducible Real-Robot Evaluation:** Real-robot experiments are sensitive to numerous factors, making direct comparison of policies difficult. The work seeks to establish a standardized evaluation platform (FoldChallenge) with consistent task definitions, held-out physical objects, controlled initializations, and a unified execution protocol. This is intended to ensure that observed improvements are due to algorithmic advantages rather than favorable experimental setups. The provision of carefully engineered reference baselines is also motivated by the need for competitive comparisons.
- **Investigate Efficient Reuse of Heterogeneous Real-Robot Data:** Real-robot data collection is expensive. A core motivation is to understand how previously collected data can be efficiently reused, rather than treating it as a disposable asset. This includes data from different models, tasks, scenes, or embodiments, as well as human intervention and recovery data collected during policy deployment. The paper posits that data reuse efficiency is a key obstacle to scaling real-robot learning, not just data quantity.
- **Organize Research around Data Reuse Axes:** To systematically study data reuse, FolDeX organizes its benchmark around four specific research axes:
 - **Recovery:** How human intervention and recovery data collected during policy deployment can be used to improve subsequent policies.
 - **Cross-Task:** How data can be transferred across different garment categories and even between rigid-object and deformable-object manipulation tasks.
 - **Cross-Scene:** How previously collected data can remain effective when environmental factors like lighting, background, and spatial layout change.
 - **Cross-Embodiment:** How real-robot experience can be transferred across different robotic platforms with varying kinematics, sensors, and action spaces.
- **Provide a Comprehensive Dataset and Evaluation System:** The objective is to release a large-scale real-robot dataset (2,000+ hours) and an operational evaluation platform that supports external policy submissions, standardized physical execution, and auditable results, thereby serving as a community resource.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FolDeX is structured as a physical-world benchmark and dataset, built entirely from real-robot data, focusing on long-horizon deformable object manipulation.

**Design Principles:**
1. **Long-horizon closed-loop manipulation:** Tasks involve sequences where early errors significantly impact subsequent actions.
2. **Deformable objects:** Emphasis on challenges posed by continuously changing shapes, self-occlusion, and contact dynamics.
3. **Heterogeneous data reuse:** Systematic organization of experience across recovery, task, scene, and embodiment axes to support data efficiency.

**Task Suite and Policy Interface:**
The primary task family is long-horizon garment folding, encompassing stages from retrieval from a basket, placement on a table (random configuration), flattening, multiple folding operations, to final stacking. The suite also includes other deformable-object tasks and a few rigid-object tasks to study rigid-to-deformable transfer.
The policy interface provides synchronized RGB observations from two wrist-mounted hand-eye cameras and one external workspace camera, natural-language instructions, and robot proprioceptive state. Policies predict future trajectories of robot end-effector poses.

**Data Collection and Organization:**
FolDeX comprises over 2,000 hours of real-robot data across more than 20 manipulation tasks and 10+ robotic embodiments. The platforms include Aloha, YAM, Piper, Astribot, Franka, UR5, a self-developed mobile dual-arm platform, and AgiBot G2, covering single-arm, dual-arm, mobile, and humanoid configurations in tabletop and household environments.
Data are organized using a common schema that includes synchronized visual observations, robot proprioception, timestamped actions, natural-language task instructions, and metadata (task, object category, object instance, scene, embodiment, operator, data source, completion status, and failure reason).

**Benchmark Tracks (Research Axes):**
The data are partitioned to enable systematic study of data reuse efficiency and transfer performance across four axes:
- **Recovery-data utilization:** Policies are deployed, and human intervention/recovery trajectories are collected. These are then combined with demonstration data to train new policies, assessing the model's ability to leverage recovery data.
- **Cross-task transfer:** Investigates the mutual benefit of training with rigid and deformable object data. It also assesses adaptation to long-horizon deformable tasks using limited deformable data after training on a rigid-object task.
- **Cross-scene transfer and generalization:** Evaluates data utility and policy adaptation/generalization under variations in spatial layout, lighting, visual background, and surrounding environment.
- **Cross-embodiment transfer:** Explores sharing task knowledge and action patterns across different robot platforms with varying kinematics, control interfaces, and action spaces. FolDeX defines standardized data partitions and evaluation protocols for each track.

**Evaluation Platform (FoldChallenge):**
FoldChallenge is an operational, publicly accessible real-robot evaluation platform that supports external policy submissions. It ensures standardized evaluation through:
- Standardized task definitions.
- Held-out physical objects.
- Controlled initializations.
- Unified execution protocol.
- Provision of competitive reference implementations (e.g., a strong $\pi_0$ baseline with Real-Time Chunking (RTC)).
- Auditable rollout videos and leaderboard-based reporting.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
To provide a comprehensive assessment beyond binary success, FoldChallenge uses:
- **Task success rate:** Binary indication of episode completion.
- **Average completion time:** Time taken for successful trials.
- **Final-state neatness:** A six-level score (0 to 5) assessing the folded garment's overall shape, compactness, flatness, structural stability, and edge alignment.
- **FoldScore:** A composite metric combining normalized final-state quality ($\bar{Q}$), complete-task success rate (R), and a time component (inverse normalized completion time, $t/T_{max}$), weighted as $35\bar{Q} + 35R + 30 \max(0, 1 - t/T_{max})$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents preliminary real-robot experiments across the four research axes, providing reference results. Evaluations use 30 independent physical trials per garment category, with randomized configurations. Success requires completing the entire long-horizon folding sequence without human intervention.

**Baselines (Multi-task vs. Task-specific, RTC vs. Standard):**
- **Average Success Rate:** RTC task-specific policies achieved the highest average success rate (82.50%). The RTC multi-task policy achieved 80.75%, while the standard multi-task policy achieved 72.50%.
- **Average FoldScore:** The RTC multi-task policy obtained the highest average FoldScore (75.59). RTC task-specific policies achieved 71.69, and standard multi-task achieved 72.30.
- **Discrepancy:** This difference indicated that binary success rate alone is insufficient to characterize policy performance; FoldScore (considering quality and efficiency) provides a more complete assessment.
- **RTC Impact:** Real-Time Chunking (RTC) improved multi-task performance, increasing average success rate from 72.50% to 80.75% and average FoldScore from 72.30 to 75.59. This improvement was particularly noted for "Pants" and "Towel" tasks, which involve more complex deformable configurations.
- **Task-Specific vs. Multi-task:** Task-specific training showed strong performance on "Shirt," "Skirt," and "Pants." However, the RTC multi-task policy significantly outperformed the task-specific policy on "Towel," achieving a 76.67% success rate compared to 50.00%, suggesting that multi-task training can provide useful transfer for certain garment categories.

**Recovery-Data Utilization:**
- **Improvement:** Augmenting the demonstration-only RTC multi-task baseline with recovery trajectories from four garment categories (Shirt, Skirt, Pants, Towel) led to notable performance gains.
- **Metrics:** The average success rate increased from 80.75% to 95.00%, and the average FoldScore improved from 75.59 to 82.53.
- **Specific Tasks:** "Pants" and "Towel" tasks achieved a 100.00% success rate with recovery data augmentation.

**Preliminary Observations on Other Transfer Axes:**
- **Cross-Task Transfer (Rigid-to-Deformable):** Experiments involving direct joint training or continued training with heterogeneous task data (rigid and deformable objects) often resulted in substantial interference in action generation or catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired behaviors. Policies rarely maintained reliable performance over complete long-horizon deformable-manipulation episodes.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** Similar severe challenges were observed. Joint training across multiple robotic embodiments or continued training from one embodiment to another frequently led to significant action-space interference and catastrophic forgetting due to differences in robot kinematics, camera configurations, control interfaces, and action representations. Policies generally failed to complete full tasks reliably under these settings.
- **Cross-Scene Transfer:** Policies demonstrated stronger robustness to variations in lighting, background, and moderate workspace layout. Cross-scene variations were less disruptive compared to changes in task dynamics or robotic embodiment.

These preliminary observations collectively indicate that heterogeneous real-robot data cannot be effectively reused through naive data mixing or simple continued training alone. They underscore the practical challenges related to negative transfer, catastrophic forgetting, and cross-embodiment action alignment, thereby validating the utility of FolDeX's structured benchmark axes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FolDeX introduces a critical resource for advancing research in embodied AI and robotic manipulation, particularly for complex deformable objects in real-world settings. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Critical Gaps in Benchmarking:** FolDeX directly addresses the identified lack of real-robot benchmarks for long-horizon deformable object manipulation, especially garment folding, which is a key challenge for household robotics. By focusing on these difficult tasks, it provides a testbed for developing more robust and capable policies than those trained solely on short-horizon or rigid-object tasks.
- **Standardizing Real-Robot Evaluation:** The establishment of FoldChallenge as an operational and publicly accessible evaluation platform is significant. It provides standardized task definitions, controlled initializations, held-out objects, and a unified execution protocol. This standardization is crucial for ensuring fair, reproducible, and interpretable comparisons between different policies, mitigating the issues of varied experimental setups that often plague real-robot evaluations. The provision of competitive baselines further enhances its utility for benchmarking.
- **Promoting Research on Data Efficiency and Reuse:** By organizing its extensive real-robot dataset along four distinct research axes—recovery, cross-task, cross-scene, and cross-embodiment—FolDeX shifts the research focus from merely scaling data collection to efficiently reusing existing, expensive real-robot data. This promotes the development of algorithms and methodologies specifically designed for heterogeneous data transfer, reducing the reliance on massive, task-specific data collection campaigns.
- **Highlighting Unresolved Challenges:** The preliminary findings, particularly concerning the difficulties of cross-task and cross-embodiment transfer with naive data mixing, underscore persistent challenges in generalization and transfer learning for real-robot systems. FolDeX serves as a physical testbed for researchers to explore solutions for negative transfer, catastrophic forgetting, and action alignment across diverse robotic platforms and tasks.
- **Supporting Real-World Applications:** The focus on garment folding and other deformable object manipulation is directly relevant to enhancing the capabilities of service robots in domestic and industrial environments. Advancements made using FolDeX could lead to more practical and versatile robots capable of handling a wider range of everyday items.
- **Community Resource:** As a large-scale real-robot dataset (2,000+ hours) coupled with an external physical evaluation system and transparent leaderboard, FolDeX provides a valuable community resource. It lowers the barrier for researchers to conduct real-world experiments and contributes to collective progress in developing more generalist and robust robotic manipulation policies.
