# 3DGen-Bench: Comprehensive Benchmark Suite for 3D Generative Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces 3DGen-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark for 3D generative models, which establishes the first large-scale human preference dataset for evaluating text-to-3D and image-to-3D outputs across five distinct criteria. The work also develops automated scoring models, 3DGen-Score and 3DGen-Eval, that demonstrate superior correlation with human judgments, with 3DGen-Score achieving an average pairwise alignment of 0.725 for text-to-3D and 0.767 for image-to-3D tasks.

## Problem

- The rapid advancement in 3D generative models has outpaced the development of robust and human-aligned evaluation methodologies.
- Existing 3D evaluation metrics (e.g., CLIP similarity) are insufficient as they lack 3D-specific prior knowledge and fail to capture the multifaceted aspects of 3D asset quality.
- There is a critical absence of large-scale human preference datasets in the 3D domain, which are essential for training automated evaluators aligned with human perception, unlike in 2D image and language generation.

## Method

- A comprehensive benchmark suite, **3DGen-Bench**, was developed, featuring a diverse ==model zoo== of 19 3D generative models and a substantial set of 1,020 text and image prompts.
- A large-scale ==human preference dataset== was collected through ==multi-criteria human voting== (68,400 comparison votes, 56,100 absolute scores) across five key dimensions of 3D asset quality.
- Two human-aligned automated scoring models, ==CLIP-based== **3DGen-Score** and ==MLLM-based== **3DGen-Eval**, were trained using a ==two-stage strategy== on the collected human preference data.

## Results

- Human evaluations established leaderboards, identifying MVDream as the top performer for text-to-3D and Wonder3D for image-to-3D, demonstrating specific strengths and weaknesses across models.
- 3DGen-Score consistently achieved higher pairwise alignment with human judgments (average **0.725** for text-to-3D, **0.767** for image-to-3D) and superior Kendall's Tau ranking correlation compared to all baseline metrics.
- Both 3DGen-Score and 3DGen-Eval showed strong generalization to unseen models and external datasets, and 3DGen-Score successfully functioned as a reward model to optimize the MVDream generative model, enhancing 3D asset quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human preference, systematically collected across multiple dimensions, serves as a highly effective and reliable foundation for evaluating 3D generative models.
> - Automated evaluators can be trained to accurately predict human preferences for 3D assets, offering scalable and efficient alternatives to manual evaluations.
> - Integrating human preference-aligned metrics as reward functions can effectively guide the optimization of 3D generative models, leading to improved output quality and coherence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D content generation has witnessed a revolutionary surge, primarily driven by advancements in diffusion models and the availability of large-scale 3D datasets. This progress has led to an explosion of powerful 3D generative models capable of creating high-fidelity assets from various inputs like text or images, with applications spanning virtual reality, gaming, film, and robotics. However, a significant bottleneck has emerged: the rapid development in 3D generation has not been matched by equivalent advancements in 3D evaluation techniques.

Traditional 3D evaluation methods suffer from several limitations. Metrics like CLIP similarity, widely used for text-image alignment, prove inadequate for 3D due to their lack of 3D-specific prior knowledge and inability to capture the multifaceted aspects of 3D assets (e.g., geometry, texture consistency across views). User studies, while reliable, are costly and difficult to scale, and often lack standardized prompt sets for fair comparisons. Existing benchmarks like T3Bench have attempted to establish more suitable prompt sets but still rely on inherently limited metrics like original CLIP for quality assessment. GPTEval3D explored the potential of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) like GPT-4V for 3D evaluation, but its closed-source nature raises concerns about uninterpretable biases. Crucially, the 3D domain largely lacks comprehensive human preference datasets that have been instrumental in advancing 2D image and language generation evaluations. These 2D benchmarks have successfully developed human-aligned metrics, a transparency and user-focus that is conspicuously absent in 3D.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap by proposing "3DGen-Bench," a pioneering effort to establish a comprehensive, human-preference-aligned benchmark for 3D generative models. By creating a large-scale dataset of human judgments and developing automated evaluators that correlate strongly with these judgments, the authors aim to standardize and democratize 3D model evaluation. This work aligns 3D evaluation with a principle well-established in other generative AI fields—that human preference is the ultimate arbiter of quality—thereby bridging the divide between subjective human perception and objective automated metrics. It moves the field towards a more equitable and effective assessment paradigm, essential for guiding future research and development in 3D generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop the first comprehensive benchmark that integrates large-scale human preferences to unify the evaluation of both text-to-3D and image-to-3D generative models. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations stemming from the current state of 3D generative AI:

1. **Address the Evaluation Lag:** The rapid progress in 3D generative models has outpaced the development of robust and reliable evaluation methodologies. Current metrics are often insufficient or lack human alignment, creating a need for a more sophisticated system.
2. **Align Evaluation with Human Perception:** The authors recognize that automatic evaluation for 3D content needs to be equitably aligned with human perception, similar to successful approaches in language and image generation. The absence of a comprehensive human preference dataset in the 3D domain is a critical void they aim to fill.
3. **Standardize Prompt Sets:** The lack of standardized prompt sets in existing user studies makes fair comparisons between different 3D generative models challenging. A key motivation is to curate a diverse and comprehensive prompt set to enable consistent and meaningful evaluations.
4. **Develop Human-Aligned Automated Evaluators:** Relying solely on human evaluations is costly and time-consuming. The paper aims to train automated scoring models that can accurately predict human preferences, offering efficient and scalable alternatives while also providing interpretability.
5. **Foster Equitable Evaluation and Advancement:** By providing a transparent, user-focused metric grounded in real-world human preference data, the authors aim to foster a more equitable evaluation landscape, which in turn will accelerate the development of 3D generative models and their downstream applications.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond superficial or biased 3D evaluation metrics by introducing a human-centric, multi-dimensional, and standardized benchmarking suite that can reliably assess the quality of 3D generative outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is meticulously designed in three key phases: building a comprehensive model zoo and prompt set, collecting large-scale human preferences, and developing human-aligned automated scoring models.

#### 3.1. Comprehensive Model Zoo for 3D Generation

- **Model Selection:** The benchmark curates 19 representative open-sourced 3D generative models, comprising 9 for text-to-3D and 13 for image-to-3D (with some overlap). This diverse selection includes optimization-based and feed-forward approaches, covering various 3D representations like point clouds, triplanes, NeRFs, and 3D Gaussian models. This ensures a broad and representative evaluation platform.
- **Model Implementation and Rendering:** Official codebases are prioritized, with `Threestudio` used for consistency where official implementations are unavailable. All experiments were conducted using default hyperparameters over four weeks on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. For evaluation, 3D models (in PLY, OBJ, or GLB formats) are rendered into three 360° panoramic video formats: RGB, normal maps, and geometry (texture-free), capturing both visual and geometric information.
- **Prompt Generation:** A substantial set of 1,020 diverse prompts (510 text, 510 image) is created, approximately ten times larger than previous works.
 - **Text Prompts:** GPT-4 is used heuristically to generate prompts by deconstructing sentences into components (count, category, attribution, spatial relation) and then applying various sentence templates. Manual screening ensures diversity and representativeness across 6 basic subjects (Vehicle, Animal, Plant, Food, Indoor/Outdoor objects) and 4 difficulty scenarios (single-object, multi-object, micro-scene).
 - **Image Prompts:** 510 high-quality and diverse images are manually collected, with backgrounds removed using `rembg` to serve as prompts. These cover various complexities, from individual objects to multi-object compositions and complex scenes.

#### 3.2. Multi-Criteria Human Voting Collection

- **Evaluation Criteria:** Five key dimensions for 3D asset assessment are defined, building upon GPTEval3D, with slight adjustments:
 1. **Geometry Plausibility:** Assesses logical and physical resemblance to real objects, avoiding distortions or noisy geometry.
 2. **Geometry Details:** Evaluates the fineness and intricacy of shapes, distinguishing detailed features from noise.
 3. **Texture Quality:** Covers aesthetic aspects (realistic material, coloring) and consistency (uniformity, seamlessness across views).
 4. **Geometry-Texture Coherence:** Assesses alignment between geometric and texture characteristics, ensuring textures naturally conform.
 5. **Prompt-Asset Alignment:** Ensures accuracy in reflecting prompt details (category, quantity, attributes, spatial relationships) and identity consistency for image-to-3D.
- **3DGen-Arena Platform:** An integrated, arena-style platform is built for efficient data collection. It supports anonymous pairwise battles where users vote on five dimensions, presenting 3D assets via three 360° panoramic videos (normal, textureless geometry, textured renderings). The platform also supports "named battles" and "direct chat."
- **Large-Scale Human Annotation:** 47 professional expert annotators are engaged, provided with detailed guidelines and compensated. They perform two types of annotations:
 1. **Comparison Votes:** Annotators choose a better model in pairwise battles across the five dimensions, resulting in 68,400 unique votes after stringent validation (e.g., strong-conflict ratio checks).
 2. **Absolute Scores:** Annotators assign numerical scores within predefined ranges for each dimension to individual models. This process involves ranking models first to ensure consistency, yielding 56,100 weighted absolute score entries after validation.

#### 3.3. Human-aligned Automatic Scoring Models

The collected dataset is used to train two complementary automated evaluators:

- **CLIP-based 3DGen-Score Model:**
 - **Architecture:** Extends a pre-trained CLIP model with three encoder modules: a prompt encoder (for text/image prompts), a normal visual encoder, and an RGB visual encoder. It processes multi-view normal and RGB renderings simultaneously.
 - **Scoring Formula:** Five dimension-specific scoring functions are formulated: Geometry Plausibility, Prompt-Asset Alignment, and Geometry-Texture Coherence are calculated as similarities between relevant embeddings, while Geometry Details and Texture Quality are linear predictors.
 - **Training Strategy:** A "two-stage" strategy is employed.
 1. **Stage 1 (Encoder Adaptation):** Vision encoders are fine-tuned with contrastive loss to align concatenated normal/RGB embeddings with prompt embeddings, incorporating external high-quality 3D assets (OmniObject3D, Cap3D) to address imperfections in generated models.
 2. **Stage 2 (Predictor Training):** Parameters of the scoring functions are optimized using KL-divergence loss against human comparison votes, with selective unfreezing of vision encoder layers.
- **MLLM-based 3DGen-Eval Model:**
 - **Architecture:** Fine-tunes `MV-LLaVA` (which uses CLIP for visual embedding and LLaVA for QA) to operate on concatenated multi-view RGB and normal images.
 - **Training Strategy:** Also a "two-stage" process.
 1. **Stage 1 (Vision Encoder Adaptation):** Finetunes the visual encoder (keeping LLaVA frozen) to understand concatenated and normal image formats, supervised by GPT-4V generated captions.
 2. **Stage 2 (Instruct Tuning):** Two sub-modules are trained: one for assigning absolute scores (on absolute score data) and another for pairwise comparison (on comparison vote data). To enhance interpretability, the model is encouraged to generate explanations, guided by GPT-4V responses.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that 3DGen-Bench is robust, diverse, and deeply aligned with human judgment, providing a powerful tool for advancing 3D generative AI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted demonstrate the efficacy of 3DGen-Bench and its proposed automated evaluators.

#### 4.1. Human Preference Evaluations

- **Model Rankings:** Based on average Elo scores derived from expert human annotations, leaderboards were established for both text-to-3D and image-to-3D tracks.
 - **Text-to-3D:** MVDream (1177.66), LucidDreamer (1112.21), and Magic3D (1088.93) emerged as the top three. MVDream's success highlights the power of multi-view diffusion models, while LucidDreamer performed well on geometry and texture. Magic3D showed strong geometric plausibility but lagged in texture quality due to SDS loss limitations.
 - **Image-to-3D:** Wonder3D (1304.05), OpenLRM (1279.67), and Stable Zero123 (1200.69) were the top performers. Wonder3D's multi-view cross-domain attention validated the importance of 2D/3D blending and multi-view consistency. OpenLRM benefited from large datasets and transformer architecture, while Stable Zero123 showed overall improvement but still had room for geometry detail enhancement.

#### 4.2. Comparisons on Human Preference Correlation

The proposed 3DGen-Score and 3DGen-Eval models were rigorously compared against various baseline metrics (CLIP Score, Aesthetic Score, HPSv2.1, PickScore, OpenShape, MV-LLaVa, GPTEval3D).

- **Pairwise Alignment:** 3DGen-Score consistently outperformed all other metrics across all five evaluation criteria in both text-to-3D (average 0.725) and image-to-3D (average 0.767) tasks, demonstrating its superior alignment with human judgments. 3DGen-Eval also showed significant improvements over MV-LLaVa and even surpassed GPT-4V in some dimensions.
- **Ranking Alignment (Kendall's Tau):** In ranking correlation, 3DGen-Score achieved the highest average ranking across all dimensions for image-to-3D (average 0.856) and the highest overall average for text-to-3D (average 0.711), with notable improvements in "Prompt-Asset Alignment." 3DGen-Eval also showed strong performance, especially in geometry-related dimensions for text-to-3D.
- **Analysis:** The CLIP-based 3DGen-Score generally outperformed the MLLM-based 3DGen-Eval, attributed to its simpler architecture and efficient training. Performance was generally stronger on the image-to-3D track, possibly due to more training data and fewer biases from text-image alignment.

#### 4.3. Generalization Performance

- **Evaluation on New Model (Instant-Mesh):** When tested on an unseen image-to-3D model (Instant-Mesh), 3DGen-Score exhibited strong generalization capabilities, surpassing existing methods in pairwise alignment, confirming its robustness as an evaluation metric.
- **Evaluation on External Dataset (3DRewardDB):** Despite limitations in external data (e.g., estimated normal maps), 3DGen-Score and 3DGen-Eval still achieved outstanding performance on 3DRewardDB, demonstrating remarkable robustness to diverse data sources and inherent estimation noise.

#### 4.4. Ablation Experiments

- The "two-stage" training strategy for 3DGen-Score was validated. Both stages contribute to improved performance: the second stage enhances alignment with human judgments, and the first stage (Encoder-Adaptation) strengthens robustness to new models.

#### 4.5. Application: Reward-based Optimization

- 3DGen-Score was successfully applied as a reward model (RM) to optimize the MVDream generative model. This demonstrated that integrating human preference-aligned feedback as a loss function (L_SDS - λr * r) can effectively enhance the quality and coherence of 3D generative outputs, leading to improvements in noise reduction, 3D prior simulation, and texture refinement. Dimension-specific optimization also showed potential for targeted improvements, with observed interactions across dimensions.

In summary, the main findings conclusively establish 3DGen-Bench as a superior, human-aligned benchmark suite, and its accompanying evaluators, 3DGen-Score and 3DGen-Eval, as robust and accurate tools for assessing 3D generative models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of 3DGen-Bench marks a pivotal advancement in the field of 3D content generation and evaluation, with profound significance and wide-ranging potential impacts:

1. **Standardized and Human-Aligned Evaluation:** 3DGen-Bench introduces the first large-scale, multi-dimensional human preference dataset for 3D models, addressing a critical gap in the research landscape. By anchoring evaluation in human judgment across five distinct criteria, it provides a much-needed standardized and equitable framework for comparing and assessing 3D generative models. This will lead to more credible and consistent benchmarks, fostering fair competition and clearer progress tracking.
2. **Accelerated Research and Development:** By providing robust automated evaluators (3DGen-Score and 3DGen-Eval) that closely align with human preferences, the paper offers efficient and scalable alternatives to costly and time-consuming manual user studies. This will significantly accelerate the iteration cycles for 3D generative model development, allowing researchers to quickly assess the impact of their innovations.
3. **Interpretability and Robustness:** 3DGen-Eval, being MLLM-based and offering interpretability through reasoning explanations, provides deeper insights into why a particular 3D model is deemed better or worse. This complements the efficiency and scalability of 3DGen-Score. Their combined strengths offer a comprehensive evaluation system that is both accurate and insightful. The demonstrated robustness to unseen models and external datasets ensures its utility beyond the immediate benchmark.
4. **Enabling Reward-Based Optimization:** The successful application of 3DGen-Score as a reward model for optimizing generative models (e.g., MVDream) opens up new avenues for improving 3D generation. This integration of human feedback into the training loop, inspired by Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) in other domains, can lead to generative models that inherently produce higher-quality, more human-preferred 3D assets. This is a significant step towards creating truly "user-friendly" 3D generative AI.
5. **Foundation for Future Work:** 3DGen-Bench serves as a foundational dataset and methodology for future research. It highlights the need for more advanced 3D embedding techniques, finer-grained (e.g., region-level) annotations, and more sophisticated evaluator architectures. The public availability of the dataset and the 3DGen-Arena platform ensures accessibility and encourages community involvement, fostering collaborative advancements.
6. **Broader Impact on Applications:** Improved 3D evaluation directly impacts applications in virtual reality, gaming, design, and robotics. Higher quality and more consistent 3D assets, refined through human-aligned metrics, will lead to more immersive experiences, more realistic virtual environments, and more capable robotic systems.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors** include the current reliance on 2D CLIP embedding due to the lack of robust native 3D embedding models, the inability to include many closed-source state-of-the-art generative models, and the instance-level nature of current annotations which could evolve into finer-grained labels in the future. Despite these, 3DGen-Bench is a monumental contribution that elevates the state of 3D generative model evaluation, paving the way for a new era of high-quality and human-centric 3D content creation.
