# BridgeFlow: Fast and Robust SE(2)-Equivariant Motion Planning with Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced BridgeFlow, an SE(2)-equivariant motion planning framework based on Flow Matching. This framework integrates a Brownian bridge informative prior, context-aware optimal transport, and a task-centric canonicalization module, enabling fast and robust trajectory generation with strong generalization across 2D and 7-DoF robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Learning-based generative motion planners face a trade-off between robust spatial generalization (equivariance) and real-time inference speed.
- Existing Flow Matching planners often use naive Gaussian priors, leading to complex vector fields and high transport costs that hinder inference efficiency and training stability.
- Many generative models either lack inherent spatial equivariance or require computationally expensive specialized neural architectures to achieve it, limiting real-time applicability.
- Current generative planners often disregard environmental geometries during training, relying on slow, inference-time optimization for obstacle avoidance.

## Method

- Developed a ==Flow Matching== framework that incorporates a ==Brownian bridge== informative prior, reducing ==optimal transport== cost and straightening the flow field for efficient few-step inference.
- Implemented a lightweight ==task-centric canonicalization== module to achieve strict ==SE(2)-equivariance==, ensuring spatial generalization without computationally heavy network architectures.
- Designed context-aware ==mini-batch optimal transport== to stabilize training by aligning prior samples with expert trajectories from identical task conditions.
- Integrated environment-aware ==Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)== to embed collision avoidance directly into the learned vector field, eliminating the need for post-hoc optimization during inference.

## Results

- Achieved **15x faster** inference than diffusion baselines in 2D, with BridgeFlow reaching **100% Success Rate** and **70.25% Valid Rate** in just **2 steps** (**0.0071s**).
- Maintained strong zero-shot SE(2) generalization, achieving **74.85% Valid Rate** on spatially transformed 2D tasks where baselines showed significant degradation.
- Outperformed baselines in 7-DoF Franka manipulation, achieving **88.0% Valid Rate** for in-distribution tasks and **80.0% Valid Rate** on out-of-distribution SE(2) transformed tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An informative Brownian bridge prior significantly reduces the optimal transport cost and straightens the flow field, enabling faster and more stable Flow Matching trajectory generation.
> - Strict SE(2)-equivariance can be efficiently achieved through a canonicalization-inference-decanonicalization pipeline, allowing standard neural networks to operate in a normalized space and generalize across spatial transformations.
> - By learning environmental constraints directly through Classifier-Free Guidance, generative planners can produce collision-free paths intrinsically, preserving the low-latency benefits of Flow Matching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Motion planning is a foundational challenge in robotics, requiring the generation of collision-free and kinematically feasible trajectories. Traditionally, this problem has been addressed by sampling-based (e.g., RRT-Connect) and optimization-based methods (e.g., CHOMP, STOMP). While these classical approaches offer theoretical guarantees, their computational demands often restrict real-time application in complex, dynamic environments.

The emergence of learning-based approaches has presented an alternative, aiming to overcome the computational bottlenecks of traditional methods. Within learning-based motion planning, two primary paradigms exist: sequential state prediction and joint trajectory generation. Sequential methods iteratively predict future states, often suffering from compounding errors and slow sequential inference, and typically lack consideration for spatial equivariance. Joint trajectory generation models, such as those based on diffusion (e.g., MPD, EDMP, Diffusion Policy), have shown promise in generating feasible and multimodal trajectories by directly modeling the entire trajectory distribution.

However, existing learning-based generative models for motion planning face several challenges. A critical one is the dilemma between achieving geometric robustness and maintaining real-time inference speed. Many models either lack inherent equivariance to rigid body transformations, treating spatially altered tasks as entirely new scenarios, or they implement equivariance through specialized, computationally intensive neural architectures. These specialized architectures can introduce significant computational overhead, hindering real-time performance. Furthermore, generative models like diffusion are known for their slow inference due to iterative denoising processes.

Flow Matching (FM) has been introduced as a method to accelerate generative modeling by constructing a smooth probability flow, enabling efficient few-step inference via ordinary differential equation solvers. While some FM-based planners have been developed (e.g., FlowMP, ActionFlow), they frequently suffer from limitations such as a lack of inherent spatial equivariance, the use of naive Gaussian priors that lead to complex vector fields and high transport costs, and sub-optimal handling of environmental awareness. Many existing generative planners often disregard environmental geometries during training, relying on computationally expensive cost-function gradient guidance during inference for obstacle avoidance.

BridgeFlow addresses these specific limitations by integrating a set of novel components within a Flow Matching framework. It aims to provide a fast, robust, and strictly SE(2)-equivariant solution that can generalize spatially without relying on computationally heavy architectures or extensive data augmentation. This work attempts to bridge the gap between geometric robustness and inference efficiency in learning-based generative motion planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind BridgeFlow is to resolve a critical trade-off prevalent in current learning-based motion planners: the dilemma between achieving robust spatial generalization through equivariance and maintaining fast, real-time inference capabilities. The authors identify several specific limitations in existing methods that motivate their proposed framework:

- **Lack of Inherent Equivariance:** Many learning-based planners do not inherently possess equivariance to rigid body transformations (like rotation and translation). This means they treat structurally identical tasks presented in different poses as entirely novel problems, necessitating extensive data augmentation or leading to brittle performance and a lack of spatial robustness when encountering transformed environments.
- **Computational Bottlenecks from Equivariant Architectures:** Approaches that enforce equivariance via specialized neural architectures (e.g., group equivariant convolutional networks or SE(3)-transformers) often introduce significant computational overhead. This overhead can severely bottleneck real-time inference, making these methods impractical for applications requiring low-latency responses.
- **Slow Inference of Iterative Generative Models:** Diffusion models, despite their ability to generate diverse and feasible trajectories, inherently suffer from slow inference speeds due to their iterative denoising process, which requires many steps to converge to a high-quality sample.
- **Inefficient Priors in Flow Matching:** While Flow Matching offers faster inference than diffusion, existing FM-based planners often utilize naive, task-agnostic Gaussian noise as their prior distribution. This uninforming prior can result in highly curved and complex vector fields, increasing the "transport cost" (kinetic energy) required to transform noise into a target trajectory, thereby reducing inference efficiency and training stability.
- **Sub-optimal Environmental Awareness:** Current generative planners frequently ignore environmental geometries during training or rely on computationally expensive methods for obstacle avoidance. This can either involve conditioning on heavy, high-dimensional sensory data, incurring computational overhead, or entirely relying on iterative, cost-function gradient guidance during inference, which undermines the speed benefits of generative models.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of BridgeFlow are:

1. **Accelerate Inference Speed while Maintaining Trajectory Quality:** By developing a Flow Matching framework that incorporates an informative prior and optimized transport mechanism to straighten the flow field and reduce transport costs, enabling rapid few-step inference.
2. **Achieve Strict SE(2)-Equivariance with Minimal Computational Overhead:** By designing a lightweight, task-centric canonicalization module that allows standard neural network architectures to operate in a normalized coordinate frame, guaranteeing spatial generalization without relying on heavy specialized equivariant networks.
3. **Embed Explicit Environmental Awareness:** By integrating environmental occupancy maps into the generative process through Classifier-Free Guidance during training, thereby enabling the model to learn collision-free path generation directly and eliminating the need for expensive inference-time optimization for obstacle avoidance.

These objectives collectively aim to create a motion planning framework that is simultaneously fast, robust, spatially generalizable, and aware of its operational environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BridgeFlow is designed as a continuous-time Flow Matching framework that models trajectory generation as an ordinary differential equation over flow time $t \in$. The overall approach integrates several components to achieve its objectives.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Formulation

The problem is defined as learning a policy $\pi$ to generate a kinematically feasible, collision-free waypoint sequence $x = [p_1, \ldots, p_N]$ conditioned on a task $P = (s, g, M)$, where $s$ and $g$ are start and goal positions, and $M$ is an environmental occupancy map. The central constraint is strict SE(2)-equivariance, meaning that for any rigid body transformation $T = (R, d) \in SE(2)$, the policy must satisfy $\pi(T(P)) = T(\pi(P))$. This translates to the learned neural vector field $v_\theta(x_t, t | P)$ satisfying $v_\theta(T(x_t), t | T(P)) = R \cdot v_\theta(x_t, t | P)$.

#### 3.2. B. Brownian Bridge Informative Prior

To address the inefficiency of naive Gaussian priors in standard Flow Matching, BridgeFlow introduces a Brownian bridge informative prior.
1. **Prior Design:** For a trajectory $x_0$ at flow time $t=0$, a linear interpolation $\mu_{lin,i} = (1 - \gamma_i)s + \gamma_i g$ is constructed between the start $s$ and goal $g$ for each waypoint $i$, where $\gamma_i$ is the normalized waypoint index. The prior $p_{info}$ for each waypoint $x_{0,i}$ is then defined as $x_{0,i} = \mu_{lin,i} + \sigma_{prior} \cdot \sqrt{\gamma_i (1 - \gamma_i)} \cdot \epsilon$, where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$. This design anchors the endpoints by enforcing zero variance at $\gamma_1 = 0$ (start) and $\gamma_N = 1$ (goal), with variance peaking in the middle, reflecting the physical intuition of fine adjustments bounded by fixed endpoints.
2. **Transport Cost Reduction:** The paper provides a theoretical analysis demonstrating that this informative prior reduces the optimal transport cost compared to a standard Gaussian prior. Under linear optimal transport, the cost $C(p_0, p_1)$ is related to $E[\Vert x_1 - x_0 \Vert^2]$. By decomposing the expert trajectory $x_1 = \mu_{lin} + \delta$, it is shown that $C_{std} - C_{info} = \Vert \mu_{lin} \Vert^2 + 2\langle \mu_{lin}, E[\delta] \rangle + (1 - \bar{\sigma}^2)D$. Assuming symmetrically distributed obstacles, $E[\delta] \to 0$, making the reduction margin strictly positive and proving $C_{info} < C_{std}$.
3. **SE(2)-Equivariance:** The Brownian bridge prior is shown to be inherently SE(2)-equivariant. Applying a transformation $T$ to a sample from the prior $x_0 \sim p_0(\cdot | P)$ results in $T(x_0)$ following the distribution of the prior conditioned on the transformed task $p_0(\cdot | T(P))$. This property is crucial for compatibility with the canonicalization module.

#### 3.3. C. Context-Aware Mini-Batch Optimal Transport

Standard mini-batch optimal transport (OT) can inadvertently pair initial noise with expert trajectories from different task contexts, leading to contradictory regression targets and training instability. BridgeFlow proposes a context-aware mini-batch OT mechanism:
- The training mini-batch is dynamically partitioned into disjoint subsets, where each subset contains trajectories sharing the exact same task condition $(s, g, M)$.
- The linear assignment problem (using the Hungarian algorithm) is then solved strictly within each local subset.
- This approach ensures that the optimal transport coupling occurs only between a prior sample generated for a specific task and an expert trajectory corresponding to that identical task, thereby stabilizing training and promoting the learning of smooth, consistent conditional flow fields.

#### 3.4. D. Environment-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)

To embed environmental awareness directly into the generative process without relying on expensive post-hoc optimization, BridgeFlow employs Classifier-Free Guidance:
- During training, the environment occupancy map condition $M$ is randomly masked with a learnable null token $\emptyset$ at a fixed dropout probability $p_{drop}$. This forces the neural network to jointly learn an environment-aware vector field $v_M$ and an unconditional vector field $v_\emptyset$.
- During inference, the final guided vector field $v_{CFG}$ is computed as $v_{CFG}(x_t, t) = v_\emptyset + \omega \cdot (v_M - v_\emptyset)$, where $\omega \geq 1$ is a guidance scale.
- This mechanism distills spatial constraints directly into the learned vector field, eliminating the need for iterative, cost-based gradient guidance during sampling and preserving the low-latency advantage of Flow Matching.

#### 3.5. E. Task-Centric Canonicalization

To achieve strict SE(2)-equivariance without requiring specialized, computationally heavy network architectures, BridgeFlow implements a lightweight task-centric canonicalization module:
1. **SE(2)-Equivariance Definition:** The paper formally defines SE(2)-equivariance for the vector field: $v_\theta(T(x_t), t | T(s), T(g), T(M)) = R_\alpha v_\theta(x_t, t | s, g, M)$, where $T = (R_\alpha, d)$ is a rigid transformation. This implies velocities only rotate, not translate.
2. **Equivariant Framework Construction:**
 - **Canonicalization Operator:** A canonicalization operator $T_{can}(p) = R_{-\theta}(p - s)$ is defined. Here, $s$ is translated to the origin, and the start-goal vector (angle $\theta = \text{atan2}(g_y - s_y, g_x - s_x)$) is rotated to align with the positive x-axis.
 - **Pipeline:** The process follows a Canonicalize-Infer-Decanonicalize pipeline:
 - **Canonicalize:** All spatial inputs (trajectory $x_t$, start $s$, goal $g$, and occupancy map $M$) are deterministically transformed into the canonical frame (e.g., $\hat{x}_t = T_{can}(x_t)$, $\hat{s}=0$, $\hat{g}=(\Vert g-s \Vert, 0)$, $\hat{M}(p) = M(T_{can}^{-1}(p))$).
 - **Infer:** A standard neural network (e.g., U-Net) predicts the vector field $\hat{v}$ entirely within this canonical space: $\hat{v} = v_\theta(\hat{x}_t, t | \hat{s}, \hat{g}, \hat{M})$. Since the canonical inputs are invariant to global transformations, the canonical network learns a single, generalized mapping.
 - **Decanonicalize:** The predicted canonical velocities $\hat{v}$ are then mapped back to the global frame via the inverse rotation: $v_{global} = R_\theta \hat{v}$.
3. **Proof of SE(2)-Equivariance:** The paper provides a formal proof that this framework guarantees strict SE(2)-equivariance. It shows that canonical inputs remain invariant under any global SE(2) transformation, leading to the same canonical vector field. The final decanonicalization step correctly applies the global rotation, satisfying the pushforward rule for vector fields.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of BridgeFlow was conducted across two distinct domains: a 2D planar navigation task (PointMass2D Dense) and a 7-DoF Franka manipulator task. The performance of BridgeFlow was compared against diffusion-based MPD and standard Flow Matching (FlowMP), with ablations demonstrating the contribution of individual components.

#### 4.1. A. Results in PointMass2D Dense Environment

1. **Generative Quality and Inference Efficiency (RQ1):**
 - BridgeFlow demonstrated faster inference and higher trajectory quality compared to baselines. At 5 inference steps, it achieved an 80.95% Valid Rate (VR) and 1.67 Collision Intensity, which was approximately double the safety performance of MPD (42.48% VR, 9.67 Intensity).
 - BridgeFlow achieved a 100% Success Rate (SR) and 70.25% VR in just 2 steps (0.0071s), while MPD required 15 DDIM steps (0.1128s) for comparable performance, indicating a 15x inference speedup.
 - Ablation studies confirmed the synergy between the Brownian bridge prior and context-aware mini-batch OT. Standard OT alone resulted in a VR of 58.38%, whereas combining it with the Brownian bridge prior increased VR to 80.95%, supporting the claim of reduced transport cost and straightened flow fields.

2. **Generalization to Unseen Contexts (RQ2):**
 - On environments with novel obstacle layouts and unseen start-goal pairs (Random Contexts), BridgeFlow maintained superior robustness with 39.4% VR and 7.5 Collision Intensity.
 - In contrast, CFG-enhanced MPD (where CFG was integrated into the baseline for fair comparison) showed a lower VR of 19.05% and an Intensity of 14.63. This suggested that CFG is more effective when applied to the structured vector fields generated by BridgeFlow's integrated components.

3. **Geometric Robustness and SE(2)-Equivariance (RQ3):**
 - When evaluated on tasks subjected to continuous SE(2) spatial transformations, baselines (MPD, FlowMP) experienced significant performance degradation. Their SRs fell to 40-52%, VRs dropped below 6%, and Collision Intensities rose to nearly 25. This indicated that these models overfit to absolute coordinates and lacked spatial reasoning.
 - BridgeFlow maintained strong performance with an SR of 96% and a VR of 74.85% (5.28 Intensity) on transformed tasks. This result supported the efficacy of the task-centric canonicalization module in achieving zero-shot generalization to spatially transformed environments.

4. **Natural Boundary Satisfaction:**
 - The paper noted an architectural advantage: BridgeFlow naturally satisfied boundary conditions without requiring heuristic "trajectory in-painting" (overwriting endpoints). The Brownian bridge prior's analytical enforcement of zero variance at endpoints during training allowed the network to learn that the vector field at these points should be zero, enabling autonomous trajectory integration by the ODE solver.

#### 4.2. B. Results on 7-DoF Franka Manipulation

1. **High-Dimensional Generative Performance and Efficiency (RQ1):**
 - In the complex 7-DoF joint space for in-distribution tasks, BridgeFlow achieved an 88.0% VR, outperforming MPD (43.0%) and FlowMP (65.0%).
 - It also generated kinematically efficient paths, indicating the effectiveness of context-aware mini-batch OT in high-dimensional state spaces.
 - While the canonicalization module introduced a minor overhead (0.045s inference time vs. FlowMP's 0.038s), BridgeFlow remained over 3x faster than MPD, satisfying real-time control requirements.

2. **Geometric Robustness and SE(2) Generalization (RQ3):**
 - For Out-of-Distribution (OOD) tasks representing SE(2) spatial transformations of the training data (e.g., planning between quadrants not seen during training), baseline models showed catastrophic performance collapse. MPD's VR dropped from 43.0% to 19.0%, and FlowMP's VR decreased from 65.0% to 21.0%.
 - BridgeFlow demonstrated robustness, maintaining an 80.0% VR on these unseen transformed tasks, further validating the effectiveness of the canonicalization framework in achieving zero-shot generalization in high-dimensional robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "BridgeFlow" addresses fundamental limitations in learning-based motion planning, offering a set of contributions with notable significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. A. Significance

- **Resolution of Equivariance-Speed Trade-off:** BridgeFlow demonstrates a method to achieve strict SE(2)-equivariance simultaneously with fast inference speeds, a critical advancement for real-time robotic applications that require both robust spatial generalization and low latency. It provides an alternative to the traditional dilemma of choosing between computationally expensive equivariant architectures and non-equivariant models that lack generalization.
- **Principled Generative Model Design:** The integration of a Brownian bridge informative prior and context-aware mini-batch optimal transport represents a principled approach to constructing more efficient and stable Flow Matching models. This design choice leads to straighter flow fields, reduced transport costs, and more consistent learning, enhancing the overall quality and efficiency of trajectory generation.
- **Efficient Environmental Awareness:** By embedding environmental awareness through Classifier-Free Guidance, BridgeFlow removes the reliance on computationally expensive inference-time optimizations for collision avoidance. This approach allows the model to inherently learn to generate collision-free paths, fully leveraging the speed advantages of Flow Matching.
- **Lightweight Equivariance Mechanism:** The proposed task-centric canonicalization module offers a lightweight and computationally efficient method to guarantee strict SE(2)-equivariance. This avoids the overhead associated with specialized equivariant neural network architectures, making it compatible with standard network designs and easier to integrate into existing learning pipelines.
- **Robust Zero-Shot Generalization:** The framework's ability to generalize robustly to entirely unseen environments and arbitrary spatial transformations without explicit data augmentation demonstrates a substantial improvement in the generalization capabilities of learning-based motion planners.

#### 5.2. B. Potential Impact

- **Enabling Real-time Robotic Deployment:** The combination of fast inference and robust generalization makes BridgeFlow a promising candidate for real-time motion planning in various robotic systems, including autonomous navigation, manipulation, and human-robot interaction in unstructured or dynamic environments.
- **Reduced Data Requirements:** By achieving zero-shot generalization through geometric equivariance, BridgeFlow has the potential to significantly reduce the need for extensive data augmentation during training, which can be time-consuming and resource-intensive, particularly in high-dimensional robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The concepts introduced, such as the Brownian bridge prior, context-aware OT, and the task-centric canonicalization, can serve as foundational components for future research in generative models for robotics and other domains where geometric robustness and efficiency are paramount.
- **Extension to 3D and SE(3) Equivariance:** The framework's modularity suggests potential for extension to 3D motion planning and SE(3)-equivariance, which would broaden its applicability to more complex manipulation and mobile robotics tasks in three-dimensional space.
- **Competitive Alternative to Diffusion Models:** BridgeFlow provides a strong alternative to iterative diffusion models for generative planning, offering a compelling trade-off between generative quality, diversity, and significantly faster inference, thereby expanding the toolkit available to researchers and practitioners in robotic control.
