# eRLT: Efficient VLA Reinforcement Learning via Action-Relevant Token Routing

> [!summary] Summary
> eRLT builds the state representation for lightweight online RL on a frozen VLA by routing action-relevant features across its tokens and layers. Learned routing tokens summarize each selected layer, and a softmax layer router fuses these summaries into one RL token. The router is initialized by expert-action prediction and then refined by the critic loss. It raises mean learning-curve AUC by up to 23.7% in simulation and 108.9% on real USB insertion.

## Problem

- Lightweight frozen-VLA online RL methods like DSRL and PLD build actor-critic state from VLA-independent visual encoders, so they cannot reuse the VLA's internal action-relevant features.
- Methods that reuse VLA features, such as RLT's final-layer reconstruction or MiDAS mean-pooling, compress a fixed layer and token set regardless of which features matter for the task.
- Controlled tests show the best source layer and token subset differ across tasks, so any fixed extraction choice wastes sample efficiency under tight online interaction budgets.

## Method

- ==Token-wise routing==: ==learnable routing tokens== are appended to the VLA prefix in a separate forward pass and attend to input-dependent cues, and their hidden states are mean-pooled at each selected layer.
- ==Layer-wise routing==: per-task softmax layer logits with a temperature weight the per-layer summaries into a single fixed-dimensional ==RL token==, letting each task choose which VLA depths contribute.
- ==Two-stage training==: a probe that predicts expert action chunks from the SFT demonstrations initializes the router, then the critic's ==TD loss== is backpropagated into the routing parameters online, with ==Polyak target copies==.

## Results

- Across four LIBERO and three RoboTwin tasks, eRLT reaches mean normalized AUC **0.626** versus **0.506** for RLT and **0.585** for DSRL, beating RLT on all seven tasks and DSRL on six.
- On a real RB-Y1 robot, USB insertion AUC rises from **0.269** to **0.562** and final success from **50%** to **90%**; ribbon-cable AUC rises from **0.484** to **0.710**.
- Ablations on Bowl-to-Plate drop AUC from **0.818** to **0.742** without token routing, **0.762** without offline initialization, **0.797** without layer routing, and **0.803** without online adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The most useful frozen-model features for RL vary by task across both layers and tokens, so a learned, task-specific readout beats a fixed choice of extraction layer.
> - Pretraining a compressed state on action prediction rather than reconstruction keeps only the information that tells actions apart, which is a better prior for a downstream critic.
> - More routing capacity is not monotonically better; with limited RL feedback, one or two routing tokens beat eight because the extra parameters are hard to coordinate.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

In real-world USB connector insertion, small alignment errors can prevent insertion at contact. Online reinforcement learning can improve a robot's pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) model from the robot's own successes and failures. Collecting high-quality demonstrations and physical robot interactions is costly, so making a fixed interaction budget more productive matters. In eRLT, by Dehao Huang et al. (paper), a small actor and critic learn to gather task-relevant cues from the tokens and layers of a frozen VLA. Every method uses 80 trajectories under a human-in-the-loop protocol. On USB insertion, eRLT's normalized learning-curve AUC is 0.562, compared with 0.269 for RLT. Final-window success is 90% versus 50%.

Shaded regions mark the warm-up phase.

### 2. A summary built for the task

A small actor and critic can learn only if their compact state representation retains the cues relevant to the task. Prior approaches take two routes. A VLA-independent visual encoder misses information already encoded inside the VLA. A fixed compression of selected VLA features may preserve the wrong information for a particular task.

eRLT supplies the actor and critic with an **RL token**, a fixed-dimensional summary. Two kinds of routing build it:

- In **token-wise routing**, learned tokens gather different information from the current visual-language input.
- In **layer-wise routing**, learned task-level weights combine summaries taken at selected VLA depths.

Training proceeds in two stages.

1. **Before online interaction.** A temporary action probe uses existing VLA SFT demonstrations to predict expert action chunks from the RL token. This objective is not a full reconstruction objective. The probe is removed afterward.
1. **During online RL.** The critic's Bellman objective updates the router as well as the critic, so the representation can adapt to value estimation. Actor updates use a detached token.

The VLA stays frozen throughout. Routing tokens are added in a separate forward pass and do not alter the frozen VLA's action proposals. eRLT changes what the actor and critic observe, not the VLA's action-generation path.

Overview of eRLT. (a) Learned tokens are appended to the multimodal input, and summaries from selected VLA layers are weighted into a single RL token for the actor and critic. (b) Expert-action-prediction initialization. (c) Replay transitions feed the critic loss and online router update.

### 3. Learning faster within a fixed budget

The primary measure is **learning-curve AUC**: the area under the evaluation success-rate curve, normalized by the evaluation horizon. It measures average success over the fixed training budget, not just the final success rate.

The simulation study covers four LIBERO tasks and three RoboTwin tasks. Within each setting, the VLA checkpoint and online RL procedure are held fixed, and only the state representation varies. Two baselines are compared:

- **RLT*** keeps a final-layer, reconstruction-compressed token fixed during online RL.
- **DSRL*** uses a VLA-independent visual encoder.

Averaged over the seven task-level AUCs, eRLT scores **0.626** versus **0.506** for RLT*, a 23.7% relative gain. eRLT beats RLT* on all seven tasks, but DSRL* has a higher AUC on Move Can–Pot. Final simulation success rates are omitted because methods eventually reach similar levels.

The evidence is improvement in AUC, not fewer generated tokens, fewer attempts, or reduced elapsed time. The authors attribute the gains to learned, task-specific cue gathering. This is their proposed explanation, not a direct measurement that the router selects a unique, causal set of "action-relevant" features.

### 4. Scope

On the ribbon-cable task, eRLT also improves AUC, but both eRLT and RLT reach 100% final-window success. From inspecting unsuccessful rollouts, the authors interpret the real-robot gains as more consistent correction of small alignment offsets before contact.

The real-world study covers two insertion tasks on one robot, with human assistance under a fixed intervention rule. The method also adds computation and memory through multi-layer feature extraction and periodic RL-token recomputation. Within that scope, eRLT's contribution is a better-performing representation for lightweight online RL under a fixed interaction budget.
