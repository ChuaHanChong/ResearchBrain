# Towards general embodied intelligence: integrating large language models, knowledge bases, and reasoning capabilities to build the next generation of AI agents

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey proposes a unified four-layer architecture (representation-knowledge-reasoning-action) integrating LLMs, structured knowledge bases, and reasoning frameworks with embodied agents toward "general embodied intelligence" (GEI). Synthesizing 170+ works across robotics, KG-augmented reasoning and multi-agent systems, it identifies five open research directions: lightweight deployment, closed-loop knowledge, hybrid reasoning, perception-action grounding, and continual learning.

## Problem

- Prior embodied-intelligence surveys each cover only isolated slices - multimodal-robot tasks, manufacturing, or module-level LLM roles - lacking a unified architecture spanning language, knowledge, reasoning, and action.
- LLM-driven embodied systems rely on general-purpose pretrained models like GPT-3.5/4, not built for low-latency control, causing responsiveness and stability problems in tight control loops.
- Most frameworks stay single-agent with shallow sensorimotor fusion, depend on heavy prompt engineering or human intervention, and lack closed-loop knowledge updating and robust safety constraints.

## Method

- Proposes a unified ==four-layer GEI architecture== - representation, knowledge, reasoning, action - mapping LLM backbones, ==RAG==/knowledge-graph modules, ==CoT==/==ToT==/==ReAct== reasoning, and perception-cognition-action embodiment into one coordinated system.
- Constructs a comparative taxonomy against five prior embodied-intelligence reviews (Table I), exposing each one's specific architectural, semantic-alignment, deployment-bottleneck, or evaluation-standard gap.
- Derives five concrete research directions - lightweight edge LLM deployment, closed-loop knowledge systems, hybrid symbolic-neural reasoning, perception-action grounding, and continual interactive learning - as **GEI**'s technical roadmap.

## Results

- Its comparative gap analysis shows all **five** prior reviews (2024-2025) omit at least one of the four GEI layers, positioning this survey as the first cross-layer synthesis.
- Surveyed reasoning-integration methods report large measured gains: Hydra beats the ToG-2 baseline by **20.3%** average across seven QA datasets, and GNN-RAG improves WebQSP/CWQ by **8.9-15.5** percentage points.
- Compiled LLM-embodied control case studies show concrete task numbers: GPT-4-driven industrial robot planning reaches **81.88%** task completion, and LLM-based RDF-triple verification attains **88%** precision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A four-layer decomposition (representation/knowledge/reasoning/action) is a reusable lens for auditing any embodied-AI stack for missing closed-loop coupling, not just for classifying papers.
> - Across surveyed systems, reasoning and knowledge gains transfer more reliably than physical grounding does - sensorimotor fusion and real-time adaptation remain the weakest layer.
> - Deploying LLMs on physical agents trades capability for latency and safety risk, so lightweight edge-optimized variants and fail-safe constraints are prerequisites, not later add-ons.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current research landscape in artificial intelligence is characterized by significant advancements in several distinct areas. Large Language Models (LLMs), exemplified by models like GPT-4 and Gemini, have demonstrated proficiency in natural language tasks, exhibiting fluency, coherence, and emergent capabilities in zero-shot and few-shot learning. Concurrently, the field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (EAI) has progressed in endowing agents, such as robots, with the ability to perceive, act, and adapt within physical environments, enabling tasks like navigation and object manipulation. Despite individual successes, these domains possess inherent limitations. LLMs are largely disembodied, operating in the symbolic realm without grounded understanding of the physical world, and are susceptible to issues such as hallucination and limited memory. Embodied agents, while physically capable, often rely on rigid, task-specific control structures and exhibit restricted high-level cognitive abilities, semantic comprehension, and long-horizon reasoning.

The integration of LLMs with sensorimotor embodiment has emerged as a promising direction towards general-purpose interactive intelligence, allowing LLMs to serve as "neural executors" for planning, memory management, and decision support in embodied systems. However, this integration faces fundamental challenges including the symbol grounding problem, the reliability of LLMs in safety-critical tasks, and the technical bottlenecks in seamlessly merging LLMs with external structured knowledge bases (KBs) due to mismatches in representation and interfacing. Furthermore, while the individual domains of LLMs, embodied intelligence, and neuro-symbolic reasoning have been extensively explored, there has been a lack of a unified perspective or a cohesive system architecture to combine these fields. Existing reviews often focus on specific aspects, such as multimodal models for robotics or LLMs in human-robot manufacturing, but do not offer a comprehensive framework for perception, cognition, and action across these intertwined disciplines.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the aforementioned gaps. It provides a comprehensive review that systematizes the synergistic integration of LLMs, embodied AI, and structured knowledge-based reasoning. The work aims to bridge the divide between cognitive strengths and physical interaction, proposing a unified framework and a systematic roadmap for the development of autonomous, language-driven, knowledge-augmented embodied agents capable of multimodal interaction, long-term planning, and adaptive reasoning in diverse environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research paper is to provide a comprehensive review and a systematic roadmap for advancing towards General Embodied Intelligence (GEI) by integrating Large Language Models (LLMs), Knowledge Bases (KBs), and Reasoning Ability (RA) within physically embodied systems.

The motivation for this work arises from several critical observations and existing limitations in the field of artificial intelligence:
- **Disembodiment of LLMs:** Despite their advanced linguistic capabilities, LLMs operate purely in the symbolic domain, lacking a grounded understanding of the physical or perceptual world. This disembodiment limits their capacity to interact with and comprehend real-world contexts, leading to issues like the "symbol grounding problem."
- **Cognitive Limitations of Embodied AI:** While embodied AI agents have made strides in physical interaction, navigation, and manipulation, they often rely on rigid, task-specific control pipelines. They exhibit limited autonomy in complex decision-making, semantic comprehension, and long-horizon reasoning, which restricts their adaptability in open-ended, multi-step scenarios.
- **Challenges in LLM Reliability:** LLMs are susceptible to "hallucinations" (generating factually incorrect or nonsensical information), possess limited explicit memory, and lack robust world models. These factors compromise their reliability, particularly in safety-critical or long-term reasoning tasks.
- **Integration Gaps with Knowledge Bases:** Although external structured knowledge sources (e.g., knowledge graphs, ontologies) can augment LLMs' reasoning capabilities, their seamless integration with large neural architectures presents technical challenges due to mismatches in representation, update mechanisms, and interfacing protocols.
- **Need for a Unified Framework:** The individual domains of LLMs, embodied intelligence, and neuro-symbolic reasoning have been actively explored, but there is a lack of a cohesive, unified perspective on how these fields can be synergistically combined. Existing review articles often lack a unified architectural design, module coordination mechanisms, solutions for semantic misalignment, or a general solution framework covering the entire perception-cognition-action process.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To review the evolution of LLM-centered intelligent systems, focusing on their integration with knowledge representation, logical reasoning, and physical embodiment.
- To analyze LLM architectures, pre-training methods, and inference mechanisms, as well as their interactions with external knowledge sources and structured reasoning frameworks.
- To examine embodied AI paradigms, specifically how agents learn and act in physical environments.
- To propose a unified framework that illustrates the synergy among LLMs, KBs, RA, and embodiment, supporting comprehensive perception, reasoning, and action.
- To identify and articulate key challenges and future research directions necessary for advancing towards GEI, providing a systematic technical roadmap. These include efficient LLM deployment, closed-loop knowledge integration, hybrid symbolic-neural reasoning, perception-action grounding, and continual learning.

Ultimately, the work is motivated by the vision of creating adaptive, multimodal AI agents capable of operating autonomously and intelligently in complex, dynamic real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper adopts a systematic review and survey methodology to analyze the convergence of Large Language Models (LLMs), Knowledge Bases (KBs), Reasoning Ability (RA), and Embodied Intelligence (EI). The approach involves a structured decomposition of the problem domain and a comprehensive synthesis of existing literature to propose a unified framework for General Embodied Intelligence (GEI).

The methodology is outlined as follows:
- **Systematic Review of Core Components:** The paper systematically reviews four fundamental technical components essential for GEI:
 - **Large Language Models (LLMs):** This section covers the basic concepts, core features (large scale, context-awareness, multimodal capabilities, emergent capabilities), technical development context (from statistical models to Transformer architecture), and key architectural elements (self-attention mechanism, multi-head attention, positional encoding). It also details the construction process (data collection, architecture design, pre-training, fine-tuning, evaluation) and execution process, providing examples of LLM applications.
 - **Knowledge Bases (KBs):** The review explores the dynamic system of LLM knowledge bases, including knowledge extraction, fusion, representation, reasoning, and retrieval. It differentiates between knowledge derived from massive text data (language rules, common sense) and structured knowledge (knowledge graphs, domain-specific databases), and discusses pre-training corpora, domain-specific fine-tuning, and interactive learning mechanisms.
 - **Reasoning Ability (RA):** This section focuses on the inference phase of LLMs, where models demonstrate understanding, analysis, and reasoning. It surveys infrastructure optimization technologies (software-level: distributed computing, quantization, service-oriented deployment; hardware-level: memory management, computation acceleration, emerging technologies) to support efficient deployment. It further analyzes the types of reasoning tasks, highlighting challenges in mathematical, logical, and knowledge reasoning, where LLMs often encounter bottlenecks such as logical jumps, numerical errors, and "knowledge hallucination."
 - **Embodied Intelligence (EI):** The paper defines embodied intelligence, classifying its components (swarm, entity, perception, evolution, common sense, cognition), and distinguishes it from disembodied AI. It traces the development of EI through five stages (Origin, Conceptual Proposal, Bionic Modeling, Fusion Learning, Large Model Integration). It also categorizes different types of embodied robots (quadruped, fixed-base, tracked, wheeled, humanoid) and elaborates on the perception (visual, force feedback, tactile), decision-making (neural-symbolic-LLM collaboration, language-guided adaptation, learning optimization), and execution (language-driven path control, tool mapping) aspects of embodied intelligent systems. Identified difficulties include universal ontology construction, cognitive function design, data scarcity, and continuous evolution.

- **Analysis of Technology Integration:** The paper provides a detailed analysis of the current state of integration among these components, specifically examining:
 - **LLM and Knowledge Base Integration:** Discusses how LLMs interact with knowledge graphs for verification, enhanced responses, and domain-specific applications (e.g., medical, agricultural, manufacturing).
 - **LLM and Reasoning Model Integration:** Reviews research on LLM reasoning frameworks (e.g., Long-to-Short, reinforcement learning for NL2SQL, GNN-RAG) and application-specific reasoning (e.g., bias detection, multimodal document understanding, drug target prediction).
 - **LLM and Embodied Intelligence Integration:** Categorizes research into task execution and autonomous planning, multimodal fusion and perception enhancement, multi-agent collaboration, and security/robustness challenges in LLM-driven embodied systems. It also covers LLM-driven embodied intelligent control execution.

- **Proposal of a Unified Framework:** A central element of the methodology is the proposition of a unified General Embodied Intelligence (GEI) technical architecture. This architecture is described as a four-layer intelligent system:
 - **Representation Layer:** Centered on LLMs for natural language understanding and generation, using Transformer-based architectures, self-supervised learning, in-context reasoning, and multimodal fusion.
 - **Knowledge Layer:** Integrates structured knowledge bases (KGs) and textual repositories, enhanced by Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and dynamic knowledge injection.
 - **Reasoning Layer:** Incorporates structured inference methods such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thought (ToT), and ReAct framework for problem decomposition and step-by-step decision-making.
 - **Action Layer (Embodied Intelligence):** Bridges abstract reasoning with physical interaction through a perception-cognition-action closed loop, language-to-action mapping, and multimodal sensory fusion, utilizing sensor systems, perception algorithms, motion control, and execution logic.

- **Identification of Key Challenges and Future Directions:** Based on the comprehensive review and proposed framework, the paper identifies five critical research directions (lightweight deployment, knowledge closure, reasoning enhancement, perception alignment, and safety/control) to guide future advancements towards GEI. This involves extrapolating from current limitations to delineate necessary innovations for adaptive, multimodal agents.

The overall approach is a synthesis-oriented review designed to map the current state of art, identify integration points and challenges, and provide a structured outlook for the development of advanced AI agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's main contribution is a comprehensive review and the proposal of a unified technical architecture and systematic roadmap for achieving General Embodied Intelligence (GEI) by integrating Large Language Models (LLMs), Knowledge Bases (KBs), and Reasoning Ability (RA) with Embodied Intelligence (EI). The findings are structured around this proposed architecture and the challenges identified for its realization.

#### 4.1. Unified GEI Technical Architecture

The core finding is a conceptual framework that integrates LLMs, KBs, RA, and EI into a hierarchical intelligent system with four main functional layers, designed to support perception, reasoning, and action in complex, dynamic environments (illustrated in Figure 12):
- **Representation Layer (LLMs):** This layer forms the cognitive core, leveraging Transformer-based LLMs to perform natural language understanding and generation. It incorporates self-supervised learning, in-context reasoning, and multimodal fusion capabilities, enabling agents to interpret complex instructions and environmental cues linguistically.
- **Knowledge Layer (KBs):** This layer augments LLMs with structured factual information from sources such as knowledge graphs and textual repositories. It integrates Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and dynamic knowledge injection mechanisms, allowing LLMs to access, interpret, and update external knowledge, thereby reducing factual errors and enhancing context awareness.
- **Reasoning Layer (RA):** Building upon the knowledge layer, this layer introduces structured inference methods. Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thought (ToT), and the ReAct framework are employed to enable agents to decompose complex problems, formulate step-by-step decisions, and engage in multi-hop or procedural reasoning, moving beyond statistical pattern matching to more robust logical deduction.
- **Action Layer (Embodied Intelligence):** This constitutes the physical intelligence layer, where abstract language reasoning is translated into concrete environmental interaction. It operates through a perception-cognition-action closed loop, facilitating language-to-action mapping and multimodal sensory fusion. Coordinated modules, including sensor systems, perception algorithms, motion control, and execution logic, enable embodied agents (e.g., robots) to perceive their surroundings, understand instructions, and execute appropriate physical actions.

#### 4.2. Current State of Technology Integration

The review synthesizes the current literature to highlight progress and challenges in integrating these components:
- **LLM and Knowledge Base Integration:** Research demonstrates LLMs can be effectively integrated with knowledge graphs for tasks like RDF triple verification, enhancing LLM responses with verified factual information (e.g., KGT framework), improving performance in fact-intensive question answering, and supporting enterprise QA systems. LLMs also facilitate knowledge graph construction. In domain-specific applications (e.g., medical, agricultural, manufacturing), LLMs augmented with KBs achieve higher accuracy in multi-hop QA, agricultural decision-making, and knowledge sharing by providing structured data support and reducing hallucinations.
- **LLM and Reasoning Model Integration:** Advances in reasoning frameworks combine LLMs with techniques to address "overthinking" (L2S framework), improve reasoning in low-resource languages (GreenMind), and enhance NL2SQL tasks (SQL-R1). GNN-RAG integrates graph neural networks with LLMs for improved knowledge graph question answering, while FiDeLiS framework addresses hallucination with deductive paths. LLMs are also applied to recommendation systems (RALLRec+), bias detection (RBD), multimodal document understanding (DocAssistant), and drug target prediction (DrugAgent), often by generating intermediate reasoning steps or leveraging multi-agent systems.
- **LLM and Embodied Intelligence Integration:**
 - **Task Execution and Planning:** LLMs enable embodied agents to interpret instructions, decompose long-horizon tasks, and generate executable action sequences (e.g., three-stage framework for industrial robotics, RoboGPT, ELLMER). They facilitate few-shot planning and generalization across diverse tasks, with applications in navigation and object manipulation.
 - **Multimodal Fusion and Perception Enhancement:** Systems integrate LLMs with visual, auditory, and tactile sensor inputs to improve perception and situation awareness. Examples include multi-level LLM architectures for safety assessment, environment generation for RL agents, and multimodal models for open-world perception and autonomous driving.
 - **Multi-Agent Collaboration:** LLM-based agents are being developed to collaborate in teams, utilizing organizational prompts, shared memory, and adaptive learning mechanisms for coordination and efficiency (e.g., ReAd framework, FaGeL).
 - **Security and Robustness:** Research identifies emerging threats like contextual backdoor attacks and jailbreaking risks in LLM-powered embodied AI. Efforts focus on evaluating robustness, mitigating behavioral misalignment, and developing defense strategies.
 - **Control Execution:** LLMs are increasingly used to generate robot control policies ("Code as Policies"), interpret sensorimotor feedback for adaptive control, and manage multi-robot systems (e.g., MultiBotGPT), bridging high-level language commands with low-level physical actions.

#### 4.3. Key Challenges and Future Directions for GEI

The paper identifies five key challenges that need to be addressed to advance towards GEI:
1. **Efficient LLM Deployment:** The computational demands of LLMs necessitate the development of lightweight, optimized models (distilled, quantized, sparse) and hardware-software co-optimization (edge AI chips, LoRA) for real-time operation in resource-constrained embodied systems.
2. **Closed-Loop Knowledge Integration:** Moving beyond static corpora requires dynamic and structured knowledge systems that support real-time Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), knowledge graph reasoning, and continual updating based on ongoing interactions and feedback.
3. **Hybrid Symbolic-Neural Reasoning:** GEI requires reasoning mechanisms that combine the pattern recognition of LLMs with the precision of symbolic logic. Frameworks like Chain-of-Thought, Tree-of-Thought, and ReAct are crucial for complex task planning and logical consistency.
4. **Perception-Action Grounding:** Embodied agents need robust semantic grounding to translate abstract language into concrete physical actions. This involves multi-modal representation learning, policy networks trained via reinforcement learning, and instruction-conditioned behavior cloning to bridge symbolic cognition and real-world embodiment.
5. **Continual and Interactive Learning:** Agents must possess capabilities for continual learning, incrementally acquiring new knowledge and adapting to changing environments and users without catastrophic forgetting. Interactive learning, leveraging human feedback (RLHF) and few-shot adaptation, is essential for lifelong improvement and alignment with human goals.

These findings collectively highlight the technical trajectory and critical areas of focus for realizing truly intelligent, adaptive, and autonomous embodied agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in its comprehensive synthesis of disparate but converging fields within artificial intelligence and its prescriptive roadmap for developing General Embodied Intelligence (GEI). By systematizing the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs), Knowledge Bases (KBs), Reasoning Ability (RA), and Embodied Intelligence (EI), the paper offers a unified perspective that addresses existing gaps in the research landscape.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, manifesting in several key areas:

- **Accelerating General Embodied Intelligence (GEI):** The paper provides a theoretical foundation and technical direction for the development of next-generation AI agents. By proposing a unified architecture and outlining critical research directions, it serves as a roadmap to overcome the limitations of current disembodied LLMs and cognitively constrained embodied systems. This systematic approach is anticipated to accelerate progress towards truly general-purpose AI that can operate autonomously and intelligently in the physical world.
- **Enhanced Autonomy and Adaptability of AI Agents:** The proposed integration will enable AI agents to move beyond rigid, task-specific behaviors. Agents designed within this framework are expected to possess enhanced cognitive abilities, allowing them to interpret complex natural language instructions, access and leverage vast knowledge, perform multi-step logical reasoning, and translate these insights into grounded physical actions. This will lead to more autonomous and adaptable agents capable of handling unforeseen circumstances and open-ended tasks in dynamic, complex environments.
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** By integrating LLMs, embodied agents can engage in more natural and intuitive human-machine interaction. The ability to understand subtle linguistic nuances, learn from human feedback, and communicate contextual decisions will make these agents more collaborative and user-friendly, expanding their utility in various domains requiring human-robot cooperation.
- **Broadening Application Domains:** The advancement of GEI is expected to have a transformative impact across numerous application domains. This includes, but is not limited to:
 - **Industrial Automation:** Robots with GEI could perform highly complex manufacturing tasks, adaptive assembly, and real-time problem-solving in dynamic factory environments.
 - **Healthcare:** Service robots could provide personalized assistance to the elderly, support medical professionals in intricate procedures, and offer intelligent rehabilitation.
 - **Logistics and Exploration:** Autonomous vehicles and exploration robots could navigate unstructured terrains, perform complex reconnaissance, and adapt to rapidly changing conditions with greater intelligence.
 - **Education and Personal Assistance:** Intelligent agents could offer more personalized tutoring, emotional support, and adaptive learning experiences.
- **Addressing Fundamental AI Challenges:** The identified key challenges—efficient LLM deployment, closed-loop knowledge integration, hybrid reasoning, perception-action grounding, and continual learning—represent fundamental hurdles in AI research. By focusing attention on these areas, the paper stimulates innovation in model compression, knowledge representation, neuro-symbolic AI, multimodal learning, and lifelong learning paradigms. Solutions to these challenges will not only benefit embodied intelligence but also contribute to the broader advancement of AI capabilities.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Research:** The very nature of GEI necessitates collaboration across diverse fields, including natural language processing, robotics, cognitive science, knowledge engineering, and machine learning. This paper emphasizes the importance of breaking down silos between these disciplines, thereby encouraging interdisciplinary research and the development of integrated solutions.

In conclusion, this work provides a forward-looking and structured approach that is instrumental in shaping the next era of artificial intelligence. By emphasizing the symbiotic relationship between language, knowledge, reasoning, and embodiment, it lays the groundwork for creating AI agents that are not only linguistically proficient but also contextually aware, factually grounded, logically coherent, and physically capable, thereby representing a critical step towards realizing human-level general intelligence.
