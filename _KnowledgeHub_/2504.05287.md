# RobustDexGrasp: Robust Dexterous Grasping of General Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich and HKUST developed a framework for zero-shot dynamic dexterous grasping of diverse objects, achieving 94.6% real-world success on 512 novel objects and 97.0% in simulation, by utilizing single-view visual input and adapting to various disturbances. This approach integrates a hand-centric object representation and a mixed curriculum learning strategy to ensure robustness.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous grasping methods often lack generalization capabilities for novel or diverse objects encountered in unstructured environments.
- Many approaches exhibit limited robustness to real-world disturbances, such as noisy observations, external forces, unexpected collisions, and actuator inaccuracies.
- Current solutions frequently rely on extensive human demonstrations, full 3D object models, or multi-camera setups, which are costly, complex, and impractical for real-world deployment with limited perceptual resources.

## Method

- A two-step reinforcement learning paradigm is used: a privileged teacher policy is trained with full visual-tactile information, and a student policy is trained with limited, real-world available perception.
- A sparse hand-centric object representation, composed of dynamic distance vectors from finger joints to object points, focuses on interaction-relevant local shape features to enhance generalization and robustness.
- A mixed curriculum learning approach combines imitation learning for efficient skill distillation with reinforcement learning for exploration and adaptation, alongside domain randomization to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- The framework achieved an overall success rate of **97.0%** when evaluated on 247,786 unseen simulated objects from the Objaverse dataset, demonstrating high generalization across diverse shapes and scales.
- The simulation-trained policy obtained an impressive **94.6%** overall success rate on 512 novel real-world objects across 12 categories, using only single-view depth observation without prior object knowledge.
- The method exhibited superior robustness to external forces, maintaining an **84.0%** success rate in real-world tests under **2.5** N forces, significantly outperforming baselines which saw greater performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A compact, hand-centric object representation effectively extracts interaction-relevant shape features from limited single-view perception, enabling strong zero-shot generalization across diverse object shapes and sizes.
> - The combination of imitation learning and reinforcement learning within a mixed curriculum allows for efficient transfer of core grasping skills while simultaneously fostering adaptability to real-world noises and disturbances.
> - Reconstructing approximate tactile contact states from noisy proprioception and action histories through an LSTM-based encoder provides a crucial feedback mechanism, compensating for the lack of direct tactile sensing and enabling adaptive finger closure.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous grasping is a foundational capability in robotic manipulation, enabling robots to perform complex tasks ranging from pick-and-place to in-hand manipulation and tool use. However, achieving robust and generalizable dexterous grasping, particularly for novel objects in unstructured environments, remains a significant challenge. This paper addresses a critical gap in the existing research by focusing on **zero-shot dynamic dexterous grasping of general objects using single-view visual inputs, explicitly designed to be resilient to various disturbances.**

The broader research landscape for dexterous grasping can be broadly categorized:

- **Pose-based Dexterous Grasping:** Traditional and many recent methods often focus on predicting static grasping poses or fingertip positions. These approaches typically rely on accurate object models, pre-scanned object geometries, or optimization of analytical metrics like force or shape closure. While successful in controlled environments, they suffer from limited generalization to novel objects and lack real-time adaptability to unexpected disturbances. Examples include methods that require known object meshes or those that pre-compute grasps, making them brittle when faced with noisy observations or dynamic environments.
- **Dynamic Dexterous Grasping (Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based):** More recent research, particularly leveraging RL, aims to learn real-time joint action prediction for adaptive grasping. This category seeks to overcome the limitations of static pose execution by enabling continuous feedback and adaptation. Within this, several sub-approaches exist:
 - **Demonstration-based RL:** Many dynamic grasping methods leverage human or robot demonstrations to learn complex actions. While effective for specific tasks, collecting diverse, real-world demonstration data is costly and often limits generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios.
 - **Simulation-based RL:** RL offers promise for learning robust policies through extensive exploration under disturbances in simulation. However, transferring these policies to real robots (the "sim-to-real gap") is a persistent challenge, especially for complex hand-object interactions. Most purely RL-based methods often remain confined to simulation or achieve only category-level generalization.
 - **RL with constrained action spaces or model-based control:** Some methods attempt to bridge the sim-to-real gap by simplifying RL exploration (e.g., using PCA-reduced action spaces from human data) or employing analytical dynamic models for control. While these can achieve real-world deployment, they often sacrifice the full dexterity of the robot hand or limit adaptability to unmodeled disturbances.

This work distinguishes itself by tackling the problem of *robust, zero-shot, dynamic dexterous grasping for general objects* without relying on extensive human demonstrations, known object meshes, or simplified action spaces. It aims to achieve strong sim-to-real transfer with *limited real-world perception* (single-view camera, noisy proprioception, no direct tactile sensing). By focusing on real-time adaptation to diverse disturbances, the paper directly addresses a major hurdle in deploying dexterous robots in practical, uncontrolled environments, positioning it as a significant step towards truly general-purpose robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for **zero-shot dynamic dexterous grasping of diverse, general objects using single-view visual inputs**, specifically designed to maintain stability and success under various real-world disturbances.

The motivations driving this research stem from several critical limitations in existing dexterous grasping approaches:

- **Lack of Generalization:** Many prior methods struggle to generalize beyond known or pre-scanned objects, making them impractical for real-world scenarios where robots encounter novel items with varying shapes, materials, and properties. The goal is to enable robots to grasp *any* object without prior training or explicit models.
- **Limited Robustness to Disturbances:** Real-world environments are inherently uncertain. Robots need to cope with noisy observations, unexpected collisions, external forces, and actuator inaccuracies. Current methods, especially those relying on static pose execution, often fail when confronted with such perturbations, leading to fragile grasps. The motivation is to imbue robots with human-like adaptability to dynamic changes.
- **Perceptual Constraints:** Full 3D object models or multi-camera setups are often assumed in training, but real-world deployment is often constrained to limited viewpoints (e.g., a single camera) and lacks direct, high-fidelity tactile feedback. The research is motivated by the need for a solution that operates effectively under these realistic perceptual limitations.
- **Cost and Complexity of Data Collection:** Methods relying on human demonstrations or extensive robot-specific data collection are time-consuming and expensive, hindering scalability and generalization to new tasks or objects. The authors aim for a simulation-trained approach that transfers robustly to reality without these dependencies.
- **Leveraging Dexterous Hand Potential:** Dexterous hands, with their high degrees of freedom, offer immense potential for flexible and adaptive manipulation. However, realizing this potential requires sophisticated control strategies that can effectively manage complex hand-object interactions in real-time.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the vision of enabling dexterous robots to robustly and reliably grasp any everyday object they encounter, much like humans do, by developing adaptive control strategies that can operate effectively despite common real-world uncertainties and sensory limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for robust dexterous grasping employs a two-step training paradigm within a reinforcement learning (RL) context, addressing challenges of generalization and real-world perception limitations. The core components are a novel hand-centric object representation, a privileged teacher policy, and a mixed curriculum learning approach for a student policy.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The pipeline involves:
1. **Teacher Policy Training:** An RL-based teacher policy is trained in simulation with **privileged visual-tactile information** (full object point clouds, precise hand-object contacts and impulses).
2. **Student Policy Training:** A student policy is trained with **limited, real-world available perception** (single-view point clouds, noisy proprioception, no direct tactile sensing), distilling knowledge from the teacher and learning to adapt to disturbances using a mixed curriculum learning strategy.

Both policies output target arm and hand joint positions, which are then executed by low-level PD controllers.

#### 3.2. Hand-centric Object Shape Representation

To address generalization to diverse object shapes and robustness to perceptual uncertainty from limited viewpoints, the authors introduce a sparse hand-centric object representation. Instead of attempting to capture detailed global object geometry, this representation focuses on interaction-relevant local shape features.
- **Mechanism:** A 51-dimensional vector is constructed by concatenating **dynamic distance vectors** from each finger joint (including wrist and fingertips) to their nearest points on the object point cloud.
- **Rationale:** This compact, sparse representation filters out irrelevant shape variations in non-contact areas, thereby enhancing generalization to novel object shapes and improving robustness under perceptual uncertainty caused by occlusions or limited viewpoints.

#### 3.3. Visual-tactile Teacher Policy Training

The teacher policy is trained via RL in simulation to acquire fundamental grasping skills with optimal sensory information.
- **Observation Space:** `s_t = (a_{t-1}, q_t, O_t, c_t)`, where `a_{t-1}` is the previous action, `q_t` is current joint angles, `O_t` is the **real-time fully observable object point cloud**, and `c_t = {b_t, f_t}` includes binary contact states and impulses for each finger link. Additional features are extracted (e.g., finger joint-to-object distances, link heights to table, wrist orientation/position, tracking error).
- **Reward Design:** A comprehensive reward function encourages robust and safe grasping:
 - `r_dis`: Penalizes link-object distances to guide the hand towards the object.
 - `r_contact`: Rewards desired hand-object contacts while penalizing self-collision, robot-table collision, and arm-object collision. This includes both binary contact states and impulse magnitudes.
 - `r_height`: Discourages robot links from excessively approaching the table.
 - `r_reg`: Regularizes unnecessary object motion, extreme robot movements, and arm joint velocities.

#### 3.4. Student Policy Training with Limited Perception

The student policy is designed for real-world deployment, facing single-view perception and noisy proprioception without direct tactile feedback.
- **Observation Space:** `s_t = (a_{t-1}, q_t, \hat{O}_t, \hat{c}_t)`, where `\hat{O}_t` is the **single-view object point cloud** and `\hat{q}_t` is noisy joint state proprioception. Crucially, an **LSTM-based encoder** is used to reconstruct approximate contact states `\hat{c}_t = {\hat{b}_t, \hat{f}_t}` from joint state and action histories, inferring contact from the misalignment between actuator torques (actions) and joint state changes.
- **Mixed Curriculum Learning:** This approach addresses the challenge of learning both efficient grasping and adaptive motions under limited perception:
 1. **Imitation Learning (IL) Phase:** Training begins with IL using two losses:
 - `L_re`: Contact reconstruction loss, guiding the student to predict the teacher's contact states.
 - `L_act`: Action imitation loss, mimicking the teacher's actions. This rapidly distills the core grasping behaviors.
 2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Phase:** Training gradually transitions to RL by decreasing `w_act` (weight for action imitation loss) and increasing the RL rewards (based on the teacher's reward function). This encourages the student policy to explore and discover adaptive motions for handling noises and disturbances.
- **Domain Randomization:** During student policy training, parameters such as friction coefficients, proprioception noise levels for joint angles, and PD gains of low-level joint controllers are randomized. This simulates real-world perturbations and actuator inaccuracies (e.g., overheating), fostering robustness and effective sim-to-real transfer.
- **Initialization:** The student policy network is initialized with the teacher policy's weights to accelerate learning.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** UR5 robot arm with an Allegro dexterous hand, using a top-view RealSense D435i camera.
- **Control Frequency:** High-level policy runs at 5 Hz, low-level PD controllers at 100 Hz.
- **Training Environment:** Raisim simulation with 35 diverse objects.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Grasping success rate (object lifted 0.1m and stable for 5s).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the strong generalization and robustness of their proposed dexterous grasping framework.

#### 4.1. Large-Scale Generalization

- **Simulation Evaluation:** The method was evaluated on 247,786 unseen simulated objects from the Objaverse dataset, scaled into small, medium, and large categories. It achieved an **overall success rate of 97.0%**. Performance was consistently high across sizes (e.g., 94.9% for small, 97.2% for medium, 97.6% for large objects), showcasing remarkable generalization to diverse object shapes and scales.
- **Hardware Evaluation:** The policy, trained purely in simulation, was tested on 512 novel real-world objects from 12 diverse categories, varying in shape, weight (7g to 610g), material (plastic, styrofoam, rubber, wood, glass, paper, metal, cloth, sponge), and size. It achieved an impressive **overall success rate of 94.6%** using only single-view depth observation without prior object knowledge.
 - Notably, the policy generalized well to deformable objects despite being trained exclusively on rigid ones, attributed to the reconstructed tactile information enabling adaptive finger closure.
 - Primary failure modes were noisy point cloud observations for very thin or small objects, and insufficient finger torques for heavy or slippery objects due to hardware limitations.

#### 4.2. Method Comparison (Real-world)

The method was compared against several baselines on real hardware, both without explicit external disturbances (though real hardware inherently has internal disturbances) and with applied external forces.

- **Without External Disturbances:**
 - **Naive baseline:** 0.633 success rate.
 - **DexGraspNet:** 0.607 success rate (with added table collision penalty).
 - **SpringGrasp:** 0.771 success rate.
 - **DextrAH-G:** 0.927 success rate.
 - **Ours:** Achieved superior performance, ranging from 0.920 to 0.964 depending on the specific comparison setting and object set. This highlights the real-time adaptive control capabilities learned by the RL-based policy. The authors attribute the improved performance over DextrAH-G to their method's use of the full action space (not PCA-constrained) and active handling of table contacts.

- **Robustness to External Forces:**
 - Compared with DexGraspNet under 2.5 N external forces (random direction in sim, 250g weight in real after grasping).
 - **Simulation:** Ours: 0.920 success rate (vs. 0.953 without disturbance). DexGraspNet: 0.553 (vs. 0.667).
 - **Real-world:** Ours: 0.840 success rate (vs. 0.920 without disturbance). DexGraspNet: 0.480 (vs. 0.607).
 - The results clearly show that the proposed method maintains significantly higher success rates and experiences less performance degradation under external forces, validating its enhanced robustness due to the policy's ability to form stable force-closure grasps and adapt in real-time to slips.

#### 4.3. Ablation and Analysis

- **Simulation Ablation (Mixed Curriculum Learning & Privileged Teacher):**
 - "W.o. RL rewards" (0.907), "W.o. IL loss" (0.933), "W.o. Curriculum" (0.913) all showed reduced performance compared to the full method (0.953), confirming the importance of both imitation for distillation and RL for exploration and adaptation, along with the curriculum's scheduling.
 - "W.o. Priv. learning" (0.773) demonstrated substantially lower performance and higher hyperparameter sensitivity, underscoring the crucial role of the privileged teacher policy for effective initialization and supervision.
 - The comparable performance between the student policy (0.953) and the teacher policy (0.960) validates the effectiveness of the mixed curriculum learning in distilling teacher capabilities.

- **Real-world Ablation (Hand-centric Representation):**
 - Comparing the standard setting (single-view point cloud, 0.920 success) with a variant using real-time, fully observable object point clouds from FoundationPose and known meshes (0.933 success).
 - The highly comparable performance highlights the effectiveness of the sparse hand-centric representation in extracting meaningful shape features from limited single-view observations, crucial for zero-shot generalization.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

The method qualitatively demonstrates robust grasping of various challenging objects (thin, small, heavy, deformable, transparent) and adaptive adjustment of grasping poses in response to unexpected collisions (due to noisy joint states, inaccurate observations, actuator imprecision) or significant external forces. Supplementary materials showcase applications such as grasping in cluttered scenes, task-driven grasping, and grasping moving objects, highlighting the versatility of the robust low-level controller.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a substantial advancement in the field of robotic dexterous manipulation, with significant implications for both fundamental research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Robust Dexterous Grasping

The paper pushes the state-of-the-art by presenting a framework for **zero-shot dynamic dexterous grasping of general objects** that achieves high success rates (97.0% in simulation, 94.6% in real-world) under realistic perceptual constraints (single-view camera) and demonstrates superior **robustness to diverse disturbances** (external forces, observation noise, actuator inaccuracies). This addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics: enabling robots to reliably grasp novel objects in unpredictable environments without prior knowledge or extensive pre-calibration.

#### 5.2. Practical Applicability and Sim-to-Real Transfer

The ability to generalize to 512 unseen real-world objects, including challenging deformable and transparent materials, directly from simulation training without human demonstrations, is a major step towards deployable robotic systems. The work effectively bridges the **sim-to-real gap**, a critical barrier for many RL-based methods, through a combination of domain randomization and a structured curriculum learning approach. This enhances the viability of using simulation for developing complex robot behaviors, significantly reducing the cost and time associated with real-world data collection.

#### 5.3. Key Methodological Contributions

- **Sparse Hand-centric Object Shape Representation:** This novel representation offers a practical solution to the challenge of object shape generalization under perceptual uncertainty. By focusing on interaction-relevant local features, it enhances robustness to shape variations and limited viewpoints, a crucial innovation for real-world scenarios.
- **Mixed Curriculum Learning:** The innovative curriculum that smoothly transitions from imitation learning (for efficient skill distillation) to reinforcement learning (for exploration and adaptation) provides an effective strategy for training complex policies with limited perception. This approach is highly significant for training adaptive controllers.
- **Tactile Information Reconstruction:** The LSTM-based encoder for reconstructing contact states from proprioception offers a clever way to compensate for the lack of direct tactile sensing in real-world deployments, providing a vital feedback mechanism for adaptive grasping.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Downstream Applications

The robust, low-level grasping controller developed in this work serves as a powerful primitive for a wide array of higher-level manipulation tasks. As demonstrated qualitatively, it can be readily integrated with other modules (e.g., segmentation models for cluttered scenes, vision-language models for task planning, object tracking for moving objects) to enable more complex and intelligent robotic behaviors. This signifies the potential for this research to unlock new capabilities in fields like logistics, manufacturing, service robotics, and assistive robotics.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The identified limitations, such as grasping very small objects (due to hand size), dealing with highly dynamic environments (catching flying objects), and non-grasping interactions (pushing), point towards important avenues for future research. Addressing these will further expand the capabilities of dexterous robots, potentially through smaller dexterous hands, explicit object motion prediction, and refined task-specific reward designs.

In summary, this paper represents a significant leap forward in robot dexterity and autonomy. By prioritizing robustness and generalization under real-world constraints, it brings the vision of versatile and adaptable dexterous robots closer to reality, paving the way for more sophisticated and reliable robotic agents in diverse applications.
