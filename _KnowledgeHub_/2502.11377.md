# PrivilegedDreamer: Explicit Imagination of Privileged Information for Rapid Adaptation of Learned Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> PrivilegedDreamer, developed by researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology, introduces a model-based reinforcement learning framework that explicitly estimates hidden environmental parameters and conditions its world model and policy accordingly. This approach achieved 41% higher average rewards compared to the next best model, demonstrating rapid and accurate online parameter estimation crucial for tasks with parameterized reward functions.

## Problem

- Real-world control tasks often involve unobservable factors, or hidden parameters, that influence system dynamics and reward structures, making adaptation difficult for learned policies.
- Existing reinforcement learning methods, including model-free and model-based approaches like Dreamer, often treat hidden parameters implicitly, leading to suboptimal policies, slow adaptation, and poor generalization in Hidden-Parameter Markov Decision Processes (HIP-MDPs).
- A particular challenge arises when reward functions are directly parameterized by these hidden variables, a scenario where many current approaches struggle.

## Method

- A ==dual recurrent architecture== is integrated into a **DreamerV2**-like framework to explicitly estimate unobservable ==hidden parameters== from limited observations.
- An ==LSTM-based module== is introduced to provide intermediate hidden parameter estimations, which then condition the ==recurrent world model==.
- An additional ==prediction head== within the recurrent state-space model refines the hidden parameter estimation, and these estimated parameters explicitly condition the world model's representation, actor, and critic networks.

## Results

- PrivilegedDreamer achieved the highest average reward across **five** diverse HIP-MDP tasks, demonstrating a **41%** higher average reward compared to the next best model.
- The framework showed particularly strong performance in tasks with parameterized reward functions (e.g., Kuka Sorting, DMC Pointmass), yielding substantially higher rewards than baseline methods.
- It exhibited rapid and accurate online estimation of hidden parameters, converging to ground truth values within a few environmental steps, which contributed to its overall adaptability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly estimating and conditioning on hidden parameters, rather than implicitly learning their effects, is critical for robust adaptation and superior performance in HIP-MDPs.
> - A dedicated external recurrent module (LSTM) significantly improves the speed and accuracy of online hidden parameter estimation within a few environmental steps.
> - Directly feeding these high-quality hidden parameter estimates to the world model, actor, and critic networks allows for more precise policy learning and effective decision-making, especially when rewards are parameterized.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Many real-world control tasks, such as autonomous driving and robotic manipulation, operate in environments characterized by unobservable factors, referred to as hidden parameters. These parameters influence the system's dynamics and reward structures, causing difficulties for policies learned in simulated or fixed environments, leading to performance degradation during real-world deployment or sim-to-real transfer. This class of problems is formalized as Hidden-Parameter Markov Decision Processes (HIP-MDPs).

Existing approaches to address these challenges fall into several categories:
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** This technique involves training an agent in environments with randomized parameters to promote robustness. While effective in some scenarios (e.g., robotic manipulation, locomotion), DR often yields conservative policies that may be suboptimal for complex problems with wide ranges of transition or reward functions.
- **Domain Adaptation:** This approach involves incorporating observable information to adapt to hidden environmental parameters. For model-free reinforcement learning (RL), this often means training a universal policy conditioned on hidden parameters, which are then estimated online from historical observations (e.g., Rapid Motor Adaptation, RMA). For model-based RL, meta-learning or learned encoders (context vectors) are used to capture environmental information and condition policies or forward predictions.
- **Meta-Learning:** This paradigm aims to enable agents to quickly adapt to new tasks or environmental variations with minimal experience.

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) offers an alternative by learning an explicit world model to predict environmental dynamics, which can improve sample efficiency compared to model-free methods. Architectures like Dreamer (DreamerV2) have demonstrated efficacy across diverse domains, including DeepMind Control Suite, Atari games, and robotic control, by learning an environment model through a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) and training policies in an imagined latent space.

The current work positions itself at the intersection of MBRL and adaptation to HIP-MDPs. While Dreamer-like frameworks implicitly capture some dynamic variations through their latent variables, they are not explicitly designed to handle unobservable hidden parameters that directly condition transition or reward functions. Particularly, the challenge intensifies when rewards themselves are parameterized, a scenario where existing approaches often struggle. This paper addresses this gap by explicitly integrating hidden parameter estimation into a Dreamer-like MBRL framework, aiming to provide a more robust and adaptive solution for HIP-MDPs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the performance limitations of current reinforcement learning approaches when applied to real-world control problems modeled as Hidden-Parameter Markov Decision Processes (HIP-MDPs). In HIP-MDPs, crucial environmental characteristics, such as object mass, contact friction, or motor scaling, are unobservable but significantly influence the system's dynamics or the agent's reward. Standard RL algorithms, including established model-based methods like Dreamer, often treat the effects of these hidden parameters as general variance, rather than explicit dependencies. This implicit handling can lead to suboptimal policies, slow adaptation, and poor generalization, especially when the reward function itself is contingent on these hidden variables.

The authors identify specific shortcomings:
- **Limitations of existing approaches:** Domain randomization can lead to overly conservative policies. Domain adaptation and meta-learning often rely on implicit context learning or require substantial data, and their effectiveness can be limited when rewards are directly parameterized by hidden variables.
- **Dreamer's inherent design:** While Dreamer is powerful in capturing complex dynamics, its core architecture is not designed for explicit hidden parameter estimation or conditioning. The hypothesis was that simply conditioning the model on latent variables might not be sufficient to accurately capture the subtle, explicit dependencies introduced by hidden parameters.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **Develop an explicit parameter estimation module:** To design and integrate a dedicated architectural component within a model-based RL framework that can explicitly estimate unobservable hidden parameters from limited historical observation data. This module is intended to overcome the implicit and often inadequate handling of hidden parameters by existing recurrent state-space models.
2. **Condition the world model and policy networks:** To utilize these explicitly estimated hidden parameters to condition the transition model, actor, and critic networks. The aim is to make these components explicitly aware of the environmental variations, thereby enabling more precise and adaptive policy learning.
3. **Enhance performance in HIP-MDPs:** To demonstrate that this explicit estimation and conditioning strategy, as implemented in PrivilegedDreamer, leads to significantly improved performance on a diverse set of HIP-MDP tasks, particularly those where the reward function is parameterized by hidden variables, compared to state-of-the-art model-based, model-free, and domain adaptation algorithms.
4. **Validate architectural components:** To conduct ablation studies that systematically justify the necessity and contribution of each novel component introduced in the PrivilegedDreamer architecture, such as the explicit estimation module and the hidden-parameter conditioned networks.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create a more robust and adaptable reinforcement learning agent capable of operating effectively in complex, uncertain real-world environments where critical parameters are hidden from direct observation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PrivilegedDreamer framework is built upon the DreamerV2 model-based reinforcement learning architecture, extending its capabilities to explicitly address Hidden-Parameter Markov Decision Processes (HIP-MDPs). The core methodological innovations revolve around a dual recurrent architecture for explicit hidden parameter estimation and conditioning.

**A. Background (DreamerV2 and HIP-MDPs)**
- **HIP-MDPs:** The problem is formalized as an MDP where the transition function $T$ and/or reward function $R$ are parameterized by an additional hidden latent variable $\omega$, sampled from a distribution $p_\omega$. This $\omega$ represents unobservable physical quantities (e.g., mass, friction).
- **DreamerV2:** This model uses a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) to learn world dynamics. The RSSM processes input states $x_t$ and actions $a_t$ to compute a deterministic recurrent state $h_t$ (via a GRU) and a sampled stochastic state $z_t$ (via an encoder $q_\varphi$). A transition predictor $\hat{z}_t$ estimates the stochastic state from $h_t$. The combined states ($h_t, z_t$) are used to reconstruct the state $\hat{x}_t$, predict the reward $\hat{r}_t$, and predict the discount factor $\hat{\gamma}_t$. Policy learning involves an actor-critic network trained via imagined rollouts in the learned latent space, maximizing predicted discounted rewards.

**B. PrivilegedDreamer's Innovations**

1. **Explicit Imagination via LSTM (Recurrent Hidden Parameter Predictor):**
 - An independent module, $\eta_\varphi(\tilde{\omega}_t|x_t, a_{t-1})$, is introduced to estimate an intermediate hidden parameter $\tilde{\omega}_t$.
 - This module takes the current state $x_t$ and the previous action $a_{t-1}$ as input.
 - It is implemented using a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network followed by Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) layers. The recurrent nature of LSTM is chosen for its ability to model temporal dependencies and subtle relationships between observations and hidden variables.
 - The estimated $\tilde{\omega}_t$ is used to condition the recurrent world model, explicitly integrating hidden parameter information into the dynamics learning.

2. **Improving Accuracy via Additional Prediction Head (HIP Prediction Head):**
 - An additional prediction head, $p_\varphi(\hat{\omega}_t|h_t, z_t)$, is incorporated into the RSSM, analogous to the state or reward prediction heads.
 - This head predicts a final estimated hidden parameter $\hat{\omega}_t$ directly from the RSSM's latent variables ($h_t, z_t$).
 - This component serves two purposes: (1) it encourages the RSSM's latent states ($h_t, z_t$) to encode sufficient information about the hidden parameter, and (2) it further refines and improves the accuracy of the hidden parameter prediction.

3. **Hidden Variable Loss:**
 - An additional loss term is added to the world model's total loss, $L(\varphi)$. This loss is designed to train both the LSTM estimation module and the prediction head.
 - $L(\varphi) = L_{Dreamer} + E_{q_\varphi(z_{1:T}|a_{1:T},x_{1:T},\omega_{1:T})}[\sum_{t=1}^T -\ln \eta_\varphi(\tilde{\omega}_t|x_t, a_{t-1}) - \ln p_\varphi(\hat{\omega}_t|h_t, z_t)]$
 - The first term encourages accurate intermediate estimation by the LSTM, while the second term ensures the RSSM's latent states contribute to accurate final predictions of $\hat{\omega}_t$.
 - The authors highlight that relying solely on this loss for implicit learning within $h_t$ and $z_t$ is insufficient; explicit conditioning of other networks is also necessary.

4. **Hidden-parameter Conditioned Networks (ConditionedNet):**
 - The estimated hidden parameters are explicitly fed as additional inputs to critical network components:
 - **HIP-conditioned representation model:** $z_t \sim q_\varphi(z_t|h_t, x_t, \tilde{\omega}_t)$
 - **HIP-conditioned critic:** $v_t \sim v_\psi(v_t|h_t, z_t, x_t, \hat{\omega}_t)$
 - **HIP-conditioned actor:** $\hat{a}_t \sim \pi_\theta(a_t|h_t, z_t, x_t, \hat{\omega}_t)$
 - This direct conditioning ensures that the learned dynamics model and the policy/value functions explicitly incorporate the estimated hidden parameter information, enabling adaptive decision-making. The actor and critic are specifically conditioned on $\hat{\omega}_t$ from the prediction head.

5. **Additional Proprioceptive State as Inputs:**
 - The most recent raw state information ($x_t$) is also provided as an additional input to the actor and critic networks, alongside the RSSM states and estimated hidden parameters. This is hypothesized to be beneficial for continuous control tasks where immediate state details are highly relevant.

**C. Experimental Setup:**
- **Tasks:** Five continuous control HIP-MDP tasks, including three from DeepMind Control Suite (Walker Run, Pendulum Swingup, Pointmass) and two custom tasks in MuJoCo (Throwing, Kuka Sorting). The Kuka Sorting and Pointmass tasks feature reward functions explicitly dependent on the hidden parameters. Randomization ranges for physics parameters (e.g., friction, mass, motor scaling) are defined for each task.
- **Baselines:** DreamerV2, Soft Actor Critic (SAC), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA). These baselines cover model-based, model-free, on-policy, off-policy, and domain adaptation approaches.
- **Ablation Studies:** Intermediate versions of the algorithm are evaluated:
 - `Dreamer + Decoder`: Includes only the $\hat{\omega}_t$ prediction head, trained with the hidden variable loss, but without the LSTM estimation module $\eta$ and without conditioning the actor/critic.
 - `Dreamer + Decoder + ConditionedNet`: Similar to the above, but the estimated $\hat{\omega}_t$ is provided to the actor and critic networks.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Average reward over 2 million timesteps, learning curves, hidden parameter reconstruction error (L2 distance between predicted and ground truth $\omega$), and online parameter estimation accuracy within individual episodes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PrivilegedDreamer on five HIP-MDP tasks, compared against various baselines and ablated versions, yielded several key findings regarding its performance and the efficacy of its architectural components.

1. **Overall Performance Superiority:**
 - PrivilegedDreamer achieved the highest average reward across the five diverse HIP-MDP tasks, demonstrating a 41% higher average reward compared to the next best model (DreamerV2 + Decoder + ConditionedNet in the ablation or vanilla DreamerV2).
 - The learning curves generally showed PrivilegedDreamer achieving faster convergence and higher final performance than all baseline algorithms (DreamerV2, SAC, PPO, RMA) and its ablated variants.

2. **Enhanced Performance in Parameterized Reward Functions:**
 - The performance margin was particularly pronounced in the Kuka Sorting and DMC Pointmass tasks. These tasks were specifically designed with reward functions that explicitly depend on the hidden parameters. In these environments, PrivilegedDreamer achieved substantially higher rewards (e.g., 554.65 in Kuka Sorting and 670.23 in Pointmass) compared to baselines, which often yielded significantly lower or even negative average rewards (e.g., DreamerV2: 167.61 in Sorting, 488.41 in Pointmass; SAC: 74.85 in Sorting, 393.49 in Pointmass). This indicates that the explicit modeling of hidden parameters is especially critical when they influence the reward structure.

3. **Exception: Throwing Task Performance:**
 - An exception to PrivilegedDreamer's general superiority was observed in the Throwing task, where the model-free algorithm SAC achieved a higher average reward (945.65) compared to PrivilegedDreamer (788.59). The authors attribute this to the task's requirement for long-horizon predictions, where model-based RL can accumulate errors. SAC, as a model-free algorithm, directly learns a policy without explicit trajectory estimation, which may be advantageous in such scenarios. On-policy model-free methods (PPO, RMA) did not perform well in this task within the given training steps, suggesting sample inefficiency.

4. **Accurate and Rapid Hidden Parameter Estimation:**
 - **Reconstruction Error:** PrivilegedDreamer consistently exhibited faster convergence and lower reconstruction errors for the hidden parameters (e.g., Pendulum, Throwing, Pointmass) compared to its ablated counterparts. It typically achieved high accuracy within 0.5 million environmental steps.
 - **Online Estimation:** During individual episodes, PrivilegedDreamer demonstrated the ability to accurately estimate the hidden parameters online within just a few environmental steps, converging to values very close to the ground truth. In contrast, ablated versions either took significantly longer to converge to an estimate (e.g., `Dreamer + Decoder + ConditionedNet` in Pointmass needed over 500 steps) or converged to less accurate values (`Dreamer + Decoder` in Pendulum and Pointmass). These results validate the effectiveness of the external LSTM-based hidden-parameter estimation module.

5. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - The ablation studies confirmed the importance of each component of the PrivilegedDreamer architecture.
 - `Dreamer + Decoder + ConditionedNet` performed better than `Dreamer + Decoder` (e.g., 473.22 vs. 441.58 mean reward), indicating that conditioning the actor and critic networks on the estimated hidden parameters is beneficial, even without the explicit LSTM estimation module.
 - The full PrivilegedDreamer model, incorporating both the external LSTM estimation module and the conditioned networks, consistently outperformed both ablated versions, confirming that the dual recurrent architecture and explicit conditioning are crucial for optimal performance. This validates the hypothesis that providing a high-quality, explicitly estimated $\omega$ to the world model and policy networks significantly improves adaptive behavior.

In summary, PrivilegedDreamer demonstrates improved performance in HIP-MDPs, particularly when reward functions are parameterized, by accurately and rapidly estimating hidden parameters and explicitly conditioning the world model and policy networks on this information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PrivilegedDreamer framework presents a notable advancement in the field of reinforcement learning for environments with unobservable, variable parameters, termed Hidden-Parameter Markov Decision Processes (HIP-MDPs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Adaptability in Complex Environments:** The core contribution of explicitly estimating hidden parameters and conditioning the world model, actor, and critic networks on these estimates offers a more robust solution for real-world scenarios characterized by uncertainty. This is particularly impactful in domains where environmental dynamics or reward structures are not fixed but depend on unobservable factors, such as varying object masses, surface frictions, or motor characteristics in robotics.

2. **Improved Performance in Parameterized Reward Functions:** A key finding is PrivilegedDreamer's superior performance in tasks where the reward function itself is explicitly dependent on hidden parameters. Many real-world problems involve implicit or explicit trade-offs based on unknown properties (e.g., sorting objects by unknown weight for different target bins). Traditional methods often struggle with these scenarios, making PrivilegedDreamer a relevant solution for tasks requiring fine-grained adaptation to optimize rewards under hidden constraints.

3. **Advancement in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** By building upon and extending the DreamerV2 architecture, PrivilegedDreamer demonstrates how explicit parameter estimation can augment the capabilities of world models. This work suggests that integrating dedicated modules for specific types of environmental uncertainty can make model-based RL more powerful and widely applicable, especially for tasks requiring rapid adaptation to novel or varying conditions.

4. **Rapid Online Adaptation:** The ability of PrivilegedDreamer to accurately estimate hidden parameters within a few environmental steps at the beginning of an episode is crucial. This rapid online adaptation allows the policy to adjust its behavior quickly, which is critical for dynamic systems where delays in adaptation can lead to instability or suboptimal outcomes. This feature could significantly reduce the need for extensive pre-training across all possible environmental variations.

5. **Architectural Novelty and Modularity:** The introduction of a dual recurrent architecture, comprising an external LSTM-based estimation module and an additional prediction head, along with explicit conditioning of the world model and policy networks, offers a modular design. This design could potentially be adapted and integrated into other advanced world model architectures, extending their applicability to HIP-MDPs without requiring a complete redesign.

6. **Potential for Broader Applications:** The principles demonstrated by PrivilegedDreamer have direct implications for various real-world applications:
 - **Robotics:** For robotic manipulators handling objects of varying, unknown masses or frictions; for legged robots adapting to different terrains or payloads.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** For autonomous vehicles adjusting to different vehicle types or road conditions.
 - **Industrial Automation:** In sorting, assembly, or quality control tasks where properties of items may vary and are not directly measurable.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper outlines several promising avenues for future work, including extending the framework to visual control problems (e.g., Atari, vision-based robotics), tackling more complex robotic control where dynamics are highly sensitive, and exploring multi-agent scenarios where hidden parameters influence the behavior of other agents. These directions indicate the potential for this work to serve as a foundational step for addressing even more challenging problems in adaptive control and intelligent systems.

In conclusion, PrivilegedDreamer offers a practical and empirically validated method for robust policy learning in environments with hidden parameters, addressing a significant bottleneck in deploying RL agents to complex, real-world settings.
