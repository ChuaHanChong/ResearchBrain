# Thinking with Geometry: Active Geometry Integration for Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoThinker introduces an active geometry integration framework for Multimodal Large Language Models, enabling selective retrieval and use of geometric cues to improve spatial reasoning. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on various spatial intelligence benchmarks and enhances robustness in diverse downstream applications, including embodied referring and autonomous driving.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limitations in robust spatial reasoning despite advancements in general image and video understanding.
- Existing geometry integration methods often use passive fusion, treating geometric priors as a uniform stream, which leads to semantic-geometry misalignment.
- Indiscriminate passive integration of geometric data introduces redundancy and noise, potentially compromising MLLM reasoning performance and generalization.

## Method

- Developed **GeoThinker**, an ==active geometry integration== framework that allows MLLMs to selectively retrieve and integrate geometric cues based on internal reasoning demands.
- Introduced the ==Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF)== module, comprising ==Frame-wise Constraints==, ==Importance Gating==, and ==Global Scaling==, to enable context-aware geometry integration.
- Implemented a ==strategic layer selection== process within the MLLM backbone to apply SGF only at specific layers, preventing interference with early visual processing or late-stage decoding.

## Results

- **GeoThinker** achieved state-of-the-art spatial intelligence, with GeoThinker Qwen3VL-8B scoring **62.23** across multiple benchmarks (VSI-Bench, MMSI-Bench, MindCube, etc.) and **67.4** on VSTI-Bench, surpassing GPT-4o by **29.2 points**.
- Performance on downstream tasks improved, with RoboRefer gaining **+1.66%** average accuracy on RefSpatial-Bench and autonomous driving (ReCogDrive) seeing **+3.7%** in Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) and **+2.2%** in Time-To-Collision (TTC).
- The Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF) module introduced minimal computational overhead, adding less than **5%** to total FLOPs on the Qwen-2.5VL backbone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The active Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF) module effectively enables MLLMs to selectively prioritize salient object boundaries and structural edges for geometry integration, even without explicit object mask supervision.
> - Strategic, rather than universal, integration of geometric information across MLLM layers is crucial, with a fusion ratio of 0.5 demonstrating an optimal balance between spatial grounding and linguistic integrity.
> - Active geometry integration effectively mitigates task interference when combining diverse datasets, allowing the model to leverage geometric cues for spatial tasks while maintaining performance on general temporal understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has made considerable progress in general image and video understanding. A significant frontier in this domain is the development of spatial intelligence, which involves a model's ability to comprehend and reason about the 3D structure and relationships within a scene. This pursuit is central to advancements in 3D scene understanding, vision-language-action models, and embodied intelligence.

A common approach to enhancing MLLMs with spatial intelligence involves integrating geometric priors, typically derived from specialized 3D encoders such as VGGT. These priors aim to move models beyond 2D semantic perception towards a more structured understanding of the 3D world.

Existing geometry integration strategies largely fall under "passive fusion" paradigms. These methods indiscriminately incorporate geometric features, either through input-level fusion (e.g., adding or concatenating geometric features with semantic tokens) or via geometric knowledge distillation as supervision during training. However, these passive approaches face limitations. They often treat geometric inputs as a uniformly exposed stream, overlooking the fact that relevant geometric cues are often task-dependent and spatially localized. This indiscriminate fusion can lead to semantic-geometry misalignment, inject redundant noise, and consequently compromise the model's spatial reasoning performance and its generalization across complex environments.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a shift from passive fusion to "active perception." The work positions itself as an advancement in geometry-aware MLLMs, aiming to enable models to autonomously discern and selectively retrieve task-relevant geometric cues, thereby improving efficiency and effectiveness compared to prior passive integration methods. This focus on active integration seeks to bridge the gap in reliable spatial reasoning that has been identified as a persistent challenge for MLLMs aiming for human-level capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named GeoThinker, that fundamentally changes how Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) integrate geometric information for spatial reasoning. Instead of the prevalent passive fusion strategies, GeoThinker aims to enable MLLMs to actively perceive and utilize geometric cues.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed limitations in current MLLM approaches to spatial intelligence:

- **Insufficient Spatial Reasoning Capabilities:** Despite significant progress in general image and video understanding, MLLMs often exhibit a notable gap in reliable spatial reasoning. This limitation restricts their utility in applications requiring a deep understanding of 3D environments, such as robotics, autonomous navigation, and advanced scene analysis.
- **Inefficiencies of Passive Geometry Fusion:** Current methods for incorporating geometric priors from 3D encoders predominantly rely on passive fusion paradigms. These paradigms, whether through input-level feature mixing or knowledge distillation, expose geometric information as a global, undifferentiated stream.
- **Semantic-Geometry Misalignment:** The indiscriminate nature of passive fusion frequently leads to a misalignment between high-level semantic features from visual encoders and low-level geometric cues. This can confuse the model, making it difficult to associate relevant spatial details with specific semantic entities or tasks.
- **Redundant Information and Noise:** Geometric information is not uniformly relevant across an entire scene or for every task. Passive fusion injects all available geometric data, including redundant or irrelevant signals, into the MLLM. This can act as noise, hindering the model's ability to focus on critical spatial relationships and diminishing its reasoning performance.
- **Limited Generalization:** The aforementioned issues collectively impede the generalization capabilities of MLLMs in complex and dynamic 3D environments. Models struggle to adapt to new scenarios or perform effectively in tasks where precise, context-dependent spatial understanding is paramount.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose GeoThinker, driven by the motivation to empower MLLMs with "active perception." This paradigm shift aims to allow the model to dynamically discern and selectively retrieve geometric evidence based on its internal reasoning demands, thereby making the integration of spatial structures more precise, efficient, and aligned with the actual needs of the reasoning task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoThinker is an active integration framework designed to enhance MLLMs with 3D geometry priors for spatial reasoning. The framework operates by allowing the MLLM to selectively integrate task-relevant geometric cues, moving away from passive, indiscriminate fusion.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

GeoThinker utilizes a standard MLLM architecture as its backbone, specifically adopting the Qwen-VL series. This backbone processes a sequence of RGB images by projecting pixel data into latent visual features, followed by a spatial compression mechanism that aggregates spatially contiguous visual patches into representative tokens.
To model implicit 3D attributes, GeoThinker employs VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) as its 3D visual geometry encoder. VGGT extracts intermediate features from input images, embedding geometry priors. A key step involves resampling these geometric feature maps to match the token grid of the MLLM backbone, ensuring patch-aligned correspondence between semantic and geometric tokens.

#### 3.2. Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF)

The core mechanism for active geometry integration is Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF). SGF is designed to address the limitations of passive fusion by allowing semantic visual priors to query and fuse task-relevant geometry. It consists of three main components:

- **Frame-wise Constraints:** Within each SGF layer, interaction between the image hidden states (semantic features) and geometric cues is facilitated via frame-strict cross-attention. The image hidden states and geometric features are reshaped back to their original spatial dimensions. This ensures that each semantic query attends exclusively to geometric cues from the *same frame*, preserving spatial consistency and preventing interference from features across different images or frames. This mechanism maintains high generalization for both multi-view and video inputs.
- **Importance Gating:** To regulate the flow of geometric information, Importance Gating is introduced. A lightweight Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) predicts an "importance score" directly from the image hidden states. This score is then converted into an additive attention bias and added to the cross-attention logits. This process allows the model to dynamically emphasize task-relevant geometric features (e.g., object boundaries, relational links) and suppress irrelevant geometric noise, ensuring focused integration.
- **Global Scaling:** A global learnable scalar, initialized to zero, is used to control the overall intensity of the geometric injection. The output of the cross-attention, modulated by importance gating, is scaled by this parameter before being added back to the main LLM residual stream. This mechanism helps to balance semantic understanding with geometric grounding.

#### 3.3. Layer Selection

The application of SGF is strategically placed within the VLM backbone to integrate geometry without degrading the model's native semantic understanding. The selection of fusion layers is guided by a fusion ratio (ρ) and boundary constraints:
- **Exclusion of Deep Visual Layers:** Deep-stacked visual layers of the Qwen3-VL backbone are excluded to prevent perturbation of the model's early visual processing.
- **Configurable Start Offset:** For spatial-centric tasks, fusion can begin from earlier LLM layers. For more general-purpose benchmarks, fusion is deferred to ensure that subsequent geometric queries are contextually grounded by initial semantic processing.
- **End Buffer:** Fusion is avoided in the final LLM layers to preserve instruction-following priors and stabilize response generation, preventing potential interference with the model's linguistic output.

#### 3.4. Training Regimes and Details

The approach employs incremental training settings:
- **Vanilla regime:** Fine-tuning uses sampled subsets from SPAR-7M and LLaVA-Hound, with 8 frames uniformly sampled per scene.
- **Scaled regime:** The training set is expanded with data from VLM-3R, VSI-590K, PhysGame, and MindCube, increasing sampling density to 32 frames per scene.
- **General video mixture:** An additional 430k general video samples are incorporated to strengthen video understanding capabilities.

Specific hyperparameters for training include a batch size of 64, a learning rate of 1e-5, and fusion ratios (ρ) set to 0.5 for out-of-domain scenarios and 0.75 for in-domain scenarios. The importance gate hyperparameter ϵ is set to 1e-6 for specialized spatial tasks and 0.1 for in-domain training with general video data. Experiments were conducted on 8 NVIDIA H800 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GeoThinker was evaluated comprehensively across various spatial intelligence benchmarks and downstream scenarios, demonstrating consistent performance improvements.

#### 4.1. Spatial Reasoning Benchmarks

- **VSI-Bench (Vanilla Regime):** GeoThinker consistently surpassed the VG-LLM baseline across 3B and 7B model scales. It achieved average scores of 48.9 and 50.5 for the respective scales, indicating the effectiveness of Spatial-Grounded Fusion over traditional input-level fusion by selectively emphasizing task-relevant regions.
- **Cross-Benchmark Comparison (Scaled Regime):** GeoThinker achieved state-of-the-art performance across multiple spatial intelligence benchmarks (VSI-Bench, MMSI-Bench, MindCube, Viewspatial, SITE, CV-Bench). Its Qwen2.5VL-7B and Qwen3VL-8B variants reached peak average scores of 60.43 and 62.23, respectively, demonstrating comprehensive spatial-temporal understanding.
- **Robustness to General Video Mixture:** Unlike models like Cambrian-S-7B, which showed performance degradation on VSI-Bench when mixed with general video data, GeoThinker maintained or slightly improved its VSI-Bench performance (+0.6 points) while also achieving significant gains on VideoMME (+5.7 points) and MVBench (+26.3 points) with a smaller general video dataset. This suggests GeoThinker effectively mitigates task interference.
- **Robustness against Language Bias and Frame Ablation:** On the VSI-Debiased benchmark, designed to reduce non-visual shortcuts, GeoThinker consistently outperformed existing state-of-the-art models (Cambrian-S-7B, VG-LLM-8B*, SenseNova-SI). Furthermore, despite being trained on a maximum of 8/32 frames, it demonstrated robust zero-shot extrapolation capabilities to longer contexts (128 frames), achieving 68.1 on VSI-Debiased with 128 frames, surpassing models trained on larger frame windows.

#### 4.2. Downstream Scenarios

- **Embodied Referring (RefSpatial-Bench):** GeoThinker improved performance across all splits of the RefSpatial-Bench, yielding an average accuracy gain of +1.66% over the RoboRefer baseline. This included improvements on location (+1.00%), placement (+1.00%), and unseen compositional spatial relations (+3.89%), suggesting enhanced geometry-aware grounding and compositional generalization.
- **Autonomous Driving (NAVSIM navtest):** When integrated with ReCogDrive, GeoThinker consistently improved performance on safety-critical closed-loop metrics. Notable gains included +3.7% in Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), +2.2% in Time-To-Collision within bound (TTC), and an overall boost in Pedestrian Distance Margin Safety (PDMS) from 81.6% to 83.6%. These results indicate enhanced planning-critical spatial reasoning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Component Contribution:** An ablation study on a Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone confirmed the contribution of each SGF component. Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF) alone, without frame-wise constraints (FWC) and importance gating (IG), already outperformed input-level fusion baselines. Adding FWC and then IG further improved performance, demonstrating that enforcing frame-wise constraints and selective gating helps the model focus geometry integration on task-relevant regions.
- **Fusion Ratio (ρ):** Experiments on the fusion ratio revealed that setting ρ=0.5 was optimal across different model scales (3B and 7B). Applying SGF to 100% of LLM layers (ρ=1.0) led to a catastrophic performance drop, suggesting that late-stage integration interferes with the LLM’s linguistic structure and termination capabilities.
- **Computational Cost:** The SGF module introduced minimal computational overhead, accounting for less than 5% of total FLOPs. Spatial compression in the 32-frame setting significantly reduced FLOPs and inference latency, outperforming traditional dense sampling methods for long videos.

#### 4.4. Additional Benchmarks and Visualizations

- **EASI Leaderboard:** GeoThinker achieved the 6th rank overall with an average score of 55.0, demonstrating data efficiency by outperforming models trained on significantly larger datasets (e.g., SenseNova-SI-1.1-QwenVL3-8B).
- **VSTI-Bench:** GeoThinker achieved the highest average score of 67.4, significantly outperforming both proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o) and open-source baselines, particularly excelling in object-object relative position and camera movement direction tasks.
- **PhysGame:** GeoThinker showed strong performance (56.9% AVG without video mixture) on fine-grained physical understanding tasks, surpassing previous open-source state-of-the-art and competitive with proprietary models, even with sparse frame sampling.
- **General Benchmarks (AI2D, MMB, BLINK):** GeoThinker maintained competitive performance against general-purpose models, indicating robust and generalized spatial understanding.
- **DSR (Dynamic Scene Reasoning):** GeoThinker achieved superior zero-shot performance (42.7) compared to VLM-3R and VG-LLM-8B, indicating generalization to complex dynamic environments without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Visualization of Importance Gating Scores:** Heatmaps showed that GeoThinker learns to prioritize salient object boundaries and structural edges while down-weighting non-informative regions (e.g., floors, walls) without explicit object mask supervision. This selective focus emerged from training on spatial reasoning tasks.
- **Robustness to Image Resolution:** Downsampling experiments showed that GeoThinker consistently maintained focus on key semantic features even when image resolution was aggressively reduced (to 6.25% of original), indicating robustness against variations in input quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a paradigm shift from passive to active geometry integration in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), proposing a more precise and efficient method for incorporating 3D spatial priors. This approach, named GeoThinker, has several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Advancement in Spatial Intelligence:** GeoThinker represents a step forward in endowing MLLMs with stronger spatial reasoning capabilities. By enabling models to actively discern and selectively integrate task-relevant geometric cues, it addresses the limitations of indiscriminate passive fusion, which often leads to semantic-geometry misalignment and redundant information.
- **Improved Performance and Generalization:** The consistent gains achieved across diverse spatial intelligence benchmarks (VSI-Bench, MMSI-Bench, MindCube, VSTI-Bench, etc.) indicate that active perception yields more accurate and robust spatial understanding. The demonstrated generalization to unseen compositional relations in embodied referring and enhanced safety-critical planning in autonomous driving suggests practical applicability in complex real-world scenarios.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** GeoThinker exhibits robustness under challenging conditions, including debiased evaluation settings (VSI-Debiased), extrapolation to longer video contexts, and performance maintenance with mixed general video data or aggressively downsampled image resolutions. This indicates a more stable and reliable spatial reasoning system less prone to linguistic shortcuts or data distribution shifts.
- **Efficiency in Geometric Integration:** The Spatial-Grounded Fusion module, particularly with Importance Gating, enables targeted use of geometric information. This potentially leads to more computationally efficient models by avoiding the processing of irrelevant geometric noise, especially when combined with spatial compression strategies.
- **Implications for Embodied AI and Robotics:** The improvements demonstrated in embodied referring and autonomous driving tasks are particularly impactful. Stronger spatial reasoning is fundamental for robots to accurately perceive their environment, understand spatial relationships between objects, follow complex instructions, and make informed decisions in dynamic physical spaces.
- **Foundation for Future MLLMs:** The findings suggest that the ability to actively integrate spatial structures is essential for the development of next-generation spatial intelligence. This work provides a framework and empirical evidence that can guide future research in designing MLLMs with a more structured and human-like understanding of the 3D world. It opens avenues for exploring more adaptive and context-aware methods for integrating various modalities into large foundation models.
