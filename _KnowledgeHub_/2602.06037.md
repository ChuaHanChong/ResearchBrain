# Thinking with Geometry: Active Geometry Integration for Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoThinker introduces an active geometry integration framework for Multimodal Large Language Models, enabling selective retrieval and use of geometric cues to improve spatial reasoning. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on various spatial intelligence benchmarks and enhances robustness in diverse downstream applications, including embodied referring and autonomous driving.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limitations in robust spatial reasoning despite advancements in general image and video understanding.
- Existing geometry integration methods often use passive fusion, treating geometric priors as a uniform stream, which leads to semantic-geometry misalignment.
- Indiscriminate passive integration of geometric data introduces redundancy and noise, potentially compromising MLLM reasoning performance and generalization.

## Method

- Developed **GeoThinker**, an ==active geometry integration== framework that allows MLLMs to selectively retrieve and integrate geometric cues based on internal reasoning demands.
- Introduced the ==Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF)== module, comprising ==Frame-wise Constraints==, ==Importance Gating==, and ==Global Scaling==, to enable context-aware geometry integration.
- Implemented a ==strategic layer selection== process within the MLLM backbone to apply SGF only at specific layers, preventing interference with early visual processing or late-stage decoding.

## Results

- **GeoThinker** achieved state-of-the-art spatial intelligence, with GeoThinker Qwen3VL-8B scoring **62.23** across multiple benchmarks (VSI-Bench, MMSI-Bench, MindCube, etc.) and **67.4** on VSTI-Bench, surpassing GPT-4o by **29.2 points**.
- Performance on downstream tasks improved, with RoboRefer gaining **+1.66%** average accuracy on RefSpatial-Bench and autonomous driving (ReCogDrive) seeing **+3.7%** in Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) and **+2.2%** in Time-To-Collision (TTC).
- The Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF) module introduced minimal computational overhead, adding less than **5%** to total FLOPs on the Qwen-2.5VL backbone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The active Spatial-Grounded Fusion (SGF) module effectively enables MLLMs to selectively prioritize salient object boundaries and structural edges for geometry integration, even without explicit object mask supervision.
> - Strategic, rather than universal, integration of geometric information across MLLM layers is crucial, with a fusion ratio of 0.5 demonstrating an optimal balance between spatial grounding and linguistic integrity.
> - Active geometry integration effectively mitigates task interference when combining diverse datasets, allowing the model to leverage geometric cues for spatial tasks while maintaining performance on general temporal understanding.
