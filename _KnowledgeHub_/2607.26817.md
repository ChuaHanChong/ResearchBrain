# From Uncertainty to Determinism: Coarse-to-Fine Visual Floorplan Localization without Ray Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Central South University developed CF^2Loc, a rendering-free, coarse-to-fine framework for visual floorplan localization that directly estimates continuous conditional pose distributions. This approach advanced state-of-the-art performance, boosting 0.5m recall on the Zillow Indoor Dataset (ZInD) from 43.6% to 51.7% and on Structured3D (S3D) from 65.2% to 71.0% with semantic floorplans, while reducing inference time by nearly a factor of 4.

## Problem

- Visual Floorplan Localization (FLoc) faces challenges from cross-modal information asymmetry between rich visual observations and minimalist 2D floorplan maps.
- Repetitive indoor layouts commonly lead to multimodal pose distributions, where a single visual observation can correspond to multiple distinct, spatially separated locations.
- Existing ray-matching-based FLoc methods suffer from information bottlenecks due to sparse ray representations, deterministic front-ends that fail to capture multimodal distributions, and high computational overhead from map preprocessing and exhaustive matching.

## Method

- The proposed **CF²Loc** framework uses an ==image-conditioned pose diffusion model== for global probabilistic hypothesis generation, directly modeling the continuous multimodal pose distribution.
- It employs a ==localized pose refiner== for deterministic sub-meter residual correction of coarse pose candidates within canonicalized, candidate-centered floorplan crops.
- The approach bypasses intermediate ray representations and exhaustive map matching, offering a rendering-free and more efficient pipeline for FLoc.

## Results

- CF²Loc achieved new state-of-the-art recall across standard benchmarks, including an improvement in 0.5m recall on ZInD from **43.6%** to **51.7%** and on S3D from **65.2%** to **71.0%** using semantic floorplans.
- The framework demonstrated a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off, reaching over **70%** recall at 1.0m with only **5** denoising steps and reducing inference time by nearly a factor of **4** compared to prior ray-matching methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of Kernel Density Estimation for mode selection, joint map-pose rotation augmentation for robustness, and the local refiner with patch canonicalization for fine-grained accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly parameterizing the continuous conditional pose distribution using a diffusion model effectively captures multimodal pose uncertainties without an information bottleneck.
> - A coarse-to-fine strategy, combining global probabilistic sampling with local deterministic refinement, is effective for handling both large-scale ambiguities and achieving high precision.
> - Canonicalizing local floorplan patches by aligning them with coarse pose orientations simplifies the local refinement task and significantly improves fine-grained localization accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual Floorplan Localization (FLoc) represents a field within indoor localization that seeks to determine a camera's pose by aligning egocentric visual observations (e.g., RGB images) with lightweight 2D floorplan maps. This approach contrasts with traditional visual localization methods that rely on storage-intensive 3D reconstructions (e.g., Structure-from-Motion models, dense point clouds) or extensive image databases, which are often costly to acquire, store, and maintain, and are sensitive to environmental appearance changes. FLoc offers advantages in scalability, ease of map maintenance, and readiness of availability of floorplans in built environments, making it suitable for applications such as autonomous robot navigation and augmented reality.

However, FLoc is fundamentally challenged by two main factors: cross-modal information asymmetry and repetitive indoor layouts. Cross-modal asymmetry arises from the difference in information density between feature-rich visual observations and minimalist structural floorplans. Repetitive layouts, common in indoor environments (e.g., symmetric rooms, long corridors), exacerbate this by causing a single visual observation to correspond to multiple distinct, spatially separated locations, leading to a multimodal pose distribution.

Previous FLoc approaches can be broadly categorized. Early methods involved handcrafted feature matching between visual inputs and local floorplan structures, which typically required complex feature engineering. More recent advancements have focused on "ray-matching-based" methods. These approaches, including F3Loc, 3DP, RSKFLoc, SemRayLoc, and FoD, transform egocentric observations into sparse geometric or semantic ray representations, which are then matched against pre-computed map representations or rendered rays from floorplans. While these methods have demonstrated improvements in accuracy, they introduce an indirect formulation. This indirectness leads to an information bottleneck due to the compression of rich visual data into sparse ray representations. Moreover, the deterministic nature of front-end ray prediction often fails to account for the inherent multimodal pose distribution. Furthermore, ray-matching techniques typically demand resource-intensive offline map preprocessing to generate ray databases and exhaustive test-time matching, limiting their scalability and real-time applicability.

This paper positions itself as a departure from the ray-matching paradigm. It proposes a direct approach to visual FLoc by modeling the continuous conditional pose distribution, moving from probabilistic hypothesis generation to deterministic refinement. The work aims to circumvent the limitations of indirect ray prediction, information loss, and computational overhead, offering a rendering-free and more efficient solution for tackling the inherent multimodal nature of indoor localization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, accurate, and efficient visual floorplan localization (FLoc) framework that directly addresses the challenges posed by cross-modal information asymmetry and multimodal pose distributions in indoor environments, without relying on intermediate ray representations or exhaustive map matching.

The detailed motivations and specific objectives are as follows:

- **Overcoming Multimodal Pose Distributions:** The core motivation is to effectively handle the inherent multimodal nature of visual FLoc, where a single visual observation can correspond to multiple plausible, spatially separated camera poses due to structural symmetries and repetitive layouts. Existing deterministic regression methods, which implicitly assume a one-to-one mapping, often suffer from mode collapse and averaging errors in ambiguous regions. Ray-matching methods retain multiple hypotheses but do so indirectly. This paper aims to directly model and parameterize this continuous multimodal pose distribution.

- **Bypassing Limitations of Ray-Matching Methods:** A significant motivation is to address the three fundamental limitations identified in current state-of-the-art (SOTA) ray-matching-based FLoc approaches:
 1. **Information Bottleneck:** Ray-based methods compress rich visual observations into sparse 1D geometric or semantic ray representations, inevitably discarding subtle appearance and structural cues. The authors aim to avoid this irreversible information loss by directly inferring the conditional pose distribution.
 2. **Deterministic Front-End:** Existing ray predictors deterministically map observations to rays, failing to capture the multimodal pose distribution induced by cross-modal asymmetry. The objective is to enable probabilistic hypothesis generation that explicitly accounts for these ambiguities.
 3. **Computational Overhead:** Ray matching relies on map-specific ray databases that require dense pre-computation (heavy offline preprocessing) and exhaustive test-time matching, restricting scalability and real-time performance. The proposed method seeks to eliminate these requirements, offering a "rendering-free" and more efficient inference pipeline.

- **Direct Pose Distribution Estimation:** The research is motivated by the insight that visual FLoc should directly infer the conditional pose distribution p(p | I, M) over the map, rather than relying on intermediate ray representations. This direct approach is hypothesized to be more effective in handling the ill-posed nature of the problem.

- **Seamless Coarse-to-Fine Strategy:** To address the computational intractability of globally optimizing a highly non-convex continuous pose space, the authors propose a cascaded coarse-to-fine paradigm. The objective is to decouple global uncertainty tracking from local deterministic refinement, ensuring both broad coverage of hypotheses and high precision. This involves:
 - **Global Probabilistic Hypothesis Generation:** Designing a mechanism, specifically an image-conditioned pose diffusion model, to parameterize the continuous multimodal pose distribution across the entire floorplan, thereby generating plausible candidate modes without exhaustive search.
 - **Local Deterministic Refinement:** Developing a lightweight local pose refiner that predicts bounded sub-meter pose residuals within candidate-centered floorplan crops, where structural ambiguities are largely eliminated, to achieve precise local correction.

- **Efficiency and Scalability:** The overarching goal is to achieve robust global localization, accurate local refinement, and efficient inference without requiring any offline map preprocessing or test-time lookup tables, thereby enhancing the practical utility and scalability of visual FLoc systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named CF$^2$Loc, adopts a rendering-free, coarse-to-fine generative localization approach, moving from probabilistic hypothesis generation to deterministic refinement. It bypasses intermediate ray representations and exhaustive map matching by directly estimating the continuous conditional pose distribution.

**Overall Framework:**
CF$^2$Loc consists of two main stages: a coarse stage utilizing an image-conditioned pose diffusion model for global hypothesis generation, and a refinement stage employing a localized pose refiner for sub-meter residual correction.

**A. Global Pose Diffusion (Coarse Stage)**
This stage aims to estimate the continuous conditional pose distribution across the entire floorplan, given an egocentric visual observation (I) and a 2D floorplan map (M).

1. **Multimodal Feature Encoding:**
 - **Visual Encoding:** The input image `I` is processed by a frozen visual backbone (ViT-S from Depth Anything V2), followed by lightweight projection and token-mixing layers. This generates dense visual tokens `F_I` and a global image descriptor `g_I`.
 - **Floorplan Encoding:** The floorplan `M` is rasterized into a structural tensor `X_M`. For semantic floorplans, each semantic category forms an independent channel; for geometric, a single grayscale channel is used. A convolutional encoder (`E_M`) extracts spatial map features, which are then augmented with learnable positional embeddings. This yields `F_M`.
 - **Cross-Modal Fusion:** To incorporate visual information into the map representation, cross-attention is applied. Map tokens `F_M` serve as queries, while image tokens `F_I` act as keys and values. This process yields `F_M_hat`, which are observation-conditioned floorplan tokens retaining the floorplan's spatial support while encoding image-dependent localization cues.

2. **Conditional Pose Diffusion:**
 - Instead of ray prediction, the model directly processes poses. To handle angular discontinuities, the camera pose `p = (x, y, θ)` is parameterized as `p_tilde = (x, y, cos θ, sin θ)`.
 - A standard Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) formulation is used. Gaussian noise is iteratively injected into the pose representation `p_tilde` to generate noisy poses `z_t`.
 - A conditional denoising network (`ϵ_θ`) is trained to predict the injected noise (`ϵ_hat`) from the noisy pose (`z_t`), diffusion timestep (`t`), global image descriptor (`g_I`), and observation-conditioned map tokens (`F_M_hat`).
 - The model is optimized using the standard noise prediction objective (`L_diff`).

3. **Map-Pose Joint Rotation Augmentation:**
 - During training, the floorplan and its corresponding ground-truth pose are jointly rotated by discrete 90-degree increments with 0.5 probability. This augmentation encourages the model to learn relative image-to-floorplan alignment, rather than memorizing absolute scene orientations.

**B. Local Pose Refiner (Refinement Stage)**
This stage refines the coarse pose candidates generated by the diffusion model to achieve sub-meter precision.

1. **Candidate-Centered Local Refinement:**
 - For each coarse pose candidate `p_k_c = (x_k_c, θ_k_c)`, an oriented local floorplan patch `M_k_c` is extracted, centered at `x_k_c` and rotated to align with `θ_k_c`. This canonicalization simplifies local alignment by eliminating global heading variance.
 - The cropped `M_k_c` is encoded by a lightweight local map encoder to produce `F_k_c`. These local map tokens are then fused with the frozen visual tokens `F_I` (from the coarse stage) via cross-attention to yield `F_k_c_hat`.

2. **Prediction Heads:**
 - A lightweight network (`R_ϕ`) with three prediction heads processes the fused local representation. It estimates:
 - Position logits (`l_k`) for spatial localization over local map tokens.
 - Local heading vectors (`V_k`).
 - A candidate confidence score (`s_hat_k`).
 - Translation residuals (`Δ_x_hat_k`) are computed by constructing a spatial localization distribution (`π_k_j`) from `l_k` and then taking a probability-weighted sum of the metric coordinates of the local map tokens.
 - Rotation residuals (`Δ_θ_hat_k`) are determined by taking the `atan2` of the probability-weighted sum of the local heading vectors.
 - The final refined pose `p_k_r` is obtained by applying these residuals to the coarse candidate pose: `p_k_r = p_k_c ⊕ (Δ_x_hat_k, Δ_θ_hat_k)`.

3. **Training Objectives:**
 - The refiner is trained independently while the diffusion model is frozen.
 - Training candidates are generated by perturbing ground-truth poses with multi-scale Gaussian and uniform noise to simulate varying coarse localization errors.
 - The joint training objective (`L_refine`) includes:
 - SmoothL1 loss for translation residual (`Δ_x_hat`).
 - Cosine similarity loss for orientation residual (`Δ_θ_hat`).
 - `L_heat`: A cross-entropy loss that supervises the spatial localization distribution (`π_k_j`) against a target spatial Gaussian distribution centered at the ground-truth translation residual.
 - `L_score`: A binary cross-entropy loss for the predicted confidence score (`s_hat_k`), supervised by a soft target derived from the candidate pose errors (distance from ground truth).

**C. Coarse-to-Fine Inference**

1. **Global Pose Hypothesis Generation:**
 - `N` pose particles are initialized from isotropic Gaussian noise.
 - These particles are iteratively denoised using the reverse diffusion process, conditioned on visual observation and floorplan features, to yield a set of pose samples.
 - Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) is applied to these samples to estimate particle density over the SE(2) pose manifold. The local maxima of this density function are extracted as `K` coarse candidate modes (`p_k_c`).

2. **Local Pose Refinement and Re-ranking:**
 - Each coarse candidate `p_k_c` is independently fed into the local pose refiner, along with visual features (`F_I`) and its corresponding oriented local floorplan crop (`M_k_c`).
 - The refiner predicts local translation (`Δ_x_hat_k`), orientation (`Δ_θ_hat_k`), and a confidence score (`s_hat_k`).
 - The refined pose `p_k_r` is calculated for each candidate.
 - Finally, the `K` candidate hypotheses are re-ranked based on their predicted confidence scores, and the top-ranked candidate (`p_k_star_r`) is selected as the final localization pose.

This inference pipeline is designed to be efficient, requiring a few reverse diffusion steps and lightweight local refinement, thereby avoiding map-dependent ray rendering, test-time lookup tables, and exhaustive observation matching.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates CF$^2$Loc on two standard visual FLoc benchmarks: Structured3D (S3D, full) for synthetic data and the Zillow Indoor Dataset (ZInD) for real-world data. Performance is measured using recall at spatial accuracy thresholds (0.1m, 0.5m, 1.0m) and joint pose recall (1.0m and 30° orientation error).

**A. Comparative Studies**

1. **Performance without Semantic Floorplans:**
 - On the S3D (full) dataset, CF$^2$Loc without semantic information, particularly with the refinement stage, significantly outperforms all prior baselines. It improved 0.1m recall from 3.5% (SceneAligner) to 11.3%, 0.5m recall from 37.5% to 64.4%, 1.0m recall from 53.8% to 73.7%, and 1.0m/30° recall from 51.6% to 72.6%.
 - Similarly, on the real-world ZInD benchmark, CF$^2$Loc demonstrated superior performance, boosting 0.5m recall from 11.1% (LASER) and 7.9% (F3Loc) to 45.5%, and achieving 57.3% (1.0m) and 45.9% (1.0m/30°).
 - These results indicate that directly parameterizing the conditional pose distribution and refining candidate modes is more effective than deterministic ray prediction and exhaustive observation matching, especially in the absence of explicit semantic cues.

2. **Performance with Semantic Floorplans:**
 - When trained with semantic floorplans, CF$^2$Loc establishes new state-of-the-art performance. On S3D (full), it improved recalls from the previous best baseline (FoD) across all metrics, for example, 0.1m from 12.0% to 15.0% and 0.5m from 65.2% to 71.0%.
 - On ZInD, similar gains were observed, with 0.5m recall improving from 43.6% (FoD) to 51.7%, 1.0m from 53.5% to 63.2%, and 1.0m/30° from 50.2% to 54.7%.
 - The local refiner consistently provides accuracy improvements, particularly under the stringent 0.1m criterion (e.g., 12.0% to 15.0% on S3D and 8.1% to 11.5% on ZInD), while maintaining the robust global localization achieved by the diffusion stage.

3. **Qualitative Comparison:**
 - Qualitative results (Figure 3) illustrate that CF$^2$Loc can reliably identify true candidate modes and refine pose accuracy even in symmetric indoor layouts, where other methods like F3Loc, UnLoc, and SemRayLoc frequently suffer from catastrophic mode mismatches.

**B. Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off**

- The framework achieves over 70% recall at 1.0m with only 5 denoising steps, indicating low runtime overhead. Performance rapidly converges around 10 denoising steps, with diminishing returns for further iterations.
- Compared to ray-matching-based methods, CF$^2$Loc achieves a superior accuracy-latency trade-off, improving 1.0m recall from 58.8% (SemRayLoc) to over 73% while reducing inference time by nearly a factor of 4. This suggests that direct pose distribution estimation can enable both high localization precision and real-time inference without offline map preprocessing or online ray matching.

**C. Ablation Studies**

- **Mode Selection (KDE):** Incorporating candidate mode selection via Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) significantly improved 0.5m recall from 45.8% to 53.8% and 1.0m recall from 63.7% to 71.5%. This highlights the importance of preserving dominant mode hypotheses in multimodal pose distributions.
- **Joint Map-Pose Rotation Augmentation:** Applying this augmentation during training further boosted 0.5m recall to 58.5% and 1.0m recall to 73.2%, demonstrating enhanced robustness to arbitrary global floorplan orientations.
- **Local Refiner and Patch Canonicalization:** Integrating the local refiner consistently improved fine-grained localization accuracy. This improvement was particularly pronounced when enabling local patch canonicalization (rotation alignment), which caused 0.1m recall to jump from 6.9% to 11.3% and 0.5m recall from 59.6% to 64.4%. This indicates that canonicalization effectively normalizes local coordinate frames, allowing the refiner to focus on precise residual regression.

**D. Parametric Studies**

- **Local Map Size:** A 5m x 5m local map crop yielded the optimal accuracy, balancing the need for sufficient spatial context with the requirement to largely eliminate structural ambiguities within the local region.
- **Number of Candidates (K):** Selecting only the top-confidence candidate (K=1) was sufficient to achieve top-tier performance on both S3D (full) and ZInD, validating the reliability of the diffusion sampling and confidence scoring in identifying true pose modes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of visual floorplan localization (FLoc) by fundamentally rethinking the problem formulation. By moving from an indirect, ray-matching paradigm to direct conditional pose distribution estimation, the authors address core limitations of existing methods and introduce a framework with substantial scientific and practical implications.

**Scientific Significance:**

- **Novel Problem Formulation:** The work redefines visual FLoc as a direct pose distribution estimation problem, rather than relying on intermediate 1D ray prediction and exhaustive matching. This is a conceptual shift that more directly tackles the inherent cross-modal information asymmetry and multimodal nature of the problem.
- **Generative Coarse-to-Fine Framework:** The introduction of a rendering-free, coarse-to-fine generative framework provides a principled way to bridge global multimodal uncertainty modeling with local unimodal determinism. This architecture effectively handles large-scale pose ambiguities while achieving fine-grained precision.
- **Image-Conditioned Pose Diffusion:** The development of an image-conditioned pose diffusion model for continuous global hypothesis generation is a novel application of generative models in spatial localization, offering a mechanism to explicitly parameterize and sample from complex, multi-modal pose posteriors.
- **Robust Local Refinement:** The localized pose refiner operating on candidate-centered floorplan crops, particularly with local patch canonicalization, demonstrates an effective strategy for bounded sub-meter residual correction without reintroducing global ambiguities.
- **Comprehensive Validation:** Extensive evaluations on both synthetic (S3D) and real-world (ZInD) benchmarks establish new state-of-the-art accuracy across various metrics, demonstrating the robustness and generalization capabilities of the proposed method.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Accuracy and Robustness:** The demonstrated improvements in localization accuracy, particularly under stringent error thresholds and in challenging symmetric environments, have direct implications for applications requiring precise indoor positioning.
- **Increased Efficiency and Scalability:** By eliminating the need for resource-intensive offline map preprocessing (e.g., ray database generation) and exhaustive test-time matching, the CF$^2$Loc framework significantly reduces computational overhead. This "rendering-free" approach enables real-time inference, making it more scalable and practical for large-scale deployments.
- **Broader Applicability:** The framework's applicability to both geometric and semantic floorplans enhances its versatility, allowing it to be deployed in diverse indoor environments with varying levels of map detail.
- **Advancements in Robotics and AR/VR:** The ability to achieve robust and accurate indoor localization with lightweight maps is critical for autonomous robot navigation, where robots need to understand their position within a building layout. Similarly, for augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) applications, precise and low-latency localization is essential for seamless digital content overlay and immersive experiences.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work offers a scalable, rendering-free alternative to traditional ray-matching paradigms, potentially inspiring further research in generative cross-modal spatial reasoning and novel applications of diffusion models in other vision-and-language or multimodal tasks. It opens avenues for exploring more advanced diffusion schedules, sampling strategies, and robust confidence estimation techniques.
