# REA-RL: Reflection-Aware Online Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> REA-RL presents an online reinforcement learning framework designed to optimize Large Reasoning Models by reducing redundant "overthinking" while preserving essential reflection capabilities. The framework demonstrates an average 36% reduction in inference token count without compromising reasoning performance across diverse mathematical and general question-answering benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often engage in "overthinking," generating unnecessarily long reasoning sequences even after finding the correct answer, leading to high computational costs.
- Prior offline methods for achieving conciseness are time-consuming and can suffer from distribution shifts when generating training data for evolving models.
- Existing online reinforcement learning methods, when solely optimizing for length, frequently cause LRMs to lose critical self-reflection abilities, which in turn degrades performance on complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- Developed **REA-RL**, an online reinforcement learning framework that incorporates a small, efficient ==reflection model== for dynamic, online detection and truncation of overthinking tokens within reasoning paths.
- Introduced a novel "reflection reward" to discourage non-reflective behavior and a refined length reward that explicitly penalizes incorrect short answers, aiming to preserve LRM reasoning capabilities.
- Integrated parallel sampling of reasoning paths with sequential revision by the reflection model, generating efficiently truncated, non-overthinking paths that serve as positive examples for policy model training.

## Results

- REA-RL achieved a **36%** reduction in inference token count (average token ratio of **63.51%** for a 16k budget) without compromising overall performance (average accuracy of **80.74%** for 16k budget, comparable to the original model's **80.39%**).
- The trained **7B** reflection model significantly outperformed fixed truncation strategies and achieved compression ratios comparable to a **32B** model revised without gold input, but with substantially lower computational overhead.
- The reflection reward component led to an average **47%** increase in reflection probability and a **5%** performance improvement on easier datasets compared to models trained solely with length rewards, mitigating the loss of reflective behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dedicated, smaller reflection model can effectively identify and remove superfluous reasoning steps online, enabling significant inference cost reduction while facilitating more efficient training data generation.
> - Carefully designed reward functions, specifically the reflection-aware reward and refined length reward, are crucial for balancing conciseness with the preservation of LRM's critical reflection abilities during online training.
> - The framework enables LRMs to adaptively adjust their reasoning depth based on problem difficulty, reducing overthinking on easier tasks while maintaining necessary deliberation for challenging ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Challenges in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) have demonstrated substantial capabilities in complex downstream applications, exhibiting human-like deliberation and self-reflection. These attributes contribute to improved performance on intricate reasoning tasks, such as advanced mathematics. However, this thorough approach often leads to excessive computational effort, a phenomenon termed "overthinking," where models generate lengthy reasoning sequences with marginal or no additional performance benefits. This overthinking substantially increases inference costs, limiting the practical deployment of LRMs.

#### 1.2. Limitations of Prior Approaches

Existing research has explored various strategies to mitigate LRM overthinking:
- **Offline Methods:** One line of work focuses on generating shorter reasoning responses for either supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL). This approach typically involves training models on static datasets of concise reasoning paths. A key limitation of these methods is their inefficiency in online settings. The processes of generating and filtering suitable training data are often time-consuming. Furthermore, static datasets can suffer from distribution shifts as the model evolves during training, potentially leading to suboptimal results.
- **Online Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Length Rewards:** To address the inefficiencies of offline data generation, another category of methods employs online reinforcement learning. These approaches often involve sampling multiple reasoning paths in parallel for a single query and incorporate both accuracy and length rewards to encourage correct and concise responses. However, this self-iterative training paradigm has been observed to lead to unpredictable model behavior. Specifically, models trained with a sole emphasis on length rewards can lose their crucial reflection capability, reverting to simpler, less effective chain-of-thought styles. This loss of reflective capacity can subsequently degrade performance on complex reasoning tasks where deliberation and self-correction are essential. For example, a model might reduce its reasoning length at the cost of making errors or failing to perform critical checks.

#### 1.3. Positioning of the Current Work

The presented work, REA-RL, positions itself as an advancement that integrates the strengths of existing methods while addressing their respective limitations. It aims to develop an online RL framework that is "reflection-aware." This involves introducing mechanisms to efficiently scale online training and maintain the model's reflection ability, thereby preventing performance degradation typically associated with length-focused optimization in online RL. The approach seeks to balance inference efficiency with the preservation of critical reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the inference efficiency of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) without compromising their reasoning performance, specifically by addressing the problem of "overthinking."

This objective is driven by several motivations:

#### 2.1. Addressing LRM Overthinking

LRMs, while powerful, often generate excessively long reasoning sequences even after having reached the correct answer. This "overthinking" leads to a significant increase in computational cost, making their deployment in real-world applications expensive and inefficient. The paper highlights instances where models reflect multiple times without substantial benefit, consuming many tokens (e.g., a DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B example reflecting eight times for a single problem). Reducing these redundant steps is crucial for practical efficiency.

#### 2.2. Overcoming Offline Training Limitations

Previous attempts to encourage concise reasoning often rely on offline data generation and filtering, which are time-consuming and can lead to datasets whose distribution deviates from the model's evolving capabilities. This makes offline methods suboptimal for dynamic, online training scenarios where continuous adaptation is beneficial. The motivation is to develop an online solution that avoids these data generation and distribution alignment issues.

#### 2.3. Mitigating Online RL Pitfalls

While online reinforcement learning offers a dynamic training paradigm, existing methods that predominantly use length rewards often lead to an undesirable trade-off. These models tend to shorten responses, but this brevity can come at the cost of losing critical reflection abilities. The paper illustrates cases where a model trained with length rewards simplifies its reasoning process, leading to errors on complex tasks that require self-correction and deeper deliberation. The motivation is to introduce mechanisms that allow models to become more efficient without sacrificing their "reflection-aware" reasoning capabilities.

#### 2.4. Specific System Goals

To achieve the overarching objective, the authors define two specific goals for their proposed REA-RL framework:
1. **Efficient Overthinking Detection and Online Revision:** To introduce a small, efficient reflection model that can operate online. This model is designed to identify the point in a reasoning response where the final answer is likely reached, allowing the truncation of subsequent "overthinking" tokens. This enables a computationally efficient online data generation strategy that combines parallel sampling with sequential revision, generating shorter, non-overthinking paths as positive examples for training.
2. **Preservation of Reflection Ability:** To design a novel "reflection reward" that discourages non-reflective behavior. This reward aims to prevent the LRM from favoring short but shallow responses that lack the necessary self-correction or verification steps, thereby preserving the model's critical reasoning and reflection capabilities while simultaneously encouraging efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. REA-RL Framework Overview

REA-RL is an online reinforcement learning framework designed to improve the inference efficiency of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) without sacrificing performance. The framework integrates a reflection model for dynamic online revision and refines the reward mechanisms used in online RL. The workflow involves parallel sampling of multiple reasoning paths, followed by sequential revision using a trained reflection model, and finally, training the policy model with both original and revised paths using refined rewards.

#### 3.2. Overthinking Detection Mechanism

The paper defines overthinking as excessive reflection occurring after an LRM has already reached the correct answer. To detect this, a method is proposed that does not rely on comparing with external LLMs during inference.
- **Detection Process:** The "think" part of an LRM's response is segmented into chunks. A powerful LLM (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) is then used to determine if each chunk contains the correct answer to the given question. A two-step verification is applied to ensure accuracy, where chunks initially identified as containing the answer are re-verified. The reasoning process leading up to the *first* confirmed occurrence of the correct answer is considered effective; any subsequent appearances of the answer within the reasoning are classified as overthinking.
- **Response Revision:** Once the overthinking tokens (those following the first correct answer) are identified, they are removed. The LRM is then compelled to generate the final answer, effectively creating a truncated, non-overthinking revision of the original response.

#### 3.3. Online Reinforcement Learning Integration

The core RL component adopts Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- **Parallel Sampling:** For each query, `G` reasoning paths are sampled in parallel from the policy model.
- **Reward Calculation:** Each path `s_i` receives a reward `r_i`, which is a sum of multiple reward functions. These rewards are normalized within the group to calculate an advantage score `a_i` for each path. The policy model is then optimized to increase the probability of paths with high advantage.
- **Accuracy Reward (`R_Acc`):** A rule-based accuracy reward is used, assigning 1 for a correct final answer and 0 for an incorrect one.
- **Baseline Length Reward (`R_Len`):** Initially, the Kimi K1.5 length reward is used, which assigns a reward `λ` for correct responses (where `λ` is inversely proportional to length, normalized by `min_len` and `max_len` in the group) and `min(0.5, λ)` for incorrect responses.

#### 3.4. Reflection Model for Online Sequential Revision

- **Design Principle:** A smaller, efficient reflection model is introduced to perform online revisions. This model is tasked with identifying the appropriate truncation point for sampled responses, enabling both parallel sampling and sequential revision during training.
- **Reflection Model Training:** To train this model, R1-7B is used to generate responses on the training dataset. Correct responses are then annotated by Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct to label chunks as "Think" or "Result" (containing the answer). Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is then fine-tuned on this data to predict these chunk categories in a single step, distilling the capabilities of the larger model into a more efficient form.
- **Online Revision Process:** During online RL training, after the initial parallel sampling of `G` paths, the trained reflection model (`M_Reflect`) analyzes these paths to identify and remove overthinking tokens. The policy model (`M_Policy`) then completes these truncated paths, generating `G` revised responses. Both the original sampled paths and these newly generated revised paths are used as training data for `M_Policy`. This approach effectively creates an "equivalent partial advantage," penalizing overthinking tokens while rewarding more concise, valid reasoning. However, to prevent adverse effects, revised responses that become incorrect are discarded, and only the original response is retained for training.

#### 3.5. Reflection-Aware Reward Refinement

To address the issue of models losing reflection ability when solely optimized for length, two refinements are introduced:
- **Reflection Reward (`R_Reflect`):** This reward discourages non-reflective behavior by penalizing responses with low densities of "reflective tokens" (e.g., "wait", "alternatively", "check", "but"). The reward is calculated as `min(0, (D_i / D_0.2) - 1)`, where `D_i` is the reflection density of a response and `D_0.2` is the 0.2 quantile of reflection density in the training data. This design ensures that only responses in the lowest 20% of reflection densities are penalized, preventing over-encouragement of reflection.
- **Refined Length Reward (`R_RLen`):** The Kimi K1.5 length reward is modified to explicitly set the reward to zero if a response is incorrect. This change prevents the model from being rewarded for short, incorrect answers, aligning the incentive more closely with correct and concise reasoning.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (R1-7B) is used as the base LRM.
- **Training Data:** A filtered version of the DeepScaleR 40k dataset is used, retaining 30k questions where the R1-7B model could generate at least one correct answer within four samples.
- **Training Details:** GRPO-based methods are trained for one epoch. Each batch consists of 16 distinct questions, with 4 paths generated per question. A maximum generation length of 8k tokens is used. Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is applied to all attention and MLP parameters (r=16, alpha=32), with a learning rate of 2 x 10^-5.
- **Evaluation:** The approach is evaluated on five math test sets of varying difficulty: GSM8K, MATH500, Gaokao23, Amc23, and Aime24. The rStar math validator is used for correctness. For smaller datasets (Amc23, Aime24), 8 paths are sampled per question to mitigate randomness.
- **Reflection Model Training:** R1-7B generates reasoning processes (4 paths per question), which are then labeled by Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is fine-tuned on this data for one epoch to serve as the reflection model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of Overthinking Detection

Preliminary experiments using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct for overthinking detection revealed that the method is effective. Without providing the gold answer, 24% of tokens could be automatically removed from responses without performance degradation. With the gold answer as input, 34% of tokens were removed with only a minor decrease in performance. This contrasts with fixed truncation methods, which showed considerable performance decline with increased truncation ratios. Analysis also indicated that overthinking is more prevalent in easier datasets (37% token removal) than in more challenging ones (17% token removal).

#### 4.2. Comparison with Offline and Online Baselines

The REA-RL framework was evaluated against various baselines under 8k and 16k token budgets (Table 2).
- **Offline Methods (SFT, RPO):** These methods achieved comparable performance to REA-RL at the 8k budget. However, at the 16k budget, their performance degraded significantly on more challenging datasets, underscoring the advantage of online training.
- **Online Baselines (GRPO, GRPO R_Len):** GRPO, using only an accuracy reward, showed no significant performance improvement, suggesting that simply increasing training steps was not sufficient. GRPO R_Len, which incorporated the Kimi K1.5 length reward, substantially reduced inference costs but incurred a considerable performance degradation. Other related baselines (NoThink, ShorterBetter, Light-R1, DAST, Arora & Zanette) achieved more aggressive truncation but also suffered from substantial accuracy drops.
- **REA-RL Performance:** REA-RL's components consistently yielded higher accuracy at comparable truncation ratios than existing baselines.

#### 4.3. Contribution of Reflection Model and Reward Components

The individual and combined contributions of REA-RL's components were analyzed:
- **Reflection Model (`M_Reflect`):** This component was more effective in reducing response length, leading to higher efficiency. When combined with `GRPO R_Len`, it improved average accuracy while also reducing the token ratio.
- **Reflection and Refined Length Rewards (`R_RLen+Reflect`):** This reward refinement primarily contributed to accuracy enhancement, maintaining performance more effectively than the baseline length reward.
- **Combined Approach (`GRPO R_RLen+Reflect M_Reflect`):** The combination achieved a balance between efficiency and performance. It reduced inference costs by 36% (average token ratio of 63.51% for the 16k budget) without compromising overall performance (average accuracy of 80.74% for 16k budget, comparable to the original model's 80.39%). The performance improvement under an 8k budget was more limited, attributed to the inherent advantage of shorter generations under tight constraints.

#### 4.4. Reflection Model Efficacy

Further analysis of the reflection model (Table 3) showed that:
- The trained 7B reflection model (`M_Reflect`) significantly outperformed its untrained 7B base model.
- `M_Reflect` (specifically the Strong truncation strategy) achieved compression ratios comparable to a 32B model revised without gold input, but at a substantially lower computational cost.
- `M_Reflect` consistently outperformed fixed truncation strategies, especially when high truncation ratios were targeted.
- REA-RL incorporating `M_Reflect` achieved higher compression than using `M_Reflect` for inference alone, while maintaining comparable performance. This was attributed to the synergistic effect of the length reward and online training.
- Online revision via the reflection model proved to be an efficient scaling strategy, outperforming merely increasing parallel sampling (e.g., sampling 8 paths instead of 4 in GRPO) in terms of both performance and truncation ratios.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Reflection Ability and Training Dynamics

- **Impact of Length Reward:** Training with only a length reward (`GRPO R_Len`) resulted in a severe decrease in the frequency of reflective tokens on easier problems (Table 4), leading to non-reflective and sometimes erroneous behavior.
- **REA-RL Maintains Reflection:** Both the reflection model and the reflection reward components of REA-RL enhanced the model's reflection capabilities. REA-RL mitigated non-reflective behavior, leading to an average 47% increase in reflection probability and a 5% performance improvement on easier datasets compared to `GRPO R_Len`.
- **Adaptive Reflection:** REA-RL effectively mitigated overthinking by appropriately reducing reflection on easy problems (22% reduction in reflection frequency, 45% efficiency gain on easier datasets) while largely preserving it on difficult problems (only 4% reduction in reflection frequency, 27% efficiency gain on harder datasets). This demonstrates an adaptive balance between conciseness and necessary deliberation.
- **Training Dynamics:** Figure 3 illustrated that `GRPO R_Len`'s performance dropped once response length reduction passed a critical threshold. In contrast, `GRPO R_RLen+Reflect` showed gradual efficiency improvements with minimal accuracy impact. The reflection model (`GRPO R_Len M_Reflect`) accelerated length reduction and, despite initial performance degradation, recovered by imposing targeted penalties on overthinking, avoiding the deletion of valid reflection tokens. The combined approach represented a trade-off between the efficiency goals of `M_Reflect` and the reflection-preserving goals of `R_RLen+Reflect`.

#### 4.6. Generalization Capabilities

Additional experiments confirmed the generalizability of the approach:
- The reflection model effectively generalized to the general QA domain (MMLU, MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA) and to smaller base models (R1-1.5B). It could truncate trajectories with comparable performance across different model scales (4B vs 7B reflection models) and domains.
- Policy training results on general QA with R1-1.5B showed stable improvements in average accuracy while maintaining comparable average token ratios when the reflection model was introduced. Similar positive trends were observed in math-domain experiments with R1-1.5B.

#### 4.7. Ablation Studies and Heuristic Comparisons

- **Reward Refinement:** Ablation studies confirmed that the refined length reward (`R_RLen`) accelerated response shortening and maintained or improved performance by preventing rewards for incorrect answers. The reflection reward (`R_Reflect`) consistently improved performance, albeit at the cost of a slightly lower truncation ratio, underscoring its role in performance maintenance. Varying the quantile parameter for `R_Reflect` showed a trade-off between length and performance, demonstrating the reward's focus on discouraging non-reflection rather than indiscriminately encouraging all reflection.
- **Heuristic Truncation:** Compared to heuristic methods that truncate at fixed reflection tokens, the learned reflection model performed significantly better, especially on harder datasets (Table 9). This highlights the difficulty-dependent nature of optimal truncation and the necessity of a learned approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The REA-RL framework presents a significant advancement in enhancing the practical utility of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by effectively addressing the challenge of "overthinking" without compromising reasoning capabilities.

Its potential significance and impact include:
- **Increased Efficiency and Cost Reduction:** By significantly reducing the inference token count (36% reduction demonstrated), REA-RL directly lowers the computational cost associated with deploying powerful LRMs. This makes these models more economically viable for real-world applications requiring extensive reasoning.
- **Preservation of Core LRM Abilities:** Unlike prior methods that sacrificed reflection capability for brevity, REA-RL explicitly maintains and even enhances the LRM's ability to self-reflect and deliberate. This ensures that models can remain effective on complex tasks where deep reasoning and verification are crucial.
- **Improved Online Reinforcement Learning:** The work contributes to the field of online RL for LLMs by introducing a novel and efficient scaling strategy through sequential revision and a refined reward mechanism. This mitigates undesirable behaviors (like loss of reflection) commonly observed when using simplistic length rewards.
- **Adaptive Reasoning Behavior:** The system trains LRMs to adapt their reasoning depth based on problem difficulty. Models become more concise for easier problems where overthinking is prevalent, while maintaining sufficient reflection for challenging tasks. This adaptive behavior more closely mimics human-like reasoning.
- **Generalizability Across Models and Domains:** The demonstrated effectiveness across different model scales (1.5B and 7B) and diverse domains (mathematics and general question answering) suggests that REA-RL is a broadly applicable technique for optimizing a range of LRMs.
- **Practical Application:** The ability to achieve high performance with reduced inference costs makes powerful LRMs more accessible for integration into real-time systems, chatbots, educational tools, and other applications where both accuracy and responsiveness are critical.
