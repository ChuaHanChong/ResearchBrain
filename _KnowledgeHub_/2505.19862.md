# REA-RL: Reflection-Aware Online Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> REA-RL presents an online reinforcement learning framework designed to optimize Large Reasoning Models by reducing redundant "overthinking" while preserving essential reflection capabilities. The framework demonstrates an average 36% reduction in inference token count without compromising reasoning performance across diverse mathematical and general question-answering benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often engage in "overthinking," generating unnecessarily long reasoning sequences even after finding the correct answer, leading to high computational costs.
- Prior offline methods for achieving conciseness are time-consuming and can suffer from distribution shifts when generating training data for evolving models.
- Existing online reinforcement learning methods, when solely optimizing for length, frequently cause LRMs to lose critical self-reflection abilities, which in turn degrades performance on complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- Developed **REA-RL**, an online reinforcement learning framework that incorporates a small, efficient ==reflection model== for dynamic, online detection and truncation of overthinking tokens within reasoning paths.
- Introduced a novel "reflection reward" to discourage non-reflective behavior and a refined length reward that explicitly penalizes incorrect short answers, aiming to preserve LRM reasoning capabilities.
- Integrated parallel sampling of reasoning paths with sequential revision by the reflection model, generating efficiently truncated, non-overthinking paths that serve as positive examples for policy model training.

## Results

- REA-RL achieved a **36%** reduction in inference token count (average token ratio of **63.51%** for a 16k budget) without compromising overall performance (average accuracy of **80.74%** for 16k budget, comparable to the original model's **80.39%**).
- The trained **7B** reflection model significantly outperformed fixed truncation strategies and achieved compression ratios comparable to a **32B** model revised without gold input, but with substantially lower computational overhead.
- The reflection reward component led to an average **47%** increase in reflection probability and a **5%** performance improvement on easier datasets compared to models trained solely with length rewards, mitigating the loss of reflective behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dedicated, smaller reflection model can effectively identify and remove superfluous reasoning steps online, enabling significant inference cost reduction while facilitating more efficient training data generation.
> - Carefully designed reward functions, specifically the reflection-aware reward and refined length reward, are crucial for balancing conciseness with the preservation of LRM's critical reflection abilities during online training.
> - The framework enables LRMs to adaptively adjust their reasoning depth based on problem difficulty, reducing overthinking on easier tasks while maintaining necessary deliberation for challenging ones.
