# Towards Robust Zero-Shot Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong and Tsinghua University developed BREEZE, a framework for robust zero-shot reinforcement learning that enhances Forward-Backward representations. It addresses issues of extrapolation error and limited model expressivity, achieving superior or near-best performance on the ExORL benchmark and D4RL Kitchen datasets with faster convergence and improved stability compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing Forward-Backward (FB) representation-based methods in zero-shot RL often produce inconsistent and biased successor measures and Q-value distributions, including negative M-values and significant scale mismatches.
- Current FB models lack sufficient expressivity to accurately learn complex approximators and multimodal behaviors required for effective generalization to diverse tasks.
- Offline unsupervised pretraining in FB methods is susceptible to extrapolation errors from out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, leading to unstable value estimates and propagating inaccuracies.

## Method

- Introduces ==Behavior-Regularized== Optimization, which constrains action selection during value function learning to in-distribution samples using expectile regression to mitigate OOD extrapolation errors.
- Employs a Task-Conditioned Diffusion Model for policy extraction, enabling the generation of high-quality, multimodal action distributions weighted by advantage, thus improving expressivity for complex tasks.
- Utilizes Expressivity Enhancement for Representation Modeling, integrating attention-based network architectures (Transformers) for both Forward and Backward representations to better capture complex state-action-task relationships.

## Results

- BREEZE achieved superior or near-best Interquartile Mean (IQM) returns across the majority of domains and datasets in the ExORL benchmark (e.g., **693** for Walker RND, **84** for Jaco RND), outperforming prior FB-based methods.
- The framework demonstrated faster convergence and enhanced stability during training compared to baselines, evident through smoother learning curves and lower variance in locomotion and manipulation domains.
- BREEZE maintained a significant performance advantage and exhibited superior stability even on small-sample (100k transition) datasets, indicating robust performance under limited data coverage and quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mitigating offline extrapolation errors through in-sample behavioral regularization is crucial for learning stable and accurate representations in zero-shot reinforcement learning.
> - Adopting generative models, specifically task-conditioned diffusion policies, enables the capture of multimodal action distributions, which is essential for tackling complex tasks requiring diverse behaviors in a zero-shot manner.
> - Enhancing the expressivity of the neural network architectures, particularly with attention mechanisms for modeling representations, significantly improves the quality and generalization capabilities of the learned forward and backward functions.
