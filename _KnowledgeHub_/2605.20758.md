# Conflict-Aware Additive Guidance for Flow Models under Compositional Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore and Harbin Institute of Technology developed Conflict-Aware Additive Guidance (g_car) to mitigate off-manifold drift in continuous-time flow models under compositional reward settings. The method dynamically resolves gradient conflicts, significantly improving constraint satisfaction and sample quality across diverse tasks, including generative decision-making and text-guided image manipulation, with a modest computational overhead.

## Problem

- Existing inference-time guidance methods for continuous-time flow models struggle to effectively compose multiple reward functions simultaneously, particularly in complex compositional settings.
- This limitation leads to 'off-manifold drift,' where generated samples deviate significantly from the true data manifold, compromising the fidelity and validity of outputs.
- The problem is exacerbated by gradient misalignment among multiple reward functions, which can collectively drive samples into low-density regions or 'energy traps' where the generative prior is poorly calibrated.

## Method

- The paper introduces Conflict-Aware Additive Guidance (g_car), which dynamically modifies the base velocity field of flow models with a blended guidance term.
- A conflict-aware weight (w_t) is used to gate between a computationally light approximate guidance and a learnable corrective guidance (g_ψ), activating corrections primarily in regions where reward gradients are misaligned.
- The learnable corrective guidance (g_ψ) is derived from the gradient of a scalar value function trained via Terminal Value Regression (TVR) using ground-truth terminal rewards, focusing learning only on high-conflict regions for efficiency and stability.

## Results

- On a synthetic 2D dataset, g_car boosted Posterior Coverage to **93.80%** and Constraint Satisfaction to **100%** in conflict scenarios, a substantial improvement over g_cov-G's **71.70%** PC.
- In generative decision-making (Maze2D, ManiSkill2), g_car achieved average gains of **19.0%** safety and **38.75%** success in planning, and increased StackCube success from **9%** to **61%** under hybrid constraints, while reducing violations.
- For text-guided image manipulation (CelebA-HQ), g_car achieved strong identity preservation (ID 0.681, a 0.138 improvement over g_cov-G) and balanced multi-prompt alignment, generating faces with minimal distortion at competitive inference speeds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A theoretical analysis identifies gradient misalignment as a critical factor contributing to approximation error and off-manifold drift in guided sampling, showing its severe scaling with the number of reward functions and angular divergence.
> - Approximation error in guided sampling can be decomposed into coupling shift, gradient misalignment, and localized approximation errors, providing a principled understanding of off-manifold drift.
> - By selectively activating a learnable, conservative corrective vector field only when significant gradient conflicts are detected, the system can effectively rectify off-manifold drift while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of generative modeling, specifically focusing on continuous-time flow models such as Rectified Flow, Flow Matching, and Stochastic Interpolants. These models are noted for their ability to acquire robust generative priors through data-driven training. A key area of ongoing research involves controlling the generation process of these powerful models at inference time to satisfy specific user-defined or task-dependent constraints.

Inference-time guided sampling, or guidance, is a prevalent technique for this purpose. It involves injecting external reward signals into the generation process without requiring retraining or fine-tuning the base generative model. This approach offers a flexible and computationally lighter alternative to model fine-tuning or optimization-based control. Existing guidance methods, however, frequently encounter limitations when multiple constraints are applied simultaneously, particularly in "compositional reward settings." This scenario often leads to samples deviating significantly from the true data manifold, a phenomenon referred to as "off-manifold drift." This drift compromises the fidelity and validity of the generated outputs.

This paper identifies the specific causes of this off-manifold drift, particularly emphasizing the role of gradient misalignment among multiple reward functions. It proposes a method designed to actively rectify this issue, aiming to bridge the gap between computationally efficient but approximate guidance methods and highly accurate but resource-intensive "exact guidance" methods. The contribution thus advances the practical applicability of guided generative models in complex, multi-objective environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and computationally efficient method for steering state-of-the-art flow models under compositional reward settings, specifically addressing the problem of off-manifold drift. The motivation for this objective stems from several key challenges and requirements:

- **Compositional Reward Problem:** In real-world applications, generative models are often required to satisfy multiple, diverse, and potentially conflicting objectives simultaneously. For instance, in generative decision-making, trajectories must adhere to safety, smoothness, and dynamic consistency. In text-guided image manipulation, an image might need to satisfy multiple textual attributes. Existing inference-time guidance methods struggle to compose these diverse constraints effectively, leading to degraded sample quality and deviations from the intended data distribution.
- **Off-Manifold Drift:** A core failure mode of current approximate guidance methods is "off-manifold drift." This occurs when the injected guidance pushes samples into low-density regions where the pre-trained vector field is poorly calibrated, causing systematic errors. This problem is exacerbated in compositional settings because competing objectives can induce conflicting gradients, collectively driving samples away from the data manifold into regions of "vanishing energy" or "energy traps."
- **Limitations of Existing Guidance Methods:**
 - **Approximate Guidance:** While computationally lightweight and flexible, these methods (e.g., `g_cov-G`) implicitly assume optimal coupling is invariant and approximate reward functions locally, making them susceptible to off-manifold drift, especially when gradients are misaligned.
 - **Exact Guidance:** Methods such as Guidance Matching (GM) or GLASS-FKS can mitigate off-manifold drift and achieve high fidelity. However, they are typically computationally expensive, require significant training data or complex sampling procedures, and often lack the flexibility for efficient adaptation across a wide range of reward functions. GM, for instance, requires ground-truth samples satisfying all constraints, which are often unavailable.
- **Theoretical Understanding of Approximation Error:** The authors are motivated to provide a theoretical analysis that quantifies the approximation error in guided sampling. Their analysis aims to decompose this error into terms related to coupling shift, gradient misalignment, and localized approximation error. This theoretical foundation highlights that approximation error scales severely with gradient misalignment (1 − cos ϕ) and the number of reward functions (G), providing a clear direction for designing corrective mechanisms.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to enable more reliable and high-fidelity controlled generation with flow models, thereby supporting their robust deployment in applications requiring adherence to complex, heterogeneous runtime constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Conflict-Aware Additive Guidance (g_car), is designed to rectify off-manifold drift in flow models operating under compositional rewards by dynamically detecting and resolving gradient conflicts. The methodology integrates theoretical analysis with a learnable, lightweight correction mechanism.

**Theoretical Foundation: Approximation Error Analysis**

The work initiates with a theoretical analysis that establishes an upper bound on the approximation error of guided sampling. This error is defined as the Wasserstein-2 distance between the exact and the realized target distributions, `E ≜ W_2^2(p_1^⋆, p_1̂)`. The analysis decomposes this error into three primary components:

1. **Coupling Shift Error (Term A):** This error arises from the "Coupling-Invariant Approximation," which assumes that the optimal coupling between source and target distributions remains largely unchanged (P(z) ≈ 1) despite the introduction of reward-based reweighting. This assumption can lead to a mismatch between the desired target and the actual sampling process, generating off-manifold samples.
2. **Gradient Misalignment Error (Term B):** This is identified as a critical factor in compositional reward settings. It quantifies the "energy dissipation" that occurs when gradients from multiple reward functions are not perfectly collinear (i.e., `1 - cos ϕ > 0`). When gradients misalign, their vector sum (as used in approximate guidance) can point away from the true manifold, leading to spurious local minima or "energy traps" that prevent trajectories from reaching optimal regions. The analysis shows this error scales with the number of reward functions (G) and quadratically with the per-reward gradient norm (μ²), and linearly with the average angular divergence `(1 - cos ϕ)`.
3. **Localized Approximation Error (Term C):** This component results from approximating the intractable value gradient using a first-order Taylor expansion of the reward function around the expected future state. This error is influenced by the smoothness of the reward landscape (spectral norm of the Hessian, λh) and the uncertainty in predicting the terminal state (spectral norm of conditional covariance, σ1). This error term is scaled linearly by G.

This decomposition provides a theoretical justification for the observed off-manifold drift in approximate guidance methods and highlights gradient misalignment as a severe issue in compositional contexts.

**Conflict-Aware Additive Guidance (g_car)**

Building on the theoretical insights, `g_car` is formulated as a modification to the base velocity field `v_base_t` by adding a guidance term `g_car(x_t, t)`:
`v'_t(x_t, t) = v_base_t(x_t, t) + g_car(x_t, t)`

The `g_car` term itself is a weighted composition of a fast approximation (`g_approx`) and a learnable corrective guidance (`g_ψ`):
`g_car(x_t, t) = (1 - w_t) * g_approx + w_t * g_ψ(x_t, t)`

- **`g_approx`**: This is a standard, computationally light approximate guidance, often derived from a first-order Taylor expansion (e.g., `g_cov-G`). It is used when gradients are well-aligned.
- **`w_t` (Conflict-Aware Weight)**: This dynamic gating mechanism determines the blending ratio between `g_approx` and `g_ψ`. It is calculated based on the raw conflict score:
 `w_raw(x_t) = 1 - (2 / (G(G - 1))) * Σ_{j<k} (<g_j, g_k> / (||g_j|| ||g_k|| + ε))`
 Where `g_j = ∇_xt r_j(x_hat_1)` are the gradients of individual rewards. `w_raw` is then mapped to `w_t ∈ (0, 1)`. When gradients are aligned (e.g., `g_j` are parallel), `w_raw` approaches 0, and `g_car` primarily relies on `g_approx`. When gradients are misaligned (e.g., `g_j` are orthogonal or opposing), `w_raw` increases, and `g_car` increasingly incorporates `g_ψ`. This allows `g_car` to selectively activate corrections only when necessary.
- **`g_ψ` (Learnable Corrective Guidance)**: This component is designed to actively rectify off-manifold drift in regions of high gradient conflict. `g_ψ` is defined as the gradient of a learned scalar value function `V_ψ(x_t, t)`: `g_ψ(x_t, t) = ∇_x V_ψ(x_t, t)`.

**Learning `g_ψ` via Terminal Value Regression (TVR)**

Instead of conventional Fitted Value Evaluation (FVE), which can suffer from the "deadly triad" (instability due to function approximation, off-policy data, and bootstrapping), the authors propose Terminal Value Regression (TVR) for learning `V_ψ(x_t, t)`.

- **Motivation for TVR:** Flow models operate with deterministic ODE dynamics, and rewards are typically sparse, defined only at the terminal state `x_1`. This determinism allows for direct regression against the terminal reward, bypassing the need for bootstrapping.
- **Objective:** `V_ψ` is trained by minimizing a masked regression loss:
 `L(ψ) = E_{(x_t,t,x_1)∼D} [ I_t * (r(x_1) - V_ψ(x_t, t))^2 ]`
 - `x_1`: The terminal state reached by integrating the current guided dynamics from `x_t`.
 - `r(x_1)`: The ground-truth terminal reward, serving as a stable, unbiased target.
 - `I_t = 1(w(x_t) > τ)`: A hard gate that activates training for `V_ψ` only when the conflict score `w(x_t)` exceeds a threshold `τ`. This ensures computational efficiency by focusing learning on critical conflict regions and prevents `g_ψ` from perturbing trajectories in already well-aligned regions.
- **Parameterization:** The scalar value function `V_ψ(x_t, t)` is parameterized (e.g., using a neural network). Taking its gradient `∇_x V_ψ(x_t, t)` ensures that the learned `g_ψ` is a conservative (curl-free) vector field, preventing issues associated with unconstrained vector field parameterization.

This comprehensive approach combines theoretical clarity on error sources with a targeted, learnable, and efficient correction mechanism for robust controlled generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of `g_car` was empirically validated across a range of tasks, including synthetic 2D datasets, generative decision-making (planning and robotic manipulation), and text-guided image manipulation. Comparisons were made against approximate guidance (`g_cov-G`), gradient deconfliction methods (PCGrad), and exact guidance methods (GM, GLASS-FKS), as well as task-specific baselines.

#### 4.1. Synthetic Dataset (2D Mixture of Gaussians)

- **Off-Manifold Drift:** `g_cov-G` exhibited significant off-manifold drift under gradient conflict (e.g., constraint), achieving only 71.70% Posterior Coverage (PC). This was visually attributed to "energy traps" where conflicting gradients lead to erratic and off-manifold sampling trajectories.
- **Correction Efficacy:** `g_car` notably rectified this drift, boosting PC to 93.80% and achieving 100.00% Constraint Satisfaction (CS) in the conflict scenario.
- **Efficiency:** `g_car` incurred a modest inference time overhead (4.20 ms/sample vs. 0.38 ms/sample for `g_cov-G`), while exact baselines like GLASS-FKS were ~70 times slower (296 ms/sample) and GM required 20x more training data. PCGrad provided only marginal improvements (75.20% PC, +3.5 percentage points over `g_cov-G`).

#### 4.2. Generative Decision-Making as Planners (Maze2D)

- **Failure of Approximate Guidance:** `g_cov-G` generated hallucinated paths with poor prior preservation and high constraint violations across various compositional reward settings (static obstacles, static goal, dynamic obstacles, hybrid). For instance, in static obstacle avoidance, it achieved only 39% safety and 16% success.
- **`g_car` Performance:** `g_car` demonstrated significant improvements, yielding average gains of 19.0% safety and 38.75% success, with 0.68 fewer violations per trajectory. Its inference time remained competitive (168.4 ms/sample vs. 150.0 ms/sample for `g_cov-G`). PCGrad provided only marginal corrections.
- **Robustness:** `g_car` maintained 34% success even with 6 clustered obstacles, a challenging scenario where `g_cov-G` collapsed to 12%.
- **Integration with MPPI:** When integrated with the MPPI planner (`MPPI+g_car`), the combined method achieved the highest performance, with an average safety of 97.75% and success of 95%.

#### 4.3. Generative Decision-Making as Policies (ManiSkill2 Manipulation)

- **OOD Behavior:** On high-dimensional manipulation tasks (PickCube, StackCube), `g_cov-G` and PCGrad frequently produced out-of-distribution (OOD) and physically incoherent motions, leading to task failures (e.g., PCGrad resulted in 0% success on StackCube).
- **`g_car` Performance:** `g_car` effectively rectified these failures. In StackCube under hybrid constraints, it reduced the violation rate from 1.8 to 0.4 and boosted success from 9% to 61%. For PickCube, it achieved 100% success in static environments.
- **Exact Guidance Limitations:** GLASS-FKS struggled on high-precision tasks like StackCube (24-32% success) due to its inherent sampling variance.

#### 4.4. Text-guided Image Manipulation (CelebA-HQ)

- **Image Quality and Alignment:** `g_cov-G` produced hallucinated images and exhibited off-manifold drift. PCGrad struggled with balancing multiple compositional text prompts. GLASS-FKS failed to preserve reference image identity due to high sampling variance.
- **`g_car` Performance:** `g_car` achieved strong identity preservation (ID 0.681, a 0.138 improvement over `g_cov-G`) and balanced text-image alignment across multiple prompts (e.g., CLIP 0.291, BLIP-ITM 0.597, VQAScore 0.755), generating faces with minimal distortion. It achieved this with competitive inference overhead (11.26s vs. 11.08s for `g_cov-G`).

In summary, `g_car` consistently outperformed approximate baselines in compositional reward settings across diverse domains by actively rectifying off-manifold drift. It achieved performance levels comparable to or exceeding exact guidance methods while maintaining significantly lower computational costs and data requirements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of generative modeling by enhancing the reliability and effectiveness of inference-time guidance for continuous-time flow models, particularly under complex, compositional reward structures.

- **Improved Reliability and Fidelity:** By theoretically identifying gradient misalignment as a root cause of off-manifold drift and proposing a targeted, conflict-aware correction mechanism, the work significantly improves the fidelity and on-manifold adherence of generated samples. This addresses a critical limitation of existing approximate guidance methods, which often produce unrealistic or invalid outputs when faced with multiple, competing objectives.
- **Enhanced Constraint Satisfaction:** The ability of `g_car` to effectively manage and resolve gradient conflicts enables generative models to satisfy a broader range of complex and heterogeneous runtime constraints. This is demonstrated across diverse applications, from avoiding obstacles and reaching multiple goals in planning to manipulating specific attributes in image editing.
- **Computational Efficiency:** `g_car` provides a computationally lightweight solution compared to "exact guidance" methods. It achieves near-exact performance by strategically allocating computational resources, training a small corrective network only in regions of high gradient conflict. This efficiency makes it suitable for real-time applications and environments where frequent model fine-tuning is impractical.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's validation across synthetic data, robotic planning, manipulation, and image editing suggests its generalizability and potential for impact across various domains where controlled and constrained generation is crucial. Its integration with existing planning methods like MPPI further expands its utility.
- **Implications for Real-World Deployment:** The enhanced robustness and fidelity offered by `g_car` are significant for deploying large-scale generative models in sensitive applications such as autonomous systems (e.g., safe robot navigation, constrained control) and interactive content creation. The reduction in off-manifold drift helps ensure that generated trajectories or images are not only aligned with specified objectives but also remain consistent with the learned generative prior, leading to more trustworthy and usable outputs.

In conclusion, this work represents an advancement in controllable generation, providing a principled and practical approach to a common failure mode in current generative guidance paradigms.
