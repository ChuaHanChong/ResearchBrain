# VISION-SLS: Safe Perception-Based Control from Learned Visual Representations via System Level Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> VISION-SLS presents a method for safe, perception-based control of nonlinear systems by integrating learned low-dimensional visual representations with System Level Synthesis (SLS). The framework provides robust constraint satisfaction guarantees and inherently enables information-gathering behaviors from high-resolution image inputs.

## Problem

- Deploying robots reliably with visual perception while simultaneously providing robust safety and performance guarantees remains challenging.
- Existing visuomotor control methods often trade off scalability to high-dimensional problems with the ability to formally certify safety or performance.
- Model-based control struggles with high-dimensional visual inputs and actively reducing uncertainty (information gathering) while maintaining robust guarantees for nonlinear systems.

## Method

- **VISION-SLS** uses a perception module that combines pre-trained ==DINO features== with a learned projection to map high-resolution images into a low-dimensional, dynamics-aware representation.
- The method employs ==System Level Synthesis (SLS)== to directly optimize closed-loop response maps, ensuring robust constraint satisfaction by accounting for both process and calibrated perception uncertainties.
- An iterative ==Sequential Convex Programming (SCP)== approach, enhanced by an efficient ==double-Riccati== solver, makes the nonlinear, constrained output-feedback control problem computationally tractable.

## Results

- VISION-SLS achieved significantly higher success rates and lower constraint violation rates across all experiments (e.g., **98.53%** SR, **1.6%** CVR for a 4D car) compared to certainty equivalent and non-robust baselines.
- The controllers demonstrated intrinsic information-gathering, guiding the system to regions with lower perception uncertainty, which resulted in smaller final state uncertainty bounds.
- The approach successfully scaled to high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics (**59-state** humanoid) and processed high-resolution images, with hardware validation on a TurtleBot 4 demonstrating real-world applicability and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust constraint satisfaction for nonlinear systems can be guaranteed by locally linearizing dynamics and employing SLS with tightened constraints and tube radii that bound deviations due to noise and linearization errors.
> - The SLS framework naturally facilitates information-gathering behaviors, where controllers actively maneuver to reduce state uncertainty by seeking regions with more informative sensor readings.
> - Processing high-dimensional visual data in guaranteed control frameworks becomes feasible by learning a low-dimensional observation map and empirically calibrating its state-dependent error bounds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in robotics control: reliably deploying robots that operate based on visual perception while providing robust safety and performance guarantees. Existing methods in visuomotor control typically present a trade-off between scalability to high-dimensional problems and the ability to certify safety or performance.

On one hand, data-driven approaches like reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL) have demonstrated success in scaling to complex, high-dimensional visuomotor tasks, enabling end-to-end pixel-to-action policies. However, these methods often yield policies that are difficult to interpret or formally certify for safety, and they typically require extensive data. While the integration of visual foundation models (VFMs) has improved generalization through rich feature representations, it generally does not imbue the overall perception-control pipeline with calibrated guarantees.

On the other hand, model-based control methods, such as model predictive control (MPC), Lyapunov-based control, and belief-space planning (BSP), are capable of offering strong theoretical guarantees on safety and performance. However, these methods are often limited to linear or low-dimensional systems. When applied to partially observable or nonlinear systems, they commonly rely on the separation principle (estimating the state and then controlling it), which can preclude information-gathering behaviors essential for robustness to sensor uncertainties (e.g., visual occlusions) or active uncertainty reduction. Furthermore, robust constraint satisfaction under uncertain dynamics remains a significant hurdle for many practical BSP approximations.

A gap exists in methods that can simultaneously achieve scalable control from high-dimensional visual inputs, actively reduce uncertainty (information-gathering), and provide robust constraint satisfaction guarantees for nonlinear systems. This paper aims to bridge this gap by combining learned visual representations with a System Level Synthesis (SLS)-based control design framework. Previous SLS work has focused on linear time-varying (LTV) systems for output feedback or nonlinear state feedback, without fully addressing the combination of nonlinear dynamics, high-dimensional visual inputs, and robust output feedback with information-gathering properties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method, termed VISION-SLS, for nonlinear output-feedback control from high-resolution RGB images that offers robust constraint satisfaction guarantees. This is sought under realistic conditions of partial observability, sensor noise, and nonlinear dynamics. The motivation for this objective stems from the identified limitations of current approaches in robotics:

1. **Safety and Performance Guarantees in Perception-in-the-Loop Control**: Reliable robot deployment necessitates strong guarantees on safety and performance. However, output-feedback control, especially when using high-dimensional, non-smooth visual sensor outputs, complicates both control synthesis and safety verification due to partial observability and noisy feedback. The paper aims to provide these guarantees directly.

2. **Scalable Information-Seeking Behavior**: Existing guaranteed control methods often struggle with scalability to high-dimensional systems or inhibit active information-gathering behaviors. The motivation is to enable controllers that can actively reduce state uncertainty by maneuvering into regions offering more informative sensor readings, without compromising safety, and to do so for complex systems.

3. **Tractability for High-Dimensional Visual Inputs**: Directly using raw, high-dimensional visual data (e.g., RGB images with 10^5 or more pixels) in model-based control synthesis is computationally prohibitive. A key motivation is to make this feasible by incorporating learned low-dimensional representations of visual information into a guaranteed control framework.

4. **Addressing Nonlinear Dynamics and Robust Constraints**: Many real-world robotic systems exhibit nonlinear dynamics and operate under stringent safety constraints. The method seeks to provide robust constraint satisfaction guarantees for these nonlinear systems, accounting for bounded process and measurement noise.

5. **Efficient Solver for SLS-based Control**: While System Level Synthesis (SLS) offers a framework for robust control, solving the resulting optimization problems for large-scale systems can be computationally expensive. The motivation is to develop an efficient solver that leverages the underlying structure of SLS problems to make the approach practical for real-time control.

In summary, the work aims to create a practical and certified visuomotor control pipeline that enables robust, safe, and information-gathering control for nonlinear systems operating with high-dimensional visual feedback, transcending the limitations of current techniques regarding scalability, guarantees, and active uncertainty reduction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VISION-SLS method addresses the challenges of nonlinear output-feedback control from high-resolution images by introducing two main approximations and a structured optimization approach:

**A. Overall Framework (VISION-SLS Overview)**
The method couples nonlinear trajectory optimization with an SLS parameterization of the local closed-loop error dynamics. It learns reduced observations and robustly synthesizes safe nonlinear output-feedback control via an iterative, efficient solver.

**B. Controller Parameterization and Robust Constraint Satisfaction**
1. **Nominal Trajectory and Linearized Dynamics**: A nominal trajectory (z, v) for states and inputs is jointly optimized, where `z_k+1 = f(z_k, v_k)`. The nonlinear dynamics are linearized around this nominal trajectory to obtain a Linear Time-Varying (LTV) error system for deviations (Δx, Δu). A modeling error term `σ(τ_k, z_k, v_k)` is introduced to bound the linearization residual.
2. **Causal Affine Time-Varying Feedback Policies**: Instead of optimizing over general causal maps, the method restricts policies to causal affine, time-varying feedback policies of the form `u_k = v_k + K_0^k(x_0 - z_0) + Σ_{j=1}^{k-1} K_{k,j}(y_r^{j+1} - h_r(z_j))`. This structure enables efficient evaluation and optimization.
3. **System Level Synthesis (SLS) for Robust Control**: The core idea is to directly optimize the closed-loop response maps (Φ) that relate disturbances to state and input deviations. This parameterization is crucial for convex controller synthesis with robust constraint satisfaction. Proposition 1 establishes the conditions (9) for Φ to be a valid closed-loop response.
4. **Nonlinear Robust Constraint Satisfaction**: Proposition 3 extends robust constraint satisfaction from linear systems to the nonlinear context. It states that robust satisfaction of state and input constraints for the nonlinear system (1) is guaranteed if a set of tightened inequalities (26a) holds. These inequalities are derived by considering the worst-case disturbance within the specified bounds and adding the nominal trajectory. Additionally, the magnitude of deviations `∥(Δx_k, Δu_k)∥_∞` is explicitly constrained by a tube radius `τ_k` (26b) to ensure the validity of the linearization and modeling error bounds (`σ` and `η`). The disturbance scalings `Σ_j` and `Υ_j` capture state-dependent disturbance and observation uncertainties.

**C. Perception Function for High-Dimensional Visual Inputs**
1. **Learning a Lower-Dimensional Visual Representation**: To manage high-dimensional images, a "perception module" is developed. This involves a pre-trained DINO feature extractor `ψ_dino(y)` combined with a learned projection `p(·)` to map raw images `y` to a low-dimensional reduced measurement `y_r ∈ R^n_r`. A corresponding state-based reduced observation model `h_r(x)` (initially linear `C_r x`) is learned. The learning objective (29) minimizes the mismatch between `p(ψ_dino(y_i))` and `h_r(x_i)` while simultaneously encouraging observability of the underlying state `x` through a dynamics-aware observability metric (minimum singular value of the observability matrix O).
2. **Calibration of Perception Reduction Error**: After training `p(·)` and `h_r(·)`, the residual error `r_i = ∥p(ψ_dino(y_i)) - h_r(x_i)∥_∞` is empirically bounded. This is achieved by fitting a state-dependent scalar envelope `b(x)` (via a convex program (31) that minimizes a polynomial fit with slack variables) such that `p(ψ_dino(y)) - h_r(x) ∈ F_r(x)B_∞`, where `F_r(x) = b(x)I`. This calibrated, state-dependent uncertainty term is then incorporated into the robust constraint satisfaction formulation.

**D. Implementation via Sequential Convex Programming (SCP)**
1. **Efficient Solver for SLS-based Output-Feedback LQG**: The unconstrained LTV SLS output-feedback problem (11) is shown to possess a "double-Riccati" structure (Proposition 4). This allows for efficient computation of the optimal closed-loop response matrices Φ (Φ_xw, Φ_xe, Φ_uw, Φ_ue) by solving parallel backward Riccati recursions (for control gains) and Kalman recursions (for observer gains), followed by two forward passes (35, 36) to assemble the response operators (37). This significantly improves computational scalability compared to general-purpose convex solvers.
2. **SCP Algorithm**: The full nonlinear, constrained optimization problem (27) is solved using an iterative Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) approach (Algorithm 1). At each iteration, the nonlinear dynamics and constraints are linearized around the current nominal trajectory, and the cost is quadratized, yielding a convex subproblem (a Second-Order Cone Program, SOCP). This subproblem is then solved using the structure-exploiting SLS method based on Riccati recursions. The solution of the subproblem updates the nominal trajectory, and the process repeats until convergence. This coupling is heuristic but maintains feasibility and is efficient in practice.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VISION-SLS method was evaluated across four systems (simulated 4D car, 10D quadrotor, 59D humanoid, and hardware TurtleBot) and compared against certainty equivalent (CE) and non-robust (NR) baselines. Key findings include:

1. **Robust Constraint Satisfaction and Safety**: Across all simulated and hardware experiments, VISION-SLS consistently achieved high success rates (SR) and zero or very low constraint violation rates (CVR), outperforming baselines in safety. For instance, on the 4D car, VISION-SLS achieved 98.53% SR and 1.6% CVR, compared to CE (93.47% SR, 6.53% CVR) and NR (48.13% SR, 55.87% CVR). Similar trends were observed for the quadrotor and humanoid, where VISION-SLS achieved 100% SR and 0% CVR, while NR baselines showed significantly higher CVRs (e.g., 92.8% for quadrotor, 98% for humanoid). The method demonstrated that its tube tightening mechanism effectively keeps the system within safe operating regions despite disturbances and perception uncertainty.

2. **Information-Gathering Behavior**: The SLS design intrinsically enables information-gathering motion. In the light-dark domain and the 4D car task with an occluded region, VISION-SLS optimally steered the system to regions with lower perception error (lighter shades in Fig. 3c, avoiding dark occlusions in Fig. 4b). This active uncertainty reduction led to smaller final state uncertainty bounds and better overall performance compared to CE controllers, which accumulated more uncertainty.

3. **Scalability to High-Dimensional Dynamics and Visual Inputs**:
 - **High-Dimensional Dynamics**: The method was applied successfully to a 59-state Unitree G1 humanoid, demonstrating scalability to complex, high-dimensional nonlinear systems with partial state measurements. It robustly tracked a backflip trajectory, with closed-loop rollouts remaining within computed reachable tubes, unlike open-loop executions.
 - **High-Resolution Visual Inputs**: By leveraging pre-trained DINO visual features and a learned low-dimensional observation map, VISION-SLS processed high-resolution RGB images (≥ 512 × 512 pixels) for the car and quadrotor tasks. This shows that the approach effectively makes vision-based control tractable for complex visual data.

4. **Computational Efficiency**: The proposed double-Riccati structure for the unconstrained LTV SLS output-feedback problem significantly improved computational efficiency. In the light-dark benchmark, the method scaled much better with horizon length than a general-purpose convex optimization-based SLS solver (MOSEK), achieving similar optimal solution costs (Fig. 3a,b). The per-iteration complexity of the SCP solver remained close to that of a single state-feedback fast-SLS MPC step, making it practical for real-time applications.

5. **Perception Error Calibration**: The empirical calibration procedure for state-dependent overbounds on perception reduction error proved data-efficient. For the 4D car task, the method achieved over 90% empirical coverage with only 50 calibration samples, increasing to 99% with 500 samples (Table II). This suggests that adapting the certificate to new environments can be done with relatively few samples.

6. **Hardware Validation**: Successful deployment on a physical TurtleBot 4, controlling it safely from onboard images in an office environment, demonstrated the real-world applicability and robustness of VISION-SLS. The robot maintained collision avoidance and stayed within computed reachable tubes across multiple runs.

7. **Comparison with Baselines**: VISION-SLS consistently outperformed certainty-equivalent controllers (which do not perform active information gathering or robust constraint tightening under partial observability) and non-robust belief-space controllers (which perform information gathering but lack robust safety guarantees). It also performed favorably against a variant of a recent safe perception-based control algorithm (Tong et al., 2023) in a LiDAR-based driving setting, achieving 100% safety compared to 93%.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VISION-SLS framework represents a significant step towards enabling safe and reliable autonomous systems that operate in complex, real-world environments with high-dimensional visual perception. Its key contributions address long-standing challenges at the intersection of learning, control, and formal verification.

1. **Bridging the Gap between Learning and Guarantees**: By systematically integrating learned visual representations from foundation models with a formally verifiable control framework (SLS), the work offers a pathway to combine the scalability and generalization capabilities of modern perception systems with the safety guarantees of model-based control. This could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in safety-critical applications.

2. **Enabling Information-Gathering with Safety**: The method's ability to synthesize controllers that actively reduce uncertainty (information-gathering) while robustly satisfying constraints is particularly impactful. Robots can now be designed to intelligently explore their environment or adapt their trajectories to optimize sensor readings, thereby improving state estimation and overall robustness, all without compromising safety. This is crucial for navigating partially observable or dynamic environments.

3. **Tractability for High-Dimensional, Nonlinear Systems**: The use of low-dimensional learned observation maps and an efficient Riccati-based solver for SLS-based output feedback makes the approach computationally tractable for high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics and high-resolution image inputs. This scalability is essential for real-world robotics problems that involve complex kinematics, dynamics, and rich sensor data.

4. **Practical Deployment Potential**: The successful hardware validation on a TurtleBot demonstrates the practical viability of the VISION-SLS approach. The data-efficient calibration of perception error bounds further enhances its applicability, as it reduces the effort required to adapt the system to new environments while maintaining certification.

5. **Advancement in Robust Control Theory**: The introduction of a novel controller parameterization for partially observed nonlinear systems within SLS, combined with an efficient double-Riccati solver, pushes the boundaries of robust optimal control theory. It provides a structured approach to incorporate perception and dynamics uncertainties directly into the control synthesis problem.

Potential impacts include safer autonomous driving, robust aerial navigation in cluttered environments, and more reliable control of complex humanoid robots performing dynamic tasks. Future work on handling total occlusions, incorporating learned dynamics, and developing an optimal solver for the constrained SLS problem could further enhance the framework's capabilities, potentially leading to a new generation of trustworthy visuomotor controllers.
