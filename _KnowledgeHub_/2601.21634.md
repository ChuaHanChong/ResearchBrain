# RSGround-R1: Rethinking Remote Sensing Visual Grounding through Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University and Shanghai University of Finance and Economics developed RSGround-R1, a framework that enhances Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for Remote Sensing Visual Grounding (RSVG). RSGround-R1 achieves state-of-the-art performance on RSVG benchmarks and strong out-of-domain generalization by integrating Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-Tuning with a novel reinforcement fine-tuning approach featuring positional rewards and spatial consistency guidance.

## Problem

- Remote Sensing Visual Grounding (RSVG) descriptions heavily rely on positional and relational cues for small, ambiguous, and non-unique objects in vast aerial imagery, a challenge current MLLMs struggle with.
- MLLMs lack inherent fine-grained spatial reasoning capabilities, often producing vague or semantically biased outputs for RSVG queries without explicit spatial guidance.
- Standard Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) for RSVG suffers from sparse IoU-based rewards (often zero) and spatial inconsistency in rollouts due to densely distributed, visually similar objects, hindering precise localization.

## Method

- A ==two-stage post-training pipeline, RSGround-R1==, was developed, starting with ==Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-Tuning (CoT-SFT)== on a synthetic dataset to prime MLLMs with explicit positional reasoning.
- Introduced a novel ==positional reward (R_pos)== for Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), based on a 2D Gaussian kernel, to provide continuous, distance-sensitive feedback even for non-overlapping predictions.
- Implemented a ==Spatial Consistency Guided Optimization Scheme (L_SC)== in RFT, which dynamically weights the policy optimization loss to stabilize predictions and promote coherent grounding behavior across multiple rollouts.

## Results

- RSGround-R1 achieved state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing SOTA models across DIOR-RSVG (e.g., **71.81** Acc@0.5), VRSBench-VG, and RRSIS-D datasets, surpassing its GRPO baseline by over **5%** in Acc@0.5 on DIOR-RSVG.
- The framework demonstrated strong out-of-domain generalization capabilities on FAST-T and SOTA-T datasets, indicating robust and transferable spatial priors across different remote sensing domains.
- Ablation studies confirmed that CoT-SFT, the positional reward (R_pos), and the spatial consistency guided optimization (L_SC) each contribute significantly to performance, with their combination yielding peak results and sustained benefits under extended training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly structured spatial reasoning, imparted through Chain-of-Thought training, is crucial for MLLMs to effectively interpret positional cues in remote sensing imagery.
> - Continuous, distance-aware rewards, like the proposed Gaussian kernel-based positional reward, are more effective than sparse IoU rewards for guiding MLLMs toward precise localization in challenging RSVG scenarios.
> - Addressing spatial inconsistency in MLLM rollouts through adaptive optimization is vital for achieving stable and reliable visual grounding performance in remote sensing datasets with dense, similar objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Remote Sensing Visual Grounding (RSVG) is a crucial task for bridging human language understanding with vast aerial imagery, enabling applications from military target identification and urban planning to environmental monitoring. Despite its importance, RSVG presents unique challenges compared to visual grounding in natural images. Remote sensing scenes are characterized by immense spatial scales, high semantic ambiguity (e.g., small, numerous, and visually similar objects), and descriptions that heavily rely on positional and relational cues rather than just semantic attributes (as highlighted in Figure 1 of the paper).

The broader research landscape for visual grounding has seen significant advancements with the rise of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models have demonstrated impressive generalization capabilities across various tasks. However, this paper addresses a critical gap: while MLLMs are powerful, studies (Fu et al., 2024; Majumdar et al., 2024) have shown their inherent struggles with fine-grained spatial reasoning, particularly when explicit spatial cues are lacking or ambiguous. This limitation is exacerbated in RSVG, where precise localization based on relative positions is paramount.

Existing RSVG approaches often adapt general-purpose grounding or detection frameworks, which tend to prioritize category-level alignment over geometric understanding, leading to suboptimal performance in spatially ambiguous remote sensing scenes. Similarly, while Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) has emerged as an effective paradigm to enhance multimodal reasoning in VLMs (Huang et al., 2025c; Shen et al., 2025), many current implementations, especially in the remote sensing domain, are direct deployments of general RL frameworks. They often overlook the distinct spatial complexities of geospatial imagery, such as the zero-overlap issue with standard Intersection over Union (IoU) rewards and the spatial inconsistency of model rollouts due to densely distributed, visually similar objects.

RSGround-R1 positions itself as a pioneering work by specifically addressing these unfulfilled needs. It is the "first principled study of spatial reasoning in MLLMs for RSVG," moving beyond generic VLM adaptations and direct RFT deployments. By tailoring both the pre-training (Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-Tuning) and reinforcement fine-tuning (with novel positional rewards and spatial consistency guidance) to the specific characteristics of remote sensing data, this work significantly pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities for geospatial understanding. It acknowledges the need for explicit positional awareness and stable grounding behavior, thereby setting a new standard for how MLLMs can effectively tackle the complexities of RSVG.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **rethink Remote Sensing Visual Grounding (RSVG) by enhancing the spatial reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**. The authors aim to develop a framework, RSGround-R1, that can accurately localize target objects in large-scale aerial imagery based on natural language descriptions, especially those heavily reliant on positional cues.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges and observations:

1. **Unique Nature of RSVG:** Unlike natural image visual grounding, RSVG descriptions predominantly depend on positional and relational phrases (e.g., "on the lower left," "to the right of the tennis court"). Target objects are often small, ambiguous, and non-unique, making semantic recognition alone insufficient for accurate localization. This mandates strong, fine-grained positional awareness, which current MLLMs lack.
2. **MLLM Limitations in Spatial Reasoning:** Despite their impressive generalization, MLLMs (like Qwen2.5-VL) often struggle with explicit spatial reasoning. Preliminary experiments showed that without structured spatial cues, these models produce vague or semantically biased outputs for RSVG queries, failing to generate clear, position-aware thinking processes (Figure 2(i)).
3. **Sparsity of IoU-based Rewards in RFT:** Standard Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) methods often use Intersection over Union (IoU) as a reward signal. However, in RSVG scenarios, where predictions frequently do not overlap with the ground truth due to the vast search space, the IoU reward becomes zero. This provides no informative gradient to distinguish between near-miss predictions and entirely incorrect ones, hindering the model's ability to progressively refine its localization (Figure 2(ii)).
4. **Spatial Inconsistency of Rollouts:** The inherent characteristics of remote sensing imagery—large areas with densely distributed and visually similar objects—lead to inconsistent localization behaviors across different rollouts for the same query. This "spatial inconsistency" (Figure 2(iii)) results in scattered predictions, weakening the model's spatial reasoning and overall grounding stability.

In summary, the authors are motivated to move beyond generic MLLM applications for RSVG by developing a tailored, reasoning-guided framework that explicitly addresses the challenges of spatial ambiguity, sparse feedback, and unstable localization inherent in remote sensing data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RSGround-R1 framework proposes a two-stage, reasoning-guided, position-aware post-training pipeline to enhance spatial understanding in MLLMs for RSVG:

**Stage 1: Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-Tuning (CoT-SFT) for Position Awareness**

- **Motivation:** To prime the base MLLM with explicit and interpretable positional reasoning, as general MLLMs lack this inherent spatial interpretability for RSVG.
- **CoT Data Construction:** A synthetic CoT dataset is generated using a powerful MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B). For each image-sentence pair and its ground truth bounding box, a predefined CoT prompt is used to guide the MLLM to produce step-by-step reasoning processes in the format: "<think>thinking process here</think><answer>GT coordinates</answer>." This process yields approximately 30,000 high-quality CoT samples emphasizing spatial reasoning, which are then filtered for consistency.
- **CoT-SFT Cold Start Initialization:** The base model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) is fine-tuned on this CoT dataset. The objective is to maximize the conditional likelihood of generating the reasoning and answer sequence. This stage provides a "cold start" by instilling a structured, step-by-step thinking process, establishing a foundational understanding of desired spatial reasoning behavior before the more exploratory RFT stage.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) with Spatially Guided GRPO**

After CoT-SFT, the model's spatial precision and stability are further enhanced through RFT, augmented by two novel components:

- **Positional Reward for Spatial Guidance (R_pos):**
 - **Motivation:** To overcome the limitations of sparse IoU rewards (which are zero for non-overlapping predictions) by providing continuous, distance-sensitive feedback.
 - **Formulation:** A 2D Gaussian kernel is defined, centered at the ground-truth bounding box. The kernel's standard deviations are scaled by the width and height of the ground truth box, controlled by a parameter `α`. The positional reward is then computed as the mean activation of this max-normalized Gaussian kernel within the predicted bounding box region. This formulation ensures that predictions closer to the target receive higher rewards, even without direct overlap, guiding the model progressively.
 - **Integration:** This positional reward (R_pos) is combined with standard format and IoU rewards into an overall verifiable signal: R = R_format + R_IoU + βR_pos, where `β` balances the contribution of positional proximity.

- **Spatial Consistency Guided Optimization Scheme (L_SC):**
 - **Motivation:** To mitigate the spatial inconsistency observed in rollouts for RSVG, which arises from visually similar and densely packed objects, leading to unstable localization.
 - **Mechanism:** The scheme quantifies spatial consistency across multiple rollouts for the same query by computing the mean and variance of their IoU rewards. Higher mean IoU indicates accuracy, while lower variance indicates coherent predictions. An adaptive weighting factor `w` is derived using an exponential function of (1 - m(IoU)) and Var(IoU), controlled by coefficients `λ_m` and `λ_v`.
 - **Optimization:** This weighting factor `w` dynamically scales the per-token Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) loss (L_GRPO) to yield L_SC = w ⋅ L_GRPO. By assigning higher weights to samples exhibiting greater spatial dispersion (inconsistency), the optimization scheme explicitly directs the model to focus on these challenging cases, thereby promoting more stable and contextually aligned grounding behavior.

The base model used for these stages is Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, and the RFT process is based on GRPO, employing a data-efficient approach of training for only 0.4 epochs with 40% of the training data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that RSGround-R1 achieves superior performance and generalization across various RSVG benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Comparison with SOTA Models (Table 1)

- **Dominant Performance:** RSGround-R1 consistently achieves the best performance across DIOR-RSVG, VRSBench-VG, and RRSIS-D datasets. For instance, on DIOR-RSVG, it achieves 71.81 Acc@0.5, 58.71 Acc@0.7, and 63.38 mIoU, significantly outperforming all other models.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** It surpasses its direct GRPO baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-3B w/ GRPO) by over 5% in Acc@0.5 on DIOR-RSVG (71.81 vs. 66.65), despite using only 40% of the training data and training for a short duration (0.4 epoch). This highlights its superior learning effectiveness and data efficiency.
- **Outperforming RS-specific VLMs:** RSGround-R1 outperforms many dedicated remote sensing VLMs (e.g., GeoChat, VHM, SkySenseGPT, LHRS-Bot) even when they are fine-tuned with SFT, underlining the effectiveness of its specialized spatial reasoning approach.
- **Strong Base Model:** The Qwen2.5-VL-3B base model itself shows strong zero-shot performance, even outperforming some RS-specific models, but RSGround-R1 significantly builds upon this foundation.

#### 4.2. Out-of-domain Generalization (Table 2)

- **Robustness to Domain Shift:** RSGround-R1 demonstrates strong out-of-domain generalization capabilities on the FAST-T and SOTA-T datasets, which involve significant regional and sensor shifts. It consistently outperforms GRPO baselines and SFT-based methods.
- **Transferable Spatial Priors:** The improved generalization, especially on SOTA-T, suggests that the learned spatial reasoning priors are robust and transferable across different remote sensing domains.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 3, 4, 5)

- **Contribution of CoT-SFT:** Incorporating CoT-SFT alone leads to a ~3% improvement in Acc@0.5 on DIOR-RSVG (from 66.65 to 68.45), validating its role in establishing a foundational positional reasoning ability.
- **Effectiveness of Positional Reward (R_pos):** Adding R_pos yields further gains (68.10 Acc@0.5), demonstrating its effectiveness in providing continuous, informative gradients for progressive localization. Comparisons (Table 4) show R_pos outperforms other distance-aware functions (GIoU, DIoU, center distance) as rewards, which can introduce noisy signals.
- **Impact of Spatial Consistency Guided Optimization (L_SC):** L_SC provides a substantial ~3.3% improvement in Acc@0.5 (from 66.65 to 69.95), confirming its role in stabilizing predictions across rollouts and promoting coherent grounding behavior. Joint consideration of mean IoU and variance of IoU in weighting achieves the best results (Table 5).
- **Synergistic Effects:** When all three components are combined, RSGround-R1 achieves its peak performance, confirming their complementary contributions to robust spatial reasoning and precision.
- **Reduced Reward Standard Deviation:** Figure 5 visually confirms that L_SC effectively reduces the standard deviation of rewards during training, indicating more stable and reliable policy optimization.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Comparison (Figure 4)

- RSGround-R1 produces more plausible, structured, and step-wise reasoning trajectories compared to the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-3B w/ GRPO. This leads to more accurate bounding box predictions, demonstrating enhanced interpretability and precise localization.

#### 4.5. Performance under Extended Training (Figure 6)

- The benefits of RSGround-R1 are sustained and even amplified with extended training. It continues to yield steady gains and consistently outperforms the GRPO fine-tuning baseline, indicating the robustness and scalability of the approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RSGround-R1 framework represents a significant advancement in Remote Sensing Visual Grounding (RSVG) and the application of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to geospatial analysis. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Spatial Reasoning in RSVG MLLMs:** This work is the first to systematically address the challenge of spatial reasoning in MLLMs specifically for RSVG. By acknowledging and tackling the unique demands of aerial imagery (vast scale, semantic ambiguity, positional cue reliance), it provides a principled approach that moves beyond generic VLM adaptations.
2. **Enhanced Localization Accuracy and Precision:** Through its innovative components – CoT-SFT, positional reward, and spatial consistency guided optimization – RSGround-R1 achieves state-of-the-art performance, significantly improving the accuracy and precision of localizing objects based on complex language descriptions. This means more reliable identification of targets in critical applications.
3. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The framework demonstrates strong out-of-domain generalization capabilities, transferring learned spatial priors effectively across datasets with varying regional and sensor characteristics. This robustness is crucial for real-world deployment, where models must perform well on unseen data.
4. **Increased Interpretability:** The Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-Tuning (CoT-SFT) component encourages the model to generate explicit, step-by-step reasoning processes. This enhances the interpretability of MLLMs for RSVG, allowing users to understand *how* the model arrives at its localization decisions, fostering trust and enabling better debugging.
5. **Data Efficiency:** The ability of RSGround-R1 to achieve superior performance using only a small fraction (40% of data for 0.4 epoch) of the training data demonstrates remarkable data efficiency. This is particularly valuable in remote sensing, where high-quality annotated data can be expensive and time-consuming to acquire.
6. **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Fine-Grained Perception:** The positional reward provides continuous, distance-aware feedback, effectively bridging the gap between high-level language understanding and fine-grained pixel-level localization. This allows MLLMs to refine predictions even when initial guesses are far from the target.
7. **Stabilized Learning Dynamics:** The spatial consistency guided optimization scheme addresses a critical issue of inconsistent model rollouts, leading to more stable and reliable policy updates during reinforcement learning. This results in a more robust and dependable grounding model.

**Potential Applications:**
The advancements offered by RSGround-R1 have wide-ranging potential impacts across various domains:
- **Urban Planning and Infrastructure Monitoring:** More accurate and automated mapping of urban features, tracking changes in infrastructure, and assessing development.
- **Disaster Assessment and Response:** Rapid identification and localization of damaged areas, affected populations, or critical resources following natural disasters.
- **Environmental Monitoring:** Precise tracking of land-use changes, deforestation, agricultural patterns, and ecological shifts, aiding conservation efforts.
- **Autonomous Systems:** Enhancing the perception and navigation capabilities of drones and other autonomous aerial vehicles by providing fine-grained spatial understanding from natural language commands.
