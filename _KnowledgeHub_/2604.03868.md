# Risk-Constrained Belief-Space Optimization for Safe Control under Latent Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Maryland developed a risk-constrained belief-space optimization framework, leveraging Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) within a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller, to manage latent uncertainty in safety-critical systems. This approach achieved an 82% task success rate with zero external contact in a simulated dexterous manipulation task, surpassing chance-constrained methods.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems face safety challenges when critical parameters, such as object properties or environmental geometry, are unobservable (latent uncertainty).
- Existing control methods either optimize for expected performance, which offers limited protection against rare but severe safety violations, or employ robust control, which can be overly conservative.
- Managing tail risks and ensuring explicit safety guarantees are difficult when uncertainty in dynamics, costs, and safety constraints cannot be directly sensed.

## Method

- A ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework operates in a belief space, maintaining a particle filter approximation of latent parameter uncertainty.
- The control problem minimizes a risk-sensitive objective (expected cost plus CVaR of cost) subject to a hard safety constraint based on the CVaR of trajectory safety margins.
- This optimization is solved using a modified Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) algorithm, which generates and evaluates candidate trajectories under sampled uncertainties.

## Results

- The controller theoretically guarantees that a satisfied CVaR safety constraint implies a lower bound "β_s" on the probability of maintaining safety over the planning horizon.
- In a simulated vision-guided dexterous stowing task, the risk-constrained controller achieved an **82%** success rate with zero external contact for a safety confidence level "β_s = **0.95**".
- This performance notably surpassed a chance-constrained Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) baseline, which achieved a **50%** success rate while also maintaining zero contact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining the Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) of trajectory safety margins provides a probabilistic guarantee on the magnitude of potential safety violations, which is stronger than merely bounding violation probability.
> - Maintaining a belief distribution over unobservable latent parameters via a particle filter enables the controller to make informed decisions under deep uncertainty.
> - The proposed framework allows for a flexible trade-off between performance and safety, and can revert to risk-neutral behavior by adjusting a risk-weight parameter.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenges of safe control for systems operating under *latent uncertainty*, where critical parameters influencing dynamics, costs, and safety cannot be directly resolved by sensors at decision time. This type of uncertainty, arising from unknown physical properties, exogenous disturbances, or unobserved environmental geometry, necessitates advanced control strategies that account for its probabilistic structure.

Current approaches in the field typically fall into two categories:
- **Standard optimization methods** often focus on optimizing expected performance. These methods may offer limited protection against rare but severe outcomes, as they do not explicitly account for tail risks in the uncertainty distribution.
- **Robust control formulations** aim to guarantee performance under worst-case uncertainty. While providing strong guarantees, they tend to be overly conservative by not exploiting the known probabilistic structure of the uncertainty, potentially leading to suboptimal or overly cautious behaviors.

This work integrates concepts from several established research areas to overcome these limitations:
- **Risk Measures and Risk-Sensitive Optimization**: The paper leverages coherent risk measures, specifically Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR), which are standard tools for quantifying and managing tail risks in stochastic programming. CVaR has previously been applied in robotics within contexts such as Markov Decision Processes, reinforcement learning, and motion planning.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Chance Constraints**: The framework is built upon Model Predictive Control, a widely used method for online optimal control. Sampling-based MPC variants, like Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), are particularly relevant for nonlinear stochastic systems as they avoid the need for explicit gradient computations. Chance-constrained MPC addresses uncertainty by replacing hard constraints with probabilistic violation bounds, but this work argues that CVaR offers a more nuanced control over the magnitude of violations.
- **Belief-Space Planning**: This area provides a principled approach for decision-making under state and parameter uncertainty, where a belief distribution represents the current knowledge. Particle filter representations are a common tool for maintaining and updating these beliefs, especially in complex nonlinear systems.
- **Safety-Critical Control**: This field encompasses methods like Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) that certify forward invariance of safe sets. Recent extensions include stochastic and belief-based CBFs.

The novel contribution of this work lies in its integration of a risk-sensitive objective and a CVaR-based safety constraint within a belief-space MPPI framework. Specifically, the paper focuses on latent parameters that influence not only the state or disturbance distributions but also the system dynamics, costs, and safety margins. The combined application of CVaR in both the objective function (for performance) and as a hard constraint on trajectory safety margins (for risk management) under a belief distribution over latent parameters is presented as a novel approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the operational requirements of safety-critical control systems, which frequently encounter *latent uncertainty*. This uncertainty, often inherent in real-world environments, is challenging because it cannot be directly measured or resolved by sensors at the moment of decision-making. Examples include unknown physical properties of objects, unpredictable exogenous disturbances, or partially observed environmental geometry. When such uncertainty is unmodeled or inadequately addressed, it can lead to degraded system performance, task failures, or, critically, violations of safety constraints.

Current control paradigms exhibit specific limitations:
- **Expected Value Optimization**: Many standard control methods are designed to optimize the expected value of a performance metric. While effective for average-case performance, this approach offers limited protection against rare but potentially severe outcomes that reside in the tail of the uncertainty distribution. For safety-critical applications, neglecting these tail risks is unacceptable.
- **Robust Control**: Robust formulations are designed to guarantee performance even under the worst-case realization of uncertainty. While providing strong guarantees, these methods often treat uncertainty in an overly conservative manner. They typically do not exploit any known probabilistic structure of the uncertainty, which can lead to inefficient or overly cautious system behavior, reducing operational flexibility and efficiency.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a principled framework for safe control under latent uncertainty**: The framework aims to explicitly model and manage uncertainty in critical, unobservable parameters that affect system dynamics, task costs, and safety constraints.
2. **Integrate risk sensitivity into the control objective**: To move beyond expected performance, the objective is to optimize a risk-regularized performance metric, which accounts for undesirable outcomes in the tail of the performance distribution.
3. **Enforce robust safety guarantees through explicit risk constraints**: The core objective is to ensure safety by imposing a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) constraint on a trajectory safety margin over a receding horizon. This aims to directly constrain the likelihood and magnitude of severe safety violations induced by the variability of latent parameters.
4. **Provide theoretical guarantees**: Establish formal properties of the proposed risk-constrained controller, including probabilistic safety, recovery of risk-neutral behavior in appropriate limits, and cumulative safety over repeated control applications.
5. **Validate the framework in a safety-critical robotic task**: Demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach through physics-based simulations of a complex, vision-guided dexterous manipulation task involving uncertain environment geometry.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to provide a control methodology that balances performance with safety in the presence of challenging latent uncertainties, offering a more nuanced and effective approach than existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework operating in a belief space, specifically tailored for risk-sensitive control under latent uncertainty.

**System Model and Uncertainty Representation:**
The system is modeled as a partially-observed stochastic dynamical system:
- **State Dynamics**: `x_{t+1} = f(x_t, u_t, θ, w_t)`, where `x_t` is the state, `u_t` is the control input, `θ` is an unknown time-invariant latent parameter vector, and `w_t` is process noise.
- **Measurement Model**: `z_t = g(x_t, θ, v_t)`, where `z_t` is the measurement and `v_t` is measurement noise.
The latent parameter `θ` influences the system's dynamics, trajectory costs, and safety constraints. Since `θ` is unobservable, the controller maintains a posterior belief `b_t(θ) := p(θ | x_{0:t}, z_{1:t})`. Due to the potential for nonlinearities, a **particle filter** is used to approximate `b_t(θ)` with `N_p` weighted particles `{(θ^(i), ω_t^(i))}`. These particles are propagated through the dynamics and reweighted based on measurement likelihoods, with resampling applied when the effective sample size drops below a threshold.

**Trajectory Cost and Safety Margin:**
- **Trajectory Cost**: `J_H(u, θ, x_t)` is defined as the sum of stage costs `ℓ(x_{t+k}, u_{t+k}, θ)` and a terminal cost `ϕ(x_{t+H}, θ)` over a prediction horizon `H`. This cost is a random variable due to `θ` and `w_t`.
- **Safety Function**: `h(x, θ)` is a continuously-differentiable function that defines a parameter-dependent safe set `S(θ) = {x : h(x, θ) ≥ 0}`. A negative `h` indicates a safety violation.
- **Trajectory Safety Margin**: `M_H(x, θ) = min_{k=0...,H} h(x_{t+k}, θ)`. `M_H < 0` implies a violation at some point along the trajectory.

**Risk-Constrained Belief-Space Control Problem (Problem 1):**
The control problem is formulated as a receding-horizon optimization problem:
`u^* ∈ arg min_{u∈U^H} E_{b_t}[J_H] + λ_r CVaR_{β_c}(J_H)`
`subject to CVaR_{β_s}(-M_H) ≤ 0`
- **Objective Function**: Minimizes a weighted sum of the expected trajectory cost `E_{b_t}[J_H]` and the Conditional Value-at-Risk of the trajectory cost `CVaR_{β_c}(J_H)`. `λ_r` is a weight for the risk term, and `β_c` (performance confidence level) controls the tail sensitivity for performance.
- **Safety Constraint**: Enforces that the `CVaR_{β_s}(-M_H)` is less than or equal to zero. Here, `-M_H` represents a "violation variable" (positive if violated), and `β_s` (safety confidence level) determines the degree of safety conservativeness. This constraint explicitly limits the magnitude of the worst-case safety margin violations.
The expectations and CVaR calculations are performed with respect to the posterior belief `b_t(θ)` and the process noise over the planning horizon.

**Risk-Sensitive Belief-Space MPPI Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**
Problem 1 is solved using a modified Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) algorithm:
1. **Belief Update**: At each time step `t`, the current belief `b_{t-1}(θ)` is updated to `b_t(θ)` using the new observation `z_t` via particle filter reweighting and resampling.
2. **Parameter Sampling**: `N_p` latent parameter samples `θ^(i)` are drawn from `b_t(θ)`.
3. **Candidate Trajectory Generation**: `K` candidate control sequences `u^(j)` are generated by perturbing a nominal control sequence `hat{u}` with i.i.d. Gaussian noise.
4. **Trajectory Simulation**: For each candidate control `u^(j)` and each belief particle `θ^(i)`, a trajectory `x^(i,j)` is simulated over the horizon `H` using the system dynamics.
5. **Cost and Safety Margin Evaluation**: For each simulated trajectory, the trajectory cost `J_H^(i,j)` and trajectory safety margin `M_H^(i,j)` are computed.
6. **Empirical Risk Estimation**: The expectation `E_{b_t}[J_H^(j)]`, performance CVaR `CVaR_{β_c}(J_H^(j))`, and safety CVaR `CVaR_{β_s}(-M_H^(j))` are empirically estimated from the `N_p` particle rollouts for each candidate control sequence. For CVaR, this involves sorting the relevant values (costs or negative margins) and averaging the `ceil((1-β)N_p)` highest (or largest violation) values.
7. **Score Calculation**: A score `S^(j)` is computed for each candidate `u^(j)`, combining the expected cost, performance CVaR, and a penalty for violating the safety CVaR constraint.
8. **Control Update**: Importance sampling weights `ρ^(j)` are assigned to each candidate based on its score `S^(j)`. The optimal control sequence `u^*` is then computed as a weighted sum of the nominal control and the perturbations.
9. **Receding Horizon**: Only the first element of `u^*` is applied to the system, and the remaining sequence is used to warm-start the optimization in the next time step.

This methodology combines probabilistic uncertainty representation, a robust risk measure, and an efficient sampling-based controller to achieve safe and effective operation under latent uncertainty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical guarantees and empirical validation through a simulated robotic task.

**Theoretical Properties:**
The authors establish three properties for the risk-constrained controller:
1. **CVaR Safety Implication (Theorem 1)**: This theorem states that if the CVaR constraint `CVaR_{β_s}(-M_H) ≤ 0` is satisfied, then the probability of maintaining safety (i.e., `M_H ≥ 0`) is at least `β_s`. This provides a direct probabilistic safety guarantee for the entire trajectory over the prediction horizon, indicating that the controller explicitly limits the adverse tail of the safety-margin distribution. The authors highlight that this is a stronger guarantee than what can be obtained from Markov's inequality applied to the expected violation magnitude.
2. **Risk-Neutral Limit (Theorem 2)**: This theorem demonstrates that as the risk weight `λ_r` in the objective function approaches zero, any cluster point of the optimal control sequence `u^*(λ_r)` minimizes the risk-neutral objective `E_{b_t}[J_H]` over the same feasible set defined by the CVaR safety constraint. This indicates consistency, showing that the framework can recover conventional risk-neutral behavior in the appropriate limit.
3. **Cumulative Receding-Horizon Safety (Theorem 3)**: For `T` successive applications of the receding-horizon controller, each satisfying the per-horizon CVaR safety constraint, a union-bound argument is used to establish a cumulative safety guarantee: `Pr(AND_{j=1 to T} (M_H^(j) ≥ 0)) ≥ 1 - T(1 - β_s)`. This provides a probabilistic lower bound on the likelihood of remaining safe throughout multiple control steps, although the bound can become loose for large `T` or high `β_s`.

**Experimental Validation (Vision-Guided Dexterous Stowing Task):**
The framework was validated using physics-based simulations in MuJoCo for a dexterous object stowing task. A robotic manipulator had to insert a grasped box into a narrow bookcase slot with uncertain geometry (`θ = (T_slot, w_slot)`). The initial uncertainty in slot position (`σ_p = 12 mm`) exceeded the nominal lateral clearance of 8.75 mm, making safe insertion inherently probabilistic.
- **Safety Function**: The safety function `h(x, θ)` was defined as the minimum of three margins: object-environment clearance, maximum allowed environment contact force, and maximum allowed grasp force. A negative `h` indicated a violation.
- **Controller Configurations**: The CVaR-MPPI controller was tested with varying safety confidence levels `β_s` (0.50, 0.90, 0.95) and fixed `λ_r = 0.5`.
- **Baseline**: A chance-constrained MPPI (CCMPPI) baseline was used, which optimized expected cost subject to `Pr(M_H < 0) ≤ δ_H = 0.05` and estimated `M_H` using only the posterior mean of `θ`.

**Key Empirical Results:**
Table II and III summarize the performance across 11 randomized trials:
- **Impact of Safety Confidence Level (`β_s`)**:
 - **Low Risk Aversion (`β_s = 0.50`)**: Achieved a 55% success rate but incurred a 27% contact rate, with maximum external contact forces reaching 1118 N. The minimum safety margin was significantly negative (-28.4 mm).
 - **Moderate Risk Aversion (`β_s = 0.90`)**: Improved success rate to 73% and completely eliminated exterior contact (0% contact rate, 0 N max external force).
 - **High Risk Aversion (`β_s = 0.95`)**: Further increased the success rate to 82%, while maintaining zero contact.
 This demonstrates a monotonic relationship between `β_s` and safety, confirming that higher risk aversion in the CVaR constraint leads to safer trajectories and eliminates severe contacts.
- **Comparison with Chance-Constrained Baseline**:
 - The CCMPPI baseline (with `δ_H = 0.05`) achieved a 50% success rate and 0% contact.
 - Both the `β_s = 0.90` and `β_s = 0.95` CVaR-MPPI configurations achieved zero contact but significantly outperformed CCMPPI in terms of success rate (73% and 82% vs. 50%). This suggests that controlling the magnitude of violations (via CVaR) rather than just the probability of violation (via chance constraints) leads to more robust and successful task completion in the face of uncertainty.
- **Safety-Performance Tradeoff**: Increasing `β_s` from 0.50 to 0.95 resulted in a notable increase in task success and complete elimination of contacts, at a modest increase in computational time per step (from 1169 ms to 1193 ms) and a slight increase in final distance to target (from 100.9 mm to 131.8 mm for `β_s=0.95` compared to `β_s=0.90`).
- **Computational Cost**: All configurations ran at approximately 1200 ms per control step, primarily dominated by the MuJoCo forward simulations (`K × N_p = 1024` rollouts). The overhead for CVaR estimation was found to be negligible.

Figure 4 illustrates that all CVaR configurations successfully kept the `CVaR_{β_s}(-M_H)` below zero, indicating constraint satisfaction. As `β_s` increased, the CVaR trace moved closer to the boundary, reflecting the stricter constraint on the worst-case tail.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of safe control for autonomous systems operating under pervasive and often critical latent uncertainties. The integration of risk-sensitive optimization and CVaR constraints within a belief-space Model Predictive Path Integral framework addresses a fundamental challenge: enabling reliable operation in safety-critical scenarios where sensor information is incomplete or noisy, and where extreme, low-probability events can lead to severe consequences.

The significance of this work can be summarized as follows:
- **Enhanced Safety Guarantees**: By explicitly incorporating CVaR constraints on trajectory safety margins, the framework moves beyond merely bounding the probability of violation (as in chance constraints) to directly bounding the magnitude of the worst-case safety violations. The theoretical result demonstrating that `CVaR_{β_s}(-M_H) ≤ 0` implies `Pr(M_H ≥ 0) ≥ β_s` provides a strong, quantifiable probabilistic safety guarantee. This is crucial for applications where the cost of failure is high.
- **Improved Task Success and Reliability**: The experimental results from the dexterous stowing task underscore the practical benefits. Even with significant initial pose uncertainty, the risk-averse CVaR-MPPI controller achieved high task success rates (up to 82%) while completely eliminating external contact forces that could lead to damage or failure. This performance notably exceeded a chance-constrained baseline, which, while also achieving zero contact in some cases, exhibited lower task completion rates. This suggests that considering the *magnitude* of risk, rather than just its probability, guides the controller to genuinely safer and more effective trajectories.
- **Principled Uncertainty Management**: The use of a particle filter to maintain a belief distribution over latent parameters, which directly influence dynamics, costs, and safety, provides a robust mechanism for operating in partially observed environments. This approach allows the controller to reason about and act upon the probabilistic nature of the uncertainty.
- **Consistency and Flexibility**: The theoretical property demonstrating that the controller recovers risk-neutral behavior as the risk weight `λ_r` approaches zero confirms the framework's consistency with established control theory. The ability to tune `β_s` allows for explicit control over the safety-performance trade-off, enabling practitioners to select an appropriate level of risk aversion for specific applications.
- **Computational Feasibility**: Despite the sophisticated nature of the optimization, the MPPI-based implementation demonstrated practical computational efficiency, running at approximately 1.2 seconds per control step, with the primary cost stemming from physics simulations rather than the risk estimation itself.

**Potential Impact**:
The framework has significant potential impact in various safety-critical robotics and autonomous systems domains, including:
- **Dexterous Manipulation**: Tasks requiring precise interaction with the environment, such as assembly, part insertion, or intricate object handling, especially when object properties or environmental geometries are uncertain.
- **Autonomous Navigation**: For self-driving cars, aerial vehicles, or mobile robots operating in dynamic, partially observable environments with potential for unknown obstacles or disturbances.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration**: Ensuring safe interaction and coexistence with humans by rigorously constraining collision risks.
- **Exploration and Inspection**: Enabling robots to operate safely in unknown or hazardous environments while performing data collection or complex maneuvers.

By providing a robust and theoretically grounded method for managing latent uncertainty and tail risks, this research advances the state of the art in safe autonomous control, paving the way for more reliable and capable robotic systems in challenging real-world applications. Future work on correlated observation noise and time-varying latent parameters will further enhance its applicability.
