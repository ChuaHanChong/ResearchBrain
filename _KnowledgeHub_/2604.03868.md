# Risk-Constrained Belief-Space Optimization for Safe Control under Latent Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Maryland developed a risk-constrained belief-space optimization framework, leveraging Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) within a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller, to manage latent uncertainty in safety-critical systems. This approach achieved an 82% task success rate with zero external contact in a simulated dexterous manipulation task, surpassing chance-constrained methods.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems face safety challenges when critical parameters, such as object properties or environmental geometry, are unobservable (latent uncertainty).
- Existing control methods either optimize for expected performance, which offers limited protection against rare but severe safety violations, or employ robust control, which can be overly conservative.
- Managing tail risks and ensuring explicit safety guarantees are difficult when uncertainty in dynamics, costs, and safety constraints cannot be directly sensed.

## Method

- A ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework operates in a belief space, maintaining a particle filter approximation of latent parameter uncertainty.
- The control problem minimizes a risk-sensitive objective (expected cost plus CVaR of cost) subject to a hard safety constraint based on the CVaR of trajectory safety margins.
- This optimization is solved using a modified Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) algorithm, which generates and evaluates candidate trajectories under sampled uncertainties.

## Results

- The controller theoretically guarantees that a satisfied CVaR safety constraint implies a lower bound "β_s" on the probability of maintaining safety over the planning horizon.
- In a simulated vision-guided dexterous stowing task, the risk-constrained controller achieved an **82%** success rate with zero external contact for a safety confidence level "β_s = **0.95**".
- This performance notably surpassed a chance-constrained Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) baseline, which achieved a **50%** success rate while also maintaining zero contact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining the Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) of trajectory safety margins provides a probabilistic guarantee on the magnitude of potential safety violations, which is stronger than merely bounding violation probability.
> - Maintaining a belief distribution over unobservable latent parameters via a particle filter enables the controller to make informed decisions under deep uncertainty.
> - The proposed framework allows for a flexible trade-off between performance and safety, and can revert to risk-neutral behavior by adjusting a risk-weight parameter.
