# Curriculum-based Sensing Reduction in Simulation to Real-World Transfer for In-hand Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Oklahoma State University, GAC R&D Center Silicon Valley, and Colorado School of Mines developed Curriculum-based Sensing Reduction (CSR) with a Deep Random Generator (DRG) to enable Deep Reinforcement Learning policies to gradually adapt from rich simulated sensor data to limited real-world sensing. This approach allows the actor policy to learn from comprehensive information initially and then systematically reduce its reliance on hard-to-extract features, achieving up to a 58% success rate in real-world in-hand object rotation tasks with an Allegro hand, outperforming baselines.

## Problem

- Dexterous in-hand manipulation requires complex DRL policies, often trained via Sim2Real transfer, but high-fidelity simulated sensing (e.g., numerous tactile sensors) is expensive and impractical to replicate in physical robots.
- Existing Asymmetric Actor-Critic (AAC) methods use a fixed, one-step reduction of features from simulation to reality, leading to a non-adaptable actor-critic feature gap and suboptimal policy performance, requiring compromises between real-world setup effort and learning efficiency.
- Relying on high-dimensional inputs like images in DRL models increases computational burden, data requirements, and vulnerability to real-world environmental variations, further complicating Sim2Real transfer.

## Method

- **Curriculum-based Sensing Reduction (CSR):** A method to generate a stepwise feature reduction curriculum, which evaluates the importance of target features (e.g., tactile sensors) during early training using online metrics (e.g., activation number) to prioritize less critical features for removal.
- **Deep Random Generator (DRG):** A deep random neural network that replaces signals from reduced features with randomly sampled values from a normal distribution and reinitializes its weights each training epoch. This prevents the DRL model from forming new dependencies on constant values or retaining old dependencies on removed features.
- **Gradual Adaptation:** The actor policy starts training with a rich feature space (including hard-to-extract features) and progressively adapts to a more limited, real-world-accessible feature space through the CSR-defined curriculum, with DRG handling the feature removal process stably.

## Results

- The 2-step CSR + DRG configuration consistently achieved the highest task performance during training and exhibited the highest real-world success rate of **58%** on an Allegro hand block rotation task at a 10Hz control frequency, outperforming baseline AAC and alternative curriculum methods.
- CSR configurations without DRG (replacing features with zeros) performed considerably worse, struggling to recover after feature reduction steps, underscoring DRG's importance for stable training and dependency removal.
- The proposed CSR + DRG method achieved a higher number of successful tasks within a 30-second period compared to both OpenAI's and NYU-RL's related in-hand manipulation benchmarks, demonstrating its efficiency and high performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradual, curriculum-based reduction of features from the actor's observation space significantly improves learning efficiency and final task performance in Sim2Real transfer compared to abrupt, one-step reductions.
> - Using a Deep Random Generator to replace reduced feature signals with random noise is critical for maintaining training stability and effectively decoupling policy dependencies, as directly replacing them with zeros leads to severe performance degradation.
> - Feature importance can be effectively determined through online metrics, such as tactile sensor activation numbers, allowing for a principled and adaptive sequence of feature removal in the curriculum design.
