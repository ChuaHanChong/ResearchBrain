# Self-Refining Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Vision-Conditioned UAV Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named AgenticRL automates the entire reinforcement learning pipeline for vision-conditioned UAV navigation by integrating multimodal understanding, iterative reward refinement, and autonomous policy selection. This approach achieved a collective 97% simulation success rate and 91% real-world success rate for UAVs performing tasks like obstacle avoidance and gate traversal.

## Problem

- Manually designing and fine-tuning effective reward functions for deep reinforcement learning in UAV navigation is resource-intensive and often yields suboptimal or unsafe behaviors.
- Reward functions are challenging to express precisely, especially for complex UAV behaviors naturally described through language and visual context.
- Prior LLM/VLM-based reward generation methods often lack robust multimodal scene grounding and practical real-world deployment on physical UAVs.

## Method

- **AgenticRL**, a closed-loop agent-guided ==reinforcement learning== framework, integrates multimodal understanding with iterative reward refinement for UAVs.
- A multimodal GPT agent generates and refines executable Python reward functions based on high-level language instructions, visual scene context, and diagnostic feedback from policy behavior in simulation.
- The framework includes a multimodal scenario registry and a lightweight GPT agent for autonomous scenario recognition and policy selection during real-world UAV deployment.

## Results

- The framework achieved a collective **97%** Simulation Success Rate (SSR) and **91%** Real World Success Rate (RSR), with a **94%** Sim-to-Real (S2R) accuracy across five diverse UAV navigation tasks.
- The iterative reward refinement process led to a collective Reward Refinement Improvement (RRI) of **71%**, significantly enhancing policy behavior and convergence.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of multimodal inputs and the diagnostic analyzer stage for achieving robust and task-aware reward functions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward functions can be autonomously refined through an iterative closed-loop process that incorporates language instructions, visual context, and direct behavioral feedback from trained policies.
> - Multimodal agents, particularly GPT models, can effectively translate high-level task specifications and visual scene understanding into executable reward functions.
> - Diagnosing policy failures using task-specific behavioral metrics in simulation is more effective for refining reward functions than relying solely on general episodic returns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in enabling autonomous robots, including Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), to acquire complex navigational skills. However, a significant practical challenge within this field is the reliance on human-designed reward functions and the necessity for extensive manual fine-tuning. This process is time-consuming, resource-intensive, and does not consistently guarantee high success rates across diverse tasks. Poorly constructed reward functions can lead to unstable learning, the emergence of unsafe behaviors, or the exploitation of the reward structure by the learning agent.

Existing research efforts have attempted to mitigate these challenges. Approaches based on demonstrations, human preferences, or human feedback require additional supervision or task-specific data. More recently, advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have opened avenues for automating aspects of reward and policy generation. Prior works have explored language-conditioned policy synthesis, reward generation from text prompts, and iterative LLM-based reward refinement. However, these methods often exhibit limitations in multimodal scene grounding and direct applicability to real-world UAV deployment. Many rely on abstract symbolic representations or explicit human feedback, rather than direct behavioral diagnosis within embodied learning contexts.

AgenticRL positions itself within this landscape by addressing these limitations. It seeks to integrate multimodal understanding (language and visual context) with a closed-loop, self-refining process for reward design and policy optimization, specifically tailored for UAV navigation. The framework aims to overcome the manual burden of reward engineering by treating it as an autonomous, iterative process informed by policy behavior, thereby enhancing the autonomy of RL systems for robotic applications. This approach differentiates AgenticRL from related work by combining autonomous reward generation, multimodal scene grounding, closed-loop refinement based on behavioral diagnosis, and validation on a physical UAV platform, addressing the full pipeline from reward design to real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind AgenticRL stems from the recognized limitations of deep reinforcement learning in practical robotic applications, particularly for UAV navigation. While RL offers potential for learning complex tasks, its effective implementation is often hindered by the challenges associated with reward function design. Manual reward engineering is identified as a bottleneck due to several factors:
- **Time Consumption:** Manually crafting and tuning reward functions for complex UAV tasks is an arduous and time-intensive process.
- **Difficulty in Expression:** Desired UAV behaviors, especially those involving nuanced spatial reasoning, safety, and stability, are often more naturally described through natural language and visual cues rather than precise numerical objectives. Translating these semantic descriptions into effective reward functions is non-trivial.
- **Suboptimal Outcomes:** Poorly designed rewards can lead to several undesirable outcomes, including unstable learning trajectories, the emergence of unsafe or unintended behaviors, and agents exploiting superficial aspects of the reward function rather than achieving the true task objective.
- **Scalability:** The need for repeated manual fine-tuning limits the scalability of RL to a broader range of complex and diverse UAV tasks.
- **Multimodal Gap:** Existing LLM/VLM approaches for reward generation often lack robust multimodal scene grounding, making them less suitable for embodied agents operating in visually rich, dynamic environments. They may also rely on demonstrations or human feedback, which introduce additional supervisory overhead.

Against this background, the key objectives of the AgenticRL framework are:
1. **Increase Autonomy in Reward Design:** To develop a system that can autonomously generate and refine reward functions from high-level multimodal task specifications (natural language instructions and visual scene context), thereby reducing human intervention.
2. **Establish a Closed-Loop Policy Refinement Process:** To formulate reward design as an iterative, closed-loop process where a multimodal GPT agent generates an initial reward, trains a policy, evaluates its behavior through diagnosis, and then refines the reward function based on identified failure modes and behavioral feedback.
3. **Integrate Multimodal Understanding for Real-World Deployment:** To leverage multimodal scene understanding during inference to automatically identify the active scenario in real-world environments and select the most appropriate pre-trained policy for execution, enhancing the practical applicability of learned policies.
4. **Demonstrate Sim-to-Real Transferability:** To validate the framework's ability to learn effective policies in simulation and successfully transfer them to a physical UAV platform across a variety of navigation tasks, confirming its robustness and real-world utility.

These objectives collectively aim to create a more autonomous, efficient, and robust reinforcement learning pipeline for UAVs, addressing critical gaps in current robotic RL methodologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AgenticRL employs a closed-loop framework designed to autonomously generate, evaluate, and refine reward functions for UAV navigation policies, followed by a multimodal scenario selection mechanism for real-world deployment.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as training reinforcement learning policies from high-level multimodal task specifications. Each task is defined by a natural language instruction ($l_0$), a visual scene context ($I$ - image/video), and an environment observation specification ($O$). The visual context provides spatial information, while $O$ defines the state variables available to the policy and reward function. The task is modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a continuous action space. The primary challenge is that the reward function ($R$) is not explicitly provided and must be inferred. An initial reward function ($R_0$) is generated by a multimodal GPT agent ($G_\phi(l_0, I, O)$) and then used to optimize a policy objective.

#### 3.2. AgenticRL Framework: Offline Closed-Loop Refinement

The core of AgenticRL is a five-stage iterative process (Algorithm 1) performed offline:

1. **Multimodal Task Understanding:** The framework receives $l_0$, $I$, and $O$. The multimodal GPT agent interprets the task objective, identifying relevant scene entities (e.g., gates, obstacles, landing pads). The observation specification grounds the reward design by defining accessible environment variables.

2. **Reward Generation:** Conditioned on the multimodal task specification, the GPT agent generates an initial executable Python reward function ($R_0$). This function is typically composed of terms for task progress, safety penalties, and terminal completion.

3. **Policy Training:** The generated reward function ($R_k$) is integrated into a customized UAV simulation environment. A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is used to train the policy ($\pi_k$). The policy network consists of fully connected layers (512→512→256→128) with tanh activations. Training typically ranges from 25M to 70M simulation steps. To facilitate exploration, the entropy coefficient is annealed from 0.1 to 0.001, and a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-5}$ is maintained for stability.

4. **Policy Diagnosis:** The trained policy is evaluated across randomized simulation episodes. Instead of relying solely on episodic return, AgenticRL records task-specific behavioral metrics. These metrics include collision events, landing accuracy, gate traversal success, trajectory tracking error, obstacle clearance, and target reaching performance. These are aggregated into a "diagnosis packet" ($D_k$) that summarizes the policy's behavior.

5. **Reward Refinement:** The diagnosis packet ($D_k$) is analyzed to identify failure modes. The GPT agent converts $D_k$ into a refinement prompt ($p_{ref_k}$), which summarizes observed failures and suggests reward modifications. This prompt is combined with the previous prompt context ($l_k$) to generate an updated task prompt ($l_{k+1}$). Subsequently, the refined reward function ($R_{k+1}$) is generated by the GPT agent using $R_k$, $l_{k+1}$, $I$, and $O$. This refined reward then replaces $R_k$ for the next iteration of policy training, forming a closed-loop self-improvement pipeline. The process continues until a task-specific success threshold is met or maximum iterations are reached.

#### 3.3. Real-World Deployment: Multimodal Scenario Selection Agent

For real-world deployment, the framework incorporates a multimodal scenario registry mechanism (Figure 7).
- A lightweight multimodal GPT agent (GPT-4o-mini) receives real-world images/video from the UAV's camera and a natural language task prompt.
- This agent identifies the active navigation scenario by matching it against a stored registry of available scenarios.
- Upon scenario identification, the corresponding pre-trained and refined policy (along with its observation schema) is retrieved from the registry.
- ROS2 subscribers collect real-time drone and environment states to construct the observation vector.
- This observation vector is fed to the selected PPO policy for action prediction (continuous UAV velocity control commands).
- The predicted commands are published via MAVROS for real-time flight execution.
- A task completion module monitors mission success conditions.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **UAV Task Scenarios:** Five distinct tasks were used for evaluation: trajectory following, passing through a moving gate, multiple obstacle avoidance and landing, barrier crossing and landing, and circular motion generation. These tasks cover various aspects of UAV control, including spatial reasoning, interaction with dynamic elements, and precise target reaching.
- **Multimodal Agents:** GPT-5.5 was used as the multimodal agent for reward generation and refinement (offline), while a more lightweight GPT-4o-mini was employed for real-time scenario selection during inference.
- **Hardware:** Real-world experiments were conducted on a physical quadrotor equipped with ArduPilot, ROS2, an Orange Pi 8GB onboard computer, and an Intel RealSense camera. A Vicon motion capture system provided ground truth pose measurements for evaluation.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** The performance was assessed using Simulation Success Rate (SSR), Real World Success Rate (RSR), Sim-to-Real (S2R) accuracy, and Reward Refinement Improvement (RRI). These metrics are defined to quantify task completion rates and the effectiveness of the refinement process. A trial is considered successful if the task is completed without collision and within scenario-specific constraints.
- **Ablation Study:** Ablation studies were conducted to evaluate the contributions of different components, including zero-shot reward generation, few-shot reward generation, removal of the analyzer stage, and removal of vision conditioning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of AgenticRL encompassed simulation-to-real validation, real-world implementation analysis, qualitative assessment of reward refinement, and an ablation study.

#### 4.1. Simulation-to-Real Validation

The framework demonstrated robust performance in simulation, achieving a collective Simulation Success Rate (SSR) of 97% across all evaluated tasks. Critically, the policies trained and refined within AgenticRL exhibited effective transferability to a physical UAV. The collective Real World Success Rate (RSR) was 91%, with a collective Sim-to-Real (S2R) accuracy of 94%.

Specific task performances include:
- **Gate Traversal** and **Circular Motion Generation** showed high transfer performance, achieving near-perfect real-world execution.
- **Trajectory Following** exhibited reliable transfer with an S2R of 97%.
- **Obstacle Avoidance and Landing** and **Barrier Crossing and Landing**, identified as more spatially constrained and safety-critical tasks, still achieved strong real-world success rates of 82% and 89%, respectively.
These results indicate that the closed-loop reward refinement process generates policies that are effective in simulation and suitable for real-world deployment across diverse scenarios.

#### 4.2. Real-World Implementation

Real-world experiments provided qualitative evidence of the learned policies' behavior. Top-view trajectories (Figure 4) illustrated that the UAV followed task-specific paths, such as passing through a gate or generating a circular motion pattern, with smooth and continuous flight behavior.
Three-dimensional trajectories (Figure 5) further demonstrated the policies' ability to execute complex behaviors:
- In the **multiple obstacle avoidance task**, the UAV successfully reached the landing region while maintaining clearance from cylindrical obstacles.
- For **trajectory following**, the policy tracked a reference path in 3D space with minimal deviations.
- In the **barrier crossing and landing task**, the UAV navigated over a wall and descended accurately to the designated landing pad.
These observations support the quantitative success rates, indicating that AgenticRL produces meaningful and transferable flight behaviors for physical UAVs.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis of Reward Refinement

The closed-loop reward refinement process demonstrated a collective Reward Refinement Improvement (RRI) of 71% across the evaluated scenarios. Comparisons between training using initial and final refined rewards (Figure 6) indicated that improved rewards generally led to clearer convergence behavior and enhanced learning stability.
- In tasks such as **gate traversal**, **obstacles with landing**, and **wall barrier landing**, episode lengths showed improved convergence and stabilization, suggesting more consistent task completion.
- Normalized reward curves often displayed improved or recovered stability after initial drops, particularly for the obstacle and wall barrier tasks.
- For **trajectory following**, the refined policy sometimes exhibited more variation in normalized reward, which might indicate a change in tracking behavior rather than a simple increase in reward magnitude.
- **Motion generation** showed a fixed episode length at maximum horizon, consistent with its design objective.
Appendix F provides reward colormaps, qualitatively illustrating how the reward landscape is reshaped from initial to refined states, concentrating high rewards in target areas and increasing penalties around obstacles.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation study (Table 2) assessed the contribution of different components to the framework's overall performance.
- **Zero-shot** and **few-shot reward generation** yielded lower success rates compared to the full AgenticRL framework, though few-shot showed some improvement over zero-shot. This suggests that while initial performance can be reasonable in some tasks, these variants struggle with incomplete reward structures, especially in complex scenarios.
- Removing the **analyzer stage** ("w/o analyzer"), which prevents conversion of diagnosis packets into refined prompts, significantly reduced performance across tasks. This indicates the importance of behavioral diagnosis and explicit feedback in correcting reward functions.
- Removing **vision conditioning** ("w/o vision conditioning") also resulted in reduced performance, particularly for tasks like barrier crossing and landing. This finding underscores the necessity of visual scene context for generating task-aware rewards in visually dependent UAV navigation tasks.
The full AgenticRL framework consistently outperformed all ablated variants, confirming the synergistic contribution of its multimodal inputs, behavioral diagnosis, and closed-loop refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AgenticRL represents an advancement in the field of reinforcement learning for autonomous robotics, particularly for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

**Increased Autonomy in Reward Design:** The primary contribution of AgenticRL is its ability to autonomously generate and iteratively refine reward functions from high-level multimodal specifications. This reduces the heavy reliance on manual reward engineering, a known bottleneck in practical RL applications. By automating this critical step, the framework can streamline the development and deployment of RL agents for complex robotic tasks.

**Enhanced Policy Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated 71% improvement in policy behavior through closed-loop reward refinement indicates that the iterative process effectively addresses failure modes and leads to more robust and capable policies. This self-improvement mechanism is crucial for developing agents that can handle diverse and challenging scenarios without constant human oversight.

**Demonstrated Sim-to-Real Transferability:** Achieving a 91% real-world success rate and 94% sim-to-real accuracy for UAV navigation tasks is a substantial finding. It validates the framework's ability to produce policies that not only perform well in simulation but also reliably transfer to physical hardware, bridging a critical gap between simulated learning and real-world deployment. This is particularly important for safety-critical applications like UAVs.

**Practical Deployment through Multimodal Scenario Selection:** The integration of a multimodal scenario registry and selection agent for real-time inference enhances the practical utility of the framework. This mechanism allows the UAV to autonomously identify its operational context and select the appropriate pre-trained policy, enabling adaptable and context-aware autonomous navigation in dynamic real-world environments.

**Addressing Multimodal Grounding in Embodied AI:** By effectively utilizing both natural language instructions and visual scene context, AgenticRL contributes to better grounding of LLMs and VLMs in embodied robotic tasks. This moves beyond text-only or abstract reasoning environments, demonstrating how these advanced models can directly influence physical robot behavior based on environmental perception.

**Foundation for Fully Autonomous RL Pipelines:** The work lays groundwork for more comprehensive autonomous RL systems. The paper suggests future directions, including extending agentic control to environment configuration, observation design, safety constraints, and termination conditions. This could lead to genuinely end-to-end autonomous learning systems that require minimal human intervention, accelerating the adoption of AI in robotics.

**Potential for Complex and Scalable Systems:** The architecture's emphasis on agent-guided refinement and potential for multi-agent collaboration (as mentioned in future work) suggests a path toward tackling increasingly complex robotic tasks and large-scale autonomous systems where manual tuning would be intractable.

In summary, AgenticRL represents a significant step towards creating more autonomous, capable, and practical reinforcement learning systems for UAVs and potentially other robotic platforms, by intelligently leveraging multimodal AI to address fundamental challenges in reward design and policy deployment.
