# SuperMap: A Spatio-Temporal SLAM System for Visual-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> SuperMap is a real-time 4D spatio-temporal SLAM system that fuses LiDAR-visual-inertial odometry with asynchronous open-vocabulary detection, using 3D-aware instance tracking and Bayesian existence/label confidence updates to keep object identities stable under occlusion and scene change, then exposes results as a queryable scene graph for VLM-driven navigation. Outperforms prior object-level baselines on ScanNet instance segmentation.

## Problem

- Existing semantic SLAM systems are closed-vocabulary and class-level, limiting instance-level identity tracking needed for language-grounded navigation commands.
- Dynamic-SLAM methods handle short-term motion like moving people but overlook long-term changes such as objects being relocated outside the robot's view.
- Open-vocabulary scene graph methods take minutes to hours to construct offline, are unsuitable for real-time mapping, and fail to model dynamic object relationships.

## Method

- ==3D-aware hybrid tracking state== that augments ByteTrack with 3D-to-2D motion compensation, projecting each object's 3D centroid via the current pose to predict occlusion-robust 2D bounding boxes.
- **Probabilistic geometric consistency update** using log-odds depth-residual thresholding to classify map points as observable, unobservable, or disappeared, pruning stale content.
- ==Bayesian semantic fusion== maintaining a categorical label distribution per instance updated via detector confusion matrices, plus a spatio-temporal scene graph with spatial and temporal edges for VLM querying.

## Results

- On ScanNet class-level segmentation, **SuperMap** reaches **55.48%** accuracy, beating ConceptGraphs and ConceptFusion, only **1.32%** below the point-feature method RayFronts.
- Instance-level segmentation on ScanNet: SuperMap scores **63.76** mAP50 on chairs and **42.20** on windows versus near-zero mAP50 for ConceptGraphs and HOV-SG across most categories.
- Ablation on 41 annotated objects shows full system F1 of **0.6308** versus **0.5780** without 2D tracker, **0.5201** without semantic fusion, **0.5764** without geometric consistency update.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Per-frame segmentation metrics like mIoU miss the long-horizon identity-consistency requirement that matters most for real-world dynamic-scene autonomy.
> - Anchoring semantic tracking to metric 3D geometry rather than 2D-only appearance cues resolves identity fragmentation under occlusion and rapid viewpoint change.
> - Structured scene-graph serialization gives VLMs more reliable spatial and temporal grounding than raw video input, cutting hallucination as temporal depth grows.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic navigation in human environments increasingly requires systems capable of understanding and interacting with their surroundings using natural language. This necessitates a robust semantic representation of the environment that is both spatio-temporal and open-vocabulary. Existing approaches in robotics, particularly in Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) and semantic mapping, face several limitations when confronted with these requirements.

Traditional semantic SLAM systems often rely on closed-vocabulary classification, which restricts their ability to recognize novel objects or generalize to diverse environments. While recent advancements in foundation models, such as Vision-Language Models (VLMs), offer strong zero-shot recognition capabilities, their per-frame predictions can be intermittent and view-dependent. Naively integrating these models into mapping pipelines can lead to issues like identity drift, where the same object is inconsistently labeled or tracked, and stale semantics, where the map content does not accurately reflect real-world changes over time.

Furthermore, many semantic mapping methods are offline, requiring pre-collected data and extensive processing time, rendering them unsuitable for real-time robotic deployment. Dynamic SLAM methods exist, but they frequently focus on short-term motions, such as moving people, and often neglect long-term environmental changes like object relocation, appearance, or disappearance from the scene. Scene graph approaches, which organize semantic information into a structured representation suitable for language grounding, have also emerged. However, these are often computationally intensive, taking minutes to hours to construct, or lack explicit spatio-temporal capabilities to model scene evolution over extended periods.

In summary, a notable gap persists between the output of current semantic mapping systems and a robot-usable map representation that can be queried and acted upon online in dynamic, open-vocabulary settings. The paper identifies the challenge of simultaneously addressing both dynamic change detection (how objects move or change over short terms) and static object recognition (identifying objects and their relationships, including long-term changes). SuperMap aims to bridge this gap by providing a real-time, spatio-temporal, open-vocabulary, and instance-level object mapping framework that is capable of handling both short-term and long-term dynamic objects and scene changes. The work contributes to the advancement of robust, long-term robotic autonomy in complex, human-centric environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to robustly execute open-vocabulary navigation goals in human environments, such as "go to the monitor next to the whiteboard" or "return to the chair that was near the plant earlier." To achieve this, the authors identify several key challenges and corresponding motivations for developing SuperMap:

1. **Requirement for Spatio-Temporal Semantic Representation:** Robots need more than just accurate pose estimation and geometric reconstruction. They require an object-centric, spatio-temporal map that preserves instance identities, semantic attributes, and spatial relationships over time. This representation must remain valid even as the environment evolves, which is a significant challenge for existing systems.
2. **Addressing Environmental Dynamics:** Real-world environments are inherently dynamic. Objects can be occluded, relocated, removed, or newly introduced during a robot's operation. Current systems often struggle with these changes, leading to fragmented instance identities, stale map content, and inconsistent relational structures, which degrade performance over long operational horizons.
3. **Integrating Open-Vocabulary Perception:** While foundation models provide powerful zero-shot recognition, their outputs are intermittent and view-dependent. Simply integrating these raw predictions into mapping pipelines can lead to identity drift and outdated semantics. The motivation is to develop a method to reconcile these intermittent predictions with a stable, consistent map.
4. **Enabling Real-Time Operation:** Many advanced semantic mapping or scene graph generation techniques are computationally intensive, operating offline and thus unsuitable for real-time robotic tasks. A critical motivation is to develop an online system that can build and maintain a rich semantic map in real-time on onboard hardware.
5. **Facilitating Language Grounding and Reasoning:** For language-guided navigation, the map needs an efficient and queryable interface that supports compositional queries over object semantics, relations, and historical states. Existing representations often lack this capability, creating a gap between "semantic mapping outputs" and a truly robot-usable map.
6. **Simultaneous Handling of Short-Term and Long-Term Dynamics:** Current approaches predominantly address either short-term dynamic change detection (e.g., moving people) or static object recognition. Few methods effectively tackle both challenges simultaneously—understanding which objects change and how they evolve spatially and temporally. SuperMap aims to address this dual challenge.
7. **Providing an Open-Source Baseline:** The authors are motivated to release the full system as open-source, including a change-detection benchmark, comprehensive ablations, and runtime profiling. This aims to provide the community with a deployable baseline for open-vocabulary spatio-temporal mapping, fostering reproducible research.

In essence, the motivation is to create a comprehensive, online system that constructs a "4D scene memory" capable of dealing with the complexities of dynamic, semantically rich environments, thereby enabling more intelligent and robust robot navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SuperMap is designed as a real-time, spatio-temporal SLAM system that constructs and maintains an instance-level 4D semantic map. It integrates high-frequency geometric SLAM with asynchronous open-vocabulary perception. The system architecture comprises three main components: Online 3D Reconstruction, Spatio-Temporal Object Updates, and Spatio-Temporal Scene Graph Construction.

**Spatio-Temporal SLAM Problem Formulation:**
The system models the scene as a composition of objects, represented as a scene graph. It processes a continuous stream of RGB-D video sequences or point-cloud video sequences, where at each time *t*, the system estimates the camera pose *P_t*, determines instance IDs *I_t*, and updates the global map *M_t*. This process involves maximizing a posterior probability distribution, which is decomposed into three sub-tasks: pose estimation, spatio-temporal instance association, and online map update.

**A. Geometric Layer: Online 3D Reconstruction**
The foundation of SuperMap is its geometric layer, which provides accurate state estimation and dense 3D reconstruction.
- **Pose Estimation:** SuperMap utilizes SuperOdometry, an IMU-centric LiDAR-visual-inertial estimator, to obtain the robot's pose *P_t* (in SE(3)) and a colorized dense 3D reconstruction from synchronized image, depth/LiDAR, and IMU streams.
- **Metric Foundation:** This geometric prior is crucial for back-projecting 2D semantic observations into the 3D world, performing motion compensation for temporal tracking, and maintaining global map consistency. It ensures that the resulting spatio-temporal map is physically grounded for downstream navigation tasks.

**B. Instance Layer: Spatio-Temporal Instance Association**
This layer is responsible for identifying and tracking individual objects with consistent identities over time, even under occlusions and scene changes.
- **2D Instance Detection:** For each image frame, 2D instance-level detections and segmentations are first inferred using vision models, specifically GroundingDINO for language-grounded detection and SAM2 for robust instance segmentation.
- **Hybrid Tracking State:** The system defines a tracklet state *S_i(t)* for each object, comprising its 2D image centroid, bounding box width and height, and translational velocity in the image plane.
- **3D-to-2D Motion-Compensated State:** To address challenges with rapid ego-motion that typically affect 2D trackers, SuperMap refines the tracklet state. Instead of a linear motion model, the predicted centroid of an instance is derived by projecting its 3D centroid *X_i* (from the existing map *M_t-1*) using the current camera pose *P_t*. This projection acts as a prior for the Kalman Filter state transition, enabling the system to maintain object identity through occlusions and aggressive robot maneuvers.
- **Probabilistic Geometric Consistency Update:** The map *M_t* is updated by evaluating the posterior probability based on new observations.
 - **Depth Residual:** For each point *X_k* in the map, a depth residual (∆d) is calculated between its expected projected depth and the raw sensor depth at the corresponding pixel.
 - **Log-Odds Update:** The occupancy state of each map point is updated recursively using a log-odds formulation.
 - **Occupancy Classification:** Points are classified as Observable, Unobservable (behind surface), or Disappeared (in front of surface) based on thresholds applied to ∆d. Points classified as "dynamic" (e.g., in front of surface, indicating a new object or movement) are penalized in the log-odds update, effectively pruning transient or moved objects and enabling change detection.
- **Bayesian Semantic Fusion:** To handle uncertainty in vision model outputs, each instance maintains a categorical distribution of its semantic label. This belief is updated using a Bayesian fusion approach, incorporating observations from the open-vocabulary detector and its confusion matrix. Object points with a posterior belief below a certain threshold are removed, suppressing transient misclassifications and refining semantic labels.

**C. Topological Layer: Spatio-Temporal Scene Graph Construction**
This layer abstracts the detailed instance-level map into a queryable graph representation.
- **Graph Structure:** The map *M_t* is organized into a graph *G = (V, E_s, E_t)*, where nodes *V* represent object instances.
- **Spatial Edges (E_s):** These edges capture instantaneous spatial relationships between objects. They are established via class-dependent geometric predicates (e.g., "On(A, B)" if object A is approximately on top of object B, determined by z-axis overlap and XY-plane Intersection over Union). Objects are clustered based on centroid distances before edge addition.
- **Temporal Edges (E_t):** These edges link nodes across different time steps, tracing the trajectory and preserving the identity of an object throughout the sequence, based on the results of the instance association.
- **Abstract Representation:** This graph compactly captures both the instantaneous scene layout and long-term dynamics, providing an abstract representation suitable for reasoning.

**D. Visual Language Navigation via 4D Scene Graphs**
The 4D scene graph serves as a high-level interface for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), enabling language-guided navigation.
- **Spatio-Temporal Serialization:** A local subgraph is serialized into a structured text representation. Each node is encoded with its instance ID, semantic label, and 3D centroid. Spatial and temporal edges are represented as symbolic relations (e.g., "Instance 1 on Instance 2"). A schema and explicit spatio-temporal cues are prepended to encourage trajectory- and relation-aware reasoning.
- **Grounded Actuation and Waypoint Generation:** For navigation, the VLM returns target instance IDs within `<answer>` tags. A parser extracts these IDs, retrieves their corresponding 3D centroids from the map, and uses them as navigation waypoints. This mechanism translates high-level language instructions into precise, grounded robot actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SuperMap addressed research questions concerning semantic quality, spatio-temporal consistency, grounded reasoning, and online visual-language navigation, using both public benchmarks and real-robot deployments.

**A. Semantic Quality: Quantitative Results for Class-level and Instance-level Segmentation**
- **Class-level Segmentation (ScanNet Benchmark):** SuperMap achieved a competitive accuracy of 55.48% (f-mIoU without background) on the ScanNet benchmark. This performance outperformed established object-centric baselines such as ConceptGraphs and ConceptFusion. While slightly lower than the point-feature fusion method RayFronts (by 1.32%), SuperMap maintained a lower computational footprint, supporting its suitability for real-time robotic feedback loops.
- **Instance-level Segmentation (ScanNet Benchmark):** SuperMap demonstrated notable improvements in instance-level segmentation. Compared to HOV-SG and ConceptGraphs, SuperMap significantly outperformed these baselines across several categories (e.g., Chair, Window, Refrigerator, Sofa, Door), with mAP@50 scores for "Chair" at 63.76% and "Refrigerator" at 62.50%. This was attributed to the efficacy of its 3D-aware tracking-by-detection approach over methods reliant on point-feature clustering.

**B. Spatio-Temporal Consistency on Qualitative Evaluation**
A 10-minute real-world experiment in a dynamic indoor environment (30m x 20m) demonstrated SuperMap's ability to maintain map integrity over extended durations and identify environmental changes.
- The system successfully handled the disappearance of three objects (a plant, a trash can, and a chair) and the appearance of three new objects (a bucket, a cart, and a safety sign).
- Qualitative results (Fig. 3) showed that for disappearing objects, their instance IDs remained consistent over time, indicating persistent identity despite removal from observation. For appearing objects, new IDs were assigned while nearby existing objects retained their original identities, demonstrating stable re-identification and instance association.

**C. Spatial-Temporal Consistency on Quantitative Evaluation**
Quantitative assessment using manually annotated 3D bounding boxes and semantic labels for six target objects (Table IV) further supported SuperMap's performance.
- **Object Detection Recall:** SuperMap maintained high recall rates for detecting objects during their appearance interval (e.g., 1.000 for "bucket", 1.000 for "chair"), demonstrating robust instance detection. In contrast, DualMap showed near-zero recall, and Khronos struggled to produce consistent 3D estimation.
- **Change Detection Recall:** SuperMap also achieved high recall in detecting object removal during disappearance intervals (e.g., 1.000 for "bucket", 1.000 for "chair"). This indicated a robust ability to confirm object removal, unlike baselines that either failed to detect objects initially or produced artifactual "successes" during disappearance.

**D. SuperMap System Ablation Study**
An ablation study isolating the contributions of the 2D tracking, geometric consistency update, and Bayesian semantic fusion modules (Table V) showed that the full system outperformed all baseline configurations.
- The complete SuperMap achieved the highest Precision (0.8677), Recall (0.4955), and F1 score (0.6308).
- Removing the 2D tracker, semantic fusion, or geometric consistency update individually led to a reduction in performance, highlighting their respective roles in maintaining consistent identity association, enhancing resilience against momentary detection failures, and filtering out detection noise.

**E. Grounding Reasoning**
Evaluation of the 4D scene graph as a grounding engine for Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using Gemini 2.0 Flash (Fig. 4) revealed:
- **Superior Accuracy:** The structured Scene Graph Input yielded superior accuracy for complex queries compared to raw Video Input.
- **Spatial Logic:** For prompts involving spatial relationships (e.g., disambiguating an "extinguisher" relative to a "cone" and "plant"), the scene graph enabled precise distance calculation and instance retrieval. Raw video input suffered from perspective distortion and object confusion.
- **Temporal Logic:** For queries requiring tracing object trajectories (e.g., finding a "bag's" past location), the graph's temporal edges facilitated filtered, timestamped retrieval of dynamic states. Video-based reasoning relied on less reliable common-sense heuristics and exhibited hallucinations with increasing temporal depth.

**F. Visual Language Navigation**
SuperMap supported end-to-end, zero-shot, language-guided navigation in previously unseen environments (Fig. 5).
- The robot successfully interpreted natural language commands involving spatial relationships (e.g., "Go to the whiteboard next to the painting") using the real-time 4D scene graph.
- Demonstrations included selecting the correct target among visually identical objects based on spatial ordering and identifying relational cues for destination retrieval.

**G. Runtime and Memory**
- **Module Throughput:** Pose estimation maintained 10 Hz, 2D instance segmentation operated at 1 Hz, 3D mapping and bounding box updates at 3 Hz, and 4D scene graph updates at 5 Hz.
- **Resource Usage (Supplementary Table I):** SuperMap's average memory usage was 2030.09 MB (peak 2184.86 MB), with a mapping time per frame (TPF) of 0.3604 s. This indicated moderate resource consumption, suitable for onboard robotic platforms, while providing capabilities beyond lightweight per-frame methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SuperMap represents a contribution to the field of robotic perception and navigation by introducing a real-time, open-vocabulary, spatio-temporal SLAM system capable of building and maintaining an instance-level 4D semantic map. Its significance and potential impact stem from addressing several fundamental challenges in enabling intelligent robots to operate effectively in complex human environments:

1. **Bridging the Perception-Reasoning Gap:** By creating a robust 4D scene graph, SuperMap provides a structured, high-level interface between raw sensor data and symbolic inputs required by Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This abstract representation facilitates complex spatio-temporal reasoning, moving beyond per-frame object detection to understanding scene dynamics and object histories.
2. **Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** The system's ability to track objects, identify appearances and disappearances, and update map semantics consistently over long durations, even under occlusions and environmental changes, is critical for real-world robotic deployment. This enhances the robot's adaptability and operational reliability in dynamic settings where objects are moved or introduced.
3. **Enabling Advanced Language-Guided Navigation:** The structured 4D scene graph significantly improves the accuracy and efficiency of VLM grounding. This allows robots to interpret and execute complex natural language commands involving spatial relations, object histories, and compositional queries, which is a key step towards intuitive human-robot interaction and more autonomous operation.
4. **Real-Time and Onboard Operation:** SuperMap is designed for real-time operation on standard robotic computing platforms. This practicality makes it a deployable solution for various applications, distinguishing it from many offline semantic mapping or scene graph generation methods.
5. **Instance-Level and Open-Vocabulary Capabilities:** The system supports instance-level tracking and open-vocabulary object recognition, leveraging modern foundation models. This allows robots to understand a diverse range of objects without prior training for specific classes, expanding their utility in novel and unstructured environments.
6. **Foundation for Long-Term Autonomy:** By providing a continuous, updated world model with long-term memory, SuperMap lays groundwork for persistent autonomy, where robots can learn from and adapt to their environment over extended periods, rather than re-perceiving the world anew in each instance.
7. **Community Resource:** The commitment to release SuperMap as an open-source framework, including a change-detection benchmark and real-robot navigation pipeline, provides a valuable resource for the robotics research community. This can serve as a deployable baseline, encouraging further research and development in open-vocabulary spatio-temporal mapping.

In summary, SuperMap's contributions are poised to advance the capabilities of mobile robots, enabling them to navigate, understand, and interact with complex, dynamic human environments with a richer, more robust, and more intelligent understanding of the world.
