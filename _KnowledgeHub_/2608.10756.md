# Embodied Multimodal Grounding for Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation via Semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> An embodied mobile-manipulation framework builds a task-driven local Semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting field from four actively selected wrist-camera views, uses it for open-vocabulary language localization, obstacle reasoning, and reachability-aware quadruped base stance, then injects the 3D semantics only into the final five diffusion action-expert blocks. Real-robot trials reach 60% long-horizon and 74% cluttered success, beating PointVLA and DexVLA.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action policies leaning on 2D appearance cues ground targets brittlely under partial occlusion, heavy clutter, and photo-realistic screen distractors mimicking the real object.
- Correct target localization still fails in mobile manipulation when the base stance is poorly placed for the arm workspace, especially across long-horizon tasks and changing target heights.
- Prior 3D grounding work favors dense full-scene optimization or persistent global maps, and treats perception output as isolated rather than as a shared interface driving action preparation.

## Method

- **Active multi-view Semantic-3DGS**: a ==view-scoring objective== trading semantic coverage, viewpoint complementarity, and wrist motion picks four ==IK-feasible poses==; ==VGGT== initializes geometry, ==CLIP==/==DINOv2== features distilled by ==mask-aware cosine alignment==.
- **Reachability-aware base posture control**: ==CLIP-scored Gaussian support== gives a weighted 6D target pose, defining an offset pre-manipulation stance and stand/crouch height mode executed by a ==PPO leg-residual policy== trained in ==Isaac Lab==.
- **Late-Block Semantic Injection**: target heatmap, occupancy cue, PCA semantic rendering, and target-relative pose are encoded and added through ==zero-initialized MLP adapters== into only the final five frozen ==diffusion blocks==.

## Results

- Long-horizon drawer-to-chair task over **50** trials: **60%** success versus **40%** PointVLA and **28%** DexVLA; ablating the Base-RL stance module collapses success to **22%**.
- Cluttered banana-to-bowl over **50** trials: **74%** success versus **52%** single-view and **46%** PointVLA, collision-free rising **70%** to **88%** and false grasps falling **18%** to **6%**, costing **3.7s** extra.
- Late-block injection beats all-block injection at **82%** versus **75%** average success with **80ms** versus **175ms** chunk latency; height shifts to **75cm** retain **75%**, and photo false-grasp rate is **0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning only late action-expert blocks preserves pretrained visuomotor priors while adding grounding, so where you inject a new modality matters more than how much.
> - Explicit 3D support geometry, not appearance similarity, is what rejects flat-screen photo distractors, so depth-bearing evidence should gate any grasp decision.
> - Accurate grounding alone is insufficient for mobile embodiments: without reachability-aware stance preparation, better perception can actually score worse than weaker end-to-end baselines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is progressively moving towards developing autonomous agents capable of performing complex tasks in unstructured, human-centric environments. Embodied mobile manipulation represents a significant challenge within this domain, requiring the seamless integration of locomotion, perception, and contact-rich actions. Recent advancements in multimodal models and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies have shown promise in enabling language-guided perception and action for robots. However, deploying these systems robustly in real-world scenarios, particularly in household environments, continues to pose challenges.

Existing VLA systems frequently rely on two-dimensional appearance cues for target grounding. This dependency can render them brittle when confronted with common real-world complexities such as partial occlusion, heavy clutter, or deceptive appearance-only distractors (e.g., a photo of an object on a screen). Furthermore, even when a target is correctly localized, the execution of manipulation tasks can fail if the mobile base is not appropriately positioned relative to the arm's workspace. This is especially critical in long-horizon tasks or when objects are situated at varying heights, necessitating dynamic adjustment of the robot's stance.

This work addresses these identified limitations by proposing an embodied multimodal grounding framework. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level language instructions, visual observations, and practical robot actions by incorporating explicit three-dimensional scene structure and a reachability-aware approach to base positioning. The paper positions itself within the current landscape by leveraging state-of-the-art techniques like diffusion-based VLA policies and 3D Gaussian Splatting, while introducing a novel way to integrate and utilize these components. Specifically, it contrasts with purely 2D grounding methods by advocating for explicit 3D representations that offer more stable cross-view localization and better support reasoning about target pose and surrounding obstacles. It also extends beyond static-arm manipulation by focusing on the unique embodiment constraints of mobile platforms. The proposed method’s contribution is in synthesizing these elements into a cohesive system that improves robustness under a range of challenging conditions prevalent in real-world mobile manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust embodied multimodal grounding framework designed for open-vocabulary mobile manipulation with few-shot learning in local household workspaces. This framework aims to enhance the reliability and effectiveness of robotic systems in complex, real-world environments.

The motivation for this work stems from two key practical failure modes observed in current mobile manipulation systems:

1. **Brittleness of 2D Appearance-Based Target Grounding:** Many existing VLA systems heavily depend on 2D visual cues for identifying and localizing target objects. This reliance makes them susceptible to failures in scenarios involving:
 - **Partial Occlusion:** When parts of the target object are hidden from view.
 - **Clutter:** In environments with numerous distracting objects.
 - **Appearance-Only Distractors:** Situations where non-target items, such as a tablet displaying a photo-realistic image of the target, can be mistakenly identified as the actual object, leading to false grasps. The research is motivated by the need for a more robust grounding mechanism that can disambiguate targets based on their 3D presence rather than superficial 2D features.

2. **Manipulation Failures Due to Inadequate Base Positioning:** Even with accurate target localization, manipulation can fail if the mobile base of the robot is not positioned optimally relative to the arm's workspace. This issue is particularly pronounced in:
 - **Long-Horizon Tasks:** Multi-step operations where the robot's initial stance might not be suitable for all subsequent actions.
 - **Varying Target Heights:** When objects are placed at different elevations, requiring the robot to adjust its body height or approach angle. The research is motivated by the need to integrate reachability-aware base positioning to ensure the robot's arm can physically access and manipulate the target successfully, considering its kinematic limitations and the task requirements.

To address these motivations, the proposed framework centers on a compact and refreshable Semantic-3D Gaussian Splatting (Semantic-3DGS) representation. This representation is intended to serve as a shared interface, facilitating cross-modal spatial alignment across various stages: active multi-view sensing, language-conditioned 3D localization, obstacle-aware scene reasoning, reachability-aware base preparation, and semantic conditioning of a diffusion-based VLA policy. By integrating these components, the work aims to introduce explicit 3D grounding and appropriate robot body configuration, thereby improving overall system robustness under complex real-world constraints. The scope of this work is focused on open-vocabulary target grounding with few-shot manipulation, specifically training an embodiment-specific manipulation policy with a limited number of real demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed embodied multimodal grounding framework integrates several key components to achieve robust open-vocabulary mobile manipulation. The system operates in four main stages after receiving a language instruction `l`: (1) active local multi-view observation, (2) Semantic-3DGS construction and open-vocabulary 3D localization, (3) reachability-aware base repositioning, and (4) Semantic-3DGS-conditioned VLA manipulation.

**Hardware Platform:**
The robot platform used is a Unitree Go2 Edu quadruped, which supports both standing and crouching modes. It is equipped with a Unitree 4D L1 LiDAR and an onboard Jetson Orin NX for local processing. For manipulation, it features an Alicia-D 6-DoF arm with an RGB camera mounted near the gripper, used for local perception. High-level Semantic-3DGS perception and VLA inference are performed off-board on an RTX 4090 workstation, while low-level robot control and base posture policy run onboard.

**Representation and Notation:**
The core scene representation is a local Semantic-3DGS, `G = {g_k}k=1 to K`. Each Gaussian `g_k` is defined by its geometry (mean `μ_k`, covariance `Σ_k`, color `c_k`, opacity `α_k`) and two semantic feature vectors: `f_C_k` (CLIP-aligned) and `f_D_k` (DINO-aligned).

**Active Multi-view Semantic-3DGS:**
This module constructs a compact, local 3D semantic representation of the scene around the target.
- **Active View Acquisition:** Starting from an initial wrist-camera image `I^(1)`, a target phrase `q(l)` is extracted. This is used to compute a language relevance map, which, combined with SAM masks and coarse VGGT geometry, provides a rough 3D target support. A set of candidate wrist-camera poses (`V_cand`) is generated and scored by a function `J(v)`. This scoring function considers expected semantic coverage of the target (`C_sem`), complementarity of viewpoints (`C_par`), and discouragement of unnecessary wrist motion (`C_move`). The robot's wrist is moved by a standard Inverse Kinematics (IK) controller to acquire four actively selected images, with the base remaining fixed during this sensing phase.
- **Geometry Initialization and Semantic Distillation:** From the collected multi-view images `I`, a pretrained VGGT model predicts camera parameters and dense geometry, which is used to initialize the local Gaussian field. For each view, CLIP and DINOv2 feature maps and SAM masks are extracted. Mask-aware average pooling refines the CLIP features. The rendered Gaussian features are then optimized using a cosine alignment loss (`L_feat`) to distill CLIP-aligned and DINO semantic features into the Gaussians.
- **Open-Vocabulary 3D Localization:** The target phrase is encoded into a CLIP text embedding `e+`. Each Gaussian `g_k` is assigned a language relevance score `s_k` based on the cosine similarity between its `f_C_k` feature and `e+`, as well as generic negative prompts. A threshold `δ` is applied to select a set of relevant Gaussians `K`. The object's 3D position `p_W_obj` is then estimated as a weighted average of the means of these selected Gaussians. A stable 6D pose `W_T_obj` is further estimated from this support using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), with optional ICP refinement if a template is available. This explicit 3D support is designed to reduce reliance on single appearance cues and expose both target and nearby obstacle geometry.

**Reachability-aware Base Posture Control:**
After localizing the object, the system repositions the mobile base for optimal manipulation.
- The object's position `p_W_obj` is transformed into the base frame, `p_B_obj = [x_obj, y_obj, z_obj]^⊤`.
- A pre-manipulation stance (`x*`, `y*`, `ψ*`, `h*`) is calculated relative to `p_B_obj` using predefined offsets (`d_x = 0.35m`, `d_y = 0.20m`) and `atan2` for orientation.
- The robot's height mode (`h_crouch` or `h_stand`) is selected based on the object's `z_obj` height.
- A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) policy, trained in Isaac Lab, is then used to output leg-joint residuals and control the stand/crouch switch to achieve the desired base posture.

**Semantic-3DGS-Conditioned VLA Manipulation Policy:**
This module executes the manipulation actions, leveraging the 3D semantic information.
- The policy is built upon a DexVLA-style architecture, using a Qwen2-VL backbone and a ScaleDP action expert.
- From the language instruction `l` and the constructed Semantic-3DGS `G`, several cues are aggregated: (1) a language-conditioned target heatmap `H_t`, (2) an obstacle-aware occupancy cue `O_t` (derived from `G`), (3) a three-channel PCA rendering `P_t` of the distilled per-Gaussian semantic field, and (4) a target-relative pose vector `r_t = [p_B_obj, h_t]`.
- The visual cues (RGB observation `I_t`, `H_t`, `O_t`, `P_t`) are concatenated and encoded by `E_img` to produce `z_img_t`. The pose cue `r_t` is encoded by `E_pose` to produce `z_pose_t`.
- Crucially, the sum `z_sem_t = z_img_t + z_pose_t` is injected *only* into the *final five* diffusion blocks (out of `L` total blocks) of the action expert. This "Late-Block Semantic Injection" is implemented via a zero-initialized projection layer and lightweight MLP adapters `A_l` for each of the selected blocks.
- The VLM backbone and all pretrained diffusion blocks are frozen. Only the semantic encoder, projection layers, late-block adapters, and an embodiment-specific action head are trained using 10 real demonstrations per task, aiming to preserve established visuomotor priors while adding 3D grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive real-robot evaluations across various challenging scenarios to assess the performance of the proposed embodied multimodal grounding framework. The evaluation protocol involved 30 real-robot trials for most settings, with expanded 50-trial studies for long-horizon and cluttered manipulation tasks. Success was defined by completing the instruction within a specified time budget without unsafe behavior. Baselines (DexVLA, PointVLA) were adapted to the same robot embodiment and utilized the same number of demonstrations for a fair comparison.

**Few-shot Multi-task Manipulation:**
On a benchmark of few-shot multi-task manipulation scenarios, the full proposed model achieved an average success rate of 81.7%. This compares with 64.0% for PointVLA and 37.7% for DexVLA. The performance advantage of the proposed system was particularly pronounced in more complex cluttered and multi-step tasks, suggesting the efficacy of explicit 3D semantic grounding beyond simple single-object manipulation.

**Long-horizon Manipulation:**
For a multi-stage long-horizon task involving opening a drawer, retrieving an object, closing the drawer, navigating, and placing the object, the full system completed 30 out of 50 trials (60% success). PointVLA achieved 20/50 (40%) and DexVLA achieved 14/50 (28%). An ablation study showed that removing the reachability-aware base repositioning (Ours w/o Base-RL) significantly reduced the success rate to 11/50 (22%), indicating that correct object grounding alone is insufficient without proper base stance preparation.

**Height Adaptation:**
The system's ability to adapt to varying target heights was evaluated with platform height offsets of 30 cm, 60 cm, and 75 cm. The full proposed system maintained a high success rate, ranging between 75% and 80% across these different heights. In contrast, the variant without the Base-RL module failed to achieve any success (0%) when the initial base stance was retained, highlighting the importance of adaptive base posture control for varying manipulation environments.

**Photo Deception:**
To test robustness against appearance-only distractors, a real banana was replaced with a photo-realistic image of a banana displayed on a tablet. The full proposed model demonstrated high robustness, achieving 88% success in manipulating the real object and 0% false grasps on the photo distractor. RGB-only DexVLA and the single-view ablation (Ours Single-View) exhibited high false-grasp rates of 76% and 70%, respectively. PointVLA also showed robustness to the photo distractor with 0% false grasps, but the full model improved real-object success, indicating more stable localization.

**Cluttered and Occluded Manipulation:**
In a heavily cluttered banana-to-bowl task, the active multi-view Semantic-3DGS approach demonstrated substantial improvements. The full system achieved a 74% success rate, which is higher than the 52% of the single-view variant and 46% of PointVLA. It also improved the collision-free rate from 70% (single-view) to 88% and reduced false grasps from 18% (single-view) to 6%. These results indicate that active multi-view sensing and the resulting explicit 3D semantic representation enhance robustness under occlusions and dense distractors.

**Component Ablations (Clutter Task):**
Further ablation studies on the cluttered task revealed the individual contributions of different components:
- A variant using a VGGT point-map instead of Semantic-3DGS achieved 58% success.
- Removing CLIP/DINO semantic features from the Semantic-3DGS yielded 60% success.
- Removing the obstacle-aware occupancy cue resulted in 65% success.
- Injecting semantic cues into all diffusion blocks (All-block semantic injection) achieved 68% success, which was lower than the 74% of the full system with late-block injection, and also resulted in higher latency. This indicates that late-block semantic injection is more effective in preserving pretrained action priors.

**Late-block Injection and Runtime:**
The study confirmed that injecting semantic cues only into the final five diffusion blocks ("late-block injection") provides a better balance between success rate (82%) and action chunk latency (80 ms) compared to injecting into all blocks (75% success, 175 ms latency). Early or broader semantic intervention was found to disrupt pretrained action priors more significantly.
Regarding runtime, the active multi-view sensing stage (four-view wrist motion + capture, VGGT initialization, Semantic-3DGS feature update, semantic rendering + localization) adds an initial latency of approximately 18.17 seconds. Despite this initial cost, the total wall-clock time for the full cluttered task (33.2 ± 3.6 s) was competitive, and the added sensing significantly improved robustness. VLA action-chunk inference itself had a low online latency of 0.08 s per chunk.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an embodied multimodal grounding framework that contributes to the field of mobile manipulation by addressing critical challenges in real-world deployment. The introduction of a refreshable Semantic-3D Gaussian Splatting (Semantic-3DGS) representation serving as a unified interface across perception, reasoning, and action is a notable technical advancement. This approach allows for active sensing, language-conditioned 3D localization, obstacle-aware scene reasoning, and reachability-aware base preparation to be integrated into a coherent system.

The "late-block semantic injection" mechanism is significant for preserving the efficacy of pre-trained vision-language-action (VLA) models while augmenting them with robust 3D semantic cues. By strategically injecting these cues only into the final stages of the diffusion-based action expert, the framework successfully leverages transferable visuomotor priors without degrading their established capabilities. This design choice offers a practical method for combining the strengths of large pre-trained models with task-specific 3D spatial awareness.

The experimental results demonstrate improved robustness across several challenging real-robot scenarios. The system exhibits enhanced performance in:
- **Cluttered and Occluded Environments:** By utilizing explicit 3D geometry and semantics, the robot can more accurately identify targets and navigate around obstacles, reducing collisions and false grasps.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** The integration of reachability-aware base positioning ensures that the robot maintains a feasible stance throughout multi-step operations, improving overall task completion rates.
- **Height Adaptability:** The system's ability to adjust its base posture based on target height allows for versatile manipulation across different elevations, a common requirement in household settings.
- **Distractor Rejection:** The use of 3D grounding enables the robot to differentiate between real objects and appearance-only distractors, such as photo-realistic images, preventing erroneous interactions.

The potential impact of this work is in advancing the practical applicability of mobile manipulation robots in unstructured environments, particularly domestic settings. By improving robustness under common real-world conditions like clutter, occlusion, and viewpoint variation, the framework contributes towards making autonomous robots more reliable and effective for a broader range of tasks. The open-vocabulary nature and few-shot learning capability further suggest a path towards adaptable and user-friendly robotic systems that can operate with minimal explicit programming. Future research directions, such as investigating lighter onboard representations, adaptive view planning, and broader unseen-object generalization, are important for transitioning these capabilities into even more dynamic and resource-constrained real-world applications.
