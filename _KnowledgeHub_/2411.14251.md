# Natural Language Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Natural Language Reinforcement Learning (NLRL), a framework that redefines core reinforcement learning components like value functions and policies in natural language using large language models. This approach enables agents to gain a deeper, interpretable understanding of their experiences, demonstrating superior performance and more stable learning on multi-step tasks like maze navigation and board games compared to traditional and LLM-based baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) relies on scalar value functions, which limit agents' deep, structural understanding of "why" certain actions are beneficial, only indicating "what" is good.
- RL agents primarily learn passively through extensive environmental sampling, struggling to proactively adjust strategies via deliberative reasoning.
- Applying traditional scalar reward optimization to Large Language Models (LLMs) can degrade their Chain-of-Thought reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces ==Natural Language Reinforcement Learning (NLRL)==, re-conceptualizing core RL components (value functions, policy, Bellman equation) directly in natural language.
- Defines a ==Language Value Function (LVF)== as an interpretable linguistic narrative explaining the rationale behind an evaluation, implemented by training Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Leverages LLMs as ==language policy==, ==language Monte Carlo/Temporal Difference operators== for value estimation, and policy improvement operators for deliberative learning, all through unsupervised environment interaction and fine-tuning.

## Results

- Language Temporal Difference (TD) estimates produced better performance in maze navigation compared to prompt-based language value functions.
- A trained Language Value Function (LVF) achieved over **0.85** evaluation accuracy in a 5x5 Breakthrough game, significantly outperforming prompting-based LLMs (**~0.61**) and demonstrating scalability.
- Natural Language Actor-Critic learning achieved a **~0.9** win rate in Tic-tac-toe and showed superior performance and stability over prior LLM agent frameworks (e.g., RAGEN) in FrozenLake, while maintaining rigorous Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing scalar value functions with linguistic narratives enables agents to develop deeper, interpretable understanding and provide transparent reasoning for their decisions.
> - By verbalizing core RL components, agents can engage in active, deliberative learning, analyzing "root causes of success or failure" rather than relying solely on passive sampling.
> - Large Language Models can serve as effective foundational components for a language-native reinforcement learning system, facilitating both policy execution and value evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of two rapidly advancing fields: Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors argue that artificial intelligence is moving towards an "Era of Experience," where agents learn through continuous, grounded interaction rather than relying solely on pre-existing human data. This paradigm shift demands agents not only capable of acting but also of deeply understanding and evaluating their experiences to inform future decisions.

Traditional RL, while providing a robust mathematical foundation for decision-making, primarily represents value as a scalar quantity. This scalar representation, the authors contend, is a highly compressed summary that limits an agent's "deep, structural understanding" of its environment. It answers "what" (how good a state or action is) but not "why." This limitation leads to "passive" learning, where agents refine strategies mainly through observing sampled action outcomes, rather than engaging in active, deliberative reasoning about the underlying causes of success or failure.

Recent advancements in LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in complex reasoning, planning, and language generation. This has led to a growing trend of leveraging LLMs for high-level planning in autonomous agents, as seen in works like ReAct, Reflexion, Voyager, and Pangu-Agent, which enable LLMs to reason through "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) processes and interact with tools or environments. Many of these approaches use LLMs for policy execution or as a source of self-reflection or feedback, often distilling complex reasoning from larger models to smaller ones.

Natural Language Reinforcement Learning (NLRL) distinguishes itself by proposing a fundamental re-envisioning of *core RL components* themselves within the natural language domain. Unlike existing LLM-based agent frameworks that primarily use LLMs for planning or as a form of verbal feedback on scalar rewards, NLRL formalizes the value function (Language Value Function, LVF), Bellman equation, policy, and policy iteration *directly* in natural language. This moves beyond merely having an LLM generate text based on scalar feedback (e.g., RLHF, which uses human feedback to shape scalar rewards) or using LLMs for *planning* on top of traditional RL. Instead, NLRL aims to create a *language-native* RL framework where the "value" is an interpretable linguistic narrative, and operations like Monte Carlo or Temporal Difference estimation are performed through language-based aggregation.

The work also connects to the field of interpretable RL, aiming to provide concept-based explanations for decision-making. By making the value function explicitly linguistic, NLRL inherently offers a path to more transparent and explainable agent behavior. Furthermore, it relates to the emerging concept of "learning from language feedback" and LLMs as "optimization paradigms" or "generative evaluators," but uniquely applies these concepts to foundational RL mechanics. The paper posits NLRL as a step towards agents that can not only act but also understand, reason, and actively improve, thereby addressing a critical gap in current AI paradigms for the "Era of Experience."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for introducing Natural Language Reinforcement Learning (NLRL) stems from the limitations of traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) in fostering deep understanding and active learning. The authors identify several critical issues:

- **Superficial Understanding from Scalar Value Functions:** Conventional RL relies on scalar value functions (e.g., Q-values, V-values) that compress complex future trajectories into a single numerical quantity. While computationally convenient, this scalar representation fundamentally limits the agent's capacity for a deep, structural understanding of its experiences. It tells the agent "how good" a state or action is, but not "why" – the underlying rationale, contributing factors, or strategic implications.
- **Passive Learning via Sampling:** Due to this superficial understanding, traditional policy optimization methods (like policy gradients) are largely "passive." They require agents to repeatedly sample actions and observe outcomes, waiting to "stumble upon" successful behaviors to reinforce them. Without interpretable insights into *why* certain actions are beneficial, agents cannot proactively or systematically adjust their strategies through deliberative reasoning. This dependency on extensive sampling often necessitates strong pre-trained models to even initiate successful RL training.
- **Challenges in Agentic LLM Settings:** The authors specifically highlight issues in applying traditional RL to LLM-based agents, such as the observed degradation of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning when optimizing with scalar rewards. This suggests that scalar feedback may be insufficient to preserve or enhance the nuanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

To address these limitations, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Introduce Natural Language Reinforcement Learning (NLRL):** To propose a novel framework that extends core RL principles and components into their natural language counterparts.
- **Define the Language Value Function (LVF):** To redefine the value function from a scalar quantity to a rich, interpretable linguistic narrative. This narrative should articulate the rationale behind an evaluation, drawing justifications from textualized experiences and observations. The LVF aims to provide the "why" behind decisions.
- **Verbalize Core RL Components:** To extend other fundamental RL elements, including policy, the Bellman equation, and generalized policy iteration (GPI), into language-based forms. This includes language policy, language Monte Carlo and Temporal Difference operators, and language policy improvement operators.
- **Enable Practical Implementation with LLMs:** To demonstrate how these verbalized components can be practically implemented and leveraged using recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), allowing for RL-like policy and value training through unsupervised environment interactions.
- **Foster Deeper Understanding and Active Learning:** Ultimately, the overarching goal is to enable agents that can not only learn to act but also to understand, reason, and actively improve their strategies through deliberative thinking, moving beyond passive, sampling-dependent approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NLRL's core methodology involves re-conceptualizing fundamental Reinforcement Learning (RL) components—value functions, policy, Bellman equation, and policy iteration—within the natural language domain. This is primarily achieved by leveraging the advanced capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs).

The central innovation is the **Language Value Function (LVF)**. Unlike traditional scalar value functions that output a numerical estimate of cumulative reward, the LVF outputs a "rich, interpretable linguistic narrative" that explains *why* a state or action is valuable. This narrative provides context, reasoning, and strategic implications, offering a deeper understanding than a mere number. The LVF concept extends to both language state value function (V_L) and language state-action value function (Q_L).

The framework then details how various LLM roles contribute to realizing NLRL:

1. **LLMs as Language Policy (π_L):** LLMs are used to generate actions, often incorporating a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process. The policy is modeled as generating both the thought process (`c`) and the final action (`a`) given a state (`s`), i.e., `π_L(a, c | s) = π_L(c | s)π_L(a | c, s)`.
2. **LLMs as Language Value Function Approximator (Q_L, V_L):** LLMs are trained to serve as the LVF, taking task state features (text, low-dimension statistics) and outputting natural language assessments of value. This leverages LLMs' inherent language understanding and generation capabilities.
3. **LLMs as Language Monte Carlo (G1) or Temporal Difference (G2) Operators:** A crucial aspect is generating language value estimates to train the LVF. This is done by verbalizing traditional MC and TD operations:
 - **Language MC Estimate:** An LLM (`G1`) summarizes and extracts core information from multiple complete textual trajectories (rollouts) to generate a language-based cumulative reward estimate for a state or state-action pair.
 - **Language TD Estimate:** Analogous to the Bellman expectation equation, LLMs (`G1`, `G2`) combine language descriptions of immediate transitions (current state, action, reward, next state) and the *estimated language value* of the next state to produce a language value estimate for the current state. `G2` mimics the summation, aggregating intermediate transitions and future evaluations, while `G1` acts as the expectation operator, aggregating across multiple parallel samples.
4. **Training LVF Approximator with Language Value Estimation:** The language value estimates generated through unsupervised environment interaction (via language MC/TD operators) are then used as supervision to fine-tune the LLM acting as the LVF (role 2). This is akin to critic training in traditional RL (e.g., DQN, actor-critic), but the objective involves imitating natural language aggregations using cross-entropy loss from supervised fine-tuning.
5. **LLMs as Policy Improvement Operator (I):** LVFs enable "deliberative learning." For a given state, the LLM (as operator `I`) is prompted with candidate actions and their corresponding language evaluations (`Q_L`). It then performs a CoT process to analyze these evaluations and propose a "better" action, thereby improving the policy.
6. **Training Language Policy with Improved Policy:** The improved CoT-based language policy data generated by the policy improvement operator (role 5) is then used to supervised fine-tune the LLM acting as the language policy (role 1). This mirrors policy training in traditional RL.

The paper demonstrates these components through three practical use cases:

- **Language GPI by Prompting (Sec 3.4):** Purely prompt-based approach using large LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) as `G1`, `G2`, `V_L`, and `I`. It involves language TD estimation and policy improvement through prompting, suitable for proprietary models.
- **Training Natural Language Value Function (Sec 3.5):** An iterative language TD pipeline to train a tunable LLM to be a language critic/annotator (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) for a given fixed policy (e.g., MCTS in Breakthrough).
- **Natural Language Actor-Critic Learning (Sec 3.6):** A full actor-critic pipeline where both the language policy and language critic are simultaneously learned through environment interactions. This involves collecting trajectories, updating LVF via language MC/TD and fine-tuning, then improving the policy via the language improvement operator and fine-tuning. The authors note stabilization techniques like action selection masks and buffer merging to mitigate hallucination and catastrophic forgetting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents experimental validation across four multi-step agentic tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness, efficiency, and potential of the NLRL framework.

#### 4.1. Language GPI by Prompting (Maze Navigation)

- **Task:** Navigating "Double T" and "Medium" mazes, with discrete actions and textual state observations.
- **Method:** Utilizes `gpt-4o-mini` as language aggregators (`G1`, `G2`), language value function (`V_L`), and policy improvement operator (`I`) in a purely prompt-based setting.
- **Findings:**
 - **Effectiveness of Language TD:** Language TD estimates consistently produced better performance (higher average rewards, less negative) compared to simply using a prompt-based language value function without look-ahead or aggregation.
 - **Impact of Variations & Look-ahead:** Increasing the number of variations (`K`) and look-ahead steps (`N`) generally led to further improvements, indicating that richer aggregated information enhances decision-making.
 - **Policy Improvement:** Language policy improvement effectively leveraged the language-TD-enhanced language value, leading to stable benefits.

#### 4.2. Training Natural Language Value Function with Language TD (5x5 Breakthrough Game)

- **Task:** Training a language critic (board annotator) for the complex 5x5 Breakthrough board game, where LLMs have limited pre-training knowledge.
- **Method:** Trains a small `LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct` model as the LVF, with a larger `LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct` model serving as the language TD operator (`G1`, `G2`). The target policy for value estimation is a strong MCTS agent.
- **Findings:**
 - **Superior Performance over Prompting Baselines:** The trained LVF achieved significantly higher evaluation accuracy (over 0.85 on test set) compared to prompting-based LLMs (GPT-4o, LLaMA-3.1-70B/8B-Instruct), which performed poorly (~0.61, slightly better than random guess). This highlights the necessity and efficacy of NLRL's training approach in niche domains.
 - **Look-ahead Ablation:** While more look-ahead steps (e.g., 8) initially showed higher in-training accuracy, they sometimes led to slight decreases in test set accuracy, suggesting potential overfitting and the importance of hyperparameter tuning.
 - **State Scaling Law:** The algorithm demonstrated robust scalability, with consistent performance improvements as the training state dataset size increased (from 0.4k to 45k states).
 - **Qualitative Improvement:** The trained LVF provided accurate and insightful natural language annotations, significantly reducing hallucination compared to the untrained models.

#### 4.3. Natural Language Actor-Critic Learning (Tic-tac-toe and FrozenLake)

- **Task:** Full model-free actor-critic learning in Tic-tac-toe (against a random opponent) and FrozenLake (deterministic and stochastic/slippery).
- **Method:** Uses `LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct` for both language policy (`π_L`) and LVF (`Q_L`), and `LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct` for language aggregation (`G1`) and policy improvement (`I`). Employs language Monte Carlo (MC) for value evaluation. Key stabilization techniques include action selection masking and buffer merging.
- **Findings (Tic-tac-toe):**
 - **Outperformance of Baselines:** NLRL substantially outperformed all baselines, including prompting-based LLMs (GPT-4o, LLaMA-3.1-8B/70B) and a `PPO` fine-tuned `LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct` model. It achieved a win rate of approximately 0.9 against a random opponent, demonstrating robustness in stochastic environments.
 - **Component Contributions:** Ablation studies confirmed that each stabilizing component (Monte Carlo sampling, buffer merging, and action selection mask) contributed positively to the system's performance, with the full system achieving the best results.
 - **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The number of training epochs, MC sample size, and number of rollout trajectories were found to impact training stability and convergence, with higher values generally leading to more robust learning.
- **Findings (FrozenLake):**
 - **Efficiency and Stability:** Without extensive tuning, NLRL exhibited superior performance and more stable improvement compared to RAGEN (a prior RL-based LLM agent framework) on both deterministic and stochastic FrozenLake environments. RAGEN, even with its "best practice" setup, showed slower convergence and occasional performance degradation, supporting NLRL's argument about the passive nature of traditional RL when applied to CoT.
 - **Reasoning Quality:** Qualitative analysis of reasoning trajectories showed that NLRL maintained and encouraged rigorous, informative CoT as training progressed, while RAGEN-style training often led to simplified or degraded reasoning.

Overall, the results consistently demonstrate that NLRL can effectively train agents that not only perform well but also develop deeper, more interpretable understanding and engage in more active, deliberative learning by leveraging the linguistic capabilities of LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Natural Language Reinforcement Learning (NLRL) represents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in the pursuit of more intelligent and understandable AI agents. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

- **Bridging Traditional RL and LLMs:** NLRL fundamentally re-imagines core RL mechanisms—value functions, Bellman equations, policy evaluation, and policy improvement—in the natural language domain. This goes beyond merely using LLMs as high-level planners or for generating verbal feedback on scalar rewards, establishing a deeper, more organic integration of the two fields. It provides a novel blueprint for how decision-making and learning in complex environments can be grounded in linguistic understanding.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Understanding:** By replacing scalar value functions with a "Language Value Function" (LVF) that provides an interpretable linguistic narrative explaining *why* a state or action is valuable, NLRL addresses a long-standing challenge in traditional RL: the "black box" nature of value estimation. This allows agents to offer transparent reasoning for their decisions, fostering deeper understanding for both the agent itself and human observers. This is crucial for building trust and for debugging complex AI systems.
- **Enabling Active and Deliberative Learning:** The linguistic richness of the LVF enables agents to engage in "active" and "deliberative" reasoning. Instead of passively relying on extensive environmental sampling to discover successful behaviors, NLRL agents can proactively analyze the "root causes of success or failure" by evaluating nuanced linguistic feedback. This shift from passive to active learning holds the potential to significantly improve learning efficiency and adaptability in complex, open-ended environments.
- **Improved Efficiency and Robustness:** The experimental results demonstrate that NLRL can achieve competitive or superior performance compared to traditional RL methods and other LLM-based agent frameworks, often with greater efficiency and stability. For instance, its performance on FrozenLake surpasses RAGEN, and its ability to train a high-accuracy board annotator for Breakthrough from scratch is notable. The stabilizing techniques like action selection masking and buffer merging also contribute to robust training.
- **Scalable Synthetic Data Generation:** NLRL offers a novel, unsupervised approach to generate high-quality language evaluation data through environment interaction. This self-generated linguistic feedback can be invaluable for training and fine-tuning more advanced language models and agents, reducing reliance on costly human annotations or limited pre-existing datasets. This has profound implications for scaling up AI capabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** NLRL opens several promising avenues for future work. Developing a more rigorous theoretical foundation for RL in language space is a critical next step. Integrating NLRL more deeply with existing LLM agent frameworks (e.g., self-evaluation, self-improvement, planning architectures) could unlock even more powerful capabilities. Furthermore, extending NLRL beyond games to more general domains, such as complex reasoning tasks, scientific discovery, or even low-level robotic control (by abstracting observations and actions into language), represents a vast area of potential impact.

In conclusion, "Natural Language Reinforcement Learning" proposes a transformative paradigm that moves beyond simple numeric evaluations in RL, embracing the expressive power of natural language to foster agents with deeper understanding, more transparent reasoning, and more active learning capabilities. This work lays a foundational stone for the "Era of Experience" in AI, where agents can not only act effectively but also genuinely comprehend and articulate their strategic choices.
