# DrEureka: Language Model Guided Sim-To-Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> DrEureka automates sim-to-real transfer for robotics by leveraging large language models to synthesize reward functions and configure domain randomization. This framework enabled a quadruped robot to achieve 34% higher forward velocity compared to human-designed policies and successfully taught a quadruped to balance and walk on a yoga ball, a previously unsolved task.

## Problem

- Bridging the 'reality gap' between robot training in simulation and deployment in the real world due to imperfect simulation models.
- The difficulty and expert knowledge required to manually design effective reward functions for reinforcement learning that promote safe and robust robot behaviors.
- The manual and often tedious tuning of domain randomization parameters needed to create policies robust to real-world variations.

## Method

- A three-stage framework uses LLMs to synthesize safety-regularized reward functions in code based on task descriptions.
- A 'Reward-Aware Physics Prior' (RAPP) is constructed by evaluating trained policies in perturbed simulations to identify feasible ranges for physics parameters.
- LLMs are then prompted with these RAPP ranges to automatically configure domain randomization parameters, determining which parameters to randomize and their distributions.

## Results

- DrEureka-trained policies achieved approximately 34% higher forward velocity and 20% greater distance traveled for quadruped locomotion compared to human-designed policies across various terrains.
- The framework successfully enabled a novel task: a quadruped robot balancing and walking on a yoga ball, demonstrating robust sim-to-real transfer for complex, unsolved challenges.
- For dexterous manipulation, the best DrEureka policy resulted in a 300% increase in in-hand cube rotations compared to human-designed policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models can effectively reason about physical systems and robotics when provided with task context and physical parameter ranges.
> - Explicit safety instructions during LLM prompting lead to the generation of more robust and deployable robotic policies.
> - The framework intelligently determines which simulation parameters to randomize and their distributions based on task requirements, outperforming uniform randomization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Training robots in simulation and deploying them in the real world—a process known as sim-to-real transfer—has become increasingly important in robotics research. However, bridging the gap between simulation and reality typically requires meticulous human design of reward functions and domain randomization parameters. DrEureka addresses this challenge by leveraging large language models (LLMs) to automate these design processes, making robot skill acquisition more accessible and efficient.

The framework builds upon recent advances in using LLMs for robotics, but uniquely focuses on automating the sim-to-real transfer process. By combining LLM-based reward function design with automated domain randomization, DrEureka can train policies that are robust enough to transfer directly to real robots without further fine-tuning. This approach has been validated on challenging tasks including quadruped locomotion, dexterous manipulation, and even a novel task of quadruped balancing on a yoga ball.

### 2. Background and Challenges

Sim-to-real transfer in robotics faces several fundamental challenges:

1. **Reality Gap**: Physical simulations cannot perfectly model the complexities of the real world, leading to a mismatch between simulated and real environments.

2. **Reward Design**: Creating effective reward functions that guide learning while promoting safe, robust behaviors is notoriously difficult and typically requires expert knowledge.

3. **Domain Randomization**: Traditional approaches to domain randomization—varying simulation parameters to create more robust policies—often rely on manual tuning of randomization ranges.

Prior approaches have attempted to address these challenges through techniques like domain adaptation, system identification, and manual domain randomization. However, these methods typically require significant human expertise and iteration, limiting their scalability and accessibility.

### 3. The DrEureka Framework

DrEureka introduces a three-stage approach to automated sim-to-real transfer:

1. **LLM-Based Reward Function Synthesis**: Generating effective reward functions with safety considerations.
2. **Reward-Aware Physics Prior Construction**: Determining feasible ranges of physics parameters.
3. **LLM-Based Domain Randomization**: Automatically configuring domain randomization parameters.

This process requires minimal human input—primarily just a specification of the desired task and the available simulation environment. The LLM effectively serves as a robotics expert, leveraging its knowledge of physics and robotics to make informed design decisions.

### 4. Reward Function Synthesis

The first stage of DrEureka builds upon the Eureka algorithm for LLM-based reward function design, with an important addition: a safety instruction that encourages the LLM to create reward functions that promote safer and more stable behaviors suitable for real-world deployment.

The LLM receives a task description (e.g., "train a quadruped robot to run on various terrains") along with safety instructions emphasizing the importance of robustness and stability. It then generates reward functions in code that can be directly integrated into the training process.

For example, a safety-regularized reward function for quadruped locomotion might include terms that penalize excessive joint torques, unstable postures, or erratic movements, in addition to rewarding forward progress.

```python
def compute_reward(self, ...):
    # Reward forward velocity
    forward_reward = self.obs_buf[:, 0]

    # Safety regularization
    joint_torques = self.torques.clone()
    torque_penalty = torch.sum(joint_torques ** 2, dim=1)

    # Stability terms
    orientation_penalty = torch.sum(self.obs_buf[:, 3:7] ** 2, dim=1)
    height_penalty = torch.abs(self.root_positions[:, 2] - self.target_height)

    return forward_reward - 0.01 * torque_penalty - 0.1 * orientation_penalty - 0.5 * height_penalty
```

### 5. Reward-Aware Physics Prior

The second stage of DrEureka constructs what the authors call a "Reward-Aware Physics Prior" (RAPP). This involves:

1. Training a policy using the synthesized reward function in the default simulation environment.
2. Evaluating this policy in perturbed simulations with varied physics parameters (e.g., friction, gravity, mass).
3. Identifying the range of each parameter within which the policy can still perform reasonably well.

This process produces feasible ranges for each physics parameter that are grounded in the actual performance of the robot on the task. These feasible ranges provide valuable context for the subsequent domain randomization step.

As shown in the figure, the RAPP (blue distributions) identifies narrower, task-relevant parameter ranges compared to naive parameter ranges. This focused approach leads to more effective domain randomization.

### 6. LLM-Based Domain Randomization

In the final stage, the LLM is provided with the RAPP ranges and tasked with generating domain randomization configurations. The LLM analyzes the feasible ranges and uses its knowledge of robotics and physics to determine:

1. Which parameters should be randomized (not all parameters are equally important)
2. The distribution type for each parameter (uniform, normal, etc.)
3. The randomization range for each parameter (potentially different from the RAPP range)

For example, given the prompt shown below, the LLM might determine that friction should be randomized more aggressively than gravity, or that certain joint properties need tighter randomization ranges to prevent instability.

The LLM produces multiple independent domain randomization configurations, which are then used to train and evaluate policies. The final output is a policy trained with the most effective randomization strategy.

### 7. Experimental Results

DrEureka was evaluated on three challenging robotics tasks:

1. **Quadruped Locomotion**: Training a Go1 quadruped robot to walk on various surfaces
2. **Dexterous Manipulation**: Teaching a robotic hand to rotate a cube in-hand
3. **Quadruped Globe Walking**: A novel task of balancing and walking on a yoga ball

On the quadruped locomotion task, DrEureka-trained policies significantly outperformed policies trained with human-designed parameters:

The results show that DrEureka achieved approximately 34% higher forward velocity and 20% greater distance traveled compared to human-designed policies across various terrains, including default flooring, socks (low friction), grass, and sidewalk.

Training curves also demonstrate the effectiveness of DrEureka's approach.

### 8. Applications and Case Studies

One of the most impressive demonstrations of DrEureka was its application to the novel task of quadruped balancing and walking on a yoga ball—a challenging task that had not been previously solved with sim-to-real approaches.

For this task, DrEureka automated the entire design process, from reward function synthesis to domain randomization. The resulting policy enabled the quadruped to maintain balance on the ball for extended periods, both in controlled lab settings and challenging outdoor environments.

In the dexterous manipulation task, DrEureka's best policy resulted in a 300% increase in in-hand cube rotations compared to human-designed policies.

### 9. Comparisons with Existing Approaches

DrEureka was compared against several baseline approaches:

1. **Human-Designed**: Traditional manual design of reward functions and domain randomization
2. **CEM Random**: Using the Cross-Entropy Method for domain randomization without RAPP
3. **CEM RAPP**: Using CEM with RAPP bounds
4. **LLM without RAPP**: Using LLM for domain randomization without RAPP context

The comparisons showed that:

DrEureka's reward-aware approach significantly outperformed alternatives, achieving higher forward velocity in quadruped locomotion and more successful manipulations in dexterous tasks.

Additionally, the safety instruction component proved essential for real-world robustness:

Policies trained with safety regularization exhibited more stable gaits and postures, crucial for successful real-world deployment.

### 10. Discussion and Implications

DrEureka represents a significant step toward democratizing robot skill acquisition by automating key design decisions in the sim-to-real pipeline. The framework's effectiveness across diverse robotics tasks demonstrates its versatility and potential for broader applications.

Several key insights emerged from this research:

1. **Combining LLMs with Physics Simulation**: LLMs can effectively reason about physical systems when provided with appropriate context, such as the RAPP bounds.

2. **Safety-Aware Design**: Explicit safety instructions in the LLM prompting process lead to more robust and deployable policies.

3. **Task-Specific Randomization**: The LLM intelligently determines which parameters to randomize based on the specific task requirements, rather than applying uniform randomization.

DrEureka demonstrates that LLMs can serve as effective automated design tools for robotics, potentially accelerating the development and deployment of robotic systems across various applications. By reducing the need for manual design and tuning, this approach makes advanced robotics more accessible to researchers and practitioners without extensive domain expertise.

In conclusion, DrEureka makes a compelling case for integrating LLMs into the robotics development pipeline, automating traditionally manual design processes and enabling more robust sim-to-real transfer. This approach has the potential to significantly accelerate progress in robotics research and applications by making advanced robot learning more accessible and efficient.
