# Paying More Attention to Visual Tokens in Self-Evolving Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VISE (Visual Invariance Self-Evolution) introduces an unsupervised framework that directly regularizes a Large Multimodal Model's (LMM) visual conditioning using geometric and semantic invariance rewards. This approach enhances visual reasoning, reduces hallucination, and improves performance across diverse vision-language tasks without requiring human annotation or external reward models.

## Problem

- Existing self-evolving LMMs primarily optimize for answer agreement or reasoning consistency, often overlooking whether the model genuinely attends to visual content.
- This leads to "visual under-conditioning," where models rely on statistical language priors, causing hallucination, modality bypass, and unstable visual interpretation.
- Prior multi-role self-evolving frameworks can be unstable, with one role potentially dominating or the solver overfitting, and often assume that self-consistency implies correct visual grounding.

## Method

- VISE directly regularizes the LMM's visual conditioning policy through two unsupervised, self-generated invariance-based reward signals.
- A Geometric Invariance Reward (R_geo) enforces spatial consistency of predicted bounding boxes under known image transformations (e.g., affine, flip).
- A Semantic Invariance Reward (R_sem) penalizes evidence-agnostic generation by requiring the model to recognize the absence of queried objects when their visual regions are perturbed or "ghosted."

## Results

- VISE achieved substantial improvements in image captioning, with gains of +**16.85** CIDEr on COCO for Qwen3-VL-2B, outperforming baselines by 4 to 7 times.
- It improved performance across all twelve VQA and reasoning benchmarks simultaneously at the 2B scale (e.g., +**4.19** on ScienceQA, +**3.72** on MMMU), with no observed task tradeoffs.
- The method consistently reduced object hallucination, decreasing Chair-I by -**5.00** points and Chair-S by -**5.45** points, while also improving POPE accuracy by +**1.02** at 2B.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct regularization of visual conditioning via invariance-based rewards is more effective for creating robust LMMs than indirect optimization for answer agreement or consistency.
> - Geometric invariance addresses localization instability, while semantic invariance ensures predictions are driven by actual visual evidence rather than language priors.
> - Strengthening visual grounding through these unsupervised rewards leads to broad, consistent performance gains across diverse vision-language tasks without task-specific tradeoffs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have explored self-evolving frameworks to enhance visual reasoning without extensive human annotation. These approaches typically frame multimodal reasoning as a self-improving process, often involving multi-role setups (e.g., proposer–solver, questioner–reasoner) within a shared model backbone. The core mechanism involves reinforcing multi-sample agreement, leveraging trajectory-aware feedback, or incorporating tool-assisted verification to promote more reliable reasoning. Existing methods like EvoLMM, iReasoner, VisPlay, and VisionZero have demonstrated gains, particularly on structured reasoning benchmarks.

However, a recurring limitation identified in these prior self-evolving frameworks is that their reward schemes primarily optimize for answer agreement or reasoning consistency. This optimization often occurs without explicitly ensuring that the model's decoder genuinely attends to and conditions its outputs on the visual content of the input image. Consequently, models may rely on statistical language priors to produce self-consistent outputs, even when visual evidence is ambiguous or absent. This leads to a persistent failure mode termed "visual under-conditioning," characterized by insufficient attention to visual tokens during generation, hallucination, modality bypass, and unstable visual interpretation.

This work, VISE (Visual Invariance Self-Evolution), aims to address this specific gap. Instead of optimizing for answer agreement, it proposes a direct regularization of the model’s visual conditioning policy. By focusing on invariance-based rewards—specifically geometric and semantic invariance—VISE attempts to compel the decoder to maintain robust conditioning on visual evidence throughout the generation process, thereby mitigating the issues associated with visual under-conditioning that prior self-evolving methods have not fully resolved. The work distinguishes itself by operating within a single model, without specialist roles, external reward models, or reliance on human annotations, purely trained on raw unlabeled images.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the visual reasoning capabilities of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) in a purely unsupervised setting by directly addressing the problem of "visual under-conditioning."

The motivation for this objective stems from observed limitations in existing self-evolving LMMs:

- **Reliance on Language Priors:** Current self-evolving LMMs often optimize for self-consistency or answer agreement. While this can lead to correct answers, especially for structured reasoning tasks, it does not guarantee that the model's decoder attends to the actual visual content. Instead, the model can exploit statistical language priors to generate plausible-sounding, self-consistent outputs without genuinely integrating visual evidence.
- **Visual Under-Conditioning as a Failure Mode:** This reliance on language priors manifests as "visual under-conditioning," where the decoder exhibits insufficient attention to visual tokens during generation. This leads to several adverse outcomes:
 - **Hallucination:** Generating objects or details that are not present in the image but are statistically common co-occurrences with other visible elements.
 - **Modality Bypass:** The model effectively bypasses the visual input, relying predominantly on its internal language representations.
 - **Unstable Visual Interpretation:** Inconsistent descriptions or answers when challenged with fine-grained visual details or transformations.
- **Limitations of Prior Frameworks:**
 - **Multi-role Instability:** Proposer–solver or similar setups can be unstable, with one role potentially dominating or the solver overfitting, leading to local minima.
 - **Implicit Assumption:** Framing rewards around answer correctness implicitly assumes that self-consistency implies correct visual grounding, an assumption that the presence of visual under-conditioning challenges. For instance, models trained on math- and science-centric images might improve on reasoning benchmarks but degrade on tasks requiring detailed visual conditioning, such as image captioning.

To overcome these issues, the research proposes VISE, aiming to:

- **Regularize Visual Conditioning Directly:** Shift the training objective from optimizing answer agreement to directly regularizing the model’s visual conditioning policy.
- **Develop Invariance-Based Rewards:** Introduce novel, complementary invariance-based reward signals that can be self-generated without external supervision:
 - **Geometric Invariance:** Enforce spatial consistency of predicted bounding boxes under known image transformations.
 - **Semantic Invariance:** Penalize evidence-agnostic generation by requiring the model to recognize the absence of queried objects when their predicted regions are perturbed (ghosted).
- **Operate Within a Single Model:** Simplify the self-evolving framework by removing specialist roles, external reward models, and reliance on annotations or metadata.
- **Improve Generalization:** Demonstrate that stronger visual conditioning, rather than domain-specific consistency, leads to broad, consistent performance gains across diverse vision-language understanding tasks, including image captioning, visual question answering, reasoning, and hallucination reduction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VISE (Visual Invariance Self-Evolution) is an unsupervised self-evolving framework designed to regularize an LMM's visual conditioning policy. It operates within a single model architecture, leveraging invariance-based rewards derived purely from the model’s own predictions on unlabeled images.

**Problem Formulation:**
The training process uses an unlabeled collection of images. At each step, the LMM acts in a self-questioning regime:
1. **Query Generation:** The model, conditioning on an image `x`, generates a short natural-language localization query `q` describing a prominent and spatially unambiguous object.
2. **Bounding Box Prediction:** The model then predicts a bounding box `B_orig` (normalized coordinates `[0, S]^4`, where `S=1000`) for the queried object `q` on the image `x`.
The Qwen3-VL family is used as the base backbone. The vision encoder is frozen, and only the multimodal projector, feed-forward layers, and decoder attention projections are updated. This focuses updates on the decoder's utilization of visual representations, rather than modifying the already capable encoder.

**Geometric Invariance Reward (R_geo):**
This reward component addresses localization instability due to visual under-conditioning. A model that truly conditions on visual evidence should maintain consistent localization under known geometric transformations.
1. **Transformation:** A geometric transformation `T` (affine, crop, or horizontal flip, represented by a 3x3 homogeneous matrix `M`) is randomly sampled and applied to the original image `x` to create `x' = T(x)`.
2. **Second Box Prediction:** The model predicts a second bounding box `B_new` on the transformed image `x'` using the *same* query `q`.
3. **Projected Box Calculation:** The original bounding box `B_orig` is analytically projected onto the transformed image space to obtain `B_proj`.
4. **Reward Computation:** The geometric invariance reward `R_geo` is calculated based on the Generalized Intersection over Union (GIoU) between `B_proj` and `B_new`:
 `R_geo = (GIoU(B_proj, B_new) + 1) / 2`
 This reward ranges from 0 to 1, maximized when `B_new` perfectly matches `B_proj`, smoothly degrading with inconsistencies. This directly penalizes spatial inconsistencies indicative of insufficient visual conditioning.

**Semantic Invariance Reward (R_sem):**
This component addresses the model's evidence sensitivity, ensuring predictions are driven by semantic content, not just plausible spatial regions.
1. **Regional Perturbation (Ghosting):** The pixel region corresponding to `B_orig` in `x` is replaced with a Gaussian-blurred version (kernel `σ = 25.0`), creating a "ghosted" image `~x`. This degrades the visual evidence for the queried object while preserving context.
2. **Visibility Assessment:** The model assesses the visibility of the queried object under `q` on both the original image `x` (`v = vis(x, q) ∈ {0, 1}`) and the ghosted image `~x` (`~v = vis(~x, q) ∈ {0, 1}`) via greedy decoding.
3. **Reward Computation:** The semantic invariance reward `R_sem` is assigned as:
 `R_sem = 1.0 if v = 1 and ~v = 0, else 0.0`
 This binary reward is granted only if the model correctly identifies the object as present in the original image but absent/obscured in the ghosted version, thus penalizing confident predictions that are robust to visual evidence removal.

**Composite Reward and Optimization:**
- **Total Reward:** The two rewards are combined: `R_t = λ_geo * R_geo + λ_sem * R_sem`, where `λ_geo = λ_sem = 0.5`.
- **Optimization:** The model is optimized using a REINFORCE-style update with KL regularization against a frozen reference policy `π_ref`.
 - An exponential moving average baseline `b_t` is used to reduce gradient variance, yielding advantage `A_t = R_t - b_t`.
 - The loss function `L(θ) = - A_t * log p_θ(y | x, q) + β_t * Δ_t`, where `y` is the generated completion (including box coordinates). `Δ_t = log p_θ(y | x, q) - log p_ref(y | x, q)` is a KL-like divergence proxy.
 - The KL coefficient `β_t` is dynamically adapted to maintain a target divergence level `τ` (e.g., `τ = 0.020`), increasing when divergence exceeds `τ` and decreasing otherwise. This ensures stable self-evolution.

**Implementation Details:**
LoRA is used for fine-tuning. Training occurs on raw, unlabeled images (e.g., from COCO), with no annotations or external reward models. Prompts (provided in the supplementary material) are used for query generation, bounding box prediction, and semantic visibility verification.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of VISE was evaluated across 18 benchmarks covering image captioning, Visual Question Answering (VQA), reasoning, and hallucination, utilizing the Qwen3-VL backbone family and testing across various model scales and architectures.

**Image Captioning (Table 1, Figure 5):**
- **Significant Gains:** VISE achieved substantial improvements in image captioning. For Qwen3-VL-2B, it delivered gains of +16.85 CIDEr on COCO, +14.73 on NoCaps, +16.55 on Flickr30k, and +19.66 on TextCaps. These gains were 4 to 7 times larger than the strongest baselines.
- **Baseline Performance:** Consistency-based self-evolving methods (EvoLMM, iReasoner) trained on math and scientific images showed regressions in captioning performance (e.g., EvoLMM dropped -0.70 CIDEr on COCO at 2B), suggesting that optimizing for answer agreement without direct visual conditioning reinforces language priors. VisionZero-RW performed best among baselines due to its alignment with natural scenes.
- **Qualitative Observations (Figure 5):** VISE produced more specific and image-grounded descriptions compared to baselines. For instance, it correctly identified "three bears" by size and position, "elk and river color," "human-like figure on the car window," and "Trafalgar Square and Nelson’s Column," whereas baselines often provided vague descriptions ("large animals") or committed to statistically plausible but incorrect details ("wolves," "obelisk").
- **Effect of Scale:** Gains were largest at smaller model scales (e.g., +16.85 CIDEr at 2B on COCO) and attenuated with increasing model size (+8.72 at 32B), indicating that larger models start with stronger pre-trained visual conditioning.

**VQA and Reasoning (Table 2):**
- **Broad Generalization:** VISE improved performance across all twelve VQA and reasoning benchmarks simultaneously at the 2B scale (e.g., +4.19 on ScienceQA, +2.41 on InfoVQA, +1.75 on MMMU). This pattern held across all scales, with no observed task tradeoffs.
- **Baseline Tradeoffs:** Baselines often exhibited domain-specific tradeoffs. For example, VisionZero-Chart improved ChartQA (+0.96 at 2B) but reduced performance on OK-VQA (-0.45), while EvoLMM and iReasoner showed strong gains on ScienceQA (+3.59) but dropped on OK-VQA (-2.73). This suggested that their improvements were tied to domain-specific statistical regularities rather than generalized visual conditioning.
- **Multi-Discipline Reasoning:** The gain on MMMU, a multi-discipline visual reasoning benchmark, was particularly notable (+3.72 at 4B), indicating that stronger visual conditioning benefits tasks requiring complex visual reasoning across various fields.

**Hallucination (Table 3):**
- **Reduced Hallucination:** VISE consistently reduced object hallucination. At 2B, it decreased Chair-I by -5.00 points and Chair-S by -5.45 points, outperforming the best baseline (VisionZero-RW) by +2.01 and +1.40 respectively. Simultaneously, it improved POPE accuracy by +1.02.
- **Joint Improvement:** This joint improvement across hallucination metrics (Chair-I/S) and object presence reliability (POPE) was unique to VISE. Other baselines sometimes reduced sentence-level hallucination (Chair) at the cost of weakening binary object-presence reliability (POPE), indicating inconsistent grounding.
- **Mechanism:** The reduction in hallucination is attributed to the semantic invariance reward, which penalizes confident predictions of objects not visually present.

**Effect of Model Scale:**
- Gains from VISE were more pronounced in smaller models and diminished for larger models across all task groups. This is interpreted as a capacity ceiling effect, where larger pre-trained models already possess stronger visual conditioning, leaving less room for self-evolving improvements.

**Backbone Generalization (Table 4):**
- VISE's improvements were consistent across four architecturally diverse LMM backbones (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3, Gemma3, Llama-3.2). Captioning, hallucination, and VQA/reasoning gains were observed across all families, confirming that the invariance rewards are architecture-agnostic and address a general phenomenon of visual under-conditioning.

**Ablation Study (Table 5):**
- **R_geo Contribution:** Training with only the geometric invariance reward (`R_geo`) yielded moderate captioning gains (+4.83 COCO CIDEr at 2B) and modest hallucination reductions (-1.35 Chair-I), demonstrating its role in spatial consistency but its inability to penalize visually unsupported generations.
- **R_sem Contribution:** The semantic invariance reward (`R_sem`) accounted for most of VISE’s gains (+13.99 COCO CIDEr, -4.15 Chair-I at 2B), indicating that the ghosting-based signal is the primary driver for improved captioning and hallucination reduction.
- **Complementary Benefits:** Combining both rewards (full VISE model) provided additional, consistent improvements. The full model added +2.86 COCO CIDEr and a further -0.85 Chair-I reduction beyond `R_sem` alone, confirming that spatial consistency and evidence sensitivity address distinct, complementary facets of visual under-conditioning.
- **Random Reward Control (Table S3):** A control experiment with random rewards showed no significant improvement over the base model, confirming that the observed gains are directly attributable to the specific invariance rewards.

**Visual Attention Analysis (Figures 3, 4, S2-S6, S11-S16):**
- **Increased Visual Attention:** VISE-trained models consistently assigned a higher fraction of attention to image tokens across mid-to-late decoder layers during generation (mean gains of +2.84% and +2.56% for 2B and 4B models, respectively), where semantic generation decisions are made. This indicates a shift from language-prior-driven to image-conditioned decoding.
- **Representation Alignment (Figure 4):** Per-layer CKA similarity between representations of original and geometrically augmented views increased, especially in later decoder layers, confirming that `R_geo` helps maintain consistent visual representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for self-evolving Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) that demonstrates consistent improvements across a broad spectrum of vision-language tasks. The work identifies and directly addresses "visual under-conditioning," a limitation where LMMs leverage statistical language priors over actual visual evidence, leading to issues like hallucination and modality bypass.

The primary significance of VISE lies in its shift in the self-evolving paradigm. Instead of optimizing for answer agreement—an indirect proxy for visual understanding—VISE directly regularizes the model's visual conditioning policy through two unsupervised invariance-based rewards: geometric invariance and semantic invariance. This approach requires no human annotations, external reward models, or complex multi-role setups, making it a scalable and autonomous self-improvement mechanism.

The consistent and substantial performance gains observed across image captioning, VQA, reasoning, and hallucination benchmarks, without task-specific tradeoffs, suggest that genuinely strengthening visual grounding leads to more robust and generalizable LMMs. The reduction in object hallucination is particularly notable, indicating a more reliable and evidence-bound generative process. Furthermore, the demonstrated effectiveness across multiple model scales and diverse architectural backbones suggests the broad applicability of the invariance-based reward principles.

The potential impact of this work is several-fold:

- **Improved LMM Reliability:** By fostering greater attention to visual tokens and reducing reliance on language priors, VISE could lead to LMMs that are more faithful to visual input, thereby improving their trustworthiness and reducing the generation of factually incorrect or unsupported content.
- **Enhanced Unsupervised Learning:** The purely unsupervised nature of VISE, requiring only raw unlabeled images, opens avenues for training powerful LMMs without the immense cost and effort associated with large-scale human annotation or curated datasets. This could accelerate the development of LMMs in resource-scarce domains or for novel applications.
- **Guidance for Future Self-Evolving Systems:** This research provides a framework and empirical evidence that direct regularization of visual conditioning—rather than indirect optimization for output agreement—is a promising direction for self-evolving multimodal training. Future work in this area may explore other forms of invariance or evidence-conditioning signals.
- **Broader Generalization:** The finding that improved visual conditioning generalizes across diverse task formats (captioning vs. reasoning) suggests that models trained with VISE could adapt more effectively to new tasks and domains without requiring extensive fine-tuning.

In summary, VISE offers a method for cultivating more visually grounded and robust LMMs, addressing a fundamental limitation in the field and contributing to the development of more reliable and autonomously improving multimodal AI systems.
