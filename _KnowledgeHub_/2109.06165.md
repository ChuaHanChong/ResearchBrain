# CDTrans: Cross-domain Transformer for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba Group and Shandong University developed CDTrans, a pure Transformer-based framework for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation. This framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on four UDA benchmarks and demonstrates robustness to noisy pseudo-labels in category-level alignment by leveraging the cross-attention mechanism.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks experience significant performance degradation when deployed in new domains with differing data distributions, known as domain shift.
- Traditional CNN-based Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods struggle with noisy and inaccurate pseudo-labels for target domain samples, especially in fine-grained category-level alignment.
- Manually annotating large datasets for every new target domain is a prohibitively expensive and time-consuming process.

## Method

- ==CDTrans==, a novel pure Transformer-based solution for UDA, employs a ==triple-branch, weight-sharing architecture==.
- It leverages the ==cross-attention mechanism== of Transformers, which is shown to be inherently robust to noisy input pairs, for robust feature alignment between source and target domains.
- A =="Two-Way Center-Aware Pseudo Labeling"== strategy generates high-quality pseudo-labels for target samples and filters out noisy pairs to mitigate misguidance during training.

## Results

- CDTrans achieved state-of-the-art performance on the VisDA-2017 benchmark and delivered highly competitive results on Office-Home, Office-31, and DomainNet datasets.
- It demonstrated remarkable robustness by boosting accuracy on 'hard' categories (e.g., from **10.3%** to **88.6%** for 'person' on VisDA-2017), validating its ability to handle noisy pseudo-labels.
- The Two-way Center-aware Pseudo Labeling method significantly improved pseudo-label precision to **91.3%** on VisDA-2017, leading to overall performance remarkably close to using ground-truth labels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The cross-attention mechanism in Transformers is robust to noisy input pairs, effectively assigning low weights to dissimilar patches within false positive source-target pairs, thereby reducing their negative impact.
> - A two-way pseudo-labeling approach combined with center-aware filtering significantly enhances the precision of generated pseudo-labels, which is crucial for effective category-level domain adaptation.
> - A triple-branch Transformer architecture with shared weights, using self-attention for domain-specific learning and cross-attention for explicit domain alignment, along with a distillation loss, effectively learns discriminative and domain-invariant representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the critical problem of **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)** in deep learning. Deep neural networks, despite their remarkable success, often suffer from a significant performance drop when applied to new domains whose data distribution differs from the training domain, a phenomenon known as "domain shift." UDA aims to mitigate this by transferring knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain, thereby circumventing the expensive and laborious process of annotating new datasets.

Historically, most UDA methods have relied on **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**-based frameworks. These methods primarily focus on learning domain-invariant feature representations, either at the coarse-grained domain level (e.g., using Maximum Mean Discrepancy or adversarial training to align overall feature distributions) or at the fine-grained category level (e.g., aligning distributions for each specific class). A fundamental challenge for category-level UDA is the generation of **pseudo-labels** for target domain samples. These pseudo-labels are often noisy and inaccurate, which can compromise the quality of domain alignment and, consequently, the overall UDA performance.

The research emerges at a time when **Transformers** have achieved unprecedented success across various artificial intelligence domains, initially in Natural Language Processing (NLP) (Vaswani et al., 2017; Devlin et al., 2018) and more recently in computer vision (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Han et al., 2020; Khan et al., 2021). Models like Vision Transformer (ViT) and its variants (DeiT, Swin Transformer) have demonstrated comparable or superior performance to CNNs on many vision tasks. A key mechanism within Transformers is the **attention mechanism**, particularly cross-attention, which has shown remarkable efficacy in aligning features from different distributions, even across different modalities (e.g., vision-to-text, text-to-speech, vision-to-vision).

CDTrans bridges these two significant trends by introducing one of the *first pure Transformer-based solutions for UDA tasks*. The authors identify a crucial property of the cross-attention mechanism: its inherent robustness to noisy input pairs, which is highly relevant for addressing the pseudo-labeling problem in UDA. By adopting Transformers, CDTrans aims to overcome the limitations of CNN-based methods in handling noisy pseudo-labels, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in UDA by leveraging the powerful feature alignment capabilities of attention. This work represents a significant step in integrating the latest architectural advancements (Transformers) into a challenging and critical area of machine learning (UDA), setting a new direction for future research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and robust framework for **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)**, specifically targeting the challenges associated with category-level alignment. The authors aim to enable the transfer of knowledge from a rich, labeled source domain to a completely unlabeled target domain, thereby reducing the need for expensive manual annotations in new deployment environments.

More precisely, the key objectives are:

- **To leverage the power of Transformer architectures for UDA:** Recognizing the transformative success of Transformers in various vision tasks, the authors aim to explore their potential for the UDA problem, hypothesizing that their attention mechanisms could offer advantages over traditional CNNs.
- **To address the issue of noisy pseudo-labels in category-level UDA:** A core problem in many existing UDA methods, particularly those that attempt fine-grained alignment, is the inherent unreliability of pseudo-labels generated for target samples. The goal is to develop a method that is more resilient to this noise.
- **To design a high-quality pseudo-labeling strategy:** Beyond merely tolerating noise, the authors seek to improve the accuracy of pseudo-labels themselves, as better labels directly lead to more effective domain alignment.
- **To create a framework that simultaneously learns discriminative domain-specific and domain-invariant representations:** The ideal UDA solution should capture unique characteristics of each domain while also extracting generalizable features applicable across domains.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several observations:

- **The pervasive nature of domain shift:** Deep learning models frequently encounter performance degradation when deployed in real-world scenarios due to differences between training and test data distributions. UDA offers a vital pathway to make these models more adaptable and practical.
- **The computational and human cost of data annotation:** Manually labeling large datasets for every new domain is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, creating a strong imperative for unsupervised adaptation techniques.
- **Limitations of CNN-based UDA:** While CNNs have been dominant, their performance in category-level UDA is often hampered by their sensitivity to noisy pseudo-labels during feature alignment.
- **The unique capabilities of Transformer's cross-attention:** The authors observed that the cross-attention mechanism in Transformers excels at aligning features from different distributions and, crucially, demonstrates a notable robustness to noise in input pairs. This makes it an ideal candidate to tackle the core problem of noisy pseudo-labels in UDA. The paper explicitly states that "the cross-attention in Transformer is robust to the noisy input pairs for better feature alignment." This foundational insight motivated their entire approach.

By addressing these motivations and objectives, the authors sought to introduce a novel, pure Transformer-based UDA solution that not only achieves state-of-the-art performance but also fundamentally tackles the challenge of pseudo-label noise, opening new avenues for research in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **CDTrans (Cross-Domain Transformer)**, offers a novel, pure Transformer-based solution for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation. It tackles the challenge of noisy pseudo-labels by leveraging the robustness of the cross-attention mechanism and introducing a refined pseudo-labeling strategy.

The methodology can be broken down into three main components:

#### 3.1. The Cross-Attention in Transformer

The foundation of CDTrans lies in the Transformer architecture, particularly its attention mechanism.
- **Preliminary:** The paper first reviews the self-attention mechanism, as introduced in "Attention is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017) and its application in Vision Transformers (ViT) (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020), where images are broken down into sequences of patches. Self-attention computes relationships among patches within a single input.
- **Cross-Attention:** CDTrans adapts this to a cross-attention module, which takes *two* input images ($I_s$ from the source, $I_t$ from the target). Queries are derived from $I_s$, while keys and values come from $I_t$. This design allows the module to aggregate information between the two images based on patch-level similarities, effectively aligning their features.
- **Robustness to Noise:** A key insight of the authors is that cross-attention is inherently robust to noise. In the context of UDA, when pseudo-labels for target samples are inaccurate, "false positive pairs" (source-target pairs with mismatched labels) can arise. The cross-attention mechanism, by assigning low weights to dissimilar patches within such false positive pairs (as illustrated in Figure 1a), effectively downplays their negative impact on the overall alignment process. This robustness is empirically validated through an experiment showing superior performance with cross-attention even with a high rate of false positive pairs (Figure 1b).

#### 3.2. Two-Way Center-Aware Pseudo Labeling

To generate high-quality input pairs for the cross-attention module and mitigate the noise even further, the authors propose a sophisticated pseudo-labeling algorithm:
- **Two-Way Labeling:** Instead of only matching source to target or vice versa, the method constructs training pairs in both directions:
 - $P_S$: For each source image, the most similar target image (based on feature distance) is selected to form a pair.
 - $P_T$: For each target image, the most similar source image is selected.
 - The final set of training pairs $P$ is the union of $P_S$ and $P_T$. This "two-way" approach ensures that all source and target data are involved in the training, reducing potential training bias that might arise from one-sided matching.
- **Center-Aware Filtering:** To refine the accuracy of these pseudo-labels, a filtering mechanism is introduced:
 - Initially, target data probabilities for source categories are obtained using a pre-trained model on the source data.
 - Weighted k-means clustering (Eq. 5) is used to compute initial category centers in the target domain.
 - Pseudo-labels for target samples are then assigned based on their nearest cluster center (Eq. 6).
 - New centers are re-calculated using these pseudo-labels (Eq. 7).
 - Finally, selected pairs from $P$ are filtered: a pair is kept only if the pseudo-label of the target image is consistent with the ground-truth label of its corresponding source image. This "center-aware" filtering significantly boosts the precision of the pseudo-labeled pairs.

#### 3.3. CDTrans: Cross-Domain Transformer Architecture

The core of the CDTrans framework is a novel **triple-branch, weight-sharing Transformer** architecture (illustrated in Figure 2):
- **Three Branches:**
 1. **Source Branch:** Processes source images using self-attention to learn domain-specific representations. Its output is classified using a cross-entropy loss ($L_{cls}$).
 2. **Target Branch:** Processes target images using self-attention. It also learns domain-specific representations, guided by pseudo-labels with a cross-entropy loss ($L_{cls}$) and, crucially, by a distillation loss from the source-target branch.
 3. **Source-Target Branch:** This is where cross-attention is primarily applied. Its inputs are derived from the source and target branches (queries from the source, keys and values from the target). This branch explicitly performs domain alignment.
- **Weight Sharing:** A key design principle is that all three branches share the same Transformer weights and a common classifier. This enforces consistency and ensures that features learned across branches are related.
- **Loss Functions:**
 - **Classification Loss ($L_{cls}$):** Applied to the source branch (with ground-truth labels) and the target branch (with pseudo-labels) to ensure discriminative feature learning.
 - **Distillation Loss ($L_{dtl}$):** The source-target branch acts as a "teacher" for the target branch, providing a soft target distribution. The distillation loss (Eq. 8) guides the target branch to align its learned features with the robustly aligned features from the source-target branch. This helps the target branch overcome any remaining noise from its pseudo-labels.

During inference, only the target branch is activated to classify new unlabeled target images. This comprehensive methodology ensures that CDTrans not only leverages the advanced capabilities of Transformers but also meticulously addresses the critical challenge of pseudo-label noise, leading to robust and accurate UDA performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of CDTrans was evaluated on four popular UDA benchmarks: VisDA-2017, Office-Home, Office-31, and DomainNet. The experiments used DeiT-small and DeiT-base as backbones, with DeiT-small having a model size comparable to ResNet-50 for fair comparison with existing CNN-based methods.

#### 4.1. Comparison to State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Methods

- **Overall Performance:** CDTrans consistently and significantly outperformed the baseline (source-only training) across all four datasets. For instance, on VisDA-2017, it achieved nearly a 21% improvement over the baseline. This highlights the substantial generalization power gained through the proposed UDA mechanism.
- **SOTA Achieved:** CDTrans achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on VisDA-2017, demonstrating its superiority over numerous existing UDA methods. On Office-Home, Office-31, and DomainNet, it also delivered highly competitive or superior results compared to other SOTA techniques, even when those methods' backbones (like ResNet-50) were replaced with DeiT-base for a fairer comparison (denoted as "-B*").
- **Robustness to Noise (Hard Categories):** A notable finding was CDTrans's remarkable performance improvement on "hard" categories, such as "person" in the VisDA-2017 dataset. Despite a very low initial baseline accuracy (e.g., 10.3%), CDTrans boosted performance to 88.6%, indicating its exceptional robustness to poor initial classification ability and the high level of noise in pseudo-labels for such categories. This directly validates the core hypothesis about cross-attention's noise resilience.
- **Pre-training Impact:** The paper acknowledged that some competing methods, like TVT, achieved slightly better results on Office-Home and Office-31. This was attributed to TVT utilizing a ViT backbone pre-trained on ImageNet21K, a much larger dataset than ImageNet1K, which CDTrans and most other UDA methods used for pre-training. This suggests that CDTrans's performance could potentially be further enhanced with larger pre-training datasets.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The authors conducted comprehensive ablation studies to validate the contributions of their proposed components.

- **Different Pseudo Labeling Methods (Table 5):**
 - **One-way vs. Two-way Labeling:** "One-way-source" and "One-way-target" (where pairs are formed in only one direction) showed clear biases and yielded lower average accuracies (76.6% and 76.9% respectively on VisDA-2017) compared to the "Two-way" method (77.1%), which ensures all data points are utilized.
 - **Two-way vs. Two-way + Center-Aware:** The proposed "Tw+Ca" (Two-way + Center-aware filtering) method significantly improved performance from 77.1% to 88.4% average accuracy on VisDA-2017. Crucially, while it slightly reduced the recall of selected pairs (97.8% source, 94.8% target), it dramatically boosted the precision of these pairs to 91.3%. This confirms that the center-aware filtering effectively removes noisy pairs, leading to much better alignment.
 - **Comparison to Other Pseudo-labeling:** Tw+Ca also outperformed other pseudo-label generation methods like RPLL (80.3%) and MRKLD+LRENT (83.3%), demonstrating its superior quality.
 - **Proximity to Groundtruth:** The "Tw+Ca" method achieved results remarkably close to the "Groundtruth" upper bound (91.5% average accuracy), underscoring the high quality of the generated pseudo-labels.

- **Different Loss Functions (Table 6):**
 - **Target Branch Classification Loss:** Adding a classification loss ($L_{cls}$) to the target branch (using pseudo-labels) significantly improved performance over a source-only trained model (from 67.1% to 86.0% average on VisDA-2017), validating the basic self-training concept.
 - **Source-Target Branch Classification Loss:** Further adding a classification loss to the source-target branch (where cross-attention is applied) further improved accuracy to 86.7%, demonstrating the benefit of explicit feature alignment using cross-attention.
 - **Distillation Loss:** Replacing the classification loss in the source-target branch with the proposed distillation loss ($L_{dtl}$) led to the best performance (88.4%). This confirms the effectiveness of guiding the target branch's learning with the robustly aligned features from the source-target "teacher" branch.

In summary, the experimental results robustly support the key claims of the paper: Transformers, particularly their cross-attention mechanism, offer inherent advantages for UDA, especially in handling pseudo-label noise. The meticulously designed two-way center-aware pseudo-labeling and the multi-loss, triple-branch architecture synergistically contribute to achieving state-of-the-art performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CDTrans paper makes several significant contributions to the field of Unsupervised Domain Adaptation and deep learning generally, with substantial potential impact.

#### 5.1. Significance of Contributions

- **Pioneering Transformer-based UDA:** CDTrans stands out as one of the first, if not the first, "pure Transformer-based solution" for UDA. This is a crucial step in translating the success of Transformers from standard vision tasks to the more complex domain adaptation problem. It demonstrates that the architectural advantages of Transformers, particularly their attention mechanisms, are highly relevant and effective in this context.
- **Robustness to Noisy Pseudo-labels:** The paper clearly identifies and experimentally validates the inherent robustness of the cross-attention mechanism to noise in input pairs. This addresses a long-standing fundamental problem in category-level UDA, where noisy pseudo-labels often degrade performance. By showing that Transformers can intrinsically mitigate this issue, CDTrans offers a more stable and reliable foundation for UDA.
- **Novel Two-Way Center-Aware Pseudo-Labeling:** The proposed pseudo-labeling method is a notable contribution. Its "two-way" approach ensures comprehensive data utilization and reduces bias, while the "center-aware filtering" significantly boosts pseudo-label precision. This meticulous strategy for generating high-quality supervision for the unlabeled target domain is a key enabler for CDTrans's success.
- **Effective Triple-Branch Architecture:** The weight-sharing triple-branch Transformer framework, combined with the strategic application of self-attention for domain-specific learning and cross-attention for domain alignment, provides a powerful and coherent solution. The distillation loss from the robustly aligned source-target branch to the target branch further refines the learning process, demonstrating an elegant integration of different learning objectives.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** By achieving state-of-the-art results on several challenging UDA benchmarks, CDTrans not only validates its theoretical underpinnings but also establishes a new performance benchmark for the community, demonstrating a significant leap over previous CNN-based methods.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Accelerated Adoption of Transformers in UDA:** This work will likely inspire a surge of research into Transformer-based UDA methods. Researchers will be encouraged to explore various Transformer architectures, attention mechanisms, and their combinations for domain adaptation, moving away from a sole reliance on CNNs.
- **More Reliable Real-World AI Deployments:** UDA is critical for deploying AI models in dynamic environments where data distribution shifts are common (e.g., autonomous driving, medical imaging across different hospitals, industrial inspection systems). CDTrans's robustness to noisy target data makes it particularly valuable for practical applications where collecting perfectly labeled new data is unfeasible.
- **Improved Pseudo-Labeling Techniques:** The success of the two-way center-aware pseudo-labeling method could influence future research on self-training and semi-supervised learning, pushing for more sophisticated and robust ways to generate and filter pseudo-labels.
- **Cross-Modal Learning Applications:** The principles demonstrated by CDTrans, especially the power of cross-attention for aligning disparate distributions and handling noise, could extend to related areas like multi-modal learning or few-shot learning, where data scarcity or domain differences are key challenges.
- **Open-Sourced Contribution:** The provision of code and models (via GitHub) is a valuable contribution that enables reproducibility, facilitates benchmarking, and lowers the barrier for other researchers and practitioners to build upon this work, accelerating progress in the field.

In conclusion, CDTrans marks a pivotal moment in UDA research by demonstrating the profound advantages of Transformer architectures. It not only achieves superior performance but also offers fundamental insights into overcoming long-standing challenges like pseudo-label noise. This work has undeniably pushed the frontiers of UDA and shed bright light on a promising new direction for the broader domain adaptation community.
