# EA-WM: Event-Aware Generative World Model with Structured Kinematic-to-Visual Action Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> EA-WM, an Event-Aware Generative World Model, generates robotic videos that accurately preserve robot spatial geometry and interaction dynamics. It achieves this by transforming low-dimensional kinematic actions into spatially grounded visual fields and integrating them through an event-aware fusion mechanism, outperforming existing baselines on the WorldArena benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing robotic world models often treat video generation as auxiliary, leading to videos that do not accurately preserve precise robot spatial geometry or complex robot-object interaction dynamics.
- There is a domain misalignment between abstract, low-dimensional control signals (e.g., joint values) and the high-dimensional nature of video synthesis, causing inaccuracies in rendered robot geometry and physical interactions.
- Conventional architectures lack dedicated mechanisms for effective interaction between action and video pathways, often causing generators to overlook fine-grained interaction details.

## Method

- **EA-WM** develops ==Kinematic-to-Visual Action Fields (KVAFs)== that convert low-dimensional robot actions into a spatially grounded visual representation by projecting 3D robot geometry into the camera plane.
- Introduces ==Event-Aware Bidirectional Fusion (EAF)==, sparsely inserted within the **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)** architecture, to facilitate dynamic and targeted information exchange between video and KVAF streams.
- Employs ==Event-Difference Latent Supervision (EDLS)== to guide EAF modules, encouraging them to focus on temporal changes and interaction dynamics by supervising predicted event latents against VAE-encoded frame-difference videos.

## Results

- EA-WM achieved an overall **P3CScore of 76.60** on the WorldArena benchmark, improving by **+5.52 points** over the strongest baseline (CogVideoX), with interaction quality **0.682**, trajectory accuracy **0.430**, and instruction following **0.792**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both KVAFs (**−5.63 P3CScore** drop without them) and the EAF mechanism (**−1.80 P3CScore** drop without it) for maintaining high accuracy in robot motion and interaction dynamics.
- KVAF-conditioned generation achieved the **highest P3CScore of 78.13** across all six selected metrics compared to mainstream action- or trajectory-conditioned baselines, supporting visual action fields as a conditioning interface.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming abstract kinematic control signals into a visually aligned, spatially grounded representation (KVAFs) improves the accuracy of robot trajectory and 3D structure preservation in generated videos.
> - An event-aware fusion mechanism, guided by supervision on temporal differences (EDLS), enables the generative model to effectively allocate attention to critical regions of motion and interaction, enhancing physical consistency.
> - Jointly modeling visual and kinematic streams through structured action fields and dynamic interaction modules yields superior performance in robotic video world modeling, particularly in physics adherence and controllability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research builds upon advancements in video foundation models, which have demonstrated increased capabilities in synthesizing temporally coherent and high-fidelity videos. These models, including latent video diffusion models, space-time diffusion architectures, and diffusion-transformer systems like CogVideoX and the Wan series, learn appearance, motion, and scene evolution priors from large video corpora. This progress has motivated the exploration of video generation models as tools for world simulation, particularly in robotics, where predicting visual state changes based on actions is a fundamental requirement.

Existing robotic world models have adapted these video priors for various functions:
- **Predictive Simulators:** Methods such as those by Rigter et al. and Wu et al. utilize video world models to simulate the visual outcomes of candidate actions, facilitating action-conditioned video prediction, visual planning, model-based reinforcement learning, and fine-grained robot-object interaction simulation.
- **Robot-Learning Infrastructure:** Approaches like those from Jang et al. and Liang et al. leverage generated rollouts to synthesize additional robot trajectories, provide video-based supervision for policy learning, and offer scalable proxies for evaluating Visual-Language-Action (VLA) policies without requiring repeated physical robot executions.
- **World-Action Models (WAMs):** More recent work, exemplified by DreamZero and Zhu et al., integrates video generation and action modeling within unified generative frameworks. These models demonstrate that future videos can offer dense world representations for action generation, policy learning, planning, and value estimation.

Despite these developments, a gap persists in effectively leveraging control signals to guide accurate video synthesis. Current WAMs predominantly treat video generation as an auxiliary component for optimizing action prediction. This emphasis on action prediction often leads to generated videos that do not accurately preserve the precise spatial geometry of robot motion or the complex dynamics of robot-object interactions. This limitation is becoming more critical as the reliance on high-quality video rollouts for predictive simulators and synthetic data engines increases.

A fundamental challenge identified is the domain misalignment between abstract, low-dimensional control signals (e.g., raw joint-space parameters, end-effector vectors, or abstract tokens) and the high-dimensional nature of video synthesis. Such low-dimensional representations often lack explicit spatial context and are closely tied to specific robot embodiments, requiring the video generator to implicitly infer cross-domain kinematics. This can result in inaccuracies in rendering robot geometry or capturing nuanced physical interactions. Furthermore, conventional architectures frequently lack a dedicated mechanism for effective interaction between the action and video pathways, potentially causing the generator to overlook fine-grained interaction details. This paper aims to address these limitations by proposing a method that explicitly aligns action signals with the visual domain and facilitates event-aware interaction between action and video streams.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an Event-Aware Generative World Model (EA-WM) that effectively closes the loop between kinematic control and visual perception to generate robot videos. The model aims to produce videos that are not only visually plausible but also accurately preserve precise robot spatial geometry and fine-grained robot-object interaction dynamics in generated rollouts.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the broader research landscape:
- **Insufficient Exploitation of Action Signals for Video Synthesis:** While recent world-action models (WAMs) jointly optimize future videos and actions, they tend to treat video generation as a secondary representation primarily supporting policy learning or action prediction. This approach leads to an under-exploration of the inverse problem: how to leverage action information to guide and improve the accuracy of video synthesis itself. The consequence is often a degradation in the quality of generated rollouts, specifically regarding robot spatial geometry and interaction dynamics.
- **Degraded Rollout Quality for Downstream Tasks:** High-quality video rollouts are increasingly crucial for applications such as predictive simulators, planning substrates, and policy evaluation environments. When generated videos fail to maintain precise robot geometry and interaction dynamics, their utility in these critical downstream tasks is diminished. Therefore, mitigating this degradation in rollout quality is a key motivation.
- **Domain Misalignment in Action Representations:** Standard practices often inject robot actions as abstract, low-dimensional tokens, such as raw joint-space parameters or end-effector vectors. These representations are computationally compact but carry limited explicit spatial context relevant to the visual domain. This forces the video generator to implicitly deduce complex cross-domain kinematics, frequently resulting in inaccuracies in rendering robot geometry or capturing nuanced physical interactions. The paper is motivated to address this by introducing a representation that is geometrically grounded and aligned with the visual domain.
- **Lack of Effective Interaction Mechanisms:** Conventional world model architectures often lack dedicated and effective interaction mechanisms between the action and video processing pathways. This can lead to the video generator overlooking fine-grained interaction information between the robot and objects, further compromising the physical consistency and realism of generated videos. The motivation is to design an architecture that facilitates continuous and targeted information exchange between these streams.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need to develop a robotic world model that can leverage structured action signals to produce high-fidelity, physically consistent video rollouts that accurately reflect robot motion and robot-object interactions, thereby enhancing the utility of generative world models for various robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EA-WM is an action-guided robotic video world model that builds upon the Wan2.2-TI2V backbone, a diffusion-transformer architecture. The methodology focuses on transforming low-dimensional robot actions into a spatially grounded visual representation and integrating this information through an event-aware fusion mechanism.

**Overall Pipeline:**
Given an initial RGB observation, a language instruction, and a sequence of robot actions, EA-WM's objective is to generate future robot videos that are visually plausible and consistent with the commanded robot motion and interactions. It processes both RGB videos and action-derived visual fields within a shared latent space.

**Structured Kinematic-to-Visual Action Fields (KVAFs):**
Instead of directly injecting low-dimensional actions, EA-WM first converts them into KVAFs.
- **Input Data:** For a given timestep $t$, the system uses arm joint values ($q_t$), gripper states ($g_t$), end-effector poses ($\xi_t$), and camera parameters (intrinsic matrix $K_t$ and world-to-camera extrinsic matrix $E_t$).
- **3D Robot Geometry Recovery:** Forward kinematics is applied to recover the 3D world-frame poses of each robot link ($T^W_k(t)$) from joint values. This yields a set of 3D robot keypoints, including arm skeletons, joint landmarks, and gripper endpoints.
- **Camera Projection:** These 3D keypoints are then projected into the target camera plane using the camera parameters. Points behind the camera are excluded.
- **Visual Rendering:** The projected 2D points are rasterized onto a black canvas to create a visual field $V_t$. This rendering includes depth-aware arm skeletons (where depth is mapped to a color palette), joint landmarks, gripper geometry, end-effector heatmaps (truncated Gaussian heatmaps centered at the end-effector location), and pose axes (rendered in RGB to indicate orientation).
- **Purpose:** KVAFs bring action conditioning into the same image domain as future video generation, providing spatially grounded and temporally aligned motion cues.

**Event-Aware Generative World Model Architecture:**
EA-WM extends the Wan2.2-TI2V backbone.
- **Latent Space Encoding:** Both the RGB video sequence and the constructed KVAF sequence are encoded into a shared latent space using the same pretrained video Variational Autoencoder (VAE). During training, these latents are noised according to a flow-matching process.
- **Dual-Stream Processing:** The noised video latent and KVAF latent are represented as two token streams, $H_v$ and $H_k$. The video stream is processed by the original Wan2.2 Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks, while the KVAF stream is processed by a full-depth copy of these DiT blocks. This maintains the KVAF information as a structured visual stream.
- **Event-Aware Bidirectional Fusion Modules:** These modules are sparsely inserted at selected layers ($S$) within the DiT architecture.
 - **Event MLP:** At each fusion layer $ℓ \in S$, an event Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) computes a shared event representation ($M_ℓ$) from the current video and KVAF tokens. From $M_ℓ$, it predicts a per-token event gate ($G_ℓ$) and an event latent ($\hat{E}_ℓ$).
 - **Gated Cross-Attention:** The event gate $G_ℓ$ modulates bidirectional cross-attention mechanisms between the two streams. This allows video tokens to read from KVAF tokens ($CA_{v \leftarrow k}$) and KVAF tokens to read from video tokens ($CA_{k \leftarrow v}$), facilitating controlled information exchange based on event saliency.
 - **Residual Fusion:** The outputs of the cross-attention are added to their respective stream tokens in a gated residual manner ($H_v \leftarrow H_v + G_ℓ \odot CA_{v \leftarrow k}$, etc.). At non-fusion layers, the streams are updated independently.

**Event-Difference Latent Supervision (EDLS):**
EDLS is introduced to guide the model's attention towards temporal changes and interaction dynamics.
- **Event Latent Target Construction:** A frame-difference video is computed from the input RGB video sequence ($\Delta I_\tau = |I_\tau - I_{\tau-1}|$). This difference sequence is then encoded by the same video VAE to produce the event latent target ($E$).
- **Supervision:** The predicted event latent ($\hat{E}_ℓ$) from the fusion module is supervised against the EDLS target $E$. This supervision encourages the shared event representation ($M_ℓ$) to encode meaningful temporal changes and interaction cues. Consequently, the event gate ($G_ℓ$), derived from the same $M_ℓ$, becomes sensitive to regions undergoing state transitions and interactions, influencing how much information is exchanged between the video and KVAF streams.

**Training Objective:**
The total training objective ($L$) combines three components:
1. Video flow matching loss: Measures the difference between the predicted video latent and its flow-matching target.
2. KVAF flow matching loss: Measures the difference between the predicted KVAF latent and its flow-matching target.
3. EDLS loss: Measures the difference between the unpatchified predicted event latent ($\hat{E}_ℓ$) and the VAE-encoded frame-difference target ($E$).
These components are weighted by a timestep-dependent flow-matching weight $\omega(t)$ and an EDLS strength factor $\lambda_{evt}$.

**Training Stages:**
The training is conducted in two stages:
1. The event-aware fusion modules are frozen, and LoRAs (Low-Rank Adaptations) for the main DiT, KVAF-branch, and KVAF head are trained to stabilize the individual streams.
2. The fusion modules are unfrozen to learn event-aware cross-stream interaction, guided by EDLS.

The model is evaluated on the WorldArena benchmark using the RoboTwin dataset, assessing metrics related to Physics Adherence, 3D Accuracy, and Controllability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EA-WM involved quantitative comparisons, qualitative analysis, and ablation studies on the WorldArena benchmark, utilizing various metrics to assess video perception and functional utility.

**Quantitative Analysis (Table 1):**
EA-WM achieved an overall P3CScore of 76.60, demonstrating an improvement of 5.52 points over the strongest baseline, CogVideoX (71.08). The model exhibited specific gains in metrics related to action-induced motion and interaction:
- **Interaction Quality:** Improved from 0.594 (CogVideoX) to 0.682.
- **Trajectory Accuracy:** Increased from 0.353 (CogVideoX) to 0.430.
- **Instruction Following:** Rose from 0.727 (CogVideoX) to 0.792.
These results suggest that the integration of KVAFs and event-aware bidirectional fusion contributes to better preservation of robot-object interactions, more accurate adherence to commanded motion trajectories, and enhanced consistency with task instructions in generated videos. EA-WM also showed improvements in 3D consistency, with Depth Accuracy increasing by 0.049 and Perspectivity by 0.042 compared to the strongest baselines. Overall, EA-WM outperformed all compared models on five out of six selected metrics, indicating stronger physics adherence, 3D consistency, and controllability in robotic video world modeling.

**Qualitative Analysis (Figure 3):**
Qualitative comparisons across four randomly selected RoboTwin 2.0 tasks illustrated the visual differences. Compared to the Wan2.2 baseline, EA-WM generated videos that were closer to ground truth in terms of interaction fidelity and task progress. Specific observations included:
- More accurate gripper-object contact relations.
- Improved preservation of object identity and local geometry.
- Maintenance of more physically plausible robot poses during manipulation.
This qualitative evidence suggests that EA-WM's joint modeling of KVAF and video streams, coupled with event-aware bidirectional fusion, enables the model to better follow action-induced motion cues and focus on regions of significant object state changes and robot-object interactions.

**Ablation Study (Table 2 & Figure 4):**
The ablation studies examined the contributions of KVAFs and the Event-Aware Fusion (EAF) mechanism.
- **Impact of KVAFs:** Replacing KVAFs with numerical action representations resulted in a P3CScore drop of 5.63 points (from 76.60 to 70.97). This variant showed notable declines in Trajectory Accuracy and Depth Accuracy. This indicates that camera-aligned KVAFs provide valuable geometric motion cues, particularly for preserving robot trajectories and 3D structure. Qualitatively, the variant without KVAFs struggled with accurately modeling grasping trajectories and action-induced motion.
- **Impact of EAF:** Removing the event-aware fusion mechanism (while retaining bidirectional cross-attention) led to a P3CScore drop of 1.80 points (from 76.60 to 74.80). This variant exhibited declines in Interaction Quality and Perspectivity, suggesting that EDLS-guided fusion effectively helps the model attend to motion changes and robot-object interaction regions. Qualitatively, the variant without EAF struggled with preserving object consistency (e.g., relative shape, size, spatial arrangement of blocks) despite inferring grasping routes.
The full EA-WM model, combining both KVAFs and EAF, achieved the best overall P3CScore and demonstrated a more balanced performance across various metrics.

**Additional Analysis (Tables 3 & 4, Figure 5):**
- **Action Recovery from KVAFs:** A heuristic procedure to recover numerical actions from generated KVAF videos showed that KVAFs contain partially recoverable action information. While numerical action prediction from raw actions achieved lower errors (e.g., translation error 0.004) compared to KVAF recovery (0.0155), the analysis indicated that KVAFs encode interpretable spatial and physical information. Qualitative overlays confirmed that generated KVAFs align with the generated robot motion, providing clear visual cues for robot configuration and intent.
- **KVAF-conditioned Generation:** Comparisons with mainstream action- or trajectory-conditioned baselines (e.g., CtrlWorld, IRASim) showed that KVAF-conditioned generation achieved the highest P3CScore of 78.13, outperforming all competitors across all six selected metrics. This finding supports the hypothesis that representing actions as camera-aligned visual fields constitutes a more effective conditioning interface for robotic video world modeling, leading to enhanced physics adherence, 3D consistency, and controllability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers contributions to the field of robotic world modeling through the introduction of EA-WM. The model addresses identified limitations in current approaches, particularly the insufficient utilization of action signals to guide accurate video synthesis and the domain misalignment between low-dimensional control signals and high-dimensional visual generation.

**Key Contributions:**
- **Structured Kinematic-to-Visual Action Fields (KVAFs):** The development of KVAFs provides a method to project low-dimensional kinematic states and actions directly into the target camera view. This approach establishes a geometrically grounded and image-domain aligned representation for action conditioning, which can help resolve the domain misalignment issue observed in previous methods that rely on abstract action tokens.
- **Event-Aware Bidirectional Fusion with EDLS:** The introduction of an event-aware bidirectional fusion mechanism, guided by Event-Difference Latent Supervision (EDLS), enables the model to dynamically allocate attention to critical regions of robot motion, object state transitions, and robot-object interaction dynamics. This mechanism is designed to facilitate targeted information exchange between the visual and kinematic streams, leading to more physically consistent generated rollouts.

**Demonstrated Performance:**
EA-WM demonstrated performance on the WorldArena benchmark, achieving the highest overall P3CScore among compared models. The quantitative and qualitative results indicate improvements in physical adherence, 3D geometric accuracy, and fine-grained controllability of generated robot videos. This suggests that the proposed methodology enhances the model's ability to preserve precise robot spatial geometry and capture complex robot-object interaction dynamics, which are often overlooked when video generation is treated as an auxiliary task.

**Potential Impact:**
The improved accuracy and physical consistency of robot video rollouts generated by EA-WM could have several impacts on robotic research and development:
- **Enhanced Predictive Simulators:** More accurate world models can serve as more reliable predictive simulators for evaluating the visual consequences of robot actions, which is beneficial for visual planning and model-based reinforcement learning.
- **Improved Synthetic Data Generation:** The ability to generate high-fidelity, action-consistent robot trajectories can support the synthesis of additional training data for robot learning algorithms, potentially reducing the need for extensive manual data collection.
- **More Robust Policy Evaluation:** By providing a more accurate representation of robot-object interactions and spatial geometry, EA-WM could contribute to more robust environments for evaluating Visual-Language-Action (VLA) policies.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations. The construction of KVAFs relies on accurate robot kinematics, camera calibration, and synchronized action-state logs. While effective in simulation, real-world deployment could be affected by calibration errors, occlusions, or sensor noise. Additionally, current KVAFs primarily encode robot-centric geometric motion cues, with object-state changes and interactions captured indirectly via event-aware fusion. Extending KVAFs to explicitly include object-centric or contact-aware visual fields is identified as a potential area for future research.

In summary, this work contributes a method designed to improve the fidelity and physical consistency of robotic video generation by integrating structured action information directly into the visual domain and enhancing interaction mechanisms within the world model.
