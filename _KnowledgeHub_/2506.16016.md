# Dual-Objective Reinforcement Learning with Novel Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman Formulations

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces novel Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) formulations to expand reinforcement learning to dual-objective tasks, such as Reach-Always-Avoid and Reach-Reach. The proposed DOHJ-PPO algorithm demonstrates leading performance on continuous control benchmarks, achieving higher total success rates and faster task completion, with notable robustness to environmental stochasticity.

## Problem

- Existing Hamilton-Jacobi Reinforcement Learning (HJ-RL) formulations were limited to single-objective tasks (e.g., simple reach, avoid, or reach-avoid).
- Integrating hard constraints and balancing multiple, often competing, objectives in RL typically requires intricate reward engineering and hyperparameter tuning with methods like Lagrangian-based Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs).
- Challenges in multi-target achievement where rewards are sparse or reaching one goal negatively impacts the ability to reach another.

## Method

- Derived novel ==Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman== (HJB) formulations and value functions for two dual-objective problems: Reach-Always-Avoid (RAA) and Reach-Reach (RR), using state augmentation to capture trajectory history.
- Developed decomposition theorems to break down complex dual-objective problems into compositions of simpler, established HJ-RL problems, allowing for reuse of solutions.
- Introduced DOHJ-PPO (Dual-Objective Hamilton-Jacobi Proximal Policy Optimization), an algorithm that learns composed and decomposed objectives concurrently, utilizing coupled resets and specialized reward functions adapted for non-standard Bellman updates.

## Results

- DOHJ-PPO achieved top-tier performance on diverse continuous control tasks (Hopper, F16, SafetyGym, HalfCheetah), consistently outperforming baselines in achieving total success for both objectives.
- The algorithm demonstrated superior efficiency, reaching both objectives in fewer steps compared to other methods.
- DOHJ-PPO showed robustness to stochastic dynamics, maintaining significant performance advantages (e.g., 8-**22%** peak gap for RAA, over **30%** for RR in HalfCheetah) over baselines under injected noise.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dual-objective problems like RAA and RR can be algebraically decomposed into simpler, sequential or parallel HJ-RL subproblems, which simplifies their solution.
> - State augmentation with auxiliary variables tracking extremal values (e.g., best reward seen, worst penalty seen) is sufficient to make these historically-dependent objectives Markovian.
> - Stochastic relaxations of the Bellman equations, combined with a custom PPO algorithm using specialized advantage estimates, effectively enable learning optimal policies in stochastic, continuous control environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research advances the field of Reinforcement Learning by tackling the challenging problem of satisfying multiple, often competing, objectives under hard constraints. It specifically builds upon and significantly extends the **Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) perspective of dynamic programming (DP) in RL**, a relatively recent development that has shown great promise in safety and target-achievement tasks.

Traditionally, RL primarily relies on discounted-sum objectives, where agents maximize a cumulative sum of rewards. However, this approach can struggle with hard constraints, as satisfying them typically requires careful "reward engineering and parameter tuning," especially in Lagrangian-based methods for Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs). While CMDPs and Multi-Objective RL (MORL) approaches exist, they often focus on maximizing expected discounted sums or finding Pareto-optimal policies for vector-valued rewards. This paper distinguishes itself by focusing on value functions defined by *extremal values* (minimum penalty, maximum reward) over a trajectory, which offer a more direct interpretation of constraint satisfaction.

The foundational HJ-RL works (e.g., Fisac et al., Hsu et al., So et al.) introduced special Bellman equations like the Safety Bellman Equation (SBE) and Reach-Avoid Bellman Equation (RABE). These allowed agents to achieve single objectives such as *Reach* (achieving a reward threshold), *Avoid* (avoiding a penalty threshold), or *Reach-Avoid* (reaching a goal while avoiding obstacles). This current work generalizes these single-objective formulations to **compositional, dual-objective problems**, which is a crucial step forward.

The authors also contrast their approach with **Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) in RL**. LTL-RL methods typically involve augmenting the state space with an automaton representing the LTL specification, leading to complex Non-Markovian Reward Decision Processes (NMRDPs). While the problems addressed (Reach-Always-Avoid and Reach-Reach) can be described using LTL, this paper offers a "fundamentally different" approach. Instead of automaton-based state augmentation for general LTL, it derives explicit, tractable Bellman forms through *decomposition*, directly leveraging existing HJ-RL solutions for subproblems. This distinction is crucial, as the paper argues that the algebraic properties of TL predicates do not directly translate to value function decompositions for optimal control, necessitating their rigorous theoretical justifications.

In summary, this work carves out a unique niche by:
- **Generalizing HJ-RL**: Moving from single-objective to dual-objective satisfaction within the HJ-RL framework.
- **Direct Constraint Satisfaction**: Offering a principled approach to hard constraints that avoids the tuning overhead of Lagrangian methods.
- **Alternative to LTL-RL**: Providing a decomposition-based approach that is distinct from automaton-based LTL-RL for specific compositional tasks.
- **Focus on Extremal Values**: Emphasizing the significance of worst-case penalties and best-case rewards over a trajectory, critical for safety-critical applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to extend the capabilities of Hamilton-Jacobi Reinforcement Learning to address more complex, compositional tasks involving the simultaneous satisfaction of multiple objectives and hard constraints. The authors identify a limitation in existing HJ-RL formulations, which are predominantly confined to single-objective scenarios like simple reach, avoid, or reach-avoid problems.

Specifically, the paper introduces and aims to solve two novel dual-objective problems:

1. **The Reach-Always-Avoid (RAA) Problem**: This problem requires an agent to achieve a distinct reward threshold (reach a goal) *and* continuously avoid a penalty threshold (avoid a hazard), even after the initial goal has been met.
 - **Motivation**: Many real-world applications demand not just reaching a goal safely, but maintaining safety thereafter. For instance, an autonomous drone might need to enter a specific airspace (goal) and then continuously avoid no-fly zones or collisions (always avoid) during its subsequent operations. The F16 example in the paper, where the jet must enter desired airspace without crashing *afterward*, vividly illustrates this need. Traditional Reach-Avoid formulations might only ensure avoidance *until* the goal is reached, not perpetually.

2. **The Reach-Reach (RR) Problem**: This problem focuses on achieving thresholds for two distinct rewards.
 - **Motivation**: Agents often need to accomplish multiple objectives sequentially or concurrently. A robot might need to visit two different waypoints, or a delivery vehicle might have two distinct drop-off locations. The Hopper example, where the agent needs to jump into one target and then achieve a second target, highlights the challenge of chaining objectives. Standard RL often struggles with such multi-target achievement, especially if rewards are sparse or if reaching one goal negatively impacts the ability to reach another.

The overarching motivation stems from the inherent difficulties of incorporating **hard constraints** and **dual-objective satisfaction** into RL. Existing methods, like Lagrangian-based constrained RL (CMDPs), often necessitate "intricate reward engineering and parameter tuning," making them cumbersome for real-world deployment. By leveraging the HJ framework's focus on extremal values, the authors aim to provide a more direct and robust solution where the value function itself inherently reflects the satisfaction of hard constraints, thereby reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning. Their goal is to develop "new perspectives and performant tools for constrained decision-making" that are more aligned with mission-critical applications where failure is not an option.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around establishing novel Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) formulations for the RAA and RR problems and then developing a practical deep reinforcement learning algorithm to solve them.

#### 3.1. Novel Value Functions and State Augmentation

The authors define the RAA and RR value functions based on extremal values over an agent's trajectory:
- **RAA Value Function**: $V^{\pi}_{RAA}(s) = \min \{ \max_t r(s^{\pi}_t), \min_t q(s^{\pi}_t) \}$
- **RR Value Function**: $V^{\pi}_{RR}(s) = \min \{ \max_t r_1(s^{\pi}_t), \max_t r_2(s^{\pi}_t) \}$
These formulations inherently capture the worst-case outcome between the objectives, ensuring dual-satisfaction. A crucial insight is the need for **state augmentation**. Unlike standard MDPs where the optimal policy depends only on the current state, these objectives (min/max over time) make the optimal action dependent on the trajectory history. To address this, the authors augment the state $S$ with auxiliary variables:
- For RAA: $(y_t, z_t)$ track $y_t = \max_{\tau \le t} r(s^{\pi}_\tau)$ (best reward seen so far) and $z_t = \min_{\tau \le t} q(s^{\pi}_\tau)$ (worst penalty seen so far).
- For RR: $(y_t, z_t)$ track $y_t = \max_{\tau \le t} r_1(s^{\pi}_\tau)$ and $z_t = \max_{\tau \le t} r_2(s^{\pi}_\tau)$.
They rigorously prove (Theorem 3) that this specific choice of augmentation is sufficient, meaning no further historical information could improve the optimal policy's performance.

#### 3.2. Value Decomposition Theorems

The central theoretical contribution lies in decomposing these complex dual-objective problems into compositions of previously studied, simpler HJ-RL problems. This significantly simplifies the problem by allowing the reuse of established solutions.
- **RAA Decomposition (Theorem 1)**: The RAA problem for $q(s)$ and $r(s)$ can be solved by first finding the optimal value function for an *Avoid* problem ($V^*_A(s)$) solely based on $q(s)$. Then, the RAA problem is equivalent to a *Reach-Avoid* problem using the original penalty $q(s)$ and a *modified reward function* $r_{RAA}(s) := \min \{r(s), V^*_A(s)\}$. This elegantly combines an "always avoid" objective with a "reach while avoiding" objective.
- **RR Decomposition (Theorem 2)**: The RR problem for $r_1(s)$ and $r_2(s)$ can be solved by first finding the optimal value functions for two separate *Reach* problems ($V^*_{R1}(s)$ and $V^*_{R2}(s)$) for each reward. Subsequently, the RR problem is equivalent to a single *Reach* problem with a *modified reward function* $r_{RR}(s) := \max \{ \min \{r_1(s), V^*_{R2}(s)\}, \min \{r_2(s), V^*_{R1}(s)\} \}$. This decomposition captures the "reach either-or" nature effectively.
These theorems prove that the optimal policies for the decomposed problems can be composed to yield an optimal policy for the original dual-objective problem.

#### 3.3. Stochastic Relaxations and Policy Gradient

Recognizing that real-world applications often involve stochasticity and that most performant RL methods are stochastic, the authors extend the deterministic Bellman equations to their stochastic counterparts.
- They generalize the Stochastic Reachability Bellman Equation (SRBE) to a **Stochastic Reach-Avoid Bellman Equation (SRABE)**. This involves replacing the deterministic max/min operations over future values with expectations over actions drawn from a stochastic policy $\pi$.
- A discounted version of SRABE is used to ensure contraction and facilitate learning.
- Theorem 4 provides theoretical justification, demonstrating that the maximum of the stochastic value function (estimated by SRABE) corresponds to the optimal deterministic value function in the limit.
- A policy gradient result is derived for the SRABE, crucial for optimizing the policy via gradient-based methods like PPO, involving a modified dynamics function to handle the non-linearities introduced by the min/max operations.

#### 3.4. DOHJ-PPO Algorithm

To practically solve these problems, the authors propose **DOHJ-PPO (Dual-Objective Hamilton-Jacobi Proximal Policy Optimization)**, a custom variant of PPO:
- **Concurrent Learning**: Instead of solving decomposed objectives sequentially, DOHJ-PPO learns the composed and decomposed objectives *simultaneously* using separate actor and critic networks for each.
- **Coupled Resets**: To ensure the decomposed policies are relevant to the composed task, trajectories for training decomposed actors/critics are initialized with states sampled from the *composed* trajectories. This "coupled reset" mechanism is critical for performance.
- **Specialized Reward Functions**: The modified rewards ($r_{RAA}$ and $r_{RR}$) derived from the decomposition theorems are used. These are dynamically computed using the current (bootstrapped) values from the decomposed critics.
- **Bellman-GAE**: The algorithm incorporates Generalized Advantage Estimates (GAE) that are adapted for the non-standard Bellman updates (Reach, Avoid, Reach-Avoid) to provide more stable and accurate advantage estimates for policy updates.

This comprehensive approach, combining rigorous theoretical decomposition with practical algorithmic design (DOHJ-PPO), forms the backbone of their solution for dual-objective HJ-RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical evidence across various environments to support its theoretical contributions and the efficacy of DOHJ-PPO.

#### 4.1. DQN Grid-World Demonstration (Figure 2)

- **Qualitative Validation**: In a simple 2D grid-world, using Deep Q-Networks (DQN) with the novel Bellman formulations, the authors qualitatively demonstrate the distinct behaviors induced by RAA and RR objectives compared to simpler Reach (R) and Reach-Avoid (RA) tasks.
- **RAA vs. RA**: RAA policies successfully guide the agent to a goal while ensuring perpetual avoidance of hazards, even after reaching the goal. In contrast, RA policies might lead the agent to regions where future collisions are inevitable once the goal is reached, as their objective is only to avoid *until* the goal is met.
- **RR vs. R**: RR policies successfully guide the agent to visit *both* distinct targets, demonstrating true multi-goal achievement. Simple R policies would cease activity or prioritize only one target. This confirms the desired behavioral distinctions predicted by the theory.

#### 4.2. Continuous Control Tasks with DOHJ-PPO (Figure 4, Figure 6 in Appendix)

- **Environments**: DOHJ-PPO was evaluated on challenging continuous control tasks, including Hopper, F16, SafetyGym, and HalfCheetah, involving both RAA and RR objectives.
- **Baselines**: A wide array of state-of-the-art baselines were used for comparison, including various Lagrangian-based constrained RL methods (CPPO, PPO-LAG, P2BPO, LOGBAR), existing HJ-RL baselines (RESPO, RCPPO, RA), and methods inspired by STL/LTL-RL and MORL (D-STL PPO, SPARSE, MORL-based PPO).
- **Performance Metrics**: Success rate (achieving both objectives), Partial Success rate (achieving at least one objective), and Mean Steps to Success (speed of achievement).
- **Key Findings**:
 - **Dominant Performance**: DOHJ-PPO consistently achieved top-tier performance, often securing first or second place across all tasks and environments.
 - **Total Success**: As the complexity of dual-objective tasks increased (e.g., HalfCheetah), DOHJ-PPO's dominance became more pronounced, significantly outperforming baselines in achieving *total success* (both objectives). Most baselines could achieve partial success at a high rate, but struggled with balancing and fully satisfying both objectives.
 - **Efficiency**: DOHJ-PPO demonstrated faster achievement times, reaching both objectives in the fewest number of steps compared to baselines (Appendix Figure 6).
 - **Versatility**: DOHJ-PPO was the sole performant algorithm in *both* RAA and RR task categories, highlighting its general applicability to different forms of dual-objective satisfaction.

#### 4.3. Comparison in Stochastic Dynamics (Figure 5)

- **Robustness Test**: To assess robustness, DOHJ-PPO was tested in the HalfCheetah environment with affine Gaussian noise injected into the dynamics, simulating real-world stochasticity.
- **RAA Task**: DOHJ-PPO showed significant performance improvements (8-22% peak gap) over top baselines at moderate noise levels, only reaching equally poor performance as others at very high noise.
- **RR Task**: DOHJ-PPO exhibited even more substantial dominance, with over 30% improvement in peak performance in most high-noise regimes, and still over 15% better than the best baseline in the moderate noise case.
- **Validation of SRABE**: These results empirically validate the use of SRBE and SRABE approximations, demonstrating that DOHJ-PPO's design, which accounts for stochastic policies, effectively handles certain levels of dynamic randomness.

Overall, the experimental results robustly support the theoretical claims, showcasing that DOHJ-PPO provides a direct, robust, and performant solution for complex dual-objective tasks, particularly those involving hard constraints, without requiring extensive tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in reinforcement learning, particularly for applications requiring rigorous safety guarantees and multi-objective satisfaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Extension of Hamilton-Jacobi Reinforcement Learning**: The paper successfully generalizes the powerful HJ-RL framework from simpler single-objective (Reach, Avoid, Reach-Avoid) tasks to more complex, compositional dual-objective problems like Reach-Always-Avoid (RAA) and Reach-Reach (RR). This theoretical extension opens the door for HJ-RL to address a much broader array of real-world challenges in control and decision-making.

2. **Principled Approach to Hard Constraints and Dual-Satisfaction**: By introducing novel value functions and Bellman formulations that inherently capture "worst-case" or "best-case" outcomes over time, the work provides a more direct and intuitive way to enforce hard constraints and ensure dual-objective satisfaction. This stands in contrast to conventional methods that rely on intricate reward engineering or tuning of Lagrangian multipliers, making the approach more robust and easier to implement in mission-critical systems.

3. **Enhanced Safety and Multi-Target Achievement in Practice**: DOHJ-PPO, the proposed algorithm, demonstrates superior performance in balancing and achieving multiple objectives across various continuous control tasks, including challenging robotic and aerospace environments (Hopper, F16, SafetyGym, HalfCheetah). Its ability to achieve higher success rates and faster task completion, especially for total satisfaction of both objectives, is crucial for applications where both safety and goal completion are paramount.

4. **Robustness to Stochasticity**: The empirical validation under stochastic dynamics is a key strength. By incorporating stochastic relaxations (SRBE and SRABE) into the Bellman formulations, the method shows remarkable resilience to noise, indicating its practical applicability in uncertain environments. This makes the theoretical framework more relevant for real-world deployment where perfect deterministic dynamics are rare.

5. **Theoretical Foundation and Roadmap for Future Work**: The derivation of decomposition theorems is a major theoretical contribution, providing a "roadmap" for extending complex Bellman formulations. This suggests a powerful paradigm for breaking down high-level, complex objectives into a graph of interconnected, simpler HJ-Bellman values. This foundational work paves the way for future research into iteratively decomposing even more elaborate temporal logic specifications, potentially leading to more sophisticated and verifiable behaviors in autonomous systems.

6. **Broader Impacts on Safe RL**: As a method designed for Safe Reinforcement Learning, the work directly contributes to preventing undesirable behaviors in autonomous systems. This has positive societal implications for areas such as self-driving cars (avoiding crashes), robotics (safe human-robot interaction), and even AI agents in digital environments (adhering to ethical guidelines). The authors acknowledge the dual-use potential of safety methods but anticipate a net positive impact similar to other Safe RL advancements.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust theoretical framework and a highly performant algorithm for solving complex dual-objective tasks with hard constraints. By building on and significantly extending the HJ-RL paradigm, it offers a powerful new toolset for developing safer, more capable, and more intelligent autonomous systems, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in constrained reinforcement learning.
