# Reinforcing General Reasoning without Verifiers

> [!summary] Summary
> VeriFree proposes a novel approach to training large language models (LLMs) for general reasoning that eliminates the need for explicit verifiers by directly optimizing the likelihood of a reference answer conditioned on the model's generated reasoning trace. The method achieves substantial accuracy gains on challenging general reasoning benchmarks like MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA, and demonstrates improved learning efficiency and transferability of reasoning skills to diverse domains.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods, like DeepSeek-R1-Zero, are limited to domains with rule-based verifiers (e.g., math, code).
- General reasoning domains (e.g., chemistry, law) lack precise verifiers, forcing reliance on computationally expensive and potentially biased model-based verifiers.
- Model-based verifiers introduce issues like dependence on a strong verifier LLM, susceptibility to reward hacking, and significant computational overhead during training.

## Method

- ==VeriFree== re-conceptualizes the RL objective to directly maximize the probability of the reference answer given the input and the model's self-generated reasoning trace, acting as an ==implicit reward==.
- The objective is analytically transformed to remove the need for an external verifier, leveraging the model's own ==likelihood of the correct answer given its reasoning== as the reward signal.

## Results

- VeriFree improved average accuracy on MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA by **1**2%** to 40%** over base Qwen3 models (1.7B, 4B, 8B), matching or surpassing instruct models and a model-based verifier baseline.
- The method demonstrated faster convergence and higher final accuracy compared to the `Verifier` baseline, showing improved learning efficiency.
- VeriFree-trained models exhibited strong transferability, improving performance on math reasoning benchmarks even without any math-specific training examples in their dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The standard reinforcement learning objective for reasoning can be precisely recovered by maximizing the conditional probability of a reference answer given a generated reasoning trace, eliminating the need for explicit verifiers.
> - This verifier-free approach yields lower variance gradients (due to Rao-Blackwellization) and provides a continuous reward signal, leading to more stable and efficient policy learning.
> - Weighting contributions from generated reasoning traces by their likelihood of producing the correct answer effectively down-weights low-quality reasoning, which is crucial for performance.
