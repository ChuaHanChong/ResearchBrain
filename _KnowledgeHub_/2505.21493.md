# Reinforcing General Reasoning without Verifiers

> [!summary] Summary
> VeriFree proposes a novel approach to training large language models (LLMs) for general reasoning that eliminates the need for explicit verifiers by directly optimizing the likelihood of a reference answer conditioned on the model's generated reasoning trace. The method achieves substantial accuracy gains on challenging general reasoning benchmarks like MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA, and demonstrates improved learning efficiency and transferability of reasoning skills to diverse domains.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods, like DeepSeek-R1-Zero, are limited to domains with rule-based verifiers (e.g., math, code).
- General reasoning domains (e.g., chemistry, law) lack precise verifiers, forcing reliance on computationally expensive and potentially biased model-based verifiers.
- Model-based verifiers introduce issues like dependence on a strong verifier LLM, susceptibility to reward hacking, and significant computational overhead during training.

## Method

- ==VeriFree== re-conceptualizes the RL objective to directly maximize the probability of the reference answer given the input and the model's self-generated reasoning trace, acting as an ==implicit reward==.
- The objective is analytically transformed to remove the need for an external verifier, leveraging the model's own ==likelihood of the correct answer given its reasoning== as the reward signal.

## Results

- VeriFree improved average accuracy on MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA by **1**2%** to 40%** over base Qwen3 models (1.7B, 4B, 8B), matching or surpassing instruct models and a model-based verifier baseline.
- The method demonstrated faster convergence and higher final accuracy compared to the `Verifier` baseline, showing improved learning efficiency.
- VeriFree-trained models exhibited strong transferability, improving performance on math reasoning benchmarks even without any math-specific training examples in their dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The standard reinforcement learning objective for reasoning can be precisely recovered by maximizing the conditional probability of a reference answer given a generated reasoning trace, eliminating the need for explicit verifiers.
> - This verifier-free approach yields lower variance gradients (due to Rao-Blackwellization) and provides a continuous reward signal, leading to more stable and efficient policy learning.
> - Weighting contributions from generated reasoning traces by their likelihood of producing the correct answer effectively down-weights low-quality reasoning, which is crucial for performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical limitation in the rapidly advancing field of Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, particularly concerning the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for performance improvement. The recent "DeepSeek-R1-Zero-style" training paradigm, which leverages RL with *verifiable rewards* (RLVR), has demonstrated remarkable success in enhancing LLM capabilities in domains like mathematical reasoning and code generation. This success is primarily attributed to the availability of rule-based programs or test cases that can definitively verify the correctness of an LLM's output, allowing for a binary (correct/incorrect) reward signal.

However, the broader research landscape faces a significant challenge: applying this highly effective RLVR paradigm to *general reasoning domains*. Fields such as chemistry, healthcare, engineering, law, biology, business, and economics lack the precise, rule-based verification mechanisms present in math or coding. This absence of a "ground truth verifier" has prevented the widespread adoption of R1-Zero-style training in these crucial areas.

Current attempts to bridge this gap typically involve using an *additional LLM as a model-based verifier*. This approach, similar to the reward models used in RL from Human Feedback (RLHF), tries to approximate verification. While providing a workaround, these model-based verifiers introduce several drawbacks:
- **Reliance on a strong verifier LLM**: The performance of the training heavily depends on the quality and strength of this separate verifier model, which itself is a complex LLM that needs to be trained.
- **Susceptibility to reward hacking**: The LLM being trained might learn to exploit the verifier's biases or limitations, optimizing for the proxy reward rather than true reasoning ability.
- **Computational burden**: Maintaining an additional large model in memory and querying it during training significantly increases computational overhead and memory requirements, making the process less efficient and scalable.

"Reinforcing General Reasoning without Verifiers" introduces **VeriFree**, a novel approach that fundamentally rethinks the RLVR framework to circumvent the need for *any* explicit verifier—be it rule-based or model-based. By demonstrating that the objective of RLVR can be equivalently optimized by directly maximizing the likelihood of a reference answer conditioned on a generated reasoning trace, VeriFree offers a conceptually simpler, computationally more efficient, and potentially more robust alternative.

The paper positions VeriFree against other "verifier-free" or latent variable optimization approaches, such as JLB and LaTRO. While these methods also aim to learn reasoning without explicit verification by treating reasoning as a latent variable, VeriFree distinguishes itself by showing that it *exactly recovers the original verifier-based objective in expectation* under a single-correct-answer assumption. The authors hypothesize that this precise recovery, combined with a crucial weighting mechanism that down-weights contributions from low-quality reasoning traces, is why VeriFree consistently outperforms these prior approaches and even matches or surpasses traditional verifier-based baselines. This work represents a significant step towards democratizing and extending advanced reasoning capabilities in LLMs across diverse real-world domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a verifier-free method for training Large Language Models (LLMs) with reinforcement learning (RL) that effectively enhances general reasoning capabilities, thereby extending the success of DeepSeek-R1-Zero-style training beyond domains with rule-based verifiable rewards.**

The motivation behind this objective stems from several critical limitations and challenges observed in the current landscape of LLM training for reasoning:

1. **Limited Applicability of Current RLVR Paradigms**: While methods like DeepSeek-R1-Zero have achieved impressive advancements in reasoning for specific tasks (e.g., code, mathematics), their reliance on "rule-based verifiable rewards" severely restricts their applicability. Real-world domains such as chemistry, healthcare, engineering, law, biology, business, and economics, which profoundly benefit from advanced reasoning, typically lack such precise, rule-based verification mechanisms. The authors are motivated to unlock these broader domains for powerful RL-based training.

2. **Drawbacks of Model-Based Verifiers**: The existing workaround to the lack of rule-based verifiers in general domains involves using an additional LLM as a "model-based verifier." This approach, though functional, introduces significant practical and theoretical issues:
 - **Dependency on a Strong Verifier**: It necessitates the availability and training of a separate, high-performing verifier LLM, which itself is a complex and resource-intensive task.
 - **Vulnerability to Reward Hacking**: Optimizing against a model-based reward can lead to "reward hacking," where the trained LLM learns to generate outputs that please the verifier but do not genuinely reflect improved reasoning.
 - **High Computational Overhead**: Maintaining and querying an additional LLM in memory during training incurs substantial computational and memory costs, making the training process less efficient and scalable. The authors are motivated to bypass these practical burdens.

3. **Desire for Simpler and More Robust Training**: The authors aim to devise a training methodology that is not only effective but also simpler, more robust, and computationally less demanding than current alternatives. By eliminating the explicit verifier, VeriFree seeks to streamline the training pipeline and reduce potential sources of instability or fragility inherent in multi-model systems.

In essence, the research is driven by the ambition to generalize the profound benefits of RL-based reasoning enhancement to a wider array of real-world problems by fundamentally reimagining how reward signals are derived, moving away from explicit verification towards an implicit, probabilistic approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VeriFree proposes a novel verifier-free approach to reinforcement learning for LLMs by directly maximizing the probability of generating the reference answer given the question and the model's own reasoning trace. This contrasts with traditional RLVR (like R1-Zero) that samples a full response (reasoning + answer) and then externally verifies the generated answer.

**Core Idea and Theoretical Foundation:**
The method begins by re-evaluating the standard RL objective for LLMs, `J_Verifier(θ; x, y*) = E_{z~πθ(·|x)} [E_{y~πθ(·|x,z)} [R_Verifier(y; y*)]]`, where `R_Verifier` is a binary reward (1 if correct, 0 otherwise) for a generated answer `y` against a ground-truth `y*`.
VeriFree's key insight is that, assuming a unique correct answer, this objective can be analytically transformed. By marginalizing out the generated answer `y`, the expected reward given a reasoning trace `z` simplifies to `πθ(y*|x,z)` – the probability that the model assigns to the reference answer `y*` conditioned on the input `x` and the generated reasoning trace `z`. This probability `πθ(y*|x,z)` becomes the "reward" signal, `R_VeriFree`.
The corresponding gradient estimator, `∇θ J_VeriFree`, then naturally decomposes into two terms:
1. **Reasoning Term**: `πθ(y*|x,z) * ∇θ log πθ(z|x)`. This acts as a policy gradient, where the reasoning trace `z` is rewarded by the likelihood of producing the correct answer given that trace.
2. **Reference Answer Term**: `πθ(y*|x,z) * ∇θ log πθ(y*|x,z)`. This is a reward-weighted supervised learning term, pushing the model to increase the probability of the reference answer `y*` given `z`.

**Advantages of VeriFree's Approach:**
- **Verifier-Free**: Eliminates the need for any external verification component (rule-based or model-based), simplifying the training pipeline and removing associated computational and robustness issues.
- **Lower Variance**: The analytical marginalization of `y` (the generated answer) results in a policy gradient estimator with lower variance compared to verifier-based methods. This is proven via Rao-Blackwellization, leading to more stable and efficient learning.
- **Effective Reward Shaping**: The `πθ(y*|x,z)` acts as a continuous, differentiable reward signal, providing richer feedback than the binary 0/1 reward of traditional verifiers.

**Practical Implementation Details:**
- **Variance Reduction**: VeriFree is compatible with and utilizes techniques like RLOO (Reinforce Leave-One-Out baseline) for the reasoning term and corrected response-length normalization to further stabilize training.
- **Tokenization Handling**: A critical practical challenge is the precise separation of the reasoning trace `z` and the answer `y` (or `y*`). Instead of naive text-based splitting, VeriFree defines the end of `z` at the token corresponding to `<answer` (without the closing `>`). This ensures tokenization consistency between sampling and optimization, preventing off-policy mismatches and training instability.
- **Training Pipeline**:
 - **Zero-Shot Fine-tuning**: The method directly fine-tunes base LLMs (Qwen3-1.7B, 4B, 8B) without an intermediate supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage, adhering to the "zero" setting from DeepSeek-R1-Zero.
 - **No KL Regularization**: Unlike many RL-based LLM alignment methods, VeriFree does not employ KL regularization losses or penalties, removing the need to maintain a separate reference model in memory.
 - **Rollout**: For each question, the policy model generates 8 responses (`group_size=8`). These responses are then parsed, the model's generated answer is replaced with the dataset's reference answer, and a single forward pass computes `πθ(y*|x,z)`. This forward pass is computationally efficient as it does not involve autoregressive decoding.
 - **Hardware**: Training is performed on a single node with 8x H100 GPUs.

**Dataset and Evaluation:**
- **Training Data**: A curated dataset called "WebData" (approx. 61,000 samples) derived from WebInstruct, filtered for quality using Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct. This dataset covers diverse general reasoning domains.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks**:
 - **General Reasoning**: MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, GPQA. These are challenging multiple-choice question-answering benchmarks across various disciplines.
 - **Math Reasoning (Transferability)**: MATH-500, OlympiadBench, Minerva Math, GSM8K, AMC, and AIME24. Math-Verify is used for answer checking.
- **Baselines**: Compared against a "Verifier" baseline (a model-based verifier trained on Gemini 2.0 Flash data using Dr. GRPO), various Qwen3 base and instruct models, and other prominent open-source RL-tuned models like General-Reasoner-7B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Oat-Zero.

**Comparison to Related Work (JLB, LaTRO):**
The paper explicitly contrasts VeriFree with JLB and LaTRO, which also explore verifier-free objectives by treating reasoning as a latent variable. VeriFree's key differentiator is its exact recovery of the original RLVR objective in expectation, whereas JLB and LaTRO optimize subtly different objectives (e.g., using `log πθ(y*|x,z)` as a reward). Crucially, VeriFree weights the "reference answer term" by `πθ(y*|x,z)`, which intelligently discounts updates from low-quality reasoning traces. JLB and LaTRO, by contrast, use a fixed weight of 1, potentially reinforcing flawed reasoning if it happens to lead to the correct answer. This nuanced difference is hypothesized to be a key reason for VeriFree's superior empirical performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the strong effectiveness and practical advantages of VeriFree in enhancing the general reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

1. **Significant Improvement in General Reasoning Capabilities**:
 - VeriFree consistently yields substantial gains in average accuracy across Qwen3 base models (1.7B, 4B, 8B) on the MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA benchmarks. Improvements range from 12% to 40% over the base models, showcasing its efficacy in fine-tuning LLMs for general reasoning.
 - On both MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA, VeriFree-tuned models achieve performance comparable to, and often surpass, both the instruct models (including their "thinking" mode) and the `Verifier` baseline (which relies on an external model-based verifier). This is a critical finding, as VeriFree achieves this without any explicit verification.

2. **Superior Learning Efficiency**:
 - When compared directly against the `Verifier` baseline, VeriFree consistently demonstrates faster convergence and higher final accuracy (Figure 4, Left). This improved learning efficiency is attributed to VeriFree's continuous reward signals and its lower gradient variance (due to Rao-Blackwellization), which provide more stable and informative policy gradients.

3. **Model Confidence as an Effective Proxy for Reasoning**:
 - An analysis on the Qwen3-8B base model reveals a strong positive correlation (ρ=0.82) between the MMLU-Pro evaluation accuracy and the average model confidence `πθ(y*|x,z)` (the VeriFree reward signal) during training (Figure 4, Right). This empirically validates that the model's self-estimated confidence in generating the correct answer, given its reasoning, serves as a reliable proxy for its emergent reasoning capabilities.

4. **Transferability of Reasoning Skills**:
 - To assess the generality of learned reasoning, a VeriFree model was trained *without any math-related examples* in its dataset. This model not only improved on general reasoning tasks but also exhibited strong transfer to math-specific benchmarks (Figure 5). This highlights VeriFree's ability to induce foundational reasoning capabilities that generalize across diverse domains, rather than merely memorizing domain-specific patterns.

5. **Ablation Study Insights**:
 - **Importance of Tokenization Strategy**: The precise, tokenization-aware splitting of reasoning traces from answers (ending at `<answer` instead of `<answer>`) is crucial for training stability and performance. Text-based splitting leads to significant instability and poorer convergence (Figure 6, Left), underscoring the importance of careful handling of token boundaries in LLM training.
 - **Effectiveness of RLOO**: Removing the RLOO (Reinforce Leave-One-Out) baseline from VeriFree results in a consistent performance drop of over 3% (Figure 6, Left). This confirms that RLOO is vital for stabilizing learning and guiding the model towards better generalization.
 - **Equivalence Class Impact**: While VeriFree primarily focuses on maximizing the probability of a *single* reference answer, an ablation study explored incorporating "equivalence classes" (multiple valid answers). This modification yielded only *slight performance improvements* on math benchmarks (Figure 6, Right). This suggests that using a single reference answer is already highly effective and provides a sufficient learning signal, though future work might explore better leveraging answer equivalence.

In summary, the results position VeriFree as a highly competitive and practically advantageous method for RL-tuning LLMs for general reasoning, offering strong performance, improved efficiency, and broad applicability without the complexities of external verifiers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Reinforcing General Reasoning without Verifiers" carries significant implications for the future development and application of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in extending their advanced reasoning capabilities to a much wider array of real-world problems.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Paradigm Shift in RL for LLMs**: The most profound significance lies in introducing a truly verifier-free paradigm for R1-Zero-style reinforcement learning. By demonstrating the gradient equivalence between explicit verification and maximizing the conditional probability of a reference answer, VeriFree offers a conceptually elegant and practically superior alternative. This moves beyond the limitations of rule-based systems and the complexities of model-based verifiers that have constrained the application of powerful RL techniques.

2. **Democratization and Accessibility**: By eliminating the need for strong, specialized verifier LLMs and their associated computational overhead (e.g., maintaining an additional large model in memory during training), VeriFree significantly lowers the barriers to entry for developing advanced reasoning systems. This democratization allows researchers and practitioners with more modest computational resources to leverage sophisticated RL alignment techniques, potentially leading to broader innovation across various domains.

3. **Enhanced Practicality and Robustness**: The method offers substantial practical benefits:
 - **Reduced Compute Requirements**: Training with VeriFree is simpler and less memory-intensive, making it more efficient and scalable.
 - **Increased Robustness**: It bypasses issues like reward hacking (inherent in model-based verifiers) and intricate tokenization challenges, leading to more stable and reliable training.
 - **Simpler Pipeline**: A unified model handles both reasoning and "implicit verification," streamlining the overall training process.

4. **Superior Performance and Generalizability**: Despite its conceptual simplicity and practical advantages, VeriFree is shown to match or even surpass the performance of more complex verifier-based methods and strong instruct models on challenging general reasoning benchmarks like MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, and GPQA. Its demonstrated ability to learn general, transferable reasoning skills (even improving on math tasks without explicit math training) is crucial for building truly versatile AI assistants.

5. **Theoretical Contributions**: The formal derivation of gradient equivalence and the proof of lower variance (via Rao-Blackwellization) provide a solid theoretical underpinning for VeriFree's empirical success. This contributes to a deeper understanding of how LLMs can be effectively trained through self-supervision or implicit signals.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated AI in Diverse Fields**: VeriFree opens the door for significant advancements in LLM applications across fields previously underserved by RL-based reasoning, including healthcare diagnostics, legal analysis, scientific discovery (e.g., chemistry, biology), complex engineering problem-solving, and sophisticated economic modeling.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work offers a fresh perspective for the LLM RL community, providing a robust baseline and inspiring further research into implicit reward mechanisms, more efficient alignment techniques, and the development of even more general-purpose reasoners.
- **Ethical Considerations**: As LLMs gain more general reasoning capabilities, the paper rightly highlights the need for responsible usage, transparent reporting of limitations, and continued focus on value alignment and safeguards against misuse. The increased power of these models necessitates concurrent development of ethical guidelines and control mechanisms.

In conclusion, VeriFree represents a significant step forward in LLM alignment, demonstrating that powerful reasoning capabilities can be cultivated through a simpler, more efficient, and robust verifier-free RL approach. This innovation has the potential to democratize access to advanced AI reasoning and accelerate its integration into a multitude of real-world domains.
