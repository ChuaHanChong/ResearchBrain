# RynnWorld-Teleop: An Action-Conditioned World Model for Digital Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from DAMO Academy introduced RynnWorld-Teleop, an action-conditioned world model that enables "digital teleoperation" for robotic manipulation. This system synthesizes high-fidelity, robot-centric egocentric videos from human hand gestures in real-time, generating scalable, action-grounded data that improves robot policy learning, including zero-shot Sim2Real transfer and boosting success rates by up to 20% on complex tasks.

## Problem

- Robot learning struggles with the scarcity of diverse, large-scale trajectory data needed to train robust, general-purpose agents.
- Traditional physical teleoperation is resource-intensive, constrained by hardware availability, fixed environments, and manual resets, which limits data diversity and throughput.
- Prior action-conditioned video generation models are often human-centric or passive, failing to provide action-grounded robotic movements or bridge the "embodiment gap" for effective robot control.

## Method

- Developed **RynnWorld-Teleop**, an ==action-conditioned world model== that enables "digital teleoperation" by synthesizing robot-centric egocentric videos from an operator's ==depth-aware skeletal hand-pose== stream, conditioned on a single reference image via a ==Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== and ==conditional flow matching==.
- Employs a ==progressive cross-domain training== paradigm, pre-training on large-scale human egocentric videos and then fine-tuning on robot teleoperation data to transfer dexterous manipulation skills.
- Utilizes ==streaming autoregressive distillation== (causal flow-matching warm-up + ==Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD)==) to transform a bidirectional teacher model into a ==causal student== for real-time (40.0 FPS), low-latency (25 ms per frame), and high-fidelity video generation suitable for closed-loop interaction.

## Results

- Policies trained with RynnWorld-Teleop-generated data demonstrated effective zero-shot Sim2Real transfer and, when used to augment real-world datasets, improved success rates by up to **20%** on high-precision bimanual tasks like Lid Placement.
- The distilled causal student model achieves real-time performance at **40.0 FPS** (480x832 resolution) with an average latency of **25 ms per frame** on an NVIDIA H100 GPU, surpassing existing action-conditioned world models.
- RynnWorld-Teleop outperforms general-purpose image-to-video models in visual quality and temporal coherence, and it generalizes to out-of-distribution visual states (unseen objects/backgrounds) while maintaining physically plausible dynamics and consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding 3D hand-pose information into a 2D depth-aware skeletal latent, rather than simple 2D projections, is crucial for providing explicit 3D cues and effective action grounding in the generated robotic scenes.
> - Pre-training on large-scale human egocentric videos is essential for the world model to acquire "physical common sense" and robust hand-object interaction priors, preventing visual artifacts and ensuring stable synthesis during robotic domain adaptation.
> - A two-stage sequential distillation curriculum, combining a causal flow-matching warm-up with Distribution Matching Distillation, effectively translates a high-quality bidirectional teacher model into a real-time causal student for interactive digital teleoperation without sacrificing fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning, particularly with the emergence of vision-language-action (VLA) models and world models, shows potential for developing general-purpose autonomous agents. However, a significant obstacle to scaling these advancements is the scarcity of diverse, large-scale trajectory data. Traditional physical teleoperation, while yielding high-quality data, is constrained by hardware availability, fixed laboratory environments, the need for manual environment resets, and the logistical challenges of acquiring a wide range of real-world objects. These limitations restrict the collection of data reflecting the long-tail distribution of interactions necessary for robust generalization in unstructured environments.

This paper proposes "digital teleoperation" as a paradigm to address this data scarcity by decoupling data collection from physical hardware. This approach involves replacing a real robot with a generative world model, which can synthesize egocentric videos based on an operator's hand-pose stream.

Prior research has explored related avenues but has not fully achieved the proposed digital teleoperation paradigm:
- **Human-to-robot video translation methods** (e.g., Phantom, Masquerade, X-Humanoid, Mitty, H2R) focus on visually translating human actions into robot movements in pre-recorded videos. While these methods bridge the visual domain gap, they are typically passive and observation-only, lacking the generation of underlying robotic actions or support for closed-loop interaction.
- **Action-conditioned egocentric world models** (e.g., Hand2World, GeneratedReality, InterDyn, CosHand) synthesize future frames based on human-controlled signals. These models primarily remain human-centric, rendering human hands rather than robot effectors, thus leaving the "embodiment gap" unbridged.

RynnWorld-Teleop is presented as a system that addresses these limitations by simultaneously being robot-centric, action-grounded, and real-time, enabling the generation of complete state-action trajectories for imitation learning without requiring physical robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work is to overcome the data scarcity bottleneck in robot learning. Current methods of collecting high-quality robot data, typically through physical teleoperation, are resource-intensive. Each demonstration requires operator time dedicated to specific hardware and workspaces, limiting throughput and the diversity of scenarios that can be captured. This constraint hinders the development of robust, general-purpose robotic agents capable of operating in varied, unstructured environments.

The key objectives of this research are:
1. **To formalize and instantiate "digital teleoperation"** as a new paradigm for robot data generation. This paradigm aims to decouple operator time from physical infrastructure by using a generative world model in place of a real robot.
2. **To develop an action-conditioned, robot-centric world model** that can synthesize high-fidelity egocentric videos from an operator's hand-pose stream, conditioned on a single reference image of a target scene.
3. **To ensure the generated videos are "action-grounded"**: The operator's joint-level hand poses should serve as embodiment-agnostic, ground-truth action labels that can be retargeted to any specific robot embodiment.
4. **To achieve real-time performance**: The system must operate at frame rates compatible with interactive control, allowing operators to remain in the control loop and chain complex skills.
5. **To demonstrate the utility of digitally teleoperated data** for training robot policies. This includes evaluating zero-shot Sim2Real transfer and the benefits of augmenting real-world datasets with synthetic data.

To achieve these objectives, the authors identified three core requirements for a practical digital teleoperation system: (i) it must be robot-centric to enable teleoperation of a robot, (ii) it must be action-grounded such that every generated frame corresponds to a recoverable joint-level action signal, and (iii) it must operate in real-time to support closed-loop interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RynnWorld-Teleop is designed as a robot-centric, action-conditioned world model capable of streaming high-fidelity interactive video from an egocentric viewpoint at real-time frame rates. It synthesizes a sequence of robotic egocentric videos given a reference image and a sequence of hand gestures. The framework integrates three core designs: depth-aware action representation, progressive cross-domain training, and streaming autoregressive distillation.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The system builds upon the Wan-I2V architecture, which uses a 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and a Transformer-based denoiser (DiT). The training objective follows the conditional flow matching (CFM) framework, where a network predicts a velocity field conditioned on a reference image latent and a depth-aware skeletal control latent.

#### 3.2. Depth-Aware Action Representation

Actions are represented as sequences of skeletal videos derived from 21-joint hand tracking. To address the depth ambiguity in 2D projections, a depth-modulated rendering technique is employed. This involves dynamically scaling the color mapping and diameter of each joint and bone based on its camera-space depth, providing explicit 3D cues in the 2D latent conditioning signal. The rendered pose video is then projected into a latent space using a pretrained VAE encoder, ensuring spatial and temporal alignment with the target video latent.

#### 3.3. Action-Conditioned Video Generation

The world model is built on a Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT). It conditions the generation process on hand-pose sequences. Given a reference image latent and a control latent, the model predicts subsequent motion.
- **Pose-to-Video Conditioning**: An additive patch-embedding scheme with distribution alignment is used to integrate pose information. A dedicated control patch-embedding layer runs in parallel to the original video embedding layer. Their outputs are fused via a learnable scalar gate, `α`. The control latent is aligned to the video latent distribution before patchification to ensure statistical compatibility, with the control patch-embedding layer zero-initialized and `α` set to a small value to preserve the generative prior.

#### 3.4. Progressive Cross-Domain Training

A two-stage training paradigm is adopted to transfer dexterous manipulation skills from human priors to robotic embodiments. All data are converted into paired (2D Skeleton, RGB) sequences.
- **Stage 1: Egocentric Human Pretraining**: The model is initially trained on large-scale egocentric human video datasets, specifically EgoDex (81-frame clips) and VITRA (25-frame segments). This stage enables the DiT to learn fundamental hand-object interaction dynamics and the mapping from skeletal configurations to visual synthesis. Skeleton guidance is generated by transforming 3D hand poses into 2D camera-frame projections.
- **Stage 2: Robotic Domain Adaptation**: The pretrained model is fine-tuned on paired teleoperation data, where human gestures are mapped to robotic actions via Inverse Kinematics (IK). This stage adapts the model to function as a robot-centric world model. A new real-world dataset focused on complex bimanual coordination across four distinct tasks (Dual Picking, Block Pushing, Bimanual Lifting, Lid Placement) was collected, comprising 1,800 high-quality demonstration episodes. Robot trajectories are segmented into 81-frame clips, and human hand movements are recorded using a motion capture (MoCap) system to provide synchronized 2D skeleton sequences.

#### 3.5. Autoregressive Distillation

To support interactive, closed-loop applications, the bidirectional teacher model is distilled into a causal student model for autoregressive, frame-level video generation.
- **Causal Streaming Architecture**: The student model uses a causal temporal mask and a fixed-size KV cache for low-latency inference. Its attention at time `t` is restricted to past frames. The embedding of the reference image is retained as a persistent sink token, and generated KV states are appended to the cache to maintain long-term consistency.
- **Causal Flow-Matching Warm-up**: Training begins with a causal flow-matching objective to bridge the gap between bidirectional and causal processing, establishing the student's streaming generation and control-following capabilities.
- **Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD)**: This refinement stage enables high-fidelity synthesis in a few denoising steps (e.g., four steps). It uses a learned critic and the frozen teacher to provide score-based gradient guidance. Gradients are backpropagated through the persisted KV cache across successive chunks, minimizing boundary artifacts and ensuring smooth temporal continuity.

#### 3.6. RynnWorld-Teleop as a Digital Teleoperation System

The world model is embedded in a closed loop to form a complete data generation pipeline for policy learning:
- **Retargeting**: Raw 6-DoF poses from Vive trackers worn by the operator are used to compute target end-effector poses for each arm. An iterative damped least-squares (DLS) inverse kinematics solver, regularized by a null-space shoulder prior, converts these into a 54-dimensional robot action vector (dual 7-DoF arms + dual 20-DoF dexterous hands). This ensures synchronized, embodiment-specific robot actions.
- **Skeletal-Conditioned Synthesis**: The initial RGB frame of a demonstration serves as the reference image. The operator's hand-pose stream is rendered into a 2D depth-aware skeletal sequence, which conditions RynnWorld-Teleop to synthesize the robotic execution video. The recorded robot actions are synchronized with the generated video frames. The reference image can also be user-provided or synthetically edited to instantiate new scenes.
- **Mitigating Drift via Chunked Re-anchoring**: To counteract visual drift in long-horizon autoregressive generation, a chunk-based strategy (81-frame segments) is used. For subsequent chunks, the generation is re-anchored by providing the actual egocentric frame from the robot's camera at that timestep as the new reference image, maintaining physical grounding.

#### 3.7. Data Engine Comparison with Traditional Simulation

The authors highlight RynnWorld-Teleop's advantages over traditional physical simulators:
- **Zero 3D Asset Overhead**: It instantiates environments implicitly from a single reference image, bypassing the need for explicit 3D meshes and URDFs.
- **No Visual Domain Gap**: It synthesizes frames within the real-world pixel distribution, capturing complex lighting, reflections, and sensor noise without domain randomization.
- **Implicit Physics vs. Manual SysID**: By pre-training on human videos, the model absorbs "physical common sense" to generate plausible contact dynamics without explicit differential equations or tedious system identification.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RynnWorld-Teleop encompasses its capabilities as a generative model and, more significantly, as a digital teleoperation system for generating robot training data.

#### 4.1. Digital Teleoperation System Evaluation

- **Experimental Setup**: Experiments were conducted on a TIANJI M6 mobile robot with dual WUJI dexterous hands and an egocentric RealSense D435i camera. Four diverse bimanual tasks were used: Dual Picking, Block Pushing, Bimanual Lifting, and Lid Placement. Generalization was tested by randomizing initial environment states, including 6-DoF poses of objects. Success Rate (percentage of successful completions over 35 trials within 120 seconds) was the primary metric.
- **Policy Learning via Generative Data Scaling**: Policies trained exclusively on RynnWorld-Teleop-generated data demonstrated effective zero-shot Sim2Real transfer.
 - Augmenting 300 real-world episodes with 300 RynnWorld-Teleop-generated episodes consistently improved success rates across three state-of-the-art policies (Diffusion Policy (DP), π0.5, and π0) on nearly all tasks.
 - The most significant improvements were observed in high-precision tasks like Lid Placement, where π0.5's success rate increased from 42.86% to 62.86% (+20%) and π0's from 34.29% to 54.29% (+20%).
 - π0, when trained solely on 300 RynnWorld-Teleop-generated episodes (without any real data), achieved success rates of 82.86% in Block Pushing and 77.14% in Bimanual Lifting, indicating the physical grounding and realism of the synthesized data.
- **Feature Distribution Analysis**: Using t-SNE on I3D-extracted features from randomly sampled frames, the distribution of RynnWorld-Teleop-generated data showed significant overlap with real-world data. This alignment indicates that the generative world model effectively captures the underlying distribution of robotic manipulation, including complex hand-object interactions and environmental lighting.
- **Latency Analysis for Real-time Control**: The distilled causal student model achieved a throughput of 40.0 FPS at 480 × 832 resolution on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU, with an average per-frame latency of approximately 25 ms. This performance exceeds typical frame rates of existing action-conditioned world models and matches or surpasses real-world robotic camera frequencies, enabling fluid interactive digital teleoperation.

#### 4.2. Action-Conditioned World Model Evaluation

- **Experimental Setup**: Two benchmarks were used: EgoDex-Test (50 human-centric video sequences, 81 frames each) for general interaction priors and ablation studies, and Robotic-Test (20 robot-specific video sequences, 81 frames each from the self-collected dataset) for evaluating performance on robotic embodiments. Metrics included PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD, along with FPS.
- **Action-Conditioned World Modeling**: RynnWorld-Teleop reportedly outperformed general-purpose image-to-video (I2V) models (e.g., Wan, CogVideoX) and other action-conditioned video generation models (e.g., InterDyn, CosHand, Mask2IV) in terms of visual quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and temporal coherence (FVD).
 - Vanilla supervised fine-tuning of a base I2V model (Wan-2.2-TI2V-5B) still trailed RynnWorld-Teleop significantly (e.g., FVD 1223 vs. 585), suggesting that specialized depth-aware pose representation and distribution-aligned conditioning are important for complex robotic dexterity.
 - The distilled Causal student model maintained high fidelity while achieving interactive generation speeds.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Visual States**: RynnWorld-Teleop demonstrated generalization to unseen objects (e.g., replacing a block with a red sphere) and unseen backgrounds (e.g., overlaying a patterned tablecloth) by modifying the single reference image via image editing. The model maintained temporal consistency, visual fidelity, and physically plausible dynamics under these shifts, indicating it learns a generalized interaction prior decoupled from specific object textures and background appearances.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Conditioning Strategy**: Comparing the distribution-aligned additive scheme (used in RynnWorld-Teleop) with standard concatenation fusion, the additive method resulted in substantially better performance (FVD 585 vs. 1191). This suggests that direct concatenation disrupts the pre-trained latent distribution of the base DiT, leading to unstable synthesis.
- **Value of Human Egocentric Pre-training**: A variant trained without Stage 1 human pre-training showed a severe performance collapse (FVD 2598, LPIPS 0.453), with visual artifacts like "ghosting," blurred textures, and loss of object permanence. This finding confirms the importance of transferring knowledge from large-scale human egocentric datasets for learning complex hand-object physics.
- **Effectiveness of Sequential Distillation**: The study validated the two-stage distillation curriculum. Removing Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) resulted in a significant quality gap (PSNR 22.25 vs. 19.25). Directly applying DMD without the causal warm-up phase led to training instability and blurred textures, confirming the warm-up's role in providing a stable initialization for effective knowledge transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RynnWorld-Teleop introduces "digital teleoperation" as a viable framework for generating large-scale, diverse robotic training data. This work presents a solution to the data scarcity problem that currently bottlenecks the advancement of general-purpose robotic agents.

The key contributions and potential impacts are:
- **Decoupling Data Collection from Physical Constraints**: By replacing real robots with a generative world model, the system enables data collection to be bound only by operator imagination, rather than physical hardware availability or laboratory setups. This can significantly increase the throughput and diversity of collected robot trajectories.
- **Scalable and High-Fidelity Data Engine**: RynnWorld-Teleop's ability to synthesize high-fidelity, action-consistent egocentric videos, coupled with its real-time performance, positions it as a scalable data engine. The empirical evidence of zero-shot Sim2Real transfer and improved policy success rates through data augmentation supports its utility as both a substitute for and amplifier of physical teleoperation.
- **Embodiment-Agnostic Action Labels**: The use of operator hand-pose streams as embodiment-agnostic action labels simplifies data collection and enhances reusability, allowing the same gesture data to be retargeted to various robot platforms.
- **Implicit Physics and OOD Generalization**: The model's capacity to absorb physical priors from human videos and generalize to out-of-distribution visual states (unseen objects, backgrounds) suggests a pathway towards more robust and adaptable robot learning without the need for explicit 3D assets or complex physics simulations for every new scenario.

**Limitations**:
The authors acknowledge several limitations. While the depth-modulated rendering captures 3D spatial dynamics, the model occasionally struggles with complex physical phenomena such as fine-grained liquid dynamics or highly deformable objects. Addressing these cases may necessitate richer training data specific to such interactions. Another limitation is that bridging the embodiment gap currently requires per-platform fine-tuning, which could limit scalability across diverse robot fleets. The authors propose cross-embodiment foundation world models, possibly conditioned on robot kinematic descriptors, as a promising future direction to mitigate this.

Overall, RynnWorld-Teleop represents a step towards making human intuition a scalable digital control signal for robot learning, potentially making digital teleoperation a standard component in future robot data pipelines.
