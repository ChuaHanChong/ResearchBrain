# Drive My Way: Preference Alignment of Vision-Language-Action Model for Personalized Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Drive My Way (DMW) proposes a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework for personalized autonomous driving, learning long-term driver preferences from user profiles and adapting to real-time natural language instructions. The system demonstrated superior alignment with both consistent driver habits and immediate style commands in complex driving scenarios, achieving higher user ratings and performance compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Current autonomous driving systems optimize for generalized objectives, failing to account for individual, context-dependent human driving behaviors and preferences.
- Existing personalization approaches are limited to rigid preset driving modes or manual adjustments, and struggle to interpret real-time, natural language instructions from users.
- Many language-driven personalization methods neglect a driver's long-term habits and cumulative experiences, while also lacking robust evaluation in complex, dynamic driving scenarios.

## Method

- Develops a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework integrating visual observations, user profiles for long-term habits, and natural language instructions for real-time adaptation.
- Constructed a ==Personalized Driving Dataset== (PDD) with 30 drivers across diverse scenarios to capture individual driving styles and behaviors.
- Employs reinforcement fine-tuning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for policy specialization and a style-aware reward adaptation mechanism to align with short-term instructions.

## Results

- DMW significantly improved driving performance and efficiency, achieving an **18.77%** efficiency gain for aggressive instructions compared to **3.70%** for SimLingo, while also demonstrating distinct and appropriate behavioral shifts for conservative instructions.
- The framework achieved higher Alignment Scores and average user ratings for both in-distribution and out-of-distribution drivers, indicating superior capture and generalization of individual long-term driving preferences.
- DMW consistently received the highest user ratings across various styles and scenarios, confirming its ability to adapt to short-term natural language instructions and provide a more human-aligned driving experience compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a user embedding learned from long-term profiles with real-time natural language instructions enables a VLA model to achieve a comprehensive and adaptive personalized driving policy.
> - Dynamically adjusting reward components and thresholds based on driving instructions and context is crucial for both reliable and personalized driving behaviors, allowing for flexible trade-offs between safety, efficiency, and comfort.
> - Contrastive learning with carefully designed encoders can effectively learn user embeddings that capture distinct long-term driving habits, which can then generalize to unseen drivers and scenarios.
