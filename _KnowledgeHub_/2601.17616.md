# Split-on-Share: Mixture of Sparse Experts for Task-Agnostic Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Split-on-Share (SETA) framework, developed by researchers at Iowa State University and Argonne National Laboratory, enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to learn new tasks continually without forgetting previous knowledge by dynamically managing unique and shared parameter experts. It achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy on domain-specific tasks, showing 30.47% aggregate retention and 19.28% average forgetting while preserving general reasoning capabilities with minimal parameter expansion.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) suffer from catastrophic forgetting, rapidly losing previously acquired knowledge when learning new tasks sequentially, a challenge known as the plasticity-stability dilemma.
- Existing continual learning strategies, particularly those using Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA, struggle to optimally balance stability and plasticity due to uniform treatment of parameters or insufficient isolation of task-specific knowledge.
- Many continual learning methods require external task identifiers during inference, limiting their practical deployment in real-world, task-agnostic environments.

## Method

- SETA employs gradient-based sparse subspace selection, predominantly in the attention Value (V) projection, to identify and adapt high-impact parameter sub-blocks.
- It partitions sparse capacity into strictly frozen task-specific Unique Experts (E_u) and plastic Shared Experts (E_s) using a Split-on-Share (SoS) mechanism, dynamically evolving the architecture and applying adaptive elastic anchoring to protect shared knowledge.
- A unified content-based router with adaptive gating expansion enables task-agnostic inference by dynamically selecting relevant expert modules based solely on input tokens.

## Results

- SETA achieved state-of-the-art average inference accuracy (Acc_t) across most domain-specific tasks, reaching 59.00% on FOMC (vs. I-LoRA's 53.9%) and 46.80% on C-STANCE (vs. I-LoRA's 44.4%).
- The framework demonstrated superior knowledge retention and lower catastrophic forgetting, with an aggregate retention score (R_T) of 30.47% and an average forgetting (F_T) of 19.28%, outperforming baselines like I-LoRA (R_T 27.32%, F_T 27.52%).
- It preserved general reasoning capabilities, showing the lowest "GEN loss" of -18.42 on common sense benchmarks (MMLU, BBH, PIQA) and maintained high parameter efficiency with logarithmic capacity growth (O(log T)), expanding to only 1.25% of LLaMA-2 7B parameters after six tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing knowledge into distinct unique and shared sparse experts, managed through dynamic splitting and elastic anchoring, provides a more robust solution to the plasticity-stability dilemma than uniform parameter treatment.
> - Focusing sparse fine-tuning on high-utility gradient regions, specifically the attention Value projection, efficiently captures new features while minimizing interference with existing knowledge.
> - Logit-invariant gating and a unified content-based router allow for scalable, task-agnostic continual learning, making LLM adaptation practical in dynamic real-world settings without explicit task identifiers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Siddika et al. directly addresses one of the most significant challenges in Artificial Intelligence, particularly for powerful models like Large Language Models: **continual learning (CL)**. The core issue, known as the **plasticity-stability dilemma** or **catastrophic forgetting**, describes the tendency of neural networks to rapidly forget previously acquired knowledge when learning new tasks sequentially. This phenomenon severely limits the practical deployment of LLMs in dynamic, real-world environments where models must constantly adapt to new information without losing old skills.

Existing continual learning strategies generally fall into three categories:
1. **Replay-based methods:** Rehearse historical data (e.g., Experience Replay).
2. **Regularization-based techniques:** Penalize updates to critical parameters (e.g., Elastic Weight Consolidation, Gradient Episode Memory).
3. **Parameter Isolation approaches:** Physically separate task-specific modules (e.g., Progressive Neural Networks).

However, the authors identify a critical limitation shared by these approaches: they often treat model parameters uniformly within their respective subspaces. This uniform treatment fails to distinguish between generalized, reusable features and unique, task-specific knowledge, leading to suboptimal trade-offs between learning new tasks (plasticity) and retaining old knowledge (stability).

The problem is further exacerbated in **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)** regimes, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), which are widely used for LLMs due to computational constraints. While PEFT methods reduce memory overhead by training small modules, recent studies show that restricting updates to narrow, low-rank subspaces can create a "geometric disconnect" between task loss minima, leading to a "lossy approximation" of model weights that increases susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting. Existing PEFT-based CL methods like I-LoRA and O-LoRA attempt to mitigate this, but still face challenges in optimally balancing stability and plasticity.

SETA positions itself as a **modular framework for sparse subspace adaptation** that transcends the limitations of existing methods by explicitly addressing the "Shared Subspace Dilemma." It leverages **continual sparse fine-tuning**, which can uncover internal knowledge structures by identifying specific attention sub-blocks (particularly in the Value projection) based on high-utility gradient signals. By integrating this sparse fine-tuning with a novel **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** paradigm, SETA offers a structural solution that differentiates and manages shared and unique knowledge, moving beyond mere loss-based constraints or uniform parameter treatment. This approach represents an advancement in architectural/parameter isolation strategies within the context of efficient LLM adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the SETA framework is to **resolve the fundamental plasticity-stability conflict in Large Language Model continual learning** by enabling models to acquire new capabilities without catastrophically forgetting previous knowledge, especially within parameter-efficient fine-tuning regimes.

The authors articulate three specific research questions (RQs) that drive the design of SETA, each addressing a particular limitation in the state-of-the-art literature:

- **RQ1: How can sparse parameter patterns be leveraged to decouple the acquisition of novel features from the retention of historical representations?**
 - **Motivation:** Existing methods struggle to isolate updates, leading to interference. Sparse tuning inherently identifies specific, impactful parameter sub-blocks.
 - **SETA's approach:** Introduces a subspace-based expert decomposition, framing sparse continual learning within a Mixture-of-Experts paradigm. This ensures conflicting updates operate in isolated subspaces rather than competing for monolithic blocks.

- **RQ2: Is it possible to automatically differentiate between reusable shared knowledge and task-specific unique knowledge within a single unified framework?**
 - **Motivation:** The "Shared Subspace Dilemma" means parameters important for multiple tasks risk overwriting (plasticity) or being unadaptable (stability). Uniform regularization is too rigid.
 - **SETA's approach:** Employs a **Split-on-Share (SoS) mechanism** combined with adaptive elastic anchoring. This dynamically assigns parameters: overlapping ones become **shared experts (E_s)** and are stabilized, while non-overlapping ones become **unique experts (E_u)** and are strictly frozen, acting as immutable memory.

- **RQ3: How can the relevant expert modules be dynamically retrieved during inference without relying on external task identifiers?**
 - **Motivation:** Many continual learning methods require task IDs during inference, which is impractical for real-world, task-agnostic deployment.
 - **SETA's approach:** Implements **adaptive gating expansion with logit invariance**. This allows a unified router network to evolve with experts and dynamically select relevant parameters based solely on input tokens, enabling true task-agnostic inference and scalability.

By addressing these questions, SETA aims to provide a structural and dynamic solution to continual learning, moving beyond the uniform treatment of parameters and enabling more robust and efficient LLM adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SETA framework is a modular, Mixture-of-Sparse-Experts architecture designed for task-agnostic continual learning in LLMs. Its methodology systematically tackles the plasticity-stability dilemma through several integrated components:

#### 3.1. Sparse Subspace Selection

SETA begins by identifying the most relevant sparse sub-matrices for fine-tuning. It uses a **gradient-based parameter selection strategy** inspired by Fisher Information.
- **Process:** Pre-trained weight matrices are partitioned into l x l sub-blocks. A warm-up round computes an importance score for each block, representing the average absolute gradient magnitude. The top-k blocks with the highest scores form the active sparse subspace.
- **Targeted Adaptation:** Empirical analysis reveals that approximately 95% of these high-magnitude gradients concentrate in the **attention Value (V) projection** within the transformer layers, attributed to "Softmax Saturation" suppressing gradients in Query (Q) and Key (K) projections. Therefore, block selection is restricted exclusively to the V-projection, focusing resources on high-impact regions responsible for content adaptation. Layers with insufficient importance are skipped, leveraging residual connections.

#### 3.2. Design of Unique and Shared Experts

To prevent parameter interference, SETA partitions the identified sparse capacity into two distinct functional sets:
- **Task-specific Unique Experts (E_u):** Constructed from the disjoint sparse subspace (indices P_t \ P_1:t-1) for the current task T_t. These parameters are exclusive to T_t and are strictly frozen (∇E_u = 0) for all future tasks, acting as immutable memory.
- **Shared Experts (E_s):** Occupy the intersection subspace (indices P_t ∩ P_1:t-1). These parameters capture structural feature overlap between tasks and are plastic, serving as a basis for positive backward transfer.
The model's effective sparse subspace W is thus a summation of the frozen base model W_0 and these functional deltas: W = W_0 + ΣΔW_u + ΣΔW_s.

#### 3.3. Split-on-Share (SoS) Expert Evolution

The SoS algorithm governs the dynamic evolution of the expert architecture as new tasks arrive, deciding which parameters become shared or remain unique.
- **Layer-wise Topological Filter:** For each layer, it quantifies the raw intersection (I_l) and unique remainder (R_l) between the current task's active indices and the cumulative historical subspace.
- **Conditional Set Operations:**
 - **Filtering Small Overlaps:** If |I_l| falls below an Expert Creation Threshold (τ_ect), it's considered stochastic noise and reintegrated into the unique subspace (R_l ← R_l ∪ I_l).
 - **Merging Tiny Fragments:** If |R_l| falls below a Tiny Remainder Threshold (τ_trt), it's merged into the shared manifold (I_l ← I_l ∪ R_l) to maintain computational efficiency.
- **Expert Formation:** The validated intersection I_l instantiates the plastic shared expert E_s (with weight inheritance from the previous task). The stabilized residuals R_l form the frozen historical unique expert E_u(t-1). Indices unique to the new task initialize the current unique expert E_u(t). This mechanism ensures logarithmic capacity expansion (O(log T)), selectively merging redundant functionalities.

#### 3.4. Historical Knowledge Preservation

SETA employs a dual strategy to prevent catastrophic forgetting:
- **Orthogonal Subspace Isolation:** During training of task t, all prior unique experts E_u(k<t) are strictly frozen (zero-gradient constraint), ensuring their features remain immutable. Optimization is restricted to the active topology (plastic E_s and current E_u(t)).
- **Adaptive Elastic Anchoring:** The plastic shared expert E_s is protected from semantic drift by applying adaptive regularization. A penalty R(E_s) = Σ n_j ||ΔW_j - ΔW*_j||^2 is applied, where n_j is the sharing frequency. This creates a "potential well" for highly shared weights, forcing the unconstrained unique expert E_u(t) to absorb high-variance updates.

#### 3.5. Adaptive Gating Expansion

To enable seamless expert splitting and robust inference, SETA ensures logit invariance:
- **Weight Inheritance:** When an historical expert E_t-1 splits into E_s and E_u(t-1), their respective gating parameters W_g(s) and W_g(u) are initialized such that the original pre-activation logits for an input token x are preserved. This guarantees that the probability mass is equivalently distributed across the new configuration, preventing cold-start errors in the router.

#### 3.6. Objective Function

The composite objective function for tasks t > 1 combines the standard prediction accuracy loss for the active parameters (E_u(t) and E_s) with the adaptive elastic anchoring penalty specifically for the shared expert E_s. For the initial task, only the sparse fine-tuning loss is minimized.

#### 3.7. Task-Agnostic Inference

SETA achieves task-agnostic deployment through a **Unified Content-Based Router**.
- **Mechanism:** A single linear transformation (gating network W_g) maps the input token x to a global logit vector over the entire expert population (N = |E_s| + |E_u|). The output is a weighted superposition of the frozen backbone W_0 and the retrieved sparse functional deltas from both shared and unique experts.
- **Dynamic Expert Activation:** The router dynamically selects the top-k experts based on input tokens alone, without requiring explicit task IDs. If an input shares features with multiple domains, W_g triggers multi-expert activation, reconstructing functional capacity from the distributed parameter space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by Siddika et al. rigorously evaluate SETA's performance against ten PEFT-based baselines using LLaMA-2 7B as the backbone, across diverse domain-specific and general benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Domain-Specific Continual Learning

- **State-of-the-Art Accuracy:** SETA consistently established new state-of-the-art results in average inference accuracy (Acc_t) across most domain-specific tasks. For example, on the FOMC financial task, SETA achieved 59.00%, significantly outperforming I-LoRA's 53.9%. On C-STANCE, SETA reached 46.80% compared to I-LoRA's 44.4%.
- **Improved Knowledge Retention:** SETA exhibited superior robustness in challenging domains where baselines like I-LoRA suffered catastrophic performance drops (e.g., I-LoRA dropping to 8.1% on Task 2, while SETA maintained 25.0%). This confirms SETA's sparse adaptation better captures specialized knowledge and leverages geometric connectivity to improve retention.

#### 4.2. Higher Aggregate Retention and Lower Catastrophic Forgetting

- **Aggregate Retention Score (R_T):** SETA achieved a higher R_T of 30.47% compared to I-LoRA's 27.32% and Seq-Train's 20.23%, indicating it retains a greater absolute volume of knowledge over the task sequence.
- **Average Forgetting (F_T):** SETA demonstrated superior stability with an F_T of 19.28%, which is considerably lower than I-LoRA's 27.52% and Seq-Train's 35.50%. This metric quantifies the decline from peak accuracy, confirming SETA's effectiveness in mitigating forgetting.

#### 4.3. Balanced Stability and Plasticity

- The sequential performance analysis (Table 4) shows SETA achieves a better trade-off between retaining past knowledge and learning new tasks. For instance, after five tasks, SETA retained 36.00% accuracy on Task 1, surpassing I-LoRA (35.8%) and PP (16.4%). Simultaneously, it effectively learned new tasks, scoring 73.60% on Task 2 and 46.50% on Task 4, demonstrating robust long-term memory without sacrificing new learning capabilities.

#### 4.4. Preservation of General Reasoning Capabilities

- **Resilience to Forgetting of Common Sense:** When evaluated on general benchmarks (MMLU, BBH, PIQA) for zero-shot performance after domain-specific fine-tuning, SETA demonstrated the highest overall resilience. It secured the best "GEN loss" of -18.42, indicating minimal erosion of inherent reasoning and common sense knowledge compared to other methods, many of which experienced significant performance degradation.

#### 4.5. Mechanism Analysis and Efficiency

- **Subspace Split-on-Share Dynamics:** The "Merging Tiny Fragments" strategy was validated, showing a clear compositional shift where the shared expert (blue) became dominant, progressively absorbing universal features, while task-specific unique experts (colored bands) maintained stable, minimal footprints. This confirmed the effective compression of redundant representations into the shared subspace.
- **Dynamic Capacity Expansion:** Analysis of active module density across layers revealed that SETA's inference capacity expands dynamically. Early tasks use sparse routing, while later, more diverse tasks recruit a denser mix of historical Shared Modules and new Unique Modules. This ensures computational footprint expands only when necessary.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** SETA maintained a minimal memory footprint. Trainable parameters increased marginally from 6.34M (Task 1) to 8.10M (Task 6), representing only 0.98% to 1.25% of the LLaMA-2 7B model. This is comparable to or lower than I-LoRA (8.39M), confirming efficient adaptation.
- **Logarithmic Capacity Growth:** The Split-on-Share mechanism enables sub-linear growth (O(log T)) in active blocks, achieving over 50% reduction compared to linear accumulation of independent experts by the final task.
- **Shared Subspace Utilization:** Elastic anchoring proved effective, with shared expert utilization showing logarithmic convergence, peaking at 64.3% for the Top-1280 configuration. This demonstrates that the shared expert robustly stabilizes as a semantic anchor.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SETA framework presents a significant advancement in the field of continual learning for Large Language Models, offering both theoretical and practical implications:

#### 5.1. Robust Solution to Catastrophic Forgetting

SETA provides a principled and effective solution to the long-standing plasticity-stability dilemma. By structurally decomposing knowledge into distinct unique and shared experts and employing dynamic splitting and anchoring mechanisms, it fundamentally re-architects how LLMs adapt to sequential tasks. This moves beyond heuristic regularization or naive architectural expansion, offering a more robust foundation for continuous adaptation.

#### 5.2. Enhanced LLM Adaptability and Scalability

The ability of SETA to prevent catastrophic forgetting while maintaining high performance on new tasks is crucial for deploying LLMs in dynamic, real-world environments. LLMs can now be updated with new information, domain-specific knowledge, or evolving requirements without the prohibitive cost of retraining from scratch or the risk of losing previously learned general capabilities. The logarithmic capacity expansion ensures that this adaptability is scalable, making it feasible for longer task sequences.

#### 5.3. Task-Agnostic Inference

One of SETA's most impactful features is its task-agnostic inference capability. By using a unified content-based router that dynamically selects relevant expert combinations based solely on input tokens, SETA eliminates the need for explicit task identifiers during deployment. This simplifies integration into complex systems where tasks might be implicit or unknown, broadening the applicability of continually learning LLMs.

#### 5.4. Superior Parameter and Computational Efficiency

SETA achieves state-of-the-art performance while maintaining a remarkably low memory footprint, modifying only a tiny fraction (approximately 1%) of the total LLM parameters. This parameter efficiency translates into reduced computational costs, faster training times, and lower energy consumption. Such efficiency makes continual learning more accessible, environmentally sustainable, and economically viable for a wider range of researchers and practitioners, particularly for large-scale models.

#### 5.5. Novel Architectural Paradigm

The Split-on-Share mechanism, combined with gradient-based sparse selection and adaptive gating, introduces a novel architectural paradigm for continual learning. It demonstrates that strategic decomposition and dynamic management of model capacity, rather than uniform treatment, is key to effective lifelong learning. This approach could inspire future research in modular neural network design, sparse learning, and adaptive expert systems beyond LLMs.

#### 5.6. Broader Societal and Environmental Impact

The parameter-efficient and adaptable nature of SETA contributes positively to responsible AI development. By lowering the barrier to deploying adaptable language technologies, it can broaden access to advanced LLM capabilities. The reduced computational overhead also aligns with efforts towards more environmentally sustainable AI, mitigating the energy demands associated with large model training and updates. However, the authors responsibly highlight potential concerns regarding bias propagation and the need for fairness-aware expert routing in future work, acknowledging the societal responsibilities that accompany powerful AI systems.
