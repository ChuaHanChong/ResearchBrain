# TREK: Distill to Explore, Reinforce to Refine

> [!summary] Summary
> TREK (Teacher-Routed Exploration via Forward KL) introduces a method to overcome the exploration bottleneck in on-policy reinforcement learning for large language models by employing distillation to expand the student model's sampling support toward novel, verified solution modes. This approach boosts performance on challenging mathematical reasoning and agentic tasks, demonstrating gains of up to +14.2 percentage points and accelerating training efficiency.

## Problem

- On-policy reinforcement learning methods like GRPO struggle with challenging reasoning prompts because the student policy often fails to sample useful trajectories, creating an 'exploration bottleneck'.
- Even with a capable verifier, the student model may repeatedly generate incorrect trajectories if the correct solution modes are outside its current sampling support.
- Existing distillation techniques primarily improve supervision or credit assignment for *already generated* behaviors, rather than actively expanding the student's ability to discover new solution paths.

## Method

- **TREK** (Teacher-Routed Exploration via Forward KL) uses a staged approach that repurposes ==distillation== for ==exploration support expansion== by leveraging a ==proposal source== (e.g., a stronger external model or a self-contextualized student) to generate verified candidate solutions.
- It identifies ==hard prompts== where the student's unaided pass rate is low and then selectively retrieves ==student-proximal== verified proposals for these prompts, ranking them by ==reachability== (a trimmed length-normalized NLL) to the student's current policy.
- A short ==forward-KL consolidation== phase explicitly pushes the student's policy support towards these selected proposals, followed by the standard ==GRPO== refinement for further optimization.

## Results

- Mathematical reasoning performance improved: Qwen3-8B scores on AIME 2025 increased from **36.9%** to **40.3%** (**+3.4 points**) using DeepSeek-V4 proposals.
- Agentic task success rates significantly rose: ALFWorld improved from **75.8%** to **82.8%** (**+7.0 points**), and ScienceWorld nearly doubled from **12.5%** to **26.7%** (**+14.2 points**) with external proposals.
- Training efficiency was accelerated, with TREK reaching target success rates approximately **five times** faster than GRPO alone on agentic tasks by injecting verified solution modes early in training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'exploration bottleneck' is a distinct and critical failure mode in on-policy RL for LLMs, where the primary limitation is the student's inability to discover solution trajectories, not the absence of a reward signal.
> - Distillation can be effectively re-conceptualized as a mechanism for 'exploration support expansion,' directly pushing a student model's policy to cover new, verified solution modes.
> - Efficiently selecting 'student-proximal' proposals, which are both correct and somewhat reachable by the current student, is crucial for stable and effective knowledge transfer, balancing exploration with learnability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work positions itself within the field of improving large language model (LLM) reasoning capabilities through methods such as distillation, curriculum learning, and reinforcement learning (RL) from verifiable rewards. Specifically, it builds upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an on-policy RL technique that optimizes LLMs by sampling candidate responses and assigning relative advantages based on outcome rewards or programmatic verifiers.

The paper identifies a limitation of GRPO and similar on-policy methods: their effectiveness is contingent on the current policy already sampling useful reasoning trajectories. On more challenging prompts, the student model may repeatedly generate plausible but incorrect trajectories, leading to an "exploration bottleneck." In this scenario, the verifier is capable of identifying a correct solution if one were generated, but the student's on-policy sampling distribution does not sufficiently cover the relevant solution modes.

Existing teacher-informed and on-policy distillation methods primarily focus on improving supervision, credit assignment, or reliability for trajectories that the student already generates. These include techniques for measuring teachability, adapting supervision, weighting tokens, or smoothing sparse rewards. TREK distinguishes itself by addressing a different, upstream failure mode: the lack of exploration coverage. It proposes a novel application of distillation, not for imitation or credit shaping on existing behaviors, but for *exploration support expansion* to enable the student to discover and subsequently sample solution modes that are currently outside its on-policy support. This approach aims to make useful behaviors discoverable and sampleable, thereby complementing existing GRPO-based refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work is to overcome the exploration bottleneck observed in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) when applied to hard reasoning prompts. The authors identify that GRPO struggles when the student policy's current sampling support does not encompass the correct solution modes, causing it to repeatedly generate similar incorrect trajectories even with a competent verifier available. The scarce resource in this scenario is not the reward signal itself but the student's ability to explore the solution space effectively.

The key objectives are as follows:

1. **Identify and Characterize the Exploration Bottleneck:** To explicitly define and empirically demonstrate that missing exploration support constitutes a distinct failure mode for GRPO on challenging reasoning tasks, where the student's unaided sampling rarely yields reward-bearing trajectories.
2. **Repurpose Distillation for Exploration Support Expansion:** To formulate a broader conceptualization of distillation, moving beyond its traditional roles of imitation or token-level credit assignment, and instead employing it as a mechanism to expand the student policy's sampling support towards novel, verified solution modes.
3. **Develop a General and Flexible Methodology (TREK):** To design a staged procedure that can leverage a "proposal source" (e.g., a stronger external model, an internal white-box model, or the same student model augmented with inference-time context) to generate verified candidate solutions. A crucial objective is to ensure this methodology is general, by only requiring verified output trajectories from the proposal source, thus making it compatible with black-box systems where internal logits or hidden states are unavailable.
4. **Efficiently Select Informative Proposals:** To devise a mechanism within TREK to efficiently identify and select, from the potentially numerous verified proposals, those that are "student-proximal" – meaning they are sufficiently close to the student's current support to be effectively consolidated. This filtering aims to optimize the transfer of knowledge and ensure training stability.
5. **Accelerate Learning on Hard Tasks:** To demonstrate that by injecting these verified solution modes early in training, TREK can significantly accelerate the student's ability to achieve high success rates on previously hard-to-solve tasks, thereby improving training efficiency and reducing the computational resources required for exploration.

In essence, the motivation is to enable the student model to "see" and "learn to sample" solutions it would not discover on its own, making the first problem of making useful behavior sampleable at all, before GRPO refines those behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **TREK (Teacher-Routed Exploration via Forward KL)**, is a staged procedure designed to address the exploration bottleneck in GRPO. It integrates distillation for exploration support expansion into the GRPO training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup and Failure Mode (Section 2.1)

The framework begins with a student policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)$ and a verifier $V(y) \in \{0, 1\}$ that determines the success of a response $y$. Standard GRPO optimizes $\pi_\theta$ using relative advantages derived from rewards of responses sampled from the current policy. The core limitation addressed is that GRPO can only reinforce modes that the student already samples. On hard prompts, the student's unaided pass rate, $p_S(x)$, is low, indicating a failure to discover useful trajectories.
To measure how "reachable" a verified teacher trajectory $y_T$ is by the student, TREK defines a trimmed length-normalized Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL), $d_S(y_T|x)$. This metric averages token-level NLLs after removing the lowest $\alpha$ and highest $\beta$ fractions of token losses, preventing boilerplate from making a trajectory seem artificially close and outliers from making it seem artificially far. A smaller $d_S$ indicates higher reachability. A prompt-relative form, $\Delta d(y_T|x)$, is also used for analysis.

#### 3.2. Prompt Routing and Reachability (Section 2.2)

TREK initiates a routing mechanism to determine when to invoke the proposal source:
1. **Hard Prompt Identification:** For each prompt $x$, the unaided student pass rate $p_S(x)$ is estimated from $K$ rollouts. Prompts with $p_S(x) \le \tau_{low}$ are identified as "routed hard prompts" and become candidates for proposal generation. Prompts above this threshold continue with ordinary GRPO.
2. **Proposal Source Invocation:** Only on these routed hard prompts, a "proposal policy" $\pi_T(\cdot|x)$ is queried. This $\pi_T$ can be:
 - A larger, stronger external black-box model.
 - A white-box teacher model.
 - The same student model augmented with additional inference-time context (e.g., verifier-guided retry, self-consistency, reflection, longer reasoning budget, or "failure-lesson memory" as described in Appendix C for the self-context variant). The key is that it discovers solutions the unaided student misses.
3. **Proposal Selection:**
 - The proposal source generates up to $M$ candidate trajectories.
 - Only trajectories that pass the verifier $V(y_T)=1$ are retained.
 - Among the verified trajectories, TREK ranks them by increasing $d_S(y_T|x)$ (i.e., by student likelihood/reachability).
 - The top-$r$ most student-proximal verified proposals are selected to form the set $Y_{reach}(x)$. This step ensures that consolidation focuses on modes that are both correct and somewhat accessible to the current student, balancing exploration with learnability.

#### 3.3. Proposal Learning and On-Policy Refinement (Section 2.3)

Once $Y_{reach}(x)$ is established for routed hard prompts, a short "support-expansion update" is applied:
1. **Forward-KL Consolidation:** Proposal learning minimizes $L_{FKL}(x) = KL(q_{prop}(\cdot|x) \parallel \pi_\theta(\cdot|x))$, where $q_{prop}$ is the distribution of the selected top-$r$ verified proposals. This forward KL objective directly penalizes the student for assigning low probability mass to the retained proposal modes, explicitly pushing the student's support towards them.
2. **Black-box Compatibility:** In settings where teacher probabilities are not available (e.g., black-box teachers or self-context augmentation), $L_{FKL}$ simplifies to teacher-forced negative log-likelihood on the retained proposal samples: $L_{prop}(x, y_T) = - \sum \log \pi_\theta(y_{T,t} | x, y_{T,<t})$.
3. **Staged Training Schedule:** The overall process is staged:
 - **Hard prompt mining:** Identify prompts with low $p_S(x)$.
 - **Proposal selection:** Generate, verify, and select top-$r$ student-proximal proposals.
 - **Short forward-KL warm-start:** Apply the NLL-based update on the collected proposal dataset $D_{scaf} = \{(x, y_T) : p_S(x) \le \tau_{low}, y_T \in Y_{reach}(x)\}$. This phase is brief (e.g., one epoch).
 - **Ordinary GRPO refinement:** After the forward-KL phase, these prompts return to the normal sampling pool, and GRPO resumes its standard on-policy optimization.
This pipeline ensures that the student first gains exposure to novel, verified solutions and then refines its policy through its own rollouts.

#### 3.4. Algorithm and Implementation Details (Section 2.4, Appendix B, C)

The paper details the full algorithm (Algorithm 1), including periodic refreshes of $p_S(x)$ and reachability statistics. For math tasks, Qwen3 models (1.7B, 8B, 14B) are used, with DeepSeek-V4 as an external proposal teacher, or the same Qwen3 model with a "failure-lesson memory" for the self-context variant. For agentic tasks (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is the student, with DeepSeek-V4 or self-context proposals. Evaluation is avg@16 for math and success rate for agentic tasks. All methods are matched on student optimization steps. A pipelined scheduling option is discussed to overlap GRPO with proposal generation, improving efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that TREK consistently improves performance across different model scales and task domains, particularly on challenging prompts where direct GRPO struggles due to exploration limitations.

#### 4.1. Mathematical Reasoning Results (Table 1, Figure 1a-b, Appendix A)

- **AIME Benchmarks (Harder Tasks):** TREK consistently improved Qwen3 models across 1.7B, 8B, and 14B scales on AIME 2024 and AIME 2025.
 - With **DeepSeek-V4 proposals**: Qwen3-8B improved AIME 2025 from 36.9% to 40.3% (+3.4 points) and AIME 2024 from 47.9% to 51.1% (+3.2 points). Qwen3-14B saw even larger gains, with AIME 2024 improving from 47.4% to 53.8% (+6.4 points) and AIME 2025 from 39.7% to 44.6% (+4.9 points). Similar gains were observed for Qwen3-1.7B.
 - With **self-context proposals** (Qwen3 model augmented with failure-lesson memory): Gains were also observed, though generally smaller than with DeepSeek-V4. For Qwen3-8B, AIME 2025 reached 38.5% and AIME 2024 reached 49.6%. This indicates that the mechanism is effective even without a stronger external teacher.
- **OPD vs. Forward-KL Ablation:** On the matched self-context variant, replacing forward-KL consolidation with On-Policy Distillation (OPD)-style supervision resulted in weaker performance, trailing TREK (self-context) by 1.1–1.6 points on AIME 2024 and 1.8–2.9 points on AIME 2025. This supports the design choice of forward-KL as a more effective objective for direct support expansion.
- **MATH-500 Benchmark (Easier Task):** On MATH-500, where direct GRPO was already near-saturated (e.g., 88.3% for Qwen3-8B), TREK still showed modest improvements (e.g., Qwen3-8B improved to 91.5% with DeepSeek-V4 proposals), but the gains were smaller due to limited headroom.

#### 4.2. Agentic Task Results (Table 2, Table 3, Figure 1c)

- **ALFWorld (Household Planning):**
 - Direct GRPO achieved 75.8% success rate.
 - TREK with DeepSeek-V4 proposals raised it to 82.8% (+7.0 points).
 - TREK with self-context proposals reached 80.4% (+4.6 points).
 - OPD (self-context) was 78.3%, further confirming forward-KL's advantage in this setting.
- **ScienceWorld (Science Experiment Simulator):**
 - Direct GRPO achieved 12.5% success rate.
 - TREK with DeepSeek-V4 proposals more than doubled the success rate to 26.7% (+14.2 points).
 - TREK with self-context proposals reached 23.4% (+10.9 points).
- **Gains Concentrated on Hardest Task Types (ALFWorld):** A detailed per-task-type breakdown for ALFWorld (Table 3) showed that TREK's improvements were most significant on the task types with the lowest GRPO baseline success rates. For example, "Heat & Place" gained +19.1 points (from 59.5% to 78.6%), and "Examine in Light" gained +12.6 points (from 56.2% to 68.8%). Task types already performing well, such as "Pick & Place" (91.2%), saw negligible change. This provides direct evidence that TREK effectively targets and improves performance on the student's weakest exploration areas.
- **Accelerated Training (Figure 3):** TREK significantly accelerated early training on both ALFWorld and ScienceWorld. For instance, on ALFWorld, TREK (self-context) surpassed 60% train success rate by step 20, whereas GRPO remained below 50% and required approximately five times more steps to reach comparable levels. This suggests TREK enhances training efficiency by injecting verified solution modes into the student's support early, reducing the need for extensive random exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TREK addresses a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning for language models: the exploration bottleneck on hard reasoning tasks. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

1. **Overcoming GRPO Limitations:** By specifically targeting prompts where the student's on-policy support is insufficient to discover correct solutions, TREK directly augments the capabilities of GRPO and similar on-policy RL methods. This makes these methods more robust and effective across a wider range of problem difficulties.
2. **Generalizability through Output-Only Interface:** TREK's design, which relies solely on verified output trajectories from a proposal source, makes it highly flexible. It can leverage a diverse array of external black-box teachers, white-box teachers, or even the same model with additional inference-time context (self-context variant). This broad applicability means it can be integrated into various practical scenarios without requiring access to internal teacher model parameters or logits.
3. **Enhanced Training Efficiency:** The empirical results demonstrate that TREK significantly accelerates learning, especially on the hardest task types. By pre-seeding the student's policy with "reachable" verified solutions, it reduces the number of expensive on-policy rollouts and optimization steps needed for the student to discover these modes through trial and error. This has substantial practical implications for reducing computational costs and training time for LLMs in complex environments or reasoning tasks.
4. **Novel View of Distillation:** TREK reframes distillation not merely as imitation or credit assignment on existing student behaviors, but as a mechanism for *exploration support expansion*. This conceptual shift opens new avenues for integrating teacher knowledge to improve student capabilities, particularly in regimes of sparse reward and challenging exploration.
5. **Scalability Across Model Sizes and Domains:** The method's effectiveness was shown across different scales of Qwen3 models (1.7B, 8B, 14B) and diverse domains, including mathematical reasoning (AIME) and agentic tasks (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld). This indicates its potential for broad application in developing more capable and efficient LLMs.
6. **Foundation for Future Work:** The paper suggests that the staged decomposition of TREK could serve as an ingredient for continual skill acquisition, enabling models to learn new tasks by incorporating context-conditioned proposals. It also indicates extensibility to other post-training frameworks like DAPO and GSPO that share similar exploration bottlenecks.

In summary, TREK provides a practical and principled method to mitigate a critical limitation of on-policy RL for LLMs, enhancing their ability to explore and learn from complex problem spaces, and broadening the utility of distillation in the context of reinforcement learning.
