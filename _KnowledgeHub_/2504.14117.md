# PEFT A2Z: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Survey for Large Language and Vision Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a structured overview of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) techniques, examining their mechanisms, trade-offs, and applications across language, vision, and multimodal domains. It details how PEFT methods, such as LoRA and various adapter designs, enable the efficient adaptation of large models by updating only a small fraction of parameters, thereby addressing the resource and storage challenges of full fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Traditional fine-tuning of large language and vision models demands substantial computational resources, memory, and storage, making it inaccessible for many researchers and organizations.
- Full fine-tuning often leads to overfitting on small datasets, catastrophic forgetting of previously learned knowledge, and parameter inefficiency due to redundancy.
- Storing and deploying multiple fully fine-tuned models for various tasks results in significant storage requirements and practical deployment challenges.

## Method

- The paper surveys ==Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)== techniques that adapt large pre-trained models by updating only a fraction of their parameters or introducing a limited number of new ones.
- PEFT methods are classified into ==additive== (e.g., adapters, prompt tuning), ==reparameterized== (e.g., LoRA), selective (e.g., BitFit), hybrid, and unified approaches.
- These methods enable efficient model adaptation by reducing memory, computational overhead, and storage requirements while aiming to maintain performance comparable to full fine-tuning.

## Results

- PEFT techniques have been successfully applied across language, vision, and multimodal domains, enabling efficient adaptation of large models to specialized tasks like fine-grained classification, object detection, and cross-modal understanding.
- Specific PEFT methods, such as adapters and prompt tuning, have shown effectiveness in language tasks, while **LoRA** significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters in large models without sacrificing performance.
- The survey highlights the practical impact of PEFT in broadening the adoption of powerful AI models by making fine-tuning more accessible and resource-efficient.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods can be systematically categorized into five main families: additive, reparameterized, selective, hybrid, and unified approaches, each with distinct mechanisms and trade-offs.
> - Reparameterized methods like LoRA leverage low-rank decomposition to reduce trainable parameters by over 99% for models like GPT-3 while maintaining performance comparable to full fine-tuning.
> - The choice of PEFT method depends on specific task requirements, computational resources, and desired performance characteristics, with different methods being more effective for knowledge-intensive, structural, or generative tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) and vision language models (VLMs) has transformed artificial intelligence, enabling breakthrough capabilities across various applications. However, these models come with significant computational costs, particularly when adapted to specific downstream tasks. Traditional fine-tuning approaches require updating all parameters of pre-trained models, leading to substantial resource requirements and practical limitations.

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as a critical solution to this challenge, allowing adaptation of large models by updating only a fraction of their parameters while maintaining comparable performance to full fine-tuning. As illustrated in the comprehensive timeline below, the field of PEFT has evolved rapidly since 2019, branching into various methodological families.

This survey provides a structured overview of PEFT techniques, examining their mechanisms, trade-offs, and applications across language, vision, and multimodal domains. By understanding these approaches, researchers and practitioners can make informed decisions when adapting large models to specific tasks within resource constraints.

### 2. Challenges of Traditional Fine-Tuning

Traditional fine-tuning of large models presents several significant challenges:

1. **Resource Requirements**: Full fine-tuning demands substantial computational resources, memory, and storage. For instance, fine-tuning a 175B parameter model like GPT-3 requires approximately 700GB of GPU memory for 16-bit precision, placing it beyond the reach of most organizations and researchers.

2. **Overfitting**: Large models can easily overfit when fine-tuned on small datasets, particularly in low-resource scenarios. This leads to poor generalization and reduced performance on unseen data.

3. **Catastrophic Forgetting**: When fine-tuned on new tasks, models often lose performance on previously learned knowledge, a phenomenon known as catastrophic forgetting. This is particularly problematic in continual learning scenarios.

4. **Parameter Inefficiency**: Studies have shown that many parameters in large models are redundant during fine-tuning, with updates concentrating in specific regions. This suggests that full parameter updates are unnecessary and inefficient.

5. **Storage and Deployment**: Each fully fine-tuned model requires storing a complete copy of the base model parameters, leading to significant storage requirements when maintaining multiple specialized models.

These challenges have driven the development of parameter-efficient approaches that update only a small fraction of parameters or introduce a limited number of new parameters during adaptation.

### 3. Taxonomy of PEFT Methods

PEFT methods can be categorized into five main families based on their underlying principles and mechanisms:

1. **Additive Fine-Tuning**: Introduces new, trainable modules while keeping original model parameters frozen. This includes adapter-based methods and prompt-based approaches.

2. **Reparameterized PEFT**: Leverages low-rank decomposition techniques to efficiently parameterize updates to the model.

3. **Selective Fine-Tuning**: Updates only a subset of the original model parameters based on various selection criteria.

4. **Hybrid Approaches**: Combines multiple PEFT techniques to leverage their complementary advantages.

5. **Unified Fine-Tuning**: Provides flexible frameworks that can implement multiple PEFT strategies within a common architecture.

### 4. Additive Fine-Tuning Methods

Additive fine-tuning methods insert new, trainable parameters while keeping the original model parameters frozen. These approaches can be further divided into adapter-based methods and prompt-based methods.

#### 4.1. Adapter-Based Methods

Adapters are small, trainable modules inserted between layers of a pre-trained model. The original adapter design introduced by Houlsby et al. consists of a bottleneck architecture with down-projection, non-linearity, and up-projection components.

Several variants of adapters have been developed:

1. **Serial Adapters**: Inserted sequentially within transformer layers, such as the original Houlsby adapter.

2. **Parallel Adapters**: Applied in parallel to the main network path, with outputs added to the main stream.

3. **Hybrid Adapters**: Combine serial and parallel components for improved performance.

4. **Domain and Task-Specific Adapters**: Specialized for specific application domains or tasks.

The key advantage of adapters is their modularity—they can be mixed and matched for different tasks without interfering with the base model.

#### 4.2. Prompt-Based Methods

Prompt-based methods prepend or modify inputs to the model, creating new trainable parameters through continuous prompt representations. Key approaches include:

1. **Prefix Tuning**: Prepends trainable continuous vectors to each transformer layer, conditioning the model's behavior.

2. **Prompt Tuning**: Adds trainable vectors only to the input layer, creating a simpler alternative to prefix tuning.

3. **P-Tuning**: Inserts trainable prompts at strategic positions in the input sequence.

The advantage of prompt-based methods is their minimal parameter overhead, though they may require more task-specific optimization to achieve performance comparable to adapter-based approaches.

### 5. Reparameterized PEFT Methods

Reparameterized methods leverage mathematical properties to efficiently modify model weights without updating all parameters directly. The most prominent approach in this category is Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).

#### 5.1. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)

LoRA is based on the hypothesis that weight updates during fine-tuning have a low intrinsic rank. Instead of directly updating a weight matrix W, LoRA decomposes the update into a product of two smaller matrices:

ΔW = BA

Where B and A are low-rank matrices (r << d, where d is the original dimension). The actual weight used during forward pass becomes:

W' = W + αBA

Where α is a scaling hyperparameter.

This approach significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters while providing performance comparable to full fine-tuning. For example, applying LoRA to attention weight matrices in a GPT-3 model can reduce trainable parameters by over 99%.

Several LoRA variants have been developed to address specific challenges:

1. **AdaLoRA**: Adapts the rank of each weight matrix dynamically during training.

2. **DyLoRA**: Implements a dynamic forward pass mechanism with conditional parameter selection.

3. **LoRA Dropout**: Applies dropout to LoRA modules for improved generalization.

The key advantages of LoRA and its variants include parameter efficiency, computational efficiency during training, and easy integration with existing model architectures.

### 6. Selective Fine-Tuning Methods

Selective fine-tuning approaches update only a subset of the original model parameters based on various selection criteria:

1. **BitFit**: Updates only the bias terms in the model, which constitute less than 0.1% of total parameters but can effectively adapt the model to new tasks.

2. **Parameter-Importance Based Methods**: Update parameters based on their importance to task performance, identified through gradient magnitude or other significance metrics.

3. **Structured Methods**: Focus on updating specific architectural components, such as the attention mechanism or feed-forward networks.

These methods trade off fine-grained control for simplicity, often requiring fewer implementation changes than additive or reparameterized approaches.

### 7. Hybrid and Unified Approaches

Hybrid approaches combine multiple PEFT techniques to leverage their complementary strengths:

1. **Unified Frameworks**: Implement multiple PEFT strategies within a common architecture, providing flexibility to choose the most suitable approach for a given task.

2. **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Integration**: Combine PEFT with MoE approaches to enable both parameter efficiency and conditional activation.

3. **Multi-Technique Applications**: Apply different PEFT methods to different components of the model based on their characteristics.

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) based approaches particularly excel in scenarios requiring specialization across diverse tasks.

### 8. Applications Across Domains

PEFT techniques have been successfully applied across various domains:

#### 8.1. Language Models

For large language models, PEFT has enabled efficient adaptation to specialized domains (medical, legal, financial) and specific tasks (summarization, translation, question-answering).

The effectiveness of PEFT for language tasks often depends on the specific task requirements:

1. **Knowledge-Intensive Tasks**: Often benefit from parameter-wise selective fine-tuning to update factual knowledge.

2. **Structural Tasks**: Such as parsing or classification, typically work well with additive methods like adapters.

3. **Generation Tasks**: May benefit from prompt-based or low-rank approaches that preserve the generative capabilities of the base model.

#### 8.2. Vision Models

PEFT has been applied to vision transformers and other vision models for tasks such as:

1. **Fine-grained Image Classification**: Adapting models to specialized categories within broad domains.

2. **Object Detection and Segmentation**: Enabling efficient adaptation to specific visual contexts.

3. **Visual Feature Extraction**: Creating domain-specific feature representations while preserving general visual understanding.

### 9. Open Challenges and Future Directions

Despite significant progress, several challenges and research directions remain in the PEFT field:

1. **Theoretical Understanding**: Current approaches are largely empirical, with limited theoretical foundation explaining why certain PEFT methods work better than others for specific tasks.

2. **Scaling Efficiency**: As models continue to grow, ensuring that PEFT methods maintain their efficiency advantage becomes increasingly important.

3. **Adaptability Across Architectures**: Many PEFT methods are designed for specific architectures, with limited investigation into general principles that apply across diverse model types.

4. **Combination with Inference Optimization**: Integration of PEFT with quantization, pruning, and other inference optimization techniques requires further exploration.

5. **Privacy and Security**: Understanding the privacy implications of fine-tuning strategies, particularly in scenarios involving sensitive data.

Future research directions include:

1. **Fully Automated PEFT Selection**: Systems that automatically determine the optimal PEFT strategy based on task characteristics and resource constraints.

2. **Cross-Modal Transfer**: Methods for transferring PEFT modules across modalities to leverage knowledge from one domain in another.

3. **Dynamic Adaptation**: Approaches that allow models to dynamically adjust their parameters based on input characteristics without explicit fine-tuning.

4. **Hardware-Aware PEFT**: Fine-tuning strategies optimized for specific hardware configurations to maximize efficiency.

### 10. Conclusion

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning represents a critical advancement in making large language and vision models more accessible and practical. By requiring only a fraction of the resources needed for full fine-tuning, PEFT methods enable broader adoption and application of these powerful models.

The landscape of PEFT techniques continues to evolve, with additive, reparameterized, selective, hybrid, and unified approaches each offering distinct advantages for different scenarios. The choice of PEFT method depends on factors including task requirements, available computational resources, and desired performance characteristics.

As models continue to grow in size and capability, the importance of parameter-efficient adaptation will only increase. Future research will likely focus on developing more theoretically grounded, efficient, and adaptable PEFT techniques that further democratize access to state-of-the-art AI models.

By understanding the trade-offs between different PEFT approaches and their applicability across domains, researchers and practitioners can make informed decisions when adapting large models to specific applications, balancing performance requirements with resource constraints.
