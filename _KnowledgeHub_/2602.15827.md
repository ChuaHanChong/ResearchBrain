# Perceptive Humanoid Parkour: Chaining Dynamic Human Skills via Motion Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Amazon FAR, UC Berkeley, CMU, and Stanford developed the Perceptive Humanoid Parkour (PHP) framework, enabling a Unitree G1 humanoid to perform dynamic parkour skills such as climbing a 1.25m wall and vaulting at 3.41 m/s in real-world scenarios. This was accomplished by generating diverse, long-horizon reference trajectories from sparse human motion data using motion matching and distilling a multi-skill visuomotor policy with a hybrid DAgger and RL objective.

## Problem

- Endowing humanoids with human-level agility and adaptivity for highly dynamic, contact-rich motions like parkour remains challenging.
- Efficiently learning and composing diverse, long-horizon dynamic skills for humanoids is difficult due to sparse human motion data and the complexity of training from scratch.
- Integrating perceptive capabilities with robust whole-body control for adaptive, context-aware decision-making in varied environments poses a significant hurdle.

## Method

- A modular, three-stage framework, Perceptive Humanoid Parkour (**PHP**), leverages motion matching for kinematic skill composition and a hybrid DAgger and RL objective for policy distillation.
- Motion matching is employed to synthesize diverse, long-horizon kinematic reference trajectories by chaining sparse human parkour skills and locomotion, exposing the policy to varied approach conditions.
- A single, depth-based visuomotor policy is trained using a hybrid DAgger and PPO objective to distill multiple expert policies, providing success-driven feedback for dynamic, contact-rich behaviors.

## Results

- The Unitree G1 humanoid robot successfully performed dynamic parkour skills in real-world environments, including climbing a 1.25 m wall (**96%** of its height) in 3.63 seconds and executing a cat vault with a peak speed of **3.41** m/s.
- The robot demonstrated the ability to traverse multi-obstacle courses by chaining various skills and adapt to real-time obstacle perturbations (approximately 0.5 m displacement) using onboard depth perception.
- In simulation, the PHP framework achieved high success rates (e.g., 0.95 on 76 cm wall at **1.0** m/s, 0.90 on 94 cm wall at **2.0** m/s), outperforming baselines that lacked explicit long-horizon skill composition or the hybrid training objective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Motion matching effectively transforms sparse human motion data into dense, diverse, and long-horizon kinematic reference trajectories, enabling flexible composition of complex parkour skills without explicit hand-crafted transitions.
> - Combining DAgger with a PPO objective is essential for training visuomotor policies for highly dynamic, contact-rich humanoid behaviors, as the RL component addresses critical actions for task success that pure imitation might miss.
> - The distillation framework scales well with increased model capacity and rollout throughput, facilitating the learning of a broad range of dynamic skills for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is progressing toward enabling robots to perform complex, dynamic maneuvers in varied environments. While significant advancements have been made in stable locomotion on diverse terrains for both quadrupedal and humanoid robots, endowing humanoids with the agility and adaptivity characteristic of highly dynamic human motions, such as parkour, remains an active area of research.

Previous work in perceptive terrain traversal for legged robots has demonstrated capabilities like traversing sparse footholds, gaps, and tall obstacles, particularly with quadrupedal platforms. However, translating this success to humanoids introduces challenges related to their high-dimensional whole-body control and the difficulty of training such behaviors from scratch using reinforcement learning (RL). Many prior humanoid locomotion efforts have focused on lower-dynamic tasks like stair climbing or stepping onto low platforms, often employing teacher-student pipelines where state-based experts are distilled into vision-based students.

Another critical aspect of this landscape is the utilization of human motion data. Such data is valuable for generating agile and natural humanoid behaviors, reducing the need for extensive reward engineering. However, dynamic human motion data, especially for contact-rich maneuvers, is inherently scarce and often consists of short, isolated clips. This scarcity poses a challenge for learning long-horizon tasks that require diverse within-skill variations and smooth transitions between heterogeneous skills. Existing methods for skill chaining, such as those relying on implicit learning (e.g., Adversarial Motion Priors - AMP) or learned kinematic models, often face limitations in data efficiency, trajectory quality in low-data regimes, or computational cost for real-time planning.

This work addresses these challenges by integrating existing paradigms—human motion data utilization, teacher-student distillation, and perceptive control—with a novel emphasis on **motion matching for long-horizon skill composition**. It positions itself as an advancement in enabling humanoids to perform complex, multi-skill, perception-driven parkour by efficiently leveraging sparse human motion data to generate dense, adaptable reference trajectories, and by improving the scalability of policy distillation for highly dynamic, contact-rich behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to autonomously perform long-horizon, vision-based parkour across challenging obstacle courses. This encompasses achieving both human-like agility in individual dynamic skills and the ability to seamlessly compose and adapt these skills based on perceived environmental context.

The motivation for this work arises from several core challenges identified in current humanoid robotics research:

1. **Achieving Human-level Agility and Adaptivity:** Humanoids currently lack the dynamic expressiveness and adaptive decision-making capabilities seen in humans traversing complex, varied terrains. Parkour serves as a specific testbed for these capabilities, demanding robust control in high-dimensional action spaces for highly dynamic, contact-rich skills (e.g., climbing high walls, vaulting at speed).
2. **Coupling Control with Exteroception:** For robots to adapt to environmental variations and respond to perturbations, low-level control must be tightly integrated with perception, particularly vision. This enables context-aware decision-making, such as selecting the appropriate skill (step over, climb, vault, roll off) based on obstacle geometry and height.
3. **Scalable Skill Consolidation:** Generalizing beyond isolated maneuvers to traverse complex obstacle courses requires consolidating numerous diverse and highly dynamic skills into a single visuomotor policy. This becomes increasingly difficult as the number and variety of skills grow, demanding a scalable training approach.
4. **Addressing Motion Data Scarcity and Diversity for Long-Horizon Tasks:** While human motion data is beneficial for learning dynamic behaviors, datasets for fast, contact-rich maneuvers are typically sparse, containing only a few short demonstrations per skill. Long-horizon tasks like parkour, however, require rich within-skill variation (e.g., adapting approach to obstacles) and smooth transitions between skills. Current methods often struggle to generate diverse, adaptive, and smoothly chaining behaviors from such limited data.

To address the data scarcity and skill composition problem, the authors are motivated to employ **motion matching**. This technique can densify sparse motion libraries by synthesizing diverse transitions and long-horizon motions, preserving realism and fluidity. This provides the necessary varied kinematic reference trajectories for subsequent policy learning.

To address the scalable learning of visuomotor policies for dynamic skills, the authors identify limitations in pure imitation learning (e.g., DAgger) for highly dynamic, contact-rich humanoid behaviors. Imitation learning may not adequately account for episode outcomes, leading to sub-optimal actions (e.g., insufficient torque for a climb) even if they exhibit low per-step imitation loss. This motivates the development of a **hybrid DAgger and RL objective** during policy distillation, where the RL component provides task-level corrective feedback to steer the student towards successful traversals.

In summary, the key objectives are to develop a modular framework that:
- Leverages human motion data to generate agile, long-horizon parkour trajectories.
- Trains a single, depth-based, multi-skill policy capable of autonomous, context-aware decision-making.
- Achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of these dynamic parkour behaviors onto a physical humanoid robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Perceptive Humanoid Parkour (PHP) framework employs a modular, three-stage approach: kinematic skill composition via motion matching, expert policy training, and multi-skill student policy distillation.

**A. Skill Composition via Motion Matching**
This stage aims to generate diverse, long-horizon kinematic reference trajectories by composing atomic parkour skills with locomotion.
1. **Motion Database and Feature Precomputation:** Human motion data, retargeted to a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid using OmniRetarget, forms the motion database. Each frame `i` is associated with a kinematic pose `q_i` and a 27-dimensional matching feature vector `x_i`. This feature vector, expressed in the character's local coordinate frame, concatenates:
 - Future root trajectory: Planar root positions and facing directions at 0.33s, 0.67s, and 1s into the future.
 - Local foot state: Positions and linear velocities of left and right feet relative to the root frame.
 - Root velocity: Linear velocity of the root.
 The database is augmented by mirroring all motion clips to improve data coverage.
2. **Query Feature Construction:** At runtime, a query feature `x̂_t` is constructed from the current robot configuration `q_t` and a 2D velocity command. The pose-based part (`local foot state`, `root velocity`) is extracted from `q_t`. The command-based part (`future root trajectory`) is generated by converting the 2D velocity command into a future root trajectory using a critically damped spring model for both planar velocity and heading direction, evaluated at specified future horizons.
3. **Long-Horizon Parkour Trajectory Synthesis:** Motion matching, a nearest-neighbor search `arg min_i ||x̂_t - x_i||^2` in the feature space, is used to select the best matching frame.
 - **Composition Strategy:** Trajectories are synthesized as "Locomotion → Parkour Skill → Locomotion," routing all skills through a shared locomotion manifold. This allows consistent transitions without requiring hand-captured transitions for every skill pair.
 - **Locomotion Mode:** Standard motion matching is used with a locomotion database. Playback advances sequentially.
 - **Locomotion → Skill Transition:** When a skill `k` is commanded, the search window `C_t` is restricted to a pre-skill entry window `E_k` (approach phase before the main maneuver). The robot transitions to the matched entry frame, and the paired terrain asset is placed relative to the robot's current root pose. The skill clip is replayed sequentially until its end.
 - **Skill → Locomotion Transition:** After a skill concludes, motion matching resumes with the locomotion database.
4. **Transition Smoothing:** Inertialization is applied during transitions between clips to ensure continuity by computing and gradually decaying an offset between the current and target motions.
5. **Dataset Construction and Randomization:** Long-horizon reference trajectories are generated by rolling out this composition procedure.
 - **Diverse Approaches:** By randomizing initial approach distances (e.g., 1.5-4.5 m) and pre-skill locomotion durations (0.1-3 s), the system induces diverse stride phases and entry poses, densifying the distribution of pre-skill states.
 - **Terrain Randomization:** Obstacle geometries (width, dimensions perturbed by ±5 cm) and yaw (±45°) are randomized around the synthesized trajectory to improve robustness without invalidating the reference.
 - **Distractors:** Randomly sized and posed distractor boxes are added to improve robustness to irrelevant objects.

**B. Learning a Highly-Dynamic Visuomotor Policy**
This stage involves training skill-specific expert policies and distilling them into a single, depth-based student policy.

1. **Training Expert Policies with Motion Tracking:**
 - **Methodology:** Expert policies are trained using RL-based motion tracking, building on approaches like BeyondMimic and OmniRetarget.
 - **Observations:** Include reference joint position/velocity, reference pelvis pose error, pelvis linear/angular velocity, current joint position/velocity, previous action, and a 0.7m x 0.7m height scan (for terrain adaptation). Critically, privileged global observations (pelvis global position and velocity) are provided to the expert to learn recovery behaviors.
 - **Rewards and Terminations:** DeepMimic-style tracking rewards (body position, orientation, velocity, anchor position/orientation) are used, alongside regularization terms (action smoothness, joint limits, self-contacts). Tracking-based early termination is implemented.
 - **Adaptive Sampling:** Prioritizes sampling from frequently failing regions to facilitate learning of difficult skills (e.g., high-wall climbing).
 - **Domain Randomization:** Standard physical parameter randomization (friction, restitution, joint offsets, COM offset) and root velocity perturbations are applied.

2. **Distilling a Unified Student Policy with DAgger and RL:**
 - **Motivation:** Pure DAgger (imitation learning) is found insufficient for highly dynamic skills, as per-step imitation objectives do not explicitly account for episode outcomes or high-magnitude torque bursts required for success.
 - **Hybrid Objective:** A combined objective `L = λ_PPO L_PPO + λ_D L_D` is used, where `L_PPO` is the PPO objective and `L_D` is the DAgger loss. The PPO component provides a success-driven signal, encouraging the exploitation of expert behaviors necessary for task completion.
 - **Observations:** Proprioception (pelvis gravity vector, angular velocity, joint positions/velocities, previous action) and depth images (rendered with Nvidia WARP) are provided. A 2D velocity command is also an input.
 - **Camera Modeling and Depth Artifacts:** Simulated camera is calibrated, extrinsics are randomized (±2.5cm, ±2.5°), realistic depth noise is injected (without Gaussian blur), and observation delay (60-80ms) is randomized to simulate hardware latency.
 - **Curriculum:** A warmup curriculum gradually shifts from DAgger to PPO:
 - `λ_D` linearly tunes down to 0.1 over the first half of training.
 - Termination threshold for the student is relaxed from 0.5m to 1m (on a linear schedule) to prevent premature termination of valid mirrored executions.
 - Adaptive learning rate and KL-based exploration are enabled only when `λ_PPO` exceeds 0.1.
 - **Adaptive Sampling (Disabled for Student):** Disabled to avoid undersampling "borderline" clips that don't fail in simulation but exhibit jittery behavior, which could lead to sim-to-real gaps. Skills are sampled evenly.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents both real-world demonstrations and quantitative simulation results to evaluate the Perceptive Humanoid Parkour framework.

**A. Real-World Results (Unitree G1 Humanoid)**
The experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot demonstrate the system's ability to execute highly dynamic, long-horizon parkour behaviors using onboard depth perception.

1. **Human-Level Agility:**
 - **High-Wall Climb:** The robot successfully climbed a 1.25 m wall (96% of its height) in 3.63 seconds from toe-off, a pace comparable to a human parkourist. The motion sequence, including toe-off, pull-up, swing, and stable stand, exhibited closely matched timing with human performance.
 - **Cat Vault:** The robot cleared a 0.4 m high, 0.5 m long obstacle, covering over 2 m forward (154% of its height) in 0.8 seconds from toe-off to toe-on. It achieved a peak forward speed of 3.41 m/s and an average of 2.53 m/s, demonstrating momentum preservation.
 - **Drop Landing:** The robot performed a controlled drop landing from a 1.25 m platform, absorbing impact and stabilizing its posture through lower-body joint flexion.

2. **Multi-Obstacle Course and Adaptivity:**
 - The policy demonstrated generalization to complex, multi-obstacle courses despite training on single-obstacle traversals. This capability is attributed to motion-matching-based skill composition that generates long-horizon references chaining skills through locomotion segments, exposing the policy to diverse approach conditions.
 - The robot composed multiple skills, including stepping, low and high wall climbs, into a continuous run over various obstacles. The visuomotor policy generated smooth skill transitions online.
 - **Adaptation to Perturbations:** The robot adapted to real-time obstacle displacements (approximately 0.5 m) during execution by adjusting its approach and maneuver timing, completing the traversal.

**B. Quantitative Results in Simulation**
Evaluation was conducted on parkour traversal tasks with obstacles of varying heights (36 cm, 58 cm, 76 cm, 94 cm) and two commanded speeds (1.0 m/s, 2.0 m/s), with randomized approach distances and yaw. Success rate was measured as traversing the obstacle and an additional 1.5 m without falling.

1. **Baseline Comparison (Table I):**
 - **Velocity Tracking:** This baseline, relying on reward shaping without human reference motion, succeeded on 36 cm obstacles but failed on higher ones (0% success for 58cm, 76cm), indicating limitations for dynamic, whole-body parkour skills without human priors.
 - **Uncomposed Motion Data:** Training on isolated locomotion and atomic parkour clips yielded poor performance (e.g., 0.06 success on 1.0 m/s, 36 cm obstacle, dropping to 0% on harder tasks). The robot frequently failed to transition into climbing or jumping, highlighting the necessity of explicit long-horizon skill composition and diverse visual context.
 - **End-to-end Depth Policy:** Training a single depth-based policy directly on motion-matching data showed some capability for low obstacles (0.95 success on 1.0 m/s, 36 cm), but performance significantly degraded on harder tasks (e.g., 0.08 on 1.0 m/s, 76 cm). This suggests challenges in RL exploration for highly dynamic skills when training from scratch.
 - **Ours (PHP):** The proposed framework achieved significantly higher success rates across all tasks, consistently reaching near-perfect scores (e.g., 0.95-1.00 for most tasks, including 0.95 on 1.0 m/s, 76 cm and 0.90 on 2.0 m/s, 94 cm). This demonstrates the effectiveness of combining motion matching for composition with the teacher-student distillation pipeline.

2. **Ablation Study (Table II):**
 - **Motion Matching Density:**
 - `Extreme Distances` (only min/max approach distances): Led to reduced success rates (e.g., 0.62 for 1.0 m/s, 76 cm), indicating failure to generalize to intermediate approach distances where precise contact timing is critical.
 - `Half Density` (half of full data): Generally lower success rates on harder skills (e.g., 0.32 for 1.0 m/s, 76 cm), particularly where reduced local density impacted precise hand placement timing. This confirmed that dense coverage of approach conditions is crucial for robust skill execution.
 - **Training Scalability:**
 - Reduced parallel environments (1/4 or 1/2) and smaller MLP capacities (3-layer or 4-layer) generally resulted in lower success rates, especially for challenging parkour tasks (e.g., 3-layer MLP had 0.00 success on 1.0 m/s, 94 cm obstacle). This indicates that the distillation framework benefits from increased model capacity and rollout throughput, scaling favorably unlike from-scratch RL.
 - **RL in Distillation:**
 - `DAgger Only`: Exhibited a substantial performance drop (e.g., 0.03 success for 1.0 m/s, 76 cm). The student struggled with high-magnitude torque bursts (e.g., for pull-ups), suggesting that per-step imitation alone is insufficient for critical, dynamic actions that determine episode success.
 - `DAgger + RL Alive Reward` and `DAgger + RL Root Tracking`: Achieved success rates comparable to the full whole-body tracking reward on difficult skills. This indicates that the RL objective, when co-trained with DAgger, primarily acts as a success-driven exploitation signal, compensating for DAgger's conservatism, and is relatively robust to detailed reward design. The DAgger term's continuous presence was found critical for regularization, preventing jittery behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a modular framework, Perceptive Humanoid Parkour (PHP), that advances the capabilities of humanoid robots in performing dynamic and adaptive locomotion.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Efficient Kinematic Skill Composition:** The use of motion matching to chain retargeted human motions into diverse, long-horizon trajectories is a significant contribution. It addresses the challenge of motion data scarcity by generating a dense set of reference motions that enable flexible composition and smooth transitions between heterogeneous skills, while preserving the agility and fluidity of human movements. This method implicitly exposes the policy to a wide range of approach conditions, which is crucial for learning context-aware behaviors.
- **Scalable Teacher-Student Training Framework:** The hybrid DAgger and RL objective for distilling multiple expert policies into a single visuomotor policy provides a scalable solution for learning a diverse set of highly dynamic skills. The integration of RL effectively mitigates the limitations of pure imitation learning for contact-rich maneuvers, where specific, high-magnitude actions are critical for success but may be underestimated by per-step imitation losses. This approach facilitates zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, a common challenge in robotics.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Agility and Adaptivity:** The successful deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, showcasing highly dynamic parkour skills (e.g., high-wall climbing, fast vaulting) and long-horizon multi-obstacle traversal with closed-loop adaptation to real-time perturbations, represents a notable achievement in physical humanoid robotics. The performance levels, particularly in high-wall climbing, demonstrate capabilities comparable to human parkourists in terms of pace and coherence.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement in Humanoid Locomotion:** This work pushes the boundaries of agile and adaptive whole-body control for humanoids, moving beyond stable walking to complex, dynamic interactions with the environment. This is critical for developing robots capable of operating in unstructured and challenging real-world environments.
- **Robustness to Environmental Variation:** The framework's ability to adapt to varying obstacle geometries, heights, and real-time perturbations is crucial for practical robotic deployment, where environments are inherently uncertain and dynamic.
- **Foundation for More Complex Tasks:** The ability to chain diverse, dynamic skills provides a foundation for tackling even more complex humanoid tasks that require sequential, multi-modal interactions, such as rescue operations, construction, or personal assistance in dynamic settings.
- **Efficient Use of Human Data:** By leveraging motion matching to densify sparse human motion data, the approach offers a method for efficiently utilizing limited, high-quality human demonstrations, reducing the need for extensive manual reward engineering or from-scratch RL exploration.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that could be addressed in future work:
- **Semantic Scene Understanding:** The current system lacks semantic understanding of the scene. Incorporating richer conditioning signals, such as language, could enable finer control over diversity and styles of motion.
- **Perception and Hardware Constraints:** The onboard depth camera's short range and narrow field-of-view can lead to perceptual ambiguity, forcing the robot to commit to actions before complete obstacle geometry is visible. Improved sensing and semantic scene understanding could reduce this ambiguity.
- **Hardware Capabilities:** The current hardware lacks strong hands or grippers, limiting the exploration of more extreme climbing beyond robot height or hanging maneuvers, suggesting future work could explore platforms with enhanced manipulation capabilities.
