# Perceptive Humanoid Parkour: Chaining Dynamic Human Skills via Motion Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Amazon FAR, UC Berkeley, CMU, and Stanford developed the Perceptive Humanoid Parkour (PHP) framework, enabling a Unitree G1 humanoid to perform dynamic parkour skills such as climbing a 1.25m wall and vaulting at 3.41 m/s in real-world scenarios. This was accomplished by generating diverse, long-horizon reference trajectories from sparse human motion data using motion matching and distilling a multi-skill visuomotor policy with a hybrid DAgger and RL objective.

## Problem

- Endowing humanoids with human-level agility and adaptivity for highly dynamic, contact-rich motions like parkour remains challenging.
- Efficiently learning and composing diverse, long-horizon dynamic skills for humanoids is difficult due to sparse human motion data and the complexity of training from scratch.
- Integrating perceptive capabilities with robust whole-body control for adaptive, context-aware decision-making in varied environments poses a significant hurdle.

## Method

- A modular, three-stage framework, Perceptive Humanoid Parkour (**PHP**), leverages motion matching for kinematic skill composition and a hybrid DAgger and RL objective for policy distillation.
- Motion matching is employed to synthesize diverse, long-horizon kinematic reference trajectories by chaining sparse human parkour skills and locomotion, exposing the policy to varied approach conditions.
- A single, depth-based visuomotor policy is trained using a hybrid DAgger and PPO objective to distill multiple expert policies, providing success-driven feedback for dynamic, contact-rich behaviors.

## Results

- The Unitree G1 humanoid robot successfully performed dynamic parkour skills in real-world environments, including climbing a 1.25 m wall (**96%** of its height) in 3.63 seconds and executing a cat vault with a peak speed of **3.41** m/s.
- The robot demonstrated the ability to traverse multi-obstacle courses by chaining various skills and adapt to real-time obstacle perturbations (approximately 0.5 m displacement) using onboard depth perception.
- In simulation, the PHP framework achieved high success rates (e.g., 0.95 on 76 cm wall at **1.0** m/s, 0.90 on 94 cm wall at **2.0** m/s), outperforming baselines that lacked explicit long-horizon skill composition or the hybrid training objective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Motion matching effectively transforms sparse human motion data into dense, diverse, and long-horizon kinematic reference trajectories, enabling flexible composition of complex parkour skills without explicit hand-crafted transitions.
> - Combining DAgger with a PPO objective is essential for training visuomotor policies for highly dynamic, contact-rich humanoid behaviors, as the RL component addresses critical actions for task success that pure imitation might miss.
> - The distillation framework scales well with increased model capacity and rollout throughput, facilitating the learning of a broad range of dynamic skills for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
