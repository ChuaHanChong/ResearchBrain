# DeepScan: A Training-Free Framework for Visually Grounded Reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepScan is a training-free framework that equips large vision-language models (LVLMs) with robust visually grounded reasoning by mimicking human bottom-up scanning processes. It achieved a 16.3% accuracy improvement over vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the V* benchmark, outperforming existing training-free and several 70B general LVLMs.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) struggle with visually grounded reasoning, especially in complex or noisy scenes.
- LVLMs suffer from "attention sink" and "attention drift," leading to hallucinations and suboptimal performance in fine-grained understanding.
- Existing training-free grounding methods primarily use a top-down localization strategy that is susceptible to noisy contexts and similar objects.

## Method

- A ==Hierarchical Scanning== component employs a ==bottom-up visual grounding paradigm== to robustly localize visual evidence using local cues and multi-scale extraction.
- A ==Refocusing== component optimizes the evidence view by collaboratively searching for an evidence-centric crop with an optimal surrounding context.
- An ==Evidence-Enhanced Reasoning== component integrates both fine-grained localized evidence and the optimized coarse-grained view into a multi-image prompt for the LVLM's final answer generation.

## Results

- DeepScan achieved **90.6%** overall accuracy on V* with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, demonstrating a **16.3%** improvement over the vanilla model and outperforming other training-free baselines by up to **6.3%**.
- It attained state-of-the-art performance on perception tasks like V* Attribute (**93.0-93.9%**), surpassing several 70B general LVLMs and competing favorably with RL-based methods.
- The framework scaled effectively across diverse LVLMs (**7B** to **72B** parameters), improving Qwen2.5-VL-72B by **9.4%** on V* while achieving a favorable performance-latency trade-off (**3.1s** vs. **6.9s** for DeepEyes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mimicking human bottom-up scanning provides a more robust mechanism for visually grounded reasoning, especially for fine-grained details, compared to traditional top-down methods.
> - Point-based proxies, which fuse geometric and semantic information, combined with morphological post-processing, are effective for extracting integral and precise visual evidence from local cues.
> - Optimizing the contextual view around localized evidence, through both zoom-in and zoom-out actions, significantly enhances reasoning performance by balancing necessary information with distracting context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities across diverse visual tasks. However, a critical limitation remains their struggle with **visually grounded reasoning**, especially in complex or noisy scenes. Unlike humans, who can robustly localize and focus on critical visual evidence to answer questions, LVLMs often suffer from "attention sink" (distraction by noisy context) or "attention drift" (misattributing attention to semantically similar objects). This leads to hallucinations and suboptimal performance in fine-grained understanding tasks.

Existing approaches to enhance LVLM grounding broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based methods:** These approaches train LVLMs with visually supervised rewards or curiosity-driven objectives to enable active perception and localization. While effective, they are often computationally costly, challenging to train, and difficult to generalize across different LVLM architectures and scales.
2. **Training-free paradigms:** These methods leverage external visual experts (e.g., GroundingDINO, LangSAM), tree search, or self-refinement mechanisms to enhance LVLMs at inference time without additional training.

DeepScan primarily competes within the "training-free" paradigm but critically distinguishes itself by addressing a fundamental flaw prevalent in most existing methods: their **top-down grounding strategy**. This traditional approach typically involves an image-level search for coarse proxies (region proposals, detection boxes, textual descriptions) followed by refinement. The authors argue that this one-shot localization of complete evidence is highly susceptible to noisy contexts and similar objects.

Inspired by human cognitive processes, DeepScan proposes a novel **bottom-up grounding paradigm**. Instead of starting with an image-level guess and refining it, DeepScan first identifies discriminative local cues and then progressively recovers the complete evidence from these cues. This approach aims to provide a more robust and human-like mechanism for visually grounded reasoning, particularly for fine-grained understanding where subtle details are crucial. By being training-free and compatible with various LVLMs, DeepScan offers a scalable and adaptable solution that overcomes the generalization limitations of RL-based methods and the robustness issues of prior training-free approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DeepScan is to equip Large Vision-Language Models with robust and interpretable visually grounded reasoning capabilities, especially in challenging environments characterized by noisy context or subtle targets. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific motivations and objectives:

- **Mimic Human Cognition:** The core inspiration comes from how humans tackle complex visual tasks. For example, when solving "spot-the-difference" puzzles, people first scan local patches for subtle cues and then integrate these cues into the broader context to identify discrepancies. DeepScan aims to replicate this "bottom-up" reasoning process.
- **Overcome Top-Down Grounding Limitations:** Current LVLMs and many existing grounding methods suffer from "attention sink" and "attention drift" due to their reliance on one-shot, coarse-to-fine localization. DeepScan seeks to mitigate these issues by introducing a more robust evidence localization strategy.
- **Enhance Fine-Grained Visual Understanding:** Many visual tasks require precise identification and reasoning about small or subtle objects and their attributes or spatial relationships. DeepScan is motivated to significantly improve LVLMs' performance on such fine-grained tasks.
- **Provide Interpretable Answers:** By explicitly localizing and highlighting the visual evidence, DeepScan aims to make the LVLM's reasoning process more transparent and its answers more interpretable, allowing users to verify the grounding.
- **Develop a Training-Free and Scalable Framework:** A key practical objective is to create a framework that can be seamlessly integrated with existing LVLMs of various architectures and scales without requiring additional model training or fine-tuning, thereby minimizing adaptation costs and maximizing generalizability.
- **Optimize Evidence View:** Beyond just localization, the framework aims to ensure that the LVLM receives an "optimal" view of the evidence, complete enough for reasoning but free from excessive distracting context.

In essence, DeepScan strives to enable LVLMs to perceive, localize, and reason about visual information with human-like precision and robustness, translating a fundamental cognitive ability into a practical AI framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepScan is a training-free framework structured around three main components: **Hierarchical Scanning**, **Refocusing**, and **Evidence-Enhanced Reasoning**. It augments LVLMs with two plug-and-play models: a *search expert* (BLIP-ITM base) for local cue exploration and a *visual expert* (LangSAM) for image-level segmentation and detection.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Scanning (Bottom-Up Visual Grounding)

This component is designed to robustly localize visual evidence by leveraging local cues and multi-scale extraction, addressing the shortcomings of one-shot, top-down approaches.

- **Local Cue Exploration:**
 - The input image `I` is partitioned into patches `p`.
 - For each patch `p` and question `q`, a search expert (BLIP-ITM base) generates an attention map `S_p` indicating potential cue regions using GradCAM.
 - Otsu's method is applied to `S_p` to binarize it into `S_p+`, identifying distinct cue regions `G_k^p`.
 - These cues are represented by **point-based proxies `c_k^p`**. Unlike conventional region proposals, these proxies combine geometric information (distance to cue boundary) and semantic information (normalized attention scores) to pinpoint semantically significant regions within the cue. This makes them robust to incomplete evidence spanning multiple patches.
 - These in-patch proxies are then lifted to the image coordinates `C'_p`.
- **Multi-scale Evidence Extraction:**
 - For each proxy `c'` in `C'_p`, the visual expert (LangSAM) performs **point-prompt segmentation** on the full image `I` to generate an evidence mask `m`.
 - To ensure evidence integrity and counteract artifacts (e.g., holes, incomplete context) inherent to single-point prompts, **morphological post-processing** (closing and dilation) is applied to `m` to produce an enhanced mask `m+`.
 - The bounding box `b` of `m+` and the cropped evidence `e` (from `I` using `b`) form an evidence candidate `(b, e)`.
 - The LVLM then makes a binary judgment on whether `e` is relevant to `q`. If affirmed, it's added to the evidence set `E`.
 - A global visited mask is maintained to avoid redundant computation on already-processed regions.
- **Heuristic Acceleration:** To optimize efficiency, especially since smaller, less salient evidence yields higher performance gains, DeepScan pre-filters candidates by area and evaluates only the top-k smallest regions. This limits LVLM evaluations and speeds up inference significantly, with `k=1` retaining ~96% of performance while doubling speed.

#### 3.2. Refocusing (Evidence View Optimization)

Hierarchical Scanning localizes evidence, but its precise context window might be suboptimal. Refocusing refines this by collaboratively searching for an evidence-centric view with optimal surrounding context.

- **Initialization:** The search starts with `V_1`, an aggregated view encompassing all evidence `e_m` found by Hierarchical Scanning.
- **States & Successor Function:** Candidate views are generated by two actions:
 - **Zoom-In (IN):** Narrows the context by cropping `V` to the union of detections conditioned on `q` using the visual expert.
 - **Zoom-Out (OUT):** Enlarges the context by isotropically expanding `V` with a scaling factor `s` (set to 1.5).
- **Selection Policy:** An LVLM-based reward function `R(V)` is defined. It combines an indicator (1 if `V` contains sufficient evidence for `q`, 0 otherwise, judged by LVLM) with a size regularizer (inverse of view area `HW/hw`). The policy prefers the smallest view that still contains all necessary evidence.
- **Search Space Design:** Based on empirical principles and observations about `V_1`'s properties, the search space is pruned to a concise set of four views `{V_1, V_2, V_3, V_4}` (where `V_2 = IN(V_1, q)`, `V_3 = OUT(V_1, s)`, `V_4 = IN(V_3, q)`). This depth-2 expansion is traversed in a depth-first, greedy manner to select the best view `V*`.

#### 3.3. Evidence-Enhanced Reasoning

The final stage integrates the localized and refined evidence to produce comprehensive answers.

- **Hybrid Evidence Memory (H):** This memory stores both fine-grained evidence `e` (from Hierarchical Scanning) and the optimized coarse-grained view `V*` (from Refocusing).
- **Multi-Image Prompt:** The hybrid memory `H` is materialized as an ordered multi-image prompt `[e_1..., V*]`. This rich representation allows the LVLM to resolve specific object attributes from fine-grained details while inferring spatial relationships and broader context from the coarser `V*`.
- **Reasoning:** The LVLM `REASON(H, q)` then generates the final, accurate, and well-grounded answer `A`.

**Engineering Optimizations (from Supplementary):** The authors implemented batch processing for attention map calculation, candidate judgment, and view justification, along with parallelized post-processing. Migrating to the vLLM backend with PagedAttention and optimized CUDA kernels further boosts efficiency, resulting in an ~8x speedup over the initial implementation. This addresses the potential latency concerns of multi-step reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DeepScan demonstrates significant improvements in visually grounded reasoning across various benchmarks and LVLM backbones.

- **Fine-Grained Visual Understanding (V* Bench, HR-Bench):**
 - DeepScan, when integrated with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieved a **90.6% overall accuracy on V***, marking a substantial **16.3% improvement** over the vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
 - It consistently **outperforms other training-free baselines** (e.g., DyFo, ZoomRefine) on V* Bench and HR-Bench, with gains of 6.3%, 3.6%, and 2.6% respectively.
 - Remarkably, DeepScan **attains state-of-the-art performance** on perception tasks, surpassing even several 70B general LVLMs (e.g., LLaVA-OV-72B, InternVL3-78B) and competing favorably with leading **RL-based methods** (e.g., DeepEyes) without any additional fine-tuning. For instance, on the V* Attribute subset, DeepScan reaches 93.0-93.9%, demonstrating strong evidence localization capabilities for perception-heavy tasks.
- **General Visual Tasks (TreeBench):**
 - On TreeBench, designed for "thinking-with-images" capabilities, DeepScan achieves competitive overall results (42.5% overall accuracy, 37.3% mIoU) and shows strong perception ability. It surpasses RL-based DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner, and TreeVGR by 7.3, 1.6, and 5.5 mIoU, respectively, confirming the superiority of Hierarchical Scanning.
 - It outperforms DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner, and TreeVGR by 5.0%, 3.5%, and 1.5% in overall accuracy without fine-tuning, demonstrating its effectiveness even in complex reasoning tasks.
- **Scalability and Generalizability:** DeepScan consistently improves diverse LVLMs across different architectures (LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL) and parameter scales (7B, 32B, 72B) without adaptation cost (Fig. 7). When scaled to Qwen2.5-VL-72B, DeepScan-72B achieves 94.2% overall on V*, representing a 9.4% gain over the vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-72B (84.8%).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Framework Components:** All stages contribute to performance gains. Hierarchical Scanning is the primary driver, Refocusing adds further gains while maintaining a good performance-latency trade-off, and Evidence-Enhanced Reasoning provides additional marginal improvements with negligible overhead.
 - **External Experts:** The performance of DeepScan is largely insensitive to the scale of the external search and visual experts, suggesting flexibility in expert choice.
 - **Hierarchical Scanning:** It significantly outperforms detection-based grounding. Morphological post-processing is crucial for both performance and inference speed. The proposed point-based proxy design, fusing semantic and geometric information, yields noticeably better results than alternatives. Adaptive patch sizing (576x576 for single-object, 768x768 for multi-object) further boosts performance.
 - **Refocusing:** Both Zoom-In and Zoom-Out actions are indispensable. The carefully designed search space is empirically shown to be complete and efficient, requiring fewer expansions than MCTS or A* for similar budgets. A moderate Zoom-Out scale (s=1.5) is optimal, as excessive expansion harms grounding and reasoning.
- **In-Depth Analysis:**
 - **Qualitative Analysis:** DeepScan effectively handles "attention drift" and accurately localizes subtle evidence, outperforming GPT-4o and DyFo in case studies (Fig. 9, 14).
 - **Grounding Paradigms:** Quantitative analysis confirms that bottom-up localization significantly outperforms a one-shot variant, particularly on fine-grained tasks (Tab. 7).
 - **Impact of Grounding on Reasoning:** Excessive zoom-in (IoU approaching 1) can remove essential context, degrading reasoning performance. The size regularizer in the reward function is key to avoiding over-cropping. Scaling LVLM backbones benefits second-order reasoning more significantly than perception.
- **Performance-Latency Trade-off:** With engineering optimizations, DeepScan achieves higher accuracy (90.1% vs. 89.0%) than DeepEyes with ~2.2x faster speed (3.1s vs. 6.9s) and ~35% fewer tokens. It offers a favorable trade-off against Dyfo, gaining +6.3% accuracy with marginal latency overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepScan makes a significant contribution to the field of large vision-language models by addressing a critical limitation: the lack of robust, visually grounded reasoning. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** By introducing a bottom-up grounding paradigm via Hierarchical Scanning, DeepScan effectively mitigates the problems of "attention sink" and "attention drift" that plague existing top-down approaches. This enables accurate localization of even subtle or partially occluded visual evidence in noisy or complex scenes, a crucial step towards more reliable AI systems.
- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Understanding:** The framework's ability to precisely localize and refine views of small, task-relevant objects directly translates into substantial performance gains in fine-grained visual understanding tasks, which are often difficult for general LVLMs. This expands the practical applicability of LVLMs to domains requiring meticulous visual analysis.
- **Training-Free and Scalable Solution:** DeepScan's plug-and-play nature means it can be integrated with various pre-trained LVLMs across different architectures and scales (from 7B to 72B parameters) without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning. This dramatically reduces the computational cost and expertise barrier for deploying robust grounded reasoning, making advanced LVLM capabilities more accessible.
- **Interpretable AI:** By explicitly localizing and presenting the visual evidence alongside the answer, DeepScan inherently provides a degree of interpretability. Users can see *why* an LVLM arrived at a particular conclusion, fostering trust and enabling easier debugging and verification of AI decisions.
- **Inspiration for Cognitive AI:** The framework's design, inspired by human bottom-up scanning and contextual re-evaluation, offers valuable insights into how human-like visual intelligence could be engineered into AI systems. It encourages further exploration of biologically plausible mechanisms for robust perception and reasoning.
- **Broad Practical Applications:** The ability to reliably ground visual information has profound implications for various applications:
 - **GUI Agents:** Anchoring actions to correct UI widgets and explaining user interactions.
 - **Embodied AI/Robotics:** Enabling precise manipulation of small parts in complex environments.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** More accurate perception of partially occluded signs, pedestrians, or road conditions.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Pinpointing subtle anomalies in scans.
- **Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** While multi-step reasoning can introduce latency, DeepScan's optimized batching and use of efficient backends demonstrate a favorable performance-efficiency trade-off compared to other state-of-the-art methods, making it viable for latency-sensitive applications.

**Limitations and Future Work (from Supplementary):** The authors acknowledge potential limitations, primarily in inference latency (despite optimizations) and reasoning failures when evidence is widely separated, leading to large noisy merged views. Future work includes developing adaptive partitioning strategies based on evidence saliency to further improve efficiency, and exploring generative composition techniques to reassemble localized fine-grained evidence into compact layouts, suppressing inter-evidence context.

In conclusion, DeepScan represents a significant advancement in empowering LVLMs with robust, interpretable, and human-like visually grounded reasoning. Its training-free nature, strong empirical performance, and architectural insights position it as a foundational contribution to the ongoing development of more capable and trustworthy multimodal AI.
