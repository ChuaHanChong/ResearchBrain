# HANDOFF: Humanoid Agentic Task-Space Whole-Body Control via Distilled Complementary Teachers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Caltech and IHMC developed HANDOFF, a humanoid whole-body controller, that enables versatile loco-manipulation using a compact 10-dimensional task-space command interface. This controller, trained via distillation of complementary expert policies, significantly expanded the robust manipulation workspace while maintaining competitive velocity tracking on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- High-level task planners struggle to synthesize the dense, full-body kinematic references typically required by state-of-the-art whole-body controllers (WBCs).
- Generating detailed motion references often requires laborious data collection, retargeting, and feasibility filtering for each new skill, limiting planner versatility and scalability.
- Existing controllers either require dense inputs, use architectural decompositions, or abstract actions in ways that limit planner modularity, creating a gap for a truly 'planner-friendly' interface.

## Method

- A whole-body controller, **HANDOFF**, was developed that operates on a compact, explicit ==10-dimensional command vector==, including planar base velocities, root height, and bilateral pelvis-frame wrist targets.
- A ==context-based multi-teacher distillation== pipeline was used to integrate expertise from three specialized policies: a ==WBC motion-tracking teacher==, a ==locomotion teacher==, and a ==fall-recovery teacher==.
- The student policy, using a soft ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== head, employs ==context-conditioned gating== and ==action-sliced KL divergence distillation== to smoothly blend these complementary behaviors into a single, unified controller.

## Results

- HANDOFF achieved a robust manipulation workspace volume of **0.31 m³** with **97.7%** feasibility, outperforming state-of-the-art WBCs like FALCON (**0.06 m³**) and SONIC (**0.26 m³**).
- It demonstrated velocity tracking errors competitive with SOTA controllers, achieving mean absolute errors of **0.06 m/s** for `v_x` and **0.06 rad/s** for `ω_z`.
- The controller was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for various natural-language-driven loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., pick-and-place, bimanual hand-off) without requiring task-specific data collection or controller fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling diverse, complementary expert policies (e.g., motion-tracking, locomotion, fall recovery) into a single student policy effectively enables robust and varied humanoid behaviors without explicit runtime switching.
> - A compact 10-D command interface can serve as an intuitive and effective bridge between high-level agentic planners (including VLMs) and low-level whole-body control, reducing the planning burden.
> - Context-conditioned action-sliced distillation, using continuous and discrete contextual signals, allows for dynamic blending of expert supervision across different parts of the robot's action space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots in real-world environments for complex loco-manipulation tasks presents a significant challenge in robotics. This work addresses a critical interface problem between high-level task planning and low-level whole-body control (WBC). Traditionally, state-of-the-art WBCs often necessitate dense kinematic or spatial references from planners. These references, such as full-body joint trajectories or detailed keypoint streams, are difficult for planners to synthesize directly from abstract task semantics. Generating such detailed motion often requires extensive data collection through human teleoperation or motion capture, followed by complex retargeting and dynamic feasibility filtering for each new skill or task variation. This process constrains the planner to function primarily as a data-replay mechanism, limiting its versatility and scalability.

Existing approaches in humanoid control fall into several categories, each with trade-offs. Motion-tracking WBCs, exemplified by works like SONIC, ResMimic, and HOVER, achieve high expressivity but demand dense, controller-specific kinematic inputs, thereby reducing planner modularity. Other controllers adopt more compact, mid-level commands but often resort to architectural decompositions. For instance, systems like HOMIE and FALCON manage lower-body locomotion with compact commands while still requiring explicit upper-body joint references. Similarly, approaches like OmniH2O and HOVER offer 3-point interfaces (head and hands) but require dense trajectories for each point. Latent-action interfaces, such as LeVERB, propose abstract "verb" vocabularies but may sacrifice planner modularity by obscuring the direct mapping to physical actions. Furthermore, recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models aim to enable robots to understand and act based on natural language instructions, but these high-level planners require a practical, executable action space at the control layer.

This research introduces a whole-body controller, HANDOFF, that targets a compact, explicit command space to bridge this gap. The authors propose a command interface that is intended to be intuitive, general, modular, and whole-body expressive, directly addressing the limitations of existing dense-reference and split-architecture controllers. By distilling multiple complementary expert policies into a single, unified controller, HANDOFF attempts to reconcile conflicting control requirements (e.g., precise motion tracking, robust locomotion, and fall recovery) under a single command interface. This approach aims to provide a robust control foundation that can be driven by a variety of high-level planners, including VLM-powered agentic systems, without requiring extensive task-specific data collection or controller fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a humanoid whole-body controller, named HANDOFF, that can reliably execute a diverse set of loco-manipulation skills using a compact and explicit command interface. This interface is designed to be intuitive for various types of planners and to promote modularity within the robot's control stack.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in deploying humanoid robots for complex tasks in real-world environments:
- **Burden of Dense Kinematic References**: Current state-of-the-art whole-body controllers (WBCs) typically demand dense, full-body kinematic reference streams from task planners. These streams often consist of exact joint positions or end-effector trajectories for every degree of freedom, generated at high frequencies.
- **Difficulty for Planners**: Task planners struggle to synthesize such dense kinematic streams directly from high-level, semantic task descriptions (e.g., "fetch the coffee"). This often necessitates laborious processes like collecting human teleoperation or motion capture data, retargeting it to the specific robot embodiment, and filtering it for dynamic feasibility. This pipeline must be repeated for every new skill or task variant, significantly increasing development overhead.
- **Limited Planner Autonomy**: The reliance on dense kinematic references effectively reduces the role of the planner to that of a data-replay engine, tied to a pre-existing library of demonstrations. This limits the robot's ability to adapt to novel situations or synthesize new behaviors dynamically.
- **Need for a "Planner-Friendly" Interface**: The authors identify a critical need for a command space that possesses four key properties:
 1. **Intuitive**: Easily generated by humans, geometric planners, or advanced AI systems like Vision-Language Models (VLMs).
 2. **General**: Capable of supporting a wide range of loco-manipulation tasks through a single, consistent interface.
 3. **Modular**: Decouples the planner, perception system, and controller, allowing each component to be developed, improved, or swapped independently without requiring extensive modifications to the others.
 4. **Whole-body Expressive**: Compact commands should still elicit complex, coordinated full-body behaviors, rather than requiring explicit control of every joint.

To address these points, the authors propose a specific, compact 10-dimensional command vector: `c_t = [v_x, v_y, ω_z, z, p_P_L, p_P_R]`. This vector includes planar base velocity commands (`v_x, v_y, ω_z`) for locomotion, a commanded root height (`z`) for actions like squatting, and bilateral pelvis-frame wrist targets (`p_P_L, p_P_R`) for manipulation. Each component is designed to align with common planner outputs (e.g., locomotion stacks generate velocities, grasp planners generate end-effector targets).

The motivation for using a multi-teacher distillation approach stems from the observation that no single training regime or expert policy can robustly deliver all the required capabilities simultaneously. A motion-tracking expert might excel at replicating human-like postures and reaches but struggle with robust velocity-controlled locomotion or fall recovery. Conversely, a dedicated locomotion expert might track velocities effectively but lack the whole-body coordination for complex manipulation. Fall recovery, a crucial safety feature, typically requires specialized training. The challenge is to fuse these complementary but often conflicting expert behaviors into a single, cohesive policy without explicit runtime switching.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HANDOFF controller is developed using a context-based multi-teacher distillation pipeline, integrating expertise from three distinct specialist policies into a single student policy. The overall system architecture is designed to accept a compact 10-D command interface and produce 29-DoF whole-body actions for a humanoid robot.

#### 3.1. Teacher Training

Three complementary teachers are trained independently using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in their respective specialized regimes:

1. **Whole-Body Control (WBC) Motion-Tracking Teacher:**
 - **Purpose:** To provide a strong prior on expressive full-body postures, reaches, squats, and bilateral coordination, largely derived from human motion.
 - **Architecture:** A 29-DoF policy trained with an asymmetric actor-critic PPO.
 - **Data:** Retargeted human motion clips from a publicly available dataset.
 - **Actor Observations:** An 11-frame history of proprioceptive states combined with the full 29-D reference joint angles from the current motion clip frame.
 - **Critic Observations (Privileged):** Includes measured base linear velocity, reference root pose, key-body positions, and domain-randomization parameters (unavailable to the actor during deployment).
 - **Data Curation:** Dynamically infeasible squat frames within the motion data are corrected using a closed-form Control Barrier Function (CBF) projection on the static center-of-pressure (CoP) margin. This filtering ensures that the learned motions are safe for real-robot deployment.

2. **Locomotion Teacher:**
 - **Purpose:** To provide robust and accurate velocity tracking for locomotion.
 - **Architecture:** A 15-DoF body-slice policy, focusing on the legs and waist.
 - **Training Environment:** Flat terrain.
 - **Arm Handling:** The arms are driven by curriculum-blended motion-data samples. This approach ensures the policy is robust to the center-of-mass (CoM) shifts induced by arm movements during the later distillation phase.
 - **Actor Observations:** Commanded velocity, projected gravity, base angular velocity, joint state, last action, and a 4-D gait-phase block.
 - **Critic Observations (Privileged):** Measured base linear velocity.
 - **Reward Stack:** Combines linear and angular velocity tracking with gait and stance shaping rewards.

3. **Fall-recovery Teacher:**
 - **Purpose:** To enable the robot to recover from falls and resume normal operation.
 - **Architecture:** A 29-DoF full-body policy.
 - **Training Method:** Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) framework, utilizing a discriminator and a small torso-anchor task reward.
 - **Data:** A curated mix of locomotion and paired fall-and-recovery sequences.
 - **Training Setup:** A fraction of training environments are initialized in a delayed fallen state at reset to ensure that the recovery distribution is adequately represented during training.

#### 3.2. Student Policy (HANDOFF) Distillation

The core of the methodology is the distillation of these three complementary teachers into a single student policy using PPO combined with multi-teacher KL divergence distillation and a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) training approach.

- **Student Architecture:** The student policy uses a soft Mixture-of-Experts head with three experts, one corresponding to each teacher. These experts share a 64-D latent representation generated by a proprioceptive history temporal-convolutional encoder.
- **Inputs:** The student receives the 10-D planner command `c_t` (planar base velocity, root height, bilateral pelvis-frame wrist targets) and an 11-frame proprioception history.
- **Outputs:** 29-DoF actions.
- **Context-Conditioned Gating:** A regime signal `x_t = (||c_vel_t||, recover_t)` guides the distillation process:
 - `||c_vel_t||`: The magnitude of the commanded base velocity, acting as a continuous context.
 - `recover_t`: A binary flag indicating whether the robot is in a recovery state, acting as a discrete context.
- **Action-Sliced KL Distillation:** The action space is divided into a body slice `a_B` (legs + waist, 15 DoF) and an arm slice `a_A` (arms, 14 DoF).
 - **Body Slice (`a_B`) Supervision:** Supervised by a continuous-context convex blend of the WBC and locomotion teachers' KL divergences. A sigmoid weight `α = σ((||c_vel_t|| - 0.1)/0.02)` smoothly transitions supervision: below 0.1 m/s commanded velocity, the body slice primarily follows the WBC teacher; above it, it follows the locomotion teacher.
 - **Arm Slice (`a_A`) Supervision:** Primarily supervised by the WBC motion-tracking teacher.
 - **Fall-Recovery Supervision:** When `recover_t` is active, the fall-recovery teacher (AMP) assumes full supervision over the entire 29-DoF action space through a binary-masked KL term.
- **MoE Routing Supervision:** A load-balancing loss ensures that experts are utilized appropriately, and a dedicated recovery-routing loss directs gate mass to the fall-recovery expert when the recovery flag is active. This setup avoids explicit runtime controller switching, integrating all behaviors into a single, coordinated policy.
- **Extensibility:** The framework is designed to be extensible, allowing new specialist teachers (e.g., for specific terrains or tasks) to be added as new expert heads and context channels without altering existing components.
- **Stability Rewards (Optional):** An optional whole-body stability reward stack, encompassing CoM and LIPM capture-point-in-support-polygon penalties, ankle/hip/step hierarchy, and linear/angular momentum-change penalties, can be integrated during the training of the WBC and locomotion teachers, as well as the student, to enhance overall balance and robustness.

#### 3.3. Agentic Planner

To demonstrate the practical utility of the 10-D command interface, a modular agentic planning stack is implemented:
- **High-Level Reasoner:** Decomposes natural language instructions into atomic tasks using regex parsing, with an LLM fallback for novel instructions.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** Projects 2D detections and bounding boxes from RGB-D camera data onto a 3D point cloud to generate pelvis-frame waypoints.
- **Waypoint Tracker:** Converts these waypoints into planar base velocity commands (`v_x, v_y, ω_z`).
- **Skill Selector:** Once near a target, it determines the root height (`z`) and bilateral wrist targets (`p_P_L, p_P_R`) from the target waypoint, incorporating a kinematics-based wrist correction for gripper alignment.
- **Command Flow:** This planner generates the 10-D command stream for the HANDOFF controller, which then outputs 29-DoF actions to the robot hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HANDOFF was conducted using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot (29 DoF) equipped with a Jetson Thor and Dex1-1 grippers, both in simulation (mjlab/MuJoCo) and on hardware. Policies were trained using the Rsl-rl framework.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation Metrics

- **Velocity Tracking:** Measured as the mean absolute error `|Δv_·| = E_t[|v_cmd_·(t) - v_base_·(t)|]` for each planar base velocity component (`v_x`, `v_y`, `ω_z`) over a sweep of commanded velocities.
- **Manipulation Workspace:** Defined as the "robust workspace volume," which is the hull volume of reachable wrist targets scaled by the feasibility fraction. A trial is considered feasible if both wrists stay within 15 cm of the target, the policy does not fall, and pelvis horizontal drift remains under 25 cm. The robust workspace was restricted to the forward half-space (`x ≥ 0` in the pelvis frame) for relevance to common loco-manipulation tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Table 2a, Figure 6)

Ablation studies demonstrated the contribution of each component to HANDOFF's performance:
- **"Direct" (WBC motion-tracking teacher alone):** Exhibited the weakest performance across all velocity tracking axes and a smaller robust workspace (0.20 m³), highlighting its limitations in pure velocity tracking.
- **"+ Dual teacher" (WBC + locomotion teachers distilled):** Showed a significant improvement in velocity tracking, particularly for `v_y` and `ω_z`, and an increased robust workspace (0.31 m³).
- **"+ RandCmd" (with randomized commands):** Further reduced the lateral velocity (`v_y`) error, indicating improved robustness to varied command inputs.
- **"+ Split KL + MoE (Ours)":** Closed the remaining `v_x` tracking gap and demonstrated the effectiveness of the context-based, action-sliced distillation.
- **"+ AMP recovery (Ours + Rec.)":** Added the crucial capability of fall recovery, a binary capability absent in other variants, while maintaining comparable performance on other metrics.
- **"+ Stability (Ours + Stab.)":** Led to a substantial increase in the robust workspace to 0.31 m³ and improved feasibility to 97.7%, demonstrating the benefit of incorporating whole-body stability rewards.
- **"+ Stab. + Rec. (Ours + Stab. + Rec.)":** Combined the benefits of both stability rewards and fall recovery, achieving a robust workspace of 0.31 m³ and competitive `|Δv_x|` of 0.06 m/s.

#### 4.3. Comparison with State-of-the-Art (SOTA) WBCs (Table 2b, Figures 7 & 8)

HANDOFF was compared against several SOTA whole-body controllers (FALCON, OpenHomie, AMO, SONIC), adapted with a differential-IK head to produce pelvis-frame wrist targets for a fair comparison.
- **Velocity Tracking:** HANDOFF variants, especially "Ours + Stab. + Rec.," achieved velocity tracking errors competitive with the SOTA controllers. For instance, "Ours + Stab. + Rec." showed `|Δv_x|` of 0.06 m/s, `|Δv_y|` of 0.18 m/s, and `|Δω_z|` of 0.06 rad/s, positioning it within the SOTA performance cluster. The curves for HANDOFF variants closely mirrored the ideal unit-slope line, particularly in non-saturation regions.
- **Robust Manipulation Workspace:** HANDOFF demonstrated superior performance in robust manipulation workspace. "Ours + Stab." achieved the largest robust workspace volume of 0.31 m³ with 97.7% feasibility, outperforming FALCON (0.06 m³), OpenHomie (0.15 m³), AMO (0.22 m³), and SONIC (0.26 m³). This indicates HANDOFF's ability to maintain balance and reach targets across a wider range of motion, largely attributed to its native learning of complex coupling between wrist targets, locomotion, and balance, rather than relying on external kinematic translation layers.

#### 4.4. Agentic Deployment in Simulation and Real Hardware (Figure 5)

- **Simulation:** The controller successfully demonstrated loco-manipulation task continuation after a fall recovery event. This was achieved using the same 10-D command stream without any controller-side modifications, confirming the seamless integration of the recovery specialist.
- **Real Hardware (Unitree G1):** HANDOFF was deployed on a Unitree G1 with onboard sensing (ZED-M RGB-D camera) and compute (Nvidia Jetson Thor). The robot successfully executed various natural-language-driven tasks, including pick-and-place, pick-transport-place, squat-pick, bimanual-pick-and-hand-off, and bilateral pick-and-place. A key finding was that these hardware rollouts required no task-specific data collection or controller fine-tuning, demonstrating the generalizability and practical feasibility of the controller and its interface.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable contribution to humanoid robotics by addressing the fundamental challenge of integrating high-level task planning with low-level whole-body control. The proposed HANDOFF controller and its compact 10-D task-space interface have several significant implications:

- **Bridging the Planning-Control Gap**: By offering an intuitive, general, modular, and expressive command interface, HANDOFF effectively reduces the burden on high-level planners. This alleviates the need for planners to synthesize dense, full-body kinematic references, which has historically been a bottleneck for deploying humanoids in complex, dynamic environments. This makes it more feasible for various planning paradigms, including geometric planners, Large Language Models (LLMs), and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), to command complex robot behaviors.
- **Enhanced Modularity and Scalability**: The decoupled architecture, where the controller is distinct from the perception and planning layers, fosters independent development and innovation. Researchers can improve planning algorithms, perception systems, or the controller itself without necessitating extensive modifications across the entire stack. This modularity is crucial for scaling humanoid capabilities to new tasks and environments.
- **Robust and Versatile Loco-Manipulation**: The multi-teacher distillation approach successfully synthesizes complementary expertise—motion tracking for expressive manipulation, dedicated locomotion for effective navigation, and fall recovery for resilience—into a single, unified policy. This results in a robust controller capable of coordinating complex full-body behaviors, including reaching while walking, squatting, and recovering from disturbances, without requiring explicit runtime mode switching.
- **Competitive Performance and Practicality**: Quantitative evaluations demonstrate that HANDOFF achieves velocity tracking performance comparable to state-of-the-art controllers while significantly expanding the robust manipulation workspace. This indicates its practical viability for real-world applications requiring both dynamic mobility and precise manipulation.
- **Validation on Physical Hardware**: The successful deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for diverse, natural-language-driven tasks, notably without task-specific data collection or controller fine-tuning, validates the real-world applicability and robustness of the proposed framework. This demonstrates the potential for rapid deployment of new skills on physical robots.
- **Foundation for Agentic Systems**: HANDOFF provides a strong control foundation for future agentic systems powered by advanced AI models. By offering an executable and expressive command space, it enables VLMs and other high-level AI agents to translate abstract instructions into coordinated physical actions more effectively, moving closer to truly autonomous and intelligent humanoid robots.
- **Facilitating Research**: The stated intention to open-source the full framework is expected to accelerate future research in humanoid control, planning, and learning by providing a robust, validated baseline for further development.
