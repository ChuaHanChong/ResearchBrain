# OrbiSim: World Models as Differentiable Physics Engines for Embodied Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> OrbiSim recasts a world model as a fully differentiable physics engine: an object-centric recurrent dynamics core predicts explicit physical states from scene assets, actions, and physical parameters, while a state-guided latent diffusion module renders matching frames. Decoupling physics from rendering keeps gradients clean, yielding better long-horizon prediction than generative world models and analytical policy gradients that beat model-free, imitation, and Dreamer-style baselines under sparse rewards.

## Problem

- Classical physics engines rely on non-differentiable numerical solvers, blocking gradient flow from control objectives back through environment transitions and scene configuration.
- Existing differentiable simulators buy gradients by simplifying dynamics, losing fidelity on complex contact, while generative world models synthesize pixels without exposing explicit physical state.
- Latent world models entangle physics inside abstract latents, offer no customizable assets or physical parameters, and degrade rendering fidelity needed for simulation-grade evaluation.

## Method

- **OrbiSim**-Dynamics: object-centric ==recurrent state-space== core, one token per entity, ==Transformer== coupling module for interactions, ==AdaLN== injecting actions, mass, friction, gravity as conditioning.
- **OrbiSim**-Vision: ==latent-diffusion== renderer conditioned on predicted states via spatial condition maps concatenated to ==U-Net== features plus object-level cross-attention tokens, with visual anchoring on recent latents.
- ==StaInf== and ==PhyInf== inference modules recover visible states and hidden properties for real-to-sim identification; ==analytical policy gradients== backpropagate terminal rewards through dynamics only, bypassing the renderer.

## Results

- On robosuite Push, OrbiSim reaches PSNR100 **19.98**, LPIPS100 **0.1428**, FVD **533.9**, TrajErr **0.4468**, versus AdaWorld's **12.83** PSNR100 and **1305.8** FVD.
- Sparse-reward Push success rate is **42.71%** against DreamerV3 **25.00%**, behavior cloning **19.79%**, PPO+RND **2.08%**, PPO **1.04%**, SAC **0.00%**.
- Ablations confirm design: removing decoupling raises TrajErr to **0.8134** and FVD to **689.1**; OOD scenes with unseen counts, shapes, and extreme friction still give FVD **597.3**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating dynamics from rendering trades a little single-frame PSNR for large gains in temporal coherence and trajectory accuracy over long autoregressive rollouts.
> - Routing policy gradients through a compact state-space core rather than a monolithic pixel model avoids gradient degradation and makes sparse terminal rewards learnable.
> - Asset-conditioned interfaces with explicit physical parameters buy generalization to unseen object counts, shapes, articulation, and deformables without architectural redesign.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advancement of embodied intelligence is closely linked to the capabilities of simulation software. The existing landscape for robotic simulation can be broadly categorized into traditional physics engines and various forms of world models, each with distinct strengths and limitations that OrbiSim aims to address.

**Traditional Physics Engines:** Classical physics engines such as MuJoCo, PhysX, and Bullet are widely used as backends for robotic learning platforms (e.g., robosuite, Isaac Gym/Lab) due to their high physical fidelity. However, these engines are fundamentally constrained by their reliance on non-differentiable or only partially differentiable numerical solvers. This characteristic introduces discontinuities that hinder gradient propagation through environment transitions, making tasks like gradient-based policy optimization and system identification challenging. While a category of differentiable simulators (e.g., Brax, Warp, DiffTaichi, GradSim) has emerged to address differentiability, they often achieve this by compromising physical accuracy or simplifying dynamics, particularly struggling with complex contact interactions pertinent to high-fidelity robotics.

**Generative World Models:** In parallel, recent years have witnessed rapid progress in world models that learn predictive representations of environment dynamics directly from data. These models, including examples like Vid2World, AdaWorld, DreamDojo, Motus, and LAWM, demonstrate capabilities in visual perception, video prediction, and counterfactual reasoning for planning and reinforcement learning. However, the majority of existing world models primarily function as visual predictors rather than comprehensive physical simulators. They typically lack the structural integrity to serve as drop-in replacements for traditional engines in closed-loop training pipelines. Specific limitations include the absence of explicit physical states, inability to accept structured scene assets or physical parameters as inputs, and a lack of a fully differentiable execution pathway that connects environment configuration to downstream control objectives. This often results in an entanglement of visual synthesis with temporal transitions, encoding physics implicitly within large parameter spaces, which hampers reliable gradient propagation for precise robotic control.

**Latent World Models:** A subcategory of world models, latent world models (e.g., PlaNet, Dreamer, IRASim, PIN-WM, WorldGym), has been influential in model-based reinforcement learning by enabling compact, controllable representations of environment dynamics and efficient policy optimization in latent space. Despite their empirical success in some robotics applications, these models often provide limited support for customizable assets (e.g., variable object geometries, compositional environments) and tend to deprioritize high-fidelity visual rendering. Consequently, their applicability is restricted in scenarios requiring photorealistic simulation or rendering-based evaluation, and they are generally poorly aligned with simulation-oriented settings that demand explicit physical and visual control.

**OrbiSim's Contribution:** OrbiSim positions itself at the intersection of these paradigms by proposing a novel approach that redefines world models as a fully differentiable physics engine. It aims to bridge the gap between the high physical fidelity and structured input capabilities of traditional simulators and the data-driven learning and differentiability of world models. By establishing a unified, physically-grounded pathway that integrates structured scene assets, neural dynamics, and visual generation, OrbiSim seeks to overcome the limitations of prior approaches and enable end-to-end differentiable simulation for embodied intelligence. This work is presented as part of the broader "NeoWorld Project."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of OrbiSim is driven by the identified limitations of existing simulation and world modeling paradigms when applied to embodied intelligence. The primary motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the Non-Differentiability of Classical Physics Engines:** Traditional physics engines, while offering high fidelity, are fundamentally non-differentiable or only partially differentiable. This property prevents the direct propagation of gradients through environmental transitions, making it difficult to optimize policies using gradient-based methods, especially for tasks involving sparse rewards or complex contact dynamics. A key motivation for OrbiSim is to provide a fully differentiable physics engine that allows for end-to-end optimization of robot policies and environmental parameters.

- **Overcoming the Functional Limitations of Existing World Models:** Many contemporary world models excel at visual prediction and imagination but do not function as full, physically explicit simulators. They often lack the capacity to accept structured scene assets, modify physical parameters directly, or expose explicit physical states. This limits their utility in robotic training pipelines where precise control over environmental configurations and clear access to physical states are crucial. OrbiSim aims to redefine world models to operate as functional simulators that integrate structured scene assets and provide explicit physical state predictions.

- **Enabling End-to-End Differentiability in the Entire Simulation Loop:** A central objective is to establish a unified, physically-grounded pathway that is fully differentiable throughout the entire simulation loop. This includes everything from explicit state transitions to the generation of visual observations. Such end-to-end differentiability would enable tasks traditionally intractable for classical simulators, such as differentiable contact modeling, gradient-based policy optimization under sparse rewards, and intuitive physical inference directly from observations.

- **Achieving High Predictive Fidelity and Control Performance:** The project aims for OrbiSim to achieve competitive physical consistency compared to classical physics solvers while substantially outperforming state-of-the-art generative world models in terms of video prediction fidelity and long-horizon rollout stability. This involves ensuring that the learned dynamics accurately reflect real-world physics and that the visual outputs are high-fidelity and temporally consistent.

- **Supporting Real-to-Sim System Identification:** A practical motivation is to facilitate efficient Real-to-Sim system identification. By providing a differentiable pipeline, OrbiSim seeks to enable the optimization of scene parameters from real-world data, even when only pixel-level observations are available. This requires robust inference mechanisms to map raw visual data to precise physical states and hidden properties.

- **Facilitating Analytical Policy Gradients for Downstream Control:** OrbiSim is motivated to provide analytical policy gradients for efficient reinforcement learning, particularly for robotic manipulation tasks with sparse rewards. By exposing a differentiable pathway from rewards through dynamics to policy parameters, the framework aims to significantly improve the learning efficiency and performance of robot agents.

- **Demonstrating General-Purpose and Asset-Conditioned World Representation:** A design goal is to create a general-purpose world representation that supports heterogeneous object types through appropriate state and geometry encodings, rather than being limited to task-specific designs. This would allow OrbiSim to generalize across diverse robotic manipulation scenarios involving articulated or deformable objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OrbiSim is designed as a differentiable world model that functions as an execution engine for embodied manipulation tasks. Its methodology centers on a decoupled neural architecture comprising two specialized modules: OrbiSim-Dynamics and OrbiSim-Vision, which mirror the separation of physics and rendering in classical simulation software. This modularity is complemented by mechanisms for end-to-end differentiability, Real-to-Sim system identification, and gradient-based policy optimization.

#### 3.1. Unified Asset and World Representation

The system represents embodied manipulation as a physically grounded simulation process. It characterizes the environment through three core components:
- **Physical state (x_t):** A time-dependent vector encompassing the states of the robot (x_p_t) and multiple interacting objects ({x^(i)_t}). This includes quantities like pose, velocities, and joint configurations, varying based on asset type.
- **Visual observation (o_t):** A high-dimensional RGB image generated by the engine, reflecting the visual appearance of x_t.
- **Static scene descriptors (¯x):** Time-invariant properties categorized into:
 - **Asset attributes (¯x^(i)):** Intrinsic properties of each object, such as geometry (meshes, point clouds encoded by geometric encoders), mass, inertia, and friction.
 - **World configurations (¯x_w):** Global parameters like gravity or contact properties.
This asset-conditioned framework supports heterogeneous object types and complex geometries within a unified representation.

#### 3.2. OrbiSim-Dynamics: Object-Centric Physics Core

OrbiSim-Dynamics models the evolution of physical states as a deterministic, history-aware recurrent process. Given past states (x_0:t-1), previous action (a_t-1), and static scene descriptors (¯x), it predicts the next state (ˆx_t).
- **Architecture:** It employs an object-centric paradigm, treating each entity (robot and objects) as a discrete token with its own latent and recurrent memory. A Transformer-based Coupling Module processes these tokens, utilizing global self-attention to model inter-entity dependencies like collisions and articulated constraints.
- **Conditioning:** Physical constants (¯x^(i), ¯x_w) and control actions (a_t-1) are incorporated via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN), modulating the dynamics flow. The robot token is modulated by actions and world configurations, while object tokens are modulated by their attributes and world configurations. This structured modulation provides inductive bias for generalization across environments.
- **Training:** The module is trained with three loss terms: a latent alignment loss ($L_{tra}$), an encoding consistency loss ($L_{enc}$), and a state reconstruction loss ($L_{dec}$). Optimization occurs over T-step autoregressive rollouts, balancing one-step accuracy (teacher forcing) and long-horizon stability (gradually replacing ground-truth with model predictions).

#### 3.3. OrbiSim-Vision: State-Guided Diffusion

OrbiSim-Vision transforms predicted physical states into high-fidelity visual observations (ˆo_t) using a conditional image generation module.
- **Mechanism:** This is formulated as a Latent Diffusion Process, grounded in the scene context (o_t-K:t-1) and the predicted physical state (ˆx_t) from OrbiSim-Dynamics, along with static scene descriptors (¯x).
- **Conditioning:** The denoising process operates in latent space and is explicitly conditioned through:
 - **Visual Anchoring:** Concatenating a perturbed version of the most recent latent (y_t-1,(0)) to the denoising sequence.
 - **Physical Grounding:** Encoding ˆx_t and ¯x into spatial condition maps (geometric cues concatenated to U-Net feature maps) and object-level tokens (cross-attention modulation within the U-Net).
- **Training:** A conditional denoising network ($D_\phi$) is trained with a standard diffusion denoising objective ($L_{vis}$), enforcing physical consistency. Training also involves randomizing context frame length and sampling to improve robustness to varying rollout settings.

#### 3.4. End-to-End Differentiability Features

The combined OrbiSim-Dynamics and OrbiSim-Vision modules form a fully differentiable execution engine.
- **Real-to-Sim System Identification (SysID):** To address the challenge of inferring physical properties from pixels, a pipeline with two specialized modules is introduced:
 - **State Inference (StaInf):** Maps image latents from a single RGB frame to explicit visible physical states (e.g., robot end-effector position, object poses, sizes, colors).
 - **Physics Inference (PhyInf):** Infers unobservable properties (e.g., mass, friction) from a short-horizon video window (64 frames) by optimizing through the frozen OrbiSim-Dynamics module. This rollout-based supervision encourages inferred parameters to reproduce observed motion, better matching system identification goals.
- **Policy Optimization via Analytical Gradients:** OrbiSim enables analytical policy gradients for reinforcement learning. Reward gradients for terminal objectives ($R(x_T)$) propagate directly through the differentiable dynamics (Eq. 7), avoiding issues like gradient degradation. This supports policy learning even under sparse-reward settings, either directly from initial physical states or after inference from raw observations using StaInf and PhyInf.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OrbiSim was evaluated across diverse physics-rich benchmarks, focusing on its generative fidelity, physical consistency, and the benefits of its differentiable gradient pathways for downstream reinforcement learning.

#### 4.1. Generative Fidelity and Physical Consistency

- **Benchmark Manipulation Tasks:** On the Robosuite Push and Isaac Lab Stack tasks, OrbiSim consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to state-of-the-art generative world models like Vid2World and AdaWorld. OrbiSim maintained better temporal coherence (lower FVD), higher video prediction fidelity (higher PSNR, lower LPIPS), and significantly lower trajectory error (TrajErr), indicating a more robust alignment between physical dynamics and visual synthesis over long horizons (e.g., 225 steps for Isaac Lab Stack). Qualitative visualizations supported these quantitative results, showing accurate capture of intricate manipulator rotations and complex multi-object collisions under varying physics coefficients.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **"w/o Decoupling":** Removing the decoupled dynamics-vision architecture led to improved single-frame reconstruction but severely degraded temporal consistency and trajectory accuracy, emphasizing the importance of separating dynamics modeling from visual rendering.
 - **"w/o Random Sampling":** Disabling random sparsification of visual context resulted in slight degradation across metrics, suggesting that context sparsification enhances robustness to autoregressive noise.
 - **"w/o Object-Centric":** Replacing object-centric modeling with a global state representation caused consistent but moderate performance drops, with visualizations showing impaired modeling of object interactions, underscoring the value of object-level structure for multi-body dynamics.
- **Generalization to Heterogeneous Assets:** OrbiSim exhibited extensibility to complex object types. In AdaManip Articulated tasks, it accurately followed joint-constrained part motion. In the Physion Drape scenario, it captured geometry-conditioned cloth deformation. These results suggest the asset-conditioned design allows accommodation of diverse geometries and physical properties without architectural redesign.
- **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios:** Under unseen object counts, shapes, and extreme physical parameters, OrbiSim maintained strong generation quality, substantially outperforming AdaWorld, particularly over long horizons. This indicated that the asset-conditioned and object-centric design facilitates robust generalization beyond the training distribution.
- **OrbiSim-Dynamics Evaluation:** The dynamics core effectively modeled complex state transitions and showed high sensitivity to physical configurations, accurately distinguishing between low- and high-friction regimes while maintaining predictive smoothness over long rollouts.

#### 4.2. Differentiability Evaluation on Policy Optimization

- **Robosuite Push Task:** OrbiSim achieved superior performance and convergence speed in the sparse-reward Robosuite Push task compared to model-free reinforcement learning baselines (SAC, PPO, PPO+RND), Behavior Cloning (BC), and the model-based DreamerV3. Model-free methods struggled with sparse rewards, often failing to learn. BC replicated expert behavior but lacked generalization to OOD states. DreamerV3 performed better but lacked robustness under varying physical settings. OrbiSim's differentiable dynamics provided informative optimization signals, enabling effective policy learning under terminal-only supervision.
- **Isaac Lab Stack Task:** While not directly benchmarked for policy gradients in the main text, imitation learning experiments on this task highlighted the sensitivity of pure imitation learning to distributional shifts, motivating the use of reinforcement learning with world models for robust long-horizon decision-making.

#### 4.3. Real-to-Sim Modules Performance

- **State Inference (StaInf):** Demonstrated high accuracy in recovering visible scene quantities from a single RGB frame, including object position, rotation, size, color, and robot pose.
- **Physics Inference (PhyInf):** Successfully inferred hidden physical parameters (e.g., object density, table friction) from a 64-frame video window. The inferred parameters were sufficient to reproduce observed motion under the frozen OrbiSim-Dynamics model with low errors in object and robot states, supporting simulation-consistent trajectory prediction.

#### 4.4. Scalability and Robustness

- **Multi-Object/Multi-Task Scalability:** In extended multi-object and multi-task Robosuite datasets (Push_cubes, Pick_and_place), OrbiSim maintained higher visual quality and consistency than AdaWorld, despite some performance degradation in complex scenarios. VRAM usage for OrbiSim-Dynamics scaled approximately linearly with the number of objects, indicating computational manageability.
- **Robustness to Noise:** OrbiSim exhibited resilience to moderate and strong Gaussian noise on both physical and visual states, showing low degradation in visual generation quality. This suggests the framework can tolerate imperfections in state estimation, beneficial for real-to-sim scenarios.

#### 4.5. Comparison to Newton Simulator

When optimizing initial velocity for a ball bouncing task, OrbiSim-Dynamics, after training on Newton data, yielded an optimized velocity and rollout trajectory that showed a tolerable difference compared to direct optimization within the Newton simulator. This demonstrated the effectiveness of OrbiSim's analytical gradients and its broader applicability compared to specialized differentiable simulators like NVIDIA Newton.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OrbiSim represents a notable advancement in robotic simulation and learning by introducing a novel paradigm that redefines world models as fully differentiable physics engines. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Simulation Gap:** OrbiSim effectively bridges the gap between traditional, high-fidelity, but non-differentiable physics engines and data-driven generative world models that often lack explicit physical grounding. This unification offers the precision of physics-based simulation with the flexibility and differentiability of neural models.

- **Enhanced Robotic Simulation and Policy Training:** By enabling end-to-end differentiability throughout the entire simulation loop—from explicit state transitions to visual observation generation—OrbiSim supports tasks traditionally challenging or intractable for classical simulators. This includes differentiable contact modeling and, critically, gradient-based policy optimization under sparse rewards. This capability can lead to more efficient and robust training of embodied intelligence agents.

- **Facilitating Real-to-Sim Transfer:** The integrated Real-to-Sim inference pipeline, comprising the State Inference (StaInf) and Physics Inference (PhyInf) modules, provides a structured method for mapping raw visual data into precise simulation configurations. This is a crucial step for deploying policies trained in simulation to the real world, as it enables robust system identification of visible states and hidden physical properties directly from observations.

- **Generalization and Robustness:** The asset-conditioned and object-centric design of OrbiSim demonstrates strong generalization capabilities. It accommodates heterogeneous object types (rigid, articulated, deformable) and maintains performance under out-of-distribution scenarios (unseen object counts, shapes, extreme physical parameters). Its observed robustness to noisy state inputs further enhances its potential for real-world applications where data quality can be inconsistent.

- **Analytical Gradients for Efficiency:** The provision of analytical policy gradients directly through the differentiable dynamics offers a substantial advantage for reinforcement learning. This allows for direct optimization towards task-specific reward signals, improving convergence speed and performance, particularly in challenging sparse-reward settings where model-free methods struggle.

#### 5.1. Limitations

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge several limitations. OrbiSim's generalization to broader robot morphologies, diverse scene sources, and real-world domains remains an area for further exploration. While it shows promising robustness within related simulator families and supports heterogeneous assets, it is not yet a complete, general-purpose industrial simulator.

#### 5.2. Overall Impact

Despite its current limitations, the fully differentiable formulation and demonstrated scalability of OrbiSim in data-rich settings position it as a promising step toward practical deployment in embodied intelligence research and applications. Its capacity to provide a unified, differentiable, and physically-grounded simulation environment could accelerate progress in areas like robot learning, manipulation, and interaction with complex, dynamic environments.
