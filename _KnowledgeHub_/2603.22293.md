# TIPS: Turn-Level Information-Potential Reward Shaping for Search-Augmented LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The Turn-Level Information-Potential Reward Shaping (TIPS) framework provides dense, turn-level feedback for search-augmented LLMs by leveraging a teacher model's log-likelihood of correct answers. TIPS enhanced average Exact Match by 11.8% and F1 by 13.6% over PPO across eight QA benchmarks, also demonstrating increased training stability.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) for search-augmented Large Language Models (LLMs) suffers from sparse, outcome-only terminal rewards, making credit assignment difficult over long reasoning sequences.
- In multi-turn tool-using scenarios, each tool call changes the information state, and multiple intermediate trajectories can lead to the same outcome, reducing the informativeness of terminal rewards.
- This sparsity leads to high-variance updates and unstable training dynamics, including policy collapse or drift, particularly in complex, long-horizon tasks like open-domain question answering.

## Method

- The Turn-Level ==Information Potential== Reward Shaping (**TIPS**) method introduces dense, turn-level rewards by quantifying the "informativeness" of each interaction segment.
- It defines an "answer potential" using a periodically refreshed "teacher model" (a copy of the policy) to compute the log-probability of correct answers given the current context.
- The turn-level reward is then calculated as the change in this answer potential, which is formalized as policy-invariant potential-based reward shaping (PBRS) and integrated into PPO training.

## Results

- TIPS consistently outperformed PPO and GRPO baselines, achieving average gains of **11.8%** in Exact Match and **13.6%** in F1 over PPO on Qwen-2.5-**7B** Instruct across eight QA benchmarks.
- The method demonstrated significantly more stable training dynamics, converging smoothly to higher performance plateaus without the policy collapse or stagnation seen in baseline approaches.
- TIPS generalized effectively across various Qwen and Llama models, with its benefits being most pronounced for multi-hop and out-of-domain tasks, and for models initially having weaker search capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Quantifying the information gain about answer correctness at each turn, through changes in a teacher model's belief, serves as an effective and dense reward signal.
> - Formalizing this turn-level information reward as potential-based reward shaping (PBRS) ensures that the original task's optimal policy remains unchanged, promoting stable learning.
> - A periodically refreshed copy of the policy itself functions as the most effective "teacher" for calculating information potential, highlighting the importance of behavioral alignment.
