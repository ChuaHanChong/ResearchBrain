# RoboCOIN: An Open-Sourced Bimanual Robotic Data COllection for INtegrated Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboCOIN is introduced as an open-source, multi-embodiment dataset for bimanual robotic manipulation, developed by researchers from BAAI and various institutions, providing over 180,000 demonstrations across diverse platforms. Its hierarchical annotations and CoRobot data quality framework enhance VLA model performance on complex bimanual tasks, with up to a 23% increase in success rates.

## Problem

- A scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and richly structured datasets for bimanual robotic manipulation hindered the development of generalist policies.
- Hardware heterogeneity across numerous robotic platforms presented challenges for unified data collection and model training.
- Existing datasets lacked deep structural understanding beyond raw action trajectories, limiting advanced reasoning and generalization capabilities in robot learning models.

## Method

- Developed **RoboCOIN**, an open-sourced dataset comprising over 180,000 bimanual demonstrations collected from 15 distinct robotic platforms across 421 tasks.
- Introduced a ==hierarchical capability pyramid== for ==multi-level annotations== (trajectory-level concepts, segment-level subtasks, frame-level kinematics) to enable structured robot learning.
- Created **CoRobot**, an integrated data processing framework, which includes the ==Robot Trajectory Markup Language (RTML)== for quality control and a ==semi-automatic annotation toolchain== for efficient data management.

## Results

- The hierarchical annotation scheme improved VLA model success rates on a half-humanoid robot, raising simple task success from **80%** to **90%** and complex task success from **20%** to **70%**.
- RTML filtering identified and removed **35.3%** of low-quality trajectories, with grasping failures (**52.7%**) and velocity violations (**46.2%**) being the most common issues in human teleoperation.
- Training the GR00T-N1.5 model with RTML-filtered data (GR00T-Fine) resulted in a **16%** performance gain, and further augmenting with high-quality segments (GR00T-Mine) achieved a total **23%** improvement over unfiltered data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical Annotation Integration (HAI) substantially boosts VLA model performance, increasing success rates for complex bimanual tasks from 20% to 70% by providing layered contextual information.
> - Rigorous data quality control via RTML is critical, filtering out an average of 35.3% of low-quality trajectories and leading to a significant 16% to 23% improvement in robotic policy performance.
> - The combination of multi-embodiment data, structured annotations, and a robust data processing framework offers a foundational resource for developing more generalist and dexterous bimanual robotic policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of dexterous robotic manipulation, particularly bimanual tasks, is a central challenge in robotics. Bimanual manipulation is considered essential for robots to perform complex, human-like tasks in unstructured environments, spanning applications from manufacturing to home assistance. Data-driven methods, which underpin much of the recent progress in robotics, rely on large-scale, diverse datasets for effective generalization across different robotic platforms.

However, the existing research landscape exhibits several limitations regarding bimanual manipulation data. Many foundational datasets for robot learning, such as Meta-world, LIBERO, and CALVIN, are simulation-based, leading to sim-to-real transfer challenges. While initiatives like Open-X-Embodiment aggregate real-world multi-embodiment data, they primarily focus on single-arm tasks, limiting their applicability to bimanual scenarios. Proprietary datasets, such as the π0 dataset, offer extensive bimanual trajectories but restrict access to the broader research community. More recent contributions like AgiBot World and Galaxea Open-World have provided large-scale bimanual data, yet these often concentrate on a single robot embodiment, thus constraining multi-embodiment generalization.

Furthermore, existing benchmarks frequently supply only low-level action trajectories, without offering structured learning insights into the manipulation process. This absence of hierarchical annotations hinders the development of robotic systems that can understand and reason about complex tasks. The hardware heterogeneity across diverse bimanual platforms also presents a significant barrier to collecting scalable and varied datasets.

RoboCOIN directly addresses these deficiencies by providing a large-scale, multi-embodiment, bimanual manipulation dataset that is fully open-source. Its key contribution to the landscape lies in its unprecedented diversity of robotic platforms and its hierarchical annotation structure, which aims to facilitate the development of more generalizable and robust bimanual manipulation models capable of understanding and executing complex tasks across varied hardware.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to establish a comprehensive, open-source data resource for advancing bimanual robotic manipulation. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Address Data Scarcity and Heterogeneity:** The paper highlights the critical need for large-scale and diverse datasets in bimanual manipulation, which has been constrained by the significant hardware heterogeneity across different robotic platforms. RoboCOIN aims to bridge this gap by aggregating data from 15 distinct bimanual robot embodiments.
2. **Enhance Model Generalization:** A core motivation is to enable data-driven models to generalize effectively across a wide spectrum of robotic platforms and real-world scenarios. The dataset is designed to provide the necessary scale and diversity for models to learn transferable skills and robust control policies.
3. **Facilitate Structured Learning and Reasoning:** Existing datasets often provide only low-level action trajectories. RoboCOIN aims to move beyond this by introducing a "Hierarchical Capability Pyramid" with granular annotations, supporting learning from high-level conceptual understanding to segment-level subtasks and frame-level kinematics. This structure is intended to improve robotic models' ability for temporal reasoning, task planning, and fine-grained control.
4. **Promote Open Science and Community Development:** By releasing the entire dataset and codebase as open-source, the authors intend to provide a valuable, readily accessible resource for the research community. This aims to lower barriers to entry, foster collaboration, and accelerate the development of bimanual and multi-embodiment manipulation research.
5. **Ensure Data Quality and Manage Complexity:** The paper acknowledges challenges in data collection, such as variability in human operator expertise and the complexities of multi-embodiment data management. A key objective is to develop an efficient data processing pipeline, CoRobot, to facilitate quality assessment, automated annotation, and unified multi-embodiment data management. This pipeline is motivated by the need to ensure the physical and semantic integrity of collected trajectories and simplify the deployment of research efforts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in RoboCOIN involves a multi-faceted approach encompassing data collection, structured organization, hierarchical annotation, and an integrated data processing pipeline.

**A. Data Collection and Storage**
Data was collected from 15 distinct robotic platforms, designed to cover a range of bimanual configurations including bimanual robots, half-humanoid robots, and humanoid robots. These platforms feature various hardware characteristics, such as diverse camera placements (head, wrist, chest, back, third-person views) and different end-effector types (parallel grippers and dexterous hands). All data was acquired through human teleoperation, utilizing methods such as leader-follower isomorphic arms, exoskeletons, and motion capture systems, intended to ensure high-quality and diverse demonstrations. Kinematic data, including joint angles, end-effector poses, and gripper states, was recorded at high frequency, adhering to standardized conventions for consistency across platforms (e.g., radians for joint states and Euler angles, meters for distances, 0-1 normalization for gripper states). Temporal synchronization across all data streams was maintained using timestamp alignment. Detailed language annotations accompany each demonstration.

**B. Statistics and Taxonomy**
The RoboCOIN dataset comprises over 180,000 demonstrations across 421 distinct bimanual tasks within 16 diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Robotic Platforms:** The 15 platforms are categorized into Dual-arm Robots (33.2% of the data), Half-humanoid Robots (49.0%), and Humanoid Robots (17.8%). This distribution provides morphological diversity for bimanual manipulation research.
- **Scenarios:** Data collection spans 16 scenarios, categorized into Residential (47.1%), Commercial (18.6%), and Working (34.3%) environments, reflecting varied real-world applications.
- **Task Taxonomy:** A two-dimensional taxonomy structures the tasks based on:
 - **Action Collaboration:** 39 action patterns are classified by the degree of bimanual collaboration required, distinguishing between Low-collaboration Tasks (58.9%) and High-collaboration Tasks (41.1%). A synonym mapping mechanism is employed to standardize terminology.
 - **Object Flexibility:** 432 object types are classified as Rigid Objects (83.9%), Hinged Objects (4.2%), or Deformable Objects (11.9%).

**C. Hierarchical Capability Pyramid**
This core component introduces a multi-resolution annotation structure with three levels:
- **Trajectory-level Annotations:** These capture global concepts and task objectives, including scene descriptions, environment settings, and object placements. This provides a unified representation for global spatial and physical reasoning.
- **Segment-level Annotations:** Tasks are decomposed into executable subtasks with temporal alignment to specific video frames. Each segment includes step-by-step instructions and labels for exception cases (e.g., grasping failures), facilitating temporal reasoning and task planning.
- **Frame-level Annotations:** These provide dense kinematic details for each frame, such as motion parameters (direction, velocity, acceleration) for both arm end-effectors, and the status of grippers or dexterous hands. This data supports real-time feedback control and precise motion execution.

**D. CoRobot Data Processing Pipeline**
An integrated pipeline named CoRobot was developed for efficient data processing and deployment:
- **Robot Trajectory Markup Language (RTML) Evaluation:** RTML is a domain-specific language, defined in YAML format, used to convert expert rules into machine-readable constraints. It enforces principles of motion stability, pose consistency, and execution efficiency. Constraints are defined globally (e.g., workspace boundaries, velocity/acceleration limits) and locally (phase-specific parameters for subtasks like approach, grasp, place). An RTML evaluator automatically assesses trajectory quality and provides a score for data filtering.
- **Hierarchical Annotation Pipeline:** This semi-automatic pipeline generates multi-level annotations:
 - **Trajectory-Level:** Open-vocabulary detectors (e.g., Grounding DINO) localize objects, and Large Language Models (e.g., Deepseek R1) generate high-level scene descriptions based on structured object information.
 - **Segment-Level:** Rule-based tools identify potential segmentation points (e.g., gripper opening/closing, stationary states); human annotators then fine-tune these points and provide subtask labels.
 - **Frame-Level:** A cascaded sliding window framework extracts gripper kinematics (displacement, velocity, acceleration). Predefined thresholds convert these numerical metrics into consistent text labels (e.g., "Move: down, Velocity: slow"). Human annotators review and refine annotations to ensure accuracy.
- **Unified Robotic Interface:** Built on the LeRobot framework, this interface aims to address hardware heterogeneity by providing:
 - **Unified Control Abstraction:** Integrates various vendor-provided robot SDKs, compatible with ROS/ROS 2, ensuring a homogeneous control layer across diverse hardware platforms.
 - **Structured Data Management:** Employs an atomic data storage strategy, partitioning datasets into minimal, reusable subsets tagged by embodiment, task type, and environment, enabling flexible data querying and composition.
 The entire system is open-source and provides a one-click installation via "pip install robocoin".

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate RoboCOIN focused on its impact on multi-embodiment adaptability, the effectiveness of its hierarchical capability pyramid, and the contribution of the RTML data quality control.

**A. Impact of Multi-Embodiment Adaptability (Q1)**
Experiments were performed in the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation environment using ARX-X5 and Franka Emika Panda robots, which were unseen during the initial RoboCOIN data collection. The performance of the π0 model, a vision-language-action (VLA) model, was compared between a baseline (fine-tuned on RoboTwin 2.0 tasks) and a RoboCOIN-enhanced version (post-trained on a RoboCOIN-10K subset, then fine-tuned).
- **ARX-X5 Platform:** The RoboCOIN-enhanced model improved the average success rate from 17.3% to 30.4%, representing a 75.7% relative improvement. High-collaboration tasks showed a 94.1% relative improvement (from 20.4% to 39.6%), while low-collaboration tasks improved by 61.5% (from 15.6% to 25.2%).
- **Franka Emika Panda Platform:** The average success rate increased from 8.5% to 26.6%, a 212.9% relative improvement. For tasks such as "Place Dual Shoes," success rates increased from 0% to 9% on ARX-X5 and from 0% to 3% on Franka Emika Panda, indicating that previously infeasible tasks became achievable. These results suggest that RoboCOIN's multi-embodiment data provides robust and transferable manipulation priors, particularly for complex bimanual, high-collaboration tasks, enhancing generalization to novel hardware.

**B. Impact of Hierarchical Capability Pyramid (Q2)**
Evaluations were conducted on a Realman RMC-AIDA-L robot using the π0 model, comparing a baseline model with an augmented version incorporating the hierarchical capability pyramid. The augmented model received enriched language inputs with structured hierarchical task, subtask, and action descriptions.
- **In-Distribution Performance:** The hierarchical capability pyramid led to consistent performance gains, with an average success rate improvement from 28% to 43%, a 53.6% relative increase. For the "Peach Drawer" task, the success rate notably increased from 20% to 70%.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Robustness:** Testing on OOD variants of the "Pass Bowl" task (with changes in lighting, background, and object appearance) showed that the pyramid-augmented model improved average OOD success rates from 22.5% to 57.5%, a 155.6% relative improvement. The baseline model experienced a 43.8% relative performance drop from in-distribution to OOD, while the pyramid-augmented model dropped by 17.9%, indicating enhanced robustness to environmental variations.
- **Qualitative Case Study:** Case studies demonstrated that the hierarchical pyramid improved scene-level perception, segment-level subtask transition, and frame-level fine manipulation, aligning with its multi-level structural design.

**C. Impact of RTML (Q3)**
Experiments on a Unitree G1edu-u3 platform investigated the effect of RTML-filtered data on GR00T N1.5 policy learning.
- **Data Quality Enhancement:** RTML filtering removed an average of 35.3% of low-quality trajectories, indicating a substantial proportion of inconsistencies in the initial human demonstrations. Failure analysis revealed that grasping (52.7%) and moving (17.8%) were the most common failure phases, while velocity violation (46.2%) and duration violation (24.5%) were dominant metric-level causes.
- **Model Performance Improvement:**
 - Fine-tuning on the original dataset (**GR00T-Raw**) resulted in a 72% success rate.
 - Fine-tuning on data filtered by global RTML constraints (**GR00T-Coarse**) achieved a 75% success rate (4.2% relative gain).
 - Fine-tuning on data filtered by both global and phase-wise constraints (**GR00T-Fine**) resulted in an 88% success rate (22.2% relative gain). This demonstrates that RTML, particularly with phase-level constraints, significantly enhances data quality and subsequently improves the performance of trained policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboCOIN represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in the domain of bimanual and multi-embodiment learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Bimanual Manipulation:** By providing a large-scale, diverse dataset spanning 15 distinct robotic platforms and over 180,000 demonstrations across 421 tasks, RoboCOIN offers a resource for developing more sophisticated bimanual manipulation capabilities. This addresses a recognized gap in existing datasets, which often focus on single-arm tasks or single-embodiment bimanual setups.
2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The experimental results indicate that training with RoboCOIN data improves the generalization capabilities of VLA models to unseen robotic hardware and enhances their robustness to out-of-distribution environmental variations. This is crucial for deploying robots in real-world, dynamic environments.
3. **Structured Learning and Reasoning:** The Hierarchical Capability Pyramid, with its multi-level annotations (trajectory, segment, and frame-level), facilitates structured learning. This allows models to acquire a deeper understanding of task logic, temporal sequencing, and fine-grained control, potentially leading to more intelligent and adaptable robotic behaviors beyond reactive control.
4. **Improved Data Quality and Management:** The CoRobot data processing pipeline, including the RTML for trajectory validation and the semi-automatic annotation framework, offers a systematic approach to managing data quality and annotation efficiency. This infrastructure is designed to reduce the overhead associated with collecting and curating large-scale robotic datasets.
5. **Catalyst for Open Science:** The open-source nature of both the dataset and the CoRobot codebase lowers the barrier to entry for researchers globally. This is expected to foster collaborative research, accelerate innovation, and promote reproducibility in multi-embodiment and bimanual manipulation studies.
6. **Broader Applications:** The diverse range of scenarios (residential, commercial, industrial) and tasks covered by RoboCOIN supports the development of robots capable of assisting in various human environments, contributing to advances in areas such as assistive robotics, logistics, and manufacturing.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as operator bias in teleoperation, scalability challenges in manual annotation, and the heuristic dependence of RTML, RoboCOIN establishes a foundational benchmark and toolkit that is anticipated to stimulate significant progress in making robots more dexterous, adaptable, and capable of complex bimanual interactions.
