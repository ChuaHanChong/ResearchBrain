# Towards Robotic Dexterous Hand Intelligence: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A holistic survey of robotic dexterous hands that links four layers usually reviewed in isolation: a hardware anatomy of actuation, transmission and perception; a task-centric review of control and learning methods traced chronologically; a consolidation of datasets, modalities and evaluation protocols; and a mapping of current bottlenecks onto future directions, tabulating 29 hand platforms and 15 dexterous manipulation datasets.

## Problem

- Prior dexterous-hand studies assume different embodiments, sensing setups, task settings, training data and evaluation protocols, so systematic comparison and trajectory tracing are impossible.
- Existing reviews are layer-specific: hardware-oriented, sensing-oriented or learning-oriented, and learning surveys typically abstract away the hardware and sensing assumptions their methods depend on.
- Even holistic and scenario-driven reviews rarely connect hardware, method evolution, datasets and evaluation together, and omit a dedicated treatment of field-wide limitations.

## Method

- A **hardware anatomy taxonomy**: four ==actuation== classes (electromagnetic, fluidic, smart-material, hybrid), six ==transmission architectures== (tendon, linkage, gear, screw, belt-pulley, integrated), and a ==proprioception-tactile-vision perception stack==.
- A **task-centric methodological review** across five task families (in-hand, grasping and pick-and-place, tool and device use, human interaction, bimanual), each split into ==RL==, ==diffusion policy==, ==imitation==, ==representation learning== and ==VLA== lanes on chronological timelines.
- A **three-stage closed-loop research workflow** (task planning, policy training with ==domain randomization==, deployment with ==failure logging==) plus dataset consolidation by collection family, modality composition and evaluation protocol.

## Results

- Table I anatomizes **29** hands from 2006 to 2026, spanning **3 to 26 DoF**, **0.2 to 4.8 kg**, and **0.1 to 25 kg** payload, showing no convergence on a single architecture.
- Table II consolidates **15** datasets from 2022 to 2025, scaling from **700** human demos to **1B** generated demos, with CEDex at **20M** grasps over **500K** objects.
- Reported peaks in the reviewed literature include over **90%** zero-shot grasp success across **1,287** unseen cluttered scenes and **91.2%** success on unseen objects for VLA grasping systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task success rate is only interpretable alongside the protocol: nominal completion, cross-instance generalization and robustness to sensing perturbation are different claims.
> - A dataset's modality mix encodes an assumption about achievable intelligence, deciding whether it supports grasp prediction, contact-rich closed-loop control or semantically conditioned manipulation.
> - Hybrid actuation and motion synergies suggest progress comes from functional intelligence and system-level trade-offs, not from adding mechanical degrees of freedom.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic dexterous hands, central to contact-rich manipulation, has experienced rapid advancements driven by progress in hardware, sensing, control, simulation, and data generation. This expansion has led to an increasing number of specialized review studies. Previous surveys have typically focused on specific layers or subsystems within dexterous robotics. For instance, some reviews concentrate on hardware aspects such as hand structures, actuation, and transmission mechanisms, comparing rigid, tendon-driven, underactuated, and soft hand designs. Others are perception-oriented, classifying tactile and multimodal sensing for in-hand manipulation, or control-focused, examining learning paradigms like reinforcement learning and imitation learning, often without detailed consideration of the underlying hardware and sensing assumptions. While more holistic surveys exist that cover broader views across hardware, sensing, and control, they frequently lack a dedicated discussion of the field's overarching limitations and future directions. Furthermore, application-specific reviews derive design requirements for particular scenarios, such as industrial settings or handling fragile objects.

This survey differentiates itself by providing a comprehensive and integrated analysis. It addresses the challenge that existing studies often operate under diverse assumptions regarding hand embodiments, sensory configurations, task settings, training data, and evaluation protocols, which complicates systematic comparison and obscures the field's developmental trajectory. By connecting hardware analysis, methodological evolution, data resources, and evaluation practices, and dedicating a section to limitations and future directions, this work aims to offer a structured understanding of dexterous hand research. It seeks to clarify the most significant open challenges and position different research lines within a unified analytical framework, thereby complementing and extending the scope of prior, more specialized reviews.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a holistic and structured understanding of dexterous hand research by reviewing the field from four complementary perspectives: hardware-level analysis, control and learning methodologies, datasets and evaluation practices, and current limitations alongside future directions. This comprehensive approach aims to clarify the most important open challenges for future study in dexterous hand intelligence.

The motivation behind this work stems from several observed gaps and challenges within the rapidly evolving domain of robotic dexterous hands:
- **Diverse and Unsystematic Development:** The field has seen rapid progress across various sub-disciplines, including hardware design, advanced sensing, sophisticated control algorithms, high-fidelity simulation, and scalable data generation. However, this progress has often occurred under disparate assumptions concerning hand embodiments, sensory configurations, task definitions, training data sources, and evaluation protocols. This heterogeneity makes it difficult for researchers to systematically compare studies, understand their relative contributions, and discern the overall developmental trajectory of the field.
- **Limitations of Existing Reviews:** While several valuable review studies exist, they typically emphasize selected layers or subsystems. Hardware-focused surveys detail mechanical designs, sensing-oriented reviews categorize perception technologies, and learning-based surveys discuss control paradigms. Few prior works make a concerted effort to jointly connect hardware characteristics, methodological evolution, data resources, and evaluation practices in an integrated manner. Furthermore, a dedicated and consolidated discussion of the field's overarching limitations and corresponding future directions is often absent or limited.
- **Need for a Unified Framework:** There is a clear need for an analytical framework that can contextualize different research lines. By presenting a multi-faceted view that integrates hardware, methods, data, and evaluation, the survey intends to enable researchers to compare representative works under clearer assumptions and to better understand the interdependencies between these components. This unified perspective is crucial for identifying key bottlenecks and guiding future research towards more robust and generalizable dexterous manipulation systems.
- **Clarifying Open Challenges:** The survey is motivated by the desire to systematically identify and summarize the major limitations of current dexterous hand research. By explicitly linking these bottlenecks to promising future directions, the authors aim to provide clear guidance for the research community on areas requiring significant attention and innovation.

In essence, the motivation is to synthesize the fragmented advancements in dexterous hand research into a coherent, interlinked narrative, thereby facilitating a more informed and strategic approach to addressing the complex challenges involved in achieving robotic dexterous hand intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this survey involves a systematic, four-pronged approach to review the landscape of dexterous hand research. The literature collection was primarily conducted through the EI and Web of Science databases, with articles selected based on relevance to dexterous hands in their title, abstract, or subject/topic, followed by a screening process to identify representative works for in-depth analysis.

The survey's analytical framework is organized around four complementary aspects:

1. **Hardware-Level Analysis (Section II: Anatomy of the Dexterous Hand):**
 - **Actuation Systems:** Categorizes actuation principles into electromagnetic (EA), fluidic (FA), smart-material (SMA), and hybrid actuation (HA). For each category, it discusses energy sources, force generation mechanisms, power density, dynamic response, and inherent compliance, highlighting their respective trade-offs in compactness, compliance, and power.
 - **Transmission Systems:** Systematically reviews mechanisms such as tendon-driven (TDT), linkage-based (LBT), gear-based (GT), lead and roller-screw (LRST), belt cable and pulley (BCPT), and direct or integrated transmission (DIT). The analysis focuses on how these mechanisms impact force efficiency, motion accuracy, compliance, and structural complexity.
 - **Perception Systems:** Traces the evolution of perception from single-modal sensors to tightly integrated multimodal approaches. It dissects proprioception (joint pose, motion state, force/torque, tendon tension sensing), tactile sensing (resistive, capacitive, piezoelectric, optoelectronic, fluidic, vision-based), and multimodal vision perception, emphasizing their fusion at data, feature, and decision levels for robust, context-aware manipulation.
 - **Comparative Summary:** Provides a table (Table I) summarizing representative dexterous hand platforms, including their release year, actuation, transmission, tactile sensing, number of fingers, degrees of freedom (DoF), electrical envelope, and basic capabilities (e.g., payload, weight).

2. **Control and Learning Methods (Section III: Research on Dexterous Hand):**
 - **General Workflow:** Outlines a three-stage closed-loop research workflow for dexterous hand control: task planning, policy training (in simulation and with real data), and real-world deployment with feedback for refinement. This highlights the hierarchical nature and iterative process often adopted.
 - **Task-Oriented Analysis:** Examines current progress by categorizing dexterous manipulation tasks into:
 - **In-Hand Manipulation:** Reviews Reinforcement Learning (RL), Diffusion Policy, Imitation Learning (IL), Model-based Control, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, tracing their chronological development and application to complex contact dynamics.
 - **Grasping & Pick-and-Place:** Discusses the application of RL, Diffusion Policy, IL, Representation Learning, and VLA to foundational skills, considering grasp generation, execution, and adaptation under uncertainty.
 - **Tool & Device Operation:** Explores RL, IL, and VLA approaches to reasoning about functional affordances and precise force interactions required for tool use.
 - **Human Interaction:** Reviews RL and IL methods for learning safe, compliant interaction strategies in dynamic human-robot collaboration.
 - **Bimanual Manipulation:** Covers RL, Diffusion Policy, IL, VLA, and Representation Learning frameworks for synchronized control, coordination, and force allocation in dual-hand tasks.

3. **Datasets and Evaluation Practices (Section IV: Dataset of Dexterous Hand Research):**
 - **Data Acquisition:** Classifies methods into optimization-based, learning-based, and human capture, discussing their trade-offs in throughput, quality, and realism.
 - **Data Modality:** Analyzes the common observation channels in datasets, including geometric state, visual observations (RGB/RGB-D), contact-related modalities (tactile signals, force/torque), and the emerging integration of semantics and task-level conditioning.
 - **Evaluation:** Discusses common evaluation metrics (grasp quality, task success rate, generalization across objects/scenes) and emphasizes the growing importance of reliability and trustworthiness criteria for real-world deployment, such as robustness to occlusion, out-of-distribution detection, and recovery rates.

4. **Current Limitations and Future Directions (Section V: Current Limitations and Future Directions):**
 - This section integrates the identified limitations directly with corresponding future research avenues, linking current bottlenecks to coherent research directions. These include addressing the trade-off between bionic complexity and hardware feasibility, developing robust and interpretable multimodal perception, advancing learning frameworks beyond benchmark-centric optimization, and overcoming system integration and industrialization barriers.

By organizing the field around these four interconnected aspects, the survey aims to provide a holistic and structured understanding, enabling a clearer interpretation of methodological progress in conjunction with hardware design, data resources, and assessment practices.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey presents a multifaceted understanding of the dexterous hand research landscape, encompassing hardware design, control methodologies, data practices, and critical limitations.

#### 4.1. Hardware Design and Trade-offs

- **Actuation and Transmission Diversity:** There is no single universally optimal actuation or transmission solution; each presents fundamental trade-offs. Actuation systems are categorized into electromagnetic (EA), fluidic (FA), smart-material (SMA), and hybrid (HA), each varying in power density, dynamic response, and compliance. Similarly, transmission systems (tendon-driven, linkage-based, gear-based, screw-based, belt-pulley, and integrated/direct) involve compromises among force capacity, precision, compliance, and structural complexity. Hybrid actuation is emerging as a practical strategy to balance these competing requirements.
- **Evolution of Perception Systems:** Perception has evolved from single-modal sensors to sophisticated multimodal fusion, integrating proprioception (joint angles, forces, tendon tension), tactile sensing (contact forces, texture), and vision. The trend is towards high precision, miniaturization, and strong anti-interference capabilities, with multimodal fusion becoming critical for robust perception in unstructured environments.
- **System-Level Anatomy:** A comparative summary of existing dexterous hands (Table I) reveals a wide range of design envelopes, indicating that hardware choices directly shape achievable torque, bandwidth, control rates, and overall manipulation performance, reflecting diverse design philosophies rather than convergence on a single architecture.

#### 4.2. Methodological Evolution in Control and Learning

- **Hierarchical Control Workflow:** Dexterous hand control is increasingly formulated as a three-stage closed-loop workflow: high-level task planning, policy training (leveraging simulation and real-world data), and real-time deployment with feedback. This hierarchical structure improves data efficiency, modularity, and generalization.
- **Dominance of Learning-Based Paradigms:** Learning-based methods, particularly Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL), have become prevalent across various tasks, addressing challenges like high dimensionality, contact-rich dynamics, and partial observability.
- **Emergence of Diffusion Policies and VLAs:** Diffusion Policies are gaining traction for generating continuous, smooth action trajectories, improving fine-grained control and robustness in tasks like in-hand manipulation and grasping. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are introduced to provide high-level semantic grounding, task decomposition, and goal-conditioned guidance, complementing low-level controllers, especially in grasping, tool use, and bimanual tasks.
- **Task-Specific Adaptations:** The survey demonstrates how different learning paradigms are adapted to specific dexterous tasks:
 - **In-Hand Manipulation:** Focuses on improving data efficiency, reset-free learning, and integrating multimodal feedback (vision, tactile) for reorientation and precise control.
 - **Grasping & Pick-and-Place:** Explores RL for contact-rich strategies, Diffusion Policies for multimodal grasp distributions, IL for learning fine-grained behaviors from human demonstrations, and Representation Learning for stable, generalizable features. VLA models bring semantic understanding and multi-task capability.
 - **Tool & Device Operation:** RL learns complex contact dynamics, IL transfers human tool-use demonstrations, and VLA models provide semantic understanding of tool functions and long-horizon task reasoning.
 - **Human Interaction:** RL learns safe, compliant interaction strategies, and IL provides priors from human handover patterns.
 - **Bimanual Manipulation:** RL handles coordinated control of high-DoF systems, Diffusion Policies model multi-modal coordination, IL improves training efficiency by leveraging human-like motion, and VLA supports role allocation and task decomposition.

#### 4.3. Dataset Characteristics and Evaluation Practices

- **Diverse Data Acquisition:** Datasets are acquired through optimization-based simulation, learning-based simulation (e.g., reinforcement learning, generative models), and human capture (motion capture, wearable sensors), often combining these approaches.
- **Multimodal Data Modalities:** Curated datasets increasingly integrate complementary observation channels, including geometric state (poses, point clouds), visual observations (RGB/RGB-D), and critical contact-related modalities (tactile signals, force/torque). There is an emerging trend to include semantics and task-level conditioning for learning task-aware manipulation.
- **Heterogeneous Evaluation Metrics:** Evaluation is multifaceted. For grasp- and pose-centric datasets, criteria include physical plausibility, stability, and diversity. For policy performance, task success rate is dominant, but its interpretation depends on whether it measures nominal execution, cross-instance generalization, or robustness to perturbations. Real-world and human-capture datasets additionally emphasize motion fidelity, stage completion, and demonstration quality.
- **Growing Importance of Trustworthiness:** Beyond conventional metrics, reliability and trustworthiness are becoming crucial, requiring evaluation of robustness to occlusion, sensor noise, out-of-distribution detection, failure prediction, recovery rates, and safety under human interaction.

#### 4.4. Identified Limitations and Future Directions

The survey concludes by identifying key limitations and linking them to future research directions:
- **Hardware Complexity vs. Functional Intelligence:** The trade-off between bionic complexity and practical feasibility. Future work should focus on functional intelligence and biomechanical/neural control mechanisms, such as motion synergies, to reduce control dimensionality.
- **Multimodal Perception Fusion:** Challenges in real-time synchronization and interpretation of diverse sensory inputs. Future perception systems need robust, generalizable, and trustworthy cross-modal contextual understanding.
- **Learning, Control, and Data Limitations:** Model-based control struggles with complex contact dynamics, while data-driven methods face distribution shift, limited generalization, safety concerns, and high computational demands due to data scarcity and lack of diversity. Future frameworks require robust, generalizable, and trustworthy policy learning, interpreting commands in physically plausible and semantically meaningful ways.
- **System Integration and Industrialization Barriers:** High cost, reliability issues, the sim-to-real gap, and lack of unified evaluation standards hinder deployment. Future efforts should target deployable platforms through modular design, standardized interfaces, and authoritative evaluation frameworks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey contributes to the field of robotic dexterous hands by offering a structured and integrated overview that addresses key challenges in understanding its rapid evolution.
- **Structured Understanding and Contextualization:** By synthesizing advancements across hardware, control, learning, data, and evaluation into a unified framework, the survey provides researchers with a coherent understanding of the field's interconnected components. This helps to contextualize individual research contributions and clarify their positioning within the broader landscape.
- **Facilitating Systematic Comparison:** The detailed breakdown of diverse assumptions in hand embodiments, sensory configurations, task settings, and evaluation protocols enables more systematic and informed comparison across different studies. This can help researchers to identify strengths and weaknesses of various approaches more objectively.
- **Clarifying Open Challenges and Guiding Future Research:** The dedicated section on current limitations and corresponding future directions is a significant contribution. It explicitly identifies critical bottlenecks—from hardware trade-offs and perception fusion difficulties to learning generalization and industrial deployment barriers—and suggests promising avenues for focused research. This guidance can help direct future efforts towards solving the most pressing problems.
- **Promoting Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** By connecting disparate aspects of dexterous hand research, the survey encourages interdisciplinary collaboration among hardware designers, control engineers, perception specialists, and machine learning researchers. Such integration is essential for advancing the field as a whole.
- **Advancing Deployable Dexterous Robotics:** By highlighting the importance of reliability, trustworthiness, and industrialization, the survey emphasizes the transition from laboratory prototypes to practical, deployable dexterous platforms. Its insights can help shape design and development strategies that prioritize cost-effectiveness, maintainability, and robust real-world performance.
- **Informing Standardized Evaluation:** The discussion on the heterogeneity of evaluation metrics and the call for authoritative evaluation frameworks underscore the need for standardized benchmarks. This could lead to the development of more consistent and meaningful assessment tools, fostering clearer progress measurement and accelerating innovation in dexterous hand intelligence.

Overall, this survey serves as a valuable reference for both new and experienced researchers, providing a comprehensive map of the current state of dexterous hand robotics and a strategic compass for its future development.
