# WristMimic: Full-Body Humanoid Control with Wrist-Guided Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> WristMimic is a full-body humanoid control framework from POSTECH that achieves robust human-object interaction retargeting in physics simulations by prioritizing wrist placement and learning finger behaviors from object kinematics and contact outcomes rather than direct finger pose supervision. It demonstrates superior success rates and reduced object tracking errors compared to existing methods, especially in dexterous manipulation tasks, and generalizes across different hand morphologies.

## Problem

- Existing physics-based motion imitation methods struggle with contact-rich manipulation, as direct kinematic tracking of fingers can overconstrain complex behaviors.
- Integrating dexterous hand manipulation into full-body humanoid control often requires dense, explicit finger pose supervision, which is challenging to acquire and process.
- Achieving robust, affordance-aware grasping and manipulation in physics simulations, especially with varying hand embodiments, remains difficult.

## Method

- **WristMimic** decoupled whole-body control into ==kinematic pose-guided control== for contact-free body parts (including the wrist) and ==object/contact-guided control== for contact-rich finger manipulation, trained via ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==.
- Introduced a ==prioritized alignment strategy== that dynamically adjusts reward weights for arm joints and applies ==phase-specific reset thresholds== for the wrist during interaction phases to ensure optimal wrist placement.
- Learned finger behaviors implicitly from object motion and contact outcomes through an ==affordance-aware policy==, entirely without explicit finger pose supervision.

## Results

- Achieved a **98.9%** success rate on the OMOMO dataset, reducing object position error to **7.3 cm** and rotation error to **12.2 degrees**, significantly outperforming prior methods like InterMimic (**86.6%** success).
- Demonstrated superior performance on the dexterous ParaHome dataset with an **83.3%** success rate, substantially higher than InterMimic (**0.1%**) and SkillMimicV2 (**1.3%**).
- The finger-agnostic formulation enabled generalization across different hand morphologies (e.g., SMPL-X, InterMimic, OmniGrasp hands) without requiring finger-specific motion data, maintaining comparable performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The wrist serves as a critical 'natural gate' between contact-free body motion and contact-rich manipulation, effectively guiding grasp affordance without direct finger supervision.
> - Object kinematics and contact outcomes provide a more robust and effective learning signal for dexterous manipulation than direct supervision of high-dimensional finger poses.
> - Explicitly prioritizing wrist placement through reward modulation and strict reset conditions during contact phases is crucial for achieving successful and stable grasping in physics simulations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of retargeting human-object interaction (HOI) demonstrations to physics-based simulations for humanoid control. This field has seen significant progress in physics-based motion imitation, particularly for locomotion, where methods like AMP, ASE, and more recent universal trackers have demonstrated effective whole-body control using kinematic trajectory tracking. However, extending these methods to manipulation, especially contact-rich interactions with objects, remains complex. This complexity arises because direct kinematic trajectory tracking for hands often fails to capture the necessary contact forces and dynamics required for successful object manipulation.

Previous efforts in physics-based manipulation have introduced various strategies, including task-specific reward designs, contact graphs for task-agnostic imitation (e.g., PhysHOI), skill-level imitation (e.g., SkillMimic), and universal whole-body HOI control via teacher-student distillation (e.g., InterMimic). Other works have focused on dexterous manipulation at the hand level, exploring grasp pose generation using differentiable contact modeling (e.g., Grasp’d, DexGraspNet) and learning closed-loop control policies through reinforcement learning (e.g., solving Rubik's cube) or imitation learning (e.g., EgoMimic, DexUMI). Some approaches, like DexMachina and SPIDER, have advanced dexterous retargeting by focusing on object-state tracking and physics-informed embodiment bridging.

A common thread in existing manipulation methods is their reliance on either dense kinematic supervision of detailed finger motion or a focus on hand-level manipulation without comprehensive integration into full-body control. WristMimic proposes a departure from this by decoupling whole-body control and reducing the reliance on explicit finger pose supervision. It positions itself by identifying the wrist as a crucial interface, distinct from prior approaches that emphasize either full kinematic finger tracking or purely hand-centric manipulation without considering the whole body. This work therefore contributes to the landscape by offering an alternative perspective on integrating dexterous manipulation into whole-body retargeting while seeking to reduce the data requirements and complexity associated with detailed finger-level tracking.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WristMimic is to enable effective retargeting of human-object interaction demonstrations to physics-based simulations, ensuring that the simulated humanoid not only reproduces human body motion but also successfully manipulates objects by recovering correct object kinematics and contact dynamics.

The motivation for this approach stems from several limitations observed in existing methods:
1. **Insufficiency of Position-Only Hand Trajectories**: For body parts that do not directly interact with objects, such as the torso or arms during reaching, standard kinematic trajectory tracking is often effective. However, for hands, which are critical for object interaction, position-based kinematic data alone cannot adequately encode the contact forces essential for successful manipulation. Directly tracking these trajectories can overconstrain the contact-rich and complex behavior of fingers.
2. **Decoupling Control Regimes**: The authors posit a fundamental asymmetry in physics-based interaction: contact-free body motion is well-described kinematically, while finger motion during interaction is governed by contact force dynamics. This suggests that a unified kinematic tracking approach for the entire body, including fingers, might be suboptimal. The objective is to decouple whole-body control into two regimes: kinematic pose-guided control for contact-free body parts and object- and contact-guided control for contact-rich hand manipulation.
3. **Identifying the Wrist as a Bridge**: A key motivation is the insight that the wrist serves as a "natural gate" between these two control regimes. The wrist is typically contact-free, making it amenable to kinematic guidance. Simultaneously, its position and orientation determine the global configuration of the fingers and their reachable grasp affordances, playing a critical role in the success of a grasp. Different wrist poses can lead to distinct grasp configurations for the same object, highlighting the wrist's role in encoding grasp affordance.
4. **Reducing Reliance on Detailed Finger Supervision**: The goal is to develop an affordance-aware manipulation framework that requires supervision primarily for the body and wrist, rather than explicit finger pose supervision or complex finger-level capture data. This aims to simplify data requirements and enhance generalization across different hand embodiments.
5. **Achieving Reliable Wrist Placement**: To facilitate effective object- and contact-guided learning for the fingers, it is crucial to ensure that the wrist is placed in affordance-consistent and reachable configurations. This motivates the introduction of specific mechanisms for prioritizing wrist placement during interaction.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of direct kinematic finger tracking for HOI retargeting by proposing a decoupled control strategy that leverages the wrist as a crucial structural element to guide manipulation, thereby enabling robust, affordance-aware grasping and manipulation without requiring dense finger-level supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WristMimic employs a whole-body control framework designed to retarget human-object interaction demonstrations to a physics-based simulator. The core of its approach involves decoupling control strategies for different body parts and prioritizing wrist placement.

**Problem Formulation:**
The system is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where a policy network, trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), learns to generate actions in a physics-based simulator. The goal is to reproduce a reference human-object interaction sequence, requiring accurate tracking of the reference body trajectory and successful object manipulation according to its reference kinematic trajectory.

**Policy Representation for Decoupled Manipulation:**
1. **State Definition**:
 - **Proprioceptive State ($s_p_t$)**: Includes root pose and velocity, 51 actuated joint poses and velocities, object pose and velocity, binary contact markers for 52 rigid bodies, and interaction graph vectors encoding spatial relationships between humanoid bodies and the nearest object surface point.
 - **Goal State ($s_g_t$)**: Encodes differences between the current state and future reference states at multiple time horizons. It includes differences in joint rotations ($\Delta\theta$), joint positions ($\Delta p$), object rotations ($\Delta\theta_{obj}$), object positions ($\Delta p_{obj}$), interaction graph vectors ($\Delta i$), and contact markers ($\Delta c$).
2. **Decoupled Supervision**:
 - The 51 actuated joints are divided into **contact-free joints ($J_{cf}$)** (19 body joints, 2 wrist joints) and **contact-rich joints ($J_{cr}$)** (30 finger joints).
 - Crucially, kinematic pose targets (from $\Delta\theta$ and $\Delta p$) are assigned *only* to joints in $J_{cf}$. All 30 finger joints ($J_{cr}$) are explicitly excluded from direct kinematic supervision in the goal state.
 - Finger behavior is instead learned indirectly through object motion (via $\Delta\theta_{obj}$ and $\Delta p_{obj}$) and contact outcomes ($\Delta c$). The contact target $\Delta c$ includes 21 explicit contact labels for non-finger rigid bodies and 2 binary hand-level contact indicators (one per hand, indicating if any part of the hand is in contact).
3. **Reward Function**:
 - A multiplicative reward structure is used: $r_t = r_{p_t} \cdot r_{\theta_t} \cdot r_{p,obj_t} \cdot r_{\theta,obj_t} \cdot r_{c_t}$. Each component is of the form $exp(-\lambda E)$, penalizing error $E$ with component-specific weights $\lambda$.
 - Joint position and rotation tracking errors ($E_p, E_\theta$) are computed only over $J_{cf}$ (body and wrist joints), excluding finger joints.
 - Object position and rotation tracking errors ($E_{p,obj}, E_{\theta,obj}$) and contact alignment error ($E_c$) influence the learning of finger behavior.
4. **Action Space**: Actions consist of target rotations for all 51 actuated joints (including fingers) in exponential map coordinates, converted to torques via PD controllers. Even without direct kinematic supervision, finger joints are fully actuated.

**Learning Wrist-Guided Manipulation:**
The methodology integrates the wrist as a crucial element bridging contact-free body control and contact-rich manipulation.
1. **Prioritized Alignment Strategy**: To address inaccuracies in motion capture data and ensure optimal wrist placement for grasping, a prioritized alignment strategy is introduced around the initial contact phase.
 - **Contact Window**: A time window $[t_c - t_b, t_c + t_a]$ is defined around the first contact frame ($t_c$).
 - **Reward Weight Modulation**: Within this contact window, joint reward weights are dynamically adjusted:
 - Weights for upper arm joints ($J_{arm}$, shoulder and elbow) are set to 0, relaxing their kinematic constraints.
 - Weights for wrist joints ($J_{wrist}$) remain unchanged (1), maintaining strict kinematic tracking for the wrist.
 - Weights for other contact-free body joints ($J_{cf} \setminus (J_{arm} \cup J_{wrist})$) are reduced ($w_{red} < 1$).
 This modulation allows the policy to freely adjust upper arm configurations to optimally position the wrist, focusing the learning on critical hand-object contact and object dynamics.
 - **Phase-Specific Reset Thresholds**: To prevent episodes from diverging significantly during critical interaction phases, early termination conditions are applied based on wrist error for the grasping hand(s) within the contact window.
 - The contact window is subdivided into three phases: approach ($[t_c - t_b, t_c + \tau_1]$), grasping ($[t_c + \tau_1, t_c + \tau_2]$), and stabilization ($[t_c + \tau_2, t_c + t_a]$).
 - During the grasping phase, tight thresholds (e.g., 7cm position, 0.2 radians orientation) are applied to wrist deviation from the reference. Exceeding these triggers termination.
 - Looser thresholds (e.g., 15cm, 0.5 radians) are used for approach and stabilization phases.
 This design, combining reduced reward weights with strict reset boundaries, aims to guide the policy towards discovering feasible wrist poses that enable natural grasp configurations, while accommodating mocap inaccuracies.

**Implementation Details:**
The framework is implemented in Isaac Gym, a GPU-based physics simulator, and policies are trained using PPO. Training occurs with 2048 parallel environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative and qualitative evidence to support the effectiveness of the WristMimic framework, along with ablation studies and an assessment of its generalization capabilities.

**Quantitative Results:**
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art**: WristMimic was compared against InterMimic and SkillMimicV2 on 40 diverse interaction sequences from the OMOMO and ParaHome datasets.
 - WristMimic demonstrated improved performance across all evaluation metrics: success rate, object position error, and object rotation error.
 - On the OMOMO dataset, WristMimic achieved a success rate of 98.9%, significantly higher than InterMimic (86.6%), and reduced object position error to 7.3 cm and rotation error to 12.2 degrees, roughly half of InterMimic's errors.
 - On the ParaHome dataset, which includes more dexterous interactions, WristMimic maintained a success rate of 83.3%, while InterMimic and SkillMimicV2 showed significantly lower success rates (0.1% and 1.3% respectively). Object position and rotation errors were also substantially lower for WristMimic.
 - These results indicate that guiding manipulation through wrist placement and object kinematics provides a robust structural signal for object-trajectory-aware control, particularly in delicate grasping scenarios.

**Qualitative Results:**
- **Affordance-Aware Grasping**: Visual comparisons (Figure 3) showed that WristMimic successfully achieved affordance-aware grasping with diverse manipulation behaviors, while accurately reproducing whole-body motion.
- **Importance of Wrist Placement**: Qualitative examples (Figure 5) highlighted that even if a baseline method like SkillMimicV2 produced plausible finger closure, incorrect wrist placement often prevented stable object grasping. WristMimic, by maintaining precise wrist alignment, enabled fingers to adapt for successful grasping.
- **Stable Manipulation Over Time**: Sequential frames (Figure 4) demonstrated that WristMimic maintained stable object contact and natural hand poses throughout manipulation sequences.
- **Prioritizing Stability**: In some cases (e.g., moving a book), the policy prioritized stable object manipulation and trajectory tracking over strict imitation of the human body pose, such as supporting the book with both hands for stability even if the human demonstration released one hand.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Wrist Constraints**: Ablation on wrist-specific constraints (Table 2) showed that the decoupled formulation *without* wrist constraints performed significantly worse (0.0% success rate), indicating that decoupling alone is insufficient. Combining phase-specific reset thresholds with reward weight modulation yielded the best performance (86.5% success rate), demonstrating the criticality of explicit wrist guidance. Phase-specific resets provided the major individual contribution.
- **Reset Thresholds**: Further ablation on grasping-phase wrist reset thresholds (Table 4) revealed that overly tight (3.5 cm/0.1 rad) or loose (15 cm/0.5 rad) thresholds led to reduced success rates, with the optimal trade-off found at 7 cm/0.2 rad.
- **Non-Hand Body Contact**: The framework's ability to handle interactions involving non-hand body parts (e.g., pushing with a foot, sitting on a chair) was demonstrated (Table 3). These scenarios did not require wrist-like design and were handled by the standard whole-body mimicking objective, suggesting the framework's natural extension to such contexts.
- **Finger Guidance**: An additional ablation (Table A in Supplementary Material) indicated that adding explicit finger tracking guidance to WristMimic actually *decreased* performance (68.7% success rate vs. 83.3% for WristMimic), suggesting that object kinematics and contact outcomes provide a stronger and more effective learning signal than directly supervising high-dimensional finger poses.

**Hand Morphology Generalization:**
- Experiments (Table 5, Figure 7) showed that the finger-agnostic formulation enabled learning across different hand morphologies (default SMPL-X, InterMimic hand, OmniGrasp hand) without requiring finger-specific motion data. Performance remained comparable across varying hand sizes, joint lengths, and limits, implying that the policy adapts through interaction dynamics rather than kinematic supervision. The InterMimic hand, with constrained joint ranges, exhibited realistic finger poses and high performance.

**Limitations:**
- The method primarily focuses on affordance-aware grasping and object manipulation, not fine-grained in-hand manipulation or object reorientation within the hand, which would require more detailed finger pose control.
- The current framework trains scene-specific policies, meaning separate training is needed for each interaction scene, unlike methods aiming for cross-scene generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WristMimic framework presents a significant contribution to the field of physics-based humanoid control for human-object interaction, particularly in its novel approach to hand manipulation. Its key significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Reduced Supervision Requirements for Dexterous Manipulation**: By demonstrating that robust grasping and object manipulation can be achieved without explicit, dense finger pose supervision, WristMimic offers a practical pathway to reduce the data acquisition and processing burden. This could accelerate the development of manipulation skills for robots, as collecting high-fidelity finger-level motion capture data is challenging and resource-intensive.
2. **Robustness in Contact-Rich Scenarios**: The explicit focus on wrist placement and object-centric rewards, rather than precise finger trajectory tracking, appears to yield more stable and reliable interaction in physics-based simulations, especially in dexterous and contact-rich scenarios (as evidenced by its performance on the ParaHome dataset). This robustness is critical for deploying robots in complex, unstructured environments.
3. **Enhanced Generalization Across Hand Embodiments**: The finger-agnostic nature of WristMimic, where finger behaviors emerge from interaction dynamics rather than direct kinematic imitation, facilitates generalization across different hand morphologies. This implies that policies trained with this framework could be more readily transferable to robots with varying hand designs, simplifying the adaptation process for new hardware.
4. **A New Paradigm for Control Hierarchy**: The identification of the wrist as a "critical bridge" between contact-free body motion and contact-rich manipulation suggests a hierarchical control strategy that prioritizes structurally important joints. This insight could inform future research into designing control policies that intelligently allocate supervisory signals to relevant degrees of freedom, moving away from uniform, dense supervision across the entire robot body.
5. **Improved Physics-Based Retargeting Accuracy**: By decoupling control and emphasizing outcomes (object motion, contacts) for manipulation, WristMimic achieves improved accuracy in reproducing desired object trajectories while maintaining physically plausible interactions. This addresses a core challenge in retargeting, where simply replaying kinematic data often fails to capture the underlying physics.

In essence, WristMimic offers a pragmatic and effective strategy for developing manipulation capabilities in humanoid robots. Its focus on the wrist as a pivotal control point, combined with a decoupled supervision approach, holds the potential to make learning complex human-object interactions more efficient, robust, and generalizable, thus impacting the development of more capable and adaptable autonomous systems.
