# GR-3 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance Seed developed GR-3, a Vision-Language-Action model for generalist robots, which integrates a mixture-of-transformers architecture with multi-stage training across web-scale vision-language data, robot trajectories, and VR-collected human demonstrations. The model demonstrated superior generalization, improving success rates on unseen instructions from 40% to 77.1% and unseen objects from 40% to 57.8% compared to a baseline, further achieving 86.7% with efficient few-shot adaptation.

## Problem

- Generalist robots struggle to handle out-of-distribution instructions, novel objects, or unseen environments.
- High data requirements for training and adapting Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lead to high costs and time consumption.
- Maintaining robustness and reliability in complex, long-horizon, and dexterous manipulation tasks remains challenging for robotic policies.

## Method

- An end-to-end ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model, **GR-3**, based on a mixture-of-transformers architecture, including a pre-trained VLM and an Action Diffusion Transformer.
- A multi-stage training recipe that integrates imitation learning from robot trajectories, co-training with web-scale vision-language data, and few-shot fine-tuning with human VR-collected data.
- Development of ByteMini, a 22-DoF bi-manual mobile robot, specifically designed for complex manipulation tasks and robust data collection.

## Results

- GR-3 significantly improved generalization in pick-and-place, achieving **77.1%** success on unseen instructions and **57.8%** on unseen objects, compared to **40%** for the baseline.
- Demonstrated efficient few-shot adaptation, boosting success on unseen objects to **86.7%** with only 10 human VR demonstrations per object.
- Showcased robust performance in complex tasks, reaching **97.5%** average sub-task success in long-horizon table bussing and **86.7%** average task progress in dexterous cloth manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-training with web-scale vision-language data is crucial for zero-shot generalization of VLA models to unseen objects and abstract instructions.
> - Human trajectory data, efficiently collected via VR, facilitates rapid and cost-effective few-shot adaptation of policies to novel tasks and environments.
> - Architectural choices, such as RMSNorm and auxiliary "task status" supervision, enhance training stability and improve strict instruction following.
