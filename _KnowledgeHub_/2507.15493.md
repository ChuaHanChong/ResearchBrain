# GR-3 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance Seed developed GR-3, a Vision-Language-Action model for generalist robots, which integrates a mixture-of-transformers architecture with multi-stage training across web-scale vision-language data, robot trajectories, and VR-collected human demonstrations. The model demonstrated superior generalization, improving success rates on unseen instructions from 40% to 77.1% and unseen objects from 40% to 57.8% compared to a baseline, further achieving 86.7% with efficient few-shot adaptation.

## Problem

- Generalist robots struggle to handle out-of-distribution instructions, novel objects, or unseen environments.
- High data requirements for training and adapting Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lead to high costs and time consumption.
- Maintaining robustness and reliability in complex, long-horizon, and dexterous manipulation tasks remains challenging for robotic policies.

## Method

- An end-to-end ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model, **GR-3**, based on a mixture-of-transformers architecture, including a pre-trained VLM and an Action Diffusion Transformer.
- A multi-stage training recipe that integrates imitation learning from robot trajectories, co-training with web-scale vision-language data, and few-shot fine-tuning with human VR-collected data.
- Development of ByteMini, a 22-DoF bi-manual mobile robot, specifically designed for complex manipulation tasks and robust data collection.

## Results

- GR-3 significantly improved generalization in pick-and-place, achieving **77.1%** success on unseen instructions and **57.8%** on unseen objects, compared to **40%** for the baseline.
- Demonstrated efficient few-shot adaptation, boosting success on unseen objects to **86.7%** with only 10 human VR demonstrations per object.
- Showcased robust performance in complex tasks, reaching **97.5%** average sub-task success in long-horizon table bussing and **86.7%** average task progress in dexterous cloth manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-training with web-scale vision-language data is crucial for zero-shot generalization of VLA models to unseen objects and abstract instructions.
> - Human trajectory data, efficiently collected via VR, facilitates rapid and cost-effective few-shot adaptation of policies to novel tasks and environments.
> - Architectural choices, such as RMSNorm and auxiliary "task status" supervision, enhance training stability and improve strict instruction following.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of GR-3 is a significant step in the pursuit of **intelligent generalist robots**, a long-standing aspiration in robotics research aiming to create machines capable of assisting humans with diverse daily tasks. This work specifically contributes to the rapidly evolving field of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**, which integrate perception, natural language understanding, and robotic control.

Prior to GR-3, models like RT-1, RT-2, GATO, and more recently π0 and π0.5, have demonstrated the potential of leveraging large-scale pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) and extensive robot datasets to enable generalization in robotic control. These VLA models represent a paradigm shift from traditional, task-specific robotics by allowing robots to interpret and execute instructions given in natural language, even for tasks unseen during training.

However, existing VLA models face several limitations that GR-3 aims to address:
- **Instruction Following and Generalization:** Many models struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) instructions, novel object categories, complex concepts, and entirely new environments that were not explicitly included in their robot trajectory datasets. GR-3 specifically tackles abstract concepts, spatial relationships, and common-sense knowledge.
- **Data Efficiency for Adaptation:** Training VLA models typically requires vast amounts of robot demonstration data, making adaptation to new settings time-consuming and costly. GR-3 proposes an efficient few-shot adaptation strategy using readily available human trajectory data.
- **Robustness in Complex Tasks:** Ensuring reliable performance in long-horizon tasks and dexterous manipulation, especially with deformable objects, remains challenging due to cumulative errors and the need for fine-grained control. GR-3 emphasizes robust performance in these demanding scenarios.

GR-3 distinguishes itself by employing a **multi-faceted training recipe**. It combines co-training with web-scale vision-language data, efficient fine-tuning with human trajectory data collected via VR devices, and effective imitation learning from robot trajectory data. This holistic approach aims to endow the robot with stronger generalization capabilities and rapid adaptability. Furthermore, the introduction of ByteMini, a versatile bi-manual mobile robot, provides a robust hardware platform designed specifically to support the complex tasks GR-3 is trained for, marking a notable integration of hardware and software development. By comparing itself to state-of-the-art baselines like π0, GR-3 positions itself at the forefront of generalist robot policy development, pushing the boundaries of what these models can achieve in real-world, open-ended environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective behind the development of GR-3 is to construct **intelligent generalist robots capable of assisting humans with diverse daily tasks in real-world environments**. This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific challenges in contemporary robotics:

1. **Enhance Generalization Capabilities:** The primary motivation is to overcome the inherent limitation of traditional robot policies that struggle with novel scenarios. GR-3 aims to achieve "strong generalization capabilities to handle a wide range of novel objects, environments, and instructions." This includes understanding "abstract concepts relating to sizes, spatial relationships, and common-sense knowledge" that may be unseen in direct robot demonstrations.

2. **Improve Instruction Following for Out-of-Distribution Commands:** While existing VLA models can follow natural language, they often falter with "out-of-distribution instructions that involve novel object categories... and/or complex concepts that require sophisticated reasoning." A core objective for GR-3 is to "strictly follow languages and generalize well to novel objects, environments, and instructions."

3. **Enable Efficient and Cost-Effective Adaptation:** Collecting large amounts of human or robot demonstration data is expensive and time-consuming. GR-3 seeks to enable "efficiently fine-tuned with minimal human trajectory data," allowing for "rapid and cost-effective adaptation to new settings." This addresses the practical challenge of deploying robots in new, un-demonstrated environments.

4. **Ensure Robustness in Long-Horizon and Dexterous Tasks:** Many real-world tasks are complex, requiring sequential actions (long-horizon) and precise manipulation (dexterous skills). A key motivation is to develop policies that demonstrate "high robustness and reliability" in such challenging scenarios, including "bi-manual manipulation and mobile movement."

In summary, GR-3's key objectives are to:
- **Generalize:** Understand and execute instructions involving novel objects, environments, and abstract concepts in a zero-shot manner.
- **Adapt:** Learn efficiently from minimal human demonstrations, facilitating rapid deployment in new contexts.
- **Perform:** Execute complex, long-horizon, and dexterous manipulation tasks robustly and reliably.

These objectives directly tackle critical bottlenecks in bringing intelligent robots into daily human environments, moving beyond constrained lab settings to real-world versatility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GR-3 is an **end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model** designed to control a bi-manual mobile robot. Its methodology combines a sophisticated model architecture with a multi-faceted training recipe and a purpose-built hardware platform.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture: The GR-3 Model

GR-3 is structured as a **mixture-of-transformers architecture**. It takes a natural language instruction (`l`), environmental observations (`o_t`) from multiple camera views, and the current robot state (`s_t`) as inputs. Its output is a `k`-length action chunk (`a_t`) to control the robot.

- **VLM Backbone:** The model leverages a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically **Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct**, to process visual observations and language instructions. This backbone provides a strong foundation for understanding multimodal inputs.
- **Action Prediction:** Actions are predicted by an **Action Diffusion Transformer (DiT)** using a **flow-matching objective**. This approach frames action generation as predicting a vector field that transports a noisy action chunk towards the true action.
 - The DiT's flow prediction is conditioned on the current robot state and the KV cache (key and value representations) from the VLM backbone.
 - To ensure fast inference, the Action DiT has half the number of layers compared to the VLM backbone and utilizes only the KV cache from the latter half of the VLM layers.
 - **Architectural Stability Improvements:** A crucial design choice for training stability and language following capability was the application of **RMSNorm** after the linear layers within both the attention and feed-forward networks (FFN) inside the DiT blocks. This significantly improved training stability and performance.
- **Scale:** GR-3 encompasses a total of **4 Billion parameters**, indicating a substantial model size designed for handling complex representations.

#### 3.2. Training Recipe

GR-3's training is a multi-stage, multi-data approach, integrating diverse data sources to achieve generalization, efficiency, and robustness.

1. **Imitation Learning with Robot Trajectory Data:**
 - **Objective:** The primary objective is to maximize the log-likelihood of the policy on a dataset of expert demonstrations `D`. This is achieved by supervising action prediction with a **flow-matching loss**.
 - **Data Collection:** Real robot trajectories are collected via **teleoperation**. A custom **data collection scheduler** is used to diversify actions, object combinations, and background settings, ensuring thorough randomization and richer data. Post-collection quality checks filter out invalid data.
 - **Auxiliary Supervision (Task Status):** To prevent the policy from relying on spurious correlations from multiple viewpoints instead of language, an auxiliary "task status" dimension (Ongoing, Terminated, Invalid) is added to the action space. The model is trained to predict "Invalid" status when given an invalid instruction, forcing it to attend more closely to the language input and estimate task feasibility.

2. **Co-Training with Vision-Language (VL) Data:**
 - **Objective:** To imbue GR-3 with **zero-shot generalization capabilities** to out-of-distribution (OOD) instructions and unseen objects.
 - **Process:** GR-3 is jointly trained on robot trajectories (flow-matching objective for both VLM backbone and Action DiT) and large-scale web-scale VL data (next-token-prediction objective for VLM backbone only). Data types are dynamically mixed in mini-batches.
 - **VL Data Curation:** A large VL dataset is curated from various sources, covering tasks like image captioning, visual question answering, image grounding, and interleaved grounded image captioning. A filtering and re-annotation pipeline is employed to ensure high quality.
 - **Benefit:** This co-training transfers rich knowledge from large-scale VL data to policy learning, enhancing generalization without explicit robot demonstrations for novel concepts or objects.

3. **Few-Shot Generalization with Human Trajectory Data:**
 - **Objective:** To enable **efficient and cost-effective adaptation** to novel settings with minimal data.
 - **Data Collection:** Human trajectories are collected using **VR devices (PICO 4 Ultra Enterprise)**, including egocentric videos and human hand trajectories. This method is significantly faster (450 trajectories/hour) than teleoperated robot collection (250 trajectories/hour).
 - **Training Process:** After the initial training phase with robot and VL data, human trajectory data is incorporated for further co-training. For human data, missing wrist views are padded with blank images, and only hand trajectories are used for control (without arm joint states or gripper states).
 - **Benefit:** This allows GR-3 to learn from cross-embodiment data efficiently, enabling rapid fine-tuning for new objects or tasks with only a few demonstrations.

#### 3.3. Hardware & System: The ByteMini Robot

The ByteDance team developed **ByteMini**, a 22-DoF bi-manual mobile robot, specifically for data collection and policy rollout.

- **Design Principles:** Flexible manipulation (7-DoF unbiased arms with a unique sphere wrist joint for human-like dexterity in confined spaces), high reliability (omni mobile platform with lift mechanism, Quasi Direct Drive (QDD) actuators), and user-friendliness (portable screen, NUC, dual-lithium battery, wireless E-stop).
- **Sensors:** RGBD cameras are mounted on the head and both wrists for multi-view observations, crucial for fine manipulation.
- **Control System:**
 - **Whole-body compliance control:** Retargets arbitrary teleoperated human motion to feasible robot motion, addressing manipulability optimization, singularity avoidance, and joint limits in real-time. This produces high-quality expert trajectories for training.
 - **Whole-body teleoperation:** Via Meta VR Quest, allowing operators to control arms, lift, gripper, and mobile base simultaneously for efficient data collection.
 - **Trajectory optimization for policy rollout:** Uses predicted action chunks to control 19 DoFs, incorporating pure pursuit and trajectory optimization to minimize jerk and ensure stable, smooth motions during policy execution.

This integrated approach, encompassing architectural innovation, diverse data modalities, and a purpose-built robotic platform, forms the robust foundation of GR-3's capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of GR-3 was rigorously evaluated through extensive real-world experiments across three challenging tasks, comparing its performance against the state-of-the-art baseline, π0.

#### 4.1. Generalizable Pick-and-Place

This task assessed GR-3's generalization capabilities across various settings: Basic (seen environment/objects), Unseen Environments, Unseen Instructions (requiring abstract concept understanding), and Unseen Objects (unseen categories in robot data).

- **Superior Generalization:** GR-3 consistently outperformed π0 in both instruction following (IF) rate and success rate across all four settings.
 - For **Unseen Instructions**, GR-3 boosted the success rate from 40% (π0) to 77.1%.
 - For **Unseen Objects**, it raised the success rate from 40% (π0) to 57.8%.
- **Impact of Co-Training:** Ablation studies showed that **co-training with vision-language (VL) data is crucial** for GR-3's strong generalization. GR-3 without co-training (GR-3 w/o Co-Training) performed similarly to GR-3 on seen objects/environments but significantly worse than GR-3 (and even π0) on unseen instructions and objects, demonstrating the power of transferring knowledge from web-scale VL data.
- **Efficient Few-Shot Adaptation:** GR-3 demonstrated remarkable few-shot learning capabilities from human trajectory data collected via VR.
 - With only 10 human trajectories per unseen object (approx. 30 minutes total for 45 objects), GR-3's success rate on unseen objects increased from 57.8% (zero-shot) to **86.7%**.
 - This adaptation did not cause a performance drop on seen objects, highlighting a highly efficient and cost-effective fine-tuning strategy.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Table Bussing

This task evaluated GR-3's robustness in handling complex, sequential long-horizon manipulation, including mobile base movement. Experiments were conducted in a Flat setting (single general instruction) and an Instruction-Following (IF) setting (multiple sub-task instructions).

- **Robust Long-Horizon Performance:** GR-3 outperformed π0 in both settings, particularly excelling in the IF setting (97.5% average sub-task success rate for GR-3 vs. 53.8% for π0).
- **Strict Instruction Following:** GR-3 demonstrated the ability to strictly follow instructions, generalizing well to multiple objects, multiple destinations, novel destinations (e.g., "put the fork into the rubbish bin" when "fork" and "rubbish bin" weren't paired in training), and even refraining from action for "Invalid Tasks."
- **Importance of Architectural Choices:** Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of specific design choices:
 - **RMSNorm:** GR-3 w/o Norm showed significant performance drops, especially in the IF setting, underscoring its importance for language following.
 - **Task Status (TS):** GR-3 w/o Task Status also had reduced IF capabilities, validating the effectiveness of this auxiliary supervision in making the model attend to language.

#### 4.3. Dexterous Cloth Manipulation

This experiment challenged GR-3 with dexterous manipulation of deformable objects (hanging clothes on a drying rack). Evaluated settings included Basic, Position (crumpled/rotated clothes), and Unseen Instances (new patterns/sleeve lengths).

- **Proficiency in Dexterous Tasks:** GR-3 consistently outperformed π0 across all settings.
 - Achieved average task progress of **86.7% in Basic** and **83.9% in Position**, showcasing high proficiency and robustness to positional variations.
- **Generalization to Deformable Objects:** GR-3 generalized effectively to unseen clothes, achieving an average task progress of **75.8% for Unseen Instances**, even with novel patterns and sleeve lengths (e.g., short-sleeved shirts unseen in training).
- **Challenging Milestones:** Analysis identified "placing the left shoulder on the hanger" as the most challenging sub-task due to the need for delicate manipulation of folded fabric and precise grasping.

In summary, GR-3's results demonstrate its superior capabilities in generalization, instruction following, efficient adaptation, and robust performance across a wide range of complex real-world robotic manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming the chosen state-of-the-art baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GR-3 represents a substantial advancement in the field of robotics, particularly in the quest for intelligent generalist robots. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Progress Towards Generalist Robots:** GR-3 serves as a concrete step towards realizing the long-standing vision of robots capable of assisting humans in daily life. Its demonstrated ability to generalize across novel objects, environments, and abstract instructions brings closer the reality of adaptable, versatile robots.
- **Validation of Multi-Modal Co-Training:** The research strongly validates the effectiveness of co-training VLA models with a diverse mix of data types—specifically, web-scale vision-language data, robot trajectory data, and human trajectory data. This approach allows GR-3 to leverage vast amounts of internet knowledge for zero-shot generalization while grounding it in real-world robotic actions.
- **Enabling Efficient and Cost-Effective Robot Deployment:** The capability for efficient few-shot generalization from minimal human trajectory data collected via VR is a critical breakthrough. This significantly reduces the data collection burden and associated costs, making it far more practical to adapt pre-trained robot policies to new tasks or novel environments in real-world deployment scenarios. This could democratize access to advanced robotics by lowering the barrier for task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Robustness in Complex Real-World Tasks:** GR-3's strong performance on long-horizon tasks like table bussing and dexterous manipulation like cloth hanging, coupled with its ability to handle mobile base movement and bi-manual operations, indicates a high degree of robustness. This is essential for practical applications where robots must operate reliably in unstructured and dynamic environments.
- **Architectural Contributions:** The insights into architectural design choices, such as the strategic use of RMSNorm within the Action DiT and the auxiliary "task status" supervision, provide valuable lessons for future VLA model development, highlighting factors critical for stability and instruction following.
- **The ByteMini Platform:** The concurrent development of the ByteMini robot as a flexible, reliable, and user-friendly bi-manual mobile platform provides a robust foundation for GR-3's capabilities. This integration of advanced hardware and software could set a benchmark for future research and development in embodied AI.

In essence, GR-3 pushes the boundaries of robotic intelligence by demonstrating how to build more capable, adaptable, and robust robot policies. Its methodology has the potential to accelerate the transition of generalist robots from research labs to practical applications, paving the way for a future where robots can genuinely assist humans with a wide variety of tasks in their homes and workplaces. While limitations like handling highly novel concepts or recovering from out-of-distribution states remain, the foundations laid by GR-3 provide a promising roadmap for future advancements, possibly through the integration of reinforcement learning to enhance robustness further.
