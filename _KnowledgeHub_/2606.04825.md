# HapTile: A Haptic-Informed Vision-Tactile-Language-Action Dataset for Contact-Rich Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The HapTile dataset provides a comprehensive resource for contact-rich robot manipulation, integrating diverse everyday tasks with language instructions, robot action trajectories, and real-time fingertip visuotactile feedback. This dataset, collected via a haptic-informed teleoperation system, aims to advance the development of contact-aware robotic policies.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models predominantly rely on visual information, lacking critical tactile feedback for contact-rich manipulation or scenarios with visual occlusions.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, contact-grounded demonstration data necessary for training robust manipulation policies, with current tactile datasets often limited in task diversity or reproducibility.
- Common teleoperation pipelines for data collection typically do not provide haptic feedback to human operators, which can hinder demonstration quality and manipulation stability in tasks requiring physical contact.

## Method

- Introduced **HapTile**, a multimodal dataset comprising 1,726 demonstrations across 38 diverse contact-rich tasks, featuring synchronized vision, language instructions, robot proprioception, action trajectories, and fingertip visuotactile data.
- Developed a custom teleoperation platform that integrates custom-designed vision-based tactile sensors into a standard robotic gripper and provides real-time haptic feedback to the human operator.
- Standardized the robot action space to a 7D end-effector delta representation and implemented rigorous quality control to ensure high-fidelity, reproducible trajectories and generalize to various robot embodiments.

## Results

- HapTile dataset includes 1,726 demonstrations (750.33 minutes) across 38 tasks, with explicit language conditioning for policy guidance and synchronized multimodal observations including third-person and wrist camera images, raw tactile images, and robot state.
- Benchmarking studies on contact-intensive tasks demonstrated that adding raw tactile images (V+T) or marker-tracked tactile features (V+TM) generally improved policy success rates, with notable increases (e.g., **0%** to **90%** for Peg insertion with π0 V+T).
- Results showed that while tactile input is beneficial, its integration can be challenging, as the addition of marker-tracked features (V+TM) sometimes led to decreased performance compared to raw tactile images (V+T) for certain tasks and policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactile information, either as raw tactile images or marker-tracked features, generally improves or matches the performance of vision-only policies in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
> - The most effective tactile representation is task-dependent: raw tactile images are beneficial for precise contact localization (e.g., peg insertion), while marker tracking is more effective for tasks reliant on shear and force cues (e.g., wiping).
> - Providing real-time haptic feedback to human teleoperators enhances their awareness of physical interactions, contributing to higher quality and more stable demonstration trajectories for contact-rich tasks.
