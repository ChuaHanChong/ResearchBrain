# HapTile: A Haptic-Informed Vision-Tactile-Language-Action Dataset for Contact-Rich Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The HapTile dataset provides a comprehensive resource for contact-rich robot manipulation, integrating diverse everyday tasks with language instructions, robot action trajectories, and real-time fingertip visuotactile feedback. This dataset, collected via a haptic-informed teleoperation system, aims to advance the development of contact-aware robotic policies.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models predominantly rely on visual information, lacking critical tactile feedback for contact-rich manipulation or scenarios with visual occlusions.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, contact-grounded demonstration data necessary for training robust manipulation policies, with current tactile datasets often limited in task diversity or reproducibility.
- Common teleoperation pipelines for data collection typically do not provide haptic feedback to human operators, which can hinder demonstration quality and manipulation stability in tasks requiring physical contact.

## Method

- Introduced **HapTile**, a multimodal dataset comprising 1,726 demonstrations across 38 diverse contact-rich tasks, featuring synchronized vision, language instructions, robot proprioception, action trajectories, and fingertip visuotactile data.
- Developed a custom teleoperation platform that integrates custom-designed vision-based tactile sensors into a standard robotic gripper and provides real-time haptic feedback to the human operator.
- Standardized the robot action space to a 7D end-effector delta representation and implemented rigorous quality control to ensure high-fidelity, reproducible trajectories and generalize to various robot embodiments.

## Results

- HapTile dataset includes 1,726 demonstrations (750.33 minutes) across 38 tasks, with explicit language conditioning for policy guidance and synchronized multimodal observations including third-person and wrist camera images, raw tactile images, and robot state.
- Benchmarking studies on contact-intensive tasks demonstrated that adding raw tactile images (V+T) or marker-tracked tactile features (V+TM) generally improved policy success rates, with notable increases (e.g., **0%** to **90%** for Peg insertion with π0 V+T).
- Results showed that while tactile input is beneficial, its integration can be challenging, as the addition of marker-tracked features (V+TM) sometimes led to decreased performance compared to raw tactile images (V+T) for certain tasks and policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactile information, either as raw tactile images or marker-tracked features, generally improves or matches the performance of vision-only policies in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
> - The most effective tactile representation is task-dependent: raw tactile images are beneficial for precise contact localization (e.g., peg insertion), while marker tracking is more effective for tasks reliant on shear and force cues (e.g., wiping).
> - Providing real-time haptic feedback to human teleoperators enhances their awareness of physical interactions, contributing to higher quality and more stable demonstration trajectories for contact-rich tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressing towards increasingly autonomous and general-purpose systems. A significant area of focus involves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which enable robots to interpret visual observations and natural language instructions to perform tasks. Recent advancements in VLA models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and π₀, demonstrate capabilities across diverse manipulation scenarios. However, a limitation of these models, and indeed many existing robotic datasets, is the primary reliance on visual input.

Human manipulation fundamentally integrates both visual and tactile sensing. While vision provides broad contextual information like object location and identity, tactile feedback offers localized contact properties such, as force, slip, and surface deformation. This tactile information is particularly critical for compliant object handling, tasks involving occlusions, or scenarios requiring precise force control, where visual cues alone may be insufficient or ambiguous.

Within the existing research landscape, several gaps exist that HapTile aims to address:
- **Vision-Only VLA Datasets:** The majority of large-scale VLA datasets (e.g., RoboSet, DROID) are vision-centric and do not incorporate explicit tactile feedback from the robot’s end-effector.
- **Limited Multimodal Datasets:** While some studies have begun to integrate tactile or force sensing into VLA models, existing tactile manipulation datasets often lack a joint combination of task diversity, language conditioning for policy guidance, and complete action trajectories. Many are self-collected for specific tasks, limiting their generalizability. Datasets like RH20T include force sensing, but typically at the wrist level, which captures global loads rather than the fine-grained spatial contact patterns provided by fingertip sensors.
- **Lack of Policy Learning Support:** Other visuotactile datasets, such as Touch and Go, TVL, and MMWand, focus on learning generalizable representations from multimodal data but often omit robot actions and task goals, thereby limiting their direct applicability for policy learning in robotic manipulation.
- **Absence of Haptic Feedback in Teleoperation:** Many existing teleoperation systems used for data collection do not provide real-time haptic feedback to the human operator. This feedback is recognized as beneficial for improving the quality of demonstrations, enhancing operator awareness, and contributing to manipulation stability by allowing operators to feel contact interactions.

HapTile positions itself within this landscape by creating a comprehensive dataset that bridges these gaps. It provides a contact-grounded visuotactile manipulation dataset that explicitly incorporates fingertip tactile feedback and integrates real-time haptic feedback into the teleoperation process. This aims to facilitate the development of more robust and dexterous robotic manipulation policies, particularly for tasks that require intricate physical interaction and contact awareness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce HapTile, a novel dataset designed to advance imitation learning for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is supported by several specific objectives and motivations.

### 3. Key Objectives

1. **Develop a Multi-Modal, Contact-Grounded Dataset:** To provide a comprehensive dataset of everyday manipulation tasks that integrates diverse modalities, including vision, fingertip tactile sensing, robot proprioception, language instructions, and robot action trajectories. The aim is to move beyond vision-only datasets and incorporate the crucial physical interaction information captured by tactile sensors.
2. **Incorporate Haptic-Informed Demonstrations:** To embed haptic feedback directly into the teleoperation pipeline during data collection. This enables operators to perceive contact interactions in real time, with the goal of improving the quality and stability of the collected human demonstrations.
3. **Ensure Accessibility and Reproducibility:** To build the data collection platform using standard, widely available robotic hardware (UR5e manipulator, Robotiq gripper) and custom-designed, low-cost vision-based tactile sensors. This aims to make the platform and dataset accessible for broader research use and to facilitate reproducible data collection.
4. **Utilize Language for Policy Conditioning:** To use language instructions not merely as descriptive metadata but as explicit conditioning signals for the robot policy. This approach aims to enable policies to execute tasks based on high-level semantic instructions, promoting generalization across similar tasks with varying objectives.
5. **Benchmark Dataset Effectiveness:** To validate the utility of the HapTile dataset by conducting a benchmarking study. This involves training and evaluating established baseline policies (Diffusion Policy and π₀) on a subset of contact-rich tasks to demonstrate the benefits of incorporating multimodal, especially tactile, inputs.

**Motivation:**
- **Human-Robot Dexterity Gap:** Human manipulation is highly dexterous due to the coordinated use of vision and touch. Current robotic systems often lack comparable tactile perception, hindering their ability to perform complex, contact-rich tasks reliably in unstructured environments.
- **Limitations of Vision-Only Approaches:** While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown promise, their reliance on vision alone makes them vulnerable to challenges such as occlusions, ambiguous visual cues, and the inability to sense forces, slip, or material properties crucial for stable manipulation.
- **Scarcity of Relevant Data:** The development of advanced visuotactile-language-action models is constrained by the limited availability of large-scale, high-quality datasets that comprehensively combine these modalities in contact-rich scenarios. Existing datasets often lack the necessary joint information, task diversity, or proper policy conditioning.
- **Enhancing Demonstration Quality:** The quality of human demonstrations directly impacts the performance of imitation learning algorithms. Providing haptic feedback to the human teleoperator during data collection is motivated by its established role in improving human perception, precision, and stability during physical interaction, thereby leading to higher quality and more informative robot demonstrations.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The authors developed HapTile, a contact-grounded visuotactile manipulation dataset, through a structured data collection and processing methodology.

**Data Collection Platform:**
The system is designed around a standard UR5e manipulator equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper. Key components include:
- **Robot Stack:** UR5e manipulator, Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, two custom-designed fingertip vision-based tactile sensors, wrist-mounted RGB camera, and a third-person RGB camera.
- **Teleoperator Interface:** A Meta Quest headset and controller are used by human operators to command end-effector motion and gripper state.
- **Vision-Based Tactile Sensors:** Each gripper finger is outfitted with a custom sensor (approximately 20mm × 23mm × 37mm with a 16mm × 29.5mm sensing area). The sensor features a 3mm transparent silicone elastomer coated with a diffuse reflective silicone–gray ink mixture for deformation visibility. An internal COB LED strip provides uniform illumination for an IMX258 RGB camera (30 FPS, 135° FOV) that captures real-time tactile images.
- **Observation-Action Tuple:** At each control step (15 Hz), the system records:
 - `o_t`: Visual observations (third-person and wrist cameras), raw tactile images from both fingers, and robot proprioception (joint states, end-effector pose).
 - `a_t`: The expert action, represented as a 7D end-effector delta (`[∆x, ∆y, ∆z, ∆r, ∆p, ∆ψ, g]`), comprising translational and rotational deltas, and a gripper command. This delta action representation aims to decouple policy learning from specific robot kinematics.
 - `l`: A natural language instruction specifying the task goal.
 - `τ_t`: Timestamp for synchronization.

**Haptic Feedback Mechanism:**
To provide real-time haptic feedback to the teleoperator, the system processes tactile sensor data:
1. **Marker Tracking:** At the start of each trajectory, marker points are detected and tracked in the tactile image streams using the Lucas–Kanade optical flow algorithm. Robustness is enhanced by forward–backward consistency checks, marker-region masking, and reinitialization if too few valid points remain.
2. **Motion Score Calculation:** The displacement of valid markers relative to their initial reference is used to compute a scalar motion score `m_t`. This score approximates contact-induced deformation and potential slip.
3. **Haptic Signal Generation:** The motion score `m_t` is normalized (`bm_t`) and discretely mapped to vibration amplitudes for the Meta Quest controller. This binary or low-resolution haptic signal indicates whether sufficient contact force has been established, reducing sensitivity to noise and highlighting contact state transitions. Haptic feedback is active only during teleoperation.

**Dataset Statistics and Structure:**
- **Content:** HapTile comprises 1,726 demonstrations across 38 distinct tasks, categorized into 9 manipulation skills (e.g., move, put, remove, turn, fold, insert, wipe, press, stack).
- **Objects:** The dataset includes a variety of everyday objects, some from the YCB object set, possessing diverse mechanical and geometric properties.
- **Operators:** Data was collected by 9 human operators.
- **Duration:** The total interaction time spans 750.33 minutes.
- **Synchronization and Quality Control:** All modalities are synchronized within the robot control loop. Quality checks include removing corrupted trajectories, inspecting timestamp gaps, and verifying action-state consistency. Tactile images undergo perspective rectification during calibration to ensure consistent coordinate frames.

**Experimental Protocol:**
- **Randomization:** Object poses are randomized within a visible workspace, and one or two clutter objects are added to each scene to increase diversity.
- **Task Complexity:** Demonstration counts vary per task, from 30 for simpler tasks to 50–60 for more complex or longer-horizon tasks (e.g., bottle turning, board wiping).
- **Language Conditioning Design:** Each task includes two distinct target states specified by language instructions. This design encourages policies to condition on language rather than converging to a single outcome, thus improving language-action grounding.

**Evaluation and Ablation Studies:**
- **Selected Tasks:** Four contact-rich tasks (Turning a bottle upright, Wiping a whiteboard, Pouring liquid, Peg insertion) were chosen for quantitative evaluation due to their high tactile requirements.
- **Baseline Policies:** Two learning policies were used:
 - **Diffusion Policy:** A visuomotor policy learning method.
 - **π₀:** A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy, fine-tuned on the dataset.
- **Modality Configurations:** Ablation studies compared three input configurations for each policy, evaluating success rates over 10 demonstrations per setting:
 - **Vision-only (V-only):** RGB camera observations (third-person and wrist views) plus robot proprioception.
 - **Vision with tactile sensing (V+T):** V-only augmented with raw tactile images from both gripper fingers.
 - **Vision with tactile sensing and marker tracking (V+TM):** V-only augmented with raw tactile images and the extracted marker displacement features (motion score).
- **Training Details:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) fine-tuning was employed for memory efficiency. States and actions were normalized. Policies received multimodal observations and predicted action chunks, with replanning during deployment.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded a novel multimodal dataset and provided insights into the effectiveness of tactile sensing in imitation learning for contact-rich tasks.

**Dataset Creation and Characteristics:**
- HapTile was successfully created, comprising 1,726 demonstrations across 38 diverse tasks and 9 manipulation skills, collected from 9 operators over 750.33 minutes of interaction.
- The dataset uniquely combines language instructions, synchronized vision, fingertip tactile data (raw images and marker tracking features), robot proprioception, and robot action trajectories, with the additional metadata of haptic-informed teleoperation.
- The custom vision-based tactile sensors and the integration of haptic feedback into the teleoperation pipeline are reported to enhance the quality and contact awareness of the collected demonstrations.

**Baseline Evaluation on Contact-Rich Tasks (Table 2):**
The ablation studies on four selected tasks demonstrated task-dependent benefits of integrating tactile information:

1. **Turning a Bottle Upright:**
 - Diffusion Policy: V-only (80%), V+T (80%), V+TM (90%). The V+TM configuration, incorporating marker tracking, achieved the highest success rate, suggesting its utility for precise force regulation and slip avoidance on curved surfaces.
 - π₀: V-only (60%), V+T (0%), V+TM (60%). For π₀, V+T resulted in a significant performance drop, while V+TM matched vision-only.

2. **Wiping a Whiteboard:**
 - Diffusion Policy: V-only (30%), V+T (80%), V+TM (30%). V+T (raw tactile images) showed a substantial improvement over V-only, indicating the importance of raw contact information. However, V+TM performed poorly.
 - π₀: V-only (50%), V+T (0%), V+TM (100%). π₀ achieved 100% success with V+TM, outperforming V-only, consistent with the need for shear and force cues in sustained contact. V+T again led to a performance degradation.

3. **Pouring Liquid:**
 - Diffusion Policy: V-only (50%), V+T (50%), V+TM (20%). Tactile input did not consistently improve performance, with V+TM leading to a decrease.
 - π₀: V-only (30%), V+T (60%), V+TM (0%). V+T significantly improved performance for π₀, suggesting raw tactile images were more effective for precise contact localization and controlled tilt to avoid spillage. V+TM again resulted in a sharp drop to 0% success.

4. **Peg Insertion:**
 - Diffusion Policy: V-only (40%), V+T (40%), V+TM (0%). Tactile input did not improve performance for Diffusion Policy, and V+TM led to failure.
 - π₀: V-only (0%), V+T (90%), V+TM (40%). For π₀, V+T (raw tactile input) dramatically improved success from 0% to 90%, highlighting its critical role in tasks requiring fine contact geometry and precise alignment. V+TM also showed an improvement from V-only but was not as effective as V+T.

**Key Observations from Ablation Studies:**
- **General Benefit of Tactile Input:** In most cases, adding tactile input, in one form or another, either improved or matched vision-only performance for the contact-rich tasks.
- **Complementary Nature of Tactile Representations:** Raw tactile images (V+T) proved more effective for tasks requiring precise contact localization (e.g., peg insertion, pouring liquid), likely due to their high-dimensional pixel-level detail of contact geometry. Conversely, marker-tracked tactile input (V+TM) was beneficial for tasks dependent on shear and force cues, such as wiping or turning a bottle.
- **Task-Specific Performance Variability:** The optimal tactile representation varied significantly between tasks, and in some instances (e.g., Diffusion Policy with V+TM for wiping and pouring), incorporating tactile input led to a decrease in performance. This suggests potential challenges like over-reliance on high-dimensional tactile signals or the need for more sophisticated multimodal fusion strategies.
- **Policy-Specific Performance:** The two policies, Diffusion Policy and π₀, exhibited different sensitivities to tactile inputs. π₀, a larger pre-trained model, demonstrated more consistent improvements with structured tactile representations (V+TM for wiping, V+T for insertion and pouring), while Diffusion Policy showed varied and sometimes detrimental outcomes with tactile augmentation. The authors suggest this may relate to the dataset scale, where Diffusion Policy might learn effectively from fewer demonstrations, while π₀ requires more extensive fine-tuning data for novel tactile inputs.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The HapTile dataset represents a contribution to the fields of robotic manipulation, imitation learning, and multimodal sensing, with several potential impacts:

- **Advancing Contact-Aware Robot Policies:** HapTile addresses a fundamental limitation in current robotic systems by providing a dataset that explicitly grounds manipulation policies in physical contact. This is crucial for developing robots capable of reliably executing contact-rich tasks common in everyday environments, such as handling deformable objects, precise assembly, or tasks involving unknown object properties.
- **Enabling Next-Generation Visuotactile-Language-Action (VTLA) Models:** By integrating vision, language, and fingertip tactile sensing with action trajectories, HapTile provides the necessary data for training more holistic and intelligent robot policies. It facilitates research into how robots can integrate diverse sensory streams and semantic instructions to achieve complex manipulation goals, potentially leading to more robust and adaptable VTLA models.
- **Improving Data Collection Methodologies:** The incorporation of real-time haptic feedback in the teleoperation pipeline offers a novel approach to enhancing the quality and fidelity of human demonstrations. This methodology has the potential to become a standard practice for collecting imitation learning datasets, yielding more stable, precise, and informative trajectories that are critical for robot learning.
- **Promoting Reproducibility and Accessibility in Research:** The dataset is built on a standard, accessible robotic platform and utilizes custom, low-cost vision-based tactile sensors. This design choice, coupled with the commitment to open-source the dataset and code, aims to lower the barrier to entry for researchers in tactile robotics and imitation learning, thereby accelerating progress in the field through broader participation and comparative studies.
- **Benchmarking and Driving Future Research:** The included benchmarking study and ablation analyses provide valuable baseline performance figures and highlight the nuanced benefits and challenges of multimodal fusion. These results will guide future research into more effective architectures for integrating vision and tactile data, developing adaptive fusion strategies, and exploring how different tactile representations contribute to specific manipulation skills.
- **Bridging the Human-Robot Dexterity Gap:** By providing robots with data similar to how humans perceive and interact with the physical world, HapTile contributes to closing the dexterity gap between human and robotic manipulation. This could lead to robots that are more proficient in delicate tasks, robust to uncertainties, and capable of adapting to novel contact situations.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** The focus on diverse everyday manipulation tasks (e.g., folding, pouring, wiping) suggests that policies trained on HapTile could find applications in domestic robotics, industrial automation for delicate assembly, elder care assistance, and other areas requiring fine motor skills and tactile intelligence.
