# Proxy3D: Efficient 3D Representations for Vision-Language Models via Semantic Clustering and Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Proxy3D introduces an efficient framework for Vision-Language Models to comprehend 3D environments by creating compact, semantically-clustered 3D proxy representations from multi-view images. This method achieves competitive performance on 3D question answering and visual grounding benchmarks while significantly reducing the visual token sequence length, processing 700 tokens compared to 8000 tokens for correspondence-based models.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with human-level spatial intelligence, particularly in effectively reasoning about 3D scenes.
- Correspondence-based methods for 3D understanding in VLMs suffer from spatial inconsistencies, inefficient training data use, and high computational costs due to reliance on long 2D pixel-aligned token sequences.
- Representation-based methods often produce computationally inefficient 3D representations with fixed geometric priors, failing to adequately model complex spatial relationships within a compact token sequence.

## Method

- A **Proxy3D** architecture extracts ==2D visual features==, ==3D point maps==, and ==2D segmentation masks== from multi-view images, then employs ==semantic-aware K-nearest neighbors clustering== to generate compact 3D proxy representations.
- The **SpaceSpan** dataset, a 318K high-quality training set, unifies existing 3D data and incorporates 115K object-object relationship questions to enhance MLLM spatial reasoning capabilities.
- A ==progressive multi-stage training pipeline== (semantic & identifier embedding, coordinate alignment, spatial relationship, proxy representation) is implemented to iteratively develop spatial intelligence and effectively align compact 3D proxies with the language model.

## Results

- **Proxy3D** achieved competitive performance in 3D Question Answering (ScanQA, SQA3D) and demonstrated **state-of-the-art** or second-best results in 3D Visual Grounding (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer) using a substantially shorter visual token sequence length (**~700 vs 8000** tokens).
- The semantic grouping mechanism proved critical for accurate object distinction, leading to over **+20 points** increase in overall accuracy for visual grounding tasks compared to clustering without semantic awareness.
- Ablation studies confirmed that coordinate alignment significantly improves performance in tasks requiring precise positional understanding (e.g., room size, route planning), and dynamic proxy allocation effectively emphasizes objects of interest, particularly smaller ones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic-aware clustering of visual and geometric features is effective in significantly reducing the visual input sequence length for MLLMs (e.g., from 8000 to 700 tokens) without substantial loss in 3D spatial understanding.
> - A multi-stage training regimen that progressively introduces object identifiers, precise coordinate alignment, and explicit object-object spatial relationship understanding is crucial for robust 3D scene interpretation.
> - The fusion of 3D positional embeddings (Rotary for vertical, learnable Fourier for width and length) with visual features effectively injects essential geometric priors into the compact proxy representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Proxy3D" is situated within the expanding field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that aim to imbue AI systems with spatial intelligence, enabling them to reason about and interact with the 3D world. Human intelligence fundamentally relies on processing 2D visual inputs into 3D spatial information, which is then decoded into language for description and reasoning. Current MLLMs are developing similar perception capabilities, but existing evaluations indicate a significant disparity between their spatial intelligence and human-level understanding.

The paper identifies two primary categories of existing methods for 3D scene understanding in VLMs and MLLMs:
1. **Correspondence-based methods:** These approaches implicitly derive 3D spatial awareness by matching and aligning features across multiple 2D image frames. Examples include 3DRS, Video-3D LLM, SR-3D, and Ross3D. While showing promising results, these models often suffer from spatial inconsistencies, inefficient utilization of training data, a lack of global scene understanding, and high computational costs due to reliance on long sequences of pixel-aligned 2D representations. The requirement for extensive token sequences (e.g., 8000 tokens) becomes a significant bottleneck for MLLMs.
2. **Representation-based methods:** These methods explicitly model 3D scenes by leveraging 2D image features, often using classic 3D representations such as point clouds, depth maps, or graphs. While some recent work, like Spatial-MLLM, PQ3D, LLaVA-3D, LEO-VL, and VLM-3R, aims to develop unified representations that combine geometric priors with instance-level visual features, a fundamental challenge remains: constructing a sequence of tokens that accurately captures spatial information for an MLLM while minimizing its size. Naive serialization of point clouds, for instance, may not adequately model complex spatial relationships via cross-attention mechanisms.

Proxy3D addresses the core problem of reconciling the need for rich 3D spatial information with the computational efficiency demanded by MLLMs. It specifically aims to create compact, yet comprehensive, 3D proxy representations that can reduce the sequence length of vision modality input, thereby improving efficiency and potentially enhancing spatial consistency compared to conventional 2D-pipeline or high-token-count 3D representation methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an efficient method for generating 3D representations that enable Vision-Language Models to reason effectively in 3D environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Address Spatial Inconsistency and Inefficiency of Correspondence-based Methods:** Existing methods that rely on 2D pixel-aligned representations and implicit 3D understanding often struggle with spatial consistency and make inefficient use of training data. This leads to computational expensiveness and a limited global understanding of 3D scenes. Proxy3D aims to overcome these limitations by introducing explicit 3D representations that are more spatially consistent.

2. **Enhance Efficiency in Representation-based Models:** While representation-based methods explicitly model 3D scenes, they frequently produce computationally inefficient representations with fixed geometric priors. The challenge identified is to maintain accurate spatial information within a sequence of tokens while minimizing the sequence length for MLLM processing. The authors observe that encoded vision modalities often have sparse semantic distributions, which can be leveraged for compression.

3. **Develop Compact and Comprehensive 3D Proxy Representations:** The core technical motivation is to design a method, named Proxy3D, that generates a condensed set of 3D features. These "proxies" are intended to capture both semantic and geometric information of a scene effectively, allowing for shorter vision sequences without significant loss of detail. This involves using semantic-aware clustering to compress the feature sequence.

4. **Curate a Unified and Rich Spatial Reasoning Dataset:** Recognizing the limitations of existing 3D vision-language training data, particularly concerning 3D queries and object-object spatial relationships, a key objective is to curate a new dataset, SpaceSpan. This dataset aims to provide high-quality, unified data incorporating heterogeneous visual information and rich spatial reasoning-related queries.

5. **Propose a Progressive Multi-stage Training Pipeline:** To effectively align the compact 3D proxy representations with a language model and foster robust spatial understanding, the authors aim to introduce a multi-stage training scheme. This pipeline is designed to iteratively develop specific spatial intelligence skills in the MLLM, progressing from simplified object referencing to complex spatial relationship understanding with real 3D scene inputs.

In summary, the motivation behind Proxy3D is to achieve efficient and accurate 3D spatial reasoning in VLMs by creating concise, semantically-aware 3D representations and training MLLMs to effectively interpret these representations through a structured, multi-stage learning process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Proxy3D method is structured around three main components: feature extraction and semantic clustering for 3D proxy generation, a novel dataset called SpaceSpan, and a progressive multi-stage training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Proxy3D Architecture

- **Feature Extraction:**
 - **Input:** The process begins with `N` RGB image frames, each with `H × W × 3` resolution.
 - **2D Visual Encoder:** These frames are fed into a pre-trained 2D visual encoder (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B's vision encoder) to obtain feature maps `{F_i}`. The size of each `F_i ∈ R^(H' × W' × C)` depends on the encoder's latent dimension `C` and patch size `q` (e.g., `H' = ⌊H/q⌋`, `W' = ⌊W/q⌋`).
 - **Geometry Predictor:** A geometry predictor (e.g., VGGT) is used to extract a set of point maps `{P_i}` from the image frames, where `P_i ∈ R^(H×W×3)` contains 3D coordinates for each pixel. Scale factors are applied to normalize these point maps.
 - **2D Segmentation Model:** A 2D segmentation model (e.g., SAM 2) provides pixel-level segmentation masks `{M_i}`, where `M_i ∈ Z^(H×W)`.
 - **Patchification and Alignment:** Point maps and masks are patchified according to the patch size `q` to align with the image features, yielding `{M_j, P_j}` for a sequence length `L = N × H' × W'`. For each patch, `M_j` is assigned the label of the object with the largest area, and `P_j` is normalized by that object's area.
 - **Flattened Triplet:** The aligned elements form triplets `{F_j, P_j, M_j}`, which are then flattened into vectors `{f_j, p_j, m_j}` for subsequent processing.

- **Semantic Clustering:**
 - To reduce the sequence length and computational complexity, the method groups these triplets based on their semantic labels `g` from `m_j`, creating sets `G_g = {f_j, p_j | m_j = g}`.
 - Inspired by existing point cloud processing techniques, K-nearest neighbors (KNN) clustering is applied within each semantic group `G_g` to obtain a selected number of proxies `K_g`. This results in a set of 3D proxies `P = {z_g,j, c_g,j}`, where `z_g,j` are transformed visual features and `c_g,j` are their coordinates.
 - **Dynamic Proxy Allocation:** `K_g` is dynamically allocated for each semantic group based on its proportional presence in the overall sequence (`K_g ∝ |G_g|/L`), ensuring a minimum number of proxies to avoid overlooking any instance.

- **Proxy3D Sequence Serialization:**
 - The 3D group centers are traversed using Breadth-first search (BFS).
 - This traversal serializes the 3D visual embeddings into an ordered list of scene tokens, where segments spatially close to each other are also neighbors in the sequence, aiming to facilitate an LLM's understanding of spatial relationships.

- **3D Spatial Position Embeddings:**
 - To inject geometric priors, 3D position embeddings are applied. Rotary position embeddings (RoPE) encode vertical position, and learnable Fourier embeddings capture width and length information.
 - The combined embedding for a proxy `j` in group `g` is `z'_g,j = R(c_g,j∈H) z_g,j + F(c_g,j∈{W×L})`.
 - The final 3D proxy features `Z` are a concatenation of these variable-length, sorted (`by BFS`) matrices `Z_g` for each semantic group. The total sequence length `K` is significantly smaller than the original `L`.

#### 3.2. SpaceSpan Dataset and Multi-stage Training

- **SpaceSpan Dataset:** A 318K high-quality training set is curated. It aggregates 155K data points from existing 3D datasets (ScanQA, SQA3D, ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer, Scan2Cap) and 163K data points from MMScan and SR-91K. A specific subset of 115K object-object relationship questions from MMScan is included to enhance spatial reasoning. The dataset uses a unified data format.

- **Object Referencing:** To improve MLLM interpretation of complex 3D scenes from 2D embeddings, the method introduces spatial semantic positional embeddings.
 - **Identifier Embeddings:** Simplified identifier images (e.g., numbers) are generated, and their vision encoder embeddings are paired with text tokens (e.g., `<OBJ005>`) to enable precise object instance referencing with positional awareness.
 - **Semantic Embeddings:** Simplified semantic symbol images (generated by Stable Diffusion) are processed by the vision encoder to provide efficient visual representations for object categories.
 - These fused embeddings (`f_sem + f_id`) are additively injected into the serialized proxy embeddings, allowing the model to reference objects without requiring separate learnable embeddings.

- **Multi-stage Training Pipeline:** A progressive iterative training scheme is employed (illustrated in Figure 3).
 - **Stage 1: Semantic & Identifier Embedding Training:** The MLLM is trained with fused identifier and semantic embeddings, simulating simplified visual inputs. It learns to associate `<OBJXXX>` tokens with object categories and instances.
 - **Stage 2: Coordinate Alignment:** This stage focuses on training the 3D RoPE embeddings and developing spatial size awareness for each identifier embedding. Tasks involve precise coordinate determination.
 - **Stage 3: Spatial Relationship Training:** Using 115K object-object relationship questions from the SpaceSpan dataset (specifically, the MMScan slice), the MLLM is explicitly trained to understand complex spatial relationships and effectively utilize positional encoding.
 - **Stage 4: Proxy Representation Training:** In the final stage, actual 3D scene proxies (generated by the Proxy3D architecture) are used as visual inputs, fine-tuning the MLLM's knowledge from simplified representations to real scene inputs using the full 318K SpaceSpan dataset.

- **Instruction Tuning Objective:** The model is trained to minimize the negative log-likelihood loss for an autoregressive model `P_θ(t_i | t_<i, Z)`, where `t_i` is the `i`-th output token, `t_<i` are previous text tokens, and `Z` is the introduced 3D proxy sequence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Proxy3D demonstrates its performance across various 3D scene understanding tasks, emphasizing efficiency through reduced visual token sequence length.

- **Efficiency:** A notable finding is Proxy3D's ability to achieve competitive results using significantly shorter visual token sequences. For instance, Proxy3D operates with 700 tokens, which is substantially less than the 8000 tokens used by correspondence-based models like Qwen2-VL-7B, GPT4Scene, Video-3D-LLM, and 3DRS, and also less than LLaVA-3D's 3096 tokens. This reduction in sequence length directly translates to improved computational efficiency.

- **3D Question Answering (ScanQA and SQA3D):**
 - Proxy3D (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, 700 tokens) achieved competitive performance metrics on ScanQA and SQA3D benchmarks.
 - It demonstrated comparable results to correspondence-based models while using a significantly smaller number of visual tokens.
 - When compared to representation-based models like LEO-VL (750 tokens), Proxy3D showed nearly identical performance on available benchmarks, despite LEO-VL employing an additional post-training objective (SceneDPO).

- **3D Visual Grounding (ScanRefer and Multi3DRefer):**
 - Proxy3D achieved state-of-the-art or second-best results on ScanRefer and Multi3DRefer benchmarks.
 - This performance is attributed to the accurate distinction of objects facilitated by the proposed semantic grouping mechanism.
 - It outperformed object-proposal methods such as Chat-Scene and Descrip3D, which concatenate object embeddings. Proxy3D's approach of semantic-aware grouping and compression proved more effective.

- **Dense Captioning (Scan2Cap):**
 - Dense captioning proved to be a more challenging benchmark for representation-based models, including Proxy3D.
 - Proxy3D's performance was competitive but generally lagged behind correspondence-based models, suggesting a potential trade-off between the simplicity of its representations and the detailed semantics required for dense captioning.

- **Spatial Reasoning (VSI-Bench):**
 - On the VSI-Bench dataset, Proxy3D (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieved the second-best overall performance among open-source models, with an average rank of 2.
 - It was marginally surpassed only by Spatial-MLLM (which uses a reward learning step and a 7x longer sequence length, 3096 vs 450 tokens in Proxy3D's main configuration).
 - A significant gap remains between all models' performance and human-level spatial reasoning on VSI-Bench, particularly in tasks like appearance order and route planning. However, tasks like object counting and object size estimation showed performance closer to or even exceeding human levels.
 - Performance across different data splits of VSI-Bench (ARKitScenes, Scannet++, Scannet) was largely uniform, indicating robustness to dataset heterogeneity, although performance varied significantly between different spatial reasoning task types.

- **Ablation Study Findings (Table 4, Table 5, Figure 7):**
 - **Feature Map Resolution and Proxy Sequence Length:** Higher feature map resolutions and larger proxy sequence lengths generally correlated with improved performance metrics across QA, VG, and DC tasks. This provides a mechanism for tuning the complexity-accuracy balance.
 - **Coordinate Alignment:** This component significantly improved performance in tasks requiring precise positional understanding, such as room size estimation, route planning, and appearance order (demonstrated on VSI-Bench).
 - **Semantic Grouping:** This mechanism showed moderate improvements in QA benchmarks and a substantial impact on visual grounding (over 20 points increase in overall accuracy). This indicates that clustering without semantic awareness leads to inaccurate object referencing.
 - **Inter-frame Attention:** Proxy3D exhibited robustness even when inter-frame cross-attention within the vision encoder was absent, suggesting it can establish scene understanding primarily from instance-based features. This contrasts with correspondence-based methods that heavily rely on such inter-frame similarities.
 - **Dynamic Proxy Allocation:** The dynamic allocation scheme for group-aware proxies, particularly emphasizing objects of interest, was found to be effective. For Scan2Cap, an optimal number of 5 proxies was identified, demonstrating that adaptive assignment can provide more attention to small objects, but increasing proxies too much can lead to an imbalance in model understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Proxy3D framework contributes to the field of multimodal AI by offering advancements in efficient 3D scene understanding for Vision-Language Models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** By proposing compact 3D proxy representations that significantly reduce the sequence length of visual inputs (e.g., from 8000 to 700 tokens), Proxy3D addresses a major computational bottleneck in MLLMs. This efficiency can enable faster training, lower inference costs, and potentially allow for the deployment of more complex MLLMs in resource-constrained environments or for real-time applications.

2. **Improved Spatial Consistency and Reasoning:** The explicit modeling of 3D scenes through semantically-aware clustering and the structured multi-stage training pipeline contribute to more accurate and consistent spatial understanding. This helps MLLMs move beyond localized 2D perspectives towards a more unified global understanding of 3D environments, as evidenced by its strong performance in visual grounding and spatial reasoning benchmarks.

3. **Advancement in 3D-VLM Architecture:** Proxy3D introduces a novel architectural approach that integrates semantic and geometric features from various pre-trained encoders, followed by intelligent compression and serialization. This offers an alternative to both computationally intensive correspondence-based methods and naive representation-based serialization, potentially inspiring new directions in designing efficient 3D-aware MLLMs.

4. **Creation of a Unified Training Resource:** The curation of the 318K SpaceSpan dataset provides a valuable, high-quality resource for the research community. By unifying heterogeneous visual information and focusing on diverse spatial reasoning queries, it facilitates more comprehensive training and evaluation of 3D spatial intelligence in MLLMs, addressing a known limitation in the availability of such data.

5. **Progressive Skill Development in MLLMs:** The multi-stage training pipeline offers a structured methodology for iteratively developing spatial reasoning skills, from basic object referencing to complex spatial relationship understanding. This progressive learning approach could be adopted by other multimodal models to build complex capabilities more effectively and robustly.

6. **Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated robustness of Proxy3D across various 3D scene datasets (ScanNet, ARKitScenes) and its ability to solve difficult spatial reasoning questions suggest its generalizability. This indicates that the learned representations and reasoning capabilities may extend well to unseen or diverse real-world 3D environments.

In summary, Proxy3D provides a practical and effective solution for developing spatial intelligence in MLLMs with greater efficiency. Its contributions have the potential to accelerate research in embodied AI, robotics, and other applications requiring advanced 3D scene understanding and interaction, while also highlighting areas where further research, such as integrating reward learning, can close the remaining gap with human-level performance.
