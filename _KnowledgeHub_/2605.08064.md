# Proxy3D: Efficient 3D Representations for Vision-Language Models via Semantic Clustering and Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Proxy3D introduces an efficient framework for Vision-Language Models to comprehend 3D environments by creating compact, semantically-clustered 3D proxy representations from multi-view images. This method achieves competitive performance on 3D question answering and visual grounding benchmarks while significantly reducing the visual token sequence length, processing 700 tokens compared to 8000 tokens for correspondence-based models.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with human-level spatial intelligence, particularly in effectively reasoning about 3D scenes.
- Correspondence-based methods for 3D understanding in VLMs suffer from spatial inconsistencies, inefficient training data use, and high computational costs due to reliance on long 2D pixel-aligned token sequences.
- Representation-based methods often produce computationally inefficient 3D representations with fixed geometric priors, failing to adequately model complex spatial relationships within a compact token sequence.

## Method

- A **Proxy3D** architecture extracts ==2D visual features==, ==3D point maps==, and ==2D segmentation masks== from multi-view images, then employs ==semantic-aware K-nearest neighbors clustering== to generate compact 3D proxy representations.
- The **SpaceSpan** dataset, a 318K high-quality training set, unifies existing 3D data and incorporates 115K object-object relationship questions to enhance MLLM spatial reasoning capabilities.
- A ==progressive multi-stage training pipeline== (semantic & identifier embedding, coordinate alignment, spatial relationship, proxy representation) is implemented to iteratively develop spatial intelligence and effectively align compact 3D proxies with the language model.

## Results

- **Proxy3D** achieved competitive performance in 3D Question Answering (ScanQA, SQA3D) and demonstrated **state-of-the-art** or second-best results in 3D Visual Grounding (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer) using a substantially shorter visual token sequence length (**~700 vs 8000** tokens).
- The semantic grouping mechanism proved critical for accurate object distinction, leading to over **+20 points** increase in overall accuracy for visual grounding tasks compared to clustering without semantic awareness.
- Ablation studies confirmed that coordinate alignment significantly improves performance in tasks requiring precise positional understanding (e.g., room size, route planning), and dynamic proxy allocation effectively emphasizes objects of interest, particularly smaller ones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic-aware clustering of visual and geometric features is effective in significantly reducing the visual input sequence length for MLLMs (e.g., from 8000 to 700 tokens) without substantial loss in 3D spatial understanding.
> - A multi-stage training regimen that progressively introduces object identifiers, precise coordinate alignment, and explicit object-object spatial relationship understanding is crucial for robust 3D scene interpretation.
> - The fusion of 3D positional embeddings (Rotary for vertical, learnable Fourier for width and length) with visual features effectively injects essential geometric priors into the compact proxy representations.
