# Act, Sense, Act: Learning Non-Markovian Active Perception Strategies from Large-Scale Egocentric Human Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced CoMe-VLA, a framework that enables robots to learn non-Markovian active perception strategies from large-scale egocentric human data. The system integrates cognitive awareness and a dual-track memory to proactively resolve information uncertainty, achieving an 83.3% mean success rate in complex manipulation tasks while significantly reducing reliance on robot-specific demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation methods exhibit perceptual passivity, struggling when crucial information is out of view, occluded, or requires proactive investigation.
- Current active perception approaches often lack a rigorous theoretical framework and fail to leverage complex body movements or physical interaction for information discovery.
- Transferring complex human active perception behaviors to robots is challenging due to the cross-embodiment gap and the need for memory and cognitive awareness in long-horizon, non-Markovian tasks.

## Method

- Formalized active perception as a Non-Markovian Decision Process (NMDP) driven by information gain and decision branching, categorizing it into Information Discovery and Information Enrichment paradigms.
- Developed CoMe-VLA (Cognitive and Memory-aware Vision-Language-Action), an architecture based on a Vision-Language Model augmented with a Cognitive Auxiliary Head for sub-task transitions and a Dual-Track Memory System for historical context.
- Employed a three-stage progressive training pipeline, pretraining on large-scale egocentric human data (CaptainCook4D, Ego-Exo4D) and fine-tuning on robot teleoperation data aligned in a unified egocentric action space.

## Results

- CoMe-VLA achieved a mean Success Rate of 83.3% and a mean Search Time of 97.9s across five long-horizon tasks, substantially outperforming state-of-the-art general-purpose VLAs (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT: 12.7% SR).
- Increasing human data from 400k to 800k samples boosted performance from 72.0% to 87.3% SR, demonstrating that large-scale human data reduces the dependency on extensive robot-specific supervision.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of cognition (removing it reduced SR to 22.7%) and memory (removing it reduced SR to 40.7%), with the dual-track memory significantly outperforming single or partial memory systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active perception is inherently non-Markovian, requiring an explicit memory system and cognitive awareness to handle long-horizon tasks and adaptive decision-making.
> - Large-scale egocentric human data effectively provides dense exploratory priors, drastically reducing the amount of robot-specific data needed for successful transfer of active perception strategies.
> - The dual-track memory (visual and proprioceptive) and a dedicated cognitive auxiliary head are critical for maintaining consistent self- and environmental awareness, enabling autonomous transitions between exploratory and exploitative behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical gap in the field of robotic manipulation, specifically concerning **active perception in unstructured and uncertain environments**. The broader landscape of robotic manipulation has seen significant progress through imitation learning (IL) and foundation models, enabling robots to perform deterministic tasks in structured settings. However, these methods often suffer from "perceptual passivity," where robots react solely to static observations and lack the ability to proactively seek out information or adapt to dynamic changes.

**Active Perception:** Existing active perception methods are often limited. Early approaches relied on heuristic strategies (e.g., next-best-view) that decoupled perception from manipulation, or end-to-end learning methods that treated camera movement as a simple action extension for linear tasks. These methods often fail to utilize complex body movements or physical interaction (e.g., opening a drawer) to resolve occlusions or information uncertainty. This paper distinguishes itself by:
- **Formalizing active perception:** It proposes a theoretical framework that models active perception as a **non-Markovian decision process (NMDP)** driven by information gain and decision branching, moving beyond mere reactive sensing.
- **Systematic categorization:** It introduces a structured taxonomy of visual active perception paradigms (Information Discovery via Viewpoint/Manipulation, Information Enrichment).

**Learning from Human Data:** Leveraging human demonstrations is a major trend in robotics to acquire rich, generalizable priors. While many works focus on mapping hand-centric actions or learning visual priors for scene understanding, this paper emphasizes learning **proactive eye-hand coordination** by incorporating head movements into the action alignment process. This is a crucial distinction, as it treats the camera view not as a passive input stream but as an *intentional result* of active sensing behavior. The use of large-scale egocentric human data (CaptainCook4D, Ego-Exo4D) for pretraining is consistent with the trend of scaling up data for more robust and generalizable robotic policies, but the novel aspect lies in how this data is aligned and utilized to distill active perception strategies.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** The proposed CoMe-VLA framework builds upon advanced VLA models like Qwen3-VL-2B and policy architectures similar to π0 and π0.5. However, it extends these models by integrating specific mechanisms (cognitive auxiliary head and dual-track memory system) to explicitly address the temporal and cognitive demands of non-Markovian active perception tasks. This aims to overcome the "illusory exploration" and "weak visuo-motor grounding" often observed in general-purpose VLAs when applied to complex active perception scenarios. By providing explicit cognitive and memory mechanisms, CoMe-VLA pushes the boundaries of VLA models towards more intelligent, adaptable, and robust robot autonomy in challenging, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to achieve **generalizable manipulation in unconstrained environments** by proactively resolving information uncertainty, a capability defined as **active perception**. The authors identify several key motivations and specific objectives:

1. **Address Perceptual Passivity:** Existing robotic manipulation methods, largely based on imitation learning and foundation models, are often "perceptually passive." They rely on static observations and struggle when crucial information is out of view, occluded, or requires further investigation. The motivation is to move beyond this reactive paradigm to a proactive one where robots intentionally seek information.
2. **Formalize Active Perception:** Current understandings of active perception often lack a rigorous theoretical framework. A key objective is to formalize active perception as a **non-Markovian decision process (NMDP)**, driven by concepts of "information gain" and "decision branching." This formalization provides a structured way to understand and categorize diverse active perception behaviors.
3. **Leverage Large-Scale Human Egocentric Data:** Humans intrinsically perform complex active perception tasks daily, embodying rich priors for exploration and adaptive decision-making. The motivation is to distill these natural human "act-sense-act" strategies from large-scale egocentric human datasets and transfer them to robots.
4. **Develop a Cognitive and Memory-Aware Framework:** Effective long-horizon active perception requires the robot to be aware of past interactions ("I have searched the left") and to estimate task progress ("target has been found, now I should fetch it"). This motivates the development of a framework that integrates **cognitive capabilities** for autonomous sub-task transitions and **memory systems** for maintaining consistent self and environmental awareness over time.
5. **Bridge the Cross-Embodiment Gap:** Transferring skills from human data to robot platforms is challenging due to morphological and kinematic differences. An objective is to align human and robot behaviors within a **unified egocentric action space** to facilitate effective knowledge transfer.
6. **Enable Robust and Adaptive Execution:** The ultimate goal is to enable a robot to perform robust manipulation across diverse, long-horizon active perception scenarios, including those requiring viewpoint changes, physical manipulation to reveal hidden objects, and information enrichment for precise tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **CoMe-VLA (Cognitive and Memory-aware Vision-Language-Action)**, a framework designed to achieve robust active perception. Their methodology involves a theoretical formalization, a novel architecture, specialized data collection, and a multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formalization: Non-Markovian Active Perception

The paper formalizes active perception as a **Non-Markovian Decision Process (NMDP)**. This means that the robot's optimal action at any given time depends not just on the current observation, but also on the history of observations and actions. Two core mechanisms are identified:
- **Information Gain:** Actions are driven by the potential to reduce task-relevant information uncertainty, quantified as mutual information gain between an action chunk and future observation, conditioned on historical context.
- **Decision Branching:** The non-Markovian nature leads to adaptive actions based on realized observations and historical context. This manifests as "exploratory branching" (adapting information-seeking) and "exploitative branching" (adapting manipulation strategies based on resolved perceptual states).
The authors also categorize visual active perception into **Information Discovery (ID)** (Viewpoint Discovery (VD) and Manipulation Discovery (MD)) and **Information Enrichment (IE)**.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Alignment

1. **Large-Scale Egocentric Human Dataset:**
 - **Sources:** CaptainCook4D and Ego-Exo4D are used, selected for fine-grained annotations, synchronized hand/head poses, and scenarios involving cluttered environments and occlusions.
 - **Data Curation:** CaptainCook4D provides high-quality data from HoloLens. Ego-Exo4D, from Aria glasses, requires filtering for reliable hand tracking and MANO-based optimization to recover accurate 6DoF hand poses. A total of 800k human data samples (400k from each) are used.
2. **Egocentric Robot Teleoperation:**
 - **Hardware:** A Corenetic Monte02 wheel-based humanoid robot with high DoF (mobile chassis, head, two 7-DoF arms, grippers) is used. Egocentric RGB camera on the head provides visual observations.
 - **Immersive Teleoperation System:** A VR-based system (Meta Quest 3) streams the robot's egocentric vision to the operator, whose head movements control the robot's head. Hand controllers govern bimanual manipulation and chassis movement. Crucially, operators are *blind* to target locations beforehand, ensuring genuine information-seeking behaviors are captured.
 - **Data Size:** Per-task 100k-400k robot data samples are collected.
3. **Human-Robot Data Alignment:**
 - **Unified Egocentric Action Space:** Data from both human and robot is mapped into a consistent, episode-relative reference frame to mitigate embodiment-specific distribution shifts. All poses are expressed relative to the first frame of each trajectory in a body-centric local frame.
 - **Gripper Mapping:** High-dimensional human hand configurations are abstracted to a single gripper width, normalized to.
 - **Head-Chassis Aggregation:** For the robot, chassis motion and head articulation are aggregated into a composite head pose. During inference, this is decomposed into chassis and head gimbal commands via a threshold-based heuristic.

#### 3.3. CoMe-VLA Model Architecture

CoMe-VLA is a neural approximation of the NMDP, built upon **Qwen3-VL-2B** (a pre-trained Vision-Language Model) and a flow-matching action decoder.
1. **Cognitive Auxiliary Head:** This lightweight head acts as a learnable proxy for information gain. It appends a cognitive token `[COG]` to the input sequence, which queries visual and semantic histories via cross-attention. It predicts a binary cognitive label `c_t` that signals the perceptual resolution of uncertainty (e.g., target found). This label is used during inference to autonomously transition between exploratory and exploitative strategies by switching sub-task instructions.
2. **Dual-Track Memory System:** To address the non-Markovian nature, CoMe-VLA maintains consistent self and environmental awareness.
 - **Visual Track:** Leverages Qwen3-VL's multi-image capacity to encode a temporal window (current + five historical frames at 1-second intervals) of visual observations.
 - **Proprioceptive Track:** A transformer-based encoder processes historical proprioceptive states, capturing motor behavior dynamics.
 - **Integration:** The flow-matching action decoder performs cross-attention over these dual contexts, grounding decisions in the visual scene while refining trajectories with proprioceptive feedback.
3. **Implicit Data-Driven Decisions:** Instead of explicitly modeling decision branching as discrete symbolic choices, CoMe-VLA implicitly learns exploratory and exploitative paths from large-scale human egocentric data, capturing branching as continuous variations in perception-action coupling.
4. **Flow-Matching Action Decoder:** Predicts an action chunk of K future actions (viewpoint position/rotation, bimanual end-effector position/rotation, bimanual gripper states).

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

A three-stage progressive training pipeline is employed:
1. **Stage 1: Cognitive State Pretraining:** Only the VLM parameters (vision projector) and the cognitive auxiliary head are updated using large-scale human data. The objective is to establish foundational understanding of task progress, supervised by focal loss on the cognitive label.
2. **Stage 2: Cognition-Action Joint Pretraining:** All modules are unfrozen to learn active perception and manipulation priors from human data. This stage combines cognitive focal loss with MSE loss for supervising the action decoder's predicted velocities across all action components.
3. **Stage 3: Robot Data Finetuning:** The model is adapted to the robot's real dynamics and environments using collected robot-centric data. Full-model optimization continues with the same loss structure as Stage 2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate CoMe-VLA's superior performance and robustness across diverse active perception tasks.

#### 4.1. Comparative Evaluation (Q1)

- CoMe-VLA significantly outperforms state-of-the-art general-purpose VLAs (OpenVLA-OFT, π0.5) and task-specific policies (ACT, Diffusion Policy) across five challenging long-horizon tasks (Croissant Search, Can Disposal, Bottle Retrieval, Cylinder Hunt, Ring Peg).
- It achieves a mean Success Rate (SR) of **83.3%** and a mean Search Time (ST) of **97.9s**, substantially surpassing baselines (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT: 12.7% SR, 740.1s ST; π0.5: 16.0% SR, 616.0s ST).
- **Illusory Exploration:** Baselines often exhibit "random-walk" behaviors or fixed action biases rather than intentional search, leading to failures or coincidental successes.
- **Weak Visuo-Motor Grounding:** Baselines struggled with hand-eye coordination, sometimes gazing in one direction while acting in another, indicating insufficient integration of visual attention and motor control.

#### 4.2. Data Composition Analysis (Q2)

- **Impact of Human Data:** The introduction of 400k human data significantly boosts CoMe-VLA's performance (from 42.7% SR with 400k robot data only to 72.0% SR with 400k human + 400k robot data).
- **Scaling Trend:** Increasing human data to 800k further enhances performance (87.3% SR).
- **Reduced Robot Data Dependency:** With sufficient human data (800k), the model shows only marginal degradation when robot-specific supervision is reduced (e.g., 83.3% SR with 100k robot data). This indicates that large-scale human data provides dense exploratory priors, significantly reducing the sample complexity required for transfer to robot embodiments.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Q3)

1. **Ablation on Cognition:**
 - Removing cognition-based task decomposition leads to a significant decline in mean SR (from 83.3% to 22.7%) and an increase in mean ST (from 97.9s to 616.4s), highlighting the tension between high-level reasoning and low-level control without cognitive guidance.
 - Removing cognitive state pretraining (Stage 1) also degrades performance noticeably (to 52.0% SR, 311.8s ST), suggesting that establishing cognitive representation *first* is crucial for refining motor commands and preventing interference between cognition and action generation.
2. **Ablation on Memory:**
 - **No Memory:** Removing memory drastically collapses task execution (from 83.3% to 40.7% mean SR), confirming the non-Markovian nature of long-horizon active perception.
 - **Memory Architecture:** Dual-track memory (separate visual and proprioceptive tracks) consistently outperforms single-coupled memory (fused features), visual-only, and proprio-only memory. Visual-only memory sustains cognitive judgments but performs poorly in action, while proprio-only improves motion continuity but weakens cognitive adaptation. This underscores the importance of the structured dual-track design.
 - **Memory Length:** Using five historical frames (plus current) significantly outperforms shorter histories (e.g., 1 frame: 44.6% SR, 518.8s ST; 3 frames: 59.3% SR, 218.2s ST), indicating that richer temporal context improves both perception and action quality while balancing computational feasibility.

#### 4.4. Robustness Analysis (Q4)

- In adversarial tests on the Croissant Search task (target disappearance and relocation), CoMe-VLA demonstrated high robustness.
- It exhibits **persistent stochastic scanning** when the target is absent, avoiding getting trapped in fixed viewpoints.
- Upon target reintroduction into the field of view, the model rapidly achieves **visual locking** and maintains a sustained gaze.
- Seamless transitions between search and gaze modes indicate strong adaptability to high uncertainty and dynamic environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the development of intelligent robotic systems capable of operating in complex, real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Advancement in Active Perception:** By formalizing active perception as an NMDP and proposing a systematic taxonomy, the paper provides a more rigorous theoretical foundation for future research. This moves the field beyond heuristic or reactive sensing towards truly intentional and adaptive information-seeking behaviors.
2. **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** CoMe-VLA enables robots to autonomously resolve information uncertainty through active exploration and manipulation, a critical capability for tasks in unstructured and dynamic settings. This empowers robots to act more intelligently, reducing the need for explicit human intervention in ambiguous situations.
3. **Effective Human-to-Robot Skill Transfer:** The methodology for distilling exploratory priors from large-scale egocentric human data and aligning it with robot action spaces offers a highly effective pathway for transferring complex human skills. This significantly broadens the scope of tasks robots can learn, leveraging the vast repository of human behavior.
4. **Reduced Data Requirements for Robotics:** The finding that human data drastically reduces the amount of robot-specific demonstration data needed for successful task execution has profound practical implications. Collecting robot demonstrations is labor-intensive and expensive; this work offers a method to mitigate that bottleneck, accelerating the deployment of new robotic applications.
5. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The cognitive and memory-aware architecture, coupled with multi-stage training, results in policies that are not only effective but also robust to dynamic perturbations and unexpected environmental changes. This is crucial for real-world reliability and safety.
6. **Paving the Way for More Intelligent VLAs:** CoMe-VLA demonstrates how general-purpose VLA models can be augmented with specialized cognitive and memory mechanisms to tackle specific, complex robotic challenges like active perception. This could inspire further development of VLAs that are more context-aware and goal-driven.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily concerning the memory system's fixed and relatively short temporal window. While effective for current tasks, this design may struggle with much longer-horizon tasks. Future work could explore video streaming inputs and belief-aware memory representations to address this. Additionally, while human data reduces robot-specific data needs, some on-robot tele-op demonstration is still required for embodiment and environment adaptation. Leveraging reinforcement learning to autonomously explore active perception strategies in more complex and unconstrained environments is identified as a promising future direction, potentially further reducing reliance on explicit demonstrations.
