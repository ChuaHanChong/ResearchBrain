# Robotic Manipulation is Vision-to-Geometry Mapping ($f(v) \rightarrow G$): Vision-Geometry Backbones over Language and Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Vision-Geometry-Action (VGA), redefines robotic manipulation as a direct vision-to-geometry mapping using a pretrained 3D world model backbone, achieving a 98.1% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and enhanced zero-shot generalization to novel viewpoints in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation models often rely on 2D-centric vision-language or video backbones, which are misaligned with the 3D geometric nature of physical actions.
- Existing methods struggle with an "information bottleneck" where 3D inputs are projected to 2D representations before being decoded back to 3D actions.
- These models tend to overfit visual patterns rather than learning true 3D dynamics, limiting robust generalization in complex environments.

## Method

- Develop the **VGA** (==Vision-Geometry-Action==) model, utilizing a pretrained ==3D world model== (**VGGT**) as its backbone to enable direct vision-to-geometry mapping.
- Incorporate a ==Progressive Volumetric Modulation (PVM)== module for structured integration of 3D representations into the action head.
- Implement a ==joint training strategy== to predict actions and auxiliary 3D properties (camera parameters, depth maps) from shared 3D representations.

## Results

- The VGA model achieved a **98.1%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming 2D-centric VLA, 3D-VLA, and World Action Model baselines.
- Exhibited enhanced zero-shot generalization, achieving a **6%** higher success rate than leading baselines for out-of-distribution camera viewpoints in real-world scenarios.
- Ablation studies validated the contributions of Progressive Volumetric Modulation (PVM) and joint training, confirming their roles in improving geometric information flow and representation fidelity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic manipulation is fundamentally a vision-to-geometry mapping, best addressed by a native 3D representation backbone rather than 2D-centric priors.
> - A direct 3D reasoning pipeline, facilitated by components like Progressive Volumetric Modulation (PVM), bypasses the limitations of processing 3D data through 2D-centric latent spaces.
> - Pretrained 3D world models provide essential high-quality 3D representations, and parameter-efficient tuning methods are vital for maintaining their integrity during adaptation to control tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has increasingly focused on developing generalizable control policies. Recent advancements in generative AI have led to the dominance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and video-predictive policies, often referred to as World Action Models (WAMs). These approaches typically leverage backbones pretrained on large-scale internet-scale 2D image-text data or temporal pixel data. While effective in interpreting linguistic semantics and anticipating temporal sequences, their representations are primarily shaped by 2D priors, such as semantic concepts or temporal pixel changes.

The paper argues that this reliance on 2D-centric backbones introduces a fundamental discrepancy with the inherently 3D nature of physical manipulation. Robotic actions, such as reaching, grasping, and orienting, are defined by precise 3D geometric properties (positions, rotations, spatial relationships). The 2D priors of VLA and video models, while providing an implicit proxy for physics through temporal prediction, may lead to overfitting visual patterns rather than capturing true 3D dynamics, thereby limiting robust generalization in complex environments.

Previous attempts to incorporate explicit 3D geometric information into these frameworks have included using 3D inputs like depth maps or point clouds (which introduce noise, complexity, and hardware costs) or prepending 3D-aware encoders to VLM backbones. However, the authors point out that even with 3D inputs, the core reasoning in these approaches remains bottlenecked by 2D-centric VLM latents, creating a "3D-2D-3D transformation loop."

This work positions itself by proposing a paradigm shift: treating robotic manipulation fundamentally as a direct vision-to-geometry mapping (𝑓 (𝑣) → 𝐺). It aims to build a robotic foundation model where perception, reasoning, and action are physically aligned within a shared native 3D representation space, directly addressing the limitations of 2D-centric approaches. This involves replacing conventional language or video backbones with a pretrained 3D world model, offering an alternative foundation for achieving generalizable physical intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a physically grounded foundation for generalizable robotic control by formalizing robotic manipulation as a direct vision-to-geometry mapping (𝑓 (𝑣) → 𝐺). The authors contend that physical actions are intrinsically defined by 3D geometric properties and spatial relationships, which are not adequately captured by current mainstream Vision-Language-Action (VLA) or video-predictive models.

The motivation for this work arises from several identified limitations of existing approaches:
- **2D-centric Representations:** Current VLA and video models rely on backbones pretrained on large-scale 2D image-text or temporal pixel data. Their representations are largely shaped by semantic concepts or 2D priors, leading to a mismatch with the precise 3D geometric nature required for physical manipulation. This can result in models overfitting visual patterns rather than learning true 3D dynamics.
- **Representation Bottleneck:** Even when 3D inputs (e.g., depth maps, point clouds) or 3D-aware encoders are introduced into VLA frameworks, the core reasoning often remains bottlenecked by the downstream VLM's 2D-centric latent space. This creates an inefficient "3D-2D-3D transformation loop" where rich 3D information is projected into a flat 2D representation before being decoded back into 3D actions.
- **Lack of Native 3D Alignment:** While World Action Models (WAMs) aim to capture physical dynamics by predicting future frames and actions, their backbones remain fundamentally locked in 2D pixel space, optimizing for temporal changes rather than structural 3D reality. The authors argue for a more direct modeling of geometric properties alongside actions.

Driven by these insights, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- **Develop a Vision-Geometry-Action (VGA) Model:** This involves proposing a novel architecture that directly conditions action generation on pretrained native 3D representations. The core idea is to replace conventional 2D language or video backbones with a pretrained 3D world model, such as VGGT, to establish a seamless vision-to-geometry mapping.
- **Enhance Geometric Consistency:** Introduce a Progressive Volumetric Modulation (PVM) module to facilitate a high-fidelity flow of geometric information from the 3D backbone to the action decoding heads.
- **Integrate Joint Training:** Adopt a joint training strategy that simultaneously predicts robotic actions and auxiliary 3D properties (e.g., camera parameters, depth maps) from shared 3D representations. This is intended to improve both representation fidelity and cross-modal interaction.
- **Demonstrate Superiority in Spatial Manipulation:** Conduct extensive experiments in simulation to validate VGA's performance against existing VLA baselines, particularly in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning.
- **Exhibit Robust Zero-Shot Generalization:** Evaluate VGA's ability to generalize to unseen viewpoints in real-world deployments, demonstrating that a native 3D paradigm can effectively bridge the perception-action gap and ensure stability under observational variations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Vision-Geometry-Action (VGA) model is designed to leverage a pretrained 3D world model as its backbone, enabling a direct vision-to-geometry mapping for robotic control.

#### 3.1. Model Overview

At each timestep, VGA receives multi-view RGB observations, a language instruction, and robot proprioception. These inputs are processed by a pretrained VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) backbone to produce native 3D representations. These representations are then fed into decoupled decoding heads to simultaneously predict a chunk of robotic actions and auxiliary 3D properties, specifically camera parameters and depth maps.

#### 3.2. Native 3D Representation

The process begins with embedding heterogeneous modalities:
- **Multi-view RGB Observations:** Each image view is tokenized by a DINO encoder, generating patch tokens flattened into visual embeddings.
- **Robot Proprioception:** Projected into a d-dimensional embedding via a multi-layer perceptron (MLP).
- **Language Instruction:** Encoded using Qwen-GTE to capture linguistic context.
- **Learnable Tokens:** Action queries are introduced to aggregate manipulation context, and camera tokens capture camera-specific context.
All these embeddings are concatenated into a unified token sequence. This sequence is then processed by the VGGT transformer backbone, which consists of 12 layers implementing an Alternating-Attention mechanism. Even layers apply frame-wise local attention within each modality to capture spatial details, while odd layers apply cross-modal global attention over the entire token sequence to integrate information across perspectives and modalities. This process yields unified 3D representations that encode scene geometry and task-relevant semantics.

#### 3.3. Joint Training and Decoupled Inference

VGA adopts a joint training paradigm, where the shared 3D representations are decoded into multiple modalities through task-specific heads:
- **Action Head:** A regression transformer with 12 layers, following OpenVLA-OFT. It takes learnable noise embeddings, conditioned on the 3D representations via the Progressive Volumetric Modulation (PVM) module, and processes them to predict a chunk of robotic actions (chunk size C=8).
- **Auxiliary Heads:**
 - **Camera Head:** Regresses 9-dimensional camera parameters (rotation, translation, focal length) for each view from evolved camera tokens, using a refinement module and linear projection.
 - **Depth Head:** Employs a Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT) module to reconstruct pixel-wise depth maps by hierarchically aggregating multi-scale backbone tokens.
The model is optimized using a multi-task objective: action loss (regression), camera loss (Huber loss), and depth loss (aleatoric-uncertainty-weighted with a gradient term).

For real-world deployment, a decoupled inference strategy is used to reduce latency. The camera and depth heads are bypassed, allowing the model to focus solely on high-frequency action decoding from the shared 3D representations, preserving the benefits of joint training without its computational overhead during execution.

#### 3.4. Progressive Volumetric Modulation (PVM)

PVM is introduced to effectively integrate the structured 3D representations (V_t) into the action head. Instead of a standard cross-attention mechanism, PVM enables a more structured and progressive interaction. For each layer, it processes three feature sets: vision-language condition, action queries condition, and the decoder latent embedding. The decoder latent acts as a query to extract action-relevant context from the action queries, followed by refinement against the spatio-linguistic manifold. The resulting modulated feature is then adaptively aligned with the raw updated decoder state by concatenation and projection, ensuring a high-fidelity flow of geometric information into the action generation process across all layers.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The backbone and action head both use 12 transformer layers. Parameter-efficient tuning is achieved using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) with a rank of 64, applied to linear layers within transformer blocks. This strategy limits trainable parameters to approximately 500M. Training is conducted on NVIDIA A100 GPUs, with runs completing within 60 GPU hours.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from extensive experiments in both simulated (LIBERO benchmark) and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (LIBERO Benchmark)

The model, VGA, demonstrated performance in various manipulation tasks.
- **Quantitative Comparison (Q1):** VGA achieved an average success rate of 98.1% on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming several categories of baselines:
 - **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Baselines:** VGA surpassed top-tier VLA models like π0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT by 1.2% and 1.0% respectively in absolute success rate. This was attributed to the VGGT backbone's 3D representational priors, facilitating a direct mapping from geometry reasoning to spatial actions.
 - **3D Vision-Language-Action (3D-VLA) Baselines:** VGA consistently outperformed 3D-VLA variants, including SpatialVLA (by 20.0%), GeoAware (by 1.3%), and GeoVLA (by 0.4%). This suggests that merely augmenting VLA pipelines with 3D information may be limited by the 2D-centric priors of VLM backbones, while VGA’s native 3D backbone avoids this bottleneck.
 - **World Action Model (WAM) Baselines:** VGA achieved better performance than WAMs, indicating the effectiveness of pretrained 3D world models as backbones compared to video generation models.
- **Task Suite Performance:** VGA showed strong results on LIBERO-Spatial, Object, and Goal suites, emphasizing its effectiveness in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning. However, it exhibited a slight lag in the LIBERO-Long suite compared to some baselines, which the authors attributed to VGGT's geometric focus contrasting with the sequential data pretraining of VLM and WAM backbones.
- **Quality of Auxiliary 3D Prediction (Q2):** Qualitative analysis revealed that VGA produced accurate depth estimates, particularly for manipulation target objects, indicating strong geometric understanding retained within the learned 3D representations.
- **Ablation Study (Q3):**
 - **Progressive Volumetric Modulation (PVM):** Removing the PVM module resulted in a 2.4% performance drop, confirming its role in integrating 3D priors.
 - **Joint Training Strategy:** Eliminating joint training (i.e., training only with action supervision) led to a 0.9% drop, suggesting that while the pretrained VGGT backbone is the primary source of performance, joint training further refines shared 3D representations.
 - **Training Strategy (LoRA vs. Full-parameter):** Switching from LoRA to full-parameter tuning with pretrained initialization caused a significant 11% drop, indicating that LoRA effectively preserves the VGGT backbone's native 3D spatial representations.
 - **Pretrained Backbone Weights:** Random initialization of the backbone led to a substantial degradation (98.1% to 86.6% with full-parameter training, and collapse to 6.4% with LoRA), underscoring the critical importance of the high-quality 3D representations from the pretrained VGGT.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Experiments on a Franka Panda robotic arm evaluated VGA on Pick Cube, Press Button, and Stack Cube tasks under in-distribution and zero-shot out-of-distribution camera viewpoints, as well as language-grounded grasping.
- **In-Distribution Evaluation (Q4):** VGA consistently outperformed ACT and OpenVLA, with over 35% and 50% absolute gains in average success rate, respectively. It achieved competitive results with π0.5, matching its performance on Press Button and Stack Cube, and showing a small gap on Pick Cube. This indicated the effectiveness of the 3D world model design for real-world execution.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization (Zero-Shot Cross-View) (Q5):** VGA demonstrated notable zero-shot generalization to unseen camera viewpoints. It surpassed π0.5 by 6% in average success rate (58% vs. 52%), while ACT and OpenVLA performed poorly (7% and 3%). This suggests that VGA's structured 3D representations enable robust action generation even under significant observational variations.
- **Language-Grounded Manipulation (Q6):** In a challenging scenario involving grasping visually similar objects (cucumber, carrot, eggplant) with varying layouts, VGA consistently followed language commands to pick the correct object. This highlights its ability to robustly ground language instructions to visual targets, supported by the Qwen-GTE language encoder.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by formally defining and implementing robotic control as a vision-to-geometry mapping (𝑓 (𝑣) → 𝐺). This perspective offers a structured departure from the current dominance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and video-predictive models, which are often limited by their 2D-centric pretraining.

The significance of the Vision-Geometry-Action (VGA) model lies in several key aspects:
- **Paradigm Shift:** By advocating for a native 3D vision-geometry backbone (VGGT) over conventional language or video models, the research suggests a fundamental re-evaluation of how robotic perception and action should be structured. This geometry-first design is argued to better align with the intrinsic 3D nature of physical manipulation.
- **Enhanced Precision and Robustness:** The empirical results demonstrate that operating on native 3D representations enables more precise spatial manipulation in simulation and robust zero-shot generalization to unseen viewpoints in real-world environments. This suggests that explicitly modeling 3D geometry can lead to more stable and reliable robotic systems.
- **Overcoming the 2D Bottleneck:** VGA's architecture, including the Progressive Volumetric Modulation (PVM) module and joint training strategy, is designed to entirely bypass the "3D-2D-3D information bottleneck" inherent in 3D-aware VLA approaches that still rely on 2D-pretrained backbones. This highlights a potential path toward more efficient and effective utilization of 3D information.
- **Generalizable Physical Intelligence:** The consistent outperformance of VGA against strong baselines, particularly in spatial reasoning tasks and cross-view generalization, supports the hypothesis that physically grounded perception within a shared native 3D representation space is a promising direction for achieving true, generalizable physical intelligence in robots.

The potential impact of this research is manifold:
- **Advancement in Robotic Control:** The proposed framework could lead to more performant and robust robotic manipulators, especially in environments where precise 3D spatial understanding and adaptability to varied viewpoints are critical.
- **Influence on Foundation Model Design:** It challenges the prevailing trend of exclusively using large language models or video models as backbones for robotics, potentially inspiring the development of more specialized 3D geometric foundation models for embodied AI.
- **Real-world Applications:** Improved zero-shot generalization abilities could reduce the need for extensive data collection and fine-tuning in novel real-world settings, accelerating the deployment of robotic systems in diverse industrial, domestic, and assistive roles.

A recognized limitation is the model's current capacity for tasks requiring extensive commonsense knowledge or abstract language reasoning, which typically leverage large language models. Future work may explore integrating external reasoning modules to address this, without compromising the core 3D geometric foundation. Overall, this paper provides a compelling argument and an effective architecture for prioritizing 3D geometry in the design of robotic manipulation systems.
