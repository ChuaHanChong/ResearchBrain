# NavVerse: Benchmarking Indoor-to-Outdoor Embodied Navigation in Continuous Robot Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> NAVVERSE introduces a physics-enabled, continuous robot simulation benchmark for indoor-to-outdoor embodied navigation, integrating diverse scenes and novel tasks to evaluate agents' ability to traverse varied environments seamlessly. It exposes the challenges of cross-context transitions and the need for kinodynamically aware and safety-conscious navigation policies, with current baselines showing low success rates in hybrid scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI benchmarks predominantly separate indoor and outdoor navigation, neglecting the complexities and unique challenges of continuous transitions between these environments within a single episode.
- Many current simulation platforms simplify robot execution through discrete actions or lightweight dynamics, abstracting away real-world physical interactions, kinodynamic failures, and safety considerations.
- There is a lack of long-horizon, semantic navigation tasks that span large geographical areas and require reasoning over place-level semantics in urban settings.

## Method

- **NAVVERSE** is built on Isaac Sim, providing 200 diverse scenes (indoor, outdoor, and hybrid indoor-to-outdoor) with ==physics-aware terrains== and photorealistic asset population.
- The benchmark defines three navigation tasks: ==Object Navigation (ObjNav)==, ==Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN)==, and a novel long-horizon ==Place Navigation (PlaceNav)== requiring agents to find semantic points of interest.
- It implements ==continuous robot execution== with a shared low-level control stack, supports multiple action interfaces, and evaluates performance using comprehensive metrics including task completion, exploration efficiency, and safety (Collision Rate, Navigable Surface Ratio).

## Results

- Zero-shot task completion performance for all baselines remained low in hybrid scenarios (e.g., UniNaVid VLN SR of **10.67%**), indicating that physically grounded cross-context navigation is largely unsolved.
- PlaceNav showed the most severe performance degradation during indoor-to-outdoor transitions, with UniNaVid's success rate dropping by **14.01%** compared to outdoor-only scenes.
- A substantial proportion of indoor-to-outdoor episodes failed due to agents not reaching the outdoor environment (e.g., **48.42%** for UniNaVid ObjNav), highlighting the bottleneck of exit finding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'transition gap' from indoor to outdoor environments presents significant challenges, with a high proportion of failures occurring before agents successfully exit buildings or adapt post-transition.
> - Current navigation methods exhibit specialized strengths (e.g., task success vs. safety) rather than holistic competence, indicating a need for agents capable of balancing multiple objectives simultaneously.
> - Embodiment dynamics are crucial; a legged robot (Spot) successfully completed oracle paths under varying friction, while a wheeled robot (Turtlebot) showed significantly lower success rates, underscoring the necessity of physics-enabled evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robot navigation has seen substantial progress, particularly within structured indoor environments. Existing benchmarks like MP3D, HM3D, ProcTHOR, and RoboTHOR provide diverse indoor scenes for tasks such as Object Navigation (ObjNav) and Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN). Simultaneously, outdoor navigation research often utilizes datasets like KITTI, nuScenes, and TouchDown, typically relying on offline, non-interactive street-view imagery or pre-recorded trajectories.

A significant gap exists in the evaluation of robot navigation capabilities that span seamlessly between indoor and outdoor environments within a single, continuous episode. Current benchmarks predominantly treat indoor and outdoor navigation as separate problems, neglecting the complexities of the transition zone. This separation limits the study of challenges specific to these hybrid scenarios, such as exit finding, boundary traversal (e.g., curbs, steps), adaptation to drastic shifts in visual appearance and scale, and the implications of kinodynamic constraints in continuous, physics-enabled execution across heterogeneous terrains. Many existing platforms also simplify robot execution through discrete actions or lightweight dynamics, abstracting away real-world physical interactions, potential kinodynamic failures, and safety considerations.

NAVVERSE addresses this gap by providing a unified benchmark that explicitly focuses on indoor-to-outdoor navigation in a physics-enabled, continuous simulation environment. It integrates diverse scenes and tasks, emphasizing the physical viability and safety of robot execution in cross-context settings, thereby moving towards more realistic and holistic evaluations for general-purpose mobile robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce NAVVERSE, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate embodied navigation agents in continuous, physics-enabled simulation environments that encompass indoor, outdoor, and hybrid indoor-to-outdoor scenarios.

The motivation for developing NAVVERSE stems from several practical and research-oriented needs:

- **Real-world Application Requirements:** Robots deployed in various real-world scenarios, such as last-mile delivery, campus operations, and emergency response, frequently need to navigate from inside buildings to outdoor streets within a single, uninterrupted operational sequence. Current navigation systems and benchmarks are limited in their ability to support and evaluate this continuous indoor-to-outdoor traversal.
- **Underexplored "Transition Gap":** Existing benchmarks largely separate indoor and outdoor navigation, overlooking the unique challenges posed by the transition between these environments. This "transition gap" involves complex sub-problems such as identifying and traversing building exits, adapting to significant changes in environmental scale and visual appearance (e.g., lighting, structure), and maintaining robust physical interaction while encountering varied terrain and obstacles (e.g., curbs).
- **Limitations of Existing Simulation Paradigms:**
 - **Abstraction of Robot Execution:** Many benchmarks abstract away the physical execution of robots, simplifying actions to discrete steps or relying on ideal kinematics. This neglects critical aspects like kinodynamic failures, collisions, and unsafe behaviors that are inherent to real-world robot deployment.
 - **Lack of Physics Fidelity:** Simulators often prioritize either visual fidelity or physics fidelity, rarely combining both at scale. This leads to evaluations that may not accurately reflect the physical viability of navigation policies.
 - **Absence of Safety Metrics:** Current evaluation metrics often focus solely on task success and path efficiency, without adequately quantifying safety-aware execution, which is crucial for robot deployment in human environments.
- **Need for Novel Long-Horizon Tasks:** Standard Object Navigation (ObjNav) and Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks may not fully capture the complexity of real-world goal-oriented navigation that spans large geographical areas and requires reasoning over place-level semantics (e.g., finding a restaurant in a city).

To address these motivations, NAVVERSE aims to:
- Integrate residential, commercial, and urban outdoor scenes with connected hybrid indoor-to-outdoor layouts.
- Implement continuous robot execution under realistic physics, unifying multiple action interfaces and supporting different robot embodiments.
- Introduce a rich spectrum of navigation tasks, including ObjNav, VLN, and a novel, long-horizon Place Navigation (PlaceNav) task that requires agents to locate semantic points of interest in urban settings.
- Provide comprehensive evaluation metrics covering task success, path efficiency, and safety-aware execution to diagnose physical instability and transition failures.
- Facilitate the development of navigation agents capable of robust cross-context adaptation, kinodynamic-aware decision-making, and safe physical interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NAVVERSE is constructed upon the Isaac Sim platform, which provides a physics-enabled simulation environment with GPU-accelerated visual rendering and continuous control capabilities. The benchmark's construction involves three main components: scene generation, task definition, and episode generation with evaluation protocols.

#### 3.1. Scene Generation

NAVVERSE creates a diverse set of 200 scenes, split into training and evaluation sets with no scene overlap to ensure generalization assessment.

- **Indoor Scenes:** 100 scenes from GRUtopia are utilized, encompassing apartments and commercial spaces like groceries and offices. These scenes are designed with varied object categories and cluttered layouts to challenge semantic reasoning and collision avoidance. Geometry preprocessing, including decimation, dissolving, and remeshing in Blender, is applied to reduce complexity and accelerate simulation.
- **Outdoor Scenes:** 50 scenes derived from Virtual Community are enhanced for realism and physics-awareness.
 - **Real-to-sim urban layouts and physics-aware terrains:** Google 3D Tiles geometry and map-aligned urban structures are preserved. Terrain is segmented into material classes (e.g., roads, sidewalks, green areas), and geometry is refined with realistic elevation changes (e.g., road depressions of 0.2 m) to enable kinodynamic constraints and surface-dependent traversability.
 - **Rich object and photorealistic storefronts population:** Layouts are populated with objects (up to 200 cars and 1,000 sidewalk objects per scene) sampled from 121 Objaverse categories. Contextual object groups (e.g., bike racks with bicycles) are placed based on semantic region types. 101 diverse storefront assets (restaurants, cafes, banks) are generated and placed at map-aligned building frontages using depth-based extrusion and ray-casting.
- **Indoor-to-Outdoor Scenes:** 50 hybrid scenes are created by integrating indoor layouts into buildings within the outdoor world.
 - **Door-to-facade scene assembly:** A road-facing building is identified, a facade opening is created, and a compatible indoor layout is placed behind it. The indoor entrance is aligned with the facade opening, and blocking meshes are removed to connect the spaces.
 - **Consistent physical execution:** Both indoor and outdoor components share the same robot-execution protocol, physics setup, sensing, and control stack throughout an episode, facilitating seamless transitions.

#### 3.2. Task Definition

NAVVERSE defines three types of navigation tasks across the generated scenes:

- **Object Navigation (ObjNav):** Agents are required to locate and approach specific object categories. This task spans fine-grained object recognition indoors to landmark identification over larger distances outdoors.
- **Place Navigation (PlaceNav):** A novel long-horizon task where agents must reach functional destinations (e.g., restaurants, banks) within the urban layout. This requires reasoning over road topology, building frontages, storefront semantics, and long-range visibility cues.
- **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN):** Agents follow natural language instructions to reach a goal, evaluating their ability to ground linguistic cues in visual observations and execute complex trajectories.

#### 3.3. Benchmark and Evaluation

- **Episode Generation:** 10,000 episodes are generated through a three-step process: (i) offline sampling of start-to-goal paths on a navigation mesh (NavMesh) respecting traversability constraints (max walkable slope, max climb), (ii) online filtering via full physics robot execution to verify path feasibility and record data, and (iii) human validation, including VLM-based instruction generation for VLN and manual quality control. Episodes require oracle success, specific duration (5-100s), and traveled distance (5-50m).
- **Evaluation Setup:** Experiments utilize a simulated Boston Dynamics Spot robot with an RGB-D sensor (640x480, 10 FPS). Baselines' outputs (discrete actions or waypoints) are converted to the NAVVERSE interface and executed by a shared low-level stack running at 50 Hz, which includes a waypoint follower and a PID controller. A learned locomotion policy handles diverse terrains. The benchmark supports additional robot embodiments (e.g., Turtlebot for comparison).
- **Action Space:** Three action types are supported: waypoint paths, discrete actions (converted to single-step waypoints, e.g., forward 0.25-1.0m, rotate +/-15-30 degrees), and body-frame velocity commands.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Metrics are reported along three axes:
 - **Task Completion:** Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL). A geometric goal tolerance of 1.0 + r\_robot meters (1.6m for Spot) is used.
 - **Exploration Efficiency:** Coverage Efficiency (CE), which measures newly covered space per unit travel distance, relevant for large indoor-to-outdoor scenes.
 - **Safety:** Collision Rate (CR), Average Distance to Obstacles (ADO), and Navigable Surface Ratio (NSR), which assesses if the robot remains on valid or kinodynamically preferred surfaces.
- **Baselines:** Four zero-shot navigation baselines are evaluated: SGImagineNav (modular, map-based), PoliFormer (transformer-based RL policy), UniNaVid (end-to-end Visual-Language-Action), and LongNav-R1 (VLA-RL).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the four representative baselines on NAVVERSE revealed several insights regarding cross-context navigation challenges:

- **Zero-Shot Task Completion Performance:** UniNaVid (VLA model) generally achieved the highest zero-shot task-completion performance across ObjNav and PlaceNav. However, the absolute success rates for all baselines remained low (e.g., UniNaVid VLN SR of 10.67%), indicating that physically grounded cross-context navigation is not solved by current methods.
- **Divergent Strengths in Navigation Behavior:** The baselines exhibited specialized strengths rather than holistic competence. UniNaVid led in task success (SR) and search efficiency (SPL, CE). LongNav-R1 demonstrated higher average distance to obstacles (ADO), suggesting a focus on obstacle clearance. SGImagineNav achieved a lower collision rate (CR) and higher navigable surface ratio (NSR), indicating a stronger local interaction safety profile. This divergence suggests that current architectures are insufficient for simultaneously balancing success, efficiency, and safety.
- **Pronounced Challenges in PlaceNav Indoor-to-Outdoor Transitions:** PlaceNav presented the most significant performance degradation during indoor-to-outdoor transitions. UniNaVid's success rate in PlaceNav dropped by an absolute 14.01% (from 17.65% in outdoor to 3.64% in indoor-to-outdoor scenes), a more severe decline compared to ObjNav (8.91% drop) and VLN (6.67% drop). This suggests that adapting to distinct topological reasoning and point-of-interest searching between indoor and outdoor layouts poses a major bottleneck for PlaceNav.
- **Indoor-to-Outdoor Transition Gap Analysis:**
 - **Exit-Finding Bottleneck:** A substantial proportion of indoor-to-outdoor episodes failed before agents reached the outdoor environment (e.g., 48.42% for UniNaVid ObjNav and 37.33% for UniNaVid VLN were "Not Reached Outdoor"). This highlights the difficulty of identifying and traversing building exits, a failure mode not captured by separate indoor or outdoor evaluations.
 - **Post-Exit Efficiency Drop:** All evaluated methods showed a reduction in Coverage Efficiency after successfully exiting the indoor area. Agents often engaged in inefficient behaviors, such as persistent in-place rotations or back-and-forth movements, post-transition. This indicates limited adaptation to the change in scene scale and topology, failing to leverage the more open outdoor spaces effectively.
 - **Method-Specific Post-Exit Failures:** Different baselines exhibited distinct failure patterns after reaching outdoor space. PoliFormer and SGImagineNav primarily timed out, suggesting difficulties in long-horizon exploration. LongNav-R1 frequently terminated due to navigating to a "wrong goal," indicating issues with goal grounding. UniNaVid showed a mixed pattern of wrong-goal failures and timeouts. These results underscore that simply reaching the outdoor area does not guarantee successful goal attainment; adaptation of exploration strategies and robust goal grounding are still required.
- **Impact of Episode Difficulty:** As path lengths increased (from "Easy" to "Hard" difficulty levels), the success rates for all baselines consistently decreased across ObjNav, PlaceNav, and VLN. This suggests that long-horizon navigation in NAVVERSE is affected by compounding errors related to exploration, memory, terrain selection, instruction grounding, and closed-loop control, beyond just initial goal recognition.
- **Significance of Embodiment Dynamics:** A diagnostic analysis using oracle trajectories demonstrated that embodiment dynamics play a crucial role. A legged robot (Spot) successfully completed oracle paths under varying friction, while a wheeled robot (Turtlebot) showed significantly lower success rates and velocities. This finding supports the necessity of physics-enabled evaluation to prevent overestimation of kinodynamic executability when deploying policies on real robots.
- **Influence of Instruction Type (PlaceNav):** For PlaceNav, route-style VLN instructions modestly improved path efficiency (SPL) compared to "Store Category" prompts, particularly in outdoor episodes. However, VLN instructions did not improve indoor-to-outdoor success rates, which remained low. "Intention Driven" instructions, requiring an additional intent-to-place grounding step, further reduced overall and indoor-to-outdoor success rates. This indicates that while detailed route descriptions can aid efficiency, they do not resolve the fundamental challenges of exit finding or post-transition adaptation in hybrid environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NAVVERSE represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robot navigation by directly addressing a critical limitation in existing benchmarks: the lack of comprehensive evaluation for continuous, physics-enabled navigation that spans both indoor and outdoor environments.

The potential impact of this work includes:

- **Enabling Realistic Robot Deployment:** By focusing on the indoor-to-outdoor transition, NAVVERSE provides a realistic testing ground for robots intended for real-world applications such as last-mile delivery, campus security, and emergency response, where seamless cross-context navigation is a fundamental requirement. It moves research closer to real-world deployment challenges by incorporating continuous execution and full physics.
- **Identifying and Diagnosing Critical Failure Modes:** The benchmark explicitly exposes previously underexplored challenges, such as exit finding, adaptation to drastic shifts in visual appearance and scale, and kinodynamic failures arising from heterogeneous terrains and continuous physical interaction. The detailed diagnostic metrics (e.g., CR, ADO, NSR) and failure analyses provide a granular understanding of where current navigation policies struggle, offering clear targets for future research and model development.
- **Driving Development of Holistic Navigation Agents:** The findings highlight that current navigation baselines exhibit specialized capabilities (e.g., high success vs. high safety) rather than a holistic balance of task completion, efficiency, and safety. NAVVERSE motivates the development of next-generation navigation models capable of simultaneously optimizing across these critical dimensions, which are non-negotiable for safe and effective robot operation.
- **Introducing Novel and Challenging Tasks:** The introduction of PlaceNav provides a novel, long-horizon semantic navigation task that bridges the gap between room-scale and city-scale navigation. This task encourages the development of agents capable of reasoning over complex urban topologies and semantic points of interest, enriching the scope of embodied navigation research.
- **Providing a Robust and Scalable Research Platform:** Built on Isaac Sim, NAVVERSE offers a photorealistic and physics-aware simulation platform that is scalable for scene and task generation. The benchmark's structured episode generation and validation pipeline ensures high-quality, executable episodes, providing a reliable foundation for comparative studies and advancements.
- **Influencing Future Benchmark Design:** The methodology and insights from NAVVERSE could influence the design of future embodied AI benchmarks, encouraging the inclusion of more complex environmental transitions, physics-enabled execution, safety-oriented metrics, and long-horizon, semantic tasks to better reflect the complexities of real-world robot operation.
