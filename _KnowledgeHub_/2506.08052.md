# ReCogDrive: A Reinforced Cognitive Framework for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Xiaomi EV developed ReCogDrive, an end-to-end autonomous driving system that integrates a cognition-guided Vision-Language Model with a reinforcement learning-enhanced diffusion planner. This framework achieved a new state-of-the-art Predictive Driver Model Score of 90.8 on the NAVSIM navtest benchmark using only camera input and the highest scenario success rate of 45.45% on Bench2Drive.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based autonomous driving systems suffer from a domain discrepancy, as VLMs are pre-trained on generic internet data lacking specialized driving knowledge.
- The modality mismatch between discrete language outputs from VLMs and the continuous action space of trajectory planning leads to physically infeasible trajectories, parsing errors, and slow inference.
- Reliance on imitation learning in current systems results in suboptimal policies and poor generalization to complex, rare "long-tail" driving scenarios.

## Method

- A scalable ==hierarchical data pipeline==, including generation, refinement, and quality control stages, was developed to instill human-like driving cognition into the VLM.
- A cognition-guided diffusion planner translates VLM's high-level understanding into continuous, stable driving trajectories, leveraging VLM cognitive tokens as cross-attention conditions.
- Diffusion Group Relative Policy Optimization (DiffGRPO), a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, was introduced to refine the diffusion planner in a realistic simulator for enhanced safety and comfort, moving beyond imitation learning limitations.

## Results

- ReCogDrive achieved a new state-of-the-art Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 90.8 on the NAVSIM navtest benchmark, surpassing previous methods including those using LiDAR.
- The system demonstrated the highest scenario success rate of **45.45%** and a top Driving Score of 71.36 on the CARLA-based Bench2Drive leaderboard, indicating robust performance in safety-critical situations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each proposed component, including hierarchical data, the diffusion planner, and DiffGRPO, incrementally improved the PDMS, validating their individual contributions to the overall framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematic pre-training with hierarchical driving-specific data effectively bridges the domain gap between general VLMs and autonomous driving, improving performance by 1.7 PDMS.
> - Diffusion models are significantly more efficient (7.8x faster) and reliable for continuous trajectory generation compared to autoregressive text-based methods, eliminating format errors.
> - Reinforcement learning via DiffGRPO, utilizing a realistic reward system from a simulator, is crucial for optimizing driving safety and comfort, yielding a 4.3 PDMS improvement over a behavior cloning baseline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving has witnessed remarkable progress, with end-to-end systems increasingly unifying perception, prediction, and planning into a single optimization pipeline. Despite impressive performance in open-loop evaluations, these systems often struggle with "long-tail scenarios"—rare, complex driving conditions that are underrepresented in training data. This generalization gap is a significant hurdle for widespread autonomous vehicle deployment.

To address this, the research community has increasingly turned to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and large-scale pre-trained models. VLMs, trained on vast internet datasets, exhibit strong generalization abilities and rich world knowledge, making them attractive candidates for enhancing autonomous driving intelligence. Current VLM applications in autonomous driving broadly fall into two categories:
- **Dual-system approaches** (e.g., DriveVLM, Senna) where VLMs generate high-level commands or low-frequency trajectories to guide a conventional end-to-end driving system.
- **Single-system approaches** (e.g., GPT-Driver, EMMA) where standalone VLMs directly predict future trajectories, typically by formulating motion planning as a language modeling task. These methods often leverage Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for enhanced interpretability.

However, the authors identify three critical limitations with existing VLM-based autonomous driving systems, particularly single-system approaches that directly generate trajectories as text:
1. **Domain discrepancy:** VLMs are pre-trained on generic internet data, which lacks the nuanced knowledge crucial for safe and efficient driving.
2. **Modality mismatch:** The discrete nature of language space conflicts with the continuous action space required for trajectory planning, leading to issues like physically infeasible trajectories, format-violating outputs, parsing errors, and slow inference speeds.
3. **Suboptimal policies:** Heavy reliance on imitation learning (behavior cloning) can lead models to converge to suboptimal policies, especially in rare or novel scenarios, compromising safety.

Simultaneously, two other areas are gaining traction in autonomous driving research:
- **Diffusion Models:** These generative models have shown success in image generation, robotics, and traffic simulation. Their application in autonomous driving, exemplified by DiffusionDrive, GoalFlow, and Diffusion Planner, focuses on generating smooth, high-quality trajectories.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL has proven effective in improving model generalization and decision-making beyond what imitation learning can provide. Methods like RAD, CarPlanner, AlphaDrive, and Gen-Drive integrate RL to optimize for safety and comfort in closed-loop simulations.

ReCogDrive positions itself at the intersection of these trends, aiming to leverage the cognitive power of VLMs, the continuous trajectory generation capabilities of diffusion models, and the policy refinement strength of reinforcement learning. The work distinguishes itself by proposing a novel, unified framework that explicitly addresses the aforementioned limitations. It is presented as a pioneering effort to apply reinforcement learning to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby refining their planning capabilities for robust end-to-end autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of ReCogDrive is to develop a highly robust, safe, and efficient end-to-end autonomous driving system capable of generalizing to complex, real-world "long-tail" scenarios. This ambitious goal is driven by several specific objectives and motivations:

1. **Bridging the VLM-Driving Domain Gap:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing VLMs, while possessing vast world knowledge, lack the specialized, nuanced understanding required for safe and effective autonomous driving. Their pre-training on general internet data does not adequately capture the intricacies of dynamic driving environments, traffic rules, and driver behavior.
 - **Objective:** To systematically instill human-like driving cognition and priors into the VLM, enabling it to better comprehend and reason about driving scenarios.

2. **Resolving the Language-Action Modality Mismatch:**
 - **Motivation:** Many VLM-based autonomous driving systems output trajectories as discrete text tokens. This fundamental mismatch with the continuous nature of physical actions leads to several critical problems:
 - **Infeasible Trajectories:** Textual outputs can easily result in discontinuous or physically impossible driving paths.
 - **Format-Violating Outputs:** Decoding errors or malformed text can lead to parsing failures, severely impacting system reliability.
 - **Slow Inference Speed:** Autoregressive text generation is inherently sequential and thus slower than continuous action prediction, which is unacceptable for real-time autonomous driving.
 - **Objective:** To design a mechanism that efficiently translates the VLM's high-level cognitive understanding into continuous, stable, and precise driving trajectories, eliminating the issues associated with discrete language-space outputs.

3. **Overcoming Suboptimal Policies from Imitation Learning:**
 - **Motivation:** Pure imitation learning (behavior cloning), while effective for common scenarios, is limited by the expert data. It often struggles to generalize to out-of-distribution or adversarial long-tail scenarios, leading to suboptimal or even unsafe behaviors (e.g., averaging conflicting expert trajectories in complex situations). It cannot discover novel, safer policies beyond what is demonstrated.
 - **Objective:** To enhance driving safety and comfort by enabling the planner to learn directly from experience and explore more optimal behaviors through interaction with a realistic simulator, going beyond the limitations of expert demonstrations. This aims to reduce collisions and improve the overall quality of driving.

To achieve these objectives, ReCogDrive proposes a novel framework that integrates a cognitive VLM with a reinforcement learning-enhanced diffusion planner, offering a principled solution to the identified challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReCogDrive introduces a novel end-to-end autonomous driving system that strategically integrates the cognitive reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with a reinforcement learning-enhanced diffusion planner. The methodology is structured around three core innovations:

#### 3.1. Scalable Hierarchical Data Pipeline for Driving Pre-training

To bridge the substantial domain gap between general-purpose VLMs and the specific requirements of autonomous driving, the authors developed a three-stage data pipeline designed to instill human-like cognitive priors:

- **Stage 1: Generation:** This stage mimics the sequential cognitive process of human drivers. It utilizes a state-of-the-art VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) in conjunction with ground-truth data from NAVSIM to generate comprehensive annotations across four hierarchical levels:
 - **Foundational Perception:** Generating descriptions of holistic scenes, 3D object detection, and vulnerable road user identification.
 - **Dynamic Understanding:** Analyzing multi-agent dynamics, traffic state estimation, key agent identification, and motion prediction.
 - **Planning and Reasoning:** Formulating executable, safety-aware driving plans and providing concise rationales.
 - **Advanced Reasoning:** Performing counterfactual analyses and fine-grained trade-off evaluations in complex scenarios.
- **Stage 2: Refinement:** This stage integrates 12 diverse open-source driving datasets (e.g., Talk2Car, SUTD, NuScenes-QA, DriveLM, LingoQA). It involves data normalization (standardizing bounding box formats) and linguistic enrichment through an LLM. The LLM paraphrases question templates and expands terse answers to ensure semantic consistency and linguistic diversity across the heterogeneous datasets.
- **Stage 3: Quality Control:** To guarantee high data quality, Qwen2.5-VL is used to assign a quality score to each generated Question-Answer (QA) pair based on predefined linguistic and semantic criteria. Only pairs scoring above a threshold (e.g., 60) are retained, resulting in a large-scale, high-fidelity driving VQA dataset of 2.3 million pairs, including 775K specifically from NAVSIM. This systematic process ensures the VLM is adapted into a domain-specialized cognitive model.

#### 3.2. Cognition-Guided Diffusion Planner

To address the modality mismatch and generate continuous, stable trajectories, ReCogDrive proposes a cognition-guided diffusion planner:

- **VLM Guidance:** InternVL3 serves as the cognitive backbone. It processes multi-view images and textual queries, encoding them into hidden states (cognitive tokens). These tokens carry rich driving priors and are injected as cross-attention conditions into the diffusion transformer. A global semantic embedding, derived from pooling these tokens, provides stable contextual guidance during the denoising process. The VLM can optionally generate high-level driving instructions or Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
- **Diffusion Transformer Design:** The planner is built upon Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks, enhanced with lightweight yet effective architectural choices: SwiGLU FFN for expressive non-linearity, RoPE for relative positional encoding, and both QK-Norm and RMSNorm for stable optimization. Each block integrates self-attention (modeling waypoint relations) and cross-attention (injecting VLM semantic priors), facilitating a structured fusion of high-level scene cognition with low-level trajectory optimization.
- **Historical and Ego Information Fusion:** To ensure temporal consistency and physical realism, historical trajectories are encoded and concatenated with the noisy trajectory embeddings. Ego-vehicle status (e.g., speed, acceleration) is dynamically incorporated via AdaLN modulation, conditioning the planner on the vehicle's real-time physical state.

#### 3.3. Diffusion Group Relative Policy Optimization (DiffGRPO)

To move beyond the limitations of imitation learning and optimize for safety and comfort, ReCogDrive introduces DiffGRPO, a novel reinforcement learning algorithm tailored for diffusion planners:

- **Policy Formulation:** The diffusion policy is interpreted as an internal Markov Decision Process, where trajectories are gradually denoised from Gaussian noise over T steps.
- **Realistic Reward System:** Unlike prior approaches, DiffGRPO leverages the NAVSIM simulator to provide realistic, multi-faceted feedback. It evaluates sampled trajectories based on critical metrics such as collision avoidance, drivable-area compliance, and driving comfort. These metrics are aggregated into a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS), which serves as the reward for a composite action (the entire generated trajectory).
- **Group-Standardized Advantage:** For a batch of G sampled trajectories, a group-standardized advantage is computed, which normalizes rewards relative to the group's mean and standard deviation. This helps in robust policy updates.
- **Hybrid Policy Loss:** The training objective combines an RL loss (policy gradient weighted by the advantage) with a behavior cloning (BC) loss. The BC loss helps prevent policy collapse during exploration and maintains proximity to expert demonstrations, ensuring stability. A discount factor (γ=0.6) is applied across denoising steps, emphasizing later, more reliable steps to mitigate instability from early, noisy predictions.

The overall training proceeds in three stages: initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of the VLM using the hierarchical data, followed by behavior cloning of the diffusion planner, and finally, reinforcement learning fine-tuning with DiffGRPO in the simulator.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReCogDrive underwent extensive evaluation on multiple challenging autonomous driving benchmarks, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance and the effectiveness of its proposed components.

#### 4.1. Performance on NAVSIM Benchmark

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA):** ReCogDrive achieved a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 90.8 on the NAVSIM navtest benchmark, establishing a new SOTA. This is remarkable as it outperforms several leading methods, including DiffusionDrive and WoTE, which utilize both camera and LiDAR inputs, while ReCogDrive only uses camera input.
- **VLM Adaptation Effectiveness:** It improved PDMS by 7.5 points compared to directly fine-tuned InternVL3 and QwenVL2.5 models on NAVSIM trajectories, validating the efficacy of the hierarchical data pipeline and the overall training paradigm.
- **Vision-Only Superiority:** ReCogDrive also surpassed the previous vision-only SOTA, PARA-Drive, by 6.8 PDMS.
- **Extended Metrics:** On extended NAVSIM metrics (Table 6), ReCogDrive achieved SOTA scores in Driving Direction Compliance (DDC), Lane Keeping Ability (LK), Ego Progress (EP), and Time to Collision (TTC), delivering a 0.5 improvement in EPDMS over ARTEMIS.

#### 4.2. Closed-Loop Performance on Bench2Drive

- **Robustness in Safety-Critical Scenarios:** On the CARLA-based Bench2Drive leaderboard, ReCogDrive recorded the highest scenario success rate of 45.45% and the top Driving Score of 71.36.
- **Skill Proficiency:** It demonstrated exceptional performance in critical safety skills, achieving 69.09% in emergency braking and 71.34% in traffic sign compliance, while maintaining strong efficiency and a competitive multi-ability mean of 42.03%. These results underscore the framework's reliability in complex urban driving.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on ReCogDrive Components (Table 3)

The ablation study confirmed the incremental value of each proposed component:
- Baseline (InternVL3 on NAVSIM trajectory data): PDMS of 82.4.
- Adding Hierarchical Driving QA data (driving pre-training): Increased PDMS to 84.1 (+1.7).
- Introducing the Diffusion Planner for continuous trajectory prediction: Further raised PDMS to 86.5 (+2.4).
- Integrating Diffusion Group Relative Policy Optimization (DiffGRPO): Achieved the highest PDMS of 90.8 (+4.3), conclusively demonstrating the effectiveness of the RL scheme in fostering safer and more optimal driving behaviors.

#### 4.4. Comparison of Trajectory Generation Methods (Table 4)

- **Efficiency:** Generating trajectories as plain text using VLMs was significantly slower (0.5839s inference time) and prone to format errors (0.01%). In contrast, diffusion-based approaches were 7.8 times faster (approx. 0.075s) and eliminated format errors, directly addressing a key motivation.
- **Performance:** Diffusion-based planners generally yielded higher PDMS compared to MLP or query-based decoders, with architectural refinements like SwiGLU FFN, RMS Norm, RoPE, and QK Norm further boosting performance from 85.4 to 86.5 PDMS.

#### 4.5. Evaluation on Drive VQA Benchmarks (Table 5 & 7)

- ReCogDrive demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on both DriveLM and DriveBench, outperforming even closed-source generalist models like GPT-4o and specialized open-source driving models.
- It showed strong robustness across perception, prediction, planning, and behavior tasks, even with corrupted or text-only inputs, validating its robust visual question answering and reasoning capabilities in the driving domain.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results (Fig. 5, 10-14)

Qualitative comparisons highlighted that the RL-enhanced ReCogDrive generated demonstrably safer and more reliable trajectories in challenging turning scenarios compared to both traditional methods (Transfuser) and the imitation learning-only variant (ReCogDrive-IL).

#### 4.7. Hyperparameter and Guidance Mode Insights (Tables 8, 9, 10, 11)

- Optimal BC loss weight (λ = 0.001) balanced exploration and imitation.
- A minimum sampling value for noise (σ_exp_min = 0.02) during diffusion helped balance trajectory diversity and training stability.
- A discount factor of γ = 0.6 applied across denoising steps proved crucial for RL stability by downweighting noisy early steps.
- Interestingly, high-level command or Chain-of-Thought reasoning from the VLM did not yield significant performance gains on the NAVSIM benchmark, suggesting that its current diversity may not fully capture the benefits of these advanced guidance modes. The VLM primarily contributes through cognitive tokens.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReCogDrive makes several significant contributions to the field of autonomous driving, particularly at the intersection of large models and real-world application, with considerable potential impact:

1. **Advancing End-to-End Autonomous Driving:** The proposed framework offers a novel and effective paradigm for end-to-end autonomous driving systems. By seamlessly integrating the cognitive power of VLMs with the precision of diffusion planners and the robustness of reinforcement learning, ReCogDrive addresses fundamental limitations of existing approaches. This represents a substantial step towards more intelligent and reliable autonomous systems.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability:** This is perhaps the most critical impact. The transition from discrete, text-based trajectory generation to continuous, diffusion-based planning fundamentally improves the physical feasibility and smoothness of trajectories, eliminating format errors that could compromise safety. Furthermore, the DiffGRPO reinforcement learning stage explicitly optimizes for safety (collision avoidance, drivable area compliance) and comfort using a realistic simulator. This data-driven, closed-loop policy refinement is crucial for building trust in autonomous vehicles and mitigating risks in complex, rare scenarios often missed by imitation learning.

3. **Superior Generalization to Long-Tail Scenarios:** By instilling human-like cognitive priors into the VLM through a hierarchical data pipeline and leveraging the VLM's inherent world knowledge, ReCogDrive demonstrates superior generalization capabilities. Its strong performance on benchmarks like NAVSIM and Bench2Drive, especially in safety-critical situations, indicates a robust ability to handle driving conditions beyond standard training distributions.

4. **Efficiency and Practicality:** The cognition-guided diffusion planner offers a significant 7.8x speedup in inference compared to autoregressive text generation. This efficiency is paramount for real-time decision-making in autonomous vehicles. While optional, the VLM's capability for Chain-of-Thought reasoning also offers potential for greater interpretability, which is vital for debugging and regulatory approval, even if not fully leveraged by current benchmarks.

5. **Scalable Data Generation for VLMs in AD:** The hierarchical data pipeline with its automated generation, refinement, and quality control stages presents a scalable solution for creating high-fidelity driving-specific QA datasets. This reduces reliance on costly and time-consuming manual annotations, making it easier to adapt VLMs to new geographical regions, vehicle types, or specialized driving tasks.

6. **Pioneering VLA-RL Integration:** ReCogDrive is positioned as a pioneer in applying reinforcement learning to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for planning in autonomous driving. This opens new avenues for research into how rich semantic understanding from VLMs can be combined with learning-from-experience paradigms to create truly intelligent and adaptive driving agents.

7. **Industry-Academic Collaboration and Open Science:** The joint effort between Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Xiaomi EV highlights the power of combining academic rigor with industrial application focus. The open-sourcing of code and models further accelerates research in the field, enabling other researchers and developers to build upon this foundational work.

Looking ahead, the limitations identified by the authors—such as challenges with multiple camera inputs, video frame sequences, and current inference latency—point to important future research directions. However, ReCogDrive represents a substantial advancement towards developing safer, more reliable, and cognitively capable end-to-end autonomous driving systems, with clear implications for the future of self-driving technology.
