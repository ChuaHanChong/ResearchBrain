# ReCogDrive: A Reinforced Cognitive Framework for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Xiaomi EV developed ReCogDrive, an end-to-end autonomous driving system that integrates a cognition-guided Vision-Language Model with a reinforcement learning-enhanced diffusion planner. This framework achieved a new state-of-the-art Predictive Driver Model Score of 90.8 on the NAVSIM navtest benchmark using only camera input and the highest scenario success rate of 45.45% on Bench2Drive.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based autonomous driving systems suffer from a domain discrepancy, as VLMs are pre-trained on generic internet data lacking specialized driving knowledge.
- The modality mismatch between discrete language outputs from VLMs and the continuous action space of trajectory planning leads to physically infeasible trajectories, parsing errors, and slow inference.
- Reliance on imitation learning in current systems results in suboptimal policies and poor generalization to complex, rare "long-tail" driving scenarios.

## Method

- A scalable ==hierarchical data pipeline==, including generation, refinement, and quality control stages, was developed to instill human-like driving cognition into the VLM.
- A cognition-guided diffusion planner translates VLM's high-level understanding into continuous, stable driving trajectories, leveraging VLM cognitive tokens as cross-attention conditions.
- Diffusion Group Relative Policy Optimization (DiffGRPO), a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, was introduced to refine the diffusion planner in a realistic simulator for enhanced safety and comfort, moving beyond imitation learning limitations.

## Results

- ReCogDrive achieved a new state-of-the-art Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 90.8 on the NAVSIM navtest benchmark, surpassing previous methods including those using LiDAR.
- The system demonstrated the highest scenario success rate of **45.45%** and a top Driving Score of 71.36 on the CARLA-based Bench2Drive leaderboard, indicating robust performance in safety-critical situations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each proposed component, including hierarchical data, the diffusion planner, and DiffGRPO, incrementally improved the PDMS, validating their individual contributions to the overall framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematic pre-training with hierarchical driving-specific data effectively bridges the domain gap between general VLMs and autonomous driving, improving performance by 1.7 PDMS.
> - Diffusion models are significantly more efficient (7.8x faster) and reliable for continuous trajectory generation compared to autoregressive text-based methods, eliminating format errors.
> - Reinforcement learning via DiffGRPO, utilizing a realistic reward system from a simulator, is crucial for optimizing driving safety and comfort, yielding a 4.3 PDMS improvement over a behavior cloning baseline.
