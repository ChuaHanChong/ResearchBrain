# Panacea+: Panoramic and Controllable Video Generation for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Panacea+ extends the Panacea diffusion framework for synthesizing controllable, panoramic multi-view driving videos, adding a multi-view appearance noise prior and a super-resolution module atop decomposed 4D attention and ControlNet-based BEV conditioning. Synthetic data from Panacea+ boosts downstream 3D tracking, detection, and lane detection, reaching SOTA generation quality (FVD 103, FID 15.5) and AMOTA 42.7.

## Problem

- Panacea generates multi-view driving videos but lacks strong temporal consistency, limiting usefulness for tracking tasks that depend on coherent object motion across frames.
- Panacea only synthesizes low-resolution samples (256x512), capping the performance ceiling when such data augments high-resolution-hungry perception models.
- Prior generation methods (BEVGen, MagicDrive, WoVoGen, DriveDreamer) each cover only partial capability: single-view scope, no video modeling, or unvalidated temporal perception benefits.

## Method

- ==Decomposed 4D attention== UNet splits joint spatio-temporal attention into intra-view, cross-view (adjacent views only), and cross-frame attention for efficient multi-view video modeling.
- Two-stage generation pipeline first synthesizes multi-view images, then conditions a second-stage video model on those frames via zero-padded first-frame conditioning for higher fidelity.
- **Panacea+**'s ==multi-view appearance noise prior== injects first-frame latent features into subsequent-frame noise (coefficient lambda), and a cascaded ==super-resolution== module upsamples outputs to 512x1024.

## Results

- Panacea+ achieves best-in-class generation quality on nuScenes with FVD **103** and FID **15.5**, improving FVD by **36** points over base Panacea and beating SubjectDrive and DriveDreamer-2.
- Augmenting real data with Panacea+ synthetic samples raises tracking AMOTA from **37.6** to **42.7** (**+5.1**) and detection NDS from **51.2** to **53.8** (**+2.6**) at 1024x512 resolution.
- Ablations show optimal noise-prior lambda of **0.07** (FVD **139** to **103**, NDS **32.1** to **34.6**) and StableSR super-resolution edges out simple resize by **0.8** AMOTA points in tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing 4D attention into adjacent cross-view plus cross-frame components captures most consistency benefits while avoiding costly full joint attention, a reusable pattern for multi-view video models.
> - Higher-resolution synthetic data yields outsized downstream gains, suggesting synthetic-data pipelines should prioritize resolution scaling over marginal fidelity tweaks for perception transfer.
> - Injecting appearance priors from a conditioning frame into subsequent noise is a lightweight, generalizable trick for improving temporal and cross-view consistency in cascaded video diffusion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving increasingly relies on advanced Bird’s Eye View (BEV) perception methods, which have demonstrated utility across tasks such as 3D object detection, map segmentation, multi-object tracking, and 3D lane detection. Video-based BEV perception approaches, exemplified by models like StreamPETR, generally outperform image-based methods due to their ability to leverage temporal coherence in sequences, particularly for tasks like tracking. However, these video-based methods necessitate substantial volumes of high-quality video data for training.

Acquiring such data presents significant challenges, including high costs associated with manual collection and a lack of diversity in collected scenarios, particularly for rare or extreme conditions (e.g., adverse weather) due to safety concerns. This motivates research into synthesizing viable samples to augment existing datasets.

Previous generative efforts in autonomous driving have explored synthesizing image data conditioned on layouts, such as BEVGen and BEVControl, which benefit single-frame perception methods. A more complex task involves synthesizing video data to support video-based BEV perception. Works like MagicDrive, DriveDreamer, and Wovogen generate multi-view video samples based on BEV layout sequences. Panacea, the predecessor to Panacea+, was a diffusion-based video generation model designed to produce high-fidelity multi-view videos in driving scenarios, aligned with BEV layout sequences and textual control signals, demonstrating benefits for object detection tasks.

Despite these advancements, prior models, including Panacea, exhibited limitations. Specifically, Panacea required enhancement in temporal consistency, a critical aspect for tasks like tracking. Additionally, the resolution of its generated samples (256x512) was considered relatively low, potentially limiting the performance ceiling of perception models trained with such data, as higher-resolution data has been shown to improve performance in perception tasks.

Panacea+ positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified limitations. It aims to develop a more powerful and practical data generation framework by improving temporal consistency and increasing the resolution of synthesized video data, thereby offering greater utility for a broader range of autonomous driving perception tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to develop a robust and universally applicable framework for generating high-quality, controllable, multi-view video data for autonomous driving scenarios. This involved addressing several specific limitations observed in previous data generation approaches, including the authors' prior work, Panacea.

The key motivations are outlined as follows:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Diversity:** Autonomous driving perception models, particularly video-based BEV methods, require large-scale and diverse video data for effective training. Traditional data collection is expensive and limited in scope, especially for rare events or extreme environmental conditions. Synthesizing data can mitigate these challenges by generating varied scenarios.
- **Enhancing Temporal Consistency for Advanced Perception Tasks:** Tasks such as 3D object tracking heavily rely on the temporal coherence of objects within video sequences. Previous generative models, including Panacea, had room for improvement in maintaining high temporal consistency across frames. A core objective was to bolster this consistency to make synthetic data more effective for temporal modeling in perception.
- **Increasing Resolution for Improved Perception Performance:** Training perception models with higher-resolution data has been demonstrated to significantly enhance their performance. Panacea generated videos at a resolution of 256x512, which could impose a ceiling on the potential performance improvements when used for augmentation. An objective was to enable the generation of higher-resolution video samples to unlock greater performance gains for downstream perception tasks.
- **Improving Controllability and Annotation Alignment:** For synthetic data to be valuable for training autonomous driving systems, it must be precisely controllable and align with annotations (e.g., BEV layouts). The goal was to ensure that the generated videos accurately reflect commanded BEV layouts and other control signals, facilitating the creation of well-annotated training samples.
- **Developing a Universally Applicable Framework:** The research aimed to create a versatile framework whose generated data could benefit a wide range of tasks (e.g., 3D object tracking, 3D object detection, lane detection) across different datasets (e.g., nuScenes, Argoverse 2), thereby proving its broad utility for autonomous driving development.
- **Building upon and Expanding Panacea:** Panacea+ was specifically motivated as an enhanced version of the original Panacea framework, designed to address its identified shortcomings and transform it into a more powerful and practical tool for autonomous driving applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Panacea+ is an enhanced data generation framework built upon the architecture of Panacea, leveraging Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) for generating controllable multi-view videos in driving scenes. The methodology integrates several components to enhance consistency, resolution, and controllability.

**A. Foundation in Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs)**
Panacea+ operates on the principles of LDMs, which perform diffusion processes in a compact latent space rather than directly in the pixel space. This approach, derived from Stable Diffusion (SD), reduces computational complexity while maintaining high image quality. An encoder (E) maps pixel-space images to latent representations (z), and a decoder (D) reconstructs images from these latents. The core diffusion process involves iteratively denoising a normally distributed noise to synthesize new data, guided by a denoiser model.

**B. Generating High-Quality Multi-View Videos**
The framework is designed to generate multi-view videos with enhanced temporal and spatial consistency. It processes video sequences comprising multiple frames and views, structured as a joint diffused input with dimensions accommodating height, (width x views), temporal length, and latent channels.

1. **Decomposed 4D Attention:** To concurrently model multi-view and temporal consistency efficiently, Panacea+ replaces a computationally intensive 4D joint attention with a decomposed 4D attention mechanism within its UNet architecture. This mechanism comprises three main components:
 - **Intra-view attention:** Standard spatial self-attention within individual views, adapted from the original SD model.
 - **Cross-view attention:** Models correlations between adjacent views, focusing on local view relationships deemed most critical while disregarding non-adjacent view correlations for computational efficiency.
 - **Cross-frame attention:** Similar to approaches in models like VLDM, this component focuses on spatially aligned temporal patches across frames, imbuing the model with temporal awareness crucial for coherent video generation.

2. **Two-Stage Pipeline:** To improve generation quality, a two-stage training and inference pipeline is adopted.
 - **Training:** First, a dedicated set of weights for multi-view image generation is trained. Second, video generation weights are trained by concatenating a conditioned image (ground truth) with the diffused input, specifically integrating it with the first frame while subsequent frames use zero padding. This allows for efficient training comparable to a single-stage scheme.
 - **Inference:** Initially, multi-view frames are sampled using the first-stage (image generation) weights. Subsequently, a multi-view video is generated, conditioned on these initial frames, using the second-stage (video generation) weights. This decomposition is intended to enhance visual fidelity.

3. **Multi-view Appearance Noise Prior:** To further enhance consistency across views and over time, a multi-view appearance noise prior is employed. During training, the latent features of the first multi-view frame (z1) are integrated into the joint noise of subsequent frames (xi), with a coefficient (λ) controlling its influence. This ensures better content and appearance consistency between the first frame and the rest of the sequence, as well as across different views.

**C. Generating Controllable Driving Scene Videos**
Controllability is a central feature, enabling the generation of annotated training samples. Panacea+ integrates two categories of control signals:

1. **Coarse-grained Global Control (Textual Attributes):** Text prompts, encoded using CLIP, are integrated into the UNet, similar to Stable Diffusion. This allows Panacea+ to generate diverse multi-view videos corresponding to descriptions of weather conditions, time of day, or scene types (e.g., "Night-time," "Sandstorm," "Countryside").

2. **Fine-grained Layout Control (BEV Layout Sequence):** BEV layout sequences serve as the primary condition for generating samples aligned with annotations. For each BEV sequence, control elements are extracted into a 19-channel image format:
 - **Object Bounding Boxes:** Spatial representation of objects.
 - **Object Depth Maps:** Depth information for objects.
 - **Road Maps:** Representation of road structures.
 - **Camera Pose Embeddings:** Derived from camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, these vectors represent the camera ray direction for each pixel, normalized and converted into an RGB pseudo-color image.
 These 19-channel layout images are integrated into the UNet using the ControlNet framework, which uses a trainable copy of the SD encoder to incorporate these control signals.

**D. Super-Resolution Module**
A super-resolution module is cascaded after the diffusion generation process. The diffusion model initially generates videos at 256x512 resolution. This module then upscales the frames spatially to 512x1024, enabling the synthesis of high-resolution video data to support perception tasks that benefit from finer detail.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments across multiple tasks and datasets (nuScenes and Argoverse 2) to validate Panacea+'s generation quality, controllability, and benefits for autonomous driving perception models.

**A. Generation Quality**
- **Quantitative Metrics:** Panacea+ achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance in image and video synthesis quality metrics.
 - **FVD (Fréchet Video Distance):** Panacea+ attained an FVD score of 103, outperforming previous SOTA methods. This indicates improved temporal consistency, representing a 36-point improvement over the predecessor Panacea (FVD 139) and 21 points lower than SubjectDrive (FVD 124).
 - **FID (Fréchet Inception Distance):** The model achieved an FID score of 15.5, which was lower (better) than all compared SOTA methods, including image synthesis models like MagicDrive (16.20) and video synthesis models like DriveDreamer-2 (25.0). This suggests high image quality within the generated videos.

**B. Controllability**
- **Alignment with BEV Layouts:** The controllability was assessed by evaluating a pre-trained StreamPETR perception model on Panacea+'s synthetic nuScenes validation set.
 - Panacea+ generated samples achieved an NDS (nuScenes Detection Score) of 34.6, corresponding to 74% of the NDS obtained by the model on the real dataset. This indicates significant alignment between generated videos and BEV control signals.
 - This represented a 2.5-point gain in NDS compared to Panacea (32.1), demonstrating improved controllability and alignment.

**C. Enhancement for Autonomous Driving Tasks**
Panacea+'s generated samples were used to augment training datasets for various perception tasks, and performance was compared against baseline models trained solely on real data and other SOTA generation methods.

1. **3D Object Tracking (nuScenes):**
 - At 512x256 resolution, training with Panacea+ samples resulted in an AMOTA (Average Multi-Object Tracking Accuracy) of 34.6, a 3.5-point improvement over the real-data-only baseline (30.1). This was also a 0.9-point gain compared to augmentation with Panacea (33.7).
 - At 1024x512 resolution (using the super-resolution module), the AMOTA reached 42.7, a 5.1-point increase over the 1024x512 baseline (37.6).
 - Compared to SOTA methods (Table V), Panacea+ achieved the best tracking performance, with an AMOTA of 42.7, surpassing DriveDreamer-2 (31.3) by 11.4 points. This indicates the synthetic data's efficacy in enhancing tracking capabilities due to improved temporal consistency and resolution.

2. **3D Object Detection (nuScenes and Argoverse 2):**
 - When trained exclusively on Panacea+ generated samples, StreamPETR achieved an NDS of 38.4, which is 82% of the performance relative to the real dataset baseline.
 - Augmenting real data with Panacea+ samples at 512x256 resolution improved the StreamPETR NDS to 49.4, a 2.5-point increase over the real-only baseline (46.9).
 - At 1024x512 resolution, augmentation with Panacea+ samples achieved an NDS of 53.8, a 2.6-point increase over the 1024x512 baseline (51.2). The mAP improved from 40.4 to 42.6 (+2.2).
 - Compared to SOTA methods (Table V), Panacea+ achieved an NDS of 53.8 and an mAP of 42.6, surpassing all current autonomous driving scene generation methods.
 - On the Argoverse 2 dataset, augmenting training data with Panacea+ samples led to improvements in 3D object detection, with CDS increasing by 1.1 points (from 17.1 to 18.2) and mAP by 0.9 points (from 23.6 to 24.7). This demonstrated effectiveness across different datasets.

3. **Lane Detection (nuScenes):**
 - By incorporating road map control signals, Panacea+ significantly improved lane detection performance. Augmenting the MapTR model's training with synthetic data resulted in a 6.0-point increase in mAP (from 45.0 to 51.0).

**D. Qualitative Analysis**
- **Generation Quality:** Panacea+ demonstrated the ability to generate realistic multi-view videos with notable temporal and cross-view consistency, successfully synthesizing high-quality samples across all views and subsequent frames.
- **Controllability:**
 - **Textual Control:** The model showed robust coarse-grained textual control, enabling the generation of diverse driving scenarios by modifying text prompts (e.g., weather conditions like "Night-time," "Sandstorm," "Snow," "Rain," or scene types like "Countryside," "Mountain").
 - **BEV Layout Control:** Fine-grained control was observed, with generated cars and roads aligning precisely with BEV layouts while maintaining temporal and view consistency across adjacent frames and different camera views.

**E. Ablation Studies**
1. **Multi-view Appearance Noise Prior:**
 - Varying the coefficient (λ) for the appearance noise prior revealed that an optimal range enhanced both generation quality (FVD) and controllability (NDS).
 - A λ value of 0.07 during inference yielded the best overall performance, with FVD of 103 and NDS of 34.6, demonstrating improvements compared to settings without the noise prior (λ=0, FVD 139, NDS 32.1).
2. **Super-Resolution Module:**
 - Comparing two super-resolution methods (StableSR network vs. simple resize) for up-sampling to 1024x512:
 - In 3D object tracking, using StableSR resulted in a 0.8-point gain in AMOTA (42.7) compared to simple resize (41.9).
 - In 3D object detection, both methods showed similar performance (NDS 53.8 for StableSR, 53.7 for resize).
 - The study suggests that while StableSR offers marginal performance gains in some tasks, simple resize can be a more computationally cost-effective choice if considering efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Panacea+ represents an advancement in the field of data generation for autonomous driving, offering a framework that addresses critical limitations of prior approaches, including its predecessor, Panacea.

The significance and potential impact are as follows:

- **Enhanced Data Utility for Autonomous Driving Development:** By generating high-quality, high-resolution, and temporally consistent multi-view videos, Panacea+ provides a valuable resource for training and validating autonomous driving perception models. This directly contributes to mitigating the challenges of data scarcity, diversity, and annotation complexity inherent in real-world data collection.
- **Improved Performance in Downstream Perception Tasks:** The comprehensive experiments demonstrate that synthetic data from Panacea+ significantly enhances performance across a spectrum of autonomous driving tasks, including 3D object tracking, 3D object detection, and lane detection. The achieved state-of-the-art results, particularly in AMOTA for tracking and NDS for detection, underscore the practical utility of the generated data for advancing perception capabilities. The observed gains in tasks like tracking, which are highly sensitive to temporal consistency, highlight the effectiveness of the proposed methodological improvements.
- **Versatility Across Tasks and Datasets:** The framework's demonstrated effectiveness on multiple datasets (nuScenes, Argoverse 2) and various tasks indicates its broad applicability. This versatility means it can serve as a universal data augmentation tool for diverse autonomous driving research and development efforts.
- **Controllable Scenario Generation:** The integration of coarse-grained textual control and fine-grained BEV layout control allows for the generation of specific and challenging driving scenarios, including rare events and extreme weather conditions that are difficult or unsafe to collect in reality. This capability can improve the robustness and generalization of autonomous driving systems.
- **Cost-Efficiency and Practicality:** While the generation process has computational costs, the framework's two-stage approach and controlled generation allow for the creation of targeted, high-value data. The exploration of super-resolution methods also contributes to practical considerations for balancing quality and computational expense.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Panacea+ establishes a baseline for high-fidelity, controllable video generation in autonomous driving. Its identified limitations, such as reliance on existing dataset labels for control signals and computational costs, provide clear directions for future research. This includes exploring integration with simulators, developing methods for generating novel control signals, improving computational efficiency, and scaling diffusion models with transformer structures.

In conclusion, Panacea+ makes a substantial contribution by offering a powerful and versatile data generation framework that directly addresses critical needs in autonomous driving, thereby holding potential to accelerate the development and deployment of more robust and capable self-driving systems.
