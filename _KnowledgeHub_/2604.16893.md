# EasyVideoR1: Easier RL for Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> EasyVideoR1 introduces a comprehensive reinforcement learning framework for large vision-language models, specifically designed for video understanding. It achieved a 1.47x throughput improvement in RL training and enhanced the average accuracy of a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model by +2.3 points across 10 video benchmarks, including a +6.6% gain on Video-Holmes and +6.7% on VideoMathQA.

## Problem

- Systematic application of Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to video understanding faced challenges due to diverse task types and computational overhead.
- Existing RL training frameworks suffered from significant computational burden due to repeated and redundant video decoding and preprocessing, hindering efficiency.
- Reproducible and efficient evaluation of video understanding models was difficult due to sensitive hyperparameters and the sheer volume of high-dimensional video data.

## Method

- Developed **EasyVideoR1**, an ==RLVR== training framework incorporating video-friendly optimizations like ==offline preprocessing== and ==tensor caching==, significantly boosting throughput.
- Designed a comprehensive, ==task-aware reward system== with a unified routing mechanism supporting 11 distinct video and image problem types.
- Implemented an ==asynchronous multi-benchmark evaluation== framework that integrates 22 mainstream video-understanding benchmarks for efficient and reproducible accuracy assessment.

## Results

- RL training with EasyVideoR1 improved the average accuracy of the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model by **+2.3 points**, raising the average score from **62.1%** to **64.4%** across 10 video benchmarks.
- Achieved substantial accuracy gains in complex reasoning tasks (**+6.6%** on Video-Holmes) and mathematical reasoning tasks (**+6.7%** on VideoMathQA).
- Demonstrated a **1.47x** overall speedup in RL training throughput, with the reference-model forward pass time decreasing by **2.85x** due to offline video caching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling video preprocessing from the training loop and caching frames provides substantial throughput improvements, particularly by reducing redundant operations for reference models.
> - A modular and unified reward system effectively handles the diverse scoring requirements of various video understanding tasks, enabling broad applicability.
> - Hybrid online-offline training and joint image-video training paradigms offer flexible and efficient strategies for leveraging diverse data sources to improve model learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "EasyVideoR1" is positioned within the evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs) and their transition towards natively multimodal architectures, particularly those capable of video understanding. Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) has demonstrated utility in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of text-based LLMs, as exemplified by projects like DeepSeek-R1 and methods such as GRPO. As LLMs integrate visual modalities, extending these RL techniques to multimodal contexts, specifically video, becomes a relevant area of research.

However, the authors identify several challenges in applying RLVR to video understanding systematically. These include the inherent diversity of video task types (ranging from multiple-choice QA to spatial-temporal grounding), the substantial computational burden associated with repeatedly decoding and preprocessing high-dimensional video inputs, and the difficulty of ensuring reproducible evaluation across a multitude of sensitive hyperparameters specific to video.

Existing open-source RL training frameworks provide foundational infrastructure, but primarily focus on text and image modalities. Frameworks like EasyR1 and veRL offer scalable foundations for text and image, with optimizations for distributed execution. Other efforts, such as R1-V, explore cost-effective RL training for visual counting and geometric reasoning. OneThinker attempts a multi-task RL framework for heterogeneous vision tasks, including video. Despite these advancements, the authors highlight that these existing systems either concentrate on image-text scenarios or lack systematic optimizations specifically tailored for the video modality. For instance, some frameworks are noted to redundantly decode and preprocess video data multiple times within their pipelines, leading to significant slowdowns. Furthermore, the absence of evaluation code that accurately reproduces reported accuracies for video benchmarks, coupled with the sensitivity of video evaluation to various hyperparameters, constitutes a practical barrier.

EasyVideoR1 aims to address these identified gaps by providing a complete and efficient RL training framework purpose-built for video understanding. It seeks to lower the barrier for researchers to explore RL-driven video understanding by offering video-specific preprocessing acceleration, a comprehensive multi-task reward library, joint image-video training capabilities, and a large-scale asynchronous evaluation framework designed for reproducible accuracy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of EasyVideoR1 is to develop a comprehensive and efficient reinforcement learning framework specifically designed for training large vision-language models (LVLMs) on video understanding tasks. This objective is driven by several key motivations and aims to address the aforementioned challenges in the current research landscape:

1. **Bridging the Gap for Video in RLVR:** Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) has shown effectiveness in improving reasoning in text-based LLMs. With the evolution of models towards native multimodal architectures capable of video understanding, there is a clear motivation to extend these powerful RLVR techniques to the video domain. However, this area remains largely unexplored due to inherent complexities. EasyVideoR1 aims to facilitate this extension.

2. **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks in Video Processing:** Video data is high-dimensional and computationally intensive. Existing RL training frameworks, even those with multimodal support, often involve redundant video decoding and preprocessing steps across different stages of the training pipeline. This leads to significant computational overhead, making video RL training slow and costly. A core motivation is to streamline this process by eliminating redundant operations and improving training throughput.

3. **Managing the Diversity of Video Task Types:** Video understanding encompasses a wide array of distinct task types, each potentially requiring specialized reward calculation logic. These range from simple multiple-choice questions and optical character recognition (OCR) to complex temporal event localization, spatial grounding, object tracking, and dense pixel-level segmentation. Developing a unified yet flexible reward system that can accurately evaluate performance across such diverse tasks is a significant motivation.

4. **Enabling Reproducible and Efficient Evaluation:** Evaluating LVLMs on video benchmarks is challenging due to the sensitivity of results to numerous hyperparameters (e.g., frame sampling strategy, visual token budget, input resolution, prompt templates) and the sheer volume of visual tokens, which makes evaluation slow. A key motivation is to provide an evaluation framework that is both fast and comprehensive, capable of reproducing official scores, thereby facilitating fair comparisons and reliable progress tracking.

5. **Supporting Advanced Training Paradigms:** The authors identify the potential of hybrid training (combining offline curated data with online exploration) and joint image-video training (leveraging abundant image data to reinforce visual reasoning for video tasks) for advancing video RL. A motivation is to provide user-friendly interfaces within the framework to enable researchers to readily explore these paradigms.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives, as outlined by the contributions of EasyVideoR1, are:
- To implement a full video RL training pipeline incorporating offline preprocessing and tensor caching to enhance throughput.
- To develop a comprehensive, task-aware reward system capable of handling 11 distinct video and image problem types with unified routing.
- To introduce a mixed offline-online data training paradigm for improved learning, especially for challenging tasks.
- To enable joint image-video training with independently configurable pixel budgets for modality-specific optimization.
- To establish an asynchronous multi-benchmark evaluation framework covering 22 mainstream video understanding benchmarks, ensuring accuracy alignment with reported scores.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EasyVideoR1 extends existing frameworks like EasyR1 and veRL, adapting them with systematic support for video understanding RL training and evaluation. The design is structured around three main dimensions: video-friendly optimization, research-friendly interfaces for algorithm development, and a high-throughput evaluation framework.

#### 3.1. Video Friendly Optimization

This dimension addresses the computational challenges and diverse task types inherent in video understanding.

- **Efficient RL with Video Caching:**
 - **Problem:** Traditional frameworks repeatedly decode raw video files at each training step, leading to CPU-bound I/O bottlenecks.
 - **Solution:** EasyVideoR1 decouples video preprocessing from the training loop. An offline batch tool decodes, resamples, and resizes videos into cache files (`.pt` format). Each cache file is keyed by `(video_path, fps, max_frames, max_pixels)` to ensure automatic invalidation upon parameter changes. This preprocessing stage is parallelized across multi-worker processes.
 - **Storage Trade-off:** While direct tensor storage is larger than compressed video, caching frames that are already temporally sampled and spatially resized reduces the footprint. The per-video cache size is bounded by `max_frames`.
 - **Training Integration:** During training, only the cache file path is recorded. The `.pt` file loading occurs locally on each worker. `VideoMetadata` (frame rate, sampling indices, spatial dimensions) is propagated alongside cached frames to vLLM and the HuggingFace processor, ensuring consistency by skipping redundant resizing and sampling operations.

- **Mixed-Modality Pipeline Adaptation:**
 - **Problem 1: Mixed-Modality Forward Pass:** Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) typically have separate encoder branches for images and videos. In mixed image-video training, micro-batches containing only one modality can leave the other branch inactive, causing Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP) gradient synchronization failures.
 - **Solution 1:** Zero-valued dummy tensors are generated for the missing modality. Their encoder outputs are connected to the computation graph via zero-weighted addition, ensuring all parameters participate in every forward pass without contributing spurious gradients.
 - **Problem 2: Independent Resolution Budgets:** Optimal spatial resolution differs for images and videos (images benefit from high resolution, videos balance per-frame resolution with frame count).
 - **Solution 2:** Resolution configuration is decoupled into `image_max_pixels`, `video_max_pixels`, and `video_max_frames`, allowing independent tuning.

- **Task-Aware Reward System:**
 - **Problem:** Video understanding encompasses diverse task types, each requiring specialized scoring logic.
 - **Solution:** A modular reward library is provided with a unified routing mechanism. A central dispatcher examines each sample's `problem_type` and forwards it to the corresponding reward module. This design supports incremental extension. Table 1 lists 11 supported categories (e.g., multiple choice, numerical, temporal grounding, spatial grounding, OCR, math, code) and their respective accuracy scoring methods. Prompt formatting is handled dynamically via Jinja2 templates.

#### 3.2. Research-Friendly Interfaces for Algorithm Development

This dimension facilitates the exploration of advanced training paradigms.

- **Hybrid Online-Offline Training:**
 - **Motivation:** On-policy methods suffer from cold-start issues and underutilize high-quality pre-collected trajectories.
 - **Solution:** A lightweight mix-policy interface is implemented (controlled by `enable_mix_policy` and an optional quality threshold). For each training sample, the framework generates `n-1` on-policy responses and substitutes the final slot with a pre-collected offline trajectory. This group of `n` responses then proceeds through reward computation and GRPO update. This mechanism operates at the rollout layer without modifying the core GRPO algorithm.

- **Joint Image-Video Training:**
 - **Motivation:** Leveraging abundant image QA data can strengthen foundational visual reasoning while simultaneously learning video-specific temporal understanding.
 - **Solution:** Each sample includes a `data_type` field to route it to the appropriate preprocessor and decoupled resolution budget. The multimodal field schema is unified across image and video samples, maintaining consistency through data loading, rollout, reward computation, and policy updates, which simplifies mixed batch assembly. A "strict-failure" policy is adopted, raising exceptions if visual placeholder tokens do not match visual features, to enforce semantic consistency and surface data-processing bugs early.

- **Broad Model and Algorithm Coverage:**
 - **Model Support:** Natively supports Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, and Qwen3.5 series of vision-language models, including contributions for Qwen3.5.
 - **Algorithm Support:** Inherits a suite of RL algorithms from EasyR1 (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO, CISPO, Reinforce++, ReMax, RLOO) and adds new implementations for GDPO and LUFFY. All algorithms share a unified rollout and reward-computation interface for easy switching.

#### 3.3. Fast & Comprehensive Evaluation Framework

This dimension addresses the challenges of scalable and reproducible evaluation.

- **Asynchronous Inference Design:**
 - **Problem:** Sequential execution of CPU-intensive video preprocessing and GPU-intensive model inference leads to hardware underutilization.
 - **Solution 1: Precomputed Frame Caching:** All video frames are precomputed, including decoding, temporal sampling, and spatial resizing, and persisted as cache files on disk. Cache entries are keyed by video path, target frame count, sampling FPS, and spatial resolution. Cache construction is parallelized across multiple worker processes.
 - **Solution 2: Asynchronous Pipeline with AsyncLLMEngine:** The framework replaces synchronous inference with a three-stage asynchronous pipeline built on vLLM's `AsyncLLMEngine`. These stages (IO, Prefill, Decode) operate concurrently. A background thread pool continuously loads cached frames (IO), the engine processes new inputs for key-value cache construction (Prefill), and requests with completed prefill enter autoregressive token generation (Decode). This overlap keeps the GPU saturated. Chunked prefill is enabled to prevent long video sequences from monopolizing the GPU during prefill, by partitioning inputs into fixed-size token chunks.

- **Supported Benchmarks:**
 - A unified interface integrates 22 video understanding benchmarks, covering six categories (Table 2): general video understanding, long video understanding, video reasoning, STEM knowledge, spatial understanding, (spatio-)temporal grounding, and streaming video.
 - Each benchmark is registered via a lightweight configuration specifying data loading, prompt formatting, answer extraction, and scoring functions. This modularity allows easy addition of new benchmarks. The accuracy produced by the framework has been verified to closely match officially reported scores.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to assess the effectiveness of EasyVideoR1 in improving the performance of instruct models through RL training and to quantify the throughput improvements from its caching mechanism.

#### 4.1. Unlocking the Potential of Instruct Models

- **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Base Model:** Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct.
 - **Training Data:** Approximately 100,000 video samples curated from public datasets (OneThinker, Video-R1, VideoChat-R1), filtered to retain samples with a partial success rate (0 < pass rate < 1) with the base model, ensuring they were within the model's learning frontier.
 - **Training Configuration:** GRPO with DAPO clipping variant (asymmetric clip ratios ϵ_low = 0.2, ϵ_high = 0.28), KL penalty disabled. Rollout group size of 8, global batch size of 256. Constant learning rate of 1x10^-6 with AdamW optimizer. Video inputs sampled at 2 FPS, max 128 frames, 262,144 pixels per frame. Image inputs used a separate budget of 1,048,576 pixels. Maximum response length was 4,096 tokens. Training utilized 32 H200 GPUs for approximately 20 hours, with FSDP full sharding, gradient checkpointing, padding-free attention, and dynamic batching. vLLM with tensor parallelism size 2 was used for rollout inference.
 - **Evaluation:** 10 representative benchmarks from Table 2, spanning general video understanding, long video understanding, video reasoning, and STEM knowledge categories. Greedy decoding was used.

- **Results (Figure 2):**
 - **Overall Improvement:** RL training with EasyVideoR1 resulted in an average accuracy improvement of +2.3 points across the 10 benchmarks, increasing the average score from 62.1% (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct baseline) to 64.4%. This demonstrates the framework's end-to-end effectiveness.
 - **Task-Specific Gains:**
 - **Reasoning and Mathematical Tasks:** The most substantial improvements were observed in tasks requiring complex reasoning, such as Video-Holmes (+6.6% accuracy), and mathematical reasoning, as seen in VideoMathQA (+6.7% accuracy). This indicates that RL training effectively enhanced the model's deliberative reasoning capabilities on video inputs.
 - **General Video Understanding:** Consistent positive gains were noted across general video understanding benchmarks, including Video-MME (+2.1%), MVBench (+3.5%), and LVBench (+0.7%), suggesting that RL training did not degrade broad video comprehension.
 - **Comparison to Thinking Variant:** The RL-trained model achieved comparable or superior results to the officially released Qwen3-VL-8B-Think variant on most benchmarks, while operating in a standard (non-thinking) inference mode. This implies performance gains without additional reasoning overhead during inference.

#### 4.2. Throughput Improvement from Offline Preprocessing and Caching

- **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Model:** Qwen3-VL-8B.
 - **Hardware:** 32 GPUs (4 nodes x 8 GPUs).
 - **Configuration:** Global batch size of 32, video sequences up to 256 frames. All other hyperparameters were identical between two modes: `prefer_preprocessed` (cache-based loading) and `realtime_only` (on-the-fly decoding). Metrics were averaged over 95 and 68 training steps, respectively, excluding warmup.

- **Results (Figure 3):**
 - **Overall Speedup:** Cache-based loading achieved a 1.47x overall speedup. The average step time decreased from 194.5 seconds to 131.9 seconds. Token throughput increased from 797 tokens/second to 1,175 tokens/second.
 - **Phase-level Breakdown (Figure 3a):**
 - **Rollout Generation:** Time reduced from 82.1 seconds to 53.9 seconds (a 1.52x improvement). This indicates that the vLLM inference engine was no longer bottlenecked by CPU-bound video decoding during input sequence loading.
 - **Reference Model Forward Pass:** Time decreased dramatically from 53.6 seconds to 18.8 seconds (a 2.85x improvement). This significant reduction is attributed to the elimination of redundant video re-decoding that occurred in the on-the-fly mode for the reference model stage.
 - **Actor Parameter Update:** This phase's duration remained constant at approximately 54 seconds in both modes, as it operates on token-level gradients independently of video I/O.
 - **Consistency:** The total tokens processed per step remained nearly identical in both modes (approximately 4.93 million), confirming that the caching mechanism accelerated training without altering its semantics.

These results demonstrate that EasyVideoR1 effectively enhances both the reasoning capabilities of LVLMs and the efficiency of the RL training process for video understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EasyVideoR1 introduces a framework designed to advance the application of reinforcement learning to video understanding in large vision-language models. Its significance and potential impact stem from its multi-faceted approach to addressing critical challenges in this domain.

Firstly, the framework systematically tackles the **computational overhead** associated with video data. By implementing an offline preprocessing and tensor caching mechanism, EasyVideoR1 achieves a 1.47× throughput improvement in RL training. This efficiency gain, particularly the 2.85× reduction in reference model forward pass time, directly translates to reduced computational costs and faster iteration cycles for researchers, making video RL training more accessible and economically feasible.

Secondly, EasyVideoR1's **comprehensive and task-aware reward system** significantly broadens the scope of video tasks that can be effectively targeted with RL. Supporting 11 distinct problem types, from multiple-choice questions to complex spatio-temporal grounding, with a unified routing mechanism, enables models to learn and reason across a diverse range of real-world video applications. This modular design also facilitates future expansion to new task types.

Thirdly, the introduction of **research-friendly interfaces** for hybrid online-offline training and joint image-video training has the potential to accelerate algorithmic development. The mixed offline-online paradigm allows leveraging high-quality curated data alongside on-policy exploration, potentially improving sample efficiency and mitigating cold-start problems, particularly for challenging tasks. Joint image-video training enables models to benefit from abundant image data to strengthen foundational visual reasoning, which can then mutually reinforce video-specific temporal understanding.

Fourthly, the **asynchronous multi-benchmark evaluation framework** provides a robust and efficient solution for reproducible research. Integrating 22 mainstream video understanding benchmarks with verified accuracy alignment to official reports, and achieving a 6-7x speedup over vanilla inference frameworks, ensures that model improvements can be reliably and quickly assessed. This comprehensive and fast evaluation capability is crucial for tracking progress and fostering fair comparisons in the rapidly evolving field of multimodal LLMs.

Finally, the empirical validation demonstrates that RL training with EasyVideoR1 can **significantly enhance the reasoning capabilities of existing instruct models**. The improvement of Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct's average accuracy by +2.3 points, with notable gains in reasoning (+6.6% on Video-Holmes) and mathematical tasks (+6.7% on VideoMathQA), highlights the framework's effectiveness. Furthermore, the ability to achieve comparable or superior performance to a "thinking" variant without additional inference overhead suggests a pathway to more efficient and capable video understanding models.

In summary, EasyVideoR1 has the potential to substantially lower the technical barrier for researchers to engage in RL-driven video understanding, accelerate research cycles, and contribute to the development of more advanced and efficient multimodal AI systems for diverse real-world video applications, including embodied intelligence, interactive video dialogue, surveillance analysis, and autonomous driving. Its open-source nature further invites community contributions, fostering collaborative advancement in the field.
