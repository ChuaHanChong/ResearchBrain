# Entropy-Augmented Multi-Objective Policy Optimization in Multiagent Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> An entropy-augmented policy evaluation strategy for multi-objective multiagent evolutionary search. A k-nearest-neighbor entropy estimate over joint agent trajectories is scaled by a coefficient and added directly to every objective's fitness score inside NSGA-II, rewarding behavioral spread without adding a competing objective. In two multi-rover domains it raised final Pareto-front hypervolume by 18% and 48% over the plain NSGA-II baseline.

## Problem

- Multi-objective evolutionary algorithms like NSGA-II maintain diversity only in objective space via crowding distance, ignoring behavior-space diversity and risking premature convergence.
- Under sparse rewards, behaviorally distinct team policies score identical objective values early on, so promising exploratory behaviors get discarded indiscriminately during selection.
- Multi-objective quality-diversity methods enforce behavior diversity within predefined niches, where local competition can suppress policies that would otherwise become globally Pareto-dominant.

## Method

- ==kNN entropy proxy== estimated over joint rover positions across an episode: mean log distance from each sampled state to its k-th nearest neighbor in joint state space.
- ==Entropy-augmented fitness shaping== adds the entropy estimate, scaled by temperature beta, to every objective's reward rather than as a separate objective, preserving ==global Pareto ranking==.
- ==NSGA-II== evolves neural-network controller weights under ==centralized training, decentralized execution== in the multi-rover domain; population 75, 10,000 generations, beta swept over five values.

## Results

- Experiment 1 obstacle navigation: hypervolume **973.34** plus/minus **142.43** with beta **0.05** versus **822** plus/minus **164.79** baseline, an **18%** average gain across **29** trials.
- Experiment 2 temporal tradeoff: hypervolume **63.25** plus/minus **5.79** versus **42.67** plus/minus **3.33** baseline, a **48%** gain, though only **3** independent trials were run.
- Parameter tuning over beta of **0.0**, **0.05**, **0.1**, **0.25**, **0.5** found the smallest nonzero value, **0.05**, best; larger coefficients distort the true objective function.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Behavioral diversity can enter multi-objective search as a fitness bonus rather than an extra objective, keeping the Pareto structure and dominance ranking intact.
> - Entropy bonus magnitude is the critical knob: too small and it fails to separate similar policies, too large and it overwhelms task reward.
> - High variance from strong local optima means large hypervolume gains on few seeds are exploratory evidence; many trials are needed before claiming robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the intersection of multi-agent systems, multi-objective optimization, and evolutionary computation, with connections to reinforcement learning. Autonomous agent teams are increasingly deployed in complex real-world environments such as marine monitoring, space exploration, and disaster response. These applications frequently involve managing multiple, often conflicting, objectives, such as maximizing task completion while minimizing energy consumption or ensuring safety. In such scenarios, a single policy that optimally addresses all objectives simultaneously is rare; instead, a set of Pareto-optimal policies, representing various trade-offs, is often desired.

Traditional Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs), such as NSGA-II, SPEA2, and MOEA/D, have been developed to discover sets of Pareto-optimal solutions by promoting diversity within the *objective space*. They achieve this through mechanisms like crowding distance, which favors solutions that are well-distributed across the objective front. However, a limitation identified in these approaches is their neglect of diversity within the *behavior space*. Policies that exhibit distinct behaviors but yield similar objective scores, especially in early optimization stages or sparse reward environments, might be prematurely discarded or converge to homogeneous behaviors.

Quality-Diversity (QD) approaches, including Multi-Objective Quality-Diversity (MOQD) methods like Multi-Objective Map-Elites (MOME), address behavioral diversity more explicitly by optimizing Pareto fronts within predefined behavioral niches. While this offers behavioral exploration, it often results in localized competition that might suppress policies capable of emerging as globally dominant solutions.

Furthermore, the concept of entropy-driven behavioral diversity has been explored in both evolutionary computation and reinforcement learning. Maximum-entropy reinforcement learning integrates entropy directly into the objective function to encourage exploration, while novelty search methods prioritize behavioral novelty over immediate objective performance to mitigate premature convergence. These approaches often treat entropy or novelty as an objective in itself or focus on single-objective tasks.

This work positions itself to bridge the gap between objective-space diversity in global Pareto optimization and behavior-space diversity. It aims to integrate a measure of behavioral diversity directly into the fitness evaluation of a global MOEA, specifically NSGA-II, without introducing it as a separate, competing objective or segmenting the search into behavioral niches. By augmenting existing objective fitness with an entropy bonus, the authors propose a method to encourage exploration of behaviorally distinct policies within a global multi-objective optimization framework for multi-agent systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the challenges faced by autonomous agent teams operating in environments with competing objectives. In such settings, operators require a diverse set of robust control policies that offer optimal trade-offs across these objectives to adapt to evolving mission conditions or varying preferences. The limitations of existing multi-objective optimization approaches drove the specific objectives of this study:

**Motivation:**

1. **Balancing Competing Objectives:** Robot teams, particularly in complex domains like space exploration or disaster response, must continuously balance multiple performance criteria (e.g., task efficiency, energy consumption, safety). These objectives are often in conflict, necessitating the discovery of a portfolio of solutions rather than a single optimal policy.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Objective-Space Diversity:** Current MOEAs primarily focus on maintaining diversity in the *objective space*, ensuring a well-spread Pareto front. However, this focus can lead to premature convergence in the *behavior space*. Policies that perform similarly in terms of objective scores but employ qualitatively different strategies might be indistinguishably ranked and potentially discarded.
3. **Risk of Premature Convergence in Sparse Reward Settings:** In environments characterized by sparse rewards, initial behavioral differences may not immediately translate into distinct objective values. This increases the risk that behaviorally varied but objectively similar policies are prematurely pruned, potentially hindering the discovery of superior solutions that require extensive exploration. For example, two rover teams might initially yield similar low rewards, but one might be exploring more widely, positioning itself better for higher future rewards, while the other stagnates.
4. **Integrating Behavioral Diversity without Disrupting Global Optimization:** While Quality-Diversity methods account for behavioral diversity, they often do so by creating separate niches, leading to localized competition rather than a global Pareto front. The motivation was to incorporate behavioral diversity in a way that preserves the global Pareto structure, allowing for a more unified optimization process.

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Develop a Behavior-Space-Aware Fitness-Shaping Strategy:** The primary objective was to design a mechanism to infuse behavioral diversity considerations directly into the policy evaluation process of multi-objective, multi-agent evolutionary algorithms.
2. **Augment Policy Evaluation with an Entropy Bonus:** Specifically, the aim was to introduce an entropy bonus to the fitness scores of individual objectives, measured as the estimated average k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN) entropy of joint agent positions across a policy's trajectory. This bonus is intended to discourage behavioral homogeneity.
3. **Preserve the Underlying Pareto Optimization Framework:** A critical objective was to integrate this behavioral diversity signal without introducing entropy as a separate, competing objective. By augmenting existing objective rewards, the method aims to encourage exploration of behaviorally distinct policies while maintaining the integrity and global search properties of the original Pareto optimization framework (NSGA-II).
4. **Evaluate the Impact on Pareto Front Quality:** The research sought to empirically evaluate whether this entropy-augmented policy evaluation improves the overall quality and diversity of the generated Pareto fronts, as measured by hypervolume, in multi-agent domains with distinct reward structures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces an entropy-augmented policy evaluation strategy integrated into the Non-Dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) for multi-objective, multi-agent policy optimization.

**Problem Formulation:**
The multi-objective multi-agent setting is formally modeled as a Multi-Objective Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (MODec-POMDP). This involves a state space (S), a joint action space (A) across all agents, a state transition probability function (T), a vector of immediate rewards (⃗r) for *n* objectives, and local observation spaces (O) for each agent. Agents operate under a Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution (CTDE) framework, meaning policies are trained centrally but executed independently based on local observations. The goal is to maximize the total accumulated reward vector over an episode horizon, yielding a set of Pareto-optimal joint-team control policies.

**Base Algorithm:**
NSGA-II was selected as the foundational multi-objective evolutionary algorithm. It ranks solutions based on Pareto dominance and then by crowding distance to maintain diversity in the objective space. The policies evolved are the weights and biases of neural network controllers for each agent. Key hyperparameters included a population size of 75, 10,000 generations, two hidden layers of 16 neurons each, a mutation rate of 0.75, and a mutation scale of 0.5.

**Environment:**
The experiments were conducted in the Multi-Agent Rover Domain, a benchmark simulation. In this domain, teams of rovers are tasked with visiting Points of Interest (POIs) to earn rewards. Objectives are defined based on POI types, time, or distance. Rovers have local sensors, cannot communicate directly, and act simultaneously and independently. POI activation depends on the number of visiting rovers (coupling factor) and duration.

**Entropy-Augmented Policy Evaluation:**
The core of the proposed method is to augment the fitness score of each objective with a scaled entropy bonus rather than treating entropy as a distinct objective. This is designed to preserve the existing Pareto structure while encouraging the discovery of more diverse trade-off solutions.

1. **Entropy Estimation:** A proxy for entropy, ˆH, is approximated using a k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN)-based estimator. This estimator is computed over the *joint trajectories* of all agents within a single episode. The formula is:
 ˆH = (1/N) * Σ (log ϵ_n)
 where ϵ_n is the Euclidean distance from a specific sample point (joint agent position at a timestep) to its k-th nearest neighbor within the set of all joint positions recorded during that episode, and N is the total number of timesteps in the episode. The parameter *k* for kNN was set to 5.

2. **Augmented Fitness Score:** For any policy π and each objective *i*, the original fitness score f_i(π) is augmented to ˜f_i(π) by adding the scaled kNN entropy:
 ˜f_i(π) = f_i(π) + β · ˆH(π)
 Here, β is a tunable scaling factor. Increasing β favors policies that exhibit broader exploration of the state space, making them more likely to be preserved during evolution. The authors tested β values of {0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5}, with β = 0.0 representing the baseline NSGA-II without entropy augmentation.

**Performance Metric:**
Hypervolume was chosen as the primary performance metric. It measures the volume of the objective space dominated by a Pareto front relative to a reference point. Hypervolume provides a combined measure of both the convergence (how close the front is to the true Pareto front) and the coverage (how well-distributed the solutions are across the front). A negative reference point of (-1, -1) was used, a common practice for the reward range in this domain.

**Experimental Setups:**

1. **Experiment 1: Obstacle Navigation:**
 - **Objective:** Two-rover team maximizing rewards from two objectives, each associated with a different POI.
 - **Environment:** Low-value POIs were initially accessible. Higher-value POIs (5 points per POI per timestep, compared to 1 point) were placed behind a simple wall.
 - **Challenge:** Agents were required to explore beyond immediate low rewards to discover the high-value POIs, potentially accepting lower rewards initially. This setup presented a strong local optimum.

2. **Experiment 2: Temporal Tradeoff:**
 - **Objective:** Two objectives: total reward collected across all POIs during the first half of the episode, and total reward collected over the entire episode.
 - **Environment:** Inner-ring POIs provided 1 point each with a coupling factor of one (individual activation). Outer-ring POIs provided 3 points each with a coupling factor of two (requiring two rovers).
 - **Challenge:** The episode length was short, forcing agents to make trade-offs between quickly collecting easy, early rewards from inner POIs versus investing time and coordination for higher-value, resource-intensive rewards from outer POIs.

**Analysis Protocol:**
Each trial ran for 10,000 time steps. Experiment 1 involved 30 independent runs for each β value. Experiment 2, being a preliminary evaluation, used 3 independent runs per β value. The same random seed was used across β values within a run, but different random seeds were used for each independent run. The average and standard error of hypervolumes across runs and generations were collected for comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated the impact of entropy-augmented policy evaluation on the quality and diversity of Pareto fronts generated by NSGA-II, using hypervolume as the primary metric.

**Pareto Front Quality (Hypervolume) - Experiment 1: Obstacle Navigation**

- **Entropy-augmented method (β=0.05):** Achieved a mean final hypervolume of 973.34 ± 142.43.
- **Baseline NSGA-II (β=0.0):** Achieved a mean final hypervolume of 822 ± 164.79.
- **Improvement:** This represents an average increase of approximately 18% in hypervolume for the entropy-augmented method compared to the baseline.
- **Variance:** The results for Experiment 1 exhibited high variance (indicated by the standard error). The authors attributed this high variance to the strong local optimum created by the wall in the environment configuration. Discovering the higher-value POIs behind the wall likely depended heavily on early random initialization and whether agents happened to explore the correct region simultaneously.
- **Interpretation:** Despite the high variance, the entropy augmentation consistently produced higher hypervolume values in most individual runs compared to the baseline. This suggests that incorporating behavioral diversity can be beneficial even in environments with strong local optima, potentially aiding agents in escaping these traps through enhanced exploration.

**Pareto Front Quality (Hypervolume) - Experiment 2: Temporal Tradeoff**

- **Entropy-augmented method (β=0.05):** Achieved a mean final hypervolume of 63.25 ± 5.79.
- **Baseline NSGA-II (β=0.0):** Achieved a mean final hypervolume of 42.67 ± 3.33.
- **Improvement:** This constitutes a substantial average increase of approximately 48% in hypervolume for the entropy-augmented method.
- **Variance:** In contrast to Experiment 1, Experiment 2 showed lower variance in its results.
- **Interpretation:** The lower variance, combined with the significant hypervolume improvement, suggests that the temporal tradeoff domain, which lacked a strong local optimum attractor like the wall in Experiment 1, might be more consistently receptive to the benefits of entropy-augmented exploration.
- **Caveat:** The authors explicitly state that the results for Experiment 2 are considered preliminary due to the limited number of independent trials (n=3) conducted. Further evaluation with more trials is necessary to confirm the robustness and statistical significance of these findings.

**Overall Discussion of Results:**
The findings indicate that introducing a kNN entropy-based bonus into the fitness evaluation can enhance the performance of NSGA-II in multi-agent, multi-objective settings. The improvements in hypervolume suggest that encouraging behavioral diversity allows the evolutionary search to discover a broader range of high-quality, non-dominated solutions. The differing variance between experiments highlights that the method's consistency might be influenced by environmental characteristics, such as the presence and strength of local optima. The parameter β = 0.05 was identified as a promising scaling factor in these initial experiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research proposes a novel method for improving multi-objective policy optimization in multi-agent systems by integrating behavioral diversity into the fitness evaluation process. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**Significance:**

1. **Bridging the Diversity Gap:** The work addresses a recognized limitation in traditional Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs), which primarily focus on diversity in the objective space but often neglect diversity in the behavior space. By augmenting objective fitness with an entropy bonus, the approach attempts to bridge this gap, encouraging behavioral exploration within the existing global Pareto optimization framework.
2. **Preservation of Global Pareto Structure:** Unlike Quality-Diversity methods that optimize across behavioral niches, this method integrates behavioral diversity directly into the fitness of each objective, preserving the global Pareto front structure. This avoids localized competition and allows for a more unified search for globally optimal trade-offs.
3. **Enhanced Exploration and Robustness:** The observed improvements in hypervolume suggest that encouraging behavioral diversity can lead to more comprehensive exploration of the solution space. This is particularly valuable in complex multi-agent environments where premature convergence or getting stuck in local optima can hinder the discovery of truly robust and diverse policy sets.
4. **Applicability to Multi-Agent Systems:** The focus on multi-agent systems, particularly in the context of decentralized execution with centralized training, is relevant for real-world robotic team deployments that require coordinated actions under shared goals and limited communication.
5. **Simplicity of Integration:** The proposed fitness shaping strategy is conceptually straightforward and can be incorporated into existing MOEA frameworks by modifying the fitness evaluation step, potentially making it accessible for broader application.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Improved Real-World Autonomous Systems:** The ability to generate a richer and more diverse set of Pareto-optimal policies can directly benefit real-world applications of autonomous agent teams. This includes marine monitoring, where agents must balance coverage and energy; space exploration, requiring trade-offs between scientific yield and mission duration; and disaster response, where task completion, safety, and resource allocation are critical. Operators could then select policies that best suit their evolving preferences or mission conditions.
2. **Addressing Sparse Reward Challenges:** In environments with sparse or delayed rewards, where objective performance signals are infrequent, behavioral diversity can act as an intrinsic motivator for exploration. This could help agents discover effective behaviors that eventually lead to higher objective returns, overcoming exploration-exploitation trade-offs.
3. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work identifies behavioral diversity as a "promising and underexplored direction" for multi-objective multi-agent evolutionary optimization. It provides a preliminary empirical basis for further investigations into different behavioral characterizations, diversity measures, and their optimal integration into various MOEA and reinforcement learning paradigms.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**

1. **Sensitivity to Scaling Factor (β):** The effectiveness of the method is sensitive to the choice of the scaling factor β. An excessively large β can overshadow the original task objectives, distorting the optimization. Determining robust strategies for selecting or adaptively adjusting β is a critical challenge, especially in sparse reward domains.
2. **Need for Broader Evaluation:** The current results, particularly for Experiment 2, are preliminary due to limited trials. Further evaluation across a wider range of random seeds, more diverse multi-agent domains, and various problem settings is required to robustly assess the generality and consistent benefits of the proposed strategy.
3. **Complexity of Environment Configurations:** Future work should explore more complex scenarios, including additional agents, more numerous and varied POIs, and longer episode lengths, to thoroughly test the method's scalability and ability to discover novel behaviors in richer environments.
4. **Alternative Behavioral Characterizations:** While kNN entropy serves as an initial implicit summary of team behavior, exploring alternative behavioral summaries and diversity measures is warranted. These could potentially capture more task-relevant coordination patterns and diverse collaborative behaviors more effectively during the evolutionary search.
5. **Understanding Conditions for Benefit:** Further investigation is needed to identify the specific problem characteristics and conditions under which entropy augmentation provides the most significant benefits, including its sensitivity to environmental dynamics and problem complexity.

In conclusion, this research presents an initial exploration into integrating behavioral diversity into global multi-objective optimization for multi-agent systems. The preliminary findings suggest a promising avenue for improving the quality and breadth of Pareto-optimal solutions, with implications for developing more robust and adaptable autonomous agent teams. However, the identified limitations highlight crucial directions for future research to establish the method's generalizability and practical utility.
