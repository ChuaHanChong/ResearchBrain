# InternVLA-A1.5: Unifying Understanding, Latent Foresight, and Action for Compositional Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> InternVLA-A1.5, developed by the Physical Intelligence Team at Shanghai AI Laboratory, unifies understanding, latent foresight, and action for robotic control by employing a "latent-querying" approach to future prediction, where learnable tokens distill dynamics from a frozen pretrained video model. This framework improves compositional generalization and long-horizon execution on real-world and simulation benchmarks while maintaining real-time inference.

## Problem

- Existing unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often degrade the pretrained VLM's semantic knowledge and instruction-following abilities when heavily focused on generation and action objectives.
- Heterogeneous objectives within unified models (e.g., visual latent regression, flow-matching action prediction, language modeling) can interfere during joint training.
- Learning pixel-level future prediction from scratch is computationally expensive and inefficient, failing to leverage existing spatiotemporal dynamics priors from large video generation models.

## Method

- A ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture unifies vision-language understanding, latent foresight, and action generation, featuring a pretrained **VLM backbone** and a lightweight **unified expert**.
- A multi-stage training pipeline systematically transfers VLM semantics, then introduces continuous action generation via ==flow-matching== and novel ==latent-querying foresight==.
- Foresight is achieved by a small set of learnable **foresight tokens** that condition a *frozen* pretrained video generation model to predict future frames, with back-propagated loss updating only the tokens and upstream expert layers, rather than learning pixel-level generation directly.

## Results

- Achieved superior compositional generalization on held-out instruction bindings in real-world tasks, with **80.5%** success on Insert Tubes and **76.4%** on Move Tubes.
- Demonstrated robust long-horizon execution, obtaining a **76.4%** success rate on the complex MOF chemistry procedure, significantly outperforming baseline methods (e.g., π0.5 at **29.3%**).
- Ranked highest or highly competitive across **six** simulation benchmarks (e.g., **98.9%** average success rate on LIBERO, **84.8%** total score in zero-shot LIBERO-Plus), showing strong performance in diverse manipulation settings, including robustness to visual shifts and bimanual coordination.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling dynamics from frozen pretrained video generation models using compact latent queries effectively inherits spatiotemporal priors, avoiding the computational cost and semantic degradation of pixel-level generation.
> - Careful prompt design and casting diverse inputs and targets into a unified tokenized format with a single next-token loss preserve and enhance the VLM's pretrained representation.
> - The integration of explicit subtask prediction with learned dynamics priors significantly enhances the model's ability for robust long-horizon execution and reasoning about task progress.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robots capable of diverse manipulation under language instructions is a central aim in embodied artificial intelligence. Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Video Generation Models (VGMs) have motivated research into transferring these capabilities to robotic control.

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models leverage semantic priors from pretrained VLM backbones, demonstrating generalization across objects and instructions. Concurrently, Video Action and World Action models focus on predicting future visual states to acquire an understanding of physical dynamics. A growing body of research aims to unify these complementary strengths into a single model. Prior work, such as InternVLA-A1, explored combining future visual states and actions as joint training targets.

However, current unified models exhibit several limitations:
1. **Erosion of VLM Semantics:** The understanding component often deviates from large-scale VQA or language training once heavy generation and action objectives are introduced, leading to a degradation of the pretrained VLM's semantic knowledge and instruction-following ability.
2. **Interference of Heterogeneous Objectives:** The sub-modules within unified models are optimized for diverse objectives (e.g., regressing future visual latents, flow-matching action prediction, language modeling), which can lead to interference during joint training.
3. **Inefficient Future Prediction Learning:** Visual prediction modules are typically trained from scratch to reconstruct future states in pixel space. This approach often fails to leverage the spatiotemporal and dynamics priors inherent in large pretrained video generation models, incurring significant computational cost.

InternVLA-A1.5 addresses these limitations by proposing a different strategy for integrating semantic understanding, physical dynamics, and action generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of InternVLA-A1.5 are to:
1. **Preserve and enhance the semantic and instruction-following abilities** of the underlying VLM backbone.
2. **Reduce interference among heterogeneous objectives** during joint training.
3. **Exploit the dynamics priors of pretrained video generation models** without incurring the full computational cost of pixel-level generation.
4. **Enable strong compositional generalization** on unseen instruction bindings.
5. **Facilitate robust long-horizon execution** in complex manipulation tasks.
6. **Maintain real-time inference speed** for practical robotic control.

These objectives are motivated by the identified limitations in previous unified VLA models. The paper seeks a method to inject knowledge of world dynamics into a robot policy without degrading its semantic abilities or requiring the policy to learn pixel-level future generation from first principles. By addressing these points, InternVLA-A1.5 aims to contribute to the development of more general-purpose and robust robotic manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InternVLA-A1.5 adopts a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture designed to unify vision-language understanding, visual foresight, and action generation.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The architecture comprises two main components:
- **Pretrained VLM Backbone:** Qwen-3.5 2B is used for multimodal perception and reasoning. It employs a hybrid attention mechanism, interleaving three Gated DeltaNet linear attention layers with one standard full attention layer.
- **Lightweight Unified Expert:** This component shares the architectural blueprint of the VLM backbone but operates with a reduced hidden dimension (460M parameters). It interacts with the VLM only through the shared full attention layer, maintaining separate Gated DeltaNet layers for modality-specific processing.

#### 3.2. Input Formulation

At each timestep, multi-view camera observations ($o_t$) and a language instruction ($l$) are encoded into visual and text tokens. Robot proprioceptive state ($q_t$) is discretized and appended as discrete tokens, along with a control mode token. This input structure is designed to preserve the native representation capabilities of the pretrained VLM.

#### 3.3. Multi-stage Training Pipeline

The training process is divided into multiple stages:

##### 3.3.1. Pretraining Stage 1: VLM Transferring

- **Objective:** To transfer and preserve the VLM's understanding capabilities for robotic control.
- **Methodology:** The VLM backbone is co-trained on a mixture of Vision-Question-Answer (VQA) data and large-scale robot manipulation data. Both data types are cast into a unified tokenized format.
- **Targets:** The model is supervised to predict question answers (for VQA), the next subtask, and a discrete action chunk tokenized via the FAST tokenizer. Action tokens are appended to the VLM vocabulary and share the same embedding table and language head.
- **Loss Function:** A standard next-token cross-entropy loss is applied over the label portion of each sequence. The autoregressive factorization naturally conditions action prediction on the predicted subtask.

##### 3.3.2. Pretraining Stage 2: Foresight and Action Generation

- **Objective:** To introduce continuous action generation and incorporate future prediction without explicit pixel-level generation.
- **Unified Expert:** The lightweight unified expert is introduced.
- **Action Generation:** Continuous action chunks are generated using a flow-matching-based objective. The unified expert predicts a velocity field to transport Gaussian noise to the target action chunk.
- **Foresight Reasoning:** This is a core innovation. Instead of generating future pixels, InternVLA-A1.5 recasts future prediction as a latent-querying problem:
 - A small set of learnable **foresight tokens** attend to the shared multimodal context within the unified expert.
 - These tokens produce compact latent code embeddings, which serve as conditioning signals for a **frozen pretrained video generation model (WAN2.2-5B)**.
 - The video generation model is used to predict future frames (e.g., 4 frames) based on this latent code.
 - A video prediction loss is back-propagated through the frozen generator, updating only the foresight tokens and the upstream unified expert layers. This process teaches the foresight tokens to encode future-relevant information that can steer the video model, thereby inheriting spatiotemporal dynamics priors without learning pixel-level generation.
 - Crucially, the video generation branch is **discarded at inference time**, introducing no additional latency to action prediction. The foresight tokens, now encoding future-aware signals, are reused to condition the continuous action generation.
- **Training Objective:** The Stage 2 objective combines the VLM next-token supervision (from Stage 1) with the video supervision loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{video}}$) and the action flow-matching loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{action}}$), with $\mathcal{L}_{\text{video}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{action}}$ weighted by $\alpha=1$ and $\beta=10$ respectively.

##### 3.3.3. Post-training Stage

This stage follows the same recipe as Stage 2, allowing optional fine-tuning of foresight tokens on downstream demonstrations.

#### 3.4. Attention Masking Pattern

InternVLA-A1.5 employs a hybrid attention masking pattern:
- **VLM Part:** Follows the causal attention pattern of Qwen-3.5 for autoregressive language modeling (image, instruction, state, subtask tokens).
- **Unified Expert Part:** Uses group-wise causal attention across token groups (foresight tokens attend to VLM context; noisy action embeddings attend to VLM context and foresight tokens). Within each action-expert group, bidirectional attention is used.
- **Training Specifics:** During training, the unified expert is masked from attending to FAST action tokens to prevent information leakage and gradient interference. Position IDs for unified expert tokens are assigned as if appended after the optional subtask span.
- **Inference Specifics:** FAST action tokens are not decoded. The unified expert reuses the KV cache of the VLM context, optimizing repeated flow-sampling steps.

#### 3.5. Training Protocol and Hyperparameters

AdamW optimizer is used. Stage 1 pretraining runs for 300K steps, Stage 2 for 600K steps, both with a constant learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and batch size of 1024. Post-training involves 60K steps with a cosine decay learning rate from $5 \times 10^{-5}$ to $5 \times 10^{-6}$ and batch size of 128. Foresight tokens are set to 50.

#### 3.6. Data Recipe

InternVLA-A1.5 is trained on two data streams:
- **Robot Manipulation Data:** An aggregate of six sources (one synthetic, five real-world), totaling 1.2M episodes and 861M frames. Sources include InternData-A1, AgiBotWorld, UMI, DROID, Galaxea, and RoboMind 1.0. This data provides continuous action chunks, future frames for latent foresight, and FAST-tokenized discrete actions for the VLM.
- **Multimodal Co-training Data:** The InternVLA-M1 corpus, containing approximately 3M samples across four categories: General QA, Box QA (referring detection), Point QA (localization), and Trajectory QA (waypoint prediction). This stream helps maintain the VLM's instruction-following and spatial-grounding abilities.
- **Sampling Strategy:** A two-level grouped sampling scheme is used for robot data to balance different sources. The robot corpus and multimodal corpus are sampled at a fixed 0.15:0.85 ratio, prioritizing VLM semantic reinforcement while still driving action and foresight learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InternVLA-A1.5 demonstrates competitive or superior performance across a range of simulation and real-world benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Real-world Experiments

Evaluations were conducted on four tasks: Sort Tubes, Insert Tubes, Move Tubes (all instruction-following with held-out bindings), and MOF (long-horizon chemistry procedure).
- **Overall Performance:** InternVLA-A1.5 achieved the best success rate on Insert Tubes (80.5%), Move Tubes (76.4%), and MOF (76.4%), and was comparable to the leading baseline on Sort Tubes (75.9% vs. 77.8% for $\pi_{0.5}$).
- **Compositional Generalization:** On held-out instruction bindings, InternVLA-A1.5 obtained the best success rates across all three instruction-following tasks, indicating an ability to generalize beyond seen combinations.
- **Long-Horizon Execution:** For the MOF task, InternVLA-A1.5 achieved a 76.4% success rate, significantly outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ (29.3%) and Motus (0.0%). This is attributed to explicit subtask prediction and learned dynamics priors, which assist in tracking task progress and reasoning about environmental changes.
- **Inference Speed:** With the video generation branch discarded at deployment, InternVLA-A1.5 achieved real-time control, with one inference step taking approximately 0.1s on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU.

#### 4.2. Simulation Benchmarks

InternVLA-A1.5 was evaluated on six simulation benchmarks covering single-arm, bimanual, mobile manipulation, and zero-shot generalization tests.
- **SimplerEnv (WidowX):** Achieved an average success rate of 80.8%, the highest among compared methods, outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ by 23.7 points.
- **LIBERO:** Achieved a 98.9% average success rate, the highest among all methods, consistently ranking first or second across all four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), indicating balanced skill proficiency.
- **LIBERO-Plus (Zero-shot):** Obtained the highest total score of 84.8% in zero-shot evaluation, leading on background, noise, and camera perturbations, demonstrating robustness to visual and layout distribution shifts.
- **RoboTwin 2.0:** Achieved the best average of 93.2%, with performance remaining consistent between clean (93.3%) and domain-randomized (93.0%) settings, confirming robust bimanual coordination.
- **DOMINO:** Surpassed all baselines in the zero-shot setting with a 27.7% success rate and further improved to 29.3% after fine-tuning, indicating effectiveness in modeling dynamic object interactions.
- **EBench:** Delivered the best success rate across Val-Train, Val-Unseen, and Test splits, demonstrating capability in long-horizon indoor mobile manipulation.

#### 4.3. Analysis

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing the video supervision loss led to consistent performance degradation across LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin, and DOMINO, with more pronounced drops in zero-shot settings (e.g., LIBERO-Plus and DOMINO). This suggests improved invariance to visual perturbations and strengthened future-state awareness.
 - Removing learnable foresight tokens also resulted in performance degradation across all benchmarks, indicating their role as an interface for distilling structured dynamics knowledge from the pretrained video model.
- **Training Efficiency:** InternVLA-A1.5 exhibited faster convergence and achieved lower final training loss compared to $\pi_{0.5}$ and InternVLA-A1 on RoboTwin, suggesting that the pretraining approach yields a more favorable optimization landscape for downstream adaptation.
- **World Model Exploitation:** Qualitative visualizations of future rollouts conditioned on learned foresight embeddings showed plausible multi-step predictions across standard and zero-shot settings. The model accurately tracked articulated robot motion, manipulated object trajectories, and modeled downstream environmental effects (e.g., liquid level changes), reflecting an understanding of causal scene evolution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InternVLA-A1.5 contributes to the field of embodied intelligence by proposing a unified framework that effectively integrates vision-language understanding, visual foresight, and action generation. Its design choices address key limitations of previous unified models.

The primary significance lies in its "latent-querying" approach to future prediction, where a small set of foresight tokens distills task-relevant future information from a frozen pretrained video generation model. This avoids the high computational cost and semantic degradation associated with learning pixel-level generation from scratch while still inheriting valuable spatiotemporal and dynamics priors.

The findings demonstrate practical benefits:
- **Enhanced Compositional Generalization:** The preserved VLM semantics and foresight mechanism contribute to robust performance on held-out instruction bindings in real-world tasks.
- **Improved Long-Horizon Execution:** The explicit subtask prediction and learned dynamics priors facilitate sustained execution of complex, multi-step procedures.
- **Real-time Inference:** By discarding the video generation branch at deployment, the model maintains a real-time control speed, suitable for practical robotic applications.

The work also suggests two methodological insights:
1. The importance of **prompt design** for preserving VLM's pretrained representation, by casting diverse inputs and targets into a unified tokenized format with a single next-token loss.
2. The effectiveness of **querying pretrained generative models** with compact latent codes for future prediction, rather than requiring the policy to learn pixel-level generation, thus leveraging existing world knowledge efficiently.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge two limitations: the foresight supervision currently spans only a short horizon (one action chunk), limiting long-horizon imagination or explicit planning; and the frozen, generic video generator may not fully cover embodied scenes. These areas are identified for future research.
