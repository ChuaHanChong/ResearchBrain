# WorldKV: Efficient World Memory with World Retrieval and Compression

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldKV is a training-free memory framework for autoregressive video world models. World Retrieval stores evicted KV-cache chunks in GPU/CPU memory and reinserts viewpoint-relevant ones via camera/action similarity; World Compression prunes chunk tokens redundant with an anchor frame by key-key similarity, halving storage. On Matrix-Game-2.0 and LingBot-World-Fast it matches or beats full-KV revisit fidelity at roughly 2x throughput.

## Problem

- Full KV-cache attention preserves revisit consistency but memory and attention cost grow linearly with rollout, causing throughput collapse and out-of-memory during long interactive generation.
- Sliding-window inference restores real-time speed but evicts caches encoding earlier viewpoints, so revisited scenes drift or hallucinate new content instead of staying persistent.
- Existing memory-augmented world models need trained memory banks, backbone fine-tuning, or explicit 3D reconstruction that adds inference latency and architectural coupling.

## Method

- **WorldKV**'s ==World Retrieval== keeps evicted ==KV-cache chunks== indexed by camera pose or accumulated discrete actions, then reinserts the ==top-k most view-similar chunks== natively into the attention window without re-encoding.
- ==World Compression== treats each chunk's first frame as ==anchor==, scores non-anchor keys by mean cosine ==key-key similarity== to anchor keys, and prunes redundant high-similarity tokens per layer.
- A ==four-region sliding window== of 18 latent frames partitions into ==sink==, ==retrieved==, ==recent==, and denoising chunks; retrieval distance sums normalized translation L2 and rotation ==geodesic== terms.

## Results

- On LingBot-World-Fast, WorldKV reaches LPIPS **0.455** / PSNR **15.660** / FID **75.644** at **4.78 FPS**, near full-KV fidelity (**0.441** / **15.901**) at roughly double its **2.36 FPS**.
- On Matrix-Game-2.0, WorldKV beats full KV on every metric (LPIPS **0.462** vs **0.529**, PSNR **14.101** vs **13.748**, FID **93.561** vs **124.912**) while running **16.25 FPS** versus **7.82**.
- Ablations: compressing **6** chunks into a **3**-chunk budget beats uncompressed 3-to-3 retrieval; anchor-only (3 to 1.0) degrades; camera/action retrieval outperforms query-based (LPIPS **0.455** vs **0.490**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A model's own KV cache is already latent long-term world memory, so consistency can be an inference-time access problem rather than an architecture or training problem.
> - More history at lower fidelity beats less history at full fidelity: broader retrieval coverage under a fixed attention budget improves revisit consistency more than uncompressed chunks.
> - Full-history attention is not the ceiling; on short-context-trained backbones it dilutes attention with degraded out-of-distribution caches that selective retrieval avoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of autoregressive video diffusion models, specifically those designed for interactive world generation. These models have demonstrated capabilities in producing action-conditioned visual streams in real-time, which has applications in gaming, embodied AI, and robotic simulation. A primary challenge in this domain is maintaining a persistent world, meaning that content remains consistent when previously visited viewpoints are revisited.

Prior approaches to achieving persistence and long-term memory in world models include:
- **Full KV-cache attention:** Directly using the entire history of Key-Value (KV) caches in the transformer architecture has been shown to preserve spatial and temporal consistency. However, this method incurs a linearly increasing memory footprint and computational cost with rollout length, making it impractical for real-time, long-horizon generation due to out-of-memory errors and significant throughput degradation.
- **Sliding-window inference:** This standard technique addresses computational costs by limiting the attention window to recent frames, evicting older KV-cache chunks. While it maintains real-time throughput, it inherently discards long-term memory, leading to content hallucination or drift when revisiting previously seen areas.
- **Memory-augmented architectures:** Several works have explored dedicated memory modules, such as external memory banks retrieved via cross-attention, spatial compression of historical data, or explicit 3D scene representations that condition the video model on reconstructed geometry. These methods generally require specific training or fine-tuning of backbone models and, in the case of 3D representations, may introduce reconstruction latency.
- **KV Cache Management in LLMs:** In large language models (LLMs), fixed-budget KV cache management has been studied using positional heuristics, attention scores, and query-aware retrieval to mitigate the bottleneck of linearly growing sequence length. However, these methods are not directly designed for the dense spatiotemporal generation context of video models.

This paper distinguishes itself by focusing on a training-free framework that leverages the existing KV cache of pre-trained autoregressive video diffusion models as an emergent form of world memory. It addresses the limitations of both full KV-cache attention (cost) and sliding-window inference (consistency loss) without requiring architectural changes or dedicated memory training. The approach builds on the observation that the KV cache already functions as a visual memory and aims to make this memory efficiently accessible.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient framework that enables autoregressive video world models to sustain a persistent and consistent explorable world over long horizons, while maintaining real-time inference speeds. This objective is motivated by several identified challenges:

1. **Maintaining Consistency in Interactive Worlds:** For interactive applications such as gaming or embodied AI, users or agents need to navigate freely and expect consistency when revisiting previously explored areas. Existing models under sliding-window inference fail to achieve this, as past scene information is evicted, leading to visual inconsistencies, hallucinations, or content drift.

2. **Computational and Memory Cost of Full KV-cache Attention:** The paper highlights that while attending to the full history of KV caches (Full KV) can preserve consistency, its computational cost and memory footprint grow linearly with rollout length. This rapid growth quickly leads to GPU VRAM capacity exceedance and significant degradation of inference speed, making real-time interactive generation impractical. For example, on LingBot-World-Fast, the FPS drops from 8.87 to 3.61 over a one-minute rollout with full KV-cache attention.

3. **Emergent Memory in KV Caches:** A critical motivation stems from the empirical observation that, even in models not explicitly trained on long sequences (e.g., Matrix-Game-2.0 trained on short 6-frame windows), the full KV cache contains emergent long-term visual memory. When sliding-window restrictions are removed, these models can successfully reproduce previously seen viewpoints. This suggests that the memory is inherent in the KV cache; the challenge is accessing it efficiently.

4. **Limitations of Existing Memory Solutions:** Current memory-augmented architectures often require dedicated training or fine-tuning of the backbone model, which can be resource-intensive or introduce inference latency (e.g., 3D reconstruction methods). The authors sought a training-free solution to integrate long-term memory capabilities into existing pre-trained world models without modifying their core architecture or requiring additional training.

To address these motivations, the key objectives for WorldKV were:
- To preserve evicted KV-cache chunks from the active attention window for future retrieval.
- To selectively retrieve only scene-relevant historical KV-cache chunks to bound the active attention window, thereby maintaining real-time inference speed.
- To reduce the storage cost of these historical KV-cache chunks to enable storage of a longer history under fixed memory budgets.
- To achieve these goals without requiring any re-encoding, architectural changes, or fine-tuning of the base world models.
- To demonstrate that such a training-free framework can match or exceed the memory fidelity of full KV-cache attention and compete with memory-trained baselines, while significantly improving throughput.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WorldKV framework is designed to operate training-free on top of existing autoregressive video world models that utilize sliding-window inference. It consists of two complementary components: World Retrieval and World Compression.

### 4. World Retrieval

World Retrieval addresses the problem of accessing relevant past visual memory without incurring the cost of full-history attention.

- **Attention Sparsity Analysis:** The authors first analyzed the attention patterns of autoregressive world models (Matrix-Game-2.0 and LingBot-World-Fast) when processing camera/action sequences involving revisits. They observed that models do not simply attend to the most recent caches but rather reuse past KV chunks whose viewpoints correspond to the current frame. This "view-correspondence pattern" suggests that only a compact set of viewpoint-relevant KV chunks is necessary to preserve crucial context.

- **Mechanism:**
 1. **Storage of Evicted Chunks:** When KV-cache chunks are evicted from the active attention window by the sliding-window mechanism, instead of being discarded, they are stored in GPU or CPU memory. Each stored chunk is indexed by its corresponding camera/action state (e.g., absolute pose for camera models, accumulated discrete actions for keyboard action models).
 2. **Attention Window Partitioning:** The active sliding window is conceptually partitioned into four regions:
 - **Sink KV caches:** Initial frames serving as a visual anchor.
 - **Retrieved KV caches:** Chunks selected from the stored history.
 - **Recent KV caches:** Immediately preceding frames.
 - **Denoising chunk:** The frame(s) currently being generated.
 3. **Selective Retrieval:** At generation time, given the current camera/action state ($a_{cur}$), the system queries the stored history. It selects the top-$k$ most relevant chunks by computing a similarity score between $a_{cur}$ and the stored camera/action states ($a_i$). The framework is agnostic to the specific retrieval algorithm; in this work, a combined distance based on squared L2 distance in translation and geodesic distance in rotation (for camera models) or accumulated WASD/yaw/pitch commands (for keyboard models) is used.
 4. **Native Insertion:** The selected $k$ retrieved KV-cache chunks are inserted directly back into the active attention window. This process requires no re-encoding or architectural modifications, allowing the existing transformer layers to attend to them natively.

### 5. World Compression

World Compression aims to reduce the storage footprint of evicted KV-cache chunks, making it feasible to store a longer history for retrieval and enabling broader retrieval coverage.

- **Motivation:** A single 3-frame chunk in LingBot-World-Fast can occupy approximately 3.4GB across all transformer layers. Storing all chunks for a one-minute rollout would exceed typical VRAM capacities (e.g., >200GB). The observation is that temporally adjacent frames often share substantial visual content, leading to near-duplicate KV caches that encode redundant information.

- **Key-Key Similarity as Redundancy Measure:** The method uses Key-Key cosine similarity to identify redundant tokens within a short temporal chunk (e.g., 3 frames). Keys from spatiotemporally overlapping regions are expected to exhibit high similarity, while keys from newly revealed or dynamic regions diverge. Low similarity therefore indicates distinctive content that should be preserved.

- **Mechanism:**
 1. **Anchor Frame Designation:** For a chunk of $F$ consecutive frames, the first frame is designated as the anchor.
 2. **Redundancy Measurement:** For each key $k^{(f)}_j$ in a non-anchor frame $f$, its redundancy is measured as its average cosine similarity to all key vectors $K^{(a)}$ from the anchor frame at the same transformer layer.
 3. **Token Pruning:** The system pools these similarity scores across all non-anchor frames within the chunk. It then retains the bottom $P\%$ of these non-anchor tokens (i.e., those with the lowest similarity scores) as they are deemed to carry distinctive content not present in the anchor. The remaining high-similarity tokens are pruned.
 4. **Compressed Chunk Formation:** The compressed chunk consists of all tokens from the anchor frame plus the retained low-similarity tokens from the non-anchor frames. For example, with $F=3$ and $P=25\%$ retention, a 3-frame chunk (3T tokens) is reduced to approximately 1.5T tokens, achieving a 2x storage efficiency.
 5. **Layer-Independent Compression:** Compression is applied independently per transformer layer, recognizing that token importance varies across layers. This process occurs once per chunk at the time of storage.

### 6. Implementation Details

- A sliding window of 18 latent frames was used, divided into 3 sink frames, 9 retrieval frames, 3 recent frames, and 3 denoising frames.
- World Compression retained the full anchor frame and 25% of tokens from non-anchor frames within each 3-frame chunk.
- The camera/action-based retrieval strategy computed a combined distance from normalized L2 distance in translation and geodesic distance in rotation. For Matrix-Game-2.0, WASD and yaw/pitch commands were accumulated into pseudo-translation and pseudo-rotation vectors.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WorldKV was conducted on two autoregressive video world models of different scales: LingBot-World-Fast (14B parameters) and Matrix-Game-2.0 (1.3B parameters). The benchmark comprised 60 scene-trajectory pairs with diverse visual domains and at least one loop-closure event for revisit consistency evaluation. Metrics included LPIPS (lower is better), PSNR (higher is better), SSIM (higher is better), and FID (lower is better), along with throughput (FPS).

### 8. Quantitative Comparison (Table 1)

- **Sliding Window (SW) Baselines:** Both models performed poorly under SW inference across all consistency metrics (LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM, FID), confirming its inability to maintain long-term memory.
- **Full KV-cache Attention:**
 - **LingBot-World-Fast:** Full KV exhibited strong memory performance (LPIPS: 0.441, PSNR: 15.901, SSIM: 0.472, FID: 85.705), but with a significantly reduced throughput (2.36 FPS on H200, 3.83 FPS on B200), breaking real-time constraints.
 - **Matrix-Game-2.0:** Full KV showed better consistency than SW but was still notably worse than WorldKV. The paper suggests this is because Matrix-Game-2.0 was not trained on long sequences, leading to accumulated errors when attending to out-of-distribution or degraded historical KV caches.
- **WorldKV Performance:**
 - **Throughput:** WorldKV consistently maintained throughput close to SW inference (LingBot-World-Fast: 4.78 FPS on H200, 7.71 FPS on B200; Matrix-Game-2.0: 16.25 FPS), approximately doubling that of Full KV.
 - **Memory Fidelity:**
 - **LingBot-World-Fast:** WorldKV closely matched Full KV's memory fidelity (LPIPS: 0.455, PSNR: 15.660, SSIM: 0.463, FID: 75.644), with a slightly better FID score.
 - **Matrix-Game-2.0:** WorldKV outperformed both SW and Full KV across all metrics (LPIPS: 0.462, PSNR: 14.101, SSIM: 0.405, FID: 93.561), demonstrating improved consistency even for models not trained for long contexts.
- **Comparison to Memory-Trained Baselines:** WorldKV, being training-free, achieved competitive performance against memory-trained baselines like WorldPlay (8B) and Yume-1.5 (5B). For LingBot-World-Fast, WorldKV outperformed WorldPlay and Yume-1.5 on LPIPS, PSNR, and FID, with comparable SSIM. For Matrix-Game-2.0, WorldKV showed competitive results.

### 9. Qualitative Results (Figure 5 & Appendix A)

- **Consistency:** WorldKV demonstrated the ability to recover scene appearance with high fidelity during revisits, closely matching Full KV on LingBot-World-Fast and producing sharper, more consistent results than Full KV on Matrix-Game-2.0, where Full KV degraded over long horizons.
- **Outperformance of Full KV:** Appendix A illustrated cases where WorldKV reconstructed revisited scenes more faithfully than Full KV, hypothesized to be due to reduced attention dilution by selectively retrieving viewpoint-relevant chunks and pruning redundant information.
- **Sliding Window Drift:** As expected, SW inference consistently showed visible content drift and hallucination.

### 10. Ablation Studies

- **Intra-Chunk Compression Ratio (Table 2 - Top):**
 - Retaining only the anchor frame (3 → 1.0 frame-equivalents) yielded lower performance, confirming that non-anchor tokens contain distinctive information.
 - Moderate compression (e.g., 3 → 1.5 or 3 → 2.0) achieved strong performance, indicating effective preservation of informative entries.
- **Inter-Chunk Compression (Table 2 - Bottom):**
 - Under a fixed 3-chunk retrieval budget, compressing 6 chunks into 3 chunk-equivalents (6 → 3) outperformed uncompressed retrieval of only 3 chunks (3 → 3). This suggests that broader historical coverage (enabled by compression) is more beneficial than preserving fewer chunks at full resolution.
 - Aggressive compression (9 → 3) degraded performance, likely by discarding too much distinctive non-anchor information.
- **Retrieval Algorithm Ablations (Appendix C):** Both camera/action-based retrieval and query-based retrieval significantly outperformed sliding-window inference. Camera/action-based retrieval consistently performed best, suggesting viewpoint correspondence is a strong signal for interactive world models.
- **Increasing Retrieval Chunk Size (Appendix D):** Generally, increasing the number of retrieved chunks improved memory fidelity across metrics, further motivating World Compression as a method to fit more historical chunks within a fixed attention budget.

### 11. Generalization (Appendix E)

- WorldKV was successfully applied to Inspatio-World, a video-to-video 4D world model not used in the main evaluation. It enabled Inspatio-World to preserve long-term memory consistency across revisits, demonstrating the framework's generalizability beyond the specifically evaluated base models.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

The WorldKV framework presents a significant contribution to the field of autoregressive video world models by offering a training-free solution for efficient long-term memory. Its primary significance lies in enabling consistent scene revisits in interactive environments while maintaining real-time inference speeds, a critical balance that previous methods struggled to achieve simultaneously.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Training-Free Approach:** WorldKV integrates memory capabilities without requiring architectural modifications, fine-tuning, or distillation of pre-trained models. This reduces development overhead and makes the approach readily applicable to a wide range of existing and future KV-cache-based world models.
- **Improved Efficiency:** By selectively retrieving relevant KV-cache chunks and compressing redundant tokens, WorldKV maintains throughput comparable to sliding-window inference, significantly outperforming full KV-cache attention, which degrades to sub-real-time speeds for longer rollouts.
- **Enhanced Memory Fidelity:** The framework effectively leverages emergent memory within KV caches, matching or exceeding the consistency of full KV-cache attention in some cases (e.g., Matrix-Game-2.0, LingBot-World-Fast on specific revisits) and significantly outperforming sliding-window inference. It also demonstrates performance competitive with memory-trained baselines.
- **Reduced Memory Footprint:** World Compression substantially reduces the storage requirements for historical KV caches, allowing twice as much history to be stored under the same memory budget, thereby extending the accessible memory horizon.
- **Generalizability:** The successful application to Inspatio-World indicates that WorldKV is a broadly applicable memory management solution for various KV-cache-based autoregressive world models.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Interactive Simulation and Gaming:** WorldKV could facilitate more immersive and persistent virtual environments in gaming and interactive simulations, where consistent revisits to previously explored areas are crucial for user experience.
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to maintain stable and consistent world models over long durations is vital for embodied AI agents and robotic systems operating in complex environments. WorldKV could enable more robust navigation, planning, and interaction by ensuring reliable recall of past observations.
- **Accessibility of Long-Horizon Generation:** By reducing the computational and memory costs associated with long-term memory, WorldKV makes real-time, long-horizon world generation more accessible and practical, potentially broadening the adoption and development of such models.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
- **Bounded Visual Fidelity:** As an inference-time memory management method operating on a frozen backbone, WorldKV's visual fidelity is ultimately constrained by the generation quality of the underlying pretrained world model. Error accumulation in autoregressive video generation may still lead to visual artifacts in very long rollouts, suggesting a potential future direction combining WorldKV with training strategies for more stable multi-minute generation.
- **CPU Offloading Latency:** While CPU offloading offers a method to bound VRAM consumption regardless of rollout length, the host-device transfer latency for retrieving chunks currently prevents real-time generation. Future work on reducing this latency could enable real-time, multi-minute generation under bounded VRAM, expanding the practical applicability of the framework.

In conclusion, WorldKV provides an efficient and effective solution to the challenge of long-term consistency in autoregressive video world models, demonstrating that significant improvements in memory fidelity and throughput can be achieved through intelligent KV-cache management without requiring additional training resources.
