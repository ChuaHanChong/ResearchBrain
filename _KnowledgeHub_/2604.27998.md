# Latent-GRPO: Group Relative Policy Optimization for Latent Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent-GRPO is a Reinforcement Learning framework that optimizes latent reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs), achieving stable performance gains and significantly shorter reasoning chains. It improves average Pass@1 accuracy by 7.86 points on low-difficulty tasks and 14.77 points on high-difficulty tasks over Latent-SFT baselines, while reducing reasoning length by 3-4x compared to explicit GRPO.

## Problem

- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) directly to latent reasoning in LLMs is unstable, often leading to model collapse due to unconstrained exploration in continuous latent space.
- Existing latent RL methods suffer from exploration-optimization misalignment, where gradient updates can inadvertently reduce probabilities for positive-advantage latent components.
- Latent mixture non-closure causes harmful mode averaging when multiple correct latent paths for the same prefix are jointly reinforced, leading to invalid subsequent reasoning steps.

## Method

- ==Invalid Sample Advantage Masking== bounds exploration by zeroing advantages for invalid (non-terminating) trajectories.
- ==One-sided Noise Sampling== ensures a strictly positive perturbation margin for latent components, aligning gradient direction with trajectory-level advantages.
- ==Optimal Correct Path First Token Selection== avoids harmful mode averaging by reinforcing only the initial step of the highest-scoring correct path.

## Results

- **Latent-GRPO** yielded **+7.86 pp** Pass@1 over Latent-SFT on low-difficulty tasks and **+14.77 pp** on high-difficulty tasks.
- Achieves **3-4x shorter reasoning chains** vs. explicit GRPO while maintaining or improving performance.
- Ablations: One-sided Noise Sampling is critical for stability; Optimal Correct Path First Token Selection helps complex tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stable RL for latent reasoning requires explicitly addressing the challenges of off-manifold exploration, misalignment between exploration and optimization, and mode averaging in continuous latent spaces.
> - A well-defined latent manifold, typically established by supervised fine-tuning, is a prerequisite for successful and stable reinforcement learning in latent reasoning.
> - By carefully managing the stochasticity and optimization targets in the latent space, LLMs can achieve significant reasoning chain compression without sacrificing, and often improving, task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has demonstrated their capacity for complex reasoning tasks, often through the generation of explicit "Chains of Thought" (CoT). While longer reasoning chains can improve performance, this approach introduces computational redundancy, increased latency, and higher costs. This observation has motivated research into more efficient reasoning paradigms.

Latent reasoning has emerged as an alternative, aiming to compress intermediate reasoning steps into continuous vector representations, thereby shortening reasoning chains. Early latent reasoning methods, which directly used hidden states as latent tokens, often achieved compression at the cost of performance degradation. More recent approaches, such as vocabulary-superposition methods (e.g., Latent-SFT), have shown that with proper Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), latent reasoning can match or even exceed explicit SFT performance while substantially reducing chain length.

Building on the success of SFT for latent reasoning, the natural progression is to apply Reinforcement Learning (RL) to further optimize these capabilities. Existing work has laid groundwork for RL in latent spaces, including Gumbel noise injection for exploration and Gumbel re-parameterization for probability density estimation, as seen in Soft-GRPO. However, previous attempts at RL for latent reasoning have encountered significant stability issues, often struggling to outperform standard token-level RL or leading to catastrophic model collapse when attempting broader exploration.

This paper addresses the gap in stable and effective RL frameworks for latent reasoning, particularly by examining Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a widely adopted RL approach for LLMs. It identifies and proposes solutions for the specific challenges that arise when adapting GRPO to the continuous and stochastic nature of latent reasoning, thereby aiming to unlock the full potential of this more efficient reasoning paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent trade-off between reasoning performance and computational efficiency in Large Language Models. While explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has advanced LLM capabilities in complex tasks, its reliance on generating long sequences of discrete tokens leads to substantial computational overhead, increased latency, and inefficient resource utilization. Latent reasoning offers a potential solution by compressing intermediate reasoning steps into continuous representations, thereby shortening the effective "chain" and improving efficiency.

Despite the promise of latent reasoning, particularly after successful Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods like Latent-SFT, its optimization through Reinforcement Learning (RL) has remained a challenge. Existing RL approaches for latent space, such as Soft-GRPO, have exhibited significant instability and have generally failed to consistently outperform standard token-level RL. This instability often manifests as model collapse when exploration is encouraged, indicating fundamental issues in how RL interacts with the continuous, high-dimensional latent space.

Specifically, the authors identify three coupled bottlenecks that prevent stable and effective GRPO application to latent reasoning:

1. **Absence of Intrinsic Latent Manifolds:** Directly applying RL to an explicit model often fails to spontaneously induce a well-defined latent reasoning capability. Such structural foundations must be established beforehand, typically through SFT. Furthermore, unconstrained continuous exploration in RL can easily push the model into "out-of-distribution" (OOD) regions, leading to chaotic or non-terminating generations that are invalid or nonsensical within the reasoning context. This implies a need for mechanisms to keep exploration within a valid latent manifold.

2. **Exploration-Optimization Misalignment:** When using standard Gumbel perturbations to simulate stochastic sampling in the latent space, the gradient update direction can become misaligned with the actual trajectory-level advantage. This means that components of a positive-advantage latent trajectory might inadvertently have their probabilities *reduced*, while components of a negative-advantage trajectory might have their probabilities *increased*. This counter-intuitive behavior hinders effective learning and leads to instability.

3. **Latent Mixture Non-Closure:** Unlike discrete token sampling where multiple correct paths can be reinforced and distinct paths are chosen through sampling, latent reasoning operates with a single continuous latent action. If multiple correct latent paths for the same prefix are jointly reinforced, their continuous representations can be averaged, potentially leading to a "barycentric" state that does not correspond to any valid subsequent reasoning step. This mode-averaging problem is particularly detrimental at the initial reasoning steps where prefixes are identical across trajectories.

The key objective of this work is to address these identified bottlenecks by proposing Latent-GRPO, a novel and stable RL framework tailored for latent-token reasoning. By systematically tackling the issues of off-manifold exploration, optimization misalignment, and harmful mode averaging, the authors aim to enable robust and efficient RL post-training for latent reasoning models, ultimately achieving both improved task performance and significant reasoning chain compression.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Latent-GRPO is designed to address the challenges of off-manifold exploration, exploration-optimization misalignment, and latent mixture non-closure within the context of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for latent reasoning. It builds upon the mathematical framework of Soft-GRPO, which defines a Gumbel-perturbation density for latent tokens and a standard categorical density for explicit tokens, enabling a piecewise policy ratio for mixed continuous-discrete trajectories.

The methodology of Latent-GRPO introduces three core designs:

1. **Invalid Sample Advantage Masking for Bounded Exploration:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Unconstrained Gumbel exploration can push rollout trajectories off the valid latent reasoning manifold established by an initial Latent-SFT model, resulting in chaotic or non-terminating generations. Including these invalid trajectories in GRPO's group-wise advantage normalization contaminates statistics and corrupts updates for valid samples.
 - **Approach:** Latent-GRPO initializes the policy from a Latent-SFT model to provide an initial valid latent manifold. During RL training, it identifies "invalid" trajectories, defined as those that fail to emit an End-of-Sequence (EOS) or stop token before reaching a predefined maximum response length ($L_{max}$).
 - **Mechanism:** The group baseline ($\mu_R$) and standard deviation ($\sigma_R$) for advantage normalization are computed exclusively over the subset of valid trajectories ($G_{valid}$). Invalid trajectories are explicitly assigned a zero advantage ($\hat{A}_{valid,j} = 0$). This masking prevents off-manifold rollouts from influencing the optimization of valid reasoning paths, effectively bounding exploration within a meaningful region.

2. **One-sided Noise Sampling for Aligned Optimization:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** The standard Gumbel-perturbation density used in Soft-GRPO injects unconstrained Gumbel noise ($\xi_i$) which can be negative. This can lead to a "perturbation margin" ($\Delta_i = g'_i - \log p_i(\theta)$) that is negative even for positive-advantage trajectories. This results in exploration-optimization misalignment, where desired components might have their probabilities reduced.
 - **Approach:** One-sided Noise Sampling ensures a strictly positive perturbation margin for each latent component throughout the optimization process.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of using raw Gumbel noise, a "clipped-and-shifted" transformation is applied to i.i.d. standard Gumbel noise to produce $\xi_i^+$. This ensures $\xi_i^+$ is always positive and within a defined range. The target perturbed score $g^*_i = \log p_i(\theta_{old}) + \xi_i^+$ is then always greater than the original log-probability.
 - **Conditional Straight-Through Estimator (STE):** During PPO-style training, repeated updates can cause the current policy's $\log p_i(\theta)$ to exceed the fixed target $g^*_i$, making the effective margin $\Delta_i(\theta)$ negative again. To maintain the one-sided behavior, a conditional STE is applied to this margin. If $\Delta_i(\theta) < 0$, the gradient of $\Delta_i(\theta)$ is flipped (multiplied by -1) in the backward pass, while the forward pass uses the original value. This ensures that even if the target is crossed, optimization continues to push the component probability in the intended "one-sided" direction, aligning the gradient contribution with the trajectory advantage. The resulting log-likelihood, $\log \pi^+_\theta(s_t)$, is interpreted as a surrogate for policy optimization.

3. **Optimal Correct Path First Token Selection for Avoiding Path Harmful Averaging:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** The deterministic nature of continuous latent actions means that jointly reinforcing multiple *correct* latent paths under the *same prefix* can lead to "mode averaging." The averaged latent state might not correspond to any valid continuation, hindering learning. This is particularly problematic at the first reasoning step (t=1) where all trajectories share the initial prompt context.
 - **Approach:** This mechanism selectively reinforces the initial step of only the most promising correct path when multiple correct paths exist for the same prefix.
 - **Mechanism:** From the subset of correct trajectories ($G_{correct}$) in a group, each is scored by its average per-token surrogate log-probability under the rollout objective ($\bar{\ell}_j$). The trajectory with the highest score ($j^*$) is selected as the "optimal correct path." A time-dependent and trajectory-dependent mask ($M_{j,t}$) is then applied. This mask sets the advantage to zero ($\tilde{A}_{j,t} = 0$) for the first latent token (t=1) of any correct trajectory *other than* $j^*$. For all other steps (t > 1) and for the entire optimal path ($j^*$), the full valid advantage ($\hat{A}_{valid,j}$) is retained.
 - This ensures that at the critical first step, only the best-performing correct path's initial latent token is reinforced, preventing mode averaging. Subsequent latent tokens from other correct paths are still optimized, allowing for continued exploration and reinforcement once their prefixes diverge.

**Final Latent-GRPO Objective:**
These three designs are integrated into a modified PPO-style objective function. The final Latent-GRPO objective incorporates the masked advantage ($\tilde{A}_{j,t}$), which reflects Invalid Sample Advantage Masking and Optimal Correct Path First Token Selection, and a modified policy ratio ($r^*_{j,t}(\theta)$) that uses the one-sided Gumbel-margin surrogate log-likelihood ($\log \pi^+_\theta(s_t)$) for latent steps. This objective aims to achieve stable and efficient RL training for latent reasoning by addressing the core challenges identified.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated Latent-GRPO across two regimes: low-difficulty and high-difficulty tasks, using LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Math-7B, respectively. Comparisons were made against explicit reasoning models (SFT and GRPO) and latent reasoning baselines (Latent-SFT and Soft-GRPO). Performance was measured by Pass@1 (accuracy) and reasoning length (#L).

**Overall Comparison:**
Latent-GRPO consistently demonstrated stable performance gains while significantly shortening reasoning chains compared to explicit GRPO. Direct application of standard GRPO to Latent-SFT models generally resulted in training collapse, highlighting the stability challenges addressed by Latent-GRPO.

**Low-difficulty Performance (LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct):**
- **Performance Improvement:** Latent-GRPO achieved an average Pass@1 gain of 7.86 points over its Latent-SFT initialization across GSM8K-Aug, GSM-Hard, SVAMP, and MultiArith. This exceeded the 5.28-point gain of explicit GRPO over its SFT baseline.
- **Reasoning Chain Compression:** Latent-GRPO maintained substantially shorter reasoning chains, averaging 4.44x shorter than explicit GRPO. For instance, on GSM8K-Aug, Latent-GRPO had a length of 23.80 compared to explicit GRPO's 111.1.
- **Outperformance:** Latent-GRPO not only surpassed Soft-GRPO but also outperformed explicit GRPO on most low-difficulty tasks (e.g., 66.29 vs. 62.26 on GSM8K-Aug), while remaining significantly more efficient in terms of reasoning length.
- **Training Stability:** Validation scores showed that Latent-GRPO remained stable over 10k training steps, whereas explicit GRPO showed early collapse. Response length for Latent-GRPO remained controlled.

**High-difficulty Performance (Qwen2.5-Math-7B):**
- **Enhanced Gains:** The benefits of Latent-GRPO were more pronounced on high-difficulty tasks (Math500, AIME24, AIME25, GPQA). The average Pass@1 gain over Latent-SFT was 14.77 points, nearly doubling the relative improvement observed in low-difficulty settings and significantly exceeding the 7.54-point gain of explicit GRPO over SFT.
- **Outperformance of Explicit GRPO:** Latent-GRPO surpassed explicit GRPO by an average of 4.27 Pass@1 points and achieved the best Pass@1 on Math500 (80.40 vs. 75.15), AIME24 (26.56 vs. 16.61), and AIME25 (23.23 vs. 19.79).
- **Continued Compression:** Latent-GRPO maintained substantial reasoning chain compression, being 3.31x shorter on average than explicit GRPO.
- **Training Dynamics:** Training plots on Math500 demonstrated Latent-GRPO's steady improvement in validation performance while maintaining a significantly lower response length throughout training, in contrast to the instability of baselines.

**Ablation Study:**
- **One-sided Noise Sampling:** This component was identified as critical for training stability. Its removal consistently led to a sharp drop in validation performance and eventual model collapse on both low- and high-difficulty tasks, accompanied by uncontrolled response length growth. This supports the hypothesis that aligning optimization direction with advantage sign is essential.
- **Optimal Correct Path First Token Selection:** Removing this design led to a clear, though milder, degradation in performance. While some initial improvement was observed without it, the gains were smaller and training was less stable, especially on high-difficulty tasks. This suggests its importance in mitigating harmful mode averaging at the shared initial reasoning step, which becomes more crucial as task difficulty increases.

**Pass@k Performance of Latent-GRPO:**
- **Strong Sampling Capability:** Under Gumbel sampling during inference (to introduce stochasticity for pass@k evaluation), Latent-GRPO demonstrated strong pass@k capabilities. It yielded stronger pass@k curves than explicit GRPO on mathematical benchmarks.
- **Outperforming GRPO:** On Math500, sampled Latent-GRPO reached 87.8 Pass@64, exceeding GRPO's 84.2. On AIME24 and AIME25, Latent-GRPO significantly outscored GRPO at Pass@64 (e.g., up to 50.0 vs. 23.3 on AIME24).
- **Pass@1 vs. Pass@k Trade-off:** For the most challenging AIME benchmarks, Gumbel sampling caused a drop in Pass@1 compared to the no-sampling mode, but dramatically improved Pass@k performance. This suggests that noise can perturb the model away from its single best path, but also increases the diversity of sampled trajectories, improving the chances of finding a correct answer with multiple attempts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Latent-GRPO as a stable and effective Reinforcement Learning framework for post-training latent reasoning in Large Language Models. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that the efficiency benefits of latent reasoning can be realized and further optimized through RL, without sacrificing or even surpassing the performance of explicit reasoning.

The specific contributions and potential impacts include:

1. **Enabling Stable Latent RL:** Latent-GRPO addresses fundamental instability issues that have plagued prior attempts at applying RL to continuous latent spaces. By identifying and providing targeted solutions for the absence of intrinsic latent manifolds, exploration-optimization misalignment, and latent mixture non-closure, the work establishes a robust methodology for training LLMs to reason efficiently in latent representations.
2. **Dual Benefits: Performance and Efficiency:** The findings indicate that Latent-GRPO not only maintains or improves task performance (Pass@1) compared to strong baselines, including explicit GRPO, but also achieves substantial compression of reasoning chains (3-4x shorter). This dual benefit addresses a critical trade-off in LLM deployment, offering both enhanced capability and reduced computational cost and latency.
3. **Advancement in LLM Reasoning:** By providing a reliable method for RL in latent space, Latent-GRPO contributes to the broader advancement of LLM reasoning capabilities. It suggests a pathway towards models that can reason effectively with internal, compact representations, potentially leading to more sophisticated and human-like reasoning processes.
4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent gains observed across both low- and high-difficulty benchmarks, and on both in-domain and out-of-distribution datasets, suggest that Latent-GRPO fosters a more generalized and robust reasoning ability in LLMs.
5. **Enhanced Exploration and Diversity:** The strong Pass@k performance under Gumbel sampling indicates that Latent-GRPO can effectively leverage stochasticity to explore diverse latent reasoning paths. This capability is particularly valuable for complex problems where multiple valid reasoning trajectories might exist.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed analysis of bottlenecks and the tailored solutions provided offer valuable insights for future research in latent reasoning and RL for LLMs. The ablation studies, in particular, highlight the importance of specific methodological components, guiding subsequent efforts to further refine and stabilize continuous action spaces in generative models.

Overall, Latent-GRPO positions reinforcement learning as a practical and effective route for developing efficient and high-performing latent reasoning capabilities in large language models, potentially influencing the design and deployment of future LLM architectures for complex problem-solving.
