# Scaling Vision Transformers to 22 Billion Parameters

> [!summary] Summary
> A Vision Transformer with 22 billion parameters, ViT-22B, was successfully trained, establishing the feasibility of scaling visual models to the size range of large language models. This model achieves 89.5% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet and demonstrates enhanced few-shot learning, fairness, robustness, and human alignment, properties previously observed in large language models.

## Problem

- Vision models have historically lagged behind Large Language Models (LLMs) in terms of parameter scale, limiting their potential.
- The standard Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture becomes numerically unstable and difficult to train when scaled to extreme parameter counts.
- Training vision models at tens-of-billions-of-parameters scale demands specialized model-parallel infrastructure and efficient hardware utilization, engineering challenges largely unaddressed for vision architectures.

## Method

- Introduced architectural modifications including ==parallel Attention-MLP blocks== for efficiency and ==Query/Key (QK) Normalization== for training stability.
- Eliminated bias terms from QKV projections and LayerNorms to optimize accelerator utilization.
- Employed ==model parallelism== and asynchronous linear operations, training on an extended JFT dataset of approximately 4 billion images using TPUv4 accelerators.

## Results

- Achieved **89.5%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet classification and demonstrated strong performance across various vision tasks including transfer learning, semantic segmentation, and depth estimation.
- Exhibited enhanced few-shot learning capabilities, improved fairness metrics, and increased robustness to distribution shifts.
- Demonstrated a shape bias of **87%** (approaching human perception) and improved reliability metrics such as calibration and consistency, with its knowledge successfully distilled into smaller, more efficient models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling Vision Transformers to 22 billion parameters is feasible, bridging the gap between language and vision model scales.
> - Query/Key (QK) normalization is critical for ensuring training stability and preventing numerical issues in extremely large ViT models.
> - Extreme scaling of vision models unlocks emergent properties, including improved few-shot learning, enhanced fairness, robustness to distribution shifts, and stronger alignment with human visual perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction and Background

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities when scaled to billions of parameters, but vision models have historically lagged behind in terms of scale. This paper presents ViT-22B, a Vision Transformer containing 22 billion parameters, representing a significant advancement in scaling visual models to the size range of large language models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Research Objectives and Contribution

This research bridges the gap between language and vision model scaling, demonstrating that Vision Transformers can successfully operate at a scale previously only seen in language models. The work not only establishes that such scaling is feasible from an engineering perspective but also that it delivers meaningful improvements across various dimensions of model performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Architecture Innovations

To successfully scale ViT to 22 billion parameters, the researchers introduced several critical architectural modifications to the standard Vision Transformer design:

1. **Parallel Attention-MLP Blocks**: Rather than applying attention and MLP blocks sequentially, they're applied in parallel and their outputs are summed. This architectural change enables better parallelization and more efficient computation.

2. **Query/Key (QK) Normalization**: LayerNorm is applied to the queries and keys in the attention mechanism. This was found to be crucial for training stability at scale, as it prevents extreme attention logit values that lead to numerical instability.

3. **Removal of Bias Terms**: The authors removed bias terms from QKV projections and LayerNorms to improve accelerator utilization without affecting model quality.

These architectural changes were developed after discovering that the standard ViT architecture becomes unstable at extreme scales. When attempting to train large ViTs without these modifications, the researchers observed divergent training loss, exploding gradients, and unstable attention values.

#### 3.2. Training Challenges and Solutions

Training a 22-billion parameter vision model presented significant engineering challenges. The researchers developed several techniques to enable efficient training:

1. **Model Parallelism**: The model was partitioned across multiple accelerator devices using a model-parallel system built with JAX's xmap API.

2. **Asynchronous Parallel Linear Operations**: The team developed a technique to overlap computation and communication, significantly improving hardware utilization.

3. **Training Data**: ViT-22B was trained on an extended version of the JFT dataset containing approximately 4 billion images, providing sufficient data to leverage the model's capacity.

4. **TPU Infrastructure**: The model was trained on Google's TPUv4 accelerators, achieving a model flops utilization (MFU) of 54.9%, which is considered high for a model of this scale.

The authors report that the QK normalization was particularly important for training stability. Without this normalization, even small-scale models showed instability, which became catastrophic at larger scales.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Performance on Core Vision Tasks

ViT-22B demonstrates impressive performance across a variety of core computer vision tasks:

1. **Image Classification**: The model achieves 89.5% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, representing a significant improvement over smaller ViT models.

2. **Transfer Learning**: ViT-22B exhibits strong performance when fine-tuned on a diverse set of 25 image classification tasks, consistently outperforming smaller models.

3. **Semantic Segmentation**: When applied to semantic segmentation tasks, ViT-22B delivers state-of-the-art results, demonstrating its ability to perform detailed spatial understanding.

4. **Monocular Depth Estimation**: The model shows strong performance on depth estimation tasks, producing high-quality depth maps that capture scene structure accurately.

These results demonstrate that scaling benefits extend across diverse vision tasks, not just classification, suggesting that the representations learned by ViT-22B are versatile and semantically rich.

#### 4.2. Few-Shot Learning Capabilities

One of the most notable capabilities of large language models is few-shot learning—the ability to perform well with very limited examples. The researchers investigated whether ViT-22B exhibits similar capabilities in the vision domain:

1. **Few-Shot Classification**: ViT-22B shows remarkable few-shot learning abilities, significantly outperforming smaller models on 1-shot, 5-shot, 10-shot, and 25-shot classification tasks.

2. **Emergent Capabilities**: Similar to what has been observed in LLMs, the improvement in few-shot performance appears to be an emergent property that becomes more pronounced as model size increases.

3. **Consistent Advantage**: The advantage of ViT-22B in few-shot scenarios is consistent across nearly all tested datasets, suggesting this is a general property of the model rather than dataset-specific.

These results indicate that scaling vision models to LLM-like sizes unlocks similar emergent capabilities, bridging the gap between language and vision model behaviors.

#### 4.3. Fairness and Robustness

As AI systems are deployed in real-world applications, fairness and robustness become increasingly important. The researchers thoroughly investigated these aspects of ViT-22B:

1. **Demographic Fairness**: ViT-22B demonstrates better fairness metrics on facial attribute prediction tasks, with smaller performance gaps between demographic groups compared to smaller models.

2. **Robustness to Distribution Shifts**: The model shows improved robustness to various types of distribution shifts, including natural corruptions and domain shifts.

3. **Out-of-Distribution Detection**: ViT-22B demonstrates superior ability to detect out-of-distribution examples, an important safety feature for deployed systems.

These findings suggest that scaling not only improves raw performance but also addresses some of the critical challenges in responsible AI deployment.

#### 4.4. Human Alignment

A particularly interesting finding is that ViT-22B exhibits stronger alignment with human visual perception than smaller models:

1. **Shape Bias**: The model demonstrates a strong shape bias of 87%, much closer to human perception (95%) than previous computer vision models, which typically exhibit strong texture bias.

2. **Visual Reasoning**: ViT-22B shows improved performance on tasks requiring visual reasoning and object recognition in challenging contexts.

3. **Interpretable Image Understanding**: The model demonstrates more human-aligned interpretation of ambiguous or unusual images.

This alignment with human perceptual biases wasn't explicitly trained for but emerged naturally with scale, suggesting that larger models may inherently develop more human-like representations.

#### 4.5. Scaling Effects on Model Reliability

The researchers examined how scaling affects various aspects of model reliability:

1. **Calibration**: ViT-22B shows better calibration than smaller models, meaning its confidence scores more accurately reflect its true performance.

2. **Uncertainty Estimation**: The model demonstrates improved uncertainty quantification, an important feature for safety-critical applications.

3. **Consistency**: ViT-22B exhibits greater consistency in its predictions across slight variations of the same input.

These reliability improvements further enhance the model's practicality for real-world applications where predictable behavior is essential.

#### 4.6. Knowledge Distillation

While ViT-22B's size limits its direct deployment in many scenarios, the researchers demonstrated that its knowledge can be effectively distilled into smaller models:

1. **Distillation to Smaller ViTs**: The knowledge from ViT-22B was successfully distilled into ViT-B and ViT-L models, significantly improving their performance.

2. **State-of-the-Art Efficiency**: The distilled models achieve new state-of-the-art results for their parameter counts, showing that many benefits of scale can be transferred to more practical model sizes.

3. **Preservation of Properties**: Some of the improved properties, such as fairness and robustness, are partially preserved in the distilled models.

This approach provides a practical path to deploying the benefits of extreme scaling in real-world applications with resource constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Conclusion

ViT-22B represents a significant milestone in the scaling of vision models, demonstrating that many of the benefits observed in large language models also apply to the vision domain. The successful training of this 22-billion parameter model required architectural innovations, particularly query/key normalization, which was found to be essential for training stability at scale.

The resulting model not only achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks but also exhibits improved few-shot learning, fairness, robustness, reliability, and human alignment. These results suggest that the scaling laws observed in language models extend to vision, and that continued scaling of vision models may unlock further capabilities.

Furthermore, the successful distillation of ViT-22B's knowledge into smaller models provides a practical path to deploying these benefits in resource-constrained environments. This research opens new possibilities for large-scale vision models and narrows the gap between language and vision in the realm of large foundation models.
