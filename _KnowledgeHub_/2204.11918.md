# Google Scanned Objects: A High-Quality Dataset of 3D Scanned Household Items

> [!summary] Summary
> The Google Scanned Objects (GSO) dataset provides over 1,000 high-quality 3D scanned household items, released under an open-source license, to enhance realism in robotics simulation. This collection supports research in object recognition, grasping, and synthetic data generation by bridging the gap between virtual training and real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Training robots effectively requires immense amounts of data, which is costly, time-consuming, and often impractical to collect in the real world.
- Simulation offers an alternative, but its effectiveness for sim-to-real transfer is limited by the scarcity of high-quality, realistic 3D object datasets.
- Existing 3D model databases often face trade-offs between quality, scale, and accessibility due to licensing restrictions.

## Method

- Developed a custom ==3D scanning system== comprising a lighting-controlled enclosure, machine vision cameras, a projector, a DSLR, and a motorized turntable for efficient capture.
- Implemented a software pipeline that includes ==sub-pixel accurate structured light scanning==, ==multi-ray triangulation==, ==HDR image reconstruction==, and advanced ==mesh processing== to generate high-quality 3D models.
- Established a comprehensive ==curation process== for object acquisition, quality filtering, mesh processing, calculating ==physical properties==, generating ==collision volumes==, and creating detailed metadata for each object.

## Results

- The GSO dataset provides **over 1,000** diverse household objects, featuring high visual fidelity and topological accuracy with fine surface details and complex geometries captured.
- All objects are delivered in simulation-ready formats (SDF, collision meshes) with calculated physical properties (mass, center of mass, inertia tensor), compatible with Ignition Gazebo, PyBullet, and MuJoCo.
- The dataset supports robot learning, synthetic data generation, and sim-to-real transfer, offering a cost-effective alternative to real-world data collection and enabling standardized benchmarks for robot perception and manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A tailored scanning pipeline, integrating structured light, HDR imaging, and robust mesh processing, can efficiently produce a large volume of high-fidelity 3D object scans.
> - Meticulous curation, including calculating physical properties and generating simulation-ready formats, is crucial for making 3D datasets immediately usable in robotics simulators.
> - Providing over 1,000 diverse, high-quality, and open-licensed 3D household objects addresses a critical need for realistic virtual environments, enabling better sim-to-real transfer for robot learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The field of robotics faces a significant challenge: training robots effectively requires enormous amounts of data, but collecting such data in the real world is expensive, time-consuming, and often impractical. Simulation offers a compelling alternative, allowing researchers to train robots in virtual environments before deploying them in reality. However, the effectiveness of simulation depends heavily on how realistic those virtual environments are.

The Google Scanned Objects (GSO) dataset addresses this critical need by providing over 1,000 high-quality 3D scanned household items that can be used in robotics simulation. Developed by a team of researchers from Google's Robotics division in collaboration with Open Robotics and Northwestern University, this dataset represents a significant contribution to the robotics and computer vision communities by offering realistic 3D models that bridge the gap between simulation and reality.

### 2. Research Context

The development of the GSO dataset is motivated by several challenges in the current robotics research landscape:

1. **Data scarcity:** While 2D image datasets are abundant, high-quality 3D datasets remain relatively scarce.
2. **Real-world limitations:** Collecting robotic data in real environments is constrained by hardware costs, safety concerns, and the difficulty of labeling such data.
3. **Sim-to-real transfer:** Training robots in simulation and transferring that knowledge to real-world settings requires highly realistic virtual environments.
4. **Open access:** Existing 3D model databases often have licensing restrictions that limit their use in research.

Previous approaches to creating 3D object datasets have included artist-created models, photogrammetry, and various scanning techniques. However, these approaches typically suffer from trade-offs between quality, scale, and accessibility. Artist-created models may lack realism, while many high-quality scanned datasets are not freely available to the research community.

The GSO dataset distinguishes itself by offering a large collection of high-fidelity 3D scans under an open-source license, specifically targeting the needs of the robotics research community.

### 3. The Scanning Pipeline

The GSO team developed a custom 3D scanning system to efficiently capture high-resolution models. This system consists of both specialized hardware and software components:

#### 3.1. Hardware Setup

The scanning hardware consists of:
- A lighting-controlled enclosure to ensure consistent lighting conditions
- Two machine vision cameras for structured light scanning
- A projector for pattern projection
- A DSLR camera for high-resolution texture capture
- A motorized turntable to rotate objects during scanning

#### 3.2. Software Pipeline

The scanning software pipeline treats 3D scanning as a calibration problem and includes:
1. **Pattern projection and capture:** The system projects structured light patterns onto objects and captures images from multiple angles.
2. **Projector-camera synchronization:** Ensuring precise timing between the pattern projection and image capture.
3. **Mesh generation:** Using stereo vision techniques to create 3D point clouds from the captured images.
4. **Mesh alignment and merging:** Combining scans from different angles into a single cohesive 3D model.
5. **Texture mapping:** Applying high-resolution color information to the 3D mesh.

The pipeline incorporates several technical innovations to achieve high-quality results:
- Sub-pixel accurate structured light scanning
- Multi-ray triangulation for improved accuracy
- HDR image reconstruction to handle challenging surface materials
- Advanced mesh processing to create water-tight models

### 4. Dataset Creation and Curation

Creating the GSO dataset involved more than just scanning objects. The team developed a comprehensive curation process to ensure the quality and usability of the 3D models:

1. **Object acquisition:** The team collected over 1,000 household objects representing common items robots might interact with, such as kitchenware, toys, tools, and food items.

2. **Quality filtering:** Objects were filtered based on several criteria:
 - Size limitations (objects needed to fit on the scanning turntable)
 - Material properties (highly reflective or transparent objects posed challenges)
 - Copyright considerations (branded objects required permission)

3. **Mesh processing:** Raw scans underwent several processing steps:
 - Cleaning to remove artifacts and noise
 - Creating water-tight (closed manifold) meshes
 - Correcting distortions caused by reflective surfaces

4. **Physical property calculation:** For each object, the team calculated:
 - Mass based on estimated volume and typical density
 - Center of mass
 - Inertia tensor for realistic physical simulation

5. **Collision volume generation:** Simplified collision volumes were created to enable efficient physics calculations in simulation.

6. **Size reduction:** High-resolution models were decimated to create versions suitable for real-time simulation while preserving visual fidelity.

7. **Metadata creation:** Each object was tagged with descriptive information, including:
 - Object name and category
 - Physical properties
 - Scan quality metrics

### 5. Simulation Integration

A key goal of the GSO dataset was to enable seamless integration with popular robotics simulation platforms. To achieve this, the team:

1. **Created SDF models:** Converted the processed 3D scans into Simulation Description Format (SDF) models, which are widely used in robotics simulators.

2. **Generated collision meshes:** Created simplified versions of the objects for efficient collision detection.

3. **Tested in multiple simulators:** Ensured compatibility with popular simulation platforms including:
 - Ignition Gazebo
 - PyBullet
 - MuJoCo

4. **Hosted on Ignition Fuel:** Deployed the dataset on Open Robotics' Ignition Fuel platform, providing:
 - Easy search and discovery of objects
 - Version control
 - Metadata browsing
 - Direct integration with compatible simulators

This integration work makes the dataset immediately useful for robotics researchers, allowing them to populate simulated environments with realistic household objects for training and evaluation.

### 6. Dataset Characteristics

The GSO dataset offers several notable characteristics that distinguish it from other 3D object collections:

1. **High visual fidelity:** The scans capture fine surface details and textures that are crucial for visual perception tasks.

2. **Topological accuracy:** Complex object geometries, including items with holes, thin structures, and intricate shapes, are preserved in the scans.

3. **Scale and diversity:** With over 1,000 objects spanning multiple categories of household items, the dataset provides broad coverage of objects robots might encounter.

4. **Simulation-ready format:** All objects are provided in formats that can be directly imported into popular simulation environments.

5. **Open licensing:** The dataset is released under a Creative Commons license, allowing for broad use in research and education.

6. **Physical accuracy:** Object properties such as mass, center of mass, and inertia tensors enable realistic physical interactions in simulation.

### 7. Applications and Impact

The GSO dataset enables research across multiple domains:

1. **Robot learning:** Researchers can use the dataset to train robots in simulation for tasks such as:
 - Object recognition and classification
 - Pose estimation
 - Grasping and manipulation
 - Scene understanding

2. **Synthetic data generation:** The models can be used to create synthetic training data for computer vision systems, helping to bridge the reality gap.

3. **Sim-to-real transfer:** By training on realistic 3D models, robots can learn skills that transfer more effectively to real-world scenarios.

4. **Benchmark creation:** The dataset enables the creation of standardized benchmarks for robot perception and manipulation tasks.

5. **Cost reduction:** Using this dataset can significantly reduce the cost of synthetic data generation compared to traditional commercial-grade product photography or manual 3D modeling.

The impact of this work extends beyond the immediate release of the dataset. The scanning methodology and curation pipeline described in the paper provide a blueprint for creating similar datasets in the future, potentially at even larger scales or for specialized domains.

### 8. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its contributions, the GSO dataset has several limitations that present opportunities for future work:

1. **Material properties:** While the dataset captures visual appearance well, it does not include detailed material properties such as friction coefficients or deformation characteristics.

2. **Dynamic objects:** The dataset focuses on rigid objects and does not include articulated or deformable items.

3. **Scanning limitations:** Some object types remain challenging to scan, including transparent, highly reflective, or very dark objects.

4. **Domain coverage:** While broad, the dataset is focused on household items and does not cover all domains relevant to robotics.

Future work could address these limitations by:
- Expanding the dataset to include additional object categories
- Incorporating more detailed material property measurements
- Developing improved scanning techniques for challenging materials
- Adding articulated objects with movable parts

### 9. Conclusion

The Google Scanned Objects dataset represents a significant contribution to the robotics and computer vision communities by providing a large, high-quality collection of 3D scanned household items under an open license. By bridging the gap between simulation and reality, this dataset enables more effective robot learning in virtual environments before deployment in the real world.

The combination of visual fidelity, physical accuracy, and simulation integration makes the GSO dataset particularly valuable for researchers working on robot perception, manipulation, and learning. The open nature of the dataset fosters collaboration and accelerates progress in these fields.

As simulation continues to play an increasingly important role in robotics research, high-quality datasets like GSO will become essential infrastructure for the development of more capable and generalizable robotic systems. By making this dataset freely available to the research community, the authors have taken an important step toward enabling the next generation of robots that can effectively perceive and interact with the complex objects found in human environments.
