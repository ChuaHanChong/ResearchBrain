# Learning Bipedal Walking on a Quadruped Robot via Adversarial Motion Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework is presented for teaching a quadruped robot to walk stably on its two rear legs, using an adversarial motion prior (AMP) and a teacher-student reinforcement learning architecture. This approach enables robust bipedal locomotion on diverse simulated terrains and allows the robot's front limbs to be available for other functions.

## Problem

- Quadruped robots are not mechanically designed for bipedal locomotion, possessing soft, point-contact feet and rear legs with limited range of motion, which inherently reduces stability.
- Traditional model-based control methods for legged robots often lack robustness and generalization in novel environments, while learning-based methods need effective ways to achieve natural, stable gaits and bridge the sim-to-real gap.
- Enabling bipedal locomotion on quadruped robots would free their front limbs for complex manipulation tasks, but this capability is challenging to achieve without external assistance.

## Method

- A teacher-student ==reinforcement learning== framework was developed, leveraging Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) to learn bipedal gaits.
- Reference bipedal walking and running gaits were generated using Trajectory Optimization for Walking Robots (TOWR), integrating inverse kinematics to produce joint space data.
- The teacher policy was trained with privileged information and terrain data, while the student policy used a history of proprioceptive observations to infer this information, aiming to improve robustness and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The robot achieved stable bipedal walking in simulation across various terrains (uniform, wave, sloped, stairs, obstacles) with acceptable tracking of commanded base linear velocities.
- The locomotion policy demonstrated robust recovery from external disturbances, such as a 100N push force, quickly regaining stability.
- An asymmetry in ground reaction forces was observed during bipedal gait, indicating an area for future refinement in achieving more balanced and symmetrical motion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) can effectively train quadruped robots to imitate dynamically feasible, natural bipedal gaits generated by trajectory optimization.
> - A teacher-student architecture, where the student infers privileged information from proprioceptive history, is effective for learning robust blind bipedal locomotion policies on challenging terrains.
> - Domain randomization during training, combined with carefully designed reward functions, contributes to the learned policy's robustness and its potential for sim-to-real deployment.
