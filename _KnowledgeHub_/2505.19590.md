# Learning to Reason without External Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Reinforcement Learning from Internal Feedback (RLIF) and its instantiation, INTUITOR, enabling large language models to enhance reasoning abilities using only intrinsic self-certainty as a reward. The approach achieves performance comparable to supervised methods on in-domain tasks while demonstrating superior generalization to out-of-domain problems like code generation.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is expensive, time-consuming, and prone to human biases due to its reliance on extensive human annotation.
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) demands domain-specific verifiers and gold-standard solutions, limiting its applicability to tasks with objective correctness criteria and hindering generalization.
- Current LLM training paradigms face scalability issues and struggle to enable truly autonomous self-improvement mechanisms for AI systems, particularly as models' capabilities grow beyond human evaluators.

## Method

- A novel paradigm, ==Reinforcement Learning from Internal Feedback (RLIF)==, is proposed, where LLMs learn from intrinsic, self-generated signals rather than external rewards or labeled data.
- INTUITOR, an instantiation of RLIF, uses the model's own =='self-certainty'== as the sole intrinsic reward, defined as the average KL divergence between a uniform distribution and the model's next-token distribution.
- The self-certainty reward is integrated into the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework==, where scores are normalized within sampled groups to update the policy, creating a self-reinforcing learning loop.

## Results

- INTUITOR achieved performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500) comparable to supervised GRPO, using only intrinsic rewards.
- It demonstrated superior out-of-domain generalization, showing a **65%** relative improvement on LiveCodeBench and a **76%** gain on CRUXEval-O for code generation tasks compared to GRPO.
- Models fine-tuned with INTUITOR exhibited enhanced instruction-following capabilities and improved self-assessment, consistently surpassing GRPO in Length Control Win Rate on AlpacaEval and showing stronger statistical separation between correct/incorrect answer self-certainty scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing for intrinsic self-certainty encourages LLMs to generate more structured, coherent, and long-form reasoning steps, leading to improved problem-solving processes.
> - Online self-certainty, where the reward signal evolves with the policy, effectively prevents 'reward hacking' and maintains stable training dynamics, unlike fixed offline reward functions.
> - Intrinsic feedback can effectively guide LLM learning, unlocking latent capabilities and fostering generalizable reasoning skills that transfer across diverse tasks without explicit external guidance.
