# FineRS: Fine-grained Reasoning and Segmentation of Small Objects with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> FINERS introduces a multi-modal large language model framework designed for instruction-guided reasoning and pixel-level segmentation of ultra-small objects within high-resolution images. The model achieves state-of-the-art gIoU/cIoU scores of 55.1/46.5 on the new UAV-captured 4K dataset, FINERS-4k, and generalizes robustly to other high-resolution visual question answering benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with fine-grained perception and precise localization of small objects in high-resolution images due to input resolution limitations, leading to a loss of critical visual details through downsampling.
- Existing high-resolution MLLM approaches often lack pixel-level segmentation capabilities, typically providing only coarse visual reasoning or question answering without precise spatial grounding for referred objects.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality benchmark datasets specifically designed for fine-grained reasoning and segmentation of ultra-small objects in complex, high-resolution imagery, particularly from UAVs.

## Method

- A ==two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline== combines a ==Global Semantic Exploration (GSE) module== for identifying broad target regions and a ==Localized Perceptual Refinement (LPR) module== for fine-grained localization, with a frozen SAM2 model generating final pixel-level segmentation masks.
- A reinforcement learning strategy based on GRPO is employed, featuring a novel ==locate-informed retrospective reward== for GSE, which leverages the already trained LPR module to provide robust supervision for coarse region prediction.
- Task-specific multi-task reward functions are designed for both GSE and LPR, guiding optimization towards accurate textual answers, precise bounding boxes, and segmentation points, enabling unified reasoning and segmentation capabilities.

## Results

- FINERS achieved gIoU/cIoU scores of **55.1/46.5** on the FINERS-4k test set for instruction-guided segmentation, outperforming the next best baseline (Seg-zero† 7B) by **8.5/7.9 points**, with particularly strong performance on extra-small and extra-extra small objects.
- The model demonstrated strong generalization to other high-resolution VQA benchmarks, achieving **77.5%** accuracy on V* (outperforming SEAL's **75.4%**) and significantly surpassing baselines on HR-Bench 4K (**63.8%** vs. DC2's **50.0%**) and HR-Bench 8K (**58.1%** vs. DC2's **40.8%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the two-stage design, the locate-informed retrospective reward mechanism for GSE, the random input region augmentation for LPR, and the comprehensive multi-task reward functions for both stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A coarse-to-fine MLLM architecture effectively addresses the challenge of small object perception in high-resolution images by sequentially identifying broad areas and then refining details within localized views.
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly with a retrospective reward mechanism, can efficiently fine-tune MLLMs for complex multi-task vision-language problems like simultaneous reasoning and fine-grained segmentation, even with limited direct supervision for intermediate steps.
> - The creation of specialized, high-resolution datasets, such as the UAV-captured FINERS-4k, is essential for pushing the boundaries of MLLMs in challenging domains involving small object detection and segmentation in cluttered visual environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed remarkable advancements, enabling powerful capabilities across a spectrum of vision-language tasks, from visual question answering (VQA) to referring expression comprehension. However, a significant limitation persists: MLLMs often struggle with fine-grained perception and precise localization of objects, especially *small, subtle targets embedded within high-resolution, cluttered contexts*. This limitation primarily stems from the restricted input resolutions of current MLLMs, which necessitate downsampling high-resolution images, leading to a loss of critical visual details.

This paper positions itself directly at the forefront of addressing this critical gap. Existing MLLM-based approaches for reasoning and segmentation can be broadly categorized:

- **Standard-resolution MLLMs (e.g., LISA, LISA++, PixelLM):** These models integrate MLLMs with foundational segmentation models, excelling in tasks involving large, prominent objects in standard-resolution images. However, their reliance on global visual-semantic alignment falters when confronted with high-resolution scenes containing dense layouts and small, low-saliency targets. Downsampling in these models severely degrades the visibility of small objects, leading to poor performance.

- **High-resolution Image Understanding MLLMs (e.g., SEAL, DC2, MLLMs-Know):** Recognizing the resolution challenge, some methods attempt to process high-resolution images. They often mimic human visual perception by decomposing images into patches or employing hierarchical processing. While improving visual reasoning in complex scenarios, these methods typically adopt a training-free pipeline due to the scarcity of high-resolution training data. This lack of supervised fine-tuning limits their perception accuracy in complex scenes and, more importantly, restricts their ability to perform precise *pixel-level localization and segmentation*. Many only support question answering, lacking the fine-grained spatial grounding needed for tasks like instruction-guided segmentation.

- **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) for MLLMs (e.g., Visual-RFT, Seg-Zero):** The success of RFT in enhancing reasoning in LLMs has been extended to MLLMs. Visual-RFT improves performance on classification and detection, while Seg-Zero leverages RFT for reasoning segmentation. However, Seg-Zero still struggles with capturing fine-grained details in *high-resolution small-object scenarios* and cannot simultaneously generate explicit answers and accurate segmentation masks without a unified multi-task reward mechanism. It performs well on standard-resolution and regular object scenarios but faces challenges with the specific nuances of ultra-small objects in high-resolution images.

FINERS distinguishes itself by proposing a novel two-stage framework that synergistically combines a **coarse-to-fine perception pipeline** with a **reinforcement learning strategy** and a **unified multi-task reward mechanism**. Crucially, it specifically targets **instruction-guided reasoning and pixel-level segmentation of ultra-small objects within high-resolution images**, a domain largely unaddressed by prior work. Furthermore, the introduction of **FINERS-4k**, a new UAV-captured dataset, directly addresses the data scarcity issue for this challenging task, providing a much-needed benchmark for fine-grained understanding in complex aerial imagery. By integrating RFT, FINERS also emphasizes data-efficient fine-tuning, making it adaptable to scenarios with limited supervised data. This work represents a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to achieve human-like precision in perceiving and understanding the most subtle visual details in the most demanding visual environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of FINERS is to develop a robust and effective Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) framework capable of performing **joint instruction-guided reasoning and fine-grained pixel-level segmentation of extremely small objects within high-resolution images**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing MLLM Resolution Limitations:** Current MLLMs are primarily designed for standard-resolution images. When confronted with high-resolution inputs, they typically resort to downsampling, which inevitably leads to the loss of crucial fine-grained visual details. This detail degradation renders them ineffective for tasks requiring precise perception of small objects. The motivation is to overcome this inherent architectural limitation to unlock MLLMs' potential for high-resolution vision tasks.

2. **Bridging the Gap in High-Resolution Vision:** While some recent MLLMs have explored high-resolution image understanding, they often lack the ability for precise *pixel-level localization and segmentation*. Many existing approaches are training-free, limiting their accuracy, or focus solely on visual question answering without providing concrete spatial grounding. A core motivation is to develop a framework that not only understands complex questions about high-resolution scenes but also accurately segments the referred objects at a pixel level.

3. **Enhancing Small Object Perception:** Detecting and segmenting small objects (especially "extra-small" and "extra-extra small" objects as defined in the paper) in cluttered, complex backgrounds, such as those found in aerial imagery, is a long-standing challenge in computer vision. MLLMs, with their strong contextual reasoning, hold promise for this, but their visual backbones are often not equipped for such fine detail. The motivation is to leverage MLLMs' reasoning power while building specific mechanisms to enhance their perception of these subtle targets.

4. **Enabling Unified Multi-Task Capabilities:** Existing RFT-based MLLMs for vision-language tasks often specialize in either reasoning or segmentation, or struggle to provide explicit textual answers alongside pixel masks within a single, coherent framework. The authors are motivated to create a truly unified framework that can concurrently generate both accurate textual responses (for open-ended and multiple-choice VQA) and precise segmentation masks based on user instructions, requiring a sophisticated multi-task reward mechanism.

5. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Challenging Scenarios:** The lack of large-scale, high-quality benchmark datasets specifically designed for fine-grained reasoning and segmentation of ultra-small objects in complex high-resolution imagery (particularly UAV-captured scenes) has hampered research in this area. A significant motivation is to introduce such a dataset (FINERS-4k) to enable comprehensive evaluation and drive future advancements.

In essence, FINERS aims to push the boundaries of MLLMs by enabling them to perform human-like perception and reasoning on visual details that are often overlooked, thereby unlocking new capabilities for applications in complex, real-world high-resolution environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FINERS employs a sophisticated two-stage MLLM-based reinforcement learning framework designed to tackle the complexities of fine-grained reasoning and segmentation of small objects in high-resolution images.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework Architecture

The core of FINERS is a **coarse-to-fine pipeline** comprising two distinct MLLM modules:
- **Global Semantic Exploration (GSE):** This initial stage takes the original high-resolution image ($I$) and a user instruction ($Q$) as input. Its role is to perform instruction-guided semantic reasoning across the entire image. The output of GSE includes a textual response ($A_{pre}$) and a *coarse target region* ($B_{pre}^{r}$), represented as a bounding box that broadly localizes the referred objects. To manage computational complexity, GSE's coarse box is fixed in size (256x256), optimizing only its center offsets.
- **Localized Perceptual Refinement (LPR):** This second stage receives the user instruction ($Q$) and the coarse region ($B_{pre}^{r}$) predicted by GSE. It then crops the original image based on $B_{pre}^{r}$ to create a lower-resolution input ($I_c$). LPR's task is to refine this localized view, performing fine-grained reasoning to generate an accurate bounding box ($B_{pre}$) and corresponding segmentation points ($P_{pre}^1$, $P_{pre}^2$) for the target object.

Finally, the predicted bounding box and points from LPR are fed into a **frozen SAM2** (Segment Anything Model v2) to produce the final pixel-level segmentation mask ($M_{pre}$). This leverages the strong segmentation capabilities of SAM2 without requiring its fine-tuning.

#### 3.2. Training Pipeline with Reinforcement Learning (RL)

To overcome the limitations of heavy supervised fine-tuning and enhance generalization, FINERS integrates a vision Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) strategy based on the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm. The training process is decoupled into two phases:

1. **Refinement Stage for Masks (LPR Training):**
 - The LPR module is trained first. Its supervised annotations include ground-truth bounding boxes ($B_{gt}$), points ($P_{gt}^1$, $P_{gt}^2$), and textual answers ($A_{gt}$), derived from the FINERS-4k dataset.
 - To make LPR robust to variations in input regions, it is trained on image patches randomly cropped around the ground-truth bounding boxes, mimicking potential inaccuracies from the GSE stage.
 - LPR's training also includes a "thinking" process (<think> format), supervised from a prompt-based cold start.

2. **Coarse Region Selection with LPR & Global Stage for Coarse Region (GSE Training):**
 - Unlike LPR, GSE's coarse region prediction lacks direct ground-truth supervision for reward calculation. To address this, FINERS introduces a **locate-informed retrospective reward**.
 - For each training sample, *n* randomly offset coarse regions are generated, each covering the ground-truth object. These regions are passed to the *already trained and frozen LPR module*.
 - LPR predicts a refined box ($B_{pre}$) for each of these candidate coarse regions. The region that results in the highest IoU (Intersection over Union) between LPR's prediction and the actual ground-truth object ($B_{gt}$) is then selected as the "optimal" ground-truth region ($B_{gt}^r$) for training GSE. This retroactively provides robust supervision for GSE's coarse region exploration.
 - The GSE module is then optimized using these LPR-informed optimal regions ($B_{gt}^r$) and corresponding ground-truth answer labels ($A_{gt}$). This allows GSE to learn to identify appropriate coarse regions from high-resolution images, considering both visual context and textual instructions.
 - A standard KL divergence penalty is applied between the policy and a reference model during RL training.

#### 3.3. Reward Functions

FINERS designs distinct, multi-task reward functions for LPR and GSE, aligning with the GRPO strategy:

- **Rewards for LPR ($R_{LPR}$):** These focus on fine-grained accuracy.
 - **Spatial Alignment:** Point L1 ($R_{point}$), Box L1 ($R_{bL1}$), Box IoU ($R_{bIoU}$) between predictions ($B_{pre}$, $P_{pre}$) and ground-truth ($B_{gt}$, $P_{gt}$).
 - **Format Adherence:** JSON-1 format ($R_{format1}$) and Think format ($R_{think}$) rewards.
 - **Q&A Accuracy ($R_{response}$):** Binary reward for correct textual answer (exact match for multiple choice, fuzzy match for open-ended, or "object found" for IS).
 - Total: $R_{LPR} = R_{bIoU} + R_{bL1} + R_{point} + R_{format1} + R_{response} + R_{think}$.

- **Rewards for GSE ($R_{GSE}$):** These focus on coarse region quality and overall instruction following.
 - **Coarse Region Quality (Locate-informed retrospective reward):** Region IoU ($R_{regionIoU}$) and Region L1 ($R_{regionL1}$) between GSE's predicted coarse region ($B_{pre}^r$) and the LPR-selected optimal ground-truth region ($B_{gt}^r$).
 - **Region Compatibility:** Region Size ($R_{size}$) reward (ensuring compatibility with LPR input size, e.g., 512x512 when upsampled) and Box-in-Region ($R_{cover}$) reward (ensuring the ground-truth object lies fully within the predicted region).
 - **Format Adherence:** JSON-2 format ($R_{format2}$) and Think format ($R_{think}$) rewards.
 - **Q&A Accuracy ($R_{response}$):** Consistent with LPR, for the textual answer.
 - Total: $R_{GSE} = R_{regionIoU} + R_{regionL1} + R_{size} + R_{cover} + R_{format2} + R_{response} + R_{think}$.
All binary reward terms are assigned equal weights for simplicity and stability.

#### 3.4. FINERS-4k Benchmark

To support this research, the authors introduce FINERS-4k, a novel dataset.
- **Data Source:** UAV-captured 4K-resolution videos (3840 × 2060) from YouTube and proprietary footage.
- **Annotation:** Human-annotated triplets of (question, answer, mask) for small objects, with a multi-stage quality assurance process.
- **Characteristics:** Large-scale (12,132 text-mask pairs), complex environments, extreme object size variability (S, XS, XXS categories), scattered small-object distributions, and cluttered spatial contexts.
- **Tasks Supported:** Instruction-guided Segmentation (IS), Open-ended VQA (OVQA), and Multiple-choice VQA (MVQA).

The methodology elegantly combines semantic understanding (GSE) with precise visual grounding (LPR + SAM2), guided by a tailored reinforcement learning scheme and supported by a custom, challenging dataset, specifically addressing the unique challenges of small object perception in high-resolution, complex scenes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that FINERS significantly advances the state-of-the-art in fine-grained reasoning and segmentation of small objects in high-resolution images.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on FINERS-4k

- **Instruction-guided Segmentation (IS):** FINERS consistently outperforms all baseline methods, including both training-free and retrained (fine-tuned on FINERS-4k) MLLM-based approaches. On the FINERS-4k test set, FINERS achieves gIoU/cIoU scores of 55.1/46.5, notably higher than the next best (Seg-zero† 7B with 46.6/38.6). This superiority is particularly pronounced for extra-small (XS) and extra-extra small (XXS) objects, where FINERS achieves 59.0/43.1 and 47.2/27.5 respectively, indicating its robust capability in perceiving the most challenging tiny objects.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** FINERS also excels in both Open-ended VQA (OVQA) and Multiple-choice VQA (MVQA) tasks on FINERS-4k. It achieves 83.3% accuracy for MVQA and 56.7% for OVQA, significantly outperforming models like MLLMs-Know 7B + LISA 7B (51.5/45.4) which are among the stronger baselines for VQA. This confirms FINERS's unified ability to provide both accurate segmentation and precise textual answers.
- **Impact of Domain Shift and Task Complexity:** The results highlight that existing MLLMs (e.g., LISA, PixelLM) show a dramatic performance drop when applied to FINERS-4k, underscoring the dataset's challenge and the domain shift from standard-resolution, ground-level imagery to 4K UAV footage with ultra-small objects.

#### 4.2. Strong Generalization on Other High-Resolution VQA Datasets

- FINERS demonstrates excellent transferability and generalization capabilities to other public high-resolution VQA benchmarks, specifically V* and HR-Bench (4K and 8K resolutions).
- Without any additional finetuning, FINERS achieves an overall accuracy of 77.5% on V* (compared to SEAL's 75.4%) and significantly outperforms all baselines on HR-Bench 4K (63.8% vs. DC2's 50.0%) and HR-Bench 8K (58.1% vs. DC2's 40.8%). This indicates that the FINERS framework and its training methodology are robust and adaptable beyond its specific UAV domain, for diverse high-resolution, non-UAV scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies Validate Key Components

The ablation studies provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of FINERS's core architectural and training components:
- **Retrospective Reward:** Replacing the LPR-informed coarse regions with random crops for GSE supervision ("w/o Retrospective Reward") leads to a drop in performance (e.g., gIoU from 55.1 to 54.0), validating the critical role of LPR in guiding GSE's coarse region exploration.
- **Random Input Region in LPR:** Removing this box augmentation strategy ("w/o Random Input Region in LPR") also decreases performance, confirming its efficacy in making LPR robust to variations in input box quality.
- **QA Accuracy Reward:** The removal of the QA accuracy reward ("w/o QA Acc. Reward") results in a notable drop in both segmentation and VQA performance, highlighting its importance for simultaneously generating explicit answers and enhancing fine-grained perception.
- **Box Size Reward and Box-in-Region Reward (GSE):** Removing these rewards ("w/o Box Size Reward," "w/o Box-in-region Reward") from GSE's training significantly impacts performance, confirming their positive impact on generating appropriate and effective coarse regions.
- **Two-Stage Design:** Comparing with a one-stage approach (GSE directly generating object boxes, similar to a higher-resolution SegZero), the two-stage coarse-to-fine framework significantly improves localization precision, underscoring its architectural superiority for small object perception.
- **QA Rewards in Stages:** Incorporating QA accuracy rewards in both GSE and LPR (FINERS) yields the best performance. The ablation also shows that textual response on GSE (leveraging global context) provides better VQA performance compared to LPR's localized view.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Visualizations (Fig. 4 and Fig. A2) clearly demonstrate FINERS's ability to accurately localize even tiny objects within cluttered backgrounds and generate correct, instruction-guided open-ended or multiple-choice answers, along with precise segmentation masks.

In summary, the main findings confirm FINERS's superior performance in a highly challenging domain, the efficacy of its novel two-stage architecture, the critical role of its specialized reinforcement learning rewards, and the value of the new FINERS-4k benchmark.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FINERS research represents a substantial contribution to the fields of computer vision and natural language processing, particularly in advancing the capabilities of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for real-world applications. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Breaking Resolution Barriers for MLLMs:** FINERS directly addresses one of the most persistent limitations of current MLLMs: their inability to precisely understand and localize details in high-resolution images, especially for small objects. By proposing a coarse-to-fine pipeline, it effectively circumvents the detail degradation caused by downsampling, enabling MLLMs to process and interpret visual information at a much finer granularity. This is a critical step towards MLLMs achieving more human-like visual perception.

2. **Pioneering Unified Fine-grained Reasoning and Segmentation with RL:** FINERS is, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first method to unify instruction-guided reasoning and fine-grained pixel-level segmentation of small objects under a reinforcement learning paradigm. This integration allows for data-efficient training, reduces reliance on vast supervised datasets, and promotes better generalization, which is crucial for complex, specialized tasks where labeled data is scarce. The novel locate-informed retrospective reward is a key innovation in effectively coupling the coarse and fine stages, ensuring consistent perception across scales.

3. **Introducing a Crucial Benchmark (FINERS-4k):** The creation of FINERS-4k is a significant contribution in itself. This human-annotated, high-quality dataset, specifically designed with UAV-captured 4K imagery and ultra-small objects in complex scenes, fills a critical void in the research landscape. It provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating MLLMs on attribute-level reasoning and pixel-level segmentation of subtle, small-scale targets. This will undoubtedly catalyze future research and development in high-resolution, small-object understanding, pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve.

4. **Enabling Robust Multi-Task MLLMs:** The framework's ability to jointly generate explicit textual answers (for VQA tasks) and accurate segmentation masks within a unified system is a major leap. This means MLLMs can become more versatile and actionable agents, not only answering "what" and "why" but also precisely identifying "where" at a pixel level. This unified capability simplifies system design and enhances the interpretability and utility of MLLM outputs.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**

The advancements offered by FINERS have wide-ranging implications across numerous domains:

- **UAV-based Surveillance and Inspection:** The use of UAV imagery in FINERS-4k directly points to applications in aerial monitoring. This includes identifying minute anomalies in infrastructure (e.g., cracks in bridges, power lines), detecting small objects in search-and-rescue operations, or precisely locating specific items in large-scale logistical analysis.
- **Medical Imaging:** In high-resolution medical scans (e.g., microscopy, radiology), identifying tiny tumors, lesions, or cellular structures is paramount. FINERS's fine-grained perception could significantly enhance diagnostic accuracy and assist medical professionals in localizing critical areas.
- **Precision Agriculture:** Detecting early signs of plant disease, identifying individual pests, or monitoring specific plant features from aerial imagery requires highly accurate small-object segmentation.
- **Autonomous Systems:** For self-driving cars or robotic systems, recognizing distant or small road signs, minor obstacles, or subtle changes in the environment is crucial for safe and effective operation. FINERS could improve the robustness of perception modules in these systems.
- **Remote Sensing and Geospatial Analysis:** Fine-grained analysis of satellite or aerial imagery for environmental monitoring, urban planning, or resource management, where identifying small changes or specific features is essential.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the reliance of LPR on GSE's coarse region accuracy and the current decoupled training of the two stages, FINERS lays a strong foundation. It paves the way for future research into more efficient end-to-end joint optimization, mechanisms for re-exploration to mitigate missed detections, and further generalization across diverse high-resolution domains. Overall, FINERS represents a significant step towards developing truly intelligent, perceptive MLLMs capable of understanding the world in intricate detail.
