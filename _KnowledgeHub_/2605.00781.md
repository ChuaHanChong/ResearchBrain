# Map2World: Segment Map Conditioned Text to 3D World Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Map2World introduces a text-conditioned framework for generating large-scale 3D worlds from user-defined segment maps, overcoming limitations of rigid layouts and content inconsistencies. It employs a multi-diffusion strategy for seamless structural coherence and a detail enhancer network that leverages latent space learning for fine-grained realism, achieving higher quality and completeness compared to prior methods like SynCity, with a GPTscore of 7.93/10 and a World Quality score of 7.76.

## Problem

- Existing 3D world generation methods struggle with limited high-quality, world-level datasets, leading to domain-specific or incomplete outputs, and this data scarcity also hinders training detail enhancement that maintains fine-grained detail while ensuring global coherence across large 3D scenes.
- Current approaches often rely on rigid, grid-based layouts, limiting user control over complex, irregularly shaped environments.
- Scaling individual 3D assets to large worlds results in contextual disconnectedness, inconsistent object scales, and visible seams between generated regions.

## Method

- A ==multi-diffusion strategy== with ==latent fusion== is implemented to expand spatial regions in the structured latent space, enabling seamless connections and consistent scaling across overlapping 3D windows, guided by dynamically weighted text prompts and segment masks that allow flexible, arbitrary-shaped world layouts with soft boundaries evolving during denoising.
- A ==detail enhancer network== is proposed to generate fine details by learning coarse-to-fine relationships within the structured latent space, conditioned on both coarse and adjacent cube latents to maintain global coherence.
- The pipeline leverages pre-trained 3D asset generators (**TRELLIS**) and fine-tunes its structured latent decoder (D_L) to robustly generalize across diverse domains with limited world-level training data.

## Results

- Map2World successfully generates 3D worlds from arbitrary-shaped segment maps with associated text prompts, demonstrating seamless transitions and connected structures between regions, and outperforms existing methods like SynCity on grid-type segmentation maps, achieving a higher GPTscore of 7.93/10 (vs 7.48/10) and a World Quality score of 7.76 (vs 7.25), particularly excelling in world completeness and coherence.
- Ablation studies confirmed that spectral parameterization for initial latent optimization stabilized convergence at high learning rates, and the detail enhancer design yielded the best image quality metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, FID) compared to alternatives.
- The detail enhancer can be applied recursively to progressively upscale resolution, consistently generating sharper details and textures while maintaining harmony with surrounding elements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing velocity predictions from overlapping diffusion windows effectively maintains the latent prior of the base generator while ensuring smooth transitions and global consistency for large-scale scenes.
> - Optimizing the initial sparse latent in the spectral domain (using 3D FFT) significantly stabilizes the scale-aware generation process, enabling better control over the generated scene's structure.
> - Detail enhancement learns latent relationships between coarse and fine representations (leveraging asset priors, no new 3D data), conditioned on the coarse latent plus adjacent latents to harmonize with local and overall structure.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of three-dimensional (3D) worlds is identified as a task with applications in immersive content creation, gaming, autonomous driving simulation, and virtual realities. While significant progress has been made in generating individual 3D assets or objects, scaling these capabilities to coherent, large-scale 3D worlds presents challenges. A primary constraint is the limited availability of high-quality, world-level datasets, which are more complex and difficult to construct than object-centric collections.

Existing approaches to 3D world generation can be categorized into methods that reconstruct 3D scenes from generated 2D views and those that directly generate explicit 3D representations.
- **2D-to-3D Reconstruction:** Methods leveraging 2D image diffusion models often combine them with depth estimators to lift generated images into 3D. These pipelines typically suffer from view-dependent inconsistencies and incomplete reconstructions when attempting to expand scenes through outpainting. Similarly, video diffusion models, while generating scene-like sequences, often struggle with 3D consistency and are limited by memory constraints. These approaches generally do not guarantee geometric or semantic consistency across large scenes.
- **Direct Explicit 3D Generation:** Some methods train generators from scratch using explicit 3D representations. Examples include BlockFusion, NuiScene, and LT3SD. However, these are often restricted to generating only geometry without textures and are limited to specific domains (e.g., indoor or driving scenes) due to data scarcity. SCube and InfiniCube improve color estimation and global consistency but remain constrained to driving scenes.
- **Leveraging Pre-trained Asset Generators:** A more recent direction involves using powerful, off-the-shelf 3D asset generators, such as TRELLIS or CLAY, and attempting to scale their output to world-level scenes. SynCity, for instance, divides the ground into grids, generates assets for each tile using TRELLIS, and then blends boundaries. While this leverages high-quality asset generation, it often results in contextual disconnectedness between adjacent assets, inconsistencies in object scale, and a lack of overall scene coherence. Furthermore, these methods typically impose rigid, grid-like arrangements for assets, which is impractical for real-world, irregularly shaped environments.

This paper positions itself by addressing the limitations of existing methods, particularly the constraints of grid-based layouts, inconsistencies in object scale, and difficulties in maintaining global coherence across large-scale, arbitrarily shaped environments. It aims to integrate advanced 3D asset generation (specifically building on TRELLIS) with flexible user control via segment maps, while ensuring global context and scale consistency, even with limited training data for scene generation. The work draws inspiration from 2D diffusion literature on scaling models to large spatial extents (e.g., patch-based, outpainting-based, multi-window diffusion) and extends these paradigms to volumetric 3D.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce Map2World, a novel framework for text-conditioned 3D world generation that aims to overcome the limitations of existing approaches. The specific objectives and their underlying motivations are detailed below:

- **Enabling Flexible, User-Defined Segment Map Conditioning:**
 - **Motivation:** Current 3D world generation methods are often constrained by rigid grid layouts or limited to generating scenes within narrow domains. This restricts user control over the spatial organization and semantic content of the generated world. Real-world environments, such as urban districts or natural landscapes, possess complex, irregularly shaped regions that cannot be adequately represented by square-tile grids.
 - **Objective:** To develop a framework that allows users to define 3D world layouts using arbitrary-shaped segment maps, with individual text prompts assigned to each segment. This aims to provide enhanced user controllability and flexibility in designing diverse and semantically aligned 3D environments.

- **Ensuring Global-Scale Consistency and Seamless Transitions:**
 - **Motivation:** When scaling asset generators to world-level scenes, a significant challenge is maintaining consistent object scales and seamless connections between different generated regions. Modular approaches often suffer from "contextual disconnectedness" and visible gaps or abrupt transitions at the boundaries of individual assets or tiles. This compromises the realism and coherence of the overall world.
 - **Objective:** To implement a multi-diffusion strategy within a structured latent space that coordinates overlapping diffusion windows. This is intended to preserve the latent prior of the underlying asset generator (TRELLIS) while enabling seamless connections and consistent scaling across individual boundaries, supporting arbitrary resolutions and progressive generation.

- **Enhancing Fine-Grained Details without Compromising Coherence:**
 - **Motivation:** A single structured latent representation for an entire large world may have limited capacity to encode all necessary fine-grained details, such as individual leaves on trees or intricate architectural elements, while simultaneously representing large-scale structures. Directly adding details in 3D space is challenging due to the infeasibility of collecting suitable data pairs.
 - **Objective:** To propose a detail enhancer network that generates fine details for the world. This network is designed to learn the relationship between coarse and fine scenes in the latent space, incorporating global structure information to ensure that added details remain consistent with the overall scene coherence. The design leverages pre-trained asset generator priors to operate effectively even with limited world-level training data.

- **Achieving Robust Generalization Across Diverse Domains with Limited Data:**
 - **Motivation:** Training 3D world generators from scratch is hampered by the scarcity of comprehensive, high-quality world-level datasets. This limits the domain applicability and diversity of scenes that can be generated.
 - **Objective:** To design the entire pipeline to leverage strong prior knowledge from powerful, domain-agnostic 3D asset generators (e.g., TRELLIS). This approach aims to achieve robust generalization across various domains, enabling high-quality and diverse 3D world generation even when scene-level training data is limited.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived gap between advanced 3D object generation and the current capabilities of 3D world generation, particularly concerning user control, global consistency, detail fidelity, and data efficiency. Map2World seeks to bridge this gap by offering a method that is flexible, scalable, and produces coherent, high-quality 3D worlds.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Map2World proposes a text-conditioned 3D world generation framework that leverages a powerful 3D asset generator, TRELLIS, and extends its capabilities for large-scale world synthesis. The methodology involves a two-stage generation pipeline: first, expanding the structured latent space to represent an entire world with flexible segment map conditioning, and second, enriching the details within this latent space.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Structured Latent and Generation Pipeline (TRELLIS Foundation)

The method builds upon TRELLIS, a 3D asset generation model using a "structured latent" (SLAT) representation. A SLAT encodes geometry and appearance using a set of local latents on a 3D grid, where (z_i, p_i) represents a latent vector and its positional index for an active voxel.
TRELLIS employs a two-stage rectified flow model:
1. **Geometry Stage:** A flow Transformer (G_S) generates a sparse structure {p_i} by denoising Gaussian noise conditioned on a text prompt. A decoder (D_S) transforms this into a signed 3D scalar field, identifying active voxels.
2. **Texture Stage:** Another flow Transformer (G_L) estimates latent features {z_i} for the active voxels, also through iterative denoising. A decoder (D_L) transforms the structured latent into a 3D representation (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting, radiance fields, mesh).

#### 3.2. Expanding Spatial Regions in 3D Latent Space

To enable large-scale world generation beyond the 64³ voxel limit of TRELLIS, Map2World introduces strategies for spatial expansion and segment-map-guided control.

- **Latent Fusion for Rectified Flow Models:**
 - The 3D space is conceptually divided into overlapping 3D cube windows (Ω_j), each of size 64³, with adjacent windows overlapping by half their spatial resolution.
 - During denoising, the velocity field prediction for a given position `x` is aggregated from all overlapping windows. A shared 3D Gaussian kernel `W(·)` weights the predictions from each window `v_t,j(x|y)` (where `y` is the text prompt) to produce a fused velocity `v_t(x|y)`.
 - This fused velocity is used to update the latent `s_t(x|y)` in each denoising step, enabling seamless connections across window boundaries while preserving TRELLIS's latent prior. This strategy is applied to both G_S and G_L.

- **Segment-Map-Guided Latent Fusion:**
 - Map2World supports conditioning on user-defined segment maps with multiple text prompts. For `K` labels, each with a binary map `M_k` and text prompt `y_k`, the velocity at position `x` is calculated as a weighted sum of velocities estimated for each label.
 - The weighting incorporates `M_k(x)` (the segment mask) and a normalized 3D Gaussian kernel `G(σ_t)` with standard deviation `σ_t`. `σ_t` varies with diffusion time, creating soft boundaries early and sharper masks later to improve denoising stability and smooth transitions.
 - This approach supports arbitrary shapes for `M_k`, differing from grid-based approaches.

- **Optimization for Scale-Aware Initial Latent:**
 - Observations indicate that coarse geometric structures vary with initial noise samples, and similar noise samples produce similar outputs, suggesting a scale-dependent manifold in the sparse latent space.
 - To guide generation towards a desired scene scale, the initial noisy latent `S_T` for the sparse structure generator `G_S` is optimized.
 - A linear approximation of the denoising trajectory is used to calculate gradients efficiently without full backpropagation.
 - A loss function `L_linear = ||y - M([G_L(S_T) - S_T]_sg + S_T)||_2^2` guides the scale, where `M` is a target mask and `y` represents target constraints (e.g., excluded regions, ground).
 - To stabilize optimization under large learning rates, the sparse structure feature `S` is parameterized in the spectral domain using a 3D FFT, which aids in convergence.

#### 3.3. Enriching Details in 3D Latent Space

Since the global structured latent may lack fine details for numerous objects, a detail enhancer framework is proposed to increase the resolution of the generated world.

- **Rationale:** Training a network to directly add 3D details is difficult due to data scarcity. Instead, the detail enhancer learns the relationship between coarse and fine scenes in the latent space, maximizing TRELLIS's prior knowledge.
- **Dataset Curation:** For training, existing 3D scene data (from Objaverse via NuiScene43 labels) is segmented into large cubes, each then divided into eight smaller cubes. The structured latents of both the large cube (`s_O`) and its eight smaller constituent cubes (`s_j`) are extracted using the TRELLIS encoder. Small cube latents are assumed to contain more detailed information.
- **Network Architecture:** The detail enhancer generates structured latents of small cubes (`{s_j}`) conditioned on the large cube latent (`s_O`). It does not estimate all `s_j` simultaneously but predicts `s_j` given its index `j`.
 - An additional module is integrated with TRELLIS's flow Transformer (G_S and G_L) for conditioning. This design choice leverages TRELLIS's structure due to data scarcity and the latent manifold compatibility.
 - Two conditioning inputs are used:
 1. **Truncated Large Cube Latent (`s_O|j`):** The portion of `s_O` corresponding to the target small cube `j` provides rough information, analogous to a low-resolution image in super-resolution. This is concatenated with noise along the channel axis and mixed via an MLP layer (`F_θ`).
 2. **Adjacent Cube Latents (`s_Adj(j)`):** To ensure seamless connections between adjacent small cubes, the structured latents of already-generated adjacent cubes are incorporated. The noise's spatial size is temporarily expanded, concatenated channel-wise with `s_Adj(j)`, and then compressed by `F_θ` (sharing parameters with `s_O|j`).
 - The mixed feature from `F_θ` is fed into G_S and G_L. The predicted flow is then cropped to retain only the target cube's portion. The process is summarized as `v_θ(s_j, t) = G_S/L(F_θ(s_j^t, s_O|j, s_Adj(j)), t)`.
- **Initialization, Training, and Sampling:**
 - The model is initialized to behave like the original TRELLIS (before fine-tuning) by setting `F_θ`'s weights and bias to approximate an identity mapping.
 - Only `F_θ`'s parameters are updated during fine-tuning using the flow matching loss `L_θ = E_{s_j,t} ||v_θ(s_j, t) - (ε - s_j)||_2^2`.
 - During sampling, the structured latents of small cubes are estimated auto-regressively from index 0 to 7. When generating `s_0`, only `s_O` is used; for subsequent cubes, already-generated adjacent cube latents (`s_Adj(j)`) are also utilized as conditions.
 - Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is not used, as the performance difference between conditional and unconditional settings was substantial, making CFG less effective.

#### 3.4. Fine-tuning SLAT Decoder

The original TRELLIS decoder (D_L) was trained on complete objects. To address distribution discrepancies when decoding latents from partial scene cubes (derived from the detail enhancer), D_L is fine-tuned.
- Structured latents are extracted from 3D meshes of the small cubes used for detail enhancer training.
- The decoder is fine-tuned by minimizing the difference between its reconstructed 3D representation and the original mesh, using the original TRELLIS loss formulation. This aims to improve the quality of 3D representations from the enhanced structured latents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate Map2World's capabilities in flexible segment map conditioning, scale consistency, content coherence, and detail enhancement, comparing it against existing methods and analyzing its design choices.

#### 4.1. Dataset Curation

For training the detail enhancer, 43 high-quality scene meshes from Objaverse (filtered using NuiScene43 labels) were used. After excluding 8 scenes without textural information, 35 scenes were used to crop 500 cubes of various sizes (64, 128, 192, 256) per scene, ensuring each contained over 10,000 vertices. Each "large" cube was then split into eight "small" cubes. Structured latents for both large and small cubes were extracted using a pre-trained TRELLIS encoder, resulting in 17,500 pairs, split into 16,000 for training and 1,500 for validation.

#### 4.2. Comparison on World Generation

- **Flexible Segment Map Conditioning:**
 - Qualitative results (Fig. 3, S5, S6, S7, S9) demonstrate Map2World's ability to generate 3D worlds from user-defined segment maps of arbitrary shapes and sizes, each with associated text prompts. The generated worlds align with map shapes and text prompts, exhibiting seamless transitions and connected structures between adjacent regions.
 - Existing frameworks like SynCity are noted to be unable to handle such complex, free-form segment maps.

- **Grid-Type Segmentation Map Comparison (with SynCity):**
 - When compared with SynCity on grid-shaped segment maps (Fig. 4, S3, S4), Map2World showed improvements in two aspects:
 1. **Large Connected Structures:** Map2World generated large, connected structures spanning multiple tiles with the same labels, which SynCity struggled with, often producing disconnected assets.
 2. **Denser and More Complex Worlds:** Map2World produced denser and more complex worlds, integrating content seamlessly between adjacent segments. SynCity's outputs were characterized by weak coherence, unfilled gaps, and a grid-like arrangement of assets.
 - **Quantitative Comparison (GPTscore):** Map2World achieved an average GPTscore of 7.93/10 (across sharpness, world completeness, coherence, realism) for 35 generated scenes, outperforming SynCity's 7.48/10, suggesting higher quality and completeness.
 - **Quantitative Comparison (World Quality - WQ):** A custom metric, World Quality (WQ), defined as `WQ = 0.15S + 0.45W + 0.25C + 0.15R` (Sharpness, World Completeness, Coherence, Realism), emphasized structural quality and layout complexity.
 - Table 1 shows Map2World achieved a WQ of 7.76, significantly higher than SynCity (7.25) and GaussianCube (5.08). Map2World scored highly across all components, particularly in world completeness (7.8) and coherence (7.9), indicating clear, structurally consistent, and complete environments.
 - **Quantitative Comparison (CLIP-Score):** Region-wise CLIP-Scores, measured on top-view renderings, showed that Map2World had substantially clearer region-text separability compared to SynCity (Fig. S3, S4, S5, S6, S7), indicating stronger adherence to intended semantic layouts across different CLIP backbones.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Spectral Parameterization for Stable Initial Latent Optimization:**
 - The study confirmed that parameterizing the sparse structure in the spectral domain using 3D FFT (Sec. 4.1) stabilized the initial noise optimization process for scale control (Fig. 6).
 - With spectral parameterization and a high learning rate (9.0), IoU and Dice scores reached approximately 0.9 within five optimization steps. Without it, the optimization was unstable and failed to converge, or required significantly more steps with a lower learning rate.

- **Design Choices for the Detail Enhancer:**
 - Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 7) demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed detail enhancer design.
 - An IP-Adapter architecture for fine-tuning G_S/L resulted in disconnected structures (Fig. 7b).
 - Using classifier-free guidance (CFG) during fine-tuning and sampling led to severe geometric distortions and oversaturated colors (Fig. 7c).
 - Not fine-tuning the SLAT decoder (D_L) resulted in less sharp geometry and less refined textures (Fig. 7d).
 - Without the detail enhancer entirely, scenes were less detailed (Fig. 7e).
 - **Quantitative Comparison (Detail Enhancer):** Table 2 evaluated options using PSNR, LPIPS, and FID scores (with Inceptionv3, DINOv2, CLIP backbones) on rendered images from generated test-set scenes.
 - Map2World's chosen concatenation-based architecture without CFG and with D_L fine-tuning yielded the best PSNR (22.53), LPIPS (0.2137), and FID scores across all baselines, indicating high fidelity to input conditions and generation of natural, high-quality detailed structures and textures.

- **Recursive Detail Enhancement:** Recursive application of the detail enhancer (Fig. S8) demonstrated the ability to progressively upscale resolution (e.g., ×2 to ×4 from an initial ×1 output). While minor content transformations were observed, the enhancer consistently generated sharper details and textures while maintaining harmony with surrounding elements, suggesting improvement in overall quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Map2World introduces contributions to the field of 3D world generation, addressing several limitations present in previous methodologies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced User Controllability and Flexibility:** The framework's ability to generate 3D worlds conditioned on user-defined segment maps of arbitrary shapes and scales represents a notable advancement. This departs from the rigid grid-based layouts typically found in existing approaches, providing users with more precise and intuitive control over the layout and semantic composition of large-scale virtual environments. This flexibility can streamline design workflows for creators in various applications.

- **Improved Global Coherence and Scale Consistency:** By employing a multi-diffusion strategy and latent fusion in a structured latent space, Map2World addresses the issue of disconnectedness and scale inconsistencies that often plague modular 3D world generation methods. The seamless integration of elements across regions, even for large and complex scenes, contributes to the realism and structural integrity of the generated worlds. This ensures that the generated environment functions as a cohesive whole rather than a collection of disparate assets.

- **Detail Enhancement and High-Quality Output:** The proposed detail enhancer network is designed to augment fine-grained details within the generated world without compromising global structural coherence. By leveraging the prior knowledge of a high-quality 3D asset generator and incorporating global context information, the enhancer contributes to producing visually richer and more intricate scenes, which is crucial for applications requiring high fidelity.

- **Robustness and Generalization with Limited Data:** The methodology is designed to leverage strong priors from pre-trained 3D asset generators like TRELLIS. This approach allows Map2World to achieve robust generalization across diverse domains, even when world-level training data is scarce. This is a practical advantage in a field where large-scale 3D datasets are difficult and expensive to acquire, potentially broadening the applicability of 3D world generation to new content types and scenarios.

- **Applications Across Industries:** The capabilities demonstrated by Map2World could have implications for various industries:
 - **Immersive Content Creation and Gaming:** Enables faster and more flexible generation of complex and diverse virtual environments, reducing manual design effort.
 - **Autonomous Driving Simulation:** Facilitates the creation of varied and realistic simulation environments for training and testing autonomous systems, including urban, rural, and specialized scenarios.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Supports the rapid prototyping and deployment of rich, interactive 3D spaces.

In conclusion, Map2World offers a framework that pushes the boundaries of controllable and scalable 3D world generation, providing tools that offer greater creative freedom and more coherent results for the development of virtual environments. The work's focus on leveraging existing powerful asset generators and enhancing them for world-scale tasks is a significant step towards more efficient and high-quality 3D content creation.
