# Value-guided action planning with JEPA world models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an approach to enhance action planning in Joint-Embedded Predictive Architectures (JEPA) by integrating goal-conditioned value function learning directly into their representation space. The method structures the JEPA's latent space such that distances approximate negative value functions, leading to improved planning accuracy, particularly achieving up to 0.96 accuracy in specific navigation tasks when employing a quasi-distance metric.

## Problem

- Joint-Embedded Predictive Architectures (JEPA) show promise for learning predictive representations but have limitations in supporting effective action planning.
- Planning with world models often faces challenges due to complex and misleading cost landscapes in the learned representation space, leading to suboptimal plans caused by numerous local minima.
- Arbitrarily learned latent spaces do not inherently encode a clear notion of "cost-to-go" or "value-to-goal," making optimization for goal-reaching difficult.

## Method

- The proposed method shapes the JEPA's state encoder representation space by defining a ==value function== as the negative squared distance between embedded states and goals.
- Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) is utilized to train this value-aligned representation from offline, reward-free trajectories, making the latent space directly amenable to planning optimization.
- A quasi-distance is explored instead of a standard Euclidean distance for the value function approximation, allowing for asymmetry to better capture the true nature of goal-reaching costs.

## Results

- Value-guided JEPA methods consistently achieved higher planning accuracy compared to standard prediction-based JEPA baselines and other representation learning approaches.
- The `VF quasi` approach, which integrates a quasi-distance into the value function, demonstrated the best performance, reaching **0.96** planning accuracy in the WB environment and **0.63** in the Maze environment.
- Combining the value function objective with the prediction loss (`VF quasi pred`) generally resulted in decreased performance (e.g., **0.85** in WB vs. **0.96** for `VF quasi`), highlighting the benefits of a dedicated, separately trained value-aligned state encoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly shaping the JEPA's latent space with a value function objective, where distances approximate negative values, significantly enhances planning performance by providing a smoother, more guided cost landscape.
> - Using a quasi-distance for value function approximation consistently leads to superior planning accuracy across environments, suggesting its effectiveness in capturing the potentially asymmetric nature of optimal value functions.
> - Training the value-aligned state encoder separately from the action encoder and predictor is generally more effective than joint training, indicating potential conflicts between the self-supervised prediction objective and the value function objective.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper operates at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within artificial intelligence: world models, self-supervised learning, representation learning, and reinforcement learning (RL) for action planning.

**World Models** are a foundational concept in AI, aiming to build systems that can understand and predict the dynamics of their environment (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Ding et al., 2025). By learning a causal understanding of how actions influence future states, world models enable agents to reason and plan trajectories internally, without constant interaction with the real world. This capability is crucial for developing intelligent agents that can learn efficiently and perform complex tasks.

**Joint-Embedded Predictive Architectures (JEPA)**, a framework championed by Yann LeCun (LeCun, 2022), represent a specific and promising approach to building world models. JEPA models learn predictive representations through a self-supervised objective, predicting future states in a learned embedding space rather than directly in the complex observation space. This self-supervised paradigm has shown strong results in representation learning for various modalities (Assran et al., 2023; Bardes et al., 2024) and has also been explored for action planning (Sobal et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025). The core idea is that if a model can accurately predict future representations, those representations must encode meaningful information about the environment's dynamics.

However, as the authors note, while JEPA models show promise, their ability to support *effective* action planning remains limited. Planning with world models often involves Model Predictive Control (MPC), where an optimal sequence of actions is found by minimizing a cost function over predicted future states. A common issue with such methods is the presence of numerous local minima in the representation space, making optimization challenging and leading to suboptimal plans.

This paper addresses this limitation by drawing inspiration from advances in **Reinforcement Learning**, specifically the concept of **value functions**. Value functions provide a scalar measure of how "good" a state is, typically in relation to achieving a goal. Learning a goal-conditioned value function, which maps a state and a goal to a scalar, can significantly guide an agent towards desired outcomes. Recent works in RL, particularly using **Implicit Q-Learning (IQL)** (Kostrikov et al., 2021; Ghosh et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023), have demonstrated robust ways to learn such value functions from offline, unlabeled trajectories. Notably, Park et al. (2024b) and Wang et al. (2023) have explored learning structured representation spaces where distances between state embeddings approximate value functions.

The current work fits into this broader landscape by **bridging the gap between self-supervised world modeling (JEPA) and value-function-guided planning from reinforcement learning.** It aims to enhance JEPA's planning capabilities by explicitly shaping its latent representation space using principles derived from value function learning. This integration seeks to leverage the strengths of JEPA for learning robust dynamics representations with the guidance provided by a goal-conditioned value function, thereby making the latent space more amenable to effective planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly improve the action planning capabilities of Joint-Embedded Predictive Architectures (JEPA) world models.** The authors identify a crucial limitation in current JEPA-based planning: the difficulty of optimizing action sequences due to complex and potentially misleading cost landscapes in the learned representation space, characterized by numerous local minima.

To address this, the central motivation is to **shape the JEPA's representation space such that the distance between state embeddings directly approximates the negative of a goal-conditioned value function.** Specifically, for a reaching cost (i.e., penalizing every time step until a goal is reached), the desired property is for the Euclidean distance (or a quasi-distance) between an embedded state `s` and an embedded goal `g` to correspond to `-V*(s, g)`, where `V*(s, g)` is the optimal goal-conditioned value function.

The rationale behind this motivation is multifaceted:
- **Guiding Planning Optimization:** If the representation space inherently encodes the "cost-to-go" or "value-to-goal," then simply minimizing the distance to the goal in this latent space naturally guides the planning process toward optimal trajectories, potentially mitigating issues with local minima.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** Such a structured latent space offers more intuitive meaning, where "closeness" directly relates to "ease of reachability" from a given state to a goal.
- **Leveraging Offline Data:** The chosen approach, Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), is particularly suited for learning from offline, unlabeled trajectories, which aligns well with the self-supervised nature of JEPA and the often vast amounts of unlabeled data available in real-world scenarios.
- **Beyond Policy Execution:** Unlike prior work that primarily used value-aligned representations for policy execution, this research explicitly focuses on using them to improve the *planning* process itself within an MPC framework.

In essence, the authors are motivated to create a "value-aware" representation space for JEPA world models, believing that by encoding the goal-reaching cost directly into the geometry of the latent space, they can unlock superior and more robust planning performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is to integrate value function learning into the representation learning process of JEPA models.

#### 3.1. Baseline JEPA World Model

A standard JEPA model consists of a state encoder, an action encoder, and a predictor. It is trained to predict the representation of a future state given a current state and action. The prediction loss, `L_pred`, measures the difference between the predicted representation and the true future state representation. To prevent representational collapse, standard JEPA training employs techniques like `VCReg` loss (Sobal et al., 2025) or Exponential Moving Average (EMA) schemes (Assran et al., 2023; Bardes et al., 2024).

#### 3.2. Value-Guided Representation Shaping

The novel aspect of this work lies in shaping the state encoder's representation space. The authors define a value function `V_θ(s, g) = -∥E_θ(s) - E_θ(g)∥²`, where `E_θ` is the state encoder. The objective is to train `θ` such that `V_θ` approximates `V*`, the optimal goal-conditioned value function for a reaching cost `C(s, a, g) = 1_(s≠g)` (i.e., a cost of 1 for every time step the agent is not at the goal).

#### 3.3. Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) for JEPA

To learn this value-aligned representation, the authors leverage the Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) loss. For a dataset `D` of trajectories and goals, the IQL loss `L_θ_VF` is defined as:

`L_θ_VF = Σ_n Σ_t L²_τ [ -1_(s_t ≠ g_n) + γV̄_θ(s_{t+1}, g_n) - V_θ(s_t, g_n) ]`

Here:
- `L²_τ(x) = |τ - 1_(x<0)| x²` is the expectile regression loss, which focuses on different quantiles of the error distribution, allowing learning of the value function without explicit policy optimization.
- `V̄_θ` denotes a stop-gradient operator, preventing gradients from flowing through the target value function.
- `γ ∈ (0, 1)` is the discount factor, and `τ ∈ (0, 1)` is the expectile parameter, both close to 1.
- Goals `g_n` are sampled either as the last state of training trajectories or randomly from batches.

#### 3.4. Quasi-Distance Exploration

Recognizing that a general goal-conditioned value function may not be symmetric, the authors also explore replacing the Euclidean distance in `V_θ` with a **quasi-distance**, as proposed by Wang et al. (2023) and Wang & Isola (2022). A quasi-distance is a metric that satisfies the identity of indiscernibles and the triangle inequality but not necessarily symmetry. This potentially allows for a more accurate approximation of the asymmetric nature of value functions in complex environments.

#### 3.5. Training Approaches

Two main approaches are considered for combining the value function objective with standard JEPA training:
1. **"Sep" (Separate Training):** The state encoder is first trained in isolation using only the `L_VF` objective. Subsequently, the action encoder and predictor are trained with the standard `L_pred` loss.
2. **Combined Training:** All networks (state encoder, action encoder, predictor) are trained jointly, minimizing the sum of `L_VF` and `L_pred`. This allows for end-to-end optimization but might lead to conflicting gradients.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

The methodology is evaluated on two 2D environments under an offline reinforcement learning setting:
- **Wall Environment:** A square space with a wall and a door, requiring the agent to navigate from a start to a goal on the opposite side. Two variants are used: WS (small action norms) and WB (large action norms).
- **Maze Environment:** A Mujoco PointMaze-like environment where the agent navigates a random maze, requiring both position and velocity to be encoded.
Observations are image-based, potentially with additional sensory information (e.g., velocity for the maze). Datasets consist of random trajectories. Planning performance is measured by **planning accuracy**, defined as the proportion of successful plans for random initial state-goal pairs, using an MPC procedure with an MPPI optimizer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results, summarized in Table 2, provide clear insights into the effectiveness of the proposed value-guided JEPA approach compared to various baselines.

#### 4.1. Superiority of Value-Guided Approaches

- **VF (IQL-based) methods consistently outperform "intuitive" (Contrastive, Regressive) and standard prediction-based JEPA approaches (pred VCReg, pred EMA).** For example, in the WB environment, `VF` achieves 0.94 planning accuracy, significantly higher than `pred VCReg` (0.89), `Contrastive` (0.59), and `pred EMA` (0.43). Similar trends are observed in the WS and Maze environments.
- This directly supports the hypothesis that shaping the representation space with a value function objective provides valuable guidance during planning.

#### 4.2. Efficacy of Quasi-Distance

- **The `VF quasi` approach, which uses a quasi-distance instead of a Euclidean distance, consistently achieves the best performance across all environments.** It reaches 0.71 in WS, 0.96 in WB, and 0.63 in Maze. This is notably better than the `VF` approach (0.63, 0.94, 0.49 respectively) even in environments where the underlying value function might be considered symmetric (like simple wall navigation).
- This finding suggests that allowing for asymmetry through quasi-distances enhances the expressiveness of the learned networks, leading to a better approximation of the true value function and thus more effective planning, regardless of theoretical symmetry.

#### 4.3. Impact of Combined Training

- **Combining the `L_VF` objective with the `L_pred` objective (`VF pred`, `VF quasi pred`, `VF VCReg pred`) generally leads to worse performance compared to using `L_VF` (or `L_VF` with `VCReg`) alone in a "Sep" training scheme.** For instance, `VF quasi` (0.96 in WB) significantly outperforms `VF quasi pred` (0.85).
- This implies that training the state encoder separately with the value function objective and then training the predictor might be more effective. The combined loss might introduce conflicting gradients that hinder optimal representation learning for planning, or the self-supervised prediction loss might not align perfectly with the value function objective.

#### 4.4. VCReg Interaction

- Using `VCReg` (a standard JEPA regularizer to prevent collapse) in conjunction with `L_VF` (e.g., `VF VCReg`, `VF VCReg pred`) generally results in poorer planning performance compared to the `VF` and `VF quasi` methods. This suggests that the IQL loss itself might implicitly provide sufficient regularization, or that `VCReg` introduces unnecessary constraints that interfere with learning the desired value-aligned geometry.

#### 4.5. Dataset Influence

- The WB dataset (larger action norms) consistently yields better results than the WS dataset (smaller action norms). The authors hypothesize that larger actions enable trajectories to explore more of the environment, leading to richer data for learning.

#### 4.6. Discussion on Limitations (from results)

- The imperfect planning accuracies (e.g., 0.63 in Maze for the best method) indicate that the learned value functions are still not perfectly accurate, especially for distant relationships between states. This is attributed to sparse sampling of distant state triplets and a low signal-to-noise ratio of the value function gradient for states far from the goal during training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant contribution to the field of action planning with world models by demonstrating a highly effective method for integrating goal-conditioned value functions into the representation learning process of JEPA architectures.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Planning Capabilities

The most direct impact is the **substantially improved planning performance** observed across different control tasks. By shaping the latent space such that distances approximate negative value functions, the authors effectively create a more "navigable" space for planning algorithms like MPC. This mitigates the problem of local minima that often plague planning in arbitrarily learned representation spaces, leading to more robust and successful goal-reaching.

#### 5.2. Bridging Self-Supervised Learning and Reinforcement Learning

This research effectively **bridges two powerful paradigms: self-supervised representation learning with JEPA and value-based reinforcement learning.** It shows how the strengths of both can be combined to overcome limitations in each. JEPA provides strong dynamics modeling from unlabeled data, while value functions provide explicit goal-oriented guidance. This integration could pave the way for more intelligent agents that learn efficiently from vast, passive datasets and plan effectively for complex tasks.

#### 5.3. Implications for Offline RL and Data Efficiency

The use of Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) is particularly impactful as it allows for **learning from offline, reward-free trajectories.** This is crucial for real-world applications where collecting interactive online data is costly or dangerous. By making JEPA planning more effective with offline data, the work contributes to the broader goal of building data-efficient and scalable AI systems.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The discussion section highlights important avenues for future research:
- **Hierarchical Representations:** To address the "locality of training" limitation (inaccurate distant relationships), exploring hierarchical representation spaces that model longer-range transitions or coarser trajectories could be transformative. This aligns with human cognitive abilities to plan at multiple levels of abstraction.
- **Dataset Quality and Exploration:** The observations about the influence of dataset characteristics (e.g., WB vs. WS) suggest that future work should investigate how different data collection strategies or "expert" trajectories could further improve value function learning, while balancing the need for diversity.
- **Robustness to Stochasticity:** The authors acknowledge that the current IQL approach might be biased in non-deterministic (random) environments, suggesting future work on making value-guided JEPA more robust to stochasticity. This is critical for real-world deployment.
- **Generalization to Complex Environments:** While tested on simple control tasks, extending these methods to more complex, high-dimensional, and physically realistic environments (e.g., robotics) would be a natural next step, showcasing the true potential of this approach.

In summary, this paper presents a compelling method for enhancing world model-based action planning by strategically structuring latent representation spaces with value functions. Its findings open promising avenues for developing more capable and autonomous AI agents, particularly those that can learn from passive observation and plan effectively towards long-horizon goals.
