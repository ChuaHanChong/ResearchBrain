# Machine Mental Imagery: Empower Multimodal Reasoning with Latent Visual Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UMass Amherst and MIT developed Mirage, a framework that enables Vision-Language Models to perform multimodal reasoning by interleaving compact latent visual tokens with text. This approach, inspired by human mental imagery, significantly outperforms text-only baselines and explicit image-generating models on spatial reasoning tasks, showing an 11% accuracy gain on VSP Spatial Planning.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are limited in tasks requiring "visual imagination" or manipulation (e.g., spatial reasoning) due to their reliance on text-only decoding, which forces verbose verbalization of visual thought.
- Unified multimodal models that generate explicit images suffer from heavy pre-training demands, potentially hindering reasoning ability and leading to a trade-off between generation fidelity and reasoning quality.
- Existing VLMs lack the ability to produce fluid, interleaved multimodal reasoning trajectories, where visual cues are seamlessly integrated alongside textual thought.

## Method

- Integrate special ==latent visual tokens== into VLM decoding, allowing the model to generate compact visual embeddings interleaved with text instead of explicit images.
- Implement a ==two-stage fine-tuning process==: Stage 1 grounds latent tokens to visual representations using cosine similarity loss, while Stage 2 allows these tokens to adapt for task-specific reasoning via text-only cross-entropy loss.
- Apply ==Reinforcement Learning (GRPO)== with accuracy and format rewards to further optimize the end-to-end ==multimodal reasoning trajectory== for task success.

## Results

- Mirage consistently surpassed text-only baselines and unified models that generate explicit images across spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSP, BLINK-Jigsaw, SAT, COMT).
- Achieved an **11%** improvement on VSP Spatial Planning (**58%** accuracy compared to **47%** for CoT SFT) and a **3%** gain on VSP Spatial Reasoning (**87%** accuracy compared to **84%** for CoT SFT).
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of both training stages, and the framework demonstrated robustness to hyperparameter changes and showed enhanced benefits for smaller model sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Emulating human "mental imagery" through compact latent visual tokens enables effective multimodal reasoning in VLMs without the computational burden of explicit pixel-level image generation.
> - A two-stage fine-tuning process, involving initial latent grounding followed by relaxation, is essential for enabling the model to generate flexible yet visually meaningful latent tokens that adapt for downstream reasoning tasks.
> - Interleaving these learned latent visual tokens within the reasoning chain consistently enhances VLM performance on complex spatial reasoning tasks, surpassing methods that rely solely on textual chains or explicit image generation.
