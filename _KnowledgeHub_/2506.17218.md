# Machine Mental Imagery: Empower Multimodal Reasoning with Latent Visual Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UMass Amherst and MIT developed Mirage, a framework that enables Vision-Language Models to perform multimodal reasoning by interleaving compact latent visual tokens with text. This approach, inspired by human mental imagery, significantly outperforms text-only baselines and explicit image-generating models on spatial reasoning tasks, showing an 11% accuracy gain on VSP Spatial Planning.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are limited in tasks requiring "visual imagination" or manipulation (e.g., spatial reasoning) due to their reliance on text-only decoding, which forces verbose verbalization of visual thought.
- Unified multimodal models that generate explicit images suffer from heavy pre-training demands, potentially hindering reasoning ability and leading to a trade-off between generation fidelity and reasoning quality.
- Existing VLMs lack the ability to produce fluid, interleaved multimodal reasoning trajectories, where visual cues are seamlessly integrated alongside textual thought.

## Method

- Integrate special ==latent visual tokens== into VLM decoding, allowing the model to generate compact visual embeddings interleaved with text instead of explicit images.
- Implement a ==two-stage fine-tuning process==: Stage 1 grounds latent tokens to visual representations using cosine similarity loss, while Stage 2 allows these tokens to adapt for task-specific reasoning via text-only cross-entropy loss.
- Apply ==Reinforcement Learning (GRPO)== with accuracy and format rewards to further optimize the end-to-end ==multimodal reasoning trajectory== for task success.

## Results

- Mirage consistently surpassed text-only baselines and unified models that generate explicit images across spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSP, BLINK-Jigsaw, SAT, COMT).
- Achieved an **11%** improvement on VSP Spatial Planning (**58%** accuracy compared to **47%** for CoT SFT) and a **3%** gain on VSP Spatial Reasoning (**87%** accuracy compared to **84%** for CoT SFT).
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of both training stages, and the framework demonstrated robustness to hyperparameter changes and showed enhanced benefits for smaller model sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Emulating human "mental imagery" through compact latent visual tokens enables effective multimodal reasoning in VLMs without the computational burden of explicit pixel-level image generation.
> - A two-stage fine-tuning process, involving initial latent grounding followed by relaxation, is essential for enabling the model to generate flexible yet visually meaningful latent tokens that adapt for downstream reasoning tasks.
> - Interleaving these learned latent visual tokens within the reasoning chain consistently enhances VLM performance on complex spatial reasoning tasks, surpassing methods that rely solely on textual chains or explicit image generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical limitation in current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and positions itself at the intersection of multimodal reasoning, generative AI, and cognitive inspiration.

Traditionally, VLMs excel at understanding visual information in conjunction with text, often by converting visual inputs into textual descriptions or answering questions based on visual content through text-only decoding. However, the authors argue that this "text-only decoding" forces VLMs to "verbalize visual reasoning," which inherently limits their performance on tasks requiring deeper "visual imagination" or manipulation, such as spatial reasoning. Tasks like solving jigsaw puzzles or planning navigation routes often demand an internal, non-verbal manipulation of visual cues, akin to human mental imagery.

The broader landscape includes several parallel and contrasting lines of research:

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** CoT has been highly successful in improving Large Language Models (LLMs) and VLMs by eliciting step-by-step textual reasoning. While effective for verbalizable logic, the paper argues that CoT alone is insufficient for tasks that are inherently visual and non-verbal. Multimodal CoT extensions (e.g., ICoT, Visual CoT) attempt to embed visual evidence into reasoning, but often still rely on explicit image crops or bounding box annotations, which can be cumbersome and may not capture the fluid, internal nature of visual thought.
- **Unified Multimodal Models with Explicit Image Generation:** A recent trend involves training VLMs to generate both text and explicit images (e.g., Chameleon, MVoT). These models aim for a single framework capable of producing diverse multimodal outputs. However, the paper points out a significant drawback: the "heavy image-generation pre-training often hinders the reasoning ability." The cognitive demands of pixel-level image synthesis differ fundamentally from those of logical reasoning, potentially leading to a trade-off in performance. Furthermore, these models often cannot produce interleaved trajectories pertinent to input images naturally.
- **Latent Reasoning in LLMs:** Prior work in LLMs has explored guiding reasoning by introducing special "plan" or "pause" tokens or by distilling CoT rationales into latent representations. These methods aim to internalize reasoning processes within the model's hidden states, often for efficiency or structural control. Mirage draws inspiration from this by extending the concept of latent reasoning to the *multimodal* domain, treating latent tokens as "a bridge for exploring visual information into the model," rather than just enhancing textual planning or efficiency.
- **Cognitive Inspiration (Mental Imagery):** A key differentiator for Mirage is its direct inspiration from human "mental imagery." This psychological concept posits that humans don't summon photorealistic images when thinking but rather construct simplified, task-relevant "mental sketches." Mirage seeks to emulate this by generating "compact latent visual tokens" that represent essential visual cues without generating full pixel-level images. This aligns with the idea of efficient, internal visual manipulation rather than external, explicit rendering.

Mirage positions itself as a lightweight yet powerful alternative to explicit image generation, aiming to unlock the "dormant multimodal reasoning capacity of VLMs" by enabling them to reason directly in their latent visual embedding space. It offers a solution to the dilemma of improving multimodal reasoning without sacrificing reasoning quality or incurring high computational costs associated with pixel-level image generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind "Machine Mental Imagery" (Mirage) stems from the observed shortcomings of existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in handling tasks that require genuine visual reasoning and imagination.

**The primary problem identified is:**
While VLMs are highly proficient at multimodal understanding, their reliance on text-only decoding forces them to articulate complex visual thought processes verbally. This verbalization is often an inadequate substitute for true visual imagination, leading to degraded performance on tasks that inherently demand internal visual manipulation, such as spatial reasoning, planning, or completing visual patterns.

**Specific limitations and issues that motivate Mirage include:**
- **Inefficient Verbalization of Visual Reasoning:** For tasks like spatial planning or jigsaw puzzles, humans mentally picture and manipulate visual elements. VLMs, by contrast, must describe these visual manipulations in text, which can be cumbersome, verbose, and prone to error, failing to capture the fluidity and directness of visual thought.
- **Trade-off in Unified Models:** Recent attempts to overcome this by training VLMs to explicitly render images (like Chameleon) often involve "heavy image-generation pre-training." This extensive training for pixel-level synthesis can divert model capacity, leading to a "degradation of reasoning quality" as the model struggles to master both disparate goals.
- **Lack of Interleaved Multimodal Trajectories:** Existing VLM outputs are primarily sequential text. Even if they generate images, these are often isolated outputs rather than fluidly interleaved visual and textual thoughts that genuinely guide the reasoning process.

**Inspired by human cognition, the key objectives of Mirage are:**
1. **Emulate Human Mental Imagery:** To enable VLMs to reason using internal, compact, and task-relevant visual cues, similar to how humans use "mental imagery" to solve visual problems without summoning photorealistic pictures. This means moving beyond pixel-level generation to a more abstract, embedding-space manipulation.
2. **Facilitate Interleaved Multimodal Reasoning:** To empower VLMs to produce "interleaved multimodal trajectories" where "latent visual tokens" are seamlessly integrated alongside ordinary text. This allows for a native multimodal thought process that reflects how humans might switch between verbal and visual thinking.
3. **Improve Multimodal Reasoning Performance without Explicit Image Generation:** To achieve stronger multimodal reasoning capabilities on challenging benchmarks without the computational burden, capacity trade-offs, and potentially degraded reasoning quality associated with generating explicit, pixel-level images. The goal is to provide essential visual cues compactly, dedicating model capacity primarily to reasoning.
4. **Develop a Robust Training Paradigm:** To devise a multi-stage training framework that effectively grounds these latent visual tokens in meaningful visual representations while allowing them the flexibility to adapt and optimize for the downstream reasoning task.

In essence, Mirage seeks to bridge the gap between a VLM's understanding of images and its ability to "imagine" and manipulate them internally for complex reasoning, all while maintaining efficiency and reasoning fidelity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mirage introduces a novel framework for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform multimodal reasoning by interleaving compact latent visual tokens with ordinary text, inspired by human mental imagery. The methodology involves a carefully designed data generation pipeline and a two-stage fine-tuning process, further enhanced by reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Core Idea: Latent Visual Tokens

Instead of generating explicit images, Mirage augments VLM decoding with special "latent visual tokens." When the model decides to "think visually," it recasts its current hidden states as these compact visual embeddings, appending them to the context. This allows for a multimodal trajectory without generating pixel-level images, furnishing focused visual cues for subsequent reasoning steps.

#### 3.2. Data Generation (Sec. 3.1)

To supervise this interleaved reasoning pattern, Mirage requires a training corpus with multimodal reasoning chains.
- **Helper Image Creation:** For each input `(x, y)` (image-text input and ground-truth answer), a "helper image" (`I`) is synthesized using task-specific tools. These helper images are designed to explicitly deliver the visual cues needed for reasoning.
 - **VSP (Spatial Planning):** Map annotated with the ground-truth path (red arrows).
 - **VSP (Spatial Reasoning):** Snapshot of the agent's current state on the map after partial action sequence.
 - **BLINK-Jigsaw:** Concatenation of a candidate patch beside the reference image.
 - **SAT:** A powerful video generation model (CogVideoX-5B) renders a scene matching a textual description, from which the most informative frame is selected as the helper image.
 - **COMT:** Directly uses its pre-existing interleaved multimodal reasoning trajectories.
- **Reasoning Chain Synthesis:** A large reasoning VLM (Qwen2.5-VL 32B) is prompted with the original input `x`, ground-truth answer `y`, and the `helper image I`. It's instructed to generate a step-by-step reasoning chain (`o`) that "incorporates the helper image." This chain is structured as `o = o_pre ⊕ I ⊕ o_post`, effectively embedding the helper image within the textual thought process. Multiple distinct reasoning trajectories are generated per helper image for diversity.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Fine-tuning Paradigm

The synthetic data is then used to train the VLM in two distinct stages:

- **Stage 1: Joint Supervision for Latent Grounding (Sec. 3.2)**
 - **Objective:** To anchor the latent tokens firmly in the visual representation space and teach the model to weave visual cues into its output.
 - **Process:** For each `(x, I, o, y)` example:
 1. The `helper image I` is passed through the VLM's vision encoder to obtain its patch-level features `{e_1..., e_n}`.
 2. These features are *compressed* into `k` salient vectors, `{ˆe_1..., ˆe_k}`, typically using simple average pooling. These `ˆe` vectors serve as the "ground-truth" compact visual embeddings.
 3. The model is trained to:
 - Generate `o_pre` conditioned on `x` using standard cross-entropy loss.
 - Generate `k` latent tokens (last layer hidden states) at specific positions, which are supervised by the compressed `ˆe` vectors using a **cosine similarity loss (`L_visual`)**. This forces the model's internal latent representations to align with real visual features.
 - Generate `o_post` conditioned on `x`, `o_pre`, and the *ground-truth* `ˆe` vectors, again with cross-entropy loss (`L_text`).
 - **Total Loss:** `L_1 = L_visual + γL_text`, where `γ` is a loss coefficient. This stage ensures that the model learns to associate the "latent" decision with a coherent visual meaning.

- **Stage 2: Text-Only Supervision with Latent Relaxation (Sec. 3.3)**
 - **Objective:** To allow the model to adapt its self-generated latent tokens to the task objective (answering correctly) without being strictly constrained by external visual embeddings, leading to a more flexible and task-focused reasoning.
 - **Process:**
 1. The `L_visual` (cosine similarity) loss is removed.
 2. Only the standard **cross-entropy loss on text tokens (`L_text`)** is used.
 3. Crucially, for generating `o_post`, the model now attends to its *autoregressively produced* latent tokens `{e_j}` (derived from its own hidden states when it decides to generate a latent token), instead of the ground-truth `ˆe` vectors.
 4. Since these self-generated latent tokens are differentiable, gradients from the `L_text` loss can propagate back through them, implicitly optimizing their generation to best support the subsequent text generation and ultimately the task goal.
 - This stage refines the learned latent space, allowing the model to generate latent visual cues that are optimized for reasoning rather than perfect visual reconstruction.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning (RL) (Sec. 3.4)

After the two supervised fine-tuning stages, the model's reasoning capabilities are further boosted using Reinforcement Learning.
- **Approach:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is adopted.
- **Process:** The model samples multiple responses for each input query. The RL training explicitly optimizes probabilities of textual tokens while allowing gradients to flow through the latent tokens.
- **Rewards:** Two types of rewards are used:
 - `r_acc(o,x)`: Accuracy reward (1 for correct answer, 0 otherwise).
 - `r_format(o)`: Format reward (0.1 if output adheres to specified format, 0 otherwise).
This RL stage fine-tunes the entire multimodal reasoning trajectory for end-to-end task success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on diverse spatial reasoning benchmarks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the Mirage framework.

#### 4.1. Benchmarks and Setup

- **Benchmarks:** VSP (Visual-Spatial Planning: Spatial Reasoning & Spatial Planning subtasks), BLINK-Jigsaw, SAT (Synthetic & Real), and COMT (Mathematical Geometry subset).
- **Data:** 1k training instances for fine-tuning, 2k for reinforcement learning (except COMT, which has pre-existing data). Helper images and reasoning thoughts are synthesized as described in the methodology.
- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL 7B (and 3B for generalization study).

#### 4.2. Key Performance Gains

- **Superior to Text-Only Baselines:** Mirage significantly outperforms models relying solely on text-only decoding (Direct SFT, CoT SFT).
 - On VSP Spatial Reasoning, Mirage (CoT) achieves 87% average accuracy, compared to 84% for CoT SFT.
 - On VSP Spatial Planning, Mirage (CoT) achieves 58% average accuracy, compared to 47% for CoT SFT, marking an 11% improvement.
 - Consistent gains are observed across COMT, Jigsaw, and SAT tasks, underscoring that interleaved compact visual cues consistently strengthen spatial reasoning.
- **Outperforms Unified Models with Explicit Image Generation:** Mirage demonstrates a clear advantage over unified models like Anole and MVoT, which explicitly generate image tokens. Despite explicit image generation, these baselines perform poorly on interleaved reasoning tasks (e.g., Anole 61% on VSP Spatial Reasoning, MVoT 11% on Spatial Planning). This validates Mirage's core premise that avoiding heavy pixel generation and focusing on latent visual cues is beneficial for reasoning.
- **Reinforcement Learning Boost:** The addition of reinforcement learning (GRPO) further enhances Mirage's performance, yielding extra gains (e.g., +2% accuracy on VSP tasks), confirming that RL can further align the flexible latent reasoning with task goals.
- **Robustness on Spatial Planning:** While VSP Spatial Planning is challenging (synthesized thoughts sometimes perform worse than direct SFT due to task nature and thought imperfections), Mirage significantly narrows this gap, demonstrating its practical robustness even with noisy synthetic data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Two Stages:** Both training stages are critical.
 - Training with *only Stage 1* (joint supervision) leads to constrained performance, similar to unified models, as latent tokens are grounded but not optimized for task-specific reasoning.
 - Training with *only Stage 2* (text-only supervision with relaxation) performs poorly without the initial grounding, as latent vectors drift without visual alignment. This highlights the unique role of Stage 1 in anchoring latent tokens in the visual subspace before Stage 2 allows for task-adaptive refinement.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** Mirage shows robustness to variations in latent token size (`k`) and multimodal loss coefficient (`γ`). Optimal performance is observed for `k` between 2 and 6. A larger `k` (e.g., 8) leads to performance degradation, likely due to error accumulation in longer autoregressive latent sequences. This indicates that a compact set of latent tokens is sufficient and beneficial.

#### 4.4. Analysis and Insights

- **Generalization to Smaller Models:** Mirage's benefits extend to smaller models (Qwen2.5-VL 3B), achieving even larger improvements over text-only baselines (5% on Jigsaw, 10% on SAT Real), suggesting its broad applicability across model scales.
- **Synthesized Data Quality:** Analysis confirms that the synthesized helper images are indeed informative. When these images are provided as explicit input, the models achieve nearly 100% accuracy, validating the quality of the data used for latent supervision. This indicates that if Mirage can effectively internalize this information, it has a strong performance ceiling.
- **Latent Behavior Analysis (t-SNE Visualization):** Visualizing the latent embeddings shows that:
 - Text embeddings are scattered broadly.
 - Explicit visual embeddings (from the VLM encoder) cluster tightly in a distinct visual subspace.
 - Mirage's *latent tokens* form a compact cloud that is clearly separated from text but sits *just outside* the core visual cluster. This behavior is consistent with the two-stage training: Stage 1 grounds them visually, and Stage 2 allows them to adapt and become more abstract for reasoning, echoing the "sketch-like" nature of human mental imagery rather than photorealistic reproduction. They remain close to the visual manifold while retaining flexibility.

In summary, Mirage effectively demonstrates that reasoning with compact, internally generated latent visual tokens, trained through a two-stage process and refined by RL, significantly boosts multimodal reasoning capabilities in VLMs, outperforming traditional text-only and explicit image-generating approaches for visual imagination-intensive tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Machine Mental Imagery" (Mirage) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with several key implications and potential impacts:

- **Bridging the Gap in Multimodal Reasoning:** Mirage directly addresses a core limitation of VLMs: their struggle with tasks requiring genuine visual imagination and manipulation. By enabling models to "think visually" using compact latent tokens, it provides a more natural and effective mechanism for multimodal reasoning beyond mere textual description or explicit image generation. This allows VLMs to tackle complex spatial reasoning, planning, and compositional tasks more effectively.

- **Efficiency and Performance Synergy:** Unlike unified models that generate explicit images, Mirage demonstrates that high-quality multimodal reasoning does not necessarily require the computationally heavy process of pixel-level image synthesis. By focusing on compact latent representations, Mirage offers a lightweight yet powerful approach that avoids the trade-off between reasoning quality and generation fidelity. This efficiency makes it more practical for deployment in scenarios where computational resources are constrained.

- **Cognitive Alignment:** The framework's inspiration from human mental imagery is a notable contribution. By mirroring the human ability to internally construct and manipulate abstract visual cues, Mirage moves AI systems closer to a more human-like cognitive process for multimodal understanding. This cognitive alignment could lead to more intuitive and robust AI behaviors in complex real-world scenarios.

- **Novel Training Paradigm:** The two-stage training paradigm is a significant methodological contribution.
 - Stage 1's "latent grounding" ensures that the internal visual tokens are anchored in meaningful visual features, preventing them from drifting into irrelevant latent spaces.
 - Stage 2's "latent relaxation" allows these grounded tokens to dynamically adapt and optimize for the downstream reasoning task, providing flexibility that purely reconstructive approaches lack. This dual approach offers a valuable blueprint for training models that require both fidelity to input modalities and adaptability for complex tasks.
 - The further enhancement via reinforcement learning demonstrates how to align these internal reasoning trajectories with overall task objectives, a crucial step for real-world application.

- **Unlocking Deeper VLM Capabilities:** The consistent performance gains across diverse spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSP, Jigsaw, SAT, COMT) highlight Mirage's ability to unlock deeper multimodal reasoning capabilities within existing VLM architectures. This suggests that the latent spaces of VLMs hold more potential for complex internal computations than previously exploited by text-only or pixel-explicit decoding methods.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Embodied AI and Robotics:** Tasks like navigation, manipulation, and interaction in physical environments heavily rely on spatial reasoning and planning. Mirage's ability to internally simulate visual changes and reason about them could significantly improve the capabilities of embodied AI agents and robots, enabling more intelligent and adaptive decision-making.
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** As AI systems become more integrated into daily life, their ability to understand and participate in visual reasoning tasks in a natural, intuitive way (e.g., helping with design, planning, or complex visual problem-solving) will become crucial. Mirage's approach could lead to more natural and effective human-AI collaboration.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation Multimodal Models:** Mirage introduces a novel paradigm for how VLMs can process and reason about visual information. This could inspire future research into more sophisticated forms of "machine imagination," leading to new architectures and training methods that further blur the lines between perception, reasoning, and generation.
- **Reduced Resource Demands for High-Performance VLMs:** By demonstrating that explicit image generation is not a prerequisite for advanced multimodal reasoning, Mirage could encourage the development of more efficient and accessible high-performance VLMs, especially for tasks where internal visual thought is more critical than external visual rendering.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- **Synthetic Data Quality:** The quality of the generated multimodal trajectories is crucial. Future work should focus on curating higher-quality datasets or developing more robust data synthesis methods.
- **Extension to Unified Models:** Exploring how Mirage's latent reasoning can be integrated with or leveraged by existing unified models (which align latent spaces through image and text token generation) remains an open question.
- **Task Scale Beyond Spatial Reasoning:** Current evaluation is limited to spatial reasoning. Extending Mirage to broader multimodal or purely textual tasks could reveal its generalizability.

In conclusion, Mirage presents a compelling vision for future VLMs, moving beyond mere understanding and explicit generation to a more sophisticated form of internal "visual thought." This innovative approach holds the promise of making VLMs more intelligent, efficient, and cognitively aligned, paving the way for more capable and versatile AI systems.
