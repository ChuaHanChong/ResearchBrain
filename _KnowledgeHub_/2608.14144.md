# Self-Supervised Visual On-Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> S2VOPD is a self-supervised on-policy distillation method for VLMs that inverts teacher-student asymmetry: instead of giving the teacher privileged information, it degrades the student's input view (downscale+noise) while the teacher sees the clean image, distilling via generalized JSD. No labels, rewards, or stronger teacher needed. Raises Qwen3.5-4B from 70.7% to 77.4% on six perception benchmarks, beating Qwen3-VL-235B and GPT-5.4.

## Problem

- Conventional on-policy distillation needs a larger, stronger teacher model, which becomes harder to obtain as student capability approaches or exceeds available teachers.
- On-policy self-distillation removes the separate-teacher requirement but still depends on privileged supervision like ground-truth regions, reference answers, or environmental feedback.
- Privileged-information methods boost perception but degrade math reasoning (e.g., large MathVerse drops), while reward-free self-rewarding RL methods barely improve perception.

## Method

- **S2VOPD** conditions an ==EMA teacher== on the clean image and the student on an augmented view of the same image, creating asymmetry without external supervision, distilled via per-token ==generalized Jensen-Shannon divergence== restricted to top-k tokens.
- A systematic augmentation-space search across four families (information reduction, geometric, photometric, occlusion) with a composable stochastic transformation pipeline governed by per-operator probability and strength ranges.
- Best recipe composes downscaling (0.3-0.6x resolution, no resize-back) with stochastic ==DDPM-style Gaussian noise== applied only to the student view, cutting rollout and forward-pass cost too.

## Results

- S2VOPD improves Qwen3.5-4B from **70.68%** to **77.44%** average accuracy across six perception benchmarks, surpassing Qwen3-VL-Instruct-235B (**75.75%**) and GPT-5.4 (**72.77%**), matching Qwen3.5-397B (**77.44%**).
- On matched Vision-OPD-6K training data, S2VOPD beats the strongest self-rewarding baseline by **+2.0%** at 4B and **+0.7%** at 9B, recovering **96%** of the gain from privileged-information methods.
- Ablations show removing augmentation collapses gains to base level (**70.52%**), while freezing the teacher (no EMA) still retains **93%** of the gain (**75.95%** vs **76.35%**), confirming asymmetry drives the signal, not teacher improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymmetry itself, not extra information given to the teacher, is what powers distillation-withholding information from the student can substitute for a stronger teacher entirely.
> - Teacher-student predictive gap follows an inverted-U with strength: too weak yields no signal, too strong (e.g., aggressive cropping) creates large but uninformative discrepancy by destroying task-relevant evidence.
> - A balanced divergence (JSD) outperforms forward or reverse KL for asymmetric-view distillation, since forward KL over-forces imitation of inaccessible details and reverse KL discards corrective signal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of post-training methodologies for large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), specifically focusing on **on-policy distillation (OPD)**. OPD is a technique designed to enhance a student policy by leveraging dense teacher supervision along the student's own generated trajectories. A central requirement for effective OPD is an "informative teacher–student asymmetry," meaning the teacher must possess superior information or capabilities to provide a more accurate predictive target than the student itself.

Traditional OPD approaches obtain this asymmetry in two primary ways:
1. **Larger or Stronger Teacher Models:** Using a pre-trained teacher model that is significantly larger or more capable than the student.
2. **Privileged Information:** In the context of on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), where a single model acts as both teacher and student, the teacher is granted access to additional, privileged information. This can include reference solutions (e.g., ground-truth answers), environmental feedback, or specific ground-truth regions of interest within an image.

The paper identifies a growing challenge with these conventional methods: as model capabilities advance, obtaining reliable and sufficiently informative privileged information or training substantially stronger teacher models becomes increasingly difficult and resource-intensive. This limitation motivates the central question addressed by this research.

The work also draws inspiration from **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, particularly methods that construct supervision signals from multiple augmented views of the same unlabeled input. Techniques like SimCLR, BYOL, and DINO utilize augmentation to define invariances or provide learning targets across different views, enabling models to learn without explicit external labels. While these SSL methods typically operate at the representation learning level, S²VOPD adapts the principle of using augmentation to generate a learning signal at the token-level for generative distillation.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts its approach with existing uses of visual augmentation in vision-language models, such as diversifying exploration in reinforcement learning (e.g., NoisyRollout) or identifying perception-critical tokens (e.g., VPPO, PRPO). In these prior works, augmentation primarily modulates learning driven by external rewards, and often both augmented and clean views are evaluated by the *same* policy. S²VOPD differentiates itself by applying augmentation *only* to the student, with the discrepancy between the teacher (observing the clean image) and the student (observing the augmented view) serving as the *direct* training signal, thereby eliminating the need for external rewards or annotations.

In summary, this research positions itself as an alternative to reliance on external privileged information in OPD, proposing a self-supervised mechanism to generate the necessary teacher–student asymmetry directly from the input data, thereby making OPD more scalable and less dependent on costly human annotations or computationally intensive stronger teachers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address a fundamental question in on-policy distillation: **where can informative asymmetry come from when nothing privileged is available?** This question arises from the increasing difficulty and cost associated with obtaining privileged information (such as ground-truth annotations, reference solutions, or a significantly stronger, separate teacher model) as visual-language models grow in complexity and capability.

The core motivation behind S²VOPD is to develop a method that can generate an effective and informative learning signal for on-policy distillation **for free**, without relying on external annotations, rewards, or the existence of a separately trained, stronger teacher model.

To achieve this, the authors propose an inversion of the conventional approach to creating teacher–student asymmetry. Instead of *adding* privileged information to the teacher, they **subtract information from the student**. Specifically, the key objectives are:

1. **Construct Asymmetry through Visual Augmentation:** To demonstrate that an informative predictive discrepancy can be created by having the teacher observe the original, uncorrupted input image, while the student operates on a deliberately degraded or augmented view of the same image. This informational difference between teacher and student is hypothesized to induce the necessary asymmetry for distillation.
2. **Develop Self-Supervised Visual On-Policy Distillation (S²VOPD):** To introduce a concrete methodology that implements this principle, where an exponentially moving average (EMA) teacher is conditioned on the original image, and its distributions are distilled into a student policy conditioned on a strongly augmented view of the same image, along the student's own generated trajectories.
3. **Empirically Explore the Augmentation Design Space:** To systematically investigate the properties of visual augmentations that make the induced teacher–student discrepancy an effective supervisory signal. This involves a controlled, large-scale search to answer critical questions such as:
 - Does the presence of asymmetry (any augmentation) universally improve performance, or can symmetric distillation (no asymmetry) also be effective?
 - What is the optimal *strength* of augmentation? Does performance monotonically increase with the severity of augmentation, or is there a peak?
 - Must the induced information gap remain *task-consistent*? Are there types of augmentations that create a large discrepancy but an uninformative learning signal by removing task-relevant evidence?
4. **Evaluate Performance and Generalizability:** To rigorously assess S²VOPD's effectiveness across multiple fine-grained perception and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, comparing it against state-of-the-art open-source and proprietary models, as well as existing OPD methods that utilize privileged information or self-rewarding reinforcement learning. A specific goal is to demonstrate that S²VOPD can recover a significant portion of the gains achieved by methods requiring privileged information, while simultaneously improving both perception and reasoning abilities.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to establish a more scalable, annotation-free, and generalizable approach to improving vision-language models through on-policy distillation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology is **Self-Supervised Visual On-Policy Distillation (S²VOPD)**, which constructs a learning signal by introducing an informational asymmetry between a teacher and a student purely through visual augmentation, without external supervision.

#### 3.1. Self-Supervised Visual On-Policy Distillation (S²VOPD) Principle

- **Teacher-Student Setup:** The method uses a policy $\pi_\theta$ to be optimized as the student and an exponentially moving average (EMA) of the student's weights $\pi_\phi$ as the teacher. The teacher's weights $\phi$ are updated as $\phi \leftarrow (1-\eta)\phi + \eta\theta$.
- **Asymmetric On-Policy Views:** For each image-question pair $(x, q)$, the teacher observes the original, clean image $x$. In contrast, the student observes an augmented view $\tilde{x} = T(x)$, where $T$ is a stochastic transformation.
- **On-Policy Rollouts:** The student samples $n$ rollouts (sequences of tokens) $y^{(1)}, \ldots, y^{(n)}$ from its policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot|\tilde{x}, q)$, conditioned on its corrupted view $\tilde{x}$.
- **Distillation Objective:** S²VOPD then distills the teacher's next-token distribution $p_t^\tau = \pi_\phi(\cdot|x, q, y_{<t})$ (conditioned on the clean view) into the student's next-token distribution $p_t^s = \pi_\theta(\cdot|\tilde{x}, q, y_{<t})$ (conditioned on the augmented view). The training objective minimizes the expected per-token divergence between these two distributions, evaluated along the trajectories sampled by the student:
 $$L(\theta) = E_{(x,q)\sim D} E_{y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|\tilde{x},q)} \left[ \frac{1}{|y|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y|} D\left( \pi_\phi(\cdot|x, q, y_{<t}) \parallel \pi_\theta(\cdot|\tilde{x}, q, y_{<t}) \right) \right]$$
- **Divergence Function:** The divergence $D(\cdot \parallel \cdot)$ is instantiated as the generalized Jensen–Shannon divergence $D_\alpha^{\text{JS}}$, which interpolates between forward and reverse Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergences with a mixture weight $\alpha$. For this work, $\alpha=0.5$ (standard JSD) is used. Both distributions are restricted to the teacher's top-$k$ tokens at each position $t$ and then renormalized, which helps stabilize the objective.
- **Source of Supervision:** Crucially, the transformation $T$ is the sole source of the teacher's informational advantage and the resulting predictive discrepancy. This removes the need for external labels, rewards, privileged annotations, or a stronger teacher model.

#### 3.2. Constructing Asymmetry Through Augmentation

The design of the stochastic transformation $T$ is central to S²VOPD's effectiveness. $T$ is defined by three components: a set of primitive operators, their application probabilities and strength ranges, and a fixed composition policy for combining selected operators. A global probability $p$ controls whether augmentation is applied at all.

The operators are categorized into four families based on how they alter visual information:

1. **Information Reduction:** Operators that decrease usable visual information while maintaining spatial layout.
 - *Subtractive:* Downscaling, Gaussian blur, pixelation, spectral band-stop filtering.
 - *Additive:* Gaussian noise.
 - *Structural:* Visual-token dropping.
2. **Geometric:** Operators that modify spatial organization.
 - Rotation, translation, cropping, zoom-out with padding. These can alter answers to spatially grounded questions.
3. **Photometric:** Operators that change appearance without altering geometry or content.
 - Brightness, contrast, saturation, hue, gamma, sharpness, histogram equalization.
4. **Occlusion:** Operators that remove localized image regions.
 - Random erasing (with constant or noise fill), GridMask. Distinguished from information reduction by spatial locality.

**Best Recipe for Augmentation:** Through empirical exploration, the most effective transformation $T$ found comprises two information-reduction operators:
- **Downscaling:** Applied to every student view ($p=1$), reducing the image resolution (and thus the number of visual tokens) to a scale factor $s \sim U(0.3, 0.6)$ without resizing back.
- **Additive Gaussian Noise:** Applied independently with probability $\rho_{\text{noise}} = 0.5$. The noise level corresponds to a DDPM forward step at $t=200$, effectively adding Gaussian noise with $\sigma \approx 0.11$.
This specific composition serves as the default instantiation of $T$ in S²VOPD and offers a practical benefit by reducing student forward-pass computational costs.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Base Models:** Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B, state-of-the-art vision-language models.
- **Training Data:**
 - Primary training: 12K questions sampled from the natural-image domain of FineVision.
 - For fair comparison with prior methods: Vision-OPD-6K dataset (which contains ground-truth region annotations for other methods, but not used by S²VOPD).
- **Training Configuration:** Batch size of 96 prompts, $n=8$ rollouts per prompt, learning rate $2 \times 10^{-6}$ with 10 warmup steps, 65 or 130 optimizer steps, EMA decay $\eta=0.05$. Maximum prompt length 8,192 tokens, response length 1,024 tokens.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - **Perception:** V\*Bench, ZoomBench, HR-Bench 4K, HR-Bench 8K, MME-RealWorld, MME-RealWorld-Chinese.
 - **Math Reasoning:** MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision.
- **Inference & Evaluation Metrics:** Greedy decoding (max 4,096 tokens for perception; 24,576 tokens for math with T=0.3, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, presence penalty 1.5). Answers are extracted and evaluated using near-exact matching, with an LLM judge (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) adjudicating unresolved cases.
- **Baselines:**
 - Base model (Qwen3.5-4B/9B).
 - Symmetric self-distillation (no augmentation).
 - Methods with privileged information: ZwZ (ground-truth regions), Vision-OPD (ground-truth regions), OPSD (ground-truth answers).
 - Self-rewarding RL methods: TTRL, Intuitor, RENT (trained on Vision-OPD-6K for 65 steps, best checkpoint reported due to instability).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several key findings regarding the effectiveness of S²VOPD and the design principles for constructing self-supervised asymmetry.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements (Table 2)

- **Significant Gains:** S²VOPD trained on a 12K subset of FineVision improved the average accuracy of the Qwen3.5-4B base model from 70.68% to 77.44% across six fine-grained perception benchmarks, representing a gain of 6.76 percentage points.
- **Competitive with Larger Open-Source Models:** The 4B model enhanced by S²VOPD surpassed all compared open-source models, including the much larger Qwen3-VL-Instruct-235B (75.75%). It achieved performance comparable to Qwen3.5-397B (77.44%), a significantly larger model.
- **Competitive with Proprietary Models:** S²VOPD outperformed models in the GPT-5 series (e.g., GPT-5.4 at 72.77%) and was on par with Gemini-3-Flash (77.67%), trailing only Gemini-3.5-Flash and Gemini-3.1-Pro by less than 2%.
- **Superior to Privileged-Information Methods:** Despite not using ground-truth regions or answers during training, S²VOPD (77.44%) surpassed all privileged-supervised methods reported in the study, including ZwZ (74.38%), Vision-OPD (77.07%), and OPSD (72.85%) at the 4B scale.

#### 4.2. Fair Comparison with Prior Methods on Vision-OPD-6K (Table 3)

- **Best Performance without Privileged Information:** When trained on the Vision-OPD-6K dataset, S²VOPD demonstrated the best overall average performance among all methods that do not rely on privileged information. At the 4B scale, it achieved 75.33%, outperforming the strongest self-rewarding RL baseline (TTRL at 73.30%) by 2.0%. At the 9B scale, it reached 76.35%, exceeding Intuitor (75.65%) by 0.7%.
- **Competitive with Supervised Baselines:** S²VOPD at 4B also surpassed supervised baselines like ZwZ (67.86%), OPSD (72.19%), and Vision-OPD (74.77%). At 9B, S²VOPD (76.35%) matched Vision-OPD (76.56%) as the second-best overall method, despite its self-supervised nature.
- **Balanced Improvement across Perception and Reasoning:** A notable finding was S²VOPD's ability to consistently improve both visual perception and mathematical reasoning. For the 4B model, it showed gains of +5.7% in perception and +3.7% in math reasoning. This contrasts with:
 - Privileged-supervision methods (e.g., OPSD, ZwZ) which significantly improved perception but often degraded math reasoning performance (e.g., OPSD decreased MathVision by 9.3% at 4B).
 - Self-rewarding RL methods (e.g., TTRL, Intuitor, RENT) which generally improved math reasoning but exhibited limited gains on perception. S²VOPD achieved near-best perception performance while also matching strong results on math reasoning.

#### 4.3. Insights into Augmentation Design (Figure 2)

- **Asymmetry is Necessary:** Symmetric self-distillation (no augmentation, i.e., student sees the same clean image as the teacher) resulted in a degradation of performance (65.21% from a base of 70.58%), indicating that an informational gap is crucial.
- **All Augmentation Families are Effective:** Each of the four augmentation families, when applied independently, led to substantial performance improvements over both the base model and unaugmented self-distillation. Information reduction was the most effective (75.65%), followed by photometric (74.40%), geometric (74.30%), and occlusion (72.44%).
- **Strength Matters (Moderate Gap is Optimal):** Performance consistently showed an inverse U-shape relationship with augmentation strength. It improved with increasing strength up to a point, then declined if the augmentation became too severe. The optimal teacher–student predictive gap (measured as JS Divergence) was approximately 0.014. For instance, resolution reduction peaked at a scale range of 0.3-0.6.
- **Task-Consistent Gap is Crucial:** Augmentations must preserve question-relevant information. Aggressive cropping, despite inducing the largest predictive gap, resulted in degraded performance (e.g., 67.44% for strong crops). This is attributed to the cropping removing essential evidence required to answer the question, rendering the discrepancy uninformative for learning.

#### 4.4. Ablation and Analysis (Table 4, 5, 6)

- **Necessity of Asymmetry:** Ablating student-view augmentation (w/o Aug.) eliminated the performance gain, confirming that the constructed asymmetry is essential for the method's effectiveness.
- **Role of EMA Teacher:** While the EMA teacher contributes, freezing the teacher at the base model (w/o EMA) still recovered 93% of the gain, indicating that the primary benefit stems from the visual input asymmetry rather than the teacher's self-improvement.
- **Robustness to EMA Update Rate:** S²VOPD's performance remained stable across a range of teacher decay values (0.95 to 0.999), suggesting the method is not highly sensitive to the exact EMA update rate.
- **Balanced Divergence (JSD) Benefits:** The generalized Jensen–Shannon divergence (JSD, $\alpha=0.5$) consistently outperformed both forward KL ($\alpha=0.0$) and reverse KL ($\alpha=1.0$). This indicates that a balanced divergence, which avoids being overly coverage-seeking or mode-seeking, is optimal for transferring useful information while accommodating the degraded student view.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Self-Supervised Visual On-Policy Distillation (S²VOPD)" offers several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact in the field of vision-language model development:

1. **Novel Paradigm for Asymmetry Generation:** S²VOPD introduces a novel and fundamental shift in how informative teacher–student asymmetry is established for on-policy distillation. By inverting the conventional approach (subtracting information from the student rather than adding privileged information to the teacher), it demonstrates that a powerful learning signal can be generated purely from self-supervision using augmented views. This concept extends the utility of augmentation from representation learning to token-level generative distillation.

2. **Reduced Reliance on Privileged Information:** A major impact of S²VOPD is its ability to eliminate the need for costly and often difficult-to-obtain privileged information such as ground-truth annotations, external rewards, or separate, stronger teacher models. As vision-language models scale and their capabilities surpass human annotation capacity, annotation-free self-improvement methods become increasingly critical for continued progress.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Competitiveness:** The method significantly improves the performance of base models (e.g., Qwen3.5-4B), enabling them to match or exceed the performance of much larger open-source models (like Qwen3-VL-Instruct-235B) and even compete favorably with proprietary models (such as GPT-5 series) on fine-grained perception benchmarks. This demonstrates that intelligent self-supervision can yield substantial gains without proportional increases in model size or external supervision.

4. **Balanced Improvement Across Diverse Tasks:** S²VOPD consistently improves both fine-grained visual perception and mathematical reasoning capabilities. This distinguishes it from prior methods, where privileged-supervision often enhances perception at the cost of reasoning, and self-rewarding RL methods improve reasoning with limited perception gains. The ability to generalize positive impact across different cognitive tasks makes S²VOPD a more holistic and versatile improvement strategy.

5. **Empirical Insights into Augmentation Design:** The systematic exploration of the augmentation space provides valuable empirical guidelines for creating effective self-supervised learning signals. The findings that "asymmetry matters," "strength matters," and "the gap must remain task-consistent" offer actionable principles for future research in augmentation strategy design, particularly for generative models. This includes identifying optimal augmentation families (e.g., information reduction like downscaling with noise) and avoiding counterproductive augmentations (e.g., aggressive cropping).

6. **Efficiency and Scalability:** By utilizing lower-resolution student inputs (due to downscaling) and eliminating the need for complex external data sources or large teacher networks, S²VOPD contributes to more efficient and scalable training pipelines. This can lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners aiming to enhance VLM performance.

In conclusion, S²VOPD represents a step towards more autonomous and resource-efficient self-improvement mechanisms for vision-language models. Its ability to extract informative learning signals from asymmetric views of the same data, without external human intervention, positions it as a promising direction for developing increasingly capable and broadly applicable AI systems.
