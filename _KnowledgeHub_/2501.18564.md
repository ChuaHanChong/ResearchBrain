# SAM2Act: Integrating Visual Foundation Model with A Memory Architecture for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Washington, NVIDIA, and AI2 developed SAM2Act, a robotic manipulation policy integrating the SAM2 visual encoder with a multi-view transformer, achieving 86.8% success on RLBench tasks and demonstrating robust generalization. An extension, SAM2Act+, introduces a novel memory architecture, enabling 94.3% success on new memory-dependent benchmarks and outperforming prior methods by 39.3%.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies often lack high precision and robust generalization to diverse, unseen environments and objects.
- Existing imitation learning policies typically operate on a Markovian assumption, lacking mechanisms for spatial memory crucial for complex sequential tasks.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks exists to systematically evaluate spatial memory capabilities in robotic manipulation.

## Method

- **SAM2Act**: A multi-view robotic ==transformer== architecture that integrates a fine-tuned SAM2 visual encoder for rich, multi-resolution visual embeddings and a cascaded multi-resolution upsampling mechanism for enhanced precision.
- **SAM2Act**+: An extension that incorporates an explicit memory architecture (memory bank, encoder, and attention) to allow the policy to retain and condition on past observations and actions.
- MemoryBench: A new benchmark with non-Markovian tasks specifically designed to test spatial memory and action recall in robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, with a continuous push towards systems that can operate robustly and autonomously in unstructured, diverse environments. This paper, SAM2Act, positions itself at the forefront of this evolution by addressing three critical abilities for such systems: multitask interaction, generalization to unseen scenarios, and spatial memory.

Historically, robotic manipulation research has evolved from simpler 2D action-centric methods (ee.g., Transporter Networks, CLIPort) that perform well on basic pick-and-place tasks but struggle with complex spatial interactions. More recent developments have shifted towards 3D-based perception and action prediction using transformer architectures, exemplified by PerAct and RVT, and its successor RVT-2. These 3D methods improve spatial reasoning and precision by leveraging voxel-based representations or multi-view 2.5D representations. SAM2Act builds directly upon the success of RVT-2, inheriting its coarse-to-fine strategy for high-accuracy tasks, but significantly enhances it through advanced visual representations.

A major trend in robotics is the integration of **large-scale foundation models** from computer vision (like CLIP and SAM) to boost robotic agents' perception and generalization capabilities. These models provide rich, pre-trained visual embeddings that are highly effective for various downstream tasks. SAM2Act specifically leverages the **SAM2 visual encoder**, a successor to the Segment Anything Model, which is known for its robust object-centric segmentation and multi-resolution feature extraction. This integration allows SAM2Act to benefit from powerful, general-purpose visual understanding, improving its ability to handle diverse objects and scenes. The paper's novelty lies in how it effectively incorporates these multi-resolution visual features into a multi-view robotic transformer architecture for action prediction.

Furthermore, a significant gap in current robotic manipulation policies is the lack of robust **memory mechanisms**. Most existing imitation learning policies operate on a Markovian assumption, where the current action depends only on the current observation. However, many real-world tasks require agents to recall past observations or actions to make informed decisions (e.g., remembering where an object was placed or which drawer was opened). This paper directly addresses this by introducing SAM2Act+, which incorporates a memory-based architecture. This move positions the work alongside a growing body of research that seeks to equip robots with episodic and semantic memory, often drawing inspiration from human cognition or partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs). Unlike previous approaches that might use semantic maps or neural feature fields, SAM2Act+ integrates memory directly into the transformer-based policy, particularly by adapting components from SAM2's object tracking capabilities.

The introduction of **MemoryBench** is another crucial contribution to the broader landscape. It fills a void by providing a standardized benchmark specifically designed to evaluate spatial memory in robotic manipulation policies. This benchmark is critical for fostering research and development in memory-dependent tasks, which are often overlooked by existing benchmarks like RLBench that focus on long-horizon but typically Markovian scenarios.

In summary, SAM2Act and SAM2Act+ represent a significant advancement by combining state-of-the-art 3D robotic transformer architectures with powerful visual foundation models and a novel, explicit memory architecture. This holistic approach pushes the boundaries of precision, generalization, and—critically—the ability to perform tasks requiring spatial and action recall, moving closer to generalist robotic agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the SAM2Act research can be distilled into three core areas:

1. **Enhance Precision and Generalization in Robotic Manipulation:** The authors aim to develop a robotic policy capable of high-precision 3D manipulation tasks while demonstrating robust generalization to diverse, unseen environmental and object-level variations. They acknowledge that existing methods often fall short in complex scenarios or when faced with significant environmental perturbations. The motivation here is to build upon the strengths of recent 3D-based robotic transformers (like RVT-2) by integrating more powerful and versatile visual representations.
2. **Integrate Advanced Visual Foundation Models for Richer Representations:** A key motivation is to leverage the immense representational power of large-scale visual foundation models. Specifically, the paper seeks to effectively incorporate the multi-resolution, object-centric visual embeddings from the SAM2 encoder into a robotic policy. The hypothesis is that these richer visual features, combined with a sophisticated upsampling mechanism, will lead to superior spatial reasoning and more accurate action predictions, especially for high-accuracy manipulation.
3. **Address the Lack of Spatial Memory in Robotic Policies:** A crucial objective is to equip robotic agents with the ability to retain and recall information from past interactions, enabling them to solve memory-dependent tasks. Traditional behavior cloning and many reinforcement learning approaches often assume a fully observable environment (Markovian property), which limits their applicability to tasks requiring episodic memory. The authors are motivated to bridge this gap by designing an explicit memory architecture that allows the robot to build a "belief state" conditioned on historical actions and observations, thereby enhancing robustness and adaptability in sequential decision-making problems.
4. **Establish a Benchmark for Memory-Dependent Tasks:** Recognizing the absence of standardized evaluation for spatial memory in robotic manipulation, a significant objective is to introduce **MemoryBench**. This benchmark is designed to systematically assess a policy's capacity for spatial memory and action recall, providing a crucial tool for future research and development in this domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SAM2Act framework is a multi-view robotic transformer-based policy, primarily built upon the RVT-2 architecture, but with significant enhancements in visual feature representation and the addition of a memory architecture.

#### 3.1. SAM2Act (for Precision and Generalization)

The core of SAM2Act's approach to achieving high precision and generalization involves:

- **Scene Representation:** The system first reconstructs a point cloud of the scene from raw image inputs (RGB-D cameras). These point clouds are then rendered into virtual images from multiple orthogonal viewpoints, providing a comprehensive 2.5D representation of the workspace.
- **Multi-View Transformer (MVT) Backbone:** Similar to RVT-2, SAM2Act employs a two-stage (coarse-to-fine) multi-view transformer. The coarse branch identifies regions of interest and generates initial zoom-in heatmaps, while the fine branch refines these into precise action heatmaps for predicting 6-DoF end-effector poses (translation and quaternion orientation). The MVT processes language instructions (tokenized by CLIP) along with visual information.
- **SAM2 Visual Encoder Integration:** Instead of directly feeding virtual images into the MVT, SAM2Act processes them through the pre-trained **SAM2 image encoder**. This encoder generates powerful, object-centric, multi-resolution embeddings. These embeddings are crucial for providing richer visual context. The SAM2 encoder is fine-tuned using **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**, allowing efficient domain adaptation to manipulation tasks with minimal computational overhead.
- **Multi-Resolution Upsampling Mechanism:** A novel aspect is the cascaded multi-resolution upsampling method. This mechanism uses the multi-resolution embeddings from the SAM2 encoder as auxiliary inputs to progressively refine feature maps. Convex upsamplers, at each stage, double the spatial dimensions of feature maps, and the corresponding SAM2 embeddings are element-wise added after layer normalization. This direct integration of high-fidelity visual features into the translation heatmap generation process enhances spatial precision, especially for fine-grained manipulations.

#### 3.2. SAM2Act+ (for Spatial Memory)

To address memory-dependent tasks, SAM2Act introduces SAM2Act+, an extension that incorporates a memory architecture inspired by SAM2's object tracking capabilities:

- **Memory Architecture Components:** SAM2Act+ integrates three core memory components into the *coarse branch* of the SAM2Act architecture:
 - **Memory Bank:** This acts as a FIFO (First-In, First-Out) queue, storing a fixed number (N) of recent memory features. Each virtual camera view maintains its own independent memory bank, ensuring view-specific contextual recall.
 - **Memory Encoder:** This module generates memory features. It takes the predicted translation heatmap (from the coarse branch) and the *unconditioned* observation embedding from the MVT as input. By fusing these, it creates a rich memory feature that combines spatial action information with multi-view visual, language, and semantic context. This is conceptually analogous to how SAM2 encodes object masks.
 - **Memory Attention:** This module conditions the current observation features on the past memories stored in the Memory Bank. It employs transformer blocks with self-attention and cross-attention mechanisms. The cross-attention fuses the current image embedding (output of MVT) with the retrieved past memory features, allowing the model to integrate historical context into its current action prediction. 2D spatial Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) is used to maintain spatial awareness.
- **Training Strategy for Memory:** SAM2Act+ is trained in a two-stage process. First, SAM2Act is pre-trained using standard batch sampling. Then, the SAM2 image encoder and the MVT (in both coarse and fine branches) are frozen. The memory components are trained from scratch. Crucially, during fine-tuning, data is sampled in spatio-temporally consistent batches, meaning consecutive action keyframes from an episode are used. This allows the model to learn to predict actions based on a sequence of past observations and actions stored in its memory bank. The coarse branch is specifically fine-tuned for memory, as it focuses on broader contextual information, which is more relevant for memory tasks than the fine branch's focus on small, localized details.

#### 3.3. MemoryBench: A Novel Evaluation Benchmark

To systematically evaluate the spatial memory capabilities, the authors introduce MemoryBench, an extension of the RLBench simulator:

- **Non-Markovian Task Design:** MemoryBench tasks are specifically designed to violate the Markov assumption. This means that two distinct action histories can lead to visually identical current observations, forcing the agent to rely on memory to differentiate the correct subsequent action.
- **Spatial Memory Tasks:** Three novel tasks are introduced, each testing different aspects of spatial memory:
 - **`reopen_drawer`**: Tests recall of 3D spatial information (z-axis) by requiring the robot to close an initially open drawer, press a button (which closes all drawers), and then reopen the *original* drawer from memory.
 - **`put_block_back`**: Tests recall of 2D spatial information (x-y plane) by moving a block, pressing a button, and then returning the block to its *initial* position.
 - **`rearrange_block`**: Evaluates backward reasoning by requiring the robot to move a central block to an empty patch, press a button, and then move a *previously untouched* block from a specific patch (implying recall of its initial state relative to the other block).
- **Standardized Language Instructions:** Language instructions are carefully crafted to avoid unintentionally leaking spatial information, ensuring that memory is truly required.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SAM2Act and SAM2Act+ showcases significant advancements across several dimensions:

#### 4.1. Performance Across 18 RLBench Tasks

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance:** SAM2Act achieves an average success rate of **86.8% ± 0.5** across 18 RLBench tasks, outperforming the previous SOTA (RVT-2) by a significant margin of **5.4%**.
- **High Precision Tasks:** The model shows particularly strong improvements in tasks requiring high precision, such as "Insert Peg" (exceeding RVT-2 by 44%) and "Sort Shape" (outperforming RVT-2 by 29%), validating the effectiveness of its multi-resolution upsampling strategy.
- **Dominant Performance:** SAM2Act ranks first in 9 out of 18 tasks and remains highly competitive in most others, demonstrating its robust capabilities across a broad range of manipulation challenges.

#### 4.2. Semantic Generalization Across Tasks (The Colosseum Benchmark)

- **Robust Generalization:** SAM2Act demonstrates exceptional robustness to environmental and object-level perturbations. When evaluated on 20 tasks from The Colosseum benchmark under 13 different perturbation categories, SAM2Act exhibits the **smallest average performance drop of only 4.3%** (with a standard deviation of 3.59%) compared to baselines.
- **Environmental Robustness:** It is particularly robust to changes in lighting, table color/texture, addition of distractors, and camera pose.
- **Impact of SAM2 Features:** Ablation studies on The Colosseum benchmark (Appendix E.2) highlight the critical role of SAM2's multi-resolution image embeddings, showing a 14.8% performance drop (344.2% relative decrease) when these inputs are removed.

#### 4.3. Performance on MemoryBench

- **Significant Improvement with Memory Architecture:** SAM2Act+ achieves an impressive average success rate of **94.3% ± 9.0** across all memory-based tasks in MemoryBench.
- **Outperforming Baselines:** It significantly outperforms SAM2Act (without the memory architecture) by a substantial margin of **39.3%** on MemoryBench, and other non-memory-aware baselines (like RVT-2) by an even larger margin (e.g., 37.6% over RVT-2). This result strongly validates the efficacy of the proposed explicit memory modeling for memory-dependent tasks.
- **Task-Specific Success:** For instance, in "Put Block Back," SAM2Act+ achieves 100% success, and in "Rearrange Block," it scores 99.0%, demonstrating its ability to encode and retrieve both 2D and 3D spatial information.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Evaluations

- **Superior Real-World Performance:** In real-world experiments using a Franka Panda robot across four manipulation tasks, SAM2Act achieves a **75% overall task success rate**, significantly outperforming RVT-2 (43%).
- **High-Precision and Memory Tasks:** SAM2Act excels in real-world high-precision tasks (60% vs. 0% for RVT-2) and particularly in the memory-based task "Push the same button" (70% for SAM2Act+ vs. 40% for RVT-2), confirming its ability to translate simulation success to the physical world, even under out-of-distribution perturbations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "SAM2Act: Integrating Visual Foundation Model with A Memory Architecture for Robotic Manipulation" makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the field of robotics:

1. **Advancing Generalist Robotic Manipulation:** By addressing precision, generalization, and—critically—spatial memory, SAM2Act and SAM2Act+ push the boundaries towards creating more versatile and robust generalist robotic agents. These abilities are fundamental for robots to operate effectively in complex, dynamic, and human-centric environments.
2. **Effective Integration of Visual Foundation Models:** The work demonstrates a highly effective method for integrating powerful, pre-trained visual foundation models (specifically SAM2) into robotic policies. This paves the way for future robotic systems to readily leverage advancements in computer vision, reducing the need for extensive task-specific data collection and model training. The multi-resolution upsampling mechanism is a key innovation in this integration, ensuring that fine-grained visual details are preserved for precise actions.
3. **Pioneering Explicit Memory in Robotic Manipulation:** The introduction of SAM2Act+ and its memory architecture is a crucial step towards memory-aware robotic policies. This is vital for tasks that require sequential decision-making, implicit spatial recall, or tracking changes over time—a common requirement in real-world scenarios that are often beyond the scope of traditional Markovian approaches. This contribution highlights the necessity of moving beyond instantaneous observations for truly intelligent robot behavior.
4. **Introducing MemoryBench as a Standard:** The creation of MemoryBench is a valuable contribution to the research community. By providing a standardized benchmark with carefully designed non-Markovian tasks, it facilitates systematic evaluation and encourages further research into memory-based robot learning. This will accelerate progress in an area that has previously lacked dedicated assessment tools.
5. **Strong Empirical Validation:** The comprehensive experimental results, spanning state-of-the-art performance on RLBench, robust generalization on The Colosseum, groundbreaking success on MemoryBench, and positive real-world evaluations, underscore the practical viability and effectiveness of the proposed methods. The ability to generalize to unseen perturbations and perform complex tasks in the real world signifies a major step towards deployable robotic systems.

**Potential Impact:**
The immediate impact lies in enhancing the capabilities of robotic manipulators in manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics, where precision, adaptability, and complex sequential tasks are common. Longer term, this research contributes to the development of more autonomous and intelligent robots capable of operating in unstructured human environments, such as homes or hospitals, where memory of past interactions and environmental states is critical for seamless integration.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
The authors candidly acknowledge certain limitations:
- **Dexterous Continuous Control:** The current framework focuses on 6-DoF end-effector control and may not directly extend to more dexterous continuous control scenarios.
- **Fixed Memory Window Length:** SAM2Act+ relies on a fixed memory window length, which limits its adaptability to tasks of varying temporal lengths. Developing dynamic or adaptive memory mechanisms remains a challenge.
- **Semantic Memory Limitations:** The memory architecture currently appears limited to storing spatial information, struggling to retain semantic information (e.g., object color changes over time). Future work could explore richer memory representations that encode semantic properties alongside spatial data.

Despite these limitations, SAM2Act and SAM2Act+ represent a significant advancement, laying robust foundations for future research into memory-based generalist manipulation policies and intelligent robotic systems.
