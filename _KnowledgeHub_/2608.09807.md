# WRAP: Wasserstein-Robust Adaptive Plug-in for Robot Localization

> [!summary] Summary
> WRAP is an adapter-agnostic plug-in for EKF/ESKF robot localization. A causal module (learned or classical) supplies time-varying effective process and measurement noise means and covariances; a mean-preserving Wasserstein distributionally robust step then computes least-favorable covariances and a robust Kalman gain, leaving propagation, residual, gating, and retraction untouched. It cuts UWB-IMU 3-D position RMSE by 27.4% over a nominal ESKF.

## Problem

- Nominal EKF/ESKF noise laws break under abrupt sensing shifts like GNSS multipath or UWB non-line-of-sight, producing biased errors and miscalibrated covariances.
- Classical adaptive filters using innovation matching, autocovariance, or variational Bayes give inaccurate noise estimates under unrepresented deployment shifts; learned filters stay nominal under distribution shift.
- Mean and covariance mismatch enter different parts of the recursion, so fixing one leaves the other; prior robust EKF work absorbs linearization residuals instead of separating these roles.

## Method

- An ==adapter-agnostic noise-model interface== lets any causal learned, classical, Bayesian, or hybrid module supply any subset of effective process and measurement means and covariances, retaining baseline values otherwise.
- **WRAP**'s core is a ==mean-preserving Wasserstein ambiguity set== with separate process and measurement radii, reducing the minimax MSE problem to a ==Bures-Wasserstein-constrained covariance program== yielding least-favorable covariances and a robust gain.
- A warm-started ==Frank-Wolfe solver==, run only on gate-accepted updates, solves the concave covariance program online; learned adapters use a ==MUSE-style multimodal backbone== with Mamba, Transformer, or GRU.

## Results

- On **18** held-out UWB-IMU sequences, adapter-only and WRAP cut mean 3-D position RMSE by **19.8%** and **27.4%** over the nominal ESKF; DR-only gives **3.9%**.
- An isotropic-inflation ablation reaches only **19.5%**, attributing the incremental gain to directional process-covariance redistribution rather than scalar inflation; per-regime medians reach **39%** in bias-dominant constellations.
- In-sample GNSS-INS gives **16.1%** (adapter-only) versus **15.7%** (WRAP) horizontal RMSE gain, but WRAP lowers median NEES from **22.7** to **11.6**; solve costs **0.05 ms** UWB, **2.92 ms** GNSS.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating mean adaptation from covariance robustification is the key design principle: robustifying covariances cannot remove a persistent mean error, and vice versa.
> - Which component helps depends on the error regime: bias-dominant sequences favor adaptation, while tail or scale mismatch favors distributional robustness, sometimes with adaptation hurting.
> - Innovation-driven classical adapters can degrade accuracy when bias is absorbed into the state, leaving innovations well-behaved while trajectory error grows; robustification recovers them above baseline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot localization systems frequently encounter dynamic and unpredictable sensing conditions, such as multipath interference in GNSS, non-line-of-sight (NLOS) in UWB, or degradation in visual/LiDAR odometry. These conditions can alter the statistical properties of sensor noise, leading to biased errors and miscalibrated covariances in traditional filters. Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs) and Error-State Kalman Filters (ESKFs) are widely used for their real-time performance and ability to handle nonlinear or manifold-valued states. However, their accuracy is contingent upon accurate models of process and measurement noise.

The existing research landscape includes several approaches to address noise model inaccuracies:
- **Traditional Kalman filters**: Rely on fixed or manually tuned noise models, which can become suboptimal under changing conditions.
- **Adaptive Kalman filters**: Attempt to estimate noise statistics online using techniques like innovation matching, autocovariance, or Bayesian tracking. These methods can be susceptible to abrupt shifts or unrepresented changes in noise distributions.
- **Learned filters and uncertainty models**: Utilize data-driven methods to supply gains or noise statistics. While effective, these models can remain nominal under significant distribution shifts not encountered during training.
- **Distributionally Robust (DR) estimation**: Optimizes against a worst-case distribution within an ambiguity set around a nominal model. Wasserstein DR, in particular, offers a framework to compute least-favorable covariances and robust estimators under certain conditions. Previous work has explored Wasserstein DR Kalman filtering, including adaptive and residual-aware EKF extensions.

This work, WRAP, builds upon the Wasserstein DR estimation literature but introduces a distinct interface and division of labor. Unlike some prior DR approaches that might absorb linearization residuals into the ambiguity model, WRAP explicitly retains the baseline EKF/ESKF linearization and focuses on coupling separate mean-preserving ambiguity sets on process and measurement noise laws. It integrates with any causal nominal-law adapter, distinguishing itself by separating mean adaptation from covariance robustification. This allows for updating the nominal mean via an adapter while protecting the Kalman gain against residual covariance mismatch through robustification, without altering the core propagation model, residual, or retraction mechanisms of the underlying EKF/ESKF.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop an adapter-agnostic, noise-model interface**: This interface aims to supply any subset of effective process and measurement means and covariances to an existing EKF/ESKF, allowing for flexible integration with various adaptive modules.
2. **Propose a mean-preserving, noise-centric Wasserstein update**: This update is designed to robustify the Kalman gain against residual covariance mismatch while keeping the nominal noise means, as provided by the adapter, fixed. It utilizes separate ambiguity sets for process and measurement noise.
3. **Evaluate WRAP's performance with real-world data**: The evaluation focuses on quantifying improvements in localization accuracy and consistency across different challenging scenarios, including UWB–IMU and GNSS–INS, analyzing the distinct contributions of mean adaptation and covariance robustification.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the observed limitations of conventional and adaptive Kalman filters in robotic localization:
- **Biased errors and miscalibrated covariances**: Under real-world conditions (e.g., GNSS multipath, UWB NLOS), the true noise distribution can deviate significantly from the nominal Gaussian assumptions in magnitude, center, scale, or correlation. This leads to systematic errors and overconfident or underconfident covariance estimates.
- **Failure modes in EKF/ESKF**: Incorrect nominal means (ˆw, ˆv) can shift the innovation center, introducing systematic error into the state estimate. Incorrect nominal covariances (ˆΣw, ˆΣv) can misweight the relative confidence between propagation and sensing, leading to suboptimal Kalman gains and potentially affecting measurement acceptance. Critically, correcting one does not necessarily resolve the other, as they affect different parts of the filter recursion.
- **Limitations of existing adaptive/learned filters**: While these methods can estimate noise statistics, they may struggle with abrupt or unrepresented changes in the noise environment or still produce nominal models that require further protection against residual uncertainty.
- **Maintaining real-time performance and compatibility**: There is a need for a solution that can enhance the robustness of existing real-time EKF/ESKF frameworks without requiring fundamental changes to their nonlinear propagation, residual definitions, Jacobians, or state representations.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to provide a practical and effective plug-in that enhances the robustness and accuracy of EKF/ESKF-based robot localization in complex, dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WRAP methodology integrates an adaptive module with a Distributionally Robust (DR) local state estimation step into a standard EKF/ESKF framework.

#### 3.1. Robot Model and Nominal Noise

The system considers a discrete-time nonlinear robot model:
$x_{t+1} = f_t(x_t, u_t, w_t)$
$y_t = h_t(x_t, v_t)$
where $x_t$ is the robot state, $u_t$ is the known input, $y_t$ is the measurement, and $w_t$ and $v_t$ represent process and measurement uncertainties, respectively. The state space $X$ can be Euclidean or manifold-valued. WRAP operates at the local filtering interface of an EKF/ESKF, utilizing its local coordinates for error and covariance representation, without altering the baseline filter’s propagation model, residual definition, Jacobians, or state representation.
Process and measurement uncertainties are characterized by effective nominal Gaussian laws:
$\hat{Q}_{w,t} = N(\hat{w}_t, \hat{\Sigma}_{w,t})$
$\hat{Q}_{v,t} = N(\hat{v}_t, \hat{\Sigma}_{v,t})$
These nominal statistics can be fixed, sensor-reported, or, importantly, supplied by an adaptive module.

#### 3.2. Baseline EKF/ESKF Interface

The paper outlines the standard EKF/ESKF recursion to identify the points of interaction.
- **State Propagation**: The prior state $\bar{x}^-_{t}$ is computed using the previous posterior estimate $\bar{x}_{t-1}$ and the nominal process noise mean $\hat{w}_{t-1}$.
- **Measurement Prediction and Innovation**: The predicted measurement $\hat{y}_t$ is derived from the prior state $\bar{x}^-_{t}$ and the nominal measurement noise mean $\hat{v}_t$. The innovation $\nu_t$ is the difference between the actual and predicted measurements.
- **Linearization and Jacobians**: The propagation and measurement models are linearized to obtain Jacobians ($A_{t-1}, G_{t-1}, C_t, D_t$).
- **Covariance Prediction**: The nominal prior covariance $\hat{\Sigma}^-_{x,t}$ is calculated using the previous posterior covariance $\Sigma_{x,t-1}$ and the nominal process covariance $\hat{\Sigma}_{w,t-1}$.
- **Kalman Gain and Innovation Covariance**: The nominal innovation covariance $\hat{S}_t$ incorporates the nominal prior covariance and the nominal measurement covariance $\hat{\Sigma}_{v,t}$. The nominal Kalman gain $\hat{K}_t$ is then computed.
- **Posterior Update**: The state correction $\delta x_t$ is applied, and the posterior state $\bar{x}_t$ and covariance $\Sigma_{x,t}$ are updated.

The two critical failure modes identified are:
1. **Mean Mismatch**: When $\hat{w}_{t-1}$ or $\hat{v}_t$ are inaccurate, they shift the innovation center, leading to systematic errors.
2. **Covariance Mismatch**: Inaccurate $\hat{\Sigma}_{w,t-1}$ or $\hat{\Sigma}_{v,t}$ cause the filter to misweight propagation and sensing, affecting the Kalman gain and potentially measurement acceptance.

#### 3.3. Wasserstein-Robust Adaptive Plug-in (WRAP)

WRAP augments this interface with an adaptive module and a DR local estimation step.

##### 3.3.1. Adaptive Module

A causal adaptive module $A_t$ generates time-varying nominal process and measurement statistics ($\hat{w}_{t-1}, \hat{\Sigma}_{w,t-1}, \hat{v}_t, \hat{\Sigma}_{v,t}$). This module uses only information available *before* the current measurement innovation, such as past measurements, inputs, filter estimates, residuals, covariances, and sensor metadata. It can be any classical, Bayesian, learned, or hybrid method, and it can update any subset of the nominal statistics. Its role is to adapt the nominal *center* of the noise law.

##### 3.3.2. DR Local State Estimation via Covariance Update

Centered on the nominal statistics provided by the adaptive module, WRAP employs a mean-preserving Wasserstein DR step. The core idea is to solve a local minimax problem: minimizing the worst-case first-order posterior mean-square error (MSE) over admissible process and measurement noise distributions within a Wasserstein ambiguity set.
The mean-preserving Wasserstein ambiguity set $D(\hat{Q}, \theta)$ around a nominal law $\hat{Q}$ quantifies residual distributional uncertainty. Distinct radii, $\theta_{w,t-1}$ for process uncertainty and $\theta_{v,t}$ for measurement uncertainty, parameterize these sets.
Under local affine models and Gaussian nominal laws, this minimax problem reduces to a covariance reformulation (Equation 12 in the paper). This program maximizes the trace of the posterior error covariance, selecting "least-favorable" process and measurement covariances ($\Sigma^*_{w,t-1}$, $\Sigma^*_{v,t}$) within their respective Bures–Wasserstein distance constraints.
The resulting robust Kalman gain $K^*_t$ is computed using these least-favorable covariances. The posterior update then applies this robust gain. This step robustifies the *gain* against residual covariance mismatch, while the adapted means remain fixed.

##### 3.3.3. Implementation and Algorithm

WRAP approximately solves the covariance program (12) online using a warm-started Frank–Wolfe method.
**Algorithm 1 (WRAP EKF/ESKF update)**:
1. The adaptive module $A_t$ provides nominal noise statistics.
2. The baseline filter computes the prior state, predicted measurement, innovation, and Jacobians.
3. The nominal prior and innovation covariances ($\hat{\Sigma}^-_{x,t}, \hat{S}_t$) are computed.
4. A baseline gate (using $\hat{S}_t$) checks if the innovation is an outlier. If rejected, the prior state and covariance are retained.
5. If accepted, the Frank–Wolfe solver solves (12) to find the least-favorable covariances ($\Sigma^*_{w,t-1}, \Sigma^*_{v,t}$, $\Sigma^{-,*} _{x,t}, S^*_t$).
6. The robust Kalman gain $K^*_t$ is computed using these least-favorable covariances.
7. The local state correction and posterior state/covariance are updated using $K^*_t$. For an ESKF, the baseline covariance-reset map is applied.
When radii are zero, the DR step is bypassed, recovering the adapter-only EKF/ESKF update.

#### 3.4. Adaptive Modules and Implementation

Two families of adaptive implementations are evaluated:
1. **Learned sequence adapters**: Based on the MUSE multimodal architecture, using Mamba, Transformer, or GRU backbones. These models are trained offline using ground-truth supervision to predict nominal noise means and covariances.
2. **Classical online adaptive estimators**: Sage–Husa and a variational Bayesian adaptive Kalman filter (VB-AKF), which adapt noise statistics from innovations online.
All learned-adapter inputs are causal, ensuring no look-ahead. The DR covariance problem solver is capped at 50 iterations with a duality-gap tolerance of $10^{-4}$ and warm-started.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation protocol involved four variants: Nominal, Adapter-only, DR-only, and WRAP. Radii for DR were selected via cross-validation (UWB–IMU) or leave-one-sequence-out (GNSS–INS). Post-hoc diagnostics (bias-to-noise ratio $\beta$, process headroom $H_w$, upper-tail/scale mismatch $T$, and a heuristic DR-headroom proxy) were used to characterize error regimes.

#### 4.1. UWB–IMU Localization

- **Overall Performance**: On 18 held-out UWB–IMU sequences, WRAP consistently demonstrated higher mean 3-D position RMSE improvement over the nominal ESKF (+27.4%) compared to Adapter-only (+19.8%) and DR-only (+3.9%). This indicates complementarity between mean adaptation and covariance robustification.
- **Adapter Backbone Comparison**: All three learned backbones (Mamba, GRU, Transformer) showed similar trends, with Mamba exhibiting the highest average improvement, suggesting a general benefit from learned adaptation.
- **Isotropic Ablation**: Restricting both process and measurement covariances to isotropic inflations yielded an improvement of 19.5%, similar to Adapter-only (19.8%). The additional gain from the full covariance search (27.4%) was attributed to directional redistribution of process uncertainty rather than simple scalar inflation.
- **Per-Constellation Analysis**: WRAP achieved the lowest mean RMSE for constellations #1, #2, and #4. Constellation #3 was an exception where the adapter was not beneficial, and DR-only performed best.
- **Error Regime Analysis**:
 - Constellations #1 and #2 were identified as bias-dominant, where adapter-only and WRAP showed similar significant improvements (38-39%).
 - Constellation #4 exhibited both bias and tail/scale mismatch, with WRAP (32% improvement) outperforming adapter-only (22%).
 - Constellation #3, in a low-bias, tail/scale-mismatch regime, showed adapter-only degrading RMSE (-7%) while DR-only provided an 18% improvement. This suggests that the relative benefits of adaptation versus robustification vary with the error regime.
- **Learned vs. Classical Adapters**: Classical online adaptive filters (Sage–Husa, VB-AKF) without DR performed worse than the fixed-nominal baseline, potentially due to persistent measurement bias being absorbed into the state, weakening innovation signatures. With the DR step, these classical variants showed slight improvements over the baseline (1.3-1.8%). The learned adapter, benefiting from ground-truth supervision, reduced the bias-to-noise ratio and achieved substantially higher improvements.

#### 4.2. GNSS–INS Localization

- **Overall Performance**: On six in-sample outdoor sequences, adapter-only provided the highest improvement (16.1%) over the nominal KF-GINS, with WRAP closely following (15.7%). DR-only yielded a smaller improvement (4.2%).
- **Bias-Dominant Regime**: The nominal filter operated in a bias-dominant regime ($\beta = 4.1$), meaning about 94% of the squared error was associated with window means. Low DR-headroom proxy ($+0.05$) indicated minimal under-dispersion. This implied that mean adaptation was the primary driver for point-accuracy gain, explaining why adapter-only and WRAP performed similarly.
- **Consistency Statistics**:
 - The nominal filter exhibited a significantly over-tightened covariance (median NIS of 0.13 vs. ideal 3.0, and median NEES of 22.7 vs. ideal 3.0). This suggested measurement bias was absorbed into the state.
 - Adapter-only and WRAP reduced NEES (to 13.8 and 11.6, respectively), while DR-only achieved the lowest NEES (4.0), closer to the ideal. This demonstrates that DR improved consistency, particularly for cases where classical methods (Sage–Husa, VB-AKF) dramatically over-tightened covariances, leading to poor RMSE without DR. With DR, classical methods improved to 3.0-7.1% RMSE improvement.
- **Division of Labor**: This in-sample study highlighted WRAP's intended function: mean adaptation (from the learned adapter) addresses systematic bias, while covariance robustification (DR step) limits sensitivity to residual covariance misspecification, especially for over-tightened classical estimates.

#### 4.3. Runtime Evaluation

- **Jetson Orin Nano Performance**: The Frank–Wolfe solver for the DR covariance problem exhibited low latency.
 - UWB–IMU: 0.05 ms per accepted update (55 Hz rate), fitting within the 18 ms budget. Adaptive inference took 21–30 ms but ran at 1 Hz.
 - GNSS–INS: 2.92 ms per update (1 Hz rate), comfortably within the 1-second budget. Adaptive inference took 23–26 ms.
- Both components met the timing requirements for the evaluated localization stacks at their respective refresh rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents WRAP, a novel approach to enhance the robustness and accuracy of EKF/ESKF-based robot localization systems under challenging and changing sensing conditions. Its significance and potential impact lie in several key areas:

- **Modular and Adapter-Agnostic Design**: WRAP's core innovation is its ability to interface with *any* causal adaptive module, whether learned, classical, or hybrid. This plug-in architecture allows existing EKF/ESKF stacks to benefit from improved noise modeling without requiring fundamental changes to their nonlinear propagation, residual computations, or state representations. This flexibility promotes broader adoption and easier integration into diverse robotic platforms.
- **Decoupling Mean Adaptation and Covariance Robustification**: The clear separation of responsibilities—mean adaptation (by the causal module) for correcting systematic bias, and mean-preserving Wasserstein robustification (by WRAP) for protecting the Kalman gain against residual covariance mismatch—is a key contribution. This division of labor addresses distinct failure modes, allowing for a more targeted and effective approach to noise uncertainty management.
- **Demonstrated Performance Improvements**: Experimental results on real-world UWB–IMU and GNSS–INS datasets show consistent improvements in position accuracy (RMSE). WRAP often surpasses both nominal filters and adapter-only approaches, particularly when faced with complex error regimes involving both systematic bias and upper-quantile/scale mismatch. This highlights its practical utility in improving localization reliability.
- **Enhanced Consistency**: In the GNSS–INS study, WRAP demonstrated improved filter consistency (reduced NEES), indicating better alignment between estimated uncertainties and true errors. This is crucial for applications requiring reliable uncertainty quantification, such as safe robot navigation and decision-making. The ability of the DR step to mitigate over-tightened covariances from classical adaptive filters is a specific benefit.
- **Real-time Feasibility**: The low computational latency of the DR covariance solve, measured on embedded hardware, confirms WRAP's suitability for real-time robotic applications, addressing a critical requirement for practical deployment.

While acknowledging limitations such as the local nature of the minimax interpretation, coordinate-dependent radii, and non-robustified baseline gating, WRAP provides a framework for robustifying Kalman filters against a common and challenging problem in robotics. Its potential impact includes more reliable and accurate autonomous navigation in environments with unpredictable sensor performance, contributing to the safety and effectiveness of robots in diverse applications. Future work on broader generalization tests, data-calibrated radii, and online regime selection could further enhance its robustness and applicability.
