# EnergyAction: Unimanual to Bimanual Composition with Energy-Based Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called EnergyAction allows robots to perform complex bimanual tasks by compositionally transferring knowledge from pre-trained unimanual policies using Energy-Based Models (EBMs), which explicitly incorporate temporal-spatial coordination and efficient inference. The method achieved an 86.4% average success rate on 13 bimanual tasks in RLBench2, outperforming prior methods by 4.6%, and showed robust performance in low-data regimes, surpassing the next best approach by 32.5% with only 20 demonstrations.

## Problem

- Bimanual manipulation involves a high-dimensional action space and necessitates precise spatial-temporal coordination between two robotic arms, frequently leading to coordination failures like collisions or asynchronous movements.
- The collection of high-quality bimanual demonstration data is prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive, severely limiting the development of generalizable bimanual policies.
- Existing bimanual approaches struggle to effectively or fully leverage the rich manipulation knowledge already present in abundant pre-trained unimanual policies.

## Method

- **EnergyAction** formulates unimanual policies as ==Energy-Based Models (EBMs)== by interpreting the velocity field of ==flow matching== policies as the negative gradient of an implicit energy function.
- Composes bimanual policies by ==summing individual left and right unimanual energy functions==, enabling action generation for coordinated bimanual tasks.
- Introduces ==energy-based temporal-spatial coordination== mechanisms (e.g., smoothness, synchronization, end-effector/joint collision avoidance) with adaptive weighting to ensure physically plausible and safe bimanual actions and proposes ==energy-aware denoising== strategies to improve inference efficiency by dynamically adjusting the number of denoising steps.

## Results

- Achieved an **86.4%** average success rate on **13** language-conditioned bimanual tasks in RLBench2 with **100** demonstrations, outperforming the strongest baseline by **4.6%**, and demonstrated superior data efficiency with a **32.5%** advantage over the second-best approach with only **20** demonstrations.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of energy-based composition and explicit temporal-spatial coordination mechanisms to the overall performance.
- Energy-aware denoising strategies maintained competitive success rates while significantly improving inference efficiency, reducing mean denoising steps (e.g., to **1.79** for Adaptive Denoising compared to **5** fixed steps).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unimanual flow matching policies can be effectively modeled as implicit energy functions, which allows for their compositional combination to generate complex bimanual actions.
> - Explicit energy-based constraints for temporal coherence and spatial feasibility, coupled with adaptive weighting, are crucial for ensuring the physical plausibility and safety of generated bimanual actions.
> - The compositional property of EBMs provides a principled and effective method for transferring manipulation knowledge from readily available unimanual policies to data-scarce bimanual tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has achieved notable advancements, particularly in unimanual tasks, leveraging extensive training data and established model architectures. These successes have led to impressive generalization capabilities across various objects and tasks, often facilitated by large-scale demonstration datasets and scalable architectures. However, extending these capabilities to bimanual manipulation presents significant challenges. Bimanual tasks involve a high-dimensional action space and necessitate precise spatial-temporal coordination between two robotic arms.

Existing approaches to bimanual manipulation primarily fall into several categories:
- **Direct Joint Action Modeling:** Many methods attempt to directly model the joint action space of both arms using complex network architectures. This often leads to increased complexity in action generation and optimization difficulty, as the system must implicitly learn individual manipulation skills, temporal synchronization, and spatial compatibility from limited demonstrations. Such approaches frequently encounter coordination failures, including arm collisions and asynchronous movements.
- **Fixed Role Assignment:** Some approaches simplify the problem by assigning fixed roles to each arm, which can limit their generalization across tasks requiring flexible coordination.
- **Large-Scale Bimanual Foundation Models:** Recent efforts aim to learn robotic foundation models from large-scale bimanual data. However, the cost and effort associated with collecting high-quality bimanual demonstrations are substantial, hindering the widespread development of such models.
- **Unimanual Policy Leveraging:** Other methods, such as AnyBimanual and 3DFA, attempt to leverage pre-trained unimanual policies. While these approaches aim to reduce dependence on bimanual data, they may not always effectively transfer knowledge or ensure robust coordination.

This work, EnergyAction, positions itself within the landscape by addressing the limitations of existing bimanual manipulation approaches, specifically the scarcity of bimanual demonstration data and the difficulty of coordination. It aims to bridge the gap between the abundance of knowledge embedded in pre-trained unimanual policies and the challenges of bimanual tasks. The paper draws inspiration from Energy-Based Models (EBMs), a classical generative modeling approach that has found applications in various domains but has been less explored in modern robotic research for policy learning. EBMs are known for their compositional properties, allowing complex concepts to be synthesized from simpler components through energy summation. EnergyAction explicitly proposes to be the first method to model bimanual manipulation policy as a composition of unimanual policies through EBMs, integrating explicit mechanisms for temporal and spatial coordination.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of effectively transferring rich manipulation knowledge from pre-trained unimanual policies to bimanual tasks, utilizing minimal bimanual demonstration data. This objective is driven by several motivations arising from the inherent difficulties of bimanual robotic manipulation:

1. **High-Dimensional Action Space and Coordination Complexity:** Bimanual tasks involve an exponentially larger action space compared to unimanual tasks. This complexity necessitates precise spatial and temporal coordination between two arms, which is difficult for current policies that often struggle with implicit learning of physical constraints. This can lead to coordination failures, such as arm collisions and unsynchronized movements.
2. **Data Scarcity for Bimanual Tasks:** Collecting high-quality bimanual demonstration data is a prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive process, typically requiring sophisticated teleoperation systems and substantial human effort. This scarcity severely limits the development of generalizable bimanual policies. In contrast, unimanual manipulation policies have benefited from abundant training data and well-established model architectures, achieving considerable success.
3. **Ineffective Leveraging of Unimanual Knowledge:** Existing approaches for bimanual manipulation either rely on extensive bimanual datasets, which are costly, or do not fully or effectively leverage the rich manipulation knowledge already present in pre-trained unimanual policies. There is a need for a principled method to compose these individual arm capabilities into coordinated bimanual actions.
4. **Inference Efficiency:** Generative models, such as diffusion policies, often require multiple denoising steps during inference, which can be computationally intensive and impact real-time robotic applications. There is a motivation to improve inference efficiency while maintaining high action quality for practical deployment.

To address these motivations, EnergyAction sets out with the following specific objectives:
- To establish a method for modeling individual unimanual policies as Energy-Based Models (EBMs).
- To leverage the compositional properties of EBMs to fuse left and right arm unimanual policies into a cohesive bimanual policy through energy summation.
- To introduce an energy-based temporal-spatial coordination mechanism that explicitly enforces constraints to ensure generated bimanual actions are both temporally coherent (e.g., smooth motion, synchronization) and spatially feasible (e.g., collision avoidance).
- To develop energy-aware denoising strategies that dynamically adapt the number of denoising steps based on action quality assessment, aiming for computational efficiency without compromising the quality of generated actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EnergyAction proposes a compositional framework for bimanual manipulation that leverages pre-trained unimanual policies through Energy-Based Models (EBMs). The core insight is to formulate unimanual policies as energy functions, which can then be composed via energy summation to generate bimanual actions, while incorporating explicit coordination constraints and efficient inference strategies.

The methodology is structured around three key innovations:

#### 3.1. Compositional Action Generation with Energy

The framework begins by establishing a connection between unimanual flow matching policies and EBMs to enable compositional generation.
- **Unimanual Policies as Energy Functions:** Flow matching, a generative modeling technique similar to diffusion models but often more efficient for inference due to its deterministic nature, is employed for unimanual policy learning. The inference process of flow matching follows an ordinary differential equation (ODE) governed by a time-dependent velocity field `v_θ`. By interpreting this velocity field as the negative gradient of an energy function (i.e., `v_θ(a_i_t, t, c_i) = -∇_a_i E_θ(a_i_t, c_i)`) in a deterministic limit, each unimanual flow matching policy can be viewed as an implicit energy function. This energy function, `E_θ(a_i_t, c_i)`, is related to the negative logarithm of the probability distribution `p_t(a_i_t|c_i)`.
- **Unimanual to Bimanual Action Composition:** For bimanual tasks, two distinct unimanual policies (for the left and right arms), each represented by an energy function `E_θ_L(a_L_t, c_L)` and `E_θ_R(a_R_t, c_R)`, are utilized. The compositional principle of EBMs, where complex distributions are formed by summing simpler energy functions, is applied. The bimanual compositional energy `E_comp` is defined as the sum of the individual unimanual energies: `E_comp(a_L_t, a_R_t | c_L, c_R) = E_θ_L(a_L_t, c_L) + E_θ_R(a_R_t, c_R)`. This leads to a compositional velocity field `v_comp(a_t, t)` that integrates conditional and unconditional velocity fields, conceptually akin to classifier-free guidance, enabling coordinated bimanual action generation.

#### 3.2. Energy-Based Temporal-Spatial Coordination

To ensure that the compositionally generated bimanual actions are physically plausible and coordinated, EnergyAction introduces explicit energy-based constraints for both temporal coherence and spatial feasibility.
- **Temporal Coordination Constraints:**
 - **Smoothness:** The framework includes terms that penalize large changes in end-effector pose. These are `E_vel` (first-order difference, penalizing velocity), `E_accel` (second-order difference, penalizing acceleration), and `E_jerk` (third-order difference, penalizing jerk). These terms collectively encourage smooth trajectories.
 - **Synchronization:** To ensure coherent motion between arms, `E_sync` penalizes differences in both the magnitude and direction of the arms' end-effector velocities.
- **Spatial Feasibility Constraints:**
 - **End-Effector Collision Avoidance:** `E_ee` is introduced to prevent collisions between the end-effectors of the two arms. It applies a smooth penalty if the Euclidean distance between end-effectors falls below a predefined safe distance (`d_safe`).
 - **Joint Space Diversity/Collision Avoidance:** An auxiliary constraint `E_joint` is applied in joint space. By using inverse kinematics (IK) to derive joint configurations from end-effector poses, this term encourages diverse joint configurations and helps prevent self-collisions or undesirable arm postures, penalizing if the joint distance falls below a `d_joint_safe`.
- **Unified Energy-Based Optimization:** All these components are integrated into a single total energy function `E_total = E_comp + E_coord`. The coordination energy `E_coord` is a weighted sum of the six constraint terms. Adaptive weights for these constraints are dynamically predicted by a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) based on the current state (e.g., current arm poses and velocities). This adaptive weighting allows the system to prioritize different constraints based on the robot's real-time state, for instance, emphasizing collision avoidance when arms are close. The differentiability of `E_total` enables end-to-end optimization to generate actions that are semantically meaningful, smooth, coherent, and collision-free.

#### 3.3. Energy-Aware Denoising Strategies

To improve the efficiency of the inference process, two adaptive denoising strategies are proposed, which dynamically adjust the number of denoising steps based on the calculated `E_total` value.
- **Algorithm 1 (Adaptive Denoising):** This strategy determines the number of denoising steps based on predefined energy thresholds (`τ_low` and `τ_high`). If the total energy is below `τ_low`, indicating a simple or well-constrained action, only one denoising step is performed. If the energy is above `τ_high`, suggesting high uncertainty or constraint violation, the maximum number of steps (e.g., 5 for flow matching) is utilized. For intermediate energy values, the number of steps is linearly interpolated.
- **Algorithm 2 (Early Stop Denoising):** This strategy involves real-time monitoring of the total energy during iterative denoising. The denoising process is terminated early as soon as the total energy falls below `τ_low`, providing a flexible adaptation to varying action complexities. These strategies aim to reduce computational overhead compared to fixed-step denoising approaches while maintaining the quality of generated actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of EnergyAction was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, across various data regimes and compared against state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (RLBench2)

- **Overall Performance:** EnergyAction consistently achieved the highest overall performance on 13 language-conditioned bimanual tasks in RLBench2. With 100 demonstrations, it attained an average success rate of 86.4%, surpassing the strongest baseline, 3DFA, by 4.6%. In the limited-data setting with only 20 demonstrations, EnergyAction achieved 77.3%, demonstrating a substantial advantage of 32.5% over the second-best approach, indicating superior data efficiency.
- **Data Efficiency:** The framework exhibited robust and generalizable performance across all tested low-data regimes (1, 5, 10, and 20 demonstrations), affirming its ability to learn effectively with minimal bimanual data.
- **Analysis of Performance:** The superior performance was attributed to EnergyAction's strategy of compositionally transferring knowledge from unimanual policies, which addresses the challenges posed by the high-dimensional bimanual action space. Decomposing the complex bimanual coordination problem into manageable unimanual components and then composing them through the energy-based framework facilitated more efficient learning and better generalization, particularly for tasks requiring intricate coordination.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

Ablation experiments were conducted to quantify the contribution of each component within EnergyAction:
- **Impact of Coordination Energies:** The complete model, incorporating energy-based composition (E-Compose), temporal coordination (E-Temporal), and spatial coordination (E-Spatial), achieved the highest success rates. Removing E-Spatial resulted in a minor performance decrease, indicating the importance of collision avoidance and spatial feasibility. Further removal of E-Temporal led to comparable degradation, highlighting its role in enforcing action coherence.
- **Importance of Energy-Based Composition:** When only E-Compose remained without coordination mechanisms, performance deteriorated more significantly, suggesting that composition alone is insufficient for effective bimanual manipulation.
- **Criticality of the Energy Framework:** The removal of all energy-based components led to a dramatic collapse in performance, validating that the proposed energy-based framework is fundamental for transferring unimanual knowledge to bimanual tasks.

#### 4.3. Energy-Aware Denoising Strategy Analysis

- **Efficiency and Quality:** Both the Adaptive Denoising and Early Stop Denoising strategies demonstrated competitive success rates while significantly improving inference efficiency compared to a fixed-step denoising approach (5 steps).
- **Quantitative Efficiency:** Adaptive Denoising achieved mean denoising steps of 1.79 and 1.27, while Early Stop Denoising had mean steps of 2.13 and 2.32, showcasing the benefits of dynamic step adjustment.

#### 4.4. Single-Arm Task Scaling

- **Transfer Learning Effectiveness:** Experiments varying the number of unimanual training tasks (from 0 to 18) under the 20-demonstration setting showed that EnergyAction consistently and substantially outperformed AnyBimanual, with an average performance gap of 53.0%. This result suggests that the proposed energy composition architecture is more effective for knowledge transfer.
- **Inherent Framework Strength:** Notably, even without any single-arm task pretraining, EnergyAction achieved a 52.3% success rate, which surpassed the 44.8% of 3DFA (a method designed specifically for bimanual tasks). This finding indicates the inherent effectiveness and robustness of the energy-based bimanual policy framework itself, beyond merely leveraging pre-trained unimanual skills.

#### 4.5. Flexibility with Different Diffusion Policies

- **Robustness to Base Policy:** EnergyAction was tested with different underlying unimanual policies (DDPM, DDIM, and Flow Matching). All variants achieved competitive performance across different demonstration settings, confirming that the energy-based compositional framework is flexible and robust, with its compositional reasoning capability being largely decoupled from the specific choice of the unimanual generative model.

#### 4.6. Real-world Experiments

- **Real-world Validation:** Experiments on a Galaxea R1 lite robot confirmed the approach's effectiveness in real-world scenarios. EnergyAction successfully completed bimanual manipulation tasks, exhibiting temporal coherence and spatial feasibility in the arm movements.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Quantitative comparisons demonstrated that EnergyAction consistently outperformed AnyBimanual, π0-keypose, and 3DFA in real-world bimanual tasks across both 5 and 20 demonstration data regimes. This further validates the energy-based compositional framework's ability to effectively transfer unimanual knowledge to bimanual tasks in physical environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EnergyAction framework introduces a novel approach to bimanual robotic manipulation, with several implications for the field:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** A primary significance of this work lies in its ability to mitigate the substantial challenge of data scarcity in bimanual manipulation. By compositionally transferring knowledge from readily available unimanual policies, EnergyAction significantly reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming bimanual demonstration data. This makes the development of bimanual robotic systems more accessible and practical.
- **Principled Knowledge Transfer:** The use of Energy-Based Models (EBMs) for compositional policy learning provides a principled and mathematically grounded framework for combining simpler unimanual behaviors into complex bimanual actions. This approach contrasts with methods that may implicitly learn coordination or rely on more ad-hoc integration strategies.
- **Enhanced Coordination and Physical Feasibility:** The explicit energy-based temporal-spatial coordination mechanism ensures that generated bimanual actions are not only semantically correct but also physically feasible, exhibiting temporal coherence (smoothness, synchronization) and spatial safety (collision avoidance). This explicit enforcement of constraints contributes to more reliable and robust robot operation.
- **Improved Inference Efficiency:** The introduction of energy-aware denoising strategies offers a practical benefit for real-time robotic applications. By dynamically adjusting the number of denoising steps based on action quality, the framework achieves computational efficiency without compromising the quality of the generated actions, which is crucial for responsive robot control.
- **Flexibility and Robustness:** The demonstrated compatibility with different underlying unimanual generative models (DDPM, DDIM, Flow Matching) indicates the robustness and modularity of the EnergyAction framework. This flexibility allows researchers to leverage various state-of-the-art unimanual policy learning techniques within the bimanual composition paradigm.
- **Advancement in Generalizable Robotics:** By enabling effective transfer learning from unimanual to bimanual tasks, this work contributes to the broader goal of developing more generalizable and adaptable robotic systems. The ability to perform complex bimanual tasks with minimal task-specific data pushes the boundaries of current robotic capabilities.

The findings from this research could inspire future directions in compositional policy learning, particularly exploring the application of energy-based optimization strategies in diverse robotic contexts. This could lead to the development of more capable and versatile bimanual robots for a range of applications, including advanced manufacturing, complex assembly, logistics, and assistive robotics in various environments.
