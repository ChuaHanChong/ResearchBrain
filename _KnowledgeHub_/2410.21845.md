# Precise and Dexterous Robotic Manipulation via Human-in-the-Loop Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Human-in-the-Loop Sample-Efficient Robotic Reinforcement Learning (HIL-SERL), a system that enables robots to learn a diverse set of precise and dexterous manipulation skills directly in the real world. It achieved 100% success rates on various complex tasks, including dual-arm coordination and dynamic interactions, within practical training times of 1 to 2.5 hours, demonstrating significantly higher performance and faster execution compared to imitation learning methods.

## Problem

- Real-world robotic reinforcement learning suffers from high sample complexity, making training on physical robots impractical and costly.
- Designing accurate and effective reward functions for complex, open-ended manipulation tasks is notoriously difficult and time-consuming.
- Existing methods, including imitation learning, struggle to achieve precise and dexterous manipulation skills, especially for tasks involving dynamic interactions, flexible objects, or multi-arm coordination, and often lack robustness to real-world variations.

## Method

- **HIL-SERL** integrates human-in-the-loop supervision with the sample-efficient ==RLPD== RL algorithm for real-world robotic manipulation.
- Distributed system with separate replay buffers for initial human demonstrations and online robot-collected data, combined with a ==sparse reward== from an offline-trained binary classifier.
- Practical robotic components: pretrained ==ResNet-10 vision backbones==, ego-centric proprioceptive state, and an ==impedance controller with reference limiting== for safe exploration.

## Results

- HIL-SERL achieved a **100%** success rate on nearly all complex manipulation tasks within **1–2.5 hours** of real-world training; Timing Belt Assembly reached **100%** in **6 hours**.
- Policies were **101%** more successful (**100%** vs. **49.7%**) and **1.8×** faster (**5.4s** vs. **9.6s**) on average than HG-DAgger imitation learning baselines.
- Zero-shot robustness: adapted to moving motherboards during RAM insertion, re-grasped objects under disturbance, dynamically responded to flexible object deformations and external perturbations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human corrections are crucial for accelerating real-world reinforcement learning, allowing policies to learn from mistakes and converge to higher performance than pure imitation or RL from scratch.
> - Reinforcement learning can achieve superhuman performance (e.g., faster cycle times) by optimizing directly for task rewards, surpassing the limitations of human demonstration speed and suboptimality.
> - Learned policies demonstrate impressive zero-shot robustness and adaptability to real-world disturbances, naturally acquiring reactive and predictive control strategies through autonomous exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation remains a foundational challenge in robotics, with the aspiration of achieving human-level performance on dynamic and dexterous tasks being a longstanding goal. Reinforcement Learning (RL) holds immense promise for enabling autonomous acquisition of complex skills, potentially surpassing hand-designed controllers and even human teleoperation by allowing robots to learn directly from trial and error tailored to specific physical characteristics. However, realizing this potential in real-world settings has been severely hampered by several persistent challenges:
- **Sample Complexity:** RL often demands an exorbitant number of interactions, which is impractical and costly for real physical robots.
- **Reward Function Design:** Specifying effective and accurate reward functions for complex, open-ended tasks is notoriously difficult.
- **Optimization Stability:** Training deep RL policies with high-dimensional sensory inputs (like images) can be unstable and prone to failures.
- **Safety:** The exploration inherent in RL can lead to unsafe or damaging behaviors during training in the real world.

Prior research has addressed these issues in various capacities:
- **Simulation-based RL:** Achieves impressive results in simulated environments (e.g., Hwangbo et al., 2019), but struggles with the sim-to-real gap.
- **Large-scale Data Approaches:** Focus on learning from massive pre-existing real-world datasets for broad generalization (e.g., Kalashnikov et al., 2018), often leading to generalist models but not necessarily optimized for specific task performance.
- **Feature-based RL:** Utilizes hand-designed features or representations for narrowly tailored tasks (e.g., Theodorou et al., 2010), limiting generalization and requiring domain expertise.
- **Real-world RL Algorithms:** Various algorithms have been developed to enhance sample efficiency (e.g., Levine et al., 2016; Popov et al., 2017), but still often require long training times and may not achieve superhuman performance on complex, vision-based tasks.

This paper directly tackles the critical gap of developing *general-purpose vision-based methods that can efficiently acquire physically complex skills with proficiency exceeding imitation learning and hand-designed controllers, directly in the real world*. It positions itself as a significant advancement by demonstrating that RL *can* indeed learn a wide range of complex manipulation policies within practical training times, previously considered infeasible.

Specifically, this work differentiates itself from closely related efforts:
- **SERL (Luo et al., 2024a):** While building upon the SERL system, the current work, HIL-SERL, critically incorporates human *corrections* in addition to demonstrations. This seemingly minor distinction is shown to be crucial for learning from mistakes and improving performance, particularly for tasks challenging to learn from scratch. Furthermore, HIL-SERL extends to much more complex tasks, including dual-arm coordination and dynamic manipulation, which were not addressed by the original SERL.
- **Imitation Learning (IL) Methods (e.g., Behavioral Cloning, DAgger, HG-DAgger):** These methods suffer from error compounding and are inherently limited by the suboptimality of human demonstrations. This paper aims to decisively outperform IL by leveraging RL's ability to explore beyond demonstrations and optimize directly for task rewards, leading to superhuman performance.
- **Model-based Approaches for Insertion:** Traditional methods often rely on explicit state-based models or multi-stage pipelines (e.g., Tang et al., 2016; Spector et al., 2022). HIL-SERL employs a tighter perception-action loop, learning task-relevant visual features and visuomotor policies in a closed-loop manner, essential for reactive precision.
- **Dynamic and Flexible Object Manipulation:** While prior works exist for tasks like object flipping (Kormushev et al., 2010) or Jenga pushing (Fazeli et al., 2019), they often use motion capture or quasi-dynamic approaches. HIL-SERL works directly from pixel inputs and tackles more complex dynamic scenarios (e.g., Jenga whipping) and highly interactive flexible object tasks (timing belt assembly) requiring precise dual-arm coordination, which are often considered intractable.

In essence, this research represents a significant leap towards truly performant learned robotic manipulation policies, bridging the gap between theoretical promise and real-world applicability by effectively addressing the practical bottlenecks of real-world reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust, efficient, and generalizable human-in-the-loop reinforcement learning (RL) system capable of acquiring a broad spectrum of precise and dexterous robotic manipulation skills directly in real-world environments. The system aims to achieve this with practical training times and performance levels that surpass existing state-of-the-art methods, including imitation learning and traditional hand-designed controllers.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent challenges and limitations of current approaches to robotic manipulation:
- **Bridging the RL Promise-Reality Gap:** While RL theoretically offers autonomous skill acquisition that can outperform humans, its real-world application has been hindered by issues like high sample complexity (requiring millions of interactions), difficulty in defining accurate reward functions, and instability during optimization with high-dimensional inputs. The authors are motivated to show that with careful system design, these obstacles can be overcome.
- **Achieving Superhuman Performance:** Existing methods, particularly imitation learning, are often constrained by the suboptimality and data distribution of human demonstrations. The authors aim to leverage RL's optimization capabilities to not only reproduce human skills but to improve upon them, leading to faster execution times and higher success rates.
- **Tackling Unresolved Complex Tasks:** Many tasks in dexterous manipulation remain largely unsolved by current robotic methods, especially those involving dynamic interactions, precise multi-component assembly, flexible object handling, and complex dual-arm coordination. The paper explicitly targets tasks such as timing belt assembly, Jenga whipping, and multi-arm car dashboard assembly, which were previously deemed "infeasible" or "nearly insurmountable" for direct RL in the real world.
- **Enabling General-Purpose Manipulation:** The ambition is to develop a single, unified framework that can learn diverse types of control strategies—from reactive closed-loop control for precision to predictive open-loop behaviors for dynamics—without requiring task-specific modifications or extensive manual tuning.
- **Practicality and Efficiency:** A crucial motivation is to achieve high performance within "practical training times" (e.g., 1 to 2.5 hours). This addresses a major barrier for deploying learned skills in industrial or everyday settings, where prolonged training is unacceptable.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:** Robots need to operate robustly in varying conditions and recover from unexpected disturbances. The authors aim to demonstrate that RL-learned policies can naturally acquire these adaptive and robust behaviors through autonomous exploration.

By addressing these challenges, the authors seek to inspire a new generation of learned robotic manipulation techniques, benefiting both industrial applications (e.g., High-Mix Low-Volume manufacturing) and foundational research advancements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, named Human-in-the-Loop Sample-Efficient Robotic Reinforcement Learning (HIL-SERL), is a vision-based RL framework designed for real-world dexterous manipulation. It integrates several key components and design choices to overcome the inherent challenges of sample complexity, optimization stability, and reward specification in real-world robotics.

#### 3.1. System Architecture

HIL-SERL operates in a distributed fashion, composed of three main components:
- **Actor Process:** Interacts with the physical robot environment. It executes the current policy, collects experience data, and sends it to the replay buffers. The environment is modular, supporting multiple cameras, input devices (like SpaceMouse for human teleoperation), and control of single or multiple robot arms.
- **Learner Process:** This is the core learning engine. It samples data from replay buffers, optimizes the policy using the RLPD algorithm, and periodically sends the updated policy parameters back to the actor process.
- **Replay Buffers:** Two distinct buffers are maintained:
 - **Demo Buffer:** Stores a small set of initial offline human demonstrations (typically 20-30 trajectories).
 - **RL Buffer:** Stores on-policy data collected during autonomous robot rollouts and human interventions.

#### 3.2. Core Reinforcement Learning Algorithm

The underlying RL algorithm is **RLPD (Ball et al., 2023)**, chosen for its sample efficiency and ability to effectively leverage prior data. At each training step, RLPD samples data equally from both the demo buffer and the RL buffer to form a training batch. It then updates the parameters of a Q-function and a policy network using standard actor-critic loss functions, incorporating target networks for stability and entropy regularization.

#### 3.3. System Design Choices for Efficiency and Robustness

- **Pretrained Vision Backbones:** To facilitate efficient training with high-dimensional image inputs, HIL-SERL uses a ResNet-10 model, pretrained on ImageNet, as a visual backbone. This generates robust visual embeddings which are then concatenated with proprioceptive information. This approach improves optimization stability and exploration efficiency.
- **Sparse Reward Function:** Instead of hand-designing complex dense reward functions, HIL-SERL employs a simple sparse reward. An offline-trained binary classifier (using human-teleoperated positive and negative examples) determines whether the task is successful (reward = 1) or failed (reward = 0). This simplifies reward specification across diverse tasks.
- **Downstream Robotic System:**
 - **Ego-centric Proprioceptive State Representation:** Robot's proprioceptive state (e.g., end-effector pose, twist) and action commands are represented relative to the robot's initial end-effector pose for each episode. This "ego-centric" formulation enhances spatial generalization and enables the policy to adapt to variations in object placement or perturbations during execution.
 - **Impedance Controller with Reference Limiting:** For contact-rich tasks, a 1000 Hz impedance controller is used. Crucially, it incorporates reference limiting to bound interaction forces, ensuring safety during exploration and preventing damage from hard collisions.
 - **Feedforward Wrench Controller:** For dynamic tasks (e.g., Jenga whipping), the policy directly commands feedforward wrenches (forces/torques) in the end-effector frame, allowing for high-speed, dynamic actions.
- **Separate Gripper Control:** For tasks involving grasping, a separate critic network (trained via DQN) is used to evaluate discrete gripper actions ("open," "close," "stay"). This is treated as a separate MDP with a discrete action space, effectively handling the categorical nature of gripper control while the main policy handles continuous motion.

#### 3.4. Human-in-the-Loop Procedure

The human-in-the-loop (HIL) component is crucial for accelerating learning and overcoming sample complexity in long-horizon or complex tasks.
- **Mechanism:** A human operator supervises the robot during training. If the robot's policy leads to an undesirable or unrecoverable state, the human can intervene using a SpaceMouse.
- **Data Collection:** When a human intervenes, their teleoperated actions are applied instead of the policy's actions. This intervention data is stored in *both* the demo and RL replay buffers. However, only the policy's transitions (before and after the intervention) are added to the RL buffer, ensuring that the RL objective primarily optimizes the autonomous policy's behavior.
- **Intervention Strategy:** The human initially provides more frequent, guiding interventions to steer the policy towards successful behaviors from various states. As the policy improves, the frequency and duration of interventions are gradually reduced, aiming for full autonomy. This differs from pure imitation learning by using human input for RL optimization rather than just supervised learning.

#### 3.5. Training Process Overview (Fig. 3)

1. **Environment Setup:** Select cameras (wrist-mounted, side-mounted), perform image cropping, and resize images to 128x128.
2. **Reward Classifier Training:** Collect ~200 positive and ~1000 negative data points by tele-operating the robot (approx. 5 minutes). Train a binary classifier on this data to achieve >95% accuracy.
3. **Initial Demonstrations:** Collect 20-30 human demonstration trajectories to initialize the offline `demo buffer`.
4. **Online RL Training with HIL:** Start the policy training. The binary classifier provides sparse rewards. Human interventions are provided as needed, guiding the policy and enabling efficient exploration. Task resets are handled by scripted motions or manual human resets.

This integrated approach aims to combine the strengths of data-driven learning (RL, demonstrations) with human guidance and robust system engineering to achieve high performance on challenging real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with HIL-SERL across seven diverse manipulation tasks demonstrate a significant advancement in real-world robotic control. The tasks span dynamic object manipulation (e.g., object flipping, Jenga whipping), precise and delicate assembly (e.g., RAM insertion, SSD assembly), dual-arm coordination (e.g., car dashboard assembly, timing belt assembly, object handover), and multi-stage tasks (IKEA assembly).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Efficiency

- HIL-SERL achieved a **100% success rate** within a remarkably short training time of **1 to 2.5 hours** of real-world interaction on nearly all considered tasks. This is a critical breakthrough, as previous methods often required significantly longer training durations or failed to achieve such high reliability.
- For the highly complex **Timing Belt Assembly** task, HIL-SERL still reached 100% success, albeit with a longer training time of 6 hours, which is still considered practical for such an intricate problem.

#### 4.2. Superiority over Imitation Learning Baselines

A central claim of the paper is HIL-SERL's superiority over imitation learning (IL) methods, which was rigorously tested against Behavioral Cloning (BC) and HG-DAgger (an interactive IL method similar to HIL-SERL's human interaction but based on supervised learning).
- **Success Rate:** HIL-SERL demonstrated a dramatic improvement over HG-DAgger, achieving an average success rate of **100%** compared to HG-DAgger's **49.7%** (a 101% improvement). The performance gap was particularly pronounced in complex tasks like Jenga whipping (100% vs. 8%), RAM insertion (100% vs. 29%), and timing belt assembly (100% vs. 2%).
- **Cycle Time:** HIL-SERL policies were significantly faster, achieving an average cycle time of **5.4 seconds** compared to HG-DAgger's **9.6 seconds** (1.8x faster execution). This is attributed to RL's ability to optimize for discounted rewards, encouraging faster task completion beyond merely imitating human speed.
- **Human Intervention:** The intervention rate for HIL-SERL rapidly decreased throughout training, eventually reaching 0%, indicating autonomous policy improvement and reduced human supervision. In contrast, HG-DAgger policies required more frequent and persistent interventions, and their intervention rates did not consistently decrease, suggesting a struggle to improve beyond the directly provided data.
- **"Flat" BC Performance:** For tasks like Jenga whipping and object flipping (where interventions are impractical), BC policies trained with significantly more demonstrations (50-200) still performed poorly (e.g., 8% for Jenga, 46-47% for flipping), highlighting the limitations of pure imitation.

#### 4.3. Robustness and Adaptability

HIL-SERL policies exhibited impressive zero-shot robustness and adaptability to variations and external disturbances during execution:
- **Dynamic Adaptation:** The RAM insertion policy successfully inserted RAM even when the motherboard was moving.
- **Recovery from Disturbances:** For tasks like car dashboard assembly and object handover, if grippers were forcibly opened, the policy reacted by retrying the grasp and continuing the task.
- **Grasp Correction:** In the USB grasp-insertion task, the policy adapted to poor initial grasps or forced releases by re-releasing and re-grasping the object to achieve a better pose for insertion.
- **Flexible Object Handling:** In the timing belt assembly, the policy dynamically adapted to arbitrary belt deformations and external perturbations.
These robust behaviors emerge naturally from autonomous exploration during RL training, a capability largely absent in imitation learning methods.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Baseline Comparisons

Further comparisons against other state-of-the-art RL and IL methods (Diffusion Policy, Residual RL, DAPG, IBRL) underscored the effectiveness of HIL-SERL's design:
- **Necessity of Demos & Corrections:** RL training from scratch (without demonstrations or corrections) failed completely (0% success). Similarly, SERL with abundant offline demonstrations (200, 10x more than HIL-SERL) but no *online corrections* performed significantly worse (e.g., 0% for car dashboard), confirming the crucial role of real-time human interventions.
- **Outperformance over Other RL/IL Hybrids:** HIL-SERL consistently outperformed Residual RL, DAPG, and IBRL, even when those baselines were provided with substantially more initial demonstrations (200 vs. HIL-SERL's 20-30). This is attributed to HIL-SERL's off-policy nature and dynamic weighting of human data, allowing it to surpass human-level performance without being constrained by demonstration suboptimality.
- **Diffusion Policy Comparison:** Diffusion Policy, while effective for learning expressive policy distributions, performed significantly worse on these tasks (e.g., 27% RAM insertion vs. 100% HIL-SERL), suggesting its advantage does not directly translate to reactive, closed-loop precision tasks where continuous visual servoing is critical.

#### 4.5. Insights into Learned Policy Behavior

- **Reliability through "Funnels":** Analysis of state visitation heatmaps revealed that HIL-SERL policies form a distinct "funnel" shape, where state visitations concentrate around successful trajectories and narrow towards the goal. This indicates robustification of regions and increased policy confidence, supported by higher Q-value variance in critical states that are successfully navigated. In contrast, HG-DAgger showed a much sparser and less defined funnel.
- **Reactive vs. Predictive Control:** The method demonstrated the flexibility to learn distinct control strategies:
 - **Reactive Policies (e.g., RAM insertion, Car Dashboard Assembly):** Characterized by high initial action variance that rapidly decreases near the target. This signifies continuous visual servoing and error correction, allowing policies to adapt to real-time sensory feedback and even break and re-establish contact if needed.
 - **Predictive Policies (e.g., Jenga whipping):** Exhibited consistently low action variance, indicating a highly confident, precise, and pre-planned open-loop motion, akin to a reflex.
This adaptability arises "for free" through environment interaction, without explicit problem formulation for hybrid systems, showcasing the power of the RL approach.

Overall, the results unequivocally demonstrate that HIL-SERL is a general and effective method for learning a wide array of complex, vision-based robotic manipulation skills directly in the real world within practical training times, achieving unprecedented levels of success and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Precise and Dexterous Robotic Manipulation via Human-in-the-Loop Reinforcement Learning" represents a substantial leap forward in the field of robotic manipulation, with profound implications for both academic research and industrial applications.

#### 5.1. Advancement of State-of-the-Art

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that model-free reinforcement learning, combined with carefully designed system choices and human-in-the-loop feedback, can effectively tackle a diverse range of complex, vision-based manipulation tasks directly in the real world. Achieving near-perfect success rates and significantly faster cycle times (up to 1.8x faster) compared to imitation learning and prior RL approaches within practical training durations (1 to 2.5 hours) sets a new benchmark. Notably, it is claimed to be the first to achieve dual-arm coordination with image inputs for tasks like Jenga whipping and timing belt assembly in real-world settings.

#### 5.2. Practical Applicability and Industrial Impact

The demonstrated ability to acquire a wide range of manipulation skills with high performance and adaptability makes HIL-SERL particularly valuable for industries characterized by High-Mix Low-Volume (HMLV) manufacturing. In sectors such as electronics, semiconductors, automotive, and aerospace, there's a growing need for flexible and agile automation to handle product customization and shorter life cycles. HIL-SERL offers a framework that can potentially enable robots to quickly learn new, precise assembly or manipulation tasks without extensive manual programming, thus reducing deployment costs and increasing versatility on production lines.

#### 5.3. Enabling a New Generation of Robotic Learning

This work serves as a critical stepping stone for future research in learned robotic manipulation. Its success validates the potential of RL for real-world scenarios, which could inspire a shift in how robotic skills are developed and deployed.
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** The authors highlight HIL-SERL's potential as an effective tool for generating high-quality, task-specific data. Policies converged by HIL-SERL can autonomously collect large datasets, which can then be distilled into generalist robot foundation models (e.g., RT-X, Octo, OpenVLA). This could accelerate the development of more capable and broadly applicable robot intelligence.
- **Reduced Training Time through Pretraining:** While current training times are practical, future work could further reduce them by pretraining value functions that encapsulate general manipulation capabilities. These pretrained models could then be quickly fine-tuned for new, specific tasks, akin to transfer learning in other AI domains.

#### 5.4. Addressing Limitations and Future Directions

The paper thoughtfully acknowledges its limitations, opening avenues for future research:
- **Longer-Horizon Tasks:** While HIL-SERL excels at complex short-to-medium horizon tasks, scalability to significantly longer horizons with potentially compounding errors remains a challenge. Potential solutions include improved pretraining or automatic task segmentation (e.g., using vision-language models to break down tasks into manageable sub-goals).
- **Generalization to Unstructured Environments:** The experiments, while robust to variations, did not involve extensive randomization or testing in completely unstructured environments. This suggests future work could focus on increasing the training duration with more diverse randomization or integrating vision foundation models (pretrained on vast datasets) to enhance generalization to novel settings.

In conclusion, HIL-SERL not only presents a highly performant and practical solution for current robotic manipulation challenges but also lays crucial groundwork for advancing the field towards more autonomous, adaptive, and general-purpose robotic systems, ultimately bringing closer the vision of robots seamlessly operating in complex, dynamic real-world environments.
