# EVA: Efficient Reinforcement Learning for End-to-End Video Agent

> [!summary] Summary
> SenseTime Research developed EVA, an efficient reinforcement learning framework that enables multimodal large language models to act as autonomous agents for video understanding. EVA utilizes a "planning-before-perception" paradigm, allowing the agent to intelligently decide what, when, and how to watch a video based on a query, achieving improved accuracy across various benchmarks while significantly reducing visual token consumption.

## Problem

- Traditional MLLMs for video understanding process entire videos or uniformly sampled frames, leading to computational inefficiency and context overflow for long videos, or missing critical details with sparse sampling.
- Existing agent-based methods often employ a "perception-first" strategy, where pre-sampled frames limit the agent's initial understanding and constrain planning, often relying on rigid workflows and static sampling rates.
- Training autonomous agents to dynamically acquire visual information presents challenges in initial tool calling, reasoning with insufficient data, avoiding over-exploration, and exercising fine-grained control over visual input.

## Method

- EVA formulates active video understanding as a ==Markov Decision Process== (MDP), allowing the MLLM agent to learn a policy for sequential decision-making, starting with only a textual query to enable "planning before perception."
- A flexible frame-selection tool is designed, enabling the agent to control temporal windows (start/end time), number of frames (nframes), and spatial resolution (resize), supporting dynamic allocation of visual information.
- A three-stage learning pipeline integrates supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for foundational capabilities, Kahneman–Tversky Optimization (KTO) for correcting failure patterns, and Data-Enhanced Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for scalable online reinforcement learning.

## Results

- EVA achieved **51.8%** accuracy on LSDBench with approximately **6.2K** visual tokens, a +**2.6%** improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL baseline while using significantly fewer tokens.
- Consistently strong performance was observed across long-form video benchmarks (e.g., **55.0%** on LongVideoBench, **68.3%** on MLVU), generally outperforming baselines with an estimated average of 20-30 frames processed per video.
- The full EVA-GRPO training schema yielded the highest performance on benchmarks like LongVideoBench (**55.0%**) and MLVU (**68.3%**), demonstrating that progressive training with reinforcement learning leads to more deliberate, multi-round reasoning and reduced sampled frames.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "planning-before-perception" paradigm allows agents to formulate a plan based on a query before interacting with visual data, leading to more efficient and targeted information acquisition.
> - A multi-stage training approach combining SFT, KTO, and GRPO is effective in shaping complex agent behaviors, from basic tool use to refined, efficiency-driven exploration strategies.
> - Dynamically controlled visual token allocation, including temporal and spatial parameters, enables the agent to adaptively focus on relevant video segments, reducing computational load while maintaining or improving task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video understanding is a critical component of multimodal artificial intelligence, with applications spanning video question answering, retrieval, and embodied perception. Recent advancements in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have enabled the integration of vision, language, and reasoning capabilities. However, a significant challenge in video understanding with MLLMs stems from the inherent nature of video data: long token sequences, extensive temporal dependencies, and redundant frames.

Traditional approaches typically treat MLLMs as passive recognizers. These systems process entire videos or uniformly sampled frames without incorporating adaptive reasoning or selective attention mechanisms. This often leads to inefficiencies, particularly with long videos, due to the computational cost of processing redundant information or the potential for crucial details to be missed by sparse uniform sampling, a problem referred to as the "sampling dilemma."

A more recent development involves agent-based methods that equip MLLMs with external tools, such as frame-selection modules. While these methods represent an advancement towards active exploration of video content, they frequently rely on manually designed workflows, fixed parameters, and limited exploration capabilities (e.g., static sampling rates). Furthermore, many of these "agent-based" systems still operate under a "perception-first" strategy, where they are initially fed a set of uniformly sampled frames alongside a textual query. This approach can lead to redundant visual processing and limits reasoning efficiency by anchoring the agent to potentially irrelevant or noisy visual cues from the outset.

The work presented in this paper, EVA, positions itself by addressing the limitations of both passive MLLMs and existing perception-first agentic methods. It aims to develop a truly autonomous and adaptive MLLM-based agent capable of *planning before perception*. This paradigm shift intends to move MLLMs beyond being mere recognizers towards becoming active, self-directed agents that can intelligently interact with video content based on specific queries, optimizing both efficiency and accuracy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an Efficient Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework for an End-to-End Video Agent (EVA) that enables MLLM-based agents to autonomously decide *what to watch, when to watch, and how to watch* a video. This aims to facilitate query-driven and efficient video understanding.

The motivation for this work is rooted in several critical observations regarding existing video understanding systems:

- **Inefficiency of Passive MLLMs:** Most MLLMs for video understanding process entire videos or uniformly sampled frames. This approach is inefficient for long videos due to the sheer volume of visual tokens, which can lead to excessive computation and context overflow, and may still miss critical information if the uniform sampling rate is too low.
- **Limitations of Perception-First Agents:** Even advanced agent-based methods typically adopt a "perception-first" strategy. They receive pre-sampled frames and then perform reasoning or tool-calling. This means the agent's initial understanding is constrained by the provided (and potentially suboptimal) visual input, hindering true planning-driven interaction. These methods often feature rigid workflows and limited control over sampling parameters, leading to suboptimal performance and efficiency on complex or lengthy videos.
- **Need for Adaptive and Autonomous Interaction:** The authors argue for a "planning-before-perception" paradigm. This approach empowers the agent to first reason solely from the textual query to formulate a plan (what, when, how to watch) before engaging with any visual input. This paradigm is conceptualized as an iterative process involving *summary–plan–action–reflection*.
- **Challenges in Agent Learning:** Training such an autonomous agent presents several challenges:
 - **Initial Tool Calling:** Learning to generate the first tool call based only on a textual query, without prior visual context.
 - **Reasoning with Insufficient Information:** Guiding the agent to continue reasoning and gather more information when initial visual evidence is inadequate.
 - **Avoiding Over-exploration/Traps:** Ensuring the agent does not fall into loops of unnecessary iterations or expend excessive computational resources without making progress.
 - **Flexible Control:** Enabling the agent to exercise fine-grained control over visual information acquisition, including temporal windows, number of frames, and spatial resolution (zoom-in/zoom-out).

By addressing these motivations and challenges, EVA seeks to transform MLLMs from passive video recognizers into active, adaptive, and autonomous agents that can effectively balance reasoning depth with computational efficiency for diverse video understanding tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EVA formulates the active video understanding problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where the agent's state at each timestep includes the user query, interleaved text-frame history, and visual evidence from prior tool calls. The agent's policy, parameterized as $\pi_\theta(a_t | s_t)$, learns to select actions. Critically, the agent begins with only the query, no initial visual information, to enable planning before perception.

A flexible frame-selection tool is designed to allow the agent both temporal and spatial control over visual input. This tool takes parameters: `start_time`, `end_time` (for temporal window), `nframes` (number of frames to sample), and `resize` (spatial downsampling ratio). This broad action space encourages the agent to learn dynamic allocation of visual information across multiple interaction rounds.

The core of EVA's methodology is a three-stage learning pipeline that integrates supervised imitation and reinforcement learning:

#### 3.1. Data Construction

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Cold-Start Data Pipeline (EVA-SFT):**
 - **Purpose:** To instill foundational video agent capabilities, including tool-call formatting, interleaved image-text reasoning, frame-level understanding, and basic frame selection strategies. This stage provides a stable behavioral prior.
 - **Process:** Qwen2.5-VL-72B acts as a teacher MLLM. It is prompted to generate high-quality agentic video understanding data based on source video QA pairs from existing datasets (llava-video, cgbench). Prompts incorporate "Past Success Experiences," "Diverse Workflow Hints," and "Reflective Thinking Prompts."
 - **Format:** Each SFT data instance follows a "Summary + Planning + Action + Reflection" structure.
 - **Summary:** MLLM describes frame content to ground understanding.
 - **Planning:** Model proposes potential actions (tool calls), estimating costs and outcomes.
 - **Action:** Model generates tool calls (e.g., `frame_select` with parameters).
 - **Reflection:** Model evaluates if sufficient visual information is available before answering, prompting further tool calls if needed.
 - **Dataset Size:** 10,000 high-quality samples covering general and task-specific agent training.

- **Kahneman–Tversky Optimization (KTO) Data Pipeline (EVA-KTO):**
 - **Purpose:** To guide the agent to prefer effective strategies and correct typical failure patterns (e.g., guessing without evidence, inappropriate frame sampling rates) observed after SFT, thus improving convergence, robustness, and stability for subsequent online RL.
 - **Process:** KTO, unlike DPO, uses single-sample preference labels ("chosen" or "rejected"). Incorrect trajectories from the SFT construction pipeline (e.g., guessing answers without sufficient visual tokens) are categorized as "rejected" samples, often identified by an LLM-as-judge. Resampled high-quality successful trajectories serve as "chosen" data.
 - **Dataset Size:** 11,000 labeled frame-selection strategies.

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) Data Pipeline (EVA-RL):**
 - **Purpose:** To enable online reinforcement learning, allowing the model to generate multiple rollouts and iteratively learn from successes and failures with a dynamically expanding dataset.
 - **Process (Data-Enhanced GRPO):** Addresses the limitation of static GRPO by dynamically expanding the training dataset. Initially, failure cases from the KTO-trained model are collected. After several GRPO training steps, new failure cases are gathered and used as in-context examples for the teacher MLLM. The teacher MLLM then generates new open-ended QA pairs for unseen videos (from HD-VILA), conditioned on these failure examples, to increase challenge diversity and prevent reward hacking.
 - **Format:** The teacher MLLM produces open-ended QA pairs with concise answers, which are deemed more robust to reward hacking than multiple-choice options.
 - **Dataset Size:** 9,600 open-ended video QA pairs and 1,100 multiple-choice questions.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning

- **GRPO Primer:** EVA employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a KL-regularized policy optimization method. The objective is to maximize expected return while keeping the policy close to a reference model ($\pi_{ref}$) initialized by SFT and KTO (Equation 2).
- **Reward Shaping:** A composite reward function (Equation 3) combines accuracy and format rewards.
 - **Accuracy Reward ($r_{acc}$):**
 - For multiple-choice questions: `Completeness Self-Verification (CSV) reward` ($r_{csv}$). $r_{csv}=1$ only if both the base model (acting as a judge) and EVA produce the correct answer, otherwise $r_{csv}=0$.
 - For open-ended questions: Averaged `ROUGE score` ($r_{rouge}$), computed as the mean of ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L F1 scores (Equation 5).
 - **Format Reward ($r_{fmt}$):** A small compensatory reward (0.05) is given if the model generates a tool call but ultimately produces an incorrect answer. This low reward discourages superficial "guessing" while acknowledging an attempt at interaction.

#### 3.3. Experiment Settings

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, chosen for its vision input flexibility.
- **Training Details:**
 - SFT: 2 epochs, batch size 8, learning rate 2e-6.
 - KTO: same learning rate, beta 0.1, 63% chosen / 37% rejected data.
 - GRPO: 1 epoch, batch size 64, 8 rollouts per sample, learning rate 1e-6, on 32 H100 GPUs. 90% open-ended, 10% multiple-choice questions.
- **Evaluation:** EVA is evaluated on six video understanding benchmarks (LSDBench, LongVideoBench, MLVU, VideoMME, LVBench, Video-Holmes) using accuracy as the primary metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EVA across multiple benchmarks demonstrates its capabilities in efficient and adaptive video understanding.

- **Sampling Dilemma Bench (LSDBench):**
 - EVA achieved an accuracy of 51.8% while utilizing approximately 6.2K visual tokens. This represents a +2.6% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL baseline, which used significantly more tokens.
 - In comparison to closed-source models like Gemini-2.0-Flash (56.2% accuracy), EVA achieved competitive performance with a substantially lower visual token count (6.2K vs. >700K).
 - These results indicate that EVA effectively addresses the sampling dilemma by using reasoning-driven visual planning to achieve high accuracy with greater efficiency.

- **Long-Form Video Understanding (LongVideoBench, MLVU, VideoMME, LVBench):**
 - EVA demonstrated consistent strong performance, achieving accuracies of 55.0% on LongVideoBench, 68.3% on MLVU, 60.2% on VideoMME (overall), and 43.3% on LVBench.
 - It generally outperformed most open-source and adaptive agent baselines while processing an estimated average of 20–30 frames per video (with adaptively adjusted resolutions).
 - This highlights the advantage of the planning-before-perception paradigm in scenarios with extended temporal contexts, where dynamic attention allocation is more effective than fixed dense sampling.

- **Zero-Shot Performance on Video Reasoning Bench (Video-Holmes):**
 - In a zero-shot setting, EVA achieved an overall performance of 37.2%, which is competitive with or superior to many open-source models using uniformly sampled frames, such as Video-R1 (36.5%) and VideoChat-R1 (33.0%).
 - This suggests strong transferability and robust general reasoning capabilities across diverse reasoning abilities (e.g., Social Reasoning, Temporal Causal Inference) even without task-specific fine-tuning.

- **Ablation Study on Training Schema:**
 - **EVA-SFT:** Showed the lowest performance (e.g., 49.9% on LongVideoBench) with high frame and round consumption (e.g., 33.8* avg frames on LongVideoBench). This indicated SFT taught tool-calling formats but not efficient exploration.
 - **EVA-KTO:** Significantly improved performance (e.g., 53.2% on LongVideoBench) while reducing both frame consumption and interaction rounds (e.g., 35.6* avg frames on LongVideoBench).
 - **EVA-GRPO:** Achieved the highest performance (e.g., 55.0% on LongVideoBench, 68.3% on MLVU) with further reduced sampled frames, but an increase in interaction rounds. This progression suggests that GRPO-trained agents learn to engage in more deliberate, multi-round reasoning, precisely allocating visual tokens per round.

- **Ablation Study on GRPO Data Composition:**
 - Mixing open-ended (OE) data with task-specific multiple-choice (MC) data in GRPO training led to more stable training and improved performance compared to using MC-only or OE-only data. This indicates that open-ended questions help ground answers in visual content and prevent reward hacking.

- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - Despite multi-round planning, EVA's overall computation (total visual token usage) remained comparable to or lower than static uniform-sampling baselines. Inference runtime was dominated by the adaptively selected visual tokens, not the number of reasoning steps.

- **Case Studies:**
 - Visualizations demonstrated EVA's ability to generate diverse workflows, including focusing on specific video segments or briefly overviewing the video before zooming in.
 - The agent could autonomously adjust all relevant parameters (temporal range, number of frames, spatial resolution), offering a broader and more efficient action space than prior agents.

- **Evaluation on ELV-Halluc Benchmark (Semantic Aggregation Hallucination):**
 - EVA achieved the highest Overall Accuracy and reduced the Semantic Aggregation Hallucination (SAH) ratio from 8.8% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) to 5.0%. This result suggests enhanced frame-level perception and improved temporal localization capabilities, which are crucial for aggregating semantic information correctly.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EVA framework represents a development in the domain of video understanding by introducing a planning-before-perception paradigm. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Advancement in Agent Autonomy:** EVA shifts MLLMs from passive video processors to active, autonomous agents capable of query-driven interaction. This represents a step towards systems that can "decide what to watch, when to watch, and how to watch," mimicking more human-like strategic engagement with information.
- **Improved Efficiency and Accuracy:** By intelligently allocating visual tokens through iterative summary–plan–action–reflection cycles, EVA achieves substantial improvements in accuracy across various benchmarks while often consuming significantly fewer computational resources compared to brute-force or uniform sampling methods. This is particularly impactful for long-form videos where processing the entire content is impractical.
- **Robust Training Methodology:** The proposed three-stage training pipeline (SFT cold-start, KTO correction, and Data-Enhanced GRPO optimization) provides a structured and stable approach for developing complex RL-based agents. It addresses challenges like initial behavior shaping, correction of failure modes, and scalable online learning, which are critical for reproducible agentic reinforcement learning.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** The planning-before-perception paradigm promotes deeper and more grounded reasoning. The agent explicitly formulates hypotheses and selectively gathers evidence, which can lead to more robust multi-step reasoning, causal understanding, and reduced semantic aggregation hallucinations, as evidenced by performance on benchmarks like Video-Holmes and ELV-Halluc.
- **Flexible Tool Usage:** EVA demonstrates the ability to control a flexible frame-selection tool with both temporal and spatial parameters. This extended action space allows for more nuanced and efficient information gathering than previous methods with limited control dimensions.
- **Broader Applications:** The principles demonstrated by EVA could be generalized to other domains requiring adaptive information acquisition and sequential decision-making, moving beyond video to other multimodal agentic tasks where selective attention and planning are beneficial.
- **Addressing Long-Context Challenges:** The work directly contributes to overcoming the long-standing challenges of processing long token sequences and redundant information in large multimodal models, making them more practical for real-world video analysis tasks.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current reliance on pre-defined tool interfaces and potential struggles with unseen or noisy query distributions. Future work is suggested to explore more flexible tool ecosystems, self-evolving reasoning strategies, and cross-modal memory mechanisms to further enhance autonomy and generalization.
