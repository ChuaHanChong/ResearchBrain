# LooperMuscle: Fast and Stable Learning of Humanoid Whole-Body Tracking via Structured Mixture-of-Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> LooperMuscle, a composed expert actor-critic framework, improves the speed and quality of humanoid whole-body tracking (WBT) by integrating a structured Mixture-of-Experts actor and an expert-aware distributional critic with quota-routed replay. It achieves a 34.0% reduction in body-position tracking error compared to FastSAC-MLP while training approximately 8 times faster than PPO for similar tracking performance.

## Problem

- Fast off-policy reinforcement learning methods for humanoid whole-body tracking (WBT) offer speed but exhibit significantly lower tracking quality compared to slower on-policy methods.
- Monolithic policy networks in WBT struggle to effectively manage heterogeneous dynamics across different robot body parts, compromising overall fidelity.
- Traditional scalar value functions and uniform experience replay hinder fine-grained credit assignment and balanced learning across diverse body parts in complex WBT tasks.

## Method

- **LooperMuscle** uses a structured ==multi-expert actor== with K expert networks employing learnable per-joint-group gating and anti-collapse regularization to specialize control.
- An ==expert-aware distributional critic== employs per-expert distributional heads aggregated using the actor's gating weights to enable finer-grained credit assignment.
- A ==quota-routed replay buffer== with deferred curriculum scheduling tags transitions for expert contribution, ensuring balanced expert exposure and progressively introducing difficult samples.

## Results

- LooperMuscle reduced body-position tracking error by **34.0%** compared to FastSAC-MLP, achieving **0.101m** mean body error and **0.285 rad** mean joint error.
- The framework demonstrated approximately **8 times** faster training (**45 minutes**) than PPO (**360 minutes**) while providing competitive tracking quality.
- Ablation studies showed the multi-expert actor was critical, and the system was successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot for whole-body Kungfu sequences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the control policy into specialized experts significantly improves representational capacity and tracking quality for high-dimensional whole-body tasks.
> - Structuring the critic to be 'expert-aware' and routing replay data based on expert contributions enables effective credit assignment and balanced learning across policy components.
> - Progressively introducing challenging samples through deferred scheduling stabilizes early training while pushing the limits of final tracking performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) has significantly accelerated the training of robot control policies, particularly for humanoid robots, through the use of massively parallel simulation environments. This acceleration is crucial for the iterative sim-to-real development cycle. Recent advancements in off-policy RL algorithms, such as FastSAC and FastTD3, have enabled rapid training of humanoid locomotion policies, sometimes in as little as 15 minutes.

However, the current research landscape indicates a persistent gap when the task transitions from basic locomotion to complex whole-body tracking (WBT). WBT requires a policy to accurately reproduce diverse, high-dimensional human motions across all joints. While off-policy methods offer speed, their tracking quality often falls short of on-policy baselines like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which typically requires significantly longer training times. This performance disparity limits the practical utility of rapid training in WBT scenarios.

Previous work in humanoid motion imitation, such as DeepMimic, established paradigms for tracking reference motions. Subsequent research extended these to adversarial imitation, large-scale motion datasets, and progressive training curricula. Many of these approaches employ monolithic policy networks, which aim to control all body parts simultaneously. More recent efforts, including BeyondMimic, HOVER, and ASAP, have demonstrated versatile humanoid tracking and agile skills through various techniques. Concurrently, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have gained traction in deep learning and have been explored in RL for multi-task and hierarchical settings. Notably, KungfuBot2 introduced Orthogonal MoE for versatile whole-body control under PPO. Distributional RL, exemplified by DVF and QR-DQN, has also been applied to continuous control, offering return distributions rather than scalar estimates.

LooperMuscle aims to bridge the speed-performance gap in WBT by augmenting the FastSAC recipe. It addresses identified limitations of existing off-policy methods by integrating structured expert decomposition in both the actor and critic, combined with contribution-aware data routing. This positions LooperMuscle as a method that leverages the efficiency of off-policy RL while attempting to restore the tracking quality typically associated with longer-trained on-policy methods, specifically for complex WBT tasks. It combines MoE with distributional RL and a structured replay mechanism, a combination not previously explored in this specific context for humanoid WBT.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to narrow the speed-performance gap observed in humanoid whole-body tracking (WBT) when using fast off-policy reinforcement learning methods, specifically FastSAC-style approaches. While these methods offer significantly reduced training times, they often exhibit a notable degradation in tracking quality compared to on-policy algorithms like PPO, which achieve higher performance but at a greater computational cost in terms of wall-clock time.

The motivation for LooperMuscle stems from three interrelated structural limitations identified in current approaches that contribute to this speed-performance disparity in high-dimensional WBT tasks:

1. **Monolithic Policy Networks:** A single, monolithic policy network is required to manage the heterogeneous dynamics across various body parts of a humanoid robot. This often necessitates a compromise, for instance, between maintaining lower-body stability crucial for balance and locomotion, and achieving upper-body expressiveness for complex arm and torso motions. This compromise can limit the overall tracking fidelity.
2. **Scalar Value Functions:** Traditional scalar value functions provide a single, aggregate estimate of expected return. In the context of high-dimensional WBT, where numerous joints and body segments are involved, this coarse estimation makes precise credit assignment to individual body parts or their specific contributions to the overall task success challenging. This lack of granularity can impede effective learning.
3. **Uniform Experience Replay:** Standard uniform experience replay mechanisms treat all collected transitions equally, regardless of their utility or the specific policy components involved in generating them. This can lead to an imbalance in the learning signal, where transitions associated with well-performing policy components dominate, while those from under-performing or less-frequently used components may be under-represented, hindering their improvement and potentially leading to stagnation.

LooperMuscle is introduced as a composed expert actor-critic framework designed to address these limitations through an integrated architecture. The goal is to restore the tracking quality of humanoid WBT policies while maintaining the high training efficiency characteristic of FastSAC-style methods. The framework aims to achieve this by enabling specialization within the policy, facilitating finer-grained credit assignment, and ensuring balanced learning exposure for different policy components.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LooperMuscle is presented as a composed expert actor-critic framework built upon the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, specifically utilizing the FastSAC recipe as its base. It extends this foundation with three primary, interconnected mechanisms: a structured multi-expert actor, an expert-aware distributional critic, and a quota-routed replay buffer with deferred curriculum scheduling.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup and Notation

The framework addresses Whole-Body Tracking (WBT) for a 29-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) humanoid robot. The policy receives a state `s_t` comprising proprioceptive data (joint positions and velocities, body orientation and angular velocity) and motion reference signals (target joint positions, key-body positions, target orientation). The policy outputs `a_t` as target joint positions for PD controllers. The 29 joints are partitioned into G=2 semantic groups (upper-body and lower-body) to account for distinct dynamic roles.

#### 3.2. Multi-Expert Actor with Anti-Collapse Gating

To overcome the limitations of monolithic policy networks, LooperMuscle employs K expert networks fused through learnable per-joint-group gating.

- **Expert Fusion:** Each expert `k` generates a per-joint action `μ_k,j(s_t)`. The final action `a_t,j` is a weighted sum of expert outputs, incorporating `w_k,g(j)(s_t)` (gating weight for expert `k` in joint group `g(j)`), `S_k,j` (learnable per-joint scaling factor), and `c_k` (output alignment coefficient). This two-level design allows for both group-level expert recruitment and fine-grained per-joint modulation.
- **Per-Joint-Group Gating:** Gating weights `w_k,g(s_t)` are computed independently for each joint group `g` using a temperature-scaled softmax function from a shared recruitment network. A lower temperature `τ_g` promotes sharper expert selection for stability-critical lower-body joints, while a higher `τ_g` allows softer blending for expressive upper-body motions.
- **Semantic Scaling and Output Alignment:** Scaling factors `S_k,j` are initialized proportionally to the angular range of each joint, reflecting the heterogeneous scales of action magnitudes across the robot's body. Both `S_k,j` and `c_k` are learnable parameters.
- **Anti-Collapse Gating Regularization:** To ensure active participation from multiple experts and prevent a single expert from dominating, a KL-divergence-based load-balance regularizer `L_lb` is introduced. This regularizer encourages the batch-and-group-averaged gating weights `¯w` to be close to a uniform prior `u = [1/K, ..., 1/K]`, with an annealing factor `α_lb(t)` that decays during training. The overall actor loss incorporates this regularization term.

#### 3.3. Expert-Aware Distributional Critic

To facilitate finer credit assignment than a scalar critic, LooperMuscle utilizes an expert-aware distributional critic.

- **Per-Expert Distributional Heads:** For each of the M=2 critics and each expert `k`, a categorical distribution over N atoms (following C51) is defined, `Z_m,k(s,a)`. This models the full return distribution rather than a point estimate.
- **Gating-Weighted Aggregation:** The per-expert distributions are aggregated using the actor's group-averaged gating weights `˜w_k(s)`. This creates an isomorphic structure between the critic's value estimation and the actor's expert contributions, allowing the critic to attribute value contributions based on how the actor's experts are utilized.
- **Distributional Bellman Training:** The critic is trained using a distributional Bellman projection with a cross-entropy loss. This approach is intended to reduce value estimation bias, enable per-expert credit assignment due to the isomorphic structure, and maintain the stability of dual-critic SAC.

#### 3.4. Quota-Routed Replay with Deferred Scheduling

To address the issue of imbalanced training signals from uniform replay in a multi-expert setting, a structured replay mechanism is proposed.

- **Expert Contribution Tagging:** Each stored transition is augmented with metadata: an expert contribution vector `e_t` (measuring the mean absolute action contribution of each expert), a body tracking ratio `b_t` (fraction of key bodies below an error threshold), and a step identifier `t_step`. The dominant expert `k*` determines initial routing, and transitions with low `b_t` are flagged for deferred buckets.
- **Quota-Routed Sampling:** Mini-batches are composed of a uniform sample fraction and an expert-routed fraction. For the expert-routed portion, slots `N_k` are allocated proportional to a target quota `q_k` for each expert. This ensures balanced exposure, preventing strong experts from monopolizing the training signal and allowing weaker experts to improve.
- **Deferred Buckets:** Independent deferred buckets store difficult samples (those with low `b_t`). Their release into the main training stream is controlled by two progress-dependent schedules: a holdout ratio `h(ρ)` (decreasing initially) and a deferred unlock ratio `u(ρ)` (ramping up in later phases). This strategy stabilizes early convergence by holding back hard samples and progressively raises the performance ceiling by introducing them later.

#### 3.5. Closed-Loop Training

The three components are designed to interact in a closed training loop. The MoE actor's expert contributions `e_t` guide data routing in the replay buffer. The quota-routed data then shapes the distributional value estimates of the expert-aware critic, whose gradients, in turn, refine expert specialization and influence future contribution profiles of the actor. This interconnected design ensures mutual reinforcement across policy structure, data allocation, and value learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated LooperMuscle on 29-DoF humanoid whole-body tracking, comparing it against PPO (as a quality reference) and FastSAC-MLP (as a fast-training baseline). Experiments were conducted in MJLab simulation using 40 LAFAN1 motion sequences across six categories.

#### 4.1. Speed–Quality Tradeoff

- **Overall Performance:** LooperMuscle demonstrated improved tracking quality compared to FastSAC-MLP while maintaining significantly faster training than PPO. Specifically, LooperMuscle achieved a mean body position error of 0.101m, a mean joint position error of 0.285 rad, and a normalized reward of 0.723, with a training time of approximately 45 minutes. This represents a 34.0% reduction in body-position tracking error relative to FastSAC-MLP (0.153m body error) which trained in about 15 minutes. PPO, while achieving the best performance (0.082m body error, 0.243 rad joint error, 1.000 normalized reward), required approximately 360 minutes (6 hours) of training.
- **Training Budget:** FastSAC-MLP's performance saturated shortly after 15 minutes, with marginal gains even when given an extended training budget, suggesting a representational bottleneck in the monolithic actor rather than a compute limitation. LooperMuscle converted additional training time (45 min vs 15 min for FastSAC) into substantial quality gains.
- **Task Difficulty:** The paper notes that whole-body tracking is a more challenging credit-assignment problem than locomotion, explaining why PPO maintained a stronger performance advantage in WBT compared to its reported parity with FastSAC in locomotion tasks.

#### 4.2. Per-Category Results

- LooperMuscle consistently outperformed FastSAC-MLP across all six motion categories (Walk, Run/Sprint, Jump, Dance, Fight, Fall&GetUp).
- The improvement in body-error reduction ranged from 26.7% for "Walk" to 40.6% for "Fall&GetUp." This trend suggests that the proposed expert specialization and replay curriculum are more effective for more dynamic and difficult, contact-rich motions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the necessity of each proposed component:
- **MoE Actor:** Removing the Multi-Expert Actor resulted in the largest performance degradation, increasing body error by 51.5%. This configuration effectively reverted to the FastSAC-MLP baseline, indicating that expert decomposition is a primary source of representational gain for high-DoF WBT.
- **Quota Replay:** Removing Quota Replay increased body error by 25.7%, emphasizing its role in ensuring balanced expert exposure and preventing under-training of specific experts.
- **Deferred Scheduling:** Removing Deferred Scheduling led to a 17.8% increase in body error, suggesting that the progressive release of difficult samples improves optimization stability and the final performance ceiling.
- **Expert-Aware Critic:** Replacing the expert-aware critic with a standard distributional critic resulted in an 11.9% degradation, supporting the hypothesis that value estimation aligned with expert structure enhances credit assignment.
- The observed degradations were not strictly additive, indicating a coupling among the modules and supporting the closed-loop system design.

#### 4.4. Training Curves and Expert Specialization

- Training reward curves illustrated LooperMuscle's faster convergence to a higher reward level compared to FastSAC-MLP within the initial 45 minutes.
- t-SNE projections of primitive trunk features demonstrated that experts developed non-redundant specialization, with states routed to different dominant experts occupying well-separated regions. These clusters aligned with dynamic regimes (e.g., stance vs. swing phase) rather than specific motion categories, suggesting specialization by control mode.
- Radar profiles showed that LooperMuscle surpassed FastSAC-MLP across most error axes by 45 minutes, and while PPO ultimately achieved the best final profile, LooperMuscle remained competitive at a significantly lower wall-clock cost.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Concurrent MoE-Based Methods

The paper contextualized its findings by comparing with other concurrent MoE-based methods (GMT, VMS/KungfuBot2) that use PPO on different datasets and robot configurations. Despite differences in algorithms, datasets, DoF, and metrics, a consistent trend emerged: MoE architectures substantially outperformed monolithic MLPs in both on-policy and off-policy paradigms, suggesting that expert decomposition is a broadly beneficial design principle for humanoid WBT. LooperMuscle's 34.0% error reduction (FastSAC, LAFAN1, 29-DoF) was within the range of improvements (14.6% to 19.7%) observed in the PPO-based methods (AMASS, 23-DoF).

#### 4.6. Hardware Deployment Validation

LooperMuscle was validated on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot using the open-source Holosoma runtime stack. The policy was retrained with a realistic, onboard-estimable 154-dimensional observation interface (as opposed to the privileged ground-truth interface used for MJLab benchmarking). The deployed policy successfully executed whole-body Kungfu sequences with stable balance and smooth transitions, demonstrating its applicability to real-world hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LooperMuscle represents a contribution to the field of humanoid robot control by effectively narrowing the persistent speed–performance gap in whole-body tracking tasks. By integrating a semantically structured Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) actor, an expert-aware distributional critic, and quota-routed replay with deferred curriculum scheduling, the framework demonstrates a principled approach to combining training efficiency with tracking quality.

The primary significance lies in its ability to significantly improve motion tracking accuracy over FastSAC-style methods (e.g., 34% reduction in body error over FastSAC-MLP) while requiring considerably less wall-clock training time than PPO (approximately 8 times faster). This efficiency is crucial for rapid policy iteration in sim-to-real development of humanoid robots, where iterative refinement and deployment are common. The architecture's ability to decompose control responsibilities, perform fine-grained credit assignment, and balance training exposure addresses fundamental limitations of monolithic policies and uniform replay, leading to more robust and higher-quality behaviors. The empirical validation across diverse motion categories, with greater gains observed on more challenging tasks, further supports its robustness.

The successful deployment on a physical Unitree G1 robot, albeit with a retrained policy for a realistic observation interface, validates the practical applicability of the LooperMuscle recipe for real-world humanoid control. This indicates a potential to accelerate the development of complex and versatile humanoid skills that can operate outside of simulated environments. The consistent performance improvement of MoE architectures over monolithic MLPs across different RL paradigms, as highlighted by comparison with concurrent work, suggests that expert decomposition is a generally beneficial principle for high-dimensional, complex control problems in robotics.

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges several limitations:
1. **Observation Interface Discrepancy:** The primary quantitative benchmarks and ablations were conducted using MJLab's privileged observation interface, which provides ground-truth anchor-point perception unavailable on real hardware. The hardware policy required retraining under a deployable interface. A key next step is to repeat the full benchmark and ablation suite under the deployable interface to quantify associated retraining costs and establish a more unified sim-to-real pipeline.
2. **Parameter-Matched Baselines:** The comparison between MoE and monolithic MLP baselines might not fully isolate the contribution of expert structure from mere capacity differences. Future work could include parameter-matched larger MLP baselines to further clarify this.
3. **Fixed Design Choices:** Several design choices, such as the expert count (K), group partition (G), per-group temperatures, and routing fraction, were fixed. Future research could explore adaptive expert allocation, systematic variation of these parameters, and evaluation across different robot morphologies.
4. **Further Enhancements:** The authors propose exploring systematic domain randomization and diffusion-based motion priors in future work to enhance robustness and versatility.
