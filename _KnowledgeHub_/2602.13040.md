# TCRL: Temporal-Coupled Adversarial Training for Robust Constrained Reinforcement Learning in Worst-Case Scenarios

> [!summary] Summary
> Northeastern University researchers developed TCRL, a robust constrained reinforcement learning framework, to defend against temporal-coupled adversarial perturbations. The framework achieved significantly reduced safety costs, ranging from 559.38% to 19,077.78% lower than baselines, and improved rewards by 8.36% to 34.76% across various robotic control tasks under worst-case attack scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing robust Constrained Reinforcement Learning (CRL) methods primarily address single-step or temporally independent adversarial perturbations, neglecting the cumulative effects of attacks that evolve over time.
- Current robust CRL approaches struggle to implicitly perceive worst-case temporal-coupled adversaries, often requiring explicit modeling or co-training of an adversarial policy.
- There is a lack of effective robust training mechanisms for CRL agents specifically designed to counteract temporal-coupled perturbation attacks, which can lead to progressive errors and safety constraint violations.

## Method

- TCRL introduces a novel adversarial training framework featuring a worst-case-perceived cost constraint function that implicitly estimates safety costs under temporal-coupled perturbations.
- The framework incorporates a dynamic perturbation model that allows for the generation of progressively higher-intensity, temporally coupled attacks.
- A dual-constraint defense mechanism is implemented, utilizing autocorrelation and entropy stability constraints on reward values to mitigate reward degradation and preserve unpredictability against temporal-coupled attackers.

## Results

- TCRL-PPOL consistently achieved superior robustness, reducing safety costs by 559.38% to 19,077.78% and improving reward values by 8.36% to 34.76% compared to baselines under worst-case temporal-coupled attacks.
- The worst-case cost constraint function proved critical for managing safety, as its removal led to substantial increases in safety costs across all tested tasks.
- Reward-based temporal defense constraints were essential for maintaining task performance, with their omission resulting in significant reductions in reward values under temporal-coupled attacks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robustness against temporal-coupled perturbations is enhanced by implicitly estimating worst-case safety costs without needing explicit adversarial policy modeling.
> - Introducing dynamic, temporally coupled perturbation modeling allows for more realistic and challenging adversarial scenarios, better reflecting real-world conditions.
> - Combining autocorrelation and entropy stability constraints on rewards effectively counters the cumulative degradation induced by temporal-coupled attacks, maintaining both safety and task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated significant capabilities in decision-making, but its deployment in safety-critical domains often necessitates robust performance under strict safety constraints. Constrained Reinforcement Learning (CRL) addresses this by optimizing policies to maximize rewards while adhering to predefined cost limits. Applications for CRL include autonomous driving, robotics, and power grid management.

However, existing robust CRL approaches primarily focus on single-step or temporal-independent adversarial perturbations. These methods often model adversaries that generate bounded, fixed-radius perturbations at each time step, typically using L_p norm balls. This perspective limits their applicability and effectiveness in real-world scenarios where adversarial attacks or environmental disturbances can be temporally coupled, meaning their intensity or direction may evolve over time, influencing subsequent states in a correlated manner. Such temporal coupling can lead to cumulative errors, safety constraint violations, and significant performance degradation that current methods are not explicitly designed to handle.

Traditional robust RL, while addressing environmental uncertainties, is not directly applicable to CRL due to the inherent prioritization of safety constraints over reward maximization in CRL. Moreover, existing robust CRL studies largely model and defend against input perturbations but fail to account for the temporal correlation in adversarial effects. This research specifically positions itself to address this gap by developing a framework for robust CRL that explicitly considers and defends against temporal-coupled adversarial perturbations, thereby enhancing safety and performance in worst-case, dynamic adversarial environments. It aims to bridge the gap between static adversarial models and the dynamic, cumulative nature of real-world disturbances.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, TCRL (Temporal-Coupled Adversarial Training for Robust Constrained Reinforcement Learning), to enhance the robustness of CRL agents against temporal-coupled adversarial perturbations in worst-case scenarios. The motivation stems from two main challenges identified in current robust CRL methodologies:

**Challenge 1: Perceiving Worst-Case Temporal-Coupled Adversaries with Cost Constraint Functions.**
Current robust CRL approaches often model worst-case attackers against the cost constraint function as a co-training RL problem. In this setup, an agent learns to defend against an attacker that is simultaneously being trained to find optimal attack strategies. However, this co-training paradigm assumes the adversary is an explicit, learnable policy. In real-world environments, adversaries may not co-train with the agent; instead, they might directly corrupt state observations based on their own unknown, evolving strategies. This mismatch limits the robustness of agents trained against fixed or co-trained temporal-coupled attack strategies when faced with uncertain, dynamic adversaries.
The objective here is to enable the cost constraint function to perceive and account for worst-case temporal-coupled adversaries implicitly, without requiring explicit modeling or co-training of an adversarial policy. This would allow the agent to optimize its policy with respect to the highest possible safety costs that could arise within the boundaries of temporal perturbations, thereby ensuring safety even against unknown or evolving temporal attacks.

**Challenge 2: Effective Robust Training of CRL Agents under Temporal-Coupled Perturbation Attacks.**
Existing robust CRL methods have predominantly been designed for single-step perturbations and lack explicit mechanisms to model or defend against temporal robustness. The development of observational adversarial attacks in these methods often overlooks temporal-coupled effects. Consequently, these limitations prevent effective quantification of robustness under temporal-coupled perturbations and can cause existing CRL defense mechanisms to fail when confronted with such attacks. Temporal-coupled attacks can progressively increase in intensity, causing agents to deviate from desired behaviors, degrade rewards, and potentially lead to dangerous outcomes over time.
The objective is to establish an effective robust training mechanism for CRL agents that explicitly addresses temporal-coupled perturbation attacks. This involves designing defense strategies that can counteract the cumulative and evolving nature of such attacks, ensuring that the agent can maintain high rewards while simultaneously avoiding violations of safety constraints. This would enhance the agent's resilience to attacks that exploit temporal dependencies in the environment or the agent's policy.

In summary, the motivation is driven by the recognition that real-world safety-critical applications often face dynamic, temporally correlated adversarial disturbances, which existing robust CRL methods are ill-equipped to handle. The goal is to develop a framework that can perceive and defend against these complex, worst-case temporal threats, ensuring both performance and safety.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TCRL (Temporal-Coupled Adversarial Training for Robust Constrained Reinforcement Learning) is proposed as a novel adversarial training framework designed to enhance the robustness of constrained reinforcement learning agents against temporal-coupled perturbation attacks in worst-case scenarios. The framework consists of two main components: a worst-case-perceived cost constraint function and a dual-constraint defense mechanism towards the reward.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

When an agent is subjected to a temporal-coupled perturbation attack, TCRL collects safety costs and rewards. To mitigate costs and enhance rewards, it employs two primary strategies: (i) constructing a cost constraint function for worst-case safety cost estimation, and (ii) implementing a dual-constraint defense mechanism on reward signals. This dual approach aims to ensure high rewards while satisfying safety constraints, significantly enhancing CRL robustness.

#### 3.2. Temporal-coupled Perturbation Attack Modeling

To simulate realistic temporal-coupled attacks, TCRL introduces a dynamic perturbation model.
- **Temporal-coupled Perturbation Constraint Parameter ($\bar{\epsilon}_t$):** This parameter adaptively adjusts at each time step $t$. It is defined as $\bar{\epsilon}_t = \bar{\epsilon}_{t-1} + \alpha \cdot ||p_{t-1}||$, where $\alpha$ is a perturbation adjustment factor and $||p_{t-1}||$ is the magnitude of the perturbation from the previous step. Since $\alpha > 0$, $\bar{\epsilon}_t$ generally increases over time, allowing attackers to generate progressively higher-intensity perturbations.
- **$\bar{\epsilon}_t$-Temporal-coupled State Perturbation:** A perturbation $p_t$ is considered valid if it adheres to the constraint $||(s_t - \tilde{s}_t) - (s_{t+1} - \tilde{s}_{t+1})|| \le \bar{\epsilon}_t$. This implies that the difference between the current and next state perturbations must be bounded, preventing abrupt changes in the attack direction while allowing its magnitude to evolve. This modeling enables attackers to execute successive and potent attacks with a degree of stability, reflecting real-world cumulative effects.

#### 3.3. Cost Constraint Functions

This component focuses on estimating the worst-case safety cost under temporal-coupled perturbations without explicitly modeling the adversarial policy.
- **Worst-case Action Cost Value ($Q^\pi_c$):** This is defined as the expected discounted sum of immediate costs, where $a^*_t$ represents the worst-case action taken under a temporal-coupled perturbation attack. The worst-case cost value $V^\pi_c$ is similarly defined.
- **Worst-case Cost Bellman Operator ($\Gamma^\pi Q_c$):** This operator is introduced to compute the worst-case costs. For a deterministic policy $\pi$ and perturbation, it calculates the expected immediate cost plus a discounted maximum future cost over a set of possible actions.
- **Adversarial Action Set ($\Omega(s, \pi)$):** This set encompasses all actions that the policy $\pi$ might produce when the state $s$ undergoes temporal-coupled perturbations within a bounded region $B_{\epsilon}(s)$. Its computation involves identifying potential actions from corrupted states, leveraging convex relaxation techniques (e.g., Interval Bound Propagation) that derive layer-wise bounds ($\pi_{max}, \pi_{min}$) for the neural network's output. This allows defining a superset $\hat{\Omega}_{adv}$ for continuous action spaces.
- **Theoretical Foundation:** A theorem establishes that the Worst-case Cost Bellman Operator is a contraction mapping, and its unique fixed point is the worst-case cost value $Q^\pi_c$.
- **Training Worst-case Cost Network ($Q^\pi_\varphi$):** A network $Q^\pi_\varphi$ is trained to estimate these worst-case costs by minimizing a loss function ($L_{cost}$) that employs a modified Bellman equation with the maximum over actions in $\Omega(s, \pi)$. This transforms the original CRL constraint ($V^\pi_c \le C_{threshold}$) into a robust worst-case constraint.

#### 3.4. Reward-based Temporal Defense Constraints

This dual defense mechanism aims to counter temporal-coupled attackers that exploit historical reward data to degrade rewards over time.
- **Autocorrelation Constraints based on Reward Values ($C_{corr}$):** To disrupt attackers' ability to model temporal coupling in system dynamics via historical reward data, TCRL imposes a weak correlation constraint on the reward sequence. The temporal correlation penalty is defined as the sum of absolute autocorrelation function values ($\phi$) within a time window $w$. This constraint restricts the autocorrelation coefficient to remain below a threshold $\epsilon_{corr}$. A microscopic approximation with a gradient correction mechanism is used for optimization to address non-differentiability.
- **Entropy Stability Constraints based on Reward Values ($C_{ent}$):** To prevent instability from abrupt changes in reward distribution caused by temporal-coupled attacks, an entropy stability constraint is introduced. The reward space is discretized into $N$ bins, and an empirical distribution is computed within a sliding window. A time-varying entropy function $H_t$ is then derived. The constraint ensures that the entropy variation rate ($||H_t - H_{t-1}||_\infty$) stays below a maximum allowed rate $\epsilon_{ent}$, promoting a smooth evolution of the reward distribution and preserving reward unpredictability.

#### 3.5. Practical Implementation

TCRL optimizes a policy $\tilde{\pi}$ under attack conditions. The objective function (Equation 15) maximizes the expected discounted sum of rewards, subject to four constraints: the worst-case cost $V^\pi_c \le \eta$, the reward autocorrelation $C_{corr} \le \epsilon_{corr}$, the reward entropy stability $C_{ent} \le \epsilon_{ent}$, and robustness against all possible attackers $h$.
These constraints are incorporated into the policy optimization using Lagrangian relaxation (Equation 16). The Lagrangian multipliers are updated using a PID Lagrangian method to enhance stability. The algorithm iteratively collects trajectories, computes worst-case costs, and updates policy and critic networks to minimize the combined loss function.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of TCRL-PPOL, integrated with the PID-PPO-Lagrange (PPOL) framework, focused on its robustness against temporal-coupled and temporal-independent perturbation attacks across four robotic motion control tasks: Ball-Circle, Ball-Run, Car-Circle, and Car-Run. Performance was assessed based on achieved reward and safety cost.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Adversarial Attackers:**
 - **Worst-TC (Worst-case Temporal-coupled):** A proposed attacker model that integrates worst-case cost estimation with reward minimization, maximizing cost and minimizing reward simultaneously via temporal-coupled perturbations.
 - **Random attacker:** Applies random perturbations without a specific objective.
 - **Max Cost (MC) attacker:** Designed to maximize the safety cost of the trained agent.
 - **Maximum Action Difference (MAD) attacker:** Aims to reduce the reward by maximizing the KL divergence between corrupted and original policies.
- **Baselines:**
 - **PPOL-vanilla:** Standard PID-PPO-Lagrange without robustness training.
 - **PPOL-random:** PPOL trained with agents subjected to random noise.
 - **ADV-PPOL(MC):** PPOL trained against the Max-Cost (MC) attacker.

#### 4.2. Performance of CRL with Robust Training (Table 1)

TCRL-PPOL consistently demonstrated superior robustness across all four tasks and under various attack types, outperforming all baselines.

- **Robustness against Worst-TC Attacks:**
 - **Safety Cost:** While baselines showed significant degradation in safety performance and high safety costs under Worst-TC attacks, TCRL consistently achieved the lowest safety costs. For instance, TCRL reduced safety costs against Worst-TC perturbations by 559.38% to 1864.58% in Ball-Circle, 1779.49% to 15,383.33% in Ball-Run, 14.29% to 1514.29% in Car-Circle, and 100.00% to 19,077.78% in Car-Run compared to baselines. This indicates the effectiveness of TCRL's worst-case cost estimation in maintaining safety under temporal-coupled attacks.
 - **Reward:** Under Worst-TC attacks, TCRL achieved higher reward values than both its natural performance and the performance of all baselines. This unexpected outcome is attributed to TCRL's defense-oriented strategy, which prioritizes safer, lower-cost actions in natural environments, a strategy that becomes advantageous under severe adversarial conditions. TCRL improved reward values by 11.09% to 26.47% in Ball-Circle, 8.36% to 16.73% in Ball-Run, 14.62% to 34.76% in Car-Circle, and 24.04% to 33.58% in Car-Run compared to baselines under Worst-TC attack.

- **Robustness against Temporal-Independent Attacks:** TCRL also maintained low safety costs when subjected to MAD, MC, and Random attackers, demonstrating broader applicability beyond temporal-coupled attacks. ADV-PPOL (MC) performed well against the MC attacker as expected due to its specific training, but its performance degraded under temporal-coupled perturbations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 2)

An ablation study was conducted to determine the contribution of the two main components of TCRL: the cost constraint functions (TCRL-Worstcost) and the reward-based temporal defense constraints (TCRL-Reward).

- **Impact of Cost Constraint Functions (TCRL-Worstcost):** Removing the cost constraint function led to a notable increase in safety cost under Worst-TC attacks across all tasks (e.g., 13.70 in Ball-Circle, 26.04 in Ball-Run, 2.37 in Car-Circle, and 20.34 in Car-Run). This result indicates that the explicit penalization of unsafe behavior facilitated by the worst-case cost estimation is critical for managing safety under temporal-coupled perturbations.
- **Impact of Reward-based Temporal Defense Constraints (TCRL-Reward):** Omitting the reward-based temporal defense constraints resulted in a significant reduction in reward values (e.g., 121.12 in Ball-Circle, 89.38 in Ball-Run, 51.46 in Car-Circle, and 154.96 in Car-Run). This degradation highlights the importance of the autocorrelation and entropy stability constraints in mitigating reward-targeted temporal-coupled attacks and enabling the agent to maintain effective task performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robust constrained reinforcement learning by specifically addressing the critical, yet often overlooked, challenge of temporal-coupled adversarial perturbations. The development of TCRL signifies a step towards more realistic and reliable deployment of RL agents in safety-critical domains.

The proposed worst-case-perceived cost constraint function offers a method to implicitly estimate safety costs under complex temporal attacks, thereby enabling robust constraint enforcement without requiring explicit adversarial modeling. This approach can enhance the practical applicability of robust CRL by making it more resilient to unknown or adaptive adversaries.

Furthermore, the introduction of a dual-constraint reward-based defense mechanism, incorporating both autocorrelation and entropy stability, provides a method to counteract the cumulative degradation of rewards induced by temporal-coupled attacks. By disrupting adversarial patterns and preserving reward unpredictability, TCRL contributes to maintaining both safety and task performance simultaneously in hostile environments.

The demonstrated superior performance of TCRL across various robotic control tasks, showing significantly reduced safety costs and improved rewards under temporal-coupled attacks, indicates its potential to enhance the trustworthiness and deployability of RL systems. This work could impact the design of control systems for autonomous vehicles, robotics, and critical infrastructure, where the consequences of safety violations are severe and environmental disturbances can be dynamic and correlated over time. The framework provides a foundation for future research into more sophisticated temporal adversarial models and defense strategies in sequential decision-making problems.
