# PhysVideoGenerator: Towards Physically Aware Video Generation via Latent Physics Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysVideoGenerator introduces a framework that conditions a video generator on learnable physical priors, leveraging predictive signals from V-JEPA 2 representations. The work demonstrates the technical feasibility and training stability of this approach, showing that diffusion latents can recover high-level physical dynamics and that a multi-task objective can be optimized without destabilizing the generative process.

## Problem

- Current video generation models often struggle with physical consistency, leading to unrealistic object interactions, inconsistent gravity, or temporal flickering.
- Existing solutions rely either on brute-force data scaling (resource-intensive) or rigid physics simulators (difficult to generalize), lacking a tractable and universally generalizable approach.
- There is a recognized gap between purely aesthetic video generation and the need for physically consistent, temporally coherent content that captures underlying physical dynamics.

## Method

- A novel architecture, PhysVideoGenerator, integrates a 'physics predictor' network (PredictorP) into a DiT-based video generator (Latte-1) to infer high-level physical features (V-JEPA 2 embeddings) from noisy diffusion latents.
- The generator's Temporal Transformer Blocks are modified with a Physics-Cross-Attention layer, allowing temporal hidden states to attend to the predicted physics tokens, thereby injecting physical consistency.
- The model is trained using a joint optimization objective that combines the standard diffusion loss with an L2 physics prediction loss, balancing generative quality and physical plausibility.

## Results

- Successfully demonstrated the training stability and learnability of the joint optimization, with the physics prediction loss consistently decreasing and converging, often faster than the diffusion loss in early stages.
- The PhysVideoGenerator architecture and its joint training pipeline were implemented effectively, successfully managing high-dimensional V-JEPA 2 tokens and memory constraints through gradient checkpointing and mixed precision.
- The approach proved tractable on a single 40GB NVIDIA A100 GPU, showcasing its accessibility for initial experimentation and proof-of-concept validation within typical academic compute budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Noisy diffusion latents contain sufficient inherent information to recover high-level physical dynamics and representations, as demonstrated by the PredictorP's successful learning of V-JEPA 2 embeddings.
> - The proposed multi-task optimization, blending generative and predictive objectives, is stable and viable, with both diffusion and physics prediction losses converging without destabilizing each other.
> - Integrating learnable physics priors from predictive world models like V-JEPA 2 into latent video diffusion models offers a scalable and technically feasible direction for achieving physically aware video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the rise of diffusion models and transformer-based architectures. Models like the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) and its video extension, Latte, have demonstrated impressive capabilities in generating high-quality, aesthetically pleasing videos from text prompts. However, despite their visual fidelity, these models primarily learn statistical patterns from large datasets, often implicitly, and frequently struggle to capture the underlying physical dynamics of the real world. This limitation manifests as artifacts such as unnatural object interactions, inconsistent gravity, or temporal flickering, where objects might appear or disappear implausibly or move in ways that violate basic physics.

This paper addresses a critical gap between purely aesthetic video generation and the need for physically consistent, temporally coherent content. Existing solutions to enforce physical awareness generally fall into two categories:
1. **Implicit Scaling:** Relying on increasingly massive datasets and model capacities, hoping that physical understanding will emerge implicitly from sheer data volume. This approach is resource-intensive and doesn't guarantee robust physical reasoning across diverse scenarios.
2. **Explicit Simulation:** Integrating generative models with rigid physics engines or simulators. While precise, these systems are often difficult to generalize to "in-the-wild" scenes, requiring detailed scene parameterization that is impractical for general-purpose video generation.

"PhysVideoGenerator" attempts to find a middle ground by introducing a *learnable physics prior* directly into the video generation process, aiming to provide physical guidance without resorting to external simulators or relying solely on implicit scaling.

A key conceptual inspiration for this work comes from **Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)**. Pioneered by researchers including Yann LeCun (also at NYU), JEPAs are a self-supervised learning framework designed to learn abstract, predictive representations by forecasting future latent embeddings. Unlike traditional methods that reconstruct raw pixels, JEPAs focus on high-level semantic and dynamic structure, making their learned representations potentially suitable for capturing world dynamics. V-JEPA 2, a video-specific iteration, is explicitly optimized for predictive modeling of temporal dynamics, making it an ideal candidate for providing "physical commonsense."

Furthermore, this work builds upon the concept of "Representation Alignment," as seen in efforts like Video-REPA. This paradigm suggests that distilling features from powerful, pre-trained discriminative models (like DINO or JEPAs) into generative processes can enhance consistency. PhysVideoGenerator extends this by specifically targeting *physical alignment*. By aligning diffusion latents with V-JEPA 2 representations, the goal is to imbue the video generator with an understanding of physical laws—such as gravity, object permanence, and realistic interaction dynamics—beyond mere temporal smoothness. This positions PhysVideoGenerator as an innovative step towards developing generative models that are not only visually impressive but also genuinely "physically aware."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The fundamental motivation behind PhysVideoGenerator stems from the recognized shortcomings of current video generation models in representing real-world physics. While these models excel at producing high-quality aesthetic content, they often fail to capture the underlying physical dynamics, leading to visual inconsistencies like objects defying gravity, passing through each other, or exhibiting non-causal movements. This gap between aesthetic appeal and physical realism limits their applicability in critical domains such as cinematic production, virtual reality, and even scientific simulation, where physical plausibility is paramount.

The authors identify that existing approaches, either through brute-force scaling or rigid external simulators, offer neither a truly tractable nor a universally generalizable solution for physically grounded generation. This necessitated a novel approach to embed physical understanding more intrinsically into the generative process.

Against this backdrop, the primary objective of this work is clearly stated: **to design, implement, and validate the technical feasibility of a framework that conditions a video generator on learnable physical priors.** The authors were specifically motivated to investigate whether predictive signals from V-JEPA 2, a model trained on world modeling, could serve as an inductive bias for video diffusion models, guiding generation towards more structured and physically consistent dynamics without sacrificing expressive power.

The specific project goals and contributions outline this objective with greater granularity:
1. **Architectural Design of PhysVideoGenerator:** To propose and build a novel architecture that seamlessly integrates a "physics predictor" branch into a DiT-based video generator (Latte). This predictor is designed to interpret and regress V-JEPA 2 embeddings from noisy latent states.
2. **Implementation of Joint Training Pipeline:** To successfully engineer a training loop capable of simultaneously optimizing both the traditional diffusion loss and a novel physics prediction loss. This involved overcoming significant technical and memory management challenges.
3. **Feasibility Validation:** To empirically validate the stability and convergence of this multi-task learning objective. This specifically entailed demonstrating that diffusion latents contain sufficient information to recover V-JEPA 2 physical representations and that the joint training process remains stable.

The overarching aim is to establish a methodological and technical foundation for future large-scale evaluation of physics-aware generative models, paving the way for video generators that inherently understand and adhere to the laws of physics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysVideoGenerator's methodology centers on integrating a learnable physics prior into a latent video diffusion model, specifically built upon the Latte-1 architecture. The approach involves a multi-modal feature extraction pipeline, a novel physics predictor network, and a modified diffusion generator with physics-conditioned temporal attention, all trained via a joint optimization objective.

#### 3.1. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** OpenVid-1M, a large-scale collection of high-quality video-text pairs, was used for its diversity and visual fidelity. Videos were processed at 256x256 resolution with 16 frames.
- **Feature Extraction:** To streamline training, latent representations were pre-computed and cached for three modalities:
 - **VAE Latents:** From Latte-1's pre-trained Variational Autoencoder, videos were encoded into $4 \times 16 \times 32 \times 32$ latents.
 - **Text Embeddings:** Semantic conditioning was provided by the T5-v1.1-XXL encoder, producing $226 \times 4096$ embeddings.
 - **Physical Priors (V-JEPA 2):** The pre-trained V-JEPA 2 model was used to extract patch-level physical tokens, $2048 \times 1408$, representing spatiotemporal patches and their predictive dynamics.
- **Implementation:** The framework was implemented using PyTorch and the Diffusers library. The Latte-1 backbone was initialized with pre-trained weights.
- **Optimization Strategy:** A fine-tuning approach was employed, freezing the VAE, text encoder, and original spatial transformer blocks. Only the newly introduced PredictorP module (a 4-layer lightweight Transformer) and the temporal cross-attention layers were trained. Optimization used AdamW, with gradient checkpointing and mixed precision (BF16) to manage memory on a single NVIDIA A100 (40GB) GPU.

#### 3.2. Architecture (PhysVideoGenerator)

The core architecture consists of two main components:

- **The Physics Predictor Network (PredictorP):** This auxiliary network is designed to infer high-level physical features directly from the noisy diffusion state. It operates in three stages:
 1. **Latent Encoding:** A lightweight 3D Convolutional encoder processes noisy diffusion latents ($z_t$) to capture local spatiotemporal features ($h_{vis}$).
 2. **Multi-Modal Fusion:** The encoded visual features are flattened and concatenated with text embeddings ($c_{text}$) and the diffusion timestep embedding ($t_{emb}$). A Transformer Encoder then processes this fused sequence ($h_{fused}$).
 3. **V-JEPA 2 Decoding:** A Transformer Decoder, using learnable queries, cross-attends to the fused features to generate the predicted physical tokens ($\hat{p}$), effectively regressing the target V-JEPA 2 representations.

- **Temporal Physics-Cross-Attention:** To integrate the predicted physics into the generative process, the standard Temporal Transformer Blocks of Latte are modified. A Physics-Cross-Attention layer is inserted immediately after the temporal self-attention. This layer allows the hidden states representing the temporal evolution of spatial patches ($x_{temp}$) to attend to the predicted physics tokens ($\hat{p}$). By broadcasting these physics tokens, the model ensures that the temporal dynamics of every patch are globally consistent with the inferred physical laws.

#### 3.3. Joint Training Objective

The model is trained with a multi-task objective, which is a weighted sum of two losses:
$L_{total} = ||\epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}(z_t, t, c_{text}, \hat{p})||^2 + \lambda \cdot ||\hat{p} - p_{gt}||^2$
where:
- The first term is the standard **Diffusion Loss**, aiming to predict the noise ($\epsilon$) added to the latent $z_t$.
- The second term is the **Physics Loss**, which is an L2 loss between the predicted physics tokens ($\hat{p}$) and the ground-truth V-JEPA 2 tokens ($p_{gt}$).
The weighting factor $\lambda$ (set to 0.1) balances the contribution of these two objectives, encouraging the diffusion backbone to actively leverage the physics tokens for better denoising.

#### 3.4. Inference Methodology

During inference, the process follows a standard reverse diffusion pipeline. Given a text prompt, text embeddings are computed. Starting from a Gaussian latent tensor, the model iteratively denoises it. At each step, the PredictorP network predicts physics tokens from the current noisy latent, text embeddings, and timestep embedding. These predicted physics tokens are then injected into the generator via physics cross-attention. A learned gate controls the strength of this physics conditioning. After the reverse process completes, the final latent is decoded into pixel space to generate the video. A "dry-run" mode was implemented for debugging.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The primary objective of PhysVideoGenerator was to establish the *technical feasibility* of its proposed framework. The core findings directly address this central empirical question:

- **Validation of Training Stability and Learnability:** The most significant finding is the successful demonstration that the proposed joint training paradigm is viable.
 - **Convergence of Diffusion Latents to Physical Representations:** The PredictorP network successfully learned to map noisy diffusion latents to V-JEPA 2 representations. This was evidenced by the consistent decrease of the **Physics Prediction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{phys}$)** throughout training. The fact that this loss converged, and even converged faster than the diffusion loss in early stages, indicates that diffusion latents, even when noisy, contain sufficient inherent information to recover high-level physical dynamics. This is a crucial "proof of concept" for the entire approach.
 - **Stability of Multi-Task Optimization:** The **Diffusion Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{diff}$)** followed a typical training trajectory, decreasing steadily and converging, without being destabilized by the concurrent optimization of the physics prediction loss. The simultaneous decrease of both loss components, without oscillation or divergence, confirmed that the multi-task objective is well-posed and that the physics conditioning branch does not fundamentally disrupt the diffusion learning dynamics. This is a non-trivial result, as combining generative and predictive objectives can often lead to competing gradients and unstable training.
- **Architectural Design and Implementation Success:** The authors successfully designed and implemented the novel PhysVideoGenerator architecture, integrating the physics predictor branch and the temporal physics-cross-attention mechanism into a DiT-based generator. The joint training pipeline was engineered to optimize both losses simultaneously.
- **Memory and Computational Tractability:** A significant engineering challenge, given the high dimensionality of V-JEPA 2 tokens, was GPU memory management. The authors successfully mitigated memory pressure through:
 - **Gradient Checkpointing:** Reducing peak memory consumption by approximately 40%.
 - **Mixed Precision Training (BF16):** Reducing memory footprint while maintaining numerical stability.
 - **Frozen Components:** Strategically freezing the VAE, text encoder, and spatial attention blocks to focus trainable parameters on the physics-relevant modules.
 These optimizations allowed training on a single 40GB A100 GPU, demonstrating that the approach is accessible within typical academic compute budgets for initial experimentation.

**Acknowledged Limitations:**
The authors are transparent about the limitations of their current validation:
- **No Generative Quality Evaluation:** The work explicitly states that no inference was conducted to assess whether the trained model produces *physically plausible videos*. This would require substantial additional compute and systematic evaluation, which was beyond the scope of this proof-of-concept.
- **Limited Training Scale:** Training for 50 epochs on a subset of OpenVid-1M is acknowledged as insufficient for fully training either the predictor or the adapted diffusion layers. Large-scale training would be necessary to unleash the method's full potential.
- **No Ablation Studies:** The current work lacks comparisons against baselines or ablated variants, which would be essential to quantitatively attribute any quality improvements to specific architectural choices.

In summary, the main findings confirm the fundamental technical viability of the PhysVideoGenerator concept, validating the learnability of physics representations from diffusion latents and the stability of the multi-task training process. While not yet a demonstration of superior video quality, it provides a crucial foundational step.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhysVideoGenerator project, despite its early stage as a proof-of-concept, carries significant implications for the future of video generation and our understanding of how artificial intelligence can acquire physical intuition.

**1. A Scalable Path to Physically Aware Video Generation:**
The most significant impact is establishing a **technically feasible and scalable direction** for embedding physical awareness into generative models. By demonstrating that a lightweight predictor can regress complex physical representations (V-JEPA 2 tokens) directly from noisy diffusion latents, PhysVideoGenerator circumvents the limitations of relying solely on massive implicit scaling or inflexible external physics simulators. This opens a promising avenue for models to learn and apply physical commonsense without requiring explicit, costly, or non-generalizable simulations during inference. It offers a potential sweet spot between purely data-driven statistical generation and rigidly constrained physical simulation.

**2. Bridging Generative Models and World Models:**
This work represents a concrete step towards bridging the gap between high-fidelity generative models and sophisticated predictive "world models" like JEPAs. By leveraging V-JEPA 2 as an inductive bias, the framework actively encourages the generative model to "understand" and predict temporal dynamics at a semantic and physical level, rather than just reproducing visual patterns. This integration has the potential to lead to more robust, coherent, and realistic video content, especially for longer durations and complex interactions.

**3. Validation of Latent Space Information Content:**
The finding that diffusion latents contain sufficient information to recover V-JEPA 2 physical representations is itself a significant insight. It suggests that the compressed latent spaces learned by diffusion models are richer than just aesthetic descriptors; they implicitly encode much of the underlying structure and dynamics of the real world. This could spur further research into extracting and utilizing such latent information for various downstream tasks beyond generation.

**4. Foundation for Future Research and Development:**
By validating the stability of multi-task training and outlining a comprehensive evaluation protocol (Optical Flow Consistency, T-LPIPS, VideoPhy metrics), PhysVideoGenerator lays a robust methodological and technical foundation. It provides a clear blueprint and a validated starting point for future large-scale experiments, comparative analyses, and architectural extensions. This enables the research community to build upon this work, refine the approach, and ultimately achieve video generators that produce truly physically plausible content.

**5. Potential for Real-World Applications:**
If successfully scaled and refined, the implications are vast:
- **Cinematic Production & VFX:** Generating highly realistic and physically consistent special effects or entire scenes, reducing manual labor and simulation costs.
- **Virtual Reality & Gaming:** Creating more immersive and believable virtual environments where objects behave according to real-world physics.
- **Robotics & Simulation:** Developing more accurate and realistic simulation environments for training robots, where interactions with the environment are physically grounded.
- **AI for Science:** Potentially modeling physical phenomena more accurately within generative frameworks, aiding in scientific discovery.

In conclusion, PhysVideoGenerator, as a proof-of-concept, successfully demonstrates that embedding learnable physics priors from predictive world models into latent video diffusion models is a viable and stable approach. While further work is needed to fully assess its impact on generative quality, it offers a compelling vision for a new generation of video synthesis models that not only look good but also "make sense" physically, paving the way for more intelligent and realistic AI-generated content.
