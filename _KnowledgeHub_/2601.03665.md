# PhysVideoGenerator: Towards Physically Aware Video Generation via Latent Physics Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysVideoGenerator introduces a framework that conditions a video generator on learnable physical priors, leveraging predictive signals from V-JEPA 2 representations. The work demonstrates the technical feasibility and training stability of this approach, showing that diffusion latents can recover high-level physical dynamics and that a multi-task objective can be optimized without destabilizing the generative process.

## Problem

- Current video generation models often struggle with physical consistency, leading to unrealistic object interactions, inconsistent gravity, or temporal flickering.
- Existing solutions rely either on brute-force data scaling (resource-intensive) or rigid physics simulators (difficult to generalize), lacking a tractable and universally generalizable approach.
- There is a recognized gap between purely aesthetic video generation and the need for physically consistent, temporally coherent content that captures underlying physical dynamics.

## Method

- A novel architecture, PhysVideoGenerator, integrates a 'physics predictor' network (PredictorP) into a DiT-based video generator (Latte-1) to infer high-level physical features (V-JEPA 2 embeddings) from noisy diffusion latents.
- The generator's Temporal Transformer Blocks are modified with a Physics-Cross-Attention layer, allowing temporal hidden states to attend to the predicted physics tokens, thereby injecting physical consistency.
- The model is trained using a joint optimization objective that combines the standard diffusion loss with an L2 physics prediction loss, balancing generative quality and physical plausibility.

## Results

- Successfully demonstrated the training stability and learnability of the joint optimization, with the physics prediction loss consistently decreasing and converging, often faster than the diffusion loss in early stages.
- The PhysVideoGenerator architecture and its joint training pipeline were implemented effectively, successfully managing high-dimensional V-JEPA 2 tokens and memory constraints through gradient checkpointing and mixed precision.
- The approach proved tractable on a single 40GB NVIDIA A100 GPU, showcasing its accessibility for initial experimentation and proof-of-concept validation within typical academic compute budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Noisy diffusion latents contain sufficient inherent information to recover high-level physical dynamics and representations, as demonstrated by the PredictorP's successful learning of V-JEPA 2 embeddings.
> - The proposed multi-task optimization, blending generative and predictive objectives, is stable and viable, with both diffusion and physics prediction losses converging without destabilizing each other.
> - Integrating learnable physics priors from predictive world models like V-JEPA 2 into latent video diffusion models offers a scalable and technically feasible direction for achieving physically aware video generation.
