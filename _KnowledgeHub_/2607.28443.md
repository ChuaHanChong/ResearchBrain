# One Future, Every Robot: Label-Efficient Collective-State Prediction with Decentralized JEPA

> [!summary] Summary
> Collective-State JEPA (CS-JEPA), developed by ITMO University, is a recurrent joint-embedding predictive architecture that enables individual robots in a swarm to forecast a common future collective state using only local observations and bandwidth-limited communication. The approach reduces label dependency and achieves superior prediction accuracy and inter-robot agreement, demonstrating improvements in error AUC up to 0.098817 over raw-future reconstruction methods under various distribution shifts in swarm topology and size.

## Problem

- Robot swarms face the challenge of decentralized collective-state prediction, where individual agents must infer a global property from local information without a global view.
- Existing methods often lack label efficiency and struggle to generalize predictions to new swarm topologies or sizes while ensuring all robots agree on a common future collective state.
- Developing a decentralized predictor that allows all robots to produce accurate, common estimates of a *future* collective state, distinct from per-agent trajectory forecasts or consensus on the present, is a key challenge.

## Method

- **CS-JEPA** utilizes a recurrent ==joint-embedding predictive architecture== where each robot processes local observations and limited neighbor messages (256 bytes per edge) through a GRU to update its hidden state.
- A ==permutation-invariant set tokenizer== converts future robot embeddings into a fixed 17-token latent representation, serving as a size-invariant, common future collective state target for all robots.
- Self-supervised pretraining employs an L2 loss between the receiver's local prediction and a stopped-gradient target, augmented with a training-only ==receiver anchor== loss to aid individual robot dynamics, with the target branch and anchor removed at deployment.

## Results

- CS-JEPA consistently achieved lower prediction error AUC across all evaluation families (in-distribution, topology shifts, size shifts) for both prediction accuracy (up to **0.057156** improvement) and inter-robot agreement (up to **0.098817** improvement) compared to a reconstruction baseline.
- Action-conditioned CS-JEPA significantly reduced branch-value MSE by **45.5%** and improved within-context candidate-score Pearson correlation by **0.12907** for counterfactual value estimation, with these benefits transferring to unseen swarm sizes.
- The method maintained its prediction advantages under moderate communication faults (e.g., **20%** packet loss, one-step delay) and demonstrated positive transfer in failure-ramp warning tasks for out-of-distribution swarm configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting a common future latent target with JEPA is more label-efficient for decoding collective states than reconstructing raw future states, even under significant distribution shifts in swarm topology and size.
> - The decentralized JEPA objective allows individual robots to produce accurate and mutually consistent predictions of a shared future collective state using local information, without explicit inter-robot agreement optimization.
> - The architecture demonstrates robustness to various distribution shifts and resilience to moderate communication faults, suggesting its practicality for scalable and reliable swarm deployments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the problem of decentralized collective-state prediction in robot swarms, a domain where individual agents must infer a global property from local information. This area intersects several established fields:

- **Swarm Intelligence and Multi-Agent Systems:** Prior work has focused on enabling coherent global regimes (e.g., flocking, formation) from local interactions. This includes the use of permutation-invariant encoders, invariant set functions, and graph neural networks to scale across interchangeable agents. Concepts like distributed artificial group minds and global state prediction (GSP, GSP-N) also motivate local construction of global information for policy learning or collective transport with bandwidth-limited communication. Multi-agent representation learning and world models aim to improve communication and policy learning through decentralized dynamics, centralized aggregation, or emergent communication. This study extends these efforts by specifically targeting a common *future* collective state.
- **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs):** JEPAs represent a class of self-supervised learning methods that predict latent targets rather than reconstructing raw observation details. This approach has been applied to various data modalities, including images and video, and has shown promise in population-level representation learning, V2X cooperative perception, and multi-agent trajectory prediction.

This work specifically aims to bridge a gap: whether a JEPA objective can be effectively applied to represent a *future swarm as a set*, be predicted *independently at every robot* through a fixed local message interface, *transfer to new topologies and swarm sizes*, and *reduce the number of globally labeled episodes* required for downstream tasks. It investigates a common-future JEPA target as a label-efficient primitive for decentralized swarm prediction under topology and size shifts, with additional exploration into planning-relevant value estimation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective is to enable every robot in a swarm to predict the *same future collective state* from only its local observations and bandwidth-limited messages. This is formalized as **decentralized shared-state prediction**.

The motivation stems from the inherent challenge in swarm robotics: a collective regime (e.g., flocking, fragmenting) can emerge without any single robot directly observing or possessing a global view of that regime. For practical applications, a decentralized predictor that allows all robots to produce accurate, common estimates of this future collective state is considered valuable. This differs from per-agent trajectory forecasts, centralized critic states, or consensus estimates of the present.

The specific objectives addressed by the proposed **Collective-State JEPA (CS-JEPA)** are:

1. **Formulate a Precise Endpoint and Deployment Contract:** Define the exact requirements for per-robot prediction of one shared future state, including constraints on communication (one message round, 256 bytes per directed edge per step), local history (16 frames), and runtime limitations (no global pooling, target encoder, episode clock, or recorded future actions).
2. **Develop a Size-Invariant JEPA Target and Recurrent Receiver-Local Predictor:** Create an architecture capable of predicting a fixed-size global-plus-spatial token field that represents the collective state, while ensuring the predictor operates locally at each robot and is augmented during pretraining by a training-only receiver-future anchor.
3. **Demonstrate Label-Efficient Prediction under Distribution Shift:** Provide evidence that CS-JEPA is more label-efficient than raw-future reconstruction for various evaluation families, including in-distribution (ID), topology shifts (ring, mutual-kNN), and swarm sizes (2–6x larger than training), using frozen representations and a limited budget of globally labeled episodes. This aims to reduce the burden of centralized sensing and labeling.
4. **Evaluate Counterfactual Value Estimation:** Investigate if CS-JEPA can support more accurate four-step counterfactual value estimation compared to reconstruction-based methods when conditioned on candidate action plans, including transfer to unseen swarm sizes. This explores the utility of the learned representations for planning-relevant information.
5. **Assess Robustness to Communication Faults:** Examine the preservation of prediction advantages under isolated moderate message faults (e.g., packet loss, delay).
6. **Evaluate Transfer to Downstream Warning Tasks:** Determine if the frozen representations can transfer effectively to tasks such as failure-ramp warning.

The overall motivation is to establish common-future JEPA targets as a label-efficient primitive for decentralized swarm prediction, supporting robust and scalable inference capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **Collective-State JEPA (CS-JEPA)**, a recurrent joint-embedding predictive architecture designed for decentralized shared-state prediction in robot swarms.

**Problem Formulation:**
At each time step *t*, robot *i* observes a local view *o<sub>i,t</sub>* and receives messages from its neighbors *N<sub>t</sub>(i)*. The allowed information *I<sub>i,t</sub>* comprises its 16-frame local history (*o<sub>i,t-15:t</sub>*), neighbor connectivity (*N<sub>t-15:t</sub>(i)*), and received recurrent memories from neighbors (*m<sub>j→i,τ</sub>*). Robot *i*'s local view includes normalized world position, velocity, a 2D task vector, and an active bit. Neighbor features are restricted to previous recurrent memories. The objective is for each robot *i* to produce a prediction *bZ<sub>,i,t+h</sub>* of a single permutation-invariant future target *Z<sub>t+h</sub>* (at horizons *h* = 2 or 4), where *Z<sub>t+h</sub>* is generated by a target encoder *T<sub>ξ</sub>* from the future swarm state *S<sub>t+h</sub>*. The "collective state" is defined as a learned global-plus-spatial latent target, not a concatenation of all robot states, and is audited by a downstream probe for ten physical quantities (e.g., polarization, connectivity, task score).

**Decentralization and Scaling Semantics:**
The architecture generates *|V<sub>t</sub>|* distinct outputs, with no central aggregation during inference. Robots share parameters and target semantics, but each maintains its own hidden state. Information propagates through recurrent updates, not instantaneous global access. Communication is strictly local: 256 bytes per directed edge per step, with mean aggregation to handle varying neighborhood sizes, ensuring model parameters are independent of swarm size and per-robot traffic is bounded.

**Collective-State JEPA Architecture (CS-JEPA):**

1. **Receiver-Local Recurrent Transport:**
 - A frozen local encoder maps robot *i*'s observation *o<sub>i,t</sub>* to an embedding *e<sub>i,t</sub>* ∈ ℝ<sup>64</sup>.
 - Each active robot broadcasts its previous recurrent state *h<sub>j,t-1</sub>* ∈ ℝ<sup>64</sup>.
 - Robot *i* computes a mean neighbor memory *h̄<sub>,i,t-1</sub>* from its neighbors' previous states.
 - A Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) updates robot *i*'s hidden state *h<sub>i,t</sub>* using its local embedding *e<sub>i,t</sub>*, the mean neighbor memory *h̄<sub>,i,t-1</sub>*, and its previous hidden state *h<sub>i,t-1</sub>*.
 - Updates are synchronous, one message round per environment step, with a 15-step warm-up for the 16-frame context.

2. **One Variable-Size Future Target:**
 - A frozen target encoder embeds every active future robot.
 - A permutation-invariant set tokenizer converts these embeddings into a fixed 17-token representation (one global token, and a 4x4 spatial field). Spatial tokens are formed using Gaussian weights based on anchor positions.
 - This process produces a fixed 1105-dimensional target *Z<sub>t+h</sub>* regardless of swarm size.
 - A role-conditioned predictor recursively forecasts *t+2* then *t+4*, with gradients stopped through the first prediction.

3. **JEPA Loss and Receiver Anchor:**
 - The primary self-supervised loss (*L<sub>CS</sub>*) is an L2 loss between the receiver's prediction *bZ<sub>,i,t+h</sub>* and the *stopped-gradient* target *Z<sub>t+h</sub>*, normalized by the target dimension. This loss is computed independently for each robot, without explicitly optimizing for inter-robot agreement.
 - To aid individual robot dynamics, a training-only MLP (receiver anchor) predicts the receiver's *own* frozen future embedding *e<sub>b,i,t+h</sub>*. This anchor loss (*L<sub>anchor</sub>*) is also an L2 loss.
 - The total pretraining objective is *L<sub>CS-JEPA</sub> = L<sub>CS</sub> + λL<sub>anchor</sub>*, with *λ = 2*. The anchor head is removed at deployment.

**Matched Reconstruction Baseline (Future-Recon):**
This baseline shares the same frozen encoder, recurrent messages, role-conditioned predictor, 1105-D bottleneck, optimization schedule, and receiver anchor as CS-JEPA. The key difference is that Future-Recon's training-only decoder reconstructs a *raw global-plus-spatial field* of future position, velocity, and task vector features, rather than predicting the *frozen latent field* of CS-JEPA. Future-Recon has slightly more trainable parameters (9,607 additional) due to its raw decoder.

**Training-to-Deployment Separation:**
During pretraining, the online branch (local encoder, recurrent predictor) is unrolled using local information, while the target branch (target encoder, tokenizer) computes *Z<sub>t+h</sub>* once from privileged future swarm state. This privileged information is *not* injected into the online branch. At deployment, the target encoder, tokenizer, reconstruction decoder, and anchor head are discarded. The local encoder and recurrent predictor are frozen, and a linear ridge probe is fitted on the frozen predictions to decode collective variables.

**Experimental Protocol:**

- **Data:** A new, prospectively registered data family was generated, including three tasks (flocking, formation, coverage). Training and in-distribution (ID) tests used bounded-small-world graphs (N=10, 18). Out-of-distribution (OOD) tests included topology shifts (ring, mutual-kNN, N=10, 18) and size shifts (N=36, 72, 108).
- **Training Details:** 50 epochs, Adam optimizer, cosine weight decay, gradient clipping. A shared, frozen stage-0 encoder (itself a mean-target CS-JEPA encoder from earlier work) was used.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Label Efficiency:** Ridge probes (α = 10<sup>-3</sup>) were trained on 6, 12, or 24 globally labeled episodes.
 - **Accuracy:** Normalized Mean Squared Error (MSE) between decoded robot predictions and the true collective target.
 - **Agreement:** MSE between decoded robot predictions and their within-episode mean.
 - The primary endpoint was the log-budget-span-normalized trapezoidal Area Under the Curve (AUC) for error. Positive ∆ (Future-Recon AUC - CS-JEPA AUC) favors CS-JEPA.
 - **Action-Conditioned Counterfactual Value Estimation:** A separate, sealed 8-seed follow-up. Predictors received candidate 4-step plans. A scalar ridge readout mapped plan-conditioned representations to a composite value function. Metrics were value MSE and within-context candidate-score Pearson correlation.
 - **Other Audits:** Earlier independent cohort evaluations included tests for transfer under isolated moderate message faults (e.g., 20% packet loss, one-step delay) and transfer to a failure-ramp warning task.
- **Statistical Rigor:** Comparisons were paired at multiple levels. For label efficiency, five independently trained seeds were used for outer uncertainty calculation with 10,000 bootstrap resamples. For value estimation, eight seeds were used with an exact two-sided paired sign-flip test.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Anchor-Matched Target Isolation (Primary Follow-up):**
The prospectively registered five-seed follow-up demonstrated consistent performance advantages for CS-JEPA over Future-Recon across all evaluation families and label budgets.

- **Prediction Accuracy:** CS-JEPA achieved lower prediction error AUC across all four splits: ID, Ring OOD, Mutual-kNN, and Size OOD. The smallest accuracy improvement (∆ = 0.004636) was on ID, while the largest (∆ = 0.057156) was on Ring OOD. All five outer seeds consistently favored CS-JEPA for accuracy, with 95% confidence intervals entirely above zero.
- **Inter-Robot Agreement:** CS-JEPA also exhibited lower inter-robot agreement error AUC across all four splits. Improvements ranged from ∆ = 0.001342 (ID) to ∆ = 0.098817 (Ring OOD). All five outer seeds consistently favored CS-JEPA for agreement, with 95% confidence intervals entirely above zero.

These results indicate that predicting a common future latent target is more label-efficient than reconstructing the raw future field, both in terms of individual robot prediction accuracy and the consistency of predictions across robots, even for tasks beyond the training distribution. The simultaneous improvement in both metrics suggests that CS-JEPA does not achieve agreement through trivial output collapse.

**B. Action-Conditioned Counterfactual Value Estimation (Sealed Follow-up):**
A separate eight-seed study investigated the utility of CS-JEPA representations for planning.

- **Value MSE Reduction:** Action-conditioned CS-JEPA significantly reduced branch-value MSE by 45.5% (from 0.02847 to 0.01553) compared to Future-Recon. This effect was favorable in 8/8 outer seeds (p = 0.0078125).
- **Pearson Correlation Improvement:** CS-JEPA improved within-context candidate-score Pearson correlation by 0.12907 (from 0.35042 to 0.47949), also favorable in 8/8 seeds (p = 0.0078125).
- **Transfer to Unseen Size:** These improvements were consistent across various swarm sizes, including an unseen N=32.

While CS-JEPA improved value estimation accuracy and correlation, it did not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in selected-plan regret or a confirmed closed-loop connectivity advantage over Future-Recon in separate tests.

**C. Transfer Under Isolated Message Faults (Earlier-Cohort Audit):**
Earlier independent audits showed that CS-JEPA maintained its prediction advantage under moderate communication imperfections:

- **Nominal, Packet Loss (20%), and Delay (one step):** CS-JEPA showed positive effects (lower error) for both prediction accuracy and inter-robot agreement under these conditions, with all five outer seeds favoring CS-JEPA.
- **Degradation:** Performance decreased significantly under more severe conditions like 40% packet loss or "local only" (no communication), where Future-Recon often performed better.

**D. Transfer to Failure-Ramp Warning (Earlier-Cohort Audit):**
An audit on an earlier cohort for a failure-ramp warning task demonstrated positive transfer:

- **Average-Precision AUC:** CS-JEPA showed a positive aggregate average-precision AUC gain of 0.006495 for warning of impending failures, positive in 5/5 outer seeds.
- **Distribution Shift:** The positive effect was observed on ring, mutual-kNN, and unseen swarm sizes, although it was negative and uncertain on ID data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of decentralized swarm robotics by demonstrating a label-efficient method for collective-state prediction.

**Key Contributions to the Field:**

- **Label-Efficient Representation:** The primary finding indicates that a common future latent target, learned via JEPA, yields representations that are more label-efficient for decoding collective states than those derived from raw-future reconstruction. This holds true even under significant distribution shifts in swarm topology and size, suggesting practical benefits in reducing the cost and effort of obtaining global labels for training downstream collective-state predictors.
- **Decentralized Inference of Shared State:** The study successfully addresses the challenge of enabling every robot to independently predict the *same* future collective state using only local information and bandwidth-limited communication. The simultaneous improvement in prediction accuracy and inter-robot agreement, without an explicit agreement loss, suggests that the JEPA objective effectively organizes distinct local evidence into mutually compatible and accurate predictions.
- **Scalability and Robustness:** The architectural design (mean aggregation, set tokenizer) supports size-invariant parameter counts and bounded per-robot communication. Empirical results confirm the transferability of frozen representations to swarms up to six times larger than those used in training. Additionally, the demonstrated resilience to moderate message faults (e.g., 20% packet loss, one-step delay) indicates a degree of practical robustness in communication-constrained environments.
- **Relevance for Planning:** The evidence that CS-JEPA's action-conditioned representations can more accurately estimate counterfactual outcome values provides a basis for its potential use in decentralized planning contexts. This suggests that the learned representations capture planning-relevant information that could aid robots in making informed decisions about future actions.

**Potential Impact:**

The development of CS-JEPA could serve as a foundational primitive for various decentralized swarm applications. By providing a label-efficient means for robots to understand their shared future, it could:

- **Enable Advanced Collective Behaviors:** Facilitate the development of more sophisticated swarm control strategies that rely on a common understanding of the collective state, such as advanced flocking, formation control, or cooperative manipulation.
- **Improve Situational Awareness:** Provide swarm members with a shared, forward-looking awareness of the collective situation, which could be critical for tasks requiring coordinated responses or proactive decision-making.
- **Reduce Operational Costs:** Lower the dependency on extensive global labeling, making it more feasible to deploy and adapt swarm systems in real-world scenarios where centralized observation and annotation are difficult or costly.
- **Enhance Fault Tolerance:** The observed robustness to communication imperfections could contribute to more reliable swarm operations in noisy or unreliable wireless environments.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**
The work is currently based on simulator-only experiments and assumes shared normalized coordinates and noiseless observations. The use of privileged future swarm state during pretraining is a developmental simplification. The reported results are specific to the registered agent counts and graph families, and the study does not claim arbitrary-size scalability. Direct comparisons to full policy systems (like GSP/GSP-N) or other multi-agent world models under their native tasks were not conducted. Furthermore, while value estimation improved, this did not translate to confirmed superiority in closed-loop control, and aspects like safety, onboard latency, and hardware transfer remain open areas for future investigation.
