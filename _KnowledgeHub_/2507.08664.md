# Introspection of Thought Helps AI Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The Introspection of Thought (INoT) framework introduces an "LLM-Read code" called PromptCode, embedding structured iterative reasoning and multi-agent debate directly within the prompt. This approach enables AI agents to perform complex introspection, yielding an average 7.95% performance improvement over baselines while reducing token costs by 58.3% across diverse language and multimodal tasks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) inherently struggle with complex reasoning tasks that require deep planning or self-correction.
- Existing iterative reasoning frameworks incur high computational costs and inefficiency from multiple external interactions with the LLM, leading to substantial token consumption.
- Natural language prompts can be ambiguous, leading to LLM misinterpretations and a lack of universal applicability across different task types and modalities.

## Method

- The ==Introspection of Thought (INoT)== framework proposes =="PromptCode,"== an LLM-readable, XML-structured code that embeds complete reasoning logic directly within the prompt.
- PromptCode combines ==Python-like syntax with natural language== to provide precise, unambiguous instructions for LLM introspection and decision-making.
- A ==virtual multi-agent debate mechanism==, simulated internally within the LLM using PromptCode, enables iterative reflection, critique, and self-correction without external orchestration.

## Results

- The INoT framework achieved an average performance improvement of **7.95%** across six diverse benchmarks, outperforming the best baseline method by **6.41** percentage points.
- INoT significantly reduced token costs by an average of **58.3%** compared to the best-performing baseline methods, demonstrating superior operational efficiency.
- The framework consistently enhanced performance in tasks including Code (HumanEval **95.9%**, MBPP **84.3%**), Math (MATH **53.4%**, GSM8K **93.3%**), QA (HotpotQA **72.2%**, SQuAD **88.8%**), and Image QA (e.g., **6.0%** gain on LLaVA-Bench In-the-Wild).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding programmatic reasoning logic directly into the prompt allows LLMs to perform complex, iterative introspection and multi-agent debates internally, significantly reducing the need for external orchestrators.
> - A hybrid "PromptCode" that integrates Python syntax with natural language offers a precise yet flexible method for guiding LLM reasoning and decision-making.
> - Structuring prompts with XML and including an Image Augment Module for MLLMs standardizes visual analysis, leading to more robust and accurate multimodal understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper situates itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), AI Agents, and advanced Prompt Engineering. The broader landscape is characterized by the immense capabilities of modern LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, DeepSeek-V2.5, LLaVA) in interpretation and inference tasks across text and increasingly, images. However, a crucial challenge remains: these powerful models, despite their scale, still exhibit inherent limitations in handling highly complex reasoning tasks on their own.

To address these limitations, the concept of **AI Agents** has emerged. AI Agents leverage LLMs as their core cognitive engine, augmenting them with external reasoning frameworks, tools, and sophisticated prompt engineering techniques to achieve greater autonomy and tackle more intricate problems without requiring post-training or fine-tuning of the underlying LLM.

**Prompt Engineering** has become a pivotal technique in this domain. Initial methods like "Input-Output" (IO) were simple, but quickly evolved to more complex strategies like "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT), "Self-Consistent Chain-of-Thought" (SCOTT), "Logical Chain-of-Thought" (LogiCoT), and "Tree-of-Thought" (ToT). These methods aim to guide the LLM through a series of logical steps, mimicking human-like reasoning processes. More recently, "Iteration of Thought" (IoT) introduced multi-agent collaboration, while "Program of Thoughts" (PoT) and "Program-driven Self-Correction" (ProgCo) integrated code execution, albeit externally, to enhance numerical and logical reasoning. For multimodal tasks, "Image-of-Thought" (IoHT) specifically guides MLLMs through visual inference.

The current work, "Introspection of Thought (INoT)," specifically targets two major pain points in this landscape:
1. **Inefficiency and High Cost of External Iterative Reasoning:** Existing iterative frameworks (like CoT, IoT) require multiple external interactions with the LLM. Each interaction (inputting partial reasoning, receiving output, reflecting, then re-inputting) consumes significant computational resources in terms of "tokens." This makes complex, iterative reasoning expensive and slow.
2. **Lack of Generality and Rigor in Prompting:** Natural language prompts, while flexible, can be ambiguous, leading to LLMs misinterpreting instructions or failing to follow a precise reasoning path. Furthermore, many specialized prompting methods lack universality across different task types (e.g., text vs. image tasks). External code execution (PoT, ProgCo) does not allow the LLM to truly *programmatically infer* internally.

INoT proposes a novel paradigm shift by moving the **iterative reflection, self-denial, and multi-agent debate processes *inside* the LLM itself.** Instead of having an external orchestrator guiding iterative prompts, INoT designs a unique "LLM-Read code" called PromptCode, embedded directly into the prompt. This allows the LLM to internally execute a structured reasoning logic, mimicking a debate or iterative refinement process without constant external interaction. This approach pushes the boundaries of how LLMs can be made more introspective, efficient, and versatile, directly addressing the limitations of high token costs and ambiguity inherent in purely natural language-based prompt engineering. It represents a significant step towards more autonomous and internally sophisticated AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "Introspection of Thought Helps AI Agents" paper is to develop a novel AI Agent reasoning framework that enhances the performance and efficiency of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal-LLMs (MLLMs) in complex reasoning tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations stemming from the limitations of current approaches:

1. **Overcoming Inherent LLM Limitations for Complex Tasks:** While LLMs are powerful, they often struggle with very complex tasks requiring deep reasoning, planning, or self-correction when used in a straightforward manner. The authors aim to empower LLMs to handle these tasks more effectively without requiring post-training.
2. **Addressing Ambiguity and Bias in Natural Language Prompting:** Traditional prompt engineering heavily relies on natural language instructions, which can be interpreted differently by LLMs due to linguistic nuances. This ambiguity can lead to suboptimal or unexpected reasoning paths. A key motivation is to introduce a more rigorous and less ambiguous way to guide LLM reasoning.
3. **Reducing High Inference Costs of External Iterative Reasoning Frameworks:** Existing advanced reasoning frameworks, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Iteration of Thought (IoT), and Image-of-Thought (IoHT), often involve multiple iterative interactions between the AI Agent (or an external orchestrator) and the LLM. Each input and output exchange consumes "tokens," leading to substantial computational costs, especially for tasks requiring extensive reflection or debate. The authors are motivated to significantly reduce these token costs.
4. **Enabling Internal Self-Denial and Reflection within LLMs:** Rather than having an external system orchestrate reflection and self-correction, the authors aim to allow the LLM itself to perform these critical processes internally. This "introspection" is intended to make the LLM more autonomous and efficient in its reasoning.
5. **Achieving Generality across Text and Multimedia Tasks:** Many specialized reasoning frameworks are optimized for specific modalities (e.g., text for CoT, images for IoHT) or task types (e.g., numerical for PoT). The authors seek to develop a versatile framework that can universally enhance reasoning performance for both natural language (Math, Code, QA) and image interpretation tasks.

In summary, the core motivation is to design a more intelligent, efficient, and versatile AI Agent that can perform complex, self-reflective reasoning processes directly within the LLM, thereby improving performance and drastically cutting down on operational costs associated with iterative external interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose the **Introspection of Thought (INoT)** framework, which fundamentally redefines how AI Agents guide LLMs to perform complex reasoning. The core of INoT lies in designing a novel "LLM-Read code" called **PromptCode** and embedding this complete reasoning logic directly into the prompt. This enables the LLM to execute programmatic dialogue reasoning processes internally, fostering self-denial and reflection within the model itself.

The INoT prompt is structured using **XML**, chosen for its hierarchical and self-descriptive nature. This structure is intended to improve the LLM's parsing efficiency and reduce misunderstandings compared to less structured natural language prompts. The INoT prompt comprises three main modules:

1. **PromptCode Definition Module:**
- **Purpose:** This module acts as a "manual" for the LLM, teaching it how to understand and interpret PromptCode. It defines PromptCode as a structured reasoning code that explicitly outlines logical steps.
- **Design:** PromptCode is a hybrid language, blending elements of **Python programming language** with **natural language**. Python's concise syntax, clear structure, and rich semantic information (variable/function names) provide precise logical instructions, while natural language offers flexibility for conveying high-level task background, goals, or abstract concepts (e.g., "scalability," "robustness") that are hard to encode directly in code. This integration ensures both precision and interpretability for the LLM.
- **Rule Section:** Within this module, a "Rule" section explicitly introduces all other modules in the prompt. This pre-defines a clear reasoning framework and execution specifications, guiding the LLM to follow specific logical structures for reasoning and decision-making, thereby improving accuracy, consistency, and controllability.

2. **Image Augment Module:**
- **Purpose:** This optional module is specifically designed to guide Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) in tasks involving images, ensuring a systematic and thorough visual analysis.
- **Process:** When an image is present, the module instructs the MLLM to:
 - **Basic Visual Understanding:** Identify key elements (people, objects), colors, shapes, patterns, axes, legends, spatial relationships, and embedded text.
 - **Advanced Visual Analysis:** Observe lighting, shadows, reflections to infer environmental factors; analyze textures, materials, and movement cues.
 - **Context Awareness:** Integrate accompanying text, metadata, and existing knowledge to refine understanding and recognize symbolic or cultural references.
 - **Inference and Verification:** Cross-check details, avoid arbitrary assumptions, express uncertainty when information is unclear, and maintain logical consistency between visual interpretation and the task.
- **Rationale:** This structured approach standardizes image processing, enhances the depth of visual understanding, and leads to more reliable reasoning results in multimedia tasks.

3. **Reasoning Module (Reasoning Logic):**
- **Core Mechanism:** This module orchestrates a **virtual multi-agent debate mechanism** directly within the LLM, encoded using PromptCode. It simulates the iterative reflection and refinement process that typically occurs externally.
- **Process:**
 - **Initialization:** Two independent agents, `Agent_A` and `Agent_B`, are initialized to reason independently on the given task. Each agent produces an initial result and thought process.
 - **Iterative Debate Loop:** The debate proceeds for a maximum of 10 rounds or until an agreement is reached.
 - **Debate Phases (within each round):**
 - **Argument Presentation:** Both `Agent_A` and `Agent_B` present their initial arguments.
 - **Critique Phase:** `Agent_A` critiques `Agent_B`'s reasoning, and `Agent_B` critiques `Agent_A`'s reasoning.
 - **Rebuttal Phase:** Agents respond to the critiques received from each other.
 - **Adjustment Phase:** Agents refine their initial results and thought processes based on the rebuttals.
 - **Agreement Check:** After each round, the framework checks if `Agent_A`'s and `Agent_B`'s refined results converge. If they agree, the final result (without additional explanation) is outputted. Otherwise, the debate continues.
- **Rationale:** By embedding this structured, iterative debate logic directly into the prompt via PromptCode, INoT eliminates the need for repeated external interactions with the LLM. This significantly reduces token costs while maintaining or enhancing the rigor of reasoning through internal self-correction and refinement. The LLM is guided to "think like" both agents, performing the introspection itself.

In essence, INoT's methodology shifts the paradigm of AI Agent reasoning from external orchestration to internal programmatic control, leveraging a hybrid code-natural language prompt to achieve more efficient, rigorous, and versatile performance across diverse tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the significant effectiveness and efficiency of the Introspection of Thought (INoT) framework across various tasks and modalities.

1. **Overall Performance Superiority:**
- INoT achieved an average performance improvement of **7.95%** over seven selected baseline reasoning frameworks (IO, CoT, SCCOT, LogiCoT, ToT, GIoT, AIoT) across six benchmarks spanning Math, Code, and QA tasks.
- Specifically, INoT outperformed the best-performing baseline method, Tree-of-Thought (ToT), by **6.41 percentage points** in overall average performance.

2. **Task-Specific Performance Gains:**
- **Code Tasks:** INoT showed substantial improvements, achieving a pass@1 score of **95.9%** on HumanEval (compared to 90.6% for the best baseline) and **84.3%** on MBPP (compared to 74.2% for the best baseline).
- **Math Tasks:** The framework demonstrated strong capabilities, with a solve rate of **53.4%** on MATH (vs. 50.2%) and **93.3%** on GSM8K (vs. 91.2%).
- **QA Tasks:** INoT achieved F1 scores of **72.2%** on HotpotQA (vs. 66.5%) and **88.8%** on SQuAD (vs. 86.7%). These consistent gains across diverse domains validate INoT's ability to enhance reasoning and problem-solving.

3. **Significant Token Cost Reduction:**
- One of INoT's most impactful findings is its efficiency in token usage. The token cost of INoT was, on average, **58.3% lower** than the best-performing baseline method.
- The Pareto chart analysis vividly illustrates that INoT achieves the highest performance scores while maintaining the lowest token cost among all compared methods, striking an optimal balance between resource efficiency and task effectiveness.

4. **Model-Agnostic Nature:**
- Experiments using INoT with different underlying LLMs (DeepSeek-V2.5, DeepSeek-V2, Claude-3.5-sonnet, Gemma 2, Qwen2.5-coder, Llama3.2) showed that performance variation did not exceed **5%**. This indicates that INoT is a robust and stable framework, whose effectiveness is not overly reliant on the specific choice of the core LLM, highlighting its broad applicability.

5. **Multimedia Versatility and Image Augment Module Validation:**
- INoT demonstrated excellent versatility in multimedia inference, outperforming baselines on three image QA benchmarks: ScienceQA-IMG, LLaVA-Bench (COCO), and LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild).
- Average accuracy improvements were approximately **2.7%** on SQA-IMG, **4.5%** on LLaVA-Bench (COCO), and a substantial **6.0%** on LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild) compared to IO and CoT.
- An ablation study confirmed the utility of the **Image Augment Module (IAM)**. INoT *without* IAM consistently showed lower accuracy across all image QA benchmarks (e.g., 1.3% to 2.6% lower on LLaVA benchmarks), proving that the structured visual analysis guidance provided by IAM is crucial for optimal multimodal performance.

6. **Ablation Study on PromptCode:**
- An ablation experiment specifically removing the custom PromptCode logic from the code-integrated prompt resulted in a discernible decline in INoT's performance. This finding directly validates the hypothesis that the programmatic guidance embedded within PromptCode, specifying the reasoning logic, plays a significant role in achieving optimal LLM performance.

In summary, the results convincingly establish INoT as a superior reasoning framework for AI Agents, offering substantial improvements in performance across diverse tasks and modalities while dramatically reducing computational costs. Its model-agnostic nature and proven versatility make it a compelling advancement in LLM-powered AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Introspection of Thought Helps AI Agents" paper presents a highly significant advancement in the field of AI Agents and Large Language Models, with profound potential impacts on how these systems are designed, deployed, and utilized.

1. **Paradigm Shift in LLM Reasoning:** The most significant contribution of INoT is its novel paradigm for enabling **internal introspection, self-denial, and multi-agent debate within the LLM itself.** Previous iterative reasoning frameworks largely operated *externally*, treating the LLM as a black box that responds to sequences of prompts. By integrating programmatic reasoning logic directly into the prompt via PromptCode, INoT transforms the LLM into a more introspective and internally self-correcting agent. This could pave the way for a new generation of AI systems that are inherently more autonomous and robust.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Cost-Effectiveness:** The dramatic reduction in token cost (58.3% lower than baselines) is a game-changer. Token usage directly translates to computational resources and monetary cost, especially with proprietary LLMs. By minimizing external interactions and performing iterative refinement internally, INoT makes complex reasoning tasks significantly more affordable and faster. This has immense practical implications for large-scale deployments of AI agents in various industries, from customer service and education to scientific research and software development, where efficiency is paramount.

3. **Improved Performance and Reliability:** Consistently outperforming state-of-the-art prompt engineering techniques across diverse benchmarks (Math, Code, QA, Image QA) demonstrates INoT's ability to elicit more accurate and reliable reasoning from LLMs. This improved performance, coupled with the rigor introduced by programmatic guidance, leads to more trustworthy AI outputs, which is crucial for high-stakes applications.

4. **Increased Generality and Versatility:** Unlike many specialized prompt engineering methods, INoT proves its versatility across both natural language and multimodal (image) tasks. The effective integration of the Image Augment Module further extends its applicability to complex visual reasoning, a growing area of AI. This generality reduces the need for task-specific framework designs, streamlining the development of comprehensive AI agents.

5. **Rigor in Prompting through "LLM-Read Code":** The concept of PromptCode—a hybrid of Python and natural language—offers a more precise and less ambiguous way to guide LLM reasoning compared to purely natural language prompts. This structured guidance minimizes misinterpretations and ensures that LLMs adhere more strictly to the intended logical flow, leading to more predictable and controllable behavior.

6. **Future Research Directions:** INoT opens up several exciting avenues for future research. Further optimization of code-integrated prompts, exploring different programmatic constructs, and enhancing the internal reasoning logic of LLMs could lead to even more sophisticated introspective agents. Applying the INoT framework to a wider range of diverse and challenging tasks (e.g., long-context understanding, scientific discovery, complex decision-making in robotics) would be a natural next step, potentially unlocking new capabilities.

In conclusion, INoT signifies a substantial leap forward in AI agent design, demonstrating that internalizing complex reasoning processes can lead to superior performance, unprecedented efficiency, and broader applicability. Its potential impact lies in making AI agents more intelligent, reliable, and economically viable for a vast array of real-world applications.
