# Non-Euclidean Motion Planning with Graphs of Geodesically-Convex Sets

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the Graphs of Geodesically-Convex Sets (GGCS) framework, extending the Graph of Convex Sets (GCS) to Riemannian manifolds, enabling optimal, collision-free motion planning for robots with non-Euclidean configuration spaces. The method transforms the manifold problem into a solvable Euclidean GCS, demonstrating its effectiveness on high-dimensional systems like a 15-DoF mobile manipulator with competitive planning times.

## Problem

- Existing GCS-Planner is restricted to Euclidean configuration spaces, limiting its application to robots with non-Euclidean components like mobile bases (SE(2)) or continuous revolute joints (T¹).
- Sampling-based planners struggle with the "curse of dimensionality" in high-DoF systems, often producing suboptimal paths or requiring excessive computation.
- Trajectory optimizers frequently converge to local minima due to the highly nonconvex nature of collision avoidance, failing to find optimal or even feasible solutions.

## Method

- Developed the Graphs of Geodesically-Convex Sets (**GGCS**) framework, defining ==motion planning== on Riemannian manifolds using strongly geodesically-convex sets and associated g-convex cost functions.
- Transformed the **GGCS** problem into an equivalent Euclidean GCS problem by assuming the robot's configuration space is a flat Riemannian manifold and constructing an atlas of local isometries.
- Utilized an extended IRIS algorithm to generate g-convex regions and formulated affine transition maps between overlapping Euclidean charts, allowing the problem to be solved via mixed-integer convex programming.

## Results

- Successfully computed collision-free, near-optimal trajectories for diverse robotic systems, including a 5-DoF planar arm and a 15-DoF PR2 bimanual mobile manipulator in obstacle-rich, non-Euclidean environments.
- Achieved planning times ranging from approximately 25 to 66 seconds for complex PR2 tasks, which are competitive with or faster than traditional trajectory optimizers while offering stronger theoretical guarantees.
- The GGCS-Planner produced trajectories that were consistently shorter or competitive compared to sampling-based methods and trajectory optimizers, effectively navigating scenarios where other approaches struggled with local minima or artificial joint limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - For flat Riemannian manifolds, optimal collision-free paths are piecewise geodesics, with each segment contained within a geodesically-convex set.
> - A crucial theoretical equivalence exists: an optimal solution in the transformed Euclidean GCS problem directly corresponds to an optimal piecewise geodesic path in the original Riemannian manifold.
> - The presence of positive sectional curvature in a Riemannian manifold inherently induces nonconvexity in the distance function, validating the flatness assumption for the proposed method's guarantees.
