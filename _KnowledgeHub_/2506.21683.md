# Risk-Averse Total-Reward Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of New Hampshire developed the first model-free Q-learning algorithms for Entropic Risk Measure (ERM) and Entropic Value-at-Risk (EVaR) within the undiscounted total-reward reinforcement learning framework. These algorithms were shown to converge to optimal risk-averse policies in tabular environments, with computed EVaR values reaching near-zero difference from optimal linear programming solutions on Cliff Walking and Gambler's Ruin tasks.

## Problem

- Existing model-based risk-averse reinforcement learning methods for the total reward criterion (TRC) require complete knowledge of environment transition probabilities, limiting their use in unknown settings.
- Many real-world reinforcement learning problems inherently lack a natural discounting factor but still require accounting for risk and potential catastrophic outcomes.
- Standard Q-learning convergence proofs rely on the Bellman operator being a contraction, a property often absent in undiscounted Markov Decision Processes, making direct application challenging.

## Method

- Developed model-free Q-learning algorithms for ERM-TRC by leveraging ERM's ==dynamic consistency== and its elicitability through an exponential loss function, enabling stochastic gradient descent updates.
- Formulated a δ-optimal EVaR-TRC Q-learning algorithm by reducing the EVaR optimization problem into a sequence of ERM-TRC problems over a discretized set of risk levels.
- Proved almost sure convergence for both algorithms in undiscounted settings by demonstrating the monotonicity of the Bellman operator and enforcing boundedness on the Temporal Difference residuals.

## Results

- Optimal EVaR policies varied significantly with risk levels, with highly risk-averse policies proactively avoiding hazardous areas in the Cliff Walking domain.
- Simulations showed that more risk-averse policies (e.g., EVaR with α=**0.2**α=0.2α=**0.2**) produced return distributions with higher mean returns and a tighter positive range compared to less risk-averse policies.
- The proposed Q-learning algorithms demonstrated stable convergence across multiple runs and achieved near-zero differences from optimal linear programming solutions for EVaR on Cliff Walking and Gambler's Ruin after approximately 20,000 samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The elicitability of the Entropic Risk Measure (ERM) with an exponential loss function provides a theoretical basis for designing model-free, gradient-based Q-learning updates.
> - Convergence of Q-learning in undiscounted, non-contractive MDPs can be achieved by proving the monotonicity of the Bellman operator and ensuring the boundedness of value function iterates.
> - The dynamically inconsistent EVaR can be effectively optimized in an undiscounted setting by reformulating it as a series of solvable ERM problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of risk-averse reinforcement learning (RL), specifically addressing challenges within Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) that utilize the total reward criterion (TRC). Risk-averse RL is recognized as an important framework for high-stakes applications such as autonomous driving, robotic surgery, healthcare, and finance, where decisions must account for the variability and potential for catastrophic outcomes of returns, rather than solely maximizing expected rewards.

Traditional RL often focuses on risk-neutral objectives, typically employing discounted infinite-horizon MDPs where future rewards are exponentially devalued. While discounting is justified in some financial contexts, many real-world RL tasks, such as robotics or games, inherently lack a natural discounting factor but feature absorbing terminal states. The total reward criterion (TRC) addresses these undiscounted, infinite-horizon settings, generalizing problems like stochastic shortest and longest paths, under the assumption that the MDP is transient (any policy eventually terminates at a sink state with positive probability).

Previous work in risk-averse RL has explored various monetary risk measures like Value-at-Risk (VaR), Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR), Entropic Risk Measure (ERM), and Entropic Value-at-Risk (EVaR). Model-based algorithms exist for ERM and EVaR within the TRC framework, but these require full knowledge of transition probabilities, limiting their applicability in unknown or partially known environments. Some model-free approaches have been developed for discounted MDPs, or for specific risk measures like CVaR and VaR, where the optimal policies may be history-dependent or Markovian. Additionally, risk-sensitive Q-learning algorithms that apply nonlinear utility functions to temporal difference (TD) residuals have been proposed.

This paper addresses a specific gap by developing model-free Q-learning algorithms for the ERM-TRC and EVaR-TRC objectives. It extends the applicability of risk-averse methods to undiscounted settings where the environment model is unknown, leveraging properties of ERM such as dynamic consistency and elicitability, which are crucial for developing dynamic programming equations and model-free learning algorithms. By focusing on total rewards without discounting, this work provides tools for a class of problems distinct from those commonly addressed by discounted risk-averse RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to derive and provide convergence guarantees for model-free Q-learning algorithms that compute optimal stationary policies for total-reward objectives under the Entropic Risk Measure (ERM) and Entropic Value-at-Risk (EVaR). This objective is motivated by several challenges and a need for practical solutions in high-stakes, undiscounted environments:

1. **Addressing Model-Free Learning for Risk-Averse Total Rewards:** A significant challenge in risk-averse RL is that computing the risk of random returns necessitates evaluating the full distribution of returns, rather than just their expectation, as is common in risk-neutral settings. Existing model-based algorithms for ERM-TRC and EVaR-TRC are effective but rely on complete knowledge of transition probabilities, which is often unavailable in practical RL scenarios. The motivation is to overcome this dependency by developing model-free methods that can directly learn from samples.

2. **Overcoming the Lack of Contraction in Undiscounted MDPs:** Standard Q-learning and many model-free methods rely on the Bellman operator being a contraction mapping, which guarantees convergence in discounted MDPs. In the context of undiscounted Total Reward Criterion (TRC), the risk-averse Bellman operator may not possess this contraction property. This absence precludes the direct application of many standard model-free techniques. The authors are motivated to develop algorithms that can prove convergence despite this, by relying on other properties like monotonicity and additional boundedness conditions.

3. **Leveraging ERM Properties for Algorithmic Design:** The dynamic consistency of ERM is a key property that allows for the existence of dynamic programming equations and stationary optimal policies, similar to expectation-based objectives. Furthermore, ERM's elicitability property (meaning it can be estimated by minimizing a specific loss function through regression) is leveraged as a foundation for designing a stochastic gradient descent-based Q-learning algorithm. This provides a theoretical pathway to estimate ERM directly from samples.

4. **Handling EVaR in an Undiscounted Setting:** While EVaR is a coherent risk measure that addresses certain shortcomings of ERM and closely approximates quantile-based measures, it is not dynamically consistent in its raw form. The motivation here is to adapt the methodology, as done in prior discounted and undiscounted work, by reducing the EVaR optimization problem to a sequence of ERM problems over a discretized set of risk levels, thereby enabling the use of the ERM Q-learning algorithm.

5. **Applicability to Undiscounted Real-World Problems:** Many real-world problems in robotics, control, and specific game scenarios are best modeled without discounting, but require careful management of risk. The development of model-free algorithms for TRC under risk aversion directly addresses the need for robust decision-making in these domains, where uncertainty and the potential for severe outcomes are critical considerations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on extending Q-learning to the risk-averse, undiscounted Total Reward Criterion (TRC) setting for Entropic Risk Measure (ERM) and Entropic Value-at-Risk (EVaR). The approach involves several key steps:

1. **MDP Formulation and Assumptions:** The decision process is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) $(S, A, p, r, \mu)$ including a finite set of states $S$ (with a sink state $e$), finite actions $A$, transition probabilities $p$, rewards $r$, and an initial state distribution $\mu$. A crucial assumption is that the MDP is transient for any policy, ensuring that the agent eventually reaches the sink state. This property is necessary for the total reward to be well-defined, although additional conditions on the risk level $\beta$ are required for ERM to guarantee finite returns.

2. **Defining Risk-Averse Objectives (ERM and EVaR):**
 - **ERM (Entropic Risk Measure):** For a risk level $\beta > 0$, ERM is defined as $\text{ERM}_\beta[\tilde{x}] := -\beta^{-1} \cdot \log E[\exp(-\beta \cdot \tilde{x})]$. ERM is known for its dynamic consistency and elicitability, which are fundamental to the proposed algorithms.
 - **EVaR (Entropic Value-at-Risk):** For a risk level $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, EVaR is defined as $\text{EVaR}_\alpha[\tilde{x}] := \sup_{\beta > 0} (\text{ERM}_\beta[\tilde{x}] + \beta^{-1} \log \alpha)$. EVaR is a coherent risk measure and closely approximates VaR and CVaR.

3. **ERM Q-learning Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**
 - **Bellman Operator Definition:** The ERM risk-averse state-action value function $q^\star(s,a,\beta)$ is defined as a fixed point of the ERM Bellman operator $B_\beta$, where $(B_\beta q)(s, a) := \text{ERM}_{a,s, \beta}[r(s, a, \tilde{s}_1) + \max_{a'} q(\tilde{s}_1, a', \beta)]$.
 - **Elicitability and Loss Function:** To enable model-free learning, the concept of *elicitability* is leveraged. ERM is elicitable using the exponential loss function $\ell_\beta(z) := \beta^{-1}(\exp(-\beta \cdot z)-1)+z$. This property allows the optimal ERM value to be found by minimizing the expected loss. A new Bellman operator $\hat{B}_\beta$ is defined using this loss function: $(\hat{B}_\beta q)(s, a) := \arg \min_{y \in R} E_{a,s}[\ell_\beta(r(s, a, \tilde{s}_1) + \max_{a'} q(\tilde{s}_1, a', \beta) - y)]$. It is proven that $B_\beta q = \hat{B}_\beta q$, meaning their fixed points coincide.
 - **Q-learning Update Rule:** Algorithm 1 adapts the standard Q-learning update to a stochastic gradient descent approach. For a given state-action pair $(s,a)$ and risk level $\beta$, the estimate $\tilde{q}_i(s,a,\beta)$ is updated based on a sample transition $(s, a, s')$ and the Temporal Difference (TD) residual $\tilde{z}_i(\beta) = r(s, a, s') + \max_{a'} \tilde{q}_i(s', a', \beta) - \tilde{q}_i(s,a,\beta)$. The update rule is $\tilde{q}_{i+1}(s,a,\beta) \leftarrow \tilde{q}_i(s,a,\beta) - \tilde{\eta}_i \cdot (\exp(-\beta \cdot \tilde{z}_i(\beta)) - 1)$, where $\tilde{\eta}_i$ is the step size and $(\exp(-\beta \cdot \tilde{z}_i(\beta)) - 1)$ is the derivative of the exponential loss function.
 - **Boundedness Condition:** To ensure convergence in the undiscounted setting, a critical boundedness condition is introduced: the TD residual $\tilde{z}_i(\beta)$ must remain within a pre-defined range $[z_{\min}, z_{\max}]$. A heuristic (Algorithm 3) is provided to estimate these bounds.

4. **EVaR Q-learning Algorithm (Algorithm 2):**
 - **Reduction to ERM Problems:** Since EVaR is not dynamically consistent, the problem of finding an optimal EVaR policy is transformed into finding a $\delta$-optimal policy by solving a sequence of ERM-TRC problems. This involves maximizing $h(\pi, \beta) := \text{ERM}_{\pi, \mu, \beta}[\sum R_k] + \beta^{-1} \log(\alpha)$ over a finite, discretized set of $\beta$ values, $B(\beta_0, \delta)$.
 - **Algorithm Steps:** Algorithm 2 involves: 1) constructing the set $B(\beta_0, \delta)$, 2) using Algorithm 1 to compute optimal ERM value functions and policies for each $\beta$ in $B(\beta_0, \delta)$, 3) selecting the $\beta^\star$ that maximizes $h(\pi, \beta)$, and 4) returning the corresponding policy. A heuristic for choosing the initial $\beta_0$ is also presented.

5. **Convergence Analysis:** The theoretical analysis provides rigorous convergence guarantees for both algorithms. It relies on standard assumptions for Q-learning (Assumption 4.1 regarding visiting all state-action pairs, and standard step-size conditions). A key aspect of the proof is showing that the Q-learning update operator satisfies a monotonicity property (Lemma 3.4), which, combined with the boundedness of the iterates and the strong convexity and Lipschitz-continuous gradient of the loss function (Lemma 4.4), enables convergence in the undiscounted, non-contractive setting, drawing parallels to established convergence frameworks like those by Bertsekas.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical guarantees and empirical demonstrations of the proposed risk-averse Q-learning algorithms.

**Theoretical Findings:**

- **ERM Bellman Operator Fixed Point:** The paper establishes that the optimal ERM state-action value function is the unique fixed point of the ERM Bellman operator (Theorem 3.1).
- **Equivalence of Bellman Operators:** It is proven that the ERM Bellman operator defined using the standard expectation of risk and the alternative operator defined via the elicitability property (minimizing an exponential loss function) are equivalent (Theorem 3.3). This equivalence is fundamental for enabling model-free learning.
- **Convergence of ERM-TRC Q-learning:** Under standard assumptions for step sizes and sufficient exploration (visiting all state-action pairs infinitely often), and given that the TD residual remains bounded, Algorithm 1 for ERM-TRC Q-learning is proven to converge almost surely to the unique optimal ERM value function (Theorem 4.2). This proof notably deviates from standard Q-learning convergence proofs by relying on the monotonicity of the Bellman operator and an explicit boundedness condition for undiscounted MDPs, as the operator is not a contraction.
- **Convergence of EVaR-TRC Q-learning:** The paper demonstrates that the EVaR-TRC problem can be approximated by a sequence of ERM-TRC problems (Theorem 3.5). Consequently, Algorithm 2, which leverages the ERM-TRC Q-learning for a discretized set of risk levels, is proven to converge to a $\delta$-optimal stationary policy for the EVaR-TRC objective almost surely (Corollary 4.5).

**Numerical Results:**

The algorithms were evaluated on two tabular domains: Cliff Walking (CW) and Gambler's Ruin (GR).

- **Impact of Risk Aversion on Policies:** Experiments on the CW domain illustrate that different risk levels ($\alpha$) for EVaR lead to distinct optimal policies. For a more risk-averse setting ($\alpha=0.2$), the agent's policy is observed to proactively avoid the "cliff" region, demonstrating a preference for certainty and minimizing potential negative outcomes. In contrast, a less risk-averse setting ($\alpha=0.6$) yields a policy that may take paths closer to the cliff, potentially accepting higher variability for different overall objectives.
- **Return Distribution Analysis:** Simulating the optimal EVaR policies over many episodes reveals a notable difference in return distributions. For $\alpha=0.2$, the return distribution is observed to be within a positive range $(0, 2]$, with a higher mean return (1.92), indicating successful avoidance of negative outcomes. For $\alpha=0.6$, the returns are observed to be in a wider range, including negative values, with a lower mean (-0.074). This numerically supports the algorithms' ability to compute policies that effectively manage risk according to the specified risk measure.
- **Algorithm Stability:** Multiple runs (six random seeds) of Algorithm 2 on the CW domain demonstrated stability. The computed EVaR values showed a low standard deviation (0.015), indicating reliable and consistent convergence of the Q-learning algorithm to similar optimal solutions.
- **Convergence to Optimal Values:** The convergence of Algorithm 2 was assessed by comparing its computed EVaR values with those obtained from a linear programming (LP) solution (considered the optimal value). On both the CW and GR domains, the difference between the EVaR values from Q-learning and LP was observed to decrease towards zero as the number of samples increased, reaching near-zero differences around 20,000 samples. This confirms the practical convergence of the proposed Q-learning algorithms to the true optimal risk-averse value functions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of risk-averse reinforcement learning, particularly for applications requiring undiscounted objectives and model-free operation.

**Significance:**

- **First Model-Free TRC Algorithms for ERM/EVaR:** The primary significance lies in the development and rigorous theoretical validation of the first model-free risk-averse Q-learning algorithms for the Total Reward Criterion (TRC) under Entropic Risk Measure (ERM) and Entropic Value-at-Risk (EVaR). This fills a critical gap, as previous methods for these objectives in TRC primarily relied on model-based approaches, which are impractical when environmental dynamics are unknown.
- **Enabling Risk-Averse RL in Undiscounted Settings:** By providing solutions for undiscounted, infinite-horizon MDPs with absorbing states, the work extends the applicability of risk-averse RL to a broader class of real-world problems where temporal discounting is not naturally justified, such as in robotics, manufacturing control, and certain gaming scenarios.
- **Robust Theoretical Foundations:** The paper offers robust theoretical contributions, particularly in proving convergence for a non-contractive Bellman operator in an undiscounted setting. This is achieved by leveraging ERM's elicitability to formulate the problem as a stochastic gradient descent and relying on monotonicity properties and explicit boundedness conditions for the value function iterates. This theoretical framework can serve as a basis for future research in similar challenging RL settings.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Practical Application in High-Stakes Domains:** The model-free nature of the algorithms makes them suitable for direct application in high-stakes, real-world domains where explicit models are hard to obtain or verify, such as autonomous systems (e.g., self-driving cars avoiding high-risk scenarios), medical robotics (e.g., surgical planning with certainty guarantees), and financial portfolio management (e.g., minimizing probability of large losses in a total return context).
- **Foundation for Scalable Algorithms:** While the current analysis focuses on tabular settings, the gradient-based Q-learning framework is inherently compatible with function approximation techniques, including deep neural networks. This work lays a solid theoretical foundation for developing scalable risk-averse deep reinforcement learning algorithms, which could significantly broaden the scope of problems addressable by risk-averse RL.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The identified limitations, particularly concerning the choice of bounds ($z_{\min}, z_{\max}$) and the initial risk parameter ($\beta_0$), highlight important avenues for future research. Developing adaptive or self-tuning mechanisms for these parameters would enhance the practicality and robustness of the algorithms, contributing to the development of more general and less sensitive risk-averse RL methods.

**Limitations (as acknowledged by the authors):**

- **Tabular Setting Focus:** The algorithms and convergence analysis are presented for tabular domains. Extending these guarantees to settings with value function approximation, such as deep reinforcement learning, remains a future challenge, though the gradient-based approach provides a promising starting point.
- **Parameter Sensitivity ($z_{\min}, z_{\max}$):** The requirement to choose the bounds $z_{\min}$ and $z_{\max}$ for the TD residual is a practical limitation. Incorrectly chosen bounds could lead to suboptimal solutions or slow detection of divergence.
- **Parameter Sensitivity ($\beta_0$):** For EVaR optimization, the initial parameter $\beta_0$ needs to be sufficiently small to guarantee a $\delta$-optimal solution. Determining an appropriate $\beta_0$ without prior model knowledge is challenging, often requiring heuristics (like Algorithm 3, which involves multiple Q-learning runs) or domain-specific insights. Further research is needed to make this selection more robust and automated.
