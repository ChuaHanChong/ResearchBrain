# On the Direction of RLVR Updates for LLM Reasoning: Identification and Exploitation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Alibaba Group identified that the direction of policy updates, quantified by signed log probability difference (∆ log p), is more informative than update magnitude for understanding RLVR's impact on LLM reasoning. This directional analysis reveals RLVR's sparse updates concentrate on low-probability, reasoning-critical tokens, leading to a test-time extrapolation method that boosted Avg@32 scores by up to 2.92 points and a training-time reweighting method that increased Avg@32 by an average of 1.63 points on math benchmarks.

## Problem

- Prior research on Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) updates for Large Language Models (LLMs) primarily focused on the magnitude of changes (e.g., entropy, KL divergence), overlooking the crucial direction of distributional shifts.
- Magnitude-based metrics proved insufficient, yielding nearly identical histograms for base and RLVR models, thus failing to adequately distinguish how probability mass shifts from a base policy to an RL-trained policy.
- A lack of a precise metric to capture how RLVR specifically increases or decreases the probability of certain reasoning-critical tokens hindered a deeper understanding and exploitation of its effects.

## Method

- The research introduced a new metric, the ==signed token-level log probability difference (∆ log p)==, to quantify the directional shift in token probabilities from a base model to an RLVR-trained model.
- A test-time enhancement method was developed, utilizing ==selective extrapolation== of the RLVR model's distribution further along the identified 'reasoning direction' (∆ log p) for specific reasoning-critical tokens.
- A training-time enhancement was proposed through ==probability-aware advantage reweighting==, which strategically strengthens the learning signal for low-probability tokens during RLVR training.

## Results

- Token-replacement experiments showed that ∆ log p identified reasoning-critical tokens with approximately **10%** substitutions to achieve RLVR-level accuracy, outperforming entropy and KL divergence significantly.
- The test-time selective extrapolation method consistently increased Avg@32 scores for RLVR models by up to 2.92 points (e.g., DAPO-**32B** from 52.50 to 55.42 on AIME-24) and demonstrated generalizability to non-math reasoning tasks on the Minerva dataset.
- The training-time probability-aware advantage reweighting method improved Avg@32 by an average of 1.63 points (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-**7B** from 42.12 to 43.75) and Pass@16 by an average of 4.47 points, outperforming other reweighting strategies across multiple math benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The signed, token-level log probability difference (∆ log p) effectively captures directional shifts in probability mass, which magnitude-based metrics like entropy and KL divergence fail to differentiate between base and RLVR models.
> - RLVR's policy gradients disproportionately concentrate on rare, low-probability tokens, causing these tokens to undergo the largest directional shifts (high ∆ log p) and confirming their critical role in reasoning improvements.
> - By either extrapolating along the ∆ log p direction at test time or strengthening the learning signal for low-probability tokens during training, LLM reasoning performance can be consistently enhanced.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities has been a significant area of progress in artificial intelligence. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is a key algorithmic approach that has contributed to this advancement by fine-tuning LLMs using task-specific verifiers. This process allows models to generate more accurate and coherent reasoning steps.

Prior research on understanding RLVR's impact has consistently observed that the changes induced in LLMs are sparse, affecting only a minority of tokens in the output sequence. Analyses have primarily focused on the *magnitude* of these changes, often quantified through metrics such as entropy (e.g., Wang et al., 2025b) or KL divergence (e.g., Huan et al., 2025). These studies highlight *where* changes occur but have largely overlooked the *direction* of the distributional shifts from the base model to the RLVR-trained model. This paper addresses this gap by proposing that analyzing the direction of updates provides a more comprehensive understanding of how RLVR enhances reasoning. It seeks to establish a framework for both diagnosing and exploiting these directional changes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to demonstrate that the *direction* of updates implemented during RLVR training is a more informative and critical aspect for understanding its effects on LLM reasoning than merely the magnitude of these updates. The researchers aim to quantify this directional change and, subsequently, exploit this understanding to improve LLM reasoning performance.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several observations and gaps in previous research:
- **Insufficiency of Magnitude-Based Metrics**: Existing analyses using magnitude-based metrics (e.g., entropy, KL divergence) have revealed that RLVR updates are sparse. However, when comparing the distributions of base and RLVR models, these metrics yield nearly identical histograms, suggesting they do not adequately distinguish the transformations from a base policy (π_Base) to an RL-trained policy (π_RL). This indicates a lack of insight into *how* the probability mass shifts.
- **Need for Directional Quantification**: The authors argue for a metric that can capture how RLVR specifically increases or decreases the probability of certain tokens. Such a metric could differentiate reasoning-critical tokens, whose probabilities are intentionally altered by RLVR, from irrelevant ones.
- **Potential for Exploitation**: If a clear directional signal can be identified, it could potentially be amplified or leveraged to further enhance reasoning capabilities, either at test-time without additional training or during the training process itself. The ability to pinpoint specific tokens where RLVR learns crucial updates could inform more efficient and effective fine-tuning strategies.
- **Understanding Sparsity**: While sparsity of updates is an established finding, the underlying mechanisms driving this sparsity, particularly in relation to the nature of the updated tokens, require further investigation. Understanding *why* specific tokens are sparsely updated could lead to targeted improvements in RLVR algorithms.

In essence, the research is motivated by a desire to move beyond merely identifying *that* changes occur to understanding *how* and *why* specific changes are made, and then using this deeper understanding to devise practical methods for improving LLM reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research is structured around identifying directional updates in RLVR, providing a gradient-based explanation for these updates, and subsequently developing two practical applications based on these insights.

#### 3.1. Identification of Directional Updates

The core of the identification approach is the introduction of a new metric: the signed, token-level log probability difference (∆ log p).
$$ \Delta \text{log p}(y_t|x, y_{<t}) = \text{log } \pi_{\text{RL}}(y_t|x, y_{<t}) - \text{log } \pi_{\text{Base}}(y_t|x, y_{<t}) $$
This metric quantifies how the RLVR model (π_RL) shifts the probability mass for each token (y_t) given a context (x, y_<t>) compared to the base model (π_Base). Positive values indicate an increased probability for a token by RLVR, while negative values indicate a decreased probability.

- **Statistical Analysis**: The distributions of ∆ log p, entropy, and KL divergence are compared using histograms for tokens generated by both base and RLVR models. This analysis aims to visually demonstrate whether ∆ log p exhibits a distinct pattern (e.g., bimodality) that magnitude-only metrics lack.
- **Token-Replacement Interventions**: To validate the utility of ∆ log p in identifying reasoning-critical tokens, a selective token replacement experiment is conducted.
 - **Procedure**: At each decoding step, a token is initially sampled from π_Base. A metric-specific criterion `f_τ` then determines if this token should be replaced by one sampled from π_RL. The threshold `τ` controls the replacement rate for a fair comparison across metrics.
 - **Criteria**:
 - **Entropy**: Replace if `H(·|x, y_<t>) > τ` (targeting high-entropy positions).
 - **KL Divergence**: Replace if `D(·|x, y_<t>) > τ` (targeting positions of high divergence).
 - **Logp Difference**: Replace if `∆ log p(y_t|x, y_<t>) < τ` (targeting tokens penalized by RLVR, thus encouraging selection of tokens favored by RLVR).
 - **Evaluation**: The performance (reasoning accuracy) is measured against varying replacement ratios to determine which metric most efficiently recovers RLVR-level performance.

#### 3.2. Gradient-Based Explanation for Sparsity

The research investigates the mechanistic origin of sparse updates in RLVR by analyzing policy gradients.
- **Gradient Norm Analysis**: For a softmax-parameterized LLM policy, Lemma 3.1 states that the ℓ1-norm of the DAPO objective's gradient with respect to logits for a token `y_i,t` is `2|w_i,t| · (1 − π_θ(y_i,t|x, y_i,<t))`. This suggests that low-probability tokens receive disproportionately large gradient updates, contributing to sparsity.
- **Correlation between ∆ log p and Low Probability**: An empirical analysis correlates tokens grouped by their ∆ log p values with their probabilities in both base and RLVR models. This aims to show that tokens with high ∆ log p (significant directional shifts) are typically low-probability tokens.
- **Causal Verification (Training-Time Intervention)**: The importance of low-probability tokens is causally tested by training the LLM with DAPO while applying a top-p sampling strategy during rollout. This filters out low-probability tokens, and the impact on reasoning performance is observed.

#### 3.3. Practical Applications

Based on the insights from directional analysis, two methods are proposed to enhance LLM reasoning:

- **Test-Time Enhancement via Extrapolation**:
 - **Concept**: Treating ∆ log p as a "reasoning direction," the method amplifies the RLVR model's distribution further along this direction for selected reasoning-critical tokens.
 - **Extrapolated Policy**: `log π_γ_Extra(y_t|x, y_<t>) := (1 + γ) · log π_RL(y_t|x, y_<t>) − γ · log π_Base(y_t|x, y_<t>) + z(x, y_<t>)`, where `γ` controls extrapolation strength.
 - **Selective Application**: Extrapolation is applied selectively, using the `f_τ_logp` criterion to gate positions with large negative ∆ log p, thereby focusing on tokens where RLVR made significant directional changes.
 - **Theoretical Justification**: Theorem 4.1, based on a simplified tabular softmax bandit policy with Natural Policy Gradient updates, provides a theoretical basis for how extrapolating along the learned difference can increase expected reward, assuming monotonic learning along the reward's direction.

- **Training-Time Enhancement via Advantage Reweighting**:
 - **Concept**: This method strengthens the learning signal for low-probability tokens during RLVR training, leveraging the observed correlation between high ∆ log p and low-probability tokens.
 - **Modified Advantage**: The standard advantage term `ˆA_i,t` in DAPO is reweighted: `˜A_i,t = (1 + α · (1 − π_θ_old(y_i,t|x, y_i,<t))) · ˆA_i,t`, where `α` controls the reweighting strength. This upweights contributions from low-probability tokens (`1 − π_θ_old` is high for low probability).
 - **Comparison**: The method is compared against the standard DAPO baseline and other existing reweighting strategies (PPL, Dominate).

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Models**: Experiments utilize several RLVR-trained and base model pairs from the Qwen series: ORZ-32B (PPO-finetuned from Qwen2.5-32B), DAPO-32B (DAPO-finetuned from Qwen2.5-32B), and UniReason-14B (GRPO-finetuned from Qwen3-14B).
- **Datasets**: Math reasoning benchmarks AIME-24, AIME-25, and AMC are used for evaluation. The Minerva dataset, comprising undergraduate-level STEM problems, is used for evaluating generalization to non-math reasoning.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Performance is measured using Avg@k (average accuracy over k samples) and Pass@k (pass rate given k samples), typically Avg@32 and Pass@16.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Dissecting Token-Level Changes Introduced by RLVR

- **Directional vs. Magnitude-Based Metrics**: Statistical analysis revealed that histograms of entropy and KL divergence were nearly identical for tokens generated by base and RLVR models (Fig. 1b, Appendix E Figs. 13-15). In contrast, the ∆ log p distribution exhibited a clear bimodal pattern with distinct positive and negative tails for RLVR and base model generations, respectively (Fig. 1b, Appendix E Fig. 12). This indicates that ∆ log p effectively captures the directional shifts in probability mass distinguishing RLVR from the base model, unlike magnitude-only metrics.
- **Efficacy in Identifying Reasoning-Critical Tokens**: Token-replacement experiments demonstrated that selecting tokens based on ∆ log p achieved RLVR-level reasoning accuracy with the fewest substitutions (approximately 10% of tokens) compared to entropy or KL divergence (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2). This indicated that ∆ log p is more precise in isolating the sparse, reasoning-critical tokens modified by RLVR training. Furthermore, qualitative analysis showed that ∆ log p and divergence tended to select more specific mathematical reasoning tokens and symbols, while entropy favored general logical transition words (Appendix A.2 Fig. 7).
- **Gradient-Based Explanation for Sparsity**:
 - Empirical analysis confirmed that RLVR's policy gradients (specifically in DAPO) disproportionately concentrate on rare, low-probability tokens, despite their infrequency (Fig. 3a).
 - Tokens exhibiting the largest ∆ log p values were consistently found to have lower probabilities in both the base and RLVR models, and their probabilities substantially increased from the base to the RLVR model (Fig. 3b). This links the sparse high-∆ log p changes to the gradient concentration on low-probability tokens.
 - A training-time intervention showed that excluding low-probability tokens via top-p sampling during rollout led to a substantial and sharp decline in RLVR performance (Fig. 3c). This provided causal evidence for the importance of these low-probability tokens for the reasoning improvements.

#### 4.2. Exploiting RLVR’s Directional Updates

- **Test-Time Enhancement via Extrapolation**: Selective extrapolation along the ∆ log p direction at test time consistently yielded higher Avg@32 scores than the original RLVR model or simple token replacement (using π_RL) across ORZ-32B, DAPO-32B, and UniReason-14B on the AIME-24 benchmark (Fig. 4). For instance, DAPO-32B's Avg@32 increased from 52.50 (RLVR) to 55.42 (Selective Extrapolate). Applying extrapolation directly to the RLVR model also showed incremental gains up to a moderate intervention ratio (Table 1). This effect generalized to the Minerva dataset for non-math reasoning problems, where extrapolation also improved Avg@8 scores (Appendix C Fig. 10).
- **Training-Time Enhancement via Advantage Reweighting**: The proposed probability-aware advantage reweighting method consistently improved reasoning accuracy (Avg@32) and Pass@16 scores over the DAPO baseline on AIME-24, AIME-25, and AMC benchmarks for both Qwen2.5-Math-7B and Qwen3-8B-Base models (Table 2). For Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the average Avg@32 increased from 42.12 (DAPO) to 43.75 (Ours), and average Pass@16 from 57.86 to 62.33. This method also outperformed alternative reweighting strategies (PPL and Dominate) in both Avg@32 and Pass@16 metrics (Table 3). Training curves showed improved reasoning accuracy and a steady increase in response length, correlating with effective reasoning (Fig. 5). The method's performance was also found to be reproducible across multiple independent training runs (Appendix B Fig. 9). Generalization was observed on the Minerva dataset, where the reweighted models outperformed baselines (Appendix C Table 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and improvement of Large Language Models (LLMs) trained with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in several ways:

- **Novel Analytical Principle**: The work introduces and establishes the direction of change, quantified by the signed log probability difference (∆ log p), as a critical principle for analyzing RLVR's effects. This shifts the focus from merely identifying *where* changes occur (magnitude) to understanding *how* probabilities are shifted (direction), offering a more nuanced diagnostic lens than previous entropy or divergence-based analyses.
- **Enhanced Diagnostic Capability**: The ∆ log p metric is demonstrated to be more effective than magnitude-based metrics in precisely identifying the sparse, yet reasoning-critical, token-level updates introduced by RLVR. This provides researchers with a more potent tool for pinpointing consequential policy modifications.
- **Mechanistic Understanding of Sparsity**: The research provides a gradient-based explanation for the observed sparsity of RLVR updates, showing that learning inherently concentrates on rare, low-probability tokens, which correspond to tokens with high ∆ log p in the final model. This insight clarifies the underlying dynamics of RLVR training.
- **Practical Performance Enhancements**:
 - **Test-time Extrapolation**: The proposed selective test-time extrapolation method offers a way to amplify learned reasoning signals without requiring further training. This could be beneficial for deploying existing RLVR models with incremental performance gains at inference time.
 - **Training-time Reweighting**: The probability-aware advantage reweighting method provides a straightforward and effective strategy to improve RLVR training, yielding consistent performance gains across different models and reasoning benchmarks. This can be integrated into existing RLVR pipelines to enhance reasoning capabilities.
- **Generalizability**: The observed improvements from both the test-time extrapolation and training-time reweighting methods extend beyond pure mathematical reasoning tasks to broader STEM problems, indicating their potential generalizability.

The potential impact of this work lies in providing a new conceptual framework for analyzing RLVR, along with practical, validated methods for boosting LLM reasoning performance. By focusing on the direction of updates, the research opens avenues for more targeted interventions in RL processes for LLMs, potentially leading to more efficient fine-tuning, better model interpretability, and further advancements in complex reasoning tasks.

**Limitations**: The authors acknowledge that the test-time extrapolation method requires two models (base and RLVR), which incurs computational cost. Future work could explore integrating this with parameter-efficient fine-tuning or adaptively combining selection thresholds and extrapolation strengths. The reweighting approach could also be evaluated across different model scales and in conjunction with other adaptive training techniques.
