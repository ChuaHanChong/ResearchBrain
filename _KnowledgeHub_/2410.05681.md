# Whole-Body Dynamic Throwing with Legged Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a deep reinforcement learning framework for legged manipulators to perform dynamic full-body throwing, achieving greater accuracy and range to arbitrary 3D targets than arm-only methods and demonstrating successful sim2real transfer to a humanoid robot with a 13.8-meter throw.

## Problem

- Existing robotic throwing systems often rely on fixed or arm-only manipulators, limiting range, force, and generalization to arbitrary 3D targets.
- Implementing dynamic, high-speed manipulation with high-DoF legged robots presents challenges in managing complex nonlinear dynamics and maintaining stability.
- Training deep reinforcement learning agents for dynamic throwing is hindered by sparse rewards and the need to balance aggressive movements with robot stability.

## Method

- A deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework optimizes full-body joint-level controllers for two legged robot morphologies (humanoid and quadruped) in a massively parallel simulation environment.
- A composite reward function combines throwing accuracy and robot stability, with a key innovation of calculating immediate throwing reward via projectile motion equations upon release.
- An optimized adaptive curriculum, featuring learned difficulty thresholds, incrementally increases target distance and body action involvement to balance performance and stability.

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Utilizing full-body dynamics of legged manipulators significantly enhances throwing range, accuracy, and stability compared to arm-only manipulation.
