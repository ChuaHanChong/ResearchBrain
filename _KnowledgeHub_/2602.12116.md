# P-GenRM: Personalized Generative Reward Model with Test-time User-based Scaling

> [!summary] Summary
> Alibaba Group's P-GenRM introduces a personalized generative reward model for large language models, achieving state-of-the-art performance in aligning LLMs to individual user preferences and significantly improving generalization for new users through a novel test-time user-based scaling mechanism. The model demonstrated a 2.77% average improvement over prior methods on the LLaMA-3.1-8B model, with its 8B variant outperforming existing 70B models by 1.04% on personalized benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing LLM alignment techniques, primarily focused on universal values, struggle to capture and adapt to diverse, subjective, and dynamic individual user preferences.
- Current personalized alignment methods often rely on static or oversimplified user preference models, failing to reflect the nuanced variability of human choices across different contexts.
- Models frequently exhibit weak generalization capabilities for new users with limited historical feedback, limiting their real-world applicability in 'cold-start' scenarios.

## Method

- **P-GenRM** is a ==generative reward== model that transforms varied preference signals into explicit evaluation chains, including dynamic user personas and context-specific scoring rubrics.
- A three-stage training framework incorporates Persona-guided Scoring Induction (SFT), Criteria-based Reasoning Enhancement (RL with dual rewards), and Hard Negative-aware Curriculum Learning.
- A novel test-time user-based scaling mechanism combines individual-level parallel sampling with prototype-level scaling, leveraging insights from similar users to improve robustness and generalization.

## Results

- P-GenRM achieved state-of-the-art performance, with its **8B** version surpassing previous **70B** models by an average of **1.04%** on personalized benchmarks, and providing up to a **2.77%** improvement over prior SOTA methods.
- The test-time user-based scaling mechanism contributed an additional **3%** average performance boost and improved generalization on out-of-distribution datasets, yielding a Spearman correlation of **0.638** on Lamp-QA.
- Policy models trained using P-GenRM as a reward signal showed enhanced personalized generation, with **95%** confidence intervals surpassing those of significantly larger models trained without this personalized reward guidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative reward models can effectively translate diverse and dynamic user preference signals into adaptive, structured evaluation chains, providing greater transparency and flexibility than scalar reward models.
> - Combining multiple individual-level preference inferences with a collaborative filtering-inspired prototype-level scaling at test time significantly enhances performance and generalization, particularly for users with limited data.
> - A multi-stage training approach that integrates supervised learning, reinforcement learning with process and outcome rewards, and curriculum learning is essential for robustly teaching a generative model to personalize evaluations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on P-GenRM is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **Large Language Model (LLM) alignment**, specifically focusing on **personalized alignment**. Historically, LLM alignment via techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has aimed to instill universal values (e.g., helpfulness, harmlessness) into models. However, the next frontier involves tailoring LLM responses to the diverse, often subjective, preferences of individual users, moving beyond a "one-size-fits-all" approach.

Existing research in personalized alignment faces two primary limitations:
1. **Static and Oversimplified Preference Modeling:** Many methods reduce complex, dynamic, and scenario-dependent user preferences into a fixed, limited set of rules or latent variables. This fails to capture the nuanced variability of human preferences, where a user might prefer conciseness in one context but expressiveness in another.
2. **Weak Generalization to New Users (Cold-Start Problem):** Current models struggle to adapt effectively to new users with sparse feedback, limiting their real-world applicability, especially in scenarios where extensive historical data for every new user is unavailable.

P-GenRM directly addresses these limitations by introducing a **generative reward model** paradigm. Unlike discriminative reward models that output a scalar score, generative reward models produce a textual "evaluation chain" that explains the reasoning behind a score. This approach brings several advantages:
- **Interpretability:** The explicit generation of user personas and scoring rubrics makes the reward process transparent and understandable.
- **Flexibility and Adaptability:** Generative models can dynamically adapt their evaluation criteria to scenario-specific and evolving user preferences, rather than relying on a fixed set of attributes.
- **Scalability:** Leveraging the generative capabilities of LLMs allows for more sophisticated reasoning and the potential for "test-time scaling," where multiple evaluations can be aggregated.

This paper builds upon several related research areas:
- **Personalized LLM Alignment:** It extends works like SynthesizeMe (Ryan et al., 2025) which infer synthetic personas, but critically improves upon their static nature by allowing for adaptive, context-dependent personas. It also relates to methods that use user-specific parameters (Li et al., 2023a) or steer models at inference time (Wang et al., 2023a), positioning itself as a key component for the former via personalized reward modeling.
- **User Preference Modeling:** While other works define explicit multi-dimensional attributes (Dong et al., 2023) or encode preferences into system prompts (Lee et al., 2024), P-GenRM generates these preferences dynamically, offering a more flexible and granular approach.
- **Generative Reward Models (GenRM):** It follows the trend of leveraging LLMs' generative power for reward modeling (Zhang et al., 2024a; Li et al., 2023b; Liu et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; Xiong et al., 2025). P-GenRM is presented as the *first personalized generative reward model* that explicitly transforms diverse preference signals into structured evaluation chains in open-domain settings. The novel contribution of "test-time user-based scaling" further differentiates it by improving robustness and generalization through a collaborative filtering-inspired mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of P-GenRM are motivated by the inherent challenges in achieving accurate and generalizable personalized alignment of LLMs:

1. **To overcome the oversimplification of diverse user preferences:** Existing personalized reward models often reduce complex, scenario-specific preferences to a small, fixed set of evaluation principles. P-GenRM aims to transform heterogeneous preference signals (implicit historical interactions and explicit statements) into adaptive, structured evaluation chains, including dynamic user personas and context-specific scoring rubrics, thereby capturing the full spectrum of user preferences in open-ended scenarios.
2. **To enhance generalization to new users with limited feedback:** Traditional models struggle in "cold-start" scenarios where little or no historical data is available for a new user. P-GenRM seeks to develop a robust transfer mechanism that allows the model to effectively adapt and make accurate predictions for unseen users, mitigating the dependency on extensive individual user data.
3. **To leverage and extend the benefits of generative reward models:** By adopting a generative approach, the model aims to provide interpretable explanations for its scores, allowing for transparency and better understanding of *why* certain responses are preferred. This also enables test-time scalability to improve performance.

In essence, P-GenRM aims to build a more intelligent, adaptable, and interpretable personalized reward model that can better understand and apply user preferences across diverse scenarios and user populations, including those with sparse interaction histories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

P-GenRM's methodology is characterized by a three-stage training framework and a novel test-time user-based scaling mechanism.

#### 3.1. Multi-stage Training Framework

The training pipeline is designed to equip P-GenRM with robust personalized scoring capabilities:

1. **Persona-guided Scoring Induction (PSI):** This initial stage uses supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to teach the model to generate explicit evaluation chains. An instruct LLM is prompted with both implicit (historical interactions) and explicit (stated criteria) user preference signals. It first infers a scenario-specific user persona (P^(u)^_t^) and then derives a set of weighted scoring rubrics (S^(u)^_t^). Each candidate response is then evaluated against these rubrics to yield a personalized score. This stage establishes the foundational ability to translate diverse preference indicators into an adaptive, structured scoring scheme. Data for SFT is constructed through a rejection sampling process.
2. **Criteria-based Reasoning Enhancement (CRE):** To improve the generation of high-quality evaluation chains, especially when explicit user feedback is sparse, this stage employs reinforcement learning (RL) based on the GRPO (Generative Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization) algorithm. The key idea is to treat explicit preference criteria as a form of supervision during training. The P-GenRM infers plausible explicit preferences from limited historical interactions and generates evaluation chains. The reward function (R_t_) is a weighted sum of:
 - **Process Reward (PR_t_):** An LLM-as-a-judge (e.g., GPT-5) assesses how well the generated evaluation process covers user-stated or synthetic explicit preferences (score 0-1).
 - **Outcome Reward (OR_t_):** A binary reward (1 for correct preference, 0 for incorrect) based on whether the model correctly identifies the chosen response, with an additional penalty for formatting errors.
 This dual reward system ensures both the correctness of the final score and the quality/completeness of the intermediate reasoning chain.
3. **Hard Negative-aware Curriculum Learning:** Following SFT and RL, this stage further enhances the model's robustness by progressively introducing a higher proportion of challenging "hard negative" samples during training. In this phase, the process-level reward (PR_t_) is disabled to encourage a broader exploration of the solution space for difficult cases, focusing primarily on the outcome reward to improve decision-making on subtle distinctions.

#### 3.2. Test-time User-based Scaling

This novel mechanism addresses the challenges of noisy preference inference and weak generalization by combining individual-level and prototype-level insights:

1. **Offline Prototype Initialization and Optimization:**
 - **User Prototype Initiation:** User preference embeddings (derived from P^(u)^_t^, the scenario-specific user persona) are computed using a specialized embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B). K-means clustering is then applied to these embeddings to identify 'k' centroids, which serve as initial user prototypes, representing distinct clusters of user preferences.
 - **History-aware Attentive Prototype Refinement:** This iterative process refines the prototypes. For each prototype and its associated users, a prototype-augmented attention mechanism (Equation 4) assigns importance weights to historical records, making the prototype selectively exploit information relevant to the current query. This refined historical information is combined with the prototype representation (Equation 5) to form a prior that guides the model in discriminating between preferred and dispreferred responses. A pairwise loss (Equation 6) is minimized, along with regularization terms (Equation 7) that keep prototypes close to their cluster centers and ensure smooth evolution, preventing excessive drift. This updates the prototype representations to better capture subordinate users' preference choices.

2. **Test-time Dual-Granularity Scaling:** At inference time, P-GenRM dynamically scales its evaluation process:
 - **Individual-level Scaling:** For a given user query and their preference signals, P-GenRM performs parallel sampling to generate 'm' individual-level scoring schemes. This explores multiple hypotheses about the user's preferences, leading to more robust and accurate rubrics.
 - **Prototype-level Scaling:** The user's preference embedding is assigned to its nearest prototype. P-GenRM then identifies 'n' most similar users within that prototype. It simultaneously incorporates 'n' additional scores inferred from the preferences of these similar users. This mechanism, inspired by collaborative filtering, reduces noise in inferred preferences and provides strong generalization, especially for new users with sparse data, by leveraging collective wisdom from similar profiles.
 The final score for a response (s^(i)^_t^) is an aggregation of the scores from 'm' individual-level schemes and 'n' prototype-level schemes (Equation 9).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

P-GenRM demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across various benchmarks and configurations.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** P-GenRM consistently outperformed previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods on the PersonalRewardBench (comprising Chatbot Arena-personalized and PRISM-personalized datasets).
 - On the LLaMA-3.1-8B model, P-GenRM achieved an average improvement of **2.77%** over the prior SOTA.
 - On the LLaMA-3.1-70B model, it delivered an average gain of **1.99%**.
 - Notably, P-GenRM-8B even surpassed the best-performing 70B models by an average of **1.04%**, demonstrating its efficiency and effectiveness.
 - P-GenRM also significantly outperformed proprietary models like OpenAI-o3, including versions enhanced with strong prompting.
- **Effectiveness of Test-time User-based Scaling:** This mechanism proved to be a substantial performance enhancer.
 - It provided an additional **3%** average boost over the base P-GenRM model.
 - The optimal configuration (Ind-16, Pro-8) leveraged 16 individual-level samplings and 8 prototype-level samplings, outperforming even Ind-32 (32 individual samplings) with fewer total scaling steps. This highlights the synergistic benefit of combining individual and prototype-level insights.
 - Ablation studies confirmed that removing any training component (Curriculum Learning, Process Reward, Outcome Reward, or the entire RL stage) led to significant performance drops, validating the multi-stage training framework.
- **Adaptive vs. Static Personas:** P-GenRM's adaptive persona inference consistently outperformed methods using static persona priors (like SynthesizeMe), showing the value of dynamic, context-aware preference modeling.
- **Broader Preference Space:** P-GenRM was shown to exploit a broader range of personalized scoring dimensions (e.g., Philosophical Engagement, Nuance, Sensitivity) beyond the predefined criteria of datasets like PRISM, indicating its capacity to capture richer, more subjective preferences.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Distribution Users (Lamp-QA):** On the Lamp-QA dataset (an OOD benchmark for personalized long-form question answering), P-GenRM (8B) with test-time scaling (Ind-8, Pro-4) achieved the highest Spearman correlation (0.638 average) with ground-truth rankings. This performance surpassed much larger models, including Qwen3-235B-A22B, demonstrating its strong robustness and generalization capabilities for new users with sparse feedback.
- **Impact on Policy Model Training:** Policy models (LLaMA3.1-8B) trained using P-GenRM as a reward signal (via DPO or GRPO) significantly boosted their personalized generation performance, with 95% confidence intervals surpassing those of much larger 70B models. This underscores P-GenRM's efficacy as a robust reward model for downstream LLM alignment.
- **Inference Time and Efficiency:** While test-time scaling incurs an increase in inference time, this increase was deemed "limited" (e.g., 00:14:16 to 00:23:05 for P-GenRM-8B with Ind-16, Pro-8). The authors argue that it still retains lower latency than prior SOTA methods and is efficient due to shared prompt encoding and parallelization capabilities of vLLM.
- **Prototype Analysis:** PCA analysis suggested 50 prototypes as an optimal number, balancing information gain and inference cost. Performance improved significantly up to 50 prototypes and then plateaued or slightly degraded with more, indicating the balance needed to avoid overfitting or noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

P-GenRM represents a significant step forward in personalized LLM alignment, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Fidelity in Subjective Evaluation:** By transforming diverse preference signals into structured, scenario-aware evaluation chains, P-GenRM can capture the nuanced and dynamic nature of human preferences more accurately than previous methods. This leads to more precise and user-satisfying personalized responses from LLMs.
2. **Improved Generalization for Cold-Start Scenarios:** The innovative test-time user-based scaling, particularly the prototype-level scaling, effectively addresses the cold-start problem. This allows LLMs to adapt to new users with minimal interaction history by leveraging the preferences of similar users, making personalized experiences viable for a broader user base from the outset.
3. **Interpretability and Transparency:** The generative nature of P-GenRM produces explicit user personas and scoring rubrics. This transparency allows developers and even users to understand *why* an LLM makes certain choices, fostering trust and enabling easier debugging or refinement of personalized behaviors.
4. **Practical Scalability for Real-world Applications:** The efficiency of the 8B model outperforming 70B counterparts, combined with the effective test-time scaling mechanism, suggests that P-GenRM can be integrated into large-scale, real-world LLM systems (e.g., in e-commerce, customer service, or content recommendation platforms) without prohibitive computational costs. Its ability to boost policy model training further validates its utility.
5. **New Research Directions:** P-GenRM's success opens avenues for further exploration into dynamic preference modeling, the optimal granularity of user prototypes, and more sophisticated methods for aggregating diverse preference signals. The methodology might inspire similar generative approaches in other areas of AI alignment and evaluation.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors** include the higher inference time due to evaluation chain generation compared to scalar reward models, and the requirement of at least three historical preference selections for reasonable preference analysis, implying a moderate amount of initial data collection. Despite these, P-GenRM offers a robust and innovative framework for making LLMs truly personalized and user-centric.
