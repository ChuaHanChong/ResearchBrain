# ActiveGlasses: Learning Manipulation with Active Vision from Ego-centric Human Demonstration

> [!summary] Summary
> ActiveGlasses, developed by researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Noematrix, captures natural human manipulation and active perception using lightweight, head-mounted smart glasses, enabling zero-shot robot transfer through object-centric 3D policies. This system consistently outperformed baselines in tasks requiring active vision, such as occluded distant water pouring, and successfully deployed learned policies to different robot platforms without finetuning.

## Problem

- Data-driven robot learning faces "data hunger" due to slow, labor-intensive, and expensive data acquisition processes.
- Current robot manipulation data collection methods often impose physical burden on operators or neglect human-like active perception capabilities.
- Directly transferring human demonstrations to diverse robot platforms is challenging due to the inherent morphological gap.

## Method

- **ActiveGlasses** utilizes ==lightweight smart glasses== with a ==stereo camera== to record bare-hand human manipulation and ==6-DoF head movements==, capturing natural active vision.
- A ==data processing pipeline== converts raw data into ==object-centric 3D point clouds== and trajectories, removing human artifacts and unifying coordinate frames.
- A ==3D transformer-based policy== synchronously predicts absolute ==6-DoF object trajectories== and relative ==6-DoF head movements==, designed for ==cross-embodiment deployment==.

## Results

- ActiveGlasses demonstrated superior data collection efficiency and user experience compared to teleoperation and handheld device methods, especially for high-precision tasks.
- The system consistently outperformed baselines (e.g., Pi0.5, "w/o active vision" variant) in challenging tasks like Book Placement and Occluded Distant Water Pouring, achieving higher success rates.
- The object-centric 3D policy facilitated zero-shot deployment from a Flexiv Rizon4 to a UR5 robot, showing comparable performance in initial task stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating human 6-DoF head movements as a learnable active vision signal significantly improves robot performance in tasks with occlusion and precision requirements.
> - An object-centric 3D point-cloud representation enables robust policy learning and direct zero-shot transfer to various robot platforms by abstracting away embodiment-specific details.
> - Predicting absolute object trajectories and relative head movements optimizes policy learning, preventing overfitting and addressing robot workspace limitations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of data-driven robot learning relies heavily on large-scale, diverse datasets to achieve generalized robotic manipulation. However, the acquisition of such data is inherently slow, labor-intensive, and expensive, leading to a "data hunger" crisis in robotics. Current estimates suggest that accumulating robot manipulation data comparable to modern foundational AI datasets would require an impractical amount of time. This inefficiency highlights a critical need for more scalable and efficient data collection frameworks.

Existing approaches to collecting robot manipulation data primarily fall into several categories:
- **Teleoperation:** Systems where human operators directly control robot arms, often requiring specialized hardware like haptic interfaces (e.g., Force Dimension sigma.7) or VR headsets. While providing direct hardware mapping, these methods force operators to mimic robotic movements, leading to physical exhaustion and constrained, unnatural data.
- **Handheld Devices:** Operators use handheld tools equipped with sensors (e.g., UMI, FastUMI) to demonstrate tasks. These often adopt an end-effector configuration and wrist-camera placement similar to robots, bypassing the embodiment gap. However, they can be heavy, imposing physical burden, and their wrist-mounted cameras are passive, limiting active viewpoint adjustment.
- **Smart Glasses/Headsets:** Some works leverage smart glasses (e.g., EgoZero, EgoMimic) to collect ego-centric human demonstration data. These reduce the physical burden compared to handheld devices and offer a more natural perception viewpoint. A common challenge for these systems is bridging the "embodiment gap" between human hands/head and robot manipulators, often requiring robot data finetuning, action retargeting, or visual editing, which can limit scalability or generalizability. Crucially, in many of these systems, human head movements are treated as noise rather than a learnable signal, thus neglecting the potential for active perception.

Within the context of active vision, earlier humanoid designs incorporated neck structures to enable controlled sensing actions. More recent learning-based algorithms have explored active vision by mounting cameras on 2-DoF gimbals or 6-DoF robotic arms (e.g., ViA, EgoMi). These efforts aim to allow robots to dynamically adjust their viewpoint to improve task performance, particularly in occluded scenarios. However, existing active vision systems for data collection often rely on bulky VR headsets, which have limitations in weight, cost, or operator field of view.

ActiveGlasses contributes to this landscape by introducing a system that addresses multiple limitations simultaneously. It aims to capture naturally coordinated human perception-manipulation behaviors using a lightweight, head-mounted stereo camera. By formulating an object-centric 3D point-cloud policy, it explicitly models both manipulation and active head movement, thereby facilitating zero-shot transfer to various robotic platforms without relying on embodiment-specific retargeting or extensive robot-specific finetuning. This work seeks to combine the benefits of scalable, natural human demonstration with the capability of active perception, bridging the gap between human intuition and robotic execution more effectively than previous methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for ActiveGlasses stems from the recognized "data hunger" in data-driven robot learning and the limitations of existing data collection pipelines. The authors argue that achieving generalized robotic manipulation necessitates large-scale, diverse datasets, but current methods are too inefficient and costly. This inefficiency translates to an estimated 100,000 years to acquire a comparable volume of data to modern foundational AI datasets, underscoring the urgent need for more scalable frameworks.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the ActiveGlasses project are articulated around capturing human intelligence through demonstrations that inherently align with human nature in both manipulation and perception:

1. **Overcome Inefficiencies in Data Collection:** The project aims to develop a system that significantly improves the efficiency and scalability of collecting manipulation data compared to teleoperation or handheld device-based methods. This involves reducing operator burden and allowing for data collection in "the wild."

2. **Facilitate Natural Human Manipulation:** Existing systems often require operators to wear bulky devices or teleoperate robots, which restricts natural human kinematic instincts and leads to suboptimal, physically exhausting demonstrations. A core objective is to enable operators to provide bare-hand demonstrations, promoting natural movement and comfort, which is crucial for scalability.

3. **Incorporate Human-like Active Perception:** Traditional setups predominantly rely on fixed third-person cameras or passive wrist-mounted cameras. These lack the active perception capabilities of humans, who instinctively move their heads to adjust viewpoints, peer around occlusions, or focus attention. The system aims to capture this "active vision" by recording the operator's 6-DoF head trajectory alongside manipulation, treating it as a learnable, intent-driven perceptual signal.

4. **Bridge the Morphological Gap for Zero-Shot Transfer:** Direct transfer of actions from a five-fingered human hand and a moving human head to a robot system is challenging due to inherent morphological differences. A critical objective is to enable zero-shot transfer to robotic platforms without complex retargeting or robot-specific finetuning. This is to be achieved by adopting an object-centric representation, predicting the 6-DoF trajectory of the manipulated object rather than human kinematics.

5. **Develop an Active-Vision-Only Perception System:** The system is designed to rely solely on a single active vision camera for both data collection and policy inference. This simplifies the hardware setup and mirrors human visual experience, where perception is dynamically adjusted.

In summary, the overarching motivation is to create a lightweight, intuitive system that faithfully captures human manipulation and perception, yielding high-quality, scalable datasets that enable robust, generalizable robot policies capable of zero-shot deployment across diverse robot platforms, particularly in tasks involving occlusion and precise interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ActiveGlasses system is co-designed with integrated hardware and software components to collect human-like manipulation and active perception behaviors and process them into a unified, training-ready representation for policy learning.

**A. Hardware and Interface Design**
- **Perception Device:** The system utilizes a commercial XREAL Air 2 Ultra smart glasses for 6-DoF head pose tracking due to its higher sampling frequency and stability compared to the ZED Mini's IMU. A ZED Mini stereo camera is mounted on the smart glasses to provide stereo video streams, as the XREAL glasses do not support direct camera calling. This combined setup serves as the *sole* perception device.
- **User Interface (UI):** A Unity-based UI is displayed on the smart glasses. It detects user gestures (e.g., for start/end signals of an episode) and provides audio feedback.
- **Data Alignment:** Data timestamps from the stereo camera frames and 6-DoF head poses are aligned using ROS (Robot Operating System).

**B. Data Processing Pipeline**
The collected stereo videos and head trajectories undergo a multi-step processing pipeline to generate unified representations:
- **Depth Estimation and Point Cloud Reconstruction:** For each stereo image pair, FoundationStereo is used to estimate a depth map. This depth map is then back-projected to reconstruct an RGB point cloud in the camera frame.
- **Mask Generation:** Grounded-SAM is employed to segment human hands, and corresponding points are removed from the point cloud to eliminate human-specific visual artifacts. SAM2 is used to segment the manipulated object, providing an object mask for downstream pose estimation.
- **Object Trajectory Estimation:** FoundationPose, a 6-DoF pose estimation model, takes the left-view image, depth map, and object mask as input, along with the object mesh as a geometric prior. This estimates the 6-DoF pose of the manipulated object in the camera frame for each frame, forming the object trajectory.
- **Calibration and World Frame Unification:**
 - **World Frame Definition:** Three orange spheres placed on the tabletop define a planar Cartesian coordinate system (origin, x, and y axes), with the z-axis determined by the right-hand rule.
 - **Initial Calibration:** Only the first frame's data (left image, depth map) is used for initial calibration. The three spheres are segmented, and their 3D centers in the camera frame are computed to define the initial camera-to-world transform.
 - **Transform Propagation:** For subsequent frames, the camera-to-world transform is propagated using the head-pose relative motion captured by the smart glasses, avoiding re-segmenting spheres in every frame.
 - **Point Cloud Transformation:** All reconstructed point clouds are transformed into this unified world frame.

**C. Algorithm Design (Policy)**
The manipulation task is divided into three stages: pre-grasp, motion planning, and termination.
- **Pre-grasp:** For grasping actions, AnyGrasp is utilized. For tasks requiring high-precision grasp poses, a fixed strategy is employed.
- **Motion Planning:**
 - **Input:** The policy takes the processed 3D point cloud in the world frame as input. This choice addresses the spatial inconsistency introduced by varying human viewpoints in 2D image observations.
 - **Architecture:** The policy is based on RISE, a 3D transformer architecture, modified to use two separate diffusion heads.
 - **Outputs:** It synchronously predicts:
 - **Absolute Object Trajectory (6-DoF):** The target 6-DoF trajectory of the manipulated object in the task space. This object-centric representation inherently supports cross-embodiment deployment. Ablation studies indicated that an absolute representation for the object trajectory and *not* conditioning on the current object pose led to better performance, as it encouraged reliance on visual input rather than overfitting to fixed motion patterns.
 - **Relative Head Movement (6-DoF):** The relative movement of the perception head. A relative representation is used to prevent the perception arm from frequently reaching its workspace limits or failing inverse kinematics due to varied initial base states.
 - **Action Execution:** The predicted object pose trajectory is converted into end-effector poses for the robot manipulator using camera calibration.
- **Termination:** The policy includes an additional output dimension for termination. The last five frames of each demonstration episode are labeled as task completion (value of 1), while others are 0. The robot terminates the task if this output exceeds a predefined threshold.

**D. Hardware Setup for Deployment**
- **Manipulation Arm:** A Flexiv Rizon4 robot with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper is used as the primary manipulation arm.
- **Perception Arm:** An I2RT YAM Robot serves as the perception arm. The ZED Mini camera and XREAL Air 2 Ultra smart glasses are mounted on this arm via a 3D-printed adapter. The perception arm dynamically mimics the human operator's head movements, reproducing active vision. Its base position is randomized to reflect torso movements during human demonstrations.

**E. Task Design**
Three challenging real-world tasks are selected for evaluation, all involving occlusion and requiring precise interaction:
- **Book Placement:** Placing a book into an empty slot on a bookshelf, with initial partial occlusion.
- **Occluded Distant Water Pouring:** Moving a teapot over a screen to pour water into a cup, requiring viewpoint adjustment to see the cup.
- **Bread Insertion:** Inserting a slice of bread into a toaster slot, which is initially not visible.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ActiveGlasses focused on scalability, the effectiveness of active vision, policy design choices, and cross-embodiment transfer.

**A. Scalability of Data Collection**
- **Efficiency and User Experience:** ActiveGlasses demonstrated improved data collection efficiency and a more user-friendly experience compared to existing methods like UMI, VR teleoperation (Quest 3), and Force Dimension sigma.7 haptic interface. The bare-hand data collection reduced the physical burden on operators.
- **Task Performance during Collection:** This advantage was particularly noticeable in tasks requiring higher precision, such as bread insertion, where direct human demonstration with ActiveGlasses yielded higher success rates during the data collection phase itself, indicating that it could capture more optimal manipulation data.

**B. Efficacy of Active Vision**
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** ActiveGlasses consistently outperformed baselines (a variant without active vision and Pi0.5, a vision-language-action model) across all three challenging tasks (Book Placement, Occluded Distant Water Pouring, Bread Insertion).
 - In the final stage (Stage 3) of tasks, ActiveGlasses achieved success rates of 14/20 for Book Placement, 11/20 for Bread Insertion, and 10/20 for Occluded Distant Water Pouring.
 - This represented significant improvements over Pi0.5 (7/20, 6/20, 4/20 respectively) and the "w/o active vision" variant (7/20, 0/20, 4/20 respectively).
- **Addressing Occlusion and Precision:** A fixed single camera (simulated in "w/o active vision") led to poor performance, especially in scenarios with occlusion and high-precision requirements.
- **Stability of Point Cloud Representation:** The use of a point cloud representation stabilized visual observations, allowing the policy to effectively model the joint distribution between manipulation arm actions and perception arm actions (head-hand coordination) even with relatively small datasets.
- **Compensating for Missing Wrist Camera:** The 6-DoF head movements actively compensated for the lack of a wrist-mounted camera by purposefully adjusting viewpoints, enabling robust perception and manipulation under occluded and precise task conditions.

**C. Policy Design Ablations**
- **Object Trajectory Representation:**
 - **Absolute vs. Relative:** Predicting object trajectories in an absolute representation in the world frame was more effective (14/20 success for Book Placement) than a relative representation (10/20 success). The absolute representation made the correlation between observation and action easier for the policy to learn. Relative representation also introduced real-time object pose estimation challenges, which could lead to failures.
 - **Conditioning on Current Object Pose:** Providing the current object pose as an *additional* conditioning signal to the manipulation diffusion head when predicting absolute trajectories degraded performance (3/20 success). The policy tended to overfit to dominant motion patterns, ignoring visual input and making the generated trajectory less responsive to scene variations.
- **Head Trajectory Representation:** Predicting relative head trajectories was found to be crucial. Absolute head trajectory predictions frequently caused the perception arm to move large distances at the beginning of execution, often pushing it to the boundaries of its workspace and increasing the likelihood of inverse kinematics (IK) failures.

**D. Cross-Embodiment Transfer**
- **Zero-Shot Capability:** The object-centric 3D policy successfully enabled zero-shot deployment across different robotic platforms. This was demonstrated by deploying the policy, trained with Flexiv Rizon4, onto a UR5 robot for the Book Placement task.
- **Comparable Performance:** The UR5 robot achieved comparable performance in the initial stages (Stage 1 and 2: 20/20 and 16/20 respectively) to the Flexiv Rizon4.
- **Workspace Limitations:** However, the UR5 experienced more failures in the final placement stage (Stage 3: 11/20), which was attributed to its smaller workspace compared to the Flexiv, leading to reduced flexibility near its operational limits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ActiveGlasses system presents several significant contributions to robot learning, with the potential for substantial impact on the development and deployment of robotic systems:

- **Addressing the Data Scalability Challenge:** By enabling scalable, bare-hand data collection, ActiveGlasses directly tackles the "data hunger" crisis in robot learning. It offers a more efficient and less burdensome alternative to traditional teleoperation or handheld device methods, making large-scale data acquisition more feasible. This could accelerate the development of more generalized and robust robot policies.

- **Pioneering Active Vision in Human Demonstrations:** The system is identified as the first to use smart glasses for collecting human manipulation data with explicit active vision and achieving zero-shot robot transfer. By capturing and leveraging human 6-DoF head movements as a learnable signal, it enables robots to develop sophisticated active perception capabilities, which are crucial for handling complex, real-world scenarios involving occlusions and precise interactions. This moves beyond passive camera setups prevalent in much of current robot learning.

- **Enabling Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The adoption of an object-centric 3D point-cloud policy represents a notable advancement in bridging the morphological gap between human and robot embodiments. By predicting object trajectories rather than embodiment-specific actions, ActiveGlasses facilitates zero-shot transfer across different robot platforms (demonstrated with Flexiv and UR5). This enhances the generalizability and applicability of learned policies, reducing the need for platform-specific training or complex retargeting, thus lowering deployment barriers.

- **Improved Policy Robustness through Unified Representation:** The use of 3D point clouds as input, coupled with an absolute object trajectory and relative head movement output, leads to more stable and robust policy learning. The ablation studies highlight how these design choices prevent overfitting and ensure that the policy effectively utilizes visual information, contributing to higher success rates in challenging tasks.

- **Facilitating Robot Deployment in Everyday Environments:** By making data collection more natural and policies more capable of handling real-world complexities like occlusion, ActiveGlasses contributes to bringing robots closer to everyday deployment. Robots can learn from human demonstrations more intuitively and then execute tasks in dynamic, unstructured environments with a human-like understanding of perception and manipulation.

In summary, ActiveGlasses represents a step toward more intuitive, scalable, and generalizable robot learning from human demonstrations. Its focus on natural human interaction, explicit active vision, and object-centric policy design offers a pathway to developing more capable and adaptable robotic systems for a wider range of real-world applications.
