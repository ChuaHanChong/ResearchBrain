# GenPO: Generative Diffusion Models Meet On-Policy Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> GenPO integrates generative diffusion models into on-policy reinforcement learning by enabling exact log-likelihood computation for diffusion policies. The framework achieves the highest average episodic returns across eight IsaacLab benchmarks, showcasing enhanced training stability and efficiency in parallel simulation environments.

## Problem

- Generative diffusion models were largely incompatible with on-policy reinforcement learning frameworks due to the intractability of computing exact state-action log-likelihoods.
- This incompatibility prevented diffusion policies from fully leveraging the efficiency and scalability of massively parallel GPU-accelerated simulators like IsaacLab, which are optimized for on-policy training.
- Existing diffusion-based RL methods were predominantly confined to offline or off-policy settings, which often face challenges with data distribution shifts and are less efficient for rapid, synchronous data collection.

## Method

- **GenPO** develops a method for *exact ==diffusion inversion==* using invertible flow mappings, enabling precise calculation of state-action log-likelihoods.
- It introduces a *doubled dummy action mechanism* to adapt the action space, allowing for invertibility while the agent executes averaged actions in the original environment.
- The framework integrates these capabilities with PPO-like updates, using exact log-likelihoods for unbiased entropy estimation and analytical KL divergence, along with an auxiliary compression loss to guide exploration.

## Results

- GenPO achieved the highest average episodic returns across all eight IsaacLab benchmarks, consistently outperforming standard PPO and existing diffusion-based off-policy methods.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced training stability and efficiency, showing faster convergence to high reward levels and lower variance in performance on complex robotic tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the compression loss, entropy regularization, and adaptive learning rate adjustments, which are all enabled by the exact log-likelihood computation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exact log-likelihood computation for diffusion policies is achievable through invertible flow mappings, thereby enabling their integration into on-policy RL algorithms.
> - A carefully designed dummy action space can facilitate invertibility requirements of diffusion models without altering the effective action performed in the environment.
> - Auxiliary regularization, such as a compression loss, is crucial for stabilizing learning and preventing meaningless exploration when using an expanded action representation for diffusion policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical gap at the intersection of generative diffusion models and reinforcement learning (RL), specifically within the on-policy paradigm.

**Generative Models in RL:** In recent years, generative models, particularly diffusion models (e.g., DDPM, DDIM, flow matching), have gained significant traction in RL. Their inherent strengths lie in their ability to model complex, multimodal distributions, offering powerful exploration capabilities compared to traditional Gaussian policies. This makes them highly suitable for tasks requiring diverse action choices or robust adaptation to varied environments. Existing research has predominantly explored these models in two main RL settings:
- **Offline RL:** Here, policies are learned from fixed, pre-collected datasets without further environment interaction. Diffusion models have shown promise in accurately modeling behavior distributions from these datasets, leading to improved generalization and performance (e.g., Diffusion-QL, EDP, SRDP).
- **Off-policy RL:** In this setting, agents learn from data collected by a different, often older, policy, leveraging replay buffers. Diffusion policies have been integrated with off-policy algorithms like SAC, where they can be updated using Q-function gradients (e.g., DIPO, QVPO, DACER).

**The Underexplored On-Policy Frontier:** Despite the successes in offline and off-policy RL, the integration of diffusion policies into *on-policy* RL frameworks, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), has remained largely unexplored. This is a significant omission, as on-policy algorithms are crucial for applications leveraging massively parallel GPU-accelerated simulators like NVIDIA's Isaac Gym and IsaacLab. These simulators are optimized for rapid, synchronous data collection and policy updates characteristic of on-policy methods, enabling the fast training of complex robotic tasks in a fraction of the wall-clock time compared to off-policy counterparts.

**The Core Technical Challenge:** The primary reason for this gap is a fundamental technical barrier: on-policy RL algorithms, especially PPO, require the computation of state-action log-likelihoods (i.e., the probability density of an action given a state) for policy updates (e.g., for importance sampling ratios and KL divergence estimation). While straightforward for simple Gaussian policies with closed-form densities, this computation is intractable for most generative diffusion models. The non-reversibility and inconsistencies between the forward (data-to-noise) and reverse (noise-to-data) processes, particularly due to discretization errors (e.g., Euler-Maruyama approximations), prevent direct log-likelihood calculation via the change of variables formula. Previous diffusion-based RL methods have either circumvented this by operating in off-policy/offline settings or relied on heuristic approximations for policy improvement.

**GenPO's Contribution to the Landscape:** GenPO directly tackles this critical problem by introducing a novel framework that enables *exact* log-likelihood computation for diffusion policies within an on-policy RL paradigm. By successfully integrating diffusion models with PPO-like updates, GenPO unlocks the potential of diffusion policies to leverage the unparalleled speed and scalability of modern parallel simulators like IsaacLab. This bridges a crucial methodological gap, extending the benefits of generative policies—such as improved exploration and multimodality—to a highly efficient and widely adopted class of RL algorithms, thereby facilitating their practical deployment in complex robotic control tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives and motivations driving the development of GenPO are multifold:

1. **Bridge the On-Policy/Diffusion Gap:** The primary objective is to enable the stable and effective integration of generative diffusion policies into on-policy reinforcement learning frameworks. This addresses the significant void where powerful diffusion models, with their advanced generative capabilities, have been largely incompatible with the high-efficiency, on-policy training paradigms prevalent in modern robotics.
2. **Overcome Log-Likelihood Intractability:** The core technical motivation is to resolve the fundamental challenge of computing exact state-action log-likelihoods for diffusion policies. This computation is indispensable for on-policy algorithms that rely on probability density ratios, entropy regularization, and KL divergence constraints for stable policy updates. Without it, diffusion policies cannot be reliably optimized in an on-policy manner.
3. **Leverage Large-Scale Parallel Simulators:** A significant practical motivation is to allow diffusion policies to fully utilize the benefits of massively parallel GPU-accelerated simulators like IsaacLab. These platforms are optimized for on-policy RL, offering rapid training of complex, high-dimensional robotic tasks. Integrating diffusion policies would enable them to achieve substantial performance gains in real-world wall-clock time, accelerating research and deployment.
4. **Enhance Exploration and Multimodality:** By bringing diffusion policies to on-policy RL, GenPO aims to harness their inherent advantages: superior exploration capabilities (due to modeling a distribution rather than a single action output) and the ability to generate multimodal action distributions. This contrasts with traditional Gaussian policies, which often struggle with exploration in complex environments or representing diverse optimal behaviors.
5. **Achieve Robust and Efficient Policy Optimization:** The goal is to develop an on-policy framework that is not only capable of integrating diffusion policies but also ensures robust training, fast convergence, and high final performance, comparable to or exceeding current state-of-the-art on-policy methods like PPO, while retaining the benefits of generative models.

In essence, GenPO is motivated by the desire to merge the best of both worlds: the expressive power and exploration capacity of generative diffusion models with the sample efficiency, stability, and parallel scalability of on-policy reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenPO proposes a novel generative policy optimization framework built upon three core technical innovations to integrate diffusion models into on-policy reinforcement learning: exact diffusion inversion, a doubled dummy action mechanism, and the use of exact log-likelihoods for unbiased entropy and KL divergence estimation, complemented by a compression loss.

1. **Exact Diffusion Inversion for Invertible Flow Mappings:**
 - **Problem:** Traditional diffusion models suffer from an inconsistency between their forward (data-to-noise) and reverse (noise-to-data) processes, especially due to discretization approximations (e.g., Euler-Maruyama). This inconsistency prevents exact inversion and thus direct computation of log-likelihoods using the change of variables theorem, which is crucial for on-policy RL.
 - **Solution:** GenPO adopts the principle of exact diffusion inversion, inspired by methods like EDICT. This involves constructing a truly invertible flow mapping by maintaining and alternately updating two coupled "noise" vectors (or components of the action representation) during both the forward and reverse processes of the generative diffusion model. This ensures that the mapping from the standard Gaussian distribution (noise) to the action distribution is perfectly reversible.
 - **Flow Matching Integration:** The method extends this concept to flow matching models, which are generally faster for generation than classical diffusion models, allowing for efficient inference.

2. **Doubled Dummy Action Mechanism:**
 - **Problem:** The exact diffusion inversion technique, as typically formulated, requires a doubled sample space to maintain invertibility (e.g., maintaining two coupled components). This poses a challenge if the original action space has an odd dimension or if simply doubling the action space introduces unnecessary complexity and exploration issues.
 - **Solution:** GenPO reformulates the original Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a "doubled dummy action space" $\tilde{A}$. Each dummy action $\tilde{a}$ consists of two components, $(x, y)$, which are independently sampled from standard Gaussian noise (unlike some prior works that sample one noise and duplicate it). During policy execution (inference), these two components are averaged (i.e., $a = \frac{x+y}{2}$) to produce a single action in the original action space. This re-framing ensures compatibility with the doubled action space requirement of invertible diffusion dynamics without altering the fundamental action performed in the environment.

3. **Exact Log-Likelihood Computation and On-Policy Utilities:**
 - **Enabling Log-Likelihood:** With the invertible flow mapping and the doubled dummy action space, GenPO can now compute the *exact* probability density (log-likelihood) of any given state-action pair using the change of variables theorem. This is a critical breakthrough for integrating diffusion policies into on-policy RL.
 - **Unbiased Entropy Estimation:** The availability of exact log-likelihoods allows for unbiased estimation of the diffusion policy's entropy. This enables the incorporation of entropy regularization into the policy objective, which is vital for encouraging exploration and preventing premature convergence to suboptimal policies, a standard practice in robust RL algorithms like PPO and SAC.
 - **Analytical KL Divergence Estimation:** Similarly, GenPO can compute an unbiased estimate of the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the current policy and the behavior policy. This is essential for implementing KL-adaptive learning rate adjustments, a key mechanism in PPO for maintaining policy stability and preventing large, destabilizing updates.
 - **PPO Integration:** These capabilities allow GenPO to seamlessly integrate with the PPO algorithm's clipped surrogate objective, replacing the Gaussian policy's log-likelihood with the computed diffusion policy's log-likelihood.

4. **Auxiliary Compression Loss:**
 - **Problem:** The doubled dummy action space $\tilde{a} = (x, y)$ could lead to "meaningless exploration." For instance, if the optimal action is 0, both $(-1, 1)$ and $(-2, 2)$ could represent optimal dummy actions, implying that $x$ and $y$ could diverge unnecessarily, wasting learning capacity.
 - **Solution:** To mitigate this, GenPO introduces a compression loss term, $E_{x_1, y_1 \sim \pi_\theta} [(x_1 - y_1)^2]$, which encourages the $x$ and $y$ components of the dummy action to remain closely aligned. This loss, combined with a "mixing scheme" in the reverse process (where $x$ and $y$ components are averaged with a coefficient $p$), promotes information exchange and ensures the policy converges to dummy actions with minimal discrepancy, thus focusing exploration on relevant action variations.

The final policy loss combines the PPO clipped surrogate objective, entropy regularization, and the compression loss, allowing for end-to-end training of the diffusion policy within the on-policy framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GenPO's experimental evaluation on eight IsaacLab benchmarks demonstrates its superior performance and effectiveness in integrating diffusion policies with on-policy reinforcement learning.

1. **Superior Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** GenPO consistently achieves the highest average episodic returns across all eight IsaacLab benchmarks, which include complex legged locomotion (Ant, Humanoid, Anymal-D, Unitree H1, Go2), dexterous manipulation (Shadow Hand), aerial control (Quadcopter), and robotic arm tasks (Franka). This includes environments known to be challenging for RL algorithms.
2. **Outperformance Over Baselines:**
 - **Standard On-Policy (PPO):** GenPO significantly outperforms PPO, indicating that the generative diffusion policy offers advantages in exploration and expressivity over standard Gaussian policies, even within the same on-policy framework. For example, on Humanoid, GenPO achieves 273.94 average reward compared to PPO's 197.25.
 - **Off-Policy Methods (DDPG, TD3, SAC):** GenPO strongly surpasses these off-policy baselines. The paper attributes this to the benefits of synchronized data collection in on-policy settings, which enhances training stability and convergence speed in large-scale parallel simulators where off-policy methods often struggle with data distribution shifts in replay buffers.
 - **Diffusion-Based Off-Policy Methods (DACER, QVPO):** Notably, GenPO also significantly outperforms existing diffusion-based off-policy algorithms. This highlights the particular difficulty these methods face in large-scale parallel settings and their inability to track rapidly shifting data distributions, a problem that GenPO, as an on-policy method, inherently avoids.
3. **Enhanced Training Stability and Efficiency:**
 - GenPO achieves near-optimal performance with comparable sample efficiency and faster convergence than baselines. Learning curves show rapid ascent to high reward levels.
 - On challenging tasks like Unitree robots and Shadow Hand manipulation, GenPO exhibits not only higher returns but also lower variance (smaller shaded areas in learning curves, lower standard deviations in tables), indicating greater training stability and reliability.
4. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Compression Loss:** The compression loss coefficient ($\nu$) is crucial for performance. A value of $\nu=0.01$ was found to strike the best balance, preventing unnecessary exploration in the doubled action space while promoting stability. Without it ($\nu=0$), convergence slows; too high ($\nu=0.5$ or $1.0$), it overly constrains the policy, leading to poor results.
 - **Entropy and Learning Rate Adaptation:** Both components, enabled by GenPO's exact log-likelihood computation, are vital. Adaptive learning rate adjustment (based on KL divergence) leads to faster and more stable convergence, while entropy regularization enhances exploration and yields higher overall returns.
 - **Mixing Coefficient ($p$):** A mixing coefficient of $p=0.9$ was optimal. Lower values caused numerical instability in the forward mixing process, while higher values introduced redundant exploration, slowing convergence.
5. **Computational Efficiency (Supplementary):**
 - **Inference Time:** While slightly longer than Gaussian policies due to multiple denoising iterations, GenPO's inference time (e.g., 2.577ms for Ant-v0) is significantly more efficient than previous diffusion-based RL algorithms (e.g., QVPO, DACER) and deemed sufficient for real-time robotic control.
 - **Training Time:** On a single GPU, GenPO's training is slower than PPO but still superior to other diffusion-based RL methods. Crucially, GenPO effectively leverages multi-GPU parallelism, significantly accelerating training, whereas traditional PPO shows diminishing returns with more GPUs. This highlights GenPO's capability to scale with modern hardware.
6. **Impact of Design Choices (Supplementary):**
 - **Parallel Environments:** Increasing the number of parallel environments (up to 4096) generally improves learning performance, with diminishing returns beyond a certain point.
 - **Flow Policy Steps ($T$):** The policy is robust to the number of flow steps, performing well as long as $T \geq 2$.
 - **Dummy Action Interpolation ($\alpha$):** Averaging the two dummy action components (i.e., $\alpha=0.5$) yields the best performance, confirming the benefit of equally leveraging both components for exploration diversity.
 - **Time Step Embedding:** Sinusoidal time embeddings significantly improve the policy's ability to utilize temporal information, leading to better performance compared to simple scalar concatenation.

In summary, GenPO delivers on its promise, establishing generative diffusion policies as a viable and highly effective approach for on-policy reinforcement learning, particularly in computationally intensive, large-scale simulation environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GenPO represents a significant advancement in the field of reinforcement learning by successfully integrating generative diffusion models into on-policy frameworks, an area previously underexplored due to fundamental technical challenges. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Unlocking Diffusion Policies for On-Policy RL:** The most critical contribution is bridging the long-standing gap between generative diffusion models and on-policy RL algorithms like PPO. By devising a method for exact log-likelihood computation and unbiased estimation of entropy and KL divergence, GenPO enables the use of expressive diffusion policies in a robust and widely adopted RL paradigm. This is a foundational step that opens up new avenues for research and application.
2. **Leveraging Massively Parallel Simulators:** GenPO allows diffusion policies to fully capitalize on the power of modern GPU-accelerated parallel simulators (e.g., IsaacLab). These simulators are highly optimized for on-policy data collection and offer unprecedented speed for training complex robotic skills. Prior diffusion methods were largely incompatible with this ecosystem, limiting their practical scalability. GenPO directly addresses this, enabling faster experimentation and deployment of diffusion-based agents in simulated and real-world robotics.
3. **Enhanced Exploration and Multimodality in On-Policy Learning:** Diffusion policies are known for their strong exploration capabilities and ability to model multimodal action distributions. By bringing these advantages to on-policy RL, GenPO can lead to agents that learn more diverse and robust behaviors, especially in environments with sparse rewards, complex dynamics, or multiple optimal strategies. This could significantly improve performance on challenging tasks where traditional Gaussian policies might struggle.
4. **Foundation for Future Research:** GenPO provides a strong methodological foundation for future work at the intersection of generative models and on-policy RL. Researchers can now explore variations of invertible diffusion dynamics, more sophisticated integration strategies with PPO-like objectives, and applications to an even wider range of complex control problems.
5. **Towards Real-World Robotic Deployment:** The combination of powerful generative policies, efficient on-policy training, and scalable simulation makes GenPO a promising candidate for real-world robotic deployment. Rapid training in high-fidelity simulators is a crucial step towards transferring policies to physical robots, and GenPO's capabilities in this regard are a substantial step forward.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its impressive achievements, the paper acknowledges certain limitations and outlines directions for future work, primarily concerning computational efficiency.
- **Computational and Memory Overhead:** The exact diffusion inversion, while enabling log-likelihood computation, incurs a relatively high computational and memory overhead, mainly due to the need to compute Jacobian determinants. While multi-GPU parallelism mitigates this, it remains a challenge, particularly for scaling to even more complex models or very long diffusion chains.

Future work will focus on optimizing the computational and memory efficiency of GenPO. This could involve exploring more efficient approximations for Jacobian determinants, developing specialized network architectures, or investigating alternative invertible generative models that inherently offer lower computational costs while retaining expressive power. Addressing these efficiency concerns will be key to facilitating GenPO's broader adoption and deployment in even more demanding real-world applications.

In conclusion, GenPO represents a landmark contribution by breaking down a fundamental barrier between diffusion models and on-policy reinforcement learning, thus expanding the horizons for developing advanced, capable, and scalable intelligent agents for complex control tasks.
