# LRC-JEPA: Disentangling Dynamics and Residual Context for Efficient World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LRC-JEPA is a small JEPA world model trained end to end. It sends action-driven dynamics into a compact predictive latent and slow-changing visual context into learned-query residual embeddings, which serve only reconstruction. Planning rolls forward the predictive latent alone. It adds 9 points of planning success over a same-size JEPA, and its 5.5M-parameter encoder beats DINO-WM and V-JEPA2 on Bridge-v2.

## Problem

- Compact single-latent JEPA world models such as LeWorldModel must squeeze action-conditioned dynamics and predictable visual context into one low-dimensional vector, so controllable state gets tangled with nuisance appearance.
- SIGReg anti-collapse regularization fixes scale and decorrelates features but never lowers mutual information with the input, so nuisance information can fill the latent and minimality is not guaranteed.
- Rich patch-feature world models like DINO-WM and V-JEPA2 keep fine detail but make repeated test-time planning expensive. Single-latent models degrade under PCA compression and static visual distractors.

## Method

- ==Functional routing architecture==: a ViT-Tiny class token feeds a compact predictive latent z, while ==learned-query cross-attention pooling== over all tokens produces residual-context embeddings u that never enter the dynamics predictor.
- ==Action-conditioned causal transformer predictor== with ==AdaLN-Zero== rolls forward only z under teacher forcing. ==CEM-based MPC== plans by terminal latent distance on z alone, keeping planning cheap.
- **Four-term objective**: latent prediction, ==SIGReg== on z, ==temporal VICReg-style stability== on u, and cross-attention patch reconstruction from z plus u. A differentiable residual path pushes complementary dynamic content into z.

## Results

- Across Push-T, Reacher, TwoRoom and Cube, average planning success rises **9 points** over parameter-matched LeWorldModel. It scores best on Push-T and Reacher and is competitive with pretrained DINO-WM, V-JEPA2 and C-JEPA.
- On Bridge-v2 expert-action recall@1/5/10 reaches **0.38/0.64/0.72**, versus **0.36/0.54/0.62** for V-JEPA2 (303.9M) and **0.06/0.12/0.18** for LeWorldModel. Planning takes **0.45s** versus **57.56s** for DINO-WM.
- Stop-gradient on the residual path drops Push-T success from **96%** to **92%** and Bridge recall@1 from **0.38** to **0.10**. Probes improve joint-velocity correlation from **0.111** to **0.392** over LeWorldModel.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anti-collapse regularizers decide the latent's distribution but not its content. Something has to actively push nuisance out of the planning state, or limited capacity goes to waste.
> - A reconstruction-only side channel can absorb persistent appearance and leave the planning latent compact and kinematic. Splitting roles between training and inference costs nothing at planning time.
> - Gradients flowing from the reconstruction decoder into the predictive latent are load-bearing, especially on visually diverse real data. Detaching them yields a latent that is predictable but insufficient for control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

In offline, reward-free image-goal control, a world model learns from recorded images and actions, predicts what candidate actions will do, and plans toward a goal image. The difficulty is that one compact representation must carry both the changing, action-relevant state and stable visual details such as background or appearance. A small state makes repeated planning cheaper. It may also lose useful dynamics or waste capacity on context.

LRC-JEPA keeps a compact state for planning and sends persistent visual context through a separate route. The authors tested this with a stress test in the TwoRoom environment. They added the same trajectory-persistent pattern to the current and goal images. The pattern hurts both LRC-JEPA and the comparison model, LeWorldModel (LWM). Across the tested opacity levels, LRC-JEPA's measured success curve remains above LWM's. Both methods decline, and the result holds for this controlled setting. It does not prove general robustness.

(b) Success rate against the opacity α of a trajectory-persistent, action-independent pattern blended into current and goal observations. The blue LRC-JEPA curve remains above the orange LWM curve as both fall with greater opacity.

### 2. One state for control, one for context

A shared per-frame ViT produces a class token and patch tokens. The model projects the class token to a compact latent **z**. An action-conditioned predictor learns how z evolves from one frame to the next. Learned queries attend to the full token set and produce bottlenecked context embeddings **u**, which summarize spatial information. A temporal regularizer encourages u to stay stable within a trajectory while preserving variation across trajectories. A cross-attention decoder then reconstructs the frame from z and u.

Reconstruction gradients flow into both streams, including z through the residual connection. At test time, planning uses only z. The context stream and decoder do not take part. This design is a functional bias. It does not guarantee that every slowly changing feature is irrelevant to control.

LRC-JEPA architecture. During training, a shared frame encoder feeds a class-token route to predictive z and a learned-query cross-attention route to u, with an action-conditioned predictor and a reconstruction decoder. During planning, candidate actions pass through the predictor under CEM optimization toward a terminal latent distance. The context encoder and reconstruction decoder do not participate.

### 3. Does the split change planning?

**Bridge-v2.** Bridge-v2 is a real-robot dataset, and the test here is offline expert-action recovery rather than closed-loop robot success. The model receives 300 random candidate action sequences plus the demonstrated expert sequence. It ranks them by predicted terminal latent distance. Recall@1/5/10 is the fraction of cases in which the expert sequence ranks in the top 1, 5, or 10. LRC-JEPA scores **0.38/0.64/0.72**, versus **0.06/0.12/0.18** for the parameter-matched LWM. Both models have 5.5M active planning parameters.

**Simulated planning.** Against the parameter-matched LWM, LRC-JEPA improves average planning success by **9 percentage points across four simulated environments**. It achieves the highest success on Push-T and Reacher and is competitive on TwoRoom and Cube.

**Cost.** DINO-WM predicts frozen pretrained spatial patch features. Measured full planning time per trajectory is **0.45 s for LRC-JEPA versus 57.56 s for DINO-WM**, averaged over 50 trajectories on one RTX 5090 GPU. This time includes online current/goal image encoding and all CEM solves. It excludes checkpoint loading, environment stepping, video generation, and serialization.

**Stop-gradient ablation.** This ablation blocks the reconstruction gradient from z. Push-T success changes from **96% to 92%**. On Bridge-v2, recall@1/5/10 changes from **0.38/0.64/0.72 to 0.10/0.28/0.32**, and normalized expert rank (lower is better) worsens from **0.061 to 0.286**. The authors interpret this as evidence that reconstruction gradients help separate context from predictive state. The intervention supports the gradient's importance, but it does not prove the gradient is the only cause of the gain.

Effect of blocking reconstruction gradients at z. LRC-JEPA: Push-T 96.0%, Bridge recall@1/5/10 0.38/0.64/0.72, rank percentile 0.061. With stop-gradient: 92.0%, 0.10/0.28/0.32, 0.286. Lower rank percentile is better.

### 4. What the compact state buys

LRC-JEPA keeps image information available for learning through a separate context stream, while the planner rolls out only the compact predictive state. It also outperforms a parameter-matched single-latent baseline. The real-world result is offline action ranking, not physical robot execution. The theory holds under idealized assumptions, including an appearance nuisance that stays constant within a trajectory. The authors also warn that rapidly changing nuisance factors may be routed imperfectly.
