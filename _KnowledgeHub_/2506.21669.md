# SEEA-R1: Tree-Structured Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for Self-Evolving Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The SEEA-R1 framework enables embodied agents to autonomously improve their reasoning and behavior in long-horizon tasks through tree-structured reinforcement fine-tuning. This framework achieved a 46.27% success rate on the ALFWorld benchmark, exceeding GPT-4o's 24%, and demonstrated a 34.72% absolute improvement in real-world success rates with reflection and correction capabilities.

## Problem

- Embodied agents struggle with long-horizon, multi-step planning and maintaining coherence in dynamic, open-ended environments.
- Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) in embodied settings suffers from sparse intermediate rewards, hindering effective credit assignment for multi-step actions.
- RFT's dependence on hand-crafted, environment-specific reward functions limits agents' generalization to novel tasks and diverse environments.

## Method

![[2506.21669-method.png]]
*SEEA-R1 framework: an iterative self-evolution loop of Data Evolution (Policy Model interacts with the environment via MCTS to generate trajectories with Q-values and rewards) and Model Evolution (updating the Policy Model and Reward Model).*

- Introduced **SEEA-R1**, an iterative self-evolution framework with Data and Model Evolution cycles for continuous agent improvement.
- Utilized ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== in Data Evolution to generate dense process rewards (Q-values) from sparse environmental signals.
- Developed a ==Multi-modal Generative Reward Model (MGRM)== for generalized, task-agnostic reward estimation and updated the agent's policy using ==Tree-GRPO== for effective credit assignment.

## Results

- SEEA-R1 achieved a **46.27%** success rate on ALFWorld (MLLM setting), outperforming GPT-4o's **24%**, and an **85.07%** success rate in the text-only setting, comparable to GPT-4o's **81.1%**.
- In real-world physical experiments, SEEA-R1 demonstrated a **34.72%** absolute improvement in overall success rate compared to baselines, exhibiting reflection and correction capabilities.
- The self-trained MGRM achieved a **91.67%** prediction accuracy and enabled continuous success rate improvements in self-supervised settings, even on out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Monte Carlo Tree Search effectively transforms sparse outcome rewards into dense, step-wise Q-values, improving credit assignment for long-horizon tasks.
> - A Multi-modal Generative Reward Model (MGRM) can learn to provide reliable internal reward signals, enabling self-supervised policy improvement and reducing dependence on handcrafted rewards.
> - The Tree-GRPO algorithm, which integrates MCTS with Group Relative Policy Optimization, consistently outperforms DPO and SFT for embodied agent policy updates, indicating its stability and effectiveness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied agents are designed to operate in complex, interactive environments, requiring not only foundational perception and motor control but also high-level reasoning, planning, and decision-making over extended time horizons. Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have enhanced agents' abstract reasoning and perceptual capabilities in various domains. However, transferring these abilities to open-ended embodied settings presents challenges. LLMs often lack perceptual grounding, while MLLMs can struggle with structured multi-step planning, maintaining long-term coherence, and adapting to dynamic environments due to their interactive nature and continuous visual feedback requirements.

The concept of "self-evolution"—where agents autonomously generate training signals and refine their reasoning through closed-loop learning from experience—is proposed as a solution to bridge the gap between perception and cognition, enabling generalization to novel tasks and development of long-horizon planning. Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) has emerged as a promising paradigm within this context, utilizing reward-based feedback for policy refinement. However, applying RFT to embodied agents introduces two specific obstacles: first, the sparsity of intermediate rewards in long-horizon, multi-step tasks complicates effective credit assignment, thereby limiting learning signals; and second, the reliance on hand-crafted or environment-specific reward functions hinders generalization to novel tasks and diverse environments.

This work positions itself within this landscape by introducing SEEA-R1 as an RFT framework specifically designed for self-evolving embodied agents. It builds upon existing research in self-evolution learning, which emphasizes learning from experience and integrating reward mechanisms and perception-action loops. It also draws from advancements in planning for embodied agents that leverage LLMs/MLLMs for sequential decision-making, as well as reinforcement fine-tuning techniques that enhance reasoning and grounding in LLMs and MLLMs across various domains. SEEA-R1 aims to address the identified RFT challenges in embodied settings by integrating a tree-based search mechanism for denser reward signals and a learned multi-modal reward model for improved generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable self-evolving capabilities in embodied agents, allowing them to autonomously improve their reasoning and behavior in long-horizon, real-world tasks. This overarching goal is motivated by the recognized need for agents to adapt and generalize beyond static, supervised learning paradigms through continuous interaction and learning from their own experiences.

Specifically, the work is motivated by two fundamental challenges faced when applying Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) to embodied intelligence:

1. **Lack of intermediate feedback in multi-step reasoning tasks:** Embodied tasks often involve long sequences of actions with sparse, delayed rewards, meaning feedback is only received upon task completion or failure. This makes it difficult to assign credit or blame to individual intermediate decisions, which is crucial for effective policy learning through RFT. The agent lacks dense, step-wise learning signals to guide its multi-step reasoning processes.

2. **Poor generalization of handcrafted reward functions:** Traditional RFT pipelines frequently rely on reward signals that are specifically engineered for a particular simulator or task. Such hand-crafted reward functions often do not transfer effectively to new environments or tasks, thereby restricting the agent's ability to self-improve across diverse and unseen scenarios. This dependency limits scalability and autonomy in open-ended settings.

To address these motivations, SEEA-R1 sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

- **Convert sparse, delayed rewards into denser intermediate signals:** This objective aims to overcome the credit assignment problem by providing more immediate, step-by-step feedback that can improve multi-step reasoning.
- **Generalize reward estimation across tasks and scenes:** This objective seeks to eliminate the reliance on hand-crafted reward functions, thereby supporting unsupervised adaptation and enabling reward-driven self-evolution in novel and varied environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEEA-R1 is structured as a framework for continuous improvement through an iterative loop comprising two core cycles: Data Evolution and Model Evolution. This closed-loop system allows the agent to progressively enhance its reasoning, reward understanding, and task execution capabilities.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The SEEA-R1 framework operates by alternating between generating experience data and updating its constituent models. In the Data Evolution phase, the agent interacts with the environment to generate experiential data. This data is then used in the Model Evolution phase to refine both the agent's policy model and a dedicated reward model. The improved models are subsequently employed in the next Data Evolution cycle, creating a self-improvement loop.

#### 3.2. Data Evolution: Experience Generation via MCTS

To address the challenge of sparse outcome rewards in multi-step reasoning, SEEA-R1 employs Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for generating experience. MCTS converts sparse environmental signals into dense process rewards by estimating action-values, Q(s_t, a_t,i), which represent the expected future reward for taking action a_t,i in state s_t. The precision of these Q-values improves as MCTS performs more simulations, exploring a wider range of trajectories. This process guides learning with immediate, step-by-step feedback.

The MCTS implementation follows four steps:
- **Selection:** Starting from the root node, the agent traverses the tree by selecting child nodes with the highest Upper Confidence Bound for Trees (UCT) values, balancing exploration and exploitation.
- **Expansion:** Upon reaching a leaf node, the agent executes the selected action, observes the result, and expands the new non-leaf node with a set of candidate natural language actions.
- **Simulation:** From the new node, multiple rollouts are simulated until task completion, giving up, or reaching a predefined depth limit, generating full trajectories.
- **Backup:** The outcomes of these rollouts are propagated upwards through the tree to update visit counts, cumulative rewards, and action values (Q-values) along each path.

To manage computational complexity, MCTS in SEEA-R1 utilizes two-level pruning: Probabilistic Expansion (50% chance to expand all K actions per node) and a Strict Path Budget (a hard limit L=5 on full K-expansions per path).

#### 3.3. Model Evolution: Co-refining Policy Model and Reward Model

##### 3.3.1. Multi-modal Generative Reward Model (MGRM) Training

To generalize reward estimation and reduce reliance on handcrafted functions, SEEA-R1 introduces MGRM. This reward model is built upon a Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) and takes historical context (s_t) as input to predict one of three categorical outcomes: "success," "continue," or "failure."

MGRM training follows two paradigms:
- **With Ground Truth (GT) (Supervised Paradigm):** MGRM is initially fine-tuned using supervised learning on GT-aligned trajectories (e.g., ALFWorld task completion signals) to establish performance and mitigate bias. During co-training with the policy, MGRM continues supervised training, calibrating against GT rewards every 500 episodes to prevent drift.
- **Without Ground Truth (GT) (Self-supervised Paradigm):** In scenarios lacking GT rewards, MGRM employs Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The policy generates K diverse trajectories (K=10) per initial state via MCTS. MGRM's majority-voted predictions across these trajectories serve as pseudo-GT, and MGRM is trained via GRPO (reward = +1 for matching pseudo-GT, 0 otherwise).

##### 3.3.2. Embodied Agent Policy Update through Tree-GRPO

The embodied agent's policy model, π_θ, is updated using Tree-GRPO, an extension of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that integrates MCTS. The GRPO loss function is applied to groups of actions at each node of the tree-structured experiential data. This loss incorporates an importance sampling ratio, an advantage function (normalized Q-values relative to the group mean), and a KL divergence regularization term to prevent large policy shifts from a reference policy. The MCTS-generated Q-values serve as dense process rewards for calculating the advantage function, thereby providing effective credit assignment for multi-step sequences.

#### 3.4. Iterative Self-Evolution Loop

The continuous improvement of SEEA-R1 results from the iterative deployment of updated Embodied Agent and MGRM models from the Model Evolution phase into the subsequent Data Evolution phase. This cyclical process allows for progressive enhancement of the agent’s reasoning, reward understanding, and task execution abilities. The Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models are used as base models for the embodied agents.

#### 3.5. Environment and Evaluation

The primary environment for training and evaluation is ALFWorld, which offers both textual and multi-modal observations for long-horizon tasks. Evaluation primarily focuses on the "test-unseen" split to assess out-of-distribution generalization. Further evaluation is conducted on EmbodiedEval, an out-of-distribution multi-modal benchmark for diverse tasks and scenes, and in real-world physical experiments using a dual-arm ARX LIFT 2 with Mobile ALOHA-style teleoperation. The main evaluation metric is the Average Success Rate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of SEEA-R1 across various settings and benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Comparison on ALFWorld

SEEA-R1 exhibited strong performance on the ALFWorld benchmark.
- In the text+visual (MLLM) setting, SEEA-R1 achieved a 46.27% success rate, outperforming GPT-4o (24%) and other leading open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL at 11%). It also showed efficiency with an average of 23.37 steps, compared to GPT-4o's 25.10 steps.
- In the text-only (LLM) setting, SEEA-R1 attained an 85.07% success rate, which is comparable to GPT-4o's 81.1%, and an average of 14.75 completion steps.
- When operating without ground truth rewards (self-supervised paradigm) using its MGRM, SEEA-R1 still achieved 44.03% (MLLM) and 80.30% (textual) success rates, surpassing all open-source baselines in this setting.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

In physical real-world scenarios, SEEA-R1 demonstrated a 34.72% absolute improvement in overall success rate compared to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct baseline. It exhibited reflection and correction capabilities, for instance, by identifying and rectifying errors like attempting to place clothes into a closed washing machine. Notable gains were observed in complex environments like kitchens and children’s rooms.

#### 4.3. Comparative Study of Training Algorithms

Tree-GRPO consistently outperformed MCTS combined with DPO and MCTS with SFT on ALFWorld. Tree-GRPO achieved a peak success rate of 32.46%, significantly higher than MCTS+DPO's 25.37% and MCTS+SFT's 16.79%. This suggests that the on-policy nature of GRPO, when integrated with MCTS, provides more stable and effective policy improvement for long-horizon embodied tasks.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis

SEEA-R1 incorporates algorithmic optimizations, such as MCTS pruning strategies, to reduce its complexity from O(b^d) to O(T · max(D, K)). For inference, MCTS is not used; instead, SEEA-R1 employs a fast ReAct-style single-path sampling approach. This resulted in a 3.1 times higher success rate with 38.8% fewer tokens compared to the baseline at inference time.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study: Self-Evolution with Different Rewards

The study showed that SEEA-R1, using its self-trained Multi-modal Generative Reward Model (MGRM), can effectively learn and improve its policy.
- In the supervised paradigm, the MGRM enabled performance that approached the upper bound set by ground truth rewards.
- In the self-supervised paradigm, the self-supervised MGRM drove continuous success rate improvements, even surpassing the GT reward baseline in later iterations. This highlights MGRM's capacity to provide internal reward signals for robust self-evolution.
- The reward model accuracy analysis showed that the GRPO-trained MGRM achieved a prediction accuracy of 91.67% for task states ("success," "continue," "failure"), significantly improving upon a frozen, untrained MGRM baseline which had an accuracy of 51.59%.

#### 4.6. Generalization on Out-of-Distribution Embodied Benchmark

On the EmbodiedEval benchmark, SEEA-R1 (with GT reward) achieved an overall success rate of 19.88%. This represents an improvement over the base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (18.29%) and models fine-tuned with SFT or DPO (both 16.77%), indicating the strong generalization performance of the Tree-GRPO approach on unseen scenarios.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Analysis of Reasoning Traces

Qualitative analysis of reasoning traces for the task "find two CDs and put them in the safe" showed an evolution in agent capabilities. A baseline model exhibited repetitive failed actions. By iteration 5, SEEA-R1 demonstrated adaptive reasoning by attempting alternative strategies upon failure. By iteration 10, the model displayed structured planning and targeted corrective reasoning, outlining clear multi-step plans and efficiently identifying fixes for failed actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SEEA-R1 introduces a novel framework for training self-evolving embodied agents using reinforcement fine-tuning, addressing two core challenges: sparse rewards and limited generalization in embodied domains. Its primary significance lies in its capacity to enable agents to learn and improve autonomously from interactions without continuous human supervision or reliance on hand-crafted reward functions.

The introduction of **Tree-GRPO** is significant because it provides a mechanism to convert sparse, delayed outcome rewards into denser, step-wise learning signals. By integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search into Group Relative Policy Optimization, Tree-GRPO facilitates more effective credit assignment across long-horizon, multi-step reasoning tasks, which is crucial for training policies in complex embodied environments.

The **Multi-modal Generative Reward Model (MGRM)** represents a contribution towards task-agnostic reward estimation. By learning to predict task outcomes from multi-modal, multi-turn trajectories, MGRM reduces the dependency on environment-specific or hand-crafted reward signals. This enhances the generalizability and adaptability of embodied agents to novel tasks and unseen environments, supporting unsupervised adaptation and reward-driven self-evolution. The demonstrated high accuracy of the trained MGRM confirms its ability to provide reliable internal feedback.

The experimental results underscore the potential impact of SEEA-R1. Achieving state-of-the-art performance on the ALFWorld benchmark, even surpassing models like GPT-4o, in both supervised and self-supervised settings, indicates the framework's effectiveness in enhancing planning, reasoning, adaptability, and generalization. Its performance in real-world experiments, including the exhibition of reflection-correction capabilities, suggests its applicability beyond simulations. The strong generalization on the out-of-distribution EmbodiedEval benchmark further highlights its robustness to domain shifts.

By providing a framework that enables human-free, continuous self-improvement for embodied agents, SEEA-R1 has the potential to advance research in scalable embodied intelligence. The commitment to open-sourcing the framework and its modular components, including the reward model and training pipelines, is expected to facilitate further research and development within the embodied AI community.
