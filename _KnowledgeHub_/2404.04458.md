# JRDB-Social: A Multifaceted Robotic Dataset for Understanding of Context and Dynamics of Human Interactions Within Social Groups

> [!summary] Summary
> JRDB-Social extends the JRDB robot dataset with three-level social annotations: individual demographic attributes, frame-level intra-group interaction labels across 20 categories, and text-captioned group-level context (position, scene content, venue, purpose). Evaluating current multi-modal LLMs on this benchmark shows they struggle to reason about higher-level social dynamics in crowded scenes.

## Problem

- Existing human-behaviour datasets capture only one layer of the individual/intra-group/social-group hierarchy, missing the multi-level structure needed for full understanding.
- Interaction datasets are limited to few label categories or dyadic pairs, lacking crowd-scale, multi-label, frame-level annotation of concurrent group dynamics.
- Video-captioning and group-level datasets provide high-level event descriptions but omit detailed body-position, scene-context, and purpose grounding for each social group.

## Method

- ==Individual-level annotation scheme== labels each person's gender, age (five bands), and race per Kroeber's classification, layered atop existing **JRDB** pose and action labels.
- ==Intra-group dynamic interaction protocol== assigns multi-label, frame-level interaction categories across 20 classes spanning physical activity, object engagement, and social exchange for every pair.
- ==Social-group text-caption structure== describes group size, member attributes, body-position/scene-content connection, venue, and aim, evaluated via a guided-perception and holistic (counting/binary) experimental protocol with a ==five-ensemble aggregation strategy==.

## Results

- In guided perception with ground-truth boxes, Video-LLaMA and MiniGPT-4 top individual-level F1 (up to **0.74** gender) but overall average drops to **0.30** and **0.28** as context level rises.
- Holistic counting is far harder: best overall F1 falls to **0.1965** (Video-LLaMA-13B), with social-group venue/purpose scores near **0.02-0.27** across all seven tested models.
- Simplifying to holistic binary yes/no queries raises overall F1 to **0.46-0.48** for top models, yet social-group SSC accuracy still stays below **0.10** F1, confirming persistent scene-context failure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model performance degrades monotonically as reasoning moves from individual attributes to intra-group to social-group context, revealing a hierarchy of increasing difficulty for current VLMs.
> - Task framing strongly affects measured competence: the same video content yields very different scores under counting versus binary querying, so benchmark design can mask or expose model weaknesses.
> - Demographic and contextual grounding (age, race, venue, purpose) needs explicit multi-level annotation to be assessed; single-layer benchmarks systematically overstate real social-understanding capability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Understanding human social behavior is a fundamental challenge in computer vision and robotics, with applications spanning security, surveillance, and human-robot interaction. Prior research has produced datasets that contribute to vision-based human behavior understanding at various levels. Some datasets focus on individual-level information, such as human attributes and atomic actions, while others concentrate on human-human interactions, often between a limited number of individuals. A third category provides higher-level information pertaining to human groups and video captioning, describing events occurring in videos.

However, a notable limitation in the existing landscape is the tendency for these datasets to address only one aspect of a multi-level hierarchy. This narrow focus often results in an incomplete capture of the complexity, dynamics, and context inherent in human social behaviors, particularly within crowded environments. Many existing datasets also lack a perspective from a robot operating within a social environment, which is critical for developing robotic applications. The current work positions itself as an effort to bridge this gap by introducing a dataset that integrates multi-level annotations—individual, intra-group, and social group—from a robot's perspective, thereby offering a more comprehensive and contextualized understanding of human social dynamics. This holistic approach aims to provide a richer resource for developing advanced artificial intelligence systems capable of nuanced social perception and interaction.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is the introduction of JRDB-Social, a robotic dataset designed to facilitate a comprehensive understanding of the context and dynamics of human interactions within social groups. This dataset is presented as an extension of the existing JRDB dataset.

The motivation for developing JRDB-Social stems from several identified limitations in current approaches to human behavior understanding for computer vision and robotics applications:

- **Inadequacy of Micro-level Observations:** The authors contend that merely observing micro-level information, such as individual actions, is insufficient for a thorough comprehension of human behavior. Humans are inherently social beings, and their actions are often influenced by and occur within a broader social context, necessitating a multi-layered analysis.
- **Need for a Multi-layered Approach:** A comprehensive understanding of human social behavior requires integrating information across distinct hierarchical levels: individual attributes and actions, the dynamics of interactions between individuals within a group (intra-group dynamics), and the overarching context and purpose of social groups (social group level). Current datasets frequently specialize in only one of these layers, leading to fragmented insights.
- **Enhancing Real-world Applications:** A more profound understanding of social behavior is expected to improve various applications. For instance, in security and surveillance systems, integrating individual, group, and contextual data can enhance the understanding of crowd behaviors. In human-robot interaction, such as telerobots, coworker robots, and social robots, a richer understanding of human social dynamics can lead to more natural and intuitive interactions.
- **Addressing Data Limitations:** Existing datasets, despite their contributions, are identified as lacking in adequately capturing the complexity and contextual information of human social behaviors in crowded scenes. Specifically, many datasets offer a limited number of label categories for interactions, often focusing on interactions between only two individuals, and do not sufficiently represent crowd dynamics. Furthermore, they may overlook crucial details regarding how individuals interact with each other and their surroundings in a holistic manner. JRDB-Social aims to address these specific gaps by providing:
 - **Individual-level annotations:** Gender, age, and race to explore how demographic factors influence social behavior.
 - **Intra-group level annotations:** Fine-grained, dynamic multi-interactions (20 categories) between pairs within a subgroup at the frame level to capture detailed social dynamics.
 - **Social group level annotations:** Text captions describing the group's body position in relation to scene content, the presence of salient scene content nearby, the venue, and the group's aim or purpose, offering a holistic contextual overview.
- **Benchmarking Multi-modal Large Language Models (LLMs):** A further motivation is to utilize the JRDB-Social dataset as a benchmark to evaluate the capabilities of recent multi-modal LLMs and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in deciphering complex human social behavior. This aims to assess their proficiency in visual understanding and reasoning within crowded social environments and to identify the strengths and limitations of current methodologies.

In essence, the motivation is to provide a rich, multi-faceted dataset that more accurately reflects the complexity of human social interactions in real-world, crowded settings from a robotic perspective, thereby advancing both social understanding for robotic applications and the evaluation of advanced AI models.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology adopted for JRDB-Social involves extending the existing JRDB dataset with new, multi-level annotations to enable a more comprehensive understanding of human social activity within context.

**Dataset Foundation:**
JRDB-Social builds upon the JRDB dataset, which comprises 64 minutes of sensory data across 54 sequences. This data was captured by a social manipulator robot equipped with stereo RGB 360° cameras, dual LiDAR sensors for 3D point clouds, audio, and GPS. The original JRDB dataset contains over 1.8 million annotations, including 2D bounding boxes, 3D oriented cuboids, human atomic actions, social grouping, and human body pose annotations. JRDB-Social complements these existing annotations with additional information structured across three distinct levels: individual, intra-group, and social group. The data captures diverse indoor and outdoor locations within a university campus environment.

**Annotation Process:**
The annotation process employed a custom-designed toolbox that linked new annotations to the unique IDs of existing 2D and 3D bounding box annotations in JRDB. A quality assessment protocol was implemented, adhering to standards established by previous JRDB benchmarks, ensuring consistency and reliability. Annotators, selected for their expertise in behavior analysis, followed strict guidelines. Challenges inherent in real-world data, such as varying distances from the robot, diverse lighting conditions, occlusions, and crowded scenes, were addressed by assigning a difficulty level (Easy, Medium, or Hard) to each label, indicating the annotator’s confidence. To further ensure accuracy and consistency, each label underwent review by two additional individuals, supplemented by random quality assessments from multiple assessors.

**Annotation Levels:**

1. **Individual Level Attributes:**
 - **Gender:** Classified as Male or Female.
 - **Age:** Segmented into five categories: Childhood (3-12 years), Adolescence (13-20 years), Young Adulthood (21-40 years), Middle Adulthood (41-65 years), and Late Adulthood (66+ years).
 - **Race:** Classified based on Alfred L. Kroeber's system, including Caucasian/White, Negroid/Black, Mongoloid/Asian, and Others. These annotations are intended to facilitate the study of demographic influences on social behavior and enable personalized robot interactions.

2. **Intra-Group Level Dynamic Interactions:**
 - This level provides multi-label, fine-grained interaction annotations at the frame level for each pair of individuals within a social group. There are 20 distinct interaction categories, grouped into:
 - **Shared Physical Activities:** e.g., Walking Together, Standing Together, Sitting Together, Moving Together, Cycling Together, Going Upstairs Together, Going Downstairs Together, Bending Together.
 - **Joint Engagement with External Entities:** e.g., Looking Into Something Together, Looking at Something Together, Eating Something Together, Holding Something Together, Interaction with Door Together, Pointing at Something Together.
 - **Interpersonal Exchanges and Gestures:** e.g., Conversation, Looking at the Robot, Waving Hand, Shaking Hand, Hugging Each Other, Walking Toward Each Other.
 - Annotation meticulously identifies participating individuals, documents interaction frame ranges, and integrates with individual actions from JRDB-Act for enhanced accuracy.

3. **Social Group Level Context:**
 - This level provides comprehensive text descriptions for each group, aiming to capture contextual understanding. The text descriptions follow a structured, color-coded format, detailing:
 - The number of individuals and their attributes.
 - The **Engagement of Body Position with the Content (BPC)**: Describes how the majority of group members' body positions relate to scene content (e.g., sitting on a chair, standing on a platform). BPC annotations include categories like floor, ground, chair, sidewalk, stairs, desk, etc.
 - The presence of **Salient Scene Content (SSC)** in close proximity to the group: Provides information about dominant scene elements near the group (e.g., standing near a pillar or counter). SSC annotations include categories like gate, table, counter, door, wall, shelves, tree, robot, etc.
 - The **Venue Location:** Classifies the specific indoor (e.g., cafeterias, classrooms) or outdoor (e.g., open areas, streets) environment where the group is active.
 - The **Group’s Aims and Purposes:** Describes the motivation behind the group's formation and activities (e.g., moving, navigating, socializing, studying, working, eating food, waiting).

**Experiments and Evaluation of Multi-modal LLMs:**

- **Model Selection:** Prominent multi-modal LLMs were selected for evaluation, including video-based models (Video-LLaMA, VALLEY, Otter) and image-based models (MINIGPT-4, InstructBLIP).
- **Metrics:** F1 score and accuracy were used. Traditional textual similarity metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR) were deemed insufficient due to their lack of specificity for key entities embedded in structured text descriptions. Instead, the problem was reformulated as a single or multi-label classification task based on prompting questions to extract named entities.
- **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Five Ensemble Strategy:** To enhance reliability, each model underwent five iterations, with the final output aggregated.
 - **Cropping Scales:** For social group analysis, different cropping scales (e.g., frame-level, fixed black mask, fixed without mask) were explored. The frame-level method with a scaling factor of 2.5 demonstrated superior performance and was selected for further use.
 - **Guided Perception Experiment:** Models were provided with ground truth bounding boxes to focus on specific regions of interest. The objective was to evaluate the models' ability to detect individual attributes, intra-group interactions (for isolated pairs), and social group contexts (for isolated groups).
 - **Holistic Experiment:** Models were presented with the entire video scene without any external localization aids. This experiment comprised two approaches:
 - **Counting Approach:** Assessed the models’ ability to identify and quantify occurrences of specific attributes or interactions (e.g., "How many females are in the video?").
 - **Binary Approach:** Simplified the task to a Yes/No response regarding the presence or absence of specific attributes or interactions (e.g., "Do you see females in the video?").

This comprehensive methodology aims to create a detailed dataset and rigorously evaluate the current capabilities of state-of-the-art multi-modal LLMs in understanding complex human social behavior.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted using the JRDB-Social dataset yielded several findings regarding the capabilities of multi-modal Large Language Models (LLMs) in understanding human social behavior across different levels of detail and under varying guidance conditions.

**Guided Perception Experiment:**
In this experiment, where models were provided with ground truth bounding boxes to focus on specific regions of interest:
- **Individual Attributes:** Models generally demonstrated reliable performance in predicting individual attributes such as gender and age. This suggests that with explicit localization cues, current VLMs can effectively extract basic demographic information.
- **Race Detection:** The detection of race proved to be more challenging, exhibiting lower performance scores compared to gender and age. This difficulty is attributed to the subjective and complex nature of racial classification based on visual cues.
- **Top Performers:** Video-LLaMA and MiniGPT-4 were identified as the top-performing models, particularly for gender and age prediction. Their performance is posited to be a result of the quality of their training data and their architectural design.
- **Performance Trend Across Levels:** A consistent decline in performance was observed as the evaluation progressed from individual-level attributes to intra-group interactions and then to social group-level contexts. This indicates that comprehending higher-level social contexts, such as the engagement of body position with content (BPC) and salient scene content (SSC), poses a significant challenge for these models even with localization assistance. The intricacies of these attributes contributed to the observed limitations.

**Holistic Experiment (Counting Approach):**
This experiment involved presenting the entire video scene to the models without any bounding box guidance, and the task was to identify and quantify specific occurrences.
- **Significant Performance Decline:** Compared to the guided perception experiment, a substantial decline in performance was noted across all evaluated models. This suggests that capturing detailed information and accurately quantifying occurrences throughout an entire unconstrained video scene is a considerable challenge for current multi-modal LLMs.
- **Increased Difficulty at Higher Levels:** Similar to the guided perception experiment, performance further decreased when transitioning to higher-level social group contexts.

**Holistic Experiment (Binary Approach):**
In this approach, the entire video was presented, but the task was simplified to a binary (Yes/No) response regarding the presence or absence of specific attributes or interactions.
- **Improved Performance (relative to Counting):** Despite receiving the same unconstrained video input as the counting approach, models showed enhanced performance in the binary classification task. This improvement is speculated to stem from the 'hallucination problem' often present in the text encoders of these models. When quantification is not required, the models might more accurately infer the presence of an attribute without fabricating counts.
- **Persistent Challenges for Social Context:** Even with the task simplification, the models continued to encounter difficulties in accurately capturing information at the intra-group and social context levels. This observation suggests that understanding nuanced social group contexts remains a fundamental challenge for multi-modal LLMs, highlighting the need for further advancements in model architecture, training methodologies, and richer, more challenging datasets.

In summary, the results indicate that while current multi-modal LLMs can perform adequately for basic individual attribute recognition with explicit guidance, their ability to comprehend complex, multi-level social dynamics and contextual information in crowded, unconstrained environments is limited. The challenges are amplified when quantification is required and when moving towards higher-level contextual reasoning.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The introduction of JRDB-Social offers several contributions and potential impacts on the fields of computer vision, robotics, and artificial intelligence research.

- **Addressing Dataset Gaps for Holistic Social Understanding:** JRDB-Social is designed to address a recognized limitation in existing datasets, which often provide only partial views of human social behavior. By offering comprehensive, multi-level annotations—encompassing individual attributes (gender, age, race), fine-grained intra-group interactions (20 categories), and rich social group context (body position, salient scene content, venue, purpose)—from a robot's egocentric perspective, the dataset provides a more holistic and ecologically valid resource. This is significant for training and evaluating models that require a deeper, contextualized understanding of human social dynamics.

- **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction and Robotics:** The dataset is poised to significantly impact the development of more intelligent and socially aware robotic systems. Robots operating in human environments, such as social robots, telerobots, or coworker robots, require the ability to perceive and interpret complex social cues to interact naturally and appropriately. JRDB-Social's detailed annotations, particularly those related to group purpose and environmental context, can enable robots to anticipate human intentions, adapt their behaviors based on social norms, and navigate crowded spaces more effectively and safely.

- **Benchmarking and Driving Innovation in Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** By providing a new, challenging benchmark for multi-modal LLMs and VLMs, JRDB-Social can stimulate further research and development in these rapidly evolving areas. The experimental results, which highlight the current limitations of state-of-the-art VLMs in nuanced social understanding, particularly in unconstrained and complex contextual scenarios, offer clear directions for future improvements. The dataset serves as a valuable tool to push the boundaries of VLM capabilities beyond general visual comprehension towards more sophisticated social reasoning.

- **Facilitating Cross-Disciplinary Research:** The rich and detailed annotations, particularly demographic factors and group motivations, can also benefit researchers in social sciences and psychology. The dataset provides empirical data for investigating how demographic factors influence social behaviors, how different group purposes manifest in observable actions, and how environmental contexts shape human interactions, all from a novel, objective perspective.

- **Identifying and Characterizing Model Weaknesses:** The comparative evaluation of various VLMs on JRDB-Social not only benchmarks their performance but also systematically identifies areas of weakness. The observed decline in performance from individual-level to higher social group levels, and the struggles in holistic counting versus binary presence detection, highlight critical challenges related to model design, training data bias, and the potential for 'hallucination' in generative models. This diagnostic capability is essential for guiding the development of more robust and reliable AI systems.

In conclusion, JRDB-Social represents a substantial contribution by providing a multifaceted, robot-centric dataset that enables a deeper investigation into human social behavior. Its impact lies in its potential to foster the development of more socially intelligent robots and to drive advancements in AI models capable of processing and reasoning about complex human social dynamics.
