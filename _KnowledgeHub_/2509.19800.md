# Analysis of approximate linear programming solution to Markov decision problem with log barrier function

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST developed a log-barrier reformulation for Linear Programming (LP) solutions to Markov Decision Problems, providing a comprehensive theoretical foundation and enabling gradient-based approximate solutions. This approach demonstrated enhanced stability, reduced bias, and robustness to off-policy sampling when applied to deep reinforcement learning algorithms like DQN and DDPG.

## Problem

- Traditional Linear Programming (LP) formulations for Markov Decision Problems (MDPs) are often computationally intensive due to their inequality-constrained nature.
- Existing LP-based reinforcement learning methods, typically using primal-dual schemes, exhibit slow convergence rates and high computational costs.
- There was a lack of thorough theoretical understanding and practical implementation for applying log-barrier functions to LP-based MDPs to enable efficient gradient-based approximate solutions.

## Method

- The paper reformulates the inequality-constrained **LP** representation of MDPs into an unconstrained optimization problem by introducing a log-barrier function.
- This transformation enables the use of standard gradient descent for efficiently finding approximate Q-function solutions ($	ilde{Q}_
eta$) in both tabular and deep reinforcement learning settings.
- A novel loss function, derived from the log-barrier formulation, ==is proposed for Deep Q-Networks== (DQN) and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) to integrate this approach into deep reinforcement learning.

## Results

- Log-barrier DQN performs comparably to or better than standard DQN across various discrete control tasks, exhibiting enhanced stability and faster adaptation in environments like CartPole.
- Log-barrier DDPG achieved superior performance over conventional DDPG in more complex continuous control MuJoCo tasks such as Ant and Humanoid.
- The log-barrier Q-learning method effectively mitigates maximization bias and demonstrates reduced sensitivity to off-policy sampling distributions compared to standard Q-learning.
