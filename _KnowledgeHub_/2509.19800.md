# Analysis of approximate linear programming solution to Markov decision problem with log barrier function

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST developed a log-barrier reformulation for Linear Programming (LP) solutions to Markov Decision Problems, providing a comprehensive theoretical foundation and enabling gradient-based approximate solutions. This approach demonstrated enhanced stability, reduced bias, and robustness to off-policy sampling when applied to deep reinforcement learning algorithms like DQN and DDPG.

## Problem

- Traditional Linear Programming (LP) formulations for Markov Decision Problems (MDPs) are often computationally intensive due to their inequality-constrained nature.
- Existing LP-based reinforcement learning methods, typically using primal-dual schemes, exhibit slow convergence rates and high computational costs.
- There was a lack of thorough theoretical understanding and practical implementation for applying log-barrier functions to LP-based MDPs to enable efficient gradient-based approximate solutions.

## Method

- The paper reformulates the inequality-constrained **LP** representation of MDPs into an unconstrained optimization problem by introducing a log-barrier function.
- This transformation enables the use of standard gradient descent for efficiently finding approximate Q-function solutions Q̃_ eta in both tabular and deep reinforcement learning settings.
- A novel loss function, derived from the log-barrier formulation, ==is proposed for Deep Q-Networks== (DQN) and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) to integrate this approach into deep reinforcement learning.

## Results

- Log-barrier DQN performs comparably to or better than standard DQN across various discrete control tasks, exhibiting enhanced stability and faster adaptation in environments like CartPole.
- Log-barrier DDPG achieved superior performance over conventional DDPG in more complex continuous control MuJoCo tasks such as Ant and Humanoid.
- The log-barrier Q-learning method effectively mitigates maximization bias and demonstrates reduced sensitivity to off-policy sampling distributions compared to standard Q-learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The log-barrier objective function is strictly convex with a Lipschitz continuous gradient within its domain, ensuring exponential convergence of gradient descent in the tabular setting.
> - Upper and lower bounds for the approximation error, ||Q̃_η - Q*||_∞, and policy objective value deviation are established, showing linear dependence on the barrier parameter η, implying improved accuracy as η → 0.
> - The LP formulation's inherent minimization objective acts as an implicit regularizer, mitigating Q-value overestimation bias and improving stability in actor-critic methods like DDPG.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Markov Decision Problems (MDPs) traditionally relies on two primary solution methodologies: dynamic programming (DP) techniques, which are foundational to classical and modern reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms such as Q-learning and Deep Q-Networks (DQN), and linear programming (LP) formulations. Historically, DP methods have been more prevalent due to their direct connection to the Bellman equation and their adaptability to iterative solution techniques.

LP-based methods, while theoretically sound, have seen less widespread adoption in reinforcement learning. This is primarily because they typically formulate MDPs as inequality-constrained optimization problems, which can be computationally intensive and challenging to solve efficiently. However, LP formulations have recently garnered renewed interest, particularly in areas like offline reinforcement learning, where their characteristics may offer advantages over Bellman-equation-based approaches, for example, by providing certain performance guarantees or handling specific constraints. Existing LP-based RL methods often employ primal-dual schemes, which are known to exhibit relatively slow convergence rates and higher computational costs in practical applications compared to standard RL algorithms.

The current work addresses a gap in the theoretical understanding and practical implementation of LP-based MDP solutions. While barrier functions, notably the log-barrier, are classical tools in convex optimization for transforming constrained problems into unconstrained ones, their application to the LP formulation of MDPs to enable gradient-based approximate solutions has not been thoroughly investigated theoretically. Previous works have explored approximate linear programming (ALP) to reduce computational load, convex Q-learning, and primal-dual algorithms. Barrier-based techniques have also been adapted for safe RL to handle constraints, but these typically do not apply to the LP representation of MDPs. This paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a log-barrier reformulation of the LP approach to MDPs, providing a theoretical foundation, and exploring its applicability in deep reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to establish a comprehensive theoretical foundation for solving Linear Programming (LP)-based Markov Decision Problems (MDPs) in a more effective and practical manner. The authors are motivated by the inherent challenges associated with traditional LP formulations of MDPs. Specifically, these formulations typically result in inequality-constrained optimization problems, which are generally more difficult to solve efficiently compared to the Bellman-equation-based methods prevalent in dynamic programming and reinforcement learning.

While LP-based methods have recently gained traction in specific contexts, such as offline reinforcement learning, their practical utility has been limited by the reliance on primal-dual schemes. These schemes are often characterized by slow convergence rates and high computational demands, which render them less competitive than other RL approaches. The authors identify a clear need for alternative methods that can overcome these practical drawbacks while retaining the structural advantages of LP formulations.

To address this, the central idea of the paper is to leverage the log-barrier function. This function is a well-established tool in inequality-constrained optimization that allows for the transformation of a constrained problem into an unconstrained one. By applying this technique to the LP formulation of MDPs, the authors aim to enable the efficient derivation of approximate solutions using standard gradient descent optimization methods. Despite the conceptual simplicity of this approach, the authors note that a thorough theoretical interpretation and analysis of its application to MDPs has not been developed in the existing literature. Therefore, a key motivation is to bridge this theoretical gap, providing rigorous error bounds, characterizing the properties of the reformulated objective function, and analyzing the convergence behavior of gradient-based solvers. Furthermore, the paper aims to explore the practical applicability and extension of this theoretical framework to modern deep reinforcement learning algorithms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on reformulating the Linear Programming (LP) representation of a Markov Decision Problem (MDP) into an unconstrained optimization problem using a log-barrier function. This approach is then rigorously analyzed theoretically and subsequently extended to deep reinforcement learning (RL) contexts.

**LP Formulation of MDPs:**
The paper begins by considering an LP formulation for infinite-horizon discounted MDPs based on the Q-function, rather than the more common value function. The primal LP seeks to minimize a weighted sum of Q-values, $\sum_{(s,a)\in S \times A} \rho(s,a)Q(s,a)$, subject to Bellman-like inequality constraints: $R(s,a) + \gamma \sum_{s'\in S} P(s'|s,a)Q(s',a') \le Q(s,a)$ for all $(s,a,a') \in S \times A \times A$. Here, $\rho(s,a)$ is a probability distribution with strictly positive support, and $R(s,a)$ is the expected reward. The authors establish that the unique optimal solution to this LP is the true optimal Q-function, $Q^*$. The dual problem is also derived, where the dual variables $\lambda(s,a,a')$ are shown to correspond to a stationary state-action-next-action distribution under an optimal policy.

**Log-Barrier Transformation:**
To handle the inequality constraints, a log-barrier function is introduced. The original constrained LP is transformed into an unconstrained objective function, $f_\eta(Q)$:
$f_\eta(Q) := \sum_{(s,a)\in S \times A} Q(s,a)\rho(s,a) + \eta \sum_{(s,a,a')\in S \times A \times A} w(s,a,a')\varphi ((F Q)(s,a,a') - Q(s,a))$
where $\eta > 0$ is the barrier parameter, $w(s,a,a')$ are positive weights, and $\varphi(x) := -\ln(-x)$. The domain $\mathcal{D}$ of $f_\eta(Q)$ is the set of strictly feasible Q-functions. This transformation penalizes violations of the inequality constraints, forcing solutions towards the feasible region.

**Analytical Properties of the Objective Function:**
The paper analyzes the properties of $f_\eta(Q)$ and its domain $\mathcal{D}$. $\mathcal{D}$ is shown to be convex, open, bounded below, and unbounded above. The objective function $f_\eta(Q)$ itself is proven to be strictly convex within $\mathcal{D}$. Furthermore, for any sublevel set $L_c = \{Q \in \mathcal{D} : f_\eta(Q) \le c\}$, $f_\eta(Q)$ is shown to be strongly convex and have a Lipschitz continuous gradient. These properties are critical for analyzing the convergence of gradient-based optimization.

**Gradient Descent and Approximate Solutions:**
The closed-form gradient of $f_\eta(Q)$ with respect to $Q$ is derived. The minimizer of $f_\eta(Q)$, denoted $\tilde{Q}_\eta$, provides an approximate solution to the original MDP. From $\tilde{Q}_\eta$, an approximate dual variable $\tilde{\lambda}_\eta$ is also obtained. These lead to two types of policies: a deterministic "primal $\eta$-policy" $\tilde{\beta}_\eta(s) = \arg\max_{a \in A} \tilde{Q}_\eta(s,a)$ and a stochastic "dual $\eta$-policy" based on $\tilde{\lambda}_\eta$. For the tabular setting, the paper demonstrates that gradient descent with a constant step-size converges exponentially to $\tilde{Q}_\eta$.

**Error Analysis:**
A key part of the methodology involves rigorous error analysis between the approximate solution $\tilde{Q}_\eta$ and the true optimal $Q^*$. Upper and lower bounds are established for the $l_\infty$-norm of the optimality error, $||\tilde{Q}_\eta - Q^*||_\infty$, and the Bellman error, $||\tilde{Q}_\eta - T\tilde{Q}_\eta||_\infty$. These bounds are shown to depend linearly on the barrier parameter $\eta$, implying that the approximation quality improves as $\eta$ approaches zero. Similar linear bounds are derived for the deviation of the objective values of both the primal and dual $\eta$-policies from the optimal objective value $J_{\pi^*}$.

**Extension to Policy Evaluation:**
The framework is also extended to the policy evaluation problem, demonstrating its applicability to finding the Q-function for a given policy $\pi$. Similar LP formulations, dual problems, and error bounds are derived for this setting.

**Deep Reinforcement Learning Variants:**
For practical application, the theoretical foundation is extended to deep reinforcement learning (DRL). A novel loss function is proposed, derived from the log-barrier formulation, to replace the conventional Mean Squared Error (MSE) Bellman loss in Deep Q-Networks (DQN) and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG). The loss function for DQN is formulated as a stochastic approximation of $f_\eta(Q_\theta)$. For DDPG, a similar critic loss is derived. To enhance stability in DRL, particularly when facing non-sampled state-action pairs or ensuring feasibility, a modified loss function $\tilde{L}(\theta)$ is introduced, incorporating a piecewise function with a small margin $\epsilon$ for feasibility and a large penalty $\nu$ for constraint violations. The DRL variants do not employ target networks, simplifying the architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive set of theoretical findings and empirical results demonstrating the properties and efficacy of the proposed log-barrier approach for solving Markov Decision Problems (MDPs).

**Theoretical Findings:**

1. **Unique Optimal Solution for LP Formulation:** The paper establishes that the Q-function-based LP formulation of MDPs yields a unique optimal solution, which is precisely the optimal Q-function, $Q^*$.
2. **Dual Problem Interpretation:** The dual problem of the LP formulation is derived, and its optimal dual variables ($\lambda^*$) are shown to correspond to a stationary state-action-next-action probability distribution under the optimal policy. The dual objective function is linked to the optimal value function.
3. **Properties of the Log-Barrier Objective:** The unconstrained objective function $f_\eta(Q)$ resulting from the log-barrier transformation is proven to be strictly convex within its feasible domain $\mathcal{D}$. The domain $\mathcal{D}$ itself is characterized as convex, open, bounded below, and unbounded above. Furthermore, within any sublevel set $L_c$, $f_\eta(Q)$ is shown to be both strongly convex and to possess a Lipschitz continuous gradient. These properties are crucial for the theoretical guarantees of gradient-based optimization.
4. **Error Bounds for Approximate Solutions:** The paper derives both upper and lower bounds for the $l_\infty$-norm of the difference between the approximate solution $\tilde{Q}_\eta$ (minimizer of $f_\eta(Q)$) and the true optimal $Q^*$, as well as for the Bellman error of $\tilde{Q}_\eta$. These bounds explicitly demonstrate a linear dependence on the barrier parameter $\eta$, indicating that the approximation error decreases proportionally as $\eta \to 0$.
5. **Error Bounds for Policy Objective Values:** Bounds are also established for the deviation of the objective values of policies derived from $\tilde{Q}_\eta$ (primal $\eta$-policy $\tilde{\beta}_\eta$) and $\tilde{\lambda}_\eta$ (dual $\eta$-policy $\tilde{\pi}_\eta$) from the optimal objective value $J_{\pi^*}$. These bounds similarly diminish linearly with $\eta$.
6. **Exponential Convergence of Gradient Descent:** In the tabular setting, the paper proves that deterministic gradient descent applied to $f_\eta(Q)$ converges exponentially to $\tilde{Q}_\eta$, leveraging the strong convexity and smoothness properties within sublevel sets.
7. **Policy Evaluation Applicability:** The entire theoretical framework, including LP formulation, dual problem, and error bounds, is shown to be equally applicable to the policy evaluation problem.

**Empirical Results:**

1. **Tabular Setting Validation:** Experiments on a 6x6 FrozenLake-v1 environment validate that the derived gradient expression functions correctly within a gradient descent framework. It confirms that the log-barrier coefficient $\eta$ acts as an approximation factor, with smaller $\eta$ leading to more accurate (though still biased) solutions. Gradient descent in this setting converges, albeit slower than value iteration, and the log-barrier method can exhibit faster convergence and reduced bias compared to standard Q-learning, particularly under off-policy sampling.
2. **Deep Q-Network (DQN) Performance:** In discrete control tasks (e.g., Acrobot-v1, CartPole-v1, LunarLander-v3, MountainCar-v0, Pendulum-v1), Log-barrier DQN performs comparably to standard DQN in most environments. It exhibits rapid adaptation and enhanced stability in tasks like CartPole, which is attributed to its global mitigation of error propagation by directly minimizing the value function subject to LP constraints, rather than relying solely on local Bellman updates.
3. **Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) Performance:** For continuous control tasks on MuJoCo environments (Ant, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Humanoid, Hopper), Log-barrier DDPG demonstrates superior performance over conventional DDPG in more complex tasks (Ant, Humanoid). This improvement is hypothesized to stem from the LP formulation's inherent minimization objective, which acts as an implicit regularizer against the Q-value overestimation bias commonly observed in actor-critic methods. This results in a more stable critic and, consequently, more reliable actor gradients.
4. **Robustness to Off-Policy Sampling (Linear Function Approximation):** Experiments with linear function approximation on a toy MDP demonstrate that Log-barrier Q-learning is less sensitive to the sampling distribution of the behavioral policy compared to standard Q-learning. Even under adverse $\epsilon$-reverse-greedy policies, the log-barrier method converges more rapidly and maintains higher accuracy.
5. **Maximization Bias Mitigation:** In a classical maximization bias example, Log-barrier Q-learning effectively mitigates overestimation, converging to the optimal line more rapidly and maintaining unbiased action selection throughout training, using only a single Q-table, a property that even double Q-learning (designed to address this bias) does not fully achieve.
6. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Empirical ablation studies indicate that the log-barrier coefficient $\eta$ and the infeasible-region enforcement parameter $\nu$ are highly sensitive hyperparameters, requiring careful tuning for optimal performance across different environments and sampling conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the theoretical understanding and practical implementation of Linear Programming (LP)-based methods for Markov Decision Problems (MDPs). By introducing and rigorously analyzing a log-barrier formulation, the paper provides a novel pathway to solving MDPs that bypasses some of the well-known challenges of traditional LP methods.

**Theoretical Impact:**
The primary theoretical impact lies in establishing a comprehensive framework for transforming the inequality-constrained LP formulation of MDPs into an unconstrained optimization problem. The rigorous derivation of error bounds, which explicitly quantify the linear dependence of approximation error on the barrier parameter $\eta$, offers clear insights into the method's accuracy and convergence properties. Proving the convexity, strong convexity, and Lipschitz properties of the transformed objective function, along with demonstrating exponential convergence of gradient descent in the tabular setting, provides a solid mathematical foundation for this approach. This theoretical clarity can guide future research in designing more robust and efficient algorithms for solving constrained optimization problems in sequential decision-making. The dual variable interpretation also enriches the understanding of policies derived from this formulation.

**Practical Impact:**
The practical implications extend to both tabular and deep reinforcement learning (DRL) settings:
1. **Improved Efficiency for LP-based MDPs:** The log-barrier approach offers a potentially more efficient alternative to primal-dual methods, which are often slow and computationally expensive. By enabling the use of gradient-based optimization, it could make LP-based solutions more accessible and practical for a wider range of MDP problems.
2. **Enhanced Stability and Bias Mitigation in Deep RL:** The introduction of a novel log-barrier loss function for deep Q-networks (DQN) and deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) demonstrates promise in addressing critical issues in DRL. The empirical results suggest that this approach can lead to more stable learning, particularly in complex continuous control tasks where it appears to implicitly regularize against Q-value overestimation—a common problem in actor-critic methods. This could contribute to the development of more reliable and robust DRL algorithms.
3. **Robustness to Off-Policy Sampling:** The observed robustness of the log-barrier method to off-policy sampling distributions is significant. This characteristic is particularly valuable in settings like offline RL or when dealing with varying exploration strategies, where data distributions may deviate substantially from the target policy.
4. **Foundation for Constrained RL:** The log-barrier function's inherent capability to handle inequality constraints positions this framework as a natural candidate for extension to constrained reinforcement learning (CRL) or safe RL, where additional safety or resource constraints are imposed. This could facilitate the development of new algorithms that can explicitly enforce complex real-world constraints.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its promising results, the paper acknowledges a key limitation: the high sensitivity of the learning performance to the barrier coefficient $\eta$. This necessitates careful hyperparameter tuning, which can be computationally intensive. Future work is suggested to develop more principled and adaptive methods for selecting or annealing $\eta$, possibly through mirror-descent-style updates or other adaptive parameter schemes, to enhance robustness and reduce tuning effort. Further large-scale validation and robustness improvements are also identified as important directions.

In summary, this work presents a novel and theoretically grounded method for solving MDPs via LP and log-barrier functions, demonstrating notable empirical advantages in stability and bias reduction within current DRL paradigms. It lays groundwork for more robust and efficient constrained optimization in reinforcement learning.
