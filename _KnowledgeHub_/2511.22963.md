# Commanding Humanoid by Free-form Language: A Large Language Action Model with Unified Motion Vocabulary

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ShanghaiTech University and InstAdapt developed Humanoid-LLA, a framework that enables humanoid robots to execute complex, free-form natural language commands by translating them into physically stable whole-body motions. The approach leverages a unified human-humanoid motion vocabulary and a two-stage fine-tuning process, achieving an 87.6% success rate in command execution and superior motion generation quality (FID 2.626) in simulation, with successful deployment on real humanoid hardware.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to translate complex, free-form natural language instructions into nuanced, contextually appropriate whole-body behaviors.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale datasets pairing language instructions with physically executable whole-body humanoid motion data.
- High degrees of freedom and complex dynamics inherent to humanoids lead to physical instability, where minor motion errors can cascade into falls.

## Method

- A unified discrete human-humanoid motion vocabulary is created using a VQ-VAE with implicit partitioning, maximizing the utility of existing human motion data while ensuring cross-embodiment consistency.
- A two-stage fine-tuning framework is introduced, combining supervised motion Chain-of-Thought (CoT) learning with reinforcement learning (RL) refined by comprehensive physical feedback.
- A privileged tracking controller is distilled into a vocabulary-directed student policy, which directly links discrete motion tokens from the language model to stable physical robot control.

## Results

- Humanoid-LLA achieved an **87.6%** command execution success rate in simulation, outperforming baselines like UniAct-T2M (**82.5%**) and RLPF (**80.0%**).
- The model demonstrated superior motion generation quality with a lower FID score of **2.626** (compared to **6.171** for the best baseline) and higher R-Precision (**0.447** vs. **0.320**).
- Successful deployment on real Unitree G1 and Booster T1 humanoid robots validated its ability to execute both simple and abstract instructions stably in physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting raw motion descriptions with LLM-generated "motion Chain-of-Thought" explanations significantly enhances semantic understanding and enables the model to interpret and decompose complex instructions.
> - The sequential combination of supervised learning for broad language-motion alignment and reinforcement learning for physical grounding is crucial for achieving both expressive and stable whole-body humanoid control.
> - A unified discrete motion vocabulary allows for effective leveraging of abundant human motion data to learn language-motion semantics while simultaneously ensuring the physical executability of generated motions on humanoids.
