# Commanding Humanoid by Free-form Language: A Large Language Action Model with Unified Motion Vocabulary

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ShanghaiTech University and InstAdapt developed Humanoid-LLA, a framework that enables humanoid robots to execute complex, free-form natural language commands by translating them into physically stable whole-body motions. The approach leverages a unified human-humanoid motion vocabulary and a two-stage fine-tuning process, achieving an 87.6% success rate in command execution and superior motion generation quality (FID 2.626) in simulation, with successful deployment on real humanoid hardware.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to translate complex, free-form natural language instructions into nuanced, contextually appropriate whole-body behaviors.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale datasets pairing language instructions with physically executable whole-body humanoid motion data.
- High degrees of freedom and complex dynamics inherent to humanoids lead to physical instability, where minor motion errors can cascade into falls.

## Method

- A unified discrete human-humanoid motion vocabulary is created using a VQ-VAE with implicit partitioning, maximizing the utility of existing human motion data while ensuring cross-embodiment consistency.
- A two-stage fine-tuning framework is introduced, combining supervised motion Chain-of-Thought (CoT) learning with reinforcement learning (RL) refined by comprehensive physical feedback.
- A privileged tracking controller is distilled into a vocabulary-directed student policy, which directly links discrete motion tokens from the language model to stable physical robot control.

## Results

- Humanoid-LLA achieved an **87.6%** command execution success rate in simulation, outperforming baselines like UniAct-T2M (**82.5%**) and RLPF (**80.0%**).
- The model demonstrated superior motion generation quality with a lower FID score of **2.626** (compared to **6.171** for the best baseline) and higher R-Precision (**0.447** vs. **0.320**).
- Successful deployment on real Unitree G1 and Booster T1 humanoid robots validated its ability to execute both simple and abstract instructions stably in physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting raw motion descriptions with LLM-generated "motion Chain-of-Thought" explanations significantly enhances semantic understanding and enables the model to interpret and decompose complex instructions.
> - The sequential combination of supervised learning for broad language-motion alignment and reinforcement learning for physical grounding is crucial for achieving both expressive and stable whole-body humanoid control.
> - A unified discrete motion vocabulary allows for effective leveraging of abundant human motion data to learn language-motion semantics while simultaneously ensuring the physical executability of generated motions on humanoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on enabling humanoid robots to execute complex, free-form natural language commands while maintaining physical stability. This area is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), vision-language-action (VLA) models, and humanoid robot control.

Existing paradigms for language-conditioned whole-body control of humanoid robots typically fall into two categories:
1. **Motion Mimicking Frameworks:** This approach involves generating human-like motions from text using off-the-shelf models and subsequently retargeting these motions to humanoid robots. While effective for generating diverse human motions, a limitation of this method is that human-centric motions often violate humanoid dynamics, necessitating extensive manual curation for physical feasibility. The direct coupling of generation with retargeting can introduce significant challenges in maintaining physical plausibility on a robot.
2. **Distillation Frameworks:** These methods transfer knowledge from a high-fidelity, privileged tracking controller to a text-conditioned policy. While this paradigm generally achieves strong physical fidelity, it often compresses semantic understanding and motor control into a single variational model. This compression can weaken the model's grounding in language and lead to less precise action selection.

Both of these established paradigms have been primarily limited to mapping simple instructions to motions, struggling with the comprehensive semantic understanding, reasoning, and generative capabilities required to translate complex, high-level linguistic commands into nuanced and contextually appropriate whole-body behaviors for humanoids.

Recent advancements in LLMs and VLA models have begun to integrate foundation models with embodied systems, showing promising results in manipulation tasks. However, applying these to free-form language control for humanoid robots remains challenging due to the high degrees of freedom and complex dynamics inherent to humanoids. Specific persistent issues include the scarcity of paired language instructions and physically executable whole-body humanoid motion data, and the extreme sensitivity of humanoid motions to physical instability, where minor errors can cascade into falls.

This paper aims to bridge the gap between high-level language understanding and physically stable whole-body execution for humanoids, distinguishing itself by developing a framework that explicitly addresses data scarcity through a unified motion vocabulary and enhances physical stability through a systematic two-stage fine-tuning process. This positions Humanoid-LLA as an attempt to move beyond simple command execution towards general-purpose, physically robust, and semantically rich humanoid control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to follow free-form natural language instructions by translating unconstrained natural language directly into executable whole-body motions. This capability is identified as a critical step toward achieving seamless human-robot interaction and realizing general-purpose embodied artificial intelligence.

The motivation for developing the Humanoid-LLA model stems from two core challenges observed in the current landscape of humanoid robot control:

1. **Paired Language-Humanoid Motion Data Scarcity:**
 - Collecting large-scale datasets of language instructions paired with physically executable whole-body motions for humanoids is costly and difficult.
 - Existing pipelines for generating humanoid motions from human text-motion data often discard a substantial portion of the human motion data because it is not directly executable or retargetable onto a specific humanoid robot. This filtering process reduces the effective coverage of motion-language pairs and consequently weakens the language grounding capabilities of trained models.
 - The research aims to overcome this limitation by maximizing the utility of existing motion data, particularly abundant human text-motion corpora, for humanoid control.

2. **Physical Instability of Whole-Body Humanoid Motions:**
 - Humanoid robots possess a high degree of freedom and complex dynamics, making their whole-body motions highly susceptible to physical instability.
 - Even small errors in motion generation or execution can be amplified through contact dynamics, leading to balance loss and falls.
 - Existing methods often struggle to balance motion diversity with physical plausibility, frequently sacrificing one for the other. The motivation is to develop a robust control strategy that ensures physical fidelity and stability without compromising the diversity and expressiveness of generated motions.

To address these challenges, the authors outline several specific objectives for their Humanoid-LLA model:
- To directly map expressive natural language instructions into executable humanoid actions.
- To leverage the reasoning and analytical capabilities of modern large language models for interpreting and decomposing high-level commands.
- To build a unified human-humanoid discrete motion vocabulary that allows for the full utilization of human motion data to learn language-motion alignment, while also incorporating humanoid-executable subsets for physical grounding.
- To introduce a novel two-stage fine-tuning framework that combines supervised motion Chain-of-Thought (CoT) learning with reinforcement learning (RL) refined by physical feedback, thereby ensuring robustness and stability.
- To demonstrate superior generalization to novel language commands and diverse motion generation, while maintaining high physical fidelity, through extensive evaluation in both simulation and real-world cross-embodiment experiments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Humanoid-LLA framework comprises three interconnected components designed to bridge high-level language semantics with physically-grounded control: building a unified human-humanoid motion vocabulary, distilling a vocabulary-directed policy, and fine-tuning a large language-action model.

#### 3.1. Unified Human-Humanoid Vocabulary

This stage focuses on creating a shared, discrete representation for both human and humanoid motions, thereby addressing data scarcity and enabling cross-embodiment alignment.

- **Humanoid Motion Canonicalization:** Humanoid motions are first transformed into a root-centered, structured representation that aligns with common human motion parameterizations. This canonicalization involves aggregating joint configurations and kinematic signals, normalized to a consistent coordinate frame, to facilitate learning a shared vocabulary.
- **Implicit Partitioning Tokenization:** A Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) with implicit partitioning is employed to learn a unified tokenizer. This method maps human and retargeted humanoid motions into the same discrete vocabulary, ensuring semantic consistency across embodiments. To mitigate the information bottleneck often associated with discrete interfaces and preserve fine-grained kinematic details crucial for stable control, each latent vector is factorized into sub-blocks, which are then quantized by multiple sub-codebooks. This approach increases representational bandwidth without requiring a single, monolithic codebook.
- **Cross-embodiment Optimization:** The dual-branch VQ-VAE is optimized using a combined objective function that includes intra-modal and cross-modal reconstruction losses, along with a commitment loss. This ensures that a motion token derived from either human or humanoid data can be accurately reconstructed into the corresponding motion primitive in both embodiments. The cross-modal reconstruction specifically enforces that the same discrete token consistently represents the same motion primitive across human and humanoid forms, which is critical for a semantically unified tokenization.

#### 3.2. Vocabulary-directed Humanoid Action Distillation

This component establishes a stable link between the discrete motion vocabulary and physical robot control.

- **Fully-constrained Teacher Controller:** A privileged teacher tracking policy (π_track) is trained using a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This teacher policy learns to track dense humanoid-retargeted reference states with high fidelity. Its observations include the robot's proprioception (root linear and angular velocity, joint positions, velocities, and previous action history) and a goal state comprising kinematic reference motion (relative body pose, joint positions, and velocities). The teacher's action is the normalized robot target joint positions for a PD controller. The training objective maximizes accumulated reward, a weighted sum of task rewards, regularization, and penalties.
- **Vocabulary-directed Student Controller:** The learned teacher policy (π_track) is then distilled into a vocabulary-directed student policy (π_vocab). This student policy is designed to generate full-body actions based on sequences of motion tokens from the unified vocabulary. Modeled as a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE), π_vocab consists of a vocabulary prior, a residual encoder, and an action decoder. The student's motion vocabulary observation incorporates the current motion token (possibly masked). The training objective, based on the Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) algorithm, minimizes the difference between the teacher's reference actions and the student's actions, alongside a KL divergence term between the encoder and prior distributions. This process shifts the control input from continuous reference trajectories to compact motion tokens, allowing the humanoid to execute token sequences generated by the language model.

#### 3.3. Large Language-Action Model Fine-tuning

This final stage integrates the language model with the physically grounded control system, enabling direct mapping from abstract language to executable robot actions without online decoding or retargeting.

- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) with Augmented Human Data:**
 - To address the limited expressivity and potential misalignment in existing text-motion datasets, the authors employ a vision-language model (Qwen2.5-VL) to augment raw motion descriptions with richer "motion Chain-of-Thought (CoT)" explanations. These CoT descriptions provide detailed reasoning and decomposition for abstract or compositional instructions, while atomic commands remain unchanged. This augmentation enhances semantic understanding.
 - Motion token generation is framed as an autoregressive, text-conditioned language modeling task. The input is a textual description (potentially augmented with CoT), and the supervision target is a sequence of discrete motion tokens from the unified vocabulary.
 - The model is trained with a standard next-token prediction loss using the full human text-motion corpus. At this SFT stage, the focus is on language-motion semantic alignment; physical executability on humanoids is not strictly enforced, leveraging the broader human motion data.
- **RL Fine-tuning with Humanoid Feedback:**
 - The SFT-trained model is further adapted to humanoid-specific requirements using reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a variant of PPO. This method samples multiple candidate outputs for an input prompt, assigns scalar rewards, and normalizes rewards within the group to calculate relative advantages, thereby encouraging the policy to prefer better-performing candidates.
 - The policy is optimized using a clipped surrogate objective, regularized to stay close to a reference model. This injects physical priors into token generation, enabling physics-aware token sequences.
 - **Physical Fidelity Reward Design:** A comprehensive reward scheme is designed to achieve motions that are both physically robust and expressively varied, moving beyond simple binary tracking rewards.
 - `r_format`: A binary reward that ensures the model's output adheres to a structured template, including `<think>...</think>` and `<motion>...</motion>` segments, and that motion tokens appear in a cyclic sub-codebook order. This guides the model to produce valid structured responses.
 - `r_dist`: A continuous distributional reward that encourages the generated motion distribution to match physically plausible trajectories and align semantically with paired motion descriptions. This is achieved using contrastive motion and text encoders trained on physically plausible humanoid datasets, measuring both motion fidelity (generated vs. reference motion) and semantic fidelity (generated motion vs. reference text).
 - `r_track`: A continuous tracking reward that quantifies how well a generated token sequence can be executed in simulation by the distilled vocabulary-directed controller. It combines position and acceleration reward terms.
 - The overall reward (`r`) is the sum of `r_format`, `r_dist`, and `r_track`. This multi-faceted reward design aims to balance semantic consistency, motion diversity, and physical stability for real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Humanoid-LLA involved extensive experiments in simulation and real-world environments, comparing its performance against several state-of-the-art baselines across a range of generation and physics-based metrics.

#### 4.1. Text-to-Humanoid Evaluation

The results, summarized in Table 1 of the paper, indicate that Humanoid-LLA consistently outperforms baseline methods across both motion generation quality and physical executability metrics.

- **Generation Metrics:**
 - **FID (Fréchet Inception Distance):** Humanoid-LLA achieved an FID score of 2.626, which is a substantial improvement over the best baseline (LangWBC at 6.171). A lower FID indicates better distributional similarity to physically plausible humanoid motions.
 - **R-Precision (top-3):** The model demonstrated higher motion-language alignment with an R-Precision of 0.447, exceeding the next best baseline (LangWBC at 0.320).
 - **MM-Dist (Motion-Motion Distance):** Humanoid-LLA recorded a lower MM-Dist of 4.911, compared to LangWBC's 5.587, indicating better alignment between generated and reference motions.
 - **Diversity (Div.):** The model maintained high motion diversity, scoring 7.122, which is higher than other baselines, suggesting it does not sacrifice expressiveness for physical fidelity.

- **Physics-Based Metrics:**
 - **Success Rate (Succ.):** Humanoid-LLA achieved an 87.6% success rate in executing commands, surpassing UniAct-T2M (82.5%) and RLPF (80.0%). This indicates enhanced physical robustness.
 - **MPJPE (Mean Per-Joint Position Error):** The mean per-joint position error was 56.43 mm, lower than UniAct-T2M (60.33 mm), signifying more accurate joint control.
 - **E_vel (Velocity Error) and E_acc (Acceleration Error):** The model exhibited lower velocity error (8.92 mm/frame) and acceleration error (8.74 mm/frame²), outperforming UniAct-T2M (9.15 and 8.93, respectively), further confirming superior tracking fidelity.

The paper highlights that while baselines like MDM+Retarget offered diversity but lacked physical fidelity, and methods like LangWBC prioritized low-level executability over language generalization, Humanoid-LLA managed to achieve improvements across both evaluation axes. This outcome is attributed to the integration of LLM-generated tokens with a vocabulary-directed controller and subsequent fine-tuning with humanoid physical feedback.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies (Table 2) were conducted to assess the contribution of individual components to the overall performance of Humanoid-LLA.

- **Humanoid-LLA w/o CoT (Chain-of-Thought):** Removing the motion CoT augmentation led to a significant degradation in text-motion alignment (FID increased from 2.626 to 10.423; R-Precision dropped from 0.447 to 0.270) and a decrease in execution success (64.90%). This finding suggests that the CoT mechanism is crucial for the model's semantic planning and its ability to interpret and decompose complex instructions into consistent token sequences.
- **Humanoid-LLA w/o RLFT (Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning):** Without RL fine-tuning, performance declined substantially in both executability (Success Rate dropped from 87.6% to 68.64%; MPJPE increased from 56.43 to 78.31) and overall generation quality (FID increased from 2.626 to 5.132). This indicates that the RL fine-tuning, guided by physical feedback, is essential for providing physical grounding and correcting distributional mismatches that cannot be resolved by supervised learning alone.
- **Humanoid-LLA w/o r_dist (Distributional Reward):** Excluding the distributional reward term (r_dist) resulted in a slight degradation in FID and R-Precision, while maintaining a high success rate. This confirms that r_dist primarily contributes to preserving the semantic distribution and expressiveness of the generated motions.
- **Humanoid-LLA w/o r_track (Tracking Reward):** Removing the tracking reward term (r_track) caused a notable drop in the success rate (from 87.6% to 76.72%) and an increase in MPJPE. This indicates that r_track is critical for ensuring stable physical execution in simulation.

The ablation studies collectively demonstrate that each component contributes to the superior performance of Humanoid-LLA, with motion CoT enhancing semantic understanding, RL fine-tuning providing physical grounding, and the combined reward terms balancing expressiveness, semantic alignment, and physical stability.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

The paper also reported successful deployment of Humanoid-LLA on real humanoid hardware, specifically the Unitree G1 and Booster T1 robots. These real-world cross-embodiment experiments (illustrated in Figure 3) demonstrated the model's ability to execute both simple and abstract instructions, confirming its semantic consistency and physical robustness outside of simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Humanoid-LLA framework represents a advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence and humanoid robot control. Its significance and potential impact can be delineated as follows:

- **Enabling Free-form Language Humanoid Control:** The core contribution of Humanoid-LLA is its ability to translate unconstrained natural language instructions directly into executable whole-body motions for humanoid robots. This capability is a fundamental step towards creating robots that can interact with humans more intuitively and perform tasks described in natural language, reducing the need for specialized programming or teleoperation.
- **Addressing Critical Challenges in Embodied AI:** The work directly tackles two significant hurdles in humanoid control: the scarcity of paired language-humanoid motion data and the inherent physical instability of complex whole-body motions. By providing effective solutions to these problems, Humanoid-LLA contributes to broader progress in making embodied AI systems more robust and adaptable.
- **Unified Motion Vocabulary for Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The introduction of a unified human-humanoid motion vocabulary is a key methodological innovation. This shared latent space allows the model to leverage abundant human motion data for learning rich language-motion alignments, while also incorporating the nuances of humanoid dynamics for physical grounding. This approach mitigates data scarcity and facilitates knowledge transfer across different embodiments, which has implications for developing more generalized robot learning systems.
- **Systematic Two-Stage Fine-tuning Framework:** The sequential combination of supervised motion Chain-of-Thought (CoT) learning and reinforcement learning (RL) with physical feedback provides a robust training paradigm. This framework enables Humanoid-LLA to acquire both semantic reasoning capabilities (through CoT for complex instructions) and physical stability (through RL with simulator-based rewards), leading to enhanced generalization and real-world applicability.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated superior performance across both motion generation quality and physical executability metrics, in comparison to state-of-the-art baselines, indicates a tangible improvement in the capabilities of humanoid robots. The high success rates and low error metrics, coupled with maintained motion diversity, suggest that Humanoid-LLA offers a more balanced and effective solution to the expressiveness-fidelity trade-off.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment on physical humanoid hardware (Unitree G1 and Booster T1) underscores the practical viability and generalizability of the proposed framework. This moves the technology beyond theoretical demonstration in simulation to real-world operational capability, fostering confidence in its potential for practical applications.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies extensions such as richer multimodal grounding and longer-horizon planning as important future directions. Humanoid-LLA lays a foundation for these advancements by establishing a robust framework for language-to-motion translation and physically-aware control, potentially catalyzing further research in complex robot behaviors and human-robot collaboration.

In summary, Humanoid-LLA contributes to bridging the semantic-to-physical gap in humanoid robot control, moving closer to the vision of general-purpose robots that can understand and execute diverse and complex human commands in dynamic, real-world environments.
