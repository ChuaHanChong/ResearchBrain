# A Systematic Survey of Prompt Engineering in Large Language Models: Techniques and Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic survey organizes 41 prompt engineering techniques across 12 application domains for Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models, providing a structured overview of methodologies, applications, and their respective strengths and limitations. This work offers a consolidated resource for navigating the diverse landscape of prompt engineering, aiming to clarify the state-of-the-art and identify open challenges.

## Problem

- The rapid, unorganized proliferation of prompt engineering techniques created a fragmented understanding of the field.
- Researchers and practitioners found it challenging to navigate the myriad of prompting methods and identify suitable approaches for specific problems.
- Traditional fine-tuning of Large Language Models is often computationally expensive, time-consuming, and requires large amounts of task-specific labeled data.

## Method

- A ==systematic review== approach was employed to meticulously analyze a broad spectrum of ==prompt engineering== techniques.
- Techniques were organized and categorized by their primary application areas, such as ==reasoning and logic==, ==reducing hallucination==, and ==code generation==.
- Detailed summaries for each prompting technique were provided, including methodology, applications, models involved, datasets utilized, and an assessment of strengths and limitations.

## Results

- A comprehensive taxonomy was created, categorizing 41 distinct prompt engineering techniques across 12 major application domains.
- Detailed summaries for each technique were compiled, outlining their specific methodology, applications, involved models, utilized datasets, and critical strengths and limitations.
- A practical reference table was generated, summarizing crucial information for quick comparison of techniques based on application area, prompt acquisition, and other characteristics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prompt engineering offers an efficient alternative to traditional fine-tuning for adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) to diverse tasks without altering their core parameters.
> - Different prompt engineering techniques demonstrate varying levels of effectiveness across a range of application areas, including enhancing reasoning, reducing hallucination, and facilitating code generation.
> - A structured understanding of the fragmented landscape of prompt engineering techniques is essential for effective LLM deployment and guiding future research.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has fundamentally reshaped the landscape of artificial intelligence. These models, pre-trained on vast datasets, demonstrate remarkable capabilities across a multitude of tasks, from natural language generation and comprehension to complex reasoning. However, unlocking their full potential for specific downstream applications often requires intricate adjustments. Traditionally, this involved extensive fine-tuning, a process that is computationally expensive, time-consuming, and demands large amounts of task-specific labeled data.

In this context, **prompt engineering** has emerged as a revolutionary and indispensable technique. It offers an elegant and efficient alternative: by strategically designing "prompts" – task-specific instructions or input contexts – researchers and practitioners can guide the pre-trained models to elicit desired behaviors without altering their core parameters. This paradigm shift has democratized access to advanced AI capabilities, enabling quicker adaptation to new tasks and significantly reducing the barriers to entry for deploying LLMs in diverse applications.

However, the field of prompt engineering is still nascent and is expanding at an exponential rate, leading to a proliferation of techniques, methodologies, and applications. This rapid growth, while indicative of its success, has also resulted in a fragmented understanding of the landscape. Researchers and practitioners often find it challenging to navigate the myriad of prompting methods, understand their nuances, and identify the most suitable approach for a given problem. There is a "lack of systematic organization and understanding of the diverse prompt engineering methods and techniques," as highlighted by the authors.

This survey paper directly addresses this critical gap. It fits into the broader research landscape by providing a much-needed comprehensive, structured, and systematic overview of prompt engineering. By organizing the scattered knowledge, it aims to clarify the state-of-the-art, identify commonalities and distinctions between techniques, and offer a unified framework for understanding this "burgeoning field." It positions itself as a foundational resource for a maturing domain, crucial for both newcomers seeking to grasp the fundamentals and experienced researchers looking for a consolidated view of advancements and open challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this systematic survey are clearly articulated by the authors in their abstract and introduction:

1. **Address the Lack of Systematic Organization:** The paramount motivation is to fill the existing gap in the literature regarding a structured and organized understanding of prompt engineering techniques. Despite the proliferation of methods, a comprehensive categorization and analysis have been missing.
2. **Provide a Structured Overview:** The core objective is to offer a structured overview of recent advancements in prompt engineering. This involves categorizing diverse methods based on their application areas, which helps in making sense of the vast and rapidly growing body of research.
3. **Summarize Key Details for Each Approach:** For every prompting technique discussed, the authors aim to provide a concise summary. This summary includes the specific prompting methodology, its primary applications, the LLMs or VLMs involved in its development or testing, and the datasets utilized for evaluation.
4. **Delve into Strengths and Limitations:** Beyond mere description, the survey seeks to critically analyze each prompting approach by detailing its advantages (strengths) and disadvantages (limitations). This comparative insight is crucial for practitioners and researchers to make informed decisions about which technique to employ or further investigate.
5. **Facilitate Understanding and Future Research:** Ultimately, the survey aims to enable a better understanding of this fast-developing field. By illuminating open challenges and identifying future opportunities, the authors intend for the paper to serve as a valuable resource that inspires and facilitates future research endeavors in prompt engineering.
6. **Create Reference Tools:** To enhance accessibility and utility, the authors aim to include concrete reference tools such as a taxonomy diagram and a summary table. These visual and tabular aids are designed to consolidate information on datasets, models, and critical points of each technique, making the survey a practical guide.

In essence, the authors are motivated by the need to bring order to the burgeoning chaos of prompt engineering research, providing a consolidated, analytical, and forward-looking resource for the AI community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employed a systematic survey approach to achieve their objectives, meticulously analyzing a broad spectrum of prompt engineering techniques. Their methodology can be broken down into several key steps:

1. **Systematic Review:** The paper states it "Employing a systematic review approach" to "meticulously delve into the intricacies of diverse cutting-edge prompting methods." This implies a structured process of identifying, selecting, and critically appraising relevant research papers on prompt engineering. While the specific search criteria or database sources are not detailed, the comprehensive nature of the survey suggests a broad collection of literature.

2. **Scope Definition:** The survey focuses on prompt engineering in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), encompassing both natural language instructions and learned vector representations. It covers "over 41 distinct techniques," indicating a wide breadth of coverage.

3. **Application-Centric Categorization:** A core aspect of their methodology is organizing prompt engineering techniques "categorized by application area." This is a highly practical and user-centric approach, making it easier for readers to find techniques relevant to specific problems they are trying to solve. The identified application areas include:
- New Tasks Without Extensive Training
- Reasoning and Logic
- Reduce Hallucination
- User Interaction
- Fine-Tuning and Optimization
- Knowledge-Based Reasoning and Generation
- Improving Consistency and Coherence
- Managing Emotions and Tone
- Code Generation and Execution
- Optimization and Efficiency
- Understanding User Intent
- Metacognition and Self-Reflection

4. **Detailed Technique Summaries:** For each of the identified techniques, the authors provide a structured summary. This includes:
- **Prompting Methodology:** A description of how the prompt is designed and used.
- **Applications:** The specific tasks or domains where the technique is effective.
- **Models Involved:** The particular LLMs or VLMs that have been used with the technique (e.g., GPT-3, PaLM, LLaMA).
- **Datasets Utilized:** The benchmarks or datasets on which the technique was evaluated.
- **Strengths and Limitations (Pros and Cons):** A critical assessment of the advantages and disadvantages of each approach.

5. **Taxonomy and Summary Table Creation:** As part of their synthesis, the authors developed key analytical artifacts:
- A **taxonomy diagram (Figure 2)**: This visual representation illustrates how the techniques are organized within the various application domains, providing a clear hierarchical structure.
- A **comprehensive table (Table 1)**: This table summarizes crucial information for each technique, including its application area, prompt acquisition method (manual, LM generated, retrieval based, hybrid), prompt turn (single, multi), specific language models, datasets, and evaluation metrics. This tabular summary allows for quick comparison and detailed referencing.

By systematically following these steps, the authors aimed to provide a comprehensive, organized, and insightful analysis of the diverse landscape of prompt engineering, moving beyond a simple listing of methods to a structured understanding of their characteristics and utility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a systematic survey paper, the "findings" are not experimental results derived from novel experiments conducted by the authors. Instead, the main findings and "results" of this paper lie in its comprehensive synthesis, organization, and critical analysis of the existing body of prompt engineering research. The key outcomes of their meticulous review are:

1. **A Comprehensive Taxonomy of Techniques:** The most significant finding is the creation of a detailed taxonomy (Figure 2) that categorizes 41 distinct prompt engineering techniques across 12 major application domains. This organization itself is a critical contribution, providing a much-needed framework for understanding the diverse landscape. The taxonomy moves beyond simple linear lists, showing how techniques branch out based on their intended purpose.

2. **Categorization by Application Area:** The survey successfully demonstrates that prompt engineering techniques can be effectively grouped by their primary application goals. This includes:
- **New Tasks Without Extensive Training:** Covering foundational methods like Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Prompting, which enable LLMs to tackle unseen tasks with minimal or no examples.
- **Reasoning and Logic:** A substantial category encompassing techniques designed to enhance LLMs' reasoning capabilities, from Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and its derivatives (Auto-CoT, LogiCoT, Self-Consistency) to more complex graph-based (GoT) and tree-based (ToT) reasoning, as well as self-refinement and logic-injection methods. This highlights the ongoing efforts to make LLMs more robust and logical thinkers.
- **Reduce Hallucination:** Techniques like Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG), ReAct, Chain-of-Verification (CoVe), and Chain-of-Note (CoN) are identified as crucial for grounding LLM outputs in factual knowledge and reducing fabricated information.
- **Code Generation and Execution:** Specific methods like Scratchpad, Program of Thoughts (PoT), Structured CoT (SCoT), and Chain-of-Code (CoC) illustrate the specialized prompt engineering required for LLMs to generate and even execute code.
- **Other Specialized Applications:** The survey also identifies techniques tailored for user interaction (Active-Prompt), fine-tuning (APE), knowledge integration (ART), consistency improvement (CCoT), emotion management (Emotion Prompting), optimization (OPRO), user intent understanding (RaR), and metacognition/self-reflection (Take a Step Back Prompting).

3. **Detailed Summaries and Comparative Analysis:** For each of the 41 techniques, the authors systematically present the methodology, applications, models, datasets, and critically, their strengths and limitations. This structured presentation serves as a valuable analytical resource, allowing readers to quickly grasp the essence of each method and understand its comparative efficacy and potential drawbacks. For instance, the discussion of CoT emphasizes its ability to guide step-by-step reasoning but notes that manual creation of high-quality CoT examples is time-consuming, leading to the development of Auto-CoT.

4. **Identification of Key Trends and Gaps:** By organizing the existing literature, the survey implicitly reveals several trends: the increasing sophistication of reasoning prompts (from linear CoT to graphical GoT), the growing importance of external knowledge integration (RAG, CoN), and the focus on self-correction and verification (Self-Refine, CoVe) to improve reliability. The "lack of systematic organization" (which the paper aims to fix) also points to the broader challenge of keeping up with the rapid pace of innovation in AI.

5. **Data and Model Landscape:** Table 1 provides a concise overview of the models (e.g., GPT-3, PaLM, LLaMA, Vicuna, ChatGPT, Codex) and datasets (e.g., GSM8K, HotpotQA, BIG-Bench, HumanEval) commonly used for evaluating these prompting techniques. This highlights the benchmarks and LLM architectures most frequently engaged in prompt engineering research.

In summary, the main "results" of this survey are the successful creation of a clear, actionable, and comprehensive map of the prompt engineering landscape, dissecting its complexities into manageable and understandable categories, and providing a foundational reference for future work.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This systematic survey of prompt engineering holds significant implications for both the academic research community and industry practitioners working with Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its potential impact can be categorized as follows:

1. **Democratizing and Accelerating Research:** By providing a structured and organized overview of prompt engineering, the paper significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers. Instead of wading through hundreds of individual papers, new researchers can quickly grasp the fundamental concepts, diverse techniques, and established methodologies. This will accelerate research by enabling more informed decisions about which techniques to build upon, compare against, or adapt for novel applications.

2. **Enhancing Practical Application:** For practitioners and developers, the survey serves as an invaluable guide. The application-centric categorization allows them to directly identify prompting strategies relevant to their specific tasks, whether it's enhancing reasoning, reducing hallucinations, or generating code. The summarized strengths and limitations for each technique provide crucial insights for selecting the most appropriate method, leading to more effective and efficient deployment of LLMs in real-world scenarios. This can directly translate into better performing AI applications with reduced development costs (compared to model fine-tuning).

3. **Illuminating Open Challenges and Future Directions:** Beyond summarizing the known, the survey's systematic analysis implicitly highlights areas where current prompt engineering falls short. By bringing together disparate techniques, it makes it easier to spot common limitations (e.g., scalability, interpretability, robustness to noisy inputs, handling of complex logical reasoning). The conclusion explicitly mentions "biases, factual inaccuracies, and interpretability gaps" as persistent challenges. This clear articulation of open problems guides future research, potentially inspiring novel hybrid approaches, meta-learning strategies, or ethical considerations in prompt design.

4. **Standardization and Benchmarking:** The comprehensive tables detailing models, datasets, and metrics for each technique contribute towards a more standardized understanding of prompt engineering evaluations. This can foster more consistent benchmarking across different studies, facilitating clearer comparisons and a better understanding of true performance gains.

5. **A Foundational Reference:** This survey is poised to become a foundational reference in the prompt engineering domain. Its comprehensive nature, clear organization, and detailed summaries make it an essential resource for students, academics, and industry professionals alike. It will likely be cited extensively as a definitive overview of the field's state-of-the-art.

6. **Ethical Considerations:** The conclusion thoughtfully touches upon "ethical considerations," emphasizing "responsible development and deployment to ensure positive integration into our lives." This acknowledgment underscores the broader societal impact of prompt engineering and highlights the need for careful consideration of fairness, bias, and responsible AI practices as the field advances.

In essence, this survey paper is not just a review; it is a critical piece of infrastructure for a rapidly maturing field. By bringing order and clarity to the complex landscape of prompt engineering, it empowers the AI community to build more capable, reliable, and ethically sound LLM applications.
