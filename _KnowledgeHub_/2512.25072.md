# Coordinated Humanoid Manipulation with Choice Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley developed a modular teleoperation system and a Choice Policy learning framework to enable full-body coordinated manipulation for humanoid robots. Their approach facilitated efficient data collection and captured multimodal human behaviors, leading to robust performance on complex real-world tasks such as dishwasher loading and whiteboard wiping.

## Problem

- Integrating whole-body control (head, hands, and legs) for high-degree-of-freedom humanoids in long-horizon tasks is exceedingly difficult for existing Learning from Demonstrations (LfD) methods.
- Current teleoperation systems for humanoids often lack precise hand-eye coordination or are overly complex, hindering the collection of high-quality, coordinated demonstration data.
- Policy learning from multimodal human demonstrations faces a dilemma: slow diffusion policies are computationally intensive, while standard behavior cloning averages actions, leading to unstable or 'fuzzy' behaviors.

## Method

- A modular teleoperation interface was developed using VR control, decomposing complex humanoid control into intuitive submodules for arms, hands, active hand-eye coordination, and locomotion.
- The '==Choice Policy==' learning framework was introduced, which is an imitation learning approach inspired by multi-choice learning, capable of modeling multimodal behaviors efficiently.
- Choice Policy generates K distinct candidate action sequences and their corresponding scores in a single forward pass, then selects the best action sequence based on predicted quality for real-time execution.

## Results

- Choice Policy achieved significantly higher success rates on the multi-stage dishwasher loading task (e.g., **90%** handover, **70%** insertion) compared to Diffusion Policy and standard Behavior Cloning.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that without active hand-eye coordination, the insertion phase of the dishwasher loading task experienced near **0%** success, confirming its critical importance.
- On the complex real-world whiteboard wiping task, Choice Policy showed improved robustness and better overall success rates compared to Behavior Cloning, despite the inherent difficulty of the task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modular teleoperation drastically reduces the complexity of controlling high-DoF humanoids, making it practical to collect high-quality, whole-body coordinated demonstrations.
> - The Choice Policy framework successfully resolves the trade-off between modeling multimodal expert behaviors and maintaining fast, real-time inference speeds, offering a robust alternative to prior methods.
> - Active hand-eye coordination, where the robot's head tracks its manipulating hand, is critical for maintaining visual feedback and achieving precise, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is rapidly advancing, with a long-term vision of deploying robots in human-centric environments for a variety of tasks, from domestic assistance to industrial logistics. A central challenge in realizing this vision is enabling humanoid robots to achieve robust, whole-body coordination across their head, hands, and legs to interact effectively with the world. This paper directly confronts this challenge by presenting a comprehensive system for scalable data collection and learning.

Existing approaches to teaching robots complex skills often rely on **Learning from Demonstrations (LfD)**, where human operators teleoperate robots to generate expert trajectories. However, current LfD methods face several limitations when applied to high-degree-of-freedom humanoids performing long-horizon, coordinated tasks:
- **Integration Complexity:** Combining control for the head, multiple arms, dexterous hands, and dynamic locomotion simultaneously is exceedingly difficult. Many systems focus on upper-body manipulation or decouple locomotion, leading to incomplete solutions.
- **Teleoperation Challenges:** While motion capture, VR, and keypoint retargeting have improved teleoperation (e.g., H2O, OmniH2O, HumanPlus, TWIST, Sonic), precise manipulation often suffers from retargeting errors. VR-based controls improve wrist and hand pose accuracy, but simultaneously controlling all degrees of freedom remains a significant burden for human operators. Crucially, active head control for dynamic visual feedback is often missing or rudimentary.
- **Policy Learning Limitations:** Once demonstrations are collected, learning robust autonomous policies is hindered by the inherent *multimodality* of human actions. For a given observation, multiple valid expert actions might exist due to human preferences or task variations.
 - **Diffusion policies** (e.g., Diffusion Policy) excel at modeling these multimodal distributions but are computationally intensive, requiring iterative sampling steps that make them too slow for real-time reactive control in dynamic humanoid tasks.
 - **Standard behavior cloning (BC)** offers fast inference but tends to average multimodal actions, leading to suboptimal, unstable, or "fuzzy" behaviors that fail in complex scenarios.
 - Alternative approaches involving action space discretization or tokenized representations have not yet proven effective for the high-dimensional, tightly coordinated control required by humanoids.

This work carves out its niche by addressing these critical gaps. It introduces a **modular teleoperation interface** that simplifies the collection of high-quality, whole-body coordinated demonstrations, including active hand-eye coordination, which is often overlooked. Complementing this, the paper proposes **Choice Policy**, a novel imitation learning framework designed to efficiently capture multimodal behaviors without sacrificing real-time inference speed, thereby offering a practical alternative to slow diffusion models and averaging behavior cloning. By tackling both efficient data collection and robust multimodal learning for full-body humanoids, this research pushes the boundaries of autonomous humanoid manipulation into more complex and unstructured real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to perform sophisticated, long-horizon manipulation tasks in human-centric, unstructured environments, demanding seamless coordination across all body parts. To achieve this ambitious goal, the authors set forth several key objectives, driven by the identified limitations in current humanoid robotics:

1. **Develop an Intuitive and Modular Teleoperation System:** A primary motivation was to overcome the inherent complexity of controlling high-degree-of-freedom humanoids. The authors aimed to design a teleoperation interface that is intuitive for human operators, decomposes full-body control into manageable submodules, and efficiently collects high-quality demonstrations encompassing hand-eye coordination, precise grasping, arm movement, and robust locomotion. This was crucial for generating the rich, diverse datasets needed for advanced policy learning.

2. **Introduce a Scalable and Expressive Policy Learning Framework:** The researchers sought to develop an imitation learning algorithm capable of modeling the multimodal nature of human demonstrations. The core motivation here was to reconcile the conflicting demands of *expressiveness* (to capture diverse expert behaviors) and *efficiency* (to enable real-time, reactive control on physical robots). This meant designing a policy that could account for variations in demonstrations without resorting to slow, iterative inference methods like diffusion policies, or oversimplifying behaviors like standard behavior cloning.

3. **Demonstrate the Importance of Whole-Body Coordination:** A critical objective was to empirically validate the necessity of tight coordination between head, hands, and legs for successful execution of complex tasks. Specifically, the paper aims to highlight the instrumental role of active hand-eye coordination in maintaining visual feedback during intricate manipulation sequences.

4. **Validate on Challenging Real-World Tasks:** The ultimate motivation was to demonstrate the practicality and effectiveness of their proposed system on real humanoid robots performing complex, multi-stage tasks in physical environments, thereby moving beyond simulated or simplified scenarios.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to bridge the gap between theoretical advancements in robot learning and the practical deployment of truly capable humanoid robots in diverse, unstructured human environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper is two-pronged, addressing both the data collection aspect and the policy learning aspect:

#### 3.1. A. Modular Teleoperation Interface

To efficiently collect high-quality, whole-body coordinated demonstrations, the authors developed a modular teleoperation system using a VR controller as the primary input device. This system decomposes complex humanoid control into four intuitive submodules:

1. **Arm Control:** This module enables "on-demand" end-effector tracking. When a trigger button is pressed, the VR controller's relative pose changes are mapped to the robot's arm end-effector pose, and inverse kinematics solve for target joint positions. This allows the operator to focus on one arm at a time, preventing idle arm drift and allowing for iterative re-centering of the workspace for extended movements, significantly reducing fatigue and improving precision.
2. **Hand Control:** Hand movements are simplified yet expressive. The four non-thumb fingers are grouped and actuated by a grip button, while the joystick independently controls the thumb. Both inputs provide continuous-valued signals for fine-grained grasp adjustments, covering a broad range of grasp types (e.g., power, precision). This reduction in dimensionality simplifies control compared to high-DoF finger tracking, leading to smoother and more reliable grasps.
3. **Hand-Eye Coordination:** A crucial and novel component, this module allows the head to actively track the selected (left or right) manipulating hand via a button trigger. The system calculates the desired yaw and pitch angles to orient the head camera towards the hand's 3D position in the robot base frame. This ensures the manipulation region remains within the field of view, reducing occlusions and providing continuous visual feedback critical for precise tasks.
4. **Locomotion Control:** For tasks requiring lower-body movement, the VR controller's joystick commands a pre-trained reinforcement learning (RL) policy. This RL policy, trained in simulation and transferred to the real robot (running at 100 Hz), handles omnidirectional walking, standing, and turning based on velocity commands. The joystick can toggle between locomotion commands and thumb control, allowing integrated loco-manipulation.

The system was deployed on two advanced humanoid platforms: the Fourier GR-1 (44 DoF) and the Robotera Star-1 (55 DoF), equipped with dexterous hands. This modular design simplifies teleoperation, making it learnable for users in minutes, and balances usability with the flexibility required for data-driven policy learning.

#### 3.2. B. Choice Policy: A Scalable Learning Framework

To learn autonomous skills from the multimodal demonstrations collected, the authors propose **Choice Policy**, an imitation learning approach inspired by multi-choice learning in structured prediction.

1. **Policy Architecture:**
 - **Observation Encoder:** Combines visual inputs (RGB using a frozen DINOv3 feature encoder, depth using a randomly initialized ResNet-18) and proprioceptive signals (upper body and hand joint positions via a 3-layer MLP) into a single feature vector.
 - **Action Proposal Network:** Takes the concatenated feature vector and, in a single forward pass, predicts *K* distinct candidate action sequences (each representing a multi-step prediction, i.e., an action chunk).
 - **Score Prediction Network:** In parallel, this network takes the same feature vector and outputs *K* corresponding scores, with each score representing the predicted quality (e.g., negative Mean Squared Error, MSE) of its respective action proposal compared to the ground truth.

2. **Training Objective:** The training process combines two loss components:
 - **Score Loss ($L_{score}$):** The score prediction network is trained to minimize the MSE between its predicted scores ($\sigma_t^{(k)}$) and the actual MSE ($\ell^{(k)}$) of each action proposal against the ground-truth action ($a_t$). This teaches the network to accurately assess the quality of its own proposals.
 - **Action Loss ($L_{action}$):** A "winner-takes-all" strategy is employed for the action proposal network. Only the proposal ($a_t^{(k^*)}$) that has the *smallest MSE* with the ground-truth action ($a_t$) receives gradients and is updated. This encourages the network to learn diverse, valid action modes without averaging them.
 - The total training loss is a sum of these two terms: $L = L_{action} + L_{score}$.

3. **Inference:** During deployment, Choice Policy operates with a single forward pass. It generates *K* candidate action trajectories and their corresponding scores. The action trajectory with the *highest predicted score* (i.e., lowest predicted error) is selected for execution. This mechanism allows the policy to leverage multimodality for robust behavior while maintaining fast, real-time inference speeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed system was rigorously evaluated on two challenging real-world tasks: dishwasher loading and whole-body loco-manipulation for whiteboard wiping.

#### 4.1. A. Dishwasher Loading Task

This task involved picking up a thin plate, handing it over between hands, and precisely inserting it into a dishwasher rack, performed by a Fourier GR-1 robot mounted on a shelf (to isolate locomotion effects).

- **Choice Policy Superiority:** Choice Policy consistently outperformed both Diffusion Policy (DP) and standard Behavior Cloning (BC) with action chunking. While all methods performed well in the initial "pickup" phase, Choice Policy significantly improved success rates in the more challenging "handover" (90% vs. 70%) and "insertion" (70% vs. 50%) stages. This indicates its robust ability to capture multimodal behaviors and execute long-horizon tasks reliably.
- **Criticality of Hand-Eye Coordination:** An ablation study demonstrated the profound impact of hand-eye coordination. Without adaptive head movement, all methods failed almost completely in the "insertion" phase (near 0% success), as the fixed head camera occluded the dishrack. With hand-eye coordination, the robot maintained a clear view, dramatically improving success rates for handover and insertion.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Robustness:** Choice Policy showed greater robustness in OOD evaluations (novel plate color or shifted plate position) compared to DP and BC. Although all methods experienced performance degradation in OOD settings, Choice Policy maintained higher success rates, particularly in handover and insertion, highlighting its improved generalization capabilities.
- **Ablation Study on Action Selection:** This study confirmed that the learned scoring mechanism in Choice Policy is crucial. Alternative strategies like "Random Choice," "Mean Choice" (averaging proposals), or "Single Choice" (using a fixed proposal head) performed significantly worse, often leading to complete failure in later task stages. This validates that adaptively selecting the best proposal at each step is key to exploiting multimodality.
- **Qualitative Visualization of Policy Specialization:** Visualizations revealed that Choice Policy's multiple "heads" naturally specialize in different sub-skills of the long-horizon task. For example, specific heads were consistently selected during reaching and handover, while another dominated grasping. This shows the policy's ability to decompose complex tasks and adaptively switch between specialized behaviors.

#### 4.2. B. Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation for Whiteboard Wiping Task

This more complex task involved locating an eraser, picking it up, walking to a whiteboard, and wiping a marked area, performed by a Robotera Star-1 robot.

- **Choice Policy vs. Behavior Cloning:** Diffusion Policy could not be deployed due to its slow inference and training instability. Choice Policy achieved better overall success rates compared to Behavior Cloning. Both successfully completed the initial head movement to locate the eraser. Choice Policy demonstrated higher success in grasping the eraser (2/5 vs. 1/5) and more reliable locomotion (2/5 vs. 1/5), leading to better wiping success (2/5 vs. 0/5).
- **Task Difficulty:** The overall success rates were modest (e.g., 2/5 for wiping), underscoring the extreme difficulty of integrating perception, grasping, locomotion, and whole-body adjustment in a single, long-horizon real-world task. However, Choice Policy's comparative advantage highlights its benefits in such challenging scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant step forward in enabling humanoid robots to perform complex, coordinated manipulation tasks in real-world, unstructured environments. Its impact can be primarily understood through its contributions to practical robot deployment and advancements in robot learning:

1. **Practical Path to Scalable Humanoid Skill Acquisition:** The work provides a practical and efficient pipeline for collecting high-quality, whole-body demonstrations. The modular teleoperation interface dramatically reduces the complexity for human operators, making the generation of large, diverse datasets feasible. This addresses a critical bottleneck in LfD for high-DoF robots.
2. **Bridging the Gap Between Multimodality and Real-time Inference:** Choice Policy successfully resolves the long-standing trade-off in imitation learning between modeling multimodal behaviors and maintaining fast inference speeds. By generating and scoring multiple action proposals in a single forward pass, it offers an efficient and effective alternative to computationally expensive diffusion models and over-simplified behavior cloning. This is crucial for reactive humanoid control where latency is critical.
3. **Demonstrating the Importance of Whole-Body Coordination:** The empirical results definitively highlight that active hand-eye coordination is not merely an enhancement but a critical requirement for successful execution of precise, long-horizon manipulation tasks. This emphasizes the need for future humanoid systems to tightly integrate head, arm, and hand controls.
4. **Advancing Generalization and Robustness:** The improved performance of Choice Policy in out-of-distribution scenarios, even with limited training data, suggests a step towards more robust and generalizable humanoid policies, which is essential for deployment in variable real-world settings. The qualitative visualization of specialized proposal heads also offers insight into how long-horizon tasks might be naturally decomposed and learned.
5. **Foundation for Future Humanoid Capabilities:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions. Improvements in visual perception (e.g., with more diverse data or large-scale pre-training) and the development of learned, adaptive hand-eye coordination mechanisms (rather than heuristic ones) could further enhance performance. The ability to efficiently learn from diverse, whole-body demonstrations opens avenues for humanoids to master an ever-broader range of complex tasks, from fine-grained assembly to intricate domestic chores.

In summary, "Coordinated Humanoid Manipulation with Choice Policies" offers a novel and practical framework that integrates intuitive teleoperation with an efficient multimodal learning algorithm. Its success in demonstrating complex, coordinated tasks on real humanoid robots marks a crucial advancement towards deploying more capable, versatile, and autonomous humanoids in everyday environments.
