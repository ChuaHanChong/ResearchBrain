# Optimal Transport Q-Learning for Flow Policy Steering and Acceleration

> [!summary] Summary
> Optimal Transport Q-Learning (OTQL) offers a framework for fine-tuning suboptimal flow policies and Vision Language Action (VLA) models through reinforcement learning, simultaneously achieving rapid, few-step inference. The approach utilizes advantage-weighted conditional optimal transport to guide the flow towards high-value actions, demonstrating enhanced performance and computational efficiency in robotic control.

## Problem

- Diffusion and flow policies often suffer from slow inference speeds due to multi-step SDE/ODE integration, which is impractical for real-time robotic applications and prolongs reinforcement learning (RL) training.
- Pre-trained Vision Language Action (VLA) and World Action Models (WAMs) frequently exhibit suboptimal performance and poor generalization to novel tasks or out-of-distribution states, necessitating costly human-provided demonstrations for supervised fine-tuning.
- Existing methods for accelerating flow and diffusion models to enable single-step inference typically rely on computationally expensive distillation processes, adding complexity and training overhead.

## Method

- **Optimal Transport Q-Learning (OTQL)** integrates reinforcement learning with ==Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)== using ==advantage-weighted Conditional Optimal Transport (wCOT)==.
- A ==Q-function== is trained to estimate action values, and its ==advantage function== is used to re-weight target action samples in the wCOT formulation, effectively steering the flow policy towards high-value actions by promoting straighter integration paths.
- The flow policy is updated by minimizing a ==CFM loss==, where noise-action pairs are sampled from an optimal coupling derived from an ==entropic optimal transport== problem solved between augmented and re-weighted source (noise) and target (action) measures.

## Results

- OTQL achieved an average success rate of **0.59** on **40** OGBench offline RL tasks, surpassing other fast inference methods (FQL, QAM-F) by at least **5%** on average, while maintaining competitive inference speeds (**2-3 NFEs**).
- In real-world robotic tasks, OTQL improved the average success rate of single-task policies from **36% to 86%** and enhanced a state-of-the-art VLA model (SmolVLA) from **38% to 76%** success, concurrently reducing the required NFEs from **10 to 3** (a **70%** speed-up).
- Ablation studies confirmed that OTQL-trained flows maintain consistent performance across a range of NFEs (**2 to 10**), enabling competitive results with significantly fewer integration steps, and highlighted the importance of a calibrated temperature parameter (λ) for effective advantage-weighted steering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditional Optimal Transport (COT) couplings, when appropriately weighted by an advantage function, can effectively steer generative flow policies towards high-value actions while simultaneously improving inference efficiency by learning more direct paths.
> - The integration of reinforcement learning (through an RL critic and advantage function) directly into the optimal transport formulation enables efficient fine-tuning and acceleration of flow policies within a unified training framework, circumventing the need for separate, expensive distillation steps.
> - By training the flow to approximate the dynamic COT ODE between the noise distribution and advantage-weighted action samples, few-step inference (e.g., 2-3 Neural Function Evaluations) can be achieved for real-time control without significant performance degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has seen advancements through the application of diffusion and flow policies, particularly in Vision Language Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAMs). These models demonstrate capabilities in capturing complex, multimodal robot trajectory distributions, which is beneficial for tasks requiring diverse action generation, such as those derived from human demonstrations. However, challenges persist in deploying these policies.

A primary issue is the necessity for fast inference speeds in real-time robotic applications. Many existing flow and diffusion models require multiple integration steps of Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) or Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs), leading to slow action generation. This slowness not only hinders real-time control but also prolongs the training process in reinforcement learning (RL) settings, where policy actions are frequently sampled for critic updates.

Furthermore, despite large-scale pretraining, VLA and WAM policies often exhibit suboptimal performance and lack generalization to tasks or environment states not encountered during their initial training. Standard remedies, such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with additional demonstrations, are labor-intensive and require significant human expertise and time.

Reinforcement learning offers an alternative by enabling policies to adapt from the robot's own experience, reducing the need for human-provided demonstrations. Prior work has explored training flow and diffusion policies in offline RL settings. However, many of these methods inherit the slow inference problem. Attempts to create single-step policies, for faster inference, typically involve computationally expensive distillation processes. While research has been conducted on accelerating diffusion and flow models, particularly in behavior cloning contexts, methods that effectively combine policy acceleration with RL-based post-training remain less developed.

This work aims to address these identified gaps by presenting a methodology that enables efficient fine-tuning of flow-based policies using robot experience via RL post-training, while simultaneously achieving rapid, few-step inference without recourse to computationally intensive distillation. It positions itself as a bridge between powerful but potentially slow pre-trained generalist policies and the requirements of efficient, real-time adaptation in novel robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from two primary challenges observed in the application of diffusion and flow policies in robotics: performance limitations and computational inefficiency.

First, despite the capacity of Vision Language Action (VLA) and World Action Models (WAMs) to capture complex, multimodal action distributions, these pre-trained policies frequently encounter difficulties with zero-shot generalization. They can struggle to perform effectively on tasks unseen during initial training or fail when confronted with out-of-distribution environment states. While collecting additional expert demonstrations for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is a common approach, it is a resource-intensive process requiring human effort, time, and specific expertise. The objective is to provide an alternative that leverages the robot's own interaction experience for adaptation, thereby reducing reliance on manual data collection.

Second, a critical limitation for flow and diffusion policies in robotics is their inference speed. Generating an action typically involves integrating a stochastic or ordinary differential equation over multiple steps, leading to several neural function evaluations (NFEs) per action. This multi-step inference can result in slow policy execution, which is impractical for real-time robotic control that demands reactive and continuous motions. Moreover, this slow inference propagates to the training phase of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, where policy actions are frequently generated for critic function updates, thus prolonging the overall training time. Existing acceleration techniques often involve computationally expensive distillation processes during training, which the authors aim to circumvent.

Therefore, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Fine-tuning Suboptimal Flow Policies:** To develop a methodology that enables pre-trained, potentially suboptimal, flow-based policies to adapt and improve their performance on novel tasks or under distribution shifts, using the robot's own experience through RL post-training.
2. **Accelerating Inference Speed:** To achieve significant reductions in the number of neural function evaluations (NFEs) required for action generation, thereby accelerating policy inference for real-time robotic operation, aiming for few-step inference (e.g., 2-3 NFEs).
3. **Avoiding Computational Expense:** To accomplish both fine-tuning and acceleration without relying on computationally expensive distillation techniques commonly employed in other fast inference methods.
4. **Policy Steering via Optimal Transport:** To leverage optimal transport principles, specifically conditional optimal transport (COT) couplings, to guide the flow policy. This guidance is informed by an RL critic, steering the flow towards generating high-value actions by promoting straighter integration paths from the noise distribution to optimal action samples.
5. **Empirical Validation:** To demonstrate the method's effectiveness across various settings, including offline and online reinforcement learning, and its applicability to both single-task policies and larger Vision Language Action (VLA) models in simulated and real-world robotic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Optimal Transport Q-Learning (OTQL), addresses the fine-tuning and acceleration of flow-based policies by integrating reinforcement learning principles with Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) using advantage-weighted conditional optimal transport (wCOT).

**Background on Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) and Optimal Transport (OT):**
The work builds on CFM, where a neural ODE (vector field `v_θ`) transforms an initial noise distribution `p_z` into a conditional action distribution `p_a(a|s)`. Training involves minimizing a stochastic regression loss (Eq. 1) by matching `v_θ` to a target vector field `(a - z)` derived from a linear interpolant `x(t) = ta + (1 - t)z`. The coupling distribution `q(z, a|s)`, which pairs noise samples `z` with action samples `a`, is crucial. Optimal Transport (OT) theory, specifically its dynamic (Benamou-Brenier) formulation, suggests that couplings minimizing transportation cost lead to straighter paths, which can be beneficial for reducing integration steps. Conditional Optimal Transport (COT) extends this concept to conditional distributions.

**Optimal Transport Q-Learning (OTQL) Components:**

1. **Conditional Optimal Transport for Flow Acceleration and Steering (wCOT-CFM):**
 - **Goal:** To train a flow that approximates the dynamic COT ODE between the noise distribution and a refined target policy `~π(a|s) ∝ π(a|s)e^(λA(s,a))`. Here, `A(s,a)` is the advantage function, and `λ` is an energy scale parameter. The negative advantage serves as the "energy function" `E` used in the general formulation.
 - **Unconditional OT Couplings (Mini-batch Approximation):** For a batch of `m` action-condition samples `{(a_j, c_j)}` and `m` noise samples `_j`, an approximate unconditional OT plan is computed by solving an entropic OT problem (Eq. 2) between weighted measures `z` and `a`. The cost function `C_ij` is typically squared-Euclidean distance.
 - **Conditioning via Cost Augmentation:** To enforce condition-consistent couplings (i.e., `z` and `a` with similar states `s` being paired), the condition vector `c_j` (representing `s_j`) is concatenated with both `z_i` and `a_j` to form augmented vectors `(z_i || α * c_i_bar)` and `(a_j || α * c_j)`. The cost function `C_ij` is augmented to include `α^2 ||c_i_bar - c_j||^2`, where `α` is a scaling hyperparameter. `c_i_bar` represents the source condition corresponding to the target action condition `c_j`.
 - **Energy-Weighted Target Marginal and Source Condition Sampling:** To steer the flow towards high-value actions, the target action measure is re-weighted. For each target sample `(a_j, c_j)`, unnormalized weights `w_j = e^(λA(a_j, c_j))` are calculated. These weights are then normalized to form `p_j` (Eq. 5), increasing the probability mass on samples associated with higher advantage values. Critically, to approximate the COT plan, the source conditions `c_i_bar` used in the cost augmentation must be sampled from this same tilted conditional marginal distribution `sum_j p_j δ_c_j`.
 - **Flow Training with wCOT-CFM:** At each training step, the entropic OT problem `T_ε(z_bar, a_bar)` is solved using the augmented and re-weighted source and target measures. Noise-action pairs `(z, a)` are sampled from the resulting optimal coupling `Γ*` and then used in the standard CFM loss (Eq. 1) to update the flow network `v_θ`. This process trains the flow to learn direct paths towards high-advantage actions.

2. **Policy Post-training using wCOT-CFM (Algorithm 1):**
 - **Critic Training:** A Q-function `Q_φ(s, a)` is trained using a Temporal Difference (TD) loss (Eq. 6). The Bellman target `y = r + γQ_bar_φ(s', a')` is computed using a target network `Q_bar_φ` and next actions `a'` sampled from the *current* flow policy `v_θ` (via Euler integration). For tasks involving action chunks, a chunked Q-function and discounted sum of rewards over the chunk are used. An ensemble of critics is employed for stability in offline settings.
 - **Flow Policy Update:** Given a pre-trained base policy `v_bar_θ`, the current policy `v_θ` is initialized with `bar_θ`.
 - The advantage function `A(s_j, a_j) = Q_φ(s_j, a_j) - V_hat(s_j)` is computed. `V_hat(s_j)` is estimated by averaging `Q_φ(s_j, a_k_hat)` over `k` candidate actions `a_k_hat` generated by `v_θ`.
 - These advantage values are then used as the "negative energy" in the wCOT coupling formulation described above. The flow network `v_θ` is updated using the wCOT-CFM loss.
 - **Online Data Collection:** During online scenarios, `v_θ` is used to collect new transitions `(s, a, r, s')` which are added to the replay buffer `B` for subsequent training iterations.

3. **Practical Implementations:**
 - **Weight Clamping:** To prevent `w_j` from becoming excessively large or small, which could reduce the effective batch size when the Q-function has sharp peaks, `w_j` is clamped: `w_j = min(e^(λE), w_max)`.
 - **Rejection Sampling at Inference:** For Bellman target computation and final action selection during inference, `N` candidate actions are generated from the flow, and the one with the highest `Q`-value is selected. This provides an additional mechanism to concentrate samples towards high-value regions, complementing the OT-based steering, especially when `λ` is moderate. `N=5` candidates were often sufficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates OTQL across diverse simulated and real-world robotic tasks, demonstrating its capability for policy fine-tuning and acceleration.

**Simulated Offline Reinforcement Learning (OGBench):**
- OTQL achieved an average success rate of 0.59 across 40 tasks within the OGBench benchmark, outperforming other fast inference methods (e.g., FQL and QAM-F) by at least 5% on average.
- In specific challenging tasks, OTQL demonstrated performance comparable to, or exceeding, state-of-the-art methods that typically require more Neural Function Evaluations (NFEs) for inference. For example, on `antmaze-large`, OTQL reached a success rate of 0.88, compared to DSRL (0.61) and QAM-F (0.83).
- These results indicate that OTQL can train policies effectively from suboptimal demonstrations, maintaining competitive inference speed (2-3 NFEs) while achieving performance comparable to methods utilizing higher NFE budgets.

**Simulated Offline-Online and Online Reinforcement Learning:**
- **Offline-to-Online Adaptation (OGBench):**
 - In the `cube-double` task (with action chunking), OTQL, DSRL, FQL, and QAM-F showed similar performance improvements when transitioning from offline pre-training to online fine-tuning.
 - In the `humanoidmaze-medium` task (without action chunking), OTQL achieved near 90% success with offline pre-training, a performance level that only DSRL matched during online training. FQL and QAM-F, despite comparable inference speeds to OTQL, did not exceed a 50% success rate.
- **Online Fine-tuning (MuJoCo Tasks):**
 - On `OpenMicrowave-v0` and `ArrangeBoxes-v0`, OTQL consistently demonstrated effective online adaptation.
 - With an NFE of 3 and `N=5` rejection sampling candidates (on `ArrangeBoxes-v0`), OTQL outperformed high-NFE baselines like EWFM (NFE=10, N=1) and performed comparably to QC (NFE=10, N=32) while requiring significantly fewer inference steps.
 - It maintained a notable performance advantage over other fast methods like FQL, achieving a maximum success rate that was, on average, 15% higher.

**Real-World Policy Steering and Acceleration:**
- **Single-Task Policies:**
 - OTQL was applied to adapt base flow policies (initially trained with 10 demonstrations and exhibiting ≤50% success) on three real-world tasks: `stack cups`, `tape in penholder`, and `close door`.
 - Through a semi-offline adaptation process over 50-60 episodes, OTQL increased the average success rate of these policies from 36% to 86%.
 - Concurrently, the method reduced the NFEs required for inference from 10 to 3, representing a 70% speed-up.
- **VLA Policies (SmolVLA):**
 - OTQL was used to fine-tune SmolVLA, a state-of-the-art VLA, on the `tape in penholder` and `move knight` tasks. Initially, SmolVLA was fine-tuned with SFT using 10 demonstrations, resulting in suboptimal performance (below 50% success).
 - After adaptation with OTQL using 30 additional robot rollouts (40 episodes total), the average success rate increased from 38% to 76%.
 - The inference NFE for SmolVLA was also reduced to 3, demonstrating OTQL's capability to accelerate larger VLA models.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Temperature Parameter (λ):** A high `λ` (e.g., 1.5) concentrated the transport plan on very few samples, slowing convergence, though eventually achieving adequate performance. A very small `λ` failed to prioritize high-advantage samples. An optimal range for `λ` was found to be between [0.4, 1.0] for typical chunk sizes and episode steps.
- **Rejection Sampling Candidates (N):** Rejection sampling with a low `N` (e.g., 5) improved performance in earlier training iterations, especially when dealing with moderate `λ` values and sparse high-advantage actions. Its impact diminished towards the end of training, suggesting OTQL handles most of the adaptation.
- **NFE for Flow Inference:** Flows trained with OTQL maintained consistent performance across a range of NFE values (2 to 10), demonstrating that competitive results can be achieved with significantly fewer integration steps (NFE=2-3) without the need for distillation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Optimal Transport Q-Learning (OTQL) offers contributions to the field of robotic learning, particularly in the realm of adapting and accelerating flow-based policies.

The primary significance of OTQL lies in its capacity to **efficiently fine-tune pre-trained flow policies**, including complex Vision Language Action (VLA) models, using a limited amount of robotic interaction. By leveraging the robot's own experience through reinforcement learning (RL) post-training, the method reduces the dependency on extensive and often costly human demonstrations required by traditional supervised fine-tuning approaches. This capability addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying generalist policies to novel tasks or conditions.

A key impact is the **simultaneous acceleration of policy inference**. OTQL achieves few-step inference (e.g., 2-3 Neural Function Evaluations) by incorporating optimal transport principles. This leads to straighter integration paths from the noise distribution to the target action distribution, inherently reducing the computational burden without requiring computationally expensive distillation processes. This acceleration is crucial for enabling reactive and continuous control in real-time robotic applications, where rapid action generation is a prerequisite.

The methodology's integration of policy steering (towards high-value actions identified by an RL critic) and inference acceleration within a single training framework represents an advancement. This combined approach, utilizing advantage-weighted Conditional Optimal Transport Flow Matching (wCOT-CFM), demonstrates empirical effectiveness across diverse simulated and real-world tasks, often outperforming or matching existing state-of-the-art methods while providing a substantial speed-up in inference.

OTQL serves as a **bridge between large-scale pretraining of generalist policies and their efficient deployment** in specific, novel tasks where zero-shot generalization might be insufficient. This allows for more practical and adaptable robotic systems that can refine their behaviors based on direct environmental feedback.

**Potential limitations** of OTQL include its architectural specificity, as it requires the policy to be a neural ODE-based generative model, rather than treating it as a black box. This might restrict its direct applicability to certain policy architectures. Furthermore, the effectiveness of mini-batch optimal transport, a core component, may decrease as the dimensionality of the problem increases, potentially leading to larger errors in very high-dimensional action spaces. Finally, like many real-world RL methods, OTQL inherits challenges such as the need for manual episode labeling and environment resets, which can still demand human involvement in the experimental setup. The current formulation also kept the VLM of SmolVLA frozen during fine-tuning, leaving the full fine-tuning of such large models for future investigation due to potential overfitting risks in low-data regimes.
