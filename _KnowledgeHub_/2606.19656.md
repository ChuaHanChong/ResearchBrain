# DF-ExpEnse: Diffusion Filtered Exploration for Sample Efficient Finetuning

> [!summary] Summary
> DF-ExpEnse introduces a diffusion-filtered exploration method for finetuning robotic control policies, leveraging critic ensembles for uncertainty estimation and fleet normalization for collaborative learning. This approach consistently improves sample efficiency and achieves higher success rates across various manipulation and locomotion tasks by enabling more effective online data collection.

## Problem

- Improving sample efficiency during the finetuning of pretrained generative policies for robotic decision-making.
- Developing tractable strategies for effective exploration in high-dimensional, continuous action spaces common in robotics.
- Facilitating real-time collaborative exploration among parallel robotic agents or fleets to enhance the quality of collected online experience.

## Method

- **DF-ExpEnse** employs a pretrained ==diffusion policy== to generate a diverse set of action candidates and a ==critic ensemble== to calculate an ==exploration interest score== (a ==UCB==-inspired blend of expected value and uncertainty) for each.
- ==Behavior Cloning Sampling Regularization (BC-SR)== is incorporated to maintain ==multimodal priors== by augmenting the candidate set with samples from the initial diffusion policy, preventing early convergence.
- ==Fleet normalization== enables ==collaborative exploration== by aggregating and normalizing value and disagreement scores across all agents' candidates, encouraging diverse action selection by each agent based on global context.

## Results

- DF-ExpEnse consistently demonstrated superior sample efficiency and achieved higher average success rates or reward scores across various robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks compared to baseline finetuning methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of Behavior Cloning Sampling Regularization and Fleet Normalization, showing significant performance degradation when these components were removed or disabled.
- Performance improvements correlated with larger critic ensemble and fleet sizes, indicating better uncertainty estimates and more effective collaborative exploration, while moderate action candidate set sizes (**3-5**) proved efficient.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A principled exploration strategy in continuous action spaces can be achieved by combining multimodal action generation from diffusion policies with uncertainty estimation from critic ensembles.
> - Regularization that preserves the multimodality of the initial pretrained policy (BC-SR) is crucial for sustained exploration benefits and prevents finetuning from collapsing into suboptimal behaviors.
> - Real-time cross-agent normalization of exploration interests allows robotic fleets to collaborate effectively, avoiding redundant exploration and collecting higher-quality, more diverse online experience.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges in robotic decision-making, specifically focusing on improving sample efficiency during the finetuning of pretrained policies. The broader research landscape it integrates into includes:

- **Generative Control Policies and Offline Pretraining:** Recent advancements in generative modeling, particularly diffusion policies, have enabled the effective conversion of large offline demonstration datasets into robust behavior priors. These pretrained policies offer a foundation for subsequent online adaptation through reinforcement learning (RL). The paper acknowledges their capability to generate diverse actions, which is identified as a valuable asset for exploration.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Policy Finetuning:** A growing area of research involves adapting these powerful pretrained generative policies to new tasks or improving their performance by finetuning them with self-collected online experience. Techniques like DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025) and ResFiT (Ankile et al., 2025a) represent efforts in this domain.
- **Exploration in Continuous Action Spaces:** Effective exploration is crucial for sample-efficient RL, but it is particularly challenging in continuous action spaces common in robotics, where exhaustive action evaluation is infeasible. Existing exploration strategies often struggle with balancing the quality of actions with the need to reduce uncertainty.
- **Parallelized and Fleet Learning:** The increasing availability of high-fidelity simulation environments and large-scale real-world robotic deployments (robotic fleets) enables massive parallelization of online experience collection. While this increases data quantity, the paper identifies an opportunity to enhance data *quality* by enabling collaborative exploration among agents. Prior work in fleet learning often focuses on aggregating data or merging policies, but less on real-time collaborative exploration during action selection.

DF-ExpEnse positions itself at the intersection of these trends by proposing an exploration technique that leverages the multimodal capabilities of pretrained diffusion policies and facilitates collaboration within robotic fleets. It aims to enhance the quality of online experience, thereby increasing the sample efficiency of finetuning generative control policies via reinforcement learning. The method is designed to be integrated seamlessly with existing RL finetuning strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to increase sample efficiency for robotic policy improvement by developing an effective exploration strategy during online experience collection. The authors identify two key developments that support this investigation: the emergence of pretrained generative control policies (like diffusion policies) and the growth of parallelized robotic fleets.

The research is guided by three specific motivating questions:

1. **Quantifying Exploration Interest:** A core motivation is to establish a principled method for quantifying an agent's "exploration interest" for any given action. This involves balancing the expected quality of an action (exploitation) with the potential for uncertainty reduction (exploration). The aim is to move beyond purely exploitative action selection towards strategies that can effectively identify and prioritize actions that yield valuable new information.
2. **Tractable Identification of Exploratory Actions in Continuous Spaces:** In robotic decision-making, the continuous nature of action spaces makes exhaustive evaluation of all possible actions computationally intractable. Therefore, a key objective is to find a tractable mechanism to identify a subset of reasonable actions that are worth exploring. This mechanism should ideally ensure broad coverage of the action space when necessary, while also guaranteeing a baseline level of action quality.
3. **Facilitating Collaborative Exploration in Robotic Fleets:** With the increasing deployment of robotic fleets and parallel simulation environments, there is an opportunity to move beyond individual agent exploration. The motivation here is to design a method that allows multiple agents to collaborate, sharing information in real-time to avoid redundant exploration and collectively gather higher-quality and more diverse online experience. This aims to maximize the collective benefit of parallelized data collection.

DF-ExpEnse is developed as a solution to these challenges, integrating a critic ensemble for uncertainty estimation, a diffusion policy for candidate action generation, and cross-agent communication for collective exploration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DF-ExpEnse, or Diffusion Filtered Exploration via Ensembles, is an exploration technique designed to be integrated into existing reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks for finetuning pretrained generative control policies. The approach centers on three main components: a critic ensemble for evaluating exploration interest, a diffusion policy for generating action candidates, and fleet normalization for collaborative exploration.

#### 3.1. Diffusion Filtered Exploration via Ensembles (DF-ExpEnse)

At each online experience collection timestep, DF-ExpEnse utilizes a pretrained diffusion policy, denoted $\pi_{\text{dp}}^{\theta}(\cdot)$, and an ensemble of $K$ Q-value functions (critics), $Q^{[1...K]}(\cdot)$. These critics are typically part of the underlying RL framework and are repurposed for online inference.

The process involves three steps:

1. **Action Candidate Generation:** The diffusion policy $\pi_{\text{dp}}^{\theta}(\cdot)$ generates a set of $M$ candidate actions, $[a_1, \ldots, a_M]$, conditioned on the current state $s$. This leverages the diffusion policy's multimodal modeling capability to propose a diverse yet potentially high-quality set of actions, thereby filtering the continuous action space to a tractable size for evaluation.
2. **Exploration Interest Calculation:** For each candidate action $a_m$, its exploration interest, $e_m$, is computed. This calculation combines an estimate of the action's value and the uncertainty (disagreement) among the critic ensemble.
 - **Value Estimate:** The minimum of the ensemble's Q-value predictions, $\min(Q^{[1...K]}(a_m, s))$, is used to mitigate potential overestimation.
 - **Uncertainty Estimate:** The standard deviation of the ensemble's Q-value predictions, $\text{std}(Q^{[1...K]}(a_m, s))$, serves as a measure of critic disagreement and thus uncertainty.
 - **Formula:** $e_m = \min(Q^{[1...K]}(a_m, s)) + \alpha \cdot \text{std}(Q^{[1...K]}(a_m, s))$, where $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter controlling the weight of uncertainty. This formulation is inspired by Upper Confidence Bounds (UCB) action selection.
3. **Action Selection:** The action candidate with the maximum estimated exploration interest is selected for execution: $a_{\star} \leftarrow \arg \max_{a \in [a_1, \ldots, a_M]} [e_1, \ldots, e_M]$.

The collected online experience is then used by standard RL techniques to finetune the agent. The authors note that setting $\alpha=0$ reduces DF-ExpEnse to an exploitative Max-Q selection strategy, which serves as a baseline for comparison.

#### 3.2. Behavior Cloning Sampling Regularization (BC-SR)

To prevent the finetuned policy from converging on a limited number of action modes, which could hinder exploration, DF-ExpEnse incorporates Behavior Cloning Sampling Regularization (BC-SR). This technique regularizes the set of action candidates by including $p$ samples from the *initial*, offline-pretrained diffusion policy, denoted $\pi_{\text{dp}}^{\theta_{\text{init}}}(\cdot)$. This ensures that the agent continues to consider multimodal priors from its initial pretraining, even as its current policy evolves during finetuning. BC-SR is applied at inference time to augment the candidate action set.

#### 3.3. DF-ExpEnse with Fleet Normalization

In scenarios involving a fleet of $N$ parallel agents, DF-ExpEnse introduces a mechanism for collaborative exploration through real-time cross-fleet communication:

1. **Individual Candidate Generation and Evaluation:** Each agent $n$ (from $1$ to $N$) generates its own set of $M$ candidate actions, $[a_{n,1}, \ldots, a_{n,M}]$, based on its local state $s_n$. It then computes individual value estimates ($v_{n,m}$) and critic disagreement scores ($d_{n,m}$) for its candidates using its local critic ensemble.
2. **Cross-Fleet Communication and Normalization:** Before action selection, all individual value estimates and disagreement scores from *all* candidate actions across the *entire fleet* (total $N \times M$ candidates) are aggregated. Each agent then normalizes its own $v_{n,m}$ and $d_{n,m}$ components using the mean and standard deviation computed from these fleet-wide aggregated statistics. This results in normalized value ($\bar{v}_{n,m}$) and disagreement ($\bar{d}_{n,m}$) terms.
3. **Fleet-Normalized Exploration Interest:** The exploration interest for each candidate is then re-calculated using these fleet-normalized terms: $\bar{e}_{n,m} = \bar{v}_{n,m} + \alpha \cdot \bar{d}_{n,m}$.
4. **Collaborative Action Selection:** Each agent $n$ selects its action, $a_{\star,n}$, by maximizing this fleet-normalized exploration interest among its local candidates. This mechanism encourages agents to select actions that are relatively more uncertain or valuable when contextualized by the aggregate information from the entire group, promoting diverse and collective exploration.

#### 3.4. Integration with Reinforcement Learning Finetuning

DF-ExpEnse is designed as an online exploration technique, compatible with various underlying RL algorithms that finetune diffusion policies. The experiments primarily use:

- **DSRL (Noise Aliasing):** DSRL freezes the pretrained diffusion policy and learns a noise selector (implemented as a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent) that generates input noise for the diffusion model's deterministic sampling. DF-ExpEnse modifies DSRL's online experience collection by selecting actions from a batch of candidates rather than executing a single sampled action directly.
- **ResFiT:** This method optimizes a residual policy that adjusts the output of a frozen diffusion policy. DF-ExpEnse is integrated by applying its action selection mechanism during ResFiT's online experience collection phase.

A crucial aspect is that DF-ExpEnse is *only active during online experience collection*. During test-time evaluations, it is disabled to ensure fair assessment of the finetuned policy's intrinsic quality without the influence of explicit exploration strategies. The authors also specify hyperparameters for the diffusion policy, DSRL, and DF-ExpEnse components (e.g., candidate sample size of 3, critic ensemble size of 10, fleet size of 4, $\alpha=0.5$ for most tasks).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of DF-ExpEnse was conducted across a variety of robotic manipulation tasks (RoboMimic: Lift, Can, Square, Tool Hang; DexMimicGen: Can Sort, Box Cleanup, Coffee) and locomotion tasks (OpenAI Gym: HalfCheetah-v2, Hopper-v2, Walker2D-v2). Key findings include:

#### 4.1. Consistent Sample Efficiency Improvements

DF-ExpEnse consistently demonstrated superior sample efficiency across all evaluated tasks, achieving higher average success rates or reward scores for a given amount of online experience compared to baseline methods.
- **Vs. Vanilla DSRL/ResFiT:** When integrated with DSRL and ResFiT, DF-ExpEnse led to clear performance benefits (Figures 2 and 3), indicating that its enhanced exploration strategy directly improves the quality of collected data for finetuning.
- **Vs. Max-Q Selection:** DF-ExpEnse significantly outperformed the Max-Q selection baseline (equivalent to $\alpha=0$), which is an exploitative strategy. Max-Q often performed worse than even vanilla DSRL on manipulation tasks, suggesting that greedy action selection from a sampled set can reinforce suboptimal behaviors or lead to premature convergence to inferior action modes. This outcome highlights the necessity of balancing quality with uncertainty for effective exploration.
- **Vs. Alternative Exploration Strategies:** DF-ExpEnse consistently outperformed Exploration via Elliptical Episodic Bonuses (E3B) and Plan2Explore. A notable advantage is that DF-ExpEnse achieves these gains by reusing components already present in the RL framework (critic ensembles) and does not require additional model components like dynamics models or specialized state representations (e.g., DINO embeddings for E3B).

#### 4.2. Importance of Behavior Cloning Sampling Regularization (BC-SR)

Ablation studies (Figure 4) demonstrated the critical role of BC-SR in maintaining exploration benefits:
- Removing BC-SR from DF-ExpEnse consistently degraded its sample efficiency.
- Adding BC-SR to the Max-Q baseline significantly improved its performance, suggesting it mitigates the tendency of exploitative methods to collapse into suboptimal modes.
This indicates that preserving multimodality by regularly sampling from the initial pretrained policy is important for fostering continued exploration throughout finetuning.

#### 4.3. Efficacy of Fleet Normalization

Empirical results (Figure 5) confirmed that fleet normalization, enabling cross-agent communication and contextualized action selection, consistently improved the quality of collective experience collection. This led to increased sample efficiency compared to a fleet of DF-ExpEnse agents operating in isolation, validating the benefits of collaborative exploration.

#### 4.4. Impact of Hyperparameter Sizes

Ablation studies on the RoboMimic Can task (Figure 6) investigated the effect of different component sizes:
- **Critic Ensemble Size:** Performance improved with larger ensemble sizes (from 2 to 5 to 10), with performance appearing to saturate around 5-10 critics. A small ensemble (e.g., 2) led to significantly decreased sample efficiency, attributed to noisier uncertainty estimates.
- **Action Candidate Set Size:** While there were slight improvements when increasing the sample size from 3 to 5, further increases (to 7) yielded comparable performance. This suggests a point of diminishing returns where larger candidate sets introduce redundant modes.
- **Fleet Size:** Larger fleet sizes generally correlated with improved performance, demonstrating that more agents contributing to fleet normalization can lead to more effective collaborative exploration. Performance decreased with smaller fleet sizes.

#### 4.5. Robustness to Design Decisions

Additional ablations showed that DF-ExpEnse's performance was robust to certain design choices:
- **UCB Value Estimation:** Using the mean of ensemble predictions for value estimation (standard UCB) yielded comparable performance to using the minimum (default DF-ExpEnse) (Figure 8).
- **Exploration-Weighted Sampling:** Selecting actions via sampling from a distribution weighted by exploration interest achieved comparable performance to selecting the action with maximum interest, though the latter was chosen as default for simplicity (Figure 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DF-ExpEnse holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency in Robotics:** The consistent improvements in sample efficiency demonstrated across various manipulation and locomotion tasks are highly significant for robotic applications. Data collection in the real world is often expensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive. By improving the quality of online experience, DF-ExpEnse can accelerate the finetuning process of complex robotic skills, making policy adaptation more practical and scalable for real-world deployments.
- **Principled Exploration in Continuous Action Spaces:** The method provides a principled and tractable approach to exploration in high-dimensional, continuous action spaces. By combining the multimodal generative capabilities of diffusion policies with the uncertainty estimation of critic ensembles, it effectively balances the need for high-quality actions with the imperative to reduce uncertainty, a long-standing challenge in RL.
- **Leveraging Existing Components for Efficiency:** A key strength of DF-ExpEnse is its ability to integrate seamlessly with existing RL finetuning frameworks. It reuses components like pretrained diffusion policies and critic ensembles that are already part of the optimization pipeline, avoiding the need for additional, separate model modules (e.g., dynamics models). This minimizes computational overhead and complexity, making it a resource-efficient solution.
- **Facilitating Collaborative Fleet Learning:** The introduction of fleet normalization offers a novel and effective mechanism for robotic fleets to collaborate on exploration. This allows individual agents to contextualize their exploration interests against the collective experience of the group, leading to more diverse and less redundant data collection. As parallelized robotic deployments increase, this collaborative aspect could significantly amplify the efficiency of learning across multiple agents.
- **Generalizability and Versatility:** The successful integration of DF-ExpEnse with different underlying RL finetuning techniques (DSRL and ResFiT) and its consistent performance across diverse robotic tasks highlight its generalizability. This suggests that the approach is broadly applicable to various robotic control problems involving pretrained generative policies.
- **Broader Implications for AI and Robotics:** This work contributes to the ongoing effort to make AI-driven robotic systems more adaptable and efficient. By enabling faster skill learning and refinement, it can pave the way for more sophisticated and robust autonomous robots capable of operating effectively in complex and changing environments.

The paper acknowledges potential limitations, such as the fixed exploration strategy at every timestep, which might be optimized by dynamic exploration conditioning on agent state. Furthermore, while fleet normalization is beneficial, its real-world implementation in large, distributed fleets might face latency and communication challenges, suggesting future work on localized normalization strategies.
