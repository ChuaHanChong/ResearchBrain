# Omni-SimpleMem: Autoresearch-Guided Discovery of Lifelong Multimodal Agent Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> An autonomous research pipeline generated OMNI-SIMPLEMEM, a unified multimodal memory framework that enables lifelong AI agents to effectively manage diverse experiences across text, images, audio, and video. This system achieved state-of-the-art F1 scores up to 0.613 on LoCoMo and 0.810 on Mem-Gallery benchmarks, significantly outperforming previous baselines, while also demonstrating 3.5x faster throughput.

## Problem

- AI agents struggle to effectively retain, organize, and recall the diverse, multimodal experiences accumulated during lifelong interactions.
- Existing memory systems are often unimodal or fragmented, neglect rich visual and auditory signals, and face scalability or retrieval quality degradation.
- Manual research for complex AI systems is slow and limited, while traditional AutoML cannot perform architectural redesigns or complex bug fixes crucial for optimization.

## Method

- An autonomous research pipeline, **AUTORESEARCHCLAW**, was deployed to iteratively discover and optimize **OMNI-SIMPLEMEM**, starting from a text-only memory framework.
- The pipeline analyzes performance deficiencies, generates hypotheses, implements code changes (architectural, retrieval, prompt, data pipeline), executes experiments, and evaluates using quantitative metrics.
- **OMNI-SIMPLEMEM** features selective ==novelty-based multimodal ingestion== into ==Multimodal Atomic Units (MAUs)==, ==two-tier hot/cold storage==, ==progressive pyramid retrieval== with ==hybrid dense-sparse search==, and ==knowledge graph-augmented retrieval==.

## Results

- The autonomous pipeline successfully improved LoCoMo F1 from 0.117 to **0.598** (**+411%**) and Mem-Gallery F1 from 0.254 to **0.797** (**+214%**) over approximately 72 hours, demonstrating high-impact bug fixes and architectural discoveries.
- **OMNI-SIMPLEMEM** achieved state-of-the-art F1 scores of up to **0.613** on LoCoMo and **0.810** on Mem-Gallery, consistently outperforming leading baselines by substantial margins (e.g., >25 percentage points on Mem-Gallery).
- The system demonstrated **3.5x** faster throughput than the fastest baseline (**5.81** queries/second with 8 workers) due to its architecture supporting concurrent lookups using read-only indices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous research pipelines can effectively discover and optimize complex AI systems by performing architectural redesigns, diagnosing/fixing bugs (e.g., +175% F1 from one-line fix), and refining prompt engineering, extending beyond traditional hyperparameter tuning.
> - The most impactful performance gains in complex systems often stem from structural changes, bug fixes, and prompt refinements rather than cumulative hyperparameter adjustments.
> - Multimodal memory is particularly suitable for autonomous research due to immediate scalar evaluation metrics, a modular architecture, fast iteration cycles, and version-controlled code modifications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the evolving field of AI agents, particularly those powered by large language models (LLMs), and their capacity for lifelong learning and interaction. Contemporary AI agents demonstrate capabilities such as tool use, multi-step reasoning, and cross-modal comprehension, enabling them to engage with users over extended periods. During these interactions, agents accumulate diverse forms of data, including text, images, audio, and video. A recognized limitation in the advancement of these agents is their ability to effectively retain, organize, and recall this accumulated multimodal experience.

Existing approaches to agent memory typically fall into two categories. The first involves storing raw inputs and retrieving them based on embedding similarity. This method, exemplified by works such as Lewis et al. (2020) and Borgeaud et al. (2022), faces challenges related to storage scalability and the degradation of retrieval quality as memory size increases. The second category focuses on structured memory management with explicit operations, as seen in systems like MemGPT (Packer et al., 2023) and Generative Agents (Park et al., 2023). These systems predominantly operate on text-only data, often neglecting or discarding rich visual and auditory signals that are integral to comprehensive multimodal experiences.

A common characteristic of these existing memory systems is that they are products of manual research and development cycles. This involves human researchers hypothesizing improvements, implementing them, evaluating performance, and iterating. This manual process is inherently slow, typically allowing for the exploration of only a limited number of configurations. Furthermore, tightly coupled components in complex systems can lead to missed interactions and suboptimal designs when explored manually. While traditional Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) methods (Hutter et al., 2019a) can search predefined numerical hyperparameter spaces, they are generally not equipped to perform tasks such as code comprehension, bug diagnosis, architectural redesign, or cross-component reasoning, which are crucial for optimizing complex AI systems.

Against this background, the paper positions itself at the intersection of AI agent memory systems and the emerging paradigm of autonomous scientific discovery (autoresearch). Recent research in autoresearch (Lu et al., 2024; Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Panfilov et al., 2026) has demonstrated the capability of LLM agents to autonomously discover novel algorithms that can surpass human-designed baselines, particularly in domains with well-defined, quantitative evaluation signals. This paper extends this paradigm to the optimization of complex, multi-component AI systems, specifically addressing the challenge of building unified multimodal memory for lifelong AI agents. It aims to demonstrate that an autonomous research pipeline can systematically navigate a vast design space to diagnose issues, propose architectural modifications, and address data pipeline problems, thereby overcoming the limitations of both manual research and traditional AutoML.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate OMNI-SIMPLEMEM, a unified multimodal memory framework designed for lifelong AI agents, through an autonomous research pipeline. This overarching goal is motivated by the recognized challenge that while AI agents are increasingly designed to operate over extended time horizons and interact with diverse multimodal data, their ability to effectively manage and utilize this accumulated experience remains a significant bottleneck.

More specifically, the key objectives and their underlying motivations include:

1. **To discover a unified multimodal memory framework**: The paper aims to move beyond unimodal or fragmented multimodal memory solutions by designing a system capable of retaining, organizing, and recalling heterogeneous streams of text, images, audio, and video. This is motivated by the understanding that real-world agent interactions are inherently multimodal, and current memory systems often discard valuable cross-modal information.
2. **To demonstrate the efficacy of autonomous research for complex AI systems**: A core motivation is to test whether autonomous research pipelines, previously shown to discover novel algorithms, can be applied to optimize complex, multi-component AI systems. The researchers contend that the design space for multimodal memory—encompassing architectural choices, retrieval strategies, prompt engineering, and data pipeline configurations—is too vast and interconnected for manual human exploration or traditional hyperparameter-focused AutoML. The objective is to show that an autonomous pipeline can effectively navigate this complexity, identify failure modes, propose architectural modifications, and repair system bugs without human intervention in the inner loop.
3. **To identify impactful discovery types beyond hyperparameter tuning**: The work seeks to categorize and quantify the impact of different types of autonomous discoveries. This is motivated by the hypothesis that significant performance gains in complex systems often arise from structural changes, bug fixes, or prompt refinements rather than solely from numerical hyperparameter adjustments. By identifying these high-impact discovery types, the research aims to highlight the capabilities of autonomous research that fundamentally surpass traditional AutoML.
4. **To characterize properties suitable for autonomous research**: The paper intends to analyze why multimodal memory, in particular, is a suitable domain for autonomous research. This objective is driven by the need to provide guidance for future applications of autonomous research pipelines to other complex AI system domains, thereby contributing to the broader methodology of AI-driven scientific discovery.
5. **To achieve state-of-the-art performance**: A practical objective is to ensure that the autonomously discovered OMNI-SIMPLEMEM system performs competitively, establishing new state-of-the-art results on relevant benchmarks for lifelong multimodal agent memory. This validates the effectiveness of the autonomous discovery process in producing high-quality AI systems.

In essence, the motivation stems from the limitations of current agent memory systems and traditional development methodologies when faced with the complexity of lifelong multimodal interactions. The researchers propose autonomous research as a viable and effective paradigm to overcome these limitations, leading to more capable and efficiently developed AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the deployment of an autonomous research pipeline, AUTORESEARCHCLAW (Liu et al., 2026b), to systematically discover and optimize OMNI-SIMPLEMEM. This pipeline operates iteratively, starting from an existing unimodal memory framework and progressively enhancing it to support multimodal, lifelong agent memory.

**Starting Point and Inputs:**
The autonomous research pipeline was initialized with three primary inputs:
1. **Codebase**: The SimpleMem (Liu et al., 2026a) codebase, a text-only lifelong memory framework, served as the initial baseline. The pipeline was tasked with extending this system to full multimodal support, including designing components for ingestion, storage, and retrieval of text, images, audio, and video.
2. **Benchmarks**: Two benchmark evaluation harnesses, LoCoMo (Maharana et al., 2024) and Mem-Gallery (Bei et al., 2026), were provided. These benchmarks offer quantitative evaluation metrics (primarily F1 score) to guide the optimization process.
3. **LLM Access**: API access to large language model providers was granted to the pipeline.

**Autonomous Research Pipeline (AUTORESEARCHCLAW):**
The AUTORESEARCHCLAW pipeline functions through an iterative loop comprising 23 stages organized into 8 phases (detailed in Appendix D). The core loop involves:
1. **Analysis**: Reviewing previous experimental results and identifying performance deficiencies.
2. **Hypothesis Generation**: Formulating a hypothesis for improvement based on the analysis.
3. **Implementation**: Generating and modifying Python code to implement the hypothesized change, including architectural components, retrieval strategies, prompt engineering, or data pipeline adjustments. The pipeline performs Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) validation for code changes.
4. **Execution and Evaluation**: Running experiments in a sandboxed environment and evaluating the modified system on the specified benchmarks using quantitative metrics (F1).
5. **Decision Making**: Based on the evaluation, the pipeline autonomously decides to `proceed` (if metrics improved by ≥ 0.5%), `iterate` (if results are ambiguous, prompting refinement of the current hypothesis), or `pivot` (if two consecutive degradations occur, triggering a revert to a previous stable state and exploration of a new direction). A self-healing module is incorporated for execution-level errors (e.g., API errors, dependency issues, runtime exceptions), diagnosing the error type and generating targeted fixes.

**The Discovered OMNI-SIMPLEMEM Architecture:**
Through this iterative process, the pipeline converged upon the OMNI-SIMPLEMEM architecture, structured around three primary principles:

1. **Selective Ingestion:**
 - **Novelty-Based Filtering**: Incoming multimodal inputs (text, image, audio, video) are assessed for novelty by modality-specific lightweight perceptual encoders. For vision, CLIP embeddings are used to detect scene changes; for audio, Voice Activity Detection (VAD) gates retention to filter silence; for text, Jaccard overlap with recent summaries identifies near-duplicates. Redundant content is discarded to reduce storage.
 - **Multimodal Atomic Units (MAUs)**: Filtered signals are encapsulated into a unified representation called MAUs. Each MAU, M = ⟨s, e, p, τ, m, ℓ⟩, decouples compact searchable metadata (summary `s`, embedding `e`, timestamp `τ`, modality `m`, structural links `ℓ`) from the heavy raw content (`p` points to raw content in cold storage). This creates a two-tier storage system: `hot storage` for fast retrieval of metadata, and `cold storage` for lazy access to large assets.

2. **Progressive Retrieval with Hybrid Search:**
 - **Hybrid Dense-Sparse Search**: Given a user query, dense retrieval (using FAISS for high-dimensional vector similarity search over L2-normalized MAU embeddings) and sparse retrieval (using BM25 scoring over MAU summaries for keyword matching) are performed in parallel. A key autonomous discovery was **set-union merging**, which appends BM25-only results to the dense results while preserving the dense ranking, instead of traditional score-based re-ranking.
 - **Pyramid Retrieval**: Retrieved content is expanded in stages to manage token budgets for the LLM. Level 1 returns concise summaries for top-k candidates. Level 2 loads full text or detailed captions for candidates exceeding a similarity threshold (θ). Level 3 loads raw content from cold storage (e.g., images, audio) under an explicit token budget (B), greedily expanding items based on similarity-per-token. These transitions are governed by deterministic rules.

3. **Knowledge Graph-Augmented Retrieval:**
 - OMNI-SIMPLEMEM maintains a knowledge graph (G) to capture entities and relationships across MAUs.
 - **Entity and Relation Extraction**: During MAU creation, an LLM extracts typed entities (7 categories: Person, Location, Event, Concept, Time, Organization, Object) and directed relations from summaries, linking them back to source MAUs.
 - **Entity Resolution**: To prevent fragmentation, entity resolution merges real-world entities appearing under different surface forms, using a hybrid similarity measure combining name embedding cosine similarity and Jaro-Winkler string similarity.
 - **Query-Time Expansion**: For a query, seed entities are identified, and the knowledge graph is expanded within `h` hops. Reached entities are scored by distance-decayed relevance. MAUs linked to high-scoring graph entities are merged with results from the hybrid search.

**Benchmark-Specific Optimization:**
The pipeline employs a two-phase optimization strategy for each benchmark:
1. **Development Subset Iteration**: A small, representative subset of each benchmark is used for rapid experimentation during the optimization loop (under 2 hours per experiment for LoCoMo, minutes for Mem-Gallery).
2. **Full Benchmark Evaluation**: Once the optimization trajectory converges, the final configuration is evaluated on the complete benchmark.

**Iterative Diagnosis and Repair:**
The pipeline incorporates mechanisms for two levels of failure repair:
1. **Execution Level**: Detects and fixes errors such as API failures (e.g., expired API keys, leading to a switch to a local embedding backend), dependency errors, runtime exceptions, and output format mismatches.
2. **Semantic Level**: Analyzes cases where experiments succeed but yield unexpectedly low metrics, prompting deeper inspection and hypothesis refinement.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmarks**: LoCoMo (1,986 QA pairs across multi-session dialogues, token-level F1) and Mem-Gallery (1,711 QA pairs from 240 multimodal dialogues with 1,003 grounded images, F1, EM, BLEU scores).
- **Baselines**: Six existing memory systems representing diverse philosophies were compared: MemVerse, Mem0, Claude-Mem, A-MEM, MemGPT, and SimpleMem.
- **LLM Backbones**: GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4.1-nano, GPT-5.1, and GPT-5-nano were used for evaluation.
- **Technical Details**: Dense retrieval used FAISS with `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings (384d). Visual novelty filtering employed frozen `CLIP ViT-B/32`. Knowledge graph extraction leveraged `GPT-4o` in JSON mode. Default hyperparameters included `top-k=20`, `θ=0.4`, and `B=6,000` tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings regarding both the autonomous optimization process and the performance of the discovered OMNI-SIMPLEMEM system.

**Autonomous Optimization Trajectories:**
The AUTORESEARCHCLAW pipeline successfully executed approximately 50 experiments across two benchmarks within roughly 72 hours of wall-clock time. This iterative process led to substantial performance improvements from naive baselines.
- **LoCoMo Benchmark**: The F1 score improved from an initial 0.117 to 0.598, representing a +411% increase. The trajectory involved 9 successful iterations and 2 automatically reverted failed experiments. A critical discovery (Iter 1, +175% F1) was a one-line bug fix related to a missing `response_format` parameter in an API call, which had caused excessive verbosity. Another notable discovery (Iter 5) involved autonomously generating a keyword-matching script to correct corrupted MAU timestamps, improving F1 by +7%. The pipeline also discovered that set-union merging of FAISS and BM25 results outperformed score-based fusion.
- **Mem-Gallery Benchmark**: The F1 score improved from an initial 0.254 to 0.797, representing a +214% increase, over 39 experiments across 7 phases. The largest single improvement (+53%) resulted from a counter-intuitive discovery: returning full original dialogue text instead of LLM-generated summaries significantly improved token-overlap F1. The pipeline also found that the *positioning* of prompt constraints (before vs. after the question) was more impactful than the constraint content itself, leading to a +188% improvement in one specific category. A BM25 tokenization fix (stripping punctuation) yielded a +0.018 F1 gain, exceeding the impact of multiple rounds of prompt engineering.

**Nature of Discoveries:**
A central finding is that the most impactful discoveries made by the autonomous pipeline were not hyperparameter adjustments. Bug fixes (e.g., the `response_format` error on LoCoMo, +175%), architectural changes (e.g., set-union merging, +44%), and prompt engineering (e.g., constraint positioning, +188% on specific categories) individually contributed more to performance gains than the cumulative effect of all hyperparameter tuning. This observation indicates that the pipeline's capabilities extend fundamentally beyond the scope of traditional AutoML.

**OMNI-SIMPLEMEM System Performance:**
OMNI-SIMPLEMEM was evaluated against six baseline memory systems across five LLM backbones.
- **LoCoMo**: OMNI-SIMPLEMEM consistently achieved the highest overall F1 score across all LLM backbones, ranging from 0.492 (GPT-4.1-nano) to 0.613 (GPT-5.1). This substantially outperformed SimpleMem (0.342–0.432), the previous state-of-the-art on LoCoMo. OMNI-SIMPLEMEM exhibited particular strength in multi-hop, single-hop, and open-domain question categories.
- **Mem-Gallery**: OMNI-SIMPLEMEM achieved F1 scores between 0.749 and 0.810, outperforming all baselines by considerable margins. SimpleMem, the strongest baseline, trailed OMNI-SIMPLEMEM by over 25 percentage points. These results suggest that OMNI-SIMPLEMEM's performance gains are attributed to its overall architectural design, including hybrid search, pyramid retrieval, and knowledge graph augmentation.

**Ablation Studies:**
A component ablation study on LoCoMo revealed the relative importance of OMNI-SIMPLEMEM's design choices:
- Pyramid expansion: Removal resulted in a mean F1 drop of 10.2 points (17%).
- BM25 hybrid search: Removal resulted in a mean F1 drop of 8.5 points (14%).
- LLM summarization: Removal resulted in a mean F1 drop of 7.3 points (12%).
- Reduced top-k (5 vs 20): Mean F1 drop of 4.2 points (7%).
- Metadata context: Modest mean F1 drop of 1.4 points (2%).
The two most impactful components (pyramid expansion and hybrid search) were also the ones that received the most optimization iterations from the autonomous pipeline.

**Efficiency:**
OMNI-SIMPLEMEM achieved a throughput of 5.81 queries/second with 8 parallel workers, which is 3.5 times faster than the fastest baseline. This efficiency is attributed to its architecture, which supports concurrent lookups using read-only FAISS and BM25 indices, thereby mitigating the bottleneck of sequential LLM generation present in other systems.

**Case Study: Multi-Hop Retrieval:**
A case study on a multi-hop query from LoCoMo demonstrated the synergistic operation of hybrid search, knowledge graph expansion, and pyramid retrieval. For a query requiring synthesis of facts across separate conversation sessions, OMNI-SIMPLEMEM successfully combined dense and sparse retrieval, utilized knowledge graph expansion to link entities across sessions, and progressively expanded content to generate the correct answer. In contrast, MemGPT, lacking cross-session entity linking, failed to provide a correct answer.

**Properties for Autoresearch Suitability:**
The paper identifies four properties that make multimodal memory particularly well-suited for autonomous research pipelines:
1. **Immediate scalar evaluation metrics**: Facilitates tight optimization loops.
2. **Modular architecture**: Allows for isolated modification and testing of components.
3. **Fast iteration cycles**: Enables the exploration of dozens of hypotheses within days (1-2 hours per experiment).
4. **Version-controlled code modifications**: Allows for clean reversion of failed experiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on OMNI-SIMPLEMEM represents a notable contribution to the fields of AI agents, multimodal learning, and autonomous research. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Lifelong Multimodal Memory for AI Agents**: OMNI-SIMPLEMEM addresses a critical technical bottleneck by providing a unified framework for retaining, organizing, and recalling diverse multimodal experiences over extended periods. This capability is fundamental for the development of more capable, persistent, and context-aware AI agents that can learn and interact naturally in dynamic, real-world environments. The system's state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks suggests its practical utility.

2. **Validation of Autonomous Research Paradigm for Complex Systems**: This work provides empirical evidence for the efficacy of autonomous research pipelines (specifically AUTORESEARCHCLAW) in optimizing complex, multi-component AI systems. It moves beyond previous demonstrations of autonomous discovery in more constrained settings (e.g., algorithm generation or hyperparameter tuning) by showcasing the pipeline's ability to perform architectural redesign, diagnose and fix code-level bugs, and refine prompt engineering strategies. This expands the perceived scope and utility of AI-driven research.

3. **Shift in AI System Development Methodology**: The findings suggest a potential shift in how complex AI systems are developed. By demonstrating that an AI pipeline can autonomously discover high-impact improvements (such as bug fixes and architectural changes that individually outweigh cumulative hyperparameter tuning), the research points towards a future where AI systems can self-optimize and evolve with less direct human intervention in the inner development loop. This could accelerate the pace of innovation and enhance the robustness of AI systems.

4. **Guidance for Future Applications of Autoresearch**: The identification of four key properties that make multimodal memory suitable for autonomous research (immediate scalar metrics, modular architecture, fast iteration cycles, and version-controlled code) offers a valuable roadmap for applying similar autonomous discovery pipelines to other complex AI system domains. This provides practical criteria for researchers and developers considering where autoresearch might yield the most significant benefits.

5. **Improved Efficiency and Scalability**: The achieved throughput of OMNI-SIMPLEMEM, which is 3.5 times faster than leading baselines due to architectural choices allowing parallelization, indicates practical benefits for deploying lifelong agents. Efficient memory management and retrieval are crucial for real-world applications where agents need to operate at scale without excessive computational overhead.

6. **Enhanced System Robustness**: The pipeline's ability to autonomously diagnose and repair execution-level and semantic-level failures contributes to the development of more robust and self-healing AI systems. This reduces the manual debugging and maintenance burden, making AI agents more reliable over their operational lifespan.

In conclusion, OMNI-SIMPLEMEM represents an advancement in agent memory capabilities while simultaneously serving as a robust case study for the expanded potential of autonomous scientific discovery in artificial intelligence research.
