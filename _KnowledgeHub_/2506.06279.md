# CoMemo: LVLMs Need Image Context with Image Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> Chinese Academy of Sciences and Shanghai AI Lab researchers develop CoMemo, a dual-path architecture that addresses the "lost in the middle" phenomenon and positional encoding limitations in Large Vision-Language Models by combining a Context path (autoregressive processing) with an Image Memory path (cross-attention retrieval) alongside RoPE-DHR positional encoding that preserves 2D spatial relationships in high-resolution images, achieving 17.2% relative improvement on caption tasks and 7.0% on long-generation tasks compared to single-paradigm baselines while demonstrating superior performance across seven benchmark categories including multi-image reasoning where CoMemo maintains higher accuracy for information placed in middle sequences compared to conventional LVLM-S models, with the framework employing a three-stage training strategy that prevents optimization bias between pathways through gate freezing and validates consistent improvements at both 2B and 8B parameter scales across diverse fine-tuning datasets.

## Problem

- LVLMs exhibit "lost in the middle" phenomenon inherited from LLMs, where attention distribution becomes bimodal and neglects information in middle of long input sequences
- Conventional 1D positional encoding schemes like RoPE fail to preserve 2D spatial relationships in high-resolution images, leading to sparse encodings and remote decay when processing numerous image patches
- Existing single-paradigm LVLM architectures, either cross-attention-based (LVLM-X) or autoregressive (LVLM-S), each carry inherent weaknesses that neither alone can overcome

## Method

- ==Dual-path architecture== combining Context path (LVLM-S-like autoregressive processing) with Image Memory path (LVLM-X-like cross-attention) using shared image representations
- ==RoPE-DHR positional encoding== that maps high-resolution image tiles to thumbnail coordinates, preserving 2D spatial awareness while preventing sparse encoding
- ==Three-stage training strategy== with projector/mixin tuning, gate freezing, and full-parameter finetuning to balance pathway optimization

## Results

- **17.2%** relative improvement on caption tasks and **7.0%** on long-generation tasks compared to single-paradigm baselines
- Superior performance across multi-image reasoning, long-context comprehension, and Needle-in-a-Haystack tasks with **5.6%** improvement on long-context benchmarks
- Consistent improvements at both **2B** and **8B** parameter scales while maintaining comparable training and inference efficiency to LVLM-S models

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Both LVLM-X and LVLM-S paradigms have complementary strengths that can be effectively combined through dual-stream processing
> - Positional encoding compression and 2D spatial preservation are crucial for handling dynamic high-resolution images in vision-language models
> - Training stage management prevents over-reliance on single pathways and ensures balanced multimodal representation learning

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "CoMemo: LVLMs Need Image Context with Image Memory" is situated at the forefront of research into **Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs)**, a rapidly evolving field that aims to integrate powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) with visual understanding capabilities. The work directly addresses fundamental architectural and representational challenges faced by current LVLMs, particularly in handling long and complex multimodal inputs.

The authors categorize existing LVLM architectures into two dominant paradigms:
1. **LVLM-X (Cross-Attention based):** exemplified by models like Flamingo and Idefics. These models use cross-attention mechanisms to integrate visual features into the LLM's textual representations. While offering flexible modality interaction, the paper notes that recent studies and their own observations (Figure 1) show these models often perform suboptimally compared to alternative approaches given similar backbones and training data.
2. **LVLM-S (Self-Attention based / Autoregressive):** exemplified by LLaVA and its successors (VILA, LLaVA-Next, InternVL-2.5, Qwen2-VL). This paradigm aligns visual tokens directly into the text token embedding space, enabling fully autoregressive processing using the LLM's inherent mechanisms. While more compatible with LLM architectures and generally better performing in knowledge-intensive scenarios, this approach inherits limitations from the underlying LLM design.

CoMemo positions itself as a novel framework that seeks to **transcend the limitations of both LVLM-X and LVLM-S** by proposing a **dual-path architecture** that effectively combines their strengths while mitigating their weaknesses. The paper specifically targets two critical issues inherent to the LVLM-S paradigm, which become exacerbated with increasing context length and high-resolution images:
- **"Lost in the Middle" Phenomenon:** A known limitation in LLMs (and thus LVLMs) where models struggle to retain and utilize information located in the middle of a long input sequence, exhibiting a bimodal attention distribution (focusing more on the beginning and end). This severely impacts long-context understanding.
- **Positional Encoding Challenges for High-Resolution Images:** Conventional 1D positional encoding schemes like RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding), when applied to image patch tokens, lead to sparse encodings, remote decay issues, and a loss of vital 2D structural relationships, especially with Dynamic High-Resolution (DHR) techniques that significantly increase the number of visual tokens.

By introducing a **"Context path" (LVLM-S-like)** and an **"Image Memory path" (LVLM-X-like)**, alongside a novel 2D-aware positional encoding **(RoPE-DHR)**, CoMemo represents a significant step towards more robust and efficient multimodal reasoning. It attempts to build a more 'human-like' approach to visual context processing, where both immediate context and a persistent visual memory are maintained. This work contributes to the broader goal of building LVLMs that can truly understand complex visual information in extended contexts, crucial for applications like document understanding, multi-turn visual dialogue, and long-form content analysis. It also contributes to the ongoing debate about optimal architectural designs for integrating vision and language.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind CoMemo stems from critical limitations observed in existing Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), particularly those built upon the autoregressive paradigm of Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors identify two main suboptimal characteristics:

1. **Progressive Neglect of Middle Visual Content ("Lost in the Middle"):** Inherited from LLMs, LVLMs exhibit a bimodal distribution in attention allocation, leading to a phenomenon where information in the middle of long input sequences is progressively neglected. This is particularly problematic for visual content, as crucial details might be "lost" within a lengthy stream of image tokens or when interleaved with text. This significantly degrades performance in long-context comprehension tasks.
2. **Failure to Preserve 2D Structural Relationships in High-Resolution Images:** Traditional 1D positional encoding schemes, like RoPE, are ill-suited for dynamically scaled high-resolution images. When an image is broken into numerous patches (especially with Dynamic High-Resolution or DHR), treating each patch as a sequential token leads to sparse positional encodings, causing "remote decay" (loss of positional relationship over distance) and the complete disregard of the inherent 2D spatial layout of the image.

To address these fundamental limitations, the **key objectives** of the CoMemo project are:

- **To propose CoMemo, a novel dual-path architecture:** This architecture aims to effectively alleviate the visual information neglect problem by combining two distinct visual processing paths: a "Context image path" (for direct context integration) and an "Image Memory path" (for persistent visual information retrieval). This design seeks to ensure that visual data remains salient and accessible throughout the entire processing sequence, regardless of context length.
- **To introduce RoPE-DHR, a novel positional encoding mechanism:** This mechanism is designed to overcome the shortcomings of conventional 1D positional encodings for images. Its objective is to maintain vital 2D spatial awareness while processing dynamic high-resolution images and simultaneously mitigating the remote decay issue in extended sequences.
- **To demonstrate superior performance:** The authors aim to prove CoMemo's effectiveness across a comprehensive set of multimodal benchmarks, particularly those requiring long-context comprehension, multi-image reasoning, and detailed visual question answering, showing clear improvements over conventional LVLM architectures under fair comparison settings.

In essence, CoMemo seeks to build more robust and intelligent LVLMs that can genuinely "see" and "remember" visual information over extended contexts, going beyond simple alignment to achieve deeper multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoMemo tackles the identified limitations through a combination of architectural innovations and a refined training strategy.

#### 3.1. CoMemo Architecture

The core of CoMemo is its **dual-path structure** for visual processing, aiming to provide both immediate context and a persistent memory of visual information, without modifying the underlying LLM's internal mechanisms directly.

- **Dual-Stream Structure:**
 - **Context Path:** This path functions similarly to LVLM-S models (e.g., LLaVA). Image representations (patch tokens) are treated as special tokens and concatenated directly with text tokens to form a single input sequence. This sequence is then processed by the LLM through standard autoregressive (causal self-attention) mechanisms. This path serves as the primary means for introducing image context.
 - **Memory Path:** This is an auxiliary stream designed to mitigate visual information neglect. Image representations are fed into "mixin layers" that interact with the LLM's hidden states through **cross-attention**. This allows the LLM to actively retrieve image information based on the current text query, preventing the image from being "lost in the middle" as context expands. Crucially, the two paths share identical image representations (from the same image encoder and projector), ensuring consistency.
 - **Mixin Layers:** These are interleaved with standard Transformer blocks within the LLM (at a 1:4 ratio, i.e., one mixin layer for every four LLM layers). Each mixin layer incorporates:
 - **Gated Cross-attention:** Modulates the influence of the visual memory path through learnable `attngate` values.
 - **Adaptive Feed-forward:** Enhances feature transformation via gated nonlinearity (`ffwgate`).
 - **Efficiency:** During autoregressive decoding, the memory path only requires a single-step computation between the current decoding token and cached visual memory states, avoiding the increasing key-value cache size issue of traditional cross-attention-based models.

#### 3.2. RoPE-DHR (Rotary Position Embedding for Dynamic High-Resolution)

To address the limitations of 1D positional encoding for 2D visual data, especially with DHR, CoMemo introduces RoPE-DHR, a novel position encoding mechanism.

- **Problem:** Standard RoPE assigns continuous incremental IDs, which become sparse and lose 2D relationships when applied to numerous image patches from high-resolution images (DHR). This leads to remote decay and "dimensional collapse" of 2D spatial information.
- **Solution (Hierarchical Strategy):**
 1. **Thumbnail Processing:** The image thumbnail (a downscaled version) is processed first, with its patch tokens assigned conventional RoPE base position IDs.
 2. **High-Resolution Tile Mapping:** For the higher-resolution image tiles, their 2D coordinates are mapped to their corresponding thumbnail patch index. This mapping (`i_thumb = (floor(x_tile * W_tile / W_orig) + wb_tile, floor(y_tile * H_orig / H_thumb) + hb_tile)`) ensures that tile patches inherit positional context from their thumbnail anchors, preserving 2D spatial relationships.
 3. **Decoupling:** This approach decouples positional encoding from absolute sequential positions, achieving "length reduction" (preventing sparse encoding for DHR) and "geometry preservation" (maintaining 2D spatial awareness).

#### 3.3. Training Stages

CoMemo employs a sophisticated three-stage training strategy to ensure optimal balancing between its dual visual processing pathways and address the "optimization bias" from asymmetric parameter updates.

- **Traditional LVLM Training:** Typically involves two phases: pretraining (cross-modal alignment) and fine-tuning (instruction following).
- **CoMemo's Three Stages:**
 1. **Pretraining Stage 1 (Projector & Mixin Tuning):** Only the projector module (aligning visual features to LLM embedding space) and the memory architecture parameters (mixin layers) are trainable. Other LLM components remain frozen. The goal here is to learn initial cross-modal representation alignment and establish a dynamic equilibrium between the context and memory paths.
 2. **Pretraining Stage 2 (Gate Freezing):** After a certain number of training steps in Stage 1, the gate parameters (`attngate`, `ffwgate`) within the mixin layers are frozen. This is crucial to prevent the model from over-relying on the cross-attention (memory) path, which could happen due to its more direct parameter updates compared to the context projector. This stage allows the alignment structure to be sufficiently learned without excessive bias.
 3. **Finetuning (Full-Parameter Training):** In this final stage, all model parameters become trainable, and the objective shifts to instruction-following, leveraging the balanced visual understanding capabilities learned in pretraining.

This multi-stage approach is designed to prevent an entrenched dependency on a single visual processing mechanism, allowing CoMemo to effectively combine and leverage the benefits of both autoregressive contextual processing and explicit visual memory retrieval.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate CoMemo's superior performance and its ability to address the identified challenges in LVLMs.

1. **Empirical Confirmation of LVLM Limitations:**
 - **"Lost in the Middle":** Through analysis of gradients and attention weights in InternVL2, the authors empirically confirm the "Lost in the middle" phenomenon (Figure 3). Attention is disproportionately allocated to tokens at the beginning and very end of sequences, neglecting the middle.
 - **Remote Decay in DHR:** Figure 4 illustrates how DHR, by significantly increasing the number of image tokens (e.g., 256 to 1,792 tokens), severely exacerbates the remote decay property of RoPE, further diminishing the influence of visual tokens during generation.

2. **Importance of Pathway Balancing:**
 - **DHR Allocation:** Experiments show that allocating DHR information to both pathways (DHR-B) leads to more stable and balanced model behavior compared to unilateral allocation (DHR-S or DHR-X) (Figure 5).
 - **Training Steps:** The pretraining steps profoundly affect the equilibrium between the two pathways. Insufficient pretraining leads to suboptimal alignment, while excessive pretraining induces over-reliance on specific pathways. This validates the necessity of their proposed multi-stage training strategy.

3. **Overall Performance Benchmarking (Table 1):**
 - CoMemo (2B parameters) consistently achieves top-tier performance across seven diverse benchmarks, including Captioning, Long-Generation, Multi-Image, Long-Context, Math, General VQA, and OCR-related tasks, when compared against leading open-source LVLMs of similar or larger sizes (e.g., InternVL-2.5 2B, MiniCPM-V-2 2.8B, Qwen2-VL 2B, mPlug-owl3 8B, LLama3.2-V1 11B). This demonstrates that CoMemo's architectural innovations translate into practical performance gains.

4. **Generation and Math Benchmarks (Table 2):**
 - CoMemo significantly outperforms both LVLM-X (cross-attention only) and LVLM-S (autoregressive only) baselines when trained on the same data and model settings. It achieves notable relative improvements: **17.2% on Caption** tasks (COCO, Flickr, No-Caps) and **7.0% on Long-Generation** tasks (LLaVABench, MMDU). This confirms its ability to combine the strengths of both paradigms, excelling in both concise description and extended inference. It also shows competitive performance on math reasoning tasks.

5. **Multi-image and Long-context Benchmarks (Table 3):**
 - CoMemo exhibits the best performance in scenarios requiring multi-image understanding (BLINK, Mantis, MMT) and long-context reasoning (MM-NIAH, MileBench). It achieves a **5.6% relative improvement on Long-Context** tasks.
 - Specifically, on MM-NIAH (Needle In A Haystack), CoMemo shows improved performance even when the "needle" (key information) is placed in the text (MM-NIAH-T) or image (MM-NIAH-M) within a long context. This suggests that RoPE-DHR improves long text understanding by compressing image token position space, and the memory structure effectively prevents over-focus on images, preserving language reasoning.
 - Figure 8 visually demonstrates that CoMemo effectively mitigates the "Lost in the middle" phenomenon in NIAH tasks, maintaining higher accuracy for information placed in the middle of sequences compared to LVLM-S.

6. **General VQA and OCR-Related Benchmarks (Table 4):**
 - CoMemo performs competitively, slightly outperforming LVLM-S on general VQA benchmarks (MMBench, MME, MMVP) and OCR-related tasks (AI2D, ChartQA, TextVQA). While these tasks often involve shorter contexts, the benefits of CoMemo's balanced approach and improved positional encoding still provide an edge.

7. **Efficiency and Ablation Studies (Tables 5, 6, 7):**
 - **Efficiency:** CoMemo maintains comparable training and inference latency to LVLM-S, despite its dual-path architecture, while significantly outperforming the more efficient but less performant LVLM-X.
 - **Ablation:** Detailed ablation studies confirm the contribution of each component:
 - RoPE-DHR alone significantly improves long-generation and long-context tasks.
 - The memory path alone helps alleviate image neglect.
 - Combining both (CoMemo's full design) yields the best overall performance.
 - RoPE-DHR's compression aspect is beneficial, but even without compression, the 2D-aware encoding scheme (RoPE-DHR without compression) still outperforms LVLM-S.
 - **Scaling Law & Dataset Consistency:** CoMemo demonstrates consistent superior performance at both 2B and 8B parameter scales, adhering to the scaling law. It also proves robust across different fine-tuning datasets, showcasing its generalizability.

In summary, the results rigorously validate CoMemo's effectiveness in enhancing LVLMs' ability to handle long-form generations, multi-image scenarios, and extended contexts, directly addressing the core limitations it set out to resolve.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoMemo introduces several significant contributions that could have a substantial impact on the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and their real-world applications:

1. **Directly Addressing Core LVLM Limitations:** The paper provides a direct and empirically validated solution to two pervasive challenges in LVLMs: the "lost in the middle" phenomenon and the limitations of 1D positional encoding for high-resolution 2D visual data. By mitigating attention decay and preserving 2D spatial information, CoMemo enables more reliable and robust multimodal reasoning, especially for complex, real-world inputs.

2. **Novel Dual-Path Architecture:** The proposed dual-path design, combining a "Context path" (autoregressive, LLaVA-like) with an "Image Memory path" (cross-attention, Flamingo-like), offers a principled way to leverage the strengths of both dominant LVLM paradigms. This hybrid approach allows for immediate contextual understanding while maintaining a persistent and retrievable "visual memory," leading to more comprehensive and less error-prone multimodal processing. This could inspire future LVLM architectures to explore more sophisticated multi-stream or memory-augmented designs.

3. **Enhanced Long-Context and Multi-Image Understanding:** The demonstrated superior performance in long-generation, multi-image analysis, and long-context comprehension tasks (e.g., Needle In A Haystack) is particularly impactful. Many real-world applications, such as analyzing multi-page documents, understanding video sequences, or engaging in prolonged visual dialogues, inherently require the ability to process and retain information over extended contexts. CoMemo's advancements pave the way for more capable AI systems in these domains.

4. **Advanced Positional Encoding for Vision:** RoPE-DHR is a notable innovation in how LVLMs handle high-resolution visual inputs. By transforming a 1D sequence-based encoding into a 2D-aware, hierarchical scheme, it ensures that the spatial relationships within images are preserved and remote decay is alleviated. This is crucial for tasks requiring fine-grained visual details (e.g., OCR-related tasks) and could be adopted by other LVLMs to improve their visual grounding.

5. **Refined Training Strategy:** The three-stage training paradigm highlights the importance of carefully balancing the optimization process in complex multimodal architectures. By preventing over-reliance on specific pathways during pretraining, the strategy ensures a more robust and generalized model, offering valuable insights for the training of future large-scale multimodal models.

6. **Contribution to Open-Source AI:** As part of the InternVL series, a project often associated with open-source initiatives (e.g., OpenGVLab), CoMemo's publication contributes to the broader open-source multimodal AI community. This allows other researchers to build upon and further improve these foundational models, accelerating progress in the field.

In conclusion, CoMemo pushes the boundaries of what LVLMs can achieve, particularly in scenarios demanding deep, sustained visual understanding over long contexts. Its architectural and methodological innovations offer a robust framework for developing more intelligent and versatile multimodal AI systems, promising significant advancements in various applications across computer vision, natural language processing, and human-computer interaction.
