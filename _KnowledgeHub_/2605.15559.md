# NavRL++: A System-Level Framework for Improving Sim-to-Real Transfer in Reinforcement Learning-Based Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> NavRL++ is a reinforcement-learning navigation framework spanning training to deployment. It encodes static obstacles as raycast distances and dynamic obstacles as tracked states, feeds a 12-token Transformer backbone trained with PPO and curriculum learning, then applies perturbation-aware fine-tuning over sensor noise, detection dropout, latency, and controller-gain mismatch. It reaches 94.08% overall success and zero-shot deployment on UAV and quadruped platforms.

## Problem

- Prior RL navigation work mostly tunes state, action, and reward design, offering little quantitative analysis of which sim-to-real discrepancies actually degrade real-world deployment.
- Existing sim-to-real fixes target isolated perception or policy discrepancies, ignoring system-level factors such as detection failures, input and action latency, and low-level controller response mismatch.
- Single-step policies mapping current observations to actions produce oscillatory commands under noisy delayed sensing, and sensor-specific pipelines force retraining per modality, limiting cross-platform reuse.

## Method

- Unified state representation: egocentric ==raycasting== over a voxel map gives 144 static distance beams, plus tracked dynamic-obstacle position/velocity/radius histories, making camera, LiDAR, and fusion inputs modality-agnostic.
- ==Transformer== temporal-reasoning policy: 12 tokens (CLS, static, five dynamic-history, five internal-state) through a 4-layer, 4-head encoder feeding ==PPO== actor-critic heads that output ==Beta-distribution== bounded velocities; 0.31M parameters.
- ==Perturbation-aware fine-tuning==: after clean pre-training, ==curriculum== post-training injects Gaussian state noise, 0.3 Bernoulli detection dropout, 0-100 ms input and 0-40 ms action delays, and 40% controller-gain randomization.

## Results

- Across **10,000** trials under perturbation, NavRL++ reaches **94.08%** combined success versus **63.05%** for NavRL and **92.85%** for the improved-training baseline, at **0.048 m/s2** control effort.
- Ablations: the Transformer backbone cuts control effort from **0.093** to **0.043 m/s2**; perturbation fine-tuning helps only the Transformer policy; combined perturbations cost over **15%** success in dense scenes.
- Cross-simulator Gazebo tests match Fast-Planner/EGO-Planner at **99-100%** static success and beat NavRL in dynamic scenes (**94/85/81%** vs **68/65/50%**); onboard inference is **4.1 ms** at **20 Hz**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Perception false negatives dominate the sim-to-real gap for navigation, hurting success more than noise or latency; budget robustness effort where factorized ablation says it hurts.
> - Domain randomization only pays off when the architecture can integrate history: the same perturbation fine-tuning helped a Transformer policy but degraded a single-step convolutional one.
> - Compact structured state (raycast distances, tracked obstacle states) rather than raw pixels lets one small policy transfer zero-shot across sensors and across aerial and legged embodiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robot navigation is a foundational capability for various robotic applications, including inspection, surveillance, and exploration. Historically, this field has relied on optimization-based and handcrafted algorithms, which have demonstrated efficacy in specific, well-defined environments. More recently, deep reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as an alternative, offering potential benefits in adaptability and generalization across diverse and complex environments by learning behaviors directly from interaction.

However, the application of RL to real-world robot navigation presents several challenges. A primary concern is the significant discrepancy between simulated training environments and real-world conditions, commonly known as the sim-to-real transfer problem. Existing RL navigation research has largely focused on designing the RL framework itself—such as optimizing input representations, action spaces, and reward functions—but has provided limited systematic analysis of the factors influencing sim-to-real transfer. There is a recognized lack of comprehensive insight into how various training strategies and system-level discrepancies affect real-world deployment performance.

Specifically, prior work on sim-to-real transfer in robotics has addressed isolated discrepancies, such as sensor observation mismatches through depth prediction modules or state-based inputs, and policy-level issues via domain randomization or human-in-the-loop methods. While these approaches offer solutions to specific challenges, a system-level perspective that investigates sim-to-real gaps across the entire navigation stack—encompassing sensing, perception, latency, and control execution—has been less explored.

This work positions itself within this landscape by not only introducing an RL framework but also providing a systematic empirical study to disentangle key factors affecting sim-to-real transfer. It aims to bridge the gap between simulation-trained policies and robust real-world deployment by analyzing system-level perturbations and proposing training strategies, including a post-training adaptation method, that explicitly account for these discrepancies. This holistic approach seeks to develop more reliable learned navigation policies for practical real-world applications across different robotic platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the NavRL++ framework are to address critical limitations in the field of reinforcement learning-based robot navigation, particularly concerning robust real-world deployment. The paper identifies several key motivations:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** A major motivation stems from the inherent difficulty of transferring policies trained in simulation to the real world due to domain discrepancies. Current RL navigation methods often emphasize RL framework design but offer limited systematic analysis of sim-to-real transfer, leaving a gap in understanding how training choices impact real-world performance. The authors hypothesize that a deeper understanding of these deployment discrepancies is crucial for enhancing the reliability of learned policies.
- **Systematic Empirical Analysis of Sim-to-Real Factors:** The authors aim to conduct a comprehensive empirical study that systematically disentangles and quantifies the key factors affecting sim-to-real transfer. These factors include sensor noise, perception failures, system latency, and control response mismatches. The motivation is to move beyond isolated solutions to specific discrepancies and instead provide insights into their cumulative and individual impacts across the full navigation pipeline, from sensing and perception to action generation and control execution.
- **Developing an Effective RL Framework and Training/Deployment Pipeline:** The paper seeks to introduce a complete reinforcement learning-based navigation framework, NavRL++, that covers the entire pipeline from perception to control, including both simulation training and real-world deployment. This involves designing an effective policy architecture and a robust training methodology.
- **Enhancing Transfer Robustness through Perturbation-Aware Adaptation:** Leveraging insights from their empirical analysis, a key objective is to develop a post-training adaptation strategy called perturbation-aware fine-tuning. This strategy is motivated by the observation that explicitly accounting for empirically identified domain discrepancies during fine-tuning can significantly improve the policy's robustness to real-world conditions.
- **Mitigating Perception Degradation and Improving Control Smoothness:** The work aims to address issues like perception degradation and control oscillation commonly observed in real-world deployment. To this end, the authors propose a Transformer-based temporal reasoning policy. This is motivated by the hypothesis that incorporating short-horizon observation history can enhance robustness to delayed and imperfect observations while promoting smoother control behaviors.
- **Demonstrating Multi-Platform and Cross-Task Generalization:** A practical motivation is to validate the developed approach through real-world deployment on diverse robotic platforms (e.g., aerial and legged robots) and across various navigation-centric tasks (e.g., exploration, inspection). This aims to demonstrate the zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities and practical applicability of the learned policies in complex, dynamic environments.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to advance the state of RL-based robot navigation by providing a robust, systematic, and practically applicable framework that can reliably transfer learned behaviors from simulation to diverse real-world robotic systems and tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NavRL++ framework is designed as a comprehensive system for reinforcement learning-based autonomous navigation, encompassing training and real-world deployment. It consists of a structured RL formulation, a temporal reasoning policy network, a two-stage training strategy, and a robust real-world deployment pipeline with a perception module and an optional safety shield.

**Problem Formulation:**
Navigation is framed as a local navigation task in cluttered environments, focusing on collision avoidance and goal-directed motion, modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The system targets general mobile robotic platforms, with experiments conducted on quadcopter UAVs and quadruped legged robots, equipped with RGB-D cameras, LiDAR, or both. The objective is to learn an optimal policy that maximizes cumulative discounted rewards for safe navigation.

**Reinforcement Learning Training Framework:**
The system supports multi-modal inputs: camera-only, LiDAR-only, or camera-LiDAR fusion. A state estimation module (vision-based or LiDAR-based) estimates the robot's pose and velocity, forming the navigation internal state with the goal position. A separate perception module processes sensor data history to extract structured representations of static (occupancy maps) and dynamic (obstacle states) environments. These multi-modal inputs are fed into a Transformer backbone, which integrates them using an attention mechanism. Policy learning is performed using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, with the Transformer's [CLS] token output feeding into MLP-based actor-critic networks. Training is conducted entirely in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, leveraging parallel simulation for efficiency.

**Reinforcement Learning Formulation:**
- **Input State Representation:** To minimize sim-to-real discrepancies and support multiple modalities, a distance-based representation is used for static obstacles and structured state representations for dynamic obstacles.
 - **Static Obstacles:** A voxel-based local map is used, with ego-centric ray casting (36 horizontal, 4 vertical beams, 144 distances) encoding surrounding geometry. Distances are normalized to a maximum sensing range (4m).
 - **Dynamic Obstacles:** Position, velocity, and radius of up to 5 dynamic obstacles are encoded. Temporal information (2-second history with 0.5-second intervals) is included to capture motion patterns.
 - **Robot Internal States:** Robot position, velocity, and goal position are represented in a goal-aligned coordinate frame. A 2-second temporal history is also maintained.
- **Output Action Parameterization:** Velocity commands are used as control outputs (3D for UAVs, 2D for quadruped robots). The policy outputs parameters (alpha and beta) of a Beta distribution, which generates continuous actions within a normalized range. During training, actions are sampled from this distribution; during deployment, the mean is used. This avoids boundary bias compared to Gaussian policies.
- **Reward Function Design:** A weighted sum of six components guides learning:
 - `R_v` (Velocity reward): Encourages goal-directed, high-speed motion.
 - `R_s` (Survival reward): Incentivizes long-horizon operation.
 - `R_sc` (Static collision reward): Penalizes proximity to static obstacles using log distances from raycasting.
 - `R_dc` (Dynamic collision reward): Penalizes proximity to dynamic obstacles using log distances from their relative positions.
 - `R_sm` (Smoothness reward): Penalizes large changes in robot velocity between steps.
 - `R_h` (Height reward): Penalizes excessive altitude changes in 3D navigation.

**Policy Network Design for Temporal Reasoning:**
The core policy network is a Transformer-based backbone.
- **Token Design & Temporal Encoding:** A sequence of 12 tokens is used: one [CLS] token, five for dynamic obstacle history, five for robot internal state history, and one for static obstacle information. Positional encodings preserve temporal order.
- **Modality Alignment:** Static obstacle distances are first processed by a lightweight CNN encoder. The resulting features, along with dynamic obstacle and internal states, are projected into a shared embedding dimension via an MLP.
- **Transformer Backbone:** The backbone has an embedding dimension of 64, four attention heads, and four encoder layers. It integrates all modalities and temporal histories.
- **Actor-Critic Heads:** The [CLS] token feature is fed into separate MLP-based actor and critic networks (two hidden layers, 256 neurons each) for action distribution prediction and state value estimation. The network has 0.31M parameters, enabling real-time deployment.

**RL Training Strategy:**
A two-stage curriculum learning strategy is employed:
- **Pre-training:** Policies acquire fundamental navigation behaviors in clean simulation environments (Isaac Sim, 1024 parallel robots). PPO with Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is used with Adam optimizer and entropy regularization. Environments feature randomly distributed static and dynamic obstacles with varying speeds and directions.
- **Post-training (Perturbation-Aware Fine-tuning):** The pre-trained policy is fine-tuned under controlled perturbations to improve robustness to sim-to-real discrepancies. Perturbations include:
 - **Input Noise:** Gaussian noise injected into static obstacle distances, dynamic obstacle states, and robot state estimates.
 - **Perception Failure:** Randomly dropping detected dynamic obstacles (Bernoulli sampling, p=0.3).
 - **Input Latency:** Random delays (uniform [0, 0.1]s) introduced to input states.
 - **Action Latency:** Random delays (uniform [0, 0.04]s) introduced to action outputs.
 - **Control Response:** Velocity controller gain randomized (±40% of nominal) to simulate varying robot dynamics.
 The fine-tuning uses a reduced learning rate.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Obstacle density is progressively increased across 5 stages (S1-S5) during pre-training and 3 stages (S1-S3) during post-training, starting with lower density and increasing complexity.

**Real-World Deployment:**
- **Perception Module:**
 - **Static Structures:** Occupancy mapping uses a log-odds recursive update scheme from depth cameras or LiDAR.
 - **Dynamic Obstacles:** A pipeline involves detecting objects as 3D bounding boxes (DBSCAN for LiDAR, U-depth map for RGB-D, ensemble for fusion), temporally associating new detections with tracked objects (linear propagation, cosine similarity), and refining states with a Kalman filter. Dynamic objects are identified if their velocity exceeds a threshold or if a 2D deep learning object detector classifies them as dynamic.
- **Policy Safety Shield:** An optional module filters unsafe policy outputs. It projects the policy's velocity command into a safe velocity set based on the classical Velocity Obstacle (VO) formulation. This ensures collision avoidance but can be conservative in highly cluttered environments.
- **Task Deployment:** The RL navigation policy is integrated into navigation-centric tasks:
 - **Autonomous Exploration:** A global exploration planner provides waypoint goals, and the RL policy handles local navigation and collision avoidance in unknown environments.
 - **Indoor Infrastructure Surface Inspection:** A sequence of waypoints from a floor plan guides the robot, with the RL policy providing robust local navigation and obstacle avoidance against unexpected obstacles.
All computations run onboard in real-time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of NavRL++ encompassed extensive simulation studies in NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Gazebo, alongside real-world deployments on aerial and legged robots, measuring success rate (SR), control effort (CE/TE), and path length.

**Performance Evaluation & Ablation Studies (Intra-Simulator):**
- **Overall Performance:** NavRL++ demonstrated the highest success rate with comparatively low control effort in Isaac Sim environments incorporating perturbations, outperforming baseline RL approaches (NavRL, NavRL-IT, NavRL-IT-T, NavRL-IT-PF).
- **Impact of Improved Training (NavRL-IT vs. NavRL):** Improved training strategies significantly increased success rates but also led to higher control effort and action oscillations.
- **Temporal Reasoning (NavRL-IT-T vs. NavRL-IT):** The Transformer-based temporal reasoning policy reduced control effort from 0.093 to 0.043 m/s² while maintaining a comparable success rate, indicating its role in generating smoother actions.
- **Perturbation-Aware Fine-Tuning (NavRL-IT-PF vs. NavRL-IT, and NavRL++ vs. NavRL-IT-T):** Perturbation-aware fine-tuning did not improve convolutional policies but yielded clear performance gains, especially in dynamic environments, when combined with the Transformer-based policy (NavRL++). This highlighted the synergy between temporal reasoning and adaptation to perturbations.
- **Path Length:** All methods achieved similar path lengths (within 5% variation) in long-distance static navigation scenarios, with marginal differences observed between them.

**Impact of Sim-to-Real Perturbations:**
- **Individual Perturbations:** A policy trained in a clean simulation (NavRL-IT-T) was evaluated under individual perturbations. Perception failure had the largest impact on success rate (over 5% drop). Input noise, latency, and control disturbance caused moderate reductions (1-3%).
- **Combined Perturbations:** When perturbations were combined, the success rate dropped by over 10% (more than 15% in high-density environments), and control effort increased by approximately 2.5 times compared to clean environments. NavRL++'s performance (Table II) indicated effective mitigation of these degradations through perturbation-aware fine-tuning.
- **Deployment Factors (Velocity Limits & Control Frequency):** NavRL++ showed robustness across reasonable velocity ranges. Performance remained largely preserved at control frequencies above 10 Hz, with minor degradation at 5 Hz and significant drops at 2 Hz.

**Cross-Simulator Evaluation & Performance Benchmarking:**
- **Static Environments (Gazebo):** NavRL++ achieved near 100% success rates, comparable to optimization-based planners like Fast-Planner and EGO-Planner. Optimization-based methods generally yielded lower trajectory effort (smoother trajectories).
- **Dynamic Environments (Gazebo):** NavRL++ demonstrated substantially higher success rates compared to existing RL baselines and other methods like ViGO, indicating superior robustness to moving obstacles.
- **Path Length:** Path lengths were comparable across all methods, with learning-based approaches showing slightly longer paths than optimization-based ones, consistent with Isaac Sim findings.

**Training Strategy & Parameter Evaluation:**
- **Curriculum Learning:** Progressively increasing obstacle density during training significantly improved overall success rates, particularly in dynamic environments during fine-tuning (over 15% improvement).
- **Large-Scale Training:** Increasing the number of parallel robots and frames collected per policy update accelerated convergence and improved the final success rate.
- **Goal Randomization:** Randomly sampling goal positions for each episode consistently improved success rates across all training stages and environments compared to using fixed goal sets.

**Qualitative Simulation and Real-World Experiments:**
- **Simulation:** Qualitative experiments in Isaac Sim and Gazebo visualized successful collision-free navigation in cluttered static and dynamic environments, including scenarios with moving pedestrians and complex structures like tunnels.
- **Real-World Deployment:** The same trained policy was successfully deployed zero-shot on a quadcopter UAV and a Unitree quadruped robot.
 - **Navigation:** Robots navigated cluttered environments with pedestrians, demonstrating safe collision avoidance and generalization across platforms.
 - **Tasks:** NavRL++ was successfully integrated into unknown environment exploration (map building while avoiding obstacles following global waypoints) and indoor infrastructure surface inspection (following generated waypoints while avoiding new obstacles).
- **Real-time Performance:** The policy ran at 20 Hz with an average inference time of 4.1 ms on onboard computers.

**Limitations:**
- **Perception:** Reliance on rule-based dynamic obstacle detection and tracking can lead to missed detections and sensitivity to occlusion.
- **Action Smoothness:** Trajectory smoothness remains a gap compared to optimization-based planners, with some motion oscillations observed.
- **Collision in Dynamic Environments:** Non-negligible collision rates persist in high-density dynamic settings.
- **Safety Shield:** The VO-based safety shield can be overly conservative in highly crowded scenarios, potentially leading to stagnation.
- **Narrow Spaces:** The policy may struggle in very narrow passages (<0.1-0.2m clearance), potentially due to the sparse raycasting representation of static obstacles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes contributions to the field of reinforcement learning-based robot navigation by providing a system-level framework, NavRL++, that addresses the critical challenge of sim-to-real transfer. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Holistic Approach to Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The paper shifts the focus from isolated architectural improvements to a comprehensive, system-level analysis of deployment robustness. By systematically disentangling and quantifying the impact of various sim-to-real perturbations (sensor noise, perception failures, latency, control response mismatch), the research provides fundamental insights into the practical challenges of deploying RL policies. This systematic understanding can guide future development efforts in robust robotics.
- **Enhanced Deployment Robustness:** The introduction of perturbation-aware fine-tuning, a post-training adaptation strategy, demonstrates a practical method for improving transfer robustness by explicitly integrating empirically identified domain discrepancies into the training process. This directly contributes to more reliable real-world performance for learned navigation policies.
- **Improved Control Quality and Temporal Reasoning:** The proposed Transformer-based temporal reasoning policy offers a solution to reduce control oscillation and improve robustness against delayed or imperfect observations. This leads to smoother and more stable navigation behaviors, which are crucial for safe and efficient robot operation.
- **Generalizability Across Platforms and Tasks:** The framework's ability to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and perform successfully on diverse robotic platforms (aerial and legged robots) and navigation-centric tasks (exploration, inspection) showcases its broad applicability. This suggests that NavRL++ or its underlying principles could be adopted for a wide range of mobile robot applications.
- **Practical Guidance for RL Training:** The empirical analysis highlights the importance of curriculum design, large-scale training, and goal randomization for achieving robust and high-performing RL navigation policies. These findings offer practical guidance and effective training recipes for researchers and practitioners in the field, potentially accelerating the development cycle for new RL-based robotic systems.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The stated intention to release the NavRL++ framework on GitHub fosters reproducibility and encourages further research, enabling the broader community to build upon these contributions.

In summary, NavRL++ contributes to making RL-based robot navigation more viable for real-world applications by offering a principled and empirically validated approach to tackle sim-to-real transfer at a system level. Its impact lies in improving the reliability, robustness, and generalizability of learned navigation policies, thereby advancing the autonomy capabilities of mobile robots in complex and dynamic environments. While acknowledging limitations in areas such as extreme clutter, the work lays a foundation for future research to further refine perception, action smoothness, and safety mechanisms in learned control.
