# Think Only When Needed: Prompt-Authority Control for Selective Slow-Path Intervention in Vision-Language-Action Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TOWN-VLA is a prompt-authority interface for frozen vision-language-action policies. Retrieved memory candidates are reranked by a fixed text-compatibility score, then a Top-2 fail-closed checker either renders one canonical compact instruction or restores the original prompt byte-for-byte. Without retraining the action generator, it lifts LIBERO-Plus success from 69.5% to 73.1% and real PiPER-arm success from 52.7% to 78.7%.

## Problem

- Prior retrieval and planner wrappers inject retrieved text straight into the executed instruction, treating candidate relevance as if it were permission to alter the policy input.
- Fine-tuned policies see only a stable instruction template, so appended text shifts sequence length, token positions and the instruction boundary, collapsing closed-loop control.
- Existing steering, routing and assistance-seeking systems never separate proposal quality from an explicit, logged authorization decision with an exactly recoverable fallback.

## Method

- **Compatibility-Reranked Capsule**: ==CLIP== nearest-neighbor retrieval of five candidates, reordered by a frozen score mixing CLIP proximity, object/target/context Jaccard overlap and mismatch penalties.
- **Top-2 Fail-Closed Cascade**: a hard text-level eligibility checker inspects at most two ranked candidates; the first eligible one renders a canonical put-object-relation-target instruction, otherwise the exact Base prompt is restored.
- **Task-Prior Admission**: a frozen manifest-indexed routing bit gates whether the slow path runs at all, separating spending computation from granting ==prompt authority==; logged hashes make every route auditable.

## Results

- Matched LIBERO-Plus over **10,030 episodes** per method: **69.46%** to **73.07%** (**+362 successes**, 95% CI **1.89-5.45 points**), gains on six of seven perturbation axes and all four suites.
- Prompt-form audit: raw appended text drops mean success from **92.47%** to **3.00%**; correct and length-matched meaningless appends both score **0/500** while canonical wrong-object controls reach **497/500**.
- Physical PiPER arm with frozen pi-0.5: **79/150** to **118/150** (**52.7%** to **78.7%**, Fisher exact **p = 3.16e-6**); oracle routing halves slow-path calls with identical **2,826 successes**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prompt form, not retrieved semantics, dominates frozen-policy execution: template departure alone destroys success even when appended content is task-correct.
> - Treat proposal and authority as separate rights; a fail-closed interface with bit-identical fallback makes external reasoning reversible and auditable without retraining.
> - Oracle routing shows large removable computation, but no oracle-free gate beat the base policy, so admission calibration remains the unsolved deployment bottleneck.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies represent a convergence of visual observation, language instructions, and closed-loop robot actions. Recent advancements in cross-embodiment data, generalist pretraining, and continuous-action models have expanded their capabilities, leading to practical systems that integrate frozen VLA policies with external modules such as planners, memories, verifiers, or steering mechanisms. These external modules often provide test-time guidance by modifying the input instruction to the VLA policy.

Existing research largely focuses on redesigning the VLA policy itself, improving training data, or refining action representations. Examples include large-scale models like PaLM-E, RT-1, RT-2, Open X-Embodiment, Octo, and OpenVLA, which aim for scalable language-conditioned robot control and cross-dataset adaptation. Other work explores grounding language through affordances, spatial value maps (e.g., SayCan, VoxPoser), or integrating reasoning closer to action (e.g., ECoT, InstructVLA). Fast-slow systems divide computation into semantic and reactive parts. Some approaches incorporate inspectable memory at the policy boundary to retrieve perceptual or episodic history or action priors.

This paper distinguishes itself by freezing the VLA policy and concentrating on the interface through which external textual guidance modifies the policy's input. It addresses a specific form of prompt brittleness observed in VLAs, where changes in instruction form, rather than semantic content, can lead to control failures. While prompt brittleness has been studied in large language models concerning output quality in open-loop generation, this work examines its implications for closed-loop robotic control. The proposed solution, prompt-authority control, formalizes a separation between the generation of candidate guidance and the authorization to alter the policy input, paralleling concepts of value-of-computation and selective prediction with a reject option found in metareasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and auditable interface for integrating retrieved textual guidance into frozen VLA policies without requiring policy retraining. The motivation stems from several critical observations and challenges:

Firstly, current methods of augmenting frozen VLA policies often involve "direct prompt injection," where retrieved text is appended to the original instruction and directly fed into the VLA policy. The authors demonstrate that this approach is highly unreliable. In an audited control experiment, raw appended text significantly reduced mean success rates from 92.47% to 3.00%. More critically, both semantically meaningful and length-matched meaningless appends resulted in 0 successes out of 500 states. This phenomenon is termed "prompt-form collapse," indicating that changes to the instruction's syntactic structure (e.g., sequence length, token positions, or instruction boundary) can dominate execution outcomes, even overriding potentially useful semantic content. This suggests that existing VLA policies, often fine-tuned with specific instruction templates, are brittle to out-of-template text.

Secondly, existing planners and reasoning modules propose actions or strategies but generally do not explicitly separate candidate quality from an authorization decision to modify the policy input. This lack of explicit authority control means that any proposed text, regardless of its form or potential to disrupt the policy, is often directly applied. This uncontrolled intervention introduces risks to the VLA's performance and reliability.

Thirdly, the research aims to provide verifiable properties and auditability for the interaction between external guidance systems and frozen VLA controllers. This includes ensuring that rejected guidance pathways restore the original policy input exactly and that the decision-making process for intervention is transparent and bounded.

Therefore, the key objectives are:
1. To rigorously identify and characterize "prompt-form collapse" as a significant failure mode for VLA policies when confronted with out-of-template textual inputs.
2. To formulate and operationalize "prompt-authority control" as a mechanism to govern whether and how retrieved content may modify the frozen VLA policy input.
3. To develop a system, named TOWN-VLA (Think Only When Needed), that separates candidate generation from prompt authorization, ensuring that only canonical and compatible guidance is admitted, and rejected guidance pathways result in the exact restoration of the original Base prompt.
4. To demonstrate that such an interface can improve VLA performance and robustness on complex manipulation tasks, both in simulation and on a physical robot, without altering the underlying frozen VLA policy.
5. To quantify the potential for selective slow-path computation and establish a foundation for future work on oracle-free admission calibration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, TOWN-VLA (Think Only When Needed), establishes a prompt-authority interface that wraps a frozen VLA policy, ensuring strict control over how external textual guidance can modify the policy's input. The system enforces a deterministic interface contract throughout evaluation, keeping the VLA policy's parameters and control loop unchanged.

**Problem Setup and Interface Contract:**
The core principle is that the VLA policy `π_θ` receives visual observations `o_t` and a prompt `p_star`. The Base prompt `p_base` is generated by a fixed renderer `P` from the original instruction `ℓ`. The interface initializes `u_star = ℓ`, and only an explicit authorization rule can replace it with a canonical instruction `u_cap`, setting `p_star = P(u_cap)`. Otherwise, `p_star` reverts to `p_base` byte-for-byte. This resolved prompt is fixed for the entire rollout. The system logs candidate IDs, reranking scores, checker flags, reasons, and resolved prompt hashes for auditability.

**Compatibility-Reranked Capsule:**
1. **Retrieval:** A frozen memory `M` containing demonstration trajectories (but no evaluation rollouts) is queried using CLIP text features and a nearest-neighbor index. Given an original instruction `ℓ`, `K=5` candidates `H_K(ℓ)` are retrieved. Each memory entry stores a task description, structured context, raw plan, and compact hint; only the context is used for parsing and rendering.
2. **Parsing:** A frozen parser extracts object-target pairs from both the task instruction `ℓ` (`x_ℓ`) and each stored context `h` (`y_h`). This parser operates solely on text. Fixed pick/place templates and deterministic fallbacks handle unmatched strings.
3. **Compatibility Score:** A compatibility score `s(h, x_ℓ)` is calculated for each candidate `h`. This score combines CLIP proximity (`s_clip`) with structural overlap between the task's parsed objects/targets and the candidate's parsed context (`m_obj`, `m_tgt`, `m_ctx`). It also includes explicit mismatch penalties (`r_obj`, `r_tgt`) and a rank-based tie resolution term (`η rank_clip(h)`). The coefficients for these terms are fixed before evaluation and are not fitted to outcomes.
4. **Reranking:** Candidates are then reranked in descending order based on their compatibility score, resulting in an ordered list `(h^(1), ..., h^(K))`.

**Top-2 Fail-Closed Cascade:**
This component inspects candidates and decides on prompt authorization.
1. **Eligibility Gates:** A hard checker records deterministic conflict reasons `R(h, ℓ)`. A candidate `h` is considered eligible `G_comp(h, ℓ) = 1` if no conflict reason is found. A key conflict reason is a nonempty candidate object or target having zero overlap with the task. This is a text-level rule, not involving images or robot state.
2. **Sequential Inspection:** The system sequentially inspects at most the Top-2 ranked candidates. If the first eligible candidate `h^(j*)` is found (where `j*` is either 1 or 2), the cascade short-circuits. If neither of the Top-2 candidates passes the eligibility gates, `j*` remains empty.
3. **Authorization and Rendering:** If an eligible candidate `h^(j*)` is found and the slow path is invoked (`g=1`), a canonical capsule instruction `u_cap` is rendered using a fixed template (e.g., "put <object> <relation> <target>") based on the `context(h^(j*))`. This `u_cap` then becomes `u_star`. If the context is empty or unrenderable, or if no candidate is authorized, `u_star` defaults to the original instruction `ℓ`.
4. **Fallback:** The final prompt `p_star` is then generated by `P(u_star)`. Crucially, if a candidate is rejected or the slow path is not invoked, `p_star` is bit-identical to `p_base`. This "fail-closed" mechanism ensures that rejection reverts to a known, stable input.

**Task-Prior Admission as an Oracle Control:**
To quantify the maximum possible slow-path computation savings, an oracle-side mechanism uses benchmark labels `z` to manifest-index a routing bit `g^(m)_prior(z)`. This bit determines whether the slow path (retrieval, reranking, checking) is invoked or bypassed. This oracle control is used for ablation studies to set an upper bound on removable computation, but the main evaluations typically run with the slow path "always-on" (`g=1`).

**Runtime States and Interface Guarantees:**
TOWN-VLA defines three auditable runtime states:
- **Bypass:** `P(ℓ)` is executed without retrieval.
- **Inspected-but-unauthorized:** `P(ℓ)` is executed after candidates are retrieved and checked but none are authorized.
- **Authorized:** `u_cap` is rendered and executed.
This design provides three verifiable guarantees: (I1) prompt-boundary non-interference on rejection, (I2) `π_θ` remains the sole action generator, and (I3) a memory-size-independent post-retrieval inspection budget (at most two candidates).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TOWN-VLA focused on five questions: end-to-end robustness (Q1), prompt-form failure (Q2), authorization and exact restoration (Q3), selective computation (Q4), and transfer across backbones and embodiments (Q5).

**Q1: Perturbation Breadth**
- **LIBERO-Plus Simulation:** On the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, TOWN-VLA increased the weighted mean success rate (SR) from 69.5% (OpenVLA-OFT Base Policy) to 73.1% (+3.61 points), representing 362 additional successes over 10,030 episodes. The paired-cell 95% confidence interval for this improvement was 1.89–5.45 points.
- **Perturbation Axes:** TOWN-VLA showed improvements in six out of seven perturbation axes, with the most notable gains in Language (+6.5 points) and Robot (+6.0 points). Image-only Noise was the only axis that showed a slight decrease.
- **Task Suites:** Aggregated by task suite, TOWN-VLA improved SR across all four categories: Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long-Horizon.

**Q2: Prompt-Form Collapse**
- **Raw Appended Text:** Direct appending of raw text significantly reduced mean SR from 92.47% (Base) to 3.00% over 1,200 episodes. However, when using the Compatibility-Reranked Capsule and Top-2 Fail-Closed Cascade mechanisms, SR recovered to 91.08%.
- **Semantic vs. Form:** A 500-state factorial control experiment isolated the effect of prompt form. Both the Base prompt and exact restoration achieved 499/500 successes. A correct canonical instruction yielded 497/500. In contrast, both semantically correct and length-matched meaningless appends resulted in 0/500 successes. Canonical instructions with wrong objects or targets still achieved high success rates (497/500 and 496/500, respectively), indicating that prompt form, rather than specific semantic content from the retrieval, was the dominant factor in the collapse observed with raw appends.

**Q3: Authorization and Exact Restoration**
- **Route-Level Audit:** In a 1,200-state audit (always-on setting), the reranking mechanism admitted all states, preserving the task signature in 1,100/1,200 cases. The fail-closed cascade admitted 1,000 states, preserving the signature in 940/1,000 authorized routes, and critically, restored the exact Base prompt for the remaining 200 rejected routes.
- **Broader Audit:** In a separate 900-route audit (including routing decisions), the slow path ran for 450 routes. 375 of these were authorized with preserved task signatures, and 75 were inspected but rejected. The other 450 routes bypassed retrieval. This meant 525 routes ultimately executed the exact Base prompt (450 bypasses + 75 rejections), and 375 routes executed an authorized, task-signature-preserving prompt.
- **Restricted-OOD Replication:** Under spatial and object shifts, raw Top-5 plans led to reduced SR (74.20% and 79.40%, respectively). Compatibility-Reranked Capsule recovered these to 88.20% and 96.67%, and Top-2 Fail-Closed Cascade further improved them to 89.33% and 97.53%, surpassing the Base Policy in both shift aggregates.

**Q4: Selective Computation and Gate Controls**
- **Oracle-Side Headroom:** Using an oracle (benchmark-side routing) on 3,000 paired states, the same 2,826 successes were preserved while halving slow-path calls (from 3000/3000 to 1500/3000). This also reduced decision latency by 10.24% (from 212.50 ms to 190.75 ms). This demonstrates the potential for significant computational savings without performance degradation, given perfect knowledge of when to invoke the slow path.
- **Oracle-Free Admission:** The current oracle-free admission gates (learned selector, CLIP) did not reliably identify which calls to remove. On a held-out split of 36 cells, the learned selector authorized 2/36 cells, matching the Base Policy's SR of 91.81% for those specific episodes. However, always-on intervention was 1.53 points below Base on this split, indicating that effective oracle-free admission calibration remains a challenge.

**Q5: Transfer to a Second Backbone and a Physical Robot**
- **PiPER Arm:** TOWN-VLA was evaluated on a physical PiPER arm using a frozen `π_0.5` checkpoint. Across 150 trials (50 trials per scene: no distractor, yellow-cup distractor, red-cylinder distractor), TOWN-VLA raised the overall task success rate from 52.7% (Base Policy) to 78.7%. This represents a +26.00 points improvement, with a Fisher exact test p-value of 3.16 × 10^(-6).
- **Scene-Specific Gains:** Gains were consistent across all three scenes (e.g., no distractor: 42.0% to 74.0%; yellow distractor: 54.0% to 78.0%; red distractor: 62.0% to 84.0%).
- **Post-Miss Recovery:** Descriptive analysis showed that post-miss recovery rates rose from 24.4% (Base) to 52.4% (TOWN-VLA), though this metric used method-dependent denominators due to unequal initial misses. This indicates that the prompt authority mechanism can contribute to the policy's ability to recover from initial failures through closed-loop behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work identifies "prompt-form collapse" as a critical and previously underexplored failure mode in frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, where subtle changes in instruction formatting, rather than semantic content, can severely degrade performance. By rigorously demonstrating that raw text appends can reduce success rates from over 90% to near 0%, the paper highlights a fundamental brittleness in how VLA models process composite instructions.

The introduction of "prompt-authority control" through TOWN-VLA offers a novel and verifiable solution to this problem. By explicitly separating the generation of candidate guidance from its authorization to modify the policy input, TOWN-VLA ensures that only canonical and compatible instructions are admitted, while rejected inputs are precisely reverted to the original, known-good Base prompt. This "fail-closed" design provides a robust and auditable interface for VLA policies.

The main findings demonstrate that this interface leads to tangible performance improvements. In simulation, TOWN-VLA increased LIBERO-Plus success by 3.61 points, with gains distributed across various perturbation types and task families. On a physical PiPER robot, it achieved a substantial 26.00-point increase in success rate. These improvements are achieved without any retraining or modification of the underlying frozen VLA policy, underscoring the efficiency and practical utility of the interface-level intervention.

The concept of prompt authority as an enforceable control primitive for frozen VLAs has several significant implications:
- **Enhanced Robustness:** It provides a mechanism to protect VLA policies from undesirable or performance-degrading inputs generated by external reasoning modules, making robotic systems more reliable in dynamic environments.
- **Auditable and Verifiable Systems:** The explicit logging of candidate IDs, scores, authorization decisions, and resolved prompt hashes enhances the transparency and auditability of VLA system behavior, which is crucial for safety-critical applications.
- **Efficiency in Deployment:** The ability to improve VLA performance without expensive retraining allows for faster deployment of existing frozen models into new scenarios with external guidance.
- **Foundation for Selective Computation:** The oracle-side analysis quantifies the potential for significant computational savings by selectively invoking slow-path reasoning only when beneficial. While oracle-free admission remains a calibration challenge, this work establishes a clear target for future research in adaptive slow-path control.

In conclusion, this research provides a framework for managing the interaction between symbolic reasoning and neural policy execution, addressing a key vulnerability in current VLA systems. It suggests that regulating the input boundary of frozen VLA models is a productive area for improving robustness and reliability in robotic manipulation. The identified challenge of oracle-free admission calibration points to a clear next frontier for developing more intelligent and autonomous VLA systems.
