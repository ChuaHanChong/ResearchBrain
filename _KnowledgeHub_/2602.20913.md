# LongVideo-R1: Smart Navigation for Low-cost Long Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> LongVideo-R1 presents an agent-based framework that allows multimodal large language models to efficiently understand long videos by employing hierarchical video navigation, thereby significantly reducing computational cost while maintaining competitive question answering accuracy. The system processes long videos with an average of 10.5 reasoning rounds, achieving 50.0% accuracy on the LVBench benchmark, which surpasses other agentic methods by at least 5.6%.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have finite context windows, limiting their ability to process the extensive visual information in long videos (minutes to hours).
- Existing long video understanding methods often rely on brute-force, exhaustive processing of numerous short clips, leading to high computational costs and latency that scale with video length.
- The high computational demands of current approaches restrict their deployment in real-world applications requiring low-latency responses or operating under strict processing budgets.

## Method

- **LongVideo-R1** introduces an agent-based framework that uses a ==hierarchical tree== structure to represent videos, enabling dynamic exploration of varying temporal granularities.
- The framework operates with a Chain-of-Thought-with-Tool (CoTwT) procedure, where a large reasoning model (LRM) interacts with specialized `video_cap()` and `video_qa()` tools to actively navigate and gather information.
- The agent is trained in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on a curated dataset of 33K reasoning episodes, followed by reinforcement learning with GRPO, utilizing a composite reward function to optimize for answer correctness and efficient temporal localization.

## Results

- LongVideo-R1 achieves **50.0%** overall accuracy on LVBench, outperforming other agent-based methods by at least **5.6%**, and surpasses proprietary models like GPT-4o by **1.1%** and GLM-4V-plus by **1.3%**, with an average of 10.5 reasoning rounds per question.
- The framework demonstrates superior efficiency, operating with an estimated average inference time of approximately 135 seconds per QA task on VideoMME-Long, enabling practical application to ultra-long videos (e.g., tens of hours).
- Ablation studies confirm that a larger supervised fine-tuning dataset and the inclusion of a location reward (`r_loc`) significantly improve the model's performance, particularly in Key Information Retrieval and Temporal Grounding tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient long video understanding can be achieved through goal-oriented, iterative reasoning and navigation, rather than exhaustive processing of all video segments.
> - Organizing video content into a multi-level hierarchical structure allows an MLLM agent to adaptively 'zoom in' on relevant segments, balancing the need for broad context with fine-grained detail.
> - A composite reward function, specifically including a location reward (`r_loc`), is crucial for training agents to effectively identify and navigate to the most relevant temporal segments, thereby enhancing overall QA accuracy and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have significantly enhanced the semantic understanding of visual data, extending language model capabilities to interpret images and short video clips. However, the application of MLLMs to long-form videos, which can span from minutes to hours, presents a notable challenge due to the inherent context length limitations of these models. MLLMs are typically unable to process the extensive visual information contained within long videos in a single pass.

Prior research has addressed this limitation through two primary approaches. One direction focuses on developing efficient video representations that maximize information density within limited context windows. The second, more scalable approach, involves segmenting long videos into shorter clips, processing these components separately, and then integrating the derived information to answer complex queries. The latter has evolved with the advent of large reasoning models (LRMs) and agent-based systems, where an MLLM agent strategically invokes specialized tools for information extraction and reasoning. Current agentic architectures, such as Ego-R1 and VideoTree, have demonstrated high accuracy in long video question answering (QA) tasks.

Despite these advancements, a significant gap in the research landscape concerns the computational efficiency of long-form video understanding. Existing high-accuracy agent-based systems often require exhaustive processing of all or a substantial proportion of video segments, leading to high computational costs and latency. This computational burden restricts the deployment of MLLMs in real-world applications that necessitate low-latency responses or operate under strict processing budgets, such as embodied agents or high-throughput video-chat services.

"LongVideo-R1" addresses this deficiency by introducing a research setting that prioritizes the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff in long video understanding. The work positions itself as a solution that moves beyond solely optimizing for QA accuracy to explicitly minimize computational expenditure while maintaining competitive performance. It builds upon the agent-based paradigm but innovates by replacing exhaustive search with a goal-oriented, iterative reasoning and navigation strategy, aiming for a Pareto-optimal balance between accuracy and computational cost. The paper also leverages advancements in reinforcement learning techniques, particularly specialized reward functions, to guide agent behavior toward efficient navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient framework for understanding long-form videos under strict computational budgets, moving beyond approaches that solely optimize for question answering (QA) accuracy. The authors formally define the research goal as achieving a Pareto-optimal tradeoff between QA accuracy and computational efficiency.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations of existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and current long video understanding methodologies:

1. **Context Length Limitations of MLLMs:** MLLMs possess finite context windows, which prevent them from ingesting the vast amount of visual information present in long videos (e.g., 1-2 hours). This intrinsic limitation makes comprehensive understanding challenging.
2. **Computational Cost of Brute-Force Approaches:** Current methods often resort to a "brute-force" pipeline. This involves partitioning long videos into numerous short clips, exhaustively processing each clip (e.g., generating captions, summarizing events), and then integrating these results to formulate an answer. This linear-scan approach leads to computational costs that grow proportionally with video length, resulting in prohibitively high computational overhead and latency.
3. **Barriers to Real-World Deployment:** The high computational demands of existing methods severely restrict their deployment in practical applications. These include embodied agents requiring low-latency reactions to their environment and high-throughput video-chat services operating under per-sample processing budget constraints. The absence of an efficient solution limits the real-world utility of MLLMs for long video tasks.
4. **Need for Goal-Oriented Reasoning:** The authors hypothesize that efficient long video understanding requires a paradigm shift from exhaustive search to dynamic, iterative, and goal-oriented reasoning. An effective system should be able to assess partial, high-level context and intelligently decide which video segment to examine next to locate critical information relevant to a specific query, rather than processing all segments indiscriminately.

To address these motivations, the authors propose LongVideo-R1, an agent-based framework designed to emulate human-like video exploration. The core idea is to enable the MLLM agent to:
- Actively navigate a hierarchical video structure.
- Judge whether sufficient information has been collected to answer a query.
- If not, intelligently determine the next most informative video clip to explore, thereby avoiding redundant computations.
- Halt exploration immediately upon acquiring enough knowledge to answer the query.

This approach aims to significantly reduce computational expenditure while maintaining competitive QA accuracy, thereby enabling the practical application of MLLMs for long video understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LongVideo-R1 is designed as an active, reasoning-equipped multimodal large language model (MLLM) agent focused on efficient video context navigation, aiming to reduce the redundancy associated with exhaustive search methods.

**Hierarchical Video Structure:**
To support dynamic exploration of varying temporal granularities, videos are organized into a multi-level tree structure. The entire video constitutes the root node (level 0). Each non-leaf node is partitioned into K equal-length, non-overlapping sub-clips, forming K children nodes. The default configuration uses D=3 levels and an adaptive width K, where leaf-level clips are approximately 16 seconds long. This structure allows the agent to initially survey broad segments and then "zoom in" to finer-grained content as needed.

**Chain-of-Thought-with-Tool (CoTwT) Procedure:**
LongVideo-R1 operates within a CoTwT framework, where a large reasoning model (LRM) interacts with two specialized multimodal tools:
- **`video_cap` (Video Captioning Tool):** Takes a video clip and a number of sampled frames (F) as input, outputting a textual description of the clip. This tool provides generic descriptions to aid in content localization.
- **`video_qa` (Video QA Tool):** Receives a lowest-level video clip, sampled frames (F), and a question (q), then outputs an answer. This tool is specifically used for final question answering and is restricted to the lowest-level clips.

The inference process, outlined in Algorithm 1, is iterative:
1. The agent receives the question `q` and an initial high-level caption of the entire video.
2. It uses its reasoning module (`rea`) to generate a reasoning statement, `r_l`.
3. Based on `r_l`, the model decides whether it has sufficient information to answer the question.
4. If not, it decides which tool to call (`video_cap` or `video_qa`) and on which video segment (`V_k1...,kd`). The `thinking` module can decide to drill down to a child clip, traverse to a sibling clip, or backtrack to an upper layer.
5. The chosen tool is executed, producing an observation (e.g., a new caption `t_l`).
6. The chat history is updated with the reasoning statement and tool output.
7. The process repeats until an answer is generated or a maximum iteration limit is reached.

**Data Curation:**
To train LongVideo-R1, a dataset of 33K reasoning episodes is constructed:
- **Source Data:** The CG-Bench dataset, which contains clue-grounded QA pairs, is used. This allows explicit supervision for localizing key sub-clips before answering. 800 videos and 5.6K QA pairs are selected for trajectory generation.
- **Hierarchical Captions:** The Qwen2.5-VL-72B model serves as the `video_cap` function to extract hierarchical text descriptions, with prompts modified to include absolute timestamps. Frame sampling rates and description lengths vary by hierarchical level.
- **CoTwT Trajectory Generation:** GPT-5 is employed for zero-shot inference to generate reasoning trajectories. A specific system prompt guides GPT-5 on tool usage rules (e.g., `video_cap` after parent traversal, `video_qa` only on lowest-level nodes).
- **Iterative Correction Strategy:** To enhance data quality and ensure success, two improvements are applied:
 - Instead of starting with only the root node, GPT-5 is prompted to traverse all K sub-clips at the first level, providing more global context.
 - When GPT-5 fails to produce a correct answer or valid trajectory, clue-grounded hints from CG-Bench (starting with higher-level segments and progressively becoming more precise if needed) are provided to guide the model. This minimal hinting strategy ensures correctness while encouraging generalization rather than memorization.

**Training LongVideo-R1 Agent:**
Training follows a two-stage pipeline:
1. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):**
 - A pre-trained Qwen3-8B model is fine-tuned on the curated 33K CoTwT trajectories.
 - The training samples simulate multi-round tool-using processes, with reasoning, tool calls, and answers enclosed in special tokens (`<think>`, `<tool>`, `<answer>`).
 - This stage equips the model with the ability to generate structured reasoning sequences and perform coherent tool interactions.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with GRPO:**
 - The video reasoning process is framed as an interactive exploration environment, where the model is an agent, tools are actions, and the video hierarchy is the state.
 - The GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm, adapted from Ego-R1, is used to optimize the policy model `π_θ` for improved efficiency and accuracy.
 - **Reward Design:** A composite reward function `R = w_ans * r_ans + w_loc * r_loc + w_repeat * r_repeat` is designed:
 - `r_ans`: Binary reward (1 for correct final answer, 0 otherwise).
 - `r_loc`: F1-like metric that combines coverage and precision of predicted time intervals `I_model` against ground-truth intervals `I_gt`. This encourages identifying relevant content while penalizing unnecessary exploration.
 - `r_repeat`: A penalty term to discourage the agent from repeatedly visiting the same video segments.
 - **Rollout and Optimization:** During RL training, the agent interacts with executable video tools to generate rollout trajectories, which are then used to compute policy gradients and update `π_θ`.

**Implementation Details:**
- The base LRM is Qwen3-8B.
- `video_cap` uses Qwen2.5-VL-72B.
- `video_qa` uses Qwen2.5-VL-32B.
- SFT is performed for 3 epochs, followed by RL for 2 epochs.
- Hierarchical frame sampling and image resolution adjustments ensure consistent computational cost per caption call while providing finer detail at deeper levels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate LongVideo-R1 on three long-form video QA benchmarks: LVBench, Video-MME-long, and MLVU, comparing it against proprietary models, leading open-sourced MLLMs, and other agent-based systems.

**Overall Performance and Efficiency:**
LongVideo-R1 achieves competitive QA accuracy while significantly reducing computational cost compared to linear-scan methods. It operates with an average of 10.5 rounds of reasoning and navigation/answering per question, resulting in a substantially lower computational burden. The estimated average inference time on VideoMME-Long is approximately 135 seconds (2.25 minutes) per QA task.

**Results on LVBench:**
- LongVideo-R1 achieved an overall accuracy of 50.0%, outperforming other agent-based methods by at least 5.6%.
- It surpassed several proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o by 1.1%, GLM-4V-plus by 1.3%) and most open-sourced MLLMs despite using a smaller 8B-LLM.
- The model demonstrated strong performance in Key Information Retrieval (KIR) and Temporal Grounding (TG) sub-categories, reaching 56.4% accuracy on TG, a 10.9% margin over other models. This highlights its capability in locating key temporal segments.
- Using a stronger MLLM for `video_cap` (Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) further improved overall accuracy to 60.7%, with persistent advantages in KIR and TG.

**Results on MLVU and Video-MME:**
- LongVideo-R1 performed well on MLVU (68.1%) and Video-MME (64.4% with subtitles), but did not consistently outperform all open-sourced MLLMs. This is attributed to the characteristics of these benchmarks, which contain many short videos or global questions that benefit methods employing uniform or adaptive frame sampling more directly, rather than hierarchical navigation.
- Similar to LVBench, performance on these benchmarks also improved with stronger `video_cap` tools.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **SFT Data Size:** The model trained with the full 33K SFT samples demonstrated superior performance compared to one trained with a subset of 10K samples, both after SFT and subsequent RL stages. This indicates the importance of a larger, high-quality SFT dataset.
- **Location Reward (`r_loc`):** Including the `r_loc` component in the reward function led to significant performance gains across the overall dataset and specifically in the KIR and TG subsets. This validates the effectiveness of the location reward in enhancing the model's video navigation capabilities, which contributes to overall QA performance.

**Accuracy-Efficiency Tradeoff:**
LongVideo-R1 demonstrates a favorable tradeoff between accuracy and inference time. For example, it achieved 50.0% accuracy on LVBench with an average of 3 minutes per QA, and this cost could be reduced to 2 minutes with a marginal 0.2% accuracy drop. Further analysis suggests that the tradeoff can be optimized by adjusting the scale of the MLLM used for `video_cap` and altering the maximum number of allowed tool uses.

**Case Studies and Ultra-long Videos:**
- LongVideo-R1 exhibited strong reasoning, planning, and temporal localization abilities in case studies, successfully navigating a 102-minute video to identify specific performers and count associated objects.
- The framework demonstrates capability in ultra-long video QA (tens of hours, e.g., TV dramas), efficiently navigating with 10-20 reasoning rounds, a task previously inaccessible to other methods under strict budget constraints due to their linear scaling costs.
- However, failure cases occur when the model is distracted by visually similar but irrelevant segments. The authors note that providing simple textual hints can redirect the model back to the correct segment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "LongVideo-R1" contributes to the field of multimodal AI and video understanding by addressing the practical challenge of processing extensive video content under computational constraints. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap:** The work tackles the underexplored problem of balancing high QA accuracy with low computational budgets in long video understanding. By formalizing the pursuit of a Pareto-optimal accuracy-efficiency tradeoff, it shifts the focus from purely maximizing accuracy, which often comes with prohibitive costs, to a more practical and deployable paradigm.

2. **Novel Agentic Framework:** LongVideo-R1 introduces a novel agentic framework that employs hierarchical reasoning and active exploration. Unlike previous methods that rely on exhaustive processing, this framework enables the model to intelligently navigate video content, making informed decisions about which segments to examine, similar to human cognitive processes. This selective approach reduces redundant computations significantly.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency for Long Videos:** The framework demonstrates a superior accuracy-efficiency tradeoff compared to existing agent-based algorithms and many state-of-the-art MLLMs. This efficiency makes it feasible to process ultra-long videos (e.g., TV series, documentaries spanning hours), a domain previously inaccessible under strict computational budgets. This capability unlocks new possibilities for large-scale video analysis.

4. **Contribution to Agent Training Methodologies:** The paper details a two-stage training paradigm (SFT followed by RL with GRPO) and introduces a composite reward function specifically designed to optimize for both answer correctness and efficient video localization. The inclusion of a location reward (`r_loc`) explicitly guides the agent towards accurate temporal grounding, a critical skill for complex video understanding.

5. **Data Curation for Reasoning Agents:** The creation of a high-quality dataset of 33K chain-of-thought-with-tool (CoTwT) trajectories, leveraging clue-grounded annotations and GPT-5 with an iterative correction strategy, provides a valuable resource. This dataset not only facilitates the training of LongVideo-R1 but also offers a blueprint for future data generation efforts aimed at building robust reasoning agents.

6. **Practical Implications and Deployment:** By reducing the computational cost per QA task, LongVideo-R1 lowers the barrier for deploying MLLMs in real-world applications such as embodied AI agents requiring low-latency responses, high-throughput video-chat services, and large-scale content analysis. This can enable more responsive and cost-effective intelligent systems.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens several avenues for future research, including the integration of a wider array of multimodal tools (e.g., instance recognition, segmentation), the development of advanced settings for video QA (e.g., incremental QA, amortized costs for multiple queries), and the co-optimization of agent reasoning with video description tools in a unified framework. This suggests a trajectory towards more sophisticated and adaptive video understanding systems.

In summary, LongVideo-R1 represents a step toward developing more intelligent and resource-aware MLLMs for long video understanding, emphasizing that practical utility is derived not only from high accuracy but also from computational efficiency.
