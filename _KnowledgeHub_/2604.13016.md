# Rethinking On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models: Phenomenology, Mechanism, and Recipe

> [!summary] Summary
> This research systematically investigates On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for Large Language Models (LLMs), identifying that successful distillation relies on thinking-pattern consistency and a teacher's novel capabilities, not merely higher scores. The study reveals token-level alignment dynamics and proposes practical strategies like off-policy cold starts and teacher-aligned prompts, while also highlighting OPD's limitations in long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- On-Policy Distillation (OPD) is widely adopted for LLM post-training but is often fragile, with its underlying training dynamics poorly understood.
- A stronger teacher model sometimes fails to improve a student, even when a comparatively weaker teacher succeeds, indicating a gap in understanding governing conditions.
- The mechanisms by which a teacher's token-level signal guides (or fails to guide) a student's distribution remain unclear, leading to unreliable practical application.

## Method

- The study systematically investigates ==On-Policy Distillation (OPD)== through controlled experiments with diverse LLM pairs and introduces dynamic metrics (e.g., ==overlap ratio==, ==overlap-token advantage==, ==entropy gap==) to monitor training progress.
- It formalizes OPD objectives, analyzes different supervision granularities (==sampled-token==, ==full-vocabulary==, ==Top-k==), and progresses from phenomenological observation to mechanistic analysis and practical recipe development.
- Experiments explore ==thinking-pattern consistency==, the impact of new knowledge versus scale, and test practical interventions like ==off-policy cold starts== and ==teacher-aligned prompt selection==.

## Results

- Implementing an 'off-policy cold start' (SFT warm-up on teacher-generated rollouts) and 'teacher-aligned prompt selection' significantly improved OPD reliability and final performance, by bridging initial thinking-pattern gaps.
- Sampled-token OPD achieved comparable performance to Top-k settings with k > 1, suggesting that extensive multi-token supervision beyond a small k offers negligible additional gains, while Top**-1** OPD consistently underperformed due to instability.
- Even for failing OPD configurations, the teacher's global reward signal remained correlated with rollout correctness, suggesting that failure is not always due to uninformative rewards but potentially an anisotropic reward landscape with canceling per-token advantages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successful OPD is contingent on a high degree of thinking-pattern consistency between student and teacher, and the teacher possessing genuinely new capabilities rather than just higher scale or benchmark scores.
> - OPD's effectiveness stems from a progressive alignment of high-probability tokens between student and teacher distributions, with gradients on shared tokens driving the main performance gains.
> - The quality of OPD's dense supervision degrades with trajectory depth, leading to performance plateaus or declines and training instability in longer sequences, as the teacher's signal becomes less reliable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

On-policy distillation (OPD) has emerged as a significant technique in the post-training of large language models (LLMs), with its adoption in prominent industry models like Qwen3, MiMo, and GLM-5. It is positioned as a complement to conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and outcome-reward reinforcement learning (RL). OPD distinguishes itself from off-policy distillation, which suffers from exposure bias by training on fixed teacher-generated sequences, by having the student generate its own rollouts. This allows OPD to leverage the teacher’s per-token log-probabilities as a dense reward signal on states the student actually visits. The framework has also been extended to self-distillation, where a model acts as its own teacher given privileged information, demonstrating potential for continual self-improvement.

Despite these observed successes and widespread adoption, the practical application of OPD can be fragile and its underlying training dynamics remain poorly understood. A notable failure mode exists where a demonstrably stronger teacher may fail to improve a student, even when a comparatively weaker teacher succeeds. This observation highlights a gap in current understanding regarding the conditions that govern OPD's effectiveness and the mechanisms through which the teacher's token-level signal guides or fails to guide the student's distribution. This paper addresses this gap by systematically investigating the phenomenology, underlying mechanisms, and practical strategies for on-policy distillation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed fragility and lack of comprehensive understanding of On-Policy Distillation (OPD), despite its increasing adoption in Large Language Model (LLM) post-training. While OPD is touted for its dense supervision and ability to mitigate exposure bias, the empirical reality sometimes contradicts the intuitive expectation that a stronger teacher should always yield better distillation outcomes. Specifically, the authors note instances where a highly capable teacher model fails to improve a student, even when a weaker teacher achieves successful distillation. This inconsistency motivated a deeper investigation into the core principles governing OPD.

The key objectives of this study are:
1. **Identify Governing Conditions**: To systematically investigate and empirically identify the conditions that determine whether an OPD process will succeed or fail. This involves analyzing factors related to the teacher and student models, as well as their interaction dynamics.
2. **Uncover Token-Level Mechanisms**: To probe into the token-level dynamics of OPD to understand *why* it works when successful. This includes examining how the student's probability distribution aligns with the teacher's at a granular level during training.
3. **Develop Practical Recipes**: Based on the insights gained from the phenomenological and mechanistic analyses, to propose and validate practical strategies or "recipes" that can recover OPD in configurations where it would otherwise fail. These recipes aim to address identified challenges in real-world applications.
4. **Assess Limitations and Costs**: To examine the inherent costs and potential limitations of OPD's dense token-level supervision, particularly concerning its scalability to long-horizon reasoning tasks and its reliability across varying trajectory depths.

Collectively, these objectives aim to move beyond empirical observation of OPD's utility to a more principled understanding of its operational dynamics, thereby improving its reliability and guiding its future development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a systematic approach, progressing from empirical observations (phenomenology) to mechanistic analysis, and finally to practical solutions (recipes). The methodology primarily involves controlled experiments with various LLM pairs, combined with the tracking of custom-designed dynamic metrics.

**On-Policy Distillation Formulations:**
The paper first formalizes the OPD objective, which minimizes the sequence-level reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence over student-generated trajectories. This objective can be decomposed into a sum of token-level KL divergences. Three common supervision granularities are outlined:
- **Sampled-Token OPD**: The most lightweight variant, where only the token sampled by the student is evaluated. It uses an unbiased single-sample estimator of the token-level reverse KL.
- **Full-Vocabulary OPD**: Computes divergence over the entire vocabulary at each prefix, offering denser gradients but higher memory costs.
- **Top-𝑘 OPD**: An intermediate approach that restricts divergence computation to a subset of *k* tokens, specifically the *k* tokens with the highest probability under the student. This reduces teacher-query cost while retaining multi-token supervision.

**Dynamic Metrics:**
To monitor training dynamics, the following metrics are introduced:
- **Overlap Ratio**: Measures the average proportion of tokens shared between the student's and teacher's top-𝑘 sets. A low ratio indicates policy divergence.
- **Overlap-Token Advantage**: Quantifies distributional agreement within the overlapping tokens by averaging the term $A_t(v) = \bar{p}_t(v)(\log \bar{q}_t(v) - \log \bar{p}_t(v))$, where $\bar{p}_t, \bar{q}_t$ are renormalized distributions over the intersection. Values near zero indicate high alignment.
- **Entropy and Entropy Gap**: Tracks the entropy of both student and teacher distributions on student-generated rollouts, with the absolute entropy gap $|\Delta H_t|$ indicating mode and confidence alignment.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Datasets**: Primarily the DAPO-Math-17k dataset for mathematical reasoning. Other datasets like OpenThoughts3-1.2M and DeepMath are used for specific experiments.
- **Models**: Various models from the Qwen3 and DeepSeek families are used, ranging from 1.5B to 14B parameters. These include base models, models that have undergone Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training (e.g., GRPO, JustRL, Skywork-OR1-Math), and models from different scaling tiers within the same family.
- **Evaluation**: Performance is primarily measured by average accuracy over 16 samples (avg@16) on AIME 2024, AIME 2025, and AMC 2023 benchmarks.
- **Hyperparameters**: Default OPD hyperparameters (e.g., training temperature, batch size, learning rate) are standardized across experiments unless otherwise specified for ablation.

**Specific Experimental Designs:**
1. **Phenomenology (Section 3)**:
 - **Thinking-Pattern Consistency**: Compares OPD with a base student (Qwen3-1.7B-Base) against two teachers: one base (Qwen3-4B Non-thinking) and one RL-trained (Qwen3-4B-Base-GRPO) to assess the impact of initial pattern alignment.
 - **New Knowledge, Not Just Scale**: Conducts parallel comparisons in DeepSeek and Qwen families, pitting a same-pipeline teacher against an RL post-trained teacher to differentiate between scale and novel capabilities.
 - **Reverse Distillation**: Distills an RL-trained student (JustRL-1.5B) back to its pre-RL checkpoint (R1-Distill-1.5B) and a larger, slightly stronger same-family model (R1-Distill-7B) to isolate the impact of thinking patterns and new knowledge.
2. **Mechanism (Section 4)**:
 - **Progressive Alignment**: Contrasts a successful OPD run (JustRL-1.5B → R1-Distill-1.5B) with a failing one (R1-Distill-7B → R1-Distill-1.5B) by monitoring the dynamic metrics (overlap ratio, advantage, entropy gap) to identify signatures of success.
 - **Overlap Sufficiency**: An ablation study decomposing Top-𝑘 OPD into optimization over only overlap tokens, non-overlap tokens, or full student Top-𝑘 to determine the locus of effective gradients.
3. **Practical Recipe (Section 5)**:
 - **Off-Policy Cold Start**: Introduces an SFT warm-up phase on teacher-generated rollouts before OPD, comparing its effectiveness to direct OPD.
 - **Teacher-Aligned Prompt Selection**: Investigates the impact of matching prompt templates and content to the teacher's post-training data on OPD performance and dynamics.
4. **Discussion (Section 6)**:
 - **Reward Degradation**: Examines how OPD performance varies with maximum response length and analyzes the propagation of instability through student and teacher entropy heatmaps.
 - **Global vs. Local Reward**: Compares the global correlation of sequence mean reward with rollout correctness for successful versus failing teachers.
 - **Sampled-Token Sufficiency**: Compares sampled-token OPD against Top-𝑘 OPD with varying *k* values (1, 4, 16, 64) to assess the required granularity of supervision.

This comprehensive experimental design allows for a multi-faceted investigation into the empirical conditions, underlying mechanisms, and practical improvements for on-policy distillation in LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the phenomenology, mechanism, and practical application of On-Policy Distillation (OPD).

**Phenomenology of On-Policy Distillation:**
- **Thinking-Pattern Consistency**: Successful OPD is strongly correlated with a high degree of thinking-pattern consistency between the student and teacher. Experiments showed that a teacher with an aligned thinking pattern (e.g., an RL-trained Qwen3-4B-Base-GRPO) consistently outperformed a non-thinking teacher (Qwen3-4B), even when their overall benchmark scores were comparable. Initial overlap ratio served as a strong indicator of this compatibility, with early-stage mismatches leading to persistent performance gaps.
- **New Knowledge, Not Just Scale**: Merely possessing higher benchmark scores or a larger scale within the same training pipeline does not guarantee effective distillation. Teachers that acquired genuinely *new capabilities* through additional post-training (e.g., Skywork-OR1-Math-7B, Qwen3-4B-Non-Thinking-RL-Math) yielded substantially stronger gains and higher teacher-student gap recovery rates compared to larger models from the same underlying training pipeline (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B).
- **Validation via Reverse Distillation**: Reverse distillation experiments provided further evidence. When an RL-trained student (JustRL-1.5B) was distilled by its own pre-RL checkpoint (R1-Distill-1.5B), it regressed to its pre-RL performance. Critically, a larger and slightly stronger model from the same family (R1-Distill-7B) induced an almost indistinguishable regression, despite its higher scores. This suggests that OPD primarily learns thinking patterns, and that benchmark performance can be decoupled from distillation outcome when new knowledge is absent.

**Mechanism of On-Policy Distillation:**
- **Progressive Alignment of High-Probability Tokens**: Successful OPD runs were characterized by a consistent pattern of progressive alignment between the student and teacher distributions on student-visited states. This manifested as a steady increase in the overlap ratio of their top-𝑘 tokens (from ~72% to >91%), an improvement in the overlap-token advantage towards zero, and a narrowing of the entropy gap. In contrast, failing OPD runs exhibited stagnation in these metrics from the outset. Overlapping tokens consistently accounted for 97%-99% of the total probability mass for both models, indicating alignment on the probabilistically dominant tokens.
- **Optimizing Shared Tokens Alone Suffices**: An ablation study demonstrated that restricting the distillation loss to only the intersection of the student's and teacher's top-𝑘 tokens (Overlap Top-𝑘) was sufficient to achieve nearly the same performance gains as standard Top-𝑘 OPD. Optimizing only the non-overlapping tokens resulted in substantially weaker performance. This indicates that the main gains of OPD are derived from gradients on the shared high-probability region, which drives a self-reinforcing cycle of overlap growth.

**Practical Recipe for Improving OPD:**
- **Off-Policy Cold Start**: Implementing an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase on teacher-generated rollouts (off-policy distillation) before engaging in OPD effectively bridged the initial thinking-pattern gap. This "cold start" resulted in higher initial overlap ratios and consistently stronger final performance, maintaining smoother and more stable training trajectories.
- **Teacher-Aligned Prompt Selection**:
 - **Prompt Template Alignment**: Simply aligning the prompt template with that used during the teacher's post-training significantly improved validation performance and overlap growth.
 - **Prompt Content Alignment**: Using prompts identical to the teacher's post-training data (teacher-aligned content) led to stronger performance and higher probability mass concentration on shared tokens. However, this also resulted in a notably lower student entropy, suggesting a potential trade-off where overly focused training might reduce policy diversity, implying the benefit of mixing teacher-aligned and out-of-distribution prompts.

**Discussion on Supervision Density and Reliability:**
- **Reward Quality Degrades with Trajectory Depth**: OPD's dense supervision exhibited a "sweet spot" for response length, with moderate lengths (3K-7K tokens) yielding the strongest results. Very long responses (10K-15K tokens) led to performance plateaus or declines and late-stage training instability. This instability was observed to originate at later tokens in a sequence and propagate backward, suggesting that the teacher's reward signal becomes less reliable as the student's generated prefix drifts further from states familiar to the teacher. Teacher's ability to improve student continuation also decreased monotonically with increasing prefix depth.
- **Globally Informative Reward Does Not Guarantee Local Exploitability**: Even in failing OPD configurations, the teachers' global reward signal was found to be statistically correlated with rollout correctness, exhibiting comparable AUROC values (~0.75) to successful teachers. This suggests that failure is not necessarily due to a fundamentally uninformative reward. The hypothesis presented is that a large teacher might induce an anisotropic reward landscape where per-token advantages are large but partially cancel when aggregated, leading to small effective gradients.
- **Sampled-Token Reward Is Already Sufficient**: Experiments comparing different Top-𝑘 OPD configurations with sampled-token OPD revealed that sampled-token OPD achieved comparable performance to Top-𝑘 settings with *k* > 1. Top-1 OPD consistently underperformed due to an unstable, mode-concentrated reward signal. Enlarging *k* beyond 4 offered negligible additional gains, suggesting that dense multi-token supervision might not require extensive vocabulary coverage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to a more principled understanding of On-Policy Distillation (OPD), moving beyond empirical observations to systematically uncover its underlying dynamics and conditions for success and failure. The findings carry several significant implications and highlight potential impacts:

**Enhanced Understanding of OPD Success and Failure:**
The identification of "thinking-pattern consistency" and the presence of "genuinely new knowledge" as governing conditions for OPD success provides a critical framework for diagnosing and improving distillation processes. This goes beyond the simplistic notion that a "stronger" teacher is always better, emphasizing the importance of *transferable* knowledge and compatible policy structures. This mechanistic insight can guide researchers and practitioners in selecting appropriate teacher models and designing more effective distillation pipelines.

**Improved Practical Strategies for LLM Post-Training:**
The proposed "off-policy cold start" and "teacher-aligned prompt selection" offer concrete, actionable recipes to mitigate common OPD failure modes. By demonstrating how to bridge initial thinking-pattern gaps and leverage data closer to the teacher's expertise, the research provides practical tools to enhance the reliability and efficiency of LLM post-training. This can lead to more robust and higher-performing distilled models in practice, reducing the trial-and-error often associated with complex LLM training.

**Refined Design Choices for OPD Implementations:**
The analysis of supervision granularity, showing that sampled-token OPD often suffices and that larger Top-𝑘 values beyond a certain point yield diminishing returns, can streamline OPD implementations. It challenges the assumption that more dense supervision (larger *k*) is always better, potentially reducing computational overhead without sacrificing performance. The caution against Top-1 distillation due to its instability is a valuable practical guideline.

**Identification of Fundamental Limitations and Future Research Directions:**
The finding that OPD's reward quality degrades with trajectory depth, leading to instability in long-horizon tasks, reveals a fundamental limitation of current token-level dense supervision. This suggests that existing OPD approaches may not scale cleanly to complex reasoning or agentic multi-turn interactions. This insight is crucial for guiding future research toward hybrid approaches that combine dense and sparse rewards, or curriculum learning strategies that progressively extend the supervised horizon.

**Broader Impact on AI Development:**
By improving the efficiency and effectiveness of distillation, this work contributes to the development of smaller, more deployable LLMs that retain the capabilities of larger models. This has implications for reducing the computational and energy costs associated with large models, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and sustainable. The identified trade-offs between supervision density and reliability also prompt a deeper theoretical examination of reinforcement learning and distillation in sequence generation, potentially leading to novel algorithmic designs.

**Future work** is clearly outlined, focusing on extending these findings beyond mathematical reasoning to other domains (code, open-ended tasks), isolating the impact of pre-training data, adapting insights to the burgeoning field of self-distillation, and developing solutions for long-horizon and agentic AI settings. These directions indicate a clear path for continued advancement in the field of LLM training and optimization.
