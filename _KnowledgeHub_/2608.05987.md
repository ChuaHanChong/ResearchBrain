# AgentOPSD: Recursive Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentOPSD introduces a critic-free, recursive turn-level credit assignment method that leverages self-distillation to address sparse rewards in long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning. The approach consistently improves upon GRPO and other self-distillation baselines across ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA, demonstrating enhanced robustness as task horizons lengthen.

## Problem

- Sparse and delayed rewards in long-horizon, multi-turn agentic reinforcement learning make it difficult to assign credit accurately to individual decisions.
- Current group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) methods broadcast uniform credit across all actions, failing to differentiate pivotal from routine decisions.
- Existing self-distillation techniques provide dense token-level signals but often lack alignment with turn-based agentic interactions and disregard cumulative evidence from interaction history.

## Method

- **AgentOPSD** proxies ==Bayesian turn evidence== using per-turn self-distillation contrasts, indicating how an action revises the log-odds of trajectory success.
- Implements a ==recursive belief update== mechanism that maintains a history-dependent log-odds belief state, accumulating evidential scores from prior turns.
- Aligns the marginal belief revision for each turn with the trajectory's terminal outcome to derive ==outcome-aligned, turn-level credit== scores, which then reshape the **GRPO** advantage.

## Results

- AgentOPSD consistently outperformed GRPO and various self-distillation baselines across ALFWorld (e.g., **89.1%** success for Qwen2.5-7B), WebShop, and Search-QA (e.g., **49.2%** accuracy for Qwen2.5-7B).
- The method demonstrated enhanced robustness to task horizon, showing significantly less performance degradation on longer trajectories compared to uniform-credit baselines (e.g., **-0.54** success points/turn on ALFWorld).
- Ablation studies confirmed that turn-level belief tracking, recursive belief revision, outcome-aligned credit, and an initial state prior are critical components contributing to the method's effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The causal contribution of an action should be measured by how much it revises the belief in eventual success, considering the interaction history, rather than its isolated local strength.
> - Self-distillation signals, comparing a privileged teacher to an unconditioned student, can effectively approximate the Bayesian evidence for a turn's impact on success.
> - A recursive belief update, initialized with a verifier-grounded prior and incorporating decaying evidence, enables robust, history-dependent credit assignment in long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of agentic reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs), particularly focusing on scenarios involving long-horizon, multi-turn interactions with partially observable environments. A significant challenge in this area is the **sparse and delayed nature of rewards**, where success or failure is often verifiable only at the end of an entire trajectory. This sparsity makes it difficult to assign credit accurately to individual decisions or turns that contribute to the final outcome.

Existing approaches to post-training LLM agents can be broadly categorized. Group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) methods, such as GRPO itself and its agentic variants, address sparse rewards by constructing a trajectory-level advantage signal. However, these methods typically broadcast this signal uniformly across all actions and tokens within a trajectory, failing to distinguish pivotal decisions from routine or irrelevant ones. This limitation becomes more pronounced as the interaction horizon lengthens.

A complementary line of research involves on-policy (self-)distillation. These methods provide denser, token-level supervision by training a student policy on its own rollouts, often guided by a "teacher" that leverages privileged information (e.g., retrieved skills) available during training but not inference. While self-distillation offers a richer signal than sparse terminal rewards, it often provides token-level signals that do not align naturally with agentic interactions, where environment responses occur at turn boundaries, after multiple tokens form an action. Furthermore, existing step-aware distillation methods tend to treat each turn in isolation, neglecting the cumulative evidence gathered from prior interactions.

AgentOPSD aims to bridge these gaps by transforming local self-distillation signals into **history-dependent, turn-level credit assignments**. It seeks to provide a more nuanced and temporally aware credit assignment mechanism than uniform advantage broadcasting, while integrating dense distillation signals in a principled, sequential manner that accounts for the cumulative impact of decisions. This approach contrasts with methods that use value functions or reward models, which often reintroduce the computational cost that critic-free methods like GRPO aim to avoid.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a critic-free, recursive turn-level credit assignment method for agentic reinforcement learning, specifically designed to address the challenges posed by sparse outcome rewards in long-horizon, multi-turn interactions.

The motivation for AgentOPSD stems from several observations and identified limitations in current agentic RL paradigms:

1. **Inadequacy of Uniform Credit Assignment:** In complex, multi-turn tasks, the final outcome reward is sparse, meaning it provides feedback only at the end of a long sequence of actions. Traditional group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) methods assign a uniform advantage to every decision within a trajectory. This approach fails to differentiate the causal contribution of individual actions, leading to inefficient learning. Many decisions in a trajectory might be routine, while only a few are pivotal for success or failure.
2. **Mismatched Granularity of Self-Distillation Signals:** While self-distillation (SD) offers denser, token-level supervision, its signals are often misaligned with the agentic interaction paradigm. Agents interact with environments in "turns," where a complete action (potentially comprising multiple tokens) is executed, and the environment responds. Token-level signals, when applied directly, do not inherently reflect the impact of a full turn or how individual tokens contribute to turn-level outcomes.
3. **Lack of Sequential Context in Local Signals:** Even when self-distillation signals are aggregated to a "step" or "turn" level, existing methods frequently treat each turn's signal in isolation. They do not account for the accumulated evidence or the interaction history, which means a locally strong signal might be redundant given previous turns or might not significantly revise the overall belief about trajectory success. The central challenge is therefore to transform these local OPSD signals into history-dependent turn-level credit that reflects their sequential impact.
4. **Desire for Critic-Free and Rollout-Efficient Methods:** Many existing approaches to fine-grained credit assignment, such as those relying on learned value functions (critics) or extensive additional rollouts for Monte-Carlo estimation, introduce significant computational overhead. The motivation is to achieve effective turn-level credit assignment without these additional costs, maintaining compatibility with efficient critic-free policy optimization frameworks like GRPO.

To address these motivations, the authors propose that the credit for a specific turn should not be determined solely by its local signal but by how significantly that signal **revises the estimated probability of eventual success** given the preceding interaction history. This insight forms the foundation for AgentOPSD's Bayesian-inspired recursive belief update mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AgentOPSD introduces a critic-free, recursive turn-level credit assignment mechanism designed for long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning. It integrates self-distillation signals with group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) to provide more granular and context-aware supervision.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The agent interacts with an environment over K turns, forming a trajectory τ = (s₁, a₁, o₁..., s_K, a_K, o_K) and receiving a binary outcome reward R(τ). Standard GRPO samples G trajectories, computes a sequence-level advantage A_seq for each based on the group's mean and standard deviation of rewards, and broadcasts this advantage uniformly to every token in the trajectory. AgentOPSD aims to refine this uniform credit assignment.

#### 3.2. From Outcome Contribution to Bayesian Turn Evidence

AgentOPSD reinterprets turn-level credit as the revision of a belief in eventual success.
1. **Bayes Factor Inspiration:** The ideal turn credit is conceptualized as the Bayes factor, which quantifies how an action `a_k` at state `s_k` changes the log-odds of the trajectory eventually succeeding (event C):
 `logit p(C | s_k, a_k) - logit p(C | s_k) = log [p(a_k | s_k, C) / p(a_k | s_k, ¬C)]`.
 However, these outcome-conditional action probabilities are generally intractable.
2. **Self-Distillation as an Evidence Proxy:** AgentOPSD approximates this ideal Bayes factor using a computable per-turn self-distillation contrast. It leverages a self-teacher mechanism where the policy's "teacher" branch is conditioned on privileged information (c+, a training-only retrieved skill describing useful subgoals), while the "student" branch is unconditioned.
 - For each token `y_{k,t}` within action `a_k` at turn `k`, a detached likelihood contrast `δ_{k,t}` is defined:
 `δ_{k,t} = log π_θ(y_{k,t} | h⁺_{k,t}) - log π_θ(y_{k,t} | h_{k,t})`
 where `h⁺_{k,t}` includes the privileged skill `c⁺`, and `h_{k,t}` does not. A positive `δ_{k,t}` indicates that `c⁺` increases the likelihood of the generated token.
 - These token-level contrasts are aggregated to form turn-level evidence `e_k` by summing over all tokens in action `a_k`:
 `e_k = Σ_{t=1}^{L_k} δ_{k,t} = log [π_θ(a_k | s_k, c⁺) / π_θ(a_k | s_k)]`
 This `e_k` serves as a tractable hindsight approximation to the Bayesian turn evidence, interpreted as an evidential score whose sign indicates whether `a_k` raises or lowers support for eventual success.

#### 3.3. Recursive Belief Update

To account for cumulative evidence and the history-dependent nature of credit, AgentOPSD maintains a recursive belief state in log-odds space.
1. **Initialization:** The belief state `B₀` is initialized using the group's average success rate `¯R` (clipped to avoid infinite log-odds), which serves as a verifier-grounded prior for task difficulty. Its log-odds equivalent is `ℓ₀ = logit(B₀)`.
2. **Accumulated Evidence:** A decaying evidence accumulator `c_k` is updated at each turn `k`: `c_k = γ c_{k-1} + e_k`, where `γ ∈ (0, 1]` is a decay factor (set to 0.95) that geometrically down-weights older evidence.
3. **Belief State:** The log-odds belief state `ℓ_k` is `ℓ_k = ℓ₀ + c_k`. The probability belief state `B_k` is then `σ(ℓ_k)`.
4. **Marginal Support Revision:** The importance of turn `k` is defined by its marginal support revision, `∆B_k = B_k - B_{k-1}`. This measures how much the current turn revises the accumulated belief. A first-order approximation shows `∆B_k ≈ B_{k-1}(1 - B_{k-1}) [e_k - (1 - γ)c_{k-1}]`, indicating that evidence has the greatest effect under uncertainty (when B is near 0.5) and is suppressed when belief saturates.
5. **Outcome-Aligned Credit:** The revision `∆B_k` is then aligned with the trajectory's terminal outcome by multiplying it with `sign(A_seq)`, yielding `q_k = sign(A_seq) ∆B_k`. This ensures that upward belief revisions for successful trajectories receive positive credit, while similar revisions for failed trajectories receive negative credit.

#### 3.4. Bounded Advantage Reshaping

The `q_k` values are used to reshape the original GRPO sequence-level advantage `A_seq` into a turn-level advantage `ẽA_k`.
1. **Standardization:** The `q_k` values are standardized within each trajectory (mean `μ_q` and standard deviation `σ_q`) to produce `z_k`.
2. **Bounded Multiplier:** A bounded multiplier `w_k = clip(1 + b z_k, 1 - b, 1 + b)` is applied, where `b ∈ (0, 1)` controls the range of modulation (set to 0.2). This ensures that the original advantage is never reversed in sign.
3. **Reshaped Advantage:** The final turn-level advantage `ẽA_k` is computed as `ẽA_k = A_seq [(1 - λ) + λw_k]`, where `λ ∈` (set to 0.5) controls the reshaping strength. If `λ=0`, it reverts to standard GRPO.
4. **Policy Optimization:** The policy `π_θ` is then updated by maximizing a clipped GRPO objective, where each token `y_{i,t}` inherits the reshaped advantage `ẽA_{κ_i(t)}` of its corresponding turn `κ_i(t)`. No separate distillation loss is introduced; the self-teacher signal operates only through `ẽA_k`.

The overall overhead of AgentOPSD compared to GRPO is a single teacher forward pass per trajectory; the belief reshaping operations are element-wise and do not involve additional rollouts or learned parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AgentOPSD was conducted on three interactive environments: ALFWorld (embodied reasoning), WebShop (online shopping), and Search-QA (retrieval-augmented QA), using Qwen2.5 models at 3B and 7B scales.

#### 4.1. Performance on Benchmarks

AgentOPSD consistently outperformed GRPO and a range of self-distillation baselines across the evaluated benchmarks and model scales.
- **ALFWorld:** AgentOPSD achieved 84.4% success with Qwen2.5-3B and 89.1% with Qwen2.5-7B. These figures were higher than most baselines, including GRPO (75.0% and 81.2%), GRPO+OPSD (81.2% and 80.4%), Skill-SD (73.4% and 85.1%), RLSD (79.7% and 82.0%), SDAR (84.4% and 85.9%), and StepOPSD (73.4% and 88.4%).
- **Search-QA:** AgentOPSD obtained 46.7% accuracy with Qwen2.5-3B and 49.2% with Qwen2.5-7B. This represented an improvement over GRPO (36.4% and 42.0%), GRPO+OPSD (44.6% and 47.0%), Skill-SD (44.1% and 47.8%), RLSD (43.8% and 49.0%), SDAR (43.4% and 49.0%), and StepOPSD (43.7% and 48.2%). AgentOPSD demonstrated higher performance on multi-hop QA tasks (e.g., Bam, Hotp, 2Wk, MuS) particularly with the 7B model.
- **WebShop:** AgentOPSD achieved 90.4% score and 69.5% accuracy with Qwen2.5-3B, and 90.2% score and 79.7% accuracy with Qwen2.5-7B. These results were comparable to or exceeded the best performing baselines (e.g., SDAR: 85.0% score/68.0% acc (3B), 89.4% score/82.8% acc (7B) and StepOPSD: 82.4% score/66.4% acc (3B), 87.2% score/78.1% acc (7B)).

The authors note that the gains come from the credit construction mechanism, not solely from privileged information access, as all methods using skills had access to the same retrieved skills during training.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Task Horizon

A key finding concerns AgentOPSD's robustness to task horizon. On ALFWorld (Qwen2.5-7B), AgentOPSD exhibited the smallest degradation in success rate as the number of turns increased, losing -0.54 success points per extra turn. This was notably better than uniform-credit methods like RLSD (-3.59 points/turn) and GRPO (-2.91 points/turn), supporting the motivation for turn-level credit in long-horizon interactions.

#### 4.3. Mechanism Ablation Study

An ablation study on ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-7B (full AgentOPSD: 89.1% success) revealed the contribution of each component:
- **Turn-level granularity vs. per-token accumulation:** Replacing turn-level belief tracking with per-token accumulation reduced success to 85.9%. This suggests that aligning aggregation with environment-aligned action turns is beneficial.
- **Recursive state revision vs. raw local gap:** Using the raw local gap `e_k` instead of the recursive `∆B_k` for credit assignment reduced performance to 82.8%. This confirms that measuring how a signal *revises* accumulated belief, rather than its local strength in isolation, is crucial for effective credit assignment.
- **Outcome-aligned signed direction:** Removing the `sign(A_seq)` from `∆B_k` (i.e., using only the magnitude) lowered performance to 80.5%. This indicates the importance of aligning the direction of belief revision with the trajectory's ultimate outcome.
- **State prior anchoring:** Removing the empirical group success rate `B₀` as the initial belief anchor reduced success to 78.9%. `B₀` provides a verifier-grounded reference point for task difficulty and stabilizes early belief revisions.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Sensitivity

The method demonstrated relative robustness to hyperparameters:
- **Reshaping weight (λ):** Setting `λ=0.5` consistently performed best. Smaller values (`0.25`, `0.1`, `0.01`) reduced performance, implying that down-weighting turn-level credit diminishes its benefit.
- **Evidence decay (γ):** Sweeping `γ ∈ {1.0, 0.95, 0.9, 0.8}` showed minor variations in performance (within a few points), without a strong monotone trend, suggesting that the recursion is not highly sensitive to the exact decay rate of old evidence.
- **Policy clipping (ϵ_high):** Varying `ϵ_high` had minimal impact, indicating that AgentOPSD inherits the trust-region robustness of GRPO.

Overall, the hyperparameter sensitivity was more pronounced on long-horizon tasks like ALFWorld and less so on short-horizon Search-QA tasks, aligning with the method's design for long-horizon credit assignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AgentOPSD contributes to the field of agentic reinforcement learning by offering a novel approach to credit assignment that addresses several limitations of existing methods. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Improved Credit Assignment for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The core contribution is a principled method for transforming sparse, terminal outcome rewards into fine-grained, turn-level credit signals. This is particularly impactful for long-horizon, multi-turn tasks common in agentic LLM applications, where uniform credit assignment is inefficient and inaccurate. By identifying pivotal decisions, AgentOPSD enables the model to learn more effectively from its experiences.
2. **Critic-Free and Computationally Efficient:** AgentOPSD achieves fine-grained credit assignment without requiring a learned value function (critic) or additional Monte-Carlo rollouts, which are common in other approaches. This maintains the computational efficiency benefits of group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), making it a practical solution for training large language models where computational resources are a significant concern. The overhead is limited to a single teacher forward pass per trajectory.
3. **Principled Integration of Self-Distillation:** The method provides a theoretically grounded way to integrate local self-distillation signals into a sequential credit assignment framework. By interpreting self-distillation gaps as Bayesian evidence and recursively updating a belief state, AgentOPSD moves beyond treating these signals in isolation, aligning them with the cumulative impact of actions on trajectory success.
4. **Enhanced Model Performance and Robustness:** The experimental results demonstrate consistent performance improvements over strong GRPO and self-distillation baselines across diverse interactive environments (ALFWorld, WebShop, Search-QA) and model scales. Critically, AgentOPSD shows improved robustness to increasing task horizons, indicating its ability to scale to more complex, extended interactions.
5. **Understanding of Credit Assignment Mechanisms:** The ablation studies provide insights into the necessity of different components: turn-level aggregation, recursive belief revision, outcome-aligned signaling, and prior anchoring. This enhances the understanding of *why* and *how* these elements contribute to effective credit assignment in agentic RL.
6. **Broader Applicability to Agentic LLMs:** The principles of recursive belief updating and evidence-based credit assignment could be applied to a wider range of agentic LLM tasks where sequential decision-making under sparse rewards is prevalent. This could include complex planning, code generation, scientific discovery, and human-computer interaction scenarios.

In summary, AgentOPSD represents an advancement in training more capable and robust LLM agents by addressing a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning: assigning credit accurately and efficiently across long interaction sequences, without incurring the typical costs of auxiliary models or excessive data generation.
