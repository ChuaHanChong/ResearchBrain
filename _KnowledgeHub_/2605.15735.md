# UAM: A Dual-Stream Perspective on Forgetting in VLA Training

> [!summary] Summary
> The Unified Action Model (UAM) architecture introduces a dual-stream design to mitigate the 'embodiment tax' in vision-language-action (VLA) models, preserving over 95% of the underlying VLM's multimodal competence while improving out-of-distribution robotic manipulation. This framework employs a dedicated 'Dorsal Expert' for control-oriented visual features, allowing the original VLM to retain its semantic understanding.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) tasks causes an 'embodiment tax,' leading to significant degradation of the VLM's original multimodal understanding capabilities.
- Existing mitigation strategies, such as freezing VLM parameters or co-training with auxiliary data, either restrict adaptation for action tasks or introduce complexity and potential objective interference.
- Current VLA architectures suffer from a structural bottleneck where a single encoder is forced to handle both language-grounded semantics and control-relevant visual features, causing forgetting of general VLM knowledge.

## Method

- The **Unified Action Model (UAM)** is proposed, featuring a ==dual-stream architecture== inspired by biological vision's ventral and dorsal pathways.
- UAM designates the original VLM as the **Semantic Expert** and adds a parallel **Dorsal Expert** specifically for processing control-oriented visual features.
- The Dorsal Expert is initialized with a ==generative Unified-Multimodal Model prior== and trained with a ==visual-dynamics objective==, enabling it to specialize in visuomotor control and alleviate the representational burden on the Semantic Expert.

## Results

- UAM retains over **95%** of its underlying VLM's multimodal understanding capabilities, incurring an average ==embodiment tax== of less than **5%** across various multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MMMU, MME, MMBench, TextVQA).
- UAM achieves superior out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization in robotic manipulation tasks on the bimanual ALOHA robot, performing robustly with unseen objects, novel object-target compositions, and linguistic variations.
- Qualitative analysis of attention maps shows functional decoupling, with the Semantic Expert attending to semantic entities and the Dorsal Expert focusing on robot parts, interaction regions, and global scene context, consistent with the 'What' and 'Where/How' streams.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'embodiment tax' is a quantifiable phenomenon in VLA training, where unfreezing VLM parameters for action fine-tuning substantially degrades multimodal understanding, but freezing them severely compromises action performance.
> - A dedicated architectural separation via a dual-stream design can intrinsically mitigate catastrophic forgetting, preserving VLM competence without needing to freeze parameters or rely on auxiliary vision-language data co-training.
> - Equipping the Dorsal Expert with a generative prior and visual-dynamics supervision is crucial for it to effectively learn control-relevant visual features, allowing the Semantic Expert to maintain its general semantic understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent approach in language-conditioned robot control. These models typically adapt large, pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by fine-tuning them on robotic action data. The rationale for this approach is that VLMs possess robust priors in open-vocabulary recognition, spatial and attribute reasoning, optical character recognition (OCR), multilingual grounding, and instruction following, capabilities that are difficult to replicate through robot-specific datasets alone.

However, a recurring challenge in this field is the observed degradation of the VLM's original multimodal competence during action-specific fine-tuning. This phenomenon, which the authors term the "embodiment tax," signifies a loss of general-purpose VLM capabilities when the model is adapted for control tasks. This degradation can hinder the robot's ability to generalize to novel objects, linguistic variations, or ambiguous instructions beyond its training data.

Existing approaches to mitigate this "embodiment tax" primarily involve two strategies:
1. **Parameter Freezing:** Keeping the VLM's parameters fixed during fine-tuning. While this preserves multimodal competence by design, it can restrict the model's ability to acquire control-relevant features necessary for effective manipulation.
2. **Co-training with Auxiliary Data:** Supplementing action data with additional vision-language data during fine-tuning. This allows gradients to flow through the VLM while attempting to maintain its multimodal knowledge. However, the effectiveness of this approach is dependent on the scale and diversity of the auxiliary data and may require mechanisms to prevent objective interference.

This research proposes an alternative, architecturally driven solution that draws inspiration from biological vision systems. Instead of preserving VLM competence through data augmentation or parameter constraints, the authors investigate whether structural separation within the model can inherently maintain multimodal capabilities during end-to-end action learning without these external aids. This positions the work as addressing a fundamental representational bottleneck in current VLA architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether VLA models can retain the general-purpose capabilities of their underlying VLMs without resorting to parameter freezing or auxiliary vision-language data for co-training. The authors aim to demonstrate that semantic preservation can emerge as a property of the VLA architecture itself.

The motivation for this objective stems from a critical observation: the "embodiment tax." The paper empirically demonstrates that standard fine-tuning of VLMs on action data, even with parallel Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) routing, systematically erodes the VLM's multimodal understanding. This degradation is problematic because the inherent value of a VLA is not merely its action generation capability but also the rich, general-purpose multimodal knowledge inherited from the VLM. Losing this knowledge compromises the VLA's ability to generalize in complex, real-world scenarios, particularly those involving out-of-distribution elements.

The authors hypothesize that this degradation arises from a "representational bottleneck." Current VLA designs typically route both language-grounded semantic understanding and control-relevant visual feature extraction through a single encoder (the VLM backbone). This forces the same neural pathway to reconcile two qualitatively different demands: abstract semantic recognition and concrete visuomotor control. Drawing an analogy to the two-stream organization of biological vision (ventral pathway for object identity/semantics and dorsal pathway for spatial layout/visuomotor control), the paper posits that this lack of functional separation in artificial VLAs is a primary driver of the embodiment tax. The action loss, being denser and more trajectory-driven, tends to overwrite the semantic features that make the VLM valuable.

Therefore, the key motivation is to design an architecture that structurally separates these functions, allowing a VLM-like component to preserve semantic understanding while a dedicated pathway handles control-oriented visual processing, ideally without requiring restrictive training interventions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research follows a three-stage path: measure, diagnose, and design.

#### 3.1. Measuring Forgetting

- **Task Formulation:** A VLA model $\pi_\theta$ maps an observation $I_{i,t}$ and natural language instruction $L_i$ to a low-level action $a_{i,t}$. The model is initialized from a pre-trained VLM and fine-tuned on a dataset of action trajectories $D_{act}$.
- **Forgetting Metric:** The paper quantifies VLM capability using $S(\cdot)$, the average score across a standard collection of multimodal-understanding benchmarks. Forgetting is then measured by the relative drop: $\Delta(f_{VLA}) = 1 - \frac{S(f_{VLA})}{S(f_{VLM})}$, where $f_{VLM}$ is the original VLM and $f_{VLA}$ is its action-fine-tuned counterpart. A $\Delta$ of 0 indicates no loss, while 1 indicates complete loss.
- **Empirical Setting:** Two representative VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-7B and PaliGemma) were used. An action expert was attached using three coupling configurations:
 - **Freeze-VLM:** VLM parameters are fixed; only the action expert is trained.
 - **+MoT:** Both VLM and action expert are trained, using parallel Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) routing.
 - **+MLP:** Both VLM and action expert are trained, with action prediction via a sequential MLP head.
 - Models were fine-tuned exclusively on CALVIN action data (no auxiliary VL co-training).

#### 3.2. Unified Action Model (UAM) Architecture

- **Dorsal Expert (E_dor):** Inspired by the biological dorsal stream, a parallel, visually grounded pathway, $E_{dor}$, is introduced to handle control-oriented features. This is in addition to the semantic expert $E_{sem}$ (the VLM) and an action expert $E_{act}$.
- **Three-Expert Coupling:** The UAM forms a three-expert macro-architecture, where $E_{sem}$, $E_{dor}$, and $E_{act}$ are coupled via parallel MoT routing. The action expert attends to tokens from both $E_{sem}$ and $E_{dor}$.
 - $Z_{sem} = E_{sem}(I_t, L; \theta_{sem})$
 - $Z_{dor} = E_{dor}(X_{dor}; \theta_{dor})$
 - $a_t = E_{act}(Z_{sem}, Z_{dor}; \theta_{act})$
 - $X_{dor}$ can be either the raw observation $I_t$ or learnable query tokens $q$.

#### 3.3. Designing the Dorsal Expert

The design of $E_{dor}$ was treated as an empirical question, evaluating its effectiveness based on two criteria:
1. On-par or improved action accuracy on in-distribution and out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks.
2. Minimal erosion of $E_{sem}$'s semantic competence, as measured by $\Delta$.

The design space for $E_{dor}$ included variations in:
- **Initialization:** Random, pre-trained VLM, or pre-trained generative unified-multimodal model.
- **Input Modality:** Raw observation ($I_t$) or learnable query tokens ($q$).
- **Auxiliary Loss:** With or without a visual-dynamics objective ($L_{wm}$).

The specific variants explored were:
1. **Random-init:** $E_{dor}$ randomly initialized, taking visual tokens as input, no auxiliary loss.
2. **VLM-init input vision (2a):** $E_{dor}$ initialized from a pre-trained VLM, taking visual tokens, no auxiliary loss.
3. **VLM-init input query (2b):** $E_{dor}$ initialized from a pre-trained VLM, taking learnable query tokens, no auxiliary loss.
4. **Gen-init only (3a):** $E_{dor}$ initialized from a generative UMM, taking visual tokens, no auxiliary loss.
5. **UAM (3b):** $E_{dor}$ initialized from a generative UMM, taking visual tokens, with an auxiliary visual-dynamics objective $L_{wm}$ (goal-observation prediction). $L_{total} = L_{act} + \lambda L_{wm}(\hat{I}_{t+1}, I_{t+1})$.

All variants were trained with $E_{sem}$ unfrozen. A comparison with $E_{sem}$ frozen was also performed to gauge the load-bearing capacity of $E_{dor}$. The final chosen configuration, UAM (Variant 3b), was then extensively evaluated.

#### 3.4. Training and Evaluation Details

- **Training:** UAM (Variant 3b) was initialized with the Bagel checkpoint for both the VLM expert and the Dorsal expert. It was trained end-to-end for 30,000 steps on 3,000 demonstration trajectories from an ALOHA bimanual robotic system, without parameter freezing, gradient stopping, or auxiliary VL co-training.
- **Evaluation:**
 - **Semantic Retention:** Multimodal understanding benchmarks: MMMU, MME-P/S, MMBench, MM-Vet, MathVista, MMStar, TextVQA.
 - **Action Performance:**
 - Simulated: CALVIN (ABC-D split) and RoboTwin benchmarks (in-domain manipulation).
 - Real-world: Dual-arm ALOHA system, focusing on out-of-distribution (OOD) semantic generalization tasks (unseen objects, novel object-target compositions, instruction variation, language variations like Pinyin or Chinese-English code-mixing).
 - **Representational Analysis:** Visualization of attention maps from the action head to visual tokens from $E_{sem}$ and $E_{dor}$ to understand information routing.
 - **Inference Latency:** Comparison of single-step inference speeds across different model architectures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Quantifying the Embodiment Tax

- Initial experiments demonstrated that unfreezing the VLM for action fine-tuning (using +MoT or +MLP couplings) significantly increased Action Accuracy but incurred a substantial drop in VLM Score (multimodal understanding).
- For example, Qwen2.5-7B with +MoT achieved 65.98% Action Accuracy but dropped to 37.94% VQA-AVG score, compared to Freeze-VLM with 30.12% Action Accuracy and 74.32% VQA-AVG score. The +MLP configuration showed even more severe forgetting, with VQA scores collapsing to 0. This quantified the "embodiment tax" across different VLM backbones and coupling methods.

#### 4.2. Dorsal Expert Design Study

- **Random-init:** Performed comparably to the 2-expert baseline on simulated tasks but worse on real-world OOD tasks, indicating that capacity alone is insufficient without a visual prior.
- **VLM-init:** Outperformed Random-init but showed mixed results compared to the 2-expert baseline, sometimes slightly worse on real-world tasks. The choice of input modality mattered, with actual vision tokens (Variant 2a) performing better than learnable query tokens (Variant 2b). This suggested that a semantic prior alone was not optimal for a control-oriented pathway.
- **Gen-init only (3a):** Showed similar performance to VLM-init, indicating a generative prior alone was not a significant advantage without matched supervision.
- **UAM (3b) – Generative-init with Visual-Dynamics:** This configuration achieved the highest action performance in the unfrozen setting and the smallest performance gap when $E_{sem}$ was frozen. It also demonstrated the strongest results on real-world OOD tasks. This indicates that a generative prior combined with a visual-dynamics objective enables the Dorsal Expert to become a functionally distinct, load-bearing pathway for control-relevant visual information.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Multimodal Understanding (Semantic Retention)

- UAM (7B MoT) demonstrated a retention of multimodal understanding capabilities comparable to state-of-the-art MLLMs, with a marginal performance drop of less than 5% across various VLM benchmarks (e.g., 53.7 on MMMU, 2289 on MME-S, 83.7 on MMBench).
- In contrast, OpenVLA (7B), a standard end-to-end VLA, showed a complete loss of multimodal understanding (0 score across all benchmarks) when trained without VQA co-training. Other methods using co-training (e.g., ChatVLA, $\pi_{0.5}$) still exhibited compromises in general understanding, suggesting limitations in co-training datasets and objective gaps.
- These results indicated that UAM successfully preserves the VLM's inherent vision-language capabilities through architectural design, without reliance on auxiliary data or gradient-blocking techniques.

#### 4.4. Evaluation of Robotic Manipulation (Semantic Generalization)

- UAM achieved high success rates in real-world out-of-distribution (OOD) manipulation tasks, including unseen objects, new object-target compositions, and instruction variations (e.g., 85% on Unseen Object and Distractor, 82% on Unseen Object-Target Comp, 80% on Language Variation).
- The VLM-initialized dorsal expert variant (VLM-init) showed comparable in-domain performance but underperformed the 2-expert baseline and UAM across all OOD tasks. This suggests that simply initializing with VLM weights is insufficient to transfer semantic capabilities to action generalization through a purely visual dorsal pathway, emphasizing the role of the World Model in UAM.

#### 4.5. Representational Analysis of Visual Signal Routing

- Attention map visualizations (Figure 6) revealed a clear functional bifurcation:
 - **Semantic Expert ($E_{sem}$):** Attention was concentrated on task-relevant semantic entities (e.g., target objects, goal regions), acting as the "What" stream for robust semantics alignment.
 - **Dorsal Expert ($E_{dor}$):** Attention focused on the robot arm, interaction regions, and global scene context, consistent with a "Where/How" stream that captures physical state and visual evolution.
- This qualitative analysis supports the hypothesis that UAM automatically routes visual demands for action signals into semantic-centric and dynamic-centric pathways, resolving the representational conflict in standard VLAs.

#### 4.6. Inference Delay

- Inference speed evaluations showed that while larger (7B) backbones are inherently slower than smaller (2B) ones, incorporating an additional 7B MoT expert in UAM had a minimal impact on overall inference speed compared to a 2-expert 7B MoT model. UAM's single-step denoising mechanism contributed to keeping its latency (1500ms) comparable to the 2-expert Qwen-$\pi_0$ (1300ms).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UAM research contributes to the field of VLA models by demonstrating that architectural separation, inspired by biological vision, can effectively mitigate the "embodiment tax" problem. The core significance lies in the finding that semantic preservation in VLAs can emerge from an intrinsic architectural design rather than being externally enforced through parameter freezing or auxiliary data co-training. This challenges conventional wisdom and provides a new direction for building more robust and generalizable robotic agents.

**Key impacts include:**

- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** UAM retains over 95% of the underlying VLM's multimodal competence during end-to-end action training, a substantial improvement over existing methods that often compromise VLM capabilities.
- **Enhanced Semantic Generalization:** The preserved semantic competence in the VLM pathway directly translates to superior performance in real-world out-of-distribution manipulation tasks, including novel objects, unseen compositions, and variations in language instructions. This suggests that a truly general-purpose robot policy requires maintaining high-level semantic understanding.
- **Biologically Inspired Design:** The dual-stream architecture, with distinct ventral (semantic) and dorsal (visuomotor control) pathways, provides a computational model that aligns with neurological principles, potentially offering insights into how robust perception and action are integrated.
- **Reduced Training Complexity:** By eliminating the need for parameter freezing, gradient stopping, or auxiliary vision-language co-training, UAM simplifies the training pipeline for VLAs, making it more efficient and less dependent on extensive external datasets.
- **Functional Specialization:** The demonstrated automatic routing of visual information, where the semantic expert focuses on "what" and the dorsal expert on "how/where," highlights the emergence of functional specialization within the model, leading to a more interpretable and potentially robust system.

**Potential limitations noted by the authors include:**

- The world-model-based bridge and the larger VLM backbone introduce additional training and inference complexity, albeit with optimizations that mitigate the real-world inference delay to a comparable level with other advanced VLAs.

Overall, UAM offers a flexible framework for studying embodied transfer, laying a foundation for future work on richer semantic, predictive, and control-aware interfaces in robotics. The research suggests a paradigm shift in VLA design, moving towards architectures that inherently support the preservation of broad multimodal intelligence alongside specialized control capabilities.
