# VQ-VLA: Improving Vision-Language-Action Models via Scaling Vector-Quantized Action Tokenizers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Lab and collaborating universities developed VQ-VLA, a system that enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating a scalable vector-quantized action tokenizer. This approach improves action representation precision and significantly boosts real-world robotic task success rates, particularly for long-horizon tasks, while tripling inference speed.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often rely on simple action discretization methods (e.g., binning), which can lead to loss of precision and struggle to capture complex action distributions.
- Current VLA models face challenges in efficiency and handling long-horizon robotic tasks due to slow inference speeds and the accumulation of errors over extended action sequences.
- Collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world robotic demonstration data is expensive and time-consuming, limiting the scalability of VLA model training.

## Method

- Introduces **VQ-VLA**, a two-stage pipeline that replaces simple action binning with a scalable, pre-trained, and frozen convolutional residual ==Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)==.
- Trains the VQ-VAE action tokenizer on an unprecedented scale of over 100 times more data than prior methods, leveraging both real-world and vast synthetic datasets (Open X-Embodiment, LIBERO, ManiSkill).
- Integrates the VQ-VAE into a Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone (OpenVLA), allowing the VLM to predict discrete action tokens that represent multiple actions in a single inference step, enabling efficient action chunking.

## Results

- VQ-VLA achieved an **80.98%** success rate on the LIBERO-90 simulator benchmark, a **7.45%** improvement over the OpenVLA baseline's **73.53%**.
- In real-world evaluations, the model improved average success rates across four short-horizon tasks from **23%** (baseline) to **46.25%** and demonstrated superior performance on long-horizon tasks, achieving **50%** success for "Put all cups into the basket" compared to the baseline's **15%**.
- The system achieved a nearly threefold increase in action execution frequency, reaching **11.84** Hz compared to the original OpenVLA's **4.16** Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The sim-to-real domain gap for action trajectories is empirically minimal, allowing the effective use of vast synthetic datasets to scale action tokenizers.
> - A convolutional residual VQ-VAE architecture effectively captures temporal dependencies in action sequences, outperforming MLP-based designs.
> - Scaling action tokenizers is a computationally efficient strategy to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, providing significant performance gains at a lower cost than scaling entire VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large generative models, computer vision, natural language processing, and robotic control, specifically within the rapidly evolving domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and generating human-like text and interpreting visual information. A natural extension of this progress is to endow robots with similar understanding and generation capabilities, leading to the development of VLA models that bridge multimodal inputs (visual observations, natural language instructions) with robotic actions.

Previous pioneering works like RT-1 and RT-2 have shown that discretizing continuous robot actions allows for their integration with large VLM backbones, enabling impressive zero-shot generalization capabilities by leveraging web-scale pretraining data. OpenVLA is another recent example that fine-tunes VLM backbones for robot action prediction using a simple bin-based discretization strategy. While these approaches have opened new frontiers in robotic control, they face inherent limitations. Simple binning can lead to a loss of precision, challenges in representing complex action distributions, and inefficiency in generating long sequences of actions.

This paper directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel approach to action tokenization. It builds upon the concept of tokenization, which has been highly successful in compressing high-dimensional continuous data into compact discrete sequences in LLMs (e.g., Byte Pair Encoding for text) and image/video generation models (e.g., Vector Quantization for visual signals). The core idea is that action sequences, due to their spatiotemporal continuity, are amenable to effective compression via tokenization.

The novelty of "VQ-VLA" lies in its *scalable* vector-quantized action tokenizer. While some prior works have explored VQ-VAE for action representation (e.g., MiniVLA, behavior generation with latent actions), this paper distinguishes itself by:
1. **Scaling Data Volume:** Training the tokenizer on an unprecedented scale, leveraging over 100 times more data than previous approaches, crucially incorporating vast synthetic datasets.
2. **Addressing Domain Gap:** Identifying and empirically validating that the sim-to-real domain gap for *action trajectories* is marginal, which is a pivotal insight allowing the effective use of large synthetic datasets. This differentiates it from challenges faced when transferring visual or physical properties from simulation to reality.
3. **Universality:** Aiming for a universal action tokenizer that can be adapted seamlessly to a wide range of downstream tasks, rather than being limited to single-task datasets.

By focusing on a more expressive and efficient action representation through advanced tokenization, this work contributes to making VLA models more precise, faster, and more capable of handling complex, long-horizon tasks, thereby pushing the boundaries of real-time embodied intelligence systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the performance, efficiency, and robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control by introducing a novel and scalable action tokenization strategy. The authors are motivated by several critical challenges observed in existing VLA models, particularly those relying on simpler action discretization methods.

The key motivations driving this work include:

- **Improving Action Representation Precision and Expressiveness:** Traditional methods, such such as simple binning of continuous actions, often lead to a loss of fine-grained detail and struggle to capture the inherent spatiotemporal continuity and complex distributions of robot actions. The authors hypothesize that more precise action tokenization is crucial for better action modeling, especially for intricate tasks.
- **Enhancing Training and Inference Efficiency:** Tokenization, by compressing high-dimensional continuous data into discrete sequences, can substantially reduce the computational complexity for VLA models. This is vital for achieving real-time performance and managing the large computational demands of modern generative models.
- **Tackling Long-Horizon Planning:** Long-horizon robotic tasks require sequences of coherent actions, where cumulative errors from imprecise action outputs can quickly lead to failure. Better action tokenization is expected to reduce these errors and improve the model's ability to execute extended plans.
- **Leveraging Data Scalability Economically:** The authors observe a critical insight: unlike visual or physical modalities, action trajectories exhibit a minimal domain gap between real-world and simulated environments. This characteristic presents a unique opportunity to scale action tokenizers by utilizing vast, easily generatable synthetic datasets without compromising real-world performance. This approach is significantly more cost-effective than scaling entire VLA models or collecting massive real-world robotic datasets.
- **Developing a Universal and Adaptable Solution:** The goal is to train a general action tokenizer that, once learned, can be seamlessly integrated into various VLA models and adapted to a broad spectrum of downstream tasks in a zero-shot manner, reducing the need for task-specific action representations.

In essence, the paper aims to demonstrate that a well-designed, scalable, and precisely trained vector-quantized action tokenizer can serve as a robust foundation for building more capable, efficient, and generalizable robotic manipulation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of VQ-VLA revolves around replacing the simple bin-based action tokenization in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (specifically OpenVLA) with a sophisticated, pre-trained, and frozen Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE). This is achieved through a two-stage pipeline: first, training a general convolutional residual VQ-VAE action tokenizer, and second, integrating and fine-tuning OpenVLA using this new tokenizer.

#### 3.1. Action Tokenizer Design (Residual VQ-VAE)

- **Architecture:** The proposed tokenizer is a convolutional residual VQ-VAE. Unlike previous MLP-based VQ-VAEs for action, this work employs 2D temporal convolutional layers for both the encoder ($\phi_{enc}$) and decoder ($\phi_{dec}$). This design choice is motivated by the ability of convolutional networks to effectively capture local relationships and hierarchical temporal dependencies within action sequences, addressing the scaling limitations of MLPs for structured temporal data.
- **Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ):** The encoder maps an input action sequence ($a_{t:t+n} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$) into a latent embedding ($x \in \mathbb{R}^k$). RVQ is then applied to compress $x$ into quantized residuals. This involves multiple quantization stages ($N_q$), where each subsequent stage quantizes the residual error from the previous stage, progressively refining the representation: $q(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_q} q_i(r_i)$, where $r_1=x$ and $r_{i+1} = r_i - q_i(r_i)$. The quantized embedding $q(x)$ is then fed to the decoder to reconstruct the action sequence ($\hat{a}_{t:t+n}$).
- **Loss Function:** The VQ-VAE is trained using a composite loss function, $L$, which is a weighted sum of:
 - **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rec}$):** Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the original and reconstructed action sequences, ensuring fidelity.
 - **Vector Quantization (VQ) Loss ($L_{codebook}$):** Encourages the encoder output to be close to the chosen codebook entry, enabling effective quantization.
 - **Commitment Loss ($L_{commit}$):** Ensures the codebook embeddings are updated to better represent the encoder outputs, preventing codebook collapse.
- **Embeddings for Enhanced Encoding:** To improve the encoder's ability to process diverse and structured action data, two types of embeddings are introduced before feeding action sequences to the encoder:
 - **Time Embedding:** Sinusoidal embeddings are added to encode temporal information, capturing both low and high-frequency patterns within the action sequences.
 - **Action-Type Embedding:** Learnable embeddings are used for different components of the action vector (e.g., XYZ positions, Euler angles, gripper states). This provides a strong prior for the model to distinguish and process the unique semantic roles of each action dimension.
- **Training Strategy for Tokenizer:** The VQ-VAE is trained using only action sequences as input, without additional conditional inputs (e.g., images or language), to ensure its universality and computational efficiency. A progressive training strategy is employed:
 - Initial training on real-world robotic datasets (e.g., OpenX-Embodiment), which often contain noisy trajectories.
 - Subsequent integration of cleaner and smoother synthetic data from large-scale simulated datasets (e.g., LIBERO, ManiSkill). This progressive approach helps the VQ model converge towards more stable and refined representations.
 - The authors train three versions with increasing data scale: Open X-Embodiment only (VQ$_{O}$), Open X-Embodiment + Libero (VQ$_{O+L}$), and Open X-Embodiment + Libero + ManiSkill (VQ$_{O+L+M}$).

#### 3.2. Integration with VLA Model (OpenVLA)

- **Replacement of Binning:** The pre-trained and *frozen* Residual VQ-VAE encoder replaces OpenVLA's simple bin-based action tokenizer.
- **Hierarchical Token IDs:** The VQ-VAE generates discrete codebook indices (tokens) from its multiple RVQ layers. Crucially, tokens from different VQ layers are assigned unique, non-overlapping ID ranges (e.g., Layer 1 tokens, Layer 2 tokens). This prevents semantic conflicts and maintains interpretability, as tokens with the same numerical ID from different layers represent distinct features.
- **VLM Fine-tuning:** The Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone (Prismatic-7B in OpenVLA) is fine-tuned (using LoRA) to directly predict these VQ-VAE generated tokens. The standard next-token prediction loss (cross-entropy) is used.
- **Action Chunking and Efficiency:** A major advantage of VQ-VAE integration is that it enables the VLM to predict *multiple actions in a single inference step* by tokenizing longer action sequences. This significantly reduces the number of tokens required for training and inference, leading to a substantial improvement in inference speed and computational efficiency compared to predicting one action at a time. The VLM effectively learns to predict a *chunk* of actions (e.g., 5 actions in this work) as a single tokenized output.

In summary, VQ-VLA's methodology leverages a powerful, data-scaled action tokenizer to provide a more expressive, efficient, and coherent action representation for VLA models, enhancing their capabilities across diverse robotic manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted in both simulated and real-world environments demonstrate the significant advantages of VQ-VLA.

- **Effectiveness of Convolutional Residual VQ-VAE Architecture:**
 - Preliminary simulator experiments on LIBERO-10 and LIBERO-GOAL benchmarks showed that the convolutional residual VQ-VAE significantly outperformed an MLP-based VQ-VAE. For example, on LIBERO-10, the Conv Residual VQ-VAE achieved a 60.0% success rate compared to the MLP's 53.4% when trained on the full LIBERO dataset. This validates the architectural choice for effectively capturing temporal dependencies.
 - These initial tests also showed that increasing the training data for the VQ-VAE, even within the LIBERO dataset, consistently improved success rates, providing early evidence for the scalability hypothesis.

- **Scalability with Synthetic Data:**
 - On the LIBERO-90 simulator benchmark, using VQ$_{M+R}$ (VQ-VAE trained on a mixture of ManiSkill and RLBench synthetic data) for the action tokenizer resulted in an 80.98% success rate. This represents a substantial 7.45% improvement over the OpenVLA baseline (73.53%) that uses simple binning.
 - A critical finding from ablation studies was that VQ$_{M}$ (trained only on ManiSkill) performed poorly (14.38%), highlighting that the *scale* and *diversity* of synthetic data are crucial for the tokenizer's effectiveness.

- **Real-World Performance on Short-Horizon Tasks:**
 - Integrating synthetic trajectories significantly boosted real-world performance. The average success rate across four short-horizon tasks (e.g., "Pull out a tissue paper", "Flip the pot upright") increased from 23% (baseline) to 46.25% with VQ$_{O+L+M}$ (Open X-Embodiment + LIBERO + ManiSkill data).
 - A notable 30% improvement was observed in the "Flip the pot upright" task.
 - For high-precision tasks like "Pull out a tissue paper," VQ-VLA models achieved 20%+ success rates, a marked improvement over the baseline's 5%, indicating better fine-grained control.
 - The significant impact of the large ManiSkill dataset (50 times larger than LIBERO) on real-world success rates further confirmed the value of data scaling.

- **Outstanding Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks:**
 - VQ-VLA demonstrated its most striking advantage in long-horizon tasks ("Put all cups into the basket" and "Put the toy into the drawer"). The VQ$_{O+L+M}$ model achieved 50% and 30% success rates respectively, vastly outperforming the baseline (15% and ~0-5%).
 - The model's ability to successfully complete complex sequences, such as opening a drawer and then manipulating an object inside, in all test cases, contrasts sharply with the baseline's frequent failures at early stages.
 - This superior performance is attributed to VQ-VLA's capacity to predict multiple actions per inference step, which reduces the accumulation of errors over extended task sequences, leading to higher success rates and faster task completion times.

- **Minimal Sim-to-Real Domain Gap for Actions:**
 - A dedicated analysis confirmed that a VQ-VAE trained exclusively on the synthetic LIBERO dataset (VQ$_{L}$) performed comparably to models trained partially or fully on real-world data (VQ$_{O}$, VQ$_{O+L}$) in real-world tasks. This finding empirically supports the core hypothesis that the domain gap for action trajectories themselves is minimal, justifying the use of vast synthetic data.

- **Significant Inference Speed Improvement:**
 - VQ-VLA achieved an action execution frequency of 11.84 Hz, nearly triple that of the original OpenVLA (4.16 Hz). This substantial acceleration is a direct result of the action chunking mechanism enabled by the VQ-VAE, allowing multiple actions to be predicted and executed in a single inference step.

- **Ablation Studies Validate Design Choices:**
 - **Action Chunking:** VQ-based action chunking proved significantly more effective than a naive autoregressive output approach (extending OpenVLA to output multiple actions). The autoregressive baseline showed a drastic performance drop (e.g., from 74.76% to 66.53% on LIBERO-90, and 20% to 0% on "Put the toy into the basket"), exhibiting issues like slow execution and shortcut learning (repetitive actions within a chunk). This highlights VQ-VAE's ability to generate more effective and diverse action sequences.
 - **Embedding Integration:** The inclusion of time and action-type embeddings for the VQ-VAE encoder consistently improved performance (e.g., 85.17% vs. 86.16% on LIBERO-90), demonstrating their value in representing structured action sequences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "VQ-VLA: Improving Vision-Language-Action Models via Scaling Vector-Quantized Action Tokenizers" holds significant implications for the field of robotics and embodied artificial intelligence, addressing fundamental challenges in action representation, efficiency, and scalability.

- **Advancing Vision-Language-Action Models:** This work provides a robust and empirically validated method to enhance state-of-the-art VLA models. By replacing simplistic action discretization with a powerful VQ-VAE-based tokenizer, VQ-VLA allows for more precise, expressive, and coherent action generation, which is crucial for complex robotic tasks.

- **Revolutionizing Action Representation Learning:** The core contribution lies in demonstrating the immense potential of scaling action tokenizers using massive synthetic datasets. The key insight that the sim-to-real domain gap for *action trajectories* is marginal is transformative. It unlocks a practically limitless supply of training data, circumventing the notoriously high costs and challenges associated with collecting real-world robotic demonstrations. This paradigm shift could accelerate research and development in robot learning by making data acquisition less of a bottleneck.

- **Enabling Robust Long-Horizon Robotic Capabilities:** The superior performance on long-horizon tasks is particularly impactful. Many real-world robotic applications, from assembly to domestic assistance, require a sequence of precise and sustained actions. By reducing cumulative errors and allowing multi-step action prediction, VQ-VLA offers a compelling solution for developing robots capable of executing complex, multi-stage tasks reliably over extended periods.

- **Boosting Real-Time Robotic Control:** The nearly threefold increase in inference speed is a critical practical advantage. Real-time operation is often a prerequisite for safe and effective robot deployment in dynamic environments. VQ-VLA's efficiency makes it a highly suitable candidate for real-world applications where rapid decision-making and continuous control are essential.

- **Promoting Generalizability and Adaptability:** The universal nature of the trained action tokenizer, capable of zero-shot adaptation to diverse downstream tasks, represents a move towards more generalist robot policies. This reduces the need for task-specific engineering or extensive re-training, paving the way for more versatile and broadly applicable robotic systems.

- **Cost-Effective Performance Enhancement:** The paper highlights that scaling action tokenization is computationally lightweight compared to scaling entire VLA models. This cost-effectiveness makes high-performance robotic control more accessible and efficient for researchers and practitioners, democratizing access to advanced robot learning techniques.

- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper identifies several promising avenues for future work, including exploring even larger synthetic datasets (e.g., beyond the current RLBench usage), integrating the tokenizer with other VLM optimization techniques (like distillation and quantization), and further refining the tokenizer architecture (e.g., incorporating action data frequency). This establishes VQ-VLA as a strong baseline and a catalyst for further innovation in embodied AI.

In conclusion, VQ-VLA represents a significant step forward in making robots more intelligent, efficient, and capable. By meticulously addressing the challenge of action representation through scalable vector quantization, the authors have laid a robust foundation for future developments in real-time embodied intelligence systems, promising a future where robots can perform complex tasks with greater precision, speed, and autonomy in diverse real-world settings.
