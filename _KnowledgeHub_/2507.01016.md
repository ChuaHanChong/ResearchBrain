# VQ-VLA: Improving Vision-Language-Action Models via Scaling Vector-Quantized Action Tokenizers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Lab and collaborating universities developed VQ-VLA, a system that enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating a scalable vector-quantized action tokenizer. This approach improves action representation precision and significantly boosts real-world robotic task success rates, particularly for long-horizon tasks, while tripling inference speed.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often rely on simple action discretization methods (e.g., binning), which can lead to loss of precision and struggle to capture complex action distributions.
- Current VLA models face challenges in efficiency and handling long-horizon robotic tasks due to slow inference speeds and the accumulation of errors over extended action sequences.
- Collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world robotic demonstration data is expensive and time-consuming, limiting the scalability of VLA model training.

## Method

- Introduces **VQ-VLA**, a two-stage pipeline that replaces simple action binning with a scalable, pre-trained, and frozen convolutional residual ==Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)==.
- Trains the VQ-VAE action tokenizer on an unprecedented scale of over 100 times more data than prior methods, leveraging both real-world and vast synthetic datasets (Open X-Embodiment, LIBERO, ManiSkill).
- Integrates the VQ-VAE into a Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone (OpenVLA), allowing the VLM to predict discrete action tokens that represent multiple actions in a single inference step, enabling efficient action chunking.

## Results

- VQ-VLA achieved an **80.98%** success rate on the LIBERO-90 simulator benchmark, a **7.45%** improvement over the OpenVLA baseline's **73.53%**.
- In real-world evaluations, the model improved average success rates across four short-horizon tasks from **23%** (baseline) to **46.25%** and demonstrated superior performance on long-horizon tasks, achieving **50%** success for "Put all cups into the basket" compared to the baseline's **15%**.
- The system achieved a nearly threefold increase in action execution frequency, reaching **11.84** Hz compared to the original OpenVLA's **4.16** Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The sim-to-real domain gap for action trajectories is empirically minimal, allowing the effective use of vast synthetic datasets to scale action tokenizers.
> - A convolutional residual VQ-VAE architecture effectively captures temporal dependencies in action sequences, outperforming MLP-based designs.
> - Scaling action tokenizers is a computationally efficient strategy to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, providing significant performance gains at a lower cost than scaling entire VLA models.
