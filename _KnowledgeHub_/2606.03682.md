# GN0: Toward a Unified Paradigm for Generation, Evaluation, and Policy Learning in Visual-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified framework, GN0, was developed for Vision-and-Language Navigation by researchers at the Institute of Artificial Intelligence, China Telecom, which integrates high-fidelity 3D Gaussian Splatting for data and simulation with a multi-stage learning paradigm for its navigation foundation model. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on VLN benchmarks and demonstrates robust sim-to-real transfer to real robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Limited availability and quality of navigation data for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), leading to a significant domain gap between simulation and reality.
- Traditional scene representations (e.g., meshes, panoramas) often lack the necessary visual fidelity, geometric detail, and interactivity to train generalizable VLN models.
- Challenges in developing robust VLN models capable of handling diverse, long-horizon, and dynamic tasks, including human-robot interaction.

## Method

- The **GN0** framework unifies data generation (GN-Matrix), interactive simulation and evaluation (GN-Bench), and policy learning (GN-BAE) within a comprehensive pipeline.
- Leverages ==3D Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) to create high-fidelity, photorealistic environments and dynamic human avatars, enhancing visual realism and VLM compatibility.
- Introduces a multi-stage training strategy for GN-BAE, incorporating Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), DAgger-based closed-loop adaptation, Denoised Advantage Policy Optimization (DAPO), and a dedicated NavDP Action Expert for robust and efficient navigation.

## Results

- GN-BAE achieved state-of-the-art performance, recording a **38.9%** Success Rate on the Unseen split of the GN-Bench FPV-only variant and **67.7%** SR and **63.4%** SPL on the R2R Val-Unseen split of VLN-CE.
- The model demonstrated strong sim-to-real generalization, successfully executing navigation tasks on self-developed wheeled-arm and Unitree G1 humanoid robots without any real-world training data exposure.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of DAgger adaptation in improving Success Rate and Oracle Success by mitigating covariate shift, and the benefits of DAPO in further enhancing navigation efficiency (minimizing NE and maximizing SPL).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity 3DGS-rendered Bird's Eye View (BEV) representations serve as an efficient and geometrically grounded explicit spatial memory, effectively unlocking latent spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs).
> - The DAgger adaptation stage is crucial for mitigating covariate shift by training on on-policy states with oracle supervision, which significantly enhances policy robustness and stability during closed-loop execution.
> - Preference-driven reinforcement learning (DAPO) further refines long-horizon decision-making and optimizes execution-oriented objectives, improving trajectory efficiency and sequential decision quality building upon initial imitation learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN). VLN tasks involve training intelligent agents to navigate real or simulated environments based on natural language instructions, serving as a foundational capability for general robotic intelligence.

The broader research landscape for VLN has historically faced challenges related to the availability and quality of navigation data, impacting the generalization ability of models and their capacity to handle long-horizon tasks. Traditional scene representation methods, such as meshes, panoramas, and video sequences, frequently exhibit limitations in terms of rendering detail, interactivity, and domain gap when transferring to real-world applications.

Recent advancements in 3D reconstruction, particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have gained attention for their ability to generate photorealistic renderings with rich geometric and appearance details. This paper leverages 3DGS as a promising pathway to enhance VLN by providing a visual interface compatible with Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs) that are often pre-trained on large-scale image data.

Existing VLN benchmarks, such as Room-to-Room (R2R) and Room-for-Room (R4R), primarily focus on discrete navigation over predefined graphs. More recent benchmarks like VLN-CE and VLN-PE introduce continuous control and increased physical realism, while others such as HA-VLN and SocNav explore dynamic, human-interactive environments. SAGE-Bench, while utilizing 3DGS, offers limited support for a joint study of 3DGS-based simulation, dynamic agents, and human-robot interaction within a unified framework. This work aims to address these limitations by proposing a comprehensive framework that integrates data generation, simulation, evaluation, and policy learning, emphasizing high-fidelity 3DGS environments, dynamic agents, and Bird's Eye View (BEV) representations as explicit spatial memory.

Memory mechanisms for VLN agents have explored various forms, including evolvable scene graphs (ETPNav), semantic map memories (FOM-Nav, 3D-Mem), and implicit latent representations (Uni-NaVid). This paper introduces 3DGS-rendered BEV as a geometrically grounded and efficient explicit spatial memory structure to enhance both global scene-level awareness and ego-centric local understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations driving this research are multi-faceted, aiming to advance the capabilities of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) systems:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Quality:** A significant challenge in VLN has been the limited availability and quality of navigation data. Traditional scene representations often lack the necessary detail and interactivity, leading to a domain gap between simulation and reality. This work aims to bridge this gap by curating diverse 3D scene resources and developing an automated pipeline for generating large-scale, high-fidelity navigation data. The motivation is to provide robust data support for training generalizable VLN models capable of handling complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **Leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for Enhanced Realism:** The paper is motivated by the potential of 3DGS to generate photorealistic renderings with rich geometric and appearance details, unlike conventional mesh-based simulators that often suffer from visual fidelity issues. By rendering navigation environments with 3DGS, the goal is to create a visual interface that is highly compatible with VLMs pre-trained on real-world images, thereby enabling more effective transfer of pre-trained knowledge into embodied navigation tasks.
- **Developing a Unified Paradigm:** The research seeks to establish a unified framework that seamlessly integrates data generation, interactive simulation, evaluation, and policy learning. The motivation is that a holistic approach can provide synergistic benefits, allowing for consistent development and assessment across the entire VLN pipeline, from data acquisition to model deployment.
- **Enabling Diverse and Dynamic Navigation Tasks:** A core objective is to move beyond static navigation environments and support more complex, human-centered interaction scenarios. This includes tasks such as instruction following, human following, and goal navigation in dynamic settings with moving agents. The motivation stems from the recognition that real-world robotic applications necessitate interaction with humans and adaptation to changing environments.
- **Introducing Novel Evaluation Standards with BEV:** The paper aims to introduce GN-Bench, a benchmark that provides Bird's Eye View (BEV)-based evaluation, incorporating dynamic 3DGS avatars. This is motivated by the need for evaluation metrics that better reflect spatial reasoning and human-robot interaction capabilities in dynamic, realistic environments. BEV representations are formalized as a compact and efficient memory mechanism to unlock the latent spatial reasoning capabilities of VLMs.
- **Developing an Effective Learning Strategy for Robustness:** The research seeks to develop an RL-driven navigation foundation model, Break and Establish (BAE), that can address the limitations of purely supervised learning. Specifically, supervised models tend to be overly reliant on idealized offline demonstrations, leading to covariate shift in closed-loop execution. The motivation is to use a hybrid DAgger + RL training strategy to expose the model to rollout-induced states and recovery behaviors, providing a better starting point for exploration and improving robustness to real-world perturbations.
- **Bridging Academic Research and Industrial Applications:** A practical objective is to create a dual-system architecture (GN-BAE) that ensures both flexibility and high-efficiency deployment in real-world scenarios. This aims to facilitate the transfer of advancements in embodied navigation from academic research to industrial applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The presented work, GN0, introduces a unified framework comprising three main components: GN-Matrix (for data), GN-Bench (for simulation and evaluation), and GN-BAE (for the navigation model).

#### 3.1. GN-Matrix: Large-scale Navigation Data Generation

The GN-Matrix dataset is constructed through an automated pipeline designed to provide diverse, high-fidelity 3DGS-based navigation data.
- **Scene Assets:** The dataset integrates multiple 3DGS scene sources:
 - **InteriorGS:** Utilizes 1,000 realistic indoor 3DGS reconstructions with semantic annotations for initial visual quality and object grounding.
 - **Handcrafted Large-Scale Scenes:** Incorporates 150 dense, large-scale scenes (e.g., offices, supermarkets) to provide more complex spatial layouts and support longer navigation trajectories.
 - **WorldGrow-Expanded Scenes:** Leverages the WorldGrow pipeline to generate additional indoor environments, expanding scene diversity and scale. This process includes an object enhancement pipeline that replaces low-quality generated objects with higher-quality 3DGS assets derived from HSSD.
- **Dynamic Human Avatars:** To simulate human-robot interaction, the dataset includes dynamic 3DGS human avatars. An avatar bank is created from single-view human images (SHHQ, HuGe100K) and generated avatars. SMPL-X body models are recovered and aligned with canonical 3DGS avatars. Motion sequences from Mixamo are converted into frame-wise SMPL-X parameters to drive the animated Gaussian avatars within the scenes.
- **Mission Types and Task Synthesis:** A shared pool of feasible start-goal trajectories is generated for each scene and then instantiated into three mission types:
 - **Goal-Based Navigation Tasks:** The destination is specified by a rendered goal reference or target observation, accompanied by a concise instruction.
 - **Instruction-Following Navigation Tasks:** The task is specified through route-level natural language instructions, describing intermediate navigation constraints and landmarks.
 - **Human-Following Tasks:** The agent is required to follow a moving human target, whose motion trajectories are derived from the shared path pool. The task involves maintaining a preferred relative distance and viewpoint.
 - **Multimodal Annotation:** Each generated sample includes Bird's Eye View (BEV) maps, first-person view images, historical views, and corresponding action spaces (both discrete actions and continuous pixel-level trajectories).
- **DAgger Data Collection:** To address covariate shift, an exploratory dataset is collected using the Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) algorithm. An initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) policy explores environments, and an oracle planner (A* search on dilated occupancy grids for continuous waypoints, Model Predictive Control (MPC) for discrete actions) dynamically relabels visited states with optimal expert annotations. A strict halting heuristic is applied to supervise termination.
- **Post-processing and Dataset Balancing:** The dataset undergoes post-processing, including visual condition enhancement (manipulating lighting) for robustness and action distribution balancing to reduce the dominance of forward actions and improve the representation of turning and stopping behaviors.

#### 3.2. GN-Bench: High-fidelity Simulation and Evaluation Platform

GN-Bench is a 3DGS-based benchmark supporting both policy evaluation and data generation.
- **Core Rendering Engine:** It leverages the `diff-gaussian-rasterization` framework to generate high-resolution RGB images and spatially aligned depth maps efficiently.
- **Observation Space:** Provides multi-modal observations: egocentric RGB-D streams from perspective cameras and high-fidelity, metric-scale top-down BEV projections from virtual orthogonal cameras.
- **Action Space and Kinematics:** Supports a hybrid action space:
 - `A_local`: VLN-CE compliant discrete actions (MoveForward, TurnLeft/Right) with fixed translation and rotation.
 - `A_global`: Direct spatial teleportation to global coordinates for high-level waypoint planning.
- **Collision Handling:** Implements a deterministic collision detection module using intrinsic grayscale occupancy maps from GN-Matrix, dilated morphologically to account for the agent's physical footprint. Movement vectors are truncated upon collision.
- **Global Path Planning:** Incorporates an oracle A* heuristic search algorithm on the dilated occupancy grids to compute optimal, collision-free trajectories, used for evaluating metrics like Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) and generating expert demonstrations for DAgger.
- **Dynamic 3DGS Avatars and Human-Robot Interaction:** Seamlessly integrates dynamic 3DGS human avatars into the scenes by applying rigid transformations to avatar Gaussians before rasterization. This enables evaluation of dynamic Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) tasks like active target following and reactive dynamic obstacle avoidance.

#### 3.3. GN-BAE: Navigation Foundation Model (Break and Establish)

GN-BAE is a unified embodied navigation foundation model, compatible with map-based and map-free approaches, and designed for instruction following, human following, and goal navigation.
- **Trajectory Tokenization:** Trajectory prediction is framed as discrete sequence generation. Discrete actions (<FWD>, <LEFT>, <RIGHT>, <STOP>) are represented by tokens. Continuous waypoints are discretized into 1,000 bins, with normalized BEV coordinates mapped to special tokens, providing a resolution-agnostic representation.
- **Four-Stage Training Strategy:**
 - **VLM SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning):** The Qwen3-VL base model is fine-tuned using an autoregressive cross-entropy loss on trajectory token prediction. This stage establishes basic planning capabilities.
 - **DAgger-based Closed-loop Adaptation:** Following SFT, a DAgger stage mitigates covariate shift by training the model on on-policy states relabeled with oracle actions. This adapts the policy to real closed-loop rollout distributions, improving recovery behaviors and providing a better initialization for subsequent reinforcement learning.
 - **DAPO (Reinforcement Learning):** After DAgger, the model is further optimized using DAPO (Denoised Advantage Policy Optimization) under closed-loop interaction. This stage refines long-horizon decision quality and optimizes execution-oriented objectives. A GRPO-style advantage estimator is used with a clipped policy-gradient objective, driven by a reward function that considers trajectory consistency, first-action correctness, collision penalties, length mismatch, and format penalties. The vision tower is frozen during this stage.
 - **Action Expert:** For waypoint-based outputs, a dedicated NavDP Action Expert converts the predicted BEV trajectory into executable low-level actions. This expert, trained from scratch using a DDPM-style noise prediction objective, enhances execution robustness, smoothness, and local obstacle avoidance, by using visual observations and goal conditions without depth input.
- **Dual-System Architecture:** The overall design is a dual-system architecture ensuring both flexibility (map-based and map-free) and efficiency for deployment.
- **Memory Mechanism:** For FPV-based navigation, a simple memory mechanism uses the current frame as short-term memory and the previous sixteen executed discrete action observations (concatenated into a single image) as long-term memory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance of the GN0 framework and its components:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** GN-BAE demonstrates superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods across both the GN-Bench and VLN-CE benchmarks.
- **Evaluation on GN-Bench:**
 - **FPV-only variant:** Achieves a 38.9% Success Rate (SR) on the Unseen split of GN-Bench. This result surpasses the strongest fine-tuned baseline, InternNav-S2†, by approximately 15 absolute percentage points, indicating strong spatial reasoning capabilities even with limited sensory input.
 - **BEV-augmented variant:** On the Seen split, the BEV-augmented model performs well, suggesting that global geometric priors are beneficial in familiar environments. However, on the Unseen split, the FPV-only variant records better Navigation Error (NE), Oracle Success (OS), and SR, while the BEV variant maintains a marginal advantage only in Success weighted by Path Length (SPL). This suggests that while BEV can enhance path efficiency, its reliance on map cues in novel 3DGS environments might introduce representational bias that can occasionally impact long-horizon decision-making. The FPV-only policy, relying on egocentric visual-language grounding, appears more robust to scene-level distribution shifts for goal reaching in these novel settings.
- **Evaluation on VLN-CE:**
 - GN-BAE achieves the highest overall performance on the R2R Val-Unseen split of the VLN-CE benchmark. It records an NE of 3.50m, OS of 75.6%, SR of 67.7%, and SPL of 63.4%, utilizing only single-view RGB input.
 - Compared to CorrectNav, the strongest prior RGB-only baseline, GN-BAE reduces NE by 17.5% (from 4.24m to 3.50m) and improves SR by 2.6 points and SPL by 1.1 points. These metrics indicate enhanced goal localization and trajectory efficiency. The improvement is attributed to the proposed offline reinforcement learning paradigm, which optimizes policy distribution towards high-quality navigation trajectories and mitigates covariate shift.
- **Real-world Generalization:**
 - The model, trained exclusively on high-fidelity Gaussian Splatting (GS) simulation data, exhibits strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities.
 - It successfully executes real-world navigation tasks on a self-developed wheeled-arm robot (TeleBotW) and a Unitree Robotics G1 humanoid robot without any exposure to real robot data during training, performing at an inference frequency of up to 5 Hz on an NVIDIA Orin edge device with an NVIDIA H100 GPU.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effectiveness of DAgger Adaptation:** Ablation studies confirm that incorporating the DAgger stage significantly improves both SR and OS. This suggests that DAgger effectively mitigates covariate shift by exposing the agent to rollout-induced states and providing corrective oracle supervision, thereby enhancing state coverage and online execution stability.
 - **Benefits of Preference-Driven RL (DAPO):** Removing the DAPO stage leads to a decrease in navigation efficiency. The full training pipeline consistently minimizes NE and maximizes SPL across both FPV and BEV-conditioned settings. This indicates that while initial imitation learning provides foundational behaviors, the preference-driven reinforcement learning stage is crucial for refining sequential decision-making and optimizing global trajectory efficiency.
 - The studies also show that direct application of DAPO without the preceding DAgger stage does not result in meaningful performance improvements, suggesting that DAgger's role in expanding policy entropy is a prerequisite for effective reinforcement learning exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GN0 framework represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied AI and Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) through its comprehensive and integrated approach. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Data and Simulation Fidelity:** The creation of GN-Matrix, described as the largest high-fidelity 3DGS-based navigation dataset to date, coupled with GN-Bench, an interactive 3DGS simulator and benchmark, establishes new standards for visual realism and environmental complexity in embodied navigation research. This provides researchers with more realistic and diverse data and simulation environments, which can facilitate the training of more robust and generalizable VLN models. The inclusion of dynamic 3DGS avatars and BEV-based evaluation in GN-Bench is particularly significant for advancing the study of human-robot interaction and spatial reasoning in dynamic settings.
- **Robust and Generalizable Learning Paradigm:** The GN-BAE model's multi-stage training strategy, integrating supervised learning, DAgger, and reinforcement learning, offers a robust approach to policy learning. By explicitly addressing covariate shift through DAgger and optimizing for execution-oriented objectives via DAPO, the framework improves the model's ability to recover from errors and adapt to out-of-distribution states encountered during closed-loop operation. This learning paradigm has the potential to produce more reliable and adaptable navigation policies for complex real-world scenarios.
- **Unlocking VLM Spatial Reasoning:** The formalization of high-fidelity 3DGS-rendered Bird's Eye View (BEV) representations as a compact and efficient memory mechanism is a key innovation. This approach is demonstrated to unlock latent spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs), enabling more effective navigation by providing a geometrically grounded spatial context for both long-term planning and short-term control. This could lead to new avenues for VLM-based spatial understanding and embodied intelligence.
- **Bridging Sim-to-Real Gap and Practical Deployment:** The demonstrated strong sim-to-real generalization of GN-BAE, with successful deployment on diverse real robotic platforms (wheeled-arm and humanoid) without real-world training data, highlights the practical utility of the framework. This capability is crucial for accelerating the transition of embodied AI research from laboratory settings to real-world industrial and service applications, potentially reducing the cost and complexity of robot development.
- **Support for Diverse Embodied Tasks:** The unified paradigm's ability to handle a broad spectrum of tasks, including instruction following, human following, and goal navigation, within both map-based and map-free settings, enhances the versatility of embodied agents. This expands the scope of problems that VLN systems can address, moving towards more general-purpose robotic intelligence capable of operating effectively in human-centric environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By providing a comprehensive framework spanning data, simulation, and learning, this work offers a foundation for future research in embodied navigation. Potential future directions include incorporating richer physical interactions, extending the framework to more complex embodied tasks, and further exploring multimodal spatial reasoning.
