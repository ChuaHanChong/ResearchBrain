# CGL: Advancing Continual GUI Learning via Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> The CGL framework enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to adapt continuously to evolving Graphical User Interfaces by synergistically integrating Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning. It achieves superior average Step-wise Accuracy (e.g., 82.33% on QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct) and minimizes catastrophic forgetting with a near-zero or positive Forgetting Measure on the new AndroidControl-CL benchmark.

## Problem

- GUI agents trained with static paradigms struggle to adapt to dynamic, evolving software environments, leading to performance degradation and obsolescence.
- Current GUI agent training methods face a "stability-plasticity dilemma": Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) offers fast adaptation but suffers catastrophic forgetting, while Reinforcement Learning (RL) retains knowledge but adapts slowly.
- There is a lack of dedicated, standardized benchmarks tailored for continual GUI learning in dynamic environments, hindering systematic evaluation.

## Method

- The **CGL** framework integrates Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) into a joint training objective for Multimodal Large Language Models.
- Error-Aware Routing dynamically injects SFT guidance using ground-truth demonstrations when GRPO encounters sparse rewards and struggles to find solutions.
- Entropy-Regulated Tuning adaptively controls the SFT loss weight based on policy entropy and training steps, balancing exploration during early learning and exploitation for convergence.
- Conditional Gradient Surgery resolves conflicts between SFT and GRPO gradients by projecting the SFT gradient orthogonally to the GRPO gradient when their cosine similarity is negative, preventing detrimental knowledge interference.
- A new benchmark, AndroidControl-CL, was established to enable rigorous evaluation of continual GUI learning, featuring functional super-class task splits and fine-grained bounding box annotations.

## Results

- CGL achieved the highest average Step-wise Accuracy (**82.33%** with QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct) and Trajectory-wise Accuracy (**38.03%**) on the AndroidControl-CL benchmark, outperforming all SFT and RL baselines.
- CGL exhibited the smallest Forgetting Measure (e.g., -**0.02** with QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct, -**0.52** with LLaVA-OneVision-0.5b), demonstrating effective mitigation of catastrophic forgetting and even positive backward transfer in some task orders.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic contributions of Error-Aware Routing, Entropy-Regulated Tuning, and Conditional Gradient Surgery, with each module progressively improving overall performance in continual GUI learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning provides plasticity for rapid new task adaptation but causes catastrophic forgetting, while Reinforcement Learning (specifically GRPO) ensures stability but adapts slowly in sparse-reward GUI environments.
> - Dynamically managing policy entropy through SFT weighting is crucial for escaping low-entropy local optima during initial learning and for consolidating knowledge during convergence to ensure long-term retention.
> - Explicitly resolving gradient conflicts between SFT and GRPO through conditional gradient surgery is essential to prevent knowledge interference and maintain optimization stability in hybrid continual learning systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the challenge of continual learning within Graphical User Interface (GUI) agents, a subfield experiencing development due to advancements in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). GUI agents aim to automate complex interactions within software environments by interpreting visual semantics and executing action sequences. While MLLM-powered agents have shown potential, their training paradigms are often static, which contrasts with the dynamic evolution of real-world GUI environments characterized by frequent UI updates and functional iterations.

Continual Learning (CL) is a general area in machine learning focused on enabling models to adapt to new tasks without degrading performance on previously learned tasks, a phenomenon known as catastrophic forgetting. This work positions CL in the specific context of GUI agents, highlighting unique challenges such as long-horizon action dependencies where small UI changes can invalidate established interaction policies.

Existing approaches for training GUI agents typically rely on either Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically variants like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The paper identifies a dichotomy: SFT offers fast adaptation (plasticity) but is susceptible to forgetting (instability), while GRPO demonstrates retention of prior knowledge (stability) but is characterized by slow adaptation speeds, particularly in sparse-reward environments.

This research contributes to the landscape by proposing a framework that integrates SFT and RL to reconcile the stability-plasticity trade-off, which remains an open problem in continual GUI learning. It also establishes a dedicated benchmark, AndroidControl-CL, to standardize evaluation in this dynamic domain, addressing a gap where existing datasets are not tailored for continuous adaptation scenarios. The work builds upon and extends prior research in general continual learning methods (regularization-based, dynamic architecture, rehearsal methods), continual learning for vision-language models (e.g., Multi-Modal Replay, Cross-Modal Regularization, Parameter-Efficient Adaptation), and various GUI agent paradigms (SFT-based, RL-based). By integrating and dynamically balancing SFT and RL, the authors aim to move beyond single-paradigm limitations, offering a more robust approach for GUI agent evolution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent dynamism of real-world GUI environments. Mobile applications undergo frequent updates, user interfaces evolve, and new functionalities are introduced regularly. Current GUI agents, largely trained through static paradigms, struggle to adapt to these changes without compromising their proficiency in previously mastered tasks. This leads to a degradation of performance, effectively rendering agents obsolete with software updates.

Specifically, the authors identify a critical challenge: the "stability-plasticity dilemma" in continual GUI learning. Their preliminary investigations reveal distinct behaviors between two common training methodologies:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This approach facilitates rapid adaptation to new GUI tasks. However, the aggressive gradient updates inherent in SFT tend to overwrite previously acquired knowledge, leading to catastrophic forgetting of older tasks. This rapid adaptation comes at the cost of skill retention.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly GRPO:** This method demonstrates an inherent resilience in preserving prior interaction logic, indicating stronger stability against forgetting. However, its reliance on reward signals and exploration can lead to slow adaptation speeds, especially when rewards are sparse or when an agent encounters entirely novel interaction patterns. This inefficiency makes it impractical for real-world scenarios requiring fast adaptation.

Motivated by these complementary strengths and weaknesses, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To establish a comprehensive understanding of SFT and RL behaviors in continual GUI learning:** The research aims to characterize how each paradigm contributes to plasticity (adaptation to new tasks) and stability (retention of old tasks), providing empirical evidence for their respective trade-offs.
2. **To develop a novel framework that balances adaptation efficiency and skill retention:** The overarching objective is to create a Continual GUI Learning (CGL) framework that synergistically combines SFT and RL to mitigate catastrophic forgetting while ensuring efficient acquisition of new GUI skills.
3. **To introduce mechanisms for dynamic control and conflict resolution:** The framework aims to incorporate specific components that manage the interaction between SFT and RL, including:
 - Dynamically triggering supervised updates when RL exploration is inefficient.
 - Modulating the influence of supervised learning based on the agent's uncertainty.
 - Resolving gradient interference between the two learning objectives to maintain optimization stability and prevent knowledge overwriting.
4. **To create a standardized benchmark for continual GUI learning:** Recognizing the absence of a dedicated evaluation platform for dynamic GUI environments, a crucial objective is to establish AndroidControl-CL. This benchmark is designed to simulate realistic software evolution through structured task splits and fine-grained annotations, allowing for systematic and comparable evaluation of continual GUI agents.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to advance the field by enabling GUI agents to operate robustly and effectively in the continually evolving landscape of mobile applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Continual GUI Learning (CGL) framework is built upon Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), framing the GUI agent as a policy network `π_θ` that processes visual inputs (screenshots `v_t`) and natural language instructions (`I`) to predict text-based actions (`a_t`). The core methodology centers on a joint training framework that integrates Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), controlled by three main modules: Error-Aware Routing, Entropy-Regulated Tuning, and Gradient Surgery. The global optimization objective is defined as `L = L_GRPO + λ(H, step) · L_SFT`.

**Error-Aware Routing:**
This mechanism is designed to address reward sparsity in GRPO, particularly when the agent lacks initial knowledge of a new interaction pattern. GRPO is adopted for its stability in policy updates through group-based relative advantage calculation. However, if all sampled rollouts for an instruction fail to achieve the full reward, the relative advantage signal degenerates, trapping the agent in unproductive exploration. To overcome this, Error-Aware Routing dynamically injects supervised guidance:
- For each instruction, the system evaluates `K` sampled rollouts.
- If the maximum reward across these rollouts is less than the ideal full score (`max_k r(τ_k) < r_max`), it indicates the policy cannot autonomously discover the correct solution.
- In such cases, the update is routed to an SFT step, utilizing a ground-truth demonstration (`τ*`) to optimize its likelihood.
- For spatial actions, an augmented SFT loss is used, sampling multiple valid points within the target bounding box to enhance spatial generalization. This routing strategy ensures SFT is selectively applied to rectify "pathological" biases where GRPO struggles.

**Entropy-Regulated Tuning:**
This module dynamically manages the trade-off between exploration (adapting to new tasks) and exploitation (retaining old skills) by adjusting the weight `λ(H, step)` of the SFT component in the total loss. The adjustment is based on policy entropy `H`, which quantifies the uncertainty of the agent’s action distribution, and the training step.
- **Phase 1 (Entropy Injection / Warmup):** During initial training steps for a new task (`step_w`), `λ` linearly increases to its maximum value. This is when the model often exhibits a strong bias towards incorrect actions. SFT updates in this phase are designed to assign large positive updates to low-probability ground-truth actions and negative updates to high-probability erroneous actions, thereby creating a negative covariance between log-probabilities and logit updates. This increases policy entropy (`ΔH_SFT > 0`), effectively "heating up" the distribution, breaking local minima, and forcing exploration towards correct behaviors.
- **Phase 2 (Entropy Decay / Convergence):** Once basic task competency is established, `λ` decays exponentially as a function of `H` (`λ(H) = (λ_max - λ_min) min(1, k e^(γH)) + λ_min`). In this phase, GRPO updates dominate. GRPO reinforces actions with high probabilities and positive advantages (the "Matthew Effect"), leading to a positive correlation between the distribution and updates. This process drives policy entropy down (`ΔH_GRPO < 0`), allowing the model to solidify its knowledge. By decaying `λ`, SFT interference during GRPO's precise convergence is minimized, promoting long-term retention.

**Conditional Gradient Surgery:**
To prevent destabilization and knowledge interference arising from gradient conflicts between SFT and GRPO, a conditional gradient surgery strategy is implemented:
- **Conflict Detection:** A conflict is identified if the cosine similarity between the SFT gradient (`∇_θ L_SFT`) and the GRPO gradient (`∇_θ L_GRPO`) is negative (angle > 90°).
- **Orthogonal Projection:** If a conflict is detected, the component of `∇_θ L_SFT` that opposes the `∇_θ L_GRPO` direction is surgically removed. Only the part of `∇_θ L_SFT` orthogonal to `∇_θ L_GRPO` is retained. This projection (`∇_θ L_SFT* = ∇_θ L_SFT - ((∇_θ L_SFT · ∇_θ L_GRPO) / ||∇_θ L_GRPO||^2) ∇_θ L_GRPO`) eliminates adversarial update directions while preserving constructive SFT information. If no conflict exists, the original SFT gradient is used.

**AndroidControl-CL Benchmark:**
To rigorously evaluate continual GUI learning, the authors establish AndroidControl-CL, an extension of the AndroidControl dataset.
- **Explicit, Multi-Source App Identity Annotation:** Integrates app identities from open app parameters, natural language instructions, and screenshot-based recognition.
- **Functional Super-Class Based Task Splits:** Organizes apps into seven functional categories (Shopping, Productivity, Communication, Travel, System Tools, Education and Science, Life and Entertainment) to simulate sequential learning tasks with distinct domain shifts. Each task's train/test split is 8:2 at the trajectory level.
- **Balanced Task Distribution:** Data filtering ensures a balanced number of apps per task and trajectories per app.
- **Fine-Grained Bounding Box Annotation for Click Actions:** Enhances click actions from single-point coordinates to bounding boxes encompassing interactive UI elements, achieved via automatic UI element parsing, matching original click coordinates, and manual verification.

**Metrics:** The framework is evaluated using Step-wise Accuracy, Average Step-wise Accuracy, Trajectory-wise Accuracy, Average Trajectory-wise Accuracy, and Forgetting Measure (FM) to quantify performance on both new and old tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the CGL framework employed two MLLMs (QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct and LLaVA-OneVision-0.5b) and compared performance against five baselines: SFT, SFT+KL, SFT+Replay, RIF-RFT, and GRPO. Experiments were conducted across three distinct task orders on the AndroidControl-CL benchmark.

**Initial Observations on SFT vs. GRPO:**
Preliminary analysis indicated that SFT facilitated fast adaptation to new tasks but led to significant knowledge overwriting (catastrophic forgetting). In contrast, GRPO demonstrated inherent resilience in retaining prior interaction logic but suffered from slower adaptation speeds, particularly in new or sparse-reward environments. This dichotomy served as the fundamental motivation for CGL.

**Performance under Task Order 1 (QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct and LLaVA-OneVision-0.5b):**
- **Superior Accuracy:** CGL achieved the highest average Step-wise Accuracy and Trajectory-wise Accuracy for both model scales.
 - On QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct, CGL recorded 82.33% Step-Acc. and 38.03% Trajectory-Acc., outperforming SFT (76.90%), SFT+KL (80.84%), SFT+Replay (79.80%), GRPO (81.53%), and RIF-RFT (80.44%).
 - On LLaVA-OneVision-0.5b, CGL achieved 77.84% Step-Acc. and 24.77% Trajectory-Acc., surpassing all baselines by 1.27 to 5.18 percentage points in Step-Acc.
- **Minimal Forgetting:** CGL exhibited the smallest Forgetting Measure (FM) for both backbones, indicating effective mitigation of catastrophic forgetting.
 - QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct achieved a near-zero FM (-0.02), suggesting negligible loss of old-task knowledge and outperforming SFT+KL (-1.01) and GRPO (-0.62).
 - LLaVA-OneVision-0.5b yielded an FM of -0.52, significantly lower than baselines like SFT (-6.81) and GRPO (-0.93).

**Robustness Across Multiple Task Orders (QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct):**
- CGL consistently led in average Step Accuracy (≥ 82.33%) and average Trajectory Accuracy (≥ 38.03%) across all three evaluated task orders. This demonstrated its generalizability to diverse task sequences.
- CGL outperformed naive SFT by 5.40–5.55 percentage points in Step Accuracy and GRPO by 0.80-0.86 percentage points.
- Notably, CGL achieved a positive FM (+0.13) in Task Order 2. This suggests not only the absence of forgetting but also a slight performance enhancement on previously learned tasks after acquiring new ones, a rare outcome in continual learning. In contrast, SFT showed severe forgetting (FMs from -5.33 to -6.03), and GRPO also experienced performance degradation (FMs from -0.33 to -0.62).

**Ablation Study of Module Contributions (QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct, Task Order 1):**
The ablation study confirmed the complementary contributions of each CGL component:
- Adding a KL divergence constraint to SFT (SFT+KL) improved performance over plain SFT but was still inferior to GRPO alone.
- Combining GRPO with KL (GRPO) significantly enhanced both accuracy (81.53% Step-Acc) and anti-forgetting capabilities (FM -0.62).
- Incrementally integrating modules into the GRPO baseline showed progressive improvements:
 - Dynamic SFT (D-SFT) improved Step-Acc by targeting policy weak points.
 - Entropy-Regulated Tuning (D-λ) further refined the balance between adaptation and retention, leading to 82.23% Step-Acc. and 37.62% Trajectory-Acc.
 - Conditional Gradient Surgery (Grad-Surg) mitigated gradient conflicts, reducing FM to -0.05.
 - The full CGL framework (integrating all modules) achieved the optimal performance (82.33% Step-Acc, 38.03% Traj-Acc, FM -0.02), confirming their synergistic effect.

**Analysis of Entropy-Regulated Tuning (D-λ):**
- **Trend Correlation:** Visualizations demonstrated that policy entropy and the SFT loss weight `λ` co-varied as designed. `λ` linearly increased at task-switching points (warmup phase) to inject entropy and then decayed exponentially (decay phase) to stabilize entropy.
- **Mechanism Advantage:** The isentrope diagram indicated that CGL's dynamic `λ` allowed the agent to escape low-entropy local optima by crossing isentropes, a capability not observed in GRPO.
- **Performance Validation:** Dynamic `λ` (CGL) outperformed fixed `λ` settings, which either caused excessive entropy (λ=1) or trapped the model in local optima (λ=0.01), reinforcing the importance of dynamic weighting.

**CGL Performance Across Models of Varying Scales and Comparison with Joint Training:**
- CGL demonstrated stable performance on both the lightweight LLaVA-OneVision-0.5b and the large-scale Qwen2.5-VL-3b, validating its scalability and generality.
- On Qwen2.5-VL-3b, CGL showed not only knowledge retention but also instances of positive backward transfer (e.g., Tools accuracy improving from 86.42% to 88.17%), suggesting effective consolidation of generalizable GUI logic.
- CGL significantly narrowed the performance gap between sequential continual learning and the theoretical upper bound of Multi-Task Joint Training. CGL's average trajectory accuracy across task orders (e.g., 82.33% for Order 1) was highly competitive with the GRPO-Joint-Training baseline (82.41%), with CGL under Task Order 2 even slightly outperforming it. While SFT-Joint-Training achieved a slightly higher absolute accuracy (83.48%), it did so under the impractical assumption of full data access.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research significantly contributes to the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of autonomous agents and continual learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Critical Real-World Problem:** The work directly confronts the challenge of catastrophic forgetting in GUI agents operating in dynamic environments. As software applications undergo continuous updates, existing static training paradigms render GUI agents quickly obsolete. CGL offers a solution that enables agents to adapt to new interfaces and functionalities without losing proficiency in previously learned tasks, which is crucial for their practical deployment and long-term utility.
- **Novel Hybrid Continual Learning Framework:** CGL is positioned as the first framework to systematically analyze and integrate Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for continual GUI learning. By leveraging the complementary strengths of SFT (plasticity/fast adaptation) and RL (stability/knowledge retention), the framework provides a balanced approach to the stability-plasticity dilemma, a fundamental problem in continual learning.
- **Methodological Innovations:** The proposed components—Error-Aware Routing, Entropy-Regulated Tuning, and Conditional Gradient Surgery—represent specific advancements in balancing different learning objectives. Error-Aware Routing intelligently directs learning towards SFT when RL signals are uninformative, improving adaptation efficiency. Entropy-Regulated Tuning dynamically controls the influence of SFT based on policy uncertainty, optimizing the exploration-exploitation trade-off. Gradient Surgery explicitly resolves conflicts between SFT and GRPO gradients, preventing detrimental knowledge interference. These mechanisms offer insights that could be generalizable to other continual learning contexts involving hybrid optimization.
- **Standardized Benchmark for Continual GUI Learning:** The introduction of AndroidControl-CL fills a crucial gap in the research landscape. By partitioning a real-world dataset into functionally distinct, sequential tasks with detailed annotations, it provides a realistic and rigorous platform for evaluating continual GUI learning. This benchmark facilitates comparable research, accelerates development, and allows for a more systematic understanding of agent evolution under distribution shifts.
- **Enhanced Agent Robustness and Deployability:** By achieving high accuracy on new tasks while largely mitigating forgetting of old tasks (evidenced by near-zero or even positive Forgetting Measure), CGL contributes to the development of more robust and reliable GUI agents. Such agents could be deployed in enterprise settings for automation, in personal assistant roles, or for accessibility, reducing the need for frequent retraining or manual intervention.
- **Theoretical Insights into Entropy Dynamics:** The theoretical analysis of how SFT and GRPO influence policy entropy provides a deeper understanding of their respective contributions to exploration and convergence. This can inform the design of future continual learning algorithms that require precise control over policy uncertainty.
- **Open-Sourced Resources:** The commitment to making the benchmark, code, and model publicly available will foster collaboration, accelerate progress, and lower the barrier to entry for researchers in this emerging field.

In summary, this work offers a significant step towards enabling GUI agents to operate autonomously and adaptively in ever-changing digital environments, with potential implications for automation, user experience, and the broader field of continual artificial intelligence.
