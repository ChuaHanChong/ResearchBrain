# Glance and Focus Reinforcement for Pan-cancer Screening

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology developed GF-Screen, a Glance and Focus Reinforcement Learning framework, to enhance pan-cancer screening in CT scans. This method significantly boosts accuracy, reduces false positives, and improves computational efficiency by over 5 times, currently leading the MICCAI FLARE25 challenge.

## Problem

- AI models for pan-cancer screening in CT scans struggle with extreme foreground-background imbalance, making it difficult to detect tiny lesions amidst vast healthy tissue.
- Existing methods suffer from computational inefficiency due to processing entire CT volumes and generate high false positive rates, limiting clinical applicability.

## Method

- A two-stage "**Glance and Focus**" Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework, where a ==Glance model== coarsely localizes suspicious sub-volumes, and a Focus model then performs precise segmentation.
- The Glance model learns to make non-differentiable sub-volume selection decisions using a novel Group Relative Learning (GRL) paradigm, which estimates relative advantages within sub-volume groups.
- A binary reward function is employed for the RL agent, prioritizing lesion presence detection over continuous segmentation metrics to guide the Glance model effectively.

## Results

- GF-Screen achieved a **60.8%** Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) on internal datasets and **54.1%** DSC on external datasets, outperforming other methods by up to **5.1%** DSC.
- The system reduced computational inference time by 5.7 times to 28 seconds per scan and lowered false positive rates to an average of **15.6%** on healthy datasets.
- GF-Screen currently leads the public validation leaderboard for the MICCAI FLARE25 pan-cancer challenge, showing a +**25.6%** DSC and +**28.2%** Normalized Surface Distance (NSD) improvement over the previous year's champion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Emulating the human radiologist's "glance and focus" diagnostic workflow through an AI framework provides an effective strategy for handling vast, imbalanced medical images.
> - Reinforcement Learning, specifically Group Relative Learning, can be successfully adapted to medical vision tasks for non-differentiable decision-making, such as selecting relevant image sub-volumes.
> - A binary reward system for the Glance model, prioritizing the detection of lesion presence, proved more effective than continuous segmentation metrics in guiding the RL agent for highly imbalanced pan-cancer data.
