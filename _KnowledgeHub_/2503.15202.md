# A Unified Framework for Real-Time Failure Handling in Robotics Using Vision-Language Models, Reactive Planner and Behavior Trees

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Lund University and ABB Robotics developed a unified framework that combines Vision-Language Models, reactive planning, and Behavior Trees to achieve real-time robotic failure handling through both proactive verification and dynamic execution monitoring. The system demonstrated a 100% task success rate on a physical ABB YuMi robot for various manipulation tasks, including peg-in-hole and object sorting, by effectively detecting, identifying, and correcting failures as they occur or before execution.

## Problem

- Robotic systems face frequent execution failures in dynamic environments due to unforeseen circumstances, limiting their autonomy and reliability.
- Traditional predefined failure recovery strategies lack adaptability to novel or unexpected failure modes.
- Modern learning-based methods often lack interpretability and verifiability, making them unsuitable for safety-critical robotic applications, and struggle with data requirements and generalization.

## Method

- A unified framework integrating ==Vision-Language Models== (VLMs) for context-aware reasoning, a reactive planner for dynamic Behavior Tree (BT) generation, and BTs for structured execution policies.
- Employs a three-phase failure handling process (detection, identification, correction) consistently across pre-execution verification and real-time execution monitoring.
- Leverages dynamically updated ==scene graphs== and execution history to maintain real-time situational awareness and enable adaptive BT modifications, including skill suggestions.

## Results

- The framework achieved a **100%** task success rate on the REFLECT benchmark in AI2-THOR simulations and across multiple real-world manipulation tasks with an ABB YuMi robot.
- Demonstrated **100%** success rates in failure detection, identification, and correction during real-world experiments, significantly outperforming pre-execution only approaches (**31.25%** task success).
- Proactive pre-execution checks reduced computational overhead and execution time compared to purely reactive methods by preventing foreseeable failures, while still enabling full real-time adaptability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A synergistic combination of pre-execution plan verification and real-time reactive failure monitoring is essential for robust and efficient robotic autonomy in dynamic environments.
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) effectively bridge high-level reasoning with low-level control, enabling accurate context-aware failure detection, identification of root causes, and suggestion of corrective actions.
> - Behavior Trees offer a modular, interpretable, and reactive policy representation that facilitates dynamic adaptation and incremental recovery, avoiding computationally expensive full replanning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Traditional and Learning-Based Approaches

Robotic systems, particularly in dynamic environments like small-batch manufacturing or collaborative assembly lines, frequently encounter execution failures due to unforeseen circumstances such as obstacles, sensor errors, or environmental changes. Traditionally, failure recovery methods have relied on:
- **Human intervention:** Flexible but labor-intensive and not scalable.
- **Predefined strategies:** Effective for known failure modes but lack adaptability to novel issues.
- **Automated solutions based on failure mode analysis (e.g., FMEA):** Require expert knowledge and struggle with dynamic environments.
More recently, **learning-based approaches** using reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL) have emerged. While promising for diverse tasks, these often suffer from:
- **Lack of interpretability and verifiability:** A critical drawback in safety-critical domains.
- **Extensive data requirements:** Making real-world deployment challenging.
- **Generalization issues:** For instance, IL-based methods like RACER struggle to generalize beyond demonstrated scenarios.
**Neuro-symbolic methods** attempt to combine the strengths of structured reasoning with learning, offering improved interpretability but still facing scalability challenges.

#### 1.2. Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in Robotics

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has significantly impacted robotic failure recovery due to their powerful reasoning and contextual understanding capabilities. Several approaches leverage these models:
- **REFLECT:** Provides hierarchical post-execution summaries for failure explanation and correction, but lacks real-time intervention.
- **AHA:** Fine-tunes a VLM for failure detection at task checkpoints but without structured execution policies.
- **DoReMi:** Enforces dynamic execution constraints but relies on LLM-generated constraints, which can introduce variability.
- **ReplanVLM:** Integrates pre-execution validation with execution monitoring using GPT-4V, but its reliance on LLM-driven replanning can be computationally intensive and may lack the guarantees of structured methods.
- **RECOVER:** A neuro-symbolic framework for real-time failure detection and recovery, but often requires domain-specific engineering.
- **Code-as-Monitor:** Translates natural language constraints into executable monitors for proactive and reactive failure detection but lacks explicit recovery mechanisms.

#### 1.3. Position of This Work

This paper strategically positions itself to address the limitations of existing methods by combining the strengths of:
- **Structured execution frameworks (Behavior Trees - BTs):** Providing modularity, interpretability, and efficient incremental recovery.
- **Reactive planners:** Automating BT generation and adaptation in real-time without full replanning.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Leveraging their reasoning capabilities for context-aware failure detection, identification, and correction.

Crucially, this work extends its authors' prior research, which focused solely on VLM-based pre-execution plan verification, by introducing **real-time execution monitoring and reactive failure handling**. Unlike many LLM/VLM-based approaches that are post-execution (REFLECT), lack structured policies (AHA, ReplanVLM), or require domain-specific engineering (RECOVER), this framework provides a unified, real-time, and structured approach. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level reasoning and low-level reactive control, ensuring robust and interpretable failure recovery that is both proactive (pre-execution) and dynamic (real-time). The continuous updating of scene graphs and execution history, rather than full regeneration or post-execution analysis, is also a key differentiator.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges faced by modern robotic systems in dynamic and unstructured environments, particularly in handling unexpected execution failures. While robots excel in controlled, repetitive tasks, their applicability in flexible scenarios like small-batch manufacturing or collaborative human-robot environments is limited by their susceptibility to failures (e.g., unexpected obstacles, sensor errors, misaligned objects). Such failures cause costly delays and reduce system reliability.

The paper identifies the following key limitations in existing failure recovery methods:
- **Lack of adaptability:** Traditional predefined strategies cannot cope with novel or unforeseen failure modes.
- **Poor interpretability and verifiability:** Learning-based end-to-end approaches, while effective, often lack transparency, making them unsuitable for safety-critical applications.
- **Absence of real-time intervention:** Many advanced LLM/VLM-based methods primarily perform post-execution analysis or high-level planning without direct, real-time corrective actions during execution.
- **Incomplete failure coverage:** Pre-execution checks can prevent many errors but cannot account for dynamic, runtime disturbances.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of this paper are to:
1. **Develop a unified failure recovery framework:** Integrating VLMs for advanced reasoning, a reactive planner for dynamic BT generation, and Behavior Trees for structured execution policies.
2. **Enable real-time failure handling:** Combining both pre-execution verification (to prevent errors proactively) and continuous real-time execution monitoring (to detect and correct failures as they occur).
3. **Enhance situational awareness and adaptability:** Through the incorporation of incrementally updated scene graphs (for object-robot relationships) and execution history (for tracking skill states and environmental changes).
4. **Support dynamic BT adaptation:** By verifying existing conditions, inferring and adding missing preconditions, and generating new skills when necessary.
5. **Validate the framework's effectiveness:** Through real-world experiments on a physical robot and comparative evaluations in simulation, demonstrating improved task success rates and greater adaptability.

Ultimately, the overarching goal is to significantly improve the reliability and autonomy of robotic systems by empowering them to detect, identify, and correct execution failures without human intervention, thereby reducing downtime and enhancing performance in complex, dynamic operational settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs), a reactive planner, and Behavior Trees (BTs) into a unified system for real-time robotic failure handling. The approach is structured into two main phases: pre-execution verification and real-time execution monitoring, each designed to detect, identify, and correct failures proactively or reactively.

#### 3.1. Core Components: VLMs, Reactive Planner, and Behavior Trees

- **Behavior Trees (BTs):** Serve as the hierarchical execution model, offering modularity, flexibility, and reactivity. BTs represent robot policies as compositions of reusable skills, facilitating fine-grained monitoring and interpretable decision-making. Their structure supports incremental recovery, avoiding costly full replanning.
- **Reactive Planner:** Dynamically generates and adapts BTs using a backchaining approach, selecting skills based on predefined preconditions and postconditions. This allows the robot to construct policies that adapt to environmental changes in real-time, ensuring efficiency by optimizing the BT structure.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Specifically GPT-4o, are central to the framework's reasoning capabilities. They combine visual perception with language understanding to analyze execution conditions and task states, providing corrective suggestions. VLMs are used for failure detection, identification of root causes (e.g., missing preconditions), and correction (e.g., suggesting new preconditions or skills).

#### 3.2. Failure Handling Process: Detection, Identification, and Correction

All failure handling mechanisms follow a three-phase process, inspired by chain-of-thought reasoning, to guide the VLM towards effective solutions:
1. **Detection:** Identify potential failures or anomalies where the planned or executing BT might fail.
2. **Identification:** Pinpoint the exact failing skill and the root cause, such as a missing precondition, an incorrect assumption, or a violated postcondition.
3. **Correction:** Suggest modifications to the BT, such as adding missing preconditions, updating existing conditions, or introducing new skills, to ensure successful execution.

#### 3.3. Pre-Execution Failure Verification

This phase, building on the authors' prior work, validates the entire planned BT before execution commences. The VLM proactively checks for potential failures based on initial images, scene graphs, skills, and conditions.
- **Detection:** The VLM analyzes the planned BT and current scene to flag anomalies (e.g., an obstacle blocking a target location).
- **Identification:** It diagnoses the failing skill and the root cause (e.g., `place` skill will fail because `hole` is blocked).
- **Correction:** It suggests modifications, like adding a missing precondition (`~occupied(hole)` for `place`), prompting the reactive planner to incorporate a preceding action (e.g., `remove black object`).

#### 3.4. Real-Time Failure Monitoring (Verifier and Suggestor)

To address failures that emerge during execution due to unforeseen disturbances, this module continuously monitors the robot's actions. It incorporates dynamically updated scene graphs, images, and execution history.

1. **Verifier:** Ensures alignment with expected conditions by performing checks:
 - **Precondition Verification:** Before executing a skill, the Verifier checks if its preconditions hold. If unmet (e.g., robot already holding an object when `grasp` skill requires an empty gripper), it returns `Failure`, triggering the reactive planner to adapt the BT (e.g., add a `place` skill to empty the gripper).
 - **Postcondition Verification:** After a skill execution, it checks if expected postconditions are met. If violated (e.g., object placed *on* the hole instead of *inside*), it returns `Failure`, prompting the BT to reattempt the skill.

2. **Suggestor:** Infers and proposes missing preconditions dynamically when a skill fails due to an unmet condition not explicitly part of its original definition.
 - **Detection:** Flags an anomaly if a skill is likely to fail (e.g., `place` skill on an already occupied hole).
 - **Identification:** Identifies the missing precondition (e.g., `~occupied(hole)` for `place`).
 - **Correction:** Suggests the missing precondition, allowing the reactive planner to update the BT (e.g., dynamically insert a sub-tree to clear the hole).

#### 3.5. Skill Addition

When modifying preconditions is insufficient, new skills may be required. The VLM can suggest these either pre-execution or reactively:
- **Detection:** Identifies situations where existing skills cannot resolve a detected failure (e.g., an object is too large to `grasp`).
- **Identification:** Determines the missing capability and the skill that fails (e.g., `pick` skill fails because the object is non-pickable).
- **Correction:** The VLM suggests a new skill (e.g., `push` skill), providing its name, a code template, and predefined preconditions/postconditions, ensuring compatibility with the robot's world model.

#### 3.6. Supporting Mechanisms: Scene Graph and Execution History

- **Scene Graph Representation:** An evolving scene graph tracks object-object and robot-object spatial relationships in real-time. Unlike methods that regenerate the graph, this framework continuously updates it (modifying relations, adding/removing nodes) using RGB-D images, point clouds, object detection (Grounding DINO), instance segmentation (SAM2), and 6D pose estimation. This ensures efficiency and immediate situational awareness.
- **Execution History:** Maintains a log of skill executions, precondition/postcondition verification results, and scene graph updates. Instead of explicit failure logging, it captures execution traces and changes in the scene graph to infer failures and inconsistencies. This structured history enables dynamic adaptation by detecting anomalies and refining failure handling based on observed task progression.

Inputs to the system consistently include images (front and side views), skills with conditions, known environmental conditions, the scene graph, the BT, and (for real-time) the execution history.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework's effectiveness was rigorously evaluated through both simulation benchmarks and real-world experiments, with ablation studies to assess the contribution of individual components.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (AI2-THOR)

- **Benchmark:** The framework was tested on REFLECT benchmark tasks in the AI2-THOR simulator.
- **Key Distinction from REFLECT:** REFLECT performs post-execution failure correction using hierarchical summaries and scene graph regeneration, lacking real-time adaptation. This framework, however, uses a reactive planner and BTs for dynamic policy generation and immediate, real-time failure correction. Its scene graph is continuously updated, not fully reconstructed. Furthermore, while REFLECT relies on LLM-generated corrections without guarantees, this approach leverages the structured preconditions/postconditions of the reactive planner for correctness.
- **Results:** The framework achieved a **100% success rate** across multiple runs on all REFLECT benchmark tasks. Real-time monitoring proved sufficient, negating the need for pre-execution checks in this specific simulation environment. Failures were proactively verified and reactively corrected during execution, eliminating the need for post-execution replanning and retrospective reasoning.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments (ABB YuMi Robot)

- **Setup:** An ABB YuMi robot with an RGB-D camera was used for real-world validation.
- **Tasks:** Three diverse manipulation tasks were assessed: Peg-in-hole, Object Sorting (by color), and Drawer Placement.
- **Failure Induction:** Failures were introduced by modifying object placements, adding obstructions, altering task constraints, and human intervention during execution.
- **Baseline Comparisons:** The proposed approach (Pre-execution + Reactive) was compared against two baselines:
 - **Pre-execution:** Only performed plan verification before execution.
 - **Reactive:** Only detected and corrected failures during execution.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Task Success Rate, Failure Detection Rate, Failure Identification Rate, Correction Success Rate, and Skill Suggestion Accuracy. Each experiment was repeated 10 times across 16 pre-recorded failure cases, with additional runs without induced failures to check for false positives.
- **Quantitative Results (Table II):**
 - **Task Success Rate:** Pre-execution + Reactive (Ours) achieved 100%, matching the Reactive-only baseline. Pre-execution-only performed significantly worse at 31.25%.
 - **Failure Detection/Identification/Correction Success Rates:** Ours, Reactive, and Pre-execution all achieved 100% for identification and correction, and Ours and Reactive achieved 100% for detection.
 - **Skill Suggestion Accuracy:** Ours and Reactive achieved 100%, while Pre-execution achieved 50%.
 - **Efficiency:** Although Reactive achieved similar task success rates as Ours, it was "significantly more expensive" due to increased VLM queries, additional skill executions, and longer overall execution times caused by not preventing failures proactively. Our unified approach resolves pre-execution failures early, reducing computational and execution overhead.
 - **False Positives:** No false positives were observed when tasks were run without induced failures.
- **Summary:** The unified framework (Pre-execution + Reactive) consistently demonstrated the highest performance, achieving 100% success across all metrics. This highlights the synergistic benefit of combining proactive and reactive mechanisms. While reactive handling is powerful, pre-execution checks are crucial for efficiency by preventing foreseeable issues and minimizing costly real-time interventions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Summary

To understand the contribution of individual components, ablation studies were conducted:
- **VLM vs. LLM (Vision Input):** Removing vision input (effectively degrading VLM to LLM functionality or reducing visual context) weakened spatial and scene-aware failure detection. Task success dropped from 100% (with two images) to 98% (with one image) and 95% (with no images), assuming perfect scene graph accuracy. This underscores the critical role of rich visual perception in VLM-based reasoning.
- **Scene Graph Contribution:** Without the scene graph, task success dropped to 91.25%. This confirms its importance in providing structured spatial reasoning, resolving scene ambiguity, and enabling effective failure prediction.
- **Execution History Effectiveness:** Omitting execution history tracking did not significantly impact results in the evaluated benchmarks. The authors suggest that its benefits might become more evident in more complex, longer-horizon tasks where tracking subtle deviations over time is crucial.

**Overall Findings:** The experimental results strongly validate the effectiveness of the unified framework. The combination of pre-execution and real-time reactive failure handling significantly improves task success and adaptability. VLM-based reasoning, especially when empowered by detailed visual input and a structured scene graph, is critical for accurate failure detection, identification, and correction. The approach demonstrates a robust and reliable method for autonomous failure recovery in diverse robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Key Contributions

The paper explicitly lists three key contributions:
1. **A unified failure recovery framework:** It successfully integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs), reactive planners, and Behavior Trees (BTs) for both pre-execution failure verification and real-time reactive failure handling. This is a significant advancement over methods that focus only on one aspect or lack structured execution.
2. **Real-time failure detection, identification, and correction:** Achieved through an incrementally updated execution history that tracks skill conditions, execution timestamps, and scene graph updates. This enables continuous, adaptive recovery, minimizing execution disruptions.
3. **Experimental validation in diverse environments:** The framework was thoroughly validated in both the AI2-THOR simulator and on a real-world ABB YuMi robot, demonstrating its superior performance in improving failure recovery across varied tasks and conditions.

#### 5.2. Broader Implications

This unified framework represents a crucial step towards developing more robust, autonomous, and adaptable robotic systems. Its potential impact is significant for several domains:
- **Increased Reliability and Uptime:** By enabling robots to autonomously detect and correct failures in real-time, the framework can drastically reduce downtime, maintenance costs, and the need for human intervention in industrial and service robotics.
- **Enhanced Adaptability in Dynamic Environments:** Robots can now operate more effectively in unpredictable settings like small-batch manufacturing, logistics, or collaborative workspaces where environmental changes and unexpected events are common.
- **Improved Safety in Human-Robot Interaction:** In collaborative assembly lines, real-time failure handling is paramount for safe and efficient operation. This framework contributes to safer human-robot co-existence by allowing robots to react intelligently to disturbances.
- **Bridging the Gap between High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control:** By combining the powerful reasoning of VLMs with the structured, reactive execution of BTs and planners, the framework offers a more complete solution for intelligent robotic autonomy.
- **Interpretability and Verifiability:** The use of Behavior Trees provides an interpretable execution policy, which is critical for debugging, validation, and meeting safety standards in sensitive applications, unlike opaque end-to-end learning methods.

#### 5.3. Future Directions

The authors outline several promising avenues for future work, aiming to further enhance the framework's capabilities:
- **Multimodal Sensor Integration:** Incorporating video and audio inputs for richer context-aware task monitoring could provide deeper situational understanding beyond static images.
- **Open-Source Model Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning open-source multimodal models for failure handling would reduce computational costs and improve efficiency, making the framework more accessible and deployable.
- **Autonomous Skill Generation with VLA Models:** Leveraging Vision-Language Action (VLA) models for autonomous skill generation, complete with structured preconditions and postconditions, would further expand the robot's capabilities to handle entirely novel situations. Ensuring quality through static and integration checks for these generated skills is also a key consideration.
- **Proactive "Holding Conditions" for Real-Time Monitoring:** Integrating "holding conditions" for proactive failure checks during execution could enable the system to anticipate and prevent failures even before they fully manifest, making recovery even more seamless.

These future enhancements suggest a continuous drive towards increasingly autonomous, adaptive, and efficient robotic systems, capable of robustly navigating the complexities of real-world environments.
