# RepSAM: Bridging Foundation Models to Robotic Vision via Representation-Guided Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Texas A&M University developed RepSAM, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework that adapts large foundation models like SAM for robotic vision, incorporating representation-guided layer-wise adaptation, depth-aware fusion, and edge-enhanced loss. It achieves 89.0% mIoU on robotic benchmarks, representing 97.9% of full fine-tuning performance with 158 times fewer trainable parameters, and demonstrates improved grasp success rates in robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Foundation models like SAM exhibit substantial performance degradation when directly applied to unstructured robotic environments due to domain shift, often failing with transparent objects, cluttered scenes, and sensor noise.
- Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods typically allocate adaptation capacity uniformly across model layers, which is sub-optimal given non-uniform representation shifts between source and target domains.
- Bridging the gap between improved perception metrics and actual downstream robotic task performance (e.g., grasping success) remains a challenge, with many adaptations not demonstrating real-world utility.

## Method

- **RepSAM** introduces a CKA-guided multi-scale ==LoRA== strategy that allocates adaptation capacity (LoRA ranks) proportionally to the empirically measured layer-wise representation shift between natural and robotic images, assigning higher ranks to shallow layers with greater domain gaps.
- It integrates a depth-aware fusion module to effectively handle transparent or reflective objects by leveraging geometric cues, and incorporates an edge-enhanced loss function to improve precise object boundary segmentation in cluttered scenes.
- The approach provides theoretical justification that layers with greater representation misalignment require higher adaptation capacity to achieve comparable approximation quality, supporting the CKA-guided rank allocation.

## Results

- RepSAM achieved an average mIoU of **89.0%** across six robotic benchmarks, matching **97.9%** of the mIoU from full fine-tuning (**90.9%**) while using **158x** fewer trainable parameters and **96x** less training compute.
- It outperformed other PEFT baselines by a large margin, demonstrating a **7.7%** to **9.7%** absolute mIoU improvement over strong competitors like VeRA, DoRA, and AdaLoRA.
- In simulated robotic manipulation, RepSAM improved grasp success rates to **93.2%** on standard objects and **82.1%** on transparent objects, also reducing collision rates by **8.8%** and slip rates by **7.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformer layers in foundation models exhibit non-uniform domain shifts: shallow layers show significant domain gaps (low CKA), while deeper layers demonstrate strong transferability (high CKA) between natural and robotic images.
> - Allocating adaptation capacity based on layer-wise representation similarity substantially improves efficiency and performance over uniform allocation strategies, especially for low-level feature adaptation.
> - Combining this representation-guided adaptation with domain-specific enhancements (depth fusion and edge-enhanced loss) effectively addresses unique robotic vision challenges and translates directly to improved downstream robotic manipulation task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses the challenge of deploying large foundation models, such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM), in specialized domains like robotic vision. While SAM demonstrates considerable zero-shot generalization capabilities on natural images, its performance degrades substantially in unstructured robotic environments, which often feature transparent objects, cluttered scenes, and sensor noise. This degradation highlights a domain adaptation problem.

The broader research landscape for adapting large models includes Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods, such as LoRA, DoRA, VeRA, and GaLore. These methods aim to reduce the computational cost and parameter footprint of adapting foundation models by fine-tuning only a small subset of parameters. However, the paper identifies a limitation in many existing PEFT approaches: they typically allocate adaptation capacity uniformly across all layers of the pre-trained model.

This work diverges by proposing that this uniform allocation is sub-optimal. Through systematic analysis, it reveals that different layers of a transformer-based foundation model exhibit non-uniform representation shifts between source (natural images) and target (robotic images) domains. Shallow layers often show substantial domain gaps, whereas deeper layers transfer more effectively. This observation challenges the premise of uniform adaptation.

Some recent PEFT methods, such as AdaLoRA and La-LoRA, have explored dynamic rank allocation, where rank importance is learned during optimization. However, this paper positions its approach, RepSAM, as an alternative that determines rank allocation prior to training using representation similarity. This design aims to offer computational efficiency and enhanced interpretability compared to optimization-driven methods, by grounding allocations in intrinsic domain properties rather than optimization dynamics.

Furthermore, while foundation models like SAM have been adapted to other domains such as medical imaging (MedSAM) and remote sensing (RSPrompter), their application to robotic perception with layer-wise adaptation strategies has been limited. Existing efforts in robotic adaptation, such as SAM-Adapter and SAM-DSA, have largely employed uniform adaptation strategies. This research contributes to bridging this gap by demonstrating that a representation-aware adaptation strategy can unlock substantial performance improvements for robotic perception tasks. The work also emphasizes evaluating performance in a complete robotic manipulation pipeline using simulation, addressing a methodological gap where many computer vision publications report improved metrics without demonstrating their translation to downstream task performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) framework, named RepSAM, that effectively adapts foundation models like SAM to the demanding requirements of robotic vision. The framework aims to overcome the significant performance degradation observed when these models are directly applied to unstructured robotic environments, which frequently involve challenges such as transparent objects, highly cluttered scenes, and sensor noise.

The core motivation for RepSAM stems from a detailed analysis of representation similarity across the transformer layers of SAM. Using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), the authors empirically observed a non-uniform pattern of domain shift:
- **Shallow Layers:** These layers exhibited substantial domain gaps (CKA values below 0.5) when comparing representations of natural images (SAM’s pre-training distribution) with robotic images. This indicates that low-level features encoded in these layers differ fundamentally across domains.
- **Deep Layers:** In contrast, deeper layers demonstrated strong transferability (CKA values above 0.7), suggesting that their high-level representations are largely domain-invariant. Middle layers showed moderate similarity (CKA around 0.6).

This observation served as the foundational motivation to challenge the conventional approach of allocating uniform adaptation capacity across all layers in existing PEFT methods. The authors posited that such uniform allocation might be inefficient or sub-optimal, as layers with greater domain discrepancies (e.g., shallow layers) likely require more adaptation capacity to bridge the gap, while layers with strong transferability (e.g., deep layers) may require less.

Therefore, the key objectives of RepSAM were to:
1. **Develop a Representation-Guided Rank Allocation Strategy:** Propose a method to allocate LoRA ranks based on the measured layer-wise representation shift, specifically using CKA to quantify domain gaps. This strategy aimed to be determined prior to training, offering a principled and interpretable alternative to optimization-based rank learning.
2. **Enhance Robustness in Challenging Robotic Scenarios:** Integrate additional components to address specific difficulties in robotic vision, such as a depth-aware fusion module for transparent or reflective objects and an edge-enhanced loss function for precise boundary segmentation in cluttered environments.
3. **Achieve High Performance with Parameter and Computational Efficiency:** Demonstrate that the proposed RepSAM framework can achieve performance comparable to full fine-tuning while significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters and training computational cost.
4. **Translate Perceptual Gains to Robotic Task Performance:** Validate that improvements in segmentation metrics translate into tangible benefits for downstream robotic manipulation tasks, such as increased grasping success rates and improved safety metrics in simulated environments.
5. **Provide Theoretical Justification:** Offer a theoretical framework to support the empirical finding that layers with greater representation misalignment require higher adaptation capacity.

By addressing these objectives, RepSAM aimed to create a robust, efficient, and interpretable method for adapting powerful foundation models to the nuances of robotic perception, ultimately facilitating their practical deployment in real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RepSAM implements a methodology grounded in the principle of allocating adaptation capacity proportionally to the observed layer-wise representation shift between source and target domains. This approach integrates three main components: CKA-guided multi-scale LoRA, depth-aware fusion, and edge-enhanced loss, supported by theoretical justification.

#### 3.1. CKA-Guided Multi-Scale LoRA

The core of RepSAM is its representation-guided parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy.
- **Representation Similarity Quantification:** Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) is employed to measure the similarity between feature spaces, providing a metric independent of specific linear transformations. CKA is calculated using Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) on Gram matrices derived from feature representations.
- **Domain Gap Analysis:** The authors performed a one-time CKA analysis by sampling 500 images from SAM’s pre-training distribution (SA-1B) and 500 from each target robotic dataset. Activations were extracted from all 32 transformer layers of SAM’s ViT-H encoder. This analysis, requiring approximately 30 minutes on an A100 GPU, revealed consistent patterns of transferability across layers and datasets.
- **Three Transferability Regimes:** The 32 transformer layers were categorized into three distinct regimes based on their CKA values:
 - **Shallow Layers (L1-10):** Exhibited substantial domain gaps with CKA ranging from 0.35 to 0.41. These layers were assigned a LoRA rank of `r = 16`.
 - **Middle Layers (L11-22):** Showed moderate similarity with CKA values between 0.58 and 0.63. These layers were assigned a LoRA rank of `r = 8`.
 - **Deep Layers (L23-32):** Demonstrated strong transferability with CKA values between 0.79 and 0.83. These layers were assigned a LoRA rank of `r = 4`.
- **LoRA Application:** LoRA modules are applied to the Query (Q), Key (K), Value (V), and Output (O) projections within the self-attention modules of all 32 transformer layers of the frozen ViT-H encoder. The total trainable parameters from CKA-guided LoRA modules amounted to 2.6M.

#### 3.2. Depth-Aware Fusion

To address the challenges posed by transparent or highly reflective objects, where RGB information alone is often insufficient, RepSAM integrates depth information.
- **Complementary Cues:** Depth data provides geometric cues that can resolve ambiguities in such scenarios.
- **Fusion Mechanism:** RGB and depth features are fused using a learned gating mechanism. The fused feature `F_fused` is calculated as `W_f [F_rgb; F_d] + g ⊙ F_d`, where `g = σ(MLP([F_rgb; F_d]))` represents learned gating weights that determine the reliance on depth features at each spatial location.
- **Depth Encoder:** A ResNet-18 model, with its early layers frozen, serves as the depth encoder. This component contributes an additional 1.2M trainable parameters to the overall model.

#### 3.3. Edge-Enhanced Loss

Precise object boundary segmentation is crucial for robotic manipulation tasks, particularly for accurate grasping and interaction.
- **Boundary Emphasis:** To improve edge accuracy, an explicit edge supervision term is incorporated into the total loss function.
- **Loss Formulation:** The total loss `L_total` is defined as `L_dice + L_bce + λL_edge`. Ground-truth boundaries are extracted using Sobel filtering with a 2-pixel width.
- **Weighting:** An empirical evaluation indicated that a weight of `λ = 0.5` for the edge loss term provided an optimal balance, improving boundary F1 score without degrading overall segmentation quality.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Justification

The empirical CKA-guided allocation is supported by a theoretical justification.
- **Theorem 1 (Representation Transfer Bound):** This theorem formalizes the intuition that the rank `r` required to bridge two feature spaces scales with their misalignment. Specifically, for source and target feature spaces with linear CKA similarity `ρ`, the rank `r` is `O(d(1 − ρ) · κ(X)κ(Y) / ϵ)`. Here, `d` is feature dimension, `κ` are condition numbers of feature covariances, and `ϵ` is a tolerance.
- **Implication:** This bound suggests that layers exhibiting lower CKA (i.e., higher `(1 − ρ)`) fundamentally require a higher rank for their representations to achieve comparable approximation quality, thus justifying the allocation of larger LoRA ranks to shallow layers with greater domain shifts.

Combining the 2.6M trainable parameters from CKA-guided LoRA modules, 1.2M from the depth encoder, and 0.2M from the fusion MLP, the total trainable parameter count for RepSAM is 4.0M, representing a small fraction of the 632M parameters of the full SAM ViT-H model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RepSAM encompassed a comprehensive experimental setup across multiple robotic benchmarks and simulation tasks, providing detailed insights into its performance, efficiency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets and Metrics:** RepSAM was evaluated on six robotic benchmarks: OCID, ClearGrasp (transparent objects), GraspNet (extreme clutter), WISDOM (warehouse scenarios), YCB-Video (dynamic manipulation), and LINEMOD (texture-less objects). The primary metric was mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) across semantic object classes (excluding background), with Boundary F1 score used for edge accuracy.
- **Baselines:** Comparisons included zero-shot and fully fine-tuned foundation models (SAM ViT-H, SAM 2-L), lightweight models (EfficientSAM, MobileSAM, FastSAM, HQ-SAM), and various PEFT methods (LoRA, AdaLoRA, DoRA, VeRA, SAM-Adapter, SAM-DSA).
- **Implementation:** Training utilized PyTorch 2.1 on A100 GPUs, AdamW optimizer (LR 10^-4), 50 epochs, batch size 8, and 1024x1024 resolution. All RepSAM results were averaged over 5 independent runs.

#### 4.2. Main Results

RepSAM demonstrated significant advancements across several fronts:
- **Efficiency and Accuracy Trade-off:** RepSAM achieved an average mIoU of 89.0% across the six benchmarks. This performance represents 97.9% of the mIoU obtained by full fine-tuning of SAM ViT-H (90.9%). Crucially, RepSAM accomplished this with only 4.0M trainable parameters, which is a 158x reduction compared to the 632M parameters required for full fine-tuning.
- **Outperformance of PEFT Baselines:** Among parameter-efficient methods with similar trainable parameter counts (≤5M), RepSAM substantially outperformed existing strong PEFT baselines. For instance, it achieved an improvement of 7.7% to 7.9% absolute mIoU over VeRA (81.3%) and DoRA (81.1%), respectively. The improvement over AdaLoRA was 9.7% mIoU and over SAM-DSA was 16.8% mIoU.
- **Performance in Challenging Scenarios:** RepSAM exhibited particularly strong performance on challenging datasets. On ClearGrasp, which focuses on transparent objects, it achieved 90.1% mIoU. This marks a 55.4 absolute percentage point improvement over SAM’s zero-shot performance of 34.7%, indicating the effectiveness of concentrated adaptation capacity in shallow layers for handling difficult low-level features.

#### 4.3. Statistical Significance Analysis

Paired t-tests conducted over 5 independent runs showed that all comparisons (RepSAM vs. Full FT, RepSAM vs. DoRA, RepSAM vs. SAM-DSA) were statistically significant at p < 0.01, with 95% confidence intervals excluding zero, thereby confirming the robustness of the reported performance gains.

#### 4.4. Failure Mode Analysis

Systematic analysis of 500 failure cases identified primary patterns: extreme occlusion (35%), specular reflections from mirror-like surfaces (40%), and novel geometries not seen during training (15%). Other factors like sensor noise and motion blur accounted for the remaining 10%.

#### 4.5. Contribution Attribution Analysis

Ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contributions of RepSAM's components:
- **CKA-Guided Rank Contribution:** Using only RGB input and CKA-guided rank allocation, RepSAM (CKA-rank) achieved 85.5% mIoU, outperforming DoRA (81.1%) by 4.4% mIoU. This confirmed that the representation-guided rank assignment is a significant contributor to the overall performance. Layer-resolved analysis indicated that shallow layers accounted for approximately 60% of the mIoU improvement, middle layers for 30%, and deep layers for 10%.
- **Ablation Study of Components:** Replacing CKA-guided allocation with a uniform rank 8 scheme resulted in a 7.2% mIoU drop (from 89.0% to 81.8%), underscoring the criticality of representation-aware rank assignment. The depth fusion module contributed an incremental 2.9% mIoU, and the edge-enhanced loss contributed 1.7% mIoU.
- **Rank Allocation Strategy Comparison:** CKA-guided allocation (16-8-4) consistently outperformed uniform rank configurations (r=4, r=8, r=16) and inverted allocations (4-8-16), which significantly degraded performance (to 66.8% mIoU), validating the necessity for higher adaptation capacity in shallow layers.

#### 4.6. Efficiency Benchmarks

RepSAM achieved a 96x reduction in training compute (4 GPU-hours on a single A100 vs. 384 GPU-hours for full fine-tuning) and a 158x reduction in trainable parameters. Inference latency was practical, measured at 48ms on an A100 GPU and 63ms on a Jetson AGX Orin, supporting real-time robotic applications.

#### 4.7. Simulated Manipulation Evaluation

Evaluation in PyBullet physics simulation demonstrated the translation of perceptual improvements to robotic manipulation tasks:
- **Grasp Success Rates:** RepSAM achieved 93.2% grasp success on standard objects (compared to SAM 2-L’s 91.0%) and 82.1% on transparent objects (compared to SAM 2-L’s 68.5%).
- **Safety Metrics:** With depth fusion and edge loss, RepSAM achieved 94.4% grasp success with an 8.8% lower collision rate and 7.6% lower slip rate compared to the LoRA (RGB) baseline, highlighting improved safety.
- **Cross-Dataset Transfer:** Training on OCID yielded 82.3% mIoU on ClearGrasp, demonstrating the transferability of learned representations across diverse robotic scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) framework, RepSAM, that addresses critical challenges in adapting large foundation models to robotic vision. The findings hold several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Principled Adaptation for Foundation Models:** RepSAM introduces a novel, theoretically grounded approach for allocating adaptation capacity in PEFT. By leveraging layer-wise representation similarity quantified by CKA, the framework moves beyond uniform or purely optimization-driven rank allocation strategies. This provides a more interpretable and efficient method for fine-tuning, reflecting intrinsic domain properties rather than just task-specific optimization dynamics. This principle could be extended to other domain adaptation tasks involving large pre-trained models.

- **Enhanced Robotic Perception Capabilities:** The work demonstrates substantial improvements in semantic segmentation performance for robotic vision tasks, particularly in challenging scenarios involving transparent objects and cluttered scenes. RepSAM's ability to achieve 90.1% mIoU on the ClearGrasp dataset, a 55.4 percentage point absolute improvement over SAM’s zero-shot performance, indicates a significant step forward in enabling robots to perceive and interact with complex real-world environments more effectively.

- **Efficiency and Accessibility in AI Deployment:** Achieving 97.9% of full fine-tuning performance with 158 times fewer trainable parameters and a 96 times reduction in training compute makes advanced robotic perception more accessible and sustainable. This efficiency is critical for deploying high-performing AI models on resource-constrained robotic hardware and in environments with limited computational infrastructure. The practical inference latencies observed on both high-end GPUs and edge devices further underscore its deployability.

- **Direct Impact on Robotic Manipulation:** The translation of improved perceptual metrics into tangible benefits for downstream robotic manipulation tasks is a key contribution. Higher grasp success rates (e.g., 94.4% success with RepSAM D+E) and improved safety metrics (8.8% fewer collisions, 7.6% fewer slips) in simulation directly demonstrate the practical value of RepSAM for developing more reliable and safer robotic systems. This bridges the gap between theoretical computer vision advancements and real-world robotics applications.

- **Generalizability of Findings:** The observed consistency of CKA patterns across different robotic datasets and in other ViT architectures (ViT-L/14, DINOv2) suggests that the proposed layer-wise adaptation strategy may be a general property of ViT transfer, indicating broader applicability beyond SAM.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper outlines several promising avenues for future work, including learning rank predictors from smaller CKA samples, exploring online adaptation methods, extending the framework to other foundational models like DINOv2 and CLIP, and validating performance on physical robotic platforms. These directions suggest continued evolution and broader adoption of representation-guided adaptation.

In summary, RepSAM offers a methodology that improves the performance, efficiency, and interpretability of adapting foundation models for robotic vision. Its capacity to enhance perception in difficult scenarios and directly improve robotic manipulation safety and success rates represents a significant contribution to the field of robotics and applied artificial intelligence.
