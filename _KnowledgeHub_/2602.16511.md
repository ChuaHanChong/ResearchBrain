# VIGOR: Visual Goal-In-Context Inference for Unified Humanoid Fall Safety

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Michigan researchers developed VIGOR, a unified, vision-conditioned policy enabling humanoid robots to safely recover from falls and stand up on diverse, non-flat terrains. This system demonstrated up to a 5x increase in recovery success rates compared to baseline methods in simulation and achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1 robot, significantly improving safety in complex real-world environments.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots are highly susceptible to falls, which can cause significant damage, hindering their deployment in unstructured and dynamic environments.
- Existing fall safety methods are often fragmented into separate modules (e.g., avoidance, mitigation, recovery) and primarily rely on proprioceptive sensing, failing to adapt to complex, non-flat terrains.
- Traditional learning-based approaches for fall safety treat environmental complexity monolithically, requiring extensive data and typically operating without visual terrain perception.

## Method

- **VIGOR** introduces a ==unified control policy== that seamlessly integrates fall mitigation, impact management, and stand-up recovery within a single framework, continuously conditioned on ==egocentric depth sensing==.
- The framework factorizes data complexity by leveraging ==sparse human pose priors== for kinematics and dynamics, combined with reinforcement learning for terrain-specific adaptations.
- A ==teacher-student distillation== approach is employed, where a privileged teacher guides an egocentric student policy that infers a ==visual goal-in-context== latent representation from visual and proprioceptive input for fast, vision-conditioned reactions.

## Results

- In simulation, VIGOR achieved **89.5%** stand-up and **90.5%** fall-recovery success rates on diverse terrains, outperforming baseline methods like HOST and FIRM by up to **5 times** (e.g., **15.2%** for HOST, **20.2%** for FIRM).
- Ablation studies confirmed that keypoint-based structural priors and the shared goal-in-context latent representation are critical for coordinated recovery, while privileged terrain information and egocentric vision specifically enhance safety.
- The VIGOR student policy demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to a real Unitree G1 robot, maintaining significantly higher safe success rates (e.g., **19/20** safe on stones vs. **13/20** for a blind policy) and preventing head collisions on complex real-world terrains and challenging fall scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling human pose structure from terrain variation allows for efficient learning from sparse human demonstrations, enabling generalization to diverse environments without monolithic data collection.
> - An integrated "visual goal-in-context latent representation" is crucial for enabling rapid, whole-body, and terrain-aware reactions, providing the necessary information for action planning without isolated subproblem solving.
> - Egocentric depth vision primarily enhances the safety and stability of fall recovery by enabling terrain-aware contact selection, rather than merely improving raw recovery rates, especially on complex and uneven surfaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are envisioned for deployment in unstructured and dynamic human environments. A significant challenge to their widespread adoption is their vulnerability to falls. Unlike robots with inherent stability (e.g., quadrupeds, wheeled robots), humanoids possess a high center of mass and complex contact dynamics, making them susceptible to falls that can lead to high-energy impacts and damage. Reliable fall safety, encompassing both mitigating the fall and recovering to an upright stance, is therefore a critical requirement for sustained humanoid operation.

Existing research on humanoid fall safety typically approaches the problem in fragmented stages or with limited perceptual capabilities. Many methods decompose fall-related behaviors into isolated subproblems:
- **Fall Avoidance:** Focuses on maintaining balance and preventing loss of stability, often through balance controllers.
- **Impact Mitigation:** Aims to reduce damage during a fall by redirecting momentum or ensuring safe landing configurations.
- **Stand-Up Recovery:** Addresses the process of returning to an upright posture after the robot has come to rest, usually triggered from a predefined set of post-impact poses.

These components are frequently developed in isolation, largely relying on proprioceptive sensing, and often implicitly assume flat, benign contact surfaces. This fragmentation neglects the intrinsic coupling between how a robot falls and its subsequent recovery options, which are heavily influenced by the surrounding terrain and dynamic contact sequences.

Furthermore, learning-based approaches, such as Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL), have attempted to address fall-related behaviors end-to-end. However, these methods often treat environmental complexity as a monolithic data problem, requiring extensive coverage of coupled kinematics, dynamics, and terrain variations. They also typically operate without visual terrain perception, which limits their ability to adapt to non-flat or cluttered environments. While human motion priors have been used to regularize learning, they often rely on dense trajectory tracking or periodic motion assumptions, which are not well-suited for the non-periodic, contact-rich, and terrain-dependent nature of fall recovery.

VIGOR addresses these limitations by introducing a unified fall safety approach that integrates fall mitigation and stand-up recovery within a single, vision-conditioned policy. It departs from the monolithic data complexity paradigm by factorizing human pose structure from terrain variation, enabling efficient learning from sparse human demonstrations. By explicitly incorporating egocentric depth perception, VIGOR allows the humanoid to reason about local terrain geometry, which is crucial for determining contact feasibility and redirecting momentum during a fall. This work also contributes to the field of visual whole-body control by applying visual sensing to the challenging domain of fall recovery, where rapid body rotations and self-occlusion present unique perceptual difficulties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reliable and unified fall safety system for humanoid robots, enabling them to operate effectively in cluttered and uneven environments where falls are probable. The specific motivations and objectives driving the VIGOR framework are detailed below:

- **Unify Fall Safety Phases:** A core motivation is to overcome the fragmentation prevalent in existing fall safety research. The authors argue that fall avoidance, impact mitigation, and stand-up recovery are intrinsically coupled, with actions in one phase significantly influencing subsequent options. Therefore, a key objective is to learn a single, unified control policy that seamlessly spans all phases of fall recovery, rather than separate, isolated modules.
- **Integrate Vision for Terrain Awareness:** Proprioceptive sensing alone is insufficient for fall recovery on complex terrain, as it cannot provide information about local geometry critical for selecting contact points or redirecting momentum. The work is motivated by the need for humanoids to perceive their surrounding terrain. An objective is to incorporate egocentric vision (depth sensing) to enable terrain-aware reactions, allowing the robot to make informed decisions about where and how to make contact during a fall and recovery.
- **Improve Scalability and Generalization through Data Factorization:** Traditional learning-based methods often treat the combination of pose, dynamics, and terrain as a monolithic data challenge, demanding exhaustive and costly data collection. The authors are motivated to develop a more efficient learning paradigm. Their objective is to factorize data complexity by leveraging two insights: 1) human fall and recovery poses are constrained and transferable from flat to complex terrain through alignment, and 2) fast whole-body reactions necessitate integrated perceptual-motor representations. This factorization aims to enable learning from sparse human demonstrations while simultaneously adapting to diverse simulated terrains.
- **Develop Integrated Perceptual-Motor Representations:** Rapid, whole-body reactions during a fall require tight coupling between perception and action. Explicitly predicting terrain or dynamics, or tracking reference trajectories, is deemed insufficient. A key objective is to build the policy upon a compact "visual goal-in-context latent representation." This latent integrates the next target pose with local terrain and body context into a single perceptual-motor space, providing the necessary information for action planning without requiring the robot to solve difficult subproblems in isolation.
- **Achieve Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** For a fall safety system to be practically useful, it must perform reliably on physical hardware. The research is motivated by the challenge of bridging the reality gap. An objective is to train a policy robustly in simulation, using domain randomization and a teacher-student distillation approach, such that it can transfer to a real humanoid robot (Unitree G1) without requiring any real-world fine-tuning.
- **Efficiently Leverage Human Demonstrations:** The authors aim to utilize human fall recovery demonstrations not as strict trajectory targets, but as sparse structural priors. This approach is intended to guide the learning process while allowing the reinforcement learning agent to resolve contact timing, body placement, and terrain-dependent execution details.

In summary, the motivation is to move beyond fragmented, blind, and data-intensive approaches to humanoid fall safety by developing a unified, vision-enabled, and data-efficient learning framework capable of robust zero-shot deployment in complex environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VIGOR employs a unified framework for humanoid fall safety that integrates fall mitigation, impact, and stand-up recovery into a single policy conditioned on egocentric depth sensing. The methodology is structured around two key insights and implemented using a privileged teacher-student learning paradigm.

**Core Insights:**
1. **Factorized Data Complexity:** The work posits that natural human fall and recovery poses are constrained and can be transferred from flat to complex terrain via alignment. This allows for the independent treatment of pose and terrain variations. Sparse human demonstrations on flat ground provide priors on kinematics and dynamics, while environmental interaction through reinforcement learning (RL) handles terrain-specific variations in geometry, contact timing, and momentum redirection.
2. **Integrated Perceptual-Motor Representations:** To facilitate fast, whole-body reactions during a fall, the system focuses on representing action goals within context. A compact "visual goal-in-context latent representation" is proposed, which integrates the next target pose with local terrain information and body context into a single perceptual-motor space. This latent is inferred from egocentric visual input and short-term proprioceptive history.

**Teacher-Student Learning Architecture (Fig. 3):**

1. **Motion Collection and Sparse Keyframe Extraction:**
 - Human fall–recovery demonstrations are collected from monocular videos recorded on flat ground (e.g., forward, sideways, backward recovery).
 - These videos are processed using the VideoMimic pipeline to reconstruct 3D human motion and fit an SMPL model.
 - The SMPL motions are then retargeted to the Unitree G1 humanoid, applying kinematics-aware mapping with conservative joint-limit constraints to maintain physical plausibility.
 - Instead of dense trajectories, a sparse set of uniformly sampled keyframes is extracted from these retargeted sequences. These keyframes serve as high-level structural priors, providing coarse temporal structure without over-constraining the recovery behavior.
 - During training, these reference keyframes are coarsely projected onto the simulated terrain using a vertical projection (e.g., shifting to ensure clearance above the terrain) and initialized with small perturbations relative to the terrain frame to enhance coverage of recovery configurations.

2. **Privileged Goal-in-Context Teacher Policy:**
 - A teacher policy ($\pi_{\theta}$) is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
 - **Two-Stage Training:** Initially trained on flat terrain for basic fall mitigation and stand-up, then continued on randomized non-flat terrains to adapt to diverse contact geometries and perturbations.
 - **Observations ($o_{teach_t}$):** The teacher receives privileged information, including proprioceptive features ($o_{prop_t}$), sparse multi-demo reference information ($o_{ref_t}$ derived from keyframes), and a privileged terrain scan ($h_t$) providing local height information.
 - **Goal-in-Context Latent ($z_{goal_t}$):** The $o_{ref_t}$ and $h_t$ signals are fused into a single $z_{goal_t}$ latent, which summarizes the immediate recovery target pose and local terrain.
 - **Action Output:** The teacher's actor conditions on $z_{goal_t}$ and $o_{prop_t}$ to produce joint-space control targets ($a_{teach_t}$).
 - **Reward Design (Table I):** A composite reward function guides the learning across different phases:
 - **Motion Imitation ($r_{imit_t}$):** Uses a DeepMimic-style tracking term. Crucially, it tracks *root-relative* link positions (comparing $(p_{ref_{i,t}} - p_{ref_{0,t}})$ with $(p_{i,t} - p_{0,t})$) rather than absolute world-frame positions. This ensures that the policy learns the relative body configuration while remaining robust to projection artifacts on uneven terrain.
 - **Regularization and Safety ($r_{reg_t}$):** Includes standard penalties for joint limit violations, joint velocities, accelerations, momentum change, action smoothness, and undesired contacts (e.g., near terrain edges).
 - **Post-Recovery Stabilization ($r_{post_t}$):** Rewards maintaining a stable upright configuration, reducing residual base motion and joint velocities.

3. **Egocentric Student Policy:**
 - The student policy is designed for deployment, operating solely on egocentric depth observations and short-term proprioceptive history, without privileged information.
 - **Observations ($o_{stud_t}$):** Consists of a short history of egocentric depth images ($I_{t:t-k}$) and corresponding proprioceptive signals ($o_{prop_{t:t-k}}$).
 - **Feature Encoders:** A compact CNN processes stacked depth images into a visual feature ($f_{img_t}$), and a small temporal convolutional module summarizes the proprioceptive history ($f_{hist_t}$).
 - **Predicted Goal Latent ($\tilde{z}_{goal_t}$):** These features are fused to predict the teacher's goal-in-context latent, $\tilde{z}_{goal_t}$.
 - **Action Output:** The student actor takes $\tilde{z}_{goal_t}$, current proprioception ($o_{prop_t}$), and the most recent image feature ($f_{img_t}$) to produce joint-space actions ($a_{stud_t}$).
 - **Training:** Supervised by the teacher through two loss terms:
 - **Latent-Matching Loss ($L_{latent}$):** Encourages the student to reconstruct the teacher’s goal-in-context representation ($||\tilde{z}_{goal_t} - z_{goal_t}||^2$).
 - **Behavioral Cloning Loss ($L_{BC}$):** Aligns student actions with teacher actions ($||a_{stud_t} - a_{teach_t}||^2$).
 - A DAgger-style mixing schedule gradually replaces teacher actions with student actions during rollouts.

**Domain Randomization (Table VII):**
Extensive domain randomization is applied during training to improve robustness and facilitate sim-to-real transfer. This includes randomizing dynamics (friction, restitution, CoM offset, link mass, PD gains, control delay, external pushes, joint torque dropouts) and perception (depth clipping, non-linear remapping, multiplicative noise, spatial/temporal dropout, synthetic occlusions, camera pose jitter).

**Implementation Details:**
The system is built on HumanoidVerse, extended with terrain generation and image rendering, running in IsaacGym and IsaacLab. A 23-DoF Unitree G1 model with a head-mounted depth camera is used. Control operates at 50 Hz, with depth images rendered at 30 Hz. All network components use lightweight MLP backbones with ELU activations. Real-world deployment is fully zero-shot on a Unitree G1 with an Intel RealSense camera.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VIGOR was conducted extensively in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, assessing performance across diverse fall and recovery conditions.

**A. Simulated Experiments**

Simulated experiments evaluated VIGOR under "Stand-Up" (initialized in various resting poses) and "Fall-Recovery" (initialized near the onset of falling with disturbances) regimes across diverse non-flat terrains (rough, waves, slope, inverted slope, stairs, inverted stairs). Metrics included Success, Safe Success (excluding head collisions), Time to recovery, Tracking Error, Energy consumption, and Base Displacement.

- **Comparison with Baselines (Table II):**
 - VIGOR significantly outperformed prior methods (HOST for Stand-Up, FIRM for both) designed primarily for flat terrain.
 - For Stand-Up, VIGOR achieved 89.5% success (86.7% safe success) compared to HOST's 15.2% (12.8% safe success) and FIRM's 30.8% (21.4% safe success).
 - For Fall-Recovery, VIGOR achieved 90.5% success (89.3% safe success) compared to FIRM's 20.2% (15.3% safe success).
 - VIGOR also demonstrated lower tracking error, energy consumption, and base displacement, indicating more efficient and stable recovery.
 - The full privileged Teacher policy consistently showed the highest performance, setting an upper bound.

- **Teacher Ablations (Table III):**
 - **Importance of Keypoints:** Removing keypoint-based observations (`noKeypoints`) resulted in the largest performance degradation, with success rates dropping significantly (e.g., from 97.7% to 55.3% for Stand-Up Teacher). This indicates that explicit spatial structural priors are central to coordinated recovery.
 - **Relative vs. Absolute Tracking:** Using joint-angle targets (`DofKeypoints`) instead of spatial keypoints partially recovered performance but remained below the full model. Training with absolute pose tracking (`AbsTrack`) significantly increased tracking error and reduced success, demonstrating that world-frame supervision impairs terrain transfer.
 - **Role of Privileged Terrain:** Removing access to privileged terrain information (`NoScandots`) preserved much of the raw success rate but led to a pronounced drop in safe success (e.g., from 93.0% to 75.7% safe success for Stand-Up Teacher). This suggests that privileged terrain observations primarily contribute to safety by enabling terrain-aware contact planning rather than core motion coordination.

- **Student Ablations (Table IV):**
 - **Criticality of Shared Latent:** Disabling shared latent supervision (`w.o Shared`) caused the most significant performance degradation in both Stand-Up (60.0% success, 54.3% safe success) and Fall-Recovery (57.7% success, 57.7% safe success). This finding supports that distillation of the goal-in-context representation is the primary mechanism for the student to achieve coordinated recovery.
 - **Vision for Safety:** Removing egocentric depth input (`w.o Vision`) preserved moderate raw success but consistently lowered safe success (e.g., 62.7% safe success for Stand-Up), especially in Stand-Up. This suggests that vision primarily improves terrain-aware stabilization and safety rather than basic recovery capability itself.
 - **Temporal History Benefits Stand-Up:** Removing temporal observation history (`w.o History`) slightly reduced Stand-Up performance but had a negligible or even slightly positive effect on Fall-Recovery. This implies that Stand-Up tasks, with their longer multi-contact transitions, benefit more from temporal context, while rapid Fall-Recovery is dominated by immediate reactive control.
 - The full VIGOR student model achieved the most balanced behavior across all metrics, confirming the synergistic contribution of its components.

**B. Real-World Experiments**

The VIGOR student policy was deployed zero-shot on a real Unitree G1, without any fine-tuning, under both Stand-Up and Fall-Recovery regimes on various terrains (flat, raised platform, stairs, box, stones).

- **Stand-Up (Fig. 7 Top, Table IX):**
 - On flat terrain, VIGOR and a blind policy (without vision) showed similar overall success.
 - As terrain complexity increased (box, stones), VIGOR maintained consistently higher safe success rates compared to the blind policy, which exhibited lower safety under uneven contacts. For instance, on stones, VIGOR achieved 19/20 total successes with 19/20 safe successes, whereas the blind policy achieved 16/20 total successes with 13/20 safe successes.
 - The default Unitree controller only succeeded for face-up stand-up on flat terrain, highlighting its lack of generalization.

- **Fall-Recovery (Fig. 7 Bottom, Table X):**
 - For simpler disturbances like forward and backward pushes on flat terrain, both VIGOR and the blind policy recovered successfully.
 - However, under more challenging disturbances (e.g., sideways pushes on flat ground, pushes off platforms, and stair interactions), the blind policy showed marked drops in safe success (e.g., 0/3 safe success for sideways push and diagonal stair push).
 - In contrast, VIGOR maintained high success and notably better safety across these complex scenarios, with the advantage becoming most pronounced in setups requiring terrain-aware reactions, such as diagonal stair pushes (5/5 safe success for VIGOR vs. 0/3 for blind).

- **Effect of Vision in Real-World:** Vision did not universally improve raw success in simple scenarios but primarily enhanced safety and contact-aware stabilization. Under asymmetric contacts and complex geometries (like stairs), vision significantly reduced unsafe recoveries (e.g., preventing head collisions) while maintaining high overall recovery rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VIGOR represents a notable advancement in humanoid fall safety, with several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Unified Fall Safety Policy:** By integrating fall mitigation and stand-up recovery into a single, cohesive policy, VIGOR moves beyond fragmented approaches. This unified framework is crucial for humanoids operating in dynamic and unpredictable environments, where a continuous and adaptive response to disturbances is necessary for robustness and autonomy. This can lead to more seamless and intelligent robot behavior during failures.

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The ability of VIGOR to enable humanoids to recover from falls on diverse, non-flat terrains, as demonstrated in both simulation and the real world, is a significant step forward. This capability is critical for deploying humanoids in real-world scenarios that inherently involve varied and challenging ground surfaces, where traditional, flat-ground-trained policies would fail.

- **Efficient and Scalable Learning:** The factorized data generation approach, which decouples sparse human pose priors from extensive simulated terrain variation, offers a more sample-efficient learning paradigm. This addresses a major limitation of monolithic reinforcement learning and imitation learning methods that require vast and often costly data coverage. This efficiency could accelerate the development of complex robot behaviors.

- **Crucial Role of Egocentric Vision:** The research underscores the critical importance of egocentric depth perception for terrain-aware stabilization and safe fall recovery. By showing that vision primarily contributes to safety and intelligent contact selection on complex terrains, VIGOR highlights a pathway for developing more intelligent and resilient humanoid systems that can perceive and adapt to their immediate environment.

- **Demonstrated Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot transfer of the learned policy from simulation to a real Unitree G1 robot, without any real-world fine-tuning, validates the robustness of the teacher-student learning approach and the efficacy of the extensive domain randomization. This capability is vital for practical robotics, significantly reducing the time and resources required for real-world deployment and validation.

- **Foundation for Future Humanoid Autonomy:** VIGOR provides a strong foundation for future research at the intersection of robust humanoid locomotion, fall avoidance, and fall recovery. Integrating this fall safety framework with continuous locomotion and long-horizon navigation policies could lead to more capable, resilient, and truly autonomous humanoid robots that can operate reliably in complex human-centric environments.
