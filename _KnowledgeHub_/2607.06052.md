# ThorArena: Benchmarking Humanoid Physical Interaction with Human Motion-Force Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> ThorArena introduces a benchmark for evaluating humanoid robot control policies under physical interaction, integrating human motion-force demonstrations, a force-replay protocol, and force-aware metrics. This framework revealed that existing policies designed for kinematic tasks degrade significantly under external forces, with Thor2 demonstrating superior robust tracking and survival rates in contact-rich scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid robot benchmarks primarily focus on kinematic motion tracking, neglecting synchronized interaction forces.
- Current evaluation protocols provide limited insight into how humanoid policies perform under realistic physical interactions, often failing to assess stability and robustness.
- The absence of synchronized motion and force data hinders the development and comprehensive assessment of force-aware control policies.

## Method

- Developed **ThorArena**, a standardized benchmark, by collecting a novel dataset of human whole-body motion synchronized with two-hand interaction forces across six diverse tasks.
- Introduced ==force-aware evaluation metrics==, including the ==Force-Aware Tracking Score (FATS)==, to quantify tracking accuracy, force robustness, control effort, and episode survival.
- Implemented a ==simulation-based benchmark protocol== that replays recorded interaction forces alongside reference motions, supported by a unified policy-adapter interface for diverse policies.

## Results

- Thor2 achieved the highest average Force-Aware Tracking Score (FATS) of **81.71** and a near-perfect survival rate of **1.000** under external force conditions.
- Other evaluated policies (TWIST2, GMT, SONIC) experienced substantial performance degradation and lower survival rates (e.g., **0.73-0.81**) in contact-rich tasks, highlighting their vulnerability to physical interactions.
- The study demonstrated that conventional no-force evaluation is insufficient, as performance gaps among policies were smaller without external forces, failing to expose critical force-induced instability or robustness issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evaluating humanoid policies solely under kinematic conditions is insufficient, as policies struggle with stability and accuracy when subjected to external interaction forces.
> - Physical interaction forces significantly impact a humanoid's whole-body balance, motion tracking precision, and required control effort.
> - Comprehensive evaluation requires metrics that go beyond kinematic error to include robustness to varying force levels, control effort, and long-horizon stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots is progressively targeting real-world applications that necessitate complex whole-body motion and robust physical interaction with objects and humans. Existing research has made progress in humanoid teleoperation, motion imitation, and whole-body control, enabling robots to replicate intricate human behaviors through large-scale demonstrations and simulation-based learning. However, the current landscape of humanoid datasets and benchmarks primarily focuses on kinematic motion tracking, task completion, or motion naturalness. The critical aspect of synchronized interaction forces is largely overlooked in these resources.

This omission results in evaluation protocols that offer limited insight into how humanoid policies perform under realistic physical interactions. When a humanoid robot interacts physically with its environment, the resulting forces directly influence its whole-body balance, the accuracy of its motion tracking, and the control effort required. A policy that demonstrates proficiency in free-space motion tracking may experience significant performance degradation or failure when subjected to external interaction forces on the upper body. Consequently, evaluating humanoid behaviors solely under kinematic conditions is insufficient for a comprehensive assessment of their real-world applicability during physical interaction.

Despite the expansion of humanoid datasets to include diverse motion demonstrations, most provide only pose trajectories or retargeted motions without synchronized interaction forces. Similarly, prevailing evaluation protocols typically quantify kinematic errors or task success, treating external interactions as implicit disturbances rather than integral measurements. ThorArena addresses this identified gap by introducing a standardized benchmark that integrates human motion demonstrations, measured interaction forces, and force-aware evaluation metrics within a unified framework, thereby enabling a more comprehensive characterization of force-aware humanoid control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing ThorArena stems from the increasing demand for humanoid robots to execute contact-rich tasks in real-world environments. While existing humanoid control advancements have achieved considerable kinematic motion tracking, the research community lacks standardized tools to evaluate how these policies perform under realistic physical interactions. The paper identifies several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Addressing the Limitations of Kinematic-Focused Evaluation**: Current evaluation methods predominantly focus on kinematic accuracy, neglecting the impact of synchronized interaction forces. This oversight prevents a full understanding of a policy's stability, tracking accuracy, and control robustness when external forces are applied, as a policy proficient in free-space motion may fail under physical contact. The objective is to move beyond kinematic assessment to include force-aware evaluation.

2. **Characterizing Force-Interaction Effects**: Physical interaction forces fundamentally affect a humanoid's whole-body balance, the precision of its motion tracking, and the magnitude of control effort required. The paper aims to develop a benchmark that explicitly measures these effects, enabling researchers to characterize how policies respond to varied force conditions.

3. **Bridging the Data Gap**: A significant limitation in existing humanoid datasets is the absence of synchronized interaction force measurements alongside motion data. This data scarcity hinders the development and evaluation of force-aware control policies. A key objective is to collect and release a novel dataset that pairs human whole-body motion with simultaneously recorded two-hand interaction forces across diverse, representative contact-rich tasks.

4. **Developing Force-Aware Evaluation Metrics**: To properly assess humanoid performance under interaction forces, new metrics are required. The motivation is to propose evaluation metrics that not only quantify motion tracking accuracy but also evaluate robustness to varying force levels, control effort, and long-horizon stability (survival rate). This includes developing a composite score, such as the Force-Aware Tracking Score (FATS), and complementary diagnostic metrics.

5. **Establishing a Standardized Benchmark Protocol**: The lack of a unified framework for evaluating policies under consistent physical disturbances poses a challenge for comparative research. The objective is to establish a simulation-based benchmark that can consistently replay recorded interaction forces during policy execution, along with a standardized policy-adapter interface, to allow for fair and reproducible evaluation of different whole-body control policies.

In summary, the overarching goal of ThorArena is to provide a comprehensive, reproducible, and standardized framework for evaluating humanoid whole-body control policies in contact-rich scenarios, thereby facilitating the development of more robust and adaptable humanoid robots capable of real-world physical interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ThorArena is constructed around three primary, integrated components: real-world force-interaction data acquisition, the definition of force-aware evaluation metrics, and a standardized benchmark evaluation protocol.

**A. Real-World Force-Interaction Data Acquisition**

To gather synchronized human motion and interaction-force demonstrations, a dedicated real-world acquisition system was developed. This system involves:
- **Motion Capture**: An operator wears a PICO 4 Ultra headset and body trackers to record whole-body human motion data.
- **Force Measurement**: Two force sensors, integrated with 3D-printed hooks, are utilized to measure the forces exerted through the left and right hands. These hooks also provide stable attachment points for interaction with external objects.

Six distinct physical interaction tasks were designed to encompass common contact patterns encountered in real-world scenarios:
1. Clean Table (table wiping)
2. Liftdown Water (lowering and placing a water container)
3. Liftup Water (lifting a water container)
4. Pull Chair (pulling heavy objects)
5. Push Chair (pushing heavy objects)
6. Syn Carry (cooperative carrying)

For each task, 60 raw demonstration sequences were collected, totaling 360 sequences. Each sequence records a complete task execution, capturing the evolution of both motion and force throughout the interaction. The measured forces exhibit variations in magnitude and direction, contributing to the diversity of interaction patterns. The raw data consists of time-aligned human body keypoint motion and three-axis force measurements from both hands, stored in a format that preserves their original correspondence.

**B. Force-Aware Evaluation Metrics**

To assess whole-body motion tracking under interaction forces, a set of metrics was developed to jointly characterize tracking accuracy, force robustness, control effort, and episode survival. The primary metric is the Force-Aware Tracking Score (FATS).

- **Keypoint Error (e_kp)**: This metric quantifies the whole-body keypoint root-relative error in the world frame. It is calculated as the average Euclidean distance between the reference keypoint positions and the measured robot keypoint positions across N body keypoints at each timestep *t*. The reference keypoints are re-anchored to the robot’s current root pose at every timestep, making the error invariant to global root drift.

- **Force Regimes**: To analyze performance under varying force levels, timesteps within each episode are stratified into low-, medium-, and high-force regimes. This stratification is based on the total applied hand force, using its 33rd and 66th percentiles within the dataset as thresholds.

- **Force-Aware Tracking Error (E_i)**: For each episode *i*, the keypoint RMSE from the three force regimes (E_low, E_mid, E_high) is aggregated into a weighted sum. The weights (0.2, 0.3, 0.5 for low, medium, and high force, respectively) are designed to emphasize performance in the high-force regime.

- **Episode-Level Score (S_i)**: This score combines the force-aware tracking error (E_i) with a survival factor (s_i). The survival factor is defined as the minimum of the achieved episode length (T_i) divided by the reference horizon (T_ref_i), and 1. The score is calculated as 100 * exp(-E_i / σ) * s_i, where σ = 0.15 m controls error sensitivity.

- **Force-Aware Tracking Score (FATS)**: The FATS is the average of the episode-level scores (S_i) across all M evaluated episodes. In the no-force evaluation setting, the same formulation is applied, but with force replay disabled.

- **Diagnostic Metrics**: Complementary metrics provide additional insights:
 - **Robustness Ratio (ρ)**: Defined as the ratio of keypoint RMSE in the low-force regime to that in the high-force regime (E_low / E_high). A value closer to one indicates less performance degradation under stronger forces.
 - **Power Overhead (η)**: Defined as the ratio of average mechanical power consumed in the high-force regime to that in the low-force regime (P_high / P_low). A lower value indicates less additional control effort required to manage interaction forces. These diagnostic metrics are reported separately and do not contribute to the FATS calculation.

**C. Benchmark Evaluation Protocol**

The ThorArena benchmark integrates retargeted reference motions, synchronized force recordings, a physics-based simulation environment, and various whole-body control policies through a unified policy-adapter interface.

- **Force-Replay Protocol**: The benchmark loads a specified evaluation dataset and synchronously replays the recorded left and right hand force sequences alongside the reference motion. The forces, originally acquired in the sensor frame, are transformed into the hand-local frame and then to the world frame based on the simulated hand poses before being applied to the robot's hand bodies in the simulator. A scalar force coefficient controls the magnitude of applied force, set to 1 for external-force evaluation (reproducing measured forces) and 0 for no-force evaluation (disabling external forces).

- **Full-Coverage Evaluation**: For official benchmarking, a deterministic schedule is implemented, ensuring that every demonstration sequence in the evaluation dataset is assessed once under the consistent force-replay protocol. Multiple parallel simulation environments are utilized to enhance evaluation efficiency while maintaining deterministic coverage.

- **Unified Policy-Adapter Interface**: To accommodate diverse humanoid motion policies, the benchmark provides a lightweight policy-adapter interface. Each adapter is responsible for:
 - Receiving the current simulator state and reference-motion command.
 - Constructing policy-specific observations from available benchmark signals.
 - Performing policy inference.
 - Converting the predicted action into the benchmark's standardized action representation. This design decouples the benchmark's core mechanics from the specific input requirements, action parameterizations, and joint-order conventions of individual policies.

- **Fixed Dynamics Profile**: Each evaluated policy operates within a fixed, deployment-compatible dynamics profile derived from its original training or deployment configuration. This profile defines the robot model, control and physics timesteps, action scaling, and any action delays. These parameters remain constant across all tasks and demonstration sequences for a given policy, ensuring consistent evaluation conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ThorArena benchmark was evaluated on a dataset comprising six subtasks, comparing four whole-body control policies: Thor2, TWIST2, GMT, and SONIC. Evaluations were conducted under two conditions: with recorded external interaction forces and without external forces. The primary metric was FATS, supplemented by diagnostic metrics such as survival rate, body tracking error, force robustness, and control effort. Aggregate metrics were averaged across the six subtasks.

**Overall Performance**
Thor2 consistently demonstrated superior overall tracking performance, occupying the outer region of radar plots and achieving the highest FATS scores across subtasks. The results indicate that external forces induced more substantial performance degradation for the other policies, particularly during the "push chair" task.

**Experiments with External Forces**
Under the external-force setting, which evaluates a policy's ability to maintain stable and accurate motion tracking during physical interaction:

- **Thor2 Performance**: Thor2 achieved the highest average FATS of 81.71, ranking first on all six subtasks, and maintained a near-perfect survival rate of 1.000. It also exhibited the lowest absolute keypoint errors in both low-force (27.8 mm) and high-force (33.8 mm) regimes, approximately 25% lower than the second-best policy. Thor2 maintained both high tracking scores and high episode survival (≥ 0.99 on all tasks).

- **Performance of Other Policies**: The other three policies, TWIST2, GMT, and SONIC, obtained comparatively lower average FATS scores, with more distinct performance profiles.
 - **Survival Rate**: These policies exhibited substantially lower mean episode survival during force interaction compared to Thor2. The "push chair" subtask caused the most significant degradation for TWIST2, GMT, and SONIC, with survival rates dropping to 0.73–0.81. This was attributed to the sustained horizontal forces in "push chair" that directly challenge balance, making it a demanding interaction.
 - **TWIST2**: Achieved the highest robustness ratio (0.908), indicating less degradation under stronger forces. However, this relative robustness was partly associated with a larger baseline keypoint error in the low-force regime.
 - **GMT**: Showed more accurate tracking than SONIC under strong interaction forces.
 - **SONIC**: Exhibited the lowest power overhead (1.22x) and the second-highest survival rate (0.957), outperforming TWIST2 and GMT on the two most demanding tasks. This suggests a compliant response to perturbations, prioritizing balance preservation over active disturbance rejection. However, this approach resulted in the largest keypoint errors, particularly in upper-body segments under sustained force.

**Experiments without External Forces**
In the no-force setting, which evaluates nominal motion-tracking ability without physical perturbation:

- **Overall Performance**: All policies demonstrated high survival rates, close to 1.0, indicating that performance differences primarily stemmed from tracking accuracy rather than falls or early termination.
- **Thor2**: Still performed best, achieving a FATS of 84.31 and the lowest keypoint error of 26.1 mm.
- **Ranking**: TWIST2 ranked second, followed by GMT and SONIC.
- **Comparison to External-Force Setting**: The performance gaps among policies were smaller in the no-force setting. Lower-body errors were similar across policies, with remaining differences mainly attributed to upper-body tracking accuracy.

**Conclusion from Findings**: The comparison between the external-force and no-force settings highlights that conventional no-force evaluation is insufficient for contact-rich humanoid tasks. It can assess nominal tracking quality but fails to reveal critical aspects such as force-induced instability, robustness degradation, or additional control effort. ThorArena's integrated evaluation framework, by comparing performance under both conditions, provides a more complete assessment of humanoid control policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ThorArena introduces a significant advancement in the evaluation of humanoid robot control policies, particularly for tasks involving physical interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Force-Aware Humanoid Control Research**: By providing a benchmark that explicitly incorporates synchronized human motion and force demonstrations, ThorArena allows researchers to systematically study and develop humanoid policies that are robust to external interaction forces. This moves the field beyond purely kinematic evaluations, fostering progress in areas critical for real-world deployment.

2. **Comprehensive Performance Assessment**: The proposed force-aware evaluation metrics, including the Force-Aware Tracking Score (FATS) and diagnostic metrics (robustness ratio, power overhead), offer a more complete picture of a policy's performance. They quantify not only motion tracking accuracy but also stability, control effort, and robustness under varying force levels, revealing performance characteristics that remain obscured by traditional no-force evaluations.

3. **Standardization and Reproducibility**: The unified simulation-based benchmark protocol, with its force-replay mechanism and policy-adapter interface, provides a standardized environment for evaluating diverse humanoid control policies. This standardization promotes fair comparisons between different approaches and enhances the reproducibility of research findings, which is crucial for accelerating scientific progress.

4. **Valuable Data Resource**: The collected force-interaction humanoid dataset, comprising synchronized human whole-body motion and two-hand force demonstrations across six representative contact-rich tasks, serves as a valuable public resource. This dataset can be utilized not only for benchmarking but also for training novel force-aware humanoid control policies, as demonstrated by its use in training the Thor2 policy.

5. **Accelerating Real-World Applications**: Humanoid robots are envisioned for roles in challenging, contact-rich environments. By providing a rigorous evaluation framework, ThorArena contributes to the development of more robust, stable, and energy-efficient humanoid control systems. This could accelerate their deployment in complex tasks such as assistance, manipulation, and cooperative work in human environments.

**Future Impact and Directions**: The authors indicate plans to further expand the dataset with more diverse tasks, objects, and force conditions, alongside improving the data acquisition pipeline for enhanced accuracy and scalability. Future work also includes validating the force-level stratification and metric formulation through additional experiments, extending the benchmark to other humanoid embodiments and control systems, and conducting physical robot evaluations to bridge the sim-to-real gap. These ongoing efforts are expected to solidify ThorArena's role as a foundational benchmark, continuously pushing the boundaries of force-aware humanoid interaction research and contributing to the advancement of more capable and reliable humanoid robots for practical applications.
