# End-to-End Training of Deep Visuomotor Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley demonstrated end-to-end training of deep convolutional neural networks for complex visuomotor policies, mapping raw visual inputs to robot motor torques for real-world manipulation tasks. This approach, using an improved Guided Policy Search (GPS) algorithm and a novel spatial softmax CNN architecture, achieved up to 96.3% success on high-precision tasks with 15 minutes of robot interaction, significantly outperforming modular vision-control baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic policy search methods relied on extensive hand-engineering for perception and state estimation, making systems brittle and difficult to adapt.
- Applying deep neural networks to real-world robotic control faced challenges due to data scarcity, indirect supervision, and the sample inefficiency of deep reinforcement learning.
- Modular approaches for vision and control often failed to capture complex interdependencies, limiting performance for high-precision manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A deep ==convolutional neural network (CNN)== was designed to learn end-to-end visuomotor policies, directly mapping raw monocular RGB images and robot configuration to 7-DoF joint motor torques.
- The authors developed a novel ==Bregman ADMM (BADMM)== formulation of **Guided Policy Search (GPS)**, which iteratively optimizes local ==linear-Gaussian controllers== and then trains the deep CNN policy via ==supervised learning==.
- A custom CNN architecture incorporating ==spatial softmax== and ==expected position operators== was introduced to preserve spatial resolution and extract task-relevant spatial features efficiently for control, avoiding standard pooling layers.

## Results

- End-to-end trained policies achieved up to **96.3%** success rates on training positions and **91.7%** on novel test positions for tasks like inserting a block into a shape sorting cube, substantially outperforming 'pose prediction' and 'pose features' baselines (e.g., **0%** for pose prediction on the shape cube task).
- The method demonstrated high sample efficiency, training each deep visuomotor policy in **3-4 hours** with only **15 minutes** of real robot interaction time (**156-288 trials**).
- Analysis revealed that end-to-end trained policies learned 'goal-driven visual features' that differed from those learned purely for pose prediction, adapting visual processing to optimize task performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint, end-to-end training of perception and control systems provides superior performance and generalization for complex, high-precision robotic manipulation tasks compared to separate, modular approaches.
> - The novel spatial softmax CNN architecture effectively extracts spatially aware features critical for visuomotor control, achieving significantly higher pose estimation accuracy than standard CNN designs.
> - Guided Policy Search, particularly with the BADMM formulation and a multi-stage pretraining scheme, enables sample-efficient learning of deep visuomotor policies on real robots, requiring only minutes of interaction time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of robotics, machine learning, and computer vision: enabling robots to learn complex control policies directly from raw sensory input without extensive human engineering. It specifically positions itself within the landscape of:

- **Policy Search and Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Robotics:** Prior to this work, policy search methods in robotics, while promising, often relied heavily on hand-engineered components for perception, state estimation, and low-level control. Vision systems, in particular, were typically designed separately and remained fixed during policy training, unable to adapt to the task's specific goals. This created a bottleneck, limiting the complexity and generalization capabilities of learned policies. This paper directly confronts this by questioning whether joint training of perception and control can yield better performance.

- **Deep Learning (DL) in Computer Vision:** Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) had achieved remarkable success in computer vision tasks like image classification (e.g., ImageNet, Krizhevsky et al., 2012) and speech recognition. However, applying these models to real-world robotic control presented unique challenges. Computer vision tasks often benefited from massive datasets and direct supervision, neither of which were readily available in robotics. Furthermore, many vision CNNs discard precise spatial information (via pooling layers) for classification invariance, whereas robotics tasks critically depend on accurate spatial reasoning for manipulation.

- **Deep Learning for Robotic Control:** Early attempts at using neural networks for robot control employed small, simple networks. More recent work in deep learning for robotics prior to this paper often focused on simpler task-space motions, low-dimensional underlying structures, or simulated environments that lacked real-world visual complexity. A major hurdle was the extreme sample inefficiency of traditional reinforcement learning algorithms when applied to high-dimensional deep neural networks, making them impractical for real robots where interaction time is costly. This paper explicitly aimed to overcome the "impractical number of samples for real-world robotic learning" limitation, marking a significant step towards practical deep visuomotor control.

- **Guided Policy Search (GPS):** The methodology builds upon and significantly extends previous work in Guided Policy Search (Levine and Koltun, 2013a,b, 2014; Levine and Abbeel, 2014; Levine et al., 2015). GPS is a technique designed to bridge the gap between model-free reinforcement learning and supervised learning, making it possible to train high-dimensional policies efficiently. This paper introduces a novel Bregman ADMM (BADMM) formulation for GPS, enhancing its efficiency and theoretical convergence guarantees, and, crucially, extends it to handle raw visual observations.

- **Visual Servoing:** The work also implicitly contrasts with traditional visual servoing methods, which rely on manually specified feature points and camera calibration for feedback control. By learning features and control policies end-to-end, this research offers a more flexible and autonomous approach, removing the need for hand-engineering in visual perception for control.

In essence, this work represents a pivotal moment in robotic learning, demonstrating that deep neural networks could directly learn complex, high-dimensional manipulation skills from raw visual input on real robots, efficiently and effectively, by jointly optimizing perception and control. It shifted the paradigm from modular, pipeline-based robotic systems towards truly end-to-end learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is encapsulated in the question posed by the authors: "does training the perception and control systems jointly end-to-end provide better performance than training each component separately?" This core question drives the entire investigation.

The motivation behind pursuing this objective stems from several limitations observed in prior robotic learning systems:

- **Limitations of Hand-Engineered Components:** Traditional policy search methods in robotics often necessitate extensive hand-engineering for crucial components like perception, state estimation, and low-level control. This makes the overall system complex, brittle, prone to errors, and difficult to adapt to new tasks or environments. The vision system, in particular, is highlighted as a complex component that is typically not improved during policy training, nor optimized for the specific goal of the task.
- **Challenges of Deep Learning for Robotics:** While deep neural networks showed immense promise in other domains (e.g., computer vision), their application to real-world robotic control faced significant hurdles:
 - **Data Scarcity:** Real-world robot interaction data is expensive and time-consuming to collect, making the large datasets typically required for deep learning impractical.
 - **Indirect Supervision:** Robotic tasks are defined by high-level cost functions rather than direct, explicit labels for desired actions at each step, making traditional supervised learning difficult.
 - **Partial Observability:** Robots often operate with partial state information (e.g., camera images instead of precise object poses), necessitating the inference of critical state variables from noisy, high-dimensional inputs.
 - **High Dimensionality:** Deep neural networks are high-dimensional, making direct reinforcement learning optimization extremely challenging and sample-inefficient.
- **Need for Coordinated Vision and Control:** Many manipulation tasks require intricate coordination between visual perception and motor control. A modular approach, where vision and control are developed independently, may fail to capture these complex interdependencies and limit overall task performance, especially for high-precision tasks.

To address these motivations, the authors set out to develop a method that could:
1. Learn policies that map raw image observations directly to torques at the robot's motors, integrating perception and control into a single deep neural network.
2. Achieve this end-to-end training in a sample-efficient manner, making it practical for real-world robot deployment.
3. Demonstrate superior performance and generalization compared to modular approaches where perception and control are trained separately.
4. Develop a novel deep CNN architecture suitable for spatial reasoning in robotic control, overcoming limitations of standard architectures designed primarily for classification.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around **Guided Policy Search (GPS)**, specifically an iteration of it formalized using **Bregman ADMM (BADMM)**, to train **deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs)** that map raw image observations directly to motor torques.

#### 3.1. End-to-End Visuomotor Policies with Deep CNNs

The policies are represented by deep CNNs with 92,000 parameters (86,000 in convolutional layers), mapping monocular RGB images and robot configuration (joint angles, end-effector pose, velocities) to 7-DoF joint motor torques. This direct mapping is central to the "end-to-end" concept.

#### 3.2. Guided Policy Search (GPS) with BADMM

The authors reformulate policy search as a constrained optimization problem, which is solved by an iterative dual descent method based on BADMM. This involves alternating between two phases:

- **Trajectory Optimization (Guiding Distributions):**
 - **Goal:** Generate "guiding distributions" `p_i(u_t|x_t)` (time-varying linear-Gaussian controllers) that achieve low cost for specific initial states `x_i^1`. These controllers utilize the *full state* `x_t` during training, simplifying their optimization.
 - **Method:** An iLQG-like algorithm is used, which efficiently optimizes these linear-Gaussian controllers.
 - **Unknown Dynamics:** Since real-world dynamics are unknown, a local linear-Gaussian dynamics model `p(x_t+1|x_t, u_t)` is fitted at each iteration using samples collected from the robot executing previous controllers. To improve sample efficiency and robustness, a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) is fitted globally to all observed transitions and used as a prior for fitting local dynamics at each time step.
 - **Constrained Optimization:** The trajectory optimization is constrained by a KL-divergence term `D_KL(p(τ)||p̂(τ)) ≤ ε` to ensure that the new trajectories do not deviate too drastically from the previous ones, maintaining stability and sample efficiency.
 - **BADMM Enhancement:** The BADMM formulation allows for a convex problem during trajectory optimization, making it substantially faster and easier to implement than prior GPS variants, especially with many trajectories.

- **Supervised Policy Learning (Global Policy):**
 - **Goal:** Train the deep CNN policy `π_θ(u_t|o_t)` to imitate the actions generated by the optimized guiding distributions `p_i(u_t|x_t)`. This policy, however, only receives *raw observations* `o_t` (camera images) as input, matching the test-time conditions.
 - **Method:** The policy is optimized using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) to minimize a weighted quadratic loss between its output mean and the mean action of the guiding distributions. The weighting is derived from the precision matrix of the guiding distributions, effectively penalizing deviations more strongly in "cost-sensitive" regions.
 - **Sample Efficiency:** Importance sampling is used to incorporate samples from previous iterations into the policy optimization, further enhancing data efficiency.
 - **Convergence Guarantee:** The BADMM framework provides theoretical guarantees that, at convergence, the global policy `π_θ(u_t|o_t)` and the guiding distributions `p_i(u_t|x_t)` will exhibit the same behavior, effectively transferring the performance of the local experts to the global, observation-based policy.

#### 3.3. Novel CNN Architecture

To address the challenge of spatial reasoning in visuomotor control, the authors design a custom CNN architecture (Figure 2):

- **No Pooling:** Unlike standard classification CNNs, pooling layers are omitted to preserve spatial resolution, which is critical for precise robot control.
- **Spatial Softmax and Expected Position:** The final convolutional layer's response maps are fed into a spatial softmax function. This output, which represents a probability distribution over pixel locations for each feature channel, is then used to compute the "expected image position" (a 2D coordinate) for each feature. This acts as a soft-argmax, converting pixel-wise features into a concise set of spatial feature points.
- **Feature Point-Based Control:** These learned spatial feature points are concatenated with the robot's configuration (joint angles, velocities) and passed through fully connected layers to produce motor torques. This design facilitates accurate spatial reasoning and reduces overfitting.

#### 3.4. Pretraining Scheme for Data Efficiency

To mitigate the data requirements of deep learning and accelerate training, a multi-stage pretraining approach is employed (Figure 3):

- **Vision Layer Pretraining:** The robot collects a dataset of camera images and corresponding object 3D poses (known during training by moving the object with the other arm). A pose regression CNN (using the same vision layers as the policy) is trained on this data. The first layer filters are further initialized from ImageNet-trained weights (transfer learning). This allows the visual layers to learn basic object appearance before policy learning.
- **Trajectory Pretraining:** The local linear-Gaussian controllers are initially trained for several iterations without optimizing the deep visuomotor policy. A smaller, full-state network constrains these trajectories, ensuring basic competence and compatible strategies across different initial states.
- **End-to-End Fine-tuning:** After initialization, the full visuomotor policy is trained. The motor control (fully connected) layers are fine-tuned first, followed by end-to-end optimization of the entire network. This prevents the convolutional layers from "forgetting" useful features learned during pretraining due to large initial errors from untrained upper layers.

#### 3.5. Training Setup and Instrumentation

A crucial aspect is the assumption that the "full state of the system is observable at training time, but not at test time." For manipulation tasks, this often means the target object's position (the "unobserved variable" at test time) can be known during training, for instance, by holding it in the robot's other gripper. This "instrumented training" enables efficient learning of local controllers and pose regression.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors present a comprehensive experimental evaluation across simulated and real-world robotic tasks, addressing their core research questions.

#### 4.1. Simulated Comparisons to Prior Policy Search Methods

- **Performance of Trajectory Optimization:** On diverse simulated tasks (2D/3D peg insertion, octopus arm control, swimming, walking), the proposed trajectory optimization with local linear models and a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) prior demonstrated significantly higher sample efficiency and better solution quality compared to prior methods like REPS, RWR, CEM, and PILCO. Notably, it outperformed iLQG (with known dynamics) in 3D insertion, suggesting better handling of contact discontinuities.
- **Neural Network Policies:** Guided Policy Search (GPS) successfully trained neural network policies (hundreds of parameters) for complex tasks like peg insertion (where the policy had to search for an unknown hole position) and locomotion, achieving smoother gaits for swimming and consistent walking. In contrast, prior model-free methods largely struggled to train these higher-dimensional policies with limited samples.

#### 4.2. Learning Linear-Gaussian Controllers on a PR2 Robot

- **Sample Efficiency:** The trajectory optimization algorithm (a core component of GPS) was successfully applied to a range of real-world manipulation tasks on a PR2 robot (e.g., stacking Lego, threading rings, attaching wheels, inserting a shoe tree, screwing bottle caps). It achieved high sample efficiency, requiring only 20-25 trials per task (approximately 10 minutes total, with 3-4 minutes of robot interaction time).
- **Robustness:** These linear-Gaussian controllers exhibited considerable robustness to perturbations in target object positions (e.g., 1-2 cm), outperforming a kinematic baseline significantly.

#### 4.3. Spatial Softmax CNN Architecture Evaluation

- **Superior Pose Estimation:** The proposed CNN architecture with spatial softmax and expected position operators (compared to standard CNNs with fully connected layers or pooling) achieved substantially higher accuracy in the pose estimation pretraining task (1.30 cm error vs. 2.59-4.75 cm). This validates the architectural design's ability to learn useful spatial features and its data efficiency for vision-based control.

#### 4.4. Deep Visuomotor Policy Evaluation (End-to-End Training)

This section directly addresses the central research question using a PR2 robot on four challenging, contact-rich manipulation tasks: hanging a coat hanger, inserting a block into a shape sorting cube, fitting a hammer claw under a nail, and screwing a bottle cap. These tasks involve significant variation in target position and (for hanger/hammer) grasp angle.
- **End-to-End vs. Baselines:** End-to-end training of the deep visuomotor policy consistently and significantly outperformed two baselines that used vision layers pretrained for pose prediction:
 - **"Pose Features" Baseline:** Uses the learned spatial feature points from the pose predictor as input to the control layers.
 - **"Pose Prediction" Baseline:** Directly feeds the predicted 3D pose into the control layers (mimicking a modular pipeline).
- **Quantitative Results:** For instance, on the shape cube task, end-to-end training achieved 96.3% success on training positions, 91.7% on novel spatial test positions, and 87.5% with visual distractors. In stark contrast, the "pose prediction" baseline achieved 0% success on training positions, highlighting the inadequacy of modular perception for high-precision tasks. The "pose features" baseline performed better than "pose prediction" but still significantly worse than end-to-end training.
- **Importance of Joint Optimization:** The results conclusively demonstrated that joint training of perception and control improves performance, accuracy (down to millimeters for some tasks), and generalization, especially when high precision and fine coordination are required.
- **Robustness to Visual Distractors:** The end-to-end policies showed moderate tolerance to visual distractors that were visually separated from the target, attributed to the lateral inhibition property of the spatial softmax. However, performance degraded with severe occlusion or adjacent distractors.
- **Learned Goal-Driven Features:** Analysis of the learned feature points revealed that end-to-end trained policies acquired "goal-driven visual features" that differed from those learned purely for pose prediction. These features focused more on task-relevant objects and robot parts, demonstrating the policy's ability to adapt its visual processing to optimize task performance.

#### 4.5. Computational Performance and Sample Efficiency

Training each visuomotor policy took 3-4 hours, with only about 15 minutes of actual robot interaction time (excluding pretraining data collection for vision). This impressive sample efficiency (156-288 trials for full end-to-end training depending on task) underscores the practical viability of the method for real-world robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper represents a landmark contribution to the fields of robotic learning, deep learning, and artificial intelligence, with profound significance and wide-ranging potential impact:

- **Pioneering End-to-End Visuomotor Control:** This work is one of the first to convincingly demonstrate the feasibility and tangible benefits of training deep neural networks end-to-end, directly from raw pixels to motor torques, for *complex, high-dimensional manipulation skills on real physical robots*. It moved the field beyond hand-engineered perception-control pipelines, paving the way for more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Bridging Deep Learning and Reinforcement Learning for Robotics:** The paper successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of combining data-hungry deep learning models with sample-inefficient reinforcement learning algorithms in the context of real-world robotics. The Guided Policy Search framework, particularly with its novel BADMM formulation and effective pretraining, provided a crucial bridge, making deep RL practical for robots.
- **Novel Algorithmic Contributions:**
 - **Bregman ADMM (BADMM) for GPS:** The formalization of GPS using BADMM is a significant algorithmic advance, improving the efficiency, stability, and theoretical guarantees of previous GPS methods.
 - **Spatial Softmax CNN Architecture:** The introduction of the spatial softmax and expected position layers in the CNN architecture is a critical innovation for visuomotor control, effectively extracting and utilizing spatial information from images in a data-efficient manner, tailored for control tasks.
- **Empirical Validation on Real Robots:** The extensive experimental validation on a PR2 robot across a diverse set of challenging manipulation tasks is a powerful demonstration of the method's robustness, generalizability, and practical utility. The successful performance on high-precision tasks, where modular approaches failed, provides compelling evidence for the benefits of end-to-end optimization.
- **Practical Sample Efficiency:** Achieving high-performance policies with only minutes of real-world robot interaction time is a game-changer for robotic learning, addressing one of the most significant barriers to wider adoption of learning-based control.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This paper laid a robust foundation for an explosion of subsequent research in deep reinforcement learning for robotics. It inspired countless follow-up works exploring more complex architectures (e.g., recurrent policies), improved generalization strategies, unsupervised state-space learning, and the integration of multimodal sensory inputs.
- **Industrial and Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated ability to learn complex tasks with moderate generalization in structured environments makes this work highly relevant for industrial automation, where robots perform repetitive tasks with varying visual conditions and require fine motor control.

In conclusion, "End-to-End Training of Deep Visuomotor Policies" is a seminal paper that profoundly influenced the direction of robotics and AI research. By demonstrating that robots can learn complex manipulation skills directly from visual input through efficient end-to-end deep reinforcement learning, it ushered in a new era of intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous robotic systems.
