# Metric3D: Towards Zero-shot Metric 3D Prediction from A Single Image

> [!summary] Summary
> Metric3D predicts zero-shot metric depth from a single RGB image by resolving camera-induced scale ambiguity. A canonical camera transformation module remaps images or depth labels into a shared canonical focal-length space, letting one model train stably across 8M images from 10K+ cameras, achieving state-of-the-art metric accuracy on 7 zero-shot benchmarks and winning the 2nd Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge.

## Problem

- Metric depth models train and test on a single camera intrinsic, so datasets stay small and generalization to unseen cameras or scenes fails badly.
- Affine-invariant depth models mix large-scale data for generalization but by design discard scale information, so they cannot recover real-world metric structure.
- Prior camera-conditioning approaches like CamConvs implicitly encode intrinsics in the network, which strains data diversity and network capacity, giving weaker results.

## Method

- ==Canonical Camera Transformation Module (CSTM)== remaps every training sample into a shared canonical focal length, either by rescaling ground-truth depth labels (CSTM label) or by resizing input images (CSTM image), removing focal-length ambiguity.
- A ==de-canonical transformation== at inference converts canonical-space predictions back to the original camera's metric depth using the known intrinsics, needing no per-camera retraining.
- ==Random Proposal Normalization Loss (RPNL)== applies median-absolute-deviation normalization over randomly cropped local patches instead of the whole image, sharpening fine-grained local depth contrast during supervision.

## Results

- Trained on **8 million** images spanning **11** datasets and over **10,000** cameras, the model achieves state-of-the-art rank **1.3-1.8** across NYUv2, KITTI, DIODE, ScanNet, and ETH3D zero-shot benchmarks, beating HDN and DPT-large.
- Without CSTM the model fails to converge on mixed metric datasets (AbsRel around **0.53-1.00**), while CSTM drops zero-shot AbsRel to **0.08-0.21**, far below the CamConvs baseline of **0.18-0.42**.
- Feeding predicted metric depth into Droid-SLAM on KITTI cuts translational RMS drift from **7-40** to **0.6-2.7** and improves ETH3D visual-odometry trajectory error, demonstrating direct downstream gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Resolving a single well-identified physical variable, focal length, as a canonical transformation is enough to unlock large-scale mixed-camera training without sacrificing metric scale.
> - Explicit geometric normalization using known camera intrinsics can outperform implicit learned camera-conditioning, since it removes rather than asks the network to absorb the ambiguity.
> - Accurate metric (not just relative) depth priors transfer directly to downstream geometric systems like SLAM, fixing scale drift that pure relative-depth methods cannot address.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D reconstruction from images is a cornerstone of computer vision, with wide-ranging applications from autonomous driving and robotics to augmented reality and content creation. Traditionally, robust 3D reconstruction heavily relies on **multi-view geometry**, where multiple images of a scene from different viewpoints are used to triangulate 3D points. While highly accurate, these methods are impractical for scenarios where only a single image is available, a common constraint for many real-world applications.

This limitation has spurred significant research into **monocular depth estimation**, where deep learning models learn a prior from large datasets to infer depth from a single 2D image. However, these monocular methods have historically faced a critical trade-off:
- **Metric Depth Estimation (Single Camera/Dataset):** Some methods achieve accurate *metric* depth (real-world scale measurements) but are typically trained and tested on specific datasets with a single camera model. This makes them highly specialized and unable to generalize to "in-the-wild" images captured by various cameras with unknown intrinsic parameters.
- **Affine-Invariant Depth Estimation (Large Datasets, Zero-shot Generalization):** To achieve broad generalization across diverse scenes and camera types, state-of-the-art monocular methods (e.g., MiDaS, LeReS) are trained on massive, mixed datasets. However, they typically learn only *affine-invariant* depth, meaning the predicted depth is accurate only up to an unknown scale and shift. While useful for relative depth perception and shape recovery, it cannot provide real-world metric measurements (e.g., "this table is 1.5 meters long"). This "metric ambiguity" arises precisely because different focal lengths can produce visually similar images for objects at different distances.

"Metric3D" directly addresses this fundamental challenge. It aims to bridge the gap between these two categories by enabling **zero-shot metric depth prediction from a single image**. This means the model can accurately predict real-world scale depth for any unseen image, regardless of the camera that captured it, while leveraging the power of large-scale, diverse data training. This capability is transformative, as it unlocks a new level of utility for single-image 3D understanding, moving beyond relative shape to quantifiable real-world measurements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Metric3D" is to develop a robust, zero-shot transferable model capable of recovering **metric 3D information from a single monocular image**. This goal is motivated by several critical limitations in the existing landscape of monocular depth estimation:

1. **Addressing Metric Ambiguity:** The core motivation stems from the inherent metric ambiguity in single-image depth prediction, particularly concerning varying camera focal lengths. As the authors illustrate, the same object can appear identically in an image whether it is closer with a shorter focal length or further away with a longer focal length. Existing methods either ignore camera intrinsics (leading to affine-invariant depth) or are tied to a single camera model (limiting generalization). Metric3D explicitly tackles this by showing how to incorporate camera intrinsics, specifically focal length, to resolve this ambiguity during large-scale training.

2. **Enabling Zero-Shot Generalization for Metric Depth:** State-of-the-art models that generalize well across diverse scenes (zero-shot) typically sacrifice metric accuracy, producing only affine-invariant depth. Conversely, models that produce metric depth do not generalize well. The authors aim to combine the benefits of large-scale data training (leading to strong generalization) with the ability to output true metric depth, enabling practical "in-the-wild" applications.

3. **Unlocking Downstream Applications:** The paper highlights that obtaining metric depth, rather than just relative depth, significantly benefits various computer vision applications. Specific motivations include:
 - **Single-Image Metrology:** The ability to measure real-world sizes and distances directly from a single photograph, crucial for fields like architecture, forensics, or even e-commerce.
 - **Improving Monocular SLAM:** Existing monocular Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems suffer from "scale drift," where the estimated trajectory and map accumulate errors in scale over time. Providing accurate per-frame metric depth can stabilize the scale, leading to more precise localization and denser, metrically accurate 3D maps.
 - **Large-Scale 3D Reconstruction:** Fusing metric depths from multiple views can yield consistent, real-world scaled 3D models of complex scenes.

By achieving truly zero-shot metric depth prediction, Metric3D seeks to elevate single-image 3D understanding from qualitative shape inference to quantitative real-world measurement, thereby expanding its utility across a broad spectrum of practical computer vision tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of Metric3D's methodology revolves around resolving the metric ambiguity caused by varying camera intrinsics, particularly the focal length, during large-scale training.

#### 3.1. Metric Ambiguity Analysis

The authors rigorously analyze the pinhole camera model. They observe:
- **Focal Length is Vital:** Focal length ($\hat{f}$) is proportional to the real-world depth ($d_a$) and inversely proportional to the imaging size ($\hat{S}'$), meaning $d_a = \hat{S} \cdot \hat{f} / \hat{S}'$. Changes in focal length drastically alter the perceived depth for a given imaging size. This is visually demonstrated by images of a chair captured at different distances and with different focal lengths appearing similar, leading to ambiguity if focal length is unknown.
- **Sensor and Pixel Size Irrelevance:** They mathematically demonstrate that while sensor size affects the Field of View (FOV) and pixel size affects image resolution, neither directly impacts the *metric depth estimation* itself, given the focal length and imaging size are known. This simplifies the problem, focusing efforts on focal length.
- **Assumption:** The method assumes the focal length of a training/test image is available (e.g., from image metadata), as estimating it from a single image is still a challenging and ill-posed problem.

#### 3.2. Canonical Camera Transformation Module (CSTM)

This is the central innovation to enable metric training on diverse camera models. The idea is to transform all training data into a "canonical camera space" where images are effectively perceived as captured by the same camera (with a predefined canonical focal length $f_c$). This reduces variance and allows the network to learn consistent metric relationships. Two methods are proposed:

- **CSTM Label (Transforming Ground-Truth Labels):**
 - During training, the ground-truth depth $D^*$ is scaled by a ratio $\omega_d = f_c / f$ (where $f$ is the original pixel-represented focal length) to get the canonical depth $D_c^* = \omega_d D^*$. The input image $I$ remains unchanged ($I_c = I$).
 - Inference: The predicted canonical depth $D_c$ is de-scaled by $1/\omega_d$ to recover the original metric depth $D = (1/\omega_d) D_c$.
- **CSTM Image (Transforming Input Images):**
 - During training, the input image $I$ is resized by a ratio $\omega_r = f_c / f$ to simulate the canonical camera's effect, becoming $I_c = T(I, \omega_r)$. The ground-truth depth $D^*$ is similarly resized (interpolated) but *not* scaled, becoming $D_c^* = T(D^*, \omega_r)$.
 - Inference: The predicted canonical depth $D_c$ is resized by $1/\omega_r$ to get $D = T(D_c, 1/\omega_r)$.

Crucially, **camera models are not explicitly encoded into the network architecture**. The CSTM is a pre-processing/post-processing step that utilizes the camera intrinsics *before* feeding data to a standard depth estimation network, making it easily pluggable into existing monocular models.

#### 3.3. Random Proposal Normalization Loss (RPNL)

To further boost depth accuracy, especially fine-grained details, RPNL is introduced. It's inspired by scale-shift invariant loss but addresses its limitation of potentially squeezing fine-grained differences when applied globally.
- Instead of normalizing the entire image, RPNL randomly crops multiple patches from the predicted and ground-truth depth maps.
- Median Absolute Deviation normalization is then applied independently to each paired patch.
- This forces the network to learn consistent local geometry and distribution, enhancing local contrast and detail.
- The overall loss function also combines RPNL with other common losses: scale-invariant logarithmic loss ($L_{silog}$), pairwise normal regression loss ($L_{PWN}$), and virtual normal loss ($L_{VNL}$).

#### 3.4. Network Architecture and Training Data

- **Architecture:** A UNet architecture with a ConvNext-large backbone, initialized with ImageNet-22K pre-trained weights.
- **Training Data:** A massive dataset of over 8 million images compiled from 11 diverse public RGB-D datasets (including autonomous driving, indoor scenes, stereo, and SfM-derived data). This collection features thousands of different camera models, which is critical for zero-shot generalization. The paper notes different methods for obtaining pseudo ground-truth depths for datasets lacking LiDAR (e.g., RaftStereo for stereo images, SfM for Mapillary PSD).
- **Training Details:** Trained on 48 A100 GPUs for 500K iterations, using AdamW optimizer and a polynomial learning rate decay. A data balancing strategy ensures equal representation for each dataset in a mini-batch. Images are randomly cropped to 512x960 pixels.

This comprehensive approach combines a theoretically sound solution to metric ambiguity with a powerful deep learning model trained on an unprecedented scale of diverse metric data, augmented by a novel loss function for detail preservation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Metric3D demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks and practical applications, effectively achieving its goal of zero-shot metric 3D prediction.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Zero-Shot Generalization

- **Metric Depth Benchmarks:** On standard metric depth datasets (NYUv2, KITTI) which were *unseen* during training, Metric3D achieved comparable or superior performance to existing SOTA methods that are specifically trained on these benchmarks.
- **Generalization to Diverse Unseen Datasets:** The model showed remarkable robustness on 6 additional unseen datasets (DIODE, iBIMS-1, 7Scenes, ETH3D, NuScenes), outperforming previous SOTA metric depth methods, which experienced significant performance drops on these novel scenes. This highlights Metric3D's superior generalization ability for true metric recovery.
- **Affine-Invariant Depth Benchmarks:** Even when evaluated on affine-invariant depth benchmarks (which typically align scale and shift to GT, making the task easier), Metric3D significantly outperformed all previous SOTA affine-invariant methods (e.g., MiDaS, LeReS, DPT) on 5 zero-shot datasets. This indicates that learning metric depth, when done correctly, also leads to superior affine-invariant geometry.
- **Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge:** The method officially won the championship in the 2nd Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge, providing external validation of its superior performance.

#### 4.2. Practical Applications and Impact

- **3D Scene Reconstruction:** On 9 unseen NYUv2 scenes, Metric3D produced significantly more accurate 3D point cloud reconstructions (lower Chamfer L1 distance, higher F-score) compared to multi-view (DPSNet) and single-view (LeReS, DPT, RCVD, SC-DepthV2) methods. Crucially, Metric3D achieved this without requiring per-frame scale alignment, demonstrating inherent metric consistency across views. Qualitative results showed much less noise and distortion.
- **Dense SLAM Mapping:** When naively integrated into Droid-SLAM (a SOTA monocular SLAM system), Metric3D's predicted metric depths drastically reduced scale drift. On the KITTI odometry benchmark, the translational RMS drift decreased significantly (e.g., from 33.9 to 1.44 on Seq 00), leading to much more accurate trajectories and denser, metrically correct 3D maps. Similar, though less pronounced, improvements were observed on ETH3D SLAM benchmarks.
- **Metrology in the Wild:** The method demonstrated the ability to accurately measure real-world object sizes from Flickr photos captured by various unseen cameras, using only their available metadata. This showcased its potential for practical metrology from casual "in-the-wild" images.
- **360-degree Scene Reconstruction:** The model successfully reconstructed consistent 3D point clouds from 360-degree camera rigs (e.g., NuScenes), despite varying camera intrinsics across views, highlighting its ability to handle complex multi-camera setups.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study Insights

- **Effectiveness of CSTM:** Ablation studies confirmed that both "CSTM Label" and "CSTM Image" methods are crucial for enabling metric depth prediction. Without CSTM, the model failed to converge during mixed metric dataset training. Both CSTM variants significantly outperformed a baseline without CSTM and a competing method (CamConvs) that implicitly encodes camera parameters.
- **Canonical Focal Length:** Experiments showed a canonical focal length of 1000 pixels yielded slightly better performance compared to 500 or 1500, indicating an optimal range for the canonical space.
- **RPNL Contribution:** The Random Proposal Normalization Loss consistently improved depth accuracy across various datasets compared to a baseline without it and the original full-image scale-shift invariant loss, validating its effectiveness in emphasizing local geometric details.

The findings unequivocally establish Metric3D as a groundbreaking advancement, pushing the boundaries of monocular depth estimation from relative understanding to precise metric reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Metric3D represents a significant leap forward in the field of single-image 3D vision, effectively breaking the long-standing barrier between zero-shot generalization and true metric accuracy. Its significance and potential impact are profound across several domains:

#### 5.1. Fundamental Advancement in Computer Vision

- **Solving Metric Ambiguity:** Metric3D provides a robust and elegant solution to the metric ambiguity problem inherent in single-image depth estimation. By explicitly addressing the impact of focal length through the Canonical Camera Transformation, it enables networks to learn real-world scale from diverse datasets, a capability previously elusive.
- **Combining Generalization with Accuracy:** The work successfully combines the benefits of large-scale data training (leading to robust generalization across scenes and camera types) with the ability to produce precise metric depth, a feat that prior state-of-the-art methods could not achieve simultaneously. This is a crucial step towards more truly intelligent and adaptable 3D perception systems.
- **Architectural Flexibility:** The plug-and-play nature of the CSTM, which doesn't require modifying the core network architecture, means its principles can be easily integrated into existing and future monocular depth estimation models, accelerating research and development in this area.

#### 5.2. Enabling Transformative Applications

- **Accurate Metrology from Casual Photos:** The demonstrated ability to measure real-world object sizes from arbitrary internet photos is revolutionary. This opens up possibilities for widespread "single-image metrology" in fields like construction, interior design, e-commerce (e.g., verifying product dimensions), forensics, and even personal use (e.g., measuring furniture for a room).
- **Robust Robotics and Autonomous Driving:** The significant reduction in scale drift for monocular SLAM systems is a game-changer. For robots and autonomous vehicles, maintaining accurate scale in 3D maps is vital for precise navigation, obstacle avoidance, and path planning. Metric3D can directly improve the reliability and safety of these systems without requiring additional, costly depth sensors.
- **High-Quality 3D Content Creation and Reconstruction:** For fields like virtual reality, augmented reality, and 3D modeling, Metric3D enables the reconstruction of consistent, metrically accurate 3D scenes from readily available images. This can democratize 3D content creation and improve the realism of digital environments.
- **Enhanced Image Understanding:** Beyond direct 3D applications, providing metric depth can enrich downstream tasks like object detection, scene understanding, and activity recognition, as these tasks can benefit from a more profound understanding of the spatial relationships and true sizes of objects in a scene.

#### 5.3. Paving the Way for Future Research

- **Less Reliance on Specialized Hardware:** By extracting highly accurate 3D information from standard 2D images, Metric3D reduces the dependency on specialized depth sensors (like LiDAR or RGB-D cameras) for many applications, making 3D perception more accessible and cost-effective.
- **Foundation for Multi-modal Fusion:** The accurate metric depth can serve as a strong prior or input for fusing with other sensor data, leading to even more robust and comprehensive scene understanding.
- **Ethical Considerations and Bias:** As the model generalizes to "in-the-wild" images, future work may need to consider potential biases in the training data related to camera models, scene types, or object distributions, ensuring fairness and robustness across diverse real-world scenarios.

In conclusion, Metric3D is a highly impactful contribution that pushes the boundaries of monocular 3D vision. By elegantly resolving the metric ambiguity problem and demonstrating its practical utility across various applications, it sets a new standard for zero-shot metric depth estimation and unlocks a plethora of new possibilities for understanding and interacting with the 3D world from single 2D images.
