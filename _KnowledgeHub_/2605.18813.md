# Composition of Memory Experts for Diffusion World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Composition of Memory Experts (CoME) framework enhances diffusion-based world models by distributing memory functions across specialized experts to improve temporal consistency and scalability. This approach leads to improved perceptual quality, with LPIPS reducing to 0.097 on the Memory Maze dataset, and better planning accuracy, achieving an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of 0.96 on the RECON benchmark, while operating at a fraction of the computational cost of monolithic architectures.

## Problem

- Existing world models struggle with a fundamental memory trade-off, where high-fidelity architectures (e.g., Transformers) incur quadratic computational costs for long contexts, while efficient ones (e.g., recurrent networks) lose detail over time.
- Maintaining robust cross-time consistency over long temporal horizons is challenging for future state prediction, hindering reliable planning and decision-making in reinforcement learning.
- Scaling context length naively for a single model is computationally prohibitive, leading to escalating training costs, instability, and impractical inference budgets.

## Method

- A diffusion-based world model framework, ==Composition of Memory Experts== (**CoME**), is introduced to decouple future-past consistency by distributing memory functions across specialized experts.
- Three distinct memory experts are instantiated: Short-Term Memory (STM) for local dynamics, Long-Term Memory (LTM) for episodic history via test-time finetuning with LoRA adapters, and Spatial Long-Term Memory (SLTM) for geometric coherence.
- A novel Product of Contrastive Experts (PoCE) formulation is developed to combine heterogeneous experts, effectively suppressing spurious distribution modes while preserving diversity and sharpening consensus.

## Results

- CoME achieved superior consistency and perceptual quality, evidenced by an LPIPS of **0.097**, SSIM of **0.892**, and PSNR of 23.07 on the Memory Maze dataset, outperforming a base Diffusion Transformer (LPIPS **0.209**, SSIM **0.771**, PSNR 19.16).
- The framework improved navigation planning accuracy on the RECON benchmark, yielding an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of **0.96** and Relative Pose Error (RPE) of **0.28**, surpassing existing navigation models.
- CoME demonstrated computational efficiency by achieving comparable or superior consistency at a fraction of the training and inference costs compared to brute-force scaling methods (e.g., a full-attention baseline requiring **60x** more training compute).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distributing memory functions across specialized experts can overcome the inherent memory trade-off in world models, enabling scalable and temporally consistent long-horizon predictions.
> - The Product of Contrastive Experts (PoCE) formulation provides a principled method for fusing heterogeneous memory experts, addressing the limitations of naive PoE by preventing mode collapse and preserving diversity.
> - Lightweight test-time finetuning using LoRA adapters for Long-Term Memory (LTM) efficiently injects episodic knowledge into diffusion models, allowing constant-time reuse of past experience without catastrophic forgetting or significant computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models (WMs) are designed to predict future states of an environment based on past observations, a capability critical for planning and decision-making in reinforcement learning (RL). Recent advancements, particularly in diffusion-based WMs, have shown progress in generating high-quality trajectories. However, existing WM architectures face a fundamental challenge related to memory: maintaining consistency with past observations over long temporal horizons.

Current approaches exhibit a trade-off. Transformer architectures, while capable of producing high-fidelity rollouts and preserving local detail, incur quadratic computational costs with increasing context length, limiting their scalability. Recurrent networks and state-space models (SSMs) scale more efficiently with context, but achieve this by compressing historical information into fixed-size hidden states, which can lead to a loss of detail and fidelity over extended periods. This results in a dilemma where increased temporal context improves prediction accuracy but leads to prohibitive computational demands and potential training instability.

This work addresses this established memory trade-off by proposing a departure from single-architecture solutions. Instead, it advocates for a distributed memory system, drawing inspiration from human cognition's separation of short-term and long-term memory. Within the landscape of diffusion models, the paper leverages their inherent ability for principled inference-time composition of heterogeneous experts, a technique previously explored for image generation and task planning. The integration of specialized memory modules (short-term, long-term, and spatial long-term) aims to overcome the limitations of monolithic architectures by allowing each component to handle different aspects of temporal conditioning efficiently and effectively. This approach positions the work as a contribution to the development of more scalable, robust, and consistent world models, particularly relevant for applications requiring long-horizon planning and navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the temporal consistency and scalability of diffusion-based world models by addressing the inherent memory trade-off in existing architectures. The authors aim to enable world models to predict plausible futures that remain consistent with both recent and distant past observations, without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges:

- **Memory Trade-off in Current World Models:** Existing architectures like Transformers struggle with quadratic scaling of computational costs as context length increases, limiting their ability to handle long histories. Recurrent and state-space models, while more efficient, compress information, leading to a loss of fidelity over time. This architectural bottleneck prevents world models from fully leveraging rich temporal context for accurate and consistent predictions.
- **Need for Robust Cross-Time Consistency:** In applications such as navigation and interactive reinforcement learning, agents must anticipate future states that are coherent with their past experiences (e.g., a visited room should not arbitrarily change). Maintaining such consistency is crucial for reliable planning and decision-making.
- **Limitations of Naïve Context Scaling:** Simply increasing the context window of a single model is not a sustainable solution due to escalating training costs, instability, and impractical inference budgets.
- **Absence of a Unified Memory Framework:** There is no single architecture that optimally handles diverse temporal demands, from fine local dynamics to long-term episodic recall and spatial coherence.
- **Challenges in Expert Composition:** While Product of Experts (PoE) frameworks allow combining knowledge, a naive application can lead to over-sharpening, reduced diversity, and mode collapse if experts share inconsistent modes.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose the following key objectives:

1. **Decouple Future-Past Consistency:** Instead of relying on a single architecture, they aim to distribute memory functions across a set of specialized experts, each designed for a specific temporal role.
2. **Integrate Heterogeneous Memory Models:** Develop a diffusion-based framework that allows for the principled combination of diverse memory experts.
3. **Introduce a Product of Contrastive Experts (PoCE) Formulation:** Design a novel composition mechanism that addresses the limitations of standard PoE by factoring out spurious distribution modes, thereby ensuring consistent and diverse predictions.
4. **Instantiate Complementary Memory Roles:** Define and implement three distinct memory experts:
 - **Short-Term Memory (STM):** To capture fine local dynamics from immediate past observations.
 - **Long-Term Memory (LTM):** To store episodic history efficiently in external diffusion weights via lightweight test-time finetuning, enabling constant-time reuse of past experience.
 - **Spatial Long-Term Memory (SLTM):** To enforce geometric and spatial coherence by grounding memory in spatial priors, crucial for tasks like navigation.
5. **Achieve Scalability and Efficiency:** Develop a compositional design that scales to long contexts without quadratic cost, offering better consistency at a fraction of the training and inference computational expense of brute-force scaling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Composition of Memory Experts (CoME), augments video world models with the ability to efficiently condition generation on specific past histories while preserving the diversity and generalization capacity of a pretrained prior. It leverages diffusion models' inherent support for principled inference-time composition.

#### 3.1. Background: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) and Product of Experts (PoE)

The approach is built upon Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs), which learn a data distribution by reversing a fixed forward diffusion process. A neural network, trained to estimate the noise added at each step, allows for iterative denoising to generate data. The score function of the data distribution is implicitly learned through this process, crucial for the compositional framework.

Product of Experts (PoE) combines knowledge from multiple models by defining a composite distribution as the normalized product of individual distributions. In diffusion models, this translates to composing multiple pretrained models by summing their score functions, allowing for focused generation consistent with all contributing distributions.

#### 3.2. Composition of Memory Experts

The core idea is to decompose the past history `M` into subsets of frames `{c_i}` for various experts. Each expert `p_i(x)` is a diffusion model conditioned on its specific context `c_i`. The overall conditional distribution `p(x | M)` is then approximated by the product of these expert distributions: `p(x | M) ∝ Π_i p_i(x)`.

#### 3.2.1. Product of Contrastive Experts (PoCE)

To address the issue of over-sharpening and reduced diversity that can arise from naive PoE, the authors introduce a Product of Contrastive Experts (PoCE) formulation. For each expert `p_i(x)`, a contrastive expert `˜p_i(x)` is defined as `˜p_i(x) ∝ p_i(x)^α_i * p_i(x)^(1-α_i)`, where `α_i > 1` are contrasting coefficients, and `p_i(x)` is the expert's unconditional baseline distribution (i.e., without conditioning on `c_i`).

This formulation is designed to suppress spurious modes (regions of high likelihood that are not genuinely consistent with the desired distribution) without distorting the local shapes of valid modes. A key proposition states that under assumptions of disjoint support for KDE components, the contrastive expert reweights mixture components rather than narrowing their kernels. This means modes that are genuinely emphasized by the conditional expert compared to its unconditional baseline (high contrast ratio `ρ_k`) are amplified, while spurious modes (low `ρ_k`) are suppressed, with the degree of separation controlled by `α_i`.

#### 3.3. Instantiating Multiple Memory Experts

The framework instantiates three specialized experts:

1. **Short-Term Memory (STM):**
 - **Role:** Captures local dynamics and fine details from a recent context window (`c_ST`, 10-100 images).
 - **Implementation:** A diffusion model (e.g., a Diffusion Transformer with moderately extended context length) `p_ϕ(x | c_ST)`.
 - **Contrastive Baseline:** The unconditional model `p_ϕ(x | ∅)`.

2. **Long-Term Memory (LTM):**
 - **Role:** Stores episodic knowledge and ensures consistency across hundreds of frames, addressing the scalability issues of STM.
 - **Implementation:** A separate diffusion model `p_ψ(c_LT)(x | c)` where parameters `ψ(c_LT)` are adapted through test-time finetuning on a long-term history (`c_LT`, 100-1000 images). This involves `n_mem` gradient steps using the diffusion loss, applied to low-rank adapters (LoRA layers) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and preserve generalization capacity. The base diffusion prior `p_ψ(∅)` (without specific long-term conditioning) is used for finetuning.
 - **Contrastive Baseline:** The model `p_ψ(∅)(x | ∅)`.

3. **Spatial Long-Term Memory (SLTM):**
 - **Role:** Improves long-range consistency by disambiguating locations and reducing drift in repetitive or visually ambiguous environments through spatial priors.
 - **Implementation:** A diffusion model `p_λ(x | S)` conditioned on auxiliary spatial signals `S` (e.g., camera poses, point maps) extracted from the history `M` using an encoder `Enc_λ(M)`.
 - **Contrastive Baseline:** The model `p_λ(x | ∅)`.

#### 3.4. Composition of Memory Experts (CoME) Formulation

The full CoME model combines these experts and a pretrained prior `p_θ` (a standard image-to-video diffusion model for immediate past `c`) into a unified distribution:

`p_CoME(x | c, c_ST, c_LT, S) ∝ [p_θ(x | ∅)^(1-α_0) * p_θ(x | c)^α_0] * [p_ϕ(x | ∅)^(1-α_1) * p_ϕ(x | c_ST)^α_1] * [p_ψ(∅)(x | c)^(1-α_2) * p_ψ(c_LT)(x | c)^α_2] * [p_λ(x | ∅)^(1-α_3) * p_λ(x | S)^α_3]`

The coefficients `α_i ≥ 1` balance unconditional and conditional contributions.

#### 3.5. Sampling and Test-Time Training

During sampling, the single-model score function in the reverse diffusion update is replaced with the joint score of all experts. This additive score, calculated from the weighted combination of expert noise predictors, integrates directly into the denoising process. For LTM, at the beginning of a rollout or periodically, the LoRA adapters are updated online using mini-batches from the current long-term context, injecting episodic knowledge efficiently. This establishes a recurrent loop where STM provides short-term fidelity, LTM maintains evolving long-term consistency, and SLTM resolves global ambiguities, creating a stable online generation process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CoME was conducted across synthetic and real-world datasets using metrics such as LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR for perceptual quality and reconstruction, and ATE, RPE for navigation planning.

#### 4.1. Consistency with Past Observed Frames

- **Quantitative Performance:** On the Memory Maze dataset, CoME achieved LPIPS of 0.097, SSIM of 0.892, and PSNR of 23.07, outperforming a base Diffusion Transformer (DiT) (0.209, 0.771, 19.16) and various specialized baselines (Sliding, SSM, Full attention transformer). Individual memory components (+STM, +LTM, +SLTM) consistently improved metrics over the base model, with the combination of STM and LTM showing significant gains (LPIPS 0.114, SSIM 0.862, PSNR 22.32).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The Full-attention baseline required approximately 60x the compute of STM and 12x that of the base model for training, and also over 3x more training steps. CoME achieved comparable or superior consistency at a fraction of these training and inference costs, demonstrating efficiency through specialized memory experts.
- **Context Length Impact:** Increasing the context length for LTM adaptation (from 50 to 480 frames) consistently improved perceptual quality (lower LPIPS), with no saturation observed up to 500 frames.

#### 4.2. Effect on Planning

- **Navigation Accuracy:** On the RECON benchmark, CoME improved planning accuracy, achieving an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of 0.96 and Relative Pose Error (RPE) of 0.28. This outperformed existing navigation models like GNM (ATE 1.87, RPE 0.73), NOMAD (ATE 1.93, RPE 0.52), and NWM (ATE 1.13, RPE 0.35). Even lightweight additions like STM provided tangible gains (ATE 1.05, RPE 0.32).

#### 4.3. Consistency with a Continuous Stream of Observations

- **Streaming Evaluation:** In experiments simulating continuous observation streams on Memory Maze, CoME demonstrated incremental improvements in consistency across iterations. The STM provided initial gains, while the LTM showed a compounding effect, successfully storing information in its weights and leveraging it for subsequent generations.
- **Recall Ability:** On the RealEstate10K dataset, CoME consistently outperformed baseline methods (Base, HG-v, HG-t) in semantic recall. For example, CoME achieved an LPIPS of 0.359 compared to 0.405 for the Base model, along with higher PSNR and SSIM. Qualitatively, CoME accurately retrieved exact frames and preserved consistent scene views during reversed trajectories, unlike baseline models that generated plausible but inconsistent frames.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Contrastive Expert Composition (PoCE):** The contrastive formulation was found to be essential. Applying PoCE improved the performance of individual memory experts, preventing the naive Product of Experts from collapsing or canceling out consistency gains. Without contrastive weighting, stacking multiple experts often failed to outperform the base model, sometimes even reducing consistency (e.g., LPIPS 0.192 for 'All' without contrastive vs. 0.097 with contrastive).
- **LTM Architecture and Context Frames:** Increasing the LoRA rank for LTM adaptation consistently improved performance, suggesting that LoRA provides effective implicit regularization. Full fine-tuning tended to overfit unless the context was highly diverse. Longer contexts further enhanced benefits, indicating the scalability of the LTM design.

#### 4.5. Additional Experiments

- **Memory-Cards (Discrete Recall):** CoME achieved 79.2% recall accuracy for occluded tiles, significantly outperforming a standard diffusion baseline (13% chance level).
- **DMLab (Deterministic Environment):** CoME improved LPIPS from 0.558 (baseline SD) to 0.456 (18% improvement), showing benefits in deterministic recall tasks.
- **Langevin Correction Steps:** Langevin dynamics steps during sampling did not yield significant improvements in generation quality or memory recall, suggesting the current approach is robust without them.
- **Minecraft (Oasis-style Setting):** CoME consistently improved perceptual (LPIPS 0.369 vs. Base 0.408) and reconstruction metrics (SSIM 0.574 vs. Base 0.450; PSNR 17.78 vs. Base 16.19) over base and single-expert variants, demonstrating generalizability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a novel paradigm for building and operating memory-augmented diffusion world models, offering a solution to the fundamental memory trade-off faced by existing architectures. The significance of this research lies in several key contributions:

- **Overcoming Memory Bottlenecks:** By decoupling future-past consistency and distributing memory across specialized experts, the proposed framework avoids the quadratic computational costs and fidelity loss associated with monolithic models. This enables world models to scale to longer temporal contexts without incurring prohibitive computational budgets or training instability.
- **Enhanced Temporal Consistency and Recall:** The compositional design, particularly through the integration of Short-Term Memory (STM), Long-Term Memory (LTM) via test-time finetuning, and Spatial Long-Term Memory (SLTM), significantly improves the ability of world models to generate futures consistent with both recent and distant past observations. This is critical for maintaining scene coherence and object persistence over extended periods, as demonstrated by improved LPIPS, SSIM, and PSNR metrics across various datasets, and specifically by the enhanced recall ability.
- **Introduction of Product of Contrastive Experts (PoCE):** This novel probabilistic formulation provides a principled method for fusing heterogeneous memory experts. It effectively tames spurious distribution modes that can arise from naive product-of-experts approaches, thereby preserving diversity in generated outputs while sharpening consensus. This mechanism is crucial for the stable and effective integration of multiple experts.
- **Improved Performance in Downstream Tasks:** The enhanced temporal consistency directly translates to tangible gains in practical applications, particularly in reinforcement learning navigation planning tasks. CoME's superior performance in Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) on benchmarks like RECON indicates its potential to facilitate more reliable and effective decision-making for agents in complex environments.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The approach achieves comparable or better consistency at a fraction of the training and inference cost compared to brute-force scaling methods (e.g., full-attention transformers). The use of lightweight LoRA adapters for LTM's test-time finetuning ensures efficient online adaptation without catastrophic forgetting or significant computational overhead.

The potential impact of this work extends to various domains:

- **Advanced AI Agents:** It could lead to the development of more intelligent and robust AI agents capable of long-horizon planning, complex task execution, and coherent interaction within dynamic environments.
- **Realistic Simulation and Content Generation:** Improved temporal consistency in world models has implications for generating more realistic and coherent synthetic data for training, as well as for applications in creative content generation (e.g., long-form video synthesis).
- **Robotics:** The ability to maintain spatial and temporal memory over long durations is vital for autonomous navigation, mapping, and interaction in real-world robotic systems.
- **Foundation Models:** CoME provides a generalizable framework that can be layered on top of existing diffusion-based world models, enabling them to tackle more complex memory-intensive tasks.

Future work directions include exploring explicit temporal dependencies in LTM, richer conditioning strategies, and more exhaustive forms of spatial conditioning to further enhance generative fidelity and downstream task performance.
