# Scenario Generation for Testing of Autonomous Driving Systems Using Real-World Failure Records

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT developed a framework that automates the generation of diverse and realistic autonomous driving test scenarios by leveraging real-world crash records, including categorical data and natural language narratives. This methodology creates system-specific scenarios for simulation-based testing, which revealed specific vulnerabilities and behaviors of an IDM policy.

## Problem

- Existing real-world testing relies on manually designed, static scenario templates that struggle to adapt to specific Autonomous Driving Systems (ADS) or uncover non-trivial failures.
- Current simulation-based testing often uses pre-defined scenario templates, limiting exploration to numerical variations within known scenarios and failing to discover diverse scenario definitions.
- A systematic method was lacking to extract diverse scenario definitions and contextual details from historical real-world ADS crash records, especially natural language narratives, for system-specific simulation testing.

## Method

- A modular, ==LLM-based pipeline== was developed to extract both ==meta-variables== (categorical features) and natural language narratives from historical ADS crash records.
- A ==three-step LLM-based process== (paraphrasing, scenario generation, fine-tuning) transforms narratives and templates into structured, system-specific, and critical test scenarios compatible with a target simulator.
- A ==k-means clustering== approach, using a ==hybrid feature representation== (one-hot encoded templates combined with narrative embeddings), was employed to select diverse and representative scenarios within a limited testing budget.

## Results

- The pipeline generated **20** diverse scenarios from NHTSA crash records, successfully capturing **11** out of **14** naturally occurring real-world scenario templates.
- Generated scenarios showed high accuracy in matching intended meta-variables (**17** out of **20** completely matched) and demonstrated significant improvement in criticality after fine-tuning (minimum Euclidean gap reduced for **14** out of **16** scenarios requiring it).
- Testing an IDM policy on the MetaDrive simulator with the generated scenarios revealed specific vulnerabilities, including conservative braking leading to oscillatory movements, reduced reactivity to small obstacles like cones, and susceptibility to lateral collisions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining categorical meta-variables with natural language narratives from real-world crash data enables the generation of a richer and more diverse set of test scenarios than either source alone.
> - Large Language Models can effectively translate qualitative crash narratives into quantitative, executable simulation scenario parameters and iteratively fine-tune them to induce criticality.
> - Clustering crash records based on both structural (meta-variables) and contextual (narratives) information allows for efficient coverage of diverse failure types, including rare ones, within a fixed testing budget.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The safety assurance of Autonomous Driving Systems (ADS) through rigorous pre-deployment testing and failure discovery is a critical area of research. Current approaches generally fall into several categories, each with distinct methodologies and limitations.

Traditional real-world testing often relies on manually designed scenario templates, derived from expert knowledge and historical failure data, as seen in regulatory testing protocols like those from Euro NCAP and NHTSA. These templates represent distinct failure conditions, parameterized by a limited set of variables (e.g., vehicle speed, map types). While these methods provide a user-centric baseline and are compatible with real-time testing constraints, they involve substantial manual effort, are inherently static, and may not fully adapt to stress-test specific ADS or uncover non-trivial failures that evolving sensor capabilities and functionalities might introduce. The paper notes that a vehicle validated under such tests might still fail in real-world conditions due to an undiscovered vulnerability.

An alternative is simulation-based testing, which has seen the emergence of adaptive scenario generation frameworks. These methods primarily leverage mathematical tools (e.g., Bayesian optimization, MCMC sampling, gradient-based optimization) to efficiently search for optimal numerical scenario parameters within a pre-defined scenario template. However, a significant limitation of these approaches is their assumption of access to these pre-defined templates, which are typically user-defined and not directly informed by the rich, granular details of real-world crash records. This means they excel at exploring numerical variations within a fixed scenario but may miss the diversity of scenario definitions themselves.

More recent research has explored multi-fidelity approaches that combine simulation and real-world testing, often utilizing Bayesian Experimental Design (BED) formulations and considering the cost associated with different testing fidelities. These, too, often operate under the assumption of pre-defined scenario parameterization.

This work addresses a critical gap in the existing landscape: the lack of a principled, automated method to extract diverse scenario definitions directly from historical real-world ADS crash records and adapt them for system-specific simulation testing. By leveraging the categorical and contextual information, including natural language narratives, from these records, the proposed framework complements existing numerical optimization and sampling-based simulation methods. It focuses on the upstream task of *generating the scenario templates themselves*, rather than solely optimizing parameters within fixed templates. This positions the work as foundational for more realistic and comprehensive ADS testing, bridging the gap between historical crash data and actionable simulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the critical need to ensure the safe on-road behavior of Autonomous Driving Systems (ADS) through robust pre-deployment testing and effective failure discovery. Despite advancements, current testing paradigms exhibit several limitations that this work aims to address.

**Motivation:**

1. **Limitations of Existing Testing Practices:**
 - **Manual Effort and Static Nature:** Present real-world ADS falsification relies heavily on manually designed scenario templates. This process is time-consuming, costly, and results in static test suites that require periodic revision as ADS technology evolves. These static suites may not effectively stress-test specific vehicles or systems, potentially leaving vulnerabilities undiscovered.
 - **Constrained Scenario Representation:** Current simulation-based testing, while efficient for searching optimal scenario parameters, often assumes a fixed, pre-defined scenario representation. This means they are effective at exploring numerical variations within a known scenario type but do not facilitate the discovery of novel or contextually distinct failure scenarios.
 - **Overlooking Rich Real-World Data:** Historical ADS crash records are a rich and reliable source of real-world failure conditions, capturing both common and non-trivial incidents. However, there is a lack of principled methods to systematically extract scenario definitions and contextual details from these records, especially their natural language narratives, and translate them into actionable test scenarios for specific ADS. For example, a standard "Car-to-Car Rear braking (CCRb)" template might vary vehicle speeds but ignore crucial contextual factors like work zones, which significantly impact failure likelihood.

2. **Need for Adaptive, Data-Driven Scenario Generation:** To overcome these limitations, there is a clear need for a method that can:
 - Systematically utilize the wealth of information in historical crash data.
 - Generate diverse and realistic failure scenarios that cover a broader spectrum of real-world conditions.
 - Adapt these generated scenarios to the specific testing constraints and capabilities of a given ADS or simulator.
 - Reduce the substantial manual effort currently invested in scenario design.

**Key Objectives:**

Based on these motivations, the authors establish the following objectives:

1. **Develop an Automated Scenario Generation Procedure:** To create a pipeline that utilizes both the categorical features and the incident-specific natural language narratives from real-world ADS crash records to design realistic testing scenarios.
2. **Propose a Method for System-Specific Adaptation:** To enable the adaptation of available crash records into test scenarios compatible with a specific ADS and its testing environment (e.g., a particular simulator with defined non-ego agent policies). This involves leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to transform narrative descriptions into system-specific scenario definitions.
3. **Validate the Approach:** To demonstrate the end-to-end scenario generation pipeline by applying it to NHTSA ADS crash records and generating a diverse set of scenarios for testing an IDM policy on the MetaDrive simulator for autonomous navigation and obstacle avoidance. This validation aims to show that the approach can generate accurate and diverse scenarios that align with testing conditions and reveal interesting system failures within a limited testing budget.

In essence, the work aims to create a principled, data-driven, and adaptable framework for generating meaningful test scenarios, thereby enhancing the rigor and efficiency of ADS safety validation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology establishes a scenario generation pipeline that systematically leverages real-world ADS crash records, integrating categorical data with natural language narratives through a modular LLM-based approach. The overall process consists of two main stages: (1) designing meta variables from crash records and (2) an LLM-based scenario generation process.

#### 3.1. Extracting Information from Historical Failure Data

The initial step involves parsing historical ADS crash records, specifically the NHTSA ADS crash records for 2021, to extract relevant information. This information is categorized as follows:

- **Meta Variables:** These are categorical variables that represent high-level aspects of a crash. Examples include ‘Road Type’ (Intersection, Circle, Highway/Street), ‘Work zone’ (True, False), and ‘Pre-crash movement Crash Partner (CP)/Self Vehicle (SV)’ (Proceeding Straight, Making Left Turn, Making Right Turn). The selection of these meta variables is based on their compatibility with the scenario generation capabilities of the target simulator and system. Unique combinations of these meta variables define distinct "scenario templates," providing an interpretable categorization of failure scenarios.
- **Narrative:** This refers to the natural language description of each incident available in the crash records. Narratives provide granular, contextual details about ego and non-ego trajectories, their relative locations, and other qualitative aspects of the crash scene, which complement the high-level meta variables.

#### 3.2. LLM-Based Scenario Generation

After defining scenario templates using meta variables, a three-step LLM-based pipeline is employed to transform the narratives and templates into system-specific failure scenarios:

1. **Paraphrasing a Narrative:**
 - **Purpose:** The raw narratives often contain irrelevant contextual details (e.g., exact location, time) or descriptions that are not directly actionable for scenario generation, or they might be unsuitable for the specific testing system. This step refines the narrative.
 - **Process:** An LLM agent acts as a "paraphrasing agent," taking the original narrative and system-specific constraints as input. It filters out non-essential information and generates a concise, relevant narrative that retains critical details for scenario reconstruction. This agent also helps in adapting the narrative to ensure feasibility within the chosen system (e.g., specifying non-ego agent behaviors compatible with simulator policies) and can generate plausible alternative narratives if the original is unsuited for direct replication (e.g., avoiding rear-end collisions of the ego vehicle if the policy under test is for braking).

2. **Scenario Generation Using Narrative:**
 - **Purpose:** To translate the paraphrased natural language narrative and the associated scenario template into a structured, executable scenario definition compatible with the target simulator.
 - **Process:** A second LLM agent, the "scenario generation agent," receives the paraphrased narrative and the scenario template as input. It then generates a structured scenario specification (e.g., a JSON format for the MetaDrive simulator). This specification includes concrete parameters such as map type (e.g., 'I' for intersection), ego vehicle target speed, and specific policies for non-ego agents (e.g., 'Sudden Braking', 'Accelerate-then-brake', 'IDM Policy'), along with their initial longitudinal and lateral gaps and lane offsets. The prompt for this LLM guides it to produce a schema directly usable by the simulator.

3. **System Specific Fine-Tuning:**
 - **Purpose:** To refine the initially generated scenario parameters to specifically induce a collision, near-collision, or other undesirable system behavior (e.g., abrupt braking, oscillation) while remaining consistent with the narrative.
 - **Process:** A "fine-tuning agent" (another LLM) takes the generated scenario and feedback from initial rollouts (e.g., metrics like minimum Euclidean gap, time at minimum gap). It adjusts numerical parameters like speeds, initial positions, or policy-specific values to make the scenario more critical. The agent is instructed to avoid "degenerate solutions" where collisions occur immediately or at the very end of the simulation, ensuring that the induced failure is meaningful and aligns with the crash narrative.

#### 3.3. Cost-Aware Scenario Design and Testing (Clustering)

To ensure diversity and efficient coverage within a limited testing budget, the authors employ a clustering approach:

- **Clustering Method:** The collection of crash records (or rather, the scenarios derived from them) is clustered using k-means. In this work, `k=20` clusters were used.
- **Feature Representation:** Each crash record is represented by a combined feature vector. This vector includes:
 - One-hot encoding of the 14 observed scenario templates (derived from meta variables).
 - A 6-dimensional vector embedding of the crash narratives, generated using `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` and compressed with Principal Component Analysis (PCA).
- **Purpose:** This hybrid representation captures both the categorical aspects and the contextual nuances of the crash data. By generating one representative synthetic scenario for each cluster, the approach aims to cover a wide range of failure types, including rare ones, while adhering to budget constraints and promoting diversity.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a data-driven, automated, and adaptable generation of diverse and realistic scenarios for testing ADS, overcoming limitations of purely manual or purely numerical optimization-based approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research successfully implemented and validated its scenario generation pipeline using real-world ADS crash records from NHTSA, testing an IDM policy on the MetaDrive simulator.

#### 4.1. Data Extraction and Scenario Template Distribution

- **Dataset Filtering:** From an initial 2295 NHTSA crash records, the dataset was refined to 1911 by removing entries with redacted narratives. Further filtering for reproducible pre-crash movements (Proceeding Straight, Making Left Turn, Making Right Turn) reduced the dataset to 235 relevant crashes.
- **Scenario Templates:** This process yielded 25 permissible scenario templates based on combinations of ‘Road Type’, ‘Pre-crash movement CP’, ‘Pre-crash movement SV’, and ‘Work zone’ meta variables. However, only 14 of these 25 templates were observed in the actual crash data.
- **Distribution Analysis:** The distribution of these scenario templates was found to be non-uniform. The most frequently occurring template was "Intersection, Proceeding Straight (SV), Proceeding Straight (CP), False (Work Zone)," accounting for 104 out of 235 records. Conversely, scenarios involving a 'Work Zone True' variable were rare, representing only 9 out of 235 crashes, highlighting their underrepresentation in the default data.

#### 4.2. Scenario Design and Clustering for Diversity

- **Simulator Compatibility:** The MetaDrive simulator supported various map types (I, T, O, C, S) and allowed for the definition of non-ego agent policies, including 'Sudden' braking, 'Accelerate-then-brake', and 'IDM' nominal policies, along with parameters like target speeds and initial longitudinal/lateral gaps.
- **Clustering Effectiveness:** The crash records were clustered into 20 groups using k-means, combining one-hot encoded scenario templates with a PCA-compressed 6-dimensional embedding of the narratives. This approach effectively grouped scenarios that were both rare and frequent, corresponded to different spatio-temporal types, and encoded specific contextual details from the narratives.
 - The clustering successfully captured 11 out of the 14 naturally occurring scenario templates in the historical records.
 - The inclusion of narratives in the clustering process demonstrated additional benefits, such as segregating contextually different scenarios that might share the same high-level meta variables and aiding in handling narratives that initially lacked incident-relevant information (e.g., Cluster 3).

#### 4.3. Scenario Generation Accuracy and Fine-Tuning (Addressing Q1)

- **Accuracy of Generation:** Out of the 20 scenarios generated from the clusters, 17 completely matched their intended scenario template meta variables. For the remaining 3 scenarios, minor discrepancies were observed (e.g., incorrect CP movement direction in Clusters 6 and 18, or an erroneous 'Work Zone' presence in Cluster 11).
- **Narrative Alignment:** The generated CP movements were found to align accurately with the details provided in the paraphrased narratives (e.g., Fig. 2).
- **Fine-tuning Impact:** The LLM-based fine-tuning agent significantly improved the criticality of scenarios. For 14 out of the 16 scenarios that required fine-tuning, a single iteration resulted in a substantial reduction in the minimum Euclidean gap between ego and non-ego vehicles (Table 4), indicating the agent's effectiveness in inducing closer interactions or collisions.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness for AV Testing (Addressing Q2)

- The pipeline successfully generated a diverse set of "interesting" scenarios, including both direct collisions and near-collision events, as indicated by the minimum gap metrics across all 20 scenarios (Table 4). This demonstrates the framework's capability to produce valuable test cases for ADS validation.

#### 4.5. Learnings About the Underlying System (IDM Policy) (Addressing Q3)

Testing the IDM policy on the MetaDrive simulator with the generated scenarios revealed specific behaviors and potential vulnerabilities:

- **Conservative Braking:** For scenarios involving a Crash Partner (CP) proceeding straight, the IDM policy effectively maintained a safe distance and avoided collisions. However, this often resulted in undesirable early braking and oscillatory movements (Fig. 4), indicating an overly cautious or jerky response that could impact passenger comfort or traffic flow in real-world driving.
- **Obstacle Awareness:** The IDM policy exhibited reduced reactivity to smaller obstacles, such as cones in work zones. In scenarios with a 'Work Zone True' setting, the ego vehicle frequently collided with cones (Fig. 5), suggesting a limitation in its perception or avoidance strategies for less significant, yet critical, road elements.
- **Lateral vs. Longitudinal Control:** While the policy successfully avoided head-on collisions in straightforward longitudinal motions, it demonstrated vulnerabilities in lateral motion scenarios, leading to side collisions (Fig. 2). This indicates a potential imbalance in the policy's robustness across different collision types.

These findings highlight the utility of the proposed scenario generation approach in not only creating test cases but also in effectively probing and identifying specific strengths and weaknesses of an ADS policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the methodology for testing Autonomous Driving Systems (ADS) by offering an automated, data-driven, and adaptable approach to scenario generation. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Reduced Manual Effort and Increased Efficiency:** The primary impact is the automation of scenario design, which traditionally demands substantial manual effort from experts. By leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to process natural language narratives and categorical features from historical crash records, the pipeline significantly reduces the time and cost associated with crafting realistic and diverse test scenarios. This increased efficiency allows for more frequent and comprehensive testing cycles.

2. **Enhanced Realism and Diversity of Test Scenarios:** The methodology directly taps into real-world failure data, including detailed crash narratives, to generate scenarios. This ensures that the generated test cases are grounded in actual incidents, covering both common and non-trivial failure conditions that might be missed by purely expert-driven or purely mathematical parameter exploration methods. The clustering approach further guarantees a diverse test suite, representing a broad spectrum of accident types and contextual specifics, thereby leading to more thorough system validation.

3. **System-Specific Adaptation and Stress-Testing:** Unlike static, hand-designed test suites, this approach provides a mechanism to adapt scenarios to the specific capabilities and constraints of a given ADS and its simulation environment. This "system-specific fine-tuning" allows for tailored stress-testing, exposing vulnerabilities that might otherwise remain undiscovered. The demonstrated ability to uncover undesirable behaviors (e.g., oscillatory movements, poor cone avoidance, lateral collision susceptibility) in the tested IDM policy exemplifies this impact.

4. **Complementary to Existing Testing Frameworks:** The generated scenario templates and parameterized scenarios can serve as a valuable input for existing advanced testing methods, such as Bayesian Optimization, MCMC sampling, or gradient-based optimization. This work provides the initial, diverse scenario definitions over which these methods can then efficiently search for worst-case numerical parameters, thereby creating a more complete and powerful testing ecosystem.

5. **Foundation for Future Research in AI and ADS Testing:**
 - **Synthetic Narrative Generation:** The successful application of LLMs for paraphrasing and scenario generation opens avenues for generating synthetic failure narratives in situations where real-world data might be sparse or non-existent. This could enable adaptive exploration of novel scenario templates.
 - **Visual Condition Testing:** The framework could be extended to generate visual conditions for testing vision-based ADS policies, which is a critical area for comprehensive validation.
 - **Bridging Data and Simulation:** The research contributes to bridging the gap between qualitative real-world crash data and quantitative simulation environments, fostering a more integrated approach to ADS development and safety assurance.

While acknowledging limitations such as the lack of a unified definition for "coverage" and restricted control over ego vehicle movements with fixed policies, the paper establishes a robust foundation for a new paradigm in ADS testing. By systematically transforming accident records into actionable test scenarios, it promises to make ADS validation more realistic, efficient, and ultimately, more effective in ensuring on-road safety.
