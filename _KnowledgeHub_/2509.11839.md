# TrajBooster: Boosting Humanoid Whole-Body Manipulation via Trajectory-Centric Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> TrajBooster introduces a framework that uses end-effector trajectories to transfer whole-body manipulation skills from large wheeled-humanoid datasets to bipedal humanoids, addressing data scarcity for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The approach enables a bipedal robot to perform complex tasks with higher success rates and improved generalization using significantly less real-world fine-tuning data.

## Problem

- Bipedal humanoid robots face a severe scarcity of large-scale, high-quality whole-body manipulation demonstration data for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Traditional teleoperation data collection is costly and yields limited diversity, hindering VLA model adaptation to the nuanced action spaces of new humanoid platforms.
- Existing cross-embodiment learning methods often do not provide scalable solutions for transferring full-body manipulation skills between significantly different robot morphologies, such as from wheeled to bipedal humanoids.

## Method

- A real-to-sim-to-real pipeline extracts 6D end-effector trajectories from existing wheeled-humanoid datasets (==Agibot-World==) and maps them to the workspace of a target bipedal humanoid (Unitree G1).
- A hierarchical retargeting model, trained in the Isaac Gym simulator using a Harmonized Online DAgger algorithm, converts these low-dimensional trajectories into high-dimensional, feasible whole-body actions for the Unitree G1.
- The VLA model (GR00T N1.5) is first post-pre-trained on this large synthetic dataset of retargeted actions, then fine-tuned with a minimal amount of real-world teleoperation data (approximately 10 minutes) to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- The Harmonized Online DAgger method for retargeting achieved superior tracking performance in simulation, with the lowest Mean Absolute Error for wrist position (**2.851** cm) and rotation (**6.231** degrees) compared to other training baselines.
- The VLA model with post-pre-training (PPT) and only 3K steps of real-world fine-tuning achieved higher task success rates (e.g., **100%** for 'Pick Mickey Mouse') than a model without PPT trained with 10K real-world steps.
- PPT significantly enhanced trajectory generalization, enabling **80%** success for out-of-distribution object placements where the non-PPT model failed (**0%**), and demonstrated zero-shot transfer of a novel task ('Pass the Water').

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - End-effector trajectories serve as an effective, morphology-agnostic interface for abstracting and transferring complex whole-body manipulation skills across robots with diverse physical structures.
> - A hierarchical whole-body control policy, specifically a composite arm-worker-manager structure, can efficiently translate high-level trajectory goals into robust low-level whole-body actions for bipedal humanoids.
> - Post-pre-training VLA models with extensive retargeted simulation data significantly accelerates real-world adaptation, allowing for robust performance and enhanced generalization with substantially less robot-specific data collection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen notable advancements, particularly with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that enable robots to perform tasks based on visual and linguistic instructions. Humanoid robots are a significant focus within this area due to their potential for operating in human-centric environments.

Current VLA research in humanoid robotics has predominantly focused on either broad locomotion tasks in complex environments or fine-grained manipulation confined to tabletop scenarios. While wheeled humanoid robots have demonstrated proficiency in coordinated whole-body movements for household tasks that require a wide operational workspace (e.g., squatting, reaching across varying heights), bipedal humanoids face distinct challenges. Their need to maintain dynamic balance with the lower body while manipulating with the upper body makes wide-range, whole-body manipulation particularly complex.

A critical bottleneck in advancing bipedal humanoid manipulation, especially for VLA models, is the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality demonstration data. Traditional teleoperation pipelines are resource-intensive, requiring specialized infrastructure and expert operators, resulting in limited datasets that often lack diversity. This data scarcity impedes the ability of VLAs to effectively align with the action spaces of new humanoid platforms during post-training. While pretraining on heterogeneous robot datasets offers some benefits, it has not yet sufficiently addressed the need for extensive, humanoid-relevant, whole-body demonstrations.

Cross-embodiment learning aims to transfer knowledge between robots with different physical morphologies. Previous efforts in this domain have explored various strategies, including mitigating perceptual differences through techniques like inpainting or physics-based rendering. Others have investigated embodiment-invariant action abstractions, such as latent action representations or trajectory-based methods that map human poses to robot trajectories. However, these action abstraction methods often concentrate on dexterous hand-object interactions or are limited to quadruped robots, not scaling directly to full-body motion transfer for bipedal humanoids.

This work positions itself at the intersection of VLA models, humanoid whole-body control, and cross-embodiment learning. It aims to bridge the gap in bipedal humanoid manipulation by leveraging abundant data from more mature wheeled-humanoid platforms. Specifically, it addresses the challenge of transferring complex, whole-body manipulation skills across distinct robot morphologies, a limitation not fully resolved by prior cross-embodiment approaches. The paper proposes an end-effector trajectory-centric approach to facilitate this transfer, which, to the authors' knowledge, represents the first application of extensive retargeted action data for fine-tuning a VLA model to achieve bipedal humanoid whole-body manipulation in the real world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged difficulty of equipping bipedal humanoid robots with advanced whole-body manipulation capabilities through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While VLA models show promise for generalization, they currently struggle to adapt to new robot action spaces, particularly for bipedal humanoids, due to the limited availability of high-quality, whole-body demonstrations.

The paper identifies several specific challenges that motivate the proposed TrajBooster framework:

1. **Data Scarcity for Bipedal Humanoids:** Collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality whole-body manipulation data for bipedal humanoids is costly and labor-intensive. Existing teleoperation methods are expensive, require expert operators, and typically yield small datasets with limited task and scene diversity. This bottleneck prevents VLAs from effectively learning the nuanced action spaces required for bipedal whole-body control.
2. **Complexity of Bipedal Whole-Body Manipulation:** Bipedal humanoids must simultaneously manage dynamic balance and coordinate both upper and lower body movements to perform manipulation tasks, especially those requiring reaching across a wide range of heights (beyond tabletop). This coordinated effort is more complex than manipulation on wheeled platforms or purely tabletop scenarios.
3. **Limitations of Existing Cross-Embodiment Methods:** While cross-embodiment learning exists, prior approaches often focus on perception-level alignment, latent action representations, or specific dexterous hand-object interactions. These methods typically do not provide a scalable solution for transferring full-body manipulation skills from one robot morphology (e.g., wheeled humanoid) to a fundamentally different one (e.g., bipedal humanoid) while preserving rich action details.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of the TrajBooster framework are:

1. **Leverage Abundant Data:** To utilize existing, large-scale manipulation datasets from more data-rich robot platforms (specifically, wheeled humanoids like the Agibot) to benefit bipedal humanoid VLA training.
2. **Develop a Morphology-Agnostic Interface:** To identify and exploit a common interface—end-effector trajectories—that can bridge the morphological and joint-space differences between diverse robot embodiments, allowing for effective skill transfer.
3. **Create a Cross-Embodiment Framework:** To design a real-to-sim-to-real pipeline that systematically extracts, retargets, and integrates heterogeneous robot data into a VLA model for a target bipedal humanoid.
4. **Reduce Reliance on Same-Embodiment Data:** To significantly mitigate the need for extensive, costly real-world teleoperation data collection on the target bipedal humanoid itself.
5. **Enhance VLA Action Space Comprehension and Generalization:** To enable the VLA model to better understand the action space of the bipedal humanoid, leading to improved task robustness, generalization to out-of-distribution object placements, and zero-shot skill transfer capabilities.
6. **Enable Beyond-Tabletop Manipulation:** To empower bipedal humanoids, specifically the Unitree G1, to perform complex household tasks involving coordinated whole-body movements and manipulation across a wide range of heights.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TrajBooster framework is structured as a real-to-sim-to-real pipeline comprising three main stages: Real Trajectory Extraction, Retargeting in Simulation, and Finetuning for Real Humanoid.

#### 3.1. A. Real Trajectory Extraction

The process begins by extracting manipulation data from the Agibot-World beta dataset, which contains over one million real-robot trajectories, including multi-view visual information, language instructions, and 6D end-effector poses from a wheeled humanoid. Instead of directly mapping full-body motions, TrajBooster focuses on the 6D coordinates (position and orientation) of dual-arm end-effectors as the primary goal.

A crucial step is **trajectory mapping** from the Agibot dataset to the workspace of the target Unitree G1 humanoid. This is necessary because of significant workspace discrepancies (e.g., Agibot’s arm span is 1.8 meters, while Unitree G1’s is 1.2 meters). The mapping involves:
- Aligning the x-axis of Agibot data with the G1 via z-score normalization.
- Rescaling the y-axis using a scaling factor (β = 0.6667) proportional to arm length.
- Clipping the z-axis (height) to a range of \[0.15, 1.25] meters for safety and relevance to the G1's capabilities.

#### 3.2. B. Retargeting in Simulation

This stage involves training a retargeting model in the Isaac Gym simulator to enable the Unitree G1 to track the extracted and mapped end-effector trajectories using whole-body control. The goal is to convert low-dimensional trajectory references into feasible high-dimensional whole-body actions.

### 4. Model Architecture

Given the prevalence of household tasks in the Agibot-World dataset requiring significant lower-body coordination (e.g., squatting for varying heights), a composite hierarchical model is proposed:
- **Arm Policy (P_arm):** This policy computes target joint angles for the arms using Closed-loop Inverse Kinematics (CLIK) via the Pinocchio library. Its input is the wrist pose relative to the robot's base frame (T_BE).
- **Worker Policy (P_worker):** A goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (RL) policy, trained using methods similar to prior work and enhanced with an upper-body motion curriculum to improve disturbance robustness. It outputs target joint positions for the 12-Degrees of Freedom (DoF) lower body, based on desired base velocity commands (v_x, v_y, v_yaw) and torso height (h).
- **Manager Policy (P_manager):** This policy generates the lower-body commands (v_x, v_y, v_yaw, h) directly from the target wrist poses (T_BE).
The composite hierarchical model (H) integrates these components, taking T_BE as input and outputting complete Unitree G1 joint instructions, which are then executed by PD controllers.

### 5. Hierarchical Model Training

The training of the hierarchical model proceeds in two stages:
- **P_worker Training:** This policy is trained first, following methods for robust locomotion control.
- **P_manager Training (Heuristic-enhanced Harmonized Online DAgger):** This is a key innovation.
 - **Seed Trajectory Collection:** Unitree G1 is initialized in a standing pose in MuJoCo. Upper-limb motions from a Unitree G1 manipulation dataset are replayed, and the resulting whole-body trajectories are recorded.
 - **Trajectory Augmentation:** PCHIP interpolation is applied to the seed trajectories to generate continuous height variations within the range of \[0.15 m, 1.25 m], enabling diverse whole-body manipulation.
 - **Heuristic Target Command (a*) Generation:** Privileged information from the simulator is leveraged to generate ground-truth commands:
 - h\* (height targets) are derived from the PCHIP-interpolated heights.
 - (v\*x, v\*y, v\*yaw) (velocity commands) are computed from the humanoid's base displacement relative to its initial position over a 1-second planning horizon. These commands are clipped to specified ranges.
 - **Harmonized Online DAgger:** This algorithm trains P_manager. In each iteration, P_manager executes a T-step rollout across N parallel environments in Isaac Gym, minimizing a rollout loss. To prevent catastrophic forgetting and balance computational efficiency, a harmonized Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) strategy is employed. Unlike standard DAgger which aggregates data at every iteration, new demonstrations are incorporated into the aggregated dataset (D) only once every M=10 iterations. Subsequently, a loss function over the entire aggregated dataset (L_DA) is minimized. This pipeline benefits from privileged simulation information to efficiently generate heuristic commands for P_manager training.

#### 5.1. C. Post-Pre-Training with Retargeted Data

This is an intermediate training phase for the VLA model, bridging its initial pre-training with final fine-tuning on real-world data.
- **Data Triplet Construction:** Multimodal data triplets are created by combining the retargeted action data (generated in the simulation stage) with the original language instructions and visual observations from the Agibot-World dataset. These triplets are structured as ⟨source vision, source language, target action⟩.
- **VLA Model Post-Pre-Training:** An existing pre-trained VLA model, GR00T N1.5, is post-pre-trained using this synthetic dataset. The objective function is a flow-matching loss, where the VLA model predicts a denoising vector field to transform a noised action chunk back to the ground truth. This phase aims to align the VLA's action distribution more closely with the target humanoid's real-world action space and enhance its comprehension.
- **Hierarchical Control in Inference:** During inference, the VLA model generates high-level commands, including base velocities (v_x, v_y, v_yaw), base height (h), and joint position targets for the arms and hands. These locomotion commands (v_x, v_y, v_yaw, h) are then fed into the already trained P_worker policy to generate corresponding leg joint positions, resulting in complete whole-body actions. This decomposition allows the VLA to focus on high-level task reasoning.

#### 5.2. D. Post-Training

This is the final stage, involving fine-tuning the post-pre-trained VLA model with minimal real-world data to bridge the sim-to-real visual gap and enable rapid adaptation.
- **Teleoperation Data Collection:** A small amount of real-world whole-body manipulation data (approximately 10 minutes, 28 episodes across four tasks/height configurations) is collected on the Unitree G1. Lower-body motion commands are provided by human operators via a joystick, while upper-body (arm and hand) movements are teleoperated using an Apple Vision Pro-based framework for kinematic mapping. Visual data is recorded from wrist and head RGB cameras.
- **Fine-Tuning:** The post-pre-trained VLA model is fine-tuned using this collected real-world teleoperation data, again minimizing the flow-matching loss.

### 6. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the performance of the retargeting model and the efficacy of the post-pre-training (PPT) approach for the VLA model on the Unitree G1 humanoid.

#### 6.1. A. Evaluation on Retargeting Model (Q1)

The hierarchical retargeting model, trained with Harmonized Online DAgger, was compared against several baselines in simulation: reward-based PPO, standard DAgger (M=1), online learning (rollout loss only), and standard online DAgger (M=1 with both losses).
- **Superior Tracking Performance:** The Harmonized Online DAgger (M=10) method demonstrated superior tracking performance, achieving the lowest Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in both position (E_p) and rotation (E_r) for wrist trajectories across both "Whole-body Tracking Mobile" and "Static" scenarios (Table I). For example, in the mobile scenario, it achieved E_p = 2.851 cm and E_r = 6.231 degrees, outperforming PPO (E_p = 14.326, E_r = 11.853) and standard DAgger (E_p = 4.596, E_r = 9.225).
- **Efficiency:** The M=10 variant also exhibited lower storage usage and higher learning efficiency, balancing data aggregation with computational cost effectively. The study notes that imitation learning (DAgger variants) converged faster and was more stable than PPO, which experienced slow convergence and inferior sample efficiency.

#### 6.2. B. Evaluation on VLA with Post-Pre-Training (Q2, Q3, Q4)

The VLA model, post-pre-trained with retargeted Agibot-World data and then fine-tuned with 3K steps of real-world data (termed "w. PPT (3K steps)"), was compared against baselines that were directly post-trained on real-world data for 3K and 10K steps ("w/o. PPT (3K steps)" and "w/o. PPT (10K steps)"). The dataset for post-pre-training comprised 1960 episodes (approximately 35 hours) from 176 distinct tasks, processed for Unitree G1 motion retargeting and end-effector mapping.

### 7. Accelerated Adaptation to Humanoid Action Space (Q2)

The models were evaluated on four real-world teleoperated tasks (Pick Mickey Mouse, Store Toys, Clean the Table, Pick Orange & Place) where target objects were positioned in-domain.
- **Higher Success Rates with Less Data:** The "w. PPT (3K steps)" model achieved higher success rates across all tasks compared to the "w/o. PPT (10K steps)" model (Table II). For instance, it achieved 100% success for "Pick Mickey Mouse" compared to 80% for "w/o. PPT (10K steps)". The "w/o. PPT (3K steps)" model failed to learn effectively, showing only oscillatory behavior.
- **Conclusion:** This indicates that post-pre-training significantly accelerates the downstream post-training adaptation, enabling higher performance with substantially less real-world fine-tuning data (3K steps vs. 10K steps).

### 8. Enhanced Trajectory Generalization (Q3)

To assess generalization, the "Pick Mickey Mouse" task was made more difficult by placing the object at out-of-distribution (unseen) positions.
- **Improved Robustness to Spatial Variations:** The "w. PPT (3K steps)" model achieved an 80% success rate, whereas the "w/o. PPT (10K steps)" model achieved 0% success (Table III).
- **Reduced Memorization Bias:** Analysis using the FastDTW algorithm showed that the "w/o. PPT (10K steps)" model had a smaller DTW distance to the teleoperation trajectories, indicating a tendency to closely replicate memorized motions. Conversely, the "w. PPT (3K steps)" model, with its larger DTW distance, demonstrated adaptation rather than mere imitation.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Qualitative observations (Fig. 7) supported this, showing that without PPT, the VLA mimicked the original teleoperated motion (approaching from above), while with PPT, it adapted to the new object placement (grasping from below). This suggests that post-pre-training enhances the VLA's understanding of the action space, improving generalization.

### 9. Unlocking Zero-shot Skill Generalization (Q4)

The model was evaluated on the "Pass the Water" task, which was included in the post-pre-training dataset but entirely excluded from the real-world teleoperation fine-tuning data.
- **Successful Zero-Shot Execution:** The post-pre-trained VLA model successfully executed the "Pass the Water" task on the Unitree G1 in a zero-shot manner (Fig. 8).
- **Conclusion:** This finding demonstrates that post-pre-training enables the VLA to acquire and transfer previously unseen manipulation skills, showcasing improved task generalization capabilities.

### 10. Significance and Potential Impact

The TrajBooster framework presents several areas of significance and potential impact within robotic manipulation research and development:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Bipedal Humanoids:** A primary contribution is providing a method to overcome the significant challenge of data scarcity for training VLA models on bipedal humanoids. By leveraging abundant existing data from other robot embodiments, TrajBooster offers a more scalable and cost-effective approach to developing advanced humanoid capabilities. This can reduce the reliance on expensive and laborious same-embodiment data collection.
2. **Advancement in Cross-Embodiment Learning:** The framework demonstrates a practical and effective method for cross-embodiment skill transfer by utilizing end-effector trajectories as a morphology-agnostic interface. This approach facilitates the translation of complex whole-body manipulation skills between robots with distinct physical structures, specifically from wheeled to bipedal humanoids. This is noted as the first instance of leveraging extensive retargeted action data for bipedal humanoid whole-body manipulation with a VLA model in a real-world setting.
3. **Enhanced VLA Performance and Generalization:** The research shows that the proposed post-pre-training (PPT) methodology significantly improves the VLA model's ability to adapt to a target humanoid's action space. This leads to accelerated learning, requiring substantially less real-world fine-tuning data (demonstrated by higher success rates with 3K steps of fine-tuning compared to 10K steps without PPT). Furthermore, PPT enhances trajectory generalization, making the robot more robust to variations in object placement, and enables zero-shot transfer of previously unseen manipulation skills.
4. **Enabling Beyond-Tabletop Manipulation:** By empowering the Unitree G1 humanoid to perform complex household tasks involving coordinated whole-body movements, such as squatting and cross-height manipulation, TrajBooster expands the operational capabilities of bipedal humanoids beyond traditional tabletop scenarios. This development brings humanoids closer to functioning effectively in diverse domestic environments.
5. **Practical Deployment with Minimal Real-World Data:** The ability to achieve robust whole-body manipulation and generalization with only 10 minutes of real-world teleoperation data highlights the practical efficiency of the TrajBooster framework. This efficiency is critical for rapid deployment and continuous improvement of humanoid robots in real-world applications.

**Potential future impacts, based on identified limitations, include:**
- Integrating dexterous hands with tactile sensing to allow for more precise and complex manipulation tasks.
- Developing methods for embodiment alignment in visual observations, which could further improve perception-action consistency and narrow the sim-to-real gap.
- Expanding the framework to address large-scale loco-manipulation data scarcity, thereby extending humanoid capabilities to richer mobile scenarios beyond mostly stationary tasks.
- Scaling the approach to incorporate a wider variety of heterogeneous data sources and robot platforms, moving beyond the Agibot G1 and Agibot-World dataset for broader applicability.

In summary, TrajBooster represents a step towards making advanced humanoid manipulation more accessible by mitigating the data bottleneck and improving the adaptability and generalization of VLA models in complex whole-body control tasks.
