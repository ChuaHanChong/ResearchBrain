# Natural Gradient Bayesian Filtering: Geometry-Aware Filter for Dynamical Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces the Natural Gradient Gaussian Approximation (NANO) filter, a geometry-aware approach to nonlinear Bayesian filtering that leverages natural gradient descent on the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions. This method iteratively refines posterior mean and covariance, demonstrating improved estimation accuracy and robustness compared to traditional Gaussian filters like EKF and UKF in various robotic and aerospace applications.

## Problem

- Existing Gaussian filters, such as EKF and UKF, often rely on suboptimal measurement update steps that struggle to accurately approximate the true Bayesian posterior in nonlinear or non-Gaussian systems.
- Previous gradient-based Gaussian filters typically use Euclidean gradients, which disregard the intrinsic non-Euclidean geometry of Gaussian distributions, leading to issues like parameterization sensitivity and failure to guarantee positive definiteness of covariance.
- A lack of a unified, theoretically grounded framework for Gaussian filtering that moves beyond ad-hoc linearization or moment-matching techniques to incorporate the geometric structure of probability distributions.

## Method

- Recasts Bayesian filtering (prediction and update) as ==variational optimization== problems involving ==KL divergence== over the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions.
- Develops a geometry-aware measurement update using **NANO** filter's ==natural gradient descent==, which employs the ==Fisher Information Matrix== to account for the intrinsic geometry of the Gaussian manifold.
- Utilizes ==Stein-type identities== to derive a derivative-free update rule for the **NANO** filter and incorporates ==Hessian approximation== or ==Cholesky decomposition== to guarantee positive definiteness of covariance matrices.

## Results

- NANO filter achieved significantly lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) in satellite attitude estimation (**0.589**) compared to EKF/UKF (**0.944**), demonstrating superior accuracy and robustness to non-Gaussian noise and initialization errors.
- NANO-SLAM reduced localization RMSE by over **50%** (**2.538 m**) compared to UFastSLAM (**5.147 m**) and EKF-SLAM (**7.783 m**) on the Victoria Park dataset, with an **18%** runtime overhead.
- The NANO-L extension for quadruped robot state estimation showed improved accuracy and robustness on Lie groups, reducing position Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) by about **41%** (**0.236 m** vs. **0.401 m**) compared to InEKF on unstable terrain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bayesian filtering can be understood as a sequence of projection problems on the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions, with prediction as a forward-KL projection and update as a distributional proximal step.
> - The classical Kalman filter measurement update is a special case of the proposed natural-gradient Gaussian update, exactly recovered in a single iteration for linear Gaussian measurement models, establishing a direct theoretical link.
> - Natural gradient descent provides a parameterization-invariant and numerically stable approach for optimizing Gaussian distribution parameters, inherently respecting the manifold geometry and ensuring properties like positive definite covariance.
