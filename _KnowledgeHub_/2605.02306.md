# Natural Gradient Bayesian Filtering: Geometry-Aware Filter for Dynamical Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces the Natural Gradient Gaussian Approximation (NANO) filter, a geometry-aware approach to nonlinear Bayesian filtering that leverages natural gradient descent on the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions. This method iteratively refines posterior mean and covariance, demonstrating improved estimation accuracy and robustness compared to traditional Gaussian filters like EKF and UKF in various robotic and aerospace applications.

## Problem

- Existing Gaussian filters, such as EKF and UKF, often rely on suboptimal measurement update steps that struggle to accurately approximate the true Bayesian posterior in nonlinear or non-Gaussian systems.
- Previous gradient-based Gaussian filters typically use Euclidean gradients, which disregard the intrinsic non-Euclidean geometry of Gaussian distributions, leading to issues like parameterization sensitivity and failure to guarantee positive definiteness of covariance.
- A lack of a unified, theoretically grounded framework for Gaussian filtering that moves beyond ad-hoc linearization or moment-matching techniques to incorporate the geometric structure of probability distributions.

## Method

- Recasts Bayesian filtering (prediction and update) as ==variational optimization== problems involving ==KL divergence== over the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions.
- Develops a geometry-aware measurement update using **NANO** filter's ==natural gradient descent==, which employs the ==Fisher Information Matrix== to account for the intrinsic geometry of the Gaussian manifold.
- Utilizes ==Stein-type identities== to derive a derivative-free update rule for the **NANO** filter and incorporates ==Hessian approximation== or ==Cholesky decomposition== to guarantee positive definiteness of covariance matrices.

## Results

- NANO filter achieved significantly lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) in satellite attitude estimation (**0.589**) compared to EKF/UKF (**0.944**), demonstrating superior accuracy and robustness to non-Gaussian noise and initialization errors.
- NANO-SLAM reduced localization RMSE by over **50%** (**2.538 m**) compared to UFastSLAM (**5.147 m**) and EKF-SLAM (**7.783 m**) on the Victoria Park dataset, with an **18%** runtime overhead.
- The NANO-L extension for quadruped robot state estimation showed improved accuracy and robustness on Lie groups, reducing position Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) by about **41%** (**0.236 m** vs. **0.401 m**) compared to InEKF on unstable terrain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bayesian filtering can be understood as a sequence of projection problems on the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions, with prediction as a forward-KL projection and update as a distributional proximal step.
> - The classical Kalman filter measurement update is a special case of the proposed natural-gradient Gaussian update, exactly recovered in a single iteration for linear Gaussian measurement models, establishing a direct theoretical link.
> - Natural gradient descent provides a parameterization-invariant and numerically stable approach for optimizing Gaussian distribution parameters, inherently respecting the manifold geometry and ensuring properties like positive definite covariance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

State estimation, particularly Bayesian filtering, is fundamental across diverse scientific and engineering disciplines, including aerospace, robotics, and smart grids. The core challenge in this field lies in inferring the latent state of dynamic systems from noisy and incomplete observations. While the Kalman Filter (KF) provides an exact solution for linear-Gaussian systems, most real-world applications involve nonlinear dynamics or non-Gaussian uncertainties, rendering exact Bayesian filtering intractable.

The broader research landscape for nonlinear and non-Gaussian systems is characterized by various approximation strategies. Particle filtering offers a general, sample-based approach but often incurs high computational costs. Gaussian filters, which approximate the posterior distribution as Gaussian at each time step, are widely used due to their computational efficiency. These include the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF), Gauss-Hermite Kalman Filter, and Cubature Kalman Filter (CKF). These methods typically operate under an "enabling approximation framework," where a nonlinear model is first linearized (e.g., via Taylor expansion in EKF, or moment matching in UKF/CKF) to enable a Kalman-style update.

However, existing Gaussian filters have recognized limitations. Jacobian-based linearization (EKF) struggles to preserve covariance transformations under strong nonlinearities. Moment-based methods (UKF/CKF) perform well in the prediction stage, but their measurement update optimality has been questioned, as they do not generally recover the exact optimal Gaussian approximation to the true Bayesian posterior. Recent work has highlighted a structural mismatch in the optimization problems solved by the prediction and update steps of these filters when viewed variationally. Gradient-based Gaussian filters exist, but they typically use Euclidean gradients, which are not "geometry-aware" and can suffer from issues like parameterization sensitivity, poor scaling, and difficult step-size tuning.

This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a geometry-aware approach to Gaussian filtering. It positions itself within the evolving paradigm of viewing Bayesian inference through an optimization lens, particularly leveraging information geometry to construct principled, robust, and efficient Gaussian approximations. By proposing a natural gradient descent approach for the measurement update, the work aims to bridge the gap between theoretical optimality in a variational sense and practical algorithmic design, moving beyond ad-hoc linearization techniques to a more fundamentally grounded approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a geometry-aware Gaussian filtering approach that leverages natural gradient descent on the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions. This leads to the proposed Natural Gradient Gaussian Approximation (NANO) filter, designed to iteratively refine posterior mean and covariance while respecting the intrinsic geometry of the Gaussian family and preserving covariance positive definiteness.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the existing landscape of Bayesian filtering:
- **Suboptimality of Current Gaussian Filter Updates:** While many Gaussian filters (like EKF, UKF, CKF) are efficient, their measurement update steps, based on linearization or surrogate models, are not always optimal in accurately approximating the true Bayesian posterior. Recent theoretical work has highlighted a "structural mismatch" between the optimization problems implied by prediction and update in these methods.
- **Lack of Geometry-Awareness in Gradient-Based Methods:** Prior attempts to formulate Gaussian filters using gradient descent schemes often rely on Euclidean gradients. Gaussian distributions, however, reside on a statistical manifold with a non-Euclidean geometry. Using Euclidean gradients ignores this intrinsic structure, leading to potential issues such as sensitivity to parameterization, numerical instability, poor convergence, and the inability to guarantee properties like positive definiteness of the covariance matrix.
- **Need for a Unified Theoretical Framework:** The paper aims to provide an intuitive information-geometric and optimization-centric perspective on Gaussian filtering. This framework seeks to clarify the role of approximation in nonlinear Bayesian filtering and reveal the geometric underpinnings of various Gaussian filters.
- **Desire for Practical Robustness and Accuracy:** By incorporating natural gradients, the authors seek to develop a filter that offers improved numerical robustness, faster convergence, and enhanced accuracy in practical nonlinear estimation problems, particularly when dealing with non-Gaussian noise or systems evolving on manifolds.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Present an information-geometric interpretation of Gaussian filtering, viewing prediction and measurement update as projection problems on the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions.
2. Develop a geometry-aware measurement update based on natural gradient descent that respects Gaussian distribution structure, maintains positive-definite covariances, and is invariant to parameterization.
3. Establish fundamental connections between the proposed framework and classical Kalman filtering, specifically demonstrating how the Kalman measurement update is a special case.
4. Illustrate the practical advantages of the NANO filter through case studies in diverse nonlinear estimation problems, including satellite attitude estimation, SLAM, and state estimation for quadruped and humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work is structured around an information-geometric interpretation of Bayesian filtering, leading to the development of the NANO filter.

**A. Optimization Perspective on Bayesian Filtering:**
The paper first recasts the prediction and update steps of Bayesian filtering as variational optimization problems over the space of probability densities.
- **Prediction:** The prior density `p(x_t|y_1:t-1)` is characterized as the maximizer of the expected logarithm of a candidate density `q(x_t)` under the joint distribution `p(x_t|x_t-1)p(x_t-1|y_1:t-1)`. This is equivalent to minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence `D_KL(p_t|t-1 || q(x_t))`.
- **Update:** The posterior density `p(x_t|y_1:t)` is characterized as the minimizer of a functional composed of the expected negative log-likelihood (measurement-dependent loss) under `q(x_t)` and the KL divergence `D_KL(q(x_t) || p(x_t|y_1:t-1))`. This formulation highlights the update as a distributional proximal step balancing data fit and deviation from the prior.

**B. Information Geometry Primer:**
The core conceptual tool is information geometry.
- **Statistical Manifolds:** The set of parameterized probability distributions is treated as a Riemannian manifold. The paper focuses on the finite-dimensional submanifold of Gaussian distributions.
- **Fisher Information Matrix (FIM):** The FIM is introduced as the canonical Riemannian metric on statistical manifolds. It quantifies the distinguishability between infinitesimally close distributions and is derived from the Hessian of the KL divergence. Chentsov’s theorem justifies the FIM as the unique metric compatible with the statistical notion of information loss under data processing.
- **Natural Gradient Descent:** Unlike Euclidean gradient descent, natural gradient descent accounts for the intrinsic geometry of the statistical manifold by pre-multiplying the ordinary gradient with the inverse of the FIM. This yields a parameterization-invariant direction of steepest descent, often leading to improved convergence and stability. The exponential family of distributions, which includes Gaussians, simplifies the computation of the FIM.

**C. Natural Gradient Gaussian Approximation (NANO Filter):**
The NANO filter applies this information-geometric framework to Gaussian filtering.
- **Gaussian Restriction:** The variational optimization problems for prediction and update are restricted to the Gaussian family `Q_G`.
- **Prediction Step:** The prediction step remains a forward-KL projection onto `Q_G`, which is achieved by moment matching (i.e., the predicted Gaussian's mean and covariance match the first two moments of the true prior). This is consistent with existing Gaussian filters like UKF/CKF.
- **Measurement Update (NANO filter derivation):** The key innovation lies in the measurement update. The problem (minimizing `J(ˆx, P)`) is solved using iterative natural gradient descent.
 - Parameters are stacked into a vector `v` containing the mean `ˆx` and the inverse covariance (information matrix) `P^-1`.
 - The inverse FIM for Gaussian distributions parameterized by mean and inverse covariance is explicitly derived.
 - Applying the natural gradient descent rule `δv = -F_v^-1 ∇_v J` leads to a set of coupled iterative equations (34a, 34c) for updating the inverse covariance and mean.
 - To avoid explicit computation of derivatives of the loss function `ℓ(x,y_t)`, Stein-type identities are used to transform the gradient expectations into loss-weighted moment expectations (35a, 35b), yielding a derivative-free update rule (36a, 36b).
- **Algorithm:** The NANO filter algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternates between a standard moment-matching prediction step and an iterative natural-gradient-based measurement update.

**D. Practical Implementation:**
- **Expectation Calculation:** The expectations in the NANO filter are computed using moment-matching techniques. The paper highlights unscented transformation, Gauss-Hermite quadrature, and spherical-radial cubature as possible implementations for this step.
- **Positive Definiteness Guarantee:** A critical aspect of covariance updates is maintaining positive definiteness. The paper proposes two methods:
 1. **Hessian Approximation:** The Hessian of the negative log-likelihood `∇^2_x ℓ(x,y_t)` is approximated by `G^T R^-1 G`, where `G` is the Jacobian of the measurement function. This approximation is always positive semi-definite, ensuring the updated covariance matrix remains positive definite. This is equivalent to the Gauss-Newton method.
 2. **Cholesky Decomposition:** The inverse covariance matrix is factorized using Cholesky decomposition at each iteration, and updates are applied to the Cholesky factor. Reconstructing the inverse covariance with an added `ϵI` term guarantees positive definiteness. The paper notes the Hessian Approximation as the preferred method due to its balance of accuracy and computational efficiency.

**E. NANO-filter on Manifold (NANO-L):**
For systems with states evolving on Lie group manifolds (e.g., orientation SO(3), pose SE(3)), the NANO filter is extended.
- **Error State:** Instead of directly estimating manifold states, an error state `δx` (which lives in a Euclidean tangent space) is defined relative to a nominal manifold state `¯x` using `⊞` (retraction) and `⊟` (lifting) operators.
- **Prediction:** The nominal state `¯x` evolves deterministically, and the error state `δx` prediction follows a standard linear Kalman filter recursion.
- **Update:** Natural gradient iterations are performed on the Euclidean error state `δx` using the NANO update equations, with the loss function adapted for the manifold state `¯x ⊞ δx`.
- **Composition and Reset:** After convergence, the estimated manifold state `ˆx` is obtained by composing the nominal state with the refined error estimate, and the error state is reset to zero for the next time step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields both theoretical and empirical findings.

**A. Theoretical Findings:**
- **Unified Optimization-Information Geometric Framework:** The paper successfully formulates Bayesian filtering (both prediction and update) as variational optimization problems involving KL divergence, thereby providing a unified optimization and information-geometric interpretation. Prediction is a forward-KL projection, and update is a distributional proximal step.
- **Natural Gradient for Gaussian Approximation:** It is demonstrated that the measurement update can be efficiently performed via natural gradient descent on the Gaussian statistical manifold, leading to geometry-aware parameter updates.
- **Connection to Kalman Filter:** A significant theoretical result is that for linear Gaussian measurement models, a single iteration of the proposed natural-gradient Gaussian update exactly recovers the classical Kalman filter measurement update, regardless of initialization. This establishes a direct theoretical link between the NANO filter and the foundational KF, highlighting NANO's consistency with established methods under simplifying assumptions.

**B. Experimental Results:**
The practical implications of the NANO filter are illustrated through four case studies in representative nonlinear estimation problems.
- **Satellite Attitude Estimation:**
 - **Scenario:** Estimating satellite attitude (Euler angles) using gravimeter and magnetometer measurements, under non-Gaussian process and observation noise.
 - **Results:** NANO consistently achieved lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) than both EKF and UKF under sinusoidal and constant angular velocity inputs, and with both accurate and biased initial state estimates. For accurate initialization and sinusoidal input, NANO's RMSE was 0.589, significantly lower than EKF/UKF's 0.944. This suggests NANO's superior accuracy and robustness to non-Gaussian noise and initialization errors. Computation time was higher for NANO (approx. 3.0-3.2 ms vs. EKF 0.25-0.27 ms, UKF 0.38-0.43 ms).
- **NANO for Vehicle SLAM:**
 - **Scenario:** Simultaneous Localization and Mapping for an autonomous vehicle in a 2D environment (Victoria Park dataset) using Ackermann kinematics and range-bearing landmark observations.
 - **Results:** NANO-SLAM achieved the lowest localization RMSE (2.538 m), representing more than a 50% improvement over UFastSLAM (5.147 m) and EKF-SLAM (7.783 m). The runtime (17.692 ms) was comparable to UFastSLAM (14.896 ms), with an 18% overhead. Visualizations showed NANO-SLAM's trajectory aligning most closely with the GPS reference.
- **Quadruped Robot State Estimation (NANO-L):**
 - **Scenario:** State estimation for a quadruped robot, where states evolve on a Lie group manifold (SE_3(3)), using IMU and leg odometry, tested on flat and unstable terrains with potential contact outliers. Compared against EKF and Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF).
 - **Results:** NANO-L demonstrated the best overall performance in Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Error (RE) for position, velocity, and orientation across both terrains. On unstable terrain, NANO-L reduced position ATE by about 41% relative to InEKF (0.236 m vs. 0.401 m). It also showed smaller degradation when moving from flat to unstable terrain, indicating superior robustness. Computation time was within real-time requirements (3.351 ms vs. InEKF 0.712 ms, EKF 3.905 ms).
- **Humanoid Robot State Estimation:**
 - **Scenario:** Estimating the torso position and velocity of a humanoid robot using IMU measurements and leg odometry in a simulated environment involving walking and turning.
 - **Results:** NANO achieved the best performance on all four metrics: ATE and RE for position and velocity. For instance, NANO's position ATE was 0.101 m, significantly better than EKF's 0.386 m and UKF's 0.387 m. NANO also exhibited more accurate velocity tracking and reduced trajectory deviations compared to EKF and UKF, particularly during dynamic maneuvers like turns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a robust and theoretically grounded approach to nonlinear Gaussian filtering, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Principled Framework for Gaussian Filtering:** By unifying Bayesian filtering under an optimization and information-geometric perspective, the paper provides a more fundamental understanding of Gaussian approximation. This moves beyond ad-hoc linearization techniques, offering a principled way to design and analyze Gaussian filters.
- **Geometry-Aware and Robust Updates:** The NANO filter's use of natural gradient descent ensures that the iterative updates respect the intrinsic geometry of Gaussian distributions. This leads to updates that are invariant to parameterization, maintain positive definiteness of the covariance matrix, and are numerically more stable and robust, particularly in highly nonlinear or non-Gaussian noise environments.
- **Improved Accuracy and Consistency:** The experimental results across diverse and challenging robotic and aerospace applications consistently demonstrate that NANO outperforms conventional Gaussian filters (EKF, UKF) in terms of estimation accuracy and robustness to noise and initialization errors. This suggests that the geometry-aware approach leads to a more accurate approximation of the true posterior.
- **Theoretical Link to Kalman Filter:** The demonstration that NANO recovers the exact Kalman filter update in the linear-Gaussian case provides a strong theoretical validation of the framework, establishing its consistency with the optimal solution in a special, but fundamental, scenario.
- **Extension to Manifold States:** The development of NANO-L for states evolving on Lie groups broadens the applicability of the natural gradient approach to critical domains like robotic pose estimation, where Euclidean approximations can be problematic. This is particularly impactful for high-performance mobile and legged robotics.
- **Potential for Broader Impact:** The NANO filter offers a promising alternative to existing approximate Bayesian filters for any application involving nonlinear dynamic systems and noisy measurements. Its enhanced accuracy and robustness could lead to more reliable state estimation in fields such as autonomous vehicles, aerospace guidance and navigation, human-robot interaction, and industrial automation, where precise and stable state estimates are crucial for decision-making and control. The framework also opens avenues for further research into other statistical manifolds and divergence measures for Bayesian inference.
