# Understanding and Improving Hyperbolic Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Vienna and Max Planck Institute for Biochemistry formally analyzed and addressed optimization instabilities in hyperbolic deep reinforcement learning. They introduced HYPER++, a novel agent integrating RMSNorm with learned feature scaling, a Hyperboloid model, and a categorical value loss, achieving a 52.3% improvement in mean test return on ProcGen compared to prior hyperbolic agents and outperforming Euclidean baselines across various RL benchmarks and algorithms while reducing training time.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning (RL) faces data inefficiency, partly due to a geometric mismatch between Euclidean spaces and the hierarchical, exponentially branching nature of sequential decision-making.
- Hyperbolic deep RL agents suffer from severe optimization instabilities, including gradient instability, early entropy collapse, and trust-region violations, despite hyperbolic geometry's theoretical suitability for hierarchical data.
- A lack of formal analysis on the sources of these optimization failures hinders the development of effective, targeted solutions, and existing stabilization techniques like SpectralNorm have limited efficacy.

## Method

- Conducted a formal gradient analysis of core operations in both Poincaré Ball and Hyperboloid models, identifying large-norm embeddings and the Poincaré Ball's conformal factor as key sources of gradient instability.
- Developed **HYPER**++, an agent incorporating RMSNorm with a learned feature scaling factor (ξ_θ) to provably bound Euclidean embedding norms and expand usable hyperbolic volume for stable operations.
- Switched to the Hyperboloid model, which avoids the unstable conformal factor, and employed a categorical value loss for the critic to stabilize learning against nonstationary targets in actor-critic setups.

## Results

- HYPER++ outperformed prior hyperbolic agents by **52.3%** in mean test return on ProcGen (PPO), and consistently surpassed Euclidean baselines across ProcGen (PPO/PPG) and Atari-5 (DDQN) benchmarks.
- The agent demonstrated stable learning with lower update KL-divergence, reduced PPO clipping, and approximately **30%** faster forward pass wall-clock time compared to previous hyperbolic methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that RMSNorm with learned scaling was critical for preventing learning failure, and that the Hyperboloid model and categorical value loss each contributed to enhanced performance and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradient instability in hyperbolic deep RL primarily stems from large-norm embeddings, with the Poincaré Ball's conformal factor being a major numerical issue and the exponential map's Jacobian being sensitive to growing Euclidean feature norms in both models.
> - Rigorous norm bounding of Euclidean embeddings via RMSNorm, complemented by a learned scaling factor, is crucial for maintaining stable hyperbolic operations and preventing learning failures.
> - The Hyperboloid model's absence of the conformal factor and the use of a categorical value loss for the critic synergistically contribute to improved stability and performance in hyperbolic RL agents.
