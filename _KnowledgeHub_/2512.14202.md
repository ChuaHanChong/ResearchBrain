# Understanding and Improving Hyperbolic Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Vienna and Max Planck Institute for Biochemistry formally analyzed and addressed optimization instabilities in hyperbolic deep reinforcement learning. They introduced HYPER++, a novel agent integrating RMSNorm with learned feature scaling, a Hyperboloid model, and a categorical value loss, achieving a 52.3% improvement in mean test return on ProcGen compared to prior hyperbolic agents and outperforming Euclidean baselines across various RL benchmarks and algorithms while reducing training time.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning (RL) faces data inefficiency, partly due to a geometric mismatch between Euclidean spaces and the hierarchical, exponentially branching nature of sequential decision-making.
- Hyperbolic deep RL agents suffer from severe optimization instabilities, including gradient instability, early entropy collapse, and trust-region violations, despite hyperbolic geometry's theoretical suitability for hierarchical data.
- A lack of formal analysis on the sources of these optimization failures hinders the development of effective, targeted solutions, and existing stabilization techniques like SpectralNorm have limited efficacy.

## Method

- Conducted a formal gradient analysis of core operations in both Poincaré Ball and Hyperboloid models, identifying large-norm embeddings and the Poincaré Ball's conformal factor as key sources of gradient instability.
- Developed **HYPER**++, an agent incorporating RMSNorm with a learned feature scaling factor (ξ_θ) to provably bound Euclidean embedding norms and expand usable hyperbolic volume for stable operations.
- Switched to the Hyperboloid model, which avoids the unstable conformal factor, and employed a categorical value loss for the critic to stabilize learning against nonstationary targets in actor-critic setups.

## Results

- HYPER++ outperformed prior hyperbolic agents by **52.3%** in mean test return on ProcGen (PPO), and consistently surpassed Euclidean baselines across ProcGen (PPO/PPG) and Atari-5 (DDQN) benchmarks.
- The agent demonstrated stable learning with lower update KL-divergence, reduced PPO clipping, and approximately **30%** faster forward pass wall-clock time compared to previous hyperbolic methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that RMSNorm with learned scaling was critical for preventing learning failure, and that the Hyperboloid model and categorical value loss each contributed to enhanced performance and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradient instability in hyperbolic deep RL primarily stems from large-norm embeddings, with the Poincaré Ball's conformal factor being a major numerical issue and the exponential map's Jacobian being sensitive to growing Euclidean feature norms in both models.
> - Rigorous norm bounding of Euclidean embeddings via RMSNorm, complemented by a learned scaling factor, is crucial for maintaining stable hyperbolic operations and preventing learning failures.
> - The Hyperboloid model's absence of the conformal factor and the use of a categorical value loss for the critic synergistically contribute to improved stability and performance in hyperbolic RL agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved notable successes, but faces challenges such as data inefficiency, partly attributed to the inherent geometric mismatch between the representation capacity of Euclidean spaces and the hierarchical, exponentially branching nature of sequential decision-making processes. Many RL benchmarks and real-world decision processes exhibit tree-like structures where each state can lead to multiple subsequent states, resulting in an exponential growth of possibilities with depth. Euclidean geometry, with its polynomial volume growth relative to its radius, struggles to embed such structures without significant distortion.

Hyperbolic geometry presents an alternative, offering exponential volume growth that aligns with hierarchical data structures, potentially enabling more efficient and low-distortion embeddings of trees and graphs. This property has led to its application in various deep learning domains, including classification, unsupervised representation learning, deep metric learning, and image-text alignment, showing promising results.

However, the integration of hyperbolic geometry into deep learning, especially reinforcement learning, has been hampered by optimization instabilities. These instabilities are particularly pronounced in RL due due to its nonstationary training dynamics. Previous hyperbolic deep RL agents have encountered issues such as gradient instability, early entropy collapse, and trust-region violations in policy optimization algorithms. Existing stabilization techniques, such as SpectralNorm, have been shown to have limitations, including restricting network capacity or incurring significant computational overhead. This paper addresses this gap by formally analyzing the sources of these optimization failures in hyperbolic deep RL and proposing a principled solution to enhance stability and performance. It positions itself as a contribution to the ongoing effort to leverage the geometric advantages of hyperbolic spaces while overcoming their practical optimization challenges in complex learning environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to gain a deeper understanding of the optimization challenges in hyperbolic deep reinforcement learning and to develop effective solutions to overcome them. The authors articulate several specific objectives:

- **Characterize Training Issues:** Identify and formally analyze the key factors that determine the success and failure of training hyperbolic deep RL agents. This involves a detailed examination of gradients for core operations within both the Poincaré Ball and Hyperboloid models of hyperbolic geometry.
- **Diagnose Gradient Instability:** Pinpoint the mechanisms through which large-norm embeddings destabilize gradient-based training, particularly how they lead to trust-region violations in algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Develop Principled Regularization:** Based on the insights derived from gradient analysis, design and introduce improved regularization techniques that address the weaknesses of current approaches in hyperbolic deep RL.
- **Introduce a Robust Hyperbolic Agent:** Propose and implement a new hyperbolic deep RL agent, termed HYPER++, that integrates these novel regularization and architectural components to guarantee stable learning and enhance performance.
- **Empirical Validation:** Demonstrate that HYPER++ offers stable learning, outperforms prior hyperbolic agents, and yields performance gains over Euclidean baselines across various RL benchmarks and algorithms, while also improving computational efficiency.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several critical observations in the broader research landscape:

- **Geometric Mismatch:** Sequential decision-making, a core aspect of RL, generates inherently hierarchical data with exponential branching. Euclidean spaces, with their polynomial volume growth, are geometrically mismatched for efficiently embedding such structures without significant distortion, potentially contributing to deep RL's data inefficiency.
- **Potential of Hyperbolic Geometry:** Hyperbolic spaces, characterized by exponential volume growth, offer a natural and low-distortion solution for representing hierarchical relationships, making them theoretically well-suited for RL state spaces.
- **Persistent Optimization Challenges:** Despite its theoretical advantages, hyperbolic deep learning, particularly in RL, has been plagued by severe optimization instabilities. These issues are amplified in RL due to the nonstationary nature of policy and value function learning, leading to problems like early entropy collapse and frequent trust-region violations.
- **Lack of Formal Analysis:** A formal analysis of why these optimization failures occur in hyperbolic deep RL was largely absent, hindering the development of targeted solutions.
- **Limitations of Existing Solutions:** Previous attempts to stabilize hyperbolic deep RL, such as using SpectralNorm, have demonstrated limitations by either unduly restricting model capacity or introducing computational overhead.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to unlock the full potential of hyperbolic geometry in deep RL, moving beyond theoretical promise to practical, stable, and high-performing agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a multi-faceted approach, combining formal gradient analysis with the development and empirical evaluation of a novel hyperbolic deep RL agent.

#### 3.1. Diagnosing Issues with Hyperbolic PPO Agents

The authors initiated their methodology by systematically diagnosing the training instabilities observed in existing hyperbolic PPO agents.
- **PPO Optimization Analysis:** They analyzed PPO's clipped surrogate objective, noting that high clipping fractions indicate numerous trust-region violations, suggesting unstable updates. Empirical metrics (entropy, entropy variance, update KL-divergence, PPO clipping fraction) were tracked during training on ProcGen environments (specifically BIGFISH) to confirm the occurrence of early entropy collapse and unstable updates in unregularized hyperbolic agents.
- **Formal Gradient Analysis:** A core component of the diagnosis involved a formal mathematical analysis of gradients for key operations in both the Poincaré Ball and Hyperboloid models.
 - **Poincaré Ball Analysis:** They extended prior work by analyzing the gradients of the hyperbolic multinomial logistic regression (MLR) layer and the Jacobian of the Poincaré Ball exponential map. They identified that the Poincaré Ball's conformal factor (λ_c_x) in the HNN++ MLR formulation and the sensitivity of the exponential map's Jacobian to growing Euclidean feature norms (∥x_E∥) were primary drivers of gradient explosion and vanishing gradients near the manifold boundary. The denominator (1 − c∥x_H∥^2)^2 in the MLR gradient was highlighted as a critical source of instability.
 - **Hyperboloid Analysis:** While the Hyperboloid MLR score avoids the conformal factor, the authors formally demonstrated that its exponential map Jacobian can still destabilize gradients when Euclidean feature norms grow, similar to the Poincaré Ball, albeit without the explicit conformal factor issue. This indicated a need for consistent regularization of Euclidean embedding norms regardless of the hyperbolic model used.

The diagnostic phase concluded that policy breakdown and large-norm gradients in the encoder are functions of the specific hyperbolic layers used, with the conformal factor being a major numerical instability source exacerbated by noisy gradients in actor-critic training.

#### 3.2. Stabilizing Hyperbolic Deep RL: The HYPER++ Agent

Based on the diagnostic findings, the authors designed HYPER++ with three synergistic components aimed at stabilizing different parts of the gradient backpropagation chain (Eq. 3 in the paper):

- **4.2.1 Feature Regularization for Bounded Norms:**
 - **Critique of SpectralNorm:** The authors highlighted that applying SpectralNorm only to the last layer is insufficient, and applying it to all layers excessively constrains expressivity and adds computational cost.
 - **RMSNorm:** They proposed using RMSNorm (Root Mean Square Layer Normalization) before the activation of the encoder's last linear layer. Proposition 4.2 provides theoretical guarantees that this approach bounds Euclidean embedding norms and, consequently, the conformal factor (for Poincaré Ball) or the time component (for Hyperboloid), ensuring stable hyperbolic operations without globally limiting encoder expressivity. RMSNorm was chosen over LayerNorm to avoid distorting hierarchical structures through mean-centering.
 - **Learned Euclidean Feature Scaling:** To mitigate the representational capacity loss caused by strict norm bounds (e.g., restricted usable ball radius), a novel learnable scalar factor (ξ_θ) was introduced to rescale the RMSNorm-processed Euclidean tangent embeddings. This scaling factor, constrained by ρ_max = atanh(α)/√c, allows for expanding the usable hyperbolic volume (e.g., from 0.76 to 0.95 radius in Poincaré Ball for c=1), providing orders of magnitude more volume for embeddings while maintaining stability guarantees.

- **4.2.2 Categorical Value Loss for Stable Critic Training:**
 - The traditional Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss for critic training was replaced with a categorical value loss (specifically, HL-Gauss, with C51 explored as an ablation).
 - **Rationale:** This change was motivated by the observation that hyperbolic MLR layers intrinsically output classification-oriented hyperplane distances. Aligning the critic's objective with this geometric output (i.e., treating value learning as a classification task over discrete return bins) was hypothesized to stabilize critic learning, especially under nonstationary targets inherent in RL. This stabilizes the loss derivative (first term of Eq. 3).

- **4.2.3 Hyperboloid Model for Optimization-Friendly Layers:**
 - A shift from the Poincaré Ball to the Hyperboloid model was implemented for the hyperbolic layers.
 - **Rationale:** The Hyperboloid MLR layer, unlike its Poincaré Ball counterpart, does not contain the conformal factor. This directly addresses one of the primary sources of numerical instability identified in the gradient analysis, stabilizing the hyperbolic layer Jacobian (second term of Eq. 3). The authors also noted that their RMSNorm and learned scaling strategy is transferable and beneficial to the Hyperboloid model, as its exponential map can still become ill-conditioned with large norms (Corollary 4.3).

#### 3.3. Experimental Validation

- **Environments:** The proposed HYPER++ agent was evaluated on two distinct sets of RL benchmarks:
 - **ProcGen:** A suite of 16 procedurally generated environments, using both PPO and Phasic Policy Gradient (PPG) algorithms.
 - **Atari-5:** A subset of 5 Atari games (NAMETHISGAME, PHOENIX, BATTLEZONE, DOUBLE DUNK, Q\*BERT) using the Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) algorithm.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against standard Euclidean agents, unregularized hyperbolic agents ("Hyper"), and a prior state-of-the-art hyperbolic agent ("Hyper+S-RYM" by Cetin et al., 2023).
- **Metrics:** Evaluation included normalized aggregate test rewards (median, interquartile mean (IQM), mean, optimality gap) computed using the `rliable` library. Training stability was assessed by tracking entropy, update KL-divergence, PPO clipping fraction, and various gradient norms. Wall-clock time was also measured.
- **Ablation Studies:** Extensive ablation studies were performed on ProcGen to quantify the individual and combined contributions of each HYPER++ component (RMSNorm, learned scaling, categorical loss, Hyperboloid vs. Poincaré). This included testing alternative regularization methods like SpectralNorm.

The methodology systematically moved from identifying root causes of failure through theoretical analysis to developing targeted solutions and validating their efficacy through comprehensive empirical evaluation across multiple algorithms and environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings, both theoretical and empirical, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed HYPER++ agent and elucidating the underlying issues in hyperbolic deep RL.

#### 4.1. Diagnosis of Hyperbolic Deep RL Training Issues

- **Gradient Instability:** Formal gradient analysis confirmed that large-norm embeddings are a primary source of instability. In the Poincaré Ball model, the conformal factor (λ_c_x) was identified as a key driver of gradient explosion near the manifold boundary. In both Poincaré Ball and Hyperboloid models, the Jacobian of the exponential map proved sensitive to growing Euclidean feature norms, leading to destabilized gradients.
- **PPO Trust-Region Violations:** Empirical observation showed that unregularized hyperbolic agents exhibited early entropy collapse, rapid increases in entropy variance, larger update KL-divergence, and more frequent PPO trust-region violations (higher clipping fraction). This provided empirical support for the theoretical link between large-norm embeddings and training instability.

#### 4.2. HYPER++ Performance on ProcGen (PPO)

- **Superior Performance:** HYPER++ significantly outperformed all baselines. On ProcGen, it achieved a 52.3% improvement in mean test return compared to the Hyper+S-RYM agent, and also surpassed Euclidean baselines. The agent demonstrated stable learning curves with lower update KL-divergence and markedly less clipping compared to unregularized and S-RYM agents. Aggregate metrics (median, IQM, mean, optimality gap) consistently favored HYPER++.
- **Increased Wall-Clock Efficiency:** HYPER++ reduced the forward pass wall-clock time by approximately 30% compared to Hyper+S-RYM, indicating improved computational efficiency in addition to performance gains.

#### 4.3. HYPER++ Performance on ProcGen (PPG)

- **Generality Across Algorithms:** The performance benefits of HYPER++ extended to Phasic Policy Gradient (PPG), a more advanced on-policy algorithm. HYPER++ outperformed a strong Euclidean baseline in all metrics, while Hyper+S-RYM performed substantially worse than the Euclidean agent, highlighting the robustness and generality of the HYPER++ design.

#### 4.4. HYPER++ Performance on Atari-5 (DDQN)

- **Applicability to Off-Policy Learning:** HYPER++ strongly outperformed both Euclidean and prior hyperbolic baselines across all five Atari-5 games with the Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) algorithm. This demonstrated the broader applicability of HYPER++ beyond on-policy PPO and PPG, extending its advantages to value-based, off-policy methods.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Necessity of RMSNorm and Scaling:** Removing RMSNorm and the 1/√d feature scaling led to complete learning failure, empirically confirming Proposition 4.2 and the critical importance of bounding embedding norms. This failure was characterized by growing embedding norms and near-zero gradients in the encoder's final layer.
- **Impact of Learned Scaling:** Removing the learned feature scaling component also degraded performance, underscoring its synergistic role with RMSNorm in expanding usable hyperbolic volume without destabilizing training.
- **Categorical Value Loss Benefit:** Replacing the categorical HL-Gauss loss with MSE or C51 (another distributional loss) generally degraded performance, particularly for Euclidean agents. This suggested that categorical losses provide a better geometric fit for hyperbolic agents due to the nature of hyperbolic multinomial logistic regression, thereby stabilizing the critic.
- **Hyperboloid Advantage:** Using the Poincaré Ball model instead of the Hyperboloid resulted in a modest drop in performance, consistent with the identified instability arising from the conformal factor in the Poincaré Ball.
- **SpectralNorm Limitations:** SpectralNorm, whether applied fully or only to the penultimate layer, resulted in complete learning failure, reinforcing the finding that it is not an effective stabilization strategy for this context compared to the proposed RMSNorm.
- **Euclidean Baseline with Regularization:** Equipping Euclidean agents with HYPER++'s regularization (RMSNorm + scaling) improved their performance over standard Euclidean, but still yielded slightly lower IQM than the full HYPER++ with the Hyperboloid. This indicates that while regularization is crucial, hyperbolic representations, when properly managed, can offer an additional advantage.

#### 4.6. Gradient and Loss Variance Analysis

- The categorical loss, despite having higher loss values and variance compared to MSE, was found to yield smaller, lower-variance gradients in the penultimate layer. This suggests that the categorical formulation contributes to more stable gradient dynamics, aligning with its observed performance benefits.

In summary, the research successfully identified the core optimization challenges in hyperbolic deep RL, developed a theoretically grounded and empirically validated solution in HYPER++, and demonstrated its superiority across a range of benchmarks and algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of deep reinforcement learning and hyperbolic deep learning in several ways, with potential implications for future work:

- **Formal Understanding of Hyperbolic Deep RL Failures:** The paper provides a formal, gradient-level analysis that clarifies why previous hyperbolic deep RL agents faced optimization challenges. By linking large-norm embeddings, the Poincaré Ball's conformal factor, and the Jacobian of the exponential map to gradient instability and PPO trust-region violations, the work offers a foundational understanding missing in prior literature. This diagnostic insight is significant for guiding future development in hyperbolic geometry for deep learning.
- **Development of a Robust and Efficient Agent:** The introduction of HYPER++ offers a principled and empirically validated solution to the identified optimization issues. Its three components—RMSNorm with learned feature scaling, the Hyperboloid model, and a categorical value loss—address complementary sources of instability, leading to stable, high-performance learning.
- **Demonstrated Performance Superiority:** HYPER++ significantly outperforms existing hyperbolic deep RL agents and often surpasses Euclidean baselines across diverse benchmarks (ProcGen, Atari-5) and algorithms (PPO, PPG, DDQN). This demonstrates that the theoretical advantages of hyperbolic geometry can be realized in practice when optimization challenges are properly addressed. The consistent gains across on-policy and off-policy methods suggest broad applicability.
- **Improved Computational Efficiency:** The reduction in wall-clock time compared to previous hyperbolic methods indicates that the proposed solutions not only enhance performance but also improve the practicality and efficiency of training hyperbolic deep RL agents.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for future exploration:
 - **Representation Analysis:** Further investigation into *what* specific hierarchical structures hyperbolic representations capture in RL state spaces could yield deeper insights into their cognitive advantages.
 - **Geometric-Algorithmic Interaction:** Understanding the interplay between geometric choices (e.g., curvature, model) and the design of different deep RL algorithms remains an unexplored area.
 - **Environment Suitability:** Identifying specific types of environments or tasks where hyperbolic representations provide the most significant benefits is a crucial next step.
 - **Generalization:** The findings could inform regularization strategies for other areas of hyperbolic deep learning that face similar numerical stability issues.
- **Enhanced Reproducibility:** By releasing the codebase, the authors contribute to better reproducibility in the field, which is critical for scientific progress in deep RL.

Overall, this work moves hyperbolic deep RL beyond a nascent stage characterized by theoretical promise and practical instability, establishing a robust framework that enables more effective and efficient exploration of hyperbolic geometry's benefits in sequential decision-making.
