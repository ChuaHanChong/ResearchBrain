# MT-Opt: Continuous Multi-Task Robotic Reinforcement Learning at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Robotics at Google developed MT-Opt, a distributed system for continuously training robots on multiple manipulation tasks using real-world data from a fleet of 7 robots. This system achieves approximately a 3x average improvement in success rate over single-task and naive multi-task baselines, significantly accelerating the acquisition of diverse skills.

## Problem

- Existing deep reinforcement learning methods for robots are sample-intensive and expensive, requiring extensive training for each individual skill.
- Scaling reward specification for a large and diverse set of robotic tasks is complex and labor-intensive.
- Multi-task learning in robotics faces challenges like negative transfer and inefficient data reuse when sharing experience across tasks.

## Method

- MT-Opt, a distributed, off-policy multi-task reinforcement learning system based on a shared Q-function, leverages a fleet of 7 robots for continuous data collection.
- Task definition is simplified through a Multi-Task Success Detector (SD) that learns from intuitive, example-based user input.
- Novel data management strategies, including skill-based task impersonation and data rebalancing, maximize data efficiency and mitigate negative transfer.

## Results

- MT-Opt delivered a 3x average improvement in success rate across 12 manipulation tasks compared to single-task and naive multi-task baselines.
- The system demonstrated rapid learning of novel tasks, achieving 70% success for 'pick-cloth' and 44% for 'cover-object' in less than a day of robot interaction, significantly faster than dedicated single-task policies.
- Training on a broader set of 12 tasks yielded 7% and 22% higher success rates on specific tasks ('lift-any', 'place-any') compared to a policy trained only on those two tasks, indicating stronger shared representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on a wider set of tasks enhances shared visual and action representations, leading to improved performance on individual tasks.
> - Skill-based task impersonation and within-batch data rebalancing are critical for effectively leveraging shared experience and preventing performance degradation in multi-task settings.
> - Easier, general tasks can bootstrap the learning of more complex or specific tasks by providing valuable successful and unsuccessful data points.
