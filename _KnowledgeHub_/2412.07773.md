# Mobile-TeleVision: Predictive Motion Priors for Humanoid Whole-Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A team from UC San Diego and MIT introduced Mobile-TeleVision, a humanoid whole-body control framework that uses learned predictive motion priors to combine precise upper-body manipulation via inverse kinematics with robust lower-body locomotion from reinforcement learning. This approach achieved lower upper-body tracking errors and enhanced stability in diverse real-world teleoperated tasks, demonstrated on robots like the Unitree H1.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for humanoid whole-body control compromise precision and expressiveness for high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) upper-body manipulation, especially during dynamic movements.
- Fully decoupling upper and lower-body control, while precise for manipulation, leads to instability due to the neglect of inter-body dynamics and impacts robot balance.
- Integrating robust locomotion with precise, expressive manipulation remains a core challenge, hindering humanoids from performing complex, human-like tasks in dynamic environments.

## Method

- Developed a hybrid control architecture that decouples upper-body control using inverse kinematics (IK) for precision, from lower-body control using ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== for robust locomotion.
- Introduced Predictive Motion Priors (PMP) learned via a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) to represent anticipated upper-body movements, which are then observed by the lower-body RL policy to maintain whole-body stability.
- Implemented a three-stage training pipeline encompassing human motion retargeting, CVAE training for PMP, and PPO-based RL policy training with an adaptive motion curriculum to facilitate learning complex upper-body dynamics.

## Results

- Achieved significantly lower upper-body joint position errors (**0.02355** for H1) and improved smoothness in simulation compared to purely RL-based whole-body control methods.
- Demonstrated robust locomotion and enhanced stability under external disturbances (approx. **25%** lower projected gravity error) in simulation and improved recovery times (4.46s vs. 5.38s) in real-world push tests.
- Enabled the Unitree H1 humanoid robot to perform a diverse array of real-world teleoperated loco-manipulation tasks, such as pushing a wheelchair and using an elevator, with precise arm tracking errors in the centimeter range.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predictive Motion Priors can effectively bridge decoupled control components in a humanoid, allowing a lower-body locomotion policy to anticipate and compensate for upper-body movements, thus maintaining stability.
> - Combining classical control methods (IK) for high-precision, high-DoF upper-body manipulation with learning-based methods (RL) for robust locomotion is more effective than purely RL-based whole-body control.
> - An adaptive motion curriculum significantly improves the training process for RL policies by progressively introducing more challenging upper-body motions, leading to better exploration and performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has long pursued the grand challenge of enabling robots to move and interact with the world as seamlessly and dexterously as humans. A critical hurdle in this pursuit is achieving **whole-body control (WBC)** that simultaneously enables robust locomotion and precise manipulation. This paper directly addresses this challenge, aiming to bridge a crucial gap in the current state-of-the-art.

Historically, humanoid control has been approached through dynamics modeling and classical control methods (e.g.,–). While these methods offer precision, they often struggle with adaptability and robustness in dynamic, unstructured, and unpredictable real-world environments. More recently, deep reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm, enabling legged robots to learn highly robust and dynamic locomotion skills, even for complex maneuvers like parkour. This success has led to explorations of RL for whole-body loco-manipulation in both quadrupedal,– and humanoid robots.

However, the authors identify a significant limitation in existing RL-based whole-body control: while robust locomotion has improved, these methods often sacrifice **precision and expressiveness for high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) upper-body manipulation**. When RL policies are trained to control all DoFs (both upper and lower body) simultaneously, they tend to "overfit to particular modes" and produce "unpredictable actions for arms," especially in dynamic walking scenarios. This is partly due to the high dimensionality of the action space and the complexity of learning intricate, precise manipulation alongside robust balance. Prior works like ExBody, OmniH2O, and HumanPlus have made strides in robust whole-body control, but often focus on humanoids with fewer arm DoFs (4-5) and do not prioritize or effectively achieve precise manipulation. Fully decoupling upper and lower body control, while beneficial for manipulation precision, has been shown to lead to instability due to the neglect of inter-body dynamics.

"Mobile-TeleVision" directly tackles this dilemma by proposing a **hybrid approach**. It advocates for decoupling upper-body control (using classical methods like inverse kinematics for precision) from lower-body control (using RL for robustness) but critically re-introduces interaction through "Predictive Motion Priors" (PMP). This innovative use of CVAE-learned representations allows the lower-body RL policy to *anticipate* and *account for* the precise upper-body movements, thereby maintaining stability without forcing the RL to learn intricate manipulation directly.

This work also significantly contributes to the growing field of **teleoperation for complex robotic tasks**. By providing a stable and precise control foundation, it enables operators to intuitively control humanoids for diverse loco-manipulation tasks using immersive interfaces (e.g., Apple Vision Pro). This aligns with the broader goal of extending human capabilities through advanced human-robot interfaces. The paper positions itself as advancing beyond prior RL-centric approaches by specifically addressing the need for high-fidelity manipulation alongside dynamic locomotion, a crucial step towards truly versatile and functional humanoid robots in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a novel whole-body control system for humanoid robots that seamlessly integrates **precise and expressive upper-body manipulation with robust lower-body locomotion**, particularly in dynamic and load-bearing scenarios. The authors articulate a clear motivation derived from the limitations of current state-of-the-art methods in humanoid control.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing the Precision-Robustness Trade-off:** Current Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for whole-body control, while achieving robust locomotion, often compromise the precision and expressiveness required for high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) upper-body manipulation. RL policies tend to struggle with fine positional and orientation control for complex arms, especially during dynamic movements like walking.
- **Mimicking Human Capabilities:** Humans effortlessly combine precise grasping and manipulation with dynamic locomotion (e.g., walking while carrying a box, dancing with expressive arm movements). Replicating this integrated capability in humanoids is essential for their utility in diverse real-world tasks.
- **Overcoming Instability from Full Decoupling:** While direct control methods like Inverse Kinematics (IK) offer manipulation precision, a complete separation of upper- and lower-body control can lead to instability and loss of balance, as the dynamics of the arms directly influence the robot's center of mass and overall stability.
- **Enhancing Teleoperation:** For remote human operators, efficiently controlling a humanoid to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks requires a system that handles lower-body balance autonomously, allowing the operator to focus on precise arm and hand operations. Current systems often impose a high cognitive load on the operator due to the need to manage both.

Based on these motivations, the authors set forth the following specific objectives for their proposed "Mobile-TeleVision" system:
1. **Develop a Decoupled Control Architecture:** To achieve both precision and robustness, the system aims to logically separate upper-body control (for precise manipulation) from lower-body control (for robust locomotion).
2. **Introduce Predictive Motion Priors (PMP):** To mitigate the instability caused by full decoupling, a mechanism is needed to inform the lower-body locomotion controller about the upper-body movements. The objective is to learn an effective representation of anticipated upper-body motions as "PMP."
3. **Integrate PMP into RL Policy:** The learned PMP should serve as a critical observation for the lower-body RL policy, enabling it to adapt its locomotion strategy to maintain balance in response to (or in anticipation of) upper-body actions.
4. **Demonstrate Superior Manipulation Precision:** The system must achieve significantly higher precision and smoothness in upper-body motion tracking compared to purely RL-based whole-body control methods.
5. **Maintain Robust Locomotion and Stability:** Despite the emphasis on upper-body precision, the lower-body must remain robust and stable during various dynamic tasks, including those involving external disturbances and load bearing.
6. **Validate in Real-World Teleoperation:** The final objective is to demonstrate the system's effectiveness and practical applicability through real-world experiments on high-DoF humanoid robots under teleoperation, performing a diverse set of loco-manipulation tasks.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to pave the way for more capable and versatile humanoid robots that can fluidly execute complex tasks in human environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the "Mobile-TeleVision" methodology is a novel hybrid control architecture that decouples upper-body control for precision and lower-body control for robustness, while employing learned "Predictive Motion Priors" (PMP) to bridge these two components and maintain whole-body stability. The approach is structured into three main stages: motion retargeting, motion prior training, and decoupled policy training and deployment.

**A. Decoupled Control Policy (π = [π_upper, π_lower])**
The system conceptualizes the control policy as two distinct parts:
- **Upper-Body Control (π_upper):** This is an *open-loop control* that directly outputs target joint positions (q_upper) for the robot's waist, shoulders, elbows, wrists, and hands. This direct control is achieved using **inverse kinematics (IK)** and **motion retargeting**, allowing for high precision and expressiveness without the complexities of RL.
- **Lower-Body Control (π_lower):** This is a **Reinforcement Learning (RL)** policy trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Its sole responsibility is to generate PD torque control inputs for the lower-body joints (legs) to ensure robust walking and balance. It receives locomotion goals (G_m = ⟨v, rpy, h⟩) for linear velocity, orientation, and base height.

**B. Bridging with Predictive Motion Priors (PMP)**
The key innovation lies in how the lower-body policy is informed about the upper-body movements to prevent instability despite the control decoupling. This is achieved through PMP, which are learned representations of future upper-body motions:
- **Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE):** A CVAE architecture is employed to learn these motion priors. It consists of a prior model (R), an encoder (E), and a decoder (D).
- **Input and Output:** Given a past upper-body motion sequence (M_0_t = q_upper_t-W:t-1), the CVAE's prior model (R) estimates a prior distribution for a latent vector (z_t). This latent vector (z_t ∈ R^H) represents the *desired future upper-body motion* (M_1_t = q_upper_t:t+W-1).
- **Integration:** During policy training and deployment, the lower-body RL policy observes this latent vector z_t as a critical part of its state input. This allows the locomotion policy to anticipate the upper-body dynamics and adjust its actions to maintain stability and balance. The CVAE's decoder reconstructs the future motion, while the KL divergence regularizes the latent space.

**C. Three-Stage Training Pipeline (Figure 2)**

1. **Motion Retargeting:**
 - **Data Source:** Human motion datasets (e.g., AMASS) are used as raw input.
 - **Preprocessing:** Local rotation mapping converts human motion data into joint angles suitable for the target humanoid robots (Unitree H1 and Fourier GR-1).
 - **Filtering:** Data filtering and retargeting processes, similar to those described in ExBody, are applied to obtain a retargeted motion dataset (M) specific to the robot's morphology.

2. **Motion Prior Training (CVAE):**
 - **Architecture:** The CVAE employs three-layer MLPs for its prior model (R), encoder (E), and decoder (D). The latent vector z_t has a dimension of 64.
 - **Objective:** The CVAE is trained on the retargeted motion dataset (M) to autoregressively generate future upper-body motions based on past sequences. The objective maximizes the evidence lower bound (ELBO), balancing reconstruction accuracy and latent space regularization.
 - **Output:** The trained prior model (R) of the CVAE is then used to calculate the motion prior z_t, which serves as the auxiliary state input for the RL control policy.

3. **Decoupling Policy Training & Deployment:**
 - **RL Algorithm:** PPO is used to train the lower-body policy (π_lower).
 - **Upper-Body Control:** During training, the upper-body joint positions are directly set as PD targets, sampled from the retargeted motion dataset (M) for each episode.
 - **Adaptive Motion Curriculum:** To handle the difficulty of training with hard motions and facilitate exploration, an adaptive difficulty factor (α_i) is introduced for each motion. This factor scales the amplitude of upper-body motions (q_upper_curriculum = q_0 + α_i(q_upper_target - q_0)). α_i gradually increases if the robot performs well (survival time ≥ 90% total time) and decreases otherwise, ensuring progressive learning.
 - **Reward Design:** The reward function follows ExBody with additional explicit gait periodicity rewards, as found in previous work, to encourage stable and natural locomotion.
 - **State Input for RL:** The RL policy observes proprioception (joint positions/velocities, base angular velocity, gravity, previous action), locomotion commands, and crucially, the PMP (z_t) calculated by the CVAE's prior model.

**D. Experimental Setup**
- **Robots:** Unitree H1 (for real-world and simulation) and Fourier GR-1 (for simulation).
- **Teleoperation System:** For real-world experiments on the Unitree H1, a teleoperation setup is used. The operator uses an Apple Vision Pro to track hand and head movements, which are translated into robot joint angles via IK and motion retargeting for the upper body. For lower-body control, velocity commands are provided by a separate remote controller (joysticks) or by pedals operated by the same user, enabling both separate and unified teleoperation.

This comprehensive methodology allows "Mobile-TeleVision" to leverage the strengths of both classical and learning-based control paradigms, resulting in a robust and precise whole-body control system suitable for real-world humanoid teleoperation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence through both simulation and real-world experiments that "Mobile-TeleVision" with its Predictive Motion Priors (PMP) significantly advances humanoid whole-body control.

**A. Simulation Results (Table II, Figure 3)**
The simulation experiments rigorously compared PMP against baselines such as ExBody (RL-trained upper body), ExBody (Whole - RL-trained whole body), PMP without motion prior, and PMP without motion curriculum. Metrics included precision, smoothness, locomotion accuracy, and stability.

1. **Superior Upper-Body Precision and Smoothness:**
 - PMP demonstrated significantly higher precision in upper-body motion tracking. For H1, its upper joint position error (E_upper_jpe) was 0.02355, notably lower than ExBody's 0.03125 and ExBody (Whole)'s 0.03788. Similar trends were observed for GR1.
 - Upper-body smoothness metrics, such as E_upper_acc and E_upper_action, were also markedly better for PMP, indicating less jerky and more controlled arm movements. This validates the advantage of direct IK/retargeting for manipulation.

2. **Robust Locomotion and Stability:**
 - While decoupling can inherently present trade-offs for locomotion and stability, PMP, by effectively incorporating motion priors and curriculum learning, achieved robust lower-body performance. For H1, PMP showed comparable or even better stability metrics (E_g, E_lower_acc, E_lower_action) compared to the best baselines, and significantly outperformed its own ablated versions ("w/o motion prior" and "w/o curriculum").
 - This highlights that motion priors are crucial for preventing the stability degradation typically associated with full decoupling.

3. **Robustness Under Disturbance (Figure 3):**
 - Under various perturbations (e.g., pushing the robot, changing upper-body motion playback speed), PMP consistently maintained superior tracking accuracy (approximately 40% lower E_upper_jpe compared to ExBody).
 - Crucially, PMP exhibited enhanced stability (approximately 25% lower projected gravity E_g) under these disturbances. This confirms that the motion priors enable the lower-body policy to anticipate and react effectively to dynamic upper-body movements and external forces, leading to greater whole-body robustness.

**B. Real-World Teleoperation Results (Figure 1, Figure 4, Figure 5, Table III, Table IV)**
The real-world experiments, conducted with the Unitree H1 robot and an Apple Vision Pro-based teleoperation system, showcased the practical applicability and effectiveness of PMP.

1. **Diverse Loco-Manipulation Capabilities:**
 - The system enabled the humanoid to perform a wide array of complex, real-world tasks requiring both locomotion and manipulation: dancing, pushing a wheelchair, carrying a scooter, using an elevator, collecting and throwing trash, opening a fridge, retrieving and delivering items, and using a microwave (Figure 1). This demonstrates the system's ability to handle fine, dynamic, and load-bearing tasks efficiently.

2. **Real-World Robustness (Table III):**
 - In real-world push tests, PMP (with motion prior) consistently outperformed PMP without motion prior. When pushed while standing, the motion-prior policy exhibited lower maximum pitch velocity (0.241 rad/s vs. 0.331 rad/s), a tighter pitch deviation range, and faster recovery times (4.46s vs. 5.38s).
 - Even while walking, PMP showed slightly better recovery, indicating that the motion prior provides a measurable improvement in real-world stability, particularly in more challenging scenarios like standing where balance recovery can be intricate.

3. **Precise Arm Tracking in Real-World (Table IV):**
 - The system demonstrated low arm tracking errors during teleoperated tasks. For various tabletop tasks (sorting, insertion, folding, unloading), the average position errors were in the centimeter range (e.g., 0.011m for x, 0.032m for y, 0.008m for z in sorting), and orientation errors were within acceptable ranges (e.g., 0.002 to 0.171 radians).
 - These results, despite multiple layers of smoothing and potential object interaction complexities, confirm that the IK and motion retargeting approach, supported by the stable lower body, yields highly precise manipulation.

In summary, the main findings confirm that "Mobile-TeleVision" successfully addresses the core challenge of integrating precise, high-DoF upper-body manipulation with robust lower-body locomotion. The Predictive Motion Priors, learned via CVAE, are empirically validated as a critical component for achieving both precision and stability, enabling a new level of capability for humanoid robots in dynamic, real-world teleoperation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Mobile-TeleVision" project, with its introduction of Predictive Motion Priors (PMP) for humanoid whole-body control, represents a significant advancement in the field of robotics, offering substantial potential impact across several domains.

**A. Technical Breakthrough in Humanoid Control:**
- **Hybrid Control Paradigm:** The paper successfully demonstrates a novel and effective hybrid control architecture that expertly combines the strengths of classical control (Inverse Kinematics and motion retargeting for high-fidelity manipulation) with state-of-the-art Reinforcement Learning (for robust and adaptive locomotion). This avoids the pitfalls of purely RL-based systems that sacrifice precision and the instability of naive decoupling, setting a new benchmark for integrated whole-body control.
- **Bridging Decoupled Systems:** The concept of Predictive Motion Priors (PMP), learned through a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE), is a key innovation. It elegantly solves the critical problem of enabling a decoupled lower-body controller to anticipate and account for upper-body movements, thereby maintaining whole-body stability. This mechanism provides a blueprint for integrating specialized controllers in complex robotic systems.
- **High-DoF Manipulation:** By focusing on humanoids with high-DoF arms (like the Unitree H1 with dual 6-DoF hands), the work pushes beyond previous limitations of humanoid control, which often simplified arm structures. This opens avenues for more dexterous and intricate manipulation tasks.

**B. Enabling New Capabilities for Humanoid Robots:**
- **Versatile Loco-Manipulation:** The ability to perform a diverse range of dynamic and load-bearing tasks – from dancing and pushing a wheelchair to opening a fridge and putting items in a microwave – showcases a new level of versatility for humanoids. This moves beyond isolated locomotion or static manipulation to truly integrated real-world tasks.
- **Enhanced Autonomy and Interaction:** While the immediate application is teleoperation, the underlying control framework lays the groundwork for more autonomous behaviors where the robot can plan and execute complex loco-manipulation tasks more effectively.
- **Robustness in Unstructured Environments:** The demonstrated robustness against external disturbances and varying motion speeds in both simulation and real-world signifies a crucial step towards deploying humanoids in less controlled, human-centric environments.

**C. Impact on Teleoperation and Human-Robot Interaction:**
- **Reduced Cognitive Load for Operators:** By offloading the complex task of maintaining balance to the robot's autonomous lower-body controller, the system allows human teleoperators to focus their cognitive efforts solely on precise upper-body manipulation. This greatly simplifies the control interface and increases efficiency for complex tasks.
- **Intuitive Control Interfaces:** The integration with immersive technologies like the Apple Vision Pro for intuitive hand and head tracking demonstrates a forward-looking approach to human-robot interfaces. This could democratize complex robot operation, making it accessible to a broader range of users.
- **Applications in Hazardous Environments:** The ability to precisely control humanoid robots in dynamic environments remotely has significant implications for applications in hazardous inspection, disaster response, logistics, and assistive robotics, where human presence is unsafe or impractical.

**D. Implications for Future Research:**
- **Agility vs. Precision:** The paper acknowledges limitations regarding agility trade-offs due to decoupling, hinting at future research into dynamic re-integration or adaptive coupling strategies that could allow for both ultimate precision and high agility.
- **Hardware and HRI Improvements:** The discussion on hardware DoF limitations and operator cognitive load points towards continued development in robot design for agility and more intuitive, perhaps predictive, human-robot interaction interfaces.
- **Foundation for Learning Complex Skills:** The CVAE-based motion prior provides a strong foundation for learning and generating complex, human-like motion skills, which can be further exploited in imitation learning or task-specific policy learning.

In conclusion, "Mobile-TeleVision" makes a significant contribution by presenting a robust and generalizable framework for humanoid whole-body control. By elegantly blending classical precision with learning-based robustness, it propels humanoid robots closer to practical deployment in complex, human-centric tasks, setting the stage for more capable and interactive robotic assistants and teleoperated systems in the future.
