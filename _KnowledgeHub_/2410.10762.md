# AFlow: Automating Agentic Workflow Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> AFlow treats agentic workflow design as a search over code-represented graphs of LLM-invoking nodes. It runs a Monte Carlo Tree Search variant in which an LLM proposes code edits, workflows are executed for feedback, and tree-structured experience is reused. Across six benchmarks it beats manual workflows by 5.7% and ADAS by 19.5%, and lets smaller models beat GPT-4o at 4.55% of the cost.

## Problem

- Prior automated methods such as DSPy-style prompt optimization still need a manually set-up workflow. They only tune prompts or hyperparameters inside a fixed structure, so full automation is not reached.
- Graph-based or narrowly parameterized search spaces, as in GPTSwarm, cannot express conditional logic or the diversity of workflows that different tasks need.
- ADAS represents workflows as code but searches with a linear heuristic list holding simplistic experience, so it finds effective workflows inefficiently within a limited iteration budget.

## Method

- Workflows are ==code-represented edges== over ==LLM-invoking nodes==, and the search space is formalized over prompts, edges and operators. Each ==MCTS== tree node is a complete workflow, not a single step.
- ==Operators== are predefined reusable node combinations (Generate, Format, Review and Revise, Ensemble, Test, Programmer, Custom) that encode known agentic patterns and shrink the search space.
- The MCTS variant cycles through ==soft mixed-probability selection== (uniform plus score-weighted, always including the blank template), ==LLM-based expansion==, execution evaluation with repeated runs, and ==experience backpropagation==, with early stopping.

## Results

- With GPT-4o-mini across HotpotQA, DROP, HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K and MATH, AFlow averages **80.3%** versus **76.0%** for the best manual baseline and **67.2%** for ADAS. That is **+5.7%** over manual and **+19.5%** over ADAS.
- AFlow gains most on hard tasks, e.g. MBPP **83.4** versus ADAS **53.4** and MATH **56.2** versus **35.4**. Workflows also transfer across executor models, e.g. HumanEval **93.9** to **96.2** on DeepSeek-V2.5 and GPT-4o.
- Weaker models running AFlow workflows outperform stronger models on the cost-performance Pareto front, at **4.55%** of GPT-4o's inference cost. In the GSM8K ablation, removing operators still reaches **93.1%**, above manual designs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing workflows as code and searching with tree-structured, reusable experience beats linear history lists. Keeping successes and failures per node avoids the information loss of long prompts.
> - Searching over whole workflows lets cheaper models match or beat expensive ones, so automated workflow design is a cost lever, not only an accuracy gain.
> - The best workflow depends on the executing model, as a DeepSeek-found workflow underperformed on GPT-4o-mini. Operators help efficiency but are not essential, since the search rediscovers ensemble structures unaided.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The proliferation of Large Language Models (LLMs) has ushered in an era of unprecedented capabilities in solving complex tasks across various domains. However, unlocking the full potential of LLMs often necessitates the meticulous design and refinement of "agentic workflows" – structured sequences of LLM invocations and detailed instructions. This paper, "AFLOW: Automating Agentic Workflow Generation," addresses the significant human effort traditionally required for this process, proposing a novel automated framework that reframes workflow optimization as a search problem over code-represented workflows. Leveraging Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and execution feedback, AFLOW aims to iteratively refine these workflows, ultimately enhancing LLM performance while drastically reducing manual intervention and operational costs.

### 2. Broader Research Landscape

#### 2.1. The Challenge of Agentic Workflow Design

The core challenge addressed by AFLOW is the manual, labor-intensive process of designing and refining "agentic workflows" for LLMs. These workflows, which involve sequences of LLM calls, detailed instructions, and operational logic, are critical for enabling LLMs to tackle complex, multi-step tasks such as code generation, data analysis, decision-making, and sophisticated question answering. As LLMs become more powerful and ubiquitous, the demand for tailored workflows grows, yet the manual creation process limits their scalability, adaptability to new domains, and transferability of skills across diverse tasks. This human bottleneck hinders the broader application of LLMs.

#### 2.2. Evolution of Automation in LLM Workflows

Prior research has recognized this bottleneck and attempted to automate aspects of workflow optimization. These efforts can be broadly categorized:

- **Automated Prompt Optimization:** Methods like Promptbreeder (Fernando et al., 2024) and Textgrad (Yüksekgönül et al., 2024) focus on optimizing the prompts given to LLMs within a *fixed* workflow structure. While improving performance, they do not address the workflow's architectural design.
- **Hyperparameter Optimization:** Approaches such as Archon (Saad-Falcon et al., 2024) optimize predefined parameters of a workflow, but again, the overall structure remains largely static and requires initial human design.
- **Automated Workflow Optimization:** More recent work aims to optimize the entire workflow structure. Examples include GPTSwarm (Zhuge et al., 2024), which uses graph structures with reinforcement learning, and ADAS (Hu et al., 2024), which represents workflows using code.
 - **Limitations of existing automated methods:** The paper highlights that current methods often fall short of full automation. Some require initial manual setup (e.g., prompt optimization within fixed workflows). Others, like GPTSwarm, struggle to represent complex workflows with conditional states due to limitations of basic graph structures. ADAS, while using a comprehensive code representation, suffers from efficiency limitations in its linear heuristic search, making it difficult to find effective workflows within practical iteration limits. These limitations stem from either constrained search spaces or inefficient search algorithms.

#### 2.3. Positioning AFLOW

AFLOW emerges in this landscape as an advancement specifically targeting the limitations of existing automated workflow optimization. It directly tackles the need for *fully automated and effective* workflow generation. The authors explicitly state that AFLOW reformulates workflow optimization as a search problem over code-represented workflows, which are flexible and expressive enough to capture diverse and complex task requirements, including conditional logic and parallel execution, unlike simpler graph representations.

Furthermore, AFLOW distinguishes itself by employing Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), a more sophisticated search algorithm compared to linear heuristic search, and by introducing the concept of "Operators" as reusable building blocks. This strategic combination allows AFLOW to efficiently explore the vast search space of potential workflows, going beyond mere prompt or hyperparameter tuning to genuinely automate the structural design and refinement of agentic workflows. The work also clarifies the distinction between static "agentic workflows" (predefined processes with LLM invocations) and "autonomous agents" (dynamic decision-making systems), positioning AFLOW in the former category, where human domain experience can be iteratively refined.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an automated framework capable of generating and optimizing agentic workflows for LLMs with minimal human intervention. The core motivations are multi-faceted:

1. **Reduce Human Effort:** Manually designing and refining agentic workflows is a laborious and time-consuming process. Automating this can free up human experts for higher-level tasks.
2. **Enhance Scalability and Generalizability:** The manual approach limits the application of LLMs to new, complex domains and hinders their ability to adapt to diverse tasks. Automated generation can make LLMs more versatile and widely applicable.
3. **Improve Performance:** Existing methods, even automated ones, often fall short of achieving truly effective workflows, either due to initial manual setup dependencies or inefficient search. AFLOW aims to discover workflows that consistently outperform current state-of-the-art baselines.
4. **Optimize Cost-Efficiency:** Stronger LLMs are often more expensive. If automated workflow generation can enable smaller, more cost-effective models to achieve comparable or even superior performance through optimized workflows, it dramatically lowers the barrier to widespread LLM adoption in real-world applications.
5. **Formalize Workflow Optimization:** The paper aims to formalize the workflow optimization problem, treating it as a search problem over code-represented workflows. This provides a unified framework for future research at both node and workflow optimization levels.
6. **Efficient Search Space Navigation:** The vast and complex search space of possible workflow configurations (involving node parameters and edge structures) necessitates an efficient exploration strategy. The objective is to navigate this space effectively to find optimal configurations.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

AFLOW's methodology revolves around reformulating workflow optimization as a search problem and employing a tailored Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithm, with LLMs acting as optimizers, to navigate this space.

#### 4.1. Problem Formalization

The authors define an agentic workflow $W$ as a series of LLM-invoking *nodes* ($N_i$) connected by *edges* ($E$) that dictate execution order.
- **Nodes:** Each node $N_i$ represents an LLM operation characterized by parameters:
 - **Model ($M$):** The specific LLM invoked.
 - **Prompt ($P$):** Input/task description.
 - **Temperature ($\tau$):** Controls randomness of output.
 - **Output Format ($F$):** Structured output (e.g., XML, JSON, Markdown, Raw).
- **Edges:** Represent abstract structures defining node relationships and execution sequence. The paper emphasizes **code representation** for edges, arguing it provides the most comprehensive control (linear sequences, conditional logic, loops, graph/network structures), overcoming limitations of pure graph or neural network representations for expressing complex logic.
The workflow optimization problem is thus to find an optimal $W^*$ within the search space $S = \{(N, E) | E \in \mathcal{E}\}$ that maximizes an evaluation function $G(W, T)$ for a given task $T$.

#### 4.2. AFLOW Framework Overview

AFLOW uses LLMs as optimizers within an MCTS framework. To manage the vast search space, key parameters like model ($M$), temperature ($\tau$), and output format ($F$) are fixed, allowing AFLOW to primarily focus its search on the code-represented edges ($E$) and prompts ($P$). A crucial innovation is the introduction of **Operators**: predefined, reusable combinations of nodes and edges (e.g., Ensemble, Review & Revise, Test, Programmer, Custom, Generate, Format). These operators act as foundational building blocks, enhancing search efficiency by leveraging known patterns of effective agentic operations.

#### 4.3. Monte Carlo Tree Search for Workflow Optimization

In AFLOW's MCTS, each tree node represents a *complete workflow*, allowing for the discovery of universal solutions. The process is an iterative cycle:

- **5.3.1 Initialization:** AFLOW starts with a blank template workflow ($W_0$) that defines the basic structure for invoking nodes and operators. The dataset is split into validation (20%) and test (80%) sets. An initial execution of the blank template identifies high-variance problems for the validation set, ensuring effective feedback.

- **5.3.2 Selection:** In each round, a parent workflow is selected using a **soft mixed probability selection strategy**. This strategy combines a uniform distribution (for exploration, ensuring new paths or the initial blank template can always be chosen) and a score-based weighted distribution (for exploitation, prioritizing workflows that have performed well). This balance prevents getting stuck in local optima while still leveraging past successes.

- **5.3.3 Expansion:** The selected workflow undergoes an LLM-based optimization process. An LLM (acting as the optimizer, e.g., Claude-3.5-sonnet) receives the selected workflow's code and its historical performance (modifications and their successes/failures). It then proposes new workflows by modifying the code (e.g., adding/deleting nodes, changing connections, optimizing prompts). The prompt given to the LLM optimizer explicitly guides it to add, modify, or delete elements and incorporate critical thinking methods like ensemble or self-refinement.

- **5.3.4 Evaluation:** The newly generated child workflow is directly executed on the validation set. To ensure robustness and accurate feedback, each workflow is run multiple times (5 times in experiments), and its mean and standard deviation are computed. While increasing per-iteration cost, this provides high-fidelity performance signals for the optimizer.

- **5.3.5 Backpropagation and Termination:** The performance score, the specific modifications made, and the success/failure relative to the parent workflow are recorded as "experience." This experience is propagated back to the parent node and stored in a tree-structured format. This allows AFLOW to effectively reuse past successful strategies and avoid repeating failures when revisiting workflow paths. The process terminates if the average score of the top-k workflows shows no improvement for *n* consecutive rounds (early stopping) or after a maximum number of total rounds.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

AFLOW's empirical evaluations across six diverse benchmark datasets (HotpotQA, DROP, HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K, MATH) demonstrate its significant efficacy.

#### 5.1. Overall Performance

- **Superiority over Baselines:** AFLOW consistently outperforms all baselines. It yields a **5.7% average improvement** over manually designed methods (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, Self-Consistency CoT, MedPrompt, MultiPersona Debate, Self-Refine) and a substantial **19.5% average improvement** over existing automated workflow optimization methods like ADAS.
- **Robustness on Challenging Tasks:** AFLOW shows particular strength on more complex tasks, improving over ADAS on MATH and MBPP by 57%. This highlights its ability to discover effective workflows even for intricate problem-solving scenarios.
- **High Average Performance:** Across QA, Code, and Math domains, AFLOW achieves an impressive average performance of 80.3%.

#### 5.2. Model Agnosticism and Transferability

- The study investigates whether workflows discovered by AFLOW are model-agnostic. Workflows optimized using one executor LLM (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) generally show strong performance when applied to other LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-V2.5, GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-sonnet). This suggests that AFLOW generates generalizable workflow structures rather than highly model-specific ones.
- However, the best performance for a given executor LLM is often achieved with a workflow specifically optimized using that same LLM, indicating that some degree of model-specific tuning within the workflow structure can further boost performance.

#### 5.3. Cost-Performance Efficiency

- AFLOW demonstrates a critical capability: enabling smaller, more cost-effective LLMs to outperform larger, more expensive models. The Pareto front analysis on HumanEval shows that workflows found by AFLOW (e.g., with DeepSeek-V2.5 as executor) can achieve performance parity or even superiority compared to GPT-4o's direct invocation, but at a fraction of the cost (e.g., 4.55% of GPT-4o's inference cost). This is a significant finding for real-world applications where cost is a major factor.

#### 5.4. Ablation Study: Role of Operators

- An ablation study on GSM8K revealed that while pre-defined "Operators" (human-designed effort like Ensemble, Review & Revise) certainly enhance search efficiency and lead to incremental improvements, AFLOW maintains strong performance (93.1% solve rate) even without them.
- Crucially, in the absence of pre-defined operators, AFLOW autonomously developed ensemble-like structures (e.g., generating multiple solutions and then combining them). This demonstrates the framework's inherent capability for independent workflow design and its tendency to converge on robust problem-solving strategies, marking a significant step towards full automation.

#### 5.5. Insights from Case Studies

- **GSM8K Iteration Process:** The case study illustrates AFLOW's iterative refinement, evolving from a simple template to a complex structure (Figure 5B). The tree-structured MCTS allows for single-step modifications (adding operators, refining prompts) and effective learning from both successful and unsuccessful explorations, avoiding repetition of failures. The final workflow combines multiple generations, ensemble voting, and a "Programmer" operator for verification.
- **MBPP (Code Generation):** AFLOW discovers workflows akin to current manually designed state-of-the-art approaches like AlphaCodium, which involves generating code, testing it, and fixing it if tests fail. This shows its ability to converge on expert-level designs.
- **MATH:** The optimized MATH workflow combines programmatic solutions (via the "Programmer" operator) with various reasoning and ensemble strategies, showcasing its flexibility in tailoring solutions to specific domains and automatically formatting answers into required forms.
- **HotpotQA:** The optimized workflow for HotpotQA highlights the importance of execution feedback, as AFLOW learned to effectively format answers based on evaluation signals, in addition to logical reasoning.
- **Open-ended Tasks (Appendix F):** While not numerical feedback, the extension of AFLOW to open-ended tasks (like novel generation and academic idea generation, using LLM-as-judge evaluation) suggests its broader applicability. In these cases, AFLOW could generate novel-length narratives and sophisticated research ideas, outperforming direct LLM generation, further demonstrating its adaptability.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

AFLOW represents a significant advancement in the field of LLM engineering and application, promising several transformative impacts:

#### 6.1. Addressing Current Limitations

By formalizing workflow optimization as a search problem over code-represented workflows and employing MCTS, AFLOW directly addresses the major limitations of previous approaches: the heavy reliance on manual design, the constrained search spaces of earlier automated methods, and the inefficiency of simpler search algorithms. The comprehensive problem formulation lays a strong foundation for future research.

#### 6.2. Economic and Accessibility Implications

The most striking impact is AFLOW's ability to enable weaker, more cost-effective LLMs to outperform stronger, more expensive ones when operating within AFLOW-generated workflows. This breakthrough effectively removes a significant barrier to the widespread application of agentic workflows by drastically reducing computational costs. For many organizations, the high inference costs of state-of-the-art LLMs are prohibitive. AFLOW's approach makes advanced LLM capabilities more economically accessible, democratizing the use of agentic systems across diverse industries and small to medium-sized enterprises.

#### 6.3. Future Directions

- **Broader Task Scope:** While currently focused on reasoning tasks with numerical feedback, the paper explores extending AFLOW to open-ended tasks by incorporating LLM-as-judge evaluation frameworks. This expands the potential applications to creative writing, complex problem-solving without clear metrics, and general conversational agents.
- **Full Automation:** The ablation studies demonstrating AFLOW's autonomous discovery of ensemble-like structures, even without pre-defined operators, signifies a substantial step towards truly full automation in workflow design. This suggests a future where LLM systems can not only solve problems but also independently design their own optimal problem-solving strategies.
- **Enhanced LLM Capabilities:** By automatically generating and refining optimal workflows, AFLOW enhances the inherent problem-solving capabilities of LLMs, pushing their performance boundaries further beyond what direct invocation or simpler prompting techniques can achieve.
- **Research Framework:** The formalization of the workflow optimization problem provides a unified framework that can inspire and guide future research in both the theoretical understanding and practical implementation of complex LLM systems.

In conclusion, AFLOW offers a robust, automated solution for generating highly effective and cost-efficient agentic workflows. Its innovative use of MCTS and code-based representation, combined with empirical validation across multiple domains, positions it as a pivotal development that could revolutionize how LLMs are deployed and utilized in real-world applications.
