# Agentic Large Language Models, a survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper offers a comprehensive survey of agentic Large Language Models (LLMs), defining them by their capacities to reason, act, and interact, and proposes a future research agenda. It outlines how these models address key limitations of traditional LLMs and details a "virtuous cycle" for continuous self-improvement through generated empirical data.

## Problem

- Traditional LLMs are highly sensitive to prompt formulation, making effective interaction a tedious, manual task.
- Conventional LLMs frequently produce factually incorrect yet plausible-sounding answers due to a lack of grounding and prone to hallucination.
- Existing LLMs historically struggled with complex tasks requiring multi-step mathematical or logical reasoning.
- The scalability of LLMs is increasingly constrained by the availability of novel, high-quality training data.

## Method

- The authors establish a ==tripartite taxonomy==, defining agentic LLMs by their core abilities to (1) ==reason==, (2) ==act==, and (3) ==interact==.
- The survey reviews advancements in enhancing LLM reasoning through ==multi-step techniques==, ==interpreters==, ==search trees==, ==self-reflection==, and ==retrieval augmentation==.
- It details how LLMs are empowered to act via integration with ==world models==, ==robots==, ==external tools==, and by functioning as various intelligent assistants.
- The paper explores the social and collaborative capabilities of agentic LLMs in ==multi-agent environments==, including ==role-based interaction== and ==open-ended simulations==.

## Results

- Reasoning enhancements (e.g., Chain of Thought, self-reflection, neurosymbolic integration) significantly improve LLM performance on complex tasks, reduce hallucinations, and enable formal problem-solving.
- Agentic LLMs effectively utilize external tools and APIs, control robots with zero-shot generalization in physical environments, and serve as intelligent assistants in diverse fields, with some applications achieving performance comparable to or exceeding human experts.
- Multi-agent systems exhibit emergent social behaviors, collaborative problem-solving through role-based interactions, and improved Theory of Mind reasoning in large-scale simulations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agentic LLMs represent a paradigm shift from passive predictive models to autonomous entities capable of dynamic, experience-driven learning.
> - A "virtuous cycle" connects reasoning, acting, and interacting, where actions and interactions generate new empirical data, fueling continuous LLM pretraining and finetuning to overcome data saturation.
> - The increasing autonomy and real-world actions of agentic LLMs introduce critical new ethical, safety, and liability considerations that require dedicated research attention.
