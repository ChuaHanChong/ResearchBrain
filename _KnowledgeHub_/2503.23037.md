# Agentic Large Language Models, a survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper offers a comprehensive survey of agentic Large Language Models (LLMs), defining them by their capacities to reason, act, and interact, and proposes a future research agenda. It outlines how these models address key limitations of traditional LLMs and details a "virtuous cycle" for continuous self-improvement through generated empirical data.

## Problem

- Traditional LLMs are highly sensitive to prompt formulation and historically struggled with complex tasks requiring multi-step mathematical or logical reasoning.
- Conventional LLMs frequently produce factually incorrect yet plausible-sounding answers due to a lack of grounding and prone to hallucination.
- The scalability of LLMs is increasingly constrained by the availability of novel, high-quality training data.

## Method

- The authors establish a ==tripartite taxonomy== defining agentic LLMs by their abilities to ==reason== (via ==multi-step techniques==, ==interpreters==, ==search trees==, ==self-reflection==, ==retrieval augmentation==), ==act==, and ==interact==.
- It details how LLMs are empowered to act via integration with ==world models==, ==robots==, ==external tools==, and by functioning as various intelligent assistants.
- The paper explores the social and collaborative capabilities of agentic LLMs in ==multi-agent environments==, including ==role-based interaction== and ==open-ended simulations==.

## Results

- Reasoning enhancements (e.g., Chain of Thought, self-reflection, neurosymbolic integration) significantly improve LLM performance on complex tasks, reduce hallucinations, and enable formal problem-solving.
- Agentic LLMs effectively utilize external tools and APIs, control robots with zero-shot generalization in physical environments, and serve as intelligent assistants in diverse fields, with some applications achieving performance comparable to or exceeding human experts.
- Multi-agent systems exhibit emergent social behaviors, collaborative problem-solving through role-based interactions, and improved Theory of Mind reasoning in large-scale simulations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agentic LLMs represent a paradigm shift from passive predictive models to autonomous entities capable of dynamic, experience-driven learning.
> - A "virtuous cycle" connects reasoning, acting, and interacting, where actions and interactions generate new empirical data, fueling continuous LLM pretraining and finetuning to overcome data saturation.
> - The increasing autonomy and real-world actions of agentic LLMs introduce critical new ethical, safety, and liability considerations that require dedicated research attention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions agentic Large Language Models (LLMs) as a critical evolution in the field of Artificial Intelligence, moving beyond the traditional role of LLMs as passive text generators. The authors contend that while standard LLMs have "taken the world by storm" with their linguistic prowess, they face significant limitations: challenges in prompt engineering, propensity for hallucination, difficulties in complex reasoning, and a looming problem of exhausting training data. Agentic LLMs, by augmenting core LLM capabilities with reasoning, information retrieval, and interactive tools, are presented as a direct response to these fundamental challenges.

This work addresses a burgeoning area of AI research situated at the "confluence of natural language processing, reasoning models, tool integration, reinforcement learning, agent-based modeling, and social science." It signifies a shift from viewing LLMs merely as sophisticated statistical models to conceptualizing them as autonomous agents capable of perceiving, planning, acting, and interacting within dynamic environments. A key contribution to the broader landscape is the proposal of a "virtuous cycle" (Figure 1), where agentic LLMs generate new empirical data through their interactions, which can then be used for continuous pretraining and finetuning, thereby offering a potential solution to the problem of LLMs running out of training data. This concept highlights a shift towards self-improving and self-sustaining AI systems.

The authors explicitly differentiate their survey from existing literature, such as those focusing solely on retrieval and tool use (Li, 2024), autonomy and agent construction (Wang et al., 2024b), multi-agent modeling (Gao et al., 2024), or anthropomorphic approaches to interactive agents (Xi et al., 2023). By defining agentic LLMs through their abilities to "reason, act, and interact," this survey provides a unique and comprehensive taxonomy that integrates these various facets into a coherent framework. It not only chronicles recent advancements, predominantly from 2023-2025, but also contextualizes them within decades of AI research traditions, including symbolic AI, robotics, and multi-agent systems, thereby bridging historical foundations with cutting-edge developments. This holistic perspective is crucial for understanding the complex trajectory of AI research in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper, as stated in the abstract, is to **review the growing body of work in the area of agentic Large Language Models and to provide a research agenda** for future exploration. To achieve this, the authors establish a foundational definition for agentic LLMs: "large language models that (1) reason, (2) act, and (3) interact." This definition serves as the central organizing principle for their comprehensive survey.

The motivations driving the intense interest in agentic LLMs stem from several critical challenges identified with traditional, non-agentic LLMs:
1. **Prompt Engineering Burden:** Traditional LLMs are highly sensitive to prompt formulation, making effective interaction a "tedious, manual, task" for users. Agentic LLMs aim to automate prompt improvement and enhance interaction fluidity.
2. **Hallucination:** LLMs frequently produce factually incorrect yet plausible-sounding answers due to a lack of grounding. Agentic LLMs seek to mitigate this through self-reflection, retrieval augmentation, and grounding mechanisms.
3. **Limited Reasoning Capabilities:** Standard LLMs historically struggled with complex tasks requiring mathematical or logical reasoning. Agentic LLMs are motivated by the need to integrate multi-step, deliberative reasoning abilities.
4. **Training Data Saturation:** The scalability of LLMs is increasingly constrained by the availability of novel, high-quality training data. Agentic LLMs offer a groundbreaking solution by generating new empirical data through real-world interactions and simulations, enabling continuous learning and overcoming the "plateauing" of training performance.

Beyond addressing these technical limitations, the authors are motivated by the significant potential for agentic LLMs to generate societal and scientific value. They envision applications in high-impact areas such as medical diagnosis, logistics, and financial market analysis, where intelligent agents can serve as useful assistants. Furthermore, the ability of agentic LLMs to self-reflect, interact, and play roles is seen as a means to augment the scientific research process itself, including large-scale social science simulations. The paper's ultimate goal is to map out this evolving landscape, highlight the interdependencies between reasoning, acting, and interacting, and chart a clear path for future research in this transformative field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this survey is primarily structured around a novel, tripartite taxonomy that defines agentic Large Language Models by their core capabilities: **(1) reasoning, (2) acting, and (3) interacting**. This definition serves as the organizing principle for the entire review, providing a clear framework for categorizing and analyzing the diverse and rapidly growing literature in this field.

The survey's approach is implemented through three main sections, each dedicated to one of these core capabilities:

1. **Reasoning (Section 2):** This section delves into methods that enhance LLMs' decision-making intelligence. It covers advancements in:
 - **Multi-Step Reasoning:** Discussing techniques like Chain of Thought (CoT), Zero-shot CoT, and Self Consistency, which encourage LLMs to break down complex problems into manageable steps.
 - **Interpreters and Debuggers:** Exploring how LLMs leverage external tools (e.g., Python interpreters, debuggers) for formal reasoning, code generation, and self-correction (e.g., MathPrompter, Self Debugging).
 - **Search Trees:** Examining the use of external search algorithms (e.g., Tree of Thoughts, Stream of Search) to systematically explore reasoning paths and improve problem-solving.
 - **Self-Reflection:** Reviewing approaches (e.g., Self Refine, Reflexion, Self Discover) that enable LLMs to iteratively assess and improve their own outputs, often using reinforcement learning principles.
 - **Retrieval Augmentation (RAG):** Discussing methods that integrate external, timely, or specialized information into the LLM's inference process to combat knowledge limitations and hallucinations.

2. **Acting (Section 3):** This section focuses on how LLMs are empowered to perform actions in the real world or virtual environments. It explores:
 - **Action Models & Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Discussing the integration of LLMs with world models and multimodal data (vision, language, action) to control robots and understand physical environments.
 - **Robots and Tools:** Covering how LLMs can interface with external tools and APIs (e.g., Toolformer, ToolBench) to execute commands, automate tasks, and interact with computer systems (e.g., browser tools).
 - **Assistants:** Reviewing the application of agentic LLMs in various assistant roles, including conversational, shopping, travel, medical, financial trading, and scientific research assistants.

3. **Interacting (Section 4):** This section examines the social and collaborative capabilities of agentic LLMs in multi-agent environments. It includes:
 - **Social Capabilities:** Discussing LLMs' inherent abilities in conversation, politeness, empathy, strategic behavior (e.g., game theory experiments), and theory of mind.
 - **Role-Based Interaction:** Focusing on scenarios where LLMs are assigned specific roles (e.g., creator-critic, manager-worker in CAMEL, debate systems) to collectively solve tasks or explore problem spaces.
 - **Simulating Open-Ended Societies:** Covering large-scale multi-agent simulations (e.g., Generative Agents, AgentSociety, OASIS) designed to study emergent social phenomena, conventions, and norms.

The literature selection process began with a Google Scholar search on "Agentic LLM" and expanded using a snowballing approach to capture cited works. The authors specifically focused on LLM-based approaches, excluding multi-agent systems without LLMs, and prioritized recent works (mostly 2024, with some from 2023 and 2025) from reputable academic and industrial research labs. Throughout the survey, the authors highlight the "virtuous cycle" where acting and interacting generate new training data, which in turn enhances reasoning, creating a continuous feedback loop for LLM improvement. Each major section concludes with a detailed discussion and sets the stage for a comprehensive research agenda presented at the end of the paper.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey elucidates several key findings about the burgeoning field of agentic Large Language Models, structured around their ability to reason, act, and interact:

**Interconnectedness through the "Virtuous Cycle":** A paramount finding is the mutual benefit and interdependence among reasoning, acting, and interacting capabilities, captured in the "virtuous cycle" diagram (Figure 1). Enhanced reasoning (e.g., reflection, retrieval) directly improves an agent's ability to act purposefully through tools and robots. These actions and interactions, in turn, generate novel empirical data, which can then be used to finetune and pretrain LLMs, fostering continuous learning and addressing the problem of data saturation in traditional LLM training.

**Advancements in Reasoning (Category 1):**
- **Multi-Step Reasoning:** Techniques like Chain of Thought and Tree of Thoughts have significantly improved LLMs' performance on complex reasoning tasks (e.g., math word problems) by enabling structured, step-by-step problem-solving.
- **Neurosymbolic Integration:** LLMs are increasingly being combined with symbolic AI components like interpreters, debuggers, and planners, allowing them to leverage formal languages (e.g., Python, PDDL) for more accurate and verifiable reasoning, especially in mathematical and coding tasks.
- **Self-Reflection for Robustness:** Methods such as Self Refine and Reflexion, often inspired by reinforcement learning, allow LLMs to evaluate and improve their own outputs iteratively, mitigating hallucination and enhancing decision-making. The emergence of implicit reasoning (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) suggests models can internalize reasoning processes.
- **Retrieval Augmentation:** RAG techniques effectively overcome LLMs' knowledge cutoff by dynamically retrieving timely and specialized information from external databases or the web, grounding their responses in up-to-date facts.

**Emergence of Acting Capabilities (Category 2):**
- **Grounded Actions:** World models and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable LLMs to understand and perform actions in physical environments, leading to impressive zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation and navigation tasks.
- **Tool Integration:** LLMs can effectively learn to use a wide array of external tools and APIs (e.g., calculators, search engines, web browsers, operating system commands) to extend their functionalities beyond language generation, transforming them into versatile agents (e.g., Toolformer, ToolBench).
- **Diverse Assistant Applications:** Agentic LLMs are proving highly effective as intelligent assistants across various domains, including conversational support, e-commerce, travel booking, medical diagnosis and report generation, financial market analysis, and even automating aspects of scientific research (e.g., AI Scientist, SWE-Agent). Initial results in medical and financial domains often show performance comparable to or exceeding human experts.
- **Safety Concerns:** The ability of agents to act in the real world introduces new safety and security risks, particularly concerning malicious prompts (jailbreaking) and unintended actions, which are active areas of research (e.g., AgentHarm, Rainbow Teaming).

**Dynamics of Interaction (Category 3):**
- **Intrinsic Social Intelligence:** LLMs exhibit inherent social capabilities, including politeness, empathy, and strategic thinking in simple game-theoretic scenarios, which can be further enhanced by providing memory and contextual information.
- **Collaborative Problem Solving:** In role-based multi-agent systems (e.g., CAMEL, MindStorms), LLM agents assigned specific roles can collaborate effectively, leveraging collective intelligence through dialogue and debate to solve complex tasks, often outperforming single models and reducing errors.
- **Emergent Social Phenomena:** Large-scale, open-ended multi-agent simulations (e.g., Generative Agents, AgentSociety, OASIS) reveal emergent social behaviors, such as the formation of relationships, coordination of activities, diffusion of information (and misinformation), and the spontaneous development and adherence to social conventions and norms.
- **Theory of Mind:** While initially contentious, research indicates that advanced LLMs can exhibit varying degrees of "theory of mind" reasoning, enabling them to attribute mental states to others and predict their behavior in social interactions.

Overall, the survey demonstrates that agentic LLMs are transforming AI from predictive models to autonomous entities capable of complex reasoning, real-world action, and sophisticated social interaction. This paradigm shift offers a promising path for continuous AI improvement and diverse, impactful applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The emergence of agentic Large Language Models marks a pivotal shift in the trajectory of Artificial Intelligence, bearing profound significance for both scientific advancement and societal transformation.

**1. A New Paradigm for AI Development:** The most significant impact is the conceptual redefinition of LLMs from passive predictive models to active, autonomous agents. This paradigm shift addresses the fundamental limitations of traditional LLMs, such as the saturation of training data and the prevalence of hallucinations. By generating new empirical data through real-world interaction and simulation, agentic LLMs initiate a "virtuous cycle" of continuous self-improvement. This moves AI beyond static datasets toward dynamic, experience-driven learning, promising more robust, adaptive, and generalizable AI systems. The success of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics, demonstrating zero-shot generalization, is a testament to this power.

**2. Enhanced AI Capabilities and Performance:** Agentic LLMs significantly elevate AI capabilities in several key areas:
 - **Reasoning:** The integration of explicit multi-step reasoning, symbolic tools, and self-reflection mechanisms allows LLMs to tackle complex problems with greater accuracy, transparency, and verifiability, moving towards "System 2" thinking.
 - **Action and Embodiment:** The ability to use external tools, control robots, and interact with digital and physical environments grounds LLMs in reality, enabling them to perform practical tasks in diverse settings, from office automation to complex industrial and domestic robotics.
 - **Social Intelligence:** The exploration of conversational abilities, strategic behavior, theory of mind, and emergent social dynamics in multi-agent systems paves the way for more natural, collaborative, and human-like AI interactions.

**3. Transformative Societal Applications:** The practical implications of agentic LLMs are far-reaching:
 - **Personal and Professional Assistants:** They promise to revolutionize productivity across various sectors, from personalized travel and shopping assistants to highly specialized roles in medicine (e.g., medical diagnosis, note-taking), finance (e.g., market analysis, trading advice), and logistics. The potential for agentic LLMs to outperform human professionals in certain diagnostic tasks highlights their disruptive capacity.
 - **Accelerating Scientific Discovery:** The emergence of "AI Scientist" frameworks (e.g., AI Scientist, SWE-Agent, MLGym) suggests a future where AI agents can autonomously generate hypotheses, design experiments, analyze results, and even write scientific papers, potentially accelerating the pace of scientific and technological innovation.

**4. Revolutionizing Social Science Research:** Agentic LLMs offer an unprecedented tool for social scientists. Large-scale, open-ended multi-agent simulations can model complex human social dynamics, study emergent phenomena like cooperation, conflict, and the formation of social norms, and test "what-if" scenarios at scales previously unimaginable or ethically unfeasible with human subjects. This intersection opens new frontiers for understanding human behavior and societal structures.

**5. Critical Ethical and Safety Considerations:** The increasing autonomy and agency of these LLMs present significant challenges. The paper explicitly highlights open problems regarding safety, liability, and security. As agents take actions in the real world with tangible consequences (e.g., medical advice, financial trades, robot movements), questions of accountability, privacy, fairness, and the prevention of misuse (e.g., jailbreaking, adversarial attacks) become paramount. These concerns necessitate robust safety protocols, ethical guidelines, and legal frameworks to ensure responsible deployment.

In conclusion, agentic LLMs represent a frontier where AI systems evolve from sophisticated pattern matchers to proactive, adaptive entities. This development promises to redefine the interaction between humans and machines, drive innovation across industries, and offer novel insights into complex social systems. However, realizing this potential will require concerted research efforts to ensure the safety, reliability, and ethical alignment of these increasingly autonomous AI agents.
