# PP-SSL : Priority-Perception Self-Supervised Learning for Fine-Grained Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> The Priority-Perception Self-Supervised Learning (PP-SSL) framework addresses challenges in fine-grained visual recognition by introducing an Anti-Interference Strategy and an Image-Aided Distinction Module. This framework consistently achieved higher performance than state-of-the-art self-supervised methods, notably improving rank-1 accuracy by 8.78% on the CUB-200-2011 dataset for image retrieval.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods are often susceptible to "irrelevant feature interference" (e.g., background clutter), leading to feature entanglement and suboptimal intra-class boundaries in fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) tasks.
- Current SSL approaches frequently struggle to adequately represent the subtle discriminative features required to distinguish visually similar subcategories in FGVR, resulting in a "granularity gap".
- Previous attempts to filter irrelevant visual noise in SSL relied on extensive manual annotations or specialized pre-trained saliency detectors, limiting their scalability and generality.

## Method

- The ==Anti-Interference Strategy (AIS)== utilizes ==knowledge distillation== from a pre-trained ==CLIP== model, guided by a fine-grained textual corpus, to encourage the student encoder to filter irrelevant visual features and learn semantically relevant representations.
- The ==Image-Aided Distinction Module (IADM)== adapts ==Grad-CAM== by computing gradients of the self-supervised ==contrastive loss== with respect to the original input image, guiding the network to focus on subtle, discriminative regions.
- A total loss function combines the standard ==contrastive loss== with the ==AIS== and ==IADM== losses, weighted by hyperparameters, to simultaneously enhance general feature learning and fine-grained discriminability.

## Results

- PP-SSL consistently outperformed existing self-supervised learning methods across seven public fine-grained visual recognition datasets (e.g., CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft) for both image retrieval and linear classification tasks.
- On the CUB-200-2011 dataset, PP-SSL achieved an **8.78%** higher rank-1 accuracy for image retrieval (**53.19%** vs. **44.41%** for LCR) and a **4.07%** higher Top-1 classification accuracy (**69.26%** vs. **65.19%** for LCR).
- Ablation studies confirmed the independent contributions of both the Anti-Interference Strategy and the Image-Aided Distinction Module, demonstrating their combined effectiveness in improving feature discriminability and mitigating interference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal knowledge distillation, using fine-grained textual prompts from models like CLIP, can effectively guide self-supervised learning to mitigate irrelevant visual feature interference in fine-grained tasks.
> - Gradient-based attention, computed directly on the input image from a contrastive loss, can effectively highlight and encourage the learning of subtle discriminative details critical for fine-grained distinction.
> - The original image provides rich information for fine-grained recognition, and directly applying attention mechanisms to it can be more effective for capturing subtle details than relying solely on intermediate feature maps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates within the field of self-supervised learning (SSL) in computer vision, specifically addressing challenges encountered in fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) tasks. Self-supervised learning methods, broadly categorized into clustering-based and contrastive learning-based approaches, have demonstrated capabilities in learning general feature representations from unlabeled data for various visual tasks, including image classification, object detection, and image retrieval. Contrastive learning methods, in particular, have shown proficiency in feature learning by comparing positive and negative sample pairs.

However, the existing literature indicates that applying these general SSL methods directly to FGVR tasks presents specific challenges. FGVR requires models to discern subtle visual patterns that differentiate subcategories (e.g., specific bird species or aircraft models), which is distinct from large-scale general image classification. This disparity often leads to a "granular gap" where standard SSL features may not be sufficiently discriminative for fine-grained distinctions. Furthermore, prevailing SSL methods are reported to be susceptible to "irrelevant features," such as background clutter, leading to feature entanglement and suboptimal intra-class boundaries, as well as a reduced ability to represent the subtle inter-class differences inherent in FGVR.

Prior research has attempted to mitigate these issues through various strategies. Some efforts have focused on enhancing data augmentation techniques to generate more informative views or perturb feature vectors. Other approaches have aimed to improve the encoder's focus on salient regions, either by aligning attention maps with pre-trained saliency detectors or by guiding the network to match Grad-CAM outputs. While these methods have advanced the state of the art, challenges persist in effectively minimizing interference from irrelevant factors and extracting nuanced features crucial for precise fine-grained recognition. This paper aims to contribute to this landscape by proposing a framework that directly addresses these limitations through a combination of strategies designed to filter irrelevant information and enhance the representation of subtle discriminative details.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current self-supervised learning methods when applied to fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) tasks. Fine-grained recognition demands the ability to differentiate between visually similar subcategories (e.g., distinguishing between different species of birds or models of cars), where inter-class differences are often subtle, and intra-class variations (due to pose, lighting, or background) can be substantial. Existing self-supervised learning frameworks, while effective for general visual recognition, frequently encounter two main obstacles in this context:

1. **Susceptibility to Irrelevant Feature Interference:** Models are often distracted by extraneous patterns in the images, such as background noise or contextual elements that are not determinative of the fine-grained category. This "irrelevant feature interference" leads to feature entanglement, where the learned representations do not adequately separate distinct categories, hindering recognition performance. Previous methods have shown limitations in consistently filtering out such noise without relying on extensive manual annotations or specialized pre-trained models.
2. **Inadequate Representation of Subtle Discriminative Features:** Current self-supervised methods may struggle to capture the minute visual cues that define fine-grained distinctions. They often focus on broader, image-level features, missing the crucial, subtle details that differentiate subcategories. This "granularity gap" means the learned features are not sufficiently discriminative for the nuances required by FGVR.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:

- **Develop a novel self-supervised learning framework (PP-SSL):** The overarching goal is to create a new framework specifically tailored for FGVR that can overcome the aforementioned limitations.
- **Effectively filter out irrelevant feature interference:** The framework aims to guide the model to ignore non-discriminative elements and focus its attention on relevant parts of the object. This is intended to improve the quality of learned visual representations by making them more robust to background clutter and other distractions.
- **Extract more subtle discriminative features:** The framework seeks to enhance the model's capacity to identify and represent the minute visual differences critical for distinguishing between fine-grained categories. This involves a deeper exploration of details that might otherwise be overlooked by general-purpose feature extractors.
- **Improve feature discriminability and category separation:** By achieving the first two objectives, the framework intends to produce features that are more separable in the feature space, leading to enhanced recognition accuracy for fine-grained tasks.
- **Operate within a self-supervised paradigm:** The proposed solutions should ideally not depend on large amounts of labeled data for pre-training, maintaining the advantages of self-supervised learning.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the performance gap of self-supervised learning in fine-grained visual recognition by developing a framework that explicitly addresses the issues of distracting irrelevant features and the need for highly detailed, discriminative representations.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed Priority-Perception Self-Supervised Learning (PP-SSL) framework is designed to mitigate irrelevant feature interference and enhance the extraction of subtle discriminative features for fine-grained visual recognition. It integrates two primary components: the Anti-Interference Strategy (AIS) and the Image-Aided Distinction Module (IADM), built upon a standard contrastive learning backbone.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Contrastive Learning Setup

The framework begins with a conventional contrastive learning approach. Given an input image, two augmented views, `x` and `x'`, are generated. These are processed by an image encoder (`f_θ`) and a momentum encoder (`g_θ`) to produce feature embeddings `q` and `k`, respectively. `q` and `k` derived from the same original image constitute a positive pair. Embeddings from other images in the batch or a memory queue serve as negative pairs. A contrastive loss function (`L_CL`) is then computed to pull positive pairs closer and push negative pairs apart in the embedding space.

#### 3.2. Anti-Interference Strategy (AIS)

The AIS component aims to guide the student image encoder (`f_θ`) in filtering irrelevant features and learning semantically meaningful, high-quality visual representations. This is achieved through a knowledge distillation strategy that leverages a pre-trained CLIP model.

- **Teacher and Student Models:** The CLIP image encoder acts as the teacher, and the image encoder `f_θ` from the contrastive learning framework serves as the student. The CLIP text encoder provides a bridge between modalities.
- **Fine-Grained Textual Corpus:** A corpus containing `N=8` textual descriptions is created. This corpus includes one relevant, fine-grained attribute description pertaining to the image's category (e.g., "an animal characterized by feathers, wings, and the ability to fly or perch" for a bird image) and seven other descriptions deemed irrelevant to the current image. These descriptions are designed to provide semantic guidance.
- **Text Embeddings:** The text corpus is fed into the CLIP text encoder to generate normalized text embeddings `T ∈ R^(Nxd)`. These embeddings represent a shared semantic space.
- **Knowledge Distillation:**
 1. The input image `x` is passed through the pre-trained CLIP image encoder to obtain a normalized teacher image embedding `u_t`.
 2. The student image encoder `f_θ(x)` generates initial visual features. These features undergo a 1x1 convolution, ReLU activation, normalization, and a max-out operation to produce `z'`.
 3. A Hadamard product (`⊙`) between `f_θ(x)` and `z'` is computed, followed by a learnable projector, to yield the student image embedding `u_s`.
 4. Logits `l_t` and `l_s` are calculated by matrix multiplication of `u_t` and `u_s` with the text embeddings `T`, respectively.
 5. A knowledge distillation loss (`L_AIS`), based on Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the softmax outputs of `l_t` and `l_s`, is used to align the student's semantic understanding with the teacher's. This process implicitly encourages the student model to distinguish between relevant and irrelevant textual descriptions based on the image content, thereby filtering out irrelevant visual features.

#### 3.3. Image-Aided Distinction Module (IADM)

The IADM aims to extract subtle discriminative cues directly from the original image by guiding the network's attention to critical regions. This module adapts the Grad-CAM technique.

- **Grad-CAM Computation:** Instead of using a classification loss (e.g., cross-entropy), IADM computes gradients of the self-supervised contrastive loss (`L_CL` from Eq. 1) with respect to the input image `x`. This results in `Grad-wt`, which signifies the importance of each image region for the contrastive task.
- **Region of Interest Generation:** `Grad-Img` is then computed by applying ReLU to the Hadamard product of `Grad-wt` and the original image `x`. This `Grad-Img` serves as a pseudo-label, highlighting the salient regions in the original image that contribute most to distinguishing positive from negative pairs in the contrastive learning framework.
- **Feature Alignment:** Similar to AIS, the original image `x` is processed through a 1x1 convolution, ReLU, normalization, and max-out to obtain `w`.
- **Distinction Loss:** An `L_IADM` loss, structured as `Grad-Img · log(Grad-Img / w)`, is employed. This loss encourages `w` to align with `Grad-Img`, guiding the network to focus on the detailed, discriminative regions identified by the gradient-based attention.

#### 3.4. Total Loss and Inference

The overall training objective is a weighted sum of the three loss components: `L_total = L_CL + αL_AIS + βL_IADM`, where `α` and `β` are hyperparameters controlling the influence of AIS and IADM, respectively (`α=1.2`, `β=0.01` were determined experimentally).

During inference, the additional components (AIS and IADM specific layers) are removed, making the process lightweight. Only the image encoder `f(·)` is used to generate image embeddings. The final features `v` for downstream tasks are obtained by applying an average pooling and L2 normalization to a spatially weighted output of the image encoder (`z' ⊙ f(x)`).

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The method employs a ResNet50 backbone, pre-trained on ImageNet. The contrastive module follows MoCo v2, with a momentum factor of 0.999. Training is performed with the SGD optimizer, a batch size of 128, and a learning rate of 0.03. The CLIP image and text encoders utilize the ViT-B/32 architecture.

### 4. Main findings and results

The effectiveness of the PP-SSL framework was evaluated through extensive experiments on seven public fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) datasets: CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, NABirds, Flowers102, Butterfly200, and Stanford Dogs. Evaluations were conducted using two standard protocols: image retrieval (measured by rank-1, rank-5 accuracy, and mean Average Precision (mAP)) and linear probing (measured by Top-1 and Top-5 classification accuracy). The ResNet-50 backbone, initialized with ImageNet-trained weights, was consistently used across all experiments.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of the Proposed Method

- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art (SOTA) on CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, and FGVC Aircraft (Table 1):** PP-SSL consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to recent advanced methods, LCR and OLDFS.
 - **Image Retrieval:** PP-SSL achieved higher rank-1, rank-5, and mAP scores across all three datasets. Notably, on CUB-200-2011, PP-SSL improved rank-1 accuracy by 8.78% (53.19% vs. 44.41% for LCR), 4.79% on Stanford Cars (41.25% vs. 36.46% for LCR), and 3.48% on FGVC Aircraft (36.75% vs. 32.97% for LCR).
 - **Linear Classification:** PP-SSL generally achieved higher Top-1 and Top-5 classification accuracies, particularly in low-label proportion settings (20%, 50% of labels). For example, on CUB-200-2011 with 100% labels, PP-SSL achieved 69.26% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing LCR's 65.19% and OLDFS's 66.17%.

- **Comparison with Other SSL Methods (Table 2 & 3):** When compared against a broader range of self-supervised learning methods (e.g., Dino, SimSiam, MoCo V2, DiLo, CVSA, LEWEL, ContrastiveCrop, SAM-SSL-Bilinear), PP-SSL maintained its competitive edge, consistently achieving the highest rank-1 retrieval and Top-1 classification accuracies across CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, and FGVC Aircraft (Table 2), as well as on Stanford Dogs, Flowers-102, Butterfly-200, and NABirds datasets (Table 3).

#### 4.2. Ablation Study of PP-SSL Architecture (Table 4)

Ablation experiments on the CUB-200-2011 dataset demonstrated the individual contributions of AIS and IADM:

- **Anti-Interference Strategy (AIS):** The integration of AIS led to substantial improvements in retrieval performance. For instance, combining AIS with IADM (where IADM is guided by the original image) resulted in a 6.28% increase in rank-1 accuracy (from 46.91% to 53.19%) and a 7.21% increase in rank-5 accuracy, indicating its effectiveness in mitigating irrelevant feature interference.
- **Image-Aided Distinction Module (IADM):** The IADM, specifically when computing Grad-CAM with respect to the original image rather than intermediate network layers, yielded the best performance. This suggests that the original image retains the most comprehensive details for discerning subtle distinctions. The combination of AIS and IADM achieved the overall best retrieval performance.

#### 4.3. Ablation of AIS Fine-Grained Descriptions (Table 5)

Comparing the use of coarse-grained ("a bird") versus fine-grained ("an animal characterized by feathers, wings, and the ability to fly or perch") text descriptions within AIS showed that fine-grained descriptions were more effective in mitigating irrelevant feature interference, leading to improved retrieval performance (e.g., 53.19% rank-1 with fine-grained vs. 51.73% with coarse-grained text).

#### 4.4. Attention Map Visualizations (Figure 4 & 5)

Qualitative analysis through attention map visualizations (Grad-CAM) indicated that PP-SSL demonstrates an enhanced ability to focus on discriminative object parts while reducing attention on irrelevant background features. Specifically, IADM visualizations highlighted finer discriminative features within key regions, confirming its ability to capture subtle details.

#### 4.5. Further Analysis

- **Hyperparameter Analysis (Table 6):** Sensitivity analysis for the hyperparameters `α` (AIS weight) and `β` (IADM weight) on CUB-200-2011 revealed optimal values of `α=1.2` and `β=0.01`, balancing the contributions of both modules to achieve peak performance.
- **Analysis of Preset Text Library N (Figure 6):** Experiments on the number of text prompts (N) in the AIS library showed that using N=8 text prompts yielded the highest rank-1 accuracy on CUB-200-2011.

In summary, the experimental results demonstrate that PP-SSL consistently outperforms existing self-supervised learning methods for fine-grained visual recognition across multiple benchmark datasets and evaluation metrics. The contributions of both the Anti-Interference Strategy and the Image-Aided Distinction Module were validated through ablation studies and qualitative visualizations.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research introduces PP-SSL, a self-supervised learning framework that demonstrates advancements in fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) by directly addressing two prominent challenges: irrelevant feature interference and the granularity gap in feature representation. The significance of this work can be understood through its contributions and potential impact:

- **Enhanced Performance in FGVR:** The consistent and substantial performance improvements across a wide array of benchmark FGVR datasets for both image retrieval and classification tasks indicate that PP-SSL offers a more effective solution for scenarios demanding subtle visual distinctions. This is particularly relevant in domains such as biology (species identification), industrial quality control, and specialized object recognition where fine-grained categorization is crucial.
- **Novel Strategy for Interference Mitigation:** The Anti-Interference Strategy (AIS) represents a novel approach to filtering irrelevant features in a self-supervised context. By leveraging a fine-grained textual corpus and knowledge distillation from CLIP, the framework guides the model to develop semantic understanding and concentrate on task-relevant visual cues without relying on direct manual annotations for foreground-background separation. This method has implications for building more robust vision models that are less susceptible to distracting background clutter.
- **Improved Extraction of Fine-Grained Cues:** The Image-Aided Distinction Module (IADM), which adapts Grad-CAM to compute gradients based on contrastive loss and focus on the original image, offers a new perspective on extracting minute discriminative features. This approach enables the network to explore nuanced details directly from the image, bridging the granularity gap and fostering better representation of subtle inter-class differences.
- **Advancement in Self-Supervised Learning for Specialized Tasks:** This work underscores the potential of tailoring self-supervised learning methodologies for specialized tasks like FGVR. It suggests that incorporating semantic understanding through multi-modal foundations (like CLIP) and attention-guiding mechanisms can significantly enhance the utility of SSL beyond general visual representation learning.
- **Practicality and Efficiency:** The streamlined inference phase, which removes additional modules used during training, contributes to the practical applicability of PP-SSL. This design allows for lightweight deployment while benefiting from the comprehensive learning during the self-supervised pre-training.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The findings of PP-SSL could serve as a foundation for further research in several directions. This includes exploring alternative designs for fine-grained textual prompts, integrating similar attention-guiding principles into other self-supervised learning paradigms, or extending the framework to other specialized visual recognition problems that share similar challenges.

In conclusion, PP-SSL contributes a robust and effective framework for fine-grained visual recognition under the self-supervised learning paradigm. Its ability to concurrently address feature interference and enhance discriminative detail extraction represents a step forward for the field, offering practical benefits for various applications and opening avenues for subsequent research in task-aware self-supervised learning.
