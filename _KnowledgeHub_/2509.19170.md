# Soft Tokens, Hard Truths

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces a scalable reinforcement learning method for training Large Language Models to generate continuous Chains-of-Thought (CoTs) by injecting noise into mixture embeddings, overcoming prior computational and data dependency issues. Models trained with this approach achieve comparable Pass@1 accuracy, superior Pass@32 performance due to increased reasoning diversity, and improved robustness on out-of-domain tasks compared to discrete CoT training.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) using discrete Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting are limited by the sequential and rigid nature of token sampling, restricting expressivity and parallel reasoning exploration.
- Prior attempts to implement continuous CoTs faced severe computational hurdles, limiting sequence length, and required expensive distillation from ground-truth discrete CoT datasets.
- Many existing 'soft thinking' methods were restricted to inference-only application, failing to fundamentally train models to reason continuously and sometimes defaulting to greedy discrete behavior.

## Method

- The paper introduces '==noisy soft thinking==,' a method that adds Gaussian noise to the probability-weighted mixture of embeddings fed back into the transformer during continuous CoT generation.
- This noise injection creates the necessary stochasticity for applying policy gradient-based reinforcement learning (Reinforce with RLOO) to effectively train continuous CoT generation.
- The approach is designed for scalability, allowing for training with hundreds of continuous CoT tokens, and removes the dependency on pre-existing discrete CoT annotations for training.

## Results

- Continuous CoT trained models achieved Pass@1 accuracy comparable to hard-trained models across mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MATH-500) and demonstrated superior Pass@32 scores, reflecting greater reasoning diversity.
- Fuzzy and soft training significantly improved out-of-domain robustness, resulting in lower negative log-likelihood (NLL) on benchmarks like HellaSwag, MMLU, and ARC, closely matching the base model's performance.
- Hard fine-tuning of Llama-**8B**-Instruct on GSM8K led to a performance collapse on MATH-500, which was successfully averted by fuzzy and soft training, preserving or improving performance on out-of-distribution tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous CoT training significantly enhances the diversity of generated reasoning paths, evidenced by superior Pass@32 scores compared to discrete CoT training, while maintaining competitive Pass@1 accuracy.
> - Models trained with continuous CoTs perform optimally when using standard hard (discrete token) inference at test time, simplifying deployment without requiring specialized continuous inference mechanisms.
> - Continuous CoT training exhibits a 'softer touch' during fine-tuning, better preserving the base model's out-of-domain capabilities and preventing performance degradation, unlike discrete CoT fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "Soft Tokens, Hard Truths" addresses a critical frontier in the development of Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically concerning their reasoning capabilities through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting.

**Current State of LLM Reasoning:** LLMs have demonstrated remarkable success across diverse reasoning tasks, largely due to CoT prompting, where models generate explicit intermediate "thinking tokens" before providing a final answer. However, traditional CoT is fundamentally limited by the discrete nature of language tokens. Each step in the reasoning chain must be sampled sequentially, akin to a single, irreversible decision at each juncture. This inherent discreteness can restrict the model's expressivity, hinder the exploration of multiple concurrent reasoning paths, and potentially trap the model in suboptimal local solutions. This limitation stands in contrast to human cognition, which often navigates abstract, fluid, and parallel thought processes.

**Emergence of Continuous Reasoning:** Motivated by these limitations, the research community has begun exploring "continuous CoTs" or "Soft Thinking." This paradigm shift proposes allowing LLMs to reason in continuous concept spaces, where intermediate "tokens" are not discrete symbols but rather probability-weighted mixtures of embeddings.
- **Theoretical Foundations:** Prior theoretical work, such as "Reasoning by Superposition" (Zhu et al., 2025a), has formally demonstrated that continuous thought vectors can act as superposition states, encoding multiple search frontiers simultaneously. This allows for more efficient breadth-first reasoning and theoretically proves that continuous CoTs possess greater expressivity, enabling them to solve certain problems more efficiently (e.g., directed graph reachability in O(n) vs. O(n^2) for discrete CoTs). Similarly, "Soft Thinking" (Zhang et al., 2025) proposed using probability-weighted mixtures of embeddings to implicitly follow multiple reasoning paths, showing empirical improvements in accuracy and token efficiency.

**The Practical Gap This Work Fills:** Despite these compelling theoretical and early empirical demonstrations, the practical adoption and training of continuous CoTs have been severely hampered. Previous works encountered significant difficulties:
- **Computational Constraints:** Fully backpropagating through all steps of continuous reasoning (e.g., in Coconut by Hao et al., 2024) is computationally intensive, limiting continuous CoT sequences to very few tokens (e.g., 6 steps).
- **Distillation Dependence:** Many methods required distilling continuous CoTs from pre-existing, ground-truth discrete CoTs (e.g., Coconut, Codi by Shen et al., 2025). This imposes a reliance on high-quality discrete CoT datasets, which are often expensive or impossible to obtain, and still faces computational costs.
- **Inference-Only Application:** Consequently, several studies (Zhang et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025) were restricted to applying continuous tokens only at inference time, operating on models that were originally trained with discrete tokens. Furthermore, Wu et al. (2025) critically re-examined "Soft Thinking," suggesting that vanilla soft inputs often default to relying on the single highest-probability token, effectively reducing it to greedy decoding unless noise is explicitly introduced at test time.

**"Soft Tokens, Hard Truths" Contribution:** This paper uniquely positions itself by being the *first work to introduce a scalable method to learn continuous CoTs via reinforcement learning (RL) without distilling from reference discrete CoTs.* It directly tackles the formidable training difficulties that have limited the practical realization of continuous reasoning. By introducing a controlled noise mechanism, the authors enable RL training, overcoming the computational bottlenecks and the reliance on discrete CoT annotations that plagued prior approaches. This advancement represents a crucial step in bridging the gap between the theoretical promise of continuous reasoning and its practical implementation in large-scale LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind "Soft Tokens, Hard Truths" stems from the recognized limitations of discrete token-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) and the unfulfilled potential of continuous reasoning. The authors aim to overcome significant practical hurdles that have prevented the widespread adoption and effective training of continuous CoTs.

**Motivation (Problem Statement):**

1. **Limitations of Discrete CoT:** Standard LLM reasoning, enhanced by CoT, relies on discrete tokens. This sequential, symbol-by-symbol generation limits the model's expressivity and its ability to explore diverse, parallel reasoning paths. This rigid structure is hypothesized to be less efficient and less natural than the fluid, abstract conceptual processing often seen in human thought.
2. **Theoretical Promise of Continuous CoT:** Theoretical advancements have rigorously demonstrated that continuous thought representations offer significantly greater expressivity. They can enable "superposition" states, allowing models to simultaneously encode and explore multiple reasoning frontiers, leading to more efficient problem-solving in principle. This theoretical superiority motivates the pursuit of practical continuous CoT implementations.
3. **Prior Practical Hurdles:** Previous attempts to operationalize continuous CoT faced severe limitations:
 - **Computational Infeasibility for Long Sequences:** Methods requiring full backpropagation through continuous reasoning steps were computationally prohibitive, restricting CoT lengths to a handful of tokens (e.g., 6 tokens). This scalability issue makes them impractical for complex reasoning.
 - **Dependence on Ground-Truth Distillation:** Many approaches relied on distilling continuous CoT representations from existing human-annotated or model-generated discrete CoTs. This is expensive, limits the scope to problems with available discrete traces, and still doesn't inherently teach the model to *generate* continuous thoughts from scratch.
 - **Inference-Only Usage:** Due to training difficulties, much of the prior work on "soft thinking" was confined to applying continuous tokens solely at inference time to models already trained on discrete data. This merely adapts a discrete-reasoning model to a continuous input format, rather than fundamentally training it to reason continuously. Concerns were also raised about the effectiveness of such inference-only approaches, with some suggesting they often default to greedy discrete behavior.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives of This Work

Based on these motivations, the authors set forth several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Scalable Continuous CoT Training Method:** The primary objective is to introduce a novel algorithm for fine-tuning LLMs that enables them to learn continuous CoTs. This method must be scalable, overcoming the computational limitations of prior backpropagation-based approaches.
2. **Eliminate Reliance on Ground-Truth Discrete CoTs:** A crucial goal is to remove the dependency on distilling from pre-existing discrete CoT annotations. The method should allow models to learn continuous reasoning independently.
3. **Facilitate Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Continuous CoTs:** To achieve the above, the work aims to make continuous CoT generation amenable to RL training, which requires stochasticity for exploration. This involves introducing a mechanism (noise injection) that allows for policy gradient optimization.
4. **Achieve Performance Parity and Beyond:**
 - Demonstrate that continuous CoT training can achieve performance comparable to traditional discrete CoT methods for Pass@1 (accuracy on a single attempt).
 - Surpass discrete CoT methods in Pass@k metrics (e.g., Pass@32), showcasing a greater diversity and robustness in generated reasoning paths.
5. **Assess Generalization and Robustness:** Investigate whether continuous CoT training preserves or improves the base model's performance on out-of-domain tasks, mitigating the "catastrophic forgetting" or performance degradation often associated with fine-tuning.
6. **Determine Optimal Inference Strategy:** Systematically compare various inference strategies (hard, soft, fuzzy, greedy, sampled) for models trained with continuous CoTs to identify the most effective deployment method.
7. **Analyze Behavioral Differences:** Provide insights into the qualitative differences between hard and soft reasoning processes, particularly through an analysis of CoT entropy profiles.

By achieving these objectives, the paper aims to shift continuous reasoning from a theoretical curiosity and limited-scope intervention to a practical, scalable, and beneficial paradigm for LLM training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work lies in introducing a novel and scalable method for training LLMs to generate continuous Chains-of-Thought (CoTs) using reinforcement learning (RL). This method, termed "noisy soft thinking," addresses the limitations of previous approaches by enabling exploration through noise injection.

**A. Core Concept: Noisy Soft Thinking**

The fundamental idea is to move beyond discrete token sampling during the CoT phase and instead feed a continuous representation back into the transformer. Crucially, to make this continuous process amenable to RL, noise is deliberately introduced.

1. **Standard (Hard) Tokens:** In traditional LLMs, after computing next-token probabilities (p_t-1) from the transformer's output, a discrete token (x_t) is sampled based on these probabilities. Its embedding (e_i) is then used as the input for the next step.
2. **Previous "Soft Thinking" Approaches:** Prior work (e.g., Zhang et al., 2025) proposed directly using the probability distribution to form a mixture of embeddings for the next input: h^0_t = p_t-1 E (where E is the token embedding matrix). This approach is *deterministic* and differentiable, but computationally expensive for long CoTs if using backpropagation through time (BPTT). It lacks the stochasticity required for direct Reinforce-like RL algorithms.
3. **This Work: Soft Tokens and Fuzzy Tokens:**
 - **Mechanism:** The authors modify the soft thinking process by adding Gaussian noise to the mixture embedding: **h̃^0_t = p_t-1 E + σN(0, Id)**, where σ is a standard deviation and N(0, Id) is isotropic Gaussian noise. This noisy embedding h̃^0_t is then fed into the transformer for the next step.
 - **Soft Tokens:** This variant uses a moderate temperature (τ = 0.5) for the softmax computation of p_t-1, allowing for a broader mixture of probabilities before noise injection.
 - **Fuzzy Tokens:** This variant uses a very low temperature (τ = 0.0001) for softmax, meaning p_t-1 is highly peaked, making the non-noisy embedding (p_t-1 E) very close to a single discrete token embedding. Gaussian noise is then added to this nearly-discrete embedding.
 - **Noise Scale:** The Gaussian noise scale (σ) is set to 0.33 times the root-mean-square norm of the token embeddings, ensuring it's comparable to, but slightly smaller than, the embedding magnitudes. Ablations confirmed robustness to noise scales up to 1.0.

**B. Reinforcement Learning Framework**

The introduction of exploration noise is the key innovation that makes continuous CoT trainable via RL:

1. **Stochasticity for RL:** The noise (σN(0, Id)) makes the generation of continuous CoT tokens (h̃^0_t) stochastic. This enables the application of policy gradient methods like Reinforce, which require sampling actions (in this case, noisy embeddings) to estimate gradients.
2. **Objective:** The goal is to maximize the expected reward E_((h̃,a)~π) [R(a)], where R(a) is the reward for the final answer 'a', and π is the current policy (model).
3. **Loss Function (Reinforce with RLOO):**
 - The standard Reinforce theorem is applied, minimizing a loss proportional to E * [ -R(a) * (log π(h̃^0) + log π(a|h̃^0)) ].
 - The term log π(h̃^0) is critical for training the continuous CoT generation. It is decomposed as a sum of log-probabilities over timesteps: Σ_t log π(h̃^0_t | h̃^0_<t).
 - Since the noise is Gaussian, log π(h̃^0_t | h̃^0_<t) can be easily computed as proportional to -(1/(2σ^2)) ||h̃^0_t - h^0_t||^2 + cst. This means the model learns to produce embeddings that, after noise, lead to high rewards.
 - **RLOO Baseline:** To reduce variance during RL training, the authors employ Reinforce with a per-prompt leave-one-out (LOO) group baseline (RLOO, Kool et al., 2019). For each prompt, 32 sequences are sampled, and the advantage for a given sample is its reward minus the average reward of the other 31 samples for the same prompt.
4. **Computational Overhead:** The method boasts minimal computational overhead. It primarily involves storing the probability vector p_t at each step (size V, vocabulary size) and adding noise on the first layer. This efficiency allows learning continuous CoTs with *hundreds* of tokens, a significant improvement over prior work.

**C. Experimental Setup**

1. **Base Models:** Llama 3.2 3B Instruct, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, and Qwen 2.5 3B Instruct.
2. **Training Datasets (Mathematical Reasoning):** GSM8K, MATH, and DeepScaleR.
3. **Reward Function:** A verification package (Math Verify) is used. Rewards are 100 for a correct final answer, 10 for a semantically extractable boxed answer that is incorrect, and 0 otherwise. Answers are always greedily decoded.
4. **Inference Settings:** Crucially, inference is decoupled from training. For each model (trained with hard, fuzzy, or soft CoT), performance is evaluated under six different inference settings:
 - **Hard Greedy/Sample:** Discrete tokens for CoT, with temperature τ=0.0 (greedy) or τ=1.0 (sample).
 - **Soft Greedy/Sample:** Continuous mixture embeddings for CoT, with Gaussian noise σ=0.0 (greedy) or σ=0.33 * RMS norm (sample), and temperature τ=0.5.
 - **Fuzzy Greedy/Sample:** Continuous mixture embeddings for CoT, with Gaussian noise σ=0.0 (greedy) or σ=0.33 * RMS norm (sample), and temperature τ=0.0001.
 - Final answers are *always* greedily decoded (τ=0).
5. **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - **In-domain math:** GSM8K, MATH-500, OlympiadBench.
 - **Out-of-domain general reasoning:** HellaSwag, MMLU, ARC.
6. **Metrics:** Pass@1 (greedy), Pass@k (specifically Pass@32 for diversity), and Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) for out-of-domain tasks.
7. **Ablation Studies:** Performed to assess robustness to noise scale and temperature values, as well as alternative noise placement (e.g., on hidden layers or logits).

This comprehensive methodology allows for rigorous testing of the proposed continuous CoT training approach, comparing it against discrete methods, and evaluating its impact on both in-domain performance and broader model capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy and advantages of their proposed continuous CoT training method:

1. **Pass@1 Parity and Pass@32 Gains:**
 - **Comparable Pass@1:** Across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, OlympiadBench) and LLM sizes (Llama 3B/8B, Qwen 3B), models trained with continuous CoTs (fuzzy and soft) achieved Pass@1 scores broadly comparable to, and often statistically similar to, those trained with traditional discrete (hard) CoTs. This confirms that the new training paradigm is effective without sacrificing core accuracy.
 - **Superior Pass@32:** A clear advantage for fuzzy and soft training emerged in Pass@32 metrics. Continuous CoT trained models consistently outperformed hard-trained models on average for Pass@32. This indicates that continuous CoT training fosters greater diversity in the generated reasoning paths, allowing the model to explore a wider range of solutions which, when sampled multiple times, leads to a higher chance of success.

2. **Optimal Inference Strategy: Hard Inference on Soft-Trained Models:**
 - A key practical finding is that, for all training methods (hard, fuzzy, soft), **hard inference (i.e., using discrete tokens during CoT generation at test time) generally performed best**, both for Pass@1 and Pass@32.
 - This contradicts previous claims (Zhang et al., 2025) about the benefits of soft inference on hard-trained models.
 - Crucially, this implies that models trained with continuous CoTs can be seamlessly deployed using standard, discrete token inference methods. Practitioners can reap the benefits of continuous training without altering their deployment infrastructure.

3. **Improved Out-of-Domain Robustness and Preservation of Base Model Capabilities:**
 - **"Softer Touch" on Base Model:** Continuous CoT training (fuzzy and soft) demonstrated a "softer touch" on the base model's capabilities compared to hard CoT training. On out-of-domain benchmarks like HellaSwag, ARC, and MMLU, fuzzy and soft training resulted in significantly lower negative log-likelihood (NLL) for correct answers, closer to the base model's performance. Hard training, in contrast, visibly degraded the NLL, suggesting a more brittle fine-tuning process that compromises general capabilities.
 - **Prevention of Performance Collapse:** A notable instance occurred with Llama-8B-Instruct. While the base model performed well on GSM8K, hard fine-tuning on GSM8K led to a sharp collapse in performance on the out-of-distribution MATH-500 dataset. Conversely, fuzzy and soft training on GSM8K *preserved or even improved* performance on MATH-500, demonstrating better generalization and robustness against catastrophic forgetting.

4. **Entropy Analysis Reveals Behavioral Differences:**
 - The authors analyzed the entropy of next-token predictions during CoT generation. Base Llama models exhibited high entropy with hard sampling, indicating uncertainty. However, soft or fuzzy sampling on the base model did not show this "entropy blowup."
 - Post-training, fuzzy and soft-trained models largely *preserved the entropy profile of the base model*, whether inference was greedy or sampled.
 - In stark contrast, hard training significantly *lowered the entropy profile* for hard-sampled CoTs, making them resemble greedy sampling. This reduction in entropy suggests that hard training leads the model to become overly confident in a narrow set of reasoning paths. This overconfidence is consistent with the lower Pass@32 scores and degraded NLL on out-of-domain tasks observed with hard training.

5. **Ablation Studies Confirm Robustness:**
 - Ablations showed that the fuzzy/soft training algorithm is robust to variations in noise scale (γ <= 1.0) and temperature (τ ∈ [0.0001, 0.1]), indicating that the method is not overly sensitive to precise hyperparameter tuning.
 - Regarding noise placement, adding Gaussian noise directly to the input embeddings proved most effective. Placing noise on the final hidden layer or logits was less successful, with only a specific "top-k logits" variant showing limited improvement, suggesting that the initial embedding space is a crucial point for injecting exploration.

In summary, the results firmly establish that learning continuous CoTs via RL is not only feasible but also offers substantial benefits in terms of reasoning diversity, robustness, and preservation of base model capabilities, all while maintaining competitive accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Soft Tokens, Hard Truths" carries significant implications for the future of Large Language Models and their reasoning capabilities. It addresses a critical practical bottleneck in an emerging field, validating theoretical promises and opening new avenues for research and application.

1. **Bridging Theory and Practice for Continuous Reasoning:** This paper marks a pivotal step in realizing the practical benefits of continuous reasoning in LLMs. While theoretical works had highlighted the superior expressivity and efficiency of continuous thought, practical implementation was limited by training difficulties. By introducing the first scalable RL framework for continuous CoT training without relying on ground-truth discrete CoTs, the authors have effectively bridged this gap. This demonstration is a "hard truth" that soft tokens are not just a theoretical curiosity but a viable and beneficial approach.

2. **Scalability and Efficiency:** The method's minimal computational overhead, allowing for continuous CoTs with *hundreds of tokens*, is a game-changer. Previous approaches were restricted to very short continuous sequences. This scalability makes continuous reasoning applicable to real-world, complex problems that demand longer, more intricate chains of thought. It means LLMs can now potentially engage in deeper, more elaborate continuous reasoning processes.

3. **Enhanced Reasoning Diversity and Robustness:** The significant improvements in Pass@32 scores directly demonstrate that continuous CoT training fosters a richer and more diverse set of reasoning paths. This increased diversity can lead to more robust problem-solving, as the model is less likely to get stuck on a single, potentially flawed, trajectory. This is particularly valuable in domains like mathematics where multiple valid approaches might exist, or where initial incorrect steps can be corrected by exploring alternatives.

4. **"Softer Touch" Fine-tuning and Generalization:** The finding that continuous CoT training better preserves the base model's out-of-domain performance and log-likelihood is highly significant. Traditional fine-tuning, especially with discrete CoT reinforcement, often risks "catastrophic forgetting" or performance degradation on tasks outside the fine-tuning distribution. The "softer touch" of continuous training, particularly its ability to prevent the performance collapse observed with hard training on Llama-8B-Instruct, suggests a more harmonious integration of new reasoning skills without compromising the model's broader knowledge and capabilities. This could lead to more generally intelligent and adaptable LLMs.

5. **Simplified Deployment and Adoption:** The counter-intuitive but highly practical finding that *hard inference on soft-trained models* yields the best results is a major win for deployment. It means that organizations can leverage the benefits of continuous CoT training without needing to overhaul their inference infrastructure to support continuous token processing. The trained models can be used with existing discrete decoding pipelines, easing their integration into current systems.

6. **Insights into LLM Internal Mechanics:** The entropy analysis offers valuable insights into how different training paradigms affect an LLM's internal reasoning dynamics. The observation that hard training leads to overconfident, lower-entropy CoT distributions, while continuous training maintains a more open, base-model-like entropy, suggests a fundamental difference in how these models represent and explore possibilities. This understanding can inform future research into interpretability and controlling LLM behavior.

7. **Future Research Directions:** This work opens numerous avenues for future exploration:
 - Applying continuous CoTs to a wider range of reasoning tasks beyond mathematics.
 - Investigating the properties of the learned continuous representations themselves.
 - Exploring even more sophisticated RL algorithms tailored for continuous thought spaces.
 - Understanding the neurological or cognitive parallels between continuous reasoning in LLMs and human thought processes.

In essence, "Soft Tokens, Hard Truths" transforms continuous reasoning from an academic curiosity into a viable, scalable, and advantageous paradigm for improving LLM capabilities. It offers a clear path towards building more robust, diverse, and adaptable AI systems, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in complex reasoning tasks.
