# Tri-Perspective View for Vision-Based 3D Semantic Occupancy Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> TPVFormer predicts dense 3D semantic occupancy from surround-camera images using a tri-perspective view (TPV) representation, three orthogonal 2D planes fused by a transformer encoder with image cross-attention and cross-view hybrid-attention, trained only on sparse LiDAR points, achieving LiDAR-comparable segmentation accuracy on nuScenes.

## Problem

- Voxel grids describe fine-grained 3D structure accurately but scale cubically in storage and compute, making real-time onboard deployment impractical.
- BEV representations are efficient but collapse the height axis into a single plane, discarding vertical structure needed for full 3D occupancy.
- No prior vision-only method demonstrates dense, all-voxel semantic occupancy prediction trained from only sparse LiDAR point supervision.

## Method

- ==Tri-Perspective View (TPV) representation==: three orthogonal planes (top, side, front) whose bilinearly-sampled and summed features approximate full 3D voxel features at sub-cubic complexity.
- **TPVFormer** encoder: transformer with ==deformable image cross-attention (ICA)== lifting multi-camera 2D features onto TPV grid queries via projected reference points.
- ==Cross-view hybrid-attention (CVHA)== plus stacked ==HCAB/HAB blocks== exchange information across the three TPV planes for contextual refinement before a lightweight MLP segmentation head.

## Results

- TPVFormer-Base reaches **69.4%** mIoU on nuScenes LiDAR segmentation test set, comparable to LiDAR-based methods and **12.7%** higher mIoU than a BEVFormer baseline on validation.
- On SemanticKITTI semantic scene completion, TPVFormer attains **34.25** IoU / **11.26** mIoU, outperforming MonoScene while using only **6.0M** parameters versus **15.7M** and **128G** versus **500G** FLOPS.
- Ablations show TPV resolution matters more than feature dimension (**68.86** vs **56.21** mIoU when scaling), and joint voxel-plus-point loss supervision balances point and voxel mIoU near **64%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing 3D space into three complementary orthogonal planes recovers near-voxel expressiveness while keeping BEV-level efficiency, a general recipe for dense 3D tasks.
> - A model trained with only sparse point supervision can generalize to dense, arbitrary-resolution predictions at test time without retraining, suggesting implicit interpolation captures scene continuity.
> - Vision-only pipelines can match LiDAR-based accuracy on segmentation, indicating camera-derived features already encode enough geometric cues for fine-grained 3D understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work operates within the field of 3D perception for autonomous driving, specifically focusing on vision-based approaches. Traditionally, 3D perception heavily relies on LiDAR sensors, which provide direct depth measurements. However, vision-based methods, utilizing surround-camera RGB images, are emerging as a cost-effective alternative.

Current vision-centric 3D perception methods commonly employ Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representations. BEV-based approaches map the 3D scene onto a 2D ground plane, which is efficient for tasks like 3D object detection. However, these methods implicitly encode height information, leading to difficulties in describing fine-grained 3D structures and semantic occupancy, which require a more comprehensive understanding of the entire 3D space.

An alternative is voxel-based representation, which discretizes the 3D space into cubes (voxels) and assigns features to each. While voxel representations offer high accuracy for tasks like 3D semantic occupancy prediction (e.g., LiDAR segmentation, 3D scene completion), their computational and storage complexity scales cubically with resolution ($O(HWD)$), making them inefficient for real-time autonomous driving applications, especially with multi-view camera inputs.

This research addresses the gap between the efficiency of BEV and the expressiveness of voxel representations. It aims to develop a representation that can model fine-grained 3D structures comprehensively and efficiently using only camera inputs, thereby advancing vision-centric autonomous driving systems beyond coarse object detection. The paper also draws inspiration from recent work in explicit-implicit hybrid scene representations, adapting their concepts to large-scale outdoor scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel 3D scene representation and an associated vision-based encoder that can accurately and efficiently predict the semantic occupancy of all volumes in a 3D space, using only surround-camera RGB images as input.

The key motivations driving this work include:

1. **Limitations of Existing 3D Scene Representations:**
 - **Voxel Representation:** While capable of describing fine-grained 3D structures, voxel-based methods suffer from high computational and storage costs ($O(HWD)$), which are prohibitive for real-time applications and challenging to generalize to multi-view camera inputs due to inefficiency.
 - **Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) Representation:** BEV is more efficient ($O(HW)$) by flattening the height dimension. However, this simplification leads to a loss of fine-grained 3D structural information, making it difficult to fully understand objects with complex 3D shapes or to perform dense 3D semantic occupancy prediction. While effective for 3D object detection (predicting bounding boxes), BEV struggles with tasks requiring a comprehensive and fine-grained understanding of the entire 3D scene.

2. **Need for Comprehensive 3D Understanding in Autonomous Driving:** For safer and more robust autonomous driving, a fine-grained understanding of the 3D environment is crucial. This includes not just detecting objects but also predicting the semantic class and occupancy of every point or voxel in the surrounding space, which current BEV-based vision systems struggle to achieve.

3. **Advancing Vision-Centric Perception:** The research is motivated by the potential of vision-based systems to offer a cost-effective alternative to LiDAR. Overcoming the depth perception limitations of cameras to achieve a level of 3D environmental understanding comparable to or exceeding LiDAR-based methods would represent a significant step forward for autonomous driving technology. The authors aim to demonstrate that vision-based methods can achieve comparable performance on tasks traditionally dominated by LiDAR, such as LiDAR segmentation.

4. **Addressing the Challenge of Sparse Supervision:** The paper specifically formulates a challenging task where the model is trained with only sparse LiDAR semantic labels (point-level supervision) but must produce dense semantic occupancy predictions for all voxels during inference. This simulates real-world scenarios where dense 3D ground truth is expensive and difficult to obtain.

In summary, the authors aim to introduce an expressive yet efficient 3D scene representation that bridges the gap between the coarse understanding of BEV and the computational burden of voxel-based methods, enabling fine-grained 3D semantic occupancy prediction from camera inputs for autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a Tri-Perspective View (TPV) representation coupled with a transformer-based encoder called TPVFormer. The approach is designed to efficiently capture fine-grained 3D scene structures from multi-camera RGB images.

#### 3.1. Tri-Perspective View (TPV) Representation

The TPV representation generalizes the conventional BEV by introducing three axis-aligned orthogonal planes:
- $T_{HW}$: Represents the top (bird's-eye) view.
- $T_{DH}$: Represents a side view.
- $T_{WD}$: Represents a front view.

Each plane $T \in R^{Res_1 \times Res_2 \times C}$ has a specific resolution and feature dimension $C$. This design maintains the 2D nature of each plane, avoiding the cubic complexity of voxel representations, while collectively offering a 3D description. The storage and computation complexity for TPV is $O(HW + DH + WD)$, which is significantly lower than $O(HWD)$ for voxels.

- **Point Querying Formulation:** To obtain the feature for an arbitrary 3D point $(x, y, z)$, it is projected onto each of the three TPV planes. Bilinear interpolation is used to sample features from the corresponding locations on $T_{HW}$, $T_{DH}$, and $T_{WD}$. These three sampled features are then summed to produce a comprehensive feature $f_{x,y,z}$ for the 3D point. This aggregation mechanism enables the TPV representation to model 3D scenes at arbitrary resolutions, providing distinct features for different points in space.
- **Voxel Feature Formulation:** For dense voxel feature generation, each TPV plane is conceptually broadcast along its orthogonal direction. The resulting three feature tensors, each of size $H \times W \times D \times C$, are then summed to create full-scale voxel features. This allows TPV to be converted into a voxel-like structure for dense prediction tasks.

#### 3.2. TPVFormer Encoder

TPVFormer is a transformer-based encoder designed to lift 2D image features from multiple cameras into the 3D TPV space. It comprises TPV queries, Image Cross-Attention (ICA), and Cross-View Hybrid-Attention (CVHA).

- **TPV Queries:** These are learnable parameters initialized as feature vectors for each grid cell in the three TPV planes. 3D positional embeddings are added to these queries.
- **Image Cross-Attention (ICA):** This mechanism is responsible for extracting visual information from multi-scale, multi-camera image features and mapping it to the TPV queries.
 - **Deformable Attention:** Due to the high resolution of TPV queries and image feature maps, standard cross-attention is computationally expensive. TPVFormer employs efficient deformable attention.
 - **Reference Point Sampling:** For each TPV query (e.g., $t_{h,w}$ in $T_{HW}$), reference points are sampled along the direction orthogonal to its respective plane in the real world. These 3D reference points are then projected into the pixel coordinates of each surrounding camera image. Only valid camera views (where reference points fall within the image) are used for feature sampling. Offsets and attention weights are learned via linear layers, and sampled image features are aggregated to update the TPV queries.
- **Cross-View Hybrid-Attention (CVHA):** After ICA, TPV queries have aggregated visual information but lack direct interaction between queries from different views. CVHA facilitates this interaction, allowing queries to exchange contextual information across the three TPV planes.
 - **Deformable Attention:** Similar to ICA, CVHA also uses deformable attention. For a TPV query, its reference points are grouped into three disjoint subsets, each corresponding to points sampled within its own plane and projected points on the other two perpendicular planes. These sampled features from the TPV planes are then aggregated, enhancing the contextual understanding of each TPV query.
- **Overall Structure:** TPVFormer is built by stacking two types of transformer blocks:
 - **Hybrid-Cross-Attention Block (HCAB):** Composed of both CVHA and ICA. These blocks are used in the first half of TPVFormer to effectively query visual information.
 - **Hybrid-Attention Block (HAB):** Contains only CVHA. These blocks follow HCABs, specializing in encoding contextual information by allowing deeper interaction among TPV features.

#### 3.3. Applications and Prediction Head

The TPV planes generated by TPVFormer are used for downstream tasks:
- **Point Feature Generation:** For tasks like LiDAR segmentation, point features are directly obtained by querying the TPV representation using the point querying formulation.
- **Voxel Feature Generation:** For dense prediction tasks like Semantic Scene Completion, full-scale voxel features are generated from the TPV planes using the voxel feature formulation.
- **Lightweight Segmentation Head:** A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) consisting of two linear layers and an intermediate activation layer is applied to the generated point or voxel features to predict their semantic labels.

#### 3.4. Training and Supervision

The model is trained using a combination of cross-entropy loss and Lovasz-Softmax loss. For the challenging 3D semantic occupancy prediction task, training uses only sparse LiDAR semantic labels. Pseudo-per-voxel labels are generated for empty voxels during training. For Semantic Scene Completion, voxel labels are used for supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents findings across three main tasks: 3D semantic occupancy prediction, LiDAR segmentation, and semantic scene completion (SSC), along with ablation studies.

#### 4.1. 3D Semantic Occupancy Prediction

- **Qualitative Performance:** The model, trained with sparse LiDAR point supervision, generated dense and consistent semantic occupancy predictions for all voxels in the 3D space from only surround-camera RGB images. Visualizations (Figure 1, 5, 7, 8) indicate that TPVFormer's predictions are comprehensive and can appear more complete than the sparse LiDAR segmentation ground truth.
- **Object Identification:** The model demonstrated the ability to accurately identify 3D positions and sizes of both close and distant objects. It successfully identified partially occluded objects, such as a bicycle captured by only two LiDAR points, suggesting a potential advantage of vision-based methods in certain scenarios.
- **Arbitrary Resolution at Test Time:** TPVFormer's design allows for adjusting the prediction resolution at inference time without retraining. Visualizations demonstrated that increasing the resolution captured more fine-grained details about 3D objects, such as their shape.

#### 4.2. LiDAR Segmentation (on nuScenes dataset)

- **Vision-based vs. LiDAR-based:** TPVFormer is presented as the first vision-based method for LiDAR segmentation.
- **Comparable Performance:** TPVFormer-Base, using only camera inputs, achieved a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 69.4% on the nuScenes test set (Table 1). This performance is comparable to various LiDAR-based methods, which range from 56.3% (MINet) to 81.4% (LidarMultiNet), with some achieving 77-80%. This indicates that the vision-based TPVFormer can perform effectively on a task traditionally dominated by LiDAR.
- **BEVFormer Comparison:** On the nuScenes validation set (Table 6), TPVFormer-Base achieved an mIoU of 68.9%, which is a 12.7% improvement over BEVFormer-Base (56.2%), highlighting the effectiveness of TPV in modeling fine-grained 3D structures.

#### 4.3. Semantic Scene Completion (on SemanticKITTI dataset)

- **Performance vs. Baselines:** TPVFormer achieved an SC IoU of 34.25% and an SSC mIoU of 11.26% on the SemanticKITTI test set (Table 2). It outperformed other vision-based methods, including MonoScene (SC IoU 34.16%, SSC mIoU 11.08%), which uses 3D convolution in voxel space.
- **Efficiency:** TPVFormer demonstrated higher efficiency compared to MonoScene, having fewer parameters (6.0M vs. 15.7M) and lower computational complexity (128G FLOPS/image vs. 500G FLOPS/image). This suggests a more efficient approach to dense 3D scene understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Loss Functions for LiDAR Segmentation:** Experiments on different combinations of voxel and point predictions as input to cross-entropy and Lovasz-softmax losses revealed that using both voxel and point predictions yields high mIoUs for both output types. Relying solely on voxel-level or point-level supervision caused a significant performance drop for the unsupervised output type, suggesting that TPV representation might discretize differently based on supervision type.
- **Resolutions and Feature Dimensions:** TPVFormer's performance benefited more from increased resolution (e.g., 200x200x16 vs. 100x100x8) than from increased feature dimension, which aligns with its objective of modeling fine-grained structures. TPVFormer consistently outperformed an adapted BEVFormer baseline under various configurations.
- **Number of HCAB and HAB Blocks:** The balance between HCAB (image cross-attention and cross-view hybrid-attention) and HAB (only cross-view hybrid-attention) blocks was studied. Increasing HCAB blocks improved SC IoU, indicating the importance of direct visual cues for geometry. Semantic prediction (mIoU) benefited from a moderate balance, suggesting reliance on both visual and contextual information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in vision-based 3D perception for autonomous driving, offering several significant implications:

1. **Efficient Fine-Grained 3D Understanding:** The introduction of the Tri-Perspective View (TPV) representation offers an approach to model fine-grained 3D scene structures more efficiently than traditional voxel-based methods, while being more expressive than Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representations. This efficiency ($O(HW + DH + WD)$ vs. $O(HWD)$ for voxels) is crucial for real-time applications in autonomous vehicles.
2. **Bridging the Gap between Camera and LiDAR Performance:** The demonstration that a vision-based method can achieve comparable performance to LiDAR-based methods on tasks like LiDAR segmentation marks a step in overcoming a long-standing challenge in 3D perception. This suggests the potential for autonomous driving systems to rely more heavily on cameras, which are generally more cost-effective and readily available than LiDAR sensors.
3. **Comprehensive Scene Perception from Cameras:** The ability to predict dense semantic occupancy from sparse supervision using only camera inputs is significant. This moves vision-centric autonomous driving perception beyond merely detecting coarse 3D bounding boxes to a more complete and detailed understanding of the environment, including the geometry and semantics of all spatial volumes. Such detailed perception is critical for complex driving scenarios, robust path planning, and interaction with various objects and environments.
4. **Robustness to Sparse Data and Occlusions:** The qualitative findings showing comprehensive predictions from sparse LiDAR supervision and the successful identification of partially occluded objects (e.g., a bicycle with only two LiDAR points) indicate that the TPVFormer can synthesize information effectively from multiple camera views, potentially improving robustness in scenarios where sensor data is incomplete or ambiguous.
5. **Flexibility in Resolution:** The capacity to adjust prediction resolution at test time without retraining offers practical flexibility for deployment in various applications, allowing a trade-off between detail and computational load based on real-time requirements.
6. **Potential for Broader Applications:** While specifically applied to autonomous driving, the TPV representation and TPVFormer architecture could potentially be adapted to other 3D scene understanding tasks in robotics, augmented reality, or urban planning, where efficient and comprehensive 3D reconstruction from visual input is valuable.

The work contributes to the development of more robust, efficient, and cost-effective perception systems, which are foundational for the widespread adoption of autonomous driving technology.
