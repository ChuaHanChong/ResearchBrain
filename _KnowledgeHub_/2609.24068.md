# When Does Touch Matter? Charting the Vision-Interaction Gap in Cluttered Dexterous Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> A controlled real-world study of dexterous grasping in clutter with an xArm7 and LEAP hand, fusing RGB-D target-context point clouds, per-finger and wrist wrench estimates, and 508 fingertip taxels into a diffusion policy under a shared admittance controller. Holding demonstrations, backbone, and control fixed, the combined policy reaches 24/25 trials against 14/25 for vision only, with the gap widening as clutter and grasp sensitivity compound.

## Problem

- Visual geometry inputs like partial point clouds and target masks encode shape and layout but never directly measure load, support, or slip once the hand makes contact.
- Vision degrades exactly when clutter matters most: the hand occludes the target and incidental contact silently changes the scene during retrieval.
- Prior contact-aware systems vary hardware, tasks, representation, and backbone together, so their gains cannot separate sensing information from the architecture processing it.

## Method

- ==Taxel attention encoder==: 508 three-axis taxels as position-plus-force tokens through a shared MLP and two four-head transformer blocks, read out at a class token into a 128-D summary.
- ==Wrench observation== built from ==Minimalist Compliance Control== fingertip estimates from actuator currents, transformed by dual-adjoint into the wrist frame and summed, given to the policy as a direct 30-D state.
- **VWT-Attn**: concatenate visual, proprioceptive, wrench, and taxel features to condition a ==diffusion U-Net== predicting 16-step pose and joint references, all variants executing through one shared admittance controller.

## Results

- **VWT-Attn** hits **24/25 (96%)** versus **14/25 (56%)** for vision only; on the nested chain S3-S5 the split is **15/15** against **6/15**, widening monotonically.
- Single-modality ablations confirm complementarity: wrench alone **15/25**, taxel alone **18/25**, both below the combination, whose S3-S5 advantage is **5/5** against single-modality **3/5**, **4/5**, **3/5**.
- Structured alternatives underperform simple concatenation: GNN and Reactive DP reach **21/25**, FC-full **17/25**, PolicyConsensus **15/25**, whose router collapses to roughly **0.95** static weight on vision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interaction sensing pays off conditionally, not uniformly: when the grasp stays visible and tolerant, touch adds little; value scales with occlusion and grasp ambiguity.
> - Wrench and taxel answer different questions - wrench flags weak loading early enough to abandon an attempt, taxels report whether contact is distributed enough to lift.
> - At modest demonstration scale, a simple global interaction summary with direct concatenation beats relational message passing, hand-designed hierarchies, and modality-expert routing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Retrieving a specified object from clutter matters in warehouse picking and in less structured settings such as shelves and homes. A dexterous hand must find and secure the target while clutter can occlude it, constrain approach paths, and cause incidental contact. Vision represents geometry and layout. Fingertip tactile measurements reveal local contact, and wrench estimates summarize force and torque. This work lets the learned policy read these contact signals alongside vision, while the robot's compliant control stays the same for every policy. In one case study, a vision-only policy repeatedly makes contact but lifts without the target. The full policy then starts from the scene the failed policy left behind, adjusts its grasp, and lifts the target. Because of that starting point, the case study is conservative rather than favorable to the full policy.

Vision Only (4×) → Vision + Tactile + Wrenches (1×). Vision-only contact is followed by lifts without the target. The full policy then uses that resulting scene to regrasp, stabilize, and lift. This is a sequential policy-switch case study, not two independently reset trials.

### 2. What the policy can feel

The real-world system is an xArm7 with a 16-DoF LEAP hand. Four fingertip tactile pads supply 508 distributed three-axis taxels, which are spatially distributed fingertip contact measurements. The system also has two RGB-D camera views and estimated per-finger and wrist wrenches, which are force-and-torque estimates. The policy receives a target-and-context point cloud, proprioception, and whichever interaction channels a variant enables. These inputs condition a diffusion policy that predicts arm and hand references. The policy replans after executing part of the sequence, and its references run through a shared compliant controller.

The controller is the key distinction. Whether or not the learned policy observes wrenches, wrench estimates drive compliant arm and fingertip motion. Taxel measurements do not enter the low-level controller. "Vision only" therefore means that its learned policy receives no wrench or taxel observations. It still runs on a robot with force-aware compliance. The policies share 320 demonstrations, the visual observations, the action interface, and the training protocol. The experiment tests whether wrench and taxel signals help the learned policy, not whether the robot uses any force information at all.

### 3. When interaction changes the outcome

An episode succeeds only if the specified target is grasped, lifted, and stably retained at the end without human intervention. Repeated autonomous attempts are allowed within an episode. Each policy ran five trials per condition. Two conditions deliberately separate scene difficulty from target grasp sensitivity:

- **S1** pairs dense clutter with a large, exposed, grasp-tolerant sphere.
- **S2** pairs an open scene with a small, grasp-sensitive sphere.

**S3–S5** form a nested progression of harder, more confined conditions.

Across all five conditions, the combined policy succeeds in 24/25 episodes, compared with 14/25 for vision only. On the three confined conditions, the comparison is 15/15 versus 6/15. These are small-sample episode counts. The gap is small on S1 and absent on S2, where both policies succeed in 4/5 episodes. Along S3–S5, vision only falls from 3/5 to 1/5, while the combined policy succeeds in every trial.

The single-channel ablations test whether both signals matter. Vision-plus-wrench and vision-plus-taxel policies each add one interaction channel. On S3–S5, the stronger of these two varies by condition, and neither matches the full policy's 5/5 in every condition. The authors interpret wrench feedback as signaling weak or obstructed loading early. They interpret taxels as showing how contact is distributed across fingertips. This interpretation rests on the ablations and the case study. It is not a formal identification of a unique cause.

Real-world target-grasp successes by policy-visible input across S1–S5. Every controlled variant also has proprioception and the shared compliant controller. VWT-Attn (Ours) reaches 24/25 (96%), versus 14/25 (56%) for Vision only. Along S3–S5, it succeeds 5/5 in each condition, versus 3/5, 2/5, and 1/5 for Vision only.

### 4. What the comparison can't settle

The evaluation uses one LEAP-hand embodiment, one hardware class of interaction sensors, five designed tabletop conditions, and five trials per policy-condition cell. Generality to other hands, tactile technologies, or arbitrary clutter remains untested. The authors reproduced the closest vision-only prior, ClutterDexGrasp, without the shared controller. That reproduction is therefore not a matched comparison.

### 5. Contact becomes information

This work contributes a controlled real-world test of what happens when the learned policy can use contact evidence to judge and revise a grasp before committing to a lift. In this suite, that ability made the largest difference in the confined, cluttered conditions. The result shows where interaction sensing helped in these tests. It does not establish a general law for all hands, objects, or clutter.
