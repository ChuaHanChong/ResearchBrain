# FlowRL: Matching Reward Distributions for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowRL presents a policy optimization algorithm for large language models that leverages GFlowNet principles to match reward distributions rather than merely maximizing expected reward. This approach yielded superior performance on math and code reasoning benchmarks and notably increased the diversity of generated solutions.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) fine-tuned with reward-maximizing Reinforcement Learning (RL) often suffer from "mode collapse," converging on a narrow set of high-reward solutions.
- This limitation restricts the diversity of generated reasoning paths, hindering LLMs' robustness and generalization to complex, varied problems.
- Current methods tend to over-optimize dominant reward signals, neglecting equally valid but less frequent alternative reasoning trajectories.

## Method

- A policy optimization algorithm that shifts from reward maximization to ==reward distribution matching==, leveraging principles from Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets).
- Minimizes the reverse KL divergence between the policy and a target reward-weighted distribution, incorporating a learnable partition function and a trajectory balance loss.
- Utilizes length normalization to stabilize gradients for variable-length Chain-of-Thought sequences and importance sampling to enable efficient off-policy training.

## Results

- Achieved an average accuracy of **48.39%** on math benchmarks with a **32B** model, outperforming PPO by **5.1%** and GRPO by **10.0%**.
- Secured leading performance on code reasoning tasks, including **37.43%** on LiveCodeBench Avg@16, surpassing PPO by **2.33%**.
- Demonstrated significantly higher solution diversity on AIME problems, with a diversity score of 2.28 compared to PPO's 1.31, validating its ability to generate diverse reasoning paths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing for reward distribution matching rather than reward maximization inherently promotes both higher reward and greater policy entropy (diversity).
> - Minimizing the reverse KL divergence between the policy and a reward-weighted distribution is theoretically equivalent to minimizing a GFlowNet-inspired trajectory balance loss.
> - Practical adaptations like length normalization and importance sampling are critical for stabilizing gradients and enabling efficient off-policy training when applying flow-balancing objectives to long LLM reasoning trajectories.
