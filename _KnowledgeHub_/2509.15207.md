# FlowRL: Matching Reward Distributions for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowRL presents a policy optimization algorithm for large language models that leverages GFlowNet principles to match reward distributions rather than merely maximizing expected reward. This approach yielded superior performance on math and code reasoning benchmarks and notably increased the diversity of generated solutions.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) fine-tuned with reward-maximizing Reinforcement Learning (RL) often suffer from "mode collapse," converging on a narrow set of high-reward solutions.
- This limitation restricts the diversity of generated reasoning paths, hindering LLMs' robustness and generalization to complex, varied problems.
- Current methods tend to over-optimize dominant reward signals, neglecting equally valid but less frequent alternative reasoning trajectories.

## Method

- A policy optimization algorithm that shifts from reward maximization to ==reward distribution matching==, leveraging principles from Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets).
- Minimizes the reverse KL divergence between the policy and a target reward-weighted distribution, incorporating a learnable partition function and a trajectory balance loss.
- Utilizes length normalization to stabilize gradients for variable-length Chain-of-Thought sequences and importance sampling to enable efficient off-policy training.

## Results

- Achieved an average accuracy of **48.39%** on math benchmarks with a **32B** model, outperforming PPO by **5.1%** and GRPO by **10.0%**.
- Secured leading performance on code reasoning tasks, including **37.43%** on LiveCodeBench Avg@16, surpassing PPO by **2.33%**.
- Demonstrated significantly higher solution diversity on AIME problems, with a diversity score of 2.28 compared to PPO's 1.31, validating its ability to generate diverse reasoning paths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing for reward distribution matching rather than reward maximization inherently promotes both higher reward and greater policy entropy (diversity).
> - Minimizing the reverse KL divergence between the policy and a reward-weighted distribution is theoretically equivalent to minimizing a GFlowNet-inspired trajectory balance loss.
> - Practical adaptations like length normalization and importance sampling are critical for stabilizing gradients and enabling efficient off-policy training when applying flow-balancing objectives to long LLM reasoning trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "FlowRL" addresses a critical limitation in the current paradigm of training Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex reasoning tasks, specifically their tendency towards "mode collapse" when optimized via traditional reward-maximizing Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods. This work directly builds upon and diverges from established RL algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO).

**Limitations of Current LLM RL:**
Recent advancements in LLM reasoning, particularly for tasks requiring Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, heavily rely on RL post-training (e.g., REINFORCE, PPO, GRPO). While effective, these reward-maximizing methods are prone to "overfitting to the dominant mode of the reward distribution" (Gao et al., 2023). This means they tend to converge on a few high-reward solution paths, neglecting valid but less frequent alternative reasoning trajectories. This limitation restricts the diversity of generated solutions and can hinder generalization to novel problems, making LLMs less robust in real-world applications where multiple correct, diverse approaches might exist.

**Leveraging GFlowNets and Distribution Matching:**
FlowRL distinguishes itself by shifting from a reward-maximization objective to a **reward distribution-matching** objective. This paradigm shift is inspired by Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) (Bengio et al., 2023a), a probabilistic framework designed to sample discrete, compositional objects in proportion to a given reward function, explicitly promoting diversity by balancing probability flows. FlowRL reinterprets the problem of LLM reasoning as one of aligning the model's policy distribution with a target distribution derived from the reward function, rather than simply maximizing the expected reward. This makes FlowRL a significant bridge between the GFlowNets framework and the LLM fine-tuning landscape.

**Comparison with Related Work:**
- **Traditional RL (PPO, GRPO):** FlowRL directly challenges these baselines by offering a fundamentally different optimization objective that inherently encourages diversity, rather than relying on auxiliary regularization terms.
- **Entropy Regularization:** While existing methods sometimes add entropy terms to promote diversity (e.g., Ahmed et al., 2019; Cheng et al., 2025), FlowRL’s distribution-matching objective *implicitly* maximizes both reward and policy entropy, offering a more principled and integrated approach to diversity.
- **Flow-Matching Policies:** Previous work in flow matching for policy optimization (McAllister et al., 2025; Pfrommer et al., 2025; Park et al., 2025) has largely focused on continuous control, image generation, or vision-action models. FlowRL adapts these principles specifically for LLM reasoning, tackling unique challenges like long, variable-length Chain-of-Thought trajectories.
- **Length Normalization & KL Regularization in LLMs:** Other LLM RL works (e.g., Liu et al., 2025c; Zhang et al., 2025c; Zheng et al., 2025; Azar et al., 2024; Team et al., 2025) have addressed issues like gradient stability or overfitting in different ways. FlowRL incorporates specific length normalization and importance sampling techniques tailored to its GFlowNet-inspired objective, addressing problems unique to its approach on long CoT sequences.

By integrating GFlowNets' core principle of trajectory balance into LLM post-training, FlowRL proposes a novel and robust alternative to prevailing reward-maximizing RL methods, positioning it as a potentially foundational shift in how LLMs are fine-tuned for complex, diverse reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of FlowRL is to overcome the fundamental limitation of "mode collapse" observed in current reward-maximizing Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms used for post-training Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in complex reasoning tasks.

The core motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing Mode Collapse in LLM Reasoning:** Existing advanced reasoning models that adopt reward-maximizing methods (e.g., PPO, GRPO) tend to "over-optimize dominant reward signals while neglecting less frequent but valid reasoning paths." This leads to a lack of diversity in generated reasoning trajectories, causing models to converge on a narrow set of solutions. As illustrated in Figure 1, this results in high KL divergence from the true reward distribution and limits the model's ability to explore alternative, potentially equally valid, solution strategies.
- **Promoting Diverse Exploration and Generalizable Reasoning:** The authors argue that in complex, long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, capturing a diverse distribution of plausible solutions is "essential for effective generalization." The objective is to enable LLMs to explore a broader range of reasoning paths, leading to more robust and generalizable reasoning capabilities.
- **Shifting from Reward Maximization to Distribution Matching:** Instead of merely maximizing the expected reward, FlowRL aims to align the policy's output distribution with the *full reward distribution*. This fundamental shift is hypothesized to inherently encourage mode coverage and prevent the policy from collapsing onto a single, dominant mode.
- **Developing a Practical and Stable Algorithm for Long CoT:** While GFlowNets offer a promising theoretical framework for distribution matching, their direct application to long, variable-length CoT reasoning in LLMs presents practical challenges, such as gradient explosion and sampling mismatch. A key motivation is to develop technical solutions (length normalization, importance sampling) to make the flow-balancing objective tractable and stable for large-scale LLM training.

In essence, FlowRL seeks to enable LLMs to reason not just correctly, but also *diversely*, thereby enhancing their robustness, generalization, and ability to tackle complex, open-ended problems where multiple solution paths exist.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FlowRL introduces a novel policy optimization algorithm that fundamentally shifts from reward maximization to **reward distribution matching** via flow balancing. This approach is built upon principles derived from GFlowNets, adapted to address the specific challenges of training Large Language Models (LLMs) for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

The methodology can be broken down into three key stages:

#### 3.1. From Reward Maximization to Distribution Matching

1. **Problem Formulation:** The core problem is that reward-maximizing RL methods lead to mode collapse. To counter this, FlowRL proposes optimizing the policy by aligning its output distribution, $\pi_{\theta}(y|x)$, with a target reward distribution.
2. **Reverse KL Divergence Objective:** Since the true reward distribution is intractable to enumerate or sample, FlowRL minimizes the reverse KL divergence between the policy and a *reward-weighted distribution*.
3. **Learnable Partition Function:** To transform scalar rewards, $r(x,y)$, into a valid target distribution, FlowRL introduces a **learnable partition function**, $Z_{\phi}(x)$. This function normalizes the exponential of the scaled reward, $\exp(\beta r(x,y))$, forming the target distribution $\tilde{\pi}(y|x) = \frac{\exp(\beta r(x,y))}{Z_{\phi}(x)}$. The objective is then $\min_{\theta} D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta}(y|x) \| \tilde{\pi}(y|x))$.
4. **Connection to Trajectory Balance (GFlowNets):** A crucial theoretical contribution (Proposition 1) establishes that, in terms of expected gradients, minimizing this KL objective is equivalent to minimizing the **trajectory balance loss** used in GFlowNets: $\min_{\theta} \left[ \log Z_{\phi}(x) + \log \pi_{\theta}(y|x) - \beta r(x,y) \right]^2$. This provides a practical, stable squared-loss form for optimization, treating $Z_{\phi}(x)$ as a learnable parameter rather than an intractable constant.

#### 3.2. FlowRL Objective Formulation

Building on the trajectory balance objective, FlowRL incorporates two key technical solutions to handle long CoT reasoning:

1. **Redefining Reward with Reference Model:** Following GFlowNets literature, the reward term is modified to include a reference model $\pi_{ref}(y|x)$ as a prior constraint, becoming $\exp(\beta r(x,y)) \cdot \pi_{ref}(y|x)$. The outcome reward $r(x,y)$ is also group-normalized ( $\hat{r}_i = (r_i - \text{mean}(r))/\text{std}(r)$). Substituting this into the trajectory balance objective yields:
 $\min_{\theta} \left[ \log Z_{\phi}(x) + \log \pi_{\theta}(y|x) - \beta \hat{r}_i(x,y) - \log \pi_{ref}(y|x) \right]^2$.

2. **Length Normalization (for Problem I: Exploding Gradients):** For variable-length CoT trajectories, the $\log \pi_{\theta}(y|x)$ term (which decomposes into a sum of token log-probabilities) can scale with sequence length, leading to exploding gradients. To stabilize this, FlowRL applies a reward shaping technique by normalizing the log-probabilities with respect to sequence length: $\frac{1}{|y|} \log \pi_{\theta}(y|x)$. This balances the contributions of long and short sequences.

3. **Importance Sampling (for Problem II: Sampling Mismatch):** To enable data-efficient training with off-policy data (reusing trajectories from an old policy $\pi_{old}$), FlowRL employs importance sampling. Stale trajectories are re-weighted using an importance ratio $w = \pi_{\theta}(y|x)/\pi_{old}(y|x)$. To prevent excessive policy drift, the gradient from the current policy in $w$ is detached, and PPO-style clipping is applied to bound the importance weights: $w = \text{clip}\left( \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y|x)}{\pi_{old}(y|x)}, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon \right)_{\text{detach}}$.

#### 3.3. Final FlowRL Objective

Combining these elements, the final FlowRL objective is defined as:
$L_{\text{FlowRL}} = w \cdot \left[ \log Z_{\phi}(x) + \frac{1}{|y|} \log \pi_{\theta}(y|x) - \beta \hat{r}(x,y) - \frac{1}{|y|} \log \pi_{ref}(y|x) \right]^2$
where $w$ is the clipped importance weight and $\hat{r}(x,y)$ is the normalized reward.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Backbone Models:** Qwen-2.5 (7B/32B) for math, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B for code. $\pi_{ref}$ is the fixed pre-trained version.
- **Partition Function $Z_{\phi}$:** A randomly initialized 3-layer MLP that takes the mean of the language model's hidden states (after encoding input $x$) as input, and outputs a scalar.
- **Hyperparameters:** $\beta=15$. Max response length up to 8192 tokens.
- **Theoretical Interpretation (Proposition 5):** Minimizing the KL objective is equivalent to jointly maximizing reward, $\beta r(x,y)$, and policy entropy, $H(\pi_{\theta})$, while also aligning with the reference model prior, $\log \pi_{ref}(y|x)$, and balancing with the learned normalization $\log Z_{\phi}(x)$. This highlights FlowRL's inherent ability to promote diversity beyond mere reward maximization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of FlowRL consistently demonstrates superior performance compared to existing reward-maximization RL baselines across both mathematical and code reasoning tasks, while also confirming its ability to generate more diverse solutions.

#### 4.1. Performance on Math Reasoning Benchmarks (Table 1)

- **Overall Outperformance:** FlowRL achieves the highest average accuracy across all six challenging math benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025, AMC 2023, MATH-500, Minerva, Olympiad).
 - With the **32B model**, FlowRL achieves an average accuracy of 48.39%, outperforming PPO by 5.1% and GRPO by 10.0%.
 - With the **7B model**, FlowRL achieves an average accuracy of 35.63%, also surpassing PPO (by 3.65%) and GRPO (by 3.15%).
- **Consistent Improvements:** FlowRL shows strong improvements on specific challenging benchmarks, such as MATH-500 (+28.27% over backbone with 32B model, +12.49% with 7B model) and Olympiad (+30.46% over backbone with 32B model, +10.58% with 7B model).
- **Robustness across Scales:** The consistent gains across both 7B and 32B model scales indicate that FlowRL's approach is robust and scales effectively with larger models.

#### 4.2. Performance on Code Reasoning Benchmarks (Table 2)

- **Leading Performance:** FlowRL consistently achieves the strongest performance on three challenging coding benchmarks: LiveCodeBench, CodeForces, and HumanEval+.
 - **LiveCodeBench (Avg@16):** FlowRL achieves 37.43%, outperforming PPO by 2.33% and GRPO by 4.68%.
 - **CodeForces:** FlowRL reaches a rating of 1549.47 with an 83.3% percentile ranking, significantly higher than PPO (1403.07 rating, 73.7% percentile) and GRPO (1313.82 rating, 67.1% percentile).
 - **HumanEval+ (Avg@16):** FlowRL achieves 83.28%, outperforming PPO by 0.96% and GRPO by 3.15%.
- **Strong Generalization:** These results highlight FlowRL's strong generalization capabilities, demonstrating its effectiveness not only in math but also in diverse code generation tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 3, Figure 3)

- **Importance Sampling (IS) is Critical:** Ablation experiments reveal that importance sampling is crucial for FlowRL's performance. Removing IS ("w/o IS") leads to a substantial drop in average math score from 35.63% to 26.71%, underscoring its role in correcting distribution mismatch and stabilizing training for off-policy data.
- **$\beta$ Hyperparameter:** An ablation on the $\beta$ hyperparameter (which controls the strength of reward weighting) shows that $\beta=15$ yields optimal performance, demonstrating the importance of tuning this parameter for effective reward distribution matching.

#### 4.4. Diversity Analysis (Figure 4, Table 4)

- **Enhanced Solution Diversity:** Using GPT-4o-mini to evaluate solution diversity on AIME problems, FlowRL achieves a significantly higher diversity score (2.28) compared to all baselines (R++: 1.11, GRPO: 1.23, PPO: 1.31). This nearly doubling of the diversity score provides strong empirical validation that FlowRL's flow-balanced optimization successfully promotes mode coverage and generates qualitatively different solution approaches.
- **Case Study (Table 4):** A qualitative case study on an AIME problem visually demonstrates the behavioral differences. GRPO exhibited repetitive patterns and failed to solve the problem, getting stuck in redundant mathematical operations. In contrast, FlowRL explored a more diverse and successful solution path, making strategic assumptions (e.g., $a=b$) to simplify the problem and arrive at the correct answer.

In summary, FlowRL not only achieves state-of-the-art performance in complex reasoning tasks across domains but also provides compelling evidence that its underlying mechanism—reward distribution matching—leads to a more diverse and generalizable exploration of solution spaces, addressing a fundamental limitation of previous LLM RL methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FlowRL represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model (LLM) reasoning, offering a new paradigm for fine-tuning that moves beyond traditional reward maximization. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Fundamental Shift in LLM Reinforcement Learning:** By proposing a shift from reward maximization to **reward distribution matching** via flow balancing, FlowRL offers a fundamentally different and theoretically grounded approach to LLM training. This is a conceptual leap that addresses the core issue of "mode collapse" inherent in many existing RL methods, which is crucial for building more robust and versatile AI.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The ability to explore diverse reasoning paths, as demonstrated by FlowRL's superior performance and diversity scores, directly translates into better generalization. LLMs trained with FlowRL are likely to be more robust to variations in problem types and less prone to failing when a single dominant solution strategy is insufficient. This impact extends to real-world applications where reasoning needs to be flexible and adaptive.
- **Promotion of Diverse AI Capabilities:** The explicit focus on generating diverse solutions means LLMs can offer multiple, distinct, and valid approaches to a problem. This is invaluable in domains like scientific discovery, engineering design, or creative writing, where novelty and variety are highly prized. For instance, in drug discovery (a field where GFlowNets have already shown promise), FlowRL could help generate a wider array of candidate molecules.
- **Bridging GFlowNets and LLM Alignment:** FlowRL successfully integrates the theoretical elegance of GFlowNets with the practical challenges of LLM fine-tuning. This work showcases how GFlowNets' principles, originally developed for generative modeling of discrete objects, can be effectively adapted to the sequential generation nature of LLMs for complex tasks like CoT reasoning. This bridge could inspire further cross-pollination between these two research areas.
- **Practical Solutions for Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** The introduction of length normalization and importance sampling are crucial technical contributions that make the flow-balancing objective tractable for long and variable-length CoT trajectories. These solutions address practical stability issues that would otherwise hinder the application of GFlowNets-like objectives to LLMs, paving the way for more complex and extended reasoning chains.
- **New Avenues for Research:** FlowRL opens several new research directions. Future work could explore:
 - **Adaptive Beta Parameter:** Investigating dynamic or adaptive strategies for tuning the $\beta$ hyperparameter, rather than a fixed value.
 - **Advanced Partition Function Designs:** Developing more sophisticated architectures or training strategies for the learnable partition function $Z_{\phi}(x)$.
 - **Scalability to Larger Models:** Further optimizing FlowRL for even larger, more complex LLMs and longer context windows.
 - **Multi-objective Reasoning:** Extending FlowRL to scenarios with multiple, potentially conflicting, reward signals, leveraging GFlowNets' inherent multi-objective capabilities.
 - **Interpretable Reasoning Paths:** The diverse paths could offer opportunities for better understanding *how* LLMs reason, and potentially for improving explainability.

In conclusion, FlowRL offers a powerful alternative to current LLM RL paradigms, demonstrating that matching reward distributions can yield models with superior performance, enhanced diversity, and greater generalization capabilities. Its conceptual clarity and practical adaptations make it a significant step towards more intelligent, robust, and versatile LLMs for complex reasoning tasks.
