# Unified Biomolecular Trajectory Generation via Pretrained Variational Bridge

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Renmin University of China developed the Pretrained Variational Bridge (PVB), a generative model that unifies pretraining on static molecular structures with finetuning on dynamic trajectory data. PVB effectively generates biomolecular trajectories, matching thermodynamic and kinetic observables from classical MD, and improves exploration of protein-ligand holo states by over 40% (e.g., 43.5% vs. 26.1% for Ligand RMSD < 5 Å).

## Problem

- Inconsistent training objectives between pretraining on static structures and finetuning on dynamic trajectory data limit the effective transfer of structural knowledge in deep learning-based MD simulations.
- Existing deep generative models for time-coarsened dynamics often lack stability for long-timescale trajectory generation and struggle with cross-domain generalization across diverse biomolecular systems.
- Most prior work focuses on single-molecule systems, with multi-molecular systems (e.g., protein-ligand complexes) and accelerated exploration towards specific target states remaining less explored or inefficient.

## Method

- A Pretrained ==Variational Bridge== (**PVB**) model with an encoder-decoder architecture unifies training by using the same objective for both single-structure pretraining (setting X₀=x, Y₁=x) and paired trajectory finetuning (setting X₀=x_t, Y₁=x_{t+τ}).
- The model employs Augmented Bridge Matching (ABM) for the decoder to ensure coupling preservation between latent variables Y₀ and Y₁, enabling robust and physically plausible trajectory generation by simulating a non-Markovian SDE.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) based finetuning, using a memory-efficient Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC) and adjoint matching, accelerates exploration towards specific target states like protein-ligand holo states by optimizing a control vector field.

## Results

- PVB achieved MD-level physical plausibility (VAL-CA of **0.975** for ATLAS, **0.963** for mdCATH) and superior distributional agreement in feature spaces (e.g., TIC, MSM states), capturing slow dynamical modes with a **0.929** decorrelation ratio.
- For protein-ligand complexes on MISATO, PVB showed the closest match to MD results in characterizing interactions, with EMD-ligand (**0.133** nm) and EMD-CoM (**0.089** nm) demonstrating accurate atomic-resolution dynamics.
- RL finetuning of PVB for protein-ligand holo state exploration significantly improved post-docking pose optimization, increasing cases with Ligand RMSD < 5 Å from **26.1%** (Vina) to **43.5%** (PVB+RL) and reducing mean Ligand RMSD to 5.918 Å.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying pretraining and finetuning objectives with a variational bridge framework effectively leverages cross-domain structural knowledge and improves generative fidelity for biomolecular dynamics.
> - Augmented Bridge Matching (ABM) is crucial for maintaining physical plausibility and stability in generated trajectories, preventing issues like physical implausibility seen with alternative diffusion models.
> - Reinforcement learning with memory-efficient adjoint matching can guide biomolecular trajectory generation towards specific target states, significantly enhancing the efficiency of exploring complex free energy landscapes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations serve as a foundational tool for characterizing molecular behavior at atomic resolution across various scientific disciplines, including chemistry, materials science, and drug discovery. These simulations are essential for obtaining conformational ensembles that follow physical laws, providing estimates of kinetic (e.g., dissociation rates) and thermodynamic (e.g., free energy) observables. Traditional classical MD simulations, often relying on Langevin dynamics, model temporal evolution through Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) and typically require extremely small time steps (around 1 femtosecond) for numerical stability. This requirement leads to high computational costs, severely limiting their applicability for long-timescale or high-throughput simulations.

The advent of deep learning methods has introduced new approaches to enhance MD simulations and ensemble generation. One strategy involves replacing empirical force fields with neural network potentials, such as in AI²BMD, to accelerate simulations. However, this approach generally does not overcome the fundamental limitation of sequential trajectory generation with small time steps. Another direction focuses on generative models that fit the empirical distribution from MD trajectories (e.g., AlphaFlow, Boltzmann generators for i.i.d. samples). While these methods offer efficiency in parallel sample generation, they often do not capture temporal dependencies, thus hindering the estimation of kinetic observables.

A distinct paradigm, termed time-coarsened dynamics or trajectory generation, aims to learn the conditional distribution μ(x_{t+τ} | x_t) at a significantly larger time step τ. This approach allows for time-dependent trajectory generation on a coarse-grained temporal scale, aiming to achieve substantial efficiency gains while preserving both kinetic and thermodynamic properties. Existing methods in this category (e.g., ITO, MDGEN) have faced challenges regarding the stability of trajectory generation over long timescales and often exhibit limitations in generalizing across different molecular domains.

Prior work, such as UniSim (Yu et al., 2025), has attempted to address cross-domain generalization by employing 3D molecular pretraining to develop a unified atomic representation model. However, a limitation of UniSim and similar approaches is the inconsistency between pretraining on static, single-structure data (x) and finetuning on dynamic, paired trajectory data (x_t, x_{t+τ}). This objective mismatch can impede the effective transfer and utilization of pretrained structural knowledge. Furthermore, many current methods primarily focus on single-molecule simulations, with multi-molecular systems like protein-ligand complexes remaining less explored. The presented work addresses these limitations by introducing a unified framework that reconciles pretraining and finetuning objectives and extends capabilities to multi-molecular systems, including accelerated exploration of target states.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to develop a generative model capable of unified biomolecular trajectory generation that addresses limitations in current deep learning-based MD simulation approaches. The specific motivations and objectives are detailed below:

- **Addressing Inconsistent Training Objectives between Pretraining and Finetuning:** A significant challenge in leveraging deep learning for biomolecular dynamics is the disparity between common pretraining strategies and the requirements for learning dynamics. Many methods pretrain on large datasets of static, high-resolution single molecular structures (e.g., PDB entries) to acquire broad structural knowledge. However, finetuning for trajectory generation necessitates learning conditional distributions that map a current state to a future state (x_t → x_{t+τ}), which involves temporal correlations. Existing approaches often result in an inconsistency where pretraining provides only a unified atomic representation model, failing to explicitly capture cross-domain structural information within a generative context that can seamlessly transfer to conditional dynamics. The motivation here is to design a framework that unifies training on both single-structure data and paired trajectory data, enabling consistent exploitation of cross-domain structural knowledge across all training stages.

- **Enhancing Generalization and Stability for Long-Timescale Trajectory Generation:** Current deep generative models for time-coarsened dynamics face difficulties in reliably ensuring the stability of trajectory generation over long timescales. Many models are also confined to specific molecular domains (e.g., proteins) and exhibit limited cross-domain generalization. The objective is to develop a model that can maintain physical plausibility and reproduce thermodynamic and kinetic observables accurately across diverse biomolecular systems, including proteins and protein-ligand complexes, for extended simulation durations. This involves improving the generative fidelity and stability of the simulated dynamics compared to existing methods.

- **Extending Generative Capabilities to Multi-molecular Systems and Accelerating Specific Transitions:** Most prior work has focused on single-molecule systems. However, understanding the dynamics of multi-molecular systems, such as protein-ligand complexes, is crucial for applications like drug discovery (e.g., flexible docking). Simulating the binding process from an apo (unbound) to a holo (bound) state can be computationally demanding, even with coarsened timesteps, as it often involves navigating complex free energy landscapes. The motivation is to extend the generative framework to effectively model these multi-molecular interactions and, critically, to develop mechanisms for accelerating the progression toward specific target states (e.g., the holo state in protein-ligand docking). This objective aims to support efficient post-optimization of docking poses by guiding the generative process with explicit reward functions.

In summary, the research aims to introduce a unified and robust generative model that overcomes the limitations of objective inconsistency in pretraining/finetuning, improves long-timescale stability and cross-domain generalization, and expands its utility to efficiently accelerate exploration in multi-molecular systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Pretrained Variational Bridge (PVB) is a novel generative model designed with an encoder-decoder architecture to unify pretraining on single-structure data and finetuning on paired trajectory data. This approach is supplemented by a reinforcement learning (RL)-based finetuning procedure for accelerated exploration of protein-ligand holo states.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

Molecular conformations are represented as (z, C, x), where z denotes atomic numbers, C represents covalent bonds, and x corresponds to Euclidean coordinates. The framework handles two types of systems:
- Type-I: Independent conformations (z, C, {x}), used in pretraining.
- Type-II: Time-dependent MD trajectories (z, C, {x_{∆t}, x_{2∆t}...}), used in finetuning.
The tasks are to pretrain for cross-domain full-atom generative capability and then finetune to learn dynamics, leveraging pretrained knowledge.

#### 3.2. Pretrained Variational Bridge

PVB models the generation process as a Markov chain X₀ → Y₀ → Y₁.
- **Unified Training Interface:** For single-structure pretraining (Type-I systems), (X₀, Y₁) are set to (x, x). For finetuning on paired MD data (Type-II systems), (X₀, Y₁) are set to (x_t, x_{t+τ}). The latent variable Y₀ is introduced to prevent the conditional law q(dY₁ | X₀) from collapsing to a Dirac measure in the single-structure scenario.
- **Variational Encoder (q_e):**
 - The conditional probability measure of Y₀ given X₀ is defined as q_e(dY₀ | X₀ = x₀) := N(x₀, σ²_e I), a Gaussian distribution. A relatively large σ_e (e.g., √0.5 Å) is chosen to preserve structural information and prevent decoding collapse.
 - A neural network encoder φ_e minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence L_KL between the learned distribution p_e(· | X₀) and the prior q_e(· | X₀), using the reparameterization trick.
- **Augmented Bridge Decoder (q_d):**
 - Given Y₀, a separate decoder φ_d fits the transition kernel q_d(dY₁ | Y₀).
 - The Augmented Bridge Matching (ABM) framework (De Bortoli et al., 2023) is employed to ensure the preservation of the coupling Π₀,₁ between Y₀ and Y₁.
 - The training objective for φ_d is L_ABM, a quadratic loss that minimizes the difference between the parameterized vector field φ_d and the drift term of a Brownian bridge SDE, conditioned on t, Y₀, and Y_t.
 - Trajectory generation proceeds by simulating a non-Markovian SDE: dY_t = φ*d(t, Y₀, Y_t) dt + σ dB_t.
 - Proposition 1 states that this encoder-decoder architecture provides an unbiased estimate of the target conditional law q(dY₁ | X₀).
- **Overall Objective:** The combined objective for both pretraining and finetuning is L = w_KL · L_KL + w_ABM · L_ABM, where w_KL and w_ABM are hyperparameters.

#### 3.3. Memory-Efficient Stochastic Optimal Control (RL Finetuning)

To accelerate exploration towards specific target states, particularly the holo state in protein-ligand flexible docking, PVB incorporates an RL-based finetuning procedure.
- **Problem Formulation:** The SDE for trajectory generation is modified with a control vector field u: dY_t = (φ*d(t, Y₀, Y_t) + σu(t, Y₀, Y_t)) dt + σ dB_t.
- **Optimization Objective:** The goal is to maximize the expected reward r(Y₁), defined as the negative RMSD to the holo state, while penalizing the KL divergence between the controlled path measure P_u and the original path measure P* (without control). This objective can be rewritten using the Girsanov theorem to include a penalty term proportional to the integral of ‖u(t, Y₀, Y_t)‖².
- **Memory-Efficient Adjoint Matching:** To overcome memory overhead from gradient accumulation, the optimization problem is framed as a Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC) problem. Proposition 2, based on adjoint matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024), provides a memory-efficient solution. It defines an objective L_adj that minimizes the squared difference between the control vector field u and a term involving the "lean adjoint state" (˜a).
- **Adjoint State Calculation:** The lean adjoint state ˜a(s, Y₀:₁) is propagated backward in time from a terminal condition ˜a(1, Y₀:₁) = - (1/β) ∇_{Y₁} r(Y₁) by solving a backward ODE.
- **Control Vector Field Reparameterization:** The control vector field u is reparameterized as (1/σ)(φ^u_d - φ*d), where φ^u_d is a neural network whose parameters are updated during RL finetuning, initialized from the finetuned φ*d. This avoids introducing additional networks.
- **RL Finetuning Algorithm (Algorithm 1):** The process involves sampling a diffusion time t, generating a trajectory Y₀:₁, computing ˜a₁, propagating it backward to ˜a_t, and then minimizing L_adj to update φ^u_d. Data augmentation (random SE(3)-transformation of the ligand) is applied to the initial state during training.

#### 3.4. Model Architecture Details (from Appendix C)

- **Block-Level Representation:** Molecular systems are represented hierarchically using a vocabulary of atomic types and 20 natural amino acids, enhancing cross-domain generalization. Atomic type (z) and block representation (b) are projected and summed to form initial atomic features.
- **Graph-Level Representation:** Molecules are modeled as graphs G=(V, E), where V includes invariant features and SO(3)-equivariant coordinates. Intra-molecular edges are constructed using kNN, and inter-molecular edges connect ligand atoms to pocket atoms in multi-molecular systems. Edge features include covalent bond information.
- **Neural Network Backbone:** TorchMD-NET is used as the backbone to preserve SO(3)-equivariance. A modification concatenates the topology edge feature to default edge attributes. The decoder conditions on both Y₀ and Y_t by converting their coordinates into invariant interatomic distances as edge features, processed by an equivariant graph neural network, then fed into attention layers. Geometric Vector Perceptron (GVP) is used as the output layer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experiments to evaluate PVB's performance in trajectory generation and exploration of protein-ligand holo states.

#### 4.1. Datasets and Baselines

- **Pretraining:** PCQM4Mv2, ANI-1x (small molecules); PDB (protein monomers); PDBBind2020 (protein-ligand complexes).
- **Finetuning:** ATLAS, mdCATH (protein monomers); MISATO (protein-ligand complexes); a curated subset of PDBBind2020 with short MD simulations for RL finetuning.
- **Baselines:** ITO, MDGEN, UniSim (for trajectory generation); AlphaFlow (for i.i.d. protein sample generation). All baselines were trained following standard procedures.

#### 4.2. Trajectory Generation for Proteins

- **ATLAS Dataset:** PVB was finetuned on ATLAS (790 training, 14 test systems) with a coarsened time step τ = 100 ps, generating 1,000-step trajectories.
 - **Metrics:** Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) on projected feature spaces (Radius of Gyration (Rg), torsion angles (Torus), Time-lagged Independent Components (TIC0, TIC1), Markov State Models (MSM) state occupancy), validity (VAL-CA, proportion of conformations without bond break or Cα clash), RMSE of contact maps (RMSE-CONTACT), and decorrelation fraction of TIC0 (Decorr-TIC0).
 - **Results:** PVB demonstrated comparable performance to baselines across most JSD metrics and RMSE-CONTACT, with particular strength in VAL-CA (0.975/0.023 vs. 0.106/0.050 for UniSim). It exhibited equivalent physical realism and superior distributional agreement in TIC and MSM feature spaces compared to 10 ns MD, also achieving a higher decorrelation ratio (0.929), indicating its ability to capture slow dynamical modes.
- **mdCATH Dataset:** PVB was further evaluated on mdCATH (5,049 training, 20 test domains) with τ = 1 ns, generating 500-step trajectories.
 - **Results:** PVB achieved MD-level physical plausibility (VAL-CA of 0.963/0.049) and showed good agreement with slow modes (TIC) and metastable states (MSM) from MD reference ensembles. It generally outperformed UniSim across most metrics, exhibiting greater disparity in distributional similarity for projected features on this broader dataset. Visualizations confirmed recovery of free energy landscapes and agreement in metastable state occupancies.

#### 4.3. Trajectory Generation for Protein-Ligand Complexes

- **MISATO Dataset:** PVB was applied to MISATO (13,765 training, 20 test systems) with τ = 80 ps, generating 100-step trajectories.
 - **Metrics:** Wasserstein-1 distance (EMD) for ligand RMSD (EMD-ligand) and pocket-ligand center of mass (EMD-CoM), and RMSE of contact occupancy between ligand and pocket heavy atoms (RMSE-CONTACT).
 - **Results:** PVB achieved the closest match to MD results for EMD-ligand (0.133/0.072 nm) and EMD-CoM (0.089/0.078 nm), and comparable performance in RMSE-CONTACT (0.055/0.008). These EMD values indicated accurate characterization of protein-ligand interactions at atomic resolution. Visualizations showed close correspondence in ligand RMSD and pocket-ligand CoM distance distributions and temporal evolution.

#### 4.4. Exploration for Protein-Ligand Holo State

- **PDBBind2020 Dataset with RL:** The model was evaluated on a PDBBind test set (23 systems) for post-optimization of docking poses, starting from AutoDock Vina-generated initial poses and generating 100-step (8 ns) trajectories. The predicted holo state was identified by highest binding affinity.
 - **Metrics:** Ligand RMSD and Pocket RMSD to the reference holo structure (percentiles, mean, and proportion below thresholds).
 - **Results:** PVB without RL finetuning generally showed larger ligand RMSD than the initial Vina pose, indicating insufficient sampling. In contrast, PVB with RL finetuning demonstrated substantial improvements across all metrics (e.g., mean Ligand RMSD of 5.918 Å vs. 6.368 Å for Vina; 43.5% of cases with Ligand RMSD < 5 Å vs. 26.1% for Vina). This suggested that RL finetuning enabled the model to bypass local energy minima and progress more efficiently towards the holo state. Visualizations highlighted that RL finetuning guided the system towards the correct binding pose, even from initial poses distant from the ground truth.

#### 4.5. Additional Experimental Results (Appendix G)

- **Fast-Folding Proteins:** PVB, trained on ATLAS, was used in a zero-shot manner on five fast-folding proteins to generate 10 μs trajectories. For Villin, WW domain, and λ-repressor, it recovered essential free energy landscapes and captured rapid transitions between metastable states, indicating generalization capacity. For NTL9 and Protein B, while full recreation of MD findings was limited by simulation time, initial characterization of metastable states was consistent. The model demonstrated robust stability in contact occupancy for most systems over 10 μs, with some challenges noted for larger systems like λ-repressor.
- **Ablation Study:** On the ATLAS dataset, experiments confirmed the benefits of PVB's design:
 - PVB without pretraining showed substantial performance degradation across all metrics, particularly validity (VAL-CA 0.022 vs. 0.975 for full PVB), underscoring the importance of cross-domain structural knowledge.
 - PVB without finetuning still maintained high generative fidelity (VAL-CA 0.968) from pretraining but had slightly reduced distributional similarity, confirming the unified framework's effectiveness.
 - Replacing PVB's bridge-matching decoder with an ITO-like conditional diffusion model (PVB-ITO) resulted in poor overall performance and physically implausible conformations, suggesting the specific effectiveness of the bridge matching component.
- **Physicality of Generated Protein-Ligand Complexes:** Detailed metrics (VAL-BL, VAL-BA, SCF for ligand; VAL-CA for protein; SI for stereochemical integrity) confirmed that PVB w/o RL preserved high physical plausibility. RL finetuning led to slight improvements in ligand-related metrics and notable enhancement in protein validity, indicating that RL supports physically plausible trajectory generation. Stereochemical integrity remained unchanged due to the input molecular topology.
- **Inference Efficiency:** PVB demonstrated approximately 5-10 times faster inference per step compared to the second-best baseline (MDGEN), with lower variance, indicating computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Pretrained Variational Bridge (PVB) represents a methodological advancement in the field of biomolecular trajectory generation, addressing several limitations of existing deep learning-based approaches.

- **Unified Training Framework:** The core contribution of PVB is its ability to unify training on both single-structure data and paired trajectory data through an encoder-decoder architecture with augmented bridge matching. This allows for consistent and effective transfer of rich cross-domain structural knowledge acquired during pretraining to subsequent finetuning stages focused on molecular dynamics. This consistent utilization of structural information across training objectives can improve the generalizability and robustness of generative models for diverse biomolecular systems.

- **Enhanced Generative Fidelity and Stability:** The experiments demonstrate that PVB faithfully reproduces thermodynamic and kinetic observables, aligning with those derived from classical MD simulations. The model also shows improved generative fidelity and stability over baselines, particularly in maintaining physical plausibility over long timescales, as indicated by metrics like VAL-CA and Decorr-TIC0. This improved accuracy and stability are crucial for making computational simulations more reliable and applicable to complex biological processes.

- **Accelerated Exploration for Multi-Molecular Systems:** The integration of reinforcement learning (RL) via adjoint matching for protein-ligand complexes is a significant extension. By employing explicit reward functions, PVB learns to guide trajectories toward specific target states, such as the holo state, bypassing inefficient local exploration. This capability positions PVB as an efficient tool for the post-optimization of docking poses, a critical step in structure-based drug discovery. The ability to accelerate exploration of specific binding conformations can significantly reduce the computational cost and time associated with identifying potential drug candidates.

- **Computational Efficiency:** Beyond accuracy and stability, PVB exhibits considerable inference efficiency, being significantly faster than comparable deep learning models. This efficiency, combined with the capability for time-coarsened dynamics, can enable simulations of longer timescales or higher throughput studies that are currently prohibitive with traditional MD or less efficient deep learning methods.

The potential impact of PVB spans several scientific domains:
- **Drug Discovery:** Faster and more accurate prediction and optimization of protein-ligand binding poses can accelerate the hit-to-lead and lead optimization phases of drug development. The ability to model binding kinetics accurately could also lead to improved drug design.
- **Materials Science:** Understanding the dynamics of molecular systems is vital for designing novel materials with desired properties. PVB could aid in simulating material properties at an accelerated rate.
- **Chemical Biology and Biophysics:** The faithful reproduction of thermodynamic and kinetic observables provides a powerful tool for characterizing protein folding, conformational changes, and molecular interactions, contributing to a deeper understanding of fundamental biological processes.

While PVB represents a substantial step forward, the authors identify sequential trajectory generation as a remaining limitation, suggesting that developing parallelized methods that maintain temporal correlations is an important area for future work. This indicates a recognition of ongoing challenges while highlighting the current model's advancements.
