# Beyond Perceptual Shortcuts: Causal-Inspired Debiasing Optimization for Generalizable Video Reasoning in Lightweight MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A causal-inspired debiasing optimization framework, VideoThinker, enables lightweight Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to achieve robust, generalizable video reasoning by actively countering 'perceptual shortcuts' in training data. This 3B parameter model established new state-of-the-art performance for its size, outperforming some larger models and demonstrating significant accuracy improvements on causal inference benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning, successful with large MLLMs, surprisingly degrades or fails to significantly improve reasoning in lightweight MLLMs for video understanding.
- Training datasets often contain 'perceptual shortcuts' where superficial correlations allow models to answer questions without genuine causal reasoning, leading to an 'intramodal reasoning deficit'.
- A formal causal analysis identified a 'spurious shortcut pathway' (Q 	O 	A) where input queries lead to answers via superficial observations, bypassing true reasoning.

## Method

- The **VideoThinker** framework, a two-stage ==adversarial approximation==, was developed to intervene against the intractable 'backdoor path' identified by causal analysis.
- The first stage, Bias Aware Training, creates a 'Bias Model' by fine-tuning the MLLM on a specialized dataset curated to embody shortcut behaviors, accelerating learning of spurious correlations.
- The second stage, Causal Debiasing Policy Optimization (CDPO), fine-tunes the main 'thinking model' by maximizing a repulsive KL-divergence objective against the frozen Bias Model, actively pushing it away from shortcut logic.

## Results

- VideoThinker-R1, a **3B** parameter model, achieved state-of-the-art performance for lightweight MLLMs in video reasoning, scoring **60.9%** on MVBench and **63.5%** on TempCompass, and surpassed several **7B**-scale models.
- The framework yielded a **14.2%** absolute accuracy improvement over the GRPO baseline on CLEVRER, demonstrating enhanced causal inference and robust generalization even with limited training data.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of the purpose-built Bias Model and the repulsive KL-Maximization mechanism, while robustness tests showed effectiveness even with sparse inferential problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 'Perceptual shortcuts' in training data are a primary cause of reasoning degradation in lightweight MLLMs during RL fine-tuning, as these models exploit superficial correlations instead of developing genuine understanding.
> - A Structural Causal Model revealed that standard training methods, including RL, inadvertently encourage models to rely on an efficient 'spurious shortcut pathway' (Q 	O 	A) due to input query confounding.
> - Actively repelling a policy from a specialized 'bias model' that embodies shortcut behaviors is an effective causal-inspired intervention to compel the main model to learn robust, generalizable reasoning pathways.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) capable of complex reasoning, particularly for video understanding, represents a significant area of current research. Reinforcement Learning (RL) based fine-tuning, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has demonstrated success in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large-scale MLLMs across various domains, including visual reasoning. However, a notable challenge exists in adapting these methods for lightweight MLLMs, which are crucial for deployment in resource-constrained environments like edge devices. Existing RL approaches for MLLMs have predominantly focused on refining optimization algorithms or reward signals, often overlooking inherent biases within training datasets.

This paper addresses a critical efficacy gap: the observation that proven RL techniques often fail to significantly improve, and can even degrade, the reasoning abilities of smaller MLLMs. The authors diagnose this issue as primarily stemming from "perceptual shortcuts"—superficial correlations in training data that models exploit instead of developing genuine causal understanding. This phenomenon is distinct from previously identified intermodal biases (e.g., over-reliance on language priors over visual evidence), representing an intramodal "reasoning deficit" where models rely on observational cues within a single modality (video) to answer questions that demand true inference. While shortcut learning is recognized in traditional image recognition, its advanced form in MLLMs reasoning had not been thoroughly specified or diagnosed.

The work leverages causal analysis to formalize this problem, identifying a "spurious shortcut pathway" in the model's reasoning process. By proposing a causal-inspired debiasing framework, VideoThinker, the paper positions itself as a methodological advancement that explicitly counters data biases to foster generalizable reasoning in lightweight MLLMs, thereby enabling their effective deployment in practical, resource-limited settings where larger models are infeasible.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to cultivate robust and generalizable reasoning capabilities in lightweight MLLMs, specifically for video reasoning tasks, by overcoming the limitations imposed by "perceptual shortcuts" and data biases during RL-based fine-tuning.

The motivation for this work is multifaceted:
- **Efficacy Gap in Lightweight MLLMs:** While RL fine-tuning has advanced reasoning in large MLLMs, its effectiveness surprisingly diminishes when applied to lightweight models (e.g., 3B parameters). This creates a bottleneck for deploying MLLMs in edge computing environments that demand efficiency.
- **Discovery of Perceptual Shortcuts:** The authors empirically investigated this performance discrepancy and identified a pervasive issue: "perceptual shortcuts" within widely used video reasoning datasets like CLEVRER. Many questions, categorized as "Observational," can be answered by merely describing visible events in a video, bypassing the need for complex "Inferential" reasoning, which requires simulating unobserved scenarios or counterfactuals. For example, a query like "what will collide without the yellow ball?" might be answerable by simply observing that two objects never touch, rather than reasoning about the causal effect of removing the yellow ball.
- **Diagnostic Experiment:** A diagnostic experiment using the CLEVRER dataset revealed that a significant portion (74%) of training samples consist of these "Observational" shortcuts. When a 3B base model was fine-tuned using GRPO, its accuracy on critical inferential tasks plummeted (from 73.9% to 63.1%), even as observational task performance remained stable or increased. This "capability conflict" provided concrete evidence that RL, guided by data biases, actively forced vulnerable lightweight models to unlearn genuine reasoning in favor of superficial shortcuts.
- **Formal Causal Analysis:** To understand and address this, the authors developed a Structural Causal Model (SCM) for Video Question Answering (VQA). This SCM identified a critical "backdoor path" where the input query (Q) could lead to an answer (A) via superficial observations (O), bypassing genuine reasoning (T). This pathway (Q → O → A) represents the "spurious shortcut" and is the direct result of data bias where easy instances allow models to learn superficial correlations.
- **Need for Debiasing Intervention:** The identified causal structure highlighted that standard training methods, including RL, would inevitably encourage models to exploit this "path of least resistance," thereby compromising their ability to perform true causal reasoning. This necessitated a targeted intervention to block this undesirable shortcut path and compel models to rely on the intended causal reasoning path (Q → T → A).

The overarching objective is to provide a practical and effective framework, VideoThinker, that can mitigate these perceptual biases and enable lightweight MLLMs to develop robust, generalizable, and causally sound reasoning abilities, thereby unlocking their full cognitive potential for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Preliminary

The research focuses on the multi-choice question-answering (MCQA) format for video reasoning tasks. The foundational Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework used as a baseline and for comparison is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an efficient variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). GRPO optimizes policy-level group comparisons by sampling candidate responses, assigning rule-based rewards, normalizing them into relative quality (advantage), and updating the policy to maximize a specific objective function, which includes a KL-divergence constraint to maintain proximity to a reference policy.

#### 3.2. Diagnosing Perceptual Shortcuts

To investigate the observed performance decline of lightweight MLLMs under RL fine-tuning, the authors hypothesized that data biases, specifically "perceptual shortcuts," were responsible. They conducted a diagnostic experiment using the CLEVRER dataset, a benchmark for video reasoning with detailed collision event annotations.
- **Categorization:** Counterfactual questions in CLEVRER were categorized into two types:
 - **Inferential Questions:** Require causal reasoning, simulating unobserved scenarios (e.g., "what will happen if X is removed?"). The correct answer conflicts with the original video's timeline.
 - **Observational Questions:** Can be answered by simply observing events in the original video, without needing to reason about counterfactual conditions. The correct answer describes an event that either occurs or does not occur in the original video, regardless of the hypothetical intervention.
- **Data Imbalance:** This categorization revealed that 74.0% of the CLEVRER training dataset consisted of these "easy" Observational questions.
- **Empirical Verification:** The authors fine-tuned 3B and 7B MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL) using GRPO on a subset of CLEVRER containing both question types. Results showed that for the 3B model, accuracy on Inferential tasks significantly dropped (from 73.9% to 63.1%), while Observational performance remained stable or improved. This confirmed the hypothesis: RL training, biased by shortcuts, compelled the lightweight model to unlearn genuine reasoning.

#### 3.3. Causal Analysis of Perceptual Shortcuts

A Structural Causal Model (SCM) was constructed to formalize the video question answering process, involving four key variables:
- **Q (Query):** The input video, question, and candidate answers.
- **T (Thinking):** Latent variable representing genuine causal reasoning.
- **O (Observation):** Captures superficial, statistical biases or pattern matching from the video.
- **A (Answer):** The final predicted answer.

The SCM posited the following causal relationships:
- Q → O: Query leads to superficial observational evidence.
- Q → T: Query leads to information required for genuine reasoning.
- T → A: Genuine thinking causally determines the answer (ideal path).
- O → A: Superficial observation can directly influence the answer (spurious bias path).

The analysis identified the input query Q as a confounder, creating a "backdoor path" (T ← Q → O → A) between reasoning and the answer. This backdoor path is exploited by models due to the statistical efficiency of using superficial observations, especially when data is abundant with such shortcut opportunities. The goal is to intervene to block this spurious pathway and force the model to rely on the genuine reasoning path (Q → T → A). However, the ideal causal intervention, backdoor adjustment, was deemed intractable due to the high-dimensional, continuous nature of O and the complexity of modeling P(O|Q).

#### 3.4. The VideoThinker Framework

VideoThinker is a two-stage, causal-inspired framework designed as an adversarial approximation to the intractable backdoor adjustment for practical debiasing.

##### 3.4.1. Bias Aware Training

- **Objective:** To explicitly isolate and embody the "spurious shortcut pathway" (Q → O → A) in a dedicated "Bias Model" ($\pi_{bias}$).
- **Process:**
 1. **Bias Dataset Creation ($D_{bias}$):** A specialized dataset is curated from the CLEVRER counterfactual subset. Instances where the correct answer describes an event perfectly matching an event in the ground-truth video log (i.e., observational or shortcut questions) are selected. These questions are programmatically simplified into purely observational tasks (e.g., removing the counterfactual condition) to prevent reasoning and encourage reliance on direct observation.
 2. **Bias Model Training:** The base MLLM is fine-tuned on this $D_{bias}$. To accelerate the learning of shortcut behaviors, the KL-divergence constraint typically present in GRPO is removed from the objective function. This allows the model ($\pi_{bias}$) to quickly converge to the simplest, most biased solutions.

##### 3.4.2. Causal Debiasing Policy Optimization (CDPO)

- **Objective:** To fine-tune the primary "thinking model" ($\pi_\theta$) to learn robust, generalizable reasoning by actively repelling it from the shortcut logic embodied by the frozen Bias Model ($\pi_{bias}$).
- **Algorithm:** The CDPO objective function ($J_{CDPO}(\theta)$) is maximized during training:
 $J_{CDPO}(\theta) = E[min(\dots) + \beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{bias})]$
 - **Task-specific Advantage:** The first term is a standard GRPO-like term that maximizes task-specific advantage ($\hat{A}_{i,t}$) on the full dataset, ensuring the model strives for correct and high-quality answers.
 - **Repulsive Objective:** The second term, $+\beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{bias})$, is the core causal intervention. The positive sign before the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term means the model is penalized for having an action distribution similar to the Bias Model's. By maximizing this term, the policy model ($\pi_\theta$) is actively pushed away from the shortcut behaviors learned by $\pi_{bias}$. This adversarial pressure compels $\pi_\theta$ to explore and converge on alternative, more complex reasoning pathways (Q → T → A) that are not accessible to the bias model.
 - **Hyperparameter $\beta$:** Controls the strength of this debiasing regularizer.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The base model used is Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct. Training is conducted on NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs. The Bias Model is trained for 500 steps on the curated CLEVRER bias dataset. VideoThinker-R1 is subsequently trained for 500 steps on the original CLEVRER training set, with the debiasing coefficient $\beta$ set to 0.01. Soft accuracy and format rewards are employed. For efficiency, 16 frames are sampled per video (resized to 128x28x28), with a group size G=8 and learning rate 10$^{-6}$. Evaluation is performed on six standard benchmarks: CLEVRER, MMVU, Video-Holmes (video reasoning), and MVBench, TempCompass, VideoMME (general video understanding). Evaluation involves 32 frames at 256x28x28 resolution, with top_p=0.001 and temperature=0.01. The metric is average accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the VideoThinker framework.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Arts (Table 1)

VideoThinker-R1, a 3B parameter model, established new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance among lightweight models and surpassed several larger 7B-scale open-source models across various video reasoning and general video understanding benchmarks.
- **SOTA for Lightweight Models:** VideoThinker-R1 achieved 60.9% on MVBench and 63.5% on TempCompass.
- **Cross-Scale Superiority:** It outperformed the larger Video-UTR-7B model by 2.1% on MVBench and 3.8% on TempCompass, indicating improved reasoning efficiency.
- **Targeted Debiasing Efficacy:** On CLEVRER, which requires pure causal inference, VideoThinker-R1 attained 79.1% accuracy with only 1k training samples. This represents a 14.2% absolute improvement over the GRPO baseline trained under identical conditions, highlighting VideoThinker's ability to counteract shortcut learning and develop robust reasoning from limited data.
- **Generalization Improvements:** Compared to other contemporary 3B models like VideoRFT-3B and TinyLLaVA-Video-R1, VideoThinker-R1 demonstrated holistic improvements, particularly a 7.0% lead over VideoRFT-3B on VideoMME.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (Table 2)

A series of ablation studies validated the design choices within the VideoThinker framework.
- **Choice of Bias Model:** Using a purpose-built Bias Model (trained to embody shortcut behaviors) as the repulsive target yielded the best performance, achieving 79.1% on CLEVRER and 56.8% on MMVU. This result significantly outperformed variants that repelled a best-performing model (like VideoRFT-3B) or used self-repulsion, supporting the premise that an accurate negative exemplar is crucial.
- **Repulsive Debiasing Mechanism:** The KL-Maximization (+$\beta$) approach, which actively pushes the policy away from the Bias Model, achieved superior results. In contrast, KL-Minimization (-$\beta$), which attempts to attract the policy towards the Bias Model, failed to generalize, indicating that robust reasoning emerges from avoiding flawed patterns rather than imitating them.
- **Sensitivity Analysis on $\beta$:** The optimal debiasing coefficient $\beta = 0.01$ was identified, balancing performance on the in-domain CLEVRER benchmark without over-penalizing useful foundational knowledge. Performance on the general-purpose MMVU benchmark showed robustness to $\beta$ within the tested range, suggesting the learned generalizable reasoning capability is stable.

#### 4.3. Robustness to Train Data (Figure 6)

VideoThinker-R1 demonstrated robustness to training data composition. When trained on a different dataset with a sparse proportion (1.9%) of inferential problems, VideoThinker-R1 achieved an average accuracy of 52.3%, surpassing both the GRPO baseline (50.3%) and VideoRFT-3B (50.8%). This suggests that the framework enhances fundamental reasoning by suppressing spurious signals, a skill effective even with limited counterfactual queries.

#### 4.4. Scaling to 7B Parameters (Table 3)

Evaluation at the 7B parameter scale showed that VideoThinker-R1-7B achieved results comparable to existing SOTA 7B models. However, the performance gains were more pronounced for the 3B model. This observation supports the hypothesis that "perceptual shortcuts" pose a greater challenge for limited-capacity models, making the debiasing method particularly critical for them, as larger models are inherently more robust against learning such biases.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 7)

Qualitative examples from CLEVRER and MMVU datasets illustrated the difference in reasoning. Baseline models often exhibited reliance on cognitive shortcuts, producing plausible textual rationales that conflicted with their final incorrect answers or defaulting to decisions based on superficial visual cues. VideoThinker-R1, conversely, demonstrated coherent logical analysis, correctly identifying critical objects, grounding judgments in game rules (for MMVU), and correctly concluding when information was insufficient to answer a question. These examples indicate that VideoThinker effectively suppresses reliance on spurious features and enforces logical reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions to the field of multimodal AI, particularly concerning the development and deployment of efficient models.

- **Diagnosis of a Critical Bottleneck:** The paper is the first to rigorously identify and empirically verify "perceptual shortcuts" as a primary factor hindering the development of genuine reasoning abilities in lightweight MLLMs. This diagnosis extends beyond previous notions of intermodal bias, pinpointing an intramodal reasoning deficit. The formal causal analysis, which elucidates the "backdoor path" created by data biases, provides a theoretical foundation for understanding this phenomenon.
- **Novel Causal-Inspired Debiasing Framework:** The proposed VideoThinker framework, with its two-stage debiasing process including Bias Aware Training and Causal Debiasing Policy Optimization (CDPO), introduces an innovative solution to this problem. By explicitly training a "bias model" to embody shortcut behaviors and then employing a repulsive KL-divergence objective to steer the primary model away from these flawed patterns, VideoThinker offers a practical and effective way to approximate causal intervention in a high-dimensional setting.
- **Advancement in Lightweight MLLM Capabilities:** VideoThinker-R1, built on this framework, establishes a new state-of-the-art for lightweight (3B parameter) MLLMs in video reasoning tasks. Its ability to outperform even larger 7B models on multiple benchmarks, with significantly less training data and computational resources, demonstrates a remarkable leap in reasoning efficiency. This achievement is critical for enabling the deployment of MLLMs in resource-constrained environments like edge devices, democratizing access to advanced AI capabilities.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Robustness:** The debiasing mechanism compels models to learn genuine reasoning pathways rather than superficial correlations. This results in MLLMs that are not only more accurate but also more generalizable and robust across diverse video understanding tasks, including those with sparse inferential problems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a foundation for building more generalized MLLMs and opens new avenues for exploring causal inference techniques in the context of reinforcement learning and multimodal foundation models. It highlights the importance of addressing data biases directly to unlock true cognitive potential in AI systems.
