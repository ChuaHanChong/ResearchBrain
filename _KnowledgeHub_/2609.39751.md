# Beyond Policy Alignment: Closing the Planning-Learning Loop for Robot Control with Learned World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PL-MPC extends the policy-constrained TD-M(PC)² world-model planner by changing three points in its planning-learning loop. Hybrid multi-step TD targets supervise the critic, ensemble-disagreement penalties adjust MPPI terminal values, and return-weighted distillation trains the actor. It lifts HumanoidBench balance-hard from 98 to 387 and hurdle from 199 to 466, and transfers zero-shot to a real wrench-nut task.

## Problem

- Recent TD-MPC variants such as TD-M(PC)², BMPC, BOOM and PO-MPC mainly align the learned policy with planner behavior, which fixes only one interface of the planning-learning loop.
- One-step TD targets show the critic only the immediate reward before bootstrapping, and those uncertain critic values then rank MPPI trajectories and shape future data collection.
- Planner-generated experience varies widely in quality, yet existing planner-to-policy transfer does not weight trajectories by their realized outcomes, so poor episodes are imitated as much as good ones.

## Method

- **Hybrid multi-step TD targets (MTD)**: n=3 targets use observed replay rewards where the replay slice covers them, then fall back to learned-model rollouts under the current actor before the target-critic bootstrap.
- **Adaptive terminal estimates (ATE)**: the ==MPPI== terminal value is penalized by ==target-critic ensemble disagreement==, scaled by a sigmoid of the normalized disagreement. The TD regression target is left unchanged.
- **Return-weighted actor distillation (RAD)**: the actor learns from planner-executed actions with weights given by clipped exp-normalized realized episode return, measured against a ==FIFO return queue==. It switches on after a 200K-step warmup.

## Results

- Over the TD-M(PC)² backbone, mean TAR rises on balance-hard from **98±18** to **387±255** and on hurdle from **199±13** to **466±200**. PL-MPC beats the backbone on 8 of 13 HumanoidBench tasks.
- In leave-one-out ablations, dropping MTD cuts balance-hard to **186±51** with **0%** success. On hurdle, dropping MTD raises the mean to 530, while dropping ATE or RAD lowers it to 296 or 231.
- Zero-shot real KUKA wrench-nut success is **74.2%** vs **61.3%** on the training size and **56.7%** vs **46.7%** pooled over unseen sizes. A 750K RAD warmup improves sit-hard by 170 TAR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - In planner-plus-learner systems, critic targets, terminal-value scoring and policy distillation are separate levers on one feedback loop. Fixing only policy alignment leaves gains on the table.
> - Component effects were task-dependent and non-additive: the same module helped one task and hurt another. Leave-one-out ablations show conditional effects, not a clean additive breakdown.
> - Survival-style per-step rewards and high seed variance can hide whether a task was actually completed. Report success rates and per-seed results next to mean return.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot must seat a grasped wrench on a nut. Contact can rotate the nut, so the robot must realign as it works. The controller is trained entirely in simulation and deployed without real-world fine-tuning. PL-MPC leaves the planner itself unchanged. Instead, it changes how the system learns from planner experience and how it scores future plans. The clips below are selected trials on an object size that was not seen in training. ### 2. One loop, three learning signals

In model-predictive control, planning and learning feed back on each other. The planner supplies experience. That experience trains the model, critic and policy, and the critic and policy then shape future plans.

In the baseline loop, a learned model predicts what candidate actions will do. MPPI, an online optimizer, scores candidate action sequences. A critic, the learned estimator of future return, estimates return beyond the planned horizon. Only the first planned action is executed before the system replans, and replay stores the resulting experience. [[2502.03550|TD-M(PC)²]] additionally constrains the learned actor toward the planner distribution stored in replay. PL-MPC retains that backbone and adds changes at three points:

- **Hybrid multi-step TD targets (MTD).** The critic is trained with later rewards from the replayed planner trajectory when they are available. If the target runs past the replay slice, learned-model predictions under the current actor supply the remaining rewards and states before bootstrapping. This is a hybrid target, not a fully observed three-step return.
- **Adaptive terminal estimates (ATE).** During MPPI scoring, the critic's estimate at the end of a simulated plan is reduced by a penalty that grows with disagreement among target-critic ensemble heads. Disagreement is an uncertainty proxy, not a calibrated estimate of critic error.
- **Return-weighted actor distillation (RAD).** After a warmup, the actor also imitates actions actually executed by the planner, weighted by their source episode's realized return.

### 3. What the comparisons establish

The selected metric is Total Average Return (TAR). Each seed's score is its mean return over its final five evaluations. The reported values are mean ± standard deviation across seeds. On HumanoidBench's **balance-hard** task, TAR rises from **98 ± 18** for TD-M(PC)² to **387 ± 255** for PL-MPC. The large seed variability means this is an improvement in the mean, not evidence of consistent gains across seeds.

A targeted ablation on balance-hard tests whether MTD matters. ATE and RAD are held fixed while MTD is removed. Mean TAR is **387 ± 255** with MTD versus **186 ± 51** without it. Mean environment-defined success across three seeds is **19% versus 0%**, and success requires maintaining balance for a full 1,000-step episode. This supports an MTD contribution under these conditions, but outcomes are strongly seed dependent. It does not establish that MTD alone explains the full method's gains.

The same mechanism does not dominate everywhere. On hurdle, removing MTD in a leave-one-out ablation actually raises the observed mean.

### 4. Closing

PL-MPC extends policy alignment by changing what the system learns from plans, how it scores unfinished plans, and which past plans it imitates. It leaves the world-model architecture and MPPI optimizer unchanged. On the physical robot, PL-MPC shows higher observed success rates than the baseline on the training size and two unseen sizes. This supports transfer in the tested wrench–nut setup, not a general guarantee of sim-to-real transfer. The method was evaluated only on TD-M(PC)², and transfer across other planning–learning backbones remains future work.
