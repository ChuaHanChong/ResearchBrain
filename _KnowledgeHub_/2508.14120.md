# SimGenHOI: Physically Realistic Whole-Body Humanoid-Object Interaction via Generative Modeling and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SimGenHOI framework integrates a conditional diffusion model with a reinforcement learning control policy to generate physically realistic, whole-body humanoid-object interaction sequences. This approach achieves high tracking success for long-horizon tasks, significantly reducing physical artifacts like foot sliding and hand penetration by using a key action extraction strategy and mutual fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing generative models for humanoid-object interaction (HOI) produce diverse motions but often lack physical realism, leading to implausible contacts, penetrations, and unrealistic actions.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) methods ensure physical plausibility but are typically limited to simpler, short-horizon interactions and struggle with complex, dynamic, or long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- A persistent trade-off exists between the diversity and controllability offered by generative models and the physical plausibility enforced by simulation-based methods in HOI generation.

## Method

- A conditional diffusion model based on ==Diffusion Transformers (DiT)== generates sparse "key actions" for human motion, object trajectories, and contact probabilities, conditioned on various inputs (e.g., text, object geometry, waypoints).
- A reinforcement learning-based, contact-aware whole-body control policy tracks the generated key actions in a physics simulator (Isaac Gym), correcting physical artifacts and managing object interactions through a multi-component reward function.
- A mutual fine-tuning strategy iteratively refines both the generative model and the control policy by using successfully tracked, physically plausible actions to enhance the generator and improved generations to refine the policy.

## Results

- SimGenHOI consistently outperformed baseline generative models across multiple metrics, showing lower deviation from ground truth in human and object motion, and higher precision in contact interactions.
- The system achieved a notable reduction in physical artifacts, with foot sliding reduced from **0.44** mm (raw generation) to **0.13** mm and hand penetration eliminated (**0.00** mm) after policy tracking.
- SimGenHOI demonstrated a perfect 7/7 success rate in tracking complex contact and target object placement tasks, significantly outperforming tracking baselines like "Chois + PHC-X" (0/7 success rate).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of key action design, contact guidance, and mutual fine-tuning in achieving high tracking success rates and physical realism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing long-horizon interactions into sparse "key actions" significantly improves the generative model's ability to produce physically feasible motions and enhances tracking robustness.
> - Integrating explicit contact guidance and a carefully designed reward function into a reinforcement learning policy enables robust, contact-aware whole-body control that can correct physical implausibilities from generative models.
> - An iterative mutual fine-tuning process between the generative model and the physics-based policy effectively aligns the distributions of generated motions and physically executable behaviors, leading to higher consistency and tracking success.
