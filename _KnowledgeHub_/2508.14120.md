# SimGenHOI: Physically Realistic Whole-Body Humanoid-Object Interaction via Generative Modeling and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SimGenHOI framework integrates a conditional diffusion model with a reinforcement learning control policy to generate physically realistic, whole-body humanoid-object interaction sequences. This approach achieves high tracking success for long-horizon tasks, significantly reducing physical artifacts like foot sliding and hand penetration by using a key action extraction strategy and mutual fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing generative models for humanoid-object interaction (HOI) produce diverse motions but often lack physical realism, leading to implausible contacts, penetrations, and unrealistic actions.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) methods ensure physical plausibility but are typically limited to simpler, short-horizon interactions and struggle with complex, dynamic, or long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- A persistent trade-off exists between the diversity and controllability offered by generative models and the physical plausibility enforced by simulation-based methods in HOI generation.

## Method

- A conditional diffusion model based on ==Diffusion Transformers (DiT)== generates sparse "key actions" for human motion, object trajectories, and contact probabilities, conditioned on various inputs (e.g., text, object geometry, waypoints).
- A reinforcement learning-based, contact-aware whole-body control policy tracks the generated key actions in a physics simulator (Isaac Gym), correcting physical artifacts and managing object interactions through a multi-component reward function.
- A mutual fine-tuning strategy iteratively refines both the generative model and the control policy by using successfully tracked, physically plausible actions to enhance the generator and improved generations to refine the policy.

## Results

- SimGenHOI consistently outperformed baseline generative models across multiple metrics, showing lower deviation from ground truth in human and object motion, and higher precision in contact interactions.
- The system achieved a notable reduction in physical artifacts, with foot sliding reduced from **0.44** mm (raw generation) to **0.13** mm and hand penetration eliminated (**0.00** mm) after policy tracking.
- SimGenHOI demonstrated a perfect 7/7 success rate in tracking complex contact and target object placement tasks, significantly outperforming tracking baselines like "Chois + PHC-X" (0/7 success rate).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of key action design, contact guidance, and mutual fine-tuning in achieving high tracking success rates and physical realism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing long-horizon interactions into sparse "key actions" significantly improves the generative model's ability to produce physically feasible motions and enhances tracking robustness.
> - Integrating explicit contact guidance and a carefully designed reward function into a reinforcement learning policy enables robust, contact-aware whole-body control that can correct physical implausibilities from generative models.
> - An iterative mutual fine-tuning process between the generative model and the physics-based policy effectively aligns the distributions of generated motions and physically executable behaviors, leading to higher consistency and tracking success.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of humanoid-object interaction (HOI) sequences is a challenge in computer vision, embodied AI, and robotics. Existing research in HOI generation often faces a trade-off between the diversity of generated motions and their physical realism.

Generative models, particularly diffusion-based approaches, have demonstrated proficiency in producing varied and controllable motions for humans and objects. However, these methods frequently introduce physical artifacts such as implausible contacts, penetrations between bodies and objects, and unrealistic whole-body actions. These artifacts hinder the direct execution of generated motions in physical environments or simulators. Examples include diffusion models co-synthesizing human actions and object trajectories conditioned on sparse waypoints or methods integrating grasping generators. While successful in generating diverse motions, they struggle to ensure physical consistency, leading to issues like foot sliding or hand penetration.

Conversely, reinforcement learning (RL) approaches are capable of simulating physically plausible interactions by adhering to physical rules. These methods can generate robust and physically consistent humanoid motions. However, existing RL-based solutions are often limited to simpler, short-horizon interactions and may not generalize effectively to complex, highly dynamic, or long-horizon tasks involving intricate object manipulation. Some frameworks attempt to integrate motion diffusion with tracking policies to enhance robustness, but their applicability to extended, dynamic scenarios remains a limitation.

This work, SimGenHOI, addresses this gap by proposing a unified framework that combines the motion diversity and controllability offered by generative models with the physical plausibility enforced by reinforcement learning. It aims to generate HOI sequences that are not only diverse and controllable but also physically realistic and executable in physics-aware simulations, particularly for long-horizon and dynamic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SimGenHOI is to generate physically realistic, diverse, and controllable whole-body humanoid-object interaction sequences that can be successfully executed in physical environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Overcoming Physical Artifacts in Generative Models**: Current generative models for HOI often produce outputs with physical implausibilities such as penetrations, incorrect contacts, and unrealistic whole-body movements. These artifacts prevent the direct deployment of generated motions in real-world or physics-based robotic applications. SimGenHOI seeks to mitigate these issues by integrating a physics-aware control policy.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Diversity and Realism**: The research aims to resolve the inherent trade-off between the generative diversity of motion synthesis models and the physical realism enforced by simulation-based methods. By combining a generative model with reinforcement learning, the framework intends to leverage the strengths of both paradigms, achieving both variety and physical consistency.

3. **Enabling Long-Horizon Interaction Generation**: Many existing methods focus on short or simple interactions. SimGenHOI aims to facilitate the generation of complex, long-horizon HOI sequences. This is achieved through a novel key action extraction strategy and an autoregressive generation mechanism, which decomposes long interactions into sequences of crucial actions.

4. **Developing Contact-Aware Whole-Body Control**: Accurate and physically plausible interaction with objects requires precise control over the humanoid's body, including hands and feet, and their contact dynamics. The work is motivated to develop a control policy that is specifically contact-aware, capable of tracking generated motions while actively correcting physical inconsistencies like foot sliding or penetration.

5. **Enhancing Robustness and Tracking Success**: Generated motions, especially from data-driven models, can sometimes lead to out-of-distribution (OOD) states that challenge tracking policies in simulators. SimGenHOI is motivated to improve the robustness and tracking success rates by proposing a mutual fine-tuning strategy, allowing the generative model and the control policy to iteratively refine each other.

6. **Supporting Controllability via Diverse Conditions**: The framework aims to allow users to specify HOI tasks through various conditions, including text prompts, object geometry, sparse object waypoints, and initial humanoid poses. This multi-conditional generation is essential for practical applications in robotics and animation.

In summary, the key motivation is to provide a robust and versatile framework for generating complex, physically plausible HOI, enabling higher fidelity and applicability in robotic systems and virtual environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimGenHOI is a unified framework integrating a generative model with reinforcement learning for physically realistic whole-body humanoid-object interaction. The methodology consists of three main components: an HOI generative model, a contact-aware whole-body control policy, and a mutual fine-tuning strategy.

#### 3.1. Humanoid-Object Interaction Generation

The core of motion generation is a conditional diffusion model based on the Diffusion Transformers (DiT) architecture.
- **Interaction Diffusion Model**: This model, built upon Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM), learns to reverse a fixed Markovian noising process. It uses a Transformer as its denoising network to jointly generate human motion (X), object trajectories (O), and contact probabilities for hands and feet (H).
 - **Motion Representation**: Human poses (X) are represented by joint positions and 6-DOF rotations compatible with the SMPL-X model. Object poses (O) are represented by global position and rotation matrix relative to the input object geometry. Contact probabilities (H) are predicted for four body parts over time.
 - **Conditioning**: The model is conditioned on:
 - Object's Basis Point Set (BPS) geometry embedding ($\hat{G}$).
 - Masked motions (S) for in-filling or editing tasks, preserving initial poses, sparse 2D object waypoints, and a target 3D object position.
 - Text prompt embedding ($e_{text}$) from a pre-trained CLIP model.
 - **Training Objective**: The model is trained with an L1 loss to predict the original clean data from noisy input.

- **Key Actions Extract**: To support long-horizon generation and improve physical feasibility, the generative model predicts a sparse set of "key actions" instead of dense motion sequences.
 - **Mechanism**: A recursive algorithm extracts key actions from a dense motion sequence. It starts with the first and last frames, then iteratively identifies the frame with the largest reconstruction error (between the original and linearly interpolated motion) and adds it as a new key action. This process continues until the reconstruction error for all sub-segments falls below a predefined threshold.
 - **Weighted Reconstruction Error**: A weighted reconstruction error is used, giving higher importance to critical joints like hands and feet, to ensure fine-grained details are captured accurately.
 - **Dataset Curation**: The training dataset is formed by sliding windows, where the first frame is the initial state and subsequent frames contain the extracted key actions.

- **Autoregression**: For long-horizon tasks, an autoregressive sampling strategy is adopted. The model generates short, segmented sequences of key actions with overlapping windows. The last few frames of a generated segment serve as initial conditions for the next prediction, maintaining temporal coherence and continuity. Since sparse key actions are predicted, each step effectively covers a longer temporal range, reducing iterations.

#### 3.2. Contact-Aware Whole-Body Policy

A reinforcement learning-based policy is developed to track the generated HOI sequence in a physically realistic simulator (Isaac Gym), correcting artifacts.
- **Human Motion Tracking**: The policy leverages a pre-trained humanoid motion representation model, PULSE, which provides motion priors. The humanoid state ($s_t$) includes proprioception ($s_{p_t}$) and a reference humanoid state ($s_{g_t}$).
 - **Proprioceptive State**: Joint rotations, positions, linear velocities, angular velocities, and body shapes (β).
 - **Reference State**: Relative differences in orientation, position, and velocities between the current state and the next reference motion from the generative model.
- **Contact Guidance Manipulation**: The policy incorporates object observations and contact guidance ($c_t$) from the generative model. This contact guidance provides latent actions (e.g., grasping, releasing) and temporal cues for interaction.
 - **Object State**: Includes the object's current state (position, orientation, linear/angular velocity) and reference state (relative differences to the next reference object motion, and an XNOR operation on reference contact guidance and current contact).
- **Reward Design**: A multi-component reward function trains the policy:
 - **Human Motion Tracking Reward** ($r_{g_t}$): Encourages accurate tracking of key joint positions, rotations, linear velocities, angular velocities, and alignment with predicted contact states.
 - **Object-Related Reward** ($r_{g-obj_t}$): Promotes matching of object positions, orientations, linear, and angular velocities to reference motions.
 - The overall reward ($r_t$) is a weighted sum of these components. This design compensates for the generative model's lack of fine-grained hand pose predictions by encouraging contact.
- **Early Termination (ET)**: Standard ET terminates episodes for unintended ground contact or large deviations from reference motion. SimGenHOI extends this with "Interaction Early Termination," which also triggers termination if: (i) average deviation of object keypoints exceeds 0.5m; or (ii) expected body-object contact is absent for over 10 consecutive frames.

#### 3.3. Mutual Fine-Tuning Strategy

An iterative refinement process enhances both the generative model and the control policy.
- **Process**: The control policy tracks motions generated by the diffusion model in the physics simulator. The physically plausible actions successfully tracked by the policy (which adhere to contact constraints) are then collected. These high-quality, executable samples are used to fine-tune the diffusion-based generative model, improving its ability to produce more physically feasible and trackable motions. Conversely, the improved generative model produces more realistic motions, which are then used to further fine-tune the tracking policy, increasing its tracking success rate. This cyclical process aligns the distributions of generated motion and policy behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of SimGenHOI was validated through extensive experiments on the FullBodyManipulation dataset.

#### 4.1. Humanoid-Object Interaction Generation

- **Comparison with Baselines (Table 1)**: SimGenHOI consistently outperformed several adapted baselines (InterDiff, MDM, OMOMO variants, CHOIS) across multiple generation metrics.
 - **Condition Matching**: SimGenHOI achieved lower Euclidean distances for object waypoints (T\_s, T\_e, T\_xy), indicating better adherence to specified conditions.
 - **Human Motion Quality**: While the *generative model's output alone* (OURS) showed slightly higher Foot Sliding (FS) and Foot Height (H\_feet) compared to some baselines (e.g., CHOIS), its performance was generally competitive or superior in interaction metrics.
 - **Interaction Quality**: SimGenHOI demonstrated higher contact precision (C\_prec), recall (C\_rec), F1 score (C\_F1), and contact percentage (C\_%) compared to baselines, suggesting more accurate and consistent interactions. The hand penetration score (P\_hand) was also competitive.
 - **Deviation from Ground Truth**: SimGenHOI showed significantly lower MPJPE, root translation (T\_root), object position errors (T\_obj), and object orientation errors (O\_obj), indicating closer resemblance to ground truth motions.
 - **Ablation on Key Actions**: The variant "OURS w/o w\_i" (without importance weights during key action extraction) showed a noticeable performance drop in several metrics, particularly human motion quality (higher FS, lower C\_F1), though hand penetration was lower due to less aggressive contact attempts. This suggests the importance of weighting critical joints.
 - **Denoising Steps**: Increasing denoising steps beyond 200 (e.g., OURS\_1000) yielded diminishing returns, indicating performance saturation.

#### 4.2. Physical Consistency after Simulation (Table 2)

- The results demonstrate a notable improvement in physical realism when the generated motions are tracked within the physics simulation by the SimGen-HOI policy.
- The "SimGen-HOI" row (representing the full system with policy tracking) showed significantly reduced foot sliding (FS: 0.13 mm) and eliminated hand penetration (P\_hand: 0.00 mm) compared to the raw generative model output ("OURS\_200", FS: 0.44 mm, P\_hand: 0.54 mm) or baselines like OMOMO GT and CHOIS. This highlights the policy's ability to correct physical artifacts.

#### 4.3. Contact-Aware Interaction Tracking (Table 3)

- **Tracking Success**: SimGen-HOI achieved a 7/7 success rate for both contact (Succ\_cont) and target object placement (Succ\_tgt) on 7 manually selected reference motions. This indicates its capability to successfully execute complex interaction tasks from start to target.
- **Comparison with Tracking Baselines**:
 - "Chois + PHC-X" showed low success rates (0/7 for both Succ\_cont and Succ\_tgt) and high object position/rotation errors, highlighting the difficulty of directly tracking generated motion without detailed hand information.
 - "OmniGrasp on OMOMO" achieved high success rates (6/7 and 7/7 respectively) and low object position/rotation errors, attributing to its focus on object trajectory tracking and RL-driven grasping. However, it relies on pre-grasped poses, which can be vulnerable to inaccuracies.
 - SimGen-HOI demonstrated a robust performance with high tracking duration (TTR) and competitive object position and velocity errors, confirming its ability to reliably track and complete interaction tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 4)

Conducted on small box sequences from the OMOMO test set to evaluate individual components:

- **Key Actions Design**: Removing the key action extraction and interpolation strategy ("w.o. key action") resulted in a lower success rate (34.72%) and higher object/humanoid position errors. This suggests that the sparse key action representation improves tracking robustness and motion fidelity by providing a more structured and manageable reference.
- **Contact Guidance**: Disabling contact guidance as an input to the policy ("w.o. contact") led to a lower success rate (31.94%) and reduced object tracking precision. This indicates that explicit contact cues are crucial for the policy to learn effective grasping and release strategies, even though it resulted in a slightly lower humanoid position error (by prioritizing humanoid tracking over interaction).
- **Mutual Fine-tuning**: Disabling the alternating fine-tuning process ("w.o. finetune") led to a decrease in tracking success rate (37.50%) compared to the full model (41.67%). This confirms the iterative refinement's contribution to aligning generated motions with physically executable trajectories, improving overall system performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SimGenHOI introduces a framework that makes notable contributions to the field of humanoid-object interaction, particularly in addressing the long-standing challenge of generating motions that are simultaneously diverse, controllable, and physically realistic.

1. **Bridging Generative Models and Physics-Based Control**: The primary significance lies in its successful integration of a conditional diffusion model with a reinforcement learning-based control policy. This approach effectively combines the strengths of data-driven generative models (for diversity and controllability) with physics-based simulation (for realism and executability), offering a solution to the prevalent trade-off in HOI generation.

2. **Enabling Physically Plausible Long-Horizon Interactions**: The proposed "key actions" extraction and interpolation strategy is a significant advancement. By predicting a sparse set of crucial actions rather than dense trajectories, the generative model can inherently support long-horizon interactions while enhancing physical feasibility. This reduces the complexity of generation and improves tracking robustness in subsequent physics simulation. This capability has implications for tasks requiring extended sequences of complex manipulations.

3. **Enhancing Realism with Contact-Aware Control**: The design of a contact-aware whole-body control policy is crucial for producing physically realistic interactions. By explicitly incorporating contact guidance and appropriate reward functions, the policy can effectively track generated motions, correct common artifacts like foot sliding and hand penetration, and develop robust object manipulation strategies, even for tasks not requiring high dexterity. This makes the generated motions directly executable in simulators and potentially on physical robots.

4. **Robustness through Mutual Fine-Tuning**: The iterative mutual fine-tuning strategy between the generative model and the control policy is a methodological contribution that addresses issues arising from out-of-distribution generated motions. By using successfully tracked, physically plausible actions to refine the generative model, and then using improved generations to refine the policy, the system achieves a higher degree of consistency and tracking success, leading to a more robust and self-improving framework.

**Potential Impact**:

- **Robotics**: SimGenHOI could significantly impact the development of more versatile and capable humanoid robots. The ability to generate physically realistic and long-horizon human-object interaction trajectories, conditioned by high-level text instructions or sparse waypoints, could streamline the programming and teaching of complex manipulation tasks to robots. This could lead to more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems in environments like factories, homes, or hospitals.
- **Embodied AI and Virtual Characters**: For embodied AI agents in virtual environments or video games, SimGenHOI could enable the creation of highly realistic, responsive, and natural-looking character interactions with objects, enhancing immersion and believability.
- **Computer Graphics and Animation**: The framework offers tools for animators to generate complex character-object interactions with reduced manual effort, ensuring physical consistency without extensive manual keyframing or physics-based simulation setup.
- **Human-Robot Interaction**: By generating human-like object interactions, the framework could be used to study and model human behavior, leading to better understanding for designing more intuitive and safe human-robot collaborative scenarios.

In summary, SimGenHOI represents a step towards more generalized and physically grounded generation of humanoid-object interactions, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in synthetic motion generation for practical applications.
