# WebSeer: Training Deeper Search Agents through Reinforcement Learning with Self-Reflection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University developed WEBSEER, a method for training large language model-based search agents using a unified two-stage framework that integrates self-reflection into both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. This approach enables agents to perform deeper exploration and more robust iterative reasoning in real-world web environments, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on multi-hop question answering benchmarks, including a 12.5-point average improvement over prior methods on in-domain tasks.

## Problem

- Existing agentic RAG systems often make insufficient search calls and exhibit shallow tool-use depth, leading to incomplete or suboptimal answers in complex tasks.
- Current search agents lack spontaneous self-reflection mechanisms to verify information, reformulate queries, or backtrack, which can lead to error accumulation.
- Prior work frequently focuses on retrieval from local, curated databases, limiting applicability and robustness in dynamic, open-ended real-world web scenarios.

## Method

- Introduces a unified two-stage training framework combining ==Self-Reflective Reasoning== Chain Construction (SFT) and Self-Reflective Reinforcement Learning (SRRL).
- Utilizes multi-turn rejection sampling during SFT data construction to create long-horizon, self-reflective reasoning trajectories that include examples of error handling and refinement.
- Employs SRRL with multi-attempt answer submission and a tailored reward function to encourage iterative answer refinement, strategic tool invocation, and efficiency.

## Results

- WebSeer achieved state-of-the-art results across several multi-hop question answering benchmarks, including an average accuracy of **82.4%** on in-domain tasks, surpassing the previous best method by 12.5 points.
- Demonstrated strong generalization to out-of-distribution datasets, with an **81.6%** accuracy on Bamboogle (a 12.8-point improvement) and competitive performance on complex benchmarks like FanoutQA and SimpleQA.
- Quantitative analysis showed that training progressively shapes tool-use behavior from conservative to strategic and efficient, with ablation studies confirming the critical contribution of both self-reflective SFT data and SRRL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating self-reflection into both supervised fine-tuning data generation and reinforcement learning is crucial for training agents that perform deeper and more robust reasoning, mimicking human-like iterative verification.
> - Sufficient model capacity (e.g., a 14B model) is essential for stable learning and consistent improvements in tool usage and accuracy, as smaller models often exhibit instability.
> - The composition of cold-start SFT data, particularly the balance of single-pass versus multi-refinement trajectories, significantly influences the agent's learned tool-use behavior and overall accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of WebSeer is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields in artificial intelligence: large language models (LLMs), retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), tool-augmented LLMs, and intelligent agents.

Initially, LLMs demonstrated remarkable performance across various NLP tasks, leveraging their vast parametric knowledge. However, their inherent limitations—being static, prone to outdated information, and susceptible to hallucinations—necessitated advancements. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) emerged as a crucial paradigm to overcome these by enabling LLMs to dynamically access and retrieve external, up-to-date documents.

The continuous evolution of RAG led to **agentic RAG**, a more sophisticated approach where LLMs are empowered to follow complex reasoning trajectories, browse the internet, and leverage external tools like code executors. This paradigm significantly extended the scope of LLM capabilities, moving beyond simple information retrieval to more dynamic decision-making and problem-solving in interactive environments.

Despite its promise, existing agentic RAG systems face several critical limitations that WebSeer specifically aims to address:
1. **Insufficient Search Calls and Shallow Tool-Use Depth:** Current models often exhibit short tool-use chains, prematurely generating answers based on readily available information rather than actively seeking out and verifying new or complementary knowledge. This conservative strategy leads to suboptimal behaviors and incomplete solutions, especially in multi-hop reasoning tasks.
2. **Lack of Spontaneous Self-Reflection Mechanisms:** A significant drawback of existing search agents in RAG scenarios is their inability to spontaneously reflect, cross-verify information, reformulate queries, or backtrack when facing uncertainty. This absence leads to the accumulation of errors, as initial flawed or partial retrievals amplify inaccuracies in subsequent generation steps.
3. **Neglect of Real-World Web Scenarios:** Much of the prior work in RAG and agentic LLMs has focused on retrieval from local, curated vector databases. This narrow focus limits their applicability and robustness in complex, open-ended real-world web environments, which are characterized by dynamic content, varied formats, and the need for adaptive search strategies.

WebSeer's contribution is precisely in addressing these gaps. By explicitly encouraging deeper exploration, integrating a built-in self-reflection mechanism, and designing a training framework grounded in real-world web scenarios, it advances the state of the art in training LLM-based search agents. It positions itself as a more intelligent and adaptable agent that can navigate the complexities of web-based information retrieval with human-like iterative refinement, distinguishing itself from predecessors like Search-r1 (Jin et al., 2025), R1-Searcher (Song et al., 2025), and DeepResearcher (Zheng et al., 2025) which often rely on shorter tool-use chains, external agent controllers, or are restricted to specific environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WebSeer is to develop a more intelligent and robust search agent capable of effectively tackling complex, real-world multi-hop question answering tasks in dynamic web environments. The authors are motivated by the fundamental limitations observed in current agentic RAG systems, which hinder their ability to perform deep and reliable reasoning.

Specifically, the key objectives for WebSeer are:

- **To enable deeper and more comprehensive exploration:** The agent should be able to generate longer and more elaborate tool-use trajectories, actively seeking out necessary information rather than prematurely synthesizing incomplete data.
- **To integrate spontaneous self-reflection mechanisms:** WebSeer aims to allow the model to autonomously backtrack, reformulate queries, and iteratively refine its reasoning process when faced with uncertainty or potential errors. This is crucial for mitigating error accumulation over multiple interaction steps.
- **To ensure robust performance in real-world web scenarios:** The agent is designed to interact seamlessly with the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of the open web, moving beyond the confines of local, static knowledge bases.

The motivation stems from the recognition that while LLMs and agentic RAG have shown significant promise, their practical utility for complex tasks remains constrained by these issues. The ability of an agent to "think" critically, verify its own findings, and adapt its search strategy mirrors human-like information-seeking behavior. By instilling these capabilities through a novel training paradigm, WebSeer seeks to push the boundaries of intelligent information retrieval and decision-making, ultimately making LLM-based agents more reliable and autonomous in open-domain problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WebSeer employs a novel, unified **two-stage training framework** that integrates cold start initialization with reinforcement learning, all within a self-reflection paradigm. This comprehensive approach is designed to teach the model how to engage in deeper exploration and robust iterative reasoning in real-world web environments.

#### 3.1. Tool Design

To facilitate interaction with external information sources and computational capabilities, WebSeer is equipped with three specialized tools, with answer submission also treated as a tool:

- **Search Engine:** This tool performs Google searches based on a given set of keywords, returning the top-ranked search results in a structured format (title, URL, snippet). This provides the agent with access to a vast, up-to-date knowledge base.
- **Webpage Reader:** Recognizing the prohibitive input length of raw HTML, this tool acts as a model-mediated summarization interface. Given a URL and a specific question, it fetches the HTML content and then prompts the same language model to extract the answer from the page content. This ensures lightweight comprehension of web content.
- **Code Executor:** This tool executes Python code snippets in a controlled environment, returning the standard output. It enables the agent to perform precise computations or data manipulation as needed during its reasoning process.
- **Answer Submission:** A special tool (`submit_answer`) explicitly used by the model to signal the completion of reasoning and output its final answer.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Self-Reflective Reasoning Chain Construction (SFT Data Construction)

The first stage focuses on constructing a high-quality dataset of long-horizon reasoning trajectories that explicitly incorporate reflective patterns. This addresses the limitation of existing datasets that often only contain successful, linear reasoning paths, lacking examples of error handling or refinement.

- **Multi-Turn Rejection Sampling:** The core of this stage involves a process where a **Reasoner (G)** generates a tool-augmented reasoning path culminating in an answer proposal, and an independent **Verifier (V)** assesses the factual correctness of this proposal.
 - The Verifier returns a binary judgment (CORRECT/INCORRECT) along with its own tool-augmented path.
 - A "validity predicate" ensures that the Verifier's judgment aligns with the actual correctness of the answer. If the Verifier incorrectly judges a right answer or correctly judges a wrong answer, the verifier is re-queried up to a budget (K attempts).
 - If all K attempts fail to produce a consistent verification outcome, the instance is discarded, ensuring only reliable feedback.
 - The process iterates (up to `n_max` reflection steps) until the Reasoner produces a correct answer that is also verified as correct. This results in trajectories that include multiple rounds of answer refinement and substantially longer tool-use chains.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The collected set of successful, verified trajectories is then used to fine-tune the model parameters. The SFT objective uses a masked autoregressive negative log-likelihood (NLL) that specifically excludes external observation tokens (raw tool outputs) from the loss calculation. This encourages the model to learn the agent's internal reasoning steps and tool-calling decisions, rather than memorizing raw tool observations, enhancing robustness and generalizability.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Self-Reflective Reinforcement Learning (SRRL)

The second stage builds upon the SFT-initialized model by introducing Self-Reflective Reinforcement Learning (SRRL). This stage unifies SFT and RL under the self-reflection mechanism, allowing for iterative answer refinement based on external feedback.

- **Multi-Attempt Answer Submission:** Unlike traditional RL setups, SRRL allows the model to submit answers multiple times within a single dialogue turn. When an `answer_submit` action is taken, the submitted answer's F1 score `r^(t)` against the ground truth is returned as textual feedback. If this score is below a predefined threshold, the environment allows the model to continue reasoning, enabling it to revise its strategy and potentially submit an improved answer.
- **Policy Optimization:** The authors employ the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm, enhanced with the `clip-higher` mechanism from DAPO (Yu et al., 2025). This mechanism helps accommodate asymmetric update ranges, preventing overfitting to noisy advantages and promoting stable learning.
- **Reward Design:** A trajectory-wise reward function `R(τ)` is defined, composed of two components:
 - `R_format(τ)`: Penalizes excessively long outputs and encourages answers within a "safe-zone" length, promoting conciseness and adherence to expected output formats.
 - `R_correct(τ)`: Combines the task-specific F1 score (`r`) with an exponential discount `α^T` for the number of submission attempts (`T`). This structure incentivizes correctness while discouraging an excessive number of resubmissions, promoting efficiency.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

During training, for consistency and cost-efficiency, retrieval was restricted to Wikipedia via the Google Site Search API, with full pages fetched through the official Wikipedia API. The training utilized the verl framework, sampling 12 prompts per step with 8 candidate trajectories and up to 30 interaction turns. The entire training spanned 100 steps, consuming 60 A800 GPU hours. For inference, the Google Web Search API and Jina API were used for real-time retrieval and clean text extraction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WebSeer's comprehensive evaluation across various benchmarks demonstrates its superior performance and robust generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

WebSeer consistently achieves state-of-the-art results on several multi-hop question answering (MHQA) benchmarks:

- **In-Domain Tasks:** On NQ, TQ, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA, WebSeer reached an average accuracy of **82.4%**, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art method, Search-r1, by a significant **12.5 points**.
 - Specific highlights include **72.3% accuracy on HotpotQA** and substantial gains on NQ (15.9 points) and 2WikiMultiHopQA (27.2 points).
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Datasets:** WebSeer exhibits strong generalization to OOD datasets, indicating that its learned reasoning patterns and retrieval strategies are transferable.
 - On Bamboogle, it achieved **81.6%**, a **12.8-point improvement** over the prior best.
 - On PopQA, it reached **60.6%**.
 - For harder benchmarks like FanoutQA, FRAMES, and SimpleQA, WebSeer again demonstrated strong performance, achieving **55.4% on FanoutQA** (nearly matching GPT-4o's 55.8%) and an impressive **90.0% on SimpleQA**. This suggests that even when trained under site-restricted search, WebSeer performs effectively in the open web.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Analysis

The paper provides valuable quantitative insights into the factors influencing WebSeer's performance:

- **Model Capacity Matters:** Sufficient model capacity is crucial. Only the 14B model showed consistent improvements in both tool usage and accuracy after SFT and subsequent RL. Smaller models (3B, 7B) often exhibited instability, with repetitive text or malformed JSON outputs, highlighting the role of scale in stable reasoning and reliable tool-augmented decision-making.
- **Evolution of Tool-Use Behavior:** The training process progressively shapes tool-use behavior.
 - **Pre-SFT:** Limited tool usage, typically around three calls per example, suggesting a conservative strategy.
 - **Post-SFT:** A significant shift towards more active and flexible tool engagement, with the distribution peaking at 10 calls and extending up to 50, indicating longer multi-step interactions.
 - **Post-RL:** The distribution sharpened to between 5 and 8 calls, with very high and very low counts becoming rare. This indicates a shift towards strategic and efficient tool invocation, implicitly reinforced as it aids verification and validation.
- **Impact of SFT Data Composition:** The ratio of "single-pass correct trajectories" to "multi-refinement trajectories" in the SFT data is critical. Increasing the share of longer reasoning trajectories encourages more frequent tool usage, but an appropriate balance is necessary to translate this into higher accuracy. This finding underscores the importance of carefully curated cold-start data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments confirm the criticality of each component in WebSeer's framework:

- **Contribution of SRRL:** Limiting the model to a single answer submission during reinforcement learning (i.e., removing the iterative refinement aspect of SRRL) resulted in a noticeable decrease in the final model’s performance, validating the importance of this self-reflection mechanism.
- **Importance of Cold-Start (SFT Data):** Training without the initial cold-start initialization (i.e., using only RL without complex SFT data) led to significant degradation. The 14B model "collapsed with decreasing rewards," while the 7B model generated "garbled text before tool calls," underscoring that high-quality SFT data is indispensable for stable training and effective tool-use behavior, especially for challenging tasks.

These results collectively demonstrate that WebSeer's integrated approach of self-reflective data construction and reinforcement learning, coupled with appropriate model scale, is highly effective in developing robust and intelligent search agents for complex web-based tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of WebSeer marks a significant step forward in the field of intelligent search agents and retrieval-augmented generation. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Novel Training Paradigm:** WebSeer proposes the first unified two-stage training framework that explicitly integrates a self-reflection paradigm during both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). This paradigm allows agents to mimic human-like information-seeking behaviors, where iterative refinement and verification are crucial for complex problem-solving.
- **Deeper and More Robust Reasoning:** By synthesizing multi-refinement reasoning trajectories through rejection sampling and leveraging Self-Reflective Reinforcement Learning (SRRL), WebSeer learns to perform deeper and more robust reasoning. This directly addresses the critical limitations of shallow tool-use depth and error accumulation that plague existing agentic RAG systems.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging multi-hop question answering benchmarks like HotpotQA and SimpleQA, as well as demonstrating strong generalization to out-of-distribution datasets like Bamboogle and FanoutQA, validates the effectiveness of WebSeer's approach. Its competitive performance against models like GPT-4o on certain benchmarks is particularly noteworthy.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The ability of WebSeer to generalize well to unseen question types, domains, and open web sources, despite being trained on site-restricted search data, is a crucial indicator of a robust and transferable retrieval-reasoning policy. This means the agent learns fundamental strategies rather than merely overfitting to specific training distributions.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility:** Achieving these results with a single 14B model, without the need for auxiliary agent controllers or even stronger backbone models, makes the approach more efficient and potentially accessible for broader deployment compared to more complex multi-model or larger-scale architectures.
- **Foundation for Future Agents:** WebSeer lays a robust foundation for the development of more general-purpose reasoning agents. Its framework could be extended to other complex tasks requiring multi-step planning, dynamic information acquisition, and self-correction, enabling LLMs to interact more intelligently and autonomously with dynamic, heterogeneous web environments and other tool-rich ecosystems. This paves the way for a new generation of intelligent assistants that are not only capable of retrieving information but also critically evaluating and iteratively refining their solutions.

In conclusion, WebSeer represents a pivotal advancement in making LLM-based agents more reliable, adaptive, and truly "intelligent" by fostering deeper exploration and spontaneous self-reflection in the complex landscape of real-world web-based information retrieval.
