# Generalizable Visual Reinforcement Learning with Segment Anything Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The SAM-G framework enhances visual reinforcement learning generalization by integrating the Segment Anything Model to provide task-relevant masked images to RL agents. This approach achieved a 44% relative improvement over prior state-of-the-art on "video hard" DeepMind Control Suite tasks and a 29% improvement on "video hard" Adroit dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Visual reinforcement learning agents exhibit poor generalization to unseen environments with visual variations.
- Agents often overfit to spurious visual correlations in training data rather than task-relevant features.
- Existing methods for robust visual representations struggle with challenging "video hard" generalization scenarios featuring dynamic backgrounds.

## Method

- A framework named **SAM-G** leverages the ==Segment Anything Model (SAM)== for robust, task-relevant object segmentation in visual observations.
- It employs an "Identify" phase to extract object features using ==DINOv2== and SAM encoders from a single masked training example, combined with an optional few human-provided points.
- A "Segment" phase then uses these features to generate iteratively refined segmentation masks for new input images, which are fed to the RL agent after a ==one-shot adaptation== of SAM.

## Results

- SAM-G achieved a **44%** relative improvement in average score over the prior state-of-the-art (PIE-G) on DeepMind Control Suite "video hard" tasks.
- The framework demonstrated a **29%** relative improvement over PIE-G on Adroit dexterous manipulation "video hard" tasks.
- SAM-G consistently maintained high performance across all visual generalization difficulty levels, from "color easy" to "video hard," outperforming baseline methods that showed significant degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly integrating powerful vision foundation models like SAM can provide robust, task-relevant visual information, improving RL generalization by offloading complex perceptual tasks.
> - Equipping RL agents with object understanding, akin to human perception, allows them to focus on learning optimal control policies from cleaner visual inputs, free from distracting backgrounds.
> - The modular nature of SAM-G enables its seamless integration with existing visual RL algorithms without requiring architectural modifications to the core agent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generalization in visual reinforcement learning (RL) is a fundamental and pressing challenge. Traditional RL agents, while achieving remarkable success in specific environments (e.g., video games, robotic manipulation), often exhibit poor performance when deployed in unseen environments with even subtle visual variations. This overfitting issue arises because agents tend to learn spurious correlations from the training environment's visual cues rather than extracting task-relevant features.

Existing research largely tackles this problem by focusing on acquiring more robust visual representations. These approaches can be broadly categorized into:
- **Auxiliary Supervision**: Incorporating additional loss functions (e.g., contrastive learning, predictive coding) during training to encourage the learning of invariant features.
- **Pre-training**: Utilizing large datasets (e.g., ImageNet, 3D datasets) to pre-train visual encoders, hoping to equip them with general-purpose visual understanding that can then be fine-tuned for RL tasks. PIE-G and VRL3 fall into this category.
- **Data Augmentation**: Applying various transformations (e.g., random shifts, color jitter) to observations during training to increase visual diversity and prevent overfitting to specific visual styles. DrQ-v2 is a prominent example.
- **Saliency/Mask Learning**: Attempts to explicitly guide agents to focus on task-relevant regions by learning saliency maps or masks, as seen in SGQN.

SAM-G positions itself as a novel paradigm by directly leveraging the power of **vision foundation models**, specifically the Segment Anything Model (SAM), to tackle the generalization problem. Instead of designing complex architectures or auxiliary objectives to *learn* robust visual representations or masks *within* the RL framework, SAM-G offloads the task of identifying and segmenting task-relevant objects to a pre-trained, highly capable segmentation model. This is a significant departure from prior work because it externalizes a crucial part of visual processing to an already "intelligent" vision module, effectively providing RL agents with "human-like" object understanding. While other works have tried to learn masks, SAM-G's approach of *using* a powerful, promptable foundation model for this purpose is distinct and innovative. It is orthogonal to and can be seamlessly integrated with many existing RL algorithms, which often focus on policy learning or representation learning through other means. The paper argues that humans' innate understanding of object concepts is key to their generalization ability, and SAM-G attempts to imbue RL agents with a similar capability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly improve the generalization capabilities of visual reinforcement learning agents to unseen environments**. The authors aim to enable RL policies trained in one visual setting to perform effectively in novel environments characterized by altered appearances, backgrounds, and lighting conditions, without requiring retraining or specific architectural modifications to the RL agent itself.

The key motivations driving this work are:
1. **Addressing the generalization bottleneck in visual RL**: Acknowledging that current methods, despite their advancements, still struggle with robust generalization, especially in challenging "video hard" settings where backgrounds dynamically change.
2. **Mimicking human object understanding**: Humans intuitively identify and segment task-relevant objects in cluttered or novel environments, which is crucial for flexible decision-making. The authors propose to equip RL agents with a similar "innate understanding of object concepts" rather than forcing them to learn it from scratch.
3. **Leveraging the power of modern vision foundation models**: Recognizing that models like SAM have demonstrated remarkable zero-shot segmentation abilities and can process complex visual information with high fidelity. The paper seeks to explore how these powerful perceptual capabilities can be directly harnessed to benefit decision-making processes in RL.
4. **Simplifying visual processing for RL agents**: By providing agents with high-quality masked images, SAM-G aims to reduce the burden on the RL agent's visual encoder, allowing it to focus on learning optimal control policies from cleaner, task-relevant visual inputs, free from distracting background clutter.

In essence, the core motivation is to move beyond learning robust visual representations *through* the RL objective and instead *provide* robust, task-relevant visual information *to* the RL agent by externalizing the complex task of object segmentation to a specialized, pre-trained foundation model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAM-G (Segment Anything Model for Generalizable visual RL) is a novel framework that enhances RL agents' generalization by leveraging SAM for robust object segmentation. The methodology is primarily divided into two parts: "Identify" and "Segment," with an additional "One-shot Adaptation" step.

#### 3.1. Identify

This phase focuses on extracting task-relevant object features from a **single example image and its mask** provided from the *training environment*. This single instance serves as a prompt for SAM.
- **Feature Extraction**: Image features are extracted using the encoders from two powerful vision foundation models: DINOv2 (a self-supervised vision transformer) and SAM itself. For efficiency, SAM's image encoder uses the EfficientViT-L1 architecture. DINOv2 uses ViT-B/14.
- **Point Feature Fetching**: From these extracted image features, two types of "point features" representing the task-relevant objects are derived:
 - **Type 1 (Automatic)**: Computed by averaging the spatial average-pooled and spatial max-pooled features over the masked region of the image. This provides a compact, global representation of the object.
 - **Type 2 (Human-supervised)**: For each object, 3 sparse points are manually labeled *once* in the training image. Features corresponding to these exact coordinates are directly extracted from the image features. These "extra points" are crucial for robust identification, especially in multi-object tasks or confusing backgrounds.
The stored point features (e.g., 4 points * (768D DINOv2 + 256D SAM)) act as abstract representations of the target objects.

#### 3.2. Segment

This phase utilizes the stored point features to segment task-relevant objects in *any* new image, whether from the training or generalization environment.
- **Determining Point Prompts via Correspondence**: For a new input image, its features are extracted using the same DINOv2 and SAM encoders. Then, a similarity map is constructed for each stored point feature by calculating the normalized inner product with the new image's features. The similarity maps from DINOv2 and SAM are averaged for better localization.
 - For Type 1 point features, the point of highest similarity is chosen as a positive prompt, and the point of least similarity as a negative prompt.
 - For Type 2 point features, only the point of highest similarity is used as a positive prompt, as their localized nature might make a "least similarity" point misleading.
- **Mask Prediction and Refinement**: The determined point prompts are fed into SAM's prompt encoder. SAM's mask decoder then generates initial segmentation mask logits. The process is refined iteratively:
 - After the 1st prediction, the bounding box of the mask is computed.
 - A point of *least similarity within this bounding box* (based on the original similarity map) is selected as an *additional negative point prompt*.
 - The mask decoding is repeated (2nd and 3rd times) with the accumulated prompts (old positive, old negative, and new negative points) to iteratively refine the mask and better separate foreground from background. This iterative refinement is a known technique in SAM applications but is specifically adapted here with similarity-map-guided negative points.
- **Direct Use for RL Agents**: The high-quality masked images produced by SAM are then directly fed as observations to the visual RL agents, replacing the raw RGB images. This means the RL agent's architecture remains unchanged; only its input observations are processed.

#### 3.3. One-Shot Adaptation

Before training the RL agent, SAM undergoes a rapid, parameter-efficient fine-tuning step using the initially provided single training image and its mask. This adaptation, following the PerSAM technique, only adjusts two learnable weights within SAM's mask decoder (which combine its three mask logits). This process takes roughly 10 seconds, ensuring minimal overhead while customizing SAM to better understand the specific task objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SAM-G was rigorously evaluated across 11 visual RL tasks, including 8 from the DeepMind Control Suite (DMControl) and 3 from the Adroit dexterous manipulation suite. The generalization settings were adapted from DMC-GB and RL-ViGen, featuring four levels of increasing visual difficulty: color easy, color hard, video easy, and video hard.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Superior Generalization in Challenging Settings**: SAM-G demonstrated significantly improved visual generalization capabilities, especially in the most challenging "video hard" settings.
 - On DMControl tasks (averaged), SAM-G achieved **44% relative improvement** over the previous state-of-the-art (PIE-G) in the video hard setting (average score of 770 vs. 534).
 - On Adroit tasks (averaged), SAM-G achieved **29% relative improvement** over PIE-G in the video hard setting (average score of 74.6 vs. 57.9).
 - This robust performance in "video hard" settings, which feature dynamically changing natural video backgrounds, highlights SAM-G's ability to isolate task-relevant objects despite significant visual distractors.

2. **Consistent Performance Across Difficulty Levels**: Unlike baseline methods (DrQ-v2, SVEA, PIE-G, SGQN) that showed considerable performance degradation in more difficult generalization scenarios, SAM-G maintained consistent and high performance across all settings (color easy, color hard, video easy, video hard). This underscores the framework's robustness and reliability.

3. **Seamless Integration and No Negative Impact on Training**: SAM-G was successfully implemented on top of existing RL algorithms like DrQ-v2 and PIE-G, demonstrating its modularity. Training curves showed that providing masked images did not hinder sample efficiency or convergence, and in some cases (e.g., Adroit Door task), even slightly improved sample efficiency.

4. **Effectiveness on Multi-Object Tasks**: The strong results on Adroit tasks (Pen, Door, Hammer), which involve multiple interacting objects and complex dexterous manipulation, proved SAM-G's generality beyond simpler single-object tasks often found in DMControl.

5. **Imitation Learning Potential**: Preliminary experiments applying SAM-G to imitation learning on Adroit tasks showed significant improvements over PIE-G on Door and Hammer, and comparable results on Pen. This suggests broader applicability of SAM-G as a general framework for generalizable visuomotor policy learning.

**Ablation Studies Insights:**

- **DINOv2 Integration**: Combining image features from DINOv2 and SAM encoders for similarity mapping was crucial. Removing DINOv2 led to significant performance drops, especially in "video hard" settings (e.g., 830 to 589 average on DMControl), validating the benefit of multi-model feature fusion for robust correspondence.
- **Extra Points**: The human-provided "extra points" (Type 2 point features) were critical for accurate object identification, particularly in tasks like Cartpole Swingup. Their exclusion resulted in performance degradation, emphasizing the importance of precise correspondence.
- **Mask Refinement**: Iterative mask refinement with additional negative prompts generally improved performance, confirming its role in better foreground-background separation, though a slight negative effect was observed in one specific task (Ball in cup Catch) possibly due to extreme object positions.
- **PerSAM Adaptation**: The one-shot parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PerSAM) was beneficial, helping SAM better adapt to specific task objects and reduce ambiguity between foreground and background.
- **EfficientViT for Speed**: While SAM introduces computational overhead, incorporating the EfficientViT architecture significantly reduced wall time compared to the original SAM ViT-B, making SAM-G more practical.
- **Segmentation Model Choice**: SAM-G with EfficientViT-L1 SAM significantly outperformed Mask-RCNN, highlighting the superior quality of foundation model segmentation. It also showed slight advantages over the original SAM (ViT-B) and competitive results with EfficientSAM, justifying the choice of EfficientViT for its balance of speed and accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SAM-G represents a significant step forward in visual reinforcement learning, primarily by demonstrating the transformative potential of **directly integrating powerful vision foundation models** into the RL pipeline for enhanced generalization.

**Significance:**
1. **Paradigm Shift in Visual Preprocessing for RL**: SAM-G advocates for a new approach where robust, task-relevant visual information is explicitly segmented and presented to the RL agent, rather than relying solely on the agent's visual encoder to implicitly learn this. This offloads a complex perceptual task to a highly capable, pre-trained model.
2. **Addressing a Core Challenge**: By achieving substantial and consistent performance gains in challenging generalization settings (especially "video hard"), SAM-G tackles one of the most persistent and critical problems in visual RL. This paves the way for deploying RL agents in more diverse and unpredictable real-world environments.
3. **Modular and Generalizable Framework**: The methodology is designed to be largely independent of the specific RL algorithm, allowing SAM-G to be "seamlessly incorporated" into various existing and future visual RL agents. This modularity makes it a highly impactful contribution, as it can uplift the generalization capabilities of a wide range of current methods.
4. **Inspiration for Future Research**: This work opens up a new avenue for research at the intersection of foundation models and decision-making. It suggests that similar integration of other foundation models (e.g., for depth, flow, object detection, or even language understanding) could further enhance the capabilities of RL agents.
5. **Reduced Data Dependence for Generalization**: By leveraging the vast pre-training of SAM and requiring only a single labeled image for task-relevant object identification, SAM-G reduces the need for extensive, varied training data typically required to learn generalizable representations from scratch within the RL loop.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Robust Robotic Systems**: Robots equipped with SAM-G could perform manipulation and locomotion tasks in diverse, unconstrained environments (e.g., homes, factories, outdoor settings) with varying lighting, clutter, and background changes, without needing costly recalibration or retraining.
- **Enhanced Real-World RL Applications**: The improved generalization could accelerate the deployment of visual RL in complex scenarios like autonomous driving, drone navigation, and human-robot interaction, where visual variability is immense.
- **Lower Barrier to Entry for RL Researchers**: By simplifying the visual generalization aspect, researchers can focus more on the core RL algorithms, knowing that a robust visual input stream is being provided.
- **Catalyst for Foundation Model Integration**: This paper serves as a strong case study for how specialized foundation models can be effectively combined to build more intelligent and adaptable AI systems, demonstrating a pathway for future AI architectures.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The primary acknowledged limitation is the **extended wall time** due to the computational cost of running SAM, even with efficient architectures like EfficientViT. While the authors mitigated this with EfficientViT, further advancements in model architecture, distillation, and system optimization will be crucial for real-time applications. Future work could also explore fully automatic point feature generation, reducing the minimal human supervision currently required for "extra points," or investigate more dynamic prompt generation for SAM during task execution.

In conclusion, SAM-G marks a pivotal development, demonstrating that the profound perceptual capabilities of vision foundation models can be effectively harvested to address the critical challenge of generalization in visual reinforcement learning, thereby pushing the boundaries of deployable and robust AI agents.
