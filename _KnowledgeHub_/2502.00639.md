# Half-order Fine-Tuning for Diffusion Model: A Recursive Likelihood Ratio Optimizer

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers introduced the Recursive Likelihood Ratio (RLR) optimizer, an unbiased and low-variance gradient estimator designed for fine-tuning diffusion models efficiently. This method achieved state-of-the-art performance in Text2Image and Text2Video generation on benchmarks such as VBench, exhibiting enhanced sample efficiency and preventing model collapse experienced by prior approaches.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion models with full backpropagation incurs prohibitive computational and memory overheads, making it impractical for large models.
- Truncated backpropagation, a common workaround, introduces structural bias, leading to suboptimal optimization, model collapse, and an inability to capture multi-scale information.
- Reinforcement Learning-based fine-tuning methods are memory-efficient but suffer from high variance in gradient estimation, resulting in slow convergence and sample inefficiency.

## Method

- Proposed the ==Recursive Likelihood Ratio== (RLR) optimizer, which systematically combines First-Order, Half-Order (likelihood ratio with local backpropagation), and Zeroth-Order gradient estimators to provide an unbiased and lower-variance estimate.
- The Half-Order component utilizes the inherent noise in diffusion models to enable pathwise gradient estimation over a randomly selected, fixed-horizon sub-chain, balancing variance reduction with computational cost.
- Introduced Diffusive Chain-of-Thought (DCoT), a multi-scale prompting technique that decomposes original prompts and synergizes with RLR to target gradient updates at specific diffusion stages for improved generation control.

## Results

- RLR achieved state-of-the-art Text2Image performance, significantly improving reward scores (e.g., ImageReward on SD 1.4 from 32.74 to 75.65 for HPD v2 prompts) and demonstrating superior sample efficiency and stability compared to RL and truncated BP methods.
- The optimizer obtained the best overall performance on the VBench Text2Video benchmark (weighted average of 84.63), surpassing various leading models and showing notable improvements in "Dynamic Degree" (70.69) and "Aesthetic Quality" (66.15).
- RLR exhibited lower memory consumption (**22.4 GB** VRAM) compared to truncated BP (**25.4 GB** VRAM, **78.5 GB** System RAM) and achieved target performance significantly faster (e.g., 75 total steps) than high-variance RL methods (e.g., 600 total steps).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured combination of different gradient estimation strategies (First-Order, Half-Order, Zeroth-Order) can overcome the limitations of individual approaches, yielding an unbiased and low-variance estimator for recursive generative models.
> - The inherent recursive nature of diffusion models can be leveraged by a Half-Order approach using likelihood ratios to capture multi-scale information efficiently without the full cost of backpropagation.
> - Tailoring prompts to align with the coarse-to-fine generation process of diffusion models (Diffusive Chain-of-Thought) significantly enhances fine-tuning effectiveness when combined with an appropriate gradient estimator like RLR.
