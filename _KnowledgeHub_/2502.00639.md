# Half-order Fine-Tuning for Diffusion Model: A Recursive Likelihood Ratio Optimizer

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers introduced the Recursive Likelihood Ratio (RLR) optimizer, an unbiased and low-variance gradient estimator designed for fine-tuning diffusion models efficiently. This method achieved state-of-the-art performance in Text2Image and Text2Video generation on benchmarks such as VBench, exhibiting enhanced sample efficiency and preventing model collapse experienced by prior approaches.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion models with full backpropagation incurs prohibitive computational and memory overheads, making it impractical for large models.
- Truncated backpropagation, a common workaround, introduces structural bias, leading to suboptimal optimization, model collapse, and an inability to capture multi-scale information.
- Reinforcement Learning-based fine-tuning methods are memory-efficient but suffer from high variance in gradient estimation, resulting in slow convergence and sample inefficiency.

## Method

- Proposed the ==Recursive Likelihood Ratio== (RLR) optimizer, which systematically combines First-Order, Half-Order (likelihood ratio with local backpropagation), and Zeroth-Order gradient estimators to provide an unbiased and lower-variance estimate.
- The Half-Order component utilizes the inherent noise in diffusion models to enable pathwise gradient estimation over a randomly selected, fixed-horizon sub-chain, balancing variance reduction with computational cost.
- Introduced Diffusive Chain-of-Thought (DCoT), a multi-scale prompting technique that decomposes original prompts and synergizes with RLR to target gradient updates at specific diffusion stages for improved generation control.

## Results

- RLR achieved state-of-the-art Text2Image performance, significantly improving reward scores (e.g., ImageReward on SD 1.4 from 32.74 to 75.65 for HPD v2 prompts) and demonstrating superior sample efficiency and stability compared to RL and truncated BP methods.
- The optimizer obtained the best overall performance on the VBench Text2Video benchmark (weighted average of 84.63), surpassing various leading models and showing notable improvements in "Dynamic Degree" (70.69) and "Aesthetic Quality" (66.15).
- RLR exhibited lower memory consumption (**22.4 GB** VRAM) compared to truncated BP (**25.4 GB** VRAM, **78.5 GB** System RAM) and achieved target performance significantly faster (e.g., 75 total steps) than high-variance RL methods (e.g., 600 total steps).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured combination of different gradient estimation strategies (First-Order, Half-Order, Zeroth-Order) can overcome the limitations of individual approaches, yielding an unbiased and low-variance estimator for recursive generative models.
> - The inherent recursive nature of diffusion models can be leveraged by a Half-Order approach using likelihood ratios to capture multi-scale information efficiently without the full cost of backpropagation.
> - Tailoring prompts to align with the coarse-to-fine generation process of diffusion models (Diffusive Chain-of-Thought) significantly enhances fine-tuning effectiveness when combined with an appropriate gradient estimator like RLR.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Probabilistic diffusion models (DMs) have become a prominent framework for high-fidelity data generation across various applications, including image synthesis, video generation, and multi-modal data modeling. These models, after extensive pre-training on large datasets, often require fine-tuning to align with specific requirements for downstream applications or human preferences. This post-training phase, however, presents significant challenges due to the computational complexities associated with estimating gradients through their inherent recursive chain structure.

Existing fine-tuning approaches primarily fall into two categories: methods based on full or truncated Backpropagation (BP) and methods based on Reinforcement Learning (RL). Full BP, while theoretically sound for precise gradient estimation, incurs prohibitive computational and memory overheads that scale with model size and the number of diffusion steps. For instance, fine-tuning Stable Diffusion 1.4 with full BP and a small batch size can demand terabytes of GPU memory, rendering it impractical. Consequently, truncated BP has become a common practice to reduce memory footprint by limiting the number of backpropagation steps. However, this introduces structural bias, as it neglects gradient contributions from earlier steps in the diffusion chain, potentially leading to suboptimal optimization, model collapse, or an inability to capture multi-scale information.

Reinforcement Learning-based methods address memory limitations by avoiding the caching of intermediate activations and estimating gradients. While these methods are memory-efficient and allow for flexible computation, they typically suffer from high variance in gradient estimation. This high variance can lead to slow convergence and sample inefficiency, hindering effective model adaptation.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of both truncated BP (bias, model collapse) and RL (high variance, sample inefficiency). It aims to develop a fine-tuning paradigm that balances computational tractability with optimization efficacy by proposing a novel gradient estimator that is unbiased, exhibits lower variance, and remains computationally feasible. The Recursive Likelihood Ratio (RLR) optimizer seeks to provide a more efficient, scalable, and stable approach to align foundation DMs, thereby facilitating their broader deployment in diverse applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop an efficient and accurate gradient estimator for fine-tuning diffusion models:** The core objective is to overcome the limitations of existing fine-tuning methods by creating a gradient estimator that is unbiased and possesses minimal variance under practical computational and memory constraints.
2. **Formulate a systematic design space for gradient estimators in DMs:** The authors aim to characterize the different strategies for gradient estimation (First-Order, Zeroth-Order, Half-Order) and provide a structured framework for combining them to optimize fine-tuning performance.
3. **Theoretically analyze the properties of the proposed method:** A crucial objective is to provide a rigorous theoretical investigation of the RLR optimizer, including its unbiasedness, variance properties, and convergence guarantees, to formally justify its empirical advantages and explain the deficiencies of prior methods.
4. **Empirically validate the superiority of the RLR optimizer:** The research seeks to demonstrate the practical effectiveness of RLR through extensive experiments on challenging visual generation tasks, specifically Text2Image and Text2Video, comparing it against state-of-the-art baselines.
5. **Propose synergistic techniques to enhance RLR's capabilities:** The authors also aim to introduce a novel prompt technique that naturally integrates with RLR, further leveraging its ability to capture multi-scale information for improved visual generation.

The motivation for these objectives stems directly from the critical challenges in fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion models:

- **Computational and Memory Overhead:** Full backpropagation through the hundreds or thousands of steps in a diffusion chain is computationally prohibitive and demands excessive memory, limiting its applicability.
- **Bias in Truncated Backpropagation:** Common workarounds like truncated BP introduce structural bias by ignoring gradient contributions from earlier diffusion steps. This bias can lead to suboptimal training, cause "model collapse" (where generated content degrades to noise), and prevent the model from capturing hierarchical, multi-scale features crucial for high-fidelity generation.
- **High Variance in Reinforcement Learning:** RL-based methods, while memory-efficient, suffer from high-variance gradient estimations. This results in slow convergence and sample inefficiency, making the fine-tuning process protracted and unstable.
- **Need for Multi-Scale Information Capture:** Diffusion models generate content in a coarse-to-fine manner, with different steps contributing to different levels of detail. Existing biased or high-variance methods often fail to adequately capture and leverage this multi-scale information, impeding the ability to achieve high-fidelity outputs across all scales.

By addressing these issues, the research seeks to enable more efficient, stable, and effective alignment of powerful foundation diffusion models for diverse downstream tasks, thereby expanding their practical utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around the Recursive Likelihood Ratio (RLR) optimizer, designed to provide an unbiased, low-variance gradient estimator while managing computational costs. The approach begins by formally defining the fine-tuning objective and characterizing the design space of gradient estimators in diffusion models.

**Problem Formulation:**
The fine-tuning objective is to maximize the expected reward `E[R(x_0)]` with respect to the model parameters `θ`, where `x_0` is the generated sample from the recursive denoising process `x_0 = ϕ_1:T (x_T, z_1:T; θ)`. The goal is to find an unbiased gradient estimator `G` for this objective that minimizes variance `Var(G)` under a computational budget `B`, formulated as `min_G∈G Var(G) s.t. ∇_θ E[R(x_0)] = E[G], C(G) ≤ B`.

**Unbiased Gradient Estimator Design Space (G):**
The authors define a design space by categorizing gradient estimation strategies at each step `t` of the diffusion process:
- **First-Order (FO):** Uses exact backpropagation through `ϕ_t`, providing low variance but high computational and memory cost.
- **Zeroth-Order (ZO):** Perturbs parameters directly and estimates gradients purely from function evaluations, incurring the highest variance but the lowest cost.
- **Half-Order (HO):** Proposed in this work, it utilizes the inherent noise `z_t` in the DM and applies the Likelihood Ratio (LR) technique. This enables a local computational graph of a specific length `h` (`D_θ ϕ_t:t+h-1`), balancing variance and cost. Reinforcement Learning is considered a special case of HO with `h=1`.

**Recursive Likelihood Ratio (RLR) Optimizer:**
The RLR optimizer combines these three strategies in a structured manner to address the variance-memory tradeoff. The design space for RLR (`G_RLR`) enforces that HO estimators are connected in a chain, and FO is directly connected to the reward model. Specifically, RLR employs:
1. **One-step First-Order (FO) estimator:** Applied at the first time step, directly backpropagating through the reward model `dR(x_0)/dx_0`. This leverages the reward model's structure without treating it as a black box.
2. **Fixed-horizon Half-Order (HO) sub-chain:** A local BP chain of length `h` is randomly selected to start at an index `j` (`j ∼ J(1, T-h)`) within the diffusive chain. This HO sub-chain uses the inherent noise `z_j` to enable pathwise gradient estimation, capturing multi-scale information with lower variance than ZO/RL while avoiding the full cost of BP.
3. **Zeroth-Order (ZO) estimators:** For all remaining time steps not covered by FO or HO, noise is injected directly into the model's parameters to construct ZO estimations. This ensures unbiasedness for all steps while maintaining computational efficiency by not caching intermediate activations.

**Optimizing `h` and `j`:**
- **Optimizing `h` (sub-chain length):** `h` is optimized to minimize an upper bound on the RLR estimator's variance under a memory budget constraint. The analysis considers the per-step variance and memory costs of HO (`V_h^2`, `B_h`) and ZO (`V_z^2`, `B_z`). The optimal `h*` is derived as a minimum of two terms, one constrained by memory and the other by variance reduction. Practical guidance suggests `h=2` as an effective balance.
- **Determining `j` (starting index):** The starting index `j` for the HO sub-chain is sampled from a categorical distribution `J(1, T-h)` based on the gradient norm `∥g_t∥` at each step, representing the importance of different steps.

**Diffusive Chain-of-Thought (DCoT):**
A novel prompt technique, DCoT, is proposed to complement RLR. DCoT capitalizes on the multi-scale (coarse-to-fine) generation process of DMs. It decomposes an original prompt into multi-scale prompts (coarse-level, mid-level, fine-level) corresponding to different stages of the diffusion process. By integrating DCoT, the RLR's HO sub-chain can be precisely targeted to focus gradient updates on specific time steps (scales) where generation deficiencies are identified, for example, by constraining the sampling range of `j`.

**Theoretical Analysis:**
The methodology includes rigorous theoretical analysis to support the RLR's design:
- **Bias of Truncated BP:** Proposition 6.1 formally demonstrates the structural bias of truncated BP by showing the non-zero difference between the true gradient expectation and the truncated estimator's expectation.
- **Variance Comparison:** Proposition 6.2 establishes that the variance of FO estimators is less than or equal to ZO estimators, implying HO's variance is also lower than ZO's due to its local BP structure.
- **Unbiasedness of RLR:** Theorem 6.3 formally proves that the RLR estimator, combining FO, HO, and ZO terms, is an unbiased estimator of the true gradient.
- **Convergence Rate:** Theorem 6.4 provides convergence guarantees for RLR, establishing a convergence rate dependent on the smoothness of the reward function and the variance of the RLR estimator.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical and empirical findings to support the effectiveness of the Recursive Likelihood Ratio (RLR) optimizer.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Bias of Truncated Backpropagation:** Proposition 6.1 formally proves that truncated BP estimators introduce a structural bias. This bias is quantified as the sum of unpropagated gradient terms from later diffusion steps, highlighting why truncated BP can lead to suboptimal optimization or model collapse, as observed in Figure 3.
- **Variance Comparison of Gradient Estimators:** Proposition 6.2 establishes a theoretical hierarchy for variance: First-Order (FO) estimators generally have lower or equal variance compared to Zeroth-Order (ZO) estimators. This implies that the Half-Order (HO) estimator, which incorporates local differentiation like FO, also exhibits lower variance than ZO. This theoretical backing justifies the RLR's design choice to incorporate FO and HO components to reduce overall variance.
- **Unbiasedness of RLR:** Theorem 6.3 rigorously demonstrates that the RLR estimator, by appropriately combining FO, HO (using the likelihood ratio trick), and ZO (using parameter perturbation), provides an unbiased estimate of the true gradient. This unbiasedness is a critical advantage over truncated BP, ensuring that the optimization direction aligns with the true objective.
- **Convergence Guarantees:** Theorem 6.4 provides a theoretical convergence rate for RLR, indicating that its optimization converges. The rate is dependent on the smoothness of the reward function and the variance of the RLR estimator, reinforcing that lower variance contributes to faster and more stable convergence.

#### 4.2. Empirical Findings

- **Text2Image Generation Performance:**
 - RLR consistently achieved higher reward scores across multiple human preference reward models (PickScore, HPSv2, AES, ImageReward) when fine-tuning Stable Diffusion 1.4 and 2.1.
 - For example, on SD 1.4, RLR improved PickScore from 16.24 (Base) to 20.14 and ImageReward from 32.74 (Base) to 75.65 (HPD v2 prompts). Similar improvements were observed for SD 2.1.
 - RLR outperformed RL-based (DDPO) and truncated-BP-based (Alignprop) methods. DDPO showed limited improvement, consistent with its high variance and low sample efficiency. Alignprop showed considerable improvement but suffered from structural bias that limited further gains and led to model collapse in later stages of training.

- **Sample Efficiency Analysis:**
 - Reward curves (Figure 5) illustrate RLR's superior sample efficiency and stability compared to baselines.
 - DDPO optimized rewards slowly, indicative of high variance.
 - Alignprop showed comparable performance to RLR in earlier phases but experienced severe model collapse in later phases, where RLR continued to improve.

- **Text2Video Generation Performance:**
 - RLR achieved the best overall performance on the VBench benchmark, surpassing various open-source and API-based Text2Video models (e.g., VideoCrafter, Pika, Gen-2, T2V-Turbo, DOODL, DDPO, VADER).
 - RLR obtained the highest weighted average score (84.63), notably exceeding VADER (83.45) and DDPO (80.78).
 - RLR demonstrated significant improvements in "Dynamic Degree" (70.69) and "Aesthetic Quality" (66.15), metrics that heavily influence perceived video quality.

- **Diffusive Chain-of-Thought (DCoT) Effectiveness:**
 - The proposed DCoT prompt technique, which converts original prompts into multi-scale prompts, proved effective.
 - Applying DCoT to the base SD1.4 model alone improved performance across all reward metrics (e.g., ImageReward from 33.45 to 39.24).
 - Combining DCoT with RLR (`RLR-DCoT`) yielded the best performance, further boosting scores (e.g., ImageReward from 43.09 for RLR w/o DCoT to 49.88), demonstrating a synergistic effect by enabling RLR's HO sub-chain to target specific scales of generation.

- **Ablation Study:**
 - Experiments on SD 1.4 verified the contribution of different RLR components (Table 5).
 - Removing both HO and ZO (reducing to single-step truncated BP) resulted in the worst performance.
 - Removing ZO (V2) or HO (V3) individually showed performance degradation compared to the full RLR. Notably, V2 (without ZO) performed better than V3 (without HO), indicating the importance of unbiasedness from the ZO components, even when the HO component is rearranged.
 - The full RLR optimizer achieved the highest reward scores, confirming the combined benefit of its structured FO, HO, and ZO components.

- **Memory and Time Complexity:**
 - RLR exhibited significantly lower memory consumption (22.4 GB VRAM, 0 GB System RAM) compared to Alignprop (25.4 GB VRAM, 78.5 GB System RAM), while being more memory-efficient than DDPO (12.4 GB) for its level of performance.
 - While DDPO had the lowest per-step time cost, RLR achieved the same performance target (e.g., AES = 5.4) in significantly fewer total steps (75 steps for RLR vs. 600 for DDPO), making it faster in overall training time. Alignprop had the highest per-step cost.
 - Ablations on the sub-chain length `h` (Table 9) showed diminishing performance gains for `h > 2`, with rapidly increasing memory and time costs, guiding the practical choice of `h=2`.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces the Recursive Likelihood Ratio (RLR) optimizer, offering a fine-tuning paradigm for diffusion models that addresses critical limitations of existing methods. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Resolution of Fundamental Fine-Tuning Challenges:** RLR directly tackles the persistent issues of bias in truncated backpropagation and high variance in reinforcement learning-based approaches. By providing an unbiased gradient estimator with demonstrably lower variance, RLR offers a more stable and reliable method for adapting pre-trained diffusion models to specific objectives and human preferences. This contributes to the robustness and predictability of fine-tuning outcomes, preventing common failure modes like model collapse.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** RLR strikes a balance between computational tractability and gradient accuracy. It avoids the prohibitive memory and computational costs of full backpropagation while offering better sample efficiency than pure reinforcement learning. This efficiency makes it more feasible to fine-tune large diffusion models in resource-constrained environments, broadening their practical deployment.
- **Principled Theoretical Foundation:** The rigorous theoretical analysis, including proofs of unbiasedness, variance comparisons, and convergence guarantees, provides a strong scientific foundation for RLR. This theoretical backing increases confidence in the method's efficacy and offers insights into the behavior of different gradient estimators in the context of recursive generative models.
- **Enhanced Multi-Scale Generation Control:** By strategically combining First-Order, Half-Order, and Zeroth-Order gradient estimation, RLR is better equipped to capture and leverage the multi-scale information inherent in the diffusion process. The Half-Order component, with its local backpropagation chain, allows for targeted gradient updates at specific levels of detail, which is crucial for achieving high-fidelity generation across both coarse structures and fine-grained features.
- **Synergistic Prompting Technique (DCoT):** The introduction of Diffusive Chain-of-Thought (DCoT) further enhances RLR's capabilities by enabling a more granular control over the generation process through multi-scale prompts. This innovative technique, designed to work naturally with RLR, suggests new avenues for user interaction and targeted content refinement, potentially leading to more precise and controllable generative AI.
- **Broad Applicability in Generative AI:** The empirical success of RLR on both Text2Image and Text2Video tasks, outperforming leading baselines, indicates its versatility. This method has the potential to be applied to other diffusion-based generative tasks and modalities, facilitating the alignment of a wider range of foundation models to diverse downstream applications and evolving user needs.

In summary, this work offers a significant advancement in the fine-tuning of diffusion models by providing a theoretically sound and empirically validated optimizer that is both efficient and effective. Its contributions lay the groundwork for more reliable and higher-quality content generation across various domains.
