# S4RL: Surprisingly Simple Self-Supervision for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> S4RL introduces a self-supervised approach that integrates state-based data augmentations directly into the Q-learning objective for offline reinforcement learning in robotics, enhancing Q-function approximation and reducing overfitting. This method improves the generalization and robustness of learned policies, achieving significant gains in performance and success rates on various continuous control and dexterous manipulation tasks, often outperforming baselines by up to 3 times.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) frequently suffers from distribution shift, where policies trained on static datasets encounter out-of-distribution state-action pairs, leading to overestimation bias and brittle policies.
- Neural networks parameterizing Q-functions in offline RL tend to overfit to static datasets, resulting in poor generalization and unstable value estimation in dynamic environments.
- The application of data augmentation for proprioceptive state-based observations in offline RL is largely unexplored, as typical image augmentations are often semantically irrelevant or physically unrealistic for such inputs.

## Method

- **S4RL** modifies the standard ==Q-learning== objective by averaging Q-values over multiple ==augmented versions== of a state, directly integrating ==data augmentation== to encourage a smoother state-action value function.
- Seven ==data augmentation== strategies are investigated for proprioceptive state information, including ==zero-mean Gaussian noise==, ==uniform noise==, ==random amplitude scaling==, ==dimension dropout==, ==state-switch==, ==state mix-up==, and ==adversarial state training==.
- The approach distills the benefits of the augmentation-enhanced value function into the policy improvement step, using the learned Q-networks without augmentations during policy training.

## Results

- S4RL variants, particularly with Gaussian noise (S4RL-N) and adversarial training (S4RL-Adv), consistently outperformed baseline CQL and BRAC agents across most D4RL continuous control and navigation tasks.
- The method achieved substantial improvements in policy success rates, up to approximately **20%** on average, over baseline CQL in complex dexterous manipulation environments (MetaWorld, RoboSuite), and up to **3 times** on specific tasks.
- S4RL agents maintained superior performance and robustness when trained on limited offline data (**5%**, **10%**, and **25%** of original datasets) compared to all baselines, indicating its utility under data scarcity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Local, physically plausible state-based data augmentations (e.g., Gaussian noise, adversarial perturbations, state mix-up) effectively promote robust function approximation and reduce overfitting in offline RL.
> - Augmentations that aggressively perturb or semantically alter proprioceptive states (e.g., dimension dropout, state-switch) can degrade performance by violating physical realism or omitting crucial information.
> - Integrating state-based data augmentation directly into the Q-learning objective is more effective for improving Q-function smoothness and generalization in offline RL compared to standard regularization techniques like Dropout or L2 regularization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically involves an agent learning through direct interaction with an environment. However, in many real-world scenarios, such as autonomous driving, medical applications, or industrial robotics, interacting with the physical environment can be costly, time-consuming, or dangerous. This limitation has led to the emergence of **Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL)**, also known as batch RL. Offline RL aims to learn effective policies from large, pre-collected datasets of past interactions, without requiring any further online interaction with the environment during training.

A significant challenge in Offline RL is the **distribution shift** problem. When a policy is trained solely on a static dataset, it may encounter state-action pairs during deployment that are not well-represented in the training data (out-of-distribution, or OOD). Current offline RL algorithms, particularly value-based methods like Q-learning, tend to overestimate the values of these OOD state-action pairs. This overestimation can lead to the learning of brittle policies that perform poorly when deployed in the real environment. Additionally, neural networks used to parameterize Q-functions can overfit to the static dataset, further exacerbating generalization issues.

To address these issues, prior research in Offline RL has explored several directions:
- **Constrained Policy Optimization:** Methods that enforce similarity between the learned policy and the behavior policy that collected the data, often using divergence measures like KL-divergence, MMD, or f-divergence.
- **Pessimism in Value Estimation:** Algorithms like Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) explicitly penalize OOD actions or push down the values of randomly sampled state-action distributions to mitigate overestimation.
- **Model-based Offline RL:** Learning a dynamics model from offline data to predict future states and rewards, which can then be used for planning or policy optimization.
- **Representation Learning:** Efforts to learn better representations from data, often involving self-supervision.

In parallel, **self-supervised representation learning** has shown promise in online RL, particularly for pixel-based observations. Techniques like CURL, RAD, and DrQ have demonstrated that applying data augmentations (e.g., rotations, translations, color jitters) to image observations can significantly improve performance by encouraging the learning of invariant and robust representations. These methods often employ contrastive learning or simpler approaches that average Q-values over augmented states.

However, the application of data augmentation in the context of *proprioceptive state-based* offline RL remains less explored. Proprioceptive states (e.g., joint angles, velocities) are fundamentally different from pixel data; generic image augmentations are semantically meaningless for such inputs, and inappropriate augmentations can violate physical realism or alter the reward landscape.

This work, S4RL, situates itself at the intersection of these challenges. It investigates the efficacy of applying various data augmentation schemes to *proprioceptive state observations* within the framework of offline Q-learning. By adapting self-supervision principles from pixel-based online RL to the offline setting for state-based inputs, the paper aims to develop a method that enhances function approximation and reduces overfitting, thereby improving the generalization and robustness of learned policies for robotic tasks. The "surprisingly simple" aspect refers to the direct application of data augmentation to the Q-learning objective without complex contrastive losses.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a simple, effective self-supervised offline reinforcement learning algorithm, named S4RL, that improves the performance of policies learned from static, pre-recorded robotic datasets.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key considerations:

1. **Addressing Overfitting and Poor Generalization in Offline RL:** Current offline RL algorithms frequently suffer from overfitting to the limited and static training data. This leads to policies that generalize poorly when deployed in dynamic, real-world environments, especially to state-action pairs not extensively covered in the offline dataset (out-of-distribution generalization). This is particularly problematic in applications where interactions are costly or dangerous, such as robotics, necessitating robust generalization from pre-collected data.

2. **Mitigating Overestimation Bias and Function Approximation Errors:** Many offline RL methods, especially those based on Q-learning, are susceptible to overestimating the true state-action values for OOD data. This overestimation, combined with the inherent function approximation challenges of neural networks used for Q-functions on a static distribution, contributes to unstable and suboptimal policy learning. Improving the stability and accuracy of Q-value estimation is crucial for learning effective policies.

3. **Investigating the Role of Data Augmentation for Proprioceptive States:** While data augmentation has shown significant benefits in pixel-based online RL by promoting robust representation learning, its utility and appropriate strategies for proprioceptive (state-based) observations in offline RL are largely unexplored. Standard image augmentations are often semantically irrelevant or physically unrealistic for state vectors (e.g., joint angles and velocities). The research is motivated to identify and validate augmentation strategies that are meaningful and beneficial for state-based robotic data.

4. **Enhancing Q-network Function Approximation through Smoothness:** The hypothesis is that by applying local data augmentations to states and integrating them into the Q-learning objective, the Q-network can be encouraged to learn a smoother state-action space. This smoothness implies that small perturbations to a given observation should not lead to drastically different Q-values. Such consistency can improve the Q-function's approximation capabilities, making it more robust and better generalized to unseen, but locally similar, states.

5. **Practical Application in Robotics:** The motivation extends to the practical deployment of RL in real-world robotics. Learning useful skills from extensive pre-collected robotic data without requiring further physical interaction is highly desirable for safety, efficiency, and scalability. Developing a robust offline RL method that can learn deployable policies from existing datasets would directly contribute to advancing robotic autonomy in contexts like manufacturing and manipulation.

In summary, the work aims to introduce a self-supervision mechanism via state-based data augmentation into offline Q-learning, intending to improve function approximation, reduce overfitting, and enhance the out-of-distribution generalization of learned policies, particularly for challenging robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The S4RL methodology integrates data augmentation directly into the Q-learning objective for offline reinforcement learning. The approach is designed to promote robust function approximation and reduce overfitting by encouraging smoothness in the learned state-action value function.

#### 3.1. Self-Supervision in Offline RL Framework

The core of S4RL involves modifying the standard Q-learning Bellman error minimization objective. For a given state $s_t$ from the static dataset $\mathcal{D}$, the method generates one or more augmented states, denoted as $\tilde{s}_t \sim T(s_t)$, where $T$ is a stochastic transformation.
The proposed objective function for updating the Q-network, $Q$, is:

$ \min_Q \mathbb{E}_{s_t,a_t \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ r_t + \gamma \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^{k} Q(T_i(\tilde{s}_{t+1}|s_{t+1}), a_{t+1}) - \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^{k} Q(T_i(\tilde{s}_t|s_t), a_t) \right] $

In this modified objective, instead of calculating the Q-value and target Q-value for the original state $s_t$, the algorithm averages these values over $k$ different augmentations of the same state. This averaging mechanism encourages consistency in Q-values across local perturbations of the state, thereby smoothening the learned state space. The reward function is assumed to be locally smooth, meaning $r(s_t) \approx r(\tilde{s}_t)$ for small perturbations. The policy improvement step, which trains the policy $\pi$ to maximize Q-values, uses the learned Q-networks without augmentations, distilling the benefits of the augmentation-enhanced value function.

#### 3.2. Data Augmentation Strategies

The paper investigates seven different data augmentation strategies specifically tailored for proprioceptive state information, acknowledging that transformations must preserve physical realizability and semantic meaning to be effective.

1. **Zero-mean Gaussian noise:** Adds noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma I)$ to the state: $\tilde{s}_t \leftarrow s_t + \epsilon$. The standard deviation $\sigma$ is a key hyperparameter controlling the perturbation magnitude.
2. **Zero-mean Uniform noise:** Adds noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{U}(-\alpha, \alpha)$ to the state: $\tilde{s}_t \leftarrow s_t + \epsilon$. The range $\alpha$ is a key hyperparameter.
3. **Random amplitude scaling:** Scales the state dimensions: $\tilde{s}_t \leftarrow s_t \cdot \epsilon$, where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{U}(\beta_1, \beta_2)$. This preserves the sign of the states.
4. **Dimension dropout:** Randomly zeroes out one dimension of the state vector: $\tilde{s}_t \leftarrow s_t \cdot \mathbf{1}'$, where $\mathbf{1}'$ is a vector of ones with one randomly selected element set to zero.
5. **State-switch:** Swaps the values of two randomly selected dimensions in the state. To maintain physical realizability, specific pairs of dimensions (e.g., joint angle with another joint angle, velocity with another velocity) are pre-defined for each environment.
6. **State mix-up:** Generates a new state as a convex combination of the current state and the next state in the trajectory: $\tilde{s}_t \leftarrow \lambda s_t + (1 - \lambda) s_{t+1}$, where $\lambda \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha, \alpha)$.
7. **Adversarial state training:** Perturbs the state in the direction that maximizes the Q-value deviation, within an $\epsilon$-ball: $\tilde{s}_t \leftarrow s_t + \epsilon \nabla_{s_t} J_Q(\pi(s_t))$. This aims to identify and address "weak spots" in the Q-function.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Experiments are conducted on the D4RL benchmark datasets, which include continuous control (e.g., Gym, AntMaze), navigation, and dexterous robotic manipulation tasks (Adroit, Franka Kitchen) with varying data collection policies (random, medium, expert, replay). Additional experiments use MetaWorld and RoboSuite environments for more complex robotic manipulation tasks.
- **Baselines:** The S4RL framework is evaluated by integrating it with two state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms: Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) and Behavior Regularized Actor Critic (BRAC). It is also compared against other self-supervised learning techniques adapted for state inputs: Contrastive Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning (CURL) and SAC+AutoEncoder (SAC+AE), with CQL regularization applied to these baselines.
- **Hyperparameters:** Base CQL hyperparameters are kept unchanged. For S4RL, 2 augmentations ($k=2$) are typically used. Specific noise parameters were set (e.g., $\sigma = 3 \times 10^{-4}$ for Gaussian, $\alpha = 3 \times 10^{-4}$ for Uniform, $\epsilon = 1 \times 10^{-4}$ for adversarial training).
- **Evaluations:** The performance is measured by mean normalized scores (D4RL) or percentage of goals reached (MetaWorld, RoboSuite) over multiple random seeds. Ablation studies investigate the impact of different augmentation schemes, the number of augmentations, and hyperparameter sensitivity. Experiments are also conducted on learning from limited data and with other Q-network regularization methods (Dropout, L2 regularization).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of self-supervision through state-based data augmentation in offline reinforcement learning.

#### 4.1. Role of Data Augmentations

An initial investigation into seven different data augmentation schemes revealed variations in their utility:
- **Effective Augmentations:** Zero-mean Gaussian noise (S4RL-N), zero-mean Uniform noise (S4RL-U), state mix-up (S4RL-MixUp), and adversarial state training (S4RL-Adv) consistently outperformed the baseline Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) agent. S4RL-Adv and S4RL-N exhibited the highest average rankings across the OpenAI Gym subset of D4RL tasks (average rankings of 1.83 and 1.92, respectively). These methods enabled the learning of effective policies even from data collected by a random policy (e.g., "walker-random" task, where base CQL failed) and improved performance on complex data distributions (e.g., "-medium-replay").
- **Ineffective Augmentations:** Dimension-Dropout and State-Switch generally resulted in performance worse than or comparable to the baseline CQL agent. This is attributed to their potential to omit crucial information or break physical realizability assumptions in proprioceptive state vectors, hindering the value function's ability to reason about the environment.

#### 4.2. Comparison to Other Self-Supervision Techniques

When compared against other self-supervised representation learning methods (CURL and SAC+AE, both augmented with Gaussian noise and integrated with CQL/BRAC), S4RL variants (S4RL-N, S4RL-MixUp, S4RL-Adv) demonstrated strong performance:
- **General Superiority:** S4RL-N and S4RL-Adv consistently outperformed the baselines on most D4RL tasks and were competitive in others, across both CQL and BRAC algorithms.
- **High-Dimensional States:** S4RL-Adv was particularly effective in high-dimensional state spaces, such as the Adroit dexterous manipulation environments and Franka robot tasks (Kitchen environment), often significantly outperforming all other baselines.
- **Challenging Environments:** S4RL agents maintained superior performance in complex environments requiring hierarchical control, such as the AntMaze tasks.
- **Generality:** The framework's ability to improve performance for both CQL and BRAC highlights its general applicability. On specific tasks like "pen-cloned" and "door-human," BRAC+S4RL agents learned useful skills where base CQL performed poorly, further underscoring the benefits of state-based augmentations.

#### 4.3. Dexterous Manipulation Environments

Experiments on MetaWorld and RoboSuite, which involve difficult robotic manipulation tasks, further demonstrated the efficacy of S4RL:
- **Significant Improvements:** S4RL agents, particularly S4RL-Adv, showed substantial improvements in policy success rates (up to approximately 20% on average) over baseline CQL across 7 different tasks in these domains.
- **Outperforming Behavior Policy:** S4RL-Adv was notably the only agent capable of outperforming the trained behavior policy on 3 of the 5 MetaWorld tasks, and significantly outperformed baselines by up to 3 times on tasks like "push-v1" and "pick-and-place-can."

#### 4.4. Learning with Limited Data

In scenarios with limited offline data (5%, 10%, and 25% of the original datasets), S4RL agents consistently maintained superior performance:
- **Robustness to Data Scarcity:** S4RL-N agents significantly outperformed all baselines across various data splits and data percentages on Hopper and Walker2d tasks, indicating that self-supervision through local state perturbations is particularly beneficial when data is scarce.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Q-network Regularization:** Standard regularization techniques like Monte Carlo Dropout and L2 regularization did not achieve the same level of performance improvement as S4RL-Adv, suggesting that explicit data augmentation for smoothness is more effective for the specific challenges in offline RL.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The performance of S4RL-N and S4RL-Adv was sensitive to the magnitude of perturbations. Large values for $\sigma$ (Gaussian noise) or $\epsilon$ (adversarial training) significantly reduced performance, indicating that only *local* perturbations are beneficial. The model performed best within a specific range of small perturbation magnitudes (e.g., $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$), supporting the assumption that large changes can lead to semantically different states in proprioceptive inputs.

In summary, the main findings indicate that specific, carefully chosen state-based data augmentations, particularly Gaussian noise and adversarial perturbations, when integrated simply into the Q-learning objective, can significantly improve the generalization, robustness, and overall performance of offline RL policies across a wide range of continuous control and robotic manipulation tasks, especially in challenging data distributions or limited data settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The S4RL research introduces a method that contributes to the field of Offline Reinforcement Learning, particularly for robotics, with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness in Offline RL:** The work demonstrates a simple yet effective approach to mitigate key challenges in offline RL, namely overfitting to static datasets and poor generalization to out-of-distribution data. By explicitly encouraging Q-value smoothness through local state perturbations, S4RL helps in learning more robust policies that can better adapt to variations in the environment during deployment. This is crucial for bridging the gap between learning from recorded data and performing effectively in the real world.

2. **Advancement in Robotic Learning from Offline Data:** For real-world robotics, where data collection and online interactions are often prohibitive due to costs, safety concerns, or time constraints, offline RL is an increasingly vital paradigm. S4RL's significant performance improvements on complex dexterous manipulation tasks (MetaWorld, RoboSuite) highlight its potential to enable the learning of sophisticated robotic skills from existing large-scale datasets. This can accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous robotic systems without the need for extensive, risky, or time-consuming online training.

3. **Simplicity and Broad Applicability:** The "Surprisingly Simple Self-Supervision" aspect of S4RL is a key strength. The method does not introduce complex auxiliary losses or architectural changes but rather modifies the standard Q-learning objective with straightforward data augmentations. This ease of implementation means S4RL can be readily integrated into existing and future offline RL algorithms based on Q-learning, making it a broadly applicable enhancement. Its demonstrated effectiveness across different base offline RL algorithms (CQL and BRAC) underscores its generality.

4. **Guidance for State-Based Data Augmentation:** The paper systematically investigates various data augmentation schemes for proprioceptive state inputs, distinguishing between effective and ineffective strategies. This provides valuable insights and empirical guidance for future research on representation learning in RL, especially for non-pixel data where augmentation is less straightforward than in computer vision. The finding that local, physically plausible perturbations are beneficial, while aggressive or semantically ambiguous ones are detrimental, informs the design of more appropriate augmentation strategies for continuous state spaces.

5. **Improved Performance with Limited Data:** The demonstration that S4RL performs consistently well even when trained on limited subsets of offline data suggests its utility in scenarios where extensive datasets are unavailable. This can reduce the data collection burden and make offline RL more accessible for new robotic tasks or environments where data is naturally scarce.

In summary, S4RL contributes to making offline policy learning more competitive and practical for robotics. By providing a method to learn more generalizable and robust policies from past data, it moves closer to enabling reliable deployment of learned robotic skills, with potential impacts on automation, manufacturing, and other domains requiring intelligent agents. The insights into effective state-based augmentations also lay a foundation for further advancements in self-supervised learning for continuous control.
