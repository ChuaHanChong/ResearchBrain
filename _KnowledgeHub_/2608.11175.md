# Risk-Aware Kinodynamic Motion Planning Under Uncertainty For Safe Navigation on Planetary Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage risk-aware kinodynamic planner for planetary rovers. A neural residual-dynamics model is uncertainty-quantified via conformal prediction, AO-RRT samples a dynamically feasible tree whose edge cost is closed-loop CVaR of collision-constraint violation, then sequential convex programming refines control effort and CVaR jointly. Joint collision risk drops from 0.94 to 6e-3, about 97%, in simulation and on a Leo rover.

## Problem

- Learned terrain-interaction dynamics carry unquantified model error, so planners built on them produce risky trajectories that can cause hazardous operations or mission failure.
- Prior risk-aware planners handle sensor and environment uncertainty geometrically, ignoring kinodynamic feasibility and the uncertainty inside the learned dynamics model itself.
- Two-step methods that track a risk-neutral geometric plan with a CVaR-constrained controller cannot escape a high-risk homotopy chosen without any risk awareness.

## Method

- **Residual dynamics** learned as a ==Lipschitz neural network== times control, with ==conformal prediction== at 95% confidence yielding a bounded grid of linear and angular acceleration errors.
- **AO-RRT** edge cost is closed-loop ==CVaR==: each edge reference is tracked over a horizon under every gridded disturbance, JAX-vectorized, scored against a risk-inflated ==signed distance field==.
- **SCP refinement** linearizes dynamics and a softmax-aggregated ==RiskMap==, casting ==CVaR== via the ==Rockafellar-Uryasev epigraph== with exact-l1 slacks, solved iteratively with the ==CLARABEL== conic solver.

## Results

- Risk-neutral AO-RRT plans between two high-uncertainty regions at joint collision risk **0.94**; SCP refinement within that homotopy cuts it to **0.29**, about **69%**, at control cost **18.6**.
- Risk-aware AO-RRT routes near low-uncertainty obstacles and, after SCP, reaches joint collision risk **6e-3**, about **97%** lower, at comparable control cost **18.4**.
- Hardware validation on a Leo rover with stereo camera and Jetson Orin on granular terrain: a contraction controller with slip-adjusted feedforward velocities tracks plans at **6cm** RMS error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Risk must enter at the sampling stage: convex refinement is confined to the initial homotopy, so a risk-blind seed leaves most of the achievable risk reduction unreachable.
> - Conformal prediction converts a learned dynamics residual into a finite disturbance ensemble, letting sampled rollouts estimate CVaR without assuming a parametric noise model.
> - Large risk reductions came at essentially unchanged control cost, indicating safety and effort are often not competing objectives when uncertainty is spatially heterogeneous.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous space exploration requires robotic agents to operate in environments where interaction dynamics, such as terrain mechanics for wheeled robots, may be partially unknown or subject to significant variability. Learning these interactions, while crucial for advanced autonomy, introduces uncertainties that can lead to hazardous operations, mission failures, or overly conservative motion plans. Additionally, perception systems, which are fundamental for environmental understanding, also contribute to overall uncertainty.

The broader research landscape in robotics addresses motion planning under uncertainty through various approaches. Previous studies have explored generating risk-aware geometric trajectories by considering uncertainties in the environment and sensor models. Some methodologies have employed two-step processes, such as tracking a risk-neutral geometric plan with a controller constrained by Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). However, these approaches often either focus on geometric planning rather than kinodynamic feasibility, or do not explicitly quantify and integrate the uncertainty arising from learned dynamic models into the initial trajectory generation process.

This work addresses a specific gap by focusing on quantifying and incorporating uncertainty from learned dynamics models directly into a kinodynamic motion planner. It combines state-of-the-art techniques for uncertainty quantification (conformal prediction), asymptotically optimal sampling-based planning (AO-RRT), and trajectory optimization (sequential convex programming) to generate trajectories that are both dynamically feasible and explicitly risk-aware. The application context of planetary environments highlights the critical need for robust and safe navigation solutions where the consequences of failure are substantial and recovery options are limited. By addressing these combined sources of uncertainty, the research contributes to enhancing the reliability and safety of autonomous robotic operations in extreme and unpredictable settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a method for cost-optimal kinodynamic motion planning that explicitly accounts for risk under uncertainty, particularly for robotic agents operating in planetary environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several critical challenges inherent in autonomous space exploration:

- **Environmental Uncertainty:** Planetary environments are characterized by unknown or poorly understood terrain mechanics and variable surface conditions. Robotic agents must navigate these environments, but interactions (e.g., wheel-terrain contact) are often difficult to model precisely. Relying solely on nominal models without accounting for these variabilities can lead to inaccurate predictions of robot behavior.
- **Uncertainty in Learned Dynamics:** To cope with complex, unmodeled interactions, robots increasingly use learning-based dynamics models. While these models can approximate complex behaviors, their predictions inherently carry uncertainty. If this uncertainty is not quantified and integrated into the planning process, the resulting motion plans may be based on potentially inaccurate or overly optimistic dynamic predictions, increasing the risk of deviation from intended paths or unexpected collisions.
- **Perception System Uncertainty:** Sensory inputs, essential for constructing environmental maps and localizing the robot, are also subject to noise and inaccuracies. These perception-induced uncertainties can exacerbate the problem of safe navigation by misrepresenting obstacle locations or terrain features.
- **Consequences of Failure:** In space exploration, mission failures due to unsafe motion plans can result in the loss of expensive hardware, critical data, or even the entire mission. Therefore, ensuring high levels of safety and reliability is paramount. Existing planning methods that do not explicitly account for these compounded uncertainties may generate plans that appear optimal in a risk-neutral sense but are inherently hazardous in practice.

To address these motivations, the authors define three specific contributions and objectives:

1. **Quantify Uncertainty in Learning-Based Dynamics:** The first objective is to develop a method to rigorously quantify the uncertainty associated with the output of a learning-based dynamics model. This is achieved using conformal prediction, providing probabilistic bounds on prediction errors.
2. **Formulate Risk-Aware AO-RRT:** The second objective is to integrate this quantified dynamics uncertainty, alongside obstacle uncertainty, into an Asymptotically Optimal Rapidly Exploring Random Trees (AO-RRT) planner. This involves reformulating the AO-RRT cost function to incorporate a closed-loop risk computation, specifically using Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR), to generate an initial, dynamically feasible, and risk-aware trajectory.
3. **Optimize with Sequential Convex Programming (SCP):** The third objective is to refine the initial AO-RRT trajectory using Sequential Convex Programming (SCP). This involves casting the nonlinear motion planning problem as a series of locally convex subproblems, optimizing for both CVaR and control cost within the homotopy defined by the initial AO-RRT solution, while ensuring adherence to nonlinear dynamics and trajectory smoothness.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a robust framework for autonomous navigation that minimizes the likelihood of mission-critical failures in highly uncertain and hazardous extraterrestrial environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology employs a two-step approach to achieve risk-aware kinodynamic motion planning: an initial trajectory generation phase using a modified sampling-based planner (AO-RRT) followed by a trajectory refinement phase using sequential convex programming (SCP).

#### 3.1. Dynamics Model and Uncertainty Quantification

The system dynamics are modeled as `ẋ = f(x, u, t) + d(x, u)`, where `x` is the system state, `u` is the control input, and `d(x, u)` represents an unmodeled dynamics residue term. For the wheeled robot (a first-order velocity-controlled model), states include linear and angular velocities (`v = [vx, ω]`), and controls are `u = [vcmd, ωcmd]`. The unmodeled residue `d(x, u)` is approximated using a Lipschitz-continuous neural network `Φ(v)u`.

To quantify the uncertainty of the neural network's output, **conformal prediction** is applied. This method computes error bounds between the predicted residual dynamics and ground truth measurements (obtained via a motion capture system). The output is a set `M` representing minimum and maximum error ranges (`lmin/max`, `emin/max`) in linear and angular acceleration residual terms, calculated at a 95% confidence level. This set `M` is subsequently used to sample disturbances during planning.

#### 3.2. Uncertain Obstacle Space and Risk Map

Environmental risk is quantified by defining a risk field over the robot's workspace. For each obstacle `i`, with a specified boundary-uncertainty scale `σi` and signed distance `di(x)` from its nominal boundary, a per-obstacle risk `ri(x) = exp(-di(x)^2 / 2σi^2)` is calculated. These individual risks are then fused into a single `RiskMap(x) = 1 - Π(1 - ri(x))`, which is bounded in and increases as risk accumulates from multiple nearby obstacles.

Additionally, a **Risk-Inflated Signed Distance Field (RiskSDF)** is created for use as a soft risk penalty in the AO-RRT. This involves inflating each obstacle's signed distance field `sdfi(x)` outward by a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) margin, `Fi(x) = sdfi(x) - κ(α)σi`. Here, `κ(α)` is the CVaR multiplier for a standard normal distribution at a given tail probability `α`. The `RiskSDF(x)` is then defined as the minimum `Fi(x)` across all obstacles.

#### 3.3. AO-RRT Implementation for Initial Trajectory

An Asymptotically Optimal Rapidly Exploring Random Trees (AO-RRT) algorithm is employed to generate an initial, dynamically feasible plan. The nodes of the tree represent robot states `x = [x, y, θ, vx, ω]`. Tree extension involves applying randomly sampled control inputs `u = [vcmd, ωcmd]`.

A key aspect of this implementation is the **closed-loop risk computation**. To account for uncertainty in residual dynamics, each open-loop reference edge generated by the AO-RRT is tracked in a closed loop over a horizon of `nt` steps. At each step, a controller computes tracking control, and the robot's state is advanced under disturbed dynamics, with the disturbances drawn from the uncertainty set `M` obtained via conformal prediction. This process is vectorized across all `M` disturbances.

The **CVaR of soft collision-constraint violation** is empirically estimated across this ensemble of disturbed trajectories. This is defined as the average violation over the `α`-fraction of worst-case scenarios, specifically considering violations against the `RiskSDF`. This CVaR penalty is integrated as the AO-RRT edge cost, effectively accounting for both dynamics uncertainty and obstacle position uncertainty in a single cost metric. The time-consistent nature of risk propagation in the AO-RRT tree is noted.

#### 3.4. Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) for Trajectory Refinement

The trajectory produced by the AO-RRT is dynamically feasible but may lack smoothness in states and controls. To address this, Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) is used for refinement. SCP iteratively linearizes the nonlinear motion planning problem around the current trajectory estimate and solves a convex subproblem within a trust region.

**Pointwise Terrain Risk Aggregation:** For SCP, the pointwise `RiskMap` values along the tracked sub-steps are aggregated for each state `k` and disturbance `m` using a log-sum-exp formulation: `Rk,m = (1/β) log(Σi exp(β RiskMap(x(i)k,m)))`.

**CVaR Optimization Formulation:** Risk optimization is formulated as an objective using the Rockafellar-Uryasev CVaR epigraph: `CVaRα(Rk) = min(τk + (1/αM)Σm ηk,m)`. Slack variables `ηk,m ≥ 0` are introduced, constrained by `ηk,m ≥ R̃k,m + gsk,m xk + guk,m uk - τk`. The terms `gsk,m` and `guk,m` represent the gradients of the risk map with respect to the state and control, respectively, computed using automatic differentiation.

**Convex Objective and Constraints:** The SCP problem minimizes a weighted sum of several terms: control effort, the CVaR epigraph (representing risk), terminal and defect L1 penalties (for state deviations and dynamics constraint violations), an SDF buffer, and arc length. The decision variables include states, controls, CVaR auxiliary variables (`τ`, `η`), and various slack variables.

The problem is subject to several affine constraints for each state `k` and disturbance scenario `m`:
- Initial and terminal state constraints (with L1 slack for terminal state).
- Linearized dynamics: `xk+1 = dk + Akxk + Bkuk + ν+k - ν-k`, where `Ak` and `Bk` are Jacobians of the true one-edge rollout function `f_edge`.
- SDF clearance: `∇kpk ≥ dsafe - hbar_k + ∇k pbar_k - σk` (ensuring a minimum safe distance from obstacles, with `σk` as a buffer slack).
- Control and state bounds.
- Trust region constraints: `|xk - xbar_k| ≤ ∆x` and `|uk - ubar_k| ≤ ∆u`, which limit the step size in each iteration.

The convex subproblem is solved iteratively using the CLARABEL solver until convergence criteria are met. This iterative refinement process starts with the AO-RRT trajectory as an initial solution, ensuring the SCP solution remains within the same homotopy while optimizing for smoothness, control effort, and CVaR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both simulation and hardware experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed risk-aware motion planning framework. Trajectory-level risk (`Rtraj`) is quantified as `1 - Π(1 - RiskMap(xk))` across the discretized trajectory nodes.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (Comparison of Risk-Aware vs. Risk-Neutral AO-RRT)

To isolate the impact of the risk-aware cost, comparative experiments were conducted with and without its inclusion in the AO-RRT objective:

- **AO-RRT without Risk Cost (Risk-Neutral):**
 - The AO-RRT, when minimizing only control cost, generated an initial trajectory that traversed directly between high-risk regions. This resulted in a high joint collision risk of 0.94.
 - Subsequent SCP refinement was able to reduce this risk to 0.29. This represents an approximately 69% reduction in joint collision risk compared to the initial risk-neutral AO-RRT solution. The control cost for this refined trajectory was 18.6 units. This demonstrates that SCP can refine trajectories and reduce risk even when starting from an initial solution in a high-risk homotopy, though it cannot escape it completely. The initial state without SCP would have been considered almost certain to collide.

- **AO-RRT with Risk Cost (Risk-Aware):**
 - When the risk-aware cost was active in the AO-RRT objective, the planner generated an initial trajectory that strategically navigated through areas of lower uncertainty and risk, avoiding high-risk regions.
 - The SCP refinement further optimized this trajectory, resulting in a joint collision risk of 6 × 10⁻³ (0.006). This signifies a substantial reduction in risk. Relative to the risk-neutral AO-RRT solution after SCP refinement (0.29), the risk-aware approach achieved approximately 97% less risk (0.29 -> 0.006). When compared to the initial risk-neutral AO-RRT solution (0.94), the reduction is even more pronounced.
 - The control cost for this risk-aware and refined trajectory was 18.4 units, which is comparable to the control cost of the risk-neutral trajectory after SCP refinement (18.6 units). This suggests that significant risk reduction was achieved without incurring a substantially higher control effort.

The results indicate that the risk-aware cost in AO-RRT acts as a user-settable parameter, allowing for a trade-off between generating an initial solution with higher risk-awareness (lower risk) and its potential impact on control cost.

#### 4.2. Hardware Experiments (Leo Rover)

The proposed approach was validated through hardware experiments conducted on a Leo rover. The rover was equipped with a ZED 2i stereo camera and a Jetson Orin AGX, and experiments were performed on granular terrain.

- **Experimental Procedure:** The process involved constructing a point cloud of the obstacle-rich environment, identifying obstacles, allocating uncertainties to them, and then constructing a comprehensive risk map. Subsequently, the integrated planner was used to generate a dynamically feasible trajectory. This trajectory was then tracked by a contraction-based controller, assuming unicycle dynamics, utilizing pose estimates from a Vicon motion capture system.
- **Tracking Performance:** Despite the presence of terrain-induced wheel slip, the contraction controller, which used slip-adjusted feedforward velocities, was able to track the generated trajectory with an Root Mean Square (RMS) error of 6 cm. This demonstrates the robustness of the tracking control system in dealing with real-world disturbances.
- **Visual Demonstration:** Figure 2 visually supports the simulation findings, illustrating that the trajectory generated with the risk-aware cost navigates through regions with demonstrably lower joint collision risk compared to the trajectory generated without it, which approaches the uncertain obstacle boundaries more closely.

In summary, the results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively generates trajectories that are significantly safer under uncertainty, both in simulation and on physical hardware, while maintaining comparable control costs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the field of autonomous navigation, particularly for applications in uncertain and hazardous environments like planetary surfaces. The potential impacts span improved mission safety, enhanced robotic autonomy, and advancements in motion planning methodologies.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Mission Safety for Space Exploration

The most direct impact is the provision of a demonstrably safer and more reliable motion planning solution for planetary and lunar robotics. By explicitly quantifying and managing uncertainty from both learned dynamics and environmental obstacles through CVaR, the method aims to reduce the likelihood of mission-critical failures. The demonstrated reduction in joint collision risk by over 97% across trajectories, while maintaining comparable control costs, is a critical factor for missions where hardware is irreplaceable and operational windows are limited. This directly contributes to increasing the success rate of autonomous space exploration missions.

#### 5.2. Advancements in Risk-Aware Autonomy

The work pushes the boundaries of risk-aware autonomy by integrating several advanced techniques:
- **Conformal Prediction for Dynamics Uncertainty:** The novel application of conformal prediction to quantify uncertainty in learning-based dynamics models provides a statistically rigorous approach to understanding the reliability of learned behaviors. This allows planners to account for the "known unknowns" of learned models more effectively.
- **Integrated Risk in Kinodynamic Planning:** Incorporating closed-loop risk computation (CVaR) directly into an asymptotically optimal sampling-based planner (AO-RRT) allows for the generation of initial trajectories that are inherently risk-averse and dynamically feasible, moving beyond risk-neutral or purely geometric approaches.
- **Robust Trajectory Optimization:** The use of Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) for refining these risk-aware trajectories, jointly optimizing for control cost and CVaR while adhering to nonlinear dynamics and trust region constraints, ensures that the final executed trajectory is smooth, efficient, and retains its safety guarantees.

#### 5.3. Practical Applicability and Validation

The validation of the methodology through hardware experiments on a Leo rover operating on granular terrain underscores its practical applicability. Demonstrating successful trajectory tracking despite real-world disturbances like wheel slip, with a low RMS error, indicates the robustness of the system beyond theoretical simulations. This practical verification increases confidence in the deployment of such systems in actual planetary missions.

#### 5.4. Foundations for Future Research

This work lays a foundation for future research in several areas:
- **Adaptive Risk-Awareness:** The user-settable parameter for risk-aware cost in AO-RRT suggests avenues for developing adaptive systems that can dynamically adjust risk tolerance based on mission phase, terrain conditions, or available resources.
- **Complex Dynamics and Perception:** The framework could be extended to more complex robot dynamics, larger-scale uncertainty in perception (e.g., dynamic obstacles), and multi-robot coordination under uncertainty.
- **Real-time Implementation:** Further optimization for real-time performance on resource-constrained platforms would enhance its operational utility.

In conclusion, this research provides a comprehensive and validated approach to a critical problem in autonomous robotics. By enabling safer and more reliable navigation in highly uncertain planetary environments, it contributes significantly to the long-term success and expanded capabilities of space exploration endeavors.
