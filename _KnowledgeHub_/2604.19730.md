# FASTER: Value-Guided Sampling for Fast RL

> [!summary] Summary
> FASTER is a framework that improves the computational efficiency of diffusion-based reinforcement learning policies by filtering unpromising action candidates at the initial noise level. It achieves up to an 8x reduction in inference FLOPs and a 4.5x speedup in training time, enabling large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to maintain comparable task success rates to standard best-of-N sampling with significantly lower overhead.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly those using generative diffusion models, achieve high performance through 'best-of-N' test-time scaling, which involves fully denoising multiple action candidates.
- This multi-candidate denoising process is computationally expensive, creating a bottleneck for real-time applications and large models like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models due to increased latency and resource demands.
- Existing solutions, such as distillation, can be unstable due to non-stationary training targets or introduce additional training complexity and costs.

## Method

- **FASTER** models the denoising and selection of action candidates as a ==Markov Decision Process== where unpromising candidates are progressively filtered at each denoising step.
- A lightweight ==noise-level critic (Q_dn)== is learned to predict the expected return of denoising a specific initial noise sample, allowing for ==early filtering of candidates==.
- During inference, multiple initial noise candidates are sampled, scored by Q_dn, and only the highest-scoring candidate is fully denoised by the base ==diffusion policy==, significantly reducing computational cost.

## Results

- **FASTER-EXPO** consistently achieved top performance across challenging manipulation tasks in both online and batch-online settings, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art baselines.
- The framework delivered substantial computational savings: **8x reduction** in inference FLOPs, up to **4.5x reduction** in training update time, and **1.7x decrease** in inference latency, while maintaining comparable task success rates.
- FASTER was successfully applied to a **3.3B-parameter VLA** model, enabling efficient online RL fine-tuning with **8x less compute** for inference while matching base model performance on most held-out tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The performance gains observed from 'best-of-N' action selection in diffusion policies are largely determined by variance present at the initial noise level.
> - It is possible to effectively predict the downstream value of a fully denoised action directly from its initial noise representation using a specialized, lightweight critic.
> - A simplified single-step filtering approach at the initial noise level is sufficient for achieving significant computational benefits without sacrificing performance, validating the practical implementation over a full multi-step MDP.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms employing expressive policy backbones, such as diffusion models, have demonstrated substantial performance in various domains, including robotics and image/video generation. However, a prevalent challenge in these high-performing methods is their computational expense, particularly at test-time. This expense often stems from "test-time scaling" techniques, which involve sampling multiple action candidates and selecting the most promising one, frequently based on a learned value function. This approach, while effective in enhancing performance, incurs significant computational costs by requiring the full denoising of each candidate action. Such computational demands become a critical bottleneck for large models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, where latency and resource constraints are crucial considerations.

Prior efforts to address this issue include distillation-based approaches, which attempt to amortize costs by training a separate policy to directly reproduce high-value behaviors. However, these methods can be expensive due to the need for additional policy training and can suffer from instability because the distilled policy must continuously adapt to a changing target defined by the Q-function. Other related research explores various aspects of test-time scaling, value-guided sampling for diffusion policies, and methods that operate in the initial noise or latent space of diffusion models to improve generation quality or reduce inference cost. This paper positions itself within this landscape by seeking to retain the performance benefits of multi-sample action selection while fundamentally reducing the computational overhead, specifically by intervening earlier in the generative process. It builds upon and contrasts with methods like EXPO and IDQL, which rely on best-of-N sampling, and addresses the limitations of approaches that perform full denoising or struggle with the instability of distillation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that can recover the performance benefits of sampling-based test-time scaling for diffusion-based policies without incurring the associated computational cost.

The motivation for this work arises from several key observations and challenges in modern reinforcement learning:

- **Prohibitive Computational Cost of Test-Time Scaling:** State-of-the-art RL algorithms, especially those leveraging generative models like diffusion policies, achieve high performance by sampling multiple candidate actions and selecting the best one (e.g., using a Q-function). This "best-of-N" approach necessitates denoising all N candidates, which is computationally intensive. For instance, a modest sample count of eight can multiply inference costs, making these methods impractical in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained environments.
- **Challenges with Large Models:** The computational burden is particularly acute for large models, such as modern Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which already operate close to acceptable latency limits. Multiplying their inference costs further limits their real-world applicability.
- **Inefficiency of Full Denoising:** The core difficulty lies in predicting which candidate actions will yield high value without observing their fully denoised forms. Current methods typically denoise all candidates fully before evaluating them, leading to redundant computation for discarded samples.
- **Limitations of Existing Solutions:** While distillation-based methods aim to amortize this cost, they require training a separate policy, which can be expensive and prone to instability due to a non-stationary training target. Other noise-space methods focus on optimizing initial noise but do not typically integrate with value-guided action filtering within an RL framework.

The key insight motivating FASTER is that the sample variance driving performance gains from best-of-N selection can be traced back to the early stages of the denoising process, often to the initial noise itself. This insight suggests that it might be possible to filter out unpromising action candidates much earlier, before the computationally expensive full denoising process is complete. By framing the denoising and selection of multiple candidates as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), the authors aim to learn a policy and value function in the denoising space that can progressively filter candidates based on their predicted downstream value, thereby maximizing returns while minimizing computational overhead. The goal is to create a lightweight, modular approach that can be integrated into existing generative RL algorithms, decoupling the benefits of broad action sample selection from the cost of full policy execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FASTER (Value-Guided Sampling for Fast RL) is a framework designed to reduce the computational cost of sampling-based test-time scaling in generative RL algorithms by filtering action candidates early in the denoising process. The core idea involves modeling the denoising and selection process as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).

#### 3.1. Modeling Action Denoising as an MDP

The process of denoising multiple action candidates and selecting the best one is conceptualized as a filtering MDP. This MDP aims to progressively discard unpromising candidates at each denoising step.

- **States:** A state in this filtering MDP is defined by `s_t = (s, t, C_t, {a^(t)_i}_i∈C_t)`, comprising:
 - The environment state `s`.
 - The current denoising timestep `t` (ranging from `T` for pure noise to `1` for a clean action).
 - The set `C_t` of surviving candidate indices.
 - The partially denoised intermediate actions `{a^(t)_i}` for each candidate `i` in `C_t`.
- **Actions:** At each step `t`, the policy decides to retain (`m_t,i = 1`) or discard (`m_t,i = 0`) each candidate `i` in `C_t`. A constraint is that at least one candidate must be retained (`Σ_i m_t,i ≥ 1`). The set of surviving candidates for the next step becomes `C_(t-1) = {i : m_t,i = 1}`.
- **Transitions:** A denoising step is applied to each retained candidate to produce `a^(t-1)_i`. The filtering episode terminates under two conditions:
 - When only a single candidate remains (`|C_(t-1)| = 1`). In this case, the remaining candidate is fully denoised to `t=1`.
 - When the full denoising process completes (`t = 1`). At this point, the highest-scoring survivor (if multiple remain) is executed.
- **Reward:** Non-terminal filtering steps yield a reward of zero. Upon termination, the reward is `Q_a(s, a_i*)`, where `a_i*` is the final executed action obtained from the surviving candidate `i*` and `Q_a` is the standard action-space critic. This external Q-value serves to propagate outcome information back to the denoising space without needing direct labels for every candidate.

#### 3.2. Learning the Filtering Policy

To learn the filtering policy within this MDP, an action-value function, `Q_dn(s_t, m_t)`, is learned. This function estimates the expected return of taking filtering action `m_t` in state `s_t`.
Training `Q_dn` employs a temporal-difference (TD) objective:
`L_TD = E_((s_t,m_t,s_(t-1))) [ (Q_dn(s_t, m_t) - (r_t + γ max_(m_(t-1)) Q̄_dn(s_(t-1), m_(t-1))))^2 ]`
where `r_t` is the reward defined in the MDP, and `γ` is the discount factor.

At test time, `N` initial noise candidates `{ϵ_i}` are sampled. The filtering policy, induced by `Q_dn`, is repeatedly applied to select filtering actions `m_t` until only a single candidate remains. This surviving candidate `ϵ_i*` is then fully denoised by the base policy `π_θ` to produce the final action `a_i* = π_θ(s, ϵ_i*)`.

#### 3.3. Practical Implementation (Single-Step Filtering)

For practical efficiency, the full multi-step MDP is simplified to a single decision point: filtering only occurs at `t=T` (the initial noise level).
- **Simplified State and Action:** The state collapses to `(s, ϵ)` (environment state and initial noise sample). The filtering "action" becomes the selection of a single candidate `ϵ_i*` from the `N` initial noise samples.
- **Noise-Level Critic:** A noise-level critic `Q_dn(s, ϵ)` is learned to predict the expected return of denoising a specific noise sample `ϵ` from environment state `s`.
- **Supervision:** The `Q_dn` is supervised by regressing its output to the `Q_a(s, a)` value of the action `a_i*` that results from denoising the highest-scoring noise sample `ϵ_i*`.
 `L_reg = (Q_dn(s, ϵ_i*) - sg[Q_a(s, a_i*)])^2 / 2`
 (`sg` denotes stop-gradient).
- **Inference:** `N` noise candidates are sampled, `Q_dn` scores each `(s, ϵ_i)`, the highest-scoring `ϵ_i*` is selected, and only this `ϵ_i*` is denoised by `π_θ` to produce the final action `a_i*`.
The learning and inference procedures are detailed in Algorithms 1 and 2 in the paper.

FASTER is integrated with existing sampling-based RL methods:
- **FASTER-EXPO:** Applied to EXPO, where `a_i*` serves as the base-policy proposal, and EXPO's subsequent edit step follows.
- **FASTER-IDQL:** Applied to IDQL, where `a_i*` is executed directly.

#### 3.4. Computational Profile

The computational advantage of FASTER over standard best-of-N sampling stems from replacing `N` full denoising rollouts with `N` lightweight noise-level critic evaluations and a single denoising rollout.
- **Best-of-N Cost (`C_BoN`):** `T * N * F_actor + N * F_critic` (where `T` is denoising steps, `N` is candidates, `F_actor` and `F_critic` are FLOPs for single actor/critic passes).
- **FASTER Cost (`C_FASTER`):** `N * F_critic + T * F_actor`.
FASTER reduces the actor cost from `O(T N)` to `O(T)`. In scenarios where `T = N` and `F_actor = F_critic`, this results in an approximate `T/2` speedup. This reduction is significant because `T` typically ranges from 5 to 20, and `N` from 8. For large VLA models, the `Q_dn` critic can be significantly smaller than the actor, making the `N` critic evaluations add negligible overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to answer four core questions regarding FASTER's performance, computational efficiency, applicability to VLAs, and component importance.

**Q1: How does FASTER perform compared to state-of-the-art RL methods in both the online setting and the batch-online setting?**
- **Online Setting (Figure 3, top):** FASTER-EXPO consistently achieved the strongest overall performance across challenging long-horizon manipulation tasks (Lift, Can, Square, Tool Hang) compared to baselines like EXPO, RLPD, QAM, FQL, and DSRL. It significantly outperformed highly sample-efficient methods like RLPD and methods relying on action gradients (QSM, QAM).
- **Batch-Online Setting (Figure 5, top):** In this setting, FASTER-EXPO matched the performance of EXPO when compared by training iterations. This indicates that FASTER maintains the performance capabilities of its base algorithm while introducing computational efficiencies.

**Q2: Is FASTER able to recover the performance gains of test-time scaling while reducing the computational cost?**
- **Performance Recovery:** Both FASTER-EXPO and FASTER-IDQL achieved task success rates comparable to their unfiltered counterparts (EXPO and IDQL, respectively) across the evaluation suite in the online setting (Figure 3, top; Figure 4, top). This finding suggests that the noise-level critic effectively captures the signal for high-quality action candidates at the initial noise stage.
- **Computational Cost Reduction:** FASTER significantly reduced computational costs.
 - **Inference:** Inference-time actor forward passes were reduced from `O(T N)` to `O(T)`. For FASTER-EXPO, inference latency decreased from 566 ms to 335 ms per action (a 1.7x speedup). For FASTER-IDQL, inference FLOPs were reduced by 8x (Figure 4, left).
 - **Training:** The per-step update time for FASTER-EXPO was reduced by approximately 4.5x (from 11.6 s to 2.5 s) compared to EXPO (Figure 6, left).
 - **FLOPs:** FASTER-EXPO required 4.70 × 10^12 inference FLOPs per step, an 8x reduction compared to EXPO’s 3.75 × 10^13 (Figure 6, right).
These results demonstrate that FASTER can recover performance gains while substantially lowering the computational overhead by filtering at the noise level.

**Q3: Can FASTER be applied to a pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model?**
- FASTER-EXPO was successfully applied to a 3.3B-parameter `pi05_libero` VLA model, fine-tuned on 5 held-out `libero_90` tasks.
- **Compute Savings:** For the VLA, FASTER-EXPO reduced the per-step update time by 4.5x and inference latency by 1.7x. The FLOP reduction for inference was 8x. This is particularly notable because the noise-level critic (20M parameters) was much smaller than the VLA actor (3.3B parameters), leading to highly favorable computational asymmetry.
- **Task Performance:** Despite significant compute reductions, FASTER-EXPO matched EXPO's performance on the majority of the 5 VLA tasks (Figure 7, top), achieving comparable aggregate success rates. This indicates FASTER's scalability to large, pretrained models, enabling efficient online RL fine-tuning without sacrificing performance benefits of sampling.

**Q4: What components of FASTER are most important for performance?**
Ablation studies explored the impact of the denoise critic's size, the filtering step, and the full MDP formulation:
- **Denoise Critic Size (Figure 8):** Performance remained consistent across critic sizes ranging from 0.25x to 1.0x that of the action critic (`Q_a`). This suggests FASTER is robust to variations in critic capacity and does not require a large critic, allowing for further computational savings if `Q_dn` is much smaller than `Q_a`.
- **Filtering Step Choice (Figure 9):** Performance was largely consistent when filtering at various denoising timesteps (e.g., `t=0`, `t=1`, `t=3`, `t=5`, `t=7`, `t=9`). This indicates that filtering at the initial noise level (the computationally cheapest option) is effective and sufficient, removing the need for precise tuning of the filtering timestep.
- **Full Filtering MDP vs. Single-Step (Figure 10):** Learning the full adaptive filtering policy (with `γ` implicitly encouraging earlier termination) yielded performance comparable to the simplified single-step filtering at the initial noise level. This validates the practical implementation's simplification.
- **Distillation Baseline (Figure 11):** A distillation baseline, which trained a single policy by distilling value-maximizing actions from best-of-N samples, performed significantly worse than FASTER. This gap was attributed to the instability caused by the non-stationary training target in distillation, highlighting FASTER's advantage in directly optimizing filtering in noise space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FASTER framework presents a method for addressing a significant computational challenge in modern reinforcement learning, particularly for algorithms leveraging expressive generative policies such as diffusion models. The primary significance of this work lies in demonstrating that the performance benefits of sampling multiple action candidates can be largely preserved while substantially reducing the computational burden associated with their full denoising.

Key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

- **Computational Efficiency for Generative RL:** FASTER offers a practical solution to accelerate the training and inference of diffusion-based RL policies. By shifting the filtering of action candidates to the initial noise space, it reduces the computationally intensive multi-step denoising process for `N` candidates to just one full denoising pass, preceded by `N` lightweight critic evaluations. This offers significant speedups (e.g., 1.7x to 4.5x in wall-clock time and up to 8x in FLOPs observed in experiments).
- **Enabling Large Model Deployment:** The ability to achieve performance comparable to best-of-N sampling at a fraction of the cost is particularly impactful for large-scale models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models often have billions of parameters, and the computational overhead of existing sampling strategies can be prohibitive for real-world deployment in latency-sensitive applications like robotics. FASTER makes such models more viable for practical use.
- **Modularity and Generalizability:** FASTER is designed as a lightweight and modular component that can be integrated into existing generative RL algorithms (demonstrated with EXPO and IDQL). This suggests its potential applicability across a broader range of diffusion or flow-based policies that rely on initial noise seeds for action generation.
- **Advancement in Value-Guided Policy Optimization:** The work provides evidence that the variance exploited by best-of-N selection is largely determined at the initial noise level. By learning a noise-level critic, FASTER effectively traces downstream value back to these early representations, offering a more efficient paradigm for value-guided action selection.
- **Robustness:** The ablation studies indicate that FASTER is robust to variations in the critic's capacity and the specific timestep for filtering, which simplifies its implementation and deployment. The finding that filtering at the initial noise level performs comparably to a more complex multi-step filtering policy further highlights its practical utility.

Despite these contributions, the paper notes certain limitations. FASTER is primarily designed to improve computational efficiency rather than sample efficiency, though FASTER-EXPO did show strong sample efficiency due to its base algorithm. Additionally, its applicability is restricted to policy classes that use initial noise seeds, a characteristic of many high-performing contemporary RL policies but not all. Future work could explore extending these principles to policy classes lacking such a seed structure and further enhancing sample efficiency. Overall, FASTER represents a step towards making powerful, expressive generative policies more computationally accessible for a wider array of real-world reinforcement learning applications.
