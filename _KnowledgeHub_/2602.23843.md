# OmniXtreme: Breaking the Generality Barrier in High-Dynamic Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> OmniXtreme provides a two-stage framework enabling a unified policy to control diverse, high-dynamic humanoid motions with improved fidelity and scalability. This approach achieves a 98.54% success rate in simulation on complex motion datasets and robustly executes challenging maneuvers like flips and handsprings on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot with a 91.08% real-world success rate.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid control policies face a "fidelity–scalability trade-off," where tracking quality degrades significantly when scaled to diverse and high-dynamic motion libraries.
- The "Learning Bottleneck" in simulation, caused by simple policy architectures and gradient interference, limits the ability to learn a unified policy for many varied motions.
- The "Physical Executability Bottleneck" prevents effective sim-to-real transfer due to oversimplified actuation models that neglect crucial real-world torque-speed and power constraints.

## Method

- A two-stage framework called OMNIXTREME: first, scalable flow-based policy pretraining using DAgger to distill diverse expert behaviors into a high-capacity generative policy.
- An actuation-aware post-training phase where a lightweight residual policy refines the pretrained model using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== under aggressive domain randomization and relaxed termination thresholds.
- Integration of detailed physical constraints, including power-safe actuation regularization, realistic torque-speed envelopes, and nonlinear friction, to enhance real-world executability.

## Results

- Achieved a **98.54%** success rate and **30.93** mm MPJPE in simulation across a combined LAFAN1+XtremeMotion dataset, significantly outperforming existing multi-motion control baselines.
- Demonstrated robust real-world performance on a Unitree G1 humanoid, achieving a **91.08%** overall success rate for 24 distinct high-dynamic motions, including **96.36%** for flips and **88.57%** for handsprings.
- Ablation studies confirmed that actuation-aware modeling, aggressive domain randomization, and power-safe regularization are essential and synergistic for robust sim-to-real transfer of extreme skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative flow-matching policies, combined with expert distillation, effectively scale policy capacity to handle diverse motion libraries, overcoming the traditional fidelity-scalability trade-off in simulation.
> - Integrating realistic actuation constraints like torque-speed limits, power-safe regularization, and aggressive domain randomization during a post-training phase is crucial for robust sim-to-real transfer of high-dynamic skills.
> - Decoupling the learning of high-fidelity representations from hardware-specific refinement into a two-stage process leads to a more stable and scalable training framework for complex humanoid control.
