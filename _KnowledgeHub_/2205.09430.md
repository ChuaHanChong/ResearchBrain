# Action Conditioned Tactile Prediction: case study on slip prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Two action-conditioned tactile prediction models for magnetic-based Xela uSkin sensors: ACTP predicts flattened 48-dim taxel vectors, ACTVP predicts an upscaled image representation, both LSTM chains fusing planned end-effector actions mid-network. Trained on a new 51,000-frame teleoperated grasp-and-move dataset of 11 household objects, they beat video-prediction baselines on error metrics and on downstream slip prediction.

## Problem

- Existing tactile prediction models target image-based optical sensors, leaving low-resolution magnetic sensors with uncalibrated, contact-geometry-dependent readings largely unstudied.
- No comparison study exists ranking predictive architectures on tactile signals, so effects of action conditioning, optical flow, learned priors and encoder-decoders are unknown.
- Prior video-prediction transfers assume objects fit inside the sensor field of view, so pixel-motion kernels and object masks fail for larger grasped objects needing force-change prediction.

## Method

- **ACTP**: linear action-conditioned network over raw 48-feature taxel vectors, two ==LSTMs== then two linear layers, with tiled robot end-effector pose concatenated mid-LSTM-chain and ==skip connections==.
- **ACTVP**: architecturally identical to **ACTP** but reshapes taxels into a 32x32x3 image and uses convolutional and ==ConvLSTM== layers, isolating tactile data representation as the only variable.
- Dataset plus slip pipeline: teleoperated leader-follower grasp-and-move trials with ArUco object pose, ==CUSUM anomaly detection== for binary slip labels, ==Random Forest classifier== over predicted tactile signals.

## Results

- ACTVP gives the best metrics: MAE **0.00631**, PSNR **91.83**, SSIM **0.9965**, versus SVG **0.0455** and CDNA **0.39879**; novel-object MAE **0.598** with the smallest seen-to-novel gap (**0.095**).
- Ablations show action conditioning helps (PMN **0.00854** to PMN-AC **0.00782**) while removing the optical flow addition helps further (PMN-AC-NA **0.00720**), so flow layers hurt taxel-value accuracy.
- On the slip score, ACTP and PMN-AC rank highest despite ACTP's poor MAE (**0.01943**); ACTP switches to slip ahead of ground truth and runs at **71 Hz** (**14 ms**) above the **40 Hz** loop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pixel-space reconstruction metrics can invert task ranking: the worst-MAE model predicted slip earliest, so evaluate predictive models on the downstream control use case.
> - Conditioning on planned future actions is the reliably beneficial ingredient; optical flow, learned priors and encoder-decoder bottlenecks all degraded prediction on sparse, low-resolution tactile data.
> - Predicting a tactile signal then classifying it decouples the horizon from the classifier, letting the slip prediction horizon change on the fly without retraining.
