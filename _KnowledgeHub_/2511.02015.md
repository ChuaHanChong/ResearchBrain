# Stein-based Optimization of Sampling Distributions in Model Predictive Path Integral Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Michigan introduce Stein-Optimized Path-Integral Inference (SOPPI), an algorithm that integrates Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) into Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control by applying online, per-time-step SVGD updates within the action space. This approach improves action sampling, achieves lower state mean squared errors and faster settling times in a cart-pole swing-up, reduces pushing errors for a robot arm even with noisy gradients, and enables longer walking durations and stair climbing for a bipedal walker.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control assumes unimodal (Gaussian) action distributions, leading to suboptimal control when optimal action distributions are multi-modal or sparsely distributed.
- MPPI struggles with the curse of dimensionality in high-degree-of-freedom and non-linear systems, necessitating an exponentially larger number of samples to maintain effectiveness.
- Prior integrations of Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) with MPC/MPPI suffer from issues like loss of kernel meaning in high-dimensional trajectory spaces, high computational costs from propagating gradients over an entire horizon, and vulnerability to gradient instability.

## Method

- The Stein-Optimized Path-Integral Inference (SOPPI) algorithm embeds Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) into the ==MPPI== framework, focusing on optimizing the action sampling distribution online and at each time step.
- SOPPI applies SVGD updates to the actions for individual time steps within the prediction horizon, utilizing only the immediate running cost for gradient computation, rather than optimizing over entire trajectories.
- Candidate actions are iteratively refined using SVGD before rolling out the system dynamics for each step, and the final control sequence is derived through standard MPPI weighting based on accumulated trajectory costs.

## Results

- SOPPI significantly improved state mean squared errors and achieved faster settling times for a cart-pole swing-up task, with SOPPI using 500 particles outperforming baseline MPPI with 1,000 particles.
- In a 7-DOF robot arm pushing task, SOPPI demonstrated lower mean steady-state distance errors and maintained performance effectively even when Gaussian noise was injected into gradients, showing robustness where other methods degraded significantly.
- The algorithm enabled significantly longer average walking times for a 6-DOF planar bipedal walker compared to other methods and successfully navigated unmodeled stairs, showcasing its effectiveness in unstable and out-of-distribution environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying SVGD updates in the lower-dimensional action space of individual time steps, instead of across the entire trajectory, maintains the meaningfulness of the SVGD kernel and reduces computational burden and gradient instability.
> - Dynamically optimizing action noise distributions with SVGD enables MPPI to effectively capture multi-modal optimal action distributions, leading to more robust and accurate control than unimodal assumptions.
> - The repulsive forces inherent in SVGD improve particle efficiency, allowing SOPPI to achieve comparable or superior performance with fewer samples and enhancing its robustness to noisy or imperfect dynamics gradients.
