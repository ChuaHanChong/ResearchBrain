# What You Think is What You See: Driving Exploration in VLM Agents via Visual-Linguistic Curiosity

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKUST and collaborators developed GLANCE, a framework that drives exploration in Vision-Language Model agents by aligning linguistic predictions of future states with actual visual observations. This cross-modal curiosity mechanism enables VLM agents to actively seek out discrepancies, leading to improved performance in complex, sparse-reward tasks and better grounding of their internalized world models.

## Problem

- Existing VLM agents, despite internalizing world models via Chain-of-Thought reasoning, primarily focus on passive exploitation of visited states, limiting active discovery in novel environments.
- This passive reasoning leads to insufficient exploration and suboptimal performance in sparse-reward tasks, where agents may accurately describe dead ends without seeking alternative paths.
- Traditional curiosity methods, based on purely visual prediction errors, are misaligned with VLM agents whose world models are linguistically internalized, potentially leading to visual exploration without grounded linguistic reasoning or 'hallucinations'.

## Method

- Introduces **GLANCE** (Grounding Linguistic Alignment for Curiosity Exploration), a framework that integrates a VLM agent's linguistic predictions with stable visual representations using a self-supervised cross-modal objective.
- Employs an ==alignment projector== to map the VLM's linguistic hypothesis of a future state into a visual latent space, which is then compared against an actual visual observation encoded by a ==momentum-updated target network==.
- Uses the discrepancy between linguistic prediction and visual observation as an intrinsic curiosity reward, guiding the agent's reinforcement learning policy to actively explore and refine its internal world model, and implements a ==Curriculum Exploration== mechanism that periodically rejuvenates the projector to sustain long-horizon exploration.

## Results

- GLANCE consistently achieved superior performance across five diverse agentic tasks (e.g., Sokoban, Navigation, PrimitiveSkill), outperforming baselines relying solely on extrinsic rewards (e.g., GLANCE-Full scored 0.86 vs. VAGEN-Full 0.81, and GLANCE w/ Turn-PPO scored 0.66 vs. Turn-PPO w/ Mask 0.25 in PrimitiveSkill).
- The framework's intrinsic curiosity reward served as an effective proxy for explicit reasoning supervision, enabling GLANCE to maintain higher performance even in the absence of dense reasoning rewards compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of curriculum exploration and the momentum target network, demonstrating that their removal led to diminished performance or instability, highlighting their necessity for sustained and stable learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning a VLM agent's linguistic predictions of a future state with its actual visual observations provides a self-supervised signal for grounding internal world models and driving active, hypothesis-driven exploration.
> - A momentum-updated target network is crucial for providing stable regression targets, preventing representational collapse, and ensuring consistent learning in the cross-modal alignment process.
> - Periodic re-initialization of the lightweight alignment projector through a curriculum exploration strategy is essential to sustain intrinsic curiosity and avoid premature convergence, enabling the discovery of fine-grained discrepancies over long-horizon tasks.
