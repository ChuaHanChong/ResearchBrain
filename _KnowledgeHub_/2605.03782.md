# What You Think is What You See: Driving Exploration in VLM Agents via Visual-Linguistic Curiosity

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKUST and collaborators developed GLANCE, a framework that drives exploration in Vision-Language Model agents by aligning linguistic predictions of future states with actual visual observations. This cross-modal curiosity mechanism enables VLM agents to actively seek out discrepancies, leading to improved performance in complex, sparse-reward tasks and better grounding of their internalized world models.

## Problem

- Existing VLM agents, despite internalizing world models via Chain-of-Thought reasoning, primarily focus on passive exploitation of visited states, limiting active discovery in novel environments.
- This passive reasoning leads to insufficient exploration and suboptimal performance in sparse-reward tasks, where agents may accurately describe dead ends without seeking alternative paths.
- Traditional curiosity methods, based on purely visual prediction errors, are misaligned with VLM agents whose world models are linguistically internalized, potentially leading to visual exploration without grounded linguistic reasoning or 'hallucinations'.

## Method

- Introduces **GLANCE** (Grounding Linguistic Alignment for Curiosity Exploration), a framework that integrates a VLM agent's linguistic predictions with stable visual representations using a self-supervised cross-modal objective.
- Employs an ==alignment projector== to map the VLM's linguistic hypothesis of a future state into a visual latent space, which is then compared against an actual visual observation encoded by a ==momentum-updated target network==.
- Uses the discrepancy between linguistic prediction and visual observation as an intrinsic curiosity reward, guiding the agent's reinforcement learning policy to actively explore and refine its internal world model, and implements a ==Curriculum Exploration== mechanism that periodically rejuvenates the projector to sustain long-horizon exploration.

## Results

- GLANCE consistently achieved superior performance across five diverse agentic tasks (e.g., Sokoban, Navigation, PrimitiveSkill), outperforming baselines relying solely on extrinsic rewards (e.g., GLANCE-Full scored 0.86 vs. VAGEN-Full 0.81, and GLANCE w/ Turn-PPO scored 0.66 vs. Turn-PPO w/ Mask 0.25 in PrimitiveSkill).
- The framework's intrinsic curiosity reward served as an effective proxy for explicit reasoning supervision, enabling GLANCE to maintain higher performance even in the absence of dense reasoning rewards compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of curriculum exploration and the momentum target network, demonstrating that their removal led to diminished performance or instability, highlighting their necessity for sustained and stable learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning a VLM agent's linguistic predictions of a future state with its actual visual observations provides a self-supervised signal for grounding internal world models and driving active, hypothesis-driven exploration.
> - A momentum-updated target network is crucial for providing stable regression targets, preventing representational collapse, and ensuring consistent learning in the cross-modal alignment process.
> - Periodic re-initialization of the lightweight alignment projector through a curriculum exploration strategy is essential to sustain intrinsic curiosity and avoid premature convergence, enabling the discovery of fine-grained discrepancies over long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents operating in partially observable visual environments. Recent advancements in VLM agents have enabled them to internalize world modeling capabilities directly into their policies. This is typically achieved through explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, allowing agents to maintain belief states and predict future world dynamics.

However, existing paradigms, exemplified by frameworks such as Wang et al. (2025), Chen et al. (2025), Shu et al. (2025), and He et al. (2025), primarily restrict this capability to passive exploitation of visited states. This means that agents refine their reasoning accuracy based on the data distribution encountered, rather than actively discovering novel information. This passive approach often leads to limitations in sparse-reward tasks, where agents might accurately describe a "dead end" but fail to explore alternative, more promising paths. The current literature indicates that such reliance on passive interpretation can result in superficial success without genuine understanding or comprehensive exploration.

Curiosity-driven learning, historically, has offered a solution to sparse-reward problems in reinforcement learning. Traditional curiosity methods, like those proposed by Schmidhuber (1991) and Pathak et al. (2017b), involve training an auxiliary world model to predict future observations, using the prediction error as an intrinsic reward signal. However, applying this directly to VLM agents presents a fundamental architectural mismatch. In VLM agents, the world model is often internalized within the policy through linguistic CoT reasoning, rather than being a separate, external module focused solely on visual prediction. Standard curiosity approaches, which typically focus on predicting latent visual futures from visual pasts, decouple exploration from the agent's linguistic reasoning process. This can result in VLM agents developing robust visual representations for curiosity objectives while their linguistic reasoning remains ungrounded in physical reality, potentially leading to "hallucinations" or semantic inconsistencies.

This research aims to bridge this gap by proposing a unified framework that aligns the internalized linguistic world model of VLM agents with stable visual representations. It integrates curiosity directly into the VLM's reasoning, addressing the limitation where VLM agents' "thinking" (linguistic reasoning) might not accurately predict "what they see" (visual reality). The work positions itself as a critical step in enabling VLM agents to move beyond passive observation and into active, hypothesis-driven exploration, where the discrepancy between linguistic prediction and visual observation drives learning and discovery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current VLM agents in complex, partially observable environments, particularly those characterized by sparse rewards. While these agents leverage internal world models and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for planning, their exploration capabilities are often restricted to refining reasoning over already visited states. This passive approach is insufficient for tasks requiring active discovery of novel information or overcoming "known unknowns" that challenge the agent's current understanding. The authors encapsulate this motivation with the central question: "Can VLM agents actively find signals that challenge and refine their internal world model through curiosity-driven exploration?"

The key motivations and objectives of the GLANCE framework are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Passive Reasoning Limitations:** Current VLM agents tend to rely on passive interpretation, which can lead to situations where an agent perfectly describes a non-optimal state (e.g., a "dead end" in a puzzle) without actively seeking alternative paths. This highlights a lack of an inherent epistemic drive to uncover new information.
- **Bridging the Modality Gap in Curiosity:** Traditional curiosity methods, which primarily use prediction error from visual observations as an intrinsic reward, are deemed inadequate for VLM agents. This is because the world model in VLMs is internalized via linguistic CoT reasoning, meaning a purely visual-to-visual prediction error does not guarantee that the linguistic component of the world model is grounded in physical reality. This could result in robust visual exploration but linguistically detached reasoning or "hallucinations."
- **Establishing a Unified Principle:** The core hypothesis driving GLANCE is that "what the VLM agent thinks should predict what it sees." This principle motivates the development of a unified objective where effective exploration requires the VLM agent to actively seek out interactions where its linguistic hypothesis of the future fails to align with the observed visual reality. This misalignment is intended to force the internalized world model to ground itself through active falsification.
- **Developing a Grounded Curiosity Signal:** A key objective is to project the latent representation of the VLM agent's linguistic prediction into a stable visual representation space. The discrepancy between this linguistic prediction and the actual visual observation (encoded by a momentum-updated target network) is designed to serve as a unified self-supervised objective.
- **Achieving Multi-faceted Learning:** This unified objective is intended to simultaneously achieve three goals: (i) shape the visual encoder to extract semantically actionable features relevant to the agent's reasoning, (ii) ground the internalized linguistic world model in actual physical dynamics, and (iii) generate a curiosity-driven intrinsic reward to guide the reinforcement learning policy. This transforms exploration from a stochastic search into an active falsification process.
- **Mitigating "Curiosity Drain":** A specific challenge identified is "curiosity drain," where a lightweight alignment projector can quickly overfit to the VLM's rich semantic space, causing the intrinsic reward to diminish prematurely. An objective is to introduce a mechanism, Curriculum Exploration, to periodically rejuvenate the projector, thus sustaining the curiosity drive over long-horizon learning and ensuring continuous discovery of fine-grained discrepancies.
- **Validating Effectiveness:** A final objective is to empirically demonstrate that GLANCE, by aligning thinking and seeing, can effectively solve complex or sparse agentic tasks, and that its cross-modal curiosity can enable robust exploration even in the absence of explicit extrinsic rewards.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GLANCE (Grounding Linguistic Alignment for Curiosity Exploration) framework is designed to integrate reasoning and exploration within VLM agents through a self-supervised cross-modal loop. It consists of two primary interacting components: an Online VLM Agent and a Momentum Encoder (target network).

#### 3.1. Online VLM Agent

The online stream is parameterized by `θ = (v, ℓ)`, which includes a visual encoder `f_v` and a Large Language Model (LLM) backbone `Λ_ℓ`.
1. **World Modeling and Action Generation:** At each turn `t`, the agent receives a visual observation `o_t` and maintains a history `h_t`. It then performs internalized world modeling by generating a sequence of natural language tokens `Φ_t`, structured as `<Obs>s_t</Obs><Res>z_t</Res><Pred>s_{t+1}</Pred>`. This sequence represents the inferred state (`s_t`), reasoning path (`z_t`), and a prediction of the next state transition (`s_{t+1}`). Based on this, the agent generates an executable action `a_t`.
2. **Linguistic Hypothesis State:** The latent representation corresponding to the final `</Pred>` token, denoted `h_{t+1} ∈ R^d`, is extracted from the Transformer’s final layer hidden state. This vector serves as a semantic compression of the agent’s linguistic prediction about the future state `s_{t+1}`.
3. **Alignment Projector:** A lightweight, two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) projector `g_ψ` is augmented to the online agent. Its role is to map the linguistic latent representation `h_{t+1}` from the semantic language space into the visual latent space. The online prediction `b_y_{t+1}` is computed as `b_y_{t+1} = g_ψ(h_{t+1})`. The projector employs a bottleneck architecture to encourage capture of high-level actionable semantics.

#### 3.2. Momentum Encoder (Target Network)

To provide stable regression targets for the self-supervised alignment task, a momentum target network is employed, parameterized by `ϕ`.
1. **Structure and Update:** This stream consists of a visual encoder `f_ϕ` that is structurally identical to the online visual encoder `f_v`. Unlike `f_v`, the parameters `ϕ` are not updated via gradient descent. Instead, `f_ϕ` acts as a slowly evolving teacher, maintaining a moving average of the online encoder’s weights. At the end of each training iteration, `ϕ` is updated using Exponential Moving Average (EMA): `ϕ ← αϕ + (1 − α)v`, where `α ∈` is the target decay rate.
2. **Visual Reality Representation:** After the agent executes an action `a_e_t` and observes the subsequent visual observation `o_{t+1}`, the target network encodes `o_{t+1}` into a visual reality representation: `y_{t+1} = sg(f_ϕ(o_{t+1}))`. The `sg(·)` (stop-gradient) operator is crucial to prevent representational collapse and ensure `y_{t+1}` provides a stable anchor.

#### 3.3. Grounding Reasoning via Visual Alignment (Cross-modal Prediction Objective)

GLANCE operationalizes the principle of "what the VLM agent thinks should predict what it sees" through a self-supervised cross-modal prediction objective.
1. **Prediction Loss (`L_explore`):** The objective is to minimize the mean squared error between the normalized online linguistic prediction `b_y_{t+1}` and the normalized target visual reality `y_{t+1}`:
 `L_explore(v, ψ, t) = || b_y_{t+1} / ||b_y_{t+1}||_2 - sg(y_{t+1} / ||y_{t+1}||_2) ||_2^2`.
2. **Selective Gradient Routing:** To ensure grounding without destabilizing the LLM's semantic priors, the LLM parameters (`Λ_ℓ`) are frozen. Gradients from `L_explore` explicitly update the projector `g_ψ` and, crucially, the online visual encoder `f_v`. This process compels `f_v` to learn features that are semantically consistent with the agent’s logical reasoning, effectively training it to "see what the agent thinks."

#### 3.4. Curiosity as Active Exploration

The prediction error `L_explore` is interpreted as a proxy for epistemic uncertainty and converted into an intrinsic reward.
1. **Intrinsic Reward (`r_i_t`):** `r_i_t = β · L_explore(v, ψ, t)`, where `β` is a scaling factor. Rewards are normalized by the running standard deviation of their corresponding returns to counter non-stationarity.
2. **Total Reward (`r_t`):** The agent's policy is optimized using a composite reward signal: `r_t = r_e_t + r_i_t`. Here, `r_e_t` includes sparse task rewards (`r_task_t`), reasoning quality rewards (`r_reason_t`), and format adherence rewards (`r_format_t`).

#### 3.5. Curriculum Exploration with Rejuvenation

This mechanism addresses the "curiosity drain" phenomenon, where the lightweight projector `g_ψ` might overfit rapidly, causing `L_explore` to vanish prematurely.
1. **Trigger Condition:** The system monitors the instantaneous rate of change in prediction loss, `δ_t = L^{(t)}_explore - L^{(t-1)}_explore`.
2. **Rejuvenation:** If `δ_t` remains below a convergence threshold `ϵ` for `K` consecutive steps, the projector parameters `g_ψ` are randomly re-initialized. The evolved parameters of the online visual encoder `f_v` are preserved.
3. **Effect:** This re-initialization causes a spike in prediction error, reinvigorating the intrinsic curiosity signal. It compels the agent to revisit familiar states and ground its reasoning in increasingly fine-grained visual details, thereby sustaining exploration and revealing new "known unknowns."

#### 3.6. Optimization Details

The policy is optimized using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. A Bi-Level Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) mechanism is employed for hierarchical credit assignment, propagating advantages from turn-level outcomes to individual reasoning tokens. Decoupled learning rates are used for the prediction loss/RL actor and the critic.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of GLANCE was conducted across a diverse suite of five agentic tasks: Cognitive Grid Puzzles (Sokoban, FrozenLake), Embodied 3D Control (Navigation, PrimitiveSkill), and Generative Reasoning (SVG Reconstruction). The VLM backbone used was Qwen2.5-VL-3B.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- GLANCE consistently achieved superior test-time performance across all five tasks compared to baselines that relied solely on extrinsic rewards.
- In settings with **dense extrinsic rewards** (VAGEN-Full), GLANCE-Full obtained an overall score of 0.86, outperforming VAGEN-Full (0.81). This improvement was notable in logic-heavy tasks like Sokoban, where GLANCE-Full increased the success rate from 0.79 to 0.85.
- In settings with **sparse extrinsic rewards** (VAGEN-Base), GLANCE-Base achieved an overall score of 0.80, surpassing VAGEN-Base (0.76).
- In the **Turn-level PPO setting**, GLANCE demonstrated significant improvements. For example, in the PrimitiveSkill environment, GLANCE w/ Turn-PPO achieved a success rate of 0.66, which was substantially higher than the 0.25 recorded by Turn-PPO w/ Mask. This suggests that GLANCE's cross-modal curiosity aids VLM agents in learning effective exploration policies, particularly in scenarios where task rewards are extremely sparse and precise coordinate reasoning is required.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

**Curriculum Exploration and Momentum Targets:**
- An ablation comparing GLANCE with and without adaptive projector rejuvenation showed that the full GLANCE approach achieved superior performance, especially in later stages of training on Sokoban and Navigation tasks. This indicates that the rejuvenation mechanism successfully prevents the intrinsic reward from diminishing prematurely.
- Replacing the momentum target encoder with a direct online copy led to significant instability and divergence. This finding underscores the importance of the slowly evolving teacher (EMA updated target network) in providing a consistent and stable anchor for grounding the agent's linguistic hypothesis.

**Intrinsic Curiosity vs. External Supervision:**
- When the dense reasoning reward (`r_reason_t`), typically provided by an LLM-as-a-judge, was removed, performance decreased for both VAGEN-Full and GLANCE-Full, affirming the value of external feedback.
- However, in this challenging setting lacking explicit reasoning supervision, GLANCE-Full (w/o `r_reason_t`) consistently outperformed VAGEN-Full (w/o `r_reason_t`). This result suggests that GLANCE's curiosity-driven intrinsic reward effectively serves as a proxy for reasoning supervision, implicitly encouraging the agent to maintain high-quality reasoning by aligning "thinking" with "seeing."

**Sensitivity to Exploration Weight (`β`):**
- Analysis of the exploration coefficient `β` indicated a non-monotonic relationship with performance. Moderate `β` values (e.g., `β ∈ [0.1, 0.3]`) led to optimal VLM performance, striking a balance between epistemic drive and task reward.
- An excessively high `β` (0.5) resulted in noisy exploration and diminished performance, while a too-low `β` failed to provide sufficient impetus to overcome exploration barriers.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

**Computational Efficiency:**
- GLANCE introduced a marginal computational overhead per step compared to VAGEN-Full, despite the additional forward pass for the momentum target network and a backward pass for the prediction loss. This efficiency is attributed to the lightweight projector design and the absence of gradient computation for the target network.

**Visualization of Curriculum Dynamics:**
- Visualizations of the prediction loss and task success rate over training steps revealed a distinctive sawtooth pattern. Each projector reset caused an intentional spike in prediction loss, reflecting a rejuvenation of intrinsic curiosity. Crucially, each loss spike was followed by a subsequent increase in the success rate, indicating that the curriculum mechanism helped VLM agents break through performance plateaus and continuously ground their reasoning in fine-grained environmental details.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GLANCE framework introduces several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the development of autonomous agents:

- **Paradigm Shift for VLM Agents:** This work proposes a shift in the role of VLM agents from passive inference makers to active hypothesis testers. By explicitly driving exploration through the falsification and refinement of internal beliefs against visual reality, GLANCE encourages a more proactive and engaged learning process. This move towards active, curiosity-driven agents could enable them to tackle more complex and unstructured environments.
- **Enhanced Exploration in Sparse-Reward Environments:** GLANCE provides a robust mechanism for VLM agents to explore effectively in partially observable, sparse-reward environments, where traditional, passive reasoning methods often fall short. The intrinsic curiosity signal, derived from the discrepancy between linguistic prediction and visual reality, guides agents to discover crucial information and overcome exploration challenges that would otherwise be intractable.
- **Grounded World Models:** A core impact is the improved grounding of VLM agents' internalized linguistic world models in physical dynamics. By forcing the agent's "thinking" (linguistic prediction) to align with "seeing" (visual observation), the framework reduces the likelihood of linguistic hallucinations and enhances cross-modal consistency. This is crucial for building reliable and interpretable agents capable of reasoning about the physical world.
- **Reduced Reliance on Costly Supervision:** The effectiveness of the intrinsic reward as a proxy for explicit reasoning supervision suggests that GLANCE can reduce the dependence on expensive, dense external feedback (e.g., LLM-as-a-judge). This could make the training of VLM agents more scalable, cost-efficient, and applicable in domains where such supervision is difficult or impossible to obtain.
- **Scalable and Autonomous Learning:** The framework's lightweight architecture, where the VLM simultaneously functions as both world model and policy, and its ability to learn without human demonstrations, points towards a scalable and principled path for developing autonomous embodied agents. The adaptive curriculum exploration mechanism further ensures sustained epistemic drive over long training horizons, which is vital for continuous learning in dynamic environments.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies limitations and promising avenues for future work. These include exploring methods for dynamically updating the LLM backbone (e.g., via Low-Rank Adapters like LoRA) to refine reasoning logic without language drift, and investigating more sophisticated cross-modal mechanisms beyond a lightweight MLP projector to capture finer-grained spatial-temporal correlations between CoT and future environment states. These directions suggest continued research towards more expressive and adaptable VLM agents.

In summary, GLANCE offers a principled approach to imbue VLM agents with an active, internal drive for discovery, representing a step towards more autonomous, curious, and physically-grounded intelligent systems.
