# CLASS: Contrastive Learning via Action Sequence Supervision for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLASS framework enhances robotic manipulation policy generalization by learning robust visual representations from heterogeneous data using a soft supervised contrastive learning objective. This method, which leverages Dynamic Time Warping to quantify action sequence similarity, improves success rates by up to 41% in real-world subtasks compared to existing approaches.

## Problem

- Behavior Cloning (BC) policies exhibit poor generalization when confronted with heterogeneous demonstration data, such as varying camera poses or object appearances.
- BC methods often overfit to specific state-action pairs, failing to extract underlying shared behavioral structures across diverse demonstrations.
- Current state-of-the-art BC methods, despite advancements in action sequence modeling, remain susceptible to visual shifts due to their reliance on direct action supervision.

## Method

- A two-stage framework, **CLASS**, is proposed that first pre-computes pairwise action sequence similarities across demonstrations using ==Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)==.
- It then trains a vision encoder with a soft supervised contrastive learning objective, which pulls observations with similar future action sequences closer in latent space, weighted by their DTW-based similarity.
- The framework supports both standalone representation learning and a pre-training + fine-tuning protocol for existing policy learning methods like Diffusion Policy.

## Results

- CLASS improved average success rates across five simulation tasks to **85%** with MLP and **91%** with Diffusion Policy, substantially exceeding best baselines of **63%** and **77%**.
- In real-world experiments, CLASS-DP achieved 37-**41%** higher average subtask success and 45-**55%** higher final task completion rates compared to ImageNet-DP under randomly repositioned cameras.
- Ablation studies confirmed that soft contrastive learning with DTW-based sequence similarity is crucial for the observed performance gains and improved generalization in heterogeneous environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action sequence similarity, quantified by Dynamic Time Warping, serves as an effective weak supervision signal to learn robust, behaviorally-grounded visual representations that are invariant to visual shifts.
> - Implementing soft weighting for positive pairs in the contrastive loss enables a finer-grained similarity signal, leading to more transferable representations compared to hard binary contrastive approaches.
> - The learned representations are sufficiently rich to enable effective non-parametric (retrieval-based) control, indicating their direct utility even without a policy head.
