# CLASS: Contrastive Learning via Action Sequence Supervision for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLASS framework enhances robotic manipulation policy generalization by learning robust visual representations from heterogeneous data using a soft supervised contrastive learning objective. This method, which leverages Dynamic Time Warping to quantify action sequence similarity, improves success rates by up to 41% in real-world subtasks compared to existing approaches.

## Problem

- Behavior Cloning (BC) policies exhibit poor generalization when confronted with heterogeneous demonstration data, such as varying camera poses or object appearances.
- BC methods often overfit to specific state-action pairs, failing to extract underlying shared behavioral structures across diverse demonstrations.
- Current state-of-the-art BC methods, despite advancements in action sequence modeling, remain susceptible to visual shifts due to their reliance on direct action supervision.

## Method

- A two-stage framework, **CLASS**, is proposed that first pre-computes pairwise action sequence similarities across demonstrations using ==Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)==.
- It then trains a vision encoder with a soft supervised contrastive learning objective, which pulls observations with similar future action sequences closer in latent space, weighted by their DTW-based similarity.
- The framework supports both standalone representation learning and a pre-training + fine-tuning protocol for existing policy learning methods like Diffusion Policy.

## Results

- CLASS improved average success rates across five simulation tasks to **85%** with MLP and **91%** with Diffusion Policy, substantially exceeding best baselines of **63%** and **77%**.
- In real-world experiments, CLASS-DP achieved 37-**41%** higher average subtask success and 45-**55%** higher final task completion rates compared to ImageNet-DP under randomly repositioned cameras.
- Ablation studies confirmed that soft contrastive learning with DTW-based sequence similarity is crucial for the observed performance gains and improved generalization in heterogeneous environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action sequence similarity, quantified by Dynamic Time Warping, serves as an effective weak supervision signal to learn robust, behaviorally-grounded visual representations that are invariant to visual shifts.
> - Implementing soft weighting for positive pairs in the contrastive loss enables a finer-grained similarity signal, leading to more transferable representations compared to hard binary contrastive approaches.
> - The learned representations are sufficiently rich to enable effective non-parametric (retrieval-based) control, indicating their direct utility even without a policy head.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in modern robotic manipulation: the generalization capabilities of Behavior Cloning (BC) policies, especially when confronted with diverse or "heterogeneous" demonstration data. While BC has achieved strong performance with expressive models, sequence modeling (action chunking), and large-scale datasets, it notoriously struggles with visual shifts such as varying camera poses or object appearances. This limitation stems from BC's tendency to overfit to individual state-action pairs rather than capturing underlying shared behavioral structures across demonstrations.

The broader research landscape in robot manipulation has seen significant advancements in several areas:

- **Behavior Cloning (BC) and Action Sequence Modeling**: Recent works like Diffusion Policy (DP), Action Chunking Transformer, and VQ-BET have pushed the boundaries of BC by casting policy learning as a sequence modeling problem, producing state-of-the-art results. However, these methods primarily rely on strong, direct action supervision, making them susceptible to the aforementioned generalization issues.
- **Representation Learning for Robotics**: A prominent trend in robot learning involves learning robust visual representations that can abstract away task-irrelevant variations. Methods like R3M, TCN, and VINN utilize self-supervised or time-contrastive objectives to learn visual features. These often focus on learning temporal relationships within *individual* demonstrations or using self-supervised techniques like BYOL.
- **Contrastive Learning**: Originating in computer vision, contrastive learning has been successfully applied to extract informative representations by pushing dissimilar samples apart and pulling similar samples together in a latent space. In RL and imitation learning, this has been used for goal-conditioned learning, temporal contrasts within trajectories, or pseudo-labeled actions in autonomous driving. More recently, the concept of "soft contrastive learning" has emerged, allowing for continuous similarity-based weights rather than binary positive/negative labels.

CLASS positions itself at the intersection of action sequence modeling and contrastive representation learning. Unlike prior contrastive methods that focus on intra-demonstration temporal dynamics or binary similarity, CLASS leverages *inter-demonstration* action sequence similarity (via Dynamic Time Warping) as a weak supervision signal for a *soft supervised contrastive learning* objective. This allows it to learn representations that are robust to visual shifts while capturing shared behaviors, directly addressing BC's generalization gap in heterogeneous settings. By focusing on *soft weighting of positive pairs*, CLASS further refines the contrastive signal to align with the needs of imitation and retrieval-based policy learning, where nuanced similarity is crucial. It acts as a powerful pre-training mechanism that can significantly enhance downstream policy learning methods like Diffusion Policy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind CLASS is to overcome the generalization limitations of Behavior Cloning (BC) when applied to heterogeneous datasets, characterized by significant visual shifts such as varying camera poses or object appearances. BC's tendency to overfit to specific state-action pairs in individual demonstrations hinders its ability to capture shared underlying behaviors across diverse data.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a framework for learning robust, behaviorally-grounded representations**: The goal is to create an embedding space where observations leading to similar future behaviors are mapped close together, regardless of visual variations, while observations leading to dissimilar behaviors are separated.
2. **Utilize weak supervision from action sequence similarity**: Instead of relying on direct action prediction (as in BC), CLASS aims to derive a supervisory signal from the similarity of entire action sequences, allowing for a more abstract and generalizable understanding of "behavior."
3. **Employ supervised contrastive learning with soft weighting**: To capture fine-grained behavioral similarities, the method seeks to develop a novel soft InfoNCE loss that weights positive pairs based on their degree of action sequence similarity.
4. **Improve generalization in heterogeneous settings**: Ultimately, the method aims to enhance the transferability and composability of learned representations, leading to significantly better policy performance under visual shifts, which are common in real-world data collection.
5. **Support both retrieval-based and policy fine-tuning approaches**: The learned representations should be versatile enough to enable direct control via nearest-neighbor retrieval and to serve as effective pre-training for state-of-the-art policy learning architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLASS proposes a two-stage approach for learning behaviorally grounded representations from demonstration data:

#### 3.1. Action Sequence Similarity

The first stage involves **pre-computing pairwise action sequence similarities** across all demonstrations in the dataset.
- **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)**: To account for temporal misalignments between action sequences, DTW is employed. For any two action sequences, DTW computes the minimum Euclidean distance between them by "warping" one sequence to match the other. This is crucial as identical behaviors might occur at different speeds or starting times.
- **Positive Pair Definition**: After computing DTW distances for all possible pairs, positive pairs are identified as those whose DTW distance falls below a pre-defined quantile threshold (K). This threshold allows for flexible control over the strictness of "similarity."
- **Soft Positive Weights (`w_ij`)**: For these identified positive pairs, a soft positive weight `w_ij` is assigned based on their DTW-based similarity. This weight is calculated as `1 - CDF(DTW(i, j))`, where CDF is the empirical cumulative distribution function of all anchor-positive DTW distances. This ensures that pairs with *lower* DTW distances (more similar) receive *higher* weights, providing a graded similarity signal.

#### 3.2. Soft Contrastive Learning

The second stage uses a **soft supervised contrastive learning objective** to train the vision encoder.
- **Encoder Architecture**: The vision encoder `f_θ` consists of a ResNet-18 backbone followed by a spatial softmax layer, which maps raw image observations to a latent representation `z`.
- **InfoNCE Loss with Soft Weights**: The core of the learning is a modified InfoNCE loss. For each observation (anchor) `i` in a batch, it's pulled closer in the latent space to its pre-defined positive pairs `j` (identified by DTW) while being pushed away from all other (negative) samples `k`. Crucially, the pulling force for positive pairs is modulated by the `w_ij` soft weights:
 ```
 L_CL = - (1/B) * Σ_i=1^B [ (Σ_j=1^B w_ij * log p_ij) / (Σ_j=1^B w_ij) ]
 where p_ij = exp(S_ij) / Σ_k=1, k≠i exp(S_ik) and S_ij = (ˆz_i^T ˆz_j) / τ
 ```
 Here, `ˆz` are L2-normalized latent vectors, and `τ` is a temperature hyperparameter. This formulation allows positive pairs to contribute proportionally to their behavioral similarity, providing a finer-grained learning signal.
- **Data Augmentation**: During training, input images are augmented with random cropping and Gaussian noise to enhance representation robustness.

#### 3.3. Training Schemes

CLASS supports two training schemes:
1. **Representation Learning Only**: The encoder is trained solely using the contrastive objective, without any policy head.
2. **Two-Stage Protocol (Pre-training + Fine-tuning)**: The encoder is first pre-trained with the contrastive objective. Then, a policy head is attached, and the entire network (or just the policy head) is fine-tuned using a standard Behavior Cloning (BC) loss. This leverages the robust representation learned by CLASS.

#### 3.4. Policy Evaluation

Policies are evaluated in two distinct settings:
- **Parametric Setting**: Actions are generated directly by a learned policy network (e.g., MLP or Diffusion Policy) after fine-tuning.
- **Non-parametric (Retrieval-based) Setting**: This "Rep-Only" approach relies solely on the learned representation. At inference time, the current observation's latent representation is used to retrieve the `k_nn` nearest neighbor action sequences from the training dataset. These retrieved sequences are then ensembled using a similarity-weighted average to produce the final action sequence. This showcases the quality of the learned representation itself, independent of a policy head.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis

The paper includes a theoretical proposition stating that the proposed `L_CL` loss optimizes the KL divergence between the distribution of soft positive weights (`w_ij`) and the distribution of corresponding similarities in the latent representation. This establishes a formal link between the desired action sequence similarity and the learned embedding space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate CLASS's superior performance across a wide range of simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, particularly in heterogeneous settings.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (RQ1, RQ2, RQ3)

- **Overall Performance**: CLASS consistently improves success rates across all 5 simulation tasks and both MLP and Diffusion Policy (DP) policy classes. It achieves an average success rate of 85% with MLP and 91% with DP, significantly outperforming the best baselines (63% and 77% respectively).
- **Robustness to Heterogeneity**: The most notable gains are observed under challenging heterogeneous conditions (dynamic camera and random object color setups). Here, CLASS achieves mean success rates of 76% (MLP) and 85% (DP), far surpassing the best baselines (32% and 57%). This directly addresses BC's key limitation.
- **Retrieval-based Control (Rep-Only)**: In the non-parametric setting, CLASS achieves success rates (83% average) comparable to parametric Diffusion Policy, even without a policy head. This highlights the high quality and behavioral relevance of the learned representations.
- **Generalization to Action Spaces and Multi-Task Learning**: CLASS performs strongly on Aloha-Transfer (bi-dexterous manipulation, joint position control) and LIBERO-Object (multi-task with language instructions), indicating its ability to generalize across different action spaces and handle multi-task scenarios effectively.
- **Downstream Policy Learning**: CLASS pre-training consistently boosts the performance of both MLP and Diffusion Policy, demonstrating its effectiveness as a representation learning strategy for fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. Design Choice Analysis

Ablation studies confirm the importance of key CLASS design elements:
- **Soft Contrastive Learning**: Replacing soft positive weights with uniform (hard) weights significantly degrades performance, validating the importance of graded similarity signals.
- **Sequence-level Similarity (DTW window size)**: Increasing the DTW sliding window size up to T=16 improves success rates, emphasizing the benefit of capturing longer-term action sequence similarities.
- **DTW vs. L2 Distance**: DTW consistently outperforms simple L2 distance for measuring action similarity, highlighting its ability to handle temporal misalignments.
- **Positive Pair Threshold (Quantile K)**: The choice of quantile K for positive selection presents a trade-off, with optimal performance typically found in a sweet spot (e.g., 1.0-2.5%).

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiment Results

- **Superior Real-World Performance**: On 3 real-world manipulation tasks (Two-Stack, Mug-Hang, Toaster-Load) with a Franka Research 3 robot and randomly repositioned cameras, CLASS-DP (DP with CLASS pre-training) consistently outperforms ImageNet-DP (DP with ImageNet pre-training).
- **Quantitative Gains**: CLASS-DP achieves average improvements of 37-41% in subtask success and 45-55% in final task completion rates over ImageNet-DP in both non-parametric and parametric settings. This validates CLASS's ability to generalize to realistic and visually diverse environments.

#### 4.4. Additional Findings

- **Synergy with Wrist Camera and Augmentations**: While CLASS already excels, adding a wrist-mounted camera or task-aligned augmentations (e.g., color augmentation for Push-T) further improves performance for both BC and CLASS, but CLASS *retains its significant advantage*.
- **Benefits of ImageNet Pre-training**: Using ImageNet pre-trained weights for the ResNet-18 backbone significantly boosts CLASS performance, especially in heterogeneous settings, indicating that general visual features are a good starting point.
- **Training Dynamics and Scaling**: CLASS pre-training converges much faster than BC from scratch, and also enables faster fine-tuning of policy heads. This suggests improved computational efficiency in repeated training runs. Furthermore, CLASS follows a positive scaling law, with performance improving consistently as dataset size increases, outperforming BC across all data regimes.
- **Inference Time**: Rep-Only (non-parametric) CLASS methods are generally faster (5.5 ms) than parametric MLP (7.3 ms) and Diffusion Policy (84.4 ms), making them suitable for high-frequency control, though retrieval cost scales with dataset size.
- **Representation Visualization**: t-SNE visualizations of latent spaces show that CLASS representations for the Dynamic Camera Square task are highly structured, with different demonstrations aligning and overlapping, indicating high transferability and shared structure. In contrast, BC-trained representations show poor overlap. Nearest neighbor visualizations confirm that CLASS identifies similar behaviors across varying viewpoints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLASS framework makes significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, with several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Generalization for Imitation Learning**: CLASS directly tackles a major bottleneck in Behavior Cloning – its poor generalization to heterogeneous visual conditions. By learning robust, behaviorally-grounded representations, CLASS enables policies to perform reliably even with significant shifts in camera pose, object appearance, or other visual factors. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic real-world environments.
2. **More Data-Efficient Robot Learning**: The ability to effectively leverage heterogeneous data means that large-scale datasets, which naturally contain visual variations, can be utilized more efficiently. CLASS's faster convergence during pre-training and fine-tuning also suggests improved sample efficiency and reduced computational cost over repeated training cycles.
3. **Foundation for Robust Policy Learning**: By providing a pre-trained encoder that outputs highly transferable and composable behavioral representations, CLASS serves as a powerful foundation for various downstream policy learning methods, including state-of-the-art approaches like Diffusion Policy. This modularity allows researchers to focus on policy architecture while benefiting from robust visual understanding.
4. **Enabling Retrieval-Based Control**: The strong performance of CLASS in the "Rep-Only" non-parametric setting highlights the potential for simple, yet effective, retrieval-based control. This approach requires no policy head training after representation learning, offering a fast and efficient path to robot control, especially useful for smaller datasets or when quick deployment is needed.
5. **Future Directions in Large-Scale and Cross-Embodiment Learning**: The learned representations hold promise for scaling up robot learning. The paper suggests extending CLASS to large-scale policy learning and cross-embodiment learning with a unified action space, leveraging diverse offline data for few-shot learning. This could pave the way for more general-purpose robot agents.

**Limitations and Future Work**: The authors acknowledge that CLASS has some limitations. The pre-computation of DTW distances has a quadratic cost in the number of data points, which can be prohibitive for extremely large datasets (though they suggest online DTW as a potential solution). The current work does not address suboptimal or noisy demonstrations, a common challenge in real-world data collection. Lastly, its scope is limited to vision modality. Addressing these limitations in future work could further broaden the applicability and robustness of CLASS.

In conclusion, CLASS represents a significant step forward in making imitation learning more robust and practical for real-world robot manipulation by effectively addressing the challenge of visual heterogeneity through innovative action sequence-based contrastive representation learning.
