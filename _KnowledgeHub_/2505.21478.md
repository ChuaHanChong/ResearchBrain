# Policy Optimized Text-to-Image Pipeline Design

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Technion and NVIDIA Research introduced FlowRL, a reinforcement learning framework that automates text-to-image pipeline design by generating novel, high-quality workflows. FlowRL achieves a 60% win rate against ComfyGen in human preference and automated evaluations, and generates 41% unique workflow structures, by employing a surrogate reward model to bypass costly image generation during training.

## Problem

- Existing automated text-to-image workflow design methods were computationally inefficient, requiring extensive image generation to evaluate candidate workflows during training.
- Prior LLM-based systems for workflow generation showed limited novelty, primarily classifying or interpolating predefined structures rather than synthesizing new graph topologies or model combinations.
- Manually designing optimal multi-component text-to-image pipelines is complex, demanding significant expertise and extensive experimentation.

## Method

- FlowRL employs a two-phase training strategy, starting with supervised fine-tuning of an LLM on ==prompt-flow== pairs, followed by policy optimization using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- A custom structured encoding scheme reduces workflow token length by 86.7%, enabling more efficient training of memory-intensive algorithms.
- A surrogate ensemble reward model, trained to predict human preference scores for prompt-flow combinations, eliminates the need for costly image generation during reinforcement learning, further enhanced by a component-aware hybrid reward and uncertainty-aware RL to mitigate reward hacking.

## Results

- FlowRL achieved a **60%** win rate against ComfyGen in human preference and HPS v2 automated evaluations, demonstrating superior visual quality.
- The framework generated genuinely novel workflows, with **41%** uniqueness and only **1%** overlap with its training data, significantly improving over baselines like ComfyGen (**94%** overlap).
- FlowRL matched ComfyGen's prompt adherence (GenEval score of **0.61**) and showed improved performance in complex compositional prompts, such as 'two objects' and 'binding'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing direct image generation with a robust, uncertainty-aware surrogate reward model during RL training drastically reduces computational costs and enables more extensive policy optimization.
> - Formulating text-to-image workflow design as an RL problem allows an LLM to explore a vast space of possible configurations and generate genuinely novel, high-performing pipeline structures.
> - An efficient, token-reduced workflow representation scheme is crucial for scaling LLM-based workflow generation and making advanced RL algorithms feasible.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image generation has progressed from early monolithic models, which directly translated text into images using a single neural network, to more sophisticated multi-component pipelines. These pipelines integrate specialized modules such as fine-tuned diffusion models, super-resolution blocks, adapters, and editing steps, leading to enhanced image quality and greater user control. Platforms like ComfyUI exemplify this trend by providing a node-based interface for users to construct complex generative workflows.

However, designing effective workflows within these modular systems remains a challenge. The vast combinatorial space of components and their parameter settings, coupled with prompt-dependent effectiveness, necessitates significant expertise and manual experimentation. Previous research, such as ComfyGen, attempted to automate this process using large language models (LLMs) to construct prompt-adaptive workflows. A primary limitation of ComfyGen was its reliance on generating images with pre-defined pipelines for training. This computationally intensive process restricted the size of the training dataset, leading to models that primarily classified existing flows rather than synthesizing novel graph topologies or model combinations. Consequently, these approaches exhibited limited generalization and creativity.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has advanced as a method for fine-tuning LLMs, aligning their outputs with desired preferences or metrics. Techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) have demonstrated success in optimizing LLMs for complex tasks, including those requiring structured reasoning. This paper integrates these advancements in RL with LLM-based workflow generation to address the computational inefficiencies and generalization limitations observed in prior attempts at automated pipeline design. It positions itself as an advancement that enables the generation of genuinely novel and high-quality text-to-image workflows by formulating the problem as an RL task, thereby expanding the potential for creativity and adaptability in generative AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and robust framework for the automated generation of text-to-image workflows that are both high-quality and novel, aligning with human preferences. The authors aim to overcome critical limitations identified in existing automated workflow design approaches.

The motivations for this work stem from several challenges:

- **Computational Inefficiency of Existing Automation:** Prior methods, such as ComfyGen, required extensive computation during training. They relied on generating images for hundreds or thousands of candidate workflows to obtain quality scores, a process that can take minutes per image. This substantial computational cost restricted the size and diversity of training datasets, hindering the scalability and comprehensive exploration of the workflow space. The authors sought to eliminate this bottleneck by finding a more efficient way to evaluate workflow quality during training.

- **Limited Novelty and Generalization:** A significant drawback of previous LLM-based workflow generation systems was their inability to produce genuinely novel workflow structures. These systems often functioned more as classifiers, interpolating or selecting from a predefined set of workflows rather than synthesizing original graph topologies or selecting new combinations of models. This limitation constrained the creative potential and adaptability of the automated design process, preventing the models from exploring uncharted, potentially superior, workflow configurations. The motivation was to enable the LLM to invent and explore unseen workflow designs.

- **Complexity of Workflow Design:** Manually designing optimal multi-component text-to-image pipelines requires considerable expertise, extensive experimentation, and a deep understanding of how various components (e.g., diffusion models, ControlNets, LoRAs) interact and affect final image quality under different prompts. Automating this process could democratize access to advanced generative capabilities and reduce the expert burden.

- **Leveraging Reinforcement Learning for Exploration:** The authors are motivated by the success of reinforcement learning in fine-tuning LLMs to optimize outputs based on reward signals. They hypothesize that by framing workflow generation as an RL problem, the LLM can be guided to explore a vast space of possible workflows, learn from successful configurations, and generate novel, high-performing structures more effectively than supervised approaches alone.

To address these motivations, the paper introduces a framework that incorporates a two-phase training strategy, a surrogate reward model to bypass costly image generation, an ensemble approach to mitigate reward hacking, and a component-aware reward formulation for granular feedback, all optimized using GRPO.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named FlowRL, addresses the inefficiencies and generalization limitations of prior workflow generation methods through a two-phase training strategy combined with a novel reward modeling approach and inference-time guidance.

**Overall Pipeline Overview (Figure 1):**
The training pipeline consists of two main stages:
1. **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** An LLM is initially fine-tuned on a dataset of prompt-flow pairs to learn the structure and vocabulary of workflows.
2. **Stage 2: Reward-based Policy Optimization:** The SFT-trained LLM is further optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), guided by a surrogate reward model that predicts human preference scores for prompt-flow combinations.

**Training Data:**
The model utilizes a dataset derived from ComfyGen, comprising human-created workflows augmented with randomly sampled parameters for blocks like base models, LoRAs, samplers, steps, and guidance scales. For Stage 1, the dataset is significantly expanded to 2,000 variants per baseline flow structure (compared to ComfyGen's 100) and paired with 10,000 prompts from CivitAI.com. For Stage 2, a subset containing 100,000 prompt-flow-score triplets is used for training the surrogate reward model.

**Efficient Flow Representation Scheme:**
Recognizing that direct JSON representation of ComfyUI workflows is verbose (thousands of tokens), the authors propose a custom structured encoding scheme. This scheme uses specialized tokens for key workflow elements like node names, model choices, and connections, explicitly representing relevant components while significantly reducing token count. This approach reduced the average token length per workflow by 86.7% (from ~1500 to ~200 tokens), enabling a 16x increase in batch size and a 3x improvement in training time, which is critical for memory-intensive algorithms like GRPO.

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
A Meta Llama3.1-8B LLM is fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) on the expanded dataset of prompt-flow pairs. The objective is to teach the LLM to generate syntactically correct and structurally diverse workflows conditioned on input prompts. This stage focuses on vocabulary acquisition and structural pattern recognition without requiring image generation or quality scores. The model achieves a perplexity of 1.9 on the flow dataset, indicating strong alignment with workflow structural patterns.

**Stage 2: Reward-based Policy Optimization:**
The SFT-trained LLM is further optimized to align workflow generation with human preferences using GRPO. The core challenge here is efficiently scoring candidate workflows without generating images.

- **Surrogate Reward Model Training:**
 - To avoid computationally expensive image generation, an ensemble of surrogate reward models, denoted R\_phi, is trained. Each model uses a ModernBert backbone with a custom output head that maps the CLS token to a predicted human-preference score.
 - The models are trained on prompt-flow-score triplets from the ComfyGen dataset, using Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss to predict the human preference score for the image generated by a given prompt-flow pair.

- **Component-Aware Hybrid Reward Formulation:**
 - To provide more granular feedback during policy optimization, a hybrid reward function is introduced. This function assigns a reward to each token *t* in a generated flow.
 - It combines the overall predicted score of the full flow R\_phi(p, f) with a prefix-prediction score R\_prefix\_phi(p, f[1:j]). The prefix-prediction model is separately trained to predict image scores from *randomly sampled prefixes* of flows, allowing it to attribute credit to specific components as they are constructed.

- **Uncertainty-Aware Reinforcement Learning:**
 - To mitigate reward hacking, a known issue with surrogate reward models, an ensemble of N=7 surrogate models is trained, each on a different data split.
 - This ensemble provides a mean prediction (μ) and an uncertainty estimate (σ) for any prompt-flow pair.
 - An uncertainty-aware reward function is defined: R(p, f) = μ(p, f) if σ(p, f) ≤ τ (a threshold), otherwise 0. This pessimistic approach assigns zero reward to workflows where the ensemble's prediction is unreliable, preventing the LLM from exploiting weaknesses in individual surrogate models.

- **GRPO Fine-Tuning:**
 - The LLM from Stage 1 is fine-tuned using GRPO, which is a memory-efficient RL algorithm that optimizes policies by comparing responses within groups of similar prompts, eliminating the need for a separate value function.
 - The uncertainty-aware, component-aware hybrid reward function guides the GRPO process, driving the LLM to generate workflows that are predicted to yield higher-quality images according to human preferences.

**Dual Model Guidance:**
During inference, a novel technique inspired by classifier-free guidance (CFG) is employed. This method interpolates the logits of the policy-optimized model (M\_GRPO from Stage 2) and its under-trained SFT version (M\_SFT from Stage 1). By extrapolating along the path between these two models with a guidance strength (γ), the system can further improve output quality, often at the cost of some diversity, by pushing generations towards regions favored by the more performant model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to validate FlowRL demonstrate its capabilities in generating novel, high-quality workflows, outperforming existing baselines across multiple evaluation metrics.

**Prompt Adherence (GenEval Benchmark):**
FlowRL achieves an overall GenEval score of 0.61, matching the best-performing baseline, ComfyGen, despite not being explicitly trained for prompt adherence. Notably, FlowRL surpasses other methods in the "two objects" (0.85 vs. 0.82 for ComfyGen) and "binding" (0.38 vs. 0.29 for ComfyGen) categories, indicating an improved ability to handle complex compositional prompts.

**Visual Quality (HPS v2 and User Study):**
- **HPS v2 Win Rate:** FlowRL significantly outperforms prior methods in automated preference evaluation using HPS v2. When compared head-to-head against ComfyGen, FlowRL wins approximately 60% of the comparisons. Against other baselines like SDXL, JuggernautXL, and GenArtist, FlowRL's win rate is substantially higher, indicating superior visual quality according to this metric (e.g., SDXL wins only 2% against FlowRL, meaning FlowRL wins 98%).
- **User Study:** A human preference study involving 35 randomly sampled prompts revealed that users preferred images generated by FlowRL in approximately 60% of cases when compared to ComfyGen, confirming the automated metric's findings.

**Novelty of Generated Flows:**
A key claim of FlowRL is its ability to generate genuinely novel workflows.
- ComfyGen was found to produce flows where 94% existed "as-is" in its training data, with only 7% uniqueness and a normalized Levenshtein distance (NLD) ratio of 0, indicating limited novelty.
- FlowRL, without dual-model guidance, achieved a 41% uniqueness ratio, with only 1% of generated flows existing in the training data, and an NLD ratio of 0.6.
- With dual-model guidance (γ=1.5), FlowRL maintained a 41% uniqueness ratio and 0% overlap with training data, with an NLD ratio of 0.74, demonstrating its capacity for generalization to new parameter combinations and graph structures. At higher guidance (γ=2), uniqueness dropped to 8%, but the NLD ratio further improved to 0.75, suggesting a concentration on a smaller set of structurally diverse but high-performing flows.

**Effects of Dual Model Guidance:**
The dual-model guidance mechanism at inference time was shown to trade diversity for performance. Increasing the guidance strength (γ) from 1.5 to 2 improved the HPS v2 win-rate against ComfyGen from 60% to 63%. However, this also led to a reduction in the uniqueness ratio of generated flows from 41% to 8%, indicating that stronger guidance favors a more consistent subset of high-performing workflows rather than broader exploration.

**Ablation Study:**
The ablation study confirmed the contribution of each component to FlowRL's overall performance.
- The full FlowRL model consistently outperformed all ablated variants.
- Removing the initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage resulted in a 0% win rate against ComfyGen, highlighting the critical role of pre-training for structural understanding.
- The "prefix reward" component proved beneficial, emphasizing the importance of granular reward attribution.
- Training ComfyGen with the novel encoding scheme but without RL (ComfyGen + encoded) achieved only a 37% win rate against the original ComfyGen, demonstrating that the encoding improvements work synergistically with the GRPO training, rather than being sufficient on their own.

**Reward Model Generalization (Appendix A.6):**
The surrogate reward model showed good generalization capabilities on unseen data, achieving an R^2 score of 0.643 and a Pearson correlation of 0.816. It performed very well on scenarios with only parameter or prompt changes (Pearson 0.928) and acceptably on novel graph structures using seen components (Pearson 0.667). Performance significantly decreased for flows containing entirely unseen components or tokens (Pearson 0.152), indicating a limitation in handling completely new elements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the automated design of text-to-image generation pipelines, moving beyond the limitations of prior approaches.

**Significant Contributions:**
- **Novelty and Generalization:** FlowRL is introduced as the first reinforcement learning-based approach capable of generating genuinely novel ComfyUI workflows, diverging from methods that primarily classify or interpolate existing structures. This enhances the creative capacity and adaptability of automated generative systems.
- **Computational Efficiency:** By implementing a surrogate human-preference reward model, the framework eliminates the need for costly image generation during the bulk of RL training. This significantly reduces computational requirements and enables the use of larger datasets and more extensive policy optimization.
- **Robust Policy Optimization:** The integration of GRPO with a component-aware, uncertainty-aware hybrid reward formulation offers a stable and memory-efficient method for policy optimization. The ensemble-based uncertainty estimation effectively mitigates reward hacking, enhancing the reliability of the learned policies.
- **Superior Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate that FlowRL achieves superior image quality and prompt adherence compared to existing monolithic models and prior pipeline construction approaches. The human preference studies and objective metrics consistently favor FlowRL's outputs.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Democratization of Advanced Generative AI:** By automating the complex task of workflow design, FlowRL could make advanced, multi-component text-to-image generation more accessible to users without specialized expertise.
- **Accelerated Research and Development:** Researchers and developers could more rapidly iterate on new generative ideas by leveraging automated pipeline design, streamlining the integration of independently trained, specialized modules. This could foster collaborative innovation within the generative AI community.
- **Enhanced Creative Applications:** The ability to generate novel workflows offers new avenues for creative expression and design, potentially leading to the discovery of unforeseen combinations of generative components and functionalities.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Domain Specificity:** The current approach is focused exclusively on text-to-image workflows and does not support editing tasks or video generation modules.
- **Adaptation to New Components:** Introducing entirely new workflow components or models to the LLM would necessitate retraining the entire system, which can be resource-intensive. Future work aims to explore more efficient adaptation mechanisms for novel models or blocks.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The reliance on community-created, fine-tuned models, which may not have undergone rigorous safety assessments, introduces risks related to the generation of harmful or misleading content. The authors suggest mitigation strategies such as careful curation of training models or black-listing unsafe models in output flows. Future research could integrate safety-aligned reward models to guide generations away from undesirable content.
