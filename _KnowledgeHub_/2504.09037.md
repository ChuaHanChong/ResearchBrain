# A Survey of Frontiers in LLM Reasoning: Inference Scaling, Learning to Reason, and Agentic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Salesforce AI Research, National University of Singapore, Nanyang Technological University, and A*STAR present a comprehensive survey that introduces an orthogonal categorization framework for LLM reasoning research. The survey identifies a shift towards learning-to-reason methods and agentic systems, highlighting major trends and algorithms from Chain-of-Thought prompting to advanced training paradigms.

## Problem

- The rapidly accelerating and fragmented landscape of LLM reasoning research, especially since the advent of Chain-of-Thought prompting, lacks a systematic and unified understanding.
- A need exists to categorize diverse reasoning methodologies and identify overarching trends to provide clarity for future research directions.
- The evolving nature of LLM capabilities requires a structured review to consolidate knowledge on inference scaling, learning to reason, and agentic systems.

## Method

- The paper introduces a novel ==two-dimensional, orthogonal categorization framework== that classifies LLM reasoning approaches by 'Regimes' (Inference Scaling vs. Learning to Reason) and 'Architectures' (Standalone LLM vs. Agentic Systems).
- Each dimension is further examined through 'Input Level' and 'Output Level' perspectives, providing a fine-grained analysis of techniques across different reasoning paradigms.
- The survey formalizes LLM reasoning within a ==Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework==, defining key components like ==Reasoner, Verifier, and Refiner==, and outlining common architectural patterns for agentic systems.

## Results

- The framework systematically maps techniques, revealing that many inference-scaling methods for trajectory collection can be repurposed for learning-to-reason, as seen in milestones like DeepSeek-R1.
- The analysis details the increasing adoption of agentic systems that leverage external environments, tools, and inter-agent communication to refine perceptions and actions, thereby improving reasoning accuracy and autonomy.
- The survey synthesizes the application and evolution of key learning algorithms (e.g., SFT, PPO, DPO, GRPO) and sophisticated verifier training methodologies (e.g., **ORM**, **PRM**, Generative Verifiers) as crucial for developing robust reasoning abilities and enabling cost-aware training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - There is an observed shift in LLM reasoning research from solely relying on inference-time prompting (inference scaling) towards dedicated training-time approaches (learning to reason) to enhance intrinsic capabilities.
> - Reasoning capabilities are increasingly evolving from single, static LLM operations to dynamic, interactive 'agentic systems,' encompassing both single-agent (tool-augmented) and multi-agent (collaborative) setups.
> - A growing trend focuses on developing highly capable, domain-specific reasoners for areas such as mathematical problem-solving, code generation, and tabular data analysis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reasoning is a core cognitive function investigated across philosophy, psychology, and computer science, encompassing logical inference, problem-solving, and decision-making. With the advancement of large language models (LLMs), reasoning has become a key capability distinguishing advanced AI systems. The research area, often termed LLM reasoning or reasoning language model (RLM), has experienced rapid growth since the introduction of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting in 2022. This surge is further influenced by developments such as ChatGPT's release in late 2022 and the increasing popularity of in-context learning.

Existing research has identified reasoning as challenging due to the need for multi-step processing beyond token-by-token generation, fundamental for intelligence in planning, and promising given recent LLM advancements. While scaling pre-training may not be the optimal solution for improving reasoning, methods like CoT prompting have demonstrated that modifying prompts can elicit step-by-step reasoning processes, enhancing accuracy without additional training.

This survey positions itself within this accelerating landscape by offering a structured perspective that integrates two orthogonal dimensions—reasoning regimes (inference-time vs. training-time) and system architectures (standalone LLM vs. agentic systems)—which have often been treated separately in prior reviews. Previous surveys have typically focused on specific aspects such as informal deductive reasoning, prompting techniques, in-context learning, or inference-time self-improvement. By combining these dimensions, this work aims to provide a unified framework that clarifies the diverse approaches to LLM reasoning and establishes a foundation for future research, particularly by bridging inference scaling and learning-to-reason methods, and by considering standalone LLMs alongside single-agent and multi-agent systems. The survey does not include an analysis of reasoning benchmarks, referring to other works for that specific detail.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this comprehensive survey are detailed as follows:

1. **Systematic Categorization of LLM Reasoning Methods**: The core objective is to categorize existing methods for LLM reasoning along two orthogonal dimensions:
 - **Regimes**: This dimension distinguishes between methods that achieve reasoning at inference time (referred to as "inference scaling") and those that involve dedicated training to enhance reasoning capabilities ("learning to reason").
 - **Architectures**: This dimension differentiates between reasoning processes confined to a single, "standalone LLM" and those involving "agentic compound systems" which may incorporate external tools, multi-agent collaboration, or both.
 This categorization aims to provide a structured understanding of the evolving landscape of LLM reasoning.

2. **Analysis from Input and Output Perspectives**: Within each dimension of regimes and architectures, the survey analyzes two key perspectives:
 - **Input Level**: This focuses on techniques for constructing high-quality prompts that LLMs condition on, or, in agentic systems, how perceptions are acquired and processed. For multi-agent systems, this extends to communication protocols.
 - **Output Level**: This perspective examines methods that refine multiple sampled candidates, aggregate outputs, or coordinate actions to enhance reasoning quality.

3. **Provide a Comprehensive Understanding of Emerging Trends**: The survey is motivated by the rapid acceleration of research in LLM reasoning, particularly since the introduction of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting in 2022. It aims to highlight significant emerging trends, such as the observed shift from inference-scaling techniques to learning-to-reason approaches (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) and the transition towards agentic workflows (e.g., OpenAI Deep Research, Manus Agent).

4. **Cover a Broad Spectrum of Learning Algorithms and Agentic Designs**: A detailed understanding requires covering various learning algorithms, from supervised fine-tuning to reinforcement learning (e.g., PPO, GRPO), as well as the training methodologies for reasoners and verifiers. The survey also examines key designs of agentic workflows, ranging from established patterns like generator-evaluator and LLM debate to recent innovations.

5. **Identify Open Challenges and Future Directions**: By systematically reviewing the field, the survey seeks to identify unresolved issues and emerging areas for future research. This includes challenges related to evaluation, data quality, and the development of domain-specific reasoning systems.

6. **Establish a Foundational Resource**: The overarching motivation is to equip AI researchers and practitioners with a comprehensive foundation. This foundation is intended to support the advancement of reasoning in LLMs, thereby paving the way for the development of more sophisticated, reliable, and intelligent AI systems that can move beyond conventional instruction-following to approximate Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). The need for reasoning is underscored by its challenging nature, its fundamental role in intelligence, and the promising recent advances in LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This survey systematically analyzes the landscape of LLM reasoning by organizing existing methods along two orthogonal dimensions: "Regimes" and "Architectures." Within each of these dimensions, techniques are further examined from "Input" and "Output" perspectives. The survey also delineates key components of an LLM reasoning system and foundational learning algorithms.

#### 3.1. Core Categorization Framework

1. **Reasoning Regimes**: This dimension defines *when* reasoning is achieved:
 - **Inference Scaling**: Techniques that enhance reasoning capabilities during test time by increasing computation before generating an answer. This involves optimizing reasoning without altering model parameters, often by selecting optimal trajectories.
 - **Learning to Reason**: Approaches that improve reasoning capabilities through dedicated training. This shifts computational burden from inference to training by generating and learning from simulated reasoning trajectories using online or offline learning methods.

2. **Reasoning System Architectures**: This dimension defines *what components* are involved in the reasoning process:
 - **Standalone LLM Systems**: Comprise a single LLM that may act as one or more components (reasoner, verifier, refiner). These systems operate independently without external interactions or collaboration, relying on input-output mappings or iterative self-contained reasoning.
 - **Agentic Systems**: LLM-based systems exhibiting interactiveness with an external world or other agents, and a degree of autonomy (e.g., planning). Agentic systems are further divided into:
 - **Single-Agent Systems**: A single LLM interacts with its environment via tools (e.g., external knowledge bases, verifiers, code interpreters) to refine reasoning, actions, and perceptions. Predefined interaction patterns like generator-evaluator and generator-critic-refiner are common.
 - **Multi-Agent Systems**: Multiple agents interact and communicate with each other, taking on distinct roles, exchanging messages, and coordinating actions to achieve shared goals within a common environment. Patterns like debate and reconcile are observed.

#### 3.2. Unified Perspectives (Input and Output)

Across both regimes and architectures, techniques are analyzed through:

- **Input Level**: Focuses on constructing effective prompts or, for agents, managing perceptions from the environment and communication between agents.
- **Output Level**: Involves optimizing and refining outputs, searching for solutions, or coordinating actions to produce a final result.

#### 3.3. Foundational Concepts

- **Problem Formulation**: LLM reasoning is often framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), treating reasoning as a sequential decision-making process. The definition of a "reasoning step" or "thought" varies, from individual tokens to entire responses. The objective is to maximize cumulative rewards.
- **Key Components of LLM Reasoning Systems**:
 - **Reasoner**: The policy model that generates reasoning steps based on the current state.
 - **Verifier**: Evaluates the correctness of final outcomes and/or intermediate reasoning steps, providing feedback (scalar reward or natural language). Can be outcome-level (ORM) or process-level (PRM).
 - **Refiner**: Modifies or improves reasoning trajectories based on feedback from the verifier.
- **Learning Algorithms**:
 - **Imitation Learning (Supervised Fine-tuning - SFT)**: Maximizes log probabilities of next tokens given inputs and previous tokens, using human-written or AI-generated reasoning processes.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Optimizes policies by maximizing expected cumulative rewards in an MDP. Variants discussed include Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), REINFORCE, RLOO (REINFORCE Leave-One-Out), and Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Actions can be defined at token, step, or full response levels.
 - **Preference Learning**: Aligns LLMs with human preferences, often using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its variants, by directly modeling human reward preferences without a separate reward model.
- **Learning of Verifiers and Reward Models**:
 - **Outcome Reward Models (ORM)**: Trained to provide a scalar reward for a full trajectory, typically using binary classification or pairwise Bradley-Terry loss.
 - **Process Reward Models (PRM)**: Assess correctness at each step in a solution, requiring fine-grained labels. Training often involves Monte Carlo Tree Search to automatically obtain step-level annotations.
 - **Generative Verifiers**: Produce natural language feedback alongside scores, acting as critique models or LLM-as-judge models. They can be fine-tuned via SFT or RLHF, or be hybrid discriminative-generative models.

This structured approach allows the survey to systematically analyze a wide range of reasoning techniques and highlight their interconnections and evolutionary paths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey provides a comprehensive overview of techniques for LLM reasoning, categorizing them along two primary dimensions: reasoning regimes (inference scaling vs. learning to reason) and system architectures (standalone LLM vs. agentic systems). Within these categories, various methods are detailed from input and output perspectives. The paper also identifies key trends and open challenges.

#### 4.1. Improving Reasoning with Inference Scaling

##### 4.1.1. Standalone LLMs

- **Prompt Engineering**: Techniques focus on eliciting latent reasoning abilities.
 - *Instruction Engineering*: Modifies instructions using human-designed templates (e.g., "think step by step") or automated optimization (e.g., Automatic Prompt Engineer - APE).
 - *Demonstration Engineering*: Guides LLMs to generate exemplars (analogical prompting) or retrieves relevant demonstrations (similarity- or diversity-based retrieval) for in-context learning. Many-shot ICL further scales demonstration quantity.
 - *Prompt Optimization*: Employs algorithms like Genetic Prompt Search or OPRO to iteratively refine prompts based on feedback.
- **Search and Planning**: Optimizes reasoning outputs by structuring the problem-solving process.
 - *Generating Subtasks*: Decomposes complex problems into intermediate steps, pioneered by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Zero-shot CoT. Advanced methods explicitly guide decomposition or build robust reasoning structures like Self-Discover.
 - *Exploration and Search*: Uses dynamic search through multiple reasoning paths (branching) and aggregation/evaluation strategies (ensemble-based, verifier-based) to explore ambiguous solutions, exemplified by Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT) and Graph-of-Thoughts (GoT).

##### 4.1.2. Single-Agent Systems

These systems augment LLMs with external interaction (ReAct framework).
- **Refinement with Verifiers and Reflections**: Agents use verifiers (oracle or non-oracle, ORMs or PRMs) to assess output quality and guide modifications. Generative verifiers provide natural language feedback (critique models, LLM-as-judge). Self-reflection aims for self-correction without external verifiers.
- **Enhancement through Retrieval and Tool Utilization**: Agents retrieve external knowledge (e.g., Chain-of-Knowledge, Think-on-Graph) to reduce hallucinations and provide accurate, fact-based responses. They also use specialized tools (e.g., calculators, compilers, APIs) to handle specific subtasks and improve numerical accuracy (e.g., SCIAGENT, MATHSENSEI).

##### 4.1.3. Multi-Agent Systems

These systems introduce agent-agent communication and coordinated actions.
- **Designing Communication Patterns**: Multiple agents engage in debates (e.g., RECONCILE, SocraSynth) or share information through centralized controllers or shared message pools to reach consensus or explore diverse perspectives.
- **Coordinating Action**: Agents chain outputs (e.g., Mixture-of-Agents - MoA) or dynamically adapt actions based on task requirements (e.g., Magentic-One, EVOAGENT).

#### 4.2. Learning Algorithms (Foundational)

- **Learning of Reasoner**:
 - *Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)*: Trains policy models to imitate human-written or AI-generated reasoning processes.
 - *Reinforcement Learning (RL)*: Models text generation as an MDP to maximize cumulative rewards. Approaches vary by action granularity (token, step, full response) and include PPO, REINFORCE, RLOO, and GRPO.
 - *Preference Learning*: Aligns LLMs with human preferences. DPO and its variants optimize policies directly from preference datasets, bypassing the need for a separate reward model.
- **Learning of Verifiers and Reward Models**:
 - *Outcome Reward Models (ORM)*: Provide scalar rewards for full trajectories, often trained with binary classification or pairwise loss (e.g., Bradley-Terry).
 - *Process Reward Models (PRM)*: Assess correctness for each step, typically trained using step-wise cross-entropy loss with fine-grained or automatically generated annotations (e.g., MCTS-based).
 - *Generative Verifiers*: Produce natural language feedback. These include critique models (trained with SFT or RLHF on intentionally incorrect outputs) and LLM-as-judge models (trained on preference datasets). Hybrid models combine generative and discriminative aspects.

#### 4.3. Learning to Reason (Training-Time Methods)

This regime shifts focus to training models to reason before deployment, often by simulating inference to generate trajectories for training.

##### 4.3.1. Standalone LLMs

- **Constructing Prompts**:
 - *Question Augmentation*: Uses frontier LLMs to "evolve" existing prompt sets for depth and breadth.
 - *Knowledge Graph-based Synthesis*: Leverages knowledge graphs to synthesize challenging questions with broader coverage.
- **Collecting Trajectories**:
 - *Rejection Sampling*: Selects high-quality samples by filtering outputs that don't meet quality standards based on ground-truth labels.
 - *Special Reasoning Patterns*: Encourages human-like behaviors (self-reflection, thinking-before-action) by synthesizing trajectories (e.g., Reasoning-as-Planning - RAP).
 - *Reasoning Distillation*: Distills reasoning patterns from stronger models (e.g., OpenAI o1) into smaller models.
- **Training from Trajectories**:
 - *SFT*: Fine-tunes LLMs on collected trajectories, with focus on data scaling or high-quality small datasets.
 - *Preference Learning & RL*: Uses DPO with preference pairs from code test cases or outcome correctness. RL with verifiable answers (e.g., GRPO) also shows promise.
 - *Latent Reasoning*: Aims to reduce inference time by representing reasoning implicitly, either by omitting intermediate tokens or compressing them into specialized tokens (e.g., `<pause>` tokens).

##### 4.3.2. Single-Agent Systems

- **Trajectory Collection**:
 - *Execution Feedback*: Leverages unit tests or proof assistants (e.g., Lean) to validate solutions and filter trajectories (e.g., NExT, AlphaProof).
 - *Training External Models*: Agents train auxiliary models (e.g., critics, refiners) from environmental interactions.
 - *Reasoning Search with Verifiers*: Uses MCTS or LLM-as-judge to guide decoding and identify better reasoning trajectories, even in unsupervised settings.
 - *Trajectory Distillation*: Curates interactive tool-use trajectories from stronger models (e.g., GPT-4) for mathematical and scientific reasoning, or program synthesis (e.g., CoGEX).
- **Agent Training**:
 - *SFT*: Fine-tunes agents on collected agent-environment interaction trajectories.
 - *RL*: Uses verifiable outcome rewards (GRPO) or trained PRMs during PPO training for significant performance gains.
 - *Learning with Refiner*: Trains models to iteratively refine responses, mitigating issues like unintended degradation of correct solutions.

##### 4.3.3. Multi-Agent Systems

- **Designing Communication**: Centralized controllers or shared message pools facilitate inter-agent awareness and learned protocols (e.g., MARCO, ReConcile).
- **Coordinating Actions**: Incorporates expert knowledge, graph-based coordination strategies (GCS, DICG), or hierarchical approaches (HAVEN) to improve efficiency.
- **Training from Trajectories**: Uses multi-round debates (DEBATUNE) or multi-agent interactions to generate diverse training data, fostering specialization and improving collective performance (Acc-Debate). Gradient modification can guide agents towards stable cooperation.

#### 4.4. Toward Cost-aware and Inference-aware Training

- **Cost-aware Training**: Focuses on optimizing the trade-off between computational cost and performance. This includes dynamically allocating inference budgets based on query difficulty (Damani et al.), predicting optimal inference strategies, and data-efficient training with high-quality, small datasets.
- **Inference-aware Training**: Integrates inference procedures into training. For example, explicitly considering Best-of-N (BoN) decoding during alignment or using RL to optimize BoN directly.

#### 4.5. Observed Trends

1. **From Inference Scaling to Learning to Reason**: Initial reliance on inference scaling (CoT, self-consistency) is evolving towards reasoning-specific training. While inference scaling complements learning, the field is moving towards data-driven approaches like SFT, knowledge distillation, and sophisticated RL (e.g., Deepseek-R1's pure RL with outcome supervision) for more profound improvements.
2. **From Standalone LLMs to Agentic Systems**: Reasoning is increasingly viewed as an interactive process, with agents interacting with external environments and other agents. While current agent autonomy is largely confined to planning, future developments may involve meta-level planning and proactiveness in seeking clarification.
3. **Domain-Specific Reasoners**: Given the challenges of general-purpose reasoners, a trend towards domain-specific models is observed, particularly in:
 - *Mathematical Reasoning*: Both "informal" (fine-tuning on problem-solving datasets, e.g., NuminaMath) and "formal" (integrating with proof assistants, e.g., AlphaProof, AlphaGeometry) approaches are advancing.
 - *Code Generation*: Evolving from function-level completion to competition-level programming via RL and preference learning with test cases and compiler feedback (e.g., AlphaCode, DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI's o3).
 - *Tabular Reasoning*: Involves transforming structured data for LLM processing (serialization, prompt engineering) and specialized tool use, but faces challenges with diverse feature types, robustness, and hallucinations.
 - *Reasoning in Multi-Agent Games*: Employs concepts like Theory-of-Mind (ToM) and RL to enhance strategic social reasoning in collaborative and competitive scenarios.
 - *Reward Modeling and Evaluation as a Reasoning Task*: The evaluation itself is becoming a reasoning problem, with increasing reliance on automated feedback mechanisms (tree search, LLM-as-judge) to overcome annotation costs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey significantly contributes to the field of LLM reasoning by providing a structured and comprehensive overview of its evolving landscape. Its primary significance lies in clarifying and unifying the diverse research efforts across two orthogonal dimensions: reasoning regimes (inference-time vs. training-time) and system architectures (standalone LLM vs. agentic systems). This systematic categorization, combined with an analysis from input and output perspectives, offers a foundational framework that helps researchers understand the interconnections between various techniques and identify frontiers.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:

1. **Guidance for Researchers and Practitioners**: By consolidating a vast array of methods, learning algorithms, and architectural designs, the survey serves as a valuable resource. It provides a clear roadmap for AI researchers to navigate the complexities of LLM reasoning and for practitioners to develop more sophisticated and reliable AI systems.
2. **Highlighting Key Trends and Driving Future Research**: The identification of major trends, such as the shift from inference scaling to learning-to-reason and from standalone LLMs to agentic systems, provides critical insights into the field's trajectory. This can inspire future research in areas like inference-aware learning-to-reason, automated multi-agent design, and advanced techniques for self-improvement and self-correction in LLMs.
3. **Addressing Core Challenges for Advanced AI**: The detailed discussion of emerging trends, particularly in domain-specific reasoning (mathematics, code, tabular data, multi-agent games), underscores the increasing specialization and capabilities of LLMs. This can lead to the development of highly capable AI systems for complex, real-world applications in these domains.
4. **Pinpointing Open Problems**: The clear articulation of open challenges—such as robust reasoning evaluation, a deeper understanding of LLM internal reasoning mechanisms, and addressing data scalability and quality issues—is crucial. By outlining these gaps, the survey directs scientific effort toward critical areas that need resolution to push the boundaries of AI capabilities, especially in the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
5. **Refining Evaluation and Understanding**: The emphasis on the challenges of evaluating reasoning beyond mere outcome correctness and the debate on whether LLMs truly "reason" or merely "imitate" highlights the need for more rigorous analytical and empirical methods. Advancements in these areas could lead to more trustworthy and explainable AI systems.
6. **Fostering Interdisciplinary Approaches**: The discussion of formal analysis methods (e.g., using circuit complexity theory, symbolic logic) for understanding LLM reasoning, alongside empirical studies, promotes interdisciplinary approaches. This could lead to a more robust theoretical understanding of how LLMs operate.

In conclusion, this survey acts as a timely reference point in a rapidly advancing field, structuring knowledge, highlighting innovations, and setting a comprehensive agenda for future research, ultimately aiming to advance the intelligence and reliability of LLM-based AI systems.
