# GeneralVLA-2: Geometry-Aware Reconstruction and Governed Memory for Robot Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> GeneralVLA-2 enhances a hierarchical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robot system by integrating GeoFuse-MV3D for geometry-aware 3D object reconstruction from multi-view inputs and a governed KnowledgeBank for reliable long-term memory. These improvements lead to more robust trajectory planning, achieving higher success rates in 10 out of 14 RLBench tasks and successfully completing four real-world manipulation tasks with a Franka Panda robot without additional training.

## Problem

- Monocular 3D object reconstruction methods (like SAM3D) suffer from pose ambiguity and hallucination of unseen geometry, leading to critical errors in robot manipulation tasks.
- Prior long-term memory systems in VLA robots rely solely on semantic similarity for retrieval, which does not guarantee the utility, safety, or geometric applicability of past experiences in new contexts.
- The original GeneralVLA framework's 3DAgent planner faced bottlenecks due to unreliable object geometry and unmanaged reuse of past experiences.

## Method

- ==GeoFuse-MV3D== leverages calibrated multi-view RGB-D observations, geometry-prior guidance, mask verification, and conservative fusion to generate more stable and faithful 3D object geometry.
- A governed KnowledgeBank stores structured manipulation records, enriched with metadata such as quality scores, confidence, lifecycle state, conflict links, and geometric applicability, managed by an automated verifier module.
- The system employs a precision-oriented retrieval mechanism and lifecycle management (add, merge, replace, discard, retire) to ensure retrieved memories are trustworthy, contextually relevant, and safe for planning.

## Results

- GeoFuse-MV3D improved 3D object reconstruction on the GSO-30 benchmark, achieving a **2.20%** decrease in Chamfer Distance and a **2.36%** increase in PSNR compared to the MV-SAM3D baseline.
- The governed KnowledgeBank increased success rates on agent benchmarks (e.g., **4.53%** average gain over ReasoningBank on Terminal-Bench) and reduced average steps to completion by **4.95%** on Terminal-Bench.
- GeneralVLA-2 achieved higher success rates in 10 out of 14 RLBench simulation tasks and successfully completed all four real-world language-conditioned manipulation tasks (e.g., **63.33%** success rate on "move spray bottle"), demonstrating training-free efficacy and real-world transferability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating calibrated multi-view observations with geometry priors and conservative fusion significantly refines 3D object reconstructions, overcoming monocular ambiguities and hallucinated geometry for robot planning.
> - Treating past robot experiences as "governed knowledge" through verifier-derived metadata and explicit lifecycle management substantially improves the reliability and applicability of recalled information, moving beyond simple semantic retrieval.
> - By enhancing the inputs to the 3D planning agent with accurate object geometry and trustworthy long-term memory, robotic systems can achieve more robust and efficient planning for complex tasks without extensive end-to-end policy retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems for robotic manipulation. These systems aim to enable robots to understand language instructions, perceive their environment, and execute complex manipulation tasks. GeneralVLA-2 specifically builds upon the hierarchical VLA design introduced by its predecessor, GeneralVLA, which decomposes the robot control problem into affordance perception, 3D trajectory planning, and low-level execution.

In the broader context of 3D object reconstruction, existing single-image methods like SAM3D and Fast-SAM3D can generate plausible 3D objects but are susceptible to monocular pose ambiguity and hallucination of unseen geometry. MV-SAM3D extended this to multi-view inputs. GeneralVLA-2's GeoFuse-MV3D component addresses these limitations by leveraging calibrated multi-view observations to refine 3D object models, using external geometry estimates as priors within a conservative fusion mechanism. This approach contrasts with methods that rely solely on monocular cues or perform less controlled geometry fusion.

Regarding VLA and 3D robot planning, there is a trend towards generalist robot policies, exemplified by systems such as RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, π0, and TinyVLA, which utilize large-scale visual-language and robot data. Concurrently, other lines of research, including Code-as-Policies, VoxPoser, and Manipulate-Anything, focus on using foundation models to generate intermediate plans or 3D representations. GeneralVLA-2 aligns with this latter approach by maintaining an interpretable intermediate 3D trajectory and focusing on improving the evidence used by the 3D planning agent, rather than end-to-end policy learning.

For long-horizon agents, the reuse of past experience is a known benefit, demonstrated by systems like Synapse, Agent Workflow Memory, and ReasoningBank. However, these systems often rely on semantic similarity for memory retrieval, which does not inherently guarantee the utility, safety, or geometric applicability of the retrieved knowledge in a new context. The research incorporates principles from verifier-oriented methods and LLM-as-a-judge frameworks, which provide scalable signals for evaluating trajectories and assigning confidence. GeneralVLA-2 extends this by integrating verifier signals as persistent metadata within its KnowledgeBank, moving beyond simple semantic retrieval to a governed memory system that explicitly manages quality, confidence, lifecycle, and conflict resolution. This positions the work as an advancement in robust, knowledge-guided robot planning within complex, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to enhance the reliability and effectiveness of hierarchical VLA systems for robotic manipulation, specifically by addressing two bottlenecks identified in the original GeneralVLA framework. These improvements are designed to strengthen the inputs provided to the 3DAgent, which is responsible for trajectory planning.

The first key objective is to improve the reliability of object reconstruction from robot observations.
- **Motivation:** Existing monocular SAM3D-style reconstruction methods, while capable of generating 3D objects from a single image and mask, often suffer from monocular pose ambiguity and the hallucination of unseen backside structures. For robotic manipulation, such inaccuracies in object pose or shape can lead to critical errors, impacting grasping points, clearance paths, or collision avoidance, thereby compromising the success and safety of robot operations.
- **Opportunity:** The authors identify that when calibrated multi-view RGB-D observations are available—a common scenario for robots equipped with multiple cameras or capable of short exploratory movements—they can significantly reduce the reliance on monocular hallucination. The challenge lies in integrating these observations conservatively to avoid undesirable outcomes like missing geometry, over-shrinking objects, or unintended color/opacity changes, which could adversely affect downstream planning. The goal is to produce more stable and faithful object geometry.

The second key objective is to improve the reliability and applicability of past manipulation experience for reuse in new tasks.
- **Motivation:** The original KnowledgeBank in GeneralVLA stored natural-language memories based on semantic similarity alone. This approach does not guarantee that a retrieved memory is appropriate for the current scene. Issues arise where semantically similar memories might be unsafe, outdated, contradictory, or geometrically irrelevant to the current object configuration or task. As memory grows, managing its quality and preventing accumulation of unhelpful or harmful information becomes critical.
- **Opportunity:** The authors propose treating past manipulation experience as "governed knowledge" rather than an unmanaged, append-only cache. This involves enriching each memory item with explicit metadata about its origin, reliability, current status (active, stale), potential conflicts, and geometric applicability. The motivation is to enable intelligent control over memory quality, ensuring that retrieved knowledge is trustworthy and relevant, thus allowing the planning agent to make more informed decisions and generate more reliable trajectories.

In summary, the core motivation is to provide the 3DAgent planner with both more accurate, real-time object geometry and more trustworthy, vetted long-term manipulation knowledge, thereby making robot planning more robust and efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeneralVLA-2 maintains the hierarchical structure of GeneralVLA, with specific enhancements to its object-centric 3D perception and long-term memory systems. The improved pipeline involves converting language and RGB-D observations into 3D end-effector paths, with the refined object geometry and governed memory conditioning the 3D-capable planning agent.

#### 3.1. GeoFuse-MV3D Reconstruction Branch

The GeoFuse-MV3D branch is introduced to provide refined object-centric 3D evidence when calibrated multi-view RGB-D observations of an object are available. It builds upon MV-SAM3D by incorporating geometry-prior guidance, mask verification, and conservative fusion.

1. **Input and Baseline:** The branch takes multi-view RGB observations, object masks, camera intrinsics, and camera poses as input. It starts from an initial Gaussian object representation produced by MV-SAM3D, which consists of 3D Gaussian centers and non-geometric rendering attributes (opacity, scale, rotation, spherical-harmonic appearance).
2. **Two Geometry Sources:**
 - **Source A (Geometry-Prior Main Provider):** This source refines the baseline MV-SAM3D output by incorporating an external 3D geometry-prior provider (instantiated with VGGT). It applies a lightweight appearance affine calibration to maintain visual consistency.
 - **Source B (Provider-Free Axis Compensation):** This is an independent branch that does not rely on an external prior. It applies low-dimensional axis-wise corrections (scale and shift) to the Gaussian centers based solely on the input masks and poses. This adjustment aims to improve mask agreement while staying close to the original MV-SAM3D geometry.
3. **Mask-Consistency Score and Soft Visual Hull Refinement:** For any 3D point, a mask-consistency score `s(p)` is calculated based on its projection into all input views and the corresponding mask values. Instead of performing hard deletion of points with low support, GeoFuse-MV3D applies a "soft visual hull" refinement. Low mask support translates into a small, bounded inward geometry correction (`p' = c + (p - c)(1 - λ(p))`), where `c` is the object center and `λ(p)` is a small shrink ratio. This approach aims to regularize geometry without introducing abrupt changes or removing potentially valid parts.
4. **Conservative Geometry Fusion:** When fusing the outputs from Source A and Source B, the system performs a geometry-only blend. This means only the 3D coordinates (Gaussian centers or mesh vertices) are combined (`p_out = (1-α)p_A + αp_B`), while non-geometric attributes such as color, opacity, scale, rotation, and spherical-harmonic appearance are preserved from Source A (the trusted source). This conservative approach prevents unintended appearance drift or artifacts during geometry refinement.

#### 3.2. Governed KnowledgeBank Overview

The original append-oriented KnowledgeBank is upgraded to a governed long-term memory system designed to provide more controlled and reliable experience reuse.

1. **Structured Memory Records:** Each reusable manipulation lesson is stored as a structured record `m`. This record includes the source query, reusable content, memory type (e.g., procedural hint, failure avoidance, tool usage), lifecycle state (provisional, active, summary, archived), confidence, a verifier-derived quality score, usage statistics, conflict links, and detailed verifier metadata. The distinction in memory types is crucial; for instance, a failed trajectory is stored as a constraint for failure avoidance, not as a positive action recipe.
2. **Verifier-Aided Management:** After a task's execution, the system logs the instruction, scene representation, planned trajectory, execution feedback, and outcome. An automated verifier module assesses the quality of any candidate knowledge extracted from this experience. The verifier scores knowledge based on criteria such as task completion, spatial consistency, collision safety, execution validity, and generalizability. Only sufficiently supported candidates, based on an aggregate quality score `R_t`, are considered for admission.
3. **Precision-Oriented Retrieval:** Unlike purely semantic retrieval, the governed KnowledgeBank employs a precision-oriented retrieval mechanism. The similarity score `S(q_t, X_t, m)` for a given query `q_t` and scene `X_t` considers textual relevance, memory confidence, success history, recency, usage statistics, and crucially, penalizes conflicting or stale records. This ensures that retrieved memories are not only semantically relevant but also trustworthy and applicable to the current context.
4. **Lifecycle Management and Consolidation:** The system manages the memory lifecycle through various operations. Provisional memories can be promoted to active. A consolidation gate handles new candidate knowledge by deciding to ADD, MERGE, REPLACE, or DISCARD it based on query/content relevance, signature similarity, and conflict checks. Governance periodically retires stale or failed records, resolves conflicts within memory types, summarizes repeated successful patterns, and archives overflow memories to manage an active-memory budget.
5. **Integration with 3DAgent:** The refined 3D object evidence from GeoFuse-MV3D and the structured, contextually-filtered block from the governed KnowledgeBank are provided as enhanced inputs to the 3DAgent. The 3DAgent then produces a 3D waypoint path, which is consumed by the low-level policy, maintaining compatibility with the original GeneralVLA execution stack.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides experimental evidence across multiple benchmarks to evaluate the contributions of GeoFuse-MV3D and the governed KnowledgeBank.

#### 4.1. GeoFuse-MV3D GSO-30 Evaluation

GeoFuse-MV3D was evaluated on the GSO-30 single-object multi-view benchmark, adhering to the MV-SAM3D protocol (identical object list, five input views, masks, and camera poses, with evaluation on held-out target views).
- **Quantitative Improvements:** GeoFuse-MV3D demonstrated improvements across all four reconstruction metrics compared to the MV-SAM3D baseline. Chamfer Distance (CD) decreased by 2.20%, and LPIPS decreased by 2.02%, indicating better geometric and perceptual fidelity, respectively. PSNR increased by 2.36%, and SSIM increased by 1.03%, suggesting higher image quality and structural similarity in rendered views.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation analysis revealed that the geometry-prior branch was most impactful for CD, while the provider-free axis-compensation branch yielded the strongest gains in appearance metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). The conservative geometry fusion approach, combining both sources, achieved the best overall PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, while maintaining a clear improvement in CD over the baseline. Mask-support verification contributed complementary gains.

#### 4.2. KnowledgeBank Evaluation

The KnowledgeBank module was evaluated independently using Terminal-Bench 2.0 and SWE-Bench Verified, two long-horizon agent benchmarks, to isolate the benefits of memory governance.
- **Performance vs. Baselines:** Across four different LLM backbones (Qwen-3.5-Flash, Qwen-3.5-Plus, Gemini-3-Flash, and Gemini-3.1-Pro), the governed KnowledgeBank consistently outperformed the "no memory" baseline and prior memory systems like ReasoningBank and AWM.
 - On Terminal-Bench, KnowledgeBank improved the success rate (SR) by an average of 4.53% over ReasoningBank.
 - On SWE-Bench, the resolve rate improved by an average of 3.73% over ReasoningBank.
 - The average steps (AS) to completion were reduced by 4.95% on Terminal-Bench and 5.65% on SWE-Bench, indicating improved efficiency.
- **Ablation Study:** The ablation studies confirmed the contribution of individual KnowledgeBank components. Removing governance, admission, replay, or failure handling capabilities individually led to drops in performance. The semantic retrieval variant (without full governance) showed the largest drop relative to the full system, emphasizing the importance of lifecycle management, conflict control, and failure-aware filtering beyond mere textual similarity.
- **Deployment Cost:** While KnowledgeBank added "extra tokens" due to verifier and governance calls, the reduction in average steps (AS) often led to lower total token counts and latency per task compared to prior memory baselines for most backbones, highlighting a potential efficiency gain in complex tasks.

#### 4.3. Training-free Performance for Robotic Planning

The integrated GeneralVLA-2 system was evaluated on 14 RLBench simulation tasks, focusing on training-free performance using a Franka Panda robot with RGB-D observations.
- **Task Coverage and Success:** GeneralVLA-2 successfully generated trajectories for all 14 tasks, outperforming baseline methods like VoxPoser (9 tasks), CAP (7 tasks), and Hamster (10 tasks). It achieved higher success rates in 10 tasks.
- **Impact of KnowledgeBank:** An ablation study showed that removing KnowledgeBank guidance consistently reduced success rates across tasks, confirming that governed experience reuse significantly aids trajectory planning in simulation.

#### 4.4. Real-World Robot Experiments

GeneralVLA-2 was tested on an Agilex-2.0 Piper manipulator with an Intel RealSense L515 RGB-D camera across four language-conditioned real-world tasks (move spray bottle, open drawer, open jar, sort object).
- **Real-World Efficacy:** The system achieved successful task completion on all four real-world tasks, outperforming CAP and RoboPoint. For instance, in the "move spray bottle" task, GeneralVLA-2 achieved a 63.33% success rate, compared to 6.67% for CAP and 0.00% for RoboPoint. This demonstrates the transferability and effectiveness of the proposed geometry-aware reconstruction and governed memory in physical robot deployments without additional training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The contributions of GeneralVLA-2 hold significance for the development of more capable and reliable generalist robotic manipulation systems. By addressing two critical interfaces that condition the 3D planning agent—object geometry and long-term memory—the work advances the state of hierarchical Vision-Language-Action models.

**Enhanced Reliability in 3D Object Perception:** GeoFuse-MV3D’s ability to provide more stable and geometrically consistent 3D object evidence from multi-view inputs is impactful. In robotic manipulation, precise object understanding is paramount. Reducing issues like monocular pose ambiguity and hallucinated unseen geometry, as demonstrated by the improved metrics on GSO-30, directly translates to more accurate grasping, collision avoidance, and spatial reasoning. This fosters robustness in robot tasks, particularly in scenarios where small geometric errors can lead to execution failures. The conservative, appearance-preserving fusion mechanism ensures that geometric refinements do not inadvertently compromise visual fidelity, which can be important for other perception modules.

**Improved Trustworthiness and Applicability of Learned Experience:** The introduction of a governed KnowledgeBank signifies a shift from simple experience recall to intelligent knowledge management. By incorporating explicit metadata for quality, confidence, lifecycle state, conflict detection, and geometric relevance, the system can retrieve not just semantically similar, but genuinely trustworthy and applicable manipulation knowledge. This is crucial for long-horizon planning, where accumulated experiences must guide future actions reliably, even under novel scene conditions. The demonstrated improvements on agent benchmarks like Terminal-Bench and SWE-Bench, as well as in simulation and real-world robot tasks, indicate that this governance reduces agent steps, increases success rates, and enhances overall problem-solving efficiency by preventing the re-use of suboptimal, stale, or conflicting information.

**Advancing Training-Free and Generalizable Robotics:** The strong performance of GeneralVLA-2 in training-free settings on both RLBench simulations and real-world tasks highlights its potential for practical deployment. By improving the inputs to the planner rather than requiring extensive retraining of policies, the system facilitates faster adaptation to new tasks and environments. This paradigm supports the development of more generalizable robots that can leverage existing knowledge and high-fidelity perceptual data to perform novel tasks without prohibitive data collection and learning cycles.

**Potential for Broader Impact:** While the current evaluation focuses on tabletop manipulation, the principles of geometry-aware 3D reconstruction and governed long-term memory are foundational and could be extended to more complex scenarios, such as mobile manipulation, highly occluded environments, or tasks involving deformable objects. The framework’s emphasis on interpretable intermediate representations and controllable knowledge management also opens avenues for improved human-robot interaction and human-in-the-loop recovery mechanisms in future systems.

In conclusion, GeneralVLA-2 represents a significant step towards building more robust, intelligent, and reliable VLA systems for robotics, laying groundwork for agents that can effectively perceive their world and judiciously apply past learning.
