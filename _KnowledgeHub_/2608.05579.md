# ARGUS: Aligning Robot Scene Geometry Under Shifting Views with Large 3D Vision Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ARGUS, developed by researchers from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign and Harvard University, introduces an observation pre-processing pipeline that leverages large 3D vision models to align diverse camera views into a canonical viewpoint. This method improves visuomotor policy generalization and training efficiency across varied datasets, achieving performance comparable to depth-based approaches without requiring specialized hardware.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policies often entangle task-relevant information with specific camera viewpoints, hindering effective learning and generalization to novel camera configurations.
- Existing approaches either require extensive and costly data collection, introduce sim-to-real transfer challenges, or rely on specialized depth sensors which are not universally available.
- Policies trained with fixed viewpoints struggle to generalize, while those trained on unconstrained viewpoints can also face difficulties in effective learning and generalization due to visual complexity.

## Method

- **ARGUS** employs an observation pre-processing pipeline that estimates a 3D point cloud of the scene from multiple RGB images using an off-the-shelf large 3D vision model (==VGGT==).
- The estimated point cloud is ==metrically scaled== and aligned to the robot's base frame using known camera extrinsics to establish a common spatial reference.
- A 2D RGB image of the scene is rendered from a fixed, ==canonical virtual viewpoint== using the aligned point cloud, providing a consistent visual input to the downstream visuomotor policy regardless of the original camera pose.

## Results

- ARGUS consistently achieved higher average success rates and lower standard errors across **five** novel camera configurations, outperforming baselines like KYC and VISTA in both diverse and fixed viewpoint training scenarios.
- The method demonstrated substantial training efficiency gains, achieving a **90%** success rate **6 times** faster than KYC and **4 times** faster than classical Diffusion Policy in terms of dataset passes.
- When integrated with a depth-based policy, ARGUS's RGB-derived point clouds yielded comparable performance to using raw depth data, validating its potential as a hardware-agnostic alternative for viewpoint-robust manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Normalizing observation viewpoints using large 3D vision models significantly reduces the learning burden on visuomotor policies, leading to improved training efficiency and generalization.
> - Learned 3D reconstruction from standard RGB images can provide viewpoint robustness comparable to systems relying on explicit depth sensors, reducing hardware dependency for robot manipulation tasks.
> - Pre-processing to canonicalize observations enables effective learning from both limited-viewpoint and highly diverse, unconstrained viewpoint datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot manipulation policies often face a challenge where the visual representation of a task becomes entangled with specific camera viewpoints. This entanglement hinders the ability of visuomotor policies to learn effectively from diverse datasets and generalize to novel camera configurations not encountered during training. Prior research has identified that policies trained with fixed viewpoints struggle to generalize to new camera placements, while those trained on unconstrained viewpoints can also exhibit difficulties in effective generalization.

Existing approaches to address this issue include:
- **Training on large and diverse datasets:** While successful in some contexts, this strategy can be data-inefficient and costly, as policies are implicitly forced to disentangle task semantics from scene-induced variability.
- **Dataset synthesis/augmentation:** Methods that generate multi-view data in simulation or augment real-world data aim to improve robustness to camera variation. However, simulation-based approaches introduce sim-to-real transfer challenges, and real-world augmentation methods can suffer from visual artifacts and degraded performance under large viewpoint shifts. Evaluations for these methods are often limited to small perturbations around training viewpoints.
- **Camera ray encoding:** Techniques that model 3D scene structure by encoding camera rays (e.g., Plücker coordinates) within visuomotor policies. Some implementations also incorporate compositional data collection strategies to encourage viewpoint invariance and data efficiency. However, these methods often rely on assumptions about viewpoint structure (e.g., overlapping viewpoints in consecutive demonstrations) that are difficult to guarantee in large-scale, real-world datasets where camera viewpoints are typically uncontrolled.
- **3D scene modeling with depth sensors:** Policies that leverage explicit 3D representations (e.g., point clouds, voxel grids) or construct 3D scene representations for rendering into canonical viewpoints. These methods inherently offer improved viewpoint robustness due to their grounding in 3D structure. However, they typically rely on depth sensors, which are less ubiquitous than RGB cameras and can exhibit variable performance across different hardware platforms, posing deployment challenges.

ARGUS presents an alternative approach by focusing on observation pre-processing using large-scale 3D vision models. Instead of training policies to disentangle variations from raw diverse observations, ARGUS simplifies the visual input by transforming arbitrary camera views into a canonical viewpoint. This positions ARGUS as a method that aims to reduce the learning burden on visuomotor policies by providing a viewpoint-normalized input, thereby seeking to enhance data efficiency and generalization without requiring specialized depth hardware or specific data collection patterns. It leverages advancements in large-scale 3D vision models to extract 3D scene structure from standard RGB imagery, potentially offering a more accessible and scalable solution for viewpoint-robust robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observation that visuomotor policies often conflate task-relevant information with variations caused by changes in camera viewpoint. This entanglement means policies learn object locations relative to the image plane rather than within the task space, limiting their ability to learn effectively from viewpoint-diverse datasets and generalize to novel camera configurations. The goal is to develop a method that systematically removes viewpoint-induced variability from observations before they are fed to a visuomotor policy.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a general-purpose viewpoint normalization pipeline:** The central objective is to introduce ARGUS, an observation pre-processing pipeline that utilizes large-scale 3D vision models to align image observations from arbitrary camera viewpoints into a canonical viewpoint. This normalization aims to reduce viewpoint-induced scene variation, thereby enabling more effective policy learning by presenting a consistent visual input regardless of the original camera pose.
2. **Evaluate ARGUS's effectiveness across varying viewpoint diversity:** The study aims to empirically demonstrate that ARGUS improves policy learning performance in diverse data collection paradigms. This involves evaluating its ability to facilitate learning from datasets with both limited, fixed viewpoints (demonstrating zero-shot generalization to unseen viewpoints) and highly diverse, unconstrained viewpoints (demonstrating efficient extraction of useful information from unstructured data).
3. **Assess ARGUS's impact on training efficiency and generalization:** A critical objective is to quantify how ARGUS influences the training time and sample efficiency of downstream visuomotor policies, especially when learning from complex, viewpoint-diverse datasets. The hypothesis is that by simplifying the observation space, ARGUS will reduce the learning burden, leading to faster convergence and better data efficiency.
4. **Compare ARGUS to depth-based methods:** The research seeks to compare ARGUS's performance against methods that inherently derive viewpoint robustness from depth information or 3D scene representations. This comparison aims to evaluate whether learned 3D reconstruction from RGB images, as employed by ARGUS, can serve as a competitive and effective alternative to explicit depth sensing for achieving viewpoint-robust visuomotor learning, thereby reducing reliance on specialized hardware.

In summary, the motivation is to simplify the visual learning problem for robots by decoupling viewpoint from scene understanding, and the objectives are to demonstrate that ARGUS achieves this effectively, efficiently, and with comparable performance to hardware-dependent alternatives, ultimately enhancing robot learning from large-scale, viewpoint-diverse datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ARGUS framework operates as an observation pre-processing pipeline designed to normalize camera viewpoints before observations are passed to a downstream visuomotor policy. It reformulates visuomotor policy learning into two stages: first, normalizing the viewpoint (`h: I → Ĩ`), and then mapping the normalized observation to an action (`f_θ: Ĩ → A`). ARGUS performs the `h` function.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Assumptions

The methodology assumes the availability of an imitation learning dataset captured by at least two calibrated external cameras. The task scene is fixed between training and evaluation, focusing the study on viewpoint generalization. It is also assumed that cameras, regardless of their position, are always pointed towards the task space, but their specific viewpoints are otherwise unrestricted.

#### 3.2. Overview of ARGUS Pipeline

The ARGUS pipeline consists of three sequential steps:
1. **Scene Point Cloud Estimation:** A 3D point cloud of the scene is estimated using a large 3D vision model from multiple input images.
2. **Metric Scaling and World-Frame Alignment:** The estimated point cloud is scaled to metric accuracy and aligned to the robot base frame using known camera extrinsics.
3. **Canonical Viewpoint Rendering:** A 2D RGB image of the scene is rendered from a fixed, canonical virtual viewpoint using the aligned point cloud. This rendered image then serves as input to the downstream visuomotor policy.

#### 3.3. Cross-View Point Cloud Estimation

To address the diverse visual representations from unconstrained camera viewpoints, ARGUS constructs a point cloud of the scene. Instead of relying on depth sensors, an off-the-shelf large 3D vision model, VGGT, is employed. VGGT takes multiple RGB images as input and outputs a predicted point cloud along with corresponding predicted camera extrinsics. It is noted that the VGGT output point cloud and extrinsics are not metrically scaled and are relative to one of the input camera's frames.

#### 3.4. Metric Scaling and World-Frame Alignment

Since VGGT's predicted geometry is only accurate up to an unknown global scale, ARGUS recovers the metric scale. This is achieved by calculating the ratio between the inter-camera baseline in the known real-world extrinsics and the corresponding baseline in the VGGT-estimated extrinsics. The scaled point cloud is then transformed into the robot base frame using the real-world camera extrinsics. For numerical stability, if more than two cameras are present, the pair with the largest real-world baseline is selected for scaling.

#### 3.5. Canonical Viewpoint Rendering

The scaled and world-aligned point cloud is then used to render a 2D RGB image from a fixed virtual canonical viewpoint. This canonical pose is pre-selected to maximize the visibility of the workspace and manipulated objects. Crucially, this viewpoint remains constant throughout both training and evaluation, ensuring that the downstream policy always perceives the scene from the same perspective, irrespective of the actual input camera positions. The resulting canonical image is the input to the downstream visuomotor policy.

#### 3.6. Experimental Design

The effectiveness of ARGUS was evaluated through a series of experiments addressing three core questions:
- Does ARGUS improve learning from datasets with varying viewpoint diversity?
- How does ARGUS impact training time and sample efficiency?
- How does ARGUS compare to depth-based methods?

#### 3.6.1. Tasks and Datasets

Four real-world manipulation tasks were used: "Put Marker In Cup," "Unfold Towel," "Put Brush In Bowl," and "Stack Blocks." These tasks involved relatively small objects, allowing for systematic evaluation of spatial reasoning.
Two types of training datasets were constructed:
- **D_fixed:** Dataset with limited, fixed viewpoints, compiled by fixing two cameras at the head of the table.
- **D_diverse:** Dataset with highly diverse viewpoint positions, created by sampling random camera viewpoints after collecting each demonstration. 100 demonstrations were collected for each task, yielding 200 unique camera positions per task. AprilTags were used for camera extrinsic estimation.
Evaluation was conducted across five different camera configurations, each sampled from a distinct region around the table perimeter. For each of these configurations, policies were tested on 10 random object configurations, resulting in 50 rollouts per method per task.

#### 3.6.2. Baselines and Evaluation Metrics

The downstream visuomotor policy used for all methods was Diffusion Policy to ensure direct comparability.
- **Viewpoint Diversity Evaluation:**
 - For D_diverse training, ARGUS was compared against **KYC** (which encodes camera extrinsics as Plücker coordinates) and classical **Diffusion Policy (DP)**.
 - For D_fixed training, ARGUS was compared against **VISTA** (which augments data using novel view synthesis via ZeroNVS) and classical **DP**. VISTA was adapted to use both external cameras and finetuned on D_diverse for extended viewpoint generalization (up to 135° shifts).
- **Training Efficiency Evaluation:** This study focused on the "Unfold Towel" task using D_diverse. ARGUS was compared against KYC and classical DP regarding training time (dataset passes) and data efficiency (number of demonstrations).
- **Depth-Based Methods Comparison:** ARGUS was compared with **3D Diffuser Actor**. Specifically, ARGUS was integrated into 3D Diffuser Actor by replacing its reliance on depth sensors with VGGT-predicted point clouds, then evaluated against the original 3D Diffuser Actor. In this specific comparison, ARGUS fed the scaled VGGT-predicted 3D point cloud directly to 3D Diffuser Actor's transformer architecture, without canonical viewpoint rendering.
Evaluation metrics included average success rates across rollouts and standard error across camera configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated ARGUS across various aspects of viewpoint generalization, training efficiency, and comparison to depth-based methods.

#### 4.1. High/Low Viewpoint Variance Evaluation

The study examined ARGUS's performance under both diverse-viewpoint (D_diverse) and fixed-viewpoint (D_fixed) training datasets, with evaluation performed on five novel multi-view camera configurations covering viewpoint shifts up to 135°.

- **Training on D_diverse:** ARGUS consistently demonstrated higher average success rates and lower standard errors across the five novel camera configurations compared to KYC and classical Diffusion Policy (DP). For example, on the "Unfold Towel" task, ARGUS achieved an average success rate of 8.0 ± 0.447, while KYC achieved 5.0 ± 1.483 and DP 5.6 ± 1.030. Similar performance advantages were observed across all four manipulation tasks (Tables 1). This indicates ARGUS's capability to extract useful information from highly diverse and unstructured viewpoint datasets and generalize effectively to unseen viewpoints.
- **Training on D_fixed:** When trained on a dataset with limited fixed viewpoints, ARGUS again outperformed VISTA (a data augmentation method for generalization) and classical DP. For the "Unfold Towel" task, ARGUS achieved an average success rate of 9.0 ± 0.447, significantly higher than VISTA's 5.0 ± 0.548 and DP's 2.0 ± 1.549 (Tables 2). This demonstrates ARGUS's ability to generalize to novel viewpoints even without explicit viewpoint variation in the training data, by effectively normalizing the input observations.

Overall, ARGUS maintained a relatively consistent success rate across all five camera viewpoint configurations, indicated by smaller standard error bars, suggesting robust generalization capabilities.

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency Evaluation

This evaluation focused on ARGUS's impact on the training and sample efficiency of the downstream visuomotor policy when learning from the diverse dataset (D_diverse) for the "Unfold Towel" task.

- **Training Time:** ARGUS demonstrated faster convergence. It achieved a 90% success rate 6 times faster than KYC and 4 times faster than classical Diffusion Policy, as measured by the number of dataset passes required.
- **Sample Efficiency:** ARGUS also exhibited strong data efficiency. It achieved a 70% success rate with only 40 demonstrations (representing 80 unique viewpoints), which was a higher success rate with fewer demonstrations compared to KYC and classical DP.
The authors attribute this improvement in efficiency to ARGUS simplifying the observation space by transforming unconstrained viewpoints into controlled, canonical ones. This simplification reportedly eases the learning burden on the downstream policy, allowing it to learn from a more structured input despite the inherent unstructured nature of the raw dataset.

#### 4.3. Comparison to Depth-Based Methods

This part of the study assessed ARGUS's performance when integrated with a depth-based architecture, specifically 3D Diffuser Actor, comparing it against the original depth-sensor-reliant version.

- **Comparable Performance:** The results indicated that using VGGT to construct the point cloud (as done in ARGUS) yielded performance comparable to using raw depth data in the 3D Diffuser Actor framework. This finding suggests that learned 3D reconstruction from RGB images can serve as an effective alternative to explicit depth sensing for achieving viewpoint-invariant robot manipulation. When ARGUS-derived point clouds were fed directly into 3D Diffuser Actor, the system achieved similar average success rates as the original depth-sensor-based 3D Diffuser Actor, highlighting the potential for ARGUS to enable viewpoint robustness without specialized hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Contributions and Implications

The research introduces ARGUS as an image pre-processing pipeline designed to address the challenge of viewpoint entanglement in visuomotor policies. By leveraging large-scale 3D vision models to align diverse camera observations into a canonical viewpoint, ARGUS demonstrates several significant implications for robot learning:

- **Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Generalization:** ARGUS consistently improves the learning efficiency and zero-shot generalization capabilities of downstream visuomotor policies. It reduces the learning burden by providing a normalized, consistent observation space, allowing policies to focus on task-relevant semantics rather than disentangling camera pose. This leads to faster convergence and effective learning from datasets with varying levels of viewpoint diversity, including those with unconstrained viewpoints or limited fixed views.
- **Reduced Hardware Dependency:** The work shows that learned 3D reconstruction from RGB images, facilitated by large 3D vision models, can perform comparably to policies relying on explicit depth sensors for viewpoint robustness. This suggests a pathway to achieving robust robot manipulation without the need for specialized depth sensing hardware, thereby lowering the barrier to entry and enabling more scalable and consistent deployment across different hardware platforms.
- **Compatibility with Advancements in 3D Perception:** As a pre-processing pipeline, ARGUS is inherently complementary to ongoing advancements in 3D perception models. Continued improvements in 3D vision models are expected to directly benefit ARGUS, enhancing its capabilities in scene reconstruction and canonical view generation.
- **General-Purpose Applicability:** While evaluated in the context of imitation learning, ARGUS is described as a general-purpose observation pre-processing pipeline, suggesting its potential integration into a broader range of visuomotor learning paradigms.

These findings suggest that explicitly canonicalizing observations offers a practical and scalable alternative to solely relying on large-scale data collection for learning viewpoint invariance, thereby improving the robustness and efficiency of robot manipulation systems.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its demonstrated strengths, ARGUS exhibits certain limitations:

- **Spatial Precision:** The method shows degraded performance in tasks requiring high spatial precision, particularly involving fine-grained manipulation of small objects. This is attributed to two primary causes:
 - **Translational Shifts:** Slight translational shifts between consecutive canonical images, hypothesized to arise from minor extrinsic prediction errors by VGGT that compound during canonical frame alignment, can introduce inconsistencies in the visual observation space, leading to misaligned actions by the downstream policy.
 - **Occlusion-Caused Artifacts:** Holes in the reconstructed point cloud and artifacts in canonical frame rendering, often due to occlusions, can obscure small objects.
 These observations indicate a trade-off where ARGUS currently prioritizes viewpoint robustness over some degree of spatial quality and precision.
- **Computational Overhead:** ARGUS introduces additional computational latency at evaluation time, averaging approximately 0.52 seconds per prediction using an RTX 3080 GPU, primarily due to running VGGT. While acceptable for quasi-static tasks, this overhead may restrict its applicability in real-time scenarios requiring immediate closed-loop responses or high-frequency control.

Future work directions identified include:
- **Extending Scene Invariance:** Expanding the framework beyond viewpoint normalization to address broader forms of scene invariance.
- **Improving Geometric Fidelity:** Enhancing the geometric accuracy and completeness of the 3D reconstruction to better support fine-grained manipulation tasks, while simultaneously maintaining viewpoint robustness. This would involve mitigating the issues of translational shifts and occlusion-caused artifacts.
- **Optimizing Computational Efficiency:** Addressing the computational overhead to enable real-time application in high-frequency control scenarios.
