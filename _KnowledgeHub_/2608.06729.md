# AtlasVLA: Persistent World-Ego State Modeling for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> AtlasVLA adds a dual-memory architecture to VLA policies operating on a single wrist camera: a 4D voxel-hashed Persistent World State Memory lifting 2D observations via depth back-projection, and an Ego-Working State Memory tracking task progress via intent queries. A step-wise conditioned diffusion transformer attends to both, reaching 97.6% on LIBERO and 70.8% on RLBench wrist-only, beating multi-view baselines.

## Problem

- Wrist-only VLAs suffer perception forgetting: once objects leave the narrow, constantly-shifting field of view, spatial context is lost with no persistent internal representation.
- Reactive VLAs lack historical context, causing task-progress forgetting during multi-step long-horizon manipulation, leading to repeated or skipped sub-steps.
- Prior memory-augmented VLA methods skew toward temporal caching or static pre-manipulation snapshots, leaving continuous spatial modeling under wrist-only execution unresolved.

## Method

- **Persistent World State Memory**: back-projects 2D wrist tokens into 3D via depth estimation, fusing them into a ==voxel-hashed== global map with confidence-weighted spatial aggregation and a sliding temporal window plus permanent first-frame anchor.
- **Ego-Working State Memory**: learnable intent-aware queries cross-attend to VLM tokens to extract task-progress state, consolidated into a redundancy-aware memory bank preventing intention drift.
- **World-Ego-Guided Action DiT**: a ==diffusion transformer== with decoupled step-wise conditioning, sequentially attending to retrieved ego-working context and ego-guided world-state context to generate 7-DoF actions.

## Results

- On LIBERO, AtlasVLA reaches **97.6%** average success using only a wrist camera, exceeding third-person-and-wrist baselines like OpenVLA-OFT (**97.1%**) and beating wrist-only pi0 by **6.9** points.
- On LIBERO-Long, AtlasVLA scores **94.6%**, a **7.0**-point gain over MemoryVLA, and on RLBench reaches **70.8%**, outperforming wrist-only MemoryVLA by **15.8** points.
- Ablations show removing world-state memory drops real-world long-horizon success from **69.5%** to **54.0%**, and removing ego-working memory drops it by **13.0** points, confirming both modules are load-bearing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Persistent, explicitly spatial world memory can substitute for multi-camera rigs, suggesting camera-count is a fixable engineering constraint rather than a fundamental capability limit.
> - Decoupling spatial grounding (world memory) from temporal task-tracking (ego memory) into separate attended contexts lets each module specialize rather than conflating geometry with intent.
> - Anchoring persistent memory to a permanent first-frame observation plus confidence-weighted fusion guards against drift and redundancy better than naive unbounded memory accumulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in embodied artificial intelligence have been significantly influenced by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, exemplified by frameworks such as RT-1, OpenVLA, and π0, aim to bridge high-level semantic reasoning with low-level robotic control by mapping multimodal sensory inputs to actions. They often leverage large-scale language and vision-language models and are trained on extensive cross-embodiment datasets.

Despite their empirical successes, current VLA models predominantly operate under a reactive paradigm. This means they function as reflexive systems, directly mapping immediate visual observations to actions. This reactive nature presents two critical limitations, particularly when deployed in unstructured, partially observable environments with a single, wrist-mounted camera:

1. **Perception Forgetting**: A wrist-mounted camera's field-of-view (FoV) constantly changes with the robot's movements. Task-relevant objects and spatial structures often exit the FoV, leading to a loss of awareness of the surrounding environment. This partial observability makes it difficult for the robot to track objects or understand its spatial context.
2. **Task-Progress Forgetting**: Complex, long-horizon tasks, which involve multiple sequential sub-steps, require an agent to maintain awareness of its progress. Reactive models, lacking historical context, often forget what has been accomplished or what needs to be done next, leading to compounded errors or redundant actions.

Existing research has explored memory mechanisms for robotic manipulation. Earlier approaches utilized recurrent architectures or extended context windows to implicitly aggregate past observations, but these methods often suffered from limited capacity and high computational costs. More recent works, such as MAP-VLA, MemoryVLA, and ReMem-VLA, introduce explicit memory structures like retrieval-based visual banks or recurrent latent queries to enable long-term temporal reasoning. However, these paradigms primarily focus on temporal caching and do not explicitly model or update spatial representations continuously. While some approaches, like SOMA, attempt to address out-of-vision scenarios using static spatial representations from pre-manipulation snapshots, they lack the capability for continuous scene updating during dynamic, wrist-only manipulation.

AtlasVLA positions itself within this landscape by addressing both the spatial partial observability and temporal task-progress forgetting challenges simultaneously. It proposes a framework that moves beyond a purely reactive or temporally-focused memory paradigm to establish a persistent, continuously updated world-ego state. This approach is specifically designed to enable robust manipulation using only a single wrist-mounted camera, thereby enhancing autonomy in complex, long-horizon tasks where traditional VLA models typically degrade.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind AtlasVLA stems from the observed limitations of existing reactive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly in scenarios involving partial observability and long-horizon tasks when restricted to a single wrist-mounted camera. The paper identifies two fundamental bottlenecks that AtlasVLA aims to overcome:

1. **Addressing Perception Forgetting due to Partial Observations**:
 - **Motivation**: In real-world robotic manipulation, a robot's wrist-mounted camera provides a limited, egocentric view that constantly shifts as the end-effector moves. This leads to a phenomenon where critical task-relevant objects or spatial landmarks frequently exit the camera's field of view. Current reactive VLA models, which rely on instantaneous visual inputs, "forget" these out-of-view elements, losing spatial awareness of their surroundings (e.g., the location of a box or a target area). This spatial blindness makes it challenging to execute manipulation tasks that require continuous understanding of the global environment, especially without the aid of multi-view setups.
 - **Objective**: To enable the embodied agent to continuously reconstruct and update a comprehensive internal model of the environment from transient 2D observations. This internal model should be persistent, globally updated, and capable of retaining spatial information even when objects are not within the immediate camera view, effectively resolving visual blind spots.

2. **Mitigating Task-Progress Forgetting during Long-Horizon Manipulation**:
 - **Motivation**: Many real-world robotic tasks are complex and require a sequence of multiple sub-steps (e.g., arranging objects, performing assembly). Reactive VLA models, lacking explicit mechanisms to track historical context, often fail to remember what sub-goals have been accomplished or what the current stage of the task is. This "task-progress forgetting" leads to inefficient, repetitive, or erroneous actions, preventing the successful completion of multi-step, long-horizon tasks.
 - **Objective**: To equip the agent with a memory system that explicitly tracks its ego state and the progression of the task. This memory should enable the robot to maintain a clear cognitive awareness of its place in the overarching execution sequence, thereby preventing intention drift and ensuring coherent, sequential task completion.

In essence, AtlasVLA's overarching objective is to transcend the limitations of reactive, memoryless observation by transitioning to a paradigm of proactive reasoning. This involves building and maintaining a "persistent world-ego state" that integrates both spatial awareness (what the world contains, even if not currently visible) and temporal task progress (what has been done and what needs to be done). The project aims to achieve this while operating solely on wrist-mounted camera observations, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing models, including those that rely on more extensive multi-view sensory inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AtlasVLA is designed as a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that operates exclusively on single-view wrist observations, moving from a reactive "observe → act" paradigm to proactive reasoning through a persistent world-ego state. The methodology is structured around a dual-memory architecture and a World-Ego-Guided Action Generation module.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as a sequential decision-making process for VLA-driven robotic manipulation. At each time step *t*, the agent receives a wrist-mounted visual observation *O<sub>w</sub><sup>t</sup>*, the proprioceptive robot state *S<sub>t</sub>*, and a language task instruction *L*. The goal is to learn a policy *π<sub>θ</sub>* that generates a sequence of future actions *A<sub>t</sub>* = [*a<sub>t</sub>*, *a<sub>t+1</sub>*..., *a<sub>t+k-1</sub>*]. Each action *a<sub>t</sub>* is a 7-dimensional vector representing the 6-DoF end-effector pose and the gripper's binary state. A strict wrist-only observation constraint is enforced.

#### 3.2. Persistent World State Memory

This module addresses partial observability by constructing and maintaining a dynamic, global 4D representation of the workspace.

- **Instantaneous World State Construction**:
 - For the current wrist-camera observation *I<sub>w</sub><sup>t</sup>*, 2D visual tokens are extracted using a frozen vision encoder (DINOv2 and SigLIP).
 - Depth *D<sub>w</sub><sup>t</sup>* is estimated via Depth Anything v3, which utilizes historical frames for temporal consistency.
 - Camera intrinsics *T<sub>in</sub>* and extrinsics *T<sub>ex</sub><sup>t</sup>* are acquired, with extrinsics derived from the robot state *S<sub>t</sub>* and a hand-eye calibration matrix.
 - A spatial back-projection module lifts the 2D visual tokens into a 3D latent space, yielding the current local world state *m<sub>t</sub>* (2D latent tokens with 3D positions).

- **Spatio-Temporal Embedding**:
 - To provide explicit spatial and temporal context, *m<sub>t</sub>* is augmented with 3D spatial coordinates (*P<sub>t</sub>*) and timestamps (*t*) through learnable positional encodings (*E<sub>spatial</sub>*, *E<sub>temporal</sub>*), parameterized by MLPs. This transforms the instantaneous world state into a unified 4D representation.

- **World State Spatio-Temporal Update**:
 - A persistent world state memory *M<sub>t</sub>* is maintained based on the local *m<sub>t</sub>*. To handle redundancy and computational costs in long-horizon tasks, a localized spatial fusion mechanism and a temporal sliding window are employed.
 - **Spatial Fusion**: A voxel-hashing strategy, analogous to Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) map integration, is used. The 3D space is partitioned into voxels. For incoming tokens corresponding to the same physical region, a weighted aggregation of their latent features occurs. The confidence weight *w<sub>t</sub>* for each token is derived from the depth estimation confidence, influencing its contribution to the global memory *M<sub>t</sub>(v)*.
 - **Temporal Sliding Window**: The global memory maintains a maximum temporal window size *W*. It is continuously updated, with older information outside the window potentially forgotten.
 - **Permanent Initialization**: The spatio-temporal memory constructed from the initial frame is permanently anchored, providing a consistent global context throughout the manipulation process.

#### 3.3. Ego-Working State Memory

This module prevents historical forgetting by tracking the agent's high-level semantic state and task progression.

- **Intent-aware Query**:
 - A set of learnable intent queries (*Q<sub>ego</sub>*) is introduced. These queries are fed into the VLM and attend to all tokens via cross-attention mechanisms.
 - This process extracts ego-working latent tokens (*Z<sub>ego</sub>*), which condense extensive spatio-temporal context into focused intent representations, helping the model maintain a coherent global receptive field and prevent intention drift.

- **Ego-Working Memory Bank**:
 - A temporal intent memory bank *M<sub>ego</sub>* is maintained through redundancy-aware latent consolidation.
 - Newly extracted *Z<sub>ego</sub><sup>t</sup>*, augmented with temporal embedding, retrieves relevant historical context from *M<sub>ego</sub><sup>t-1</sup>* and adaptively fuses with it.
 - A consolidation function *Cons(·)* merges temporally adjacent and semantically similar intent tokens to prevent unbounded memory growth and semantic redundancy.

#### 3.4. World-Ego-Guided Action Generation

This module integrates the dual memory states to generate robust actions.

- **Ego-Working Memory Retrieval**:
 - The current ego-working token *Z<sub>ego</sub><sup>t</sup>* retrieves relevant information from the consolidated *M<sub>ego</sub><sup>t</sup>* via a cross-attention mechanism, producing a temporally coherent ego-working context *C<sub>ego</sub><sup>t</sup>*.

- **Ego-Guided World Retrieval**:
 - The historical ego-working context *C<sub>ego</sub><sup>t</sup>* serves as a query to attend to the global world memory *M<sub>t</sub>* via cross-attention. This module selectively extracts task-relevant world states, suppressing spatial redundancy, and generates an ego-aligned world representation *C<sub>world</sub><sup>t</sup>*.

- **World-Ego-Guided Action DiT (Diffusion Transformer)**:
 - Unlike standard diffusion models with global conditioning, AtlasVLA's DiT adopts a decoupled, step-wise conditioning mechanism.
 - At each diffusion step, noisy action tokens *a<sup>k</sup>* are processed. They first attend to the ego-working context *C<sub>ego</sub><sup>t</sup>* via an ego-working attention layer (to capture task progress). Subsequently, they attend to the retrieved world representation *C<sub>world</sub><sup>t</sup>* via a world state attention layer (for geometric grounding).
 - These attention modules and subsequent feed-forward networks (FFN) follow standard Transformer design with Add & Norm layers. After multiple denoising steps, the refined tokens are decoded into the final actions *A<sub>t</sub>*.
 - For inference, action decoding uses DDIM over 10 denoising steps with a Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) scale of 1.5.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AtlasVLA was rigorously evaluated across simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RLBench) and real-world robotic environments, with a focus on its performance using solely a wrist-mounted camera.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Training**: The model was trained across 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs using PyTorch FSDP, with a global batch size of 256 and a learning rate of 2 × 10<sup>-5</sup>. The LLM scales to 7B (using OpenVLA-7b pre-training), and the DiT-based action expert comprises approximately 300 million parameters.
- **Observation**: The system exclusively conditions on a single 224x224 wrist-perspective RGB frame and text instructions to generate 7-DoF end-effector actions.
- **Inference**: Action decoding utilizes DDIM over 10 denoising steps with a classifier-free guidance (CFG) scale of 1.5.
- **Benchmarks**:
 - **LIBERO**: A simulation benchmark with a Franka robot, across five suites: Spatial, Object, Goal, Long, and 90. Models were trained with 50 demonstrations per task.
 - **RLBench**: A large-scale simulation framework with complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks, using 100 demonstrations per task.
 - **Real-world**: A Franka robot platform (RealSense D415 wrist camera), evaluated on 6 general manipulation tasks and 4 long-horizon temporal tasks. All real-world experiments strictly used wrist-camera observations without third-person visual support.

#### 4.2. Simulation Evaluation on LIBERO

AtlasVLA achieved an average success rate of 97.6% under a strict wrist-only camera setting (Table 1). This performance notably surpassed several baselines that utilized additional third-person views, outperforming π<sup>0</sup> (3rd + wrist) by 3.4%. When compared to baselines using only wrist cameras, AtlasVLA demonstrated consistent strong performance, while others like π<sup>0</sup> and MemoryVLA showed significant degradation (3.5% and 2.5% respectively) when restricted to wrist-only views.

For long-horizon tasks, AtlasVLA achieved a 94.6% success rate on the LIBERO-Long benchmark with wrist-only input, outperforming MemoryVLA (wrist) by 7.0%. This result was attributed to the combined effectiveness of the ego-working memory in tracking task progression and the persistent world state memory in building a comprehensive 3D global workspace.

#### 4.3. Simulation Evaluation on RLBench

On the RLBench benchmark (Table 2), AtlasVLA achieved an average success rate of 70.8% using solely a wrist camera. This significantly outperformed all VLA baselines, including a 7.5% improvement over MemoryVLA (3rd) and a 15.8% improvement over MemoryVLA (wrist). The consistent improvements across tasks indicate that integrating historical context into a persistent world state memory effectively overcomes wrist-view partial observability in complex environments.

#### 4.4. Real-World Evaluation

AtlasVLA demonstrated robust performance in real-world environments (Figure 3, Tables 3 and 4).
- **General Tasks**: Achieved an average success rate of 78.7% with only a wrist camera (Table 3). This outperformed MemoryVLA by 8.0% (with 3rd-person views) and 16.4% (with wrist-only views), indicating effective compensation for limited wrist-camera visibility.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks**: Achieved an average success rate of 69.5% (Table 4), outperforming π<sup>0</sup> (wrist) by 17.5% and MemoryVLA (wrist) by 9.0%. This improvement highlights the importance of persistent spatio-temporal reasoning, as AtlasVLA’s joint modeling of spatial and temporal information allowed the robot to preserve intermediate states and execute complex manipulation sequences reliably.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

Systematic ablation studies (Table 5) confirmed the contribution of each architectural component:
- **Core Memory Modules**: Removing the world state memory resulted in a catastrophic performance drop (69.5% → 54.0% in real-world long-horizon tasks), demonstrating its indispensability. Removing the ego-working memory led to a 13.0% drop (69.5% → 56.5%), confirming its importance for preventing intention drift.
- **Memory Update Strategy**: Replacing the spatio-temporal update with naive memory accumulation degraded real-world success by 11.5%, validating the effectiveness of voxel aggregation and sliding window.
- **Positional Embedding**: Removing spatial or temporal positional embeddings reduced real-world performance by 2.0% and 4.5% respectively, confirming the benefit of 4D positional encodings.
- **World State Conditioning**: Removing world state attention reduced real-world success by 8.0%, indicating its necessity for precise action synthesis.
- **Detailed Ablations (Appendix)**: Further analysis showed an ego-working state memory length of 16 tokens and a world state voxel size of 0.025m yielded optimal performance.

#### 4.6. Runtime Analysis

AtlasVLA maintained competitive inference efficiency, with a single-step inference latency of 0.158s (vs. MemoryVLA's 0.146s) and a throughput of 101.3Hz, suitable for real-time robotic deployment (Table 7). GPU VRAM consumption was 18.1GB, a modest increase of 1.4GB over MemoryVLA, demonstrating efficient memory management.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AtlasVLA represents a development in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by addressing fundamental limitations of reactive paradigms, particularly in scenarios requiring persistent environmental understanding from limited sensory input.

The primary significance of this work lies in its formal identification and effective resolution of two critical challenges in wrist-only robotic manipulation: spatial partial observation (perception forgetting) and temporal task-progress forgetting. By proposing a novel dual-memory architecture that integrates a 4D Persistent World State Memory and an Ego-Working State Memory, AtlasVLA provides a framework for embodied agents to move beyond instantaneous, reflexive behaviors toward proactive, context-aware reasoning.

The empirical results demonstrate that AtlasVLA achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulation and real-world benchmarks using solely a wrist-mounted camera. Notably, it consistently outperforms existing VLA baselines, including those that leverage multi-view camera setups, particularly in long-horizon memory-dependent tasks. The absolute success rate improvements of 9.4% on LIBERO-Long and 17.5% in real-world long-horizon tasks indicate that explicit world-ego state modeling significantly enhances task robustness and reliability.

The potential impact of AtlasVLA is substantial for the field of embodied AI and robotics:
- **Reduced Hardware Complexity**: By enabling high-performance manipulation with only a single wrist camera, AtlasVLA offers a path toward simpler, more cost-effective robotic systems, reducing the reliance on complex and expensive multi-camera configurations.
- **Enhanced Autonomy in Unstructured Environments**: The framework's ability to maintain a comprehensive internal representation of the world and task progress allows robots to operate more effectively in partially observable and dynamic real-world settings, where objects frequently move out of view.
- **Improved Long-Horizon Task Execution**: The explicit modeling of task progression and ego state significantly improves the robot's capacity to complete complex, multi-step tasks without losing context, a crucial step towards more sophisticated and human-like robotic capabilities.
- **Scalable Paradigm for Cognitive Robotics**: AtlasVLA establishes a scalable paradigm for persistent world state modeling, laying groundwork for future embodied agents to develop more advanced cognitive mapping capabilities and robustly interact with environments over extended periods. This could contribute to the development of truly autonomous robots that can adapt and perform complex sequences of actions in a generalizable manner.

In summary, AtlasVLA contributes to making robotic manipulation more robust and efficient under challenging real-world constraints, fostering advancements towards more capable and autonomous embodied AI systems.
