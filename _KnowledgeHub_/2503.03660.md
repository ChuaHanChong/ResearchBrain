# Chunking the Critic: A Transformer-based Soft Actor-Critic with N-Step Returns

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Karlsruhe Institute of Technology developed a Transformer-based critic for Soft Actor-Critic, which improves value estimation by processing short trajectory segments and integrating N-step returns without importance sampling. This approach yielded superior performance on complex, long-horizon continuous control benchmarks like Meta-World and Box-Pushing, often achieving state-of-the-art success rates.

## Problem

- Off-policy reinforcement learning algorithms struggle with accurate value estimation and long-range credit assignment in long-horizon, sparse-reward continuous control tasks.
- Standard actor-critic methods typically estimate values for individual state-action pairs, limiting their ability to reason over extended temporal sequences.
- Integrating multi-step returns into off-policy learning is challenging due to the high variance introduced by importance sampling, often leading to instability.

## Method

- Introduces a ==causal Transformer== as the critic architecture, conditioning it on short segments of state-action sequences to estimate prefix-conditioned values.
- Leverages N-step returns for critic updates by computing targets from realized trajectories and performing gradient-level averaging across multiple horizons, thereby avoiding importance sampling.
- Proposes a simple critic-parameter freezing schedule as an alternative to target networks, allowing for stable, target-free training in some local-motion tasks.

## Results

- Outperformed strong baselines on Meta-World ML1 and Box-Pushing benchmarks, achieving **96.8%** success rate in dense reward Box-Pushing and competitive results in sparse reward settings.
- Demonstrated competitive sample efficiency, solving most Meta-World ML1 tasks within approximately 5 million environment interactions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of Transformer components, causal masking, and the benefits of multi-horizon supervision with gradient-level averaging over fixed short windows or recurrent critics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sophisticated sequence modeling via a Transformer in the critic significantly enhances its ability to understand temporal context and perform long-range credit assignment.
> - Gradient-level averaging of N-step losses provides a stable and effective mechanism for incorporating multi-horizon information in off-policy learning without the pitfalls of importance sampling.
> - A reactive, step-based policy can be effectively guided by a sequence-aware critic, combining the strengths of both approaches for robust performance in complex control tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning (RL), specifically in off-policy continuous control. It builds upon Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), a method known for its sample efficiency and stability in continuous control tasks. The paper addresses limitations of current off-policy actor-critic methods, particularly in scenarios involving sparse rewards, long horizons, and high-dimensional state-action spaces, where accurate value estimation becomes challenging.

The broader landscape includes several relevant areas:

- **Multi-step Returns (N-step Returns):** These are known to reduce bootstrapping bias and accelerate credit assignment. However, in off-policy settings, they typically necessitate importance sampling (IS), which can introduce high variance and destabilize training. The paper seeks to integrate multi-step returns without the reliance on IS.
- **Temporally Extended Actions / Action Chunking:** Recent work has explored policies and critics that operate on short open-loop action sequences (e.g., movement primitives, action chunking). While these can improve exploration and speed up value propagation, they often introduce trade-offs such as reduced control frequency, dependency on a fixed chunk length hyperparameter, and complexity in learning across varying temporal resolutions. This work aims to achieve benefits of temporal modeling without requiring the policy to output action chunks.
- **Transformer Networks in RL:** Transformers have been successfully applied in RL, primarily in episodic RL (ERL) for trajectory-level primitives (e.g., TOP-ERL) or in offline RL for policy-side sequence modeling (e.g., Decision Transformer). This paper distinguishes itself by employing a Transformer architecture specifically for the critic in an online, off-policy, step-based setting, rather than for episodic control or offline policy learning.
- **Stable Critic Optimization without Target Networks:** Techniques such as slowing critic updates, partial parameter freezing, and function-class regularization have been shown to improve the stability of off-policy actor-critic methods by reducing target drift and mitigating overestimation. CrossQ, for example, demonstrated stable training without a target network using Batch Renormalization. This work investigates a critic parameter freezing schedule as an alternative to Polyak averaging.

T-SAC positions itself by enhancing the critic’s capability to model temporal structure and integrate multi-step returns, thereby improving credit assignment in long-horizon tasks, while maintaining a one-step policy and avoiding the high variance associated with importance sampling or the architectural complexities of policy-side action chunking.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the performance and stability of off-policy reinforcement learning, particularly in complex domains characterized by long-horizon tasks and sparse reward signals. The motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Challenges in Value Estimation:** Accurate value estimation is critical for effective RL, but it is particularly difficult in environments with sparse rewards, where feedback is infrequent, and in long-horizon tasks, where credit assignment across many timesteps is intricate. Existing SAC variants, while generally sample-efficient and stable, still face these challenges.
- **Leveraging Multi-step Returns while Mitigating Variance:** Multi-step returns (N-step returns) are theoretically beneficial for reducing bootstrapping bias and expediting credit assignment. However, their practical application in off-policy settings is hampered by the need for importance sampling (IS), which can introduce significant variance, leading to training instability. A key motivation is to harness the benefits of multi-step targets without incurring the variance penalty of IS.
- **Improving Temporal Modeling in the Critic:** Traditional SAC critics process state-action pairs in isolation, which may limit their ability to capture crucial temporal dependencies within trajectories. The authors propose that enriching the critic with sequence modeling capabilities can lead to more accurate and robust value estimates, especially over extended horizons.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Action Chunking:** While actor-side action chunking or movement primitives can aid exploration and value propagation, they introduce trade-offs. These include a reduction in control frequency, the coupling of performance to a fixed chunk-length hyperparameter, and challenges in tasks requiring dynamic temporal resolutions. The motivation here is to achieve similar benefits of temporal abstraction on the critic side without altering the policy’s one-step action output.
- **Simplifying and Stabilizing Critic Training:** The use of target networks, while stabilizing, can slow value propagation and add complexity. Building on work like CrossQ, the authors seek to develop a stable critic training mechanism that can eliminate the need for target networks or Polyak averaging, further simplifying the learning process.

In summary, the work aims to develop a robust and sample-efficient off-policy RL algorithm by strengthening the critic’s capacity for temporal reasoning through a Transformer architecture and a novel, importance-sampling-free multi-step return mechanism, all while maintaining a standard one-step policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Transformer-based Soft Actor-Critic (T-SAC), integrates a sequence-conditioned Transformer critic into the SAC framework, coupled with a specific approach for N-step returns and stable training.

#### 3.1. Transformer Critic Architecture

- **Sequence Conditioning:** The core of T-SAC is replacing the standard MLP critic with a Transformer-based architecture. This critic processes short trajectory segments, specifically sequences of states and actions `(s_t, a_t, a_{t+1}..., a_{t+n-1})`.
- **Causal Masking:** The Transformer critic uses a causal mask during self-attention. This ensures that when predicting the value for a prefix `(s_t, a_t..., a_{t+i-1})`, the attention mechanism only considers information from timesteps `≤ i`, preventing information leakage from future actions within the segment.
- **Output:** For a given segment of length `n`, the Transformer critic outputs `n` prefix-conditioned values, `{Q_ψ(s_t, a_t..., a_{t+i-1})}_i=1^n`, corresponding to different horizons `i`.
- **Lightweight Design:** The paper describes the Transformer as lightweight, typically using 2 layers with 128-256 hidden units. State and action tokens are embedded using separate single-layer linear embeddings.

#### 3.2. N-step Returns for Critic Updates

T-SAC employs variable-horizon N-step returns to supervise the critic.

- **Target Formulation without Importance Sampling:** Instead of using importance sampling (IS) to correct for off-policy data, T-SAC changes the target definition. For a realized prefix `(s_t, a_{t:t+i-1})` from the replay buffer, the `i`-step target `G^(i)(s_t, a_{t:t+i-1})` is calculated as the sum of discounted observed rewards `r_{t+j}` over `j = 0` to `i-1`, plus a discounted bootstrap value `V_ϕ(s_{t+i})`. The bootstrap value `V_ϕ(s)` is derived from the target Q-network and the current policy `π_θ`, `E_{a~π_θ(.|s)} [Q_ϕ(s, a)]`. Since the rewards `r_{t+j}` directly follow the recorded prefix actions, no IS is required for the reward sum component. Only the terminal bootstrap term depends on the current policy `π_θ`.
- **Gradient-level Averaging:** Rather than averaging N-step *targets* directly, which the paper suggests can dilute sparse reward signals, T-SAC computes a separate loss `L_i(ψ)` for each horizon `i` within a sampled segment (Equation 3). The gradients from these individual horizon losses are then averaged (`∇_ψ L = 1/n Σ_i ∇_ψ L_i(ψ)`). This approach aims to reduce update variance while preserving the signal from sparse, long-horizon rewards, as adjacent horizons produce correlated gradient estimates.
- **Multi-horizon Training Objective:** The overall critic objective is the mean-squared error averaged over all horizons `i ∈ {1..., n}` within a mini-batch of trajectory segments. For each segment, a random start index `t` and a window length `n` (sampled uniformly between `min_length` and `max_length`) are selected.

#### 3.3. Policy Network and Objective

- **Standard One-step Policy:** The policy (`π_θ`) remains a standard one-step network, outputting an action `a_t` based on the current state `s_t`. It typically uses Layer Normalization in its hidden layers.
- **Entropy-Regularized Objective:** The policy is optimized using a maximum-entropy objective, similar to SAC, which includes an entropy regularization term `α H(π_θ(·|s_t))`. The temperature `α` is automatically tuned to match a target entropy. Critically, unlike canonical SAC, the critic in T-SAC does *not* include entropy in its target; it estimates the standard (non-soft) action-value. This means exploration and regularization are handled entirely by the policy.

#### 3.4. Critic Parameter Freezing for Target-Free Training

- **Alternative to Polyak Averaging:** T-SAC removes the need for a separate target network and its Polyak averaging updates. Instead, it employs a critic-parameter freezing schedule inspired by CrossQ.
- **Hard-Copy Schedule:** At the start of processing a new critic segment, a snapshot of the online critic (`ϕ`) is taken (`ϕ ← ψ`). This snapshot is then used to precompute and cache bootstrap targets `V_ϕ(s)` for all windows within that segment. The online critic `ψ` is then optimized against these *cached and frozen* targets for `K` updates.
- **Freezing Interval (K):** This decoupling mechanism aims to curb target drift without requiring batch renormalization or constrained activations. The freezing interval `K` is a hyperparameter (e.g., `K=20` for Gymnasium MuJoCo).

#### 3.5. Segment Construction and Masking

- **Fixed-length Segments in Replay Buffer:** Experience is stored in the replay buffer as continuous, fixed-length segments. If an episode terminates before a segment is full, transitions from the subsequent episode are appended to fill the segment, ensuring constant tensor shapes for efficient batch processing.
- **Action Masking:** To prevent the critic from bootstrapping or attending across episode boundaries when multiple trajectories are packed into one segment, a binary action mask is applied. This mask zeros out the TD error for any horizon that attempts to cross a terminal state within the segment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

T-SAC demonstrates notable performance improvements and stability across a range of continuous control benchmarks, particularly in long-horizon and sparse-reward settings.

#### 4.1. Meta-World ML1 (50 Tasks)

- **Aggregated Success:** T-SAC consistently outperforms standard SAC and strong off-policy baselines (including PPO, GTrXL, gSDE, CrossQ, TCP, BBRL, and TOP-ERL) on the Meta-World ML1 benchmark (50 tasks), achieving stronger aggregated Interquartile Mean (IQM) success rates.
- **Efficiency:** T-SAC solves most tasks within approximately 5 million environment interactions. In comparison, TOP-ERL typically required 20 million steps to achieve similar aggregate performance.
- **Challenging Tasks:** T-SAC showed particularly strong performance on hard multi-phase tasks such as Assembly, Disassemble, Hammer, and Stick-Pull.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Success was evaluated strictly at the final timestep, and IQM success rates were computed per task and then averaged across tasks.

#### 4.2. Box Pushing (Dense and Sparse Rewards)

- **Dense Reward:** T-SAC achieved 96.8% success on the FANCYGYM Box-Pushing task with dense rewards and tight success tolerances, surpassing prior baselines (which achieved ≤ 85%) under the same protocol.
- **Sparse Reward:** Under sparse reward conditions (reward applied only at the terminal step), T-SAC with the hard-copy critic reached 60% success. This demonstrates competitive performance, though slightly below TOP-ERL's 70% in this specific sparse setting.

#### 4.3. Gymnasium MuJoCo

- **Competitive Performance:** On the five Gymnasium MuJoCo locomotion tasks (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d, HumanoidStandup), T-SAC matched or outperformed SAC, with the largest gains observed on HumanoidStandup and HalfCheetah.
- **Stability at Low Update Rates:** The critic parameter freezing schedule enabled stable training without target networks even at low update-to-data ratios (UTD ≈ 0.75), consistently matching or surpassing Polyak updates.
- **Early Convergence:** T-SAC did not exhibit slower early convergence despite conditioning the critic on multi-step sequences from an early exploratory policy.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Findings

- **Transformer Components:**
 - Removing only self-attention generally led to divergence.
 - Removing self-attention, ResNet blocks, and the causal mask (reducing the critic to a plain MLP) resulted in underperformance compared to the full Transformer critic.
 - The causal mask was important for preventing future information leakage and improved performance over an unmasked variant.
- **Multi-horizon Supervision Window:**
 - T-SAC was stable with N-step horizons up to `n=16` and benefited from longer windows, contrary to conventional guidance for `n ≤ 5` in IS-based methods.
 - Using a fixed horizon slightly reduced final performance compared to sampling from a range `[min_length, max_length]`.
- **Comparison to Multi-step MLP (Action Chunking):**
 - A multi-step MLP critic consistently underperformed the Transformer critic.
 - Policy-side action chunking with an MLP critic yielded only modest gains.
 - Chunking the Transformer critic itself was beneficial; a chunk length of 4 achieved 99.5% success on Box-Pushing Dense. This indicated the advantage of a sequence-conditioned critic under a chunked policy, even when the policy is not designed for chunking.
 - The Transformer critic with single-step temporal resolution (`min_length=max_length=1`) still outperformed an MLP critic with `chunking_length=1`, suggesting benefits from N-step returns and gradient averaging.
- **Number of Samples Generated per Step:** Generating multiple supervision windows per environment step (e.g., 4) improved performance compared to a single window, by increasing the proportion of fresh samples in each batch.
- **Recurrent Critics (GRU/LSTM):** Replacing the Transformer critic with GRU or LSTM variants under identical training conditions resulted in underperformance on Box-Pushing, suggesting advantages of the Transformer's architecture and the gradient-level averaging.
- **Robustness:** T-SAC degraded gracefully under injected action and state noise, outperforming SAC and CrossQ. Under stochastic termination and partial observability, T-SAC remained at least as stable as baselines, suggesting that sequence-conditioned critics can help mitigate such effects.
- **Gradient-level Averaging:** Figure 9b demonstrated that naively averaging N-step targets directly destabilized learning and erased progress, reinforcing the necessity of the gradient-level averaging approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The T-SAC framework represents a step in improving continuous control in challenging long-horizon and sparse-reward environments. The core contributions of the Transformer critic and the importance-sampling-free N-step return mechanism have several implications:

- **Enhanced Temporal Reasoning:** By enabling the critic to model short trajectory segments and attend to temporal dependencies, T-SAC provides more robust and coherent value estimates. This is particularly significant for tasks where credit assignment is delayed or complex.
- **Stable Multi-step Learning without Importance Sampling:** The method's approach to integrating N-step returns without importance sampling addresses a long-standing challenge in off-policy RL. This can facilitate faster credit assignment and reduced bootstrapping bias without incurring high variance, making longer effective horizons practical.
- **Decoupling Policy and Critic Abstraction:** T-SAC achieves the benefits of temporal abstraction (like those seen in action-chunking methods) on the critic side, without requiring the policy to generate open-loop action sequences. This maintains the reactivity and flexibility of a one-step policy while still leveraging a sequence-aware value function.
- **Simplified and Stable Training:** The critic parameter freezing schedule offers an alternative to traditional target networks, potentially simplifying implementation and maintaining stability. This aligns with broader research efforts to reduce complexity in deep TD learning.
- **Strong Performance on Complex Tasks:** The consistent outperformance on Meta-World ML1 and dense Box-Pushing tasks indicates the practical efficacy of T-SAC in domains requiring intricate manipulation and generalization.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancing Robotic Manipulation:** The improved ability to handle long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards could directly impact robotic manipulation, enabling agents to learn more complex, multi-stage skills in real-world settings where dense reward engineering is often difficult.
- **Foundational Research:** T-SAC's demonstration of a sequence-conditioned critic in an online, off-policy setting could inspire further research into architectural innovations for value functions, moving beyond simple MLPs to models that inherently process temporal context.
- **Alternative to Policy-side Chunking:** For scenarios where policies need to be highly reactive, but temporal context is crucial for value estimation, T-SAC offers a viable alternative to policy-side action chunking, avoiding its inherent trade-offs.

**Future Work:**

The authors identify several directions for future research:
- Extending T-SAC to discrete-action domains.
- Applying T-SAC to pixel-based or strongly partially observable settings, which currently present stability challenges.
- Deployment on real-robot tasks to assess its performance in physical environments.
- Developing theoretical guarantees for critic-side chunking.
- Conducting representation analyses of the Transformer critic.
- Exploring shared Transformer backbones for both actor and critic networks.
