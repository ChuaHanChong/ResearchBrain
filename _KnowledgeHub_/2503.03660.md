# Chunking the Critic: A Transformer-based Soft Actor-Critic with N-Step Returns

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Karlsruhe Institute of Technology developed a Transformer-based critic for Soft Actor-Critic, which improves value estimation by processing short trajectory segments and integrating N-step returns without importance sampling. This approach yielded superior performance on complex, long-horizon continuous control benchmarks like Meta-World and Box-Pushing, often achieving state-of-the-art success rates.

## Problem

- Off-policy reinforcement learning algorithms struggle with accurate value estimation and long-range credit assignment in long-horizon, sparse-reward continuous control tasks.
- Standard actor-critic methods typically estimate values for individual state-action pairs, limiting their ability to reason over extended temporal sequences.
- Integrating multi-step returns into off-policy learning is challenging due to the high variance introduced by importance sampling, often leading to instability.

## Method

- Introduces a ==causal Transformer== as the critic architecture, conditioning it on short segments of state-action sequences to estimate prefix-conditioned values.
- Leverages N-step returns for critic updates by computing targets from realized trajectories and performing gradient-level averaging across multiple horizons, thereby avoiding importance sampling.
- Proposes a simple critic-parameter freezing schedule as an alternative to target networks, allowing for stable, target-free training in some local-motion tasks.

## Results

- Outperformed strong baselines on Meta-World ML1 and Box-Pushing benchmarks, achieving **96.8%** success rate in dense reward Box-Pushing and competitive results in sparse reward settings.
- Demonstrated competitive sample efficiency, solving most Meta-World ML1 tasks within approximately 5 million environment interactions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of Transformer components, causal masking, and the benefits of multi-horizon supervision with gradient-level averaging over fixed short windows or recurrent critics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sophisticated sequence modeling via a Transformer in the critic significantly enhances its ability to understand temporal context and perform long-range credit assignment.
> - Gradient-level averaging of N-step losses provides a stable and effective mechanism for incorporating multi-horizon information in off-policy learning without the pitfalls of importance sampling.
> - A reactive, step-based policy can be effectively guided by a sequence-aware critic, combining the strengths of both approaches for robust performance in complex control tasks.
