# Worldscape-MoE: A Unified Mixture-of-Experts World Model for Scalable Heterogeneous Action Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Manifold AI developed Worldscape-MoE, a unified Mixture-of-Experts world model, to integrate diverse action controls like camera trajectories, robot joint actions, and hand motions into a single framework. The model improves physical consistency and temporal coherence, achieving state-of-the-art performance across locomotion, manipulation, and hand-motion tasks while demonstrating strong generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing world models are fragmented, typically designed around isolated control interfaces (e.g., camera trajectories, robot actions, hand-joint signals).
- This modality-specific development leads to distinct architectures, training objectives, and data pipelines, preventing effective sharing and scaling of learned world knowledge.
- The structural limitation of isolated models creates a scaling bottleneck for embodied AI, despite shared underlying physical regularities and scene dynamics.

## Method

- **Worldscape-MoE** is a unified ==Mixture-of-Experts== world model built on ==Diffusion Transformers (DiT)==, designed to absorb heterogeneous action supervision.
- It employs an ==asymmetric injection design== to integrate diverse action conditions (camera trajectories, hand-joint maps, robot control vectors) into a shared DiT backbone without disrupting pre-existing video generation priors.
- The model replaces standard feed-forward networks with ==control-aware MoE-FFNs==, featuring a "shared expert" and modality-specific experts with ==deterministic routing== based on control signal presence, alongside a progressive training strategy for stable extension.

## Results

- **Worldscape-MoE** consistently outperformed baselines, achieving the best overall locomotion score of **0.7556** on iWorld-Bench and the highest EWM score of **62.84** on WorldArena for robotic manipulation.
- Quantitative analysis of MoE confirmed meaningful expert specialization, with the shared expert dominating computation (**69.48%**) for common world knowledge and dedicated experts showing modality-dependent usage.
- The model demonstrated strong out-of-distribution generalization across all domains and successfully synthesized coupled loco-manipulation behaviors, indicating learned transferable physical regularities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Different control types represent distinct interfaces to the same underlying world, necessitating a factorization into shared world dynamics and control-specific action interpretation.
> - The Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture enables structured capacity allocation, allowing a shared expert to capture common world dynamics while dedicated experts specialize in modality-specific action semantics, avoiding interference.
> - A unified learning framework, especially with MoE, can absorb heterogeneous supervision without sacrificing individual control performance, transforming fragmented datasets into a cumulative training resource.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models are emerging as a foundational component for embodied intelligence and interactive agents, offering controllable simulators where agents can perceive, act, forecast, and acquire experience at scale. Recent advancements in high-quality video generation have significantly propelled their development. However, the current landscape of world models is characterized by fragmentation. Existing models are typically designed around isolated control interfaces, such as camera trajectories for interactive environment models (e.g., Genie, Matrix-Game series, HY-World), robot actions for embodied world models (e.g., Ctrl-World, Cosmos-Predict), or hand-joint signals for egocentric interaction models (e.g., Hand2World, Generated Reality).

This modality-specific development leads to several issues: architectures, training objectives, and data pipelines are distinct for each control type. Consequently, learned world knowledge, model capacity, and training data are siloed, preventing effective sharing and scaling across different control modalities, even when the underlying physical regularities and scene dynamics are fundamentally shared. This structural limitation represents a scaling bottleneck for the broader field of embodied AI. This work addresses this limitation by proposing a unified framework that can integrate heterogeneous action supervision within a shared representation of world dynamics, moving beyond isolated, single-control models towards a more scalable and generalizable approach. It aims to demonstrate that a single model can effectively learn from diverse control signals without compromising individual control capabilities or leading to destructive interference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that while various controllable video generation world models exist for specific control interfaces, a unified and extensible learning framework capable of absorbing heterogeneous action supervision is absent. The authors contend that different controls (e.g., camera trajectories, robot joint-action vectors, hand-joint maps) represent distinct interfaces to the *same underlying world*. Despite their representational differences, these controls constrain shared physical regularities, scene dynamics, and interaction semantics (e.g., object persistence, temporal coherence, causal action effects). This insight motivates a design principle of factorization, where a world model should decompose the problem into shared world dynamics and control-specific action interpretation, rather than building separate models for each control.

To investigate the feasibility and benefits of such a unified, scalable approach, the authors pose four specific research questions (RQs):

- **RQ1: Can a single world model support multiple heterogeneous action controls without sacrificing the performance of each individual control setting?** This question probes whether unifying diverse controls (camera, robot, hand-joint) within one model can avoid performance degradation for individual modalities, which are typically optimized in isolation. An affirmative answer would validate the viability of unified training over separate models.
- **RQ2: Does the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) structure learn meaningful expert routing and specialization, or is the gain purely from increased capacity?** This question examines whether the proposed MoE architecture facilitates a structured allocation of capacity, allowing a "shared expert" to capture common world dynamics while "control-specific experts" specialize in the action semantics of their respective modalities. This distinguishes functional specialization from mere model size increase.
- **RQ3: Does heterogeneous training improve scalability when new controls and data sources are added?** The aim here is to determine if incorporating new control modalities into the unified framework genuinely enhances overall capability and transforms fragmented datasets into a growing training resource, or if it introduces optimization conflicts that degrade previously learned controls.
- **RQ4: Does the MoE-based unified world model generalize beyond the training distributions and support loco-manipulation scenarios?** This addresses the model's ability to learn transferable world regularities. It investigates whether the learned representation can generalize to novel environments and generate complex, coupled behaviors involving both locomotion and manipulation, moving beyond fitting individual training controls.

The Worldscape-MoE framework is specifically designed to address these questions by operationalizing the observed principle of shared world dynamics and control-specific interpretation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Worldscape-MoE is a unified Mixture-of-Experts world model built on Diffusion Transformers (DiT). Its design emphasizes three core responsibilities: modality-specific control injection pathways, a shared Diffusion Transformer backbone for common spatiotemporal priors, and sparse experts for separating cross-control world knowledge from modality-specific action interpretation.

#### 3.1. Heterogeneous Action-Control World Modeling

The model operates on a standard video generation world modeling task, predicting future observations $W_{t+1}$ given a current observation $O_t$, a textual world description $C_t$, and an action condition $A_t$. The action condition $A_t$ is heterogeneous, encompassing camera trajectories ($A_{traj}$), hand-joint representations ($A_{hand}$), robot control vectors ($A_{act}$), and other extensible modalities. The predicted $W_{t+1}$ is a video segment, with its last frame $O_{t+1}$ recursively fed back for long-horizon generation.

To accommodate different action forms while preserving pre-trained video generation priors, Worldscape-MoE employs an asymmetric injection design:
- **Video frames and reference images** are encoded into latent representations using a frozen VAE.
- **Text instructions** are encoded by a frozen text encoder and injected via cross-attention.
- **Locomotion control** (camera trajectories) is transformed into dense camera-control features, mapped by a trainable Control Adapter, and fused with patch-level video tokens, indicating a visual injection function $\phi_{vis}(A)$.
- **Hand Motion control** (dense action maps) is encoded by the frozen VAE into latent control features and injected into a conditional visual branch, also implementing $\phi_{vis}(A)$.
- **Manipulation control** (low-dimensional action sequences) is encoded by a trainable Action MLP into compact action embeddings and integrated into the timestep embedding and its projected modulation vectors, acting as a temporal modulation function $\phi_{tmp}(A)$.

This approach unifies heterogeneous controls within different pathways of the same DiT backbone, enabling multi-control modeling without disrupting the pre-existing video generator's priors.

#### 3.2. Heterogeneous Control Dataset Construction

The training corpus is curated from diverse sources, organized into three categories:
- **Camera-control data:** Combines real-world datasets (RealEstate10K, DL3dv-10k), stylized simulation data (Unreal Engine with iWorld-Bench protocols), and diverse world data (Sekai, SpatialVid with re-annotated trajectories via VIPE). Captions are adapted from Matrix-Game 3.0.
- **Robotic embodied data:** Generated using a large-scale simulation pipeline on the RoboTwin platform for diverse manipulation scenarios, with segments created via a sliding window. Data generalization is enhanced using a Seedance 2.0-based augmentation pipeline (varying materials, colors, objects) and Qwen3.6-Plus for instruction generation. LIBERO dataset is also incorporated for single-arm manipulation.
- **Egocentric hand data:** Samples from EgoDex and Ego4D, with unified 3D hand reconstruction using HaMeR to project hand joints into 2D pixel coordinates for action map generation. Original dataset captions are used.

#### 3.3. Architecture of Worldscape-MoE

The core of Worldscape-MoE lies in replacing the standard feed-forward network (FFN) in each DiT block with a **control-aware MoE-FFN**.
- **Expert Set:** For $M$ control modalities, each block $l$ has an expert set $E^{(l)} = \{E^{(l)}_0, E^{(l)}_1, \dots, E^{(l)}_M\}$, where $E^{(l)}_0$ is a "shared expert" for all samples, and $E^{(l)}_r$ is a "modality-specific expert" for the $r$-th control.
- **Initialization:** All experts are initialized from the pre-trained FFN or the current shared expert, preserving video-generation priors.
- **Expert Routing:** For each sample, a binary eligibility mask $m_i$ is constructed. The shared expert is always eligible, and a modality-specific expert is activated only if its corresponding control signal is present. A router predicts sample-wise logits from mean-pooled hidden states and computes routing weights using a masked softmax over eligible experts. Ineligible experts receive zero probability and do not participate in computation or gradient updates.
- **MoE-FFN Output:** The output is a weighted sum of the active experts' outputs. This deterministic routing ensures every sample updates the shared expert (crucial for cross-control knowledge) and prevents unrelated modality experts from receiving noisy gradients.

#### 3.4. Worldscape-MoE Tuning

A progressive training strategy is developed for stable extension to new control capabilities:
- **Grouped Learning:** Newly introduced or strongly modality-specific modules (e.g., new experts, control adapters) are optimized with a base learning rate. Shared components (Shared Expert, part of adapter parameters) are fine-tuned with a smaller learning rate to prevent overfitting to the most recent or frequent control type.
- **Progressive Control Expansion:** The model is extended in stages. Stage I establishes generation priors, camera-trajectory control, and embodied task capability using camera-control and robotic-arm signals. Stage II progressively introduces new control modalities and their experts. Each new expert is initialized from the current Shared Expert, inheriting accumulated world priors before specializing. This staged, incremental approach makes the framework extensible, allowing new controls to be absorbed without retraining previous ones from scratch.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Worldscape-MoE demonstrates consistent performance improvements across diverse control regimes and exhibits key properties related to expert specialization, scalability, and generalization.

#### 4.1. Overall Evaluation

Worldscape-MoE consistently outperforms baselines across the three primary heterogeneous control regimes:
- **Locomotion Setting (iWorld-Bench):** Achieves the best overall locomotion score of 0.7556, surpassing the strongest baseline VideoX-Fun-Wan by 0.0113. It also leads in motion smoothness with 0.9941. This indicates that heterogeneous training enhances, rather than degrades, camera-level action following.
- **Robotic Manipulation Setting (WorldArena):** Reports the highest EWM (Embodied World Model) score of 62.84, exceeding CtrlWorld and Wan 2.6 by +2.86 and +3.04, respectively. This demonstrates superior functional action outcomes and object interaction consistency.
- **Egocentric Hand Motion Setting:** Attains the best performance across all evaluated metrics: 3.80 FID-VID, 110.94 FVD, 5.78 image-level FID, and 0.7325 Image Quality. Compared to a variant without MoE, it significantly reduces FID-VID, FVD, and image-level FID by 1.59, 17.93, and 9.56, respectively.

Across all evaluated primary metrics, Worldscape-MoE ranks first, suggesting that heterogeneous training, when structured appropriately, allows different control signals to reinforce each other. Locomotion benefits from broader visual dynamics, manipulation from a stronger shared physical prior, and hand control from improved temporal coherence. Qualitative comparisons also show more controllable and physically coherent world dynamics.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Analysis of MoE

Analysis of expert workload confirms that the MoE structure learns meaningful specialization:
- The **shared expert** remains the dominant computational path, accounting for 69.48% of gate-weighted computation during inference on action-only cases. This validates its role in accumulating common world knowledge.
- **Dedicated experts** exhibit clear modality-dependent usage: 20.91% for locomotion, 47.95% for manipulation, and 35.97% for hand motion.
- This pattern is interpreted as logical: camera-like locomotion largely relies on shared spatiotemporal priors with moderate expert correction, whereas manipulation and hand control, which impose more direct constraints on object contact and fine-grained interactions, require stronger modality-specific computation. These routing statistics support the intended decomposition, where shared experts model common world dynamics and dedicated experts specialize in interface-specific action semantics.

#### 4.3. MoE Ablation and Modality Extension

- **Effectiveness of MoE:** A comparison with a direct mixed-training baseline (without MoE) shows that the dense baseline suffers from cross-modal control interference, resulting in weaker control fidelity and less coherent dynamics. Worldscape-MoE achieves stronger scores across all modalities with the same training budget, confirming that the architectural separation provided by MoE turns heterogeneous supervision into a scaling resource rather than a source of gradient conflict.
- **Scalability of MoE:** Experiments demonstrate that the framework can be extended to additional control modalities (e.g., single-arm LIBERO manipulation) without significantly compromising previously acquired capabilities. While a brief transient degradation in locomotion performance is observed early in the extension phase as parameters adjust, it is gradually mitigated, and the original capabilities recover. This indicates a practical scaling path for integrating new controls by extending the expert set and control branch while retaining the shared world prior. Weight update analysis supports balanced co-adaptation across experts.

#### 4.4. Out-of-Distribution Generalization and Loco-Manipulation Control

- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** Worldscape-MoE demonstrates strong OOD generalization across locomotion, robotic manipulation, and hand-motion domains. It generates coherent rollouts under large visual shifts (e.g., generalizing simulated Robotwin arms to realistic furniture-service scenarios, transferring hand-pose control to diverse unseen scenes). This suggests that the model learns reusable scene dynamics and physical regularities, implying that heterogeneous training encourages the shared expert to represent transferable world knowledge.
- **Loco-Manipulation Control:** The model can synthesize coupled loco-manipulation behaviors in diverse environments. This capability, where an agent can approach and manipulate targets, serves as a qualitative indicator for scalability, demonstrating that learning multiple controls within a shared world representation allows for the composition of previously isolated capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Worldscape-MoE represents a significant step towards addressing the fragmentation and scalability limitations of current world models. Its core contribution is the formulation of heterogeneous action-control world modeling as a scaling problem and the provision of a unified, extensible learning framework to tackle it.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that by jointly learning from diverse control data, a single model can *improve* physical consistency, temporal coherence, and overall world-simulation quality across different modalities, rather than suffering from interference. This directly counters the conventional approach of developing isolated, single-control models and offers a promising direction for alleviating the data bottleneck often encountered in embodied AI.

**Key impacts include:**
- **Unified Learning Framework:** Worldscape-MoE's architecture, combining modality-aware control injection with shared and control-specific Mixture-of-Experts, provides a robust mechanism to integrate disparate control signals. This design enables the shared expert to accumulate general world knowledge while dedicated experts handle the specifics of each action interface, allowing for efficient knowledge transfer and reduced redundant learning.
- **Scalability and Extensibility:** The progressive MoE tuning strategy allows for the continual absorption of new control modalities without relearning previous ones from scratch. This makes the framework highly extensible, turning fragmented datasets into a cumulative training resource.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The model achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance across locomotion, manipulation, and hand-motion tasks. Crucially, it exhibits strong out-of-distribution generalization and can compose complex loco-manipulation behaviors, indicating that it learns deeper, transferable physical regularities rather than merely memorizing training data.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** By providing a more versatile and robust world model, Worldscape-MoE could serve as a core infrastructure for developing more general and data-efficient intelligent agents capable of complex interactions in diverse environments, potentially benefiting robotics, interactive decision-making, and simulation.

**Potential positive broader impacts** include fostering the development of general intelligent agents, reducing the reliance on costly real-world data collection through high-fidelity simulation, and promoting transferable physical knowledge across tasks.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges that Worldscape-MoE is computationally expensive to train and not yet optimized for real-time deployment, with training speed decreasing as more modalities are added. Future work aims to improve efficiency and scalability by compressing the DiT architecture and extending the framework to an even broader range of control modalities.

**Ethical Considerations:** The high fidelity of generated videos necessitates careful ethical consideration regarding potential misuse, such as generating deceptive synthetic videos or misrepresenting safe procedures. The authors suggest exploring safeguards like synthetic-content detection, provenance mechanisms, and anomaly detection in future work.
