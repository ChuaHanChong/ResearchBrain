# TraceGen: World Modeling in 3D Trace Space Enables Learning from Cross-Embodiment Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> TraceGen, developed by researchers at the University of Maryland and NYU, introduces a 3D trace-space world model that learns from diverse cross-embodiment videos to enable robots to acquire new manipulation skills with minimal demonstrations. It achieved an 80% success rate on manipulation tasks with five robot warm-up videos and runs 3.8-50x faster than previous world models.

## Problem

- Robots require extensive and costly task-specific demonstrations, hindering data efficiency and generalization across platforms.
- Leveraging abundant 'cross-embodiment' videos (e.g., human or different robot demonstrations) is difficult due to disparities in physical embodiment, camera perspectives, and environments.
- Existing pixel-space world models are computationally expensive and prone to hallucinations, while language token models lack fine-grained precision for accurate manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces ==TraceForge==, a scalable data pipeline that converts heterogeneous video sources into unified 3D trace annotations, paired with multimodal observations and language.
- Develops **TraceGen**, a ==flow-based world model== that operates directly in a compact '3D trace-space' to predict future scene-level motion trajectories.
- Utilizes a ==multi-encoder architecture== with frozen pretrained visual (DINOv3, SigLIP) and text (T5-base) encoders, combined via ==Prismatic-VLM fusion==, to efficiently extract multimodal features.

## Results

- Achieved an **80%** success rate across four diverse real-world manipulation tasks with only five in-domain target-robot videos for warm-up, outperforming zero-shot baselines.
- Demonstrated effective human-to-robot transfer with a **67.5%** real-robot success rate using only five uncalibrated human demonstration videos for fine-tuning.
- Exhibits superior inference efficiency, running **3.8x** faster than other trace-generation baselines and over **50x** faster than large video-generation models, enabling practical real-time operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A compact '3D trace-space' representation, which abstracts away visual details while retaining geometric motion, provides an embodiment-agnostic interface for generalizable robot learning.
> - Large-scale pretraining on diverse cross-embodiment video data is critical for establishing a robust motion prior, enabling effective few-shot adaptation and human-to-robot skill transfer.
> - Directly modeling and predicting motion in 3D trace-space drastically improves inference efficiency, making the world model practical for real-time, closed-loop robot control.
