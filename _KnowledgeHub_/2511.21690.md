# TraceGen: World Modeling in 3D Trace Space Enables Learning from Cross-Embodiment Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> TraceGen, developed by researchers at the University of Maryland and NYU, introduces a 3D trace-space world model that learns from diverse cross-embodiment videos to enable robots to acquire new manipulation skills with minimal demonstrations. It achieved an 80% success rate on manipulation tasks with five robot warm-up videos and runs 3.8-50x faster than previous world models.

## Problem

- Robots require extensive and costly task-specific demonstrations, hindering data efficiency and generalization across platforms.
- Leveraging abundant 'cross-embodiment' videos (e.g., human or different robot demonstrations) is difficult due to disparities in physical embodiment, camera perspectives, and environments.
- Existing pixel-space world models are computationally expensive and prone to hallucinations, while language token models lack fine-grained precision for accurate manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces ==TraceForge==, a scalable data pipeline that converts heterogeneous video sources into unified 3D trace annotations, paired with multimodal observations and language.
- Develops **TraceGen**, a ==flow-based world model== that operates directly in a compact '3D trace-space' to predict future scene-level motion trajectories.
- Utilizes a ==multi-encoder architecture== with frozen pretrained visual (DINOv3, SigLIP) and text (T5-base) encoders, combined via ==Prismatic-VLM fusion==, to efficiently extract multimodal features.

## Results

- Achieved an **80%** success rate across four diverse real-world manipulation tasks with only five in-domain target-robot videos for warm-up, outperforming zero-shot baselines.
- Demonstrated effective human-to-robot transfer with a **67.5%** real-robot success rate using only five uncalibrated human demonstration videos for fine-tuning.
- Exhibits superior inference efficiency, running **3.8x** faster than other trace-generation baselines and over **50x** faster than large video-generation models, enabling practical real-time operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A compact '3D trace-space' representation, which abstracts away visual details while retaining geometric motion, provides an embodiment-agnostic interface for generalizable robot learning.
> - Large-scale pretraining on diverse cross-embodiment video data is critical for establishing a robust motion prior, enabling effective few-shot adaptation and human-to-robot skill transfer.
> - Directly modeling and predicting motion in 3D trace-space drastically improves inference efficiency, making the world model practical for real-time, closed-loop robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "TraceGen" positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of robot learning, particularly addressing the critical challenge of **generalization and data efficiency** in manipulation tasks. Robots traditionally require vast amounts of task-specific, in-domain demonstrations, which are laborious and costly to collect. Meanwhile, a wealth of "cross-embodiment" videos (e.g., humans performing tasks, or different robots executing similar motions) exists, but leveraging this data has proven difficult due to inherent disparities in embodiment, camera perspectives, and environmental setups.

The research identifies significant limitations of existing approaches:
- **Pixel-space world models (e.g., video generation models like NovaFlow, AVDC):** While expressive, these models expend computational capacity on reconstructing irrelevant background details and textures. This leads to high computational costs, making real-time planning difficult, and can also result in "hallucinations" of geometry or affordances that are not physically grounded. Their inference speed is often too slow for practical robotic control.
- **Language token-space models (e.g., VLM-based planners like Gemini Robotics):** These models generate discrete token sequences, which lack the fine-grained spatial and temporal precision necessary for accurate object manipulation and downstream control. Their outputs are often abstract and may not directly translate to actionable robot movements.
- **Previous trace prediction models:** While more efficient and directly aligned with control, prior work in this area has largely been restricted to 2D traces or trained on static, in-lab demonstrations. Few 3D variants exist (e.g., 3DFlowAction), but these often focus solely on manipulated objects, requiring auxiliary modules like object detectors and heuristic filtering, which introduce error cascades and cannot capture the full scene-level motion, including the robot's end-effector.

TraceGen directly tackles these limitations by introducing a **unifying, symbolic representation called "3D trace-space."** This representation captures scene-level trajectories (of both manipulated objects and the robot's end-effector) in 3D, abstracting away appearance details and camera variations. By learning to predict motion in this compact, geometric space, TraceGen aims to achieve several advancements:
1. **Embodiment, camera, and environment invariance:** The trace-space abstraction inherently makes the model less sensitive to differences in the physical agent, observation viewpoint, or scene background.
2. **Computational efficiency:** By avoiding pixel-space generation, TraceGen can operate much faster, enabling real-time planning.
3. **Data efficiency and transferability:** Learning a strong 3D motion prior from a diverse, large-scale cross-embodiment dataset allows for rapid adaptation to new tasks and robots with only a handful of demonstrations (few-shot learning).
4. **Completeness:** By modeling "scene-level" 3D trajectories, including robot and objects together, it avoids the pitfalls of relying on external object detectors or heuristic filtering, which often lead to incomplete or erroneous physical representations.

This work contributes significantly to the field's goal of developing "generalist robots" that can acquire new skills quickly and flexibly, bridging the gap between abundant video data and the practical needs of robotic control. It provides a novel intermediate representation and a robust framework for learning from heterogeneous data sources, moving beyond the constraints of purely pixel-based or language-token-based approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind TraceGen stems from the fundamental challenge in robot learning: the prohibitive cost and time required to collect sufficient, task-specific robot demonstrations for every new task or platform. Despite the abundance of readily available videos depicting humans or other robots performing various manipulation tasks, directly reusing this "cross-embodiment" data is hampered by significant differences in physical embodiment, camera setups, and environmental contexts.

Against this backdrop, the authors articulate several key objectives:

1. **Overcome Small-Data Regimes for New Robots and Tasks:** The overarching goal is to enable robots to learn diverse manipulation skills with only a handful of demonstrations, thereby drastically reducing the data bottleneck. This necessitates finding a way to effectively exploit existing, heterogeneous video data.

2. **Develop a Unifying, Symbolic Representation: The 3D Trace-Space:** The core objective is to introduce an intermediate representation that is abstract enough to generalize across embodiments, cameras, and environments, yet precise enough to capture the geometric structure crucial for manipulation. This "trace-space" focuses on "the where and how of motion" in 3D, discarding irrelevant appearance details.

3. **Construct a World Model Operating Directly in Trace-Space (TraceGen):** Instead of predicting future frames in pixel space or generating abstract language tokens, a key objective is to build a world model that directly forecasts future motion trajectories within the compact 3D trace-space. This design aims to achieve high computational efficiency and greater relevance to control signals.

4. **Create a Scalable Data Pipeline (TraceForge):** To enable large-scale pretraining of the trace-space world model, the authors aim to develop a robust data pipeline capable of converting heterogeneous video sources (ranging from controlled lab robot videos to in-the-wild human videos) into consistent, unified 3D trace annotations, paired with multimodal observations and language.

5. **Demonstrate Efficient Few-Shot Adaptation:** A critical objective is to validate TraceGen's ability to rapidly adapt to new skills and environments with minimal target-robot data. This includes demonstrating both "Robot→Robot" adaptation (from a small set of in-domain robot videos) and, more ambitiously, "Human→Robot" transfer (from uncalibrated human demonstration videos).

6. **Achieve Superior Inference Efficiency:** Beyond performance, a key motivation is to significantly improve the computational speed of prediction compared to existing video-generation-based world models, thereby enabling practical, real-time closed-loop planning for robotics.

By addressing these objectives, TraceGen seeks to offer a practical and powerful solution for more generalizable and data-efficient robotic manipulation, leveraging the vast but previously underutilized wealth of cross-embodiment video data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TraceGen framework comprises two main components: **TraceForge**, a data pipeline for generating structured 3D trace annotations, and **TraceGen** itself, a world model trained on these annotations to predict future motion.

#### 3.1. TraceForge: Scalable Dataset Construction

TraceForge is designed to transform heterogeneous video sources into a unified 3D trace representation, creating a corpus of 123K videos and 1.8M observation–trace–language triplets. This pipeline operates directly on in-the-wild footage with moving viewpoints, overcoming limitations of prior work.

1. **Event Chunking and Instruction Generation:** Videos are first segmented into task-relevant "event chunks." For each chunk, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) is used to generate diverse task instructions in three styles: a short imperative, a multi-step decomposition, and a natural human-like request. This provides rich, varied linguistic supervision.
2. **3D Point Tracking with Camera Pose and Depth Prediction:** To extract 3D traces from videos, TraceForge leverages TAPIP3D (Tracking Any Point in Persistent 3D Geometry) with CoTracker3 for point tracking. For efficiency, the MegaSAM component in TAPIP3D is replaced with a fine-tuned VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) depth and camera pose predictor from SpatialTrackerV2, which offers comparable accuracy at significantly faster inference. This process recovers per-frame camera poses, depth maps, and tracks a uniform 20x20 grid of keypoints in 3D (represented as (x,y,z) where (x,y) are image-plane coordinates and z is depth), allowing co-training with 2D-only traces for broader data coverage.
3. **World-to-Camera Transformation:** To ensure consistency across varying camera viewpoints, all 3D traces are transformed into a fixed reference camera frame (cam_ref). This compensates for camera motion, making the traces point-of-view-consistent.
4. **Speed Retargeting:** Human and robot demonstrations often differ in execution speed. To make traces comparable across episodes and embodiments, speed retargeting is applied. This involves temporally normalizing each trace to a fixed length (L steps) by reparameterizing based on cumulative arc length and resampling, preserving the relative motion profile without distorting local velocities.

#### 3.2. TraceGen: Architecture and Training

TraceGen is a flow-based world model designed to predict future 3D motion trajectories directly from multimodal observations (RGB, depth, language). It builds upon the CogVideoX architecture and employs a Prismatic-VLM fusion strategy.

1. **Multi-Encoder Feature Extraction:**
 - **RGB Encoders:** Two frozen, pretrained encoders are used: DINOv3 (ViT-L/16) for spatially-aware geometric features and SigLIP (Base-Patch16-384) for semantically aligned features.
 - **Depth Encoder:** A third encoder processes depth maps using SigLIP with a learnable 1x1 convolutional stem adapter to project single-channel depth to the 3-channel input space expected by SigLIP.
 - **Text Encoder:** Task instructions are encoded using a frozen T5-base encoder.
 - **Prismatic VLM Fusion:** The three visual feature streams (DINOv3, SigLIP, depth-SigLIP) are concatenated and then projected to a unified dimension (D=768) via a learnable linear layer. These unified visual tokens are then combined with the text tokens to form the conditioning input for the trace decoder.

2. **Flow-based Trace Decoder:**
 - **Architecture:** Adapts CogVideoX's 3D transformer. It takes a K × L grid (K=20x20 keypoints, L=32 future timesteps) of 3D points (x,y,z). Spatial patchification (2x2 keypoints per token) reduces input dimensions. Conditioned on the fused visual and text tokens via Adaptive LayerNorms.
 - **Trace Generation via Stochastic Interpolants:** Instead of predicting absolute 3D trace values, the model is trained to predict "velocity-like increments" (temporal differences, ΔT) in keypoints. It leverages the Stochastic Interpolant framework, specifically a linear interpolation ODE (X_τ = (1-τ)X_0 + τX_1). A neural network (v_θ) learns to predict the constant velocity field (X_1 - X_0) between noise (X_0) and the ground-truth trace increments (X_1) by minimizing a mean squared error loss.
 - **Test-Time Generation:** At inference, trajectories are generated via 100-step ODE integration, starting from Gaussian noise and guided by the multimodal conditioning.
 - **Encoder Freezing:** A crucial design choice is to keep all DINOv3, SigLIP, and T5 encoders frozen during training. Only the fusion layer and the flow-based decoder are trained. This prevents catastrophic forgetting of powerful pretrained representations, reduces trainable parameters, and accelerates training.

**Execution:** The predicted 3D traces from TraceGen are converted into robot joint commands using inverse kinematics after transforming them from the camera frame to the robot's base frame. A basic tracking controller is employed to demonstrate the execution of these scene-level 3D traces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate TraceGen's superior performance, efficiency, and generalization capabilities across various challenging scenarios in real-world robotic manipulation.

1. **Performance and Efficiency Comparison (Real-World Experiments):**
 - **High Success Rate with Few-Shot Warmup:** With only five in-domain target-robot videos for warm-up, TraceGen achieves an impressive **80% success rate** across four diverse manipulation tasks (folding clothes, inserting a ball, sweeping trash, placing a block) on a Franka Research 3 robot.
 - **Outperforms Baselines:** Existing video-generation-based and trace-based world models (e.g., AVDC, NovaFlow, 3DFlowAction) generally fail (0% success) in the zero-shot setting or require proprietary large models with extremely slow inference. TraceGen, with 0.67 billion parameters, significantly outperforms these alternatives.
 - **Exceptional Inference Efficiency:** TraceGen demonstrates substantial computational benefits, running **3.8× faster** than other trace-generation baselines and over **50× faster** than large video-generation models (e.g., NovaFlow Veo 3.1, which is 600× slower than TraceGen). This high speed makes it practical for real-time, closed-loop robot control.

2. **Human→Robot Skill Transfer:**
 - **Effective Transfer from Uncalibrated Human Videos:** In a challenging setting where only five uncalibrated human demonstration videos (captured on a handheld phone in different scenes with varying backgrounds and object layouts) were used for fine-tuning, TraceGen achieved a **67.5% real-robot success rate** across the four tasks.
 - **Importance of Pretraining:** A "From Scratch" model (without TraceGen's large-scale pretraining) failed on all human→robot transfer tasks (0% success), unequivocally demonstrating the critical role of cross-embodiment pretraining. The ease of data collection for human warm-up (under 4 minutes for 20 demos) further highlights its practicality.

3. **Role of Pretraining and Warmup:**
 - **Pretraining as the Foundation:** Quantitative analysis confirms that TraceGen's performance is predominantly driven by its large-scale cross-embodiment pretraining. With five robot warm-up videos, the pretrained TraceGen achieved 80% success, whereas a randomly initialized model with the same architecture (trained only on warm-up data) achieved only 25%.
 - **Diminishing Returns from Additional Warmup Data:** Increasing the warm-up data from 5 to 15 robot videos resulted in only marginal improvement for the pretrained model (80% to 82.5%) and limited change for the scratch model (25% to 30%), reinforcing that pretraining establishes a strong, adaptable motion prior.
 - **Benefit of Cross-Embodiment Data:** Comparing different pretraining sources, the full TraceForge-123K dataset (cross-embodiment) yielded significantly higher success (70%) than pretraining on single-source datasets like SSV2 (human-centric, 25%) or Agibot (robot-centric, 45%). This indicates that both embodiment alignment (robot data) and diverse motion coverage (human + robot data) are essential for robust transfer.

4. **TraceForge Accuracy (Appendix B):** A sanity check on TraceForge's 3D trace extraction pipeline showed sub-2.3 cm endpoint error along all axes, confirming that the generated 3D traces provide reliable and accurate supervision for training TraceGen.

5. **Long Horizon Experiments (Appendix E.2):** TraceGen demonstrated the ability to compose primitive behaviors for a multi-step sorting task. While occasionally failing the first step, the pretrained model maintained a high success rate (0.8 per step) across subsequent subtasks, indicating that its compact 3D trace representation supports stable stitching of skills over longer task horizons, mitigating error accumulation seen in a "From Scratch" model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TraceGen represents a significant step forward in robotic manipulation, with several profound implications for the future of embodied AI and real-world robot deployment.

1. **Novel Unified Representation for Manipulation:** The introduction of a "3D trace-space" as a compact, symbolic representation for scene-level trajectories is a core innovation. By abstracting away irrelevant visual details (appearance, backgrounds) while retaining crucial geometric motion information, TraceGen effectively creates an **embodiment-agnostic interface**. This allows a single world model to understand and predict motion across vastly different physical agents, from human hands to diverse robot arms, a capability largely missing in prior pixel-based or language-token-based approaches. This representation is inherently more aligned with the needs of physical control.

2. **Scalable Data Curation and Pretraining:** The **TraceForge pipeline** is equally critical. It provides a robust and scalable methodology for transforming highly heterogeneous video sources (in-the-wild human videos, various robot datasets) into a consistent, structured 3D trace format. This enables the creation of large-scale, multimodal datasets (TraceForge-123K) that are essential for pretraining powerful generalist models. The ability to leverage abundant, unlabeled video data from the internet opens up vast possibilities for scaling robot learning beyond carefully curated, in-lab datasets.

3. **Unprecedented Few-Shot Learning and Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The most impactful result is TraceGen's demonstrated ability to achieve high success rates with an extremely small number of demonstrations. The 80% success with just five robot warm-up videos and, notably, the 67.5% success from only five uncalibrated human phone videos, showcases truly **data-efficient and generalizable learning**. This drastically reduces the cost and time required to deploy new skills on new robots or in new environments, moving closer to "plug-and-play" robotic capabilities. The finding that cross-embodiment pretraining is crucial and superior to single-source pretraining underscores the value of diverse data in establishing robust motion priors.

4. **Enabling Real-Time and Practical Robotics:** The orders-of-magnitude faster inference speed (50-600× faster) compared to pixel-based world models is a game-changer for practical robotics. High inference latency has been a major barrier to using complex generative models for closed-loop control and real-time planning. TraceGen's efficiency removes this bottleneck, making it a viable solution for dynamic, responsive robot operation in unstructured environments.

5. **Robustness to Real-World Variation:** The successful transfer from uncalibrated human videos, with varying camera angles, backgrounds, and object positions, highlights the model's robustness. This suggests that TraceGen can handle the inherent "messiness" of real-world data and environments, a crucial attribute for widespread robot adoption outside controlled laboratory settings.

6. **Future Directions for Generalist Agents:** TraceGen lays foundational groundwork for future research in several areas:
 - Developing more sophisticated policies and planning modules that can fully exploit the rich 3D trace predictions.
 - Exploring explicit control over trajectory modes for ambiguous tasks within the stochastic interpolant framework.
 - Scaling to even larger, internet-scale datasets with improved filtering to address suboptimal demonstrations.
 - Extending the trace-space abstraction to more diverse and non-humanoid robot morphologies, testing the limits of its generality.

In conclusion, TraceGen offers a compelling blueprint for how robots can efficiently learn complex manipulation skills by effectively utilizing a universal 3D motion language derived from diverse demonstrations. Its combination of a novel representation, a robust data pipeline, and highly efficient learning significantly advances the quest for generalist and truly capable robotic systems.
