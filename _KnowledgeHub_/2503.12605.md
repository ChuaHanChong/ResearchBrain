# Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Reasoning: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper offers the first comprehensive survey of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning, analyzing its evolution, diverse methodologies, and applications across various modalities. It consolidates scattered research, providing a systematic taxonomy, elucidating foundational concepts, and identifying future research directions to foster innovation in multimodal AI.

## Problem

- The rapid growth and scattered nature of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) research lacked a comprehensive, systematic, and up-to-date review.
- Researchers faced difficulty grasping foundational concepts, diverse methodologies, and future trajectories of MCoT due to the absence of consolidated knowledge.
- A clear framework was needed to categorize and analyze MCoT methodologies across various modalities and application scenarios.

## Method

- The authors conducted the first systematic survey solely dedicated to ==Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT)== reasoning.
- They established a foundational understanding by defining key terms (CoT, MCoT, thought paradigms) and formalizing the MCoT framework into two scenarios based on ==rationale nature==.
- The survey provided a multi-faceted analysis by categorizing MCoT research based on various modalities, detailed methodological approaches, practical applications, and available datasets/benchmarks.

## Results

- The survey presented a comprehensive taxonomy of MCoT methods based on rationale construction, structural reasoning, information enhancement, objective granularity, multimodal rationale, and test-time scaling.
- It highlighted the emergence of multimodal rationales (e.g., visual thoughts, audio integration) as a trend beyond text-only reasoning, mirroring human cognition.
- The survey showcased MCoT's broad applicability across real-world domains including embodied AI, autonomous driving, healthcare, and multimodal content generation, underscoring its versatility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MCoT reasoning demonstrates a clear progression from linear chains to complex tree, graph, and hypergraph structures, enabling richer problem-solving.
> - The effectiveness of MCoT stems from tailored adaptations and distinct strategies for different modalities, emphasizing the need for specialized processing.
> - Scaling compute during inference, or 'slow thinking,' is becoming as effective as scaling model parameters for enhancing reasoning, often facilitated by Reinforcement Learning.
