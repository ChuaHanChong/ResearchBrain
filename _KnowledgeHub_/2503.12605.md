# Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Reasoning: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper offers the first comprehensive survey of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning, analyzing its evolution, diverse methodologies, and applications across various modalities. It consolidates scattered research, providing a systematic taxonomy, elucidating foundational concepts, and identifying future research directions to foster innovation in multimodal AI.

## Problem

- The rapid growth and scattered nature of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) research lacked a comprehensive, systematic, and up-to-date review.
- Researchers faced difficulty grasping foundational concepts, diverse methodologies, and future trajectories of MCoT due to the absence of consolidated knowledge.
- A clear framework was needed to categorize and analyze MCoT methodologies across various modalities and application scenarios.

## Method

- The authors conducted the first systematic survey solely dedicated to ==Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT)== reasoning.
- They established a foundational understanding by defining key terms (CoT, MCoT, thought paradigms) and formalizing the MCoT framework into two scenarios based on ==rationale nature==.
- The survey provided a multi-faceted analysis by categorizing MCoT research based on various modalities, detailed methodological approaches, practical applications, and available datasets/benchmarks.

## Results

- The survey presented a comprehensive taxonomy of MCoT methods based on rationale construction, structural reasoning, information enhancement, objective granularity, multimodal rationale, and test-time scaling.
- It highlighted the emergence of multimodal rationales (e.g., visual thoughts, audio integration) as a trend beyond text-only reasoning, mirroring human cognition.
- The survey showcased MCoT's broad applicability across real-world domains including embodied AI, autonomous driving, healthcare, and multimodal content generation, underscoring its versatility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MCoT reasoning demonstrates a clear progression from linear chains to complex tree, graph, and hypergraph structures, enabling richer problem-solving.
> - The effectiveness of MCoT stems from tailored adaptations and distinct strategies for different modalities, emphasizing the need for specialized processing.
> - Scaling compute during inference, or 'slow thinking,' is becoming as effective as scaling model parameters for enhancing reasoning, often facilitated by Reinforcement Learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has ushered in a new era for AI. Initially, LLMs excelled in text-based tasks, demonstrating impressive language intelligence. However, the real world is inherently multimodal, necessitating the evolution of AI from text-only LLMs to multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) capable of integrating and processing diverse data types such as images, video, audio, and structured data.

A significant breakthrough in enhancing LLMs' cognitive abilities was the introduction of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. Inspired by human-like step-by-step problem-solving, CoT enables models to break down complex tasks into intermediate logical steps, improving both performance on intricate reasoning tasks and the transparency of decision-making. This technique, first popularized for text, has seen remarkable success and widespread adoption, implicitly integrated into cutting-edge systems like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek R1.

The natural progression was to extend CoT's advantages to multimodal contexts, leading to the emergence of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning. This integration represents a transformative step in AI, aiming to imbue MLLMs with sophisticated cognitive reasoning capabilities that transcend basic perceptual understanding. MCoT addresses the unique challenges posed by heterogeneous cross-modal data interactions, where visual reasoning requires precise perception, video understanding demands robust temporal dynamics modeling, and so forth. Researchers have developed a spectrum of innovative MCoT methodologies, such as Multimodal-CoT, MVoT, Video-of-Thought, Audio-CoT, and CoT3DRef, tailored to modality-specific characteristics.

This survey directly addresses a critical gap in this rapidly evolving landscape: the absence of a comprehensive, up-to-date review. While MCoT research has garnered growing attention and demonstrated promising potential in diverse applications (e.g., autonomous driving, embodied AI, healthcare, robotics, and multimodal generation), the scattered nature of publications hinders knowledge consolidation. By providing the "first systematic survey" of MCoT, this paper aims to bridge this gap, offering a structured analysis that consolidates technological developments, methodologies, practical applications, and future directions. It positions itself as an authoritative reference point, essential for fostering innovation and guiding future research in this pivotal domain en route to multimodal Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the burgeoning interest and rapid advancements in Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning, particularly in conjunction with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Despite its demonstrated effectiveness in addressing complex challenges across various modalities—including images, video, speech, 3D, and structured data—and its extensive success in practical applications like robotics, healthcare, autonomous driving, and multimodal generation, there has been a critical lack of a comprehensive, systematic, and up-to-date review of this emerging field. This absence creates a knowledge consolidation gap, making it difficult for researchers to grasp the foundational concepts, diverse methodologies, and future trajectories of MCoT.

To bridge this crucial gap, the authors set forth several key objectives:

1. **First Systematic Survey:** To present the inaugural comprehensive review dedicated solely to MCoT reasoning, establishing a foundational understanding of the field.
2. **Elucidate Foundational Concepts and Definitions:** To clearly define relevant terminologies, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Multimodal CoT (MCoT), and "thought paradigms" (chain, tree, graph, hypergraph), providing a consistent framework for understanding the field.
3. **Comprehensive Taxonomy and In-depth Analysis:** To propose a meticulous taxonomy that categorizes and analyzes current MCoT methodologies from diverse perspectives (e.g., rationale construction, structural reasoning, information enhancement, objective granularity, multimodal rationale, and test-time scaling). This structured approach aims to provide a clear overview of the varied approaches and their nuances across different application scenarios.
4. **Insights into Challenges and Future Directions:** To identify and discuss the existing limitations and distinct challenges within MCoT, such as computational sustainability, error propagation, the symbolic-neural integration gap, hallucination prevention, and data issues. Simultaneously, the survey aims to outline promising avenues for future research, inspiring innovation and directing efforts towards unresolved problems.
5. **Resource Sharing:** To compile and make publicly available all relevant resources, including the surveyed papers, datasets, and benchmarks, to support and accelerate progress within the research community.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to serve as an authoritative reference that not only consolidates current knowledge but also actively fosters innovation, guiding the research community toward the ambitious goal of achieving multimodal AGI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This survey adopts a systematic and comprehensive review methodology to analyze the vast and rapidly evolving landscape of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning. The authors structure their approach to provide a clear, multi-faceted understanding of the field, categorizing existing research along several key dimensions.

The survey begins by establishing a **foundational understanding** of MCoT. It clearly defines essential terms like "In-context Learning" (ICL), "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT), and "Multimodal CoT" (MCoT), alongside related concepts such as "thought" and "rationale." Crucially, it formalizes the MCoT framework by distinguishing between two scenarios based on the nature of the rationale: Scenario-1, where reasoning relies on text-only thoughts for multimodal input/output, and Scenario-2, which integrates multimodal signals beyond linguistic content within the rationale itself. The survey also details the evolution of "thought paradigms" from linear chains to more complex tree, graph, and hypergraph structures, explaining how these topologies enhance exploration, aggregation, and joint reasoning. A background on Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and their architectures (comprehension-only vs. comprehension-generation) is also provided.

Following the foundational overview, the survey organizes its analysis into several key sections:

1. **MCoT Reasoning Under Various Modalities:** This section systematically reviews MCoT research as applied to different modalities. It dedicates subsections to MCoT reasoning over images, video, 3D data, audio and speech, and structured data (tables and charts). A separate subsection is devoted to cross-modal CoT reasoning, highlighting approaches that integrate multiple modalities beyond just text and one other. This modality-centric breakdown emphasizes how MCoT methodologies are adapted to address the unique characteristics and challenges of each data type.

2. **Methodologies in MCoT Reasoning:** This forms the core of the survey's analytical approach, offering a comprehensive taxonomy of MCoT methods from six distinct perspectives:
- **Rationale Construction:** Categorizes methods based on how rationales are generated, distinguishing between prompt-based (guiding models via prompts), plan-based (dynamic exploration and refinement using tree/graph structures), and learning-based (embedding reasoning skills during training/fine-tuning) approaches.
- **Structural Reasoning:** Explores how reasoning steps are organized and controlled. This includes asynchronous modality modeling (decoupling perception from reasoning), defined procedure staging (explicitly pre-defined reasoning stages), and autonomous procedure staging (LLMs self-determining reasoning sequences).
- **Information Enhancing:** Discusses how external information enriches reasoning. This covers the use of expert tools (e.g., for visual or geometric operations), world knowledge retrieval (integrating external knowledge bases), and in-context knowledge retrieval (organizing information directly from inputs or generated rationales).
- **Objective Granularity:** Examines how MCoT aligns with the level of detail required by tasks, differentiating between coarse understanding, semantic grounding (instance-level), and fine-grained understanding.
- **Multimodal Rationale:** Contrasts traditional text-only rationales with emerging approaches that generate and leverage multimodal thoughts (e.g., visual or audio elements) to better mimic human cognitive processes.
- **Test-Time Scaling:** Explores strategies to enhance reasoning through deliberate, extended processing during inference, drawing parallels to "slow thinking" in human cognition. This includes slow-thinking-based models (internal/external refinement) and reinforcement learning-based models (using RL to activate or improve long-chain reasoning, sometimes leading to "aha-moments").

3. **Applications with MCoT Reasoning:** This section synthesizes the practical utility of MCoT by summarizing its extensive deployment across a wide range of real-world domains, including embodied AI, agentic systems, autonomous driving, medical and healthcare, social and human sciences, and multimodal generation.

4. **MCoT Datasets and Benchmarks:** The survey provides a crucial overview of the resources available for MCoT research, classifying them into datasets for MLLM finetuning with rationales and benchmarks for evaluating downstream capabilities (both with and without explicit rationales). A comparative performance table for MLLMs across key benchmarks is also included.

Finally, the authors conclude by discussing **Limitations, Challenges, and Future Directions**, identifying key bottlenecks and suggesting avenues for innovation. This systematic deconstruction and multi-perspective analysis allow the survey to provide a holistic and in-depth understanding of MCoT, making it a valuable resource for researchers in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive survey on Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning distills several key findings and observations from the rapidly evolving research landscape:

1. **Evolution of Thought Paradigms:** The survey highlights a clear progression in reasoning structures, from the initial linear "chain" in vanilla CoT to more sophisticated "tree," "graph," and even "hypergraph" topologies. This evolution allows for richer problem-solving strategies, including exploration of multiple reasoning paths, backtracking, aggregation of diverse thoughts, and modeling higher-order associations, which is crucial for tackling complex multimodal tasks.

2. **Modality-Specific Adaptations:** MCoT is not a one-size-fits-all solution; its effectiveness stems from tailored adaptations to different modalities. The survey showcases how distinct strategies are developed for images (e.g., precise perception, object relationship modeling), videos (temporal dynamics, long-video analysis), 3D data (spatial relationships, physical properties), audio/speech (waveform to semantics), and structured data (layout interpretation, command-based operations). This emphasizes the necessity of specialized processing strategies for heterogeneous data.

3. **Diverse Methodological Approaches:** The taxonomy reveals a rich array of methodologies developed to construct and leverage rationales:
- **Rationale Construction:** Methods vary from simple prompt-based guidance (zero-shot/few-shot prompting) to complex plan-based approaches (dynamic exploration via search algorithms or multi-agent debates) and learning-based techniques (fine-tuning models with explicit reasoning data).
- **Structural Reasoning:** Approaches range from asynchronous processing of modalities ("description then decision") to explicitly defined staged procedures (e.g., "see, think, confirm" or "planning, captioning, reasoning, summary") and even autonomous staging where models self-determine reasoning steps.
- **Information Enhancing:** Researchers leverage external expert tools (e.g., for image manipulation, 3D generation), integrate world knowledge via retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and harness in-context knowledge extracted from inputs or generated rationales (e.g., scene graphs).
- **Objective Granularity:** MCoT supports various levels of understanding, from coarse-grained overview tasks (VQA) to fine-grained semantic grounding (object detection, segmentation) and detailed instance-level analysis.

4. **Emergence of Multimodal Rationales:** While text-only rationales have been dominant, a significant trend is the exploration of multimodal rationales. This includes generating visual thoughts (e.g., highlighted regions, intermediate imaginary states, auxiliary diagrams) or integrating audio information into the reasoning process. This shift aims to emulate human-like cognitive processes more closely, where thoughts are not solely linguistic but also visual or sensory.

5. **Importance of Test-Time Scaling and "Slow Thinking":** A critical finding is the increasing recognition that scaling compute during inference ("slow thinking") can be as, or even more, effective than merely scaling model parameters. Inspired by human System 2 cognition, models like OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek R1 demonstrate enhanced reasoning through deliberate, extended processing. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful tool to activate and improve these long-chain reasoning capabilities, even enabling the emergence of "aha-moments" (reflection and backtracking) in visual reasoning scenarios without extensive supervised fine-tuning.

6. **Broad Applicability and Impact:** MCoT has proven transformative across a wide spectrum of real-world applications, including robotics (embodied AI, manipulation, navigation), autonomous driving (perception, planning, decision-making), healthcare (diagnosis, medical QA), agentic systems (GUI manipulation, personalized recommendations), social sciences (empathetic dialogue, sentiment analysis), and multimodal content generation (image, 3D, video synthesis). This widespread adoption underscores MCoT's versatility and its potential to solve complex challenges in practical domains.

7. **Growing Data and Benchmark Landscape:** The community is actively developing specialized datasets that include reasoning rationales for fine-tuning MLLMs (e.g., ScienceQA, VideoCoT, MAVIS) and diverse benchmarks to rigorously assess MCoT's performance across various tasks and modalities, including those evaluating hallucination, spatial reasoning, and agent capabilities.

In summary, the survey illustrates that MCoT is a vibrant and rapidly advancing field characterized by diverse methodologies, increasing multimodal integration, and a growing emphasis on efficient, human-like reasoning processes, driven by innovations in both model training and inference strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey on Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning holds significant importance for the advancement of artificial intelligence, particularly in the realm of multimodal AI and the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

1. **Knowledge Consolidation and Foundational Reference:** As the "first systematic survey" dedicated to MCoT, this paper serves as a crucial knowledge consolidator in a rapidly evolving and fragmented field. By elucidating foundational concepts, providing clear definitions, and offering a comprehensive taxonomy, it establishes a common framework for understanding MCoT. This makes it an authoritative reference for new researchers entering the domain and a structured guide for experienced practitioners, preventing redundant efforts and accelerating collective understanding.

2. **Driving Innovation and Research Direction:** The meticulous categorization of methodologies (e.g., rationale construction, structural reasoning, information enhancing) not only summarizes current achievements but also implicitly highlights successful paradigms and underexplored areas. More explicitly, the detailed discussion of "Limitations, Challenges, and Future Directions" directly points to critical open questions and promising avenues for future research. This includes areas like computational sustainability, error propagation in long chains, bridging the symbolic-neural integration gap, dynamic environment adaptation, hallucination prevention, and advanced data strategies. By clearly defining these challenges, the survey acts as a roadmap for future innovation.

3. **Fostering Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The survey emphasizes the value of integrating insights from cognitive science, psychology, and neuroscience to inspire more human-like reasoning architectures. This interdisciplinary call to action could lead to novel approaches that enhance both the performance and interpretability of MCoT systems, moving beyond purely data-driven methods.

4. **Enhancing Real-World Applicability and Robustness:** By detailing the successful application of MCoT in critical domains such as embodied AI, autonomous driving, healthcare, and agentic systems, the survey underscores its practical utility. Furthermore, by addressing challenges like hallucination prevention, robustness against adversarial attacks, and the need for ethical AI frameworks, it directly contributes to developing safer, more reliable, and trustworthy multimodal AI systems for real-world deployment.

5. **Promoting Transparency and Explainability:** The core essence of CoT, and by extension MCoT, is to make the reasoning process more transparent through step-by-step rationales. The survey's focus on structured reasoning and the shift towards multimodal rationales contributes to making complex AI decisions more interpretable. While acknowledging the current "black box" nature of underlying theoretical mechanisms, the emphasis on developing transparent, traceable reasoning paths is crucial for building user trust and facilitating debugging and optimization.

6. **Resource Accessibility:** The public sharing of relevant resources through a GitHub repository is a significant contribution. This direct provision of datasets, benchmarks, and surveyed papers lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and facilitates reproducibility and comparative studies, thus accelerating overall progress in the field.

In essence, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work; it is a forward-looking document designed to galvanize the research community. By providing clarity, identifying key challenges, and suggesting promising pathways, it is poised to significantly influence the trajectory of multimodal AI research, driving progress towards more capable, intelligent, and human-aligned AI systems.
