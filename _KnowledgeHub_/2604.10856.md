# BridgeSim: Unveiling the OL-CL Gap in End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> This research identifies Observational Domain Shift and Objective Mismatch as the core reasons for the Open-Loop (OL) to Closed-Loop (CL) performance gap in end-to-end autonomous driving, introducing BridgeSim, a unified cross-simulator platform, and a Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) framework that significantly enhances CL robustness of OL-pretrained policies without requiring retraining. TTA-enhanced agents achieve higher temporal consistency and safety, demonstrating substantial improvements in driving scores (e.g., +19.1 DS) in reactive traffic scenarios.

## Problem

- High performance of end-to-end driving policies on open-loop (OL) benchmarks does not reliably translate to safe or feasible behavior in dynamic closed-loop (CL) simulations.
- Scaling laws for performance observed in OL evaluations, based on factors like data volume or compute, do not consistently transfer to real-world CL deployment.
- Existing driving simulators and benchmarks often lack sufficient visual realism, scenario diversity, or consistent evaluation protocols to rigorously investigate the OL-CL gap.

## Method

- Developed **BridgeSim**, a cross-simulator CL evaluation platform with unified scenario protocols, flexible deployment settings, and structural invariance for rigorous analysis.
- Decomposed the OL-CL gap into two primary failure modes: Observational Domain Shift (perception degradation) and Objective Mismatch (biased Q-value estimation and compounding errors).
- Proposed a training-free ==Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)== (TTA) framework, combining a ==flow-matching observational calibrator==, a truncated Q-value estimator, and an adaptive replan mechanism to address these identified gaps.

## Results

- The TTA framework significantly flattened the decay curve of E2E planners' reliability in long-horizon (4**-16** seconds) CL simulations on BridgeSim-NavHard scenarios, maintaining higher temporal consistency and safety.
- TTA consistently improved CL performance across diverse open-loop baselines (e.g., DiffusionDrive, RAP) in unseen scenes and reactive traffic environments, achieving a **+19.1** Driving Score (DS) improvement with RAP in IDM mode.
- Component analysis confirmed that the truncated Q-value estimator and adaptive replan mechanism individually and combined enhanced performance and stability, demonstrating more favorable scaling dynamics compared to baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The OL-CL gap is fundamentally caused by Observational Domain Shift (perceptual differences between training and test environments) and, more critically, Objective Mismatch (discrepancy between static OL training objectives and dynamic CL reactive requirements).
> - OL training often leads to biased Q-value estimation and susceptibility to compounding execution errors due to optimizing for a static, full-sequence trajectory rather than a reactive, receding-horizon policy.
> - Merely scaling open-loop training or computational resources for trajectory proposals does not effectively improve closed-loop performance without proper adaptation that addresses objective misalignment.
