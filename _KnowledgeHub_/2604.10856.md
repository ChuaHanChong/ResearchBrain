# BridgeSim: Unveiling the OL-CL Gap in End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> This research identifies Observational Domain Shift and Objective Mismatch as the core reasons for the Open-Loop (OL) to Closed-Loop (CL) performance gap in end-to-end autonomous driving, introducing BridgeSim, a unified cross-simulator platform, and a Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) framework that significantly enhances CL robustness of OL-pretrained policies without requiring retraining. TTA-enhanced agents achieve higher temporal consistency and safety, demonstrating substantial improvements in driving scores (e.g., +19.1 DS) in reactive traffic scenarios.

## Problem

- High performance of end-to-end driving policies on open-loop (OL) benchmarks does not reliably translate to safe or feasible behavior in dynamic closed-loop (CL) simulations.
- Scaling laws for performance observed in OL evaluations, based on factors like data volume or compute, do not consistently transfer to real-world CL deployment.
- Existing driving simulators and benchmarks often lack sufficient visual realism, scenario diversity, or consistent evaluation protocols to rigorously investigate the OL-CL gap.

## Method

- Developed **BridgeSim**, a cross-simulator CL evaluation platform with unified scenario protocols, flexible deployment settings, and structural invariance for rigorous analysis.
- Decomposed the OL-CL gap into two primary failure modes: Observational Domain Shift (perception degradation) and Objective Mismatch (biased Q-value estimation and compounding errors).
- Proposed a training-free ==Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)== (TTA) framework, combining a ==flow-matching observational calibrator==, a truncated Q-value estimator, and an adaptive replan mechanism to address these identified gaps.

## Results

- The TTA framework significantly flattened the decay curve of E2E planners' reliability in long-horizon (4**-16** seconds) CL simulations on BridgeSim-NavHard scenarios, maintaining higher temporal consistency and safety.
- TTA consistently improved CL performance across diverse open-loop baselines (e.g., DiffusionDrive, RAP) in unseen scenes and reactive traffic environments, achieving a **+19.1** Driving Score (DS) improvement with RAP in IDM mode.
- Component analysis confirmed that the truncated Q-value estimator and adaptive replan mechanism individually and combined enhanced performance and stability, demonstrating more favorable scaling dynamics compared to baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The OL-CL gap is fundamentally caused by Observational Domain Shift (perceptual differences between training and test environments) and, more critically, Objective Mismatch (discrepancy between static OL training objectives and dynamic CL reactive requirements).
> - OL training often leads to biased Q-value estimation and susceptibility to compounding execution errors due to optimizing for a static, full-sequence trajectory rather than a reactive, receding-horizon policy.
> - Merely scaling open-loop training or computational resources for trajectory proposals does not effectively improve closed-loop performance without proper adaptation that addresses objective misalignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving has seen significant advancements, with numerous studies focusing on achieving high performance on open-loop (OL) benchmarks such as nuScenes and NAVSIM. These benchmarks typically evaluate a policy's ability to predict a correct trajectory given a static sequence of observations, often reporting performance comparable to or exceeding human levels. However, a recurring issue, referred to as the "OL-CL gap," manifests when these high-performing OL policies are deployed in closed-loop (CL) simulations. In CL deployment, the agent's actions directly influence the environment, and new observations are generated dynamically, leading to potential discrepancies between predicted and executed trajectories, often resulting in unsafe or infeasible behaviors.

Previous research has noted the lack of correlation between OL and CL performance, with this correlation diminishing over longer simulation horizons. Furthermore, scaling laws observed in OL performance (e.g., with data volume or test-time compute) do not reliably transfer to CL deployment. This suggests that existing OL evaluation protocols may be biased and may not adequately reflect the objectives required for realistic autonomous driving. While dedicated CL training can mitigate this gap, it often entails greater complexity and computational costs. Existing driving simulators and benchmarks, though crucial, suffer from issues such as domain gaps due to handcrafted assets, limited visual realism, lack of multi-view temporal consistency, or insufficient scenario diversity, making rigorous investigation of the OL-CL gap challenging. This work aims to systematically analyze the fundamental causes of this gap and propose a solution that improves CL robustness without requiring full end-to-end retraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify the fundamental failure modes contributing to the Open-Loop to Closed-Loop (OL-CL) gap in End-to-End (E2E) autonomous driving and to propose a practical remedy. The authors aim to clarify why policies that excel in static OL evaluations often fail in dynamic CL deployments and what adaptations are necessary to mitigate these failures.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
1. **Misleading Performance Proxy:** High scores on OL benchmarks can create a deceptive impression of a driving policy's capabilities in real-world or realistic simulated conditions. Studies have demonstrated a lack of direct correspondence between OL and CL performance, with this correlation decaying as the simulation horizon increases.
2. **Inconsistent Scaling Dynamics:** OL performance scaling laws, which predict how performance improves with increased data volume or computational resources, do not reliably transfer to CL deployment. This suggests that improving OL metrics alone may not translate to enhanced real-world driving capabilities.
3. **Limitations of OL Protocols:** Current OL evaluation protocols, while practical and scalable alternatives to costly CL simulations, may inherently overlook critical objectives that real-world driving policies must optimize, such as reactive behavior and handling compounding errors.

To address these issues, the paper proposes a detailed decomposition of the OL-CL gap, attributing it to two main sources: Observational Domain Shift and Objective Mismatch. The overarching goal is not to discredit OL evaluation but to understand its limitations and develop methods that enable OL-pretrained policies to perform robustly in CL environments. This motivation culminates in the development of a Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) framework and a new cross-simulator CL simulation platform, BridgeSim, designed for rigorous, dynamic evaluations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves three main components: introducing a unified simulation platform, decomposing the OL-CL gap into distinct failure modes, and proposing a Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) framework to address these modes.

#### 3.1. BridgeSim Platform

To rigorously investigate the OL-CL gap, the authors developed **BridgeSim**, a cross-simulator closed-loop simulation platform. BridgeSim is designed for comprehensive and dynamic evaluation of E2E driving policies in high-fidelity CL environments. Key features include:
- **Unified Scenario Protocol:** Incorporates diverse map scenarios (e.g., nuPlan, Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD), nuScenes, CARLA procedural generation) and heterogeneous traffic modes (log-replay, Intelligent Driver Model (IDM), adversarial policies) to stress-test policies.
- **Flexible Deployment Settings:** Supports varying execution frequencies and simulation horizons for OL policies.
- **Structural Invariance:** Ensures consistent sensor configurations (observational alignment) and vehicle dynamics/control execution layers (kinematic and control alignment) across different simulators to isolate policy decision-making from confounding variables.
- **Unified Evaluation Modes and Metrics:** Supports both non-reactive open-loop and reactive closed-loop evaluations, employing an Extended Predictive Driver Model Score (EPDMS) for OL and a composite BridgeSim Driving Score (combining Route Completion and EPDMS) for CL.

#### 3.2. Decomposition of the OL-CL Gap

The research formalizes the E2E driving task as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and identifies two primary failure modes:

##### 3.2.1. Observational Domain Shift

This mode occurs when the sensor observations at test time in the target environment differ from those encountered during training, leading to the perception module failing to extract coherent semantic features. This "sim-to-sim" gap degrades perception before planning. The Observational Domain Gap (Δ_obs) is quantified as the difference in expected closed-loop return between source and target domains using an identical control head. Empirical studies, using an open-loop ablation where only the input observation domain is varied, show that this shift dramatically degrades performance, but targeted calibration methods can largely recover the lost perceptual fidelity.

##### 3.2.2. Objective Mismatch

This more fundamental mode arises because OL policies are typically trained to optimize a static, full-sequence trajectory-matching objective, which does not align with the reactive, receding-horizon reality of CL deployment. In CL settings, an agent predicts a trajectory of length H but only executes a short prefix of k steps before replanning.
- **Biased Q-value Estimation:** OL training often optimizes for a long-horizon objective (k=H) using open-loop kinematic assumptions, neglecting environmental stochasticity, covariate shift, and reactive behaviors of other traffic participants. This creates a disconnect from the true closed-loop state-visitation distribution, leading to biased and overfitting Q-value estimates.
- **Compounding Execution Error:** The static nature of OL training does not instill the temporal robustness required for sequential decision-making, making policies susceptible to errors that accumulate over high-frequency executions. The Objective Mismatch Gap (Δ_obj) formalizes this cumulative performance degradation. Empirical studies reveal that this gap widens significantly as the simulation horizon (T) exceeds the planning horizon (H), and the Pearson correlation between OL and CL metrics drastically decays.

#### 3.3. Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) Framework

Motivated by the decomposition analysis, the authors propose a training-free TTA framework to enhance CL robustness of OL-pretrained policies.

##### 3.3.1. Observational Calibrator

A flow-matching-based observational calibrator is proposed to perform cross-domain observational alignment. This component learns a transport map `g: Z → Z` that aligns latent representation distributions (`D_source` and `D_target`) induced by the observation channels. It uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture and is optimized with a multi-objective loss, combining a flow-matching objective, an encoder loss for semantic consistency, and a KL divergence loss for manifold regularization.

##### 3.3.2. Test-time Policy Adaptation

This mechanism addresses the structural misalignment caused by objective mismatch without additional retraining.

##### 3.3.3. Truncated Q-value Estimation

To correct the bias in OL-trained Q-value estimators, a dynamic, test-time truncated action-value estimator is introduced. Instead of summing rewards infinitely, this estimator explicitly bounds the estimation to the reliable planning horizon H by subtracting the discounted expected value at the terminal horizon limit (`γ^H E[V_π(s_{t+H})]`). This ensures that value estimation beyond H is ignored, restricting the estimator to high-confidence windows. During CL execution, the agent selects the candidate trajectory that maximizes this truncated Q-value. The reward function (R_k) is formulated as a gated sum of continuous Quality Features (F), conditioned on Critical Constraints (C) from the EPDMS metric.

##### 3.3.4. Adaptive Replan

To mitigate compounding execution errors and temporal inconsistency, an adaptive replan mechanism is introduced. This mechanism preserves unexecuted waypoints of a previously verified plan. At each decision step, the agent evaluates whether to retain the remainder of the optimal plan from the previous cycle (`a_rem`) against newly generated candidate trajectories (`a ∈ A_t`) using the truncated Q-value estimator. The persistent plan is retained if its estimated Q-value is equal to or exceeds that of any new candidate, preventing unnecessary replanning and promoting temporal stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments using the BridgeSim platform, evaluating OL-pretrained policies under various scenarios and traffic modes.

#### 4.1. In-domain Evaluation

On BridgeSim-NavHard scenarios, the integration of the Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) framework significantly flattened the decay curve of E2E planners' reliability as the simulation horizon increased (4/8/12/16 seconds). TTA-enhanced agents maintained higher temporal consistency and safety scores in long-horizon Closed-Loop (CL) simulations, indicating successful mitigation of the Objective Mismatch issue.

#### 4.2. Unseen Scenes Evaluation

The TTA framework consistently yielded performance gains across diverse open-loop baselines (e.g., DiffusionDrive, DiffusionDriveV2, RAP). These improvements were observed in three distinct evaluation environments: BridgeSim-NavHard, BridgeSim-nuScenes, and BridgeSim-WOMD. This demonstrates the framework's cross-domain robustness, suggesting its ability to generalize across different real-world log-replay domains.

#### 4.3. Reactive Evaluation

In scenarios with reactive traffic agents (IDM and adversarial modes), the TTA framework consistently improved key CL metrics. This included progress-related metrics such as Route Completion (RC) and safety-related metrics like No At-Fault Collisions (NC) and Time-to-Collision (TTC), resulting in higher overall BridgeSim Driving Scores (DS). The improvements indicate the TTA framework's effectiveness in enhancing both efficiency and safety in dynamic, interactive simulations. For instance, TTA integrated with RAP showed a +19.1 DS improvement in IDM mode and a +14.1 DS improvement in safety-critical mode.

#### 4.4. Component Analysis and Scaling Dynamics

A component analysis of the TTA framework revealed that the proposed truncated Q-value estimator significantly outperformed the original biased baseline across all tested execution horizons (k). The addition of the adaptive replan mechanism further contributed to performance, consistently identifying optimal rollout trajectories and mitigating compounding execution errors, leading to more robust and stable driving scores across different backbone architectures.

Furthermore, experiments on test-time scaling dynamics indicated that the quality of the scoring mechanism is critical. The proposed TTA scorers consistently benefited from scaling with broader state-visitations, demonstrating more favorable scaling behavior compared to biased Q-value estimators, which did not achieve similar improvements. This suggests that without proper adaptation, scaling computational resources for trajectory proposals does not effectively improve CL performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic analysis of the Open-Loop to Closed-Loop (OL-CL) gap in End-to-End (E2E) autonomous driving, offering a clear decomposition into Observational Domain Shift and Objective Mismatch. The findings demonstrate that while observational shifts are largely recoverable through adaptation, objective mismatch, rooted in biased Q-value estimation and compounding execution errors, forms the primary structural barrier to effective CL deployment. This analysis highlights that merely scaling OL training is insufficient for achieving safe and stable behavior in long-horizon CL simulations, challenging the assumption that high OL scores directly translate to real-world readiness.

The introduction of **BridgeSim** represents a contribution to the research community, providing a unified cross-simulator CL simulation platform for rigorous, dynamic evaluations. This platform is designed to facilitate deeper empirical investigations into the OL-CL gap and serves as a benchmark for developing more robust E2E driving policies.

The proposed Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) framework offers a practical and effective remedy for mitigating CL deployment failures without requiring costly end-to-end retraining. By addressing observational shift via flow-matching calibration and objective mismatch via a truncated Q-value estimator and adaptive replanning, TTA significantly improves CL performance across diverse scenarios, unseen scenes, and reactive traffic environments. The framework's ability to yield superior scaling dynamics compared to baseline counterparts suggests a path towards more efficient and reliable deployment of E2E driving models.

The implications of this work suggest a necessary shift in E2E autonomous driving research. It indicates that OL benchmarks may have reached a saturation point, and their predictive power for real-world CL capabilities is limited. Future efforts may benefit from focusing on more realistic CL evaluation protocols and developing objective-aligned learning methods that directly optimize for CL performance, safety, and robustness. This research contributes to a more nuanced understanding of E2E driving policy evaluation and development, moving towards more reliable and adaptable autonomous systems.
