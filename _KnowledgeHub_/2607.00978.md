# Privacy-Preserving Depth-Only Open-Vocabulary 3D Semantic Segmentation Via Uncertainty-Guided Test-Time Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Uncertainty-Guided Test-Time Optimization (UTTO), refines depth-only open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation by leveraging structured prediction uncertainty and foundation model priors without real RGB input or additional training. UTTO improved mIoU by up to 5.2 points on ScanNet20 for a modified RGB-D backbone and up to 3.1 points for a geometry-only backbone, demonstrating enhanced privacy-preserving scene understanding.

## Problem

- Traditional open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation often relies on RGB images, which capture privacy-sensitive visual information in real-world environments.
- Depth-only sensing inherently lacks rich appearance cues (color, texture), leading to increased ambiguity and uncertainty in open-vocabulary predictions, especially for geometrically similar categories.
- Existing depth-only methods often use frozen models directly without robust strategies to address the semantic information gap or refine uncertain predictions at inference time.

## Method

- Introduces **UTTO**, a training-free, model-agnostic ==test-time optimization== framework that refines per-vertex class distributions using only depth information.
- Quantifies prediction ==uncertainty== via ==test-time augmentations==, using it to create reliability weights that guide the optimization of stable predictions.
- Incorporates label-free ==text prototype debiasing== and ==feature-space semantic consistency== (using ==DINO== features from depth-colorized renderings) to regularize predictions.

## Results

- UTTO consistently improved mIoU for depth-only backbones on ScanNet datasets, increasing mIoU by up to **5.2 points** on ScanNet20 for Mosaic3D-DepthOnly and **3.1 points** for OpenScene3D.
- The framework demonstrated model-agnostic effectiveness, enhancing **two** different frozen depth-only backbones.
- Refined depth-only predictions significantly improved downstream tasks, such as semantic goal grounding for robot navigation, showing an increase in candidate goal-pose hit rate from **45.4%** to **75.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured uncertainty in depth-only predictions is a valuable signal, not just a limitation, and can be leveraged to guide test-time refinement effectively.
> - Pre-trained foundation models (CLIP for text, DINO for vision) can provide powerful semantic priors to enhance depth-only understanding without needing real RGB or further training.
> - Hallucinating RGB appearance is less effective than directly refining geometry-based predictions when aiming for privacy-preserving perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of 3D scene understanding, open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, and privacy-preserving perception.
3D semantic segmentation is a foundational task for intelligent systems operating in real-world environments, enabling dense semantic parsing of physical spaces. Open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation extends this capability by allowing models to recognize and segment objects belonging to categories not seen during training, typically defined via text queries. This flexibility is crucial for applications such as robotic navigation and embodied agents, where the specific categories of interest may vary and cannot be exhaustively predefined.

A significant challenge in deploying such systems, particularly in indoor environments like homes, offices, or hospitals, is the requirement for privacy-preserving perception. Traditional 3D semantic segmentation, especially open-vocabulary methods, frequently relies on rich semantic cues derived from RGB images. These RGB observations can capture sensitive visual information, including human faces, computer screens, documents, or personal belongings, thereby raising substantial privacy concerns.

Depth-only 3D geometry offers a natural alternative for privacy-preserving perception. By restricting sensing to depth observations and derived geometric structures, such systems can maintain spatial understanding while minimizing the capture of privacy-sensitive appearance information. However, this approach introduces a significant technical hurdle: the absence of appearance-based cues (color, texture) makes open-vocabulary predictions inherently more ambiguous and uncertain, as many categories can be geometrically similar but semantically distinct. Existing depth-only open-vocabulary methods, while more privacy-compatible, often operate by directly using predictions from frozen models, without addressing the specific challenges posed by the lack of RGB information during inference.

This work addresses the underexplored area of privacy-preserving open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation under strict depth-only sensing constraints. It aims to bridge the gap between the need for privacy-preserving inputs and the performance degradation caused by the removal of rich RGB semantic cues. The paper distinguishes itself by focusing on test-time optimization to refine depth-only predictions, specifically leveraging the structured uncertainty that arises in this constrained setting as a guidance signal, rather than merely treating it as a limitation. This contrasts with methods that attempt to hallucinate RGB information, which the authors argue are less effective, or methods that rely on RGB-D inputs, which do not meet privacy requirements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust method for privacy-preserving open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation using only depth information, without reliance on real RGB data or additional model training.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical requirements and challenges:

1. **Privacy Protection in Real-World Deployments:** As intelligent systems, such as service robots or embodied agents, are increasingly deployed in sensitive indoor environments (e.g., homes, hospitals, offices), the ability to understand 3D scenes without compromising human privacy becomes paramount. RGB cameras, a common sensor modality for perception systems, inherently capture visual details that can be privacy-sensitive (e.g., faces, personal items, confidential information on screens). Restricting input to depth-only data fundamentally addresses this concern by preventing the acquisition of such appearance-based information.

2. **Enabling Flexible 3D Scene Understanding:** Open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation is a key capability for general-purpose 3D scene understanding. It allows systems to parse environments for novel categories defined at inference time, which is essential for tasks like "navigate to the sofa" where the specific target object might not be known beforehand or included in a fixed training vocabulary. The motivation is to retain this flexibility while adhering to privacy constraints.

3. **Addressing the Semantic Information Gap in Depth-Only Sensing:** While depth-only inputs provide privacy benefits, they inherently lack the rich texture and color cues that typically aid semantic discrimination in open-vocabulary tasks. This absence leads to increased uncertainty and unreliability in predictions, particularly for categories that are geometrically similar. The motivation is to overcome this semantic information gap without reintroducing privacy-sensitive data.

4. **Leveraging Uncertainty as a Guidance Signal:** The authors observe that uncertainty in RGB-free inference is not uniformly distributed across a scene. Some regions may have sufficient geometric cues for reliable predictions, while others become ambiguous without appearance data. This structured uncertainty is identified as a valuable signal. The motivation is to transform this challenge into an opportunity by using uncertainty to guide inference-time refinement, preserving reliable predictions while actively refining uncertain ones.

5. **Developing a Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Solution:** Given the real-world deployment context, retraining complex 3D models for every new environment or privacy constraint is impractical. The motivation is therefore to develop a framework that operates purely at test time, without additional model training or access to ground-truth labels during inference. Furthermore, the solution should be model-agnostic, capable of improving predictions from various existing frozen open-vocabulary 3D backbones.

In summary, the core objective is to propose UTTO (Uncertainty-Guided Test-Time Optimization), a framework that enables effective and reliable open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation from depth-only inputs by leveraging structured uncertainty and foundation model priors, all without compromising privacy or requiring model retraining. This addresses the critical need for robust, privacy-preserving perception in intelligent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, UTTO (Uncertainty-Guided Test-Time Optimization), is a model-agnostic framework designed to refine per-vertex class distributions from frozen open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation backbones at inference time. It operates exclusively on depth-only geometry, without using real RGB observations, additional training, or ground-truth labels. The framework comprises three main components: uncertainty-guided inference optimization, label-free text prototype debiasing, and feature-space semantic consistency.

#### 3.1. Uncertainty-Guided Inference Optimization

This component addresses the challenge of unreliable predictions in depth-only scenarios by quantifying prediction variation and converting it into reliability weights.
1. **Generating Multiple Predictions:** The method starts by applying a set of label-preserving test-time augmentations (e.g., rotations, voxel-grid phase shifts) to the geometry-only input scene. Each augmented input is then fed into a frozen depth-only open-vocabulary 3D segmentation backbone, producing multiple per-vertex class distributions (P^(m)) for the scene.
2. **Estimating Vertex-Level Uncertainty:** For each vertex, the hard semantic prediction (most probable class) is determined from each augmented output. Vertex-level reliability (ρ_v) is then calculated as the fraction of augmented predictions that agree on the majority class. A higher agreement indicates higher reliability, while lower agreement signifies greater uncertainty (u_v = 1 - ρ_v).
3. **Deriving Reliability Weights:** The agreement score ρ_v is converted into a reliability weight (w_v = max(ρ_v, w_min)), where w_min is a minimum threshold to prevent highly uncertain vertices from being completely disregarded.
4. **Reliability-Weighted Data Term:** The core of the optimization is a data term (E_data) that measures the squared L2 distance between the optimized per-vertex class distributions (X) and the unary observations (Y, aggregated from the test-time predictions), weighted by w_v. This term ensures that stable predictions (high w_v) are strongly constrained by their initial observation, while uncertain predictions (low w_v) are allowed more flexibility to be refined by subsequent regularization terms.

#### 3.2. Label-Free Text Prototype Debiasing

This component improves the quality of the initial unary observations by mitigating biases introduced by the text classifier used by the frozen backbone.
1. **Problem Identification:** The standard approach of using a single-template prompt (e.g., "a {category} in a scene") to generate text prototypes for open-vocabulary classification can introduce wording-specific biases. In depth-only settings, where visual cues are weaker, such biases can have a more pronounced negative effect on prediction accuracy.
2. **Prompt-Ensemble Prototypes:** To address this, the method replaces single-template prototypes with prompt-ensemble prototypes. For each semantic category, a unified set of generic templates (Q) is used to generate multiple text prompts (s_c,k). These prompts are encoded using a frozen CLIP text encoder, leveraging its language-vision alignment capabilities.
3. **Averaged Embeddings:** The normalized embeddings from these multiple prompts for a given category are then averaged and re-normalized to create a debiased class prototype (t_c). This ensemble approach reduces the impact of any single template's bias, leading to more robust text-feature alignment.
4. **Improved Unary Observations:** This debiased text-prototype classifier (T) is used to compute the class logits for the 3D backbone's features, thereby improving the initial probability predictions (P^(m)) and, consequently, the aggregated unary observations (Y) for the optimization.

#### 3.3. Feature-Space Semantic Consistency

This component introduces spatial and semantic regularization to further refine the optimized distributions.
1. **Geometric Consistency (E_geo):** A geometric graph (E_g) is constructed using k-nearest neighbors in the 3D coordinate space. This graph captures local spatial relationships, and a regularization term (E_geo) penalizes dissimilarity between the class distributions of geometrically adjacent points, promoting local smoothness.
2. **Feature-Space Semantic Consistency (E_sem):** To overcome the limitations of purely geometric cues, a semantic graph (E_s) is built in a learned feature space. This is achieved without real RGB input:
 - **Depth-Colorized Geometric Renderings:** Depth values from the geometry-only scene are colorized using a fixed colormap to generate pseudo-RGB images. These "depth-colorized" renderings encode only geometric depth variation, not real appearance.
 - **DINO Feature Extraction:** Features (ϕ_v) are extracted from these renderings using a frozen DINO encoder, which provides strong self-supervised visual features. These features are then back-projected to the 3D vertices.
 - **Semantic Graph Construction:** A k-nearest neighbors graph (E_s) is constructed in this DINO feature space, with edge weights reflecting feature similarity. A regularization term (E_sem) penalizes dissimilarity between the class distributions of semantically similar points in this feature space, enforcing consistency beyond local geometry.

#### 3.4. Final Optimization Objective

The final objective function (X*) is formulated as a weighted sum of the reliability-weighted data term (E_data), the geometric consistency term (E_geo), and the feature-space semantic consistency term (E_sem):
X* = arg min_X [E_data(X) + λ_g E_geo(X) + λ_s E_sem(X)]
This optimization effectively anchors reliable predictions while refining uncertain ones through local geometry and broader semantic agreement derived from foundation models, all without requiring real RGB input or additional training. The final semantic label for each vertex is obtained by selecting the class with the highest probability from the optimized distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the ScanNet20, ScanNet40, and ScanNet200 validation sets, evaluating open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation performance using mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and mean Accuracy (mAcc). The findings are structured to address the effectiveness of UTTO, the impact of depth-only sensing, and the contribution of individual components.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of UTTO in Privacy-Preserving Depth-Only Setting

- **Consistent Performance Improvement:** UTTO consistently improved the performance of depth-only open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation across all evaluated ScanNet datasets (20, 40, 200 classes) and with two different frozen backbones: Mosaic3D-DepthOnly (a modified RGB-D model with RGB channels zeroed out) and OpenScene3D (a geometry-only backbone).
 - For Mosaic3D-DepthOnly, UTTO increased mIoU on ScanNet20 from 31.6 to 36.8, on ScanNet40 from 19.1 to 22.4, and on ScanNet200 from 4.5 to 6.1.
 - For OpenScene3D, UTTO improved mIoU on ScanNet20 from 44.2 to 47.3, on ScanNet40 from 28.2 to 29.7, and on ScanNet200 from 4.7 to 5.9.
 - These gains indicate UTTO's model-agnostic nature and its ability to enhance predictions under the strict depth-only constraint.

#### 4.2. Impact of Removing RGB Cues and Pseudo-RGB Alternatives

- **Significant Performance Gap:** A substantial performance drop was observed when comparing Mosaic3D with full RGB-D input (mIoU 50.3 on ScanNet20) to its depth-only variant, Mosaic3D-DepthOnly (mIoU 31.6 on ScanNet20). This highlights that real RGB appearance provides critical semantic evidence for open-vocabulary 3D segmentation, and its removal creates a considerable challenge. UTTO partially mitigates this gap without reintroducing real RGB.
- **Ineffectiveness of Hallucinated Appearance:** The D2RGB+OpenSeg Fusion baseline, which attempts to recover missing appearance cues by generating pseudo-RGB views from depth, performed substantially worse than direct depth-only geometry methods. For example, its mIoU on ScanNet20 was 22.2, significantly lower than both Mosaic3D-DepthOnly and OpenScene3D. This finding suggests that hallucinated appearance is not a reliable substitute for real RGB cues in this context, and directly refining geometry-based predictions is a more effective strategy.
- **Marginal Impact on RGB-D Input:** When applied to the non-private Mosaic3D with RGB-D input, UTTO showed only marginal improvements (mIoU from 50.3 to 50.7 on ScanNet20). This indicates that UTTO's optimization is most beneficial in scenarios where RGB appearance cues are weak or absent, leading to higher prediction uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study of UTTO Components

- **Uncertainty-Guided Optimization as Core:** The "w/o Both" variant (removing both text prototype debiasing and feature-space consistency) still provided improvements over the baseline, demonstrating that the uncertainty-guided optimization core itself is effective in refining depth-only predictions.
- **Text Prototype Debiasing Contribution:** Adding text prototype debiasing consistently improved performance across backbones. For instance, with OpenScene3D, mIoU increased from 45.4 ("w/o Both") to 46.3 ("w/o Feature-space Cons." with debiasing implied). This indicates that using prompt-ensemble text prototypes helps reduce bias and improves the quality of unary observations.
- **Feature-Space Consistency:** Feature-space consistency also contributed positively, especially when combined with text debiasing. The full UTTO framework, combining all components, achieved the best performance. This suggests that foundation-model priors are most effective when jointly regulating the optimization, rather than being applied independently.

#### 4.4. Contextual Positioning of Depth-Only Open-Vocabulary Setting

- **Performance Gap with Non-Private RGB-D:** Even with UTTO, the best depth-only open-vocabulary method (OpenScene3D+UTTO, mIoU 47.3 on ScanNet20) remained below the non-private RGB-D reference (Mosaic3D+UTTO, mIoU 50.7 on ScanNet20). This underscores the persistent challenge posed by the complete absence of real appearance information.
- **Gap with Supervised Closed-Set Geometry:** A comparison with Point Transformer V3 (PTv3), a fully supervised closed-set 3D semantic segmentation model using geometry-only input, showed a substantial performance difference (PTv3 achieved mIoU 65.4 on ScanNet20 vs. OpenScene3D+UTTO's 47.3). This highlights that open-vocabulary inference without task-specific training, even with depth-only input, operates under significantly stricter constraints and faces a considerable performance gap compared to traditional supervised methods.

#### 4.5. Privacy-Preserving Semantic Goal Grounding Case Study

- **Downstream Applicability:** A case study demonstrated that UTTO improves the generation of feasible semantic navigation goals from geometry-only 3D maps. For class-level queries like "chair" or "desk," OpenScene3D+UTTO significantly improved the candidate goal-pose hit rate (from 45.4% to 75.0% total) compared to the unoptimized OpenScene3D. This indicates that UTTO's refined predictions are more useful for real-world embodied applications in privacy-sensitive settings.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that UTTO effectively improves depth-only open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation under privacy constraints by leveraging structured uncertainty and foundation model priors. The results also clarify the inherent difficulties and trade-offs associated with removing RGB appearance cues and operating in a training-free, open-vocabulary manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of 3D scene understanding and intelligent systems by addressing a critical, yet underexplored, aspect: privacy-preserving perception in open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Privacy-Preserving Deployment:** The most direct impact is providing a method that facilitates the deployment of intelligent systems (e.g., robots, AR/VR devices) in privacy-sensitive indoor environments. By demonstrating that effective open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation can be achieved using only depth information, the work offers a concrete technical solution to mitigate privacy concerns associated with capturing and processing RGB data. This is crucial for gaining public acceptance and regulatory compliance for such technologies.

2. **Advancing Depth-Only Perception:** The work significantly contributes to the domain of depth-only 3D perception. It moves beyond simply operating without RGB to actively recovering semantic information and improving prediction reliability in its absence. This is particularly impactful for environments where RGB cameras are impractical, prohibited, or compromised (e.g., low light, opaque materials).

3. **Novel Use of Uncertainty as a Guidance Signal:** The research introduces a novel approach to leverage test-time uncertainty as a constructive guidance signal for refinement, rather than merely an indicator of failure. By converting uncertainty into reliability weights, UTTO selectively focuses optimization efforts on ambiguous regions, optimizing resource allocation during inference and leading to more robust predictions. This paradigm could inspire similar uncertainty-guided refinement strategies in other constrained perception settings.

4. **Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Flexibility:** UTTO's design as a training-free, test-time optimization framework makes it highly flexible and adaptable. It can be applied to various existing frozen open-vocabulary 3D backbones without requiring expensive retraining or domain-specific labeled data. This reduces the burden of model development and allows for easier integration into diverse applications.

5. **Effective Integration of Foundation Model Priors:** The method demonstrates an effective strategy for incorporating semantic priors from large-scale foundation models (CLIP for text debiasing, DINO for feature-space consistency) into a test-time refinement process under privacy constraints. This allows the system to benefit from broad semantic knowledge without compromising the input modality.

6. **Potential for Downstream Applications:** The semantic goal grounding case study illustrates the practical applicability of UTTO for tasks like robot navigation. By providing more reliable semantic segmentation of objects from depth-only maps, robots can interpret high-level language commands (e.g., "go to the chair") and derive feasible navigation targets without requiring privacy-sensitive visual data. This opens avenues for more intuitive and privacy-aware human-robot interaction.

7. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper's findings, particularly the comparison with RGB-D and supervised methods, clearly delineate the challenges and remaining gaps in depth-only open-vocabulary perception. This can guide future research towards exploring nuanced privacy-preserving sensing protocols, such as incorporating highly filtered or extremely low-resolution appearance cues, to potentially bridge the performance gap with full RGB-D systems while maintaining privacy guarantees. It also highlights the ongoing need for more effective ways to compensate for the fundamental lack of appearance information in purely geometric data for fine-grained open-vocabulary tasks.
