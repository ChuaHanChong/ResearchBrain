# NeuralActuator: Neural Actuation Modeling for Robot Dynamics and External Force Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> A Transformer-based model called NeuralActuator was developed to enhance robot dynamics modeling and sensorless external force perception for low-cost, servo-driven robots. It predicts simulator-equivalent generalized-effort surrogates and external forces, outperforming classical baselines in force estimation by 5.5 times and improving real-robot control success rates.

## Problem

- Differentiable simulators lack accurate modeling of non-ideal actuator dynamics in low-cost, servo-driven robots, causing significant sim-to-real gaps.
- Reliable sensorless external force perception is hindered on affordable robotic platforms due to inaccurate current-to-torque mappings and unavailable high-quality effort feedback.
- Complex, time-varying, and nonlinear behaviors like friction, hysteresis, and backlash in low-cost actuators are difficult to model analytically.

## Method

- A ==Transformer==-based model, **NeuralActuator**, was designed to learn history-dependent mappings from robot telemetry to simulator-equivalent ==generalized-effort surrogates==, external forces, and motor condition scores.
- A comprehensive **Neural Actuation Dataset (NAD)** was collected using a twin-arm ==teleoperation== system, providing synchronized robot states, actuator telemetry, and ground-truth force labels.
- The model is trained end-to-end by embedding it within ==differentiable physics simulators==, using ==pose-based supervision== for dynamics and direct supervision for force and motor condition outputs.

## Results

- NeuralActuator achieved low rollout errors on the OpenManipulator-X, with an average joint-angle MAE of **2.5 degrees** and gripper slide MAE of **0.2 mm** over **100 steps**.
- It provided accurate external force estimation with a mean MAE of **0.12 N** on payload tasks, outperforming classical baselines by **5.5 times** and exhibiting minimal false-positive contact detection.
- The model reached **91.0%** accuracy in identifying mechanically restricted motor operation and improved real-robot control success rates by **10 percentage points** for object lift-and-hold tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex, history-dependent actuator non-idealities in low-cost robots can be effectively modeled via a Transformer-based neural network, improving upon traditional analytical approximations.
> - Sensorless external force perception and accurate robot dynamics modeling can be jointly learned from accessible telemetry using pose-based supervision within a differentiable simulation.
> - A unified neural architecture can simultaneously predict simulator-equivalent generalized efforts, external forces, and motor condition, enhancing robot control, diagnostics, and sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Differentiable simulators have emerged as a significant tool in robotics, facilitating gradient-based optimization for policy learning and model-based control across various tasks by accurately modeling rigid-body dynamics and, more recently, soft bodies and fluids. However, the paper identifies actuator dynamics as a comparatively underexplored area within this landscape. Current approximations, such as the linear relationship between current and joint torque ($\tau = K_t I$), are noted as unreliable for cost-effective, servo-driven robotic platforms. These low-cost platforms, often utilizing mechanically commutated actuators, exhibit significant non-idealities like gearbox friction, hysteresis, backlash, current saturation, and thermal drift. These effects are challenging to model analytically due to inaccessible internal states and their time-varying nature, leading to degraded sim-to-real transfer and unstable control behavior.

Beyond accurate forward dynamics, a reliable actuator model is also critical for force perception without the need for dedicated force/torque sensors. Existing torque-based sensing methods, used for contact perception, proprioceptive estimation, and force control, typically rely on high-quality effort feedback or well-calibrated current-to-torque mappings, which are not reliably available on low-cost platforms. This gap hinders the application of force-aware control and manipulation techniques in more affordable robotic systems.

The work builds upon and differentiates itself from prior efforts in several ways:
- **Neural-Augmented Simulation:** Previous research has augmented analytical simulators with learned residual models to bridge the sim-to-real gap. Examples include stochastic RNNs for residual dynamics, data-driven contact models, and neural modules embedded in differentiable simulators to capture unmodeled dynamics. Some approaches train supervised actuator networks using joint-torque sensing or calibrated motor-current estimates. NeuralActuator distinguishes itself by supervising its torque-surrogate head directly from pose trajectories via differentiable simulation, without requiring direct generalized-effort labels or reliable current-to-torque calibration, which are often unavailable on low-cost servo platforms.
- **Learning Actuator Models:** Prior actuator modeling research has primarily focused on permanent-magnet electrical machines, hydraulic, or twisted-string actuators. NeuralActuator specifically targets low-cost coreless DC servo actuators, which present unique challenges due to pronounced non-linearities like friction, backlash, saturation, and thermal drift affecting the effective current-torque relationship in a history-dependent manner.
- **Force Estimation and Proprioceptive Sensing:** A line of work exists in reconstructing external joint torques from model discrepancies for collision detection and safe reaction, and more recently, for richer "intrinsic" contact perception and learning-based controllers that couple force and motion. Methods for external force estimation on robot arms typically explicitly model robot dynamics and attribute residual terms to interaction wrenches, often assuming accurate torque feedback. NeuralActuator addresses the specific challenge of force estimation on low-cost, servo-driven arms where accurate torque feedback is not assumed, predicting simulator-equivalent generalized-effort surrogates and external forces in parallel from accessible telemetry.

In essence, NeuralActuator aims to fill a critical gap by providing a data-driven solution for accurate actuator modeling and sensorless force perception, specifically tailored for the non-ideal characteristics of low-cost robot platforms, thereby enhancing their capabilities in differentiable simulation and real-world interaction.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from identified limitations in current differentiable simulators and robotic systems, particularly concerning low-cost platforms. The key objectives and their underlying motivations are detailed below:

- **Objective 1: Improve Actuator Dynamics Modeling in Differentiable Simulators.**
 - **Motivation:** While differentiable simulators have significantly advanced rigid-body dynamics, actuator dynamics remain underexplored. For low-cost, servo-driven robots, the common linear current-to-joint-torque approximation ($\tau = K_t I$) is inaccurate. These platforms often use coreless brushed DC servo modules with inherent non-idealities such as gearbox friction, hysteresis, backlash, current saturation, and thermal drift. These effects lead to unreliable effort tracking, unstable impedance behavior, and substantial sim-to-real gaps, hindering the effectiveness of simulation-based policy learning and model-based control. The objective is to develop a model that accurately captures these complex, time-varying, and nonlinear actuator behaviors.

- **Objective 2: Enable Sensorless External Force Perception.**
 - **Motivation:** Reliable actuator models are foundational for force perception without dedicated force/torque sensors. Many manipulation tasks necessitate understanding physical interactions, which are mediated by force. Traditional torque-based sensing methods, however, rely on high-quality effort feedback or precisely calibrated current-torque mappings, which are difficult to obtain on low-cost platforms. An unreliable current-torque relationship undermines analogous force inference. The motivation is to provide an online, robust method for estimating external forces at the end-effector and detecting contact, allowing robots to interact with arbitrary or deformable objects without requiring precise geometric models or expensive hardware.

- **Objective 3: Develop a Multi-Task Neural Actuator Model.**
 - **Motivation:** Instead of separate models for dynamics and force estimation, the authors aim for a unified approach. The goal is to jointly predict:
 - A **simulator-equivalent generalized-effort surrogate** for trajectory propagation, learning the effective input required by the simulator from observed motion, rather than assuming an analytically derived torque.
 - **External forces** along with a **contact-probability gate** for sensorless force perception.
 - A **motor-condition score** to distinguish between normal and mechanically restricted operation, providing diagnostic capabilities.
 - This multi-task approach is motivated by the potential for shared feature learning and improved overall performance, as these aspects of robot behavior are interconnected.

- **Objective 4: Create a Dedicated Dataset for Actuation Modeling and Force Perception.**
 - **Motivation:** Training complex neural models requires high-quality, synchronized data. Current datasets often lack the specific telemetry (motor currents, temperature, voltage) or external-force labels needed to model low-cost actuator non-idealities and train force perception systems effectively. The objective is to introduce a comprehensive real-robot dataset, the Neural Actuation Dataset (NAD), which captures robot states, actuator telemetry, and external-force labels under diverse conditions (free motion, various payloads, controlled contacts, mechanically restricted operation). This dataset is designed to support the training and benchmarking of models like NeuralActuator, especially for low-cost platforms.

- **Objective 5: Validate the Model Across Diverse Robotic Platforms.**
 - **Motivation:** To demonstrate the generalizability and practical applicability of NeuralActuator, it is important to validate its performance beyond a single hardware setup. The objective is to test the model across platforms with different actuator families, degrees of freedom, and cost ranges (e.g., OpenManipulator-X, SO-101, Franka Emika Panda) to establish its robustness and effectiveness in varying real-world scenarios.

These objectives collectively aim to enhance the fidelity of robot simulations, enable advanced interaction capabilities for cost-effective robots, and provide diagnostic tools, thereby expanding the utility of robotics in research and application.

### 3. Methodology and approach

NeuralActuator employs a data-driven, Transformer-based approach integrated with differentiable physics simulators to model complex actuator dynamics and enable sensorless force perception on low-cost robotic platforms.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The problem is formulated as learning a history-dependent mapping from commands, state, and actuator telemetry (current, voltage, temperature) to three main outputs: a simulator-equivalent generalized-effort surrogate ($\tau_{pred}$), an end-effector external force ($\hat{f}_{ext}$), and a per-motor condition score ($c$). The input to the model at time $t$ is a sequence $X_t = (x_{t-8}, \ldots, x_t)$ containing nine historical feature vectors, where each $x_t$ aggregates commanded targets, proprioceptive state (joint position, velocity), actuator telemetry, and tracking-error features.

**B. Neural Actuation Dataset (NAD) Collection**
A custom twin-arm leader–follower teleoperation system was developed to efficiently collect synchronized robot-state, actuator-telemetry, and force data.
- **System Setup:** A human operator kinesthetically drives a leader arm, while a follower arm mirrors its motion and interacts with the environment. Both arms are OpenManipulator-X robots, equipped with DYNAMIXEL XM430-W350 servos that provide synchronized actuator-level measurements (joint positions, velocities, motor currents, PWM, bus voltages, coil temperatures).
- **Force Labeling:** For interaction trajectories, external forces are measured using a fixture-mounted six-axis force/torque sensor, with its three force channels used as ground truth. For payload tasks, known-weight loads (200-500g) provide nominal base-frame gravitational force labels. Force labels are transformed into the robot base frame.
- **Dataset Components:**
 - **Free motion:** Diverse trajectories without external contact (e.g., circular end-effector motions, joint sweeps) to characterize intrinsic actuator dynamics.
 - **Force-labeled interactions:** Known-weight loads (go up and stay still, pick and place) and axis-aligned interactions with a force sensor (push/pull along ±X, ±Y, ±Z), along with matched no-interaction trajectories.
 - **Mechanically restricted motor operation:** Trajectories collected with Joint 3 artificially restricted (e.g., with rubber bands) to introduce additional mechanical resistance for motor condition estimation.
- **Cross-platform Data:** Data was also collected for a 6-DoF SO-101 low-cost arm and a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda industrial arm for cross-platform validation, using platform-specific telemetry and force labeling protocols.
- **Verification:** All data streams are temporally synchronized, logged per trajectory, calibrated, and human-verified. The dataset is split into 8:1:1 for training, validation, and testing.

**C. NeuralActuator Architecture**
- **Transformer Encoder:** A Transformer encoder processes the input sequence $X_t$. It uses pre-normalized residual blocks with a query-dependent elementwise gate on the attention output. The architecture consists of four encoder layers, hidden dimension $d=192$, four attention heads, and feed-forward dimension $d_{ff}=384$.
- **Input Processing:** Input sequences are initialized with an input projection and learned positional embeddings. Features are normalized.
- **Output Heads:** After the final encoder layer, temporal mean pooling yields a pooled representation ($h_{pool}$) which feeds into four distinct output heads:
 1. **Torque-Surrogate Prediction:** An MLP head predicts a simulator-equivalent generalized-effort surrogate $\tau_{pred}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_m}$ for all actuator channels. This output is treated as a lumped generalized input for the simulator, rather than a direct, identifiable actuator torque.
 2. **External Force Prediction:** A two-stage approach. Separate linear readouts from a force-specific hidden feature predict a raw 3D force $\hat{f}_{raw} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a contact-probability gate $g \in [0, 1]$. The final force output is $\hat{f} = g \hat{f}_{raw}$. The gate target $g_{gt}$ is 1 if $\|f_{gt}\|_2 > \epsilon$ and 0 otherwise.
 3. **Motor Condition Estimation:** A separate MLP head predicts a per-motor condition vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^{n_m}$. For the OpenManipulator-X benchmark, only Joint 3's component is supervised to indicate normal (1) or mechanically restricted (0) operation.

**D. Differentiable Simulation Integration and Training**
- **Forward Dynamics:** NeuralActuator is embedded within the simulation loop of differentiable physics simulators (MuJoCo/MJX, NVIDIA Warp). For torque-actuated simulators (OpenManipulator-X, SO-101), the predicted $\tau_{pred}$ is clipped to platform-specific bounds ($\bar{\tau} = \text{clip}(\tau_{pred})$) and used to advance the simulated state ($s_{t+1} = \text{DiffSim}(s_t, \bar{\tau}_t)$). For position-actuated systems (Franka), the model supplies a simulator-control surrogate to its PD controllers.
- **Gradient Computation:** The framework enables end-to-end training via backpropagation through time, calculating gradients of the total loss with respect to NeuralActuator parameters. This includes the dependence of simulated state components on earlier predictions.
- **Model Training:**
 - **Pose-based Supervision:** The torque-surrogate head is supervised indirectly through pose trajectories. The forward integration of predicted generalized inputs into future configurations makes measured poses effective surrogate signals. Losses include Smooth L1 for joint angles ($L_{joint}$) and gripper slide coordinate ($L_{grip}$).
 - **Direct Supervision:** The force, gate, and motor-condition heads use direct supervision from their respective labels. $L_{force}$ is a Huber loss on the predicted 3D force. $L_{gate}$ is binary cross-entropy for contact probability. $L_{cond}$ is binary cross-entropy for motor condition classification.
 - **Objective Function:** A weighted sum of these losses forms the complete objective.
 - **Curriculum Learning:** Training uses task-specific truncated rollouts, with horizons increasing from 128 to 320 steps.
 - **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with cosine-annealing learning rate schedule.
- **Visual Supervision (Optional):** The model can also be refined using differentiable rendering, where a silhouette objective (L2 loss between rendered and observed robot masks) is backpropagated through the renderer, simulated configurations, and differentiable dynamics to refine camera extrinsics and NeuralActuator parameters.

**E. Comparison with Baselines:**
NeuralActuator is compared against model-based alternatives (ID-Linear, ID-Friction, Generalized Momentum Observer (GMO)) that use linear current-to-torque maps and recover end-effector force, highlighting its advantage in handling low-cost servo non-linearities.

### 4. Main findings and results

The evaluation of NeuralActuator spanned multiple robotic platforms and diverse tasks, demonstrating its capabilities in dynamics modeling, force perception, and motor condition estimation.

**A. Rollout Accuracy (OpenManipulator-X)**
- NeuralActuator demonstrated low rollout errors on held-out test datasets. For the OpenManipulator-X, average joint-angle Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for revolute joints and single-finger slide-coordinate MAE for the gripper remained low across different tasks and rollout horizons (e.g., average 2.5 degrees and 0.2 mm MAE at 100 steps, increasing moderately to 3.1 degrees and 0.2 mm MAE at 600 steps across various tasks).
- The model maintained accuracy over short horizons and showed moderate error growth over longer horizons (up to 600 steps, approximately 10 seconds). This indicates that the physics-in-the-loop learning effectively captures history-dependent input-output behavior while maintaining rigid-body dynamics structure.
- Gradient analysis showed that simulator-input gradient norms remained around O(10⁻²) and parameter-gradient cosine similarity was consistently high (at least 0.96 relative to H=128), suggesting effective gradient flow through longer rollouts (Table II).

**B. Force Estimation Accuracy**
- **External Force Sensing (OpenManipulator-X):** On contact trajectories with forces up to 9 N applied at the end-effector, the 3D external force prediction exhibited MAEs ranging from 0.36–0.39 N at 100 steps and 0.38–0.57 N at 500 steps. For reference trajectories without contact, NeuralActuator accurately predicted near-zero forces (0.00–0.02 N), indicating a low false-positive rate.
- **Payload Benchmark (OpenManipulator-X):** Across various payloads (200-500g) and tasks (go up and stay, pick and place), NeuralActuator achieved an average force MAE of 0.08–0.12 N, with maximum single-task errors not exceeding 0.24 N. It also showed some generalization capability, accurately estimating forces for unseen payloads (2.80 N predicted vs 2.56 N ground truth, and 2.40 N predicted vs 2.21 N ground truth for two unseen objects).
- **Gauge Pushing (OpenManipulator-X):** For contact with a force gauge from different directions and end-effector positions, average force MAE was 0.08 N at 100 steps, increasing to 0.10 N at 600 steps, consistently at most 0.12 N for every setting. Predicted forces tracked contact onset, sustained loading, and release (Figure 9).
- **Cross-Platform Validation (Franka Emika Panda):** For the 7-DoF Franka arm, NeuralActuator achieved a three-component force MAE ranging from 0.26 to 0.42 N on offline payload lift-and-hold trajectories, demonstrating its capability for external-force estimation on industrial-grade robots.
- **Cross-Platform Validation (SO-101):** On the 6-DoF SO-101 low-cost arm, force MAE ranged from 0.47–0.73 N at a 500-step horizon, with joint errors up to approximately 9.8 degrees, indicating applicability to other low-cost servo families.

**C. Learned Torque Surrogates**
- Qualitative analysis showed that the raw, pre-clipping torque-surrogate outputs for the OpenManipulator-X arm joints correlated with the measured motor currents over time (Figure 10). This indicates that the model learned a history-dependent, non-linear mapping between telemetry and the effective generalized input required by the simulator, without relying on a fixed linear current-torque prior.

**D. Comparison with Baselines**
- NeuralActuator significantly outperformed classical model-based sensorless baselines (ID-Linear, ID-Friction, Generalized Momentum Observer (GMO)) on the payload benchmark. The mean force MAE of NeuralActuator was 0.12 N, which was 5.5 times lower than the best classical baseline (GMO, 0.66 N). Classical methods also produced high false-positive contact rates (33–59%) in no-contact regimes, whereas NeuralActuator remained near zero. This highlights the effectiveness of NeuralActuator's data-driven approach in modeling complex non-linearities of geared servos (Table VII).

**E. Online Adaptation**
- The model demonstrated adaptability to changing actuator characteristics or hardware instances. Fine-tuning NeuralActuator in situ on 12 newly collected trajectories (7,810 frames) reduced aggregate position-tracking error by approximately 44% after 70 epochs (356 seconds). Force MAE remained comparable or slightly improved at longer horizons (Table VIII, Figure 11).

**F. Motor Condition Estimation (OpenManipulator-X)**
- NeuralActuator achieved high classification performance in distinguishing normal from mechanically restricted operation for Joint 3 (rubber band constraint). It achieved 91.0% accuracy, 96.2% recall (for the mechanically restricted class), and a 0.95 AUC-ROC on a pick-and-place task, outperforming threshold-based, SVM, and Random Forest baselines. This indicates its capability for sensorless motor diagnostics (Table XI, Figure 13).

**G. Runtime Performance**
- The JAX-based implementation with JIT compilation yielded efficient real-time inference. Mean GPU inference time was 0.25 ms, with P95 at 0.31 ms, providing a throughput of over 4,000 Hz for batch size 1. This performance is suitable for deployment at typical robot control rates (e.g., 60 Hz). The model also had a modest parameter footprint (1.44 million parameters, 5.50 MiB memory) (Table XII).

**H. Force-Aware Imitation Learning for Real-Robot Control**
- When NeuralActuator was used as a pretrained force-perception module to augment behavior cloning policies, it improved task success rates. For object lift-and-hold, success rates increased from 85% (position-only baseline) to 95%. For pick-and-place, success rates increased from 80% to 92.5%. This indicates that online force feedback from NeuralActuator enhances real-robot control performance in manipulation tasks, especially under varying payloads and intermittent contacts (Table XIII).

**I. Visual Supervision**
- An image-space refinement pipeline combining NeuralActuator with differentiable dynamics and rendering achieved a mean IoU of 0.8515 for silhouette alignment. This demonstrates the potential for visual supervision to jointly refine camera extrinsics and NeuralActuator parameters (Figure 14, Table A17).

**J. Ablation on Network Architecture**
- Comparison with MLP, GRU, and LSTM architectures (with matched parameter counts) showed that the Transformer-based NeuralActuator performed best or tied for best on four of six key metrics, including force MAE (0.23 N vs 0.41 N for LSTM), indicating the effectiveness of its architecture for capturing temporal dependencies and complex dynamics (Table XIV).

**K. Additional Physics Backend**
- NeuralActuator was successfully integrated with NVIDIA Warp, another differentiable rigid-body simulator, demonstrating its compatibility and generalizability across different simulation backends (Figure 15).

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The NeuralActuator research contributes to the field of robotics by addressing several long-standing challenges, particularly for cost-effective robotic platforms, and offers several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Advanced Capabilities for Low-Cost Robotics:** The primary significance lies in providing a robust framework for accurate dynamics modeling and sensorless force perception on low-cost, servo-driven robots. These platforms typically suffer from non-ideal actuator dynamics (e.g., friction, hysteresis, backlash, thermal effects) that make precise control and interaction challenging. By overcoming these limitations, NeuralActuator can broaden the accessibility and utility of robotics for research, education, small businesses, and a wider range of applications where budget constraints prevent the use of high-end industrial robots with sophisticated sensors.

- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Actuator dynamics are a major source of the "sim-to-real gap." By learning a simulator-equivalent generalized-effort surrogate that accounts for real-world actuator non-idealities, NeuralActuator enhances the fidelity of differentiable simulations. This improved realism facilitates the training of policies and controllers in simulation that are more directly transferable to physical robots, reducing the need for extensive real-world experimentation and recalibration.

- **Sensorless Force Perception:** The ability to accurately estimate external forces at the end-effector without dedicated force/torque sensors (which are typically expensive and heavy) is a substantial advantage. This enables force-aware manipulation, collision detection, and interaction with unstructured or deformable environments using more economical and lighter robot hardware. This can lead to safer human-robot collaboration and more dexterous manipulation capabilities in various domains, from manufacturing to service robotics.

- **Enhanced Robot Control and Diagnostics:**
 - **Control:** The integration of NeuralActuator's force perception into downstream control tasks, such as behavior cloning, demonstrated improved task success rates. This indicates that providing real-time force feedback can make controllers more robust to varying payloads and intermittent contacts, leading to more reliable and adaptable robot operation.
 - **Diagnostics:** The motor-condition estimation capability provides a valuable diagnostic tool. By distinguishing normal from mechanically restricted operation, it can enable early detection of mechanical issues, support predictive maintenance, and enhance the operational safety and longevity of robotic systems.

- **Advancement of Differentiable Simulation:** NeuralActuator's methodology for training a torque-surrogate head through pose-based supervision from differentiable simulation, without direct generalized-effort labels, represents a novel approach in integrating neural networks with physics engines. This expands the capabilities of differentiable simulators, making them more adaptable to systems where direct physical parameters or internal states are difficult to measure or model.

- **Creation of a Valuable Dataset (NAD):** The introduction of the Neural Actuation Dataset (NAD), which provides synchronized robot states, actuator telemetry, and external-force labels across various conditions and platforms, is a significant contribution to the research community. This dataset offers a standardized resource for training and benchmarking future models in low-cost actuator dynamics and force perception.

- **Generalizability and Robustness:** Validation across three distinct robot platforms (OpenManipulator-X, SO-101, Franka Emika Panda) spanning different actuator families and cost ranges, along with demonstrated online adaptation capabilities, suggests the model's robustness and potential for broad applicability in diverse robotic systems.

In summary, NeuralActuator offers a comprehensive, data-driven solution to critical problems in robot dynamics and perception, particularly for the large and growing segment of low-cost robotic hardware. Its potential impact includes making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible, improving the reliability and safety of robot operations, and driving further innovation in the field of differentiable simulation and embodied AI.
