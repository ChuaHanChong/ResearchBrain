# MokA: Multimodal Low-Rank Adaptation for MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> MokA introduces a multimodal low-rank adaptation method for Multimodal Large Language Models, explicitly addressing the heterogeneous nature of multimodal data during fine-tuning. It achieves consistent performance improvements across various multimodal tasks and LLM backbones with only a marginal increase in computational cost compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning large Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for specific tasks is computationally prohibitive due to their immense parameter scale.
- Existing Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods, adapted from Large Language Models (LLMs), often neglect the intrinsic differences and heterogeneous nature of multimodal inputs.
- Standard LoRA's shared parameters tend to be 'overly influenced by text tokens,' leading to less effective utilization of non-text tokens during MLLM fine-tuning.

## Method

- **MokA** redefines the roles of LoRA's ==low-rank decomposition== matrices to explicitly incorporate both unimodal and cross-modal adaptation for MLLMs.
- It introduces modality-specific 'A' matrices to compress unique unimodal information independently, preventing interference from other modalities.
- A task-centric cross-attention mechanism is employed where non-text tokens query text tokens, enhancing cross-modal interaction while preserving the LLM's text understanding.

## Results

- MokA consistently achieves superior performance across diverse multimodal benchmarks (audio-visual-text, visual-text, speech-text), outperforming standard LoRA and its variants (e.g., up to **8%** accuracy gain on speech-text tasks).
- Partial modality inference experiments confirmed MokA's improved utilization of individual non-text modalities, demonstrating that modality-specific 'A' matrices enhance unimodal adaptation.
- The method incurs only a modest increase in trainable parameters (e.g., **1.33%** vs **1.27%** of total for LLaMA2) and marginal inference latency while delivering notable performance gains (e.g., **3.95%** on POPE benchmark).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unimodal adaptation (learning from each modality independently) and cross-modal adaptation (facilitating interaction between modalities) are equally critical for effective MLLM fine-tuning.
> - Directly applying PEFT methods designed for LLMs to MLLMs leads to suboptimal utilization of non-textual modalities due to a text-dominated adaptation process.
> - Strategically designed modality-specific and cross-modal interaction components within a low-rank adaptation framework can significantly enhance MLLM performance and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to their impressive capabilities in understanding and generating text. Extending these capabilities to process and integrate information from multiple modalities, such as images, audio, and speech, has given rise to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). MLLMs represent a crucial step towards more human-like AI, capable of comprehending complex, real-world scenarios that inherently involve diverse sensory inputs.

However, the immense parameter scale of both LLMs and MLLMs presents a significant challenge: fully fine-tuning these models for specific downstream tasks is computationally prohibitive in terms of memory, storage, and processing power. To address this, the field has gravitated towards Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) strategies. These methods aim to achieve comparable performance to full fine-tuning by updating only a small subset of the model's parameters, thereby drastically reducing computational costs.

Among PEFT techniques, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and its numerous variants (e.g., LoRAMoE, DoRA, HydraLoRA) have gained widespread adoption due to their effectiveness and simplicity. LoRA posits that the weight updates during fine-tuning lie in a low intrinsic dimension, parameterizing these updates with two smaller matrices whose product approximates the full weight update. While highly successful in LLMs, these LoRA-based methods have been directly extended to MLLMs.

The critical gap identified by the authors of "MokA" is that most current efficient multimodal fine-tuning methods, being direct adaptations from LLM techniques, often **neglect the intrinsic differences and heterogeneous nature of multimodal scenarios**. They typically employ shared parameters to uniformly process tokens from all modalities, implicitly assuming that all modalities behave similarly during adaptation. Prior research in multimodal learning has, however, consistently shown that the inherent heterogeneity of different modalities necessitates modality-specific utilization strategies rather than a "one-size-fits-all" approach. This oversight in existing PEFT methods for MLLMs can lead to suboptimal utilization of non-textual modalities and hinder the model's overall multimodal understanding.

MokA directly addresses this fundamental limitation by proposing a "multimodal-aware" efficient fine-tuning strategy. It positions itself as a "first-step attempt" to design PEFT methods specifically tailored for MLLMs, acknowledging and incorporating the unique characteristics of multimodal data. By emphasizing both unimodal adaptation (processing each modality distinctively) and cross-modal adaptation (explicitly enhancing interactions between modalities), MokA aims to provide a more targeted and effective solution for the fine-tuning of MLLMs, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in efficient multimodal learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MokA stems from a critical empirical observation regarding the limitations of existing PEFT strategies, particularly LoRA, when applied to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The authors demonstrate that current methods, which typically employ shared LoRA parameters across all modalities, fail to adequately leverage information from non-textual inputs.

**Empirical Observation and Problem Identification:**
The authors conduct "partial modality inference experiments" using a classic LoRA module applied to an MLLM framework. In this setup, an MLLM is fine-tuned with LoRA modules processing tokens from all modalities (e.g., audio, visual, text). Subsequently, during inference, the model's performance is evaluated when *only tokens from a selected modality* (e.g., text-only, audio-only, visual-only) are passed through the LoRA adaptation pathway, while the pre-trained weights still receive full tokens from all modalities.
The results (Figures 1d-1f) reveal a "surprising phenomenon":
1. **Text token inference** can achieve performance quite comparable to the regular "full modalities" case. This suggests that the shared LoRA parameters are effectively optimized to capture textual information.
2. **Non-text token inference** (e.g., audio or visual) leads to a "noticeable drop in performance."

This discrepancy highlights a significant issue: the optimization of the all-modality-shared LoRA parameters is "overly influenced by text tokens," resulting in non-text tokens being "less effectively utilized during fine-tuning." While these shared parameters might implicitly improve some level of cross-modal interaction, the phenomenon clearly indicates that the individual contributions and unique characteristics of each modality are not being fully exploited.

**Core Hypothesis and Objectives:**
Based on this crucial observation, the authors put forth a foundational argument: **unimodal adaptation and cross-modal adaptation are equally critical in the fine-tuning of MLLMs.** They argue that the model needs to be able to:
1. **Learn from each modality independently (unimodal adaptation):** Capture the unique features and information intrinsic to each individual modality without interference from others.
2. **Ensure cross-modal interaction (cross-modal adaptation):** Facilitate effective communication and alignment between different modalities, especially between non-textual context and textual task descriptions, to support accurate reasoning and task execution.

The core objective of this paper is to **propose Multimodal low-rank Adaptation (MokA)**, a novel fine-tuning strategy explicitly designed to address the identified limitations. MokA aims to:
- Develop a parameter-efficient method that explicitly considers the multimodal nature of MLLMs.
- Ensure both effective unimodal information compression and explicit enhancement of cross-modal interaction.
- Redefine the roles of LoRA's projection matrices (A and B) to better accommodate these multimodal characteristics.
- Demonstrate consistent performance improvements across diverse multimodal scenarios and LLM backbones, validating its efficacy and versatility.

In essence, MokA seeks to provide a more "targeted solution for efficient adaptation of MLLMs" by moving beyond the direct application of LLM-centric PEFT methods and incorporating a multimodal-aware design philosophy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MokA, or Multimodal low-rank Adaptation, is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) strategy specifically tailored for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It builds upon the foundational concept of LoRA but redefines the roles of its low-rank decomposition matrices (A and B) to explicitly incorporate multimodal characteristics, thereby ensuring both unimodal and cross-modal adaptation.

**Rethinking Low-Rank Adaptation for MLLMs:**
The authors start by reformulating the standard LoRA update (W' = W0 + BA) to explicitly account for multimodal inputs. They propose that the update mechanism should capture both unimodal and cross-modal information, as expressed in Equation 3:
$h = W_0x + [\Delta W_1x_{m1}; \Delta W_2x_{m2}; \dots; \Delta W_nx_{mn}]_{\text{unimodal adaptation}} + \Delta W_{cross}[x_{m1}; x_{m2}; \dots; x_{mn}]_{\text{cross-modal adaptation}}$
This decomposition guides the design of MokA, which comprises three core components:

1. **Unimodal Matrix A:**
 - **Purpose:** To compress unique unimodal information independently and prevent interference from other modalities.
 - **Design:** Instead of a single shared matrix A, MokA introduces *modality-specific A matrices* ($A_a, A_v, A_t$ for audio, visual, and text, respectively). Each modality's token sequence ($x_i$) is processed by its dedicated matrix ($A_i x_i$).
 - **Benefit:** This ensures that embeddings of each modality are individually mapped into a low-rank space, preserving modality-specific characteristics without being diluted or overly influenced by other modalities. These matrices $A_i \in R^{r \times k}$ operate on individual modality tokens.

2. **Task-Centric Cross-Attention:**
 - **Purpose:** To explicitly strengthen the interaction between text tokens (which often convey task descriptions or prompts) and non-text tokens (which provide contextual information), emphasizing task-relevant features.
 - **Placement:** This module is strategically placed *after* the unimodal compression by matrices A. This is because the token embeddings are now in a low-rank space, significantly reducing the computational burden of cross-modal interaction.
 - **Mechanism:** Non-text tokens (e.g., $A_a x_a$, $A_v x_v$) serve as *queries*, while text tokens ($A_t x_t$) serve as both *keys* and *values*. This design decision is crucial: it allows non-text modalities to selectively integrate relevant information from the text (task description) without altering the text tokens themselves. Keeping text tokens unchanged helps preserve the LLM's inherent strong text understanding capabilities, preventing potential disruption. Hyperparameters $\lambda_a$ and $\lambda_v$ control the strength of this explicit cross-modal interaction. The cross-attention mechanism is a scaled dot-product attention as shown in Equations 5 and 6, effectively enhancing the non-text token representations. The output is a modified sequence $Ax = [A_a x_a + \lambda_a Att_{a,t,t}; A_v x_v + \lambda_v Att_{v,t,t}; A_t x_t]$.

3. **Shared Multimodal Matrix B:**
 - **Purpose:** To project the enhanced unimodal representations (after compression and cross-attention) into a shared, unified space, facilitating robust cross-modal alignment.
 - **Design:** A single shared matrix $B \in R^{d \times r}$ is applied to the output of the previous stage.
 - **Benefit:** This final projection integrates the modality-specific, cross-attention-enhanced low-rank representations into the main LLM pathway, ensuring effective alignment and fusion across all modalities for downstream tasks.

**Overall Forward Pass and Training:**
The fine-tuned weight update for an input $x = [x_a; x_v; x_t]$ is given by Equation 13, which clearly separates the unimodal adaptation part ($BA_a x_a$, $BA_v x_v$, $BA_t x_t$) and the cross-modal adaptation part ($\lambda_a B Att_{a,t,t}$; $\lambda_v B Att_{v,t,t}$). During fine-tuning, the original pretrained weight matrix $W_0$ remains frozen, while only the modality-specific $A_i$ matrices and the shared $B$ matrix are optimized. $A_i$ are initialized with Kaiming uniform distribution, and $B$ is initialized to zero for a smooth start.

**Implementation Details and Training Procedure:**
MokA is integrated into a common MLLM framework (Figure 1a) where non-text inputs are first encoded by modality-specific encoders (e.g., CLIP-ViT/L-14 for visual, BEATs for audio, Whisper for speech) and then aligned with the text embedding space via a projector (Q-former followed by a two-layer MLP). All tokens are then fed into the LLM backbone (LLaMA2/3, Qwen2, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3).
The training follows a two-stage paradigm:
1. **Pre-training:** Focuses on cross-modal alignment, with LLM backbone frozen and only projectors trainable. Various datasets like Video-LLaVA, AudioCaps, and GigaSpeech-M are used.
2. **Instruction-tuning:** Fine-tunes the model on downstream tasks, with all projectors and MokA modules being trainable.
The method is evaluated across three representative multimodal scenarios: audio-visual-text (MUSIC-AVQA, AVE), visual-text (MME_percep, MMBench, POPE, SEED-Bench), and speech-text (MMAU_mini-speech, AIR-Bench_speech-en), using a low-rank rank of 4.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation of MokA across various multimodal scenarios, LLM backbones, and benchmark datasets consistently demonstrates its efficacy and versatility. The key findings and results are summarized below:

1. **Superior Performance Across Multimodal Scenarios:**
 - **Audio-Visual-Text:** MokA consistently achieves the highest performance on both MUSIC-AVQA and AVE datasets, outperforming standard LoRA and all its variants (Multiple LoRA, LoRAMoE, DoRA, HydraLoRA, Uni-modal LoRA). This is observed across LLaMA2, Qwen2, and LLaMA3 backbones. For example, with LLaMA2, MokA achieves 75.71% on MUSIC-AVQA and 74.68% on AVE, notably higher than LoRA's 73.41% and 69.84%, respectively.
 - **Visual-Text:** MokA shows stable and consistent gains across benchmarks like MME_percep, MMBench, POPE, and SEED-Bench, for LLaMA2, LLaMA3, and Qwen2. With LLaMA2, MokA achieves 1025.86 on MME_percep, 52.74% on MMBench, 74.23% on POPE, and 40.45% on SEED-Bench, consistently surpassing LoRA and other variants.
 - **Speech-Text:** Similar improvements are observed on MMAU_mini-speech and AIR-Bench_speech-en with LLaMA2, LLaMA3, and Qwen2 backbones. With LLaMA2, MokA achieves 38.44% on MMAU and 39.64% on AIR-Bench, significantly better than LoRA's 30.33% and 31.75%.
 - **Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3:** Even with specialized VL models like Qwen2.5-VL (which has a frozen official visual connector) and the latest Qwen3, MokA still introduces noticeable improvements compared to other methods, demonstrating its broad applicability.

2. **Validation of Multimodal-Aware Design:**
 - The paper introduces two baselines ("Uni LoRA + MM LoRA" and "Uni LoRA + MM LoRA + Gate") that also incorporate aspects of unimodal and cross-modal adaptation (using more parameters). These baselines achieve competitive results, supporting MokA's core hypothesis that both unimodal and cross-modal adaptations are essential. However, MokA achieves superior results with a more efficient design.

3. **Enhanced Modality Utilization (Partial Modality Inference):**
 - Partial modality inference experiments (MokA w/o cross-attention) reveal that MokA's design significantly improves the utilization of individual non-text modalities compared to classic LoRA (Figure 5 vs. Figure 1). This confirms that MokA's modality-specific A matrices successfully enhance unimodal adaptation, making non-text tokens more effectively processed.

4. **Effectiveness of Explicit Cross-Modal Interaction:**
 - Experiments with different cross-modal interaction variants (Cross-attention*, Naive interaction) show that explicitly enhancing cross-modal interactions is generally beneficial. MokA's specific design, where non-text tokens query text tokens and text tokens remain unchanged, yields the best performance. This confirms the importance of integrating task information from text into non-text modalities without disrupting the LLM's core language understanding.

5. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Necessity:**
 - The ablation study (Table 5) confirms the necessity of each part of MokA:
 - "MokA w/o CA" (without cross-attention, relying only on modality-specific A matrices) still outperforms LoRA, validating the importance of unimodal adaptation.
 - The addition of the cross-attention module further improves performance, indicating the clear benefit of explicit cross-modal adaptation.

6. **Favorable Efficiency Trade-off:**
 - MokA introduces a modest increase in trainable parameters (e.g., 1.33% of total parameters for LLaMA2 vs. 1.27% for LoRA) and a slight increase in inference latency (e.g., 1.069x avg. forward time per sample for visual-text) compared to standard LoRA.
 - However, these minor increases in computational cost are justified by the "notable performance gain" (e.g., 3.95% on the POPE benchmark). This indicates that MokA offers a highly efficient and effective solution.

7. **Scalability:**
 - MokA's effectiveness extends to scenarios with more than three modalities, as demonstrated by an experiment on the MCUB-3 benchmark (audio, visual, point cloud, and language data), where it significantly outperforms LoRA (45.58% vs. 37.41% accuracy).

In summary, MokA delivers consistent and significant performance enhancements across a wide spectrum of multimodal tasks and LLM architectures. These improvements are achieved by a principled design that explicitly addresses unimodal and cross-modal adaptation, offering a more effective and efficient fine-tuning strategy for complex MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MokA represents a significant step forward in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in addressing the critical challenge of efficient fine-tuning. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

1. **Pioneering Multimodal-Aware PEFT:** The most significant contribution of MokA is its pioneering approach to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) that is genuinely "multimodal-aware." By empirically revealing the limitations of directly applying LLM-centric PEFT methods to MLLMs (i.e., poor utilization of non-text modalities due to text-dominated adaptation), the authors highlight a fundamental oversight in current practices. MokA's design, which explicitly incorporates modality-specific adaptation and task-centric cross-modal interaction, provides a principled framework for developing PEFT strategies that truly respect the heterogeneous nature of multimodal data. This shifts the paradigm from merely adapting LLM techniques to MLLMs to designing solutions intrinsically for MLLMs.

2. **Enhanced Multimodal Understanding and Reasoning:** By ensuring both robust unimodal information compression and explicit cross-modal interaction, MokA enables MLLMs to better process and integrate diverse sensory inputs. The modality-specific 'A' matrices preserve the unique characteristics of each input, while the cross-attention mechanism allows non-textual context to be effectively aligned with textual task descriptions. This deeper, more balanced integration of information is crucial for sophisticated multimodal understanding and reasoning tasks, leading to the consistent and significant performance gains observed across a wide range of benchmarks and scenarios.

3. **Improved Efficiency and Accessibility:** MLLMs are notoriously expensive to train and fine-tune. MokA offers a highly efficient solution that achieves substantial performance improvements with only a marginal increase in trainable parameters and inference latency compared to standard LoRA. This efficiency makes advanced MLLM capabilities more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources, fostering broader experimentation and application of these powerful models. Reducing the computational burden is key to democratizing advanced AI research and deployment.

4. **Versatility and Generalizability:** The consistent improvements demonstrated across diverse multimodal scenarios (audio-visual-text, visual-text, speech-text), multiple powerful LLM backbones (LLaMA2/3, Qwen2/2.5-VL/3), and various benchmarks underscore MokA's versatility and generalizability. This indicates that its core design principles are robust and applicable to a wide array of multimodal tasks, making it a valuable tool for future MLLM development.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The authors explicitly position MokA as a "preliminary step toward multimodal-aware adaptation," highlighting its potential to "pave the way for further exploration." This opens up new avenues for research into more sophisticated multimodal-aware PEFT techniques. Future work could explore more dynamic cross-modal interaction mechanisms, hierarchical multimodal adaptation, or context-adaptive parameter sharing strategies based on MokA's foundational ideas. It encourages the community to think beyond direct LLM adaptations and innovate specifically for multimodal challenges.

**Potential Broader Impact (as per Appendix H):**
The improvements in efficient MLLM fine-tuning brought by MokA can have a positive impact on various downstream applications. For instance:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Better integration of visual, audio, and potentially other sensor data for enhanced environmental perception and decision-making.
- **Education:** Development of more intelligent tutoring systems or interactive learning platforms that can process and respond to multimodal student inputs (e.g., speech, gestures, visual cues).
- **Assistive Technologies:** Creation of more robust multimodal assistants for individuals with disabilities.

However, the authors also responsibly acknowledge potential risks associated with MLLMs, such as the reflection or amplification of biases present in training data, or misuse in sensitive contexts. While MokA itself focuses on efficiency, its contributions can fuel the development of more capable MLLMs, making it essential to continue addressing these ethical considerations in parallel research efforts.

In conclusion, MokA provides a methodologically sound and empirically validated solution to a critical problem in MLLM development. By championing a multimodal-aware design for PEFT, it not only advances the state of efficient fine-tuning but also lays important groundwork for the next generation of more capable, versatile, and accessible multimodal AI systems.
