# Mind the Gap: Bridging Thought Leap for Improved Chain-of-Thought Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, SF Technology, and Microsoft Research Asia identified and addressed "Thought Leaps" in Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets, where human-generated reasoning inadvertently omits intermediate steps. Their CoT-Bridge model automatically detects these gaps and generates missing steps, leading to consistent performance improvements of up to +15.63% on challenging math benchmarks and enhanced generalization for large language models.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets, despite their utility, suffer from "Thought Leaps" where human experts omit seemingly obvious intermediate reasoning steps.
- These incomplete reasoning chains create logical gaps that significantly hinder Large Language Model (LLM) learning, leading to lower performance ceilings and slower convergence rates.
- Prior research has primarily focused on factual errors or answer inaccuracies, largely overlooking the structural incompleteness caused by omitted steps in CoT data.

## Method

- Formalized the "CoT **Thought Leap Bridge** Task" as a two-stage process: identifying reasoning gaps and generating the missing intermediate steps.
- Constructed ScaleQM+, a specialized dataset for training, by systematically removing steps from complete reasoning chains of the ScaleQuestMath dataset to simulate Thought Leaps.
- Developed CoT-Bridge, an LLM fine-tuned on ScaleQM+ to predict both the positions of Thought Leaps and the content of the missing reasoning steps, which is then used to augment existing CoT datasets.

## Results

- Models fine-tuned on CoT-Bridge-enhanced datasets consistently outperformed those on unbridged datasets, showing average gains of +**5.87%** on NuminaMath and +**1.35%** on MetaMathQA for Meta-Llama3.1-**8B**.
- CoT-Bridge effectively functions as a plug-and-play module, amplifying the performance of models trained with knowledge distillation (+**3.02%** average accuracy) and providing better cold start models for reinforcement learning (+**3.10%** average accuracy).
- Enhanced models demonstrated improved generalization on out-of-domain logical reasoning benchmarks, with +**2.99%** higher average accuracy for Meta-Llama3.1-**8B** and a reduced rate of invalid responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Thought Leaps in CoT datasets act as a substantial barrier to LLM learning and generalization, even more so than factual inaccuracies, significantly reducing model performance.
> - Accurate identification of the exact positions of reasoning gaps is crucial; merely adding random steps can degrade rather than improve reasoning performance.
> - Specialized training with a dedicated dataset (ScaleQM+) for the CoT Thought Leap Bridge Task yields superior and more consistent performance compared to zero-shot bridging by general-purpose LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial, yet understudied, aspect of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in large language models (LLMs): the phenomenon of "Thought Leaps." CoT reasoning, introduced by Wei et al., has revolutionized LLM performance on complex tasks, particularly in mathematics and coding, by enabling models to generate intermediate reasoning steps, mimicking human step-by-step problem-solving. This enhances both accuracy and interpretability. The quality and completeness of these reasoning chains are paramount for effective LLM learning.

In the broader landscape of LLM research, significant efforts have been made to improve CoT reasoning. These typically fall into two categories:
1. **Inference-time methods:** These approaches focus on advanced decoding strategies (e.g., Self-Consistency), search-based methods (e.g., Tree-of-Thought, Monte Carlo Tree Search), or dynamic computational adjustments (e.g., multi-round thinking).
2. **Training-based methods:** These involve fine-tuning models on high-quality CoT datasets, incorporating self-reflection, self-verification, or self-correction mechanisms through various learning paradigms (supervised, reinforcement, or preference learning). The development of high-quality mathematical datasets like MetaMathQA, MathInstruct, NuminaMath-CoT, and ScaleQuestMath has been critical to these efforts.

The contribution of "Mind the Gap" is unique because it identifies a fundamental flaw in the *data itself* rather than just optimizing how models use or generate CoTs. "Thought Leaps," where human experts inadvertently omit seemingly trivial intermediate steps, create cognitive gaps that impede LLM learning. While factual errors or answer inaccuracies in CoT have been widely studied, the structural incompleteness due to omitted steps has largely been overlooked. This work pioneers the formalization of this problem and offers a data-centric solution.

By introducing the "CoT Thought Leap Bridge Task" and the "CoT-Bridge" model, this research directly aims to enhance the *completeness and coherence* of existing CoT datasets. This is complementary to both inference-time and training-based methods. Instead of just teaching models to *reason better* given imperfect data, it *improves the data itself*, providing a more robust foundation for all downstream LLM training and reasoning techniques. Its "plug-and-play" nature allows it to seamlessly integrate with and amplify the effectiveness of existing methods like knowledge distillation and reinforcement learning, positioning it as a foundational data preprocessing step that can benefit the entire LLM reasoning ecosystem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: despite the success of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, existing mathematical CoT datasets frequently suffer from a phenomenon termed "Thought Leap." This occurs when human experts, in their demonstrations or explanations, unconsciously omit one or more intermediate reasoning steps that they consider obvious, thereby creating logical gaps in the CoT sequence. For example, a math expert might skip a basic algebraic manipulation or the explicit counting of possibilities, assuming a learner's prior knowledge.

The **key objective** of this paper is to address this pervasive issue by automatically detecting these Thought Leaps and generating the necessary missing intermediate steps to restore the completeness and coherence of CoT reasoning.

The **motivation** is multifaceted:
- **Detrimental Impact on LLM Learning:** Preliminary experiments conducted by the authors (Figure 1b, Appendix C) clearly demonstrate that models trained on CoT datasets with Thought Leaps exhibit significantly lower performance ceilings (up to 27.83% reduction in accuracy) and slower convergence rates compared to models trained on complete reasoning chains. This highlights that these gaps act as a substantial barrier to effective model learning and generalization, even more so than factual inaccuracies, as suggested by prior research.
- **Enhancing Data Quality:** By automatically bridging these gaps, the goal is to elevate the overall quality of existing mathematical reasoning datasets, making them more effective for training LLMs. This focuses on the *structural completeness* of reasoning.
- **Improving Model Performance and Generalization:** A more coherent and complete reasoning chain should enable LLMs to better understand the underlying logic, leading to improved accuracy on complex mathematical tasks. Furthermore, the authors hypothesize that mastering complete reasoning structures will enhance generalization capabilities, allowing models to perform better on out-of-domain logical reasoning tasks.
- **Creating a Universal Enhancement Module:** The ambition is for the proposed solution, CoT-Bridge, to function as a plug-and-play module that can seamlessly integrate with and boost the effectiveness of other advanced LLM training techniques, such as knowledge distillation and reinforcement learning.

In essence, the paper aims to "mind the gap" in CoT reasoning data, recognizing that addressing these subtle but critical omissions is fundamental to unlocking the full potential of LLMs for robust, generalizable, and interpretable reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology revolves around the "CoT Thought Leap Bridge Task," which is formalized as a two-stage process: first, identifying reasoning gaps, and second, generating the missing steps.

#### 3.1. Task Formalization

The authors rigorously formalize a complete Chain-of-Thought as $C^* = (Q, s^*_1, s^*_2, \dots, s^*_m)$, where transitions between adjacent steps are explicitly coherent, denoted by a completeness function $V(\cdot, \cdot) = \text{True}$. A Thought Leap exists in an incomplete CoT $C = (s_0, s_1, \dots, s_n)$ if any adjacent pair $(s_k, s_{k+1})$ yields $V(s_k, s_{k+1}) = \text{False}$. The task then becomes identifying these pairs and generating a sequence of missing intermediate steps $S'_{\text{miss}}$ such that $V(s_k, s'_{k.1}) = \text{True}$, $V(s'_{k.i}, s'_{k.i+1}) = \text{True}$, and $V(s'_{k.j}, s_{k+1}) = \text{True}$.

#### 3.2. Thought Leap Bridging Dataset Construction (ScaleQM+)

To train a model for this task, a specialized dataset called **ScaleQM+** was constructed.
- **Base Dataset:** The structurally comprehensive ScaleQuestMath dataset was chosen as an approximation of ideal, complete CoT due to its well-formed reasoning structure.
- **Step Removal Strategy:**
 - Complete reasoning chains from ScaleQuestMath ($C^*$) are systematically modified by removing intermediate steps to create incomplete chains ($C$) that contain Thought Leaps.
 - The final step (containing the answer) is always retained to preserve answer completeness. The initial reasoning step can also be removed to encourage macro-planning.
 - The number of steps removed ($k_{\text{del}}$) is scaled with the original chain length: 1-2 steps for shorter chains ($\le 10$ steps), and 1-3 steps for longer chains ($> 10$ steps).
 - Critically, 20% of the original complete chains are intentionally retained unchanged. This teaches the model to recognize instances where no bridging is needed, preventing over-generation.
- **Training Components:** For each modified chain, two ground-truth components are generated: the Thought Leap positions ($L_{\text{gt}}$) and the corresponding missing steps ($M_{\text{gt}}$).
- **Dataset Scale:** This process yielded 588k training samples (with 10k for testing), by selecting examples with at least 6 steps.

#### 3.3. Bridge Model Training (CoT-Bridge)

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-Math-7B was chosen as the base LLM for fine-tuning.
- **Training Objective:** **CoT-Bridge** is trained to learn the mapping $f: C \rightarrow (\hat{L}, \hat{M})$, meaning it takes an incomplete chain and predicts both the leap positions and the content of the missing steps.
- **Baseline Variant:** **CoT-Bridge-Random** was also implemented as a comparison. This variant learns $g: (C, L_{\text{gt}}) \rightarrow \hat{M}$, meaning it is given the *ground-truth* leap positions and only focuses on generating the missing steps. This helps isolate the impact of accurate leap identification.
- **Training Details:** Standard instruction tuning techniques were used for one epoch.

#### 3.4. Data Augmentation with Bridge Model

Once trained, CoT-Bridge is applied to existing mathematical reasoning datasets (MetaMathQA and NuminaMath-CoT).
- **Inference Process:** Given an input reasoning chain, CoT-Bridge identifies predicted leap positions and generates the corresponding intermediate steps.
- **Bridged Datasets:** These generated steps are then inserted into the original chains to create "bridged" versions (MetaMath-Bridge, NuminaMath-Bridge), which are subsequently used for downstream LLM fine-tuning.
- **Flexibility:** The approach adapts to different step delimiters (e.g., `\n` for MetaMathQA, `\n\n` for NuminaMath-CoT).

This comprehensive methodology ensures that the CoT-Bridge model learns to accurately detect gaps and fill them with contextually appropriate and logically coherent reasoning steps, thereby enhancing the quality of CoT datasets for training more capable LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks, base models, and training paradigms consistently demonstrate the efficacy of the CoT Thought Leap Bridge approach.

- **Consistent Performance Improvements:** Models fine-tuned on CoT-Bridge-enhanced datasets (MetaMath-Bridge, NuminaMath-Bridge) consistently and significantly outperform those trained on original, unbridged datasets. For Meta-Llama3.1-8B, average improvements reached +5.87% on NuminaMath and +1.35% on MetaMathQA. For Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, gains were +3.39% on NuminaMath and +3.36% on MetaMathQA. These improvements were particularly pronounced on challenging, competition-level benchmarks like AMC23 (e.g., +15.63% on Meta-Llama3.1-8B with NuminaMath).

- **Cruciality of Accurate Leap Identification:** A direct comparison between CoT-Bridge (which detects and generates) and CoT-Bridge-Random (which generates at randomly provided positions) reveals that precise gap localization is vital. CoT-Bridge-Random often degraded performance on certain benchmarks, showing that merely adding steps without strategic placement can disrupt reasoning, whereas targeted bridging enhances logical flow.

- **Superiority over Zero-Shot Bridging:** While large general-purpose LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-Instruct-72B) showed some ability to perform zero-shot bridging, their performance was less consistent and often led to performance degradation compared to the specialized CoT-Bridge. This highlights the need for dedicated training for this complex task to ensure quality control and structural adaptability.

- **Plug-and-Play Integration and Amplification:** CoT-Bridge effectively functions as a modular enhancement. It improved the quality of data generated via knowledge distillation (+3.02% average accuracy) and rejection sampling (+1.37%). Furthermore, it provided better "cold start" models for reinforcement learning (RL), leading to higher initial performance and better final outcomes (+3.10% average accuracy in RL-trained models). This demonstrates its compatibility and ability to amplify other advanced optimization techniques.

- **Improved Generalization to Out-of-Domain Reasoning:** Evaluation on five out-of-domain logical reasoning benchmarks (FOLIO, LogicQA, ProofWriter, ReClor, RuleTaker) showed that CoT-Bridge enhanced models exhibited improved OOD reasoning performance (e.g., +2.99% for Meta-Llama3.1-8B) while also reducing the rate of invalid responses. This suggests that enhancing reasoning completeness fosters a more robust understanding of general reasoning structures.

- **Contribution of Different Bridging Positions:** An ablation study categorizing bridging content by position (begin, middle, end) revealed that all three types positively contribute to model performance. Middle-type bridgings were most prevalent and critical, while begin-type helped with planning, and end-type supported logical closure.

- **High Bridging Quality and Minimal Noise Impact:** Analysis using a process supervision model (Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B) indicated that CoT-Bridge generates high-quality intermediate steps with a very low proportion of low-scoring content (e.g., only 2.08% in the 0-0.1 PRM score range for NuminaMath, significantly better than zero-shot alternatives). Further experiments confirmed that removing these low-quality steps had a limited impact on model performance, suggesting that the noise introduced by CoT-Bridge is well-managed and does not significantly hinder training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions to the field of large language models and reasoning, with considerable potential impact:

- **Novel Problem Identification and Formalization:** The paper systematically identifies and formally defines "Thought Leap" as a prevalent, yet previously understudied, issue in Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning datasets. By distinguishing it from factual errors, the research carves out a new and important area for improving LLM reasoning. This theoretical clarity is a foundational step for future research in reasoning completeness.

- **Practical Solution for Data Quality:** The introduction of the "CoT Thought Leap Bridge Task," the specialized ScaleQM+ dataset, and the **CoT-Bridge** model provides a concrete, automatable solution for enhancing the structural completeness and coherence of existing CoT datasets. This directly addresses a critical weakness in training data, which is often sourced from human experts who implicitly omit steps.

- **Enhanced LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** By rectifying these reasoning gaps, CoT-Bridge demonstrably leads to more robust, accurate, and generalized reasoning in LLMs across various mathematical and logical reasoning benchmarks. This is crucial for developing LLMs that can tackle increasingly complex, multi-step problems with human-like logical rigor. The consistent performance improvements, especially on challenging competition-level tasks, underscore this impact.

- **Plug-and-Play Enhancement Module:** The ability of CoT-Bridge to seamlessly integrate with and boost the effectiveness of other advanced training paradigms, such as knowledge distillation and reinforcement learning, highlights its versatility. This makes it a valuable tool that can be easily adopted into existing LLM development pipelines, providing a foundational data-centric improvement that amplifies the benefits of other sophisticated techniques.

- **Improved Generalization:** The observed improvements in out-of-domain logical reasoning tasks suggest that fostering reasoning completeness helps LLMs internalize more fundamental and transferable logical structures. This broadens the applicability of CoT-Bridge beyond specific domains, indicating that the benefits of coherent reasoning are widely applicable.

- **Future Research Directions:** This work opens several avenues for future research. It encourages deeper investigation into the nuances of reasoning completeness across diverse domains (e.g., law, medicine, scientific QA), the development of even more sophisticated techniques for quality control in automated CoT generation, and extensive evaluation on larger-scale LLMs to confirm generalizability at the frontier of AI capabilities. The findings also indirectly support the growing emphasis on data-centric AI approaches, where improving the quality of training data yields substantial returns.

In conclusion, "Mind the Gap" presents a timely and impactful contribution by addressing a fundamental flaw in how LLMs currently learn reasoning. By bridging thought leaps, it empowers LLMs to achieve higher levels of reasoning accuracy, robustness, and generalization, pushing the boundaries of what these models can accomplish in complex cognitive tasks.
