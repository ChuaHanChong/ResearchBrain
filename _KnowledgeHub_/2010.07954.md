# Room-Across-Room: Multilingual Vision-and-Language Navigation with Dense Spatiotemporal Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Room-Across-Room (RxR), a large-scale, multilingual Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) dataset featuring instructions in English, Hindi, and Telugu, and incorporating dense spatiotemporal grounding. RxR provides longer, more complex paths and reduces exploitable biases present in previous datasets, demonstrating the value of human behavioral data in improving agent navigation performance.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) datasets like Room-to-Room (R2R) suffered from biases toward shortest-path navigation, allowing agents to succeed without deep language understanding.
- Previous VLN datasets were limited in size and linguistic diversity, primarily focusing on English instructions, which restricted research generalizability.
- Lack of fine-grained spatiotemporal grounding in existing datasets made it difficult to train models that deeply align specific words with visual observations and actions.

## Method

- Introduces **Room-Across-Room (RxR)**, a large-scale ==multilingual VLN dataset== collected in ==Matterport3D== environments.
- Employs a ==two-level path sampling== procedure (room-level and panorama-level) to generate longer, more complex, and less biased navigation trajectories.
- Integrates ==dense spatiotemporal grounding== by aligning ==spoken instructions==, ==3D poses==, and visual observations, collected from native speakers navigating environments in English, Hindi, and Telugu.

## Results

- RxR is an order of magnitude larger than R2R, containing **126,000** navigation instructions in English, Hindi, and Telugu across **16,500** unique paths.
- Baseline agents perform significantly worse on RxR compared to R2R, confirming the dataset successfully reduces exploitable biases like 'going straight' strategies.
- A multitask model trained on both R2R and RxR achieves the best performance on both datasets, though direct transfer between them performs poorly, indicating their substantial differences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human navigation instructions often involve broad initial visual scanning before focusing on specific features, providing valuable signals for learning visual attention.
> - Authentic multilingual data reveals cross-linguistic differences in how spatial navigation is expressed, enabling research into typological variations.
> - Models trained on human follower paths, in addition to expert guide paths, consistently improve agent performance in instruction following.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) represents a critical challenge in embodied artificial intelligence, requiring computational agents to understand natural language instructions while navigating through realistic 3D environments. This paper introduces Room-Across-Room (RxR), a large-scale multilingual VLN dataset that addresses fundamental limitations in existing benchmarks and pushes the boundaries of what agents must understand to successfully navigate based on language instructions.

*An example navigation environment from the RxR dataset, showing the complex indoor spaces with multiple rooms and pathways that agents must navigate through.*

The core contribution of this work is the creation of a dataset that is significantly larger, more linguistically diverse, and more challenging than previous VLN benchmarks. RxR contains 126,000 navigation instructions across three languages (English, Hindi, and Telugu) covering 16,500 unique paths, making it an order of magnitude larger than existing datasets like Room-to-Room (R2R).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The field of Vision-and-Language Navigation has emerged as a key testbed for embodied AI systems that must integrate visual perception, language understanding, and sequential decision-making. Previous datasets in this domain, particularly the influential Room-to-Room (R2R) dataset, established the foundation for VLN research but suffered from several critical limitations that RxR aims to address.

Traditional VLN datasets exhibited strong biases toward shortest-path navigation, allowing agents to achieve high success rates through simple goal-seeking behaviors rather than genuine language understanding. Additionally, the overwhelming focus on English instructions limited the generalizability of research findings and raised questions about how spatial language varies across different linguistic contexts.

The research landscape has been characterized by efforts to improve agent performance through data augmentation, pre-training strategies, and architectural innovations. However, the fundamental data limitations meant that many advances were incremental improvements on biased benchmarks rather than steps toward truly robust language-grounded navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RxR's construction involved a sophisticated two-level path sampling procedure designed to create challenging yet natural navigation trajectories. The methodology addresses the shortest-path bias of previous datasets while ensuring paths remain realistic and followable.

*Visualization of the two-level path generation process, showing how room-level paths (left) are refined into specific panorama sequences (right).*

The path generation process operates at two levels:

**High-Level Room Sampling**: The system first generates paths through a "room graph" where each node represents a connected component of panoramas within a room. Paths traverse at most 5 rooms and 2 building levels, inherently creating longer and more complex trajectories than simple point-to-point navigation.

**Low-Level Panorama Path Selection**: Within the selected room sequence, the system finds the shortest path between randomly sampled start and end panoramas, constrained to stay within the designated rooms. This ensures local naturalness while maintaining global complexity.

A greedy selection algorithm then chooses paths to maximize panorama coverage while maintaining diversity in path lengths. The resulting dataset contains paths that are significantly longer (average 14.9m vs. R2R's 9.4m) with much greater variance, and crucially, 44.5% of paths are not the shortest route between start and goal points.

*Comparison of path characteristics between R2R and RxR, showing increased length, complexity, and more uniform environment coverage in RxR.*

#### 3.1. Dense Spatiotemporal Grounding

One of RxR's most innovative aspects is the collection of dense spatiotemporal grounding information that aligns spoken words with specific 3D poses and visual observations. This level of detail provides unprecedented supervision signals for training navigation agents.

The data collection process involved a custom web-based simulator that simultaneously recorded annotators' speech, 3D camera poses, and visual observations as they navigated through environments. Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) generated time-stamped transcriptions, which were then aligned with manual transcriptions and pose traces using dynamic time warping.

*Example showing how spoken instructions are temporally aligned with specific viewpoints and visual observations during navigation.*

This grounding enables several research directions:

- **Visual Attention Learning**: Models can learn which visual elements correspond to specific instruction phrases
- **Temporal Dynamics**: Understanding how language references evolve during navigation
- **Pragmatic Interpretation**: Studying how humans actually interpret and execute navigation instructions

The collected human behavioral data reveals interesting patterns, such as humans initially scanning broadly (observing 43-44% of available visual information) before focusing more narrowly on relevant features (27-28% at subsequent viewpoints).

#### 3.2. Multilingual Aspects

RxR's multilingual nature represents a significant advancement in VLN research, incorporating instructions in English, Hindi, and Telugu—languages chosen for their typological diversity. Critically, these instructions were created by native speakers navigating the environments, not translated from English, ensuring authentic linguistic expressions of spatial concepts.

The linguistic analysis reveals interesting cross-language differences in how spatial navigation is expressed. For example, Hindi instructions showed less use of coreference and temporal sequencing compared to English, though the authors note this could reflect annotator conventions rather than purely linguistic differences.

The multilingual aspect enables several research directions:
- **Cross-linguistic Transfer**: Understanding how navigation knowledge transfers between languages
- **Typological Analysis**: Studying how different languages encode spatial and temporal relationships
- **Multilingual Model Development**: Creating agents that can follow instructions in multiple languages

Experimental results show that monolingual agents generally outperform multilingual ones, consistent with findings in other multilingual NLP tasks where adding languages can degrade high-resource language performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper establishes baseline performance using variants of the Reinforced Cross-Modal Matching (RCM) agent architecture, adapted for RxR's increased complexity and multilingual nature. The results demonstrate both the challenging nature of the dataset and the value of its unique annotations.

Key experimental findings include:

**Dataset Difficulty**: Simple baselines perform significantly worse on RxR than on R2R, particularly strategies that exploit path biases like "going straight." This confirms that RxR successfully reduces exploitable biases.

**Value of Human Demonstrations**: Training with both guide paths (original routes) and follower paths (human interpretations) consistently improves performance across all metrics and languages, highlighting the value of human behavioral data.

**Spatiotemporal Grounding**: Initial experiments with supervising visual attention using human observation patterns show promise but require further development. The grounding supervision improved path-following metrics but not goal-reaching success rates.

**Cross-Dataset Transfer**: A multitask model trained on both R2R and RxR achieves the best performance on both datasets, demonstrating mutual benefit. However, direct transfer (training on one, testing on the other) performs poorly, emphasizing the substantial differences between datasets.

*Analysis of human visual attention patterns during navigation, showing how guides and followers scan and focus on different parts of the environment.*

The substantial gap between human performance (93.9% success rate) and the best agents indicates significant room for improvement and validates RxR as a challenging benchmark for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RxR represents a substantial advancement in embodied AI research, addressing critical limitations in existing VLN datasets while opening new research directions. Its significance extends beyond simply providing more data—it fundamentally changes what constitutes successful navigation from goal-reaching to instruction-following.

The dataset's multilingual nature pioneers research in cross-linguistic embodied AI, enabling investigation of how spatial concepts are expressed across different languages and cultures. This aspect is crucial for developing AI systems that can operate globally rather than being limited to English-speaking contexts.

The dense spatiotemporal grounding provides a new paradigm for studying the relationship between language and embodied experience. This detailed alignment between words, visual observations, and actions offers insights into human navigation strategies and provides rich supervision signals for training more capable agents.

Future research directions enabled by RxR include:
- **Advanced Grounding Models**: Leveraging the detailed alignment data to build models with human-like attention patterns
- **Multilingual Transfer Learning**: Understanding how navigation knowledge transfers across languages
- **Human-AI Interaction**: Using human follower traces to understand instruction interpretation and execution
- **Robotic Applications**: Translating improvements in simulated navigation to real-world robotic systems

The dataset's challenging nature ensures it will remain relevant as agent capabilities improve, providing a robust benchmark for measuring progress in embodied language understanding. By emphasizing true instruction-following over goal-seeking, RxR pushes the field toward developing agents with more sophisticated language grounding capabilities that are essential for real-world deployment.
