# MoE-LLaVA: Mixture of Experts for Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MoE-LLaVA introduces a Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture and a three-stage MoE-Tuning strategy for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), achieving comparable or superior performance to dense LVLMs with significantly fewer activated parameters. The model demonstrates improved object hallucination mitigation and provides insights into expert specialization within multi-modal contexts.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) incur high computational costs for training and inference due to their 'dense' architectures, where all parameters are active for every token.
- Scaling LVLMs to larger sizes leads to increasing resource demands, limiting accessibility for researchers and practitioners.
- Directly applying Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures to LVLMs often results in performance degradation.

## Method

- **MoE-LLaVA** introduces a ==sparse LVLM== architecture that replaces traditional ==Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs)== in the LLM backbone with ==Mixture of Experts (MoE)== layers, using a learnable ==router== with ==Top-k activation==.
- A novel three-stage ==MoE-Tuning== strategy is developed: ==Visual Token Adaptation==, ==General Multi-modal Understanding Pre-empowerment== (on dense model), and ==MoE Layer Training and Sparsification==.
- The training objective combines standard ==auto-regressive loss== with an auxiliary ==load balancing loss== to ensure balanced workload distribution across experts.

## Results

- MoE-LLaVA-Phi-2.7B×4 (**3.6B** activated parameters) surpasses LLaVA-1.5-7B (**6.7B** total parameters) on ScienceQA-IMG by **2.7%**.
- MoE-LLaVA-1.8B×4 (**2.2B** activated parameters) outperforms LLaVA-1.5-13B (**13B** total parameters) in hallucination mitigation on POPE by up to **1.5%** across subsets.
- The model achieves competitive or superior performance across various zero-shot image question answering and general multi-modal benchmarks (e.g., VQA-v2, GQA, MMBench) with significantly fewer activated parameters compared to dense models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The three-stage MoE-Tuning strategy is crucial for successfully applying sparsity to LVLMs without performance degradation, especially the pre-empowerment stage.
> - MoE-LLaVA effectively mitigates object hallucination, surpassing larger dense models in consistency on the POPE benchmark.
> - Experts within the MoE layers demonstrate learned specialization across layers and handle both text and image modalities simultaneously without clear preference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress, with models like LLaVA and MiniGPT-4 demonstrating impressive capabilities by integrating image encoders with powerful Large Language Models. A prevailing trend in this domain, mirroring the advancements in LLMs, has been the scaling of model size and dataset scale to achieve superior performance. Models reaching tens to even hundreds of billions of parameters (e.g., IDEFICS with 80B, InternVL extending image encoders to 6B) have showcased state-of-the-art results across various visual understanding and generation tasks.

However, this relentless pursuit of scale comes with a significant challenge: the immense computational cost associated with training and deploying such "dense" models. In dense models, all parameters are active for every token processed, leading to high resource demands. This limitation restricts accessibility for many researchers and practitioners, hindering broader innovation and deployment.

This paper addresses this critical challenge by bridging the gap between efficient sparse models, specifically Mixture of Experts (MoE), and the burgeoning field of LVLMs. MoE architectures, which conditionally activate only a subset of parameters (experts) for each input token, have already demonstrated their efficacy in Large Language Models (e.g., Mistral's Mixtral-MoE-8x7B achieves performance comparable to LLaMA 2-70B with fewer computational resources).

Prior works in multi-modal learning have explored MoE, but often with "hard routers" pre-defining experts for specific modalities or tasks, or "soft routers" in smaller-scale models. The direct application of MoE to LVLMs has been challenging due to performance degradation observed when simultaneously converting LLMs to LVLMs and sparsifying them. MoE-LLaVA directly tackles this by proposing a novel training strategy and architecture that enables effective application of soft-routed MoE to LVLMs. By doing so, it pioneers a baseline for sparse LVLMs, aiming to provide a pathway for scaling model capacity without proportionally increasing computational costs, thus making powerful multi-modal AI more efficient and accessible.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the MoE-LLaVA research are driven by the recognition of the inherent trade-off between model performance and computational cost in scaling Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs).

The primary objectives can be summarized as:

1. **Develop an effective training strategy for sparse LVLMs:** A key motivation is to overcome the performance degradation commonly observed when attempting to apply sparsity (specifically Mixture of Experts) directly to multi-modal learning. The authors aim to devise a robust training methodology that can successfully integrate MoE into LVLMs without compromising their multi-modal understanding capabilities.
2. **Propose a MoE-based sparse LVLM architecture:** The goal is to design an LVLM framework that leverages the Mixture of Experts paradigm to significantly expand the total number of parameters while maintaining a constant, or at least substantially reduced, computational cost during inference. This involves integrating learnable routers to dynamically activate a subset of experts for each input token.
3. **Establish a baseline for sparse LVLMs:** By presenting a functional and performant MoE-based LVLM, the authors intend to set a foundational benchmark for future research in efficient multi-modal learning systems. This baseline aims to demonstrate the viability and advantages of sparse LVLMs.
4. **Achieve comparable or superior performance to dense models with fewer activated parameters:** The ultimate motivation is to show that a sparse LVLM can match or even surpass the performance of larger, dense LVLMs on various visual understanding and hallucination benchmarks, but with a significantly lower computational footprint. This would validate MoE as a key enabler for efficient scaling in multi-modal AI.

In essence, the authors are motivated to push the boundaries of LVLM scalability by making them computationally more efficient, thereby democratizing access to powerful multi-modal AI and fostering further advancements in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MoE-LLaVA framework introduces a novel three-stage training strategy called **MoE-Tuning** and a sparse LVLM architecture that incorporates Mixture of Experts (MoE) layers.

#### 3.1. Architecture of MoE-LLaVA

The MoE-LLaVA architecture builds upon existing LVLM paradigms, combining a vision encoder, a visual projection layer, and a large language model backbone, but critically replaces traditional Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) in the LLM with MoE layers.

- **Vision Encoder:** A frozen CLIP-Large (Radford et al., 2021) is used as the vision encoder to extract visual features from input images.
- **Visual Projection Layer (MLP):** A two-layer MLP with GELU activation connects the visual tokens from the vision encoder to the hidden dimension of the LLM, effectively translating visual information into a format comprehensible by the language model.
- **Word Embedding Layer:** Standard word embedding is used for text inputs.
- **LLM Backbone with MoE Layers:** The core of MoE-LLaVA is a large language model (e.g., StableLM, Qwen, Phi-2, OpenChat) where a subset of its Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layers are replaced by MoE layers. In the primary configuration, MoE layers are applied alternately with dense layers, meaning half of the total layers are MoE layers.
- **MoE Layer Structure:** Each MoE layer consists of:
 - **Multiple FFNs (Experts):** An ensemble of `E` expert FFNs.
 - **Router:** A lightweight linear layer that predicts the probability of each input token being assigned to each expert using a softmax function.
 - **Top-k Activation:** For each token, the router selects and activates only the top-k experts with the highest probabilities. The outputs from these activated experts are then combined via a weighted sum, where weights are derived from the router's softmax probabilities. The remaining experts remain inactive, ensuring computational efficiency. The paper's main model configuration uses `k=2` activated experts out of `E=4` total experts.

#### 3.2. MoE-Tuning Training Strategy

The paper highlights that direct application of MoE to LVLM training leads to performance degradation. To counter this, MoE-Tuning employs a carefully designed three-stage strategy:

- **Stage I: Visual Token Adaptation:**
 - **Objective:** To enable the LLM to understand visual inputs by aligning image tokens with the LLM's input domain.
 - **Process:** Only the visual projection layer (MLP) is trained. The LLM and vision encoder remain frozen. The model learns to describe images, effectively treating image patches as pseudo-text tokens. MoE layers are not involved in this stage.
 - **Data:** LLaVA 1.5-558k pretraining data.

- **Stage II: General Multi-modal Understanding Pre-empowerment:**
 - **Objective:** To equip the model with robust general multi-modal understanding capabilities before introducing sparsity.
 - **Process:** All parameters of the LLM (which is still a dense model at this stage, without MoE layers) are trained, while the Vision Encoder remains frozen. This stage uses more complex instruction-tuning data to enhance logical reasoning and text recognition abilities.
 - **Data:** A larger, collected dataset (Hybrid-FT) including SViT-157k, LVIS-220k, LRV-331k, and MIMIC-IT-256k.
 - **Significance:** This stage provides a strong, pre-trained dense LVLM as a foundation, crucial for stable initialization of the sparse model in the next stage.

- **Stage III: MoE Layer Training and Sparsification:**
 - **Objective:** To gradually transition the pre-empowered dense LVLM into a sparse MoE-based LVLM.
 - **Process:** The weights of the FFN layers from the Stage II model are replicated multiple times to initialize the experts within the new MoE layers. Only the MoE layers (specifically, their FFN experts and router) are trained in this stage, while other parts like the vision encoder, visual projection, and other LLM layers are frozen or only their FFNs are tuned (as per ablation studies). The router learns to assign tokens to the top-k experts, creating sparse activation pathways.
 - **Data:** LLaVA 1.5-mix-665k, similar to the final instruction-tuning phase of LLaVA.

#### 3.3. Training Objectives

The total training loss ($L_{total}$) for MoE-LLaVA combines two components:

- **Auto-Regressive Loss ($L_{regressive}$):** This is the standard causal language modeling loss, optimizing the LLM to generate the correct output sequence given the concatenated visual and text tokens. The loss is only calculated for the generated text part.
- **Auxiliary Loss ($L_{aux}$):** To ensure a balanced workload distribution across experts and prevent certain experts from becoming overloaded or underutilized, a differentiable load balancing loss (from Fedus et al., 2022) is applied to each MoE layer. This loss encourages experts to handle tokens in a balanced manner, weighted by a balancing coefficient $\alpha$ (set to 0.01).

This meticulous three-stage training strategy and the specific MoE architecture design are key to MoE-LLaVA's ability to overcome the challenges of integrating sparsity into multi-modal models, allowing it to scale capacity efficiently.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on MoE-LLaVA demonstrate its significant performance and efficiency advantages, particularly in comparison to dense LVLMs.

#### 4.1. Image Understanding Evaluation

- **Zero-shot Image Question Answering (Table 3):** MoE-LLaVA consistently shows powerful image understanding capabilities across various benchmarks (VQA-v2, GQA, VisWiz, ScienceQA-IMG, TextVQA).
 - **Efficiency:** MoE-LLaVA-Phi-2.7B×4 (3.6B activated parameters) surpasses LLaVA-1.5-7B (6.7B activated parameters) on ScienceQA-IMG by 2.7%.
 - **Scalability for smaller models:** MoE-LLaVA-StableLM-1.6B×4 (2.0B activated parameters) outperforms IDEFICS-80B (65B activated parameters) across multiple benchmarks, highlighting the efficiency gains of sparsity even with much smaller activated parameter counts.
 - **Comparison to other small LVLMs:** MoE-LLaVA-Phi-2.7B×4 outperforms LLaVA-Phi (2.7B dense) by more than 6.2% on VQA-v2, demonstrating strong comprehension.
- **Evaluation under Benchmark Toolkits (Table 3):** MoE-LLaVA performs competitively or even superiorly on open-ended answer benchmarks (POPE, MME, MMBench, LLaVA-Bench, MM-Vet).
 - **Example:** MoE-LLaVA-Qwen-1.8B×4 outperforms Qwen-VL-7B by 21.5% on MMBench, despite Qwen-VL using a higher image resolution.
 - **Overall:** These results consistently indicate that MoE-LLaVA achieves comparable or superior performance to dense models while activating significantly fewer parameters.

#### 4.2. Object Hallucination Evaluation

- **POPE Benchmark (Table 4):** MoE-LLaVA exhibits superior performance in mitigating object hallucination, generating responses more consistent with the given image.
 - **Key achievement:** MoE-LLaVA-1.8B×4 (2.2B activated parameters) surpasses LLaVA-1.5-13B (13B activated parameters) by 1.0%, 1.5%, and 0.8% across adversarial, popular, and random sampling subsets of POPE, respectively.
 - **Consistency:** The balanced "yes ratio" for MoE-LLaVA indicates its ability to provide accurate feedback.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Analysis (Routing Distributions & Token Pathways)

- **Expert Load Balancing (Figure 4):** The auxiliary loss effectively balances the expert loads in MoE layers. However, deeper analysis reveals specific patterns:
 - **Task Specialization:** Experts learn to divide tasks in a specific manner across layers. For instance, Expert 3's load increases significantly in deeper layers (17-27), sometimes dominating the workload. Expert 1 is more active in initial layers. This suggests learned specialization among experts.
- **Modality Preferences (Figure 5):** Experts in MoE-LLaVA do not show a clear preference for either text or image modalities, handling both simultaneously. This indicates strong multimodal interaction within the sparse architecture.
- **Token Pathways (Figure 6):** Analyzing activated pathways shows consistent patterns for unseen tokens. Experts 2 and 3 are consistently assigned to handle tokens in deeper layers, while Experts 1 and 4 are more involved in the initialization phase.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Tables 5, 6, 7)

- **Effect of Training Strategy (Table 6):** The three-stage MoE-Tuning strategy is crucial. Directly applying MoE (variant a) or using insufficient instruction data (variant c) leads to significant performance degradation. Variant (b), which pre-empowers the LVLM in Stage II before sparsification in Stage III, significantly outperforms others, validating the rationale behind the multi-stage approach.
- **Effect of Tuning Parameters (Table 5a):** Fine-tuning only the FFN layers (and MoE layers) is sufficient to achieve comparable performance to full-parameter tuning, but with approximately 75% less training time, significantly reducing costs.
- **Effect of Number of Experts (Table 5b):** Increasing the number of total experts while keeping activated experts constant (e.g., 4 experts vs. 2 experts for Top-2 activation) shows that more sparse experts can deliver superior performance.
- **Effect of Number of Activated Experts (Table 5c):** Activating more experts (e.g., Top-2 vs. Top-1) significantly improves performance, justifying the choice of Top-2 activation.
- **Effect of Architecture (Table 5d):** An "Interval" architecture (alternating MoE and dense layers) performs best compared to placing MoE layers only in the first/second half or in all layers. "All" MoE layers do not yield better results and increase training time.
- **Effect of Model Size (Table 7 & B.1):** For smaller base models (StableLM, Qwen, Phi-2), applying MoE improves performance. However, for larger base models like OpenChat-7B, MoE-LLaVA's performance is surprisingly inferior to dense models, possibly due to insufficient multimodal instruction-tuning data for effective sparsification at that scale.

In summary, the main findings highlight that MoE-LLaVA successfully applies the Mixture of Experts paradigm to LVLMs, achieving competitive performance with significantly fewer activated parameters, excelling particularly in hallucination mitigation, and providing valuable insights into expert behavior and token routing in multi-modal sparse models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "MoE-LLaVA: Mixture of Experts for Large Vision-Language Models" carries substantial significance and potential impact on the development and deployment of advanced AI systems:

#### 5.1. Technical Significance

- **Pioneering Sparse LVLMs:** MoE-LLaVA is one of the first works to successfully adapt the Mixture of Experts architecture to Large Vision-Language Models, establishing a crucial baseline for efficient scaling in this domain. This extends the benefits of conditional computation, previously proven in LLMs, to multi-modal contexts.
- **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** By enabling LVLMs to achieve comparable or superior performance with only a fraction of their parameters activated, MoE-LLaVA directly tackles the escalating computational costs of state-of-the-art dense LVLMs. This is vital for practical deployment and for enabling research with limited computational resources.
- **Novel Training Strategy:** The proposed three-stage MoE-Tuning strategy is a significant methodological contribution. It effectively mitigates the performance degradation often encountered when applying sparsity to multi-modal models, particularly by providing a robust pre-empowerment phase. This strategy offers a blueprint for future sparse multi-modal model training.
- **Enhanced Hallucination Mitigation:** The remarkable performance of MoE-LLaVA in reducing object hallucination, even surpassing larger dense models, is a critical step towards more reliable and trustworthy multi-modal AI. This improvement suggests that expert specialization might lead to more grounded visual reasoning.
- **Insights into Expert Behavior:** The quantitative analysis of routing distributions and token pathways provides valuable mechanistic insights into how MoE layers function within a multi-modal context. Understanding expert specialization and modality preference helps in designing more effective sparse architectures.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Democratization of LVLMs:** Reduced computational requirements mean that powerful LVLMs become more accessible to a wider range of researchers, small labs, and industries with fewer resources. This can accelerate innovation and foster a more diverse research landscape.
- **Efficient Deployment:** For real-world applications, lower inference costs and smaller memory footprints make MoE-LLaVA-like models more suitable for deployment on edge devices or in resource-constrained environments, opening new possibilities for on-device AI.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** MoE-LLaVA sets a strong baseline and provides valuable insights for future research into efficient multi-modal learning. This includes exploring more sophisticated routing mechanisms, optimizing expert specialization, and applying MoE to even larger multi-modal foundational models.
- **Expansion to New Modalities and Tasks:** The paper suggests that the expert-based architecture could easily be expanded to handle additional tasks (detection, segmentation, generation) or modalities (video, depth, thermal), indicating a versatile and extensible framework for general-purpose multi-modal AI.
- **Sustainability in AI:** As models grow, their energy consumption becomes a concern. Efficient architectures like MoE-LLaVA contribute to more sustainable AI development by reducing the energy required for training and inference.

#### 5.3. Broader Societal Implications (as per Impact Statement)

The authors responsibly acknowledge potential broader impacts, common to large language and vision models:

- **Information Credibility:** Like other generative models, MoE-LLaVA can produce realistic but false or misleading content, necessitating careful deployment and ethical considerations.
- **Bias and Discrimination:** Training on internet data means the model can learn and amplify existing societal biases and discriminatory patterns, requiring ongoing efforts in data curation and bias mitigation.
- **Social Influence and Dependence:** Over-reliance on such powerful AI systems for information and problem-solving could diminish critical thinking and the pursuit of diverse information sources among users.

In conclusion, MoE-LLaVA represents a significant stride towards building more efficient, performant, and accessible Large Vision-Language Models. By demonstrating the efficacy of the Mixture of Experts paradigm in a multi-modal setting, it paves the way for future advancements in scalable and responsible AI.
