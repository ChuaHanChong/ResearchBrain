# From Human Videos to Robot Manipulation: A Survey on Scalable Vision-Language-Action Learning with Human-Centric Data

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey unifying how internet-scale human videos become training signal for Vision-Language-Action models, organizing methods into four representation-bridge paradigms, latent action abstraction, predictive world modeling, explicit 2D cues, and explicit 3D structure, then mapping datasets and distilling three unresolved bottlenecks in the human-video-to-robot pipeline.

## Problem

- Robot demonstration data is costly, safety-constrained, and tightly coupled to a specific embodiment, leaving robot-only datasets under-representing long-horizon structure and rare failure modes.
- Human videos lack aligned robot-executable action labels and proprioceptive context, and human motion does not directly map onto robot kinematics or control interfaces.
- Prior human-video methods for robotics were scattered across auxiliary uses like reward shaping, affordance extraction, or representation learning, without a unified account of how signals become action.

## Method

- Introduces a signal-centric taxonomy of four representation-bridge paradigms, ==latent action abstraction==, ==predictive world modeling==, ==explicit 2D cues==, and ==explicit 3D structure==, comparing their scalability and grounding requirements.
- Builds a dataset map classifying human-video corpora along two axes, presence of explicit 3D/geometric action labels and scripted versus in-the-wild collection, linking supervision type to transfer pipeline.
- Distills three open challenges at critical pipeline interfaces, episodizing unstructured web video, heterogeneity-aware grounding under embodiment and viewpoint mismatch, and deployment-predictive evaluation protocol design.

## Results

- Table 1 spans **24** methods across four paradigms with data scale ranging from **0.3k** clips (ATM) to over **270 million** clips (Gen2Act) and **136 million** clips (HowTo100M-based GR-2).
- Table 2 classifies **17** human-video datasets by geometric-signal availability and collection protocol; only **8** provide explicit 3D action labels versus **9** offering RGB-only unscripted supervision like Ego4D's **3,600** hours.
- Across the surveyed methods, most end-effector evaluations target simple grippers; only **five** works, GO-1, GR00T N1, villa-x, EgoVLA, and Being-H0, extend to dexterous multi-finger hands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit representations (latent actions, world models) scale flexibly across unlabeled internet video, while explicit 2D/3D cues trade that scalability for stronger, coordinate-grounded action supervision.
> - Representation choice alone does not solve transfer; episodization, embodiment/viewpoint heterogeneity, and evaluation design remain bottlenecks shared across all four paradigms.
> - Standard sim benchmarks under-represent internet-video diversity and long-horizon structure, so reported gains may not reflect true transfer efficiency under a fixed robot-data budget.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This survey is situated within the rapidly evolving field of embodied AI, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot manipulation. The broader landscape is characterized by a drive towards increasingly generalizable robotic policies, analogous to the "foundation model" paradigm seen in language and vision. This progress is largely dependent on large-scale pretraining of VLA models.

However, a significant bottleneck in this area is the scarcity of high-quality, grounded robot control data. Existing robot demonstrations, while providing direct control signals, are expensive to acquire, limited by specific embodiments, sensor suites, and control frequencies. These datasets often under-represent long-horizon task structures, rare failure modes, and the broad distribution shifts in objects and environments encountered in real-world deployment.

In response to this data scarcity, the research community has increasingly turned to human-centric data, particularly human videos, as a scalable and complementary data source. Human videos are abundant and capture rich semantic and physical interactions relevant to manipulation. This survey positions itself as a critical analysis of how this vast resource can be effectively transformed into actionable knowledge for VLA models, thereby addressing the current limitations of robot-only datasets and accelerating the development of more robust and generalizable robotic policies. It bridges the gap between the abundance of human interaction data and the specific requirements for robot learning.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is to address the significant data bottleneck hindering the scalability and generalization of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in embodied robotics. Current VLA approaches heavily rely on large collections of robot demonstrations, which are inherently costly, difficult to scale, constrained by safety protocols, and tightly coupled to specific robotic embodiments and control interfaces. This dependency limits the diversity of tasks, environments, and interaction patterns that robots can learn, thereby impacting their robustness and generalization capabilities in real-world scenarios.

To mitigate this limitation, the authors identify human videos as a compelling alternative data source. Human videos are available at a vastly larger scale, ranging from instructional videos (e.g., HowTo100M) to diverse egocentric recordings (e.g., Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS). These videos encapsulate rich semantic and physical cues related to real-world manipulation, including object interactions, procedural assemblies, and common-sense physics.

However, the direct utilization of human videos for robot control presents several challenges:
1. **Embodiment Differences:** Human kinematics and dynamics do not directly map to robot kinematics and control interfaces.
2. **Lack of Robot-Executable Actions:** Human videos typically lack explicit, task-aligned, robot-executable action labels and proprioceptive context.
3. **Unstructured Nature:** Many human videos are unstructured, making it difficult to extract discrete, task-relevant episodes suitable for policy learning.

Given these challenges, the central objective of this survey is to provide a unified and systematic understanding of how human videos are processed and transformed into effective knowledge for VLA models. The paper seeks to answer the fundamental question: "when human videos enter a VLA training pipeline, what type of information do they become, and how does that information interface with policy learning?"

To achieve this, the survey outlines three specific contributions:
1. **Signal-centric taxonomy:** To categorize existing approaches based on the intermediate representation bridges they employ to convert human videos into action-relevant signals for VLA learning.
2. **Dataset map and available signals:** To systematize representative human-video datasets along axes of geometric/3D signal availability and collection methodology (scripted vs. in-the-wild), and to summarize how different supervision affordances support various transfer pipelines.
3. **Challenges at three interfaces:** To distill and articulate the key open problems encountered in the human-video-to-robot pipeline, specifically focusing on scalable episodization, heterogeneity-aware grounding, and deployment-predictive evaluation protocols.

By addressing these objectives, the survey aims to clarify the current landscape, identify promising directions, and highlight critical challenges for future research in scalable vision-language-action learning from human-centric data.

### 3. Methodology and approach

As a survey paper, the methodology primarily involves a systematic review, categorization, and analysis of existing research in the field of scalable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) learning using human-centric data, specifically human videos. The authors develop a structured framework to organize and evaluate these diverse approaches.

The core of the methodology is the development of a **four-pronged taxonomy of "representation bridges"** (illustrated in Figure 1 and detailed in Section 3). This taxonomy categorizes methods based on the nature of the intermediate information derived from human videos to bridge the gap between human observation and robot control. The four categories are:

1. **Latent Action Abstraction:**
 - **Approach:** Methods in this category learn compact, self-supervised action representations by encoding inter-frame changes or intents into discrete codes or continuous embeddings. This bypasses the need for explicit action annotations.
 - **Mechanism:** Typically involves taking observations from two time steps (o_t and o_{t+H}) and inferring a latent vector (z_t) that can reconstruct the future observation. Information bottlenecks (e.g., VQ-VAE) are often used to force z_t to capture only action-relevant dynamics.
 - **Example techniques:** Asymmetric cropping augmentations, DINOv2 features for pixel-space supervision, optical flow consistency loss, joint learning of human and robot latent action spaces, contrastive learning, sparsity constraints.
 - **Integration:** Latent action prediction is integrated into VLA training through autoregressive pretraining, hierarchical designs with latent planners, or as pseudo-targets for action decoding.

2. **Predictive World Modeling:**
 - **Approach:** These methods focus on predicting how the environment will evolve over time, leveraging human videos as a source of predictive learning signals. The assumption is that accurately forecasting future dynamics equips the model with action-relevant information.
 - **Mechanism:** Formulated as predicting future environment states (e.g., raw pixels or visual features) conditioned on past observations and language instructions. This is often achieved via video generation models that learn to autoregressively predict future frames.
 - **Example techniques:** GPT-style world models for future frame prediction, VQGAN-based visual tokens, representation alignment between decoder's internal features and future visual features, using pretrained video generation models as frozen feature extractors, leveraging intermediate representations from video diffusion models.
 - **Integration:** Learned knowledge from world models is transferred to VLA action prediction by fine-tuning with action prediction objectives or by using generated video plans as conditioning signals for policies.

3. **Explicit 2D Representations:**
 - **Approach:** This category extracts interpretable 2D visual primitives (e.g., keypoints, bounding boxes, point trajectories) from human videos using off-the-shelf 2D vision tools.
 - **Mechanism:** The extracted 2D cues serve as explicit, image-plane supervision to facilitate the acquisition of action-relevant knowledge.
 - **Example techniques:** Dense point trajectories as action-like supervision, rendering trajectories as "Trace-of-Mark" visual prompts, annotation of diverse 2D primitives (boxes, keypoints, tracks), modeling object-centric contact points and post-contact trajectories, editing human videos to resemble robot demonstrations for 2D robot keypoint supervision.

4. **Explicit 3D Representations:**
 - **Approach:** Methods in this category leverage 3D information (e.g., 3D hand poses, object poses, joint angles, SE(3) trajectories) extracted from human videos.
 - **Mechanism:** 3D representations provide a coordinate-consistent interface, reducing viewpoint dependence and depth ambiguity, aligning more naturally with VLA action prediction. Parametric hand models (e.g., MANO) are often used as a canonical 3D representation.
 - **Example techniques:** Utilizing videos with high-quality 3D hand action annotations (from multi-view optimization or RGB-D SLAM), unifying human and robot action prediction via wrist poses and fingertip positions, inverse kinematics for robot execution, discretizing continuous MANO hand trajectories into motion tokens, combining structure-from-motion with depth foundation models, automated pipelines for converting unscripted videos to robot-aligned action segments with 3D annotations.
 - **Data sources:** Controlled settings (AR/VR headsets, depth sensors, motion capture) or recovering 3D information from in-the-wild videos.

Beyond this taxonomy, the methodology also includes:
- **Dataset systematization:** Organizing human-video datasets along two dimensions: the availability of explicit geometric/3D signals and whether the collection was scripted or in-the-wild (Table 2). This provides context on the types of data that support different representation bridges.
- **Identification of open challenges:** Distilling critical interfaces in the human-video-to-robot pipeline where significant problems persist. These challenges are categorized into transforming unstructured videos to episodes, addressing heterogeneity (embodiment and viewpoint mismatch), and designing effective evaluation protocols.

The authors use representative papers for each category, detailing their human video data sources, data scale, representations, and the types of robotic end-effectors used for evaluation (Table 1), providing a comprehensive overview of the current state of the art.

### 4. Main findings and results

The survey's main findings and results are structured around its proposed taxonomy of representation bridges, its analysis of human-video datasets, and the identified open challenges.

#### 4.1. Taxonomy of Representation Bridges (Categorization of Methods)

The survey identifies four distinct paradigms for transforming human videos into action-relevant knowledge for VLA models:

1. **Latent Actions:**
 - **Core Idea:** Deriving compact, self-supervised action representations from videos by encoding inter-frame changes into discrete codes or continuous embeddings. This approach circumvents the need for explicit action labels.
 - **Key Characteristics:** Emphasizes learning a compact, expressive representation of action-relevant dynamics that generalizes across embodiments. Examples include methods using VQ-VAE style discretization (e.g., LAPA, IGOR, UniVLA), optical flow consistency (ViPRA, Motus), or integrating robot data to align latent spaces (villa-X, CLAP). Some works focus on integrating latent action prediction into VLA training through autoregressive schemes (Moto, GO-1) or as pseudo-targets (GR00T N1).
 - **Limitations:** An open question remains whether highly compressed latent tokens can adequately represent the rich structure of complex, high-DoF dexterous manipulation.

2. **World Models:**
 - **Core Idea:** Predicting how the environment evolves over time based on human interactions, assuming that forecasting these dynamics provides action-relevant information.
 - **Key Characteristics:** Learned via video generation without explicit action supervision. Methods include GPT-style world models that autoregressively predict future frames (GR-1, GR-2), leveraging predictive visual representations (VPP, FLARE), or using intermediate representations from video diffusion models (Mimic-Video). Some approaches use video generation as an explicit planning interface (Gen2Act).
 - **Limitations:** Often incur substantial training overhead due to generating large numbers of visual tokens. Effectively distilling and transferring the knowledge from world models to action prediction is an under-explored challenge.

3. **Explicit 2D Representations:**
 - **Core Idea:** Relying on explicit and interpretable 2D visual primitives (e.g., keypoints, bounding boxes, point trajectories) extracted from videos, often using off-the-shelf vision tools.
 - **Key Characteristics:** These 2D cues serve as direct, image-plane supervision to bridge perception and action. Examples include using dense point trajectories (ATM, Magma), diverse 2D primitives for embodied reasoning (Gemini Robotics), object-centric contact points (A0), or editing human videos to provide 2D robot keypoint supervision (Masquerade).
 - **Limitations:** May inherit label noise from underlying vision models and often require additional filtering. Fundamentally limited in expressing complex manipulation due to lack of depth information and occlusion reasoning, as real-world interactions are 3D.

4. **Explicit 3D Representations:**
 - **Core Idea:** Leveraging 3D information (e.g., 3D hand poses, object poses, SE(3) trajectories) extracted from human videos to train VLA policies.
 - **Key Characteristics:** Provides a coordinate-consistent interface, reducing viewpoint dependence and depth ambiguity, and aligns more naturally with VLA action prediction. Parametric hand models (e.g., MANO) are commonly used. Approaches include using videos with high-quality 3D hand annotations (EgoVLA, H-RDT, In-N-On, Being-H0), recovering 3D information from in-the-wild videos (VidBot, VITRA, Yoshida et al.), or co-training with robot data (MotionTrans, Kareer et al.).
 - **Limitations:** Acquiring accurate 3D labels is difficult, especially for web-scale, in-the-wild data, often leading to larger errors than 2D supervision. Directly predicting actions in 3D space requires inferring underlying 3D structure from 2D projections, and its generalization effectiveness compared to 2D prediction remains an open question.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Human-Video Datasets

The survey categorizes human-video datasets into two main types (Table 2):

1. **Datasets without Explicit 3D Action Labels:**
 - **Characteristics:** Provide RGB videos with semantic annotations but lack metric 3D trajectories of hands or bodies.
 - **Usage:** Primarily support latent-action or world-model-based approaches where actions are inferred from visual state transitions.
 - **Examples:** Something-Something V2, EPIC-KITCHENS, Ego4D, HowTo100M, EgoExo4D.
 - **Collection:** Range from scripted (SSv2) to unscripted in-the-wild (Ego4D). Unscripted data often lacks predefined boundaries, complicating action segmentation.

2. **Datasets with Explicit 3D Action Labels:**
 - **Characteristics:** Offer explicit metric 3D action annotations (e.g., 3D keypoints, MANO parameters), typically captured with instrumented setups (AR/VR, depth sensors).
 - **Usage:** Enable direct supervision with explicit 2D/3D action labels, closely aligning with robot imitation learning.
 - **Examples:** HOI4D, HOT3D, EgoDex, ARCTIC, TACO, Assembly101, OakInk2, HoloAssist.
 - **Collection:** Usually smaller-scale, collected in controlled or laboratory settings, often scripted with predefined interactions, trading diversity for annotation fidelity.

#### 4.3. Open Challenges

The survey identifies three critical interfaces in the human-video-to-robot pipeline where significant challenges persist:

1. **Transforming Human Videos to Episodes:**
 - **Problem:** Web-scale human videos are rarely structured into episodes matching robot learning requirements, leading to weak alignment, ambiguous signals, and control-irrelevant dynamics.
 - **Need:** Semantic- and interaction-driven segmentation around manipulation phases and object state changes, along with standardized construction protocols.

2. **Addressing Heterogeneity:**
 - **Embodiment Mismatch:**
 - **Problem:** Kinematic and contact-mechanics differences between humans and robots, leading to under-constrained retargeting and infeasible motions.
 - **Need:** Emphasis on embodiment-invariant supervision (e.g., interaction intent, object-centric effects) and morphology-conditioned grounding mechanisms.
 - **Viewpoint Heterogeneity:**
 - **Problem:** Mismatch between predominantly exocentric or rapidly shifting egocentric human views and fixed, robot-centric views, causing weak observation of contacts and frequent occlusions.
 - **Need:** Explicit cross-view alignment and action representations anchored to interaction outcomes rather than view-specific appearance.

3. **Benchmarking and Evaluation:**
 - **Problem:** Existing benchmarks do not fully reflect the diversity, long-horizon structure, or transfer efficiency relevant to human video supervision, leading to benchmark-specific improvements that don't predict real-world deployment.
 - **Need:** Evaluation protocols that explicitly track deployment-relevant generalization and transfer efficiency, including performance vs. robot-data budget, held-out splits for novel objects/scenes, robustness under viewpoint/embodiment shifts, and comparison with/without human-video pretraining under matched budgets. Testbeds closer to human activity distributions (BEHAVIOR-1K) and scalable digital-twin tools are suggested.

These findings collectively provide a comprehensive overview of the current methods, data landscape, and critical limitations in leveraging human videos for generalizable robot manipulation.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This survey holds significant implications for the field of embodied AI and robot manipulation by systematically addressing a fundamental bottleneck: the scarcity of scalable, grounded robot control data. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

Firstly, the survey's **unified taxonomy of representation bridges** provides a crucial framework for understanding and classifying the diverse approaches currently employed to translate human video knowledge into robot-executable actions. By categorizing methods into latent actions, predictive world models, explicit 2D cues, and explicit 3D structures, it clarifies the underlying mechanisms and trade-offs. This systematization aids researchers in navigating the complex landscape, identifying the strengths and weaknesses of different approaches, and fostering cross-pollination of ideas across paradigms. For instance, it highlights that implicit representations offer scalability with diverse, unstructured video, while explicit geometric cues provide stronger grounding and a clearer interface to robot actions.

Secondly, the **detailed analysis and categorization of human-video datasets** along dimensions of 3D action labels and collection methodology (scripted vs. in-the-wild) serve as a valuable resource. It informs researchers about the types of data suitable for different transfer pipelines and the inherent challenges associated with each, such as the trade-off between diversity and annotation fidelity. This insight can guide data collection efforts and the development of new dataset-agnostic methodologies.

Thirdly, the articulation of **three key open challenges** (episodization of unstructured videos, heterogeneity-aware grounding, and deployment-predictive evaluation) is critical for directing future research. These challenges represent significant hurdles to the widespread adoption and reliability of VLA models pre-trained on human videos:
- **Scalable Episodization:** Addressing the unstructured nature of web-scale videos is essential for generating clean, task-aligned training data, which could dramatically improve learning signals and model stability.
- **Heterogeneity-aware Grounding:** Overcoming the embodiment and viewpoint mismatches is crucial for ensuring that knowledge transferred from humans is genuinely applicable and feasible for robots across diverse morphologies and sensing configurations. Progress here could lead to more robust and adaptable robot policies.
- **Deployment-Predictive Evaluation:** Developing more rigorous and representative benchmarks is paramount for accurately assessing the real-world performance and transfer efficiency of VLA models. This would enable a more objective comparison of methods and accelerate the development of truly generalizable robotic systems.

By illuminating these challenges, the survey acts as a roadmap for future investigations, encouraging the development of principled solutions that move beyond incremental improvements. Ultimately, the successful integration of knowledge from abundant human videos into robot learning pipelines, as outlined and analyzed by this survey, has the potential to accelerate the development of more intelligent, versatile, and general-purpose robots that can operate reliably in complex, unstructured real-world environments, thereby significantly impacting fields such as manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and personal assistance.
