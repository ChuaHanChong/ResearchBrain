# Maximal Brain Damage Without Data or Optimization: Disrupting Neural Networks via Sign-Bit Flips

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NVIDIA, Technion, and IBM Research developed Deep Neural Lesion (DNL) and 1P-DNL, data-free and optimization-free methods that catastrophically disrupt Deep Neural Networks. Their approach flips a small number of carefully selected sign or exponent bits, causing most image classification models to collapse with fewer than 10 flips and reducing ResNet-50 accuracy by 99.8% with two sign flips.

## Problem

- Existing weight-space adversarial attacks on Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) often require extensive computational resources, such as iterative optimization or access to training/validation data.
- It was unclear if catastrophic DNN disruption was possible with minimal attacker resources and data access, limiting the understanding of fundamental vulnerabilities.
- A gap existed in methods demonstrating severe DNN degradation with a low computational footprint and increased stealth for real-world scenarios.

## Method

- Developed **DNL** (Deep Neural Lesion), a ==data-free and optimization-free== method using heuristics like ==magnitude-based selection==, ==early-layer targeting==, and a ==one-flip-per-kernel constraint== for CNNs to identify critical parameter sign bits.
- Introduced **1P-DNL**, an enhanced variant allowing a single forward and backward pass on a random input to compute a ==hybrid importance score== for more potent parameter selection.
- Targeted the ==sign bit (Most Significant Bit)== of IEEE 754 32-bit floating-point parameters, and for LLMs, also explored ==exponent-bit flips==, to induce drastic and disruptive changes.

## Results

- DNL and 1P-DNL caused catastrophic failure in **43 out of 48** ImageNet models with **10 sign flips**, with ResNet-50 accuracy dropping by **99.8%** with just **two sign flips**.
- Object detection and instance segmentation models (Mask R-CNN, YOLOv8-seg) experienced near-complete collapse (**AP ≈ 0.00-0.01**) with **one to two sign flips** in their backbones.
- Large Language Models (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-30B-A3B, Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Nano-8B) and Text Encoders (BERT, DistilBERT, RoBERTa) achieved near-total accuracy reduction (often **0%**) with a few targeted sign or exponent flips.
- The proposed methods circumvented existing defenses like binarization and weight-scaling, while selective protection of **0.001%** of parameters halved the impact of iterative bit-flip attacks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep Neural Networks possess 'critical parameters' whose sign-bit flips can cause disproportionate and catastrophic model degradation, even when other random bit flips have minimal impact.
> - The vulnerability is cross-domain, affecting image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and large language models, indicating a fundamental architectural fragility.
> - Targeting early-layer parameters and distributing sign flips across different kernels (in CNNs) amplifies damage, suggesting specific structural sensitivities.
> - Selective protection of a tiny fraction of critical parameters (e.g., 0.001% to 1%) can significantly mitigate or nullify bit-flip attacks, offering a practical defense strategy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) are integral to various critical systems, raising concerns about their security and resilience to adversarial manipulations. This work addresses the vulnerability of DNNs to parameter-level attacks, specifically bit-flips, which can be induced through hardware or software exploits.

Prior research on adversarial attacks against DNNs largely falls into two categories: input-space attacks and weight-space attacks. Input-space attacks, such as those by Madry et al. (2018) or Goodfellow et al. (2015), involve perturbing input data (e.g., pixels in an image) to mislead the model. These often require real-time computation or physical access to the environment. Weight-space attacks, which are more relevant to the current paper, aim to alter the model's internal parameters. Existing weight-space attack methods, such as those by Rakin etal. (2019), Yao et al. (2020), and Park et al. (2021), typically necessitate significant computational resources. These often involve iterative optimization processes, multiple forward and backward passes through the network, and/or access to training or validation data (or synthetic data generated from it). Some methods have attempted to relax data requirements (e.g., Ghavami et al., 2021; Park et al., 2021) but still rely on model feedback or approximate gradients.

This paper introduces a novel approach that differentiates itself by operating under a highly restrictive threat model, characterized by being entirely "data-free" and "optimization-free" (or "one-pass" in its enhanced variant). This means the attacker requires no knowledge of the training data, no access to validation data, and performs no iterative gradient-based searches or extensive inference passes. This minimalist approach addresses a gap in the literature by demonstrating that severe DNN disruption is possible with significantly reduced attacker resources and increased stealth, contrasting with prior methods that have higher computational footprints and are more easily detectable. The findings contribute to understanding fundamental vulnerabilities in DNN architectures themselves, independent of specific data distributions or intensive optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to expose and characterize a cross-domain vulnerability in Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) that allows for catastrophic disruption by flipping only a small number of carefully selected parameter bits. The authors aim to demonstrate that, once an adversary gains write access to stored model parameters, the computational and data requirements for inducing severe model failure can be minimal.

The specific motivations behind this work include:

- **Minimizing Attacker Resources:** Existing weight-space attacks often require extensive computational resources (e.g., numerous forward/backward passes, iterative optimization) and access to training or validation data. This work seeks to determine if a highly effective attack is possible with a "disturbingly small" amount of access and computation, specifically under a data-agnostic and optimization-free threat model. This reduces the attacker’s overhead and increases stealth, making such attacks more practical in real-world scenarios where resources might be limited or detection avoidance is crucial.
- **Identifying Critical Parameters:** The research is motivated by the hypothesis that not all parameters contribute equally to a network's functionality. The authors aim to systematically identify "critical parameters" whose manipulation (specifically, sign-bit flips) can disproportionately degrade model performance, even when the vast majority of parameters remain robust to random perturbations.
- **Cross-Domain Vulnerability:** The widespread deployment of DNNs across diverse applications (image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, large language models) necessitates understanding if such vulnerabilities are architecture- or domain-specific, or if they represent a more fundamental weakness. A key motivation is to demonstrate this vulnerability across multiple domains to highlight its generality.
- **Implications for Safety-Critical Systems:** For applications like autonomous driving, traditional adversarial attacks require continuous input manipulation or physical intervention. A low-cost, stealthy parameter-level attack could pose a more potent and difficult-to-detect threat, motivating a thorough investigation into such vulnerabilities.
- **Developing Countermeasures:** By identifying the characteristics of critical parameters and the mechanisms of attack, the research aims to provide insights that can inform the development of efficient defense mechanisms. This includes demonstrating that selectively protecting a small fraction of vulnerable bits can substantially improve resilience.
- **Challenging Assumptions:** The work implicitly challenges the assumption that DNNs are robust to isolated parameter modifications, particularly when those modifications are precisely targeted, even without data or optimization.

The adversarial objective is formally defined as minimizing *k* (number of bit flips) such that the expected risk *L(f_θ'(k)(X), Y)* is maximized, where *f_θ'(k)* is the model after *k* bit-flips. This involves discrete optimization for both finding minimal *k* and selecting which *k* bits to flip, which necessitates lightweight heuristics given the infeasibility of exhaustive search.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around **Deep Neural Lesion (DNL)**, a data-free and optimization-free method for locating and flipping critical parameter bits, and its enhanced variant, **1P-DNL**.

**Threat Model:**
The attacker has direct write access to the stored model parameters (e.g., via rootkits, firmware exploits, DMA, Rowhammer, GPU cache tampering, voltage/frequency glitching). Crucially, the attacker has:
- No access to training or validation data samples (P(X) or P(Y)).
- No ability to evaluate the model on any input (no forward or backward passes for DNL, with a single forward/backward pass allowed for 1P-DNL on random inputs).
The objective is to significantly degrade model performance with minimal bit flips for stealth and practicality. The focus is on small *k* (e.g., *k* ≤ 10).

**Targeted Bit Type: Sign Bits:**
The methodology primarily focuses on flipping sign bits in the IEEE 754 32-bit floating-point representation.
- **Rationale:** Flipping the sign bit instantly switches a parameter from positive to negative or vice versa, producing drastic changes in learned features. This is often more consistently destructive at low flip budgets in vision models compared to exponent-bit flips (though exponent flips can be more destructive in language models, as explored later). Locating the sign bit in memory is straightforward (always the Most Significant Bit, MSB).

**DNL (Pass-Free Attack):**
DNL employs simple heuristics to identify critical parameters without any computational passes (forward or backward) on the model.

1. **Magnitude-Based Strategy:**
 - **Hypothesis:** Flipping the sign of high-magnitude parameters causes significant disruption, analogous to how pruning low-magnitude weights has minimal impact.
 - **Scoring:** The parameter score function is defined as S(θ_i) = |θ_i|, selecting weights with the largest absolute values.
 - **Finding:** Experiments show that focusing on the top-*k* largest weights (in absolute value) significantly disrupts most models.

2. **One-Flip-Per-Kernel Constraint for Convolutional Models:**
 - **Rationale:** Empirical analysis suggests that a single sign flip in a convolutional kernel can severely disrupt its feature extraction. Flipping multiple bits within the *same* kernel can sometimes partially offset the damage (due to cross-terms in the Hessian approximation and feature cancellation in early edge detectors).
 - **Application:** For CNNs, the attack is constrained to flip exactly one bit per kernel to maximize damage by distributing disruption across a broader range of features. This heuristic is not applied to transformer-based language models.

3. **Early-Layer Targeting:**
 - **Hypothesis:** Perturbations introduced in early layers propagate and compound throughout subsequent layers, causing greater overall damage than later-layer manipulations.
 - **Finding:** Restricting candidate flips to the first *L* layers (typically *L* ≤ 10) consistently increases attack impact. Even if largest parameters concentrate in later layers (e.g., ShuffleNetV2), targeting early layers often yields greater disruption.
 - **Algorithm 1 (DNL):**
 - Takes model parameters, number of flips *k*, and number of layers *L* as input.
 - Selects parameters from the first *L* layers.
 - Sorts these parameters by |θ_i| in descending order.
 - Selects the top *k* entries, enforcing the one-flip-per-kernel constraint for CNNs.
 - Flips the sign bit of the selected parameters.

**1P-DNL (Single-Pass Attack):**
This enhanced variant relaxes the threat model slightly to allow a single forward and backward pass on a random input to refine the selection of critical parameters. It is inspired by gradient-based pruning methods.

1. **Hybrid Importance Score:**
 - **Rationale:** Combines magnitude-based saliency with second-order information for a more potent selection.
 - **Scoring:** For a parameter θ_i, the score S(θ_i) = α|θ_i| + β[ (∂R/∂θ_i)θ_i + (1/2)H_ii θ_i^2 ].
 - In this work, α = β = 1. The Hessian diagonal H_ii is approximated by (∂R/∂θ_i)^2 (Gauss-Newton approximation). Inter-weight coupling (H_ij for j≠i) is approximated as zero to reduce computation.
 - R(θ) is defined as the sum of model outputs (e.g., class scores for random Gaussian image-like inputs, or logits for random token inputs).
 - **Algorithm 2 (1P-DNL):**
 - Generates a single random input X.
 - Computes R(θ) (e.g., sum of logits) and its gradients g = ∇_θ R(θ) in one forward and one backward pass.
 - For parameters in the first *L* layers, calculates the hybrid importance score S(θ_i).
 - Sorts parameters by S(θ_i) in descending order.
 - Selects the top *k* entries, enforcing the one-flip-per-kernel constraint for CNNs.
 - Flips the sign bit of the selected parameters.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Accuracy Reduction (AR(k)):** (Acc(θ) - Acc(θ'(k))) / Acc(θ), measuring the relative drop in accuracy after *k* flips.
- **Mean Accuracy Reduction (mAR(N)):** Average AR(k) over *k* from 1 to *N* to represent overall vulnerability.

**Defense Mechanisms:**
The authors propose selective defense strategies by leveraging the insight gained from identifying critical parameters. Only the highest-scoring (most vulnerable) parameters identified by DNL are protected, either through bit replication or Error-Correcting Codes (ECC), while the majority remain unguarded.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that Deep Neural Lesion (DNL) and its enhanced variant, 1P-DNL, can catastrophically disrupt DNNs across various domains with minimal parameter bit flips, often without data or iterative optimization.

**General Vulnerability:**
- **Random vs. Targeted Flips:** Flipping random sign bits generally has a negligible impact on performance, even for large numbers of flips (e.g., up to 100,000 bits might not consistently reduce accuracy). This contrasts sharply with the high impact of DNL's targeted sign flips.
- **DNL Effectiveness:** Both DNL and 1P-DNL caused most of the 48 ImageNet models evaluated to collapse, with 43 out of 48 models exhibiting an accuracy reduction above 60% with 10 sign flips. 1P-DNL consistently amplified damage compared to purely magnitude-based DNL.

**Specific Domain Results:**

1. **Image Classification (ImageNet and other datasets):**
 - **ImageNet:** Flipping just two sign bits in ResNet-50 reduced its ImageNet accuracy by 99.8%. Many architectures (e.g., MobileNet, MnasNet, ViT, VGG families) reached near-complete collapse (AR ≈ 100%) with fewer than 5–10 flips.
 - **Other Datasets (DTD, FGVC-Aircraft, Food101, Stanford Cars):** DNL achieved AR(5) ≥ 85% and 1P-DNL reached AR(4) ≥ 90% across all model/dataset combinations (EfficientNet-B0, MobileNetV3-Large, ResNet-50), demonstrating broad applicability.
 - **Model Size:** Model size did not show a clear correlation with attack susceptibility; most models collapsed at similar levels regardless of parameter count.
 - **Sign vs. Exponent Bits (Vision Models):** Sign-bit flips typically caused greater disruption per flip in vision models compared to exponent-bit flips, which, while effective, were less consistently destructive at low flip budgets.

2. **Object Detection & Instance Segmentation (COCO 2017):**
 - **Models:** Mask R-CNN (with ResNet-50 and ResNet-101 backbones) and YOLOv8-seg were evaluated, attacking only the backbone parameters.
 - **Results:** For Mask R-CNN backbones, a single sign flip drove bounding box AP (Average Precision) to ≈ 0.01 and mask AP to 0.00, with two flips effectively collapsing all reported metrics. YOLOv8-seg, while slightly more resilient, experienced over 77% reduction in both detection and segmentation performance with one or two sign flips.
 - **Qualitative Failures:** Examples showed Mask R-CNN preserving coarse localization but misclassifying objects, while YOLOv8-seg failed to detect objects and hallucinated detections.

3. **Reasoning Large Language Models (LLMs):**
 - **Models:** Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-30B-A3B, and Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Nano-8B were evaluated on a MATH-500 subset.
 - **Qwen3-30B-A3B (MoE):** DNL collapsed accuracy from 78% to 0% with just two sign flips, targeting two different expert down-projection weights. 1P-DNL achieved a 71.8% reduction with a single sign flip and full collapse with four flips.
 - **Other LLMs:** All three models were vulnerable. 1P-DNL notably improved efficacy for more resistant models (e.g., Qwen3-4B with 4 flips for 95.3% AR, compared to 14 flips for DNL with all layers).
 - **Random Flips (LLMs):** While targeted attacks were far stronger, random sign flips had a less negligible effect than in vision models, consistent with error compounding in autoregressive generation.
 - **Exponent-Bit Flips (LLMs):** A single targeted exponent flip reduced all three LLMs to 0% accuracy under both DNL and 1P-DNL in early blocks, often proving more destructive than sign flips. Random exponent flips were also highly destructive, consistent with extreme rescaling.
 - **Qualitative Failures:** Corrupted generations from Qwen3-30B-A3B quickly devolved into repetitive, nonsensical text.

4. **Text Encoders (GLUE tasks):**
 - **Models:** BERT, DistilBERT, and RoBERTa variants on MRPC, QNLI, and SST-2 tasks.
 - **Results:** Across all nine encoder-task pairs, attacks consistently caused severe degradation, with mean relative accuracy reduction (mAR(10)) ranging from 69.99% to 83.07%. This confirmed the vulnerability extended beyond autoregressive models.

**Comparison to Other Weight Attacks:**
- DNL and 1P-DNL uniquely combine optimization-free and data-agnostic properties while achieving accuracy reductions equal to or exceeding prior, more computationally intensive methods (e.g., BFA, DeepHammer, ZeBRA).
- For example, 1P-DNL collapsed ResNet-50 by 99.4% with a single sign flip, outperforming methods requiring many flips and iterative optimization.

**Defense Evasion:**
- DNL was shown to circumvent existing defenses like binarization, redundancy-coding (DeepNcode), and weight-scaling. For instance, binarization alone offered negligible protection against sign flips. Weight-scaling had no effect on sign-flip attacks as they are multiplicative.

**Defense Mechanisms (Proposed):**
- Selective protection of a small fraction of vulnerable sign bits (identified by DNL) provides a practical defense. Shielding only 0.001% of parameters halved the impact of an iterative Bit-Flip Attack (BFA) on ResNet-18 and ResNet-50, and guarding 1% of parameters nullified the attack on tested models. This indicates that DNL reliably identifies truly critical parameters.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work reveals a fundamental vulnerability in Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), demonstrating that even minimal, precisely targeted parameter manipulations can lead to catastrophic model failure across diverse architectures and tasks. The significance and potential impact of these findings are multi-faceted:

- **Heightened Security Concerns for Deployed DNNs:** The primary impact is on the security posture of DNNs in real-world deployments. The "data-free" and "optimization-free" nature of the Deep Neural Lesion (DNL) attack means that an attacker requires significantly fewer resources and computational access than previously thought to severely compromise a model. This lowers the bar for adversaries, making such attacks more practical and stealthy. It implies that models deployed in safety-critical systems (e.g., autonomous vehicles, medical diagnostics, critical infrastructure control) are potentially more susceptible to disruption via low-level system exploits (e.g., file-system intrusions, firmware compromises, direct memory access, Rowhammer).

- **Rethinking DNN Robustness:** The findings challenge the conventional understanding of DNN robustness. While previous research focused on robustness to input perturbations or complex weight-space attacks, this work highlights a fragility to discrete, targeted bit-flips at the most fundamental level of parameter representation. This necessitates a re-evaluation of how DNNs are secured, not just against sophisticated attacks, but also against simple, direct memory manipulations.

- **Understanding Criticality of Parameters:** The characterization of "critical parameters" (e.g., high-magnitude, early-layer placement, and the value of single-flip-per-kernel for CNNs) provides valuable insights into DNN architecture. It suggests that a small fraction of weights disproportionately influences model performance, acting as "single points of failure." This understanding can inform future neural network design, training methodologies, and pruning strategies.

- **Catalyst for Novel Defenses:** By precisely identifying the nature of the vulnerability, the research directly informs the development of more efficient and targeted defense mechanisms. The proposed "selective defense" strategy, which protects only a tiny fraction of critical sign bits, offers a practical pathway to substantially improve model resilience without incurring prohibitively high overhead (e.g., full redundancy for all parameters). This can lead to new error-correcting code implementations or memory protection schemes specifically designed for these critical parameters. The demonstration that DNL circumvents existing defenses like binarization and weight-scaling underscores the need for such novel, targeted protection strategies.

- **Implications for Hardware Security:** The paper explicitly links bit-flip attacks to various hardware-level exploits (e.g., Rowhammer, GPU cache tampering, voltage glitching). This emphasizes the importance of hardware-software co-design for secure AI systems, suggesting that robust DNNs require protections that extend beyond software-level adversarial training to secure memory, CPU, and GPU architectures.

**Limitations:**
The primary limitation noted is the assumption that an adversary can directly modify a small number of *any* stored parameters. In deployments where model parameters are sharded, compartmentalized, or only partially exposed to modification, the attack might be less effective if the adversary cannot perform a global search over all weights.

**Future Work:**
Future research could explore:
- Partial-access threat models, where only specific parts of the model's memory are vulnerable.
- Architectures, numeric formats (e.g., different quantization schemes), and training procedures specifically designed to increase resistance to lightweight bit-flip attacks. This could involve exploring alternative floating-point representations or training paradigms that distribute criticality more broadly across parameters.
