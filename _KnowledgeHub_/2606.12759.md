# Sparse2Act: Learning Action-Aligned Sparse 3D Representations for Cross-Domain Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCLA, UCSD, and the University of Utah developed Sparse2Act, a pretraining framework that teaches sparse 3D encoders to learn action-aligned geometric features for robot manipulation. This framework effectively uses 3D end-effector actions as direct geometric supervision, leading to an 86.9% success rate on the LIBERO-10 benchmark and robust cross-domain and sim-to-real transfer with limited real-world data.

## Problem

- Traditional 3D encoders for robot manipulation are typically co-trained with task-specific policies, which tightly couples the learned features to specific data distributions and action parameterizations, limiting their reusability and transferability.
- Existing pretraining objectives for 3D representations, such as reconstruction or dynamics prediction, are often indirect for the purpose of control, potentially hindering the learning of directly control-relevant features.
- Developing generalizable robot manipulation policies requires overcoming data inefficiency and improving sim-to-real transfer with limited real-world demonstrations.

## Method

- **Sparse2Act** employs a two-stage training decomposition: a pretraining stage where a ==sparse 3D== encoder and an auxiliary alignment head are trained using an action-aligned objective, followed by a fine-tuning stage where the pretrained encoder initializes a downstream policy.
- The pretraining objective uses 3D end-effector-space actions as direct geometric supervision for masked sparse 3D tokens, forcing the encoder to organize visible scene features around potential robot motions.
- A 3D Rotary Positional Encoding (3D RoPE) is integrated into the Transformer encoder to inject workspace coordinates into the attention mechanism, explicitly linking local geometry to its global position.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **86.9%** on the LIBERO-10 benchmark, significantly outperforming a Diffusion Policy baseline trained from scratch (**29.1%**) and demonstrating competitive performance against larger vision-language-action models.
- Demonstrated robust cross-domain transfer, with an encoder pretrained on LIBERO-10 achieving **73.4%** average success on Meta-World-5, notably higher than a baseline trained from scratch (**42.4%**).
- Enabled enhanced sim-to-real transfer on a physical AgileX PiPER robot, achieving **72.5%** average success on real-world tasks with a simulation-pretrained encoder and 50 real demonstrations, compared to 20-**25%** for policies trained from scratch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot actions can serve as a direct, control-grounded form of geometric supervision, effectively shaping sparse 3D encoders to learn reusable representations that are less tethered to specific downstream tasks or policy architectures.
> - The combination of masking input tokens and an action-alignment objective is crucial for performance, as it forces the encoder to infer task-space actions from partial, visible geometric context, thereby organizing sparse geometric structures around 3D end-effector motion.
> - The performance gains observed with Sparse2Act are largely attributable to the quality of the pretrained encoder's representation, making the 3D latent space more directly usable for control and reducing the reliance on high-capacity downstream decoders.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation increasingly leverages 3D representations to provide policies with explicit access to metric object shape, spatial relations, and robot-object geometry within the workspace. Traditional approaches for manipulation policies often rely on image-based observations, which implicitly learn task-relevant geometry. In contrast, methods employing point-based, voxel-based, or sparse-token-based 3D encoders offer direct access to 3D coordinates.

A common practice in developing such policies involves co-training these 3D encoders directly with the downstream task objective. This approach means that the control-relevant 3D features learned by the encoder are inherently tied to the specific data distribution, controller, and action parameterization used for the final policy. While effective for individual tasks, this coupling can limit the reusability and transferability of the learned representations across different tasks, domains, or policy architectures.

The broader research landscape in robot representation learning has explored various pretraining objectives to address this limitation. Prior work has demonstrated that pretrained features can provide a strong initialization, leading to improved data efficiency and transfer capabilities. These pretraining objectives include masked reconstruction (e.g., Point-BERT, Point-MAE) for learning geometric features, dynamics and future prediction (e.g., FVP, AFRO, PointWorld) for understanding temporal evolution, and contrastive, value-based, semantic, or multimodal signals (e.g., R3M, VIP, 3D-MVP, CLAMP, SUGAR, SPA) for emphasizing invariance, alignment, or consistency. While these methods induce various useful structures, they often rely on indirect objectives for control.

This research paper positions itself within this context by investigating a direct, control-grounded form of supervision for pretraining sparse 3D encoders. It explores whether 3D end-effector-space actions, which operate in the same metric 3D workspace as object geometry, can be directly utilized during pretraining to shape a sparse 3D encoder. This approach seeks to decouple the learning of a foundational 3D representation from the specifics of the downstream control policy, aiming to produce a more reusable and transferable encoder initialization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a pretraining framework, termed Sparse2Act, for sparse point-cloud encoders that enables the learning of action-aligned geometric features. This framework aims to produce a robust encoder initialization that enhances data efficiency and transferability for downstream robot manipulation policies.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations regarding current practices in robot manipulation:
- **Limitations of Co-training:** Existing pipelines for 3D manipulation policies typically co-train sparse 3D encoders alongside the final action models. This tight coupling means the learned 3D features are optimized for a specific downstream task, data distribution, controller, and action space. Consequently, the representations may lack generality, making them less effective for transfer to new tasks or domains without extensive retraining.
- **Benefits of Pretraining:** Prior research in robot representation learning has indicated that pretraining can significantly improve the data efficiency and transfer capabilities of policies by providing a strong initial feature set. The choice of pretraining objective is critical as it dictates the types of features the encoder learns.
- **Exploiting Shared 3D Workspace:** Explicit 3D representations provide direct access to object shape and spatial relations in metric coordinates. Simultaneously, robot task-space actions, such as end-effector movements, are also naturally expressed in this same 3D metric workspace. This shared spatial grounding presents an opportunity for a direct form of geometric supervision that can align scene features with potential robot motions.
- **Need for Control-Grounding:** While various pretraining objectives (reconstruction, dynamics, semantic prediction) have been explored, many of them are indirect for the purpose of control. The authors hypothesize that directly supervising 3D feature learning with robot actions could lead to representations that are inherently more relevant and aligned with control tasks.

Therefore, the key specific objective is to leverage task-space end-effector actions as geometric supervision for masked sparse 3D tokens. The goal is to train the encoder such that it organizes scene features in relation to the workspace motion paired with the observation. This pretraining approach is designed to be decoupled from the downstream policy's architecture and action space, allowing the pretrained encoder weights to serve as a general initialization while policies retain their native control interfaces (e.g., joint-space commands).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Sparse2Act operates through a two-stage training decomposition: a pretraining stage for the sparse 3D encoder, followed by a fine-tuning stage where the pretrained encoder initializes a downstream policy.

#### 3.1. Two-Stage Training Decomposition

- **Pretraining Stage:** An encoder E<sub>ϕ</sub> and an auxiliary alignment head h<sub>ψ</sub> are trained on a dataset D<sub>pre</sub> of point-cloud–action pairs {(P, a<sub>align</sub>)}. The objective is to minimize the L2 loss between the predicted alignment action (h<sub>ψ</sub>(E<sub>ϕ</sub>(T̃(P)))) and the ground truth a<sub>align</sub>, where T̃(P) represents the masked sparse 3D token set derived from point cloud P. This stage focuses on shaping the encoder with action-aligned geometric priors.
- **Downstream Policy Initialization:** After pretraining, the learned encoder E<sub>ϕ</sub>* is used to initialize the point-cloud encoder within a task-specific downstream policy. The policy head π<sub>θ</sub>, which fuses the encoded latent representation with proprioceptive input, is then trained on D<sub>down</sub> = {(P, s, a)} using a behavior cloning loss. The alignment head h<sub>ψ</sub> is discarded, allowing the downstream policy to utilize its preferred action parameterization. This separation ensures that pretraining data and action space can differ from those used for downstream control.

#### 3.2. Sparse 3D Encoder

- **Sparse 3D Tokens:** The input is an xyz-only point cloud of N points (P = {p<sub>i</sub>}). This focus on geometry encourages the encoder to prioritize geometry-action alignment. The raw point cloud is first cropped and downsampled to 1024 points. To create structured local geometric units, the method adopts a patch construction design similar to Point-BERT. Farthest point sampling (FPS) selects patch centers, and k-nearest-neighbor (KNN) search forms local patches. A shared point-patch embedding network maps each patch to a continuous embedding. These patches {(P<sub>j</sub>)}<sub>M</sub><sub>j=1</sub> define sparse 3D tokens T(P) = {(t<sub>j</sub>, c<sub>j</sub>)}<sub>M</sub><sub>j=1</sub>, where t<sub>j</sub> is the patch embedding and c<sub>j</sub> is its center. A Transformer encoder E<sub>ϕ</sub> processes these tokens to produce an observation latent z.
- **3D Rotary Positional Encoding (3D RoPE):** To inject workspace coordinates into the attention mechanism, 3D RoPE is applied. Normalized patch centers (c̄<sub>j</sub> = (x̄<sub>j</sub>, ȳ<sub>j</sub>, z̄<sub>j</sub>)) are used. Query and key channels within each attention head are partitioned into three groups corresponding to x, y, and z axes. A standard RoPE rotation is applied independently to each axis-wise partition of the query and key vectors based on the respective normalized coordinate. This mechanism ensures that positional information is integrated into the attention computation without altering the sparse tokenization, linking local geometry to its global position. The encoder configuration includes 1024 input points (xyz-only), 192 patch centers via FPS, k=32 nearest neighbors per center, a token dimension of 384, 6 Transformer layers, and 8 heads.

#### 3.3. 3D Action-Aligned Masked Pretraining

- **Masking Scheme:** During pretraining, a subset of token embeddings (70% in experiments) are randomly sampled and masked (dropped) before being fed into the Transformer encoder.
- **Alignment Objective:** Unlike masked autoencoding, the masked tokens are not reconstruction targets. Instead, the L2 alignment loss (L<sub>pretrain</sub> = ||â<sub>align</sub> - a<sub>align</sub>||<sup>2</sup><sub>2</sub>) is applied to the encoded *visible* geometry. This forces the encoder to infer the task-space action based on the spatial relationships among the remaining visible tokens, thereby encouraging the organization of sparse geometric structure around 3D end-effector motion.
- **Alignment Head:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with two hidden layers (256 units each) maps the encoder latent to the alignment action. Actions (e.g., 7D for LIBERO converted to 10D, 4D for Meta-World) are normalized with a training-set linear normalizer.

#### 3.4. Downstream Policy Initialization

For fine-tuning, the pretrained E<sub>ϕ</sub>* initializes the point-cloud branch of the downstream policy. A task-specific policy head (e.g., Diffusion Policy head) is attached, which fuses the encoder's latent output (z) with proprioceptive inputs (s) to generate the control action (a). The 3D alignment head used during pretraining is not carried over, allowing the downstream policy to define its own controller interface and action parameterization (e.g., joint-space delta positions).

The pretraining data can consist of mixed simulation and real-to-sim demonstrations, providing point-cloud–action pairs. Downstream fine-tuning then uses task-specific demonstration data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated Sparse2Act across various settings, including in-domain adaptation, cross-domain transfer, data efficiency, objective ablations, decoder capacity analysis, and sim-to-real transfer.

- **In-domain Adaptation (LIBERO-10 Benchmark):** The method achieved an average success rate of 86.9% on the LIBERO-10 benchmark after 500 fine-tuning steps. This significantly outperformed a Diffusion Policy (DP3) baseline trained from scratch with the same point-cloud interface, which achieved 29.1%. It also demonstrated competitive performance against larger vision-language-action (VLA) models such as π0 (85.2%) and SpatialVLA (55.5%), while operating with a sparse 3D policy rather than a large VLA backbone.
- **Cross-domain Transfer (LIBERO-to-Meta-World):**
 - When pretrained on the Meta-World-5 benchmark itself, the encoder achieved 85.6% average success. This result surpassed other 3D pretraining methods like FVP (28.4%), DP3 from scratch (42.4%), and AFRO (48.8%), indicating the effectiveness of the action-aligned pretraining objective.
 - Crucially, when the encoder was pretrained on LIBERO-10 and then fine-tuned on Meta-World-5, it still achieved 73.4% average success. This demonstrated robust cross-domain transfer capabilities, retaining a substantial portion of its performance despite the shift in task suite and scene distribution.
- **Data Efficiency and Scaling:**
 - **Demonstration-budget scaling:** The pretrained encoder was effective even with a limited number of downstream demonstrations (as few as 10), particularly for harder tasks. Performance generally improved with more task-specific data, but saturated earlier on easier tasks, suggesting that pretraining reduces the amount of downstream data required.
 - **Pretraining data scaling:** Increasing the fraction of pretraining data consistently improved performance across different Meta-World-5 tasks, whether pretraining steps were scaled proportionally with data or kept fixed. This indicates that broader pretraining coverage is beneficial.
 - **Learning curves:** In-domain pretraining converged rapidly, reaching strong performance within a few hundred fine-tuning steps (saturated by 600 steps). Cross-domain initialization from LIBERO also improved over scratch but required longer fine-tuning to reach comparable performance. The scratch baseline remained at low success rates even after 1,000 training steps.
- **Pretraining Objective Ablations (Meta-World-5):** A comparative study on Meta-World-5 (with 50 downstream demonstrations) examined different pretraining objectives:
 - Training from scratch: 19.0% success.
 - Unmasked action alignment: 50.7% success.
 - Masked reconstruction: 55.3% success.
 - Masked input + action alignment: 82.0% success.
 This ablation indicated that both masking and action alignment contribute to the performance gains, with their combination yielding the most significant improvement. The results suggest the benefit comes from forcing the encoder to connect 3D visible scene context with task-space 3D motion.
- **Attribution to the Encoder (Action-Decoder Capacity Ablation):** To determine if the gains were due to the encoder or a more complex decoder, experiments replaced the standard DP3 decoder with a simplified SimpleDP3 decoder and a basic MLP decoder.
 - The pretrained encoder consistently improved performance across all three decoder types.
 - Simplified decoders (SimpleDP3 at 75.0%, MLP at 74.7%) retained most of the performance relative to the full DP3 decoder (82.0%). This suggests that the pretrained representation itself carries the majority of the improvement, making the 3D latent more directly usable for control, rather than requiring a high-capacity decoder to compensate for a weak encoder.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (AgileX PiPER Robot):** The method was evaluated for sim-to-real transfer on four real-world manipulation tasks using an AgileX PiPER robotic arm.
 - Simulation pretraining followed by fine-tuning with 50 real-world demonstrations achieved an average success rate of 72.5%.
 - In contrast, co-training policies from scratch using 100 simulated and 50 real demonstrations for each task resulted in much lower average success rates (20% for DP3, 25% for the proposed architecture from scratch).
 This outcome indicates that pretraining the encoder in simulation significantly enhances sim-to-real performance with limited real-world data. It suggests that separating 3D representation learning from target-controller fitting during fine-tuning allows the policy to specialize more effectively on the real distribution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Sparse2Act" demonstrates that robot actions can serve as a direct, control-grounded form of geometric supervision for learning reusable sparse 3D representations. This approach has several implications and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Enhanced Reusability and Transferability:** By pretraining the 3D encoder independently using action-aligned objectives, Sparse2Act generates a foundational representation that is less tethered to specific downstream tasks or policy architectures. This directly addresses a limitation of current methods, where encoders are often co-trained, leading to task-specific rather than generalizable features. The ability to transfer pretrained encoders across different tasks, domains (e.g., LIBERO to Meta-World), and even action spaces (e.g., end-effector pose for pretraining, joint-space for fine-tuning) indicates a step towards more versatile robot learning systems.
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** The consistent improvement in performance, particularly with limited downstream demonstration budgets, suggests that the action-aligned pretrained encoder provides a robust initialization. This can significantly reduce the amount of task-specific data required to train effective manipulation policies, which is a critical advantage in data-scarce real-world robotic applications.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The success in sim-to-real experiments highlights the potential of this framework to bridge the reality gap. Pretraining in simulation allows for leveraging large synthetic datasets to build a strong geometric understanding, which can then be efficiently adapted to real robots with minimal real-world data. This separation of representation learning from real-world controller specialization offers a promising pathway for deploying policies on physical systems.
- **Understanding of Action as Supervision:** The ablation studies confirm that the gains are specifically attributable to the masked action-alignment signal and that the benefit largely resides within the pretrained encoder itself, rather than requiring a complex downstream action decoder. This reinforces the hypothesis that robot actions provide a compact and effective form of geometric supervision, shaping the encoder to organize visible geometry in relation to controllable motion.
- **Foundation for Future Robot Learning:** The method lays groundwork for developing more capable and adaptable robot manipulation policies. By providing a generalizable 3D representation, it could facilitate integration with other modalities (e.g., language, vision), enable learning in more complex and cluttered environments, and support longer-horizon tasks.

While the current work focuses on sparse point-cloud observations and encoder initialization, its principles suggest avenues for future research involving larger policy architectures, multi-frame context, language conditioning, complementary sensing modalities, and scaling to broader real-world pretraining data across more diverse task families. Overall, Sparse2Act contributes a novel perspective on how robot actions can be leveraged for representation learning, fostering advancements in the generality and efficiency of robot manipulation.
