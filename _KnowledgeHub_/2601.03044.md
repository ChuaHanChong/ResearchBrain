# SOP: A Scalable Online Post-Training System for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Agibot Research developed SOP, a scalable online post-training system, to adapt pre-trained Vision-Language-Action models for expert-level proficiency in real-world robotic manipulation. This system enables continuous learning from a fleet of robots, achieving high success rates (e.g., 0.98 for box assembly) and showing near-linear acceleration in learning time with increasing robot deployments.

## Problem

- Generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while broadly capable, lack the expert-level proficiency and reliability needed for robust real-world robotic deployment.
- Existing post-training methods for VLA models are often offline, leading to policy-data distribution shifts and compounding errors in physical environments.
- Limitations include slow data collection from single-robot deployments and task-specific fine-tuning that can degrade the model's overall generalization ability.

## Method

- **SOP** employs a closed-loop actor-learner system that enables continuous, online post-training of a pre-trained VLA policy from real-world interaction.
- It utilizes a distributed infrastructure where a fleet of robots collects on-policy data, including human interventions, which is then uploaded to a shared online experience buffer.
- A centralized cloud learner asynchronously updates the shared policy using an adaptive sampling strategy from mixed online and offline buffers, with low-latency model synchronization back to the robots.

## Results

- SOP post-trained models significantly improved success rates on real-world manipulation tasks, achieving **0.94** for Grocery Restocking, **0.96** for Laundry Folding, and **0.98** for Box Assembly.
- The system exhibited near-linear scaling, with a **2.4x** speedup in reaching a target success rate using four robot actors compared to a single actor.
- Three hours of on-policy interaction with SOP provided substantially greater performance improvement than 80 hours of additional static offline human demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Timely on-policy correction, enabled by a tight coupling of execution and learning in a closed-loop system, is crucial for mitigating distribution shifts in robot policies.
> - Distributed data collection from a fleet of robots significantly accelerates the learning process and enhances experience diversity, which is essential for rapid real-world adaptation.
> - Adaptive sampling strategies that prioritize recent on-policy data where the policy is currently struggling efficiently drive performance improvements while preserving the generalist nature of VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work stands at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, robot learning, reinforcement learning (RL), and large-scale distributed systems for AI.

In recent years, VLA models (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Octo, Gr00t N1, Pi series) have emerged as a significant advancement in robotics, showcasing impressive generalization capabilities across various tasks, objects, and embodiments by leveraging large-scale pre-training on diverse multimodal data. These models aim to create "generalist robots" that can understand complex instructions and perform a wide array of tasks. However, a critical gap remains: while these models offer broad generality, they often lack the "expert-level proficiency" and reliability required for seamless real-world deployment. A household robot, for instance, needs to not only understand how to fold laundry but also execute the task with high precision and consistency, akin to a dedicated appliance.

The challenge addressed by SOP is how to bridge this gap through "post-training"—adapting the pre-trained generalist model to achieve expert-level performance in specific deployment scenarios without sacrificing its hard-earned generality. Existing post-training approaches for VLA models are fundamentally constrained by several factors:
- **Offline nature**: Many methods rely on pre-collected datasets, which suffer from distribution shift when the policy deviates during execution, leading to compounding errors.
- **Single-robot deployment**: This limits the speed and diversity of data collection, hindering rapid improvement.
- **Task-specific adaptation**: Often, fine-tuning for one task can degrade performance on others, compromising the generalist nature of the VLA model.
- **Latency in feedback**: Even interactive learning methods like DAgger or HG-DAgger, when implemented in batch-based cycles, introduce delays between execution, human correction, and policy update, limiting their effectiveness for real-time decision-making.

The broader landscape of robot learning includes various approaches to address these challenges:
- **Supervised finetuning**: Adapts VLA models using task-specific demonstrations, but is limited by static data.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Offers improvement through interaction. Online RL (e.g., PPO) can be unstable in real-world robotics, while offline RL (e.g., RECAP, IQL) is effective but typically iterative and offline, not designed for continuous online improvement of a generalist policy.
- **Interactive and Online Learning**: Methods like DAgger and HG-DAgger involve human correction but usually in single-robot, batch-processing settings. Sample-efficient online RL methods (e.g., RLPD, SERL) leverage demonstrations but are also primarily single-robot and task-specific.
- **Distributed and Multi-task Robot Learning**: Distributed RL systems (e.g., Gorila, A3C, IMPALA) pioneered actor-learner architectures but mostly for simulation. Fleet-DAgger introduced multi-robot interactive learning but was simulation-only and not for large VLA models. Multi-task learning shares experience across tasks but often lacks online distributed collection for VLA post-training.

SOP's unique contribution is its *integrative system framework* that simultaneously addresses these limitations. It is presented as the first framework that enables *online, distributed, multi-task post-training* for *generalist VLA models* directly in the *physical world*. By tightly coupling execution and learning within a fleet-scale deployment, SOP aims to overcome the inherent scaling and efficiency bottlenecks of prior methods, thereby pushing the boundaries of what is achievable for real-world adaptable generalist robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SOP stems from the recognition that the "generality" offered by large pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while impressive, is insufficient for reliable real-world deployment. Real-world applications demand "expert-level proficiency" alongside broad task generalization. This means a robot must not only understand a wide range of tasks but also execute them with high precision, reliability, and efficiency.

The authors identify critical shortcomings in existing post-training methodologies that prevent VLA models from reaching this expert level:
- **Offline nature**: Current methods often rely on static datasets, leading to distribution shift when the trained policy is deployed and inevitably encounters states not present in the training data. This results in compounding errors over long task horizons.
- **Single-robot deployment**: Data collection is slow and limited in diversity when restricted to a single robot, impeding rapid learning and adaptation.
- **Task-specific fine-tuning**: Focusing on one task often leads to a loss of the model's hard-won generality, requiring separate models for different tasks or diminishing overall capabilities.
- **High latency in learning loops**: Even interactive learning approaches typically involve batch-based updates, creating a delay between a robot's failure, human intervention, and the model's actual improvement, making on-policy correction less effective.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of the SOP system are:
1. **Enable Timely On-Policy Correction**: To mitigate distribution shift, SOP aims to create a closed-loop system where robot failures are immediately identified and corrected, and these corrections are rapidly incorporated into the policy update process.
2. **Scale Experience Collection Through Parallel Deployment**: By utilizing a fleet of robots, SOP seeks to dramatically increase the throughput and diversity of real-world interaction data, thereby accelerating the learning process.
3. **Preserve Generality During Adaptation**: The system must allow VLA models to improve proficiency on specific tasks without "catastrophic forgetting" or specializing to the point of losing their ability to generalize across a wide range of tasks and objects. This implies maintaining a single shared policy across tasks.
4. **Achieve High Efficiency**: Post-training should be achievable within hours of real-world interaction, rather than days or weeks, making continuous improvement practical.
5. **Demonstrate Scalability**: The system should exhibit favorable scaling properties, ideally with learning speed increasing proportionally with the number of robots in the fleet.
6. **Be Algorithm-Agnostic**: SOP is designed as a system framework, meaning it should be capable of integrating different underlying post-training algorithms (e.g., imitation learning, reinforcement learning), thus providing a robust platform for future algorithmic advancements.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to develop a practical, scalable, and efficient system that empowers generalist VLA models to achieve expert-level performance directly in the physical world through continuous, online learning from fleet-scale real-world interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SOP introduces a **Scalable Online Post-training** system, a closed-loop actor–learner framework designed to adapt a pre-trained VLA policy using continuous real-world interaction from a heterogeneous robot fleet. Its core insight is the tight coupling of learning and execution to enable timely on-policy correction, scale exploration, and preserve generality.

**A. Algorithmic Framework (Algorithm 1 and Figure 2)**
The SOP system operates through a continuous feedback loop:
1. **Initialization**: A pre-trained VLA policy (πθ₀) is broadcast to all N robot actors in the fleet.
2. **Distributed On-Policy Data Collection (Actor Side)**: Each robot actor (e.g., an Agibot G1 dual-arm manipulator) continuously executes the latest available policy (πθ) in its local environment, performing various tasks (e.g., Grocery Restocking, Laundry Folding, Box Assembly).
 - **Experience Streaming**: Autonomous rollouts (τᵢπ) generated by the policy are collected.
 - **Human Intervention**: When the policy struggles or fails, optional human supervisors can provide real-time interventions (τᵢH), yielding corrected trajectories or actions. These interventions are crucial for on-policy error correction.
 - All collected trajectories (autonomous and intervened) are uploaded to a shared **online experience buffer** (Bₒₙ) in the cloud.
3. **Centralized Cloud Optimization (Learner Side)**: A centralized cloud learner operates asynchronously, continuously sampling training batches (ξ) from a mixture of the dynamic online buffer (Bₒₙ) and a static **offline buffer** (Bₒff) containing prior human demonstrations.
 - **Policy Update**: The learner updates the shared policy parameters (θ) by minimizing a post-training loss (Lₚₜ) defined by a pluggable post-training algorithm (G).
4. **Low-Latency Model Synchronization**: The updated policy (πθ) is then streamed back to all robot actors asynchronously. Actors fetch these updates at safe boundaries (e.g., between episodes) to prevent corrupting ongoing rollouts.

**B. System Infrastructure (Figure 7 and Appendix B)**
SOP employs a robust distributed data infrastructure:
- **Edge Clients on Robots**: Each robot runs an edge client that buffers frame-level observations locally, assembles them into complete episodes, and then uploads them asynchronously to a distributed object storage. Concurrently, it publishes an event notification to a message queue.
- **Decoupled Cloud Services**:
 - A **data consumption service** subscribes to the message queue, retrieves new episodes from object storage, and populates an in-memory index with frame-level metadata (lightweight identifiers, counts, weights).
 - A **training dataloader** samples from this in-memory index based on the adaptive sampling strategy and lazily fetches the heavy payload data (images, sensor readings) from object storage only when needed for training.
 - A **distribution service** uses a lightweight publish–subscribe channel to broadcast updated model parameters from the cloud learner back to the robot actors.
This architecture provides elastic horizontal scaling, persistent and fault-tolerant data management, and efficient separation of metadata and payload, allowing actors and learners to scale independently and robustly.

**C. Adaptive Sampling Strategy**
To maintain multi-task coverage while prioritizing relevant on-policy data, SOP uses a task-balanced adaptive sampling strategy:
- **Inter-task Level**: Uniform weights (ωₘ = 1/M) ensure each of the M tasks contributes equally to the training batch.
- **Intra-task Level**: For each task, the sampling ratio between its online buffer (Bᵐₒₙ) and offline buffer (Bᵐₒff) is adjusted dynamically based on recent training losses. A sliding window average of online (Lᵐₒₙ) and offline (Lᵐₒff) losses determines an online sampling ratio (ωᵐₒₙ) using a softmax-like function with a boost factor (α > 1) that prioritizes online data. This ratio is clipped to [0.2, 0.8] to prevent extreme allocations. This ensures the learner focuses on data where the policy is currently struggling, accelerating adaptation.

**D. Post-training Learning Module (Algorithm Agnostic)**
SOP is designed to be algorithm-agnostic. The paper instantiates it with two representative methods:
- **HG-DAgger (Interactive Imitation Learning)**: HG-DAgger involves human supervisors providing real-time interventions when the robot is about to fail. SOP integrates these interventions and autonomous rollouts into its continuous streaming buffer, enabling frequent asynchronous updates. This transforms HG-DAgger into a practical, fleet-scale, on-policy online post-training procedure by minimizing the latency between error, correction, and model update.
- **RECAP (Offline Reinforcement Learning)**: RECAP is an offline RL method for post-training VLA policies. SOP makes RECAP an *online* workflow by continuously incorporating fresh trajectories from the latest deployed policy into the buffer and running RECAP-style updates asynchronously on this evolving dataset. This significantly reduces policy–data staleness and enables continual, on-policy improvement. For multi-task settings, RECAP is adapted by conditioning the policy and value function on task language prompts.

This comprehensive approach combines distributed systems design with sophisticated data management and a flexible algorithmic interface, enabling efficient and scalable improvement of generalist VLA models in complex real-world environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SOP was conducted on a fleet of 10 Agibot G1 dual-arm manipulators across three challenging real-world manipulation task families: Grocery Restocking (semantic understanding, diverse objects, various shelf configurations), Laundry Folding (dexterous bimanual manipulation of deformable objects), and Box Assembly (precise multi-step procedural execution). The evaluation focused on three key questions: effectiveness, scalability with fleet size, and impact of pre-training quality.

**A. Multi-task Post-training Effectiveness (Figure 4)**
- **Significant Performance Improvement**: Across all tasks, models post-trained with SOP consistently and substantially outperformed the initial pre-trained baseline.
- **Superiority over Non-SOP Counterparts**: Combining either HG-DAgger or RECAP with SOP yielded significantly higher performance compared to their standalone, non-SOP versions. SOP + HG-DAgger demonstrated the strongest results, achieving impressive success rates of 0.94 for Grocery Restocking, 0.96 for Laundry Folding, and 0.98 for Box Assembly.
- **Enhanced Throughput**: SOP consistently improved throughput (completed episodes per hour) across all models, typically by approximately 2x. This indicates not only higher success rates but also faster, more reliable task execution. For instance, in laundry folding, SOP rapidly corrected common failure modes like missed grasps, reducing cycle time.
- **Preservation of Generality**: Crucially, these performance gains were achieved while maintaining a single shared policy across all diverse tasks, including object-diverse grocery restocking, demonstrating that SOP effectively allows for task proficiency without sacrificing the generalist nature of the VLA model.
- **Efficiency**: Effective post-training, leading to significant performance improvements, was achieved within *hours* (e.g., a 3-hour budget for experiments), a marked contrast to the multi-day cycles often associated with traditional robot learning.

**B. Scaling Robot Deployments (Table I)**
- **Improved Final Performance with Fleet Size**: Increasing the number of active robot actors (N) directly translated to higher achievable final success rates. For example, in Grocery Restocking, increasing the fleet from one to four actors raised the final success rate after 180 minutes from 0.805 to 0.925. This suggests that parallel data collection provides more diverse on-policy experience, which reduces overfitting to single-station specific idiosyncrasies.
- **Near-Linear Speedups in Learning Time**: Fleet scaling substantially accelerated the learning process. The "time-to-target" (wall-clock time to reach a success rate of 0.8) decreased significantly: from 173.6 minutes with a single actor to 126.5 minutes with two actors (1.4x faster), and further to 71.7 minutes with four actors (2.4x faster). This indicates an approximately linear relationship between fleet size and wall-clock training efficiency within the tested regime, confirming that SOP effectively translates robot parallelism into faster on-policy post-training without being bottlenecked by centralized learning or communication.

**C. Analysis of Pre-training Quality and Data Efficiency (Figure 5)**
- **Dependency on Pre-training Scale**: While SOP consistently improved performance, the initial quality of the pre-trained base policy (Base-1/8, Base-1/2, Base-Full, trained on varying data amounts) strongly influenced both the starting performance and the asymptotic performance after SOP. Models initialized with larger pre-training datasets converged to higher overall performance levels. This suggests that large-scale pre-training provides essential representational priors that SOP refines, rather than replacing the need for broad pre-training.
- **Superiority of On-Policy Data**: The study highlighted the diminishing returns of static offline data compared to on-policy interaction. For a Base-1/2 model, augmenting the offline dataset with an additional 80 hours of human-collected data yielded only a modest improvement (0.576 to 0.612 success rate). In stark contrast, applying SOP for just 3 hours of *on-policy interaction* resulted in a substantially larger improvement (0.571 to 0.800). This demonstrates that SOP's ability to collect and learn directly from policy-generated failures in the real world is far more efficient in closing the performance gap than simply adding more static expert demonstrations, which cannot anticipate the deployed policy's specific error distributions.

In summary, the main findings affirm that SOP is a highly effective, scalable, and efficient system for post-training generalist VLA models in the physical world. It consistently improves performance, leverages robot fleets for accelerated learning, and demonstrates the critical value of on-policy correction over mere offline data expansion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SOP marks a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for the deployment of generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in real-world scenarios. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Gap from Generality to Proficiency**: SOP offers a concrete, systems-level solution to the critical challenge of endowing generalist VLA models with expert-level task proficiency without sacrificing their broad generalization capabilities. This is crucial for the widespread adoption of versatile robots in diverse applications, from households to industrial settings, where reliability and precision are paramount.

2. **First-of-its-Kind Framework**: SOP is presented as the first framework that simultaneously achieves *online, distributed, and multi-task post-training* for generalist VLA models directly in the *physical world*. This integration of capabilities addresses a long-standing limitation in robot learning, where solutions have often been confined to simulation, single-robot setups, or offline learning paradigms.

3. **Efficiency and Scalability for Real-World Robotics**:
 - **Rapid Adaptation**: SOP demonstrates that significant performance improvements can be achieved within *hours* of real-world interaction, a radical departure from traditional multi-day or multi-week training cycles. This makes continuous improvement and rapid deployment feasible.
 - **Fleet-Scale Learning**: The near-linear scaling of learning efficiency with fleet size is a transformative finding. It suggests that deploying more robots can directly accelerate policy improvement, effectively turning robot deployments into a form of distributed computation for learning. This parallels the success of distributed training in other AI domains (e.g., large language models).

4. **Operationalizing On-Policy Correction**: The research strongly reinforces the notion that on-policy correction, directly addressing the deployed policy's failure modes, yields substantially greater marginal utility than merely augmenting static offline datasets. SOP provides a robust infrastructure to operationalize this insight at a fleet scale, transforming episodic fine-tuning into a continuous, closed-loop learning process.

5. **Algorithm Agnostic and Future-Proof**: By decoupling the system infrastructure from the specific post-training algorithm, SOP provides a flexible platform. It demonstrates that existing algorithms (like HG-DAgger and RECAP) can be dramatically enhanced when integrated into such a scalable, online system. This allows future algorithmic advancements to be readily incorporated and benefit from the system's inherent efficiency and scalability.

6. **Enabling Long-Horizon, Robust Deployments**: The ability to continuously refine policies through real-world interaction supports the maintenance of robust performance over long operational periods. Tasks running continuously for over 36 hours without degradation, as mentioned in the abstract, highlight SOP's capacity to facilitate durable robot autonomy.

**Potential Broader Impact**:
SOP represents a significant step towards realizing the vision of fleets of robots collaboratively learning and maintaining a shared, ever-improving policy from real-world experience. This paradigm shift could lead to:
- **Faster Development Cycles**: Robotics companies could deploy foundational VLA models and rapidly refine them on-site with real-world data from customer fleets, accelerating product development and customization.
- **More Adaptive and Resilient Robots**: Robots could continually adapt to new environments, tasks, and object variations, becoming more robust and capable over their operational lifetimes.
- **Reduced Human Supervision Burden**: While human intervention is currently a part of SOP, the system's efficiency in incorporating feedback could eventually lead to more sample-efficient and targeted human-in-the-loop learning, potentially paving the way for more autonomous correction mechanisms (e.g., learned reward models or foundation-model-based success detection) as future work.

In essence, SOP demonstrates that system-level innovations, specifically the tight coupling of execution and learning at scale, are as critical as algorithmic advancements and data quantity in pushing the boundaries of real-world robot intelligence. It lays a foundational groundwork for a future where general-purpose robots can achieve expert performance through continuous, collaborative, and scalable learning from their collective experiences in the physical world.
