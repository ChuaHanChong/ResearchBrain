# Scaf-GRPO: Scaffolded Group Relative Policy Optimization for Enhancing LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, and The University of Hong Kong introduce Scaf-GRPO, a progressive training framework that uses in-prompt hierarchical guidance to help Large Language Models overcome the 'learning cliff' in reinforcement learning from verifier rewards. This approach significantly boosts reasoning accuracy on challenging math and scientific benchmarks, improving pass@1 scores by up to a relative 44.3% on AIME24 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) training with Reinforcement Learning from Verifier Rewards (RLVR) suffer from a "learning cliff" where all attempts on difficult problems yield zero rewards, stalling progress.
- Existing guidance methods, which often provide partial correct solutions (prefixes) from a teacher, introduce distributional mismatches and may restrict the model's exploratory flexibility.
- Policy optimization algorithms like GRPO collapse to zero advantage for these difficult problems, making them "invisible" to the learning gradient.

## Method

- **Scaf-GRPO** integrates a "==guidance exemption== period" to allow initial autonomous exploration and identify true-hard problems that genuinely require assistance.
- When a true-hard problem is encountered, Scaf-GRPO conducts a progressive search through a three-tiered hint hierarchy (knowledge, planning, solution) to find the minimal effective hint.
- The identified hint is injected directly into the user prompt (in-prompt scaffolding) to augment failed trajectories in the batch, restoring a non-zero reward signal for GRPO's policy update.

## Results

- Scaf-GRPO improved Qwen2.5-Math-**7B**'s pass@1 on AIME24 by a relative **44.3%** (from **30.0%** to **43.3%**) and achieved a **9.2%** relative improvement over the leading prefix-based method, LUFFY.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization, boosting average performance by **10.3%** on Llama-3.2-**3B**-Instruct and **5.9%** on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-**1.5B** compared to vanilla GRPO.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the initial guidance exemption period and the progressive, hierarchical nature of hints were essential for optimal performance, with the full framework outperforming variants by up to **9.2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimal, hierarchical in-prompt guidance effectively reactivates the learning process on problems where LLMs previously received consistent zero rewards, avoiding learning stagnation.
> - Allowing an initial period of unguided exploration is crucial for the model to develop foundational skills and differentiate between truly intractable problems and those solvable with more training.
> - Injecting hints directly into the prompt ensures policy consistency by processing both problem and hint under a unified policy, mitigating distributional mismatches seen in prefix-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to advancements in complex reasoning tasks across domains such as mathematics, programming, and logic. A key technique driving these advancements is Reinforcement Learning from Verifier Rewards (RLVR). In RLVR, models learn to generate reasoning paths by exploring diverse strategies and receiving feedback solely on the final outcome, rather than requiring expensive step-by-step human annotations.

However, a significant challenge in RLVR, termed the "learning cliff" phenomenon by the authors, arises when LLMs encounter problems substantially beyond their current capabilities. For such problems, all exploratory attempts consistently fail, resulting in a persistent zero-reward signal. In policy optimization algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), this leads to a collapse of the advantage calculation to zero, rendering these difficult problems "invisible" to the learning gradient and stalling further progress.

Existing approaches to mitigate the learning cliff often involve incorporating off-policy guidance from a more capable "teacher" policy. These methods typically provide the student model with a prefix of a correct "golden" solution and task it with generating the remainder of the reasoning path. While this can introduce a positive reward signal, it also presents several issues: it creates a distributional mismatch between the teacher-generated prefix and the student-generated suffix, requiring complex algorithmic corrections (e.g., policy shaping or hybrid SFT-RL objectives) that can introduce bias and instability. Furthermore, this "on-rails" guidance may constrain the model's ability to explore alternative or novel reasoning strategies.

This work positions itself as an alternative to these prefix-based guidance methods. Inspired by pedagogical scaffolding theory, it proposes a progressive training framework, Scaf-GRPO, that strategically provides minimal, hierarchical, in-prompt guidance. By doing so, it aims to overcome the learning cliff while maintaining policy consistency and preserving the model's exploratory flexibility, addressing limitations observed in prior guidance paradigms. The work extends the on-policy GRPO algorithm, focusing on data augmentation rather than altering the core optimization objective or relying on off-policy data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the complex reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by directly addressing the "learning cliff" phenomenon encountered in Reinforcement Learning from Verifier Rewards (RLVR).

The motivation for this objective stems from several observations:

- **The Learning Cliff Problem:** When LLMs confront problems significantly exceeding their current competence, all solution attempts may result in incorrect answers. This leads to a persistent zero-reward signal. In policy optimization algorithms like GRPO, this uniform zero reward causes the advantage calculation to collapse to zero, effectively eliminating any learning gradient for these difficult problems. As a result, the model's progress on these "true-hard" problems stagnates, forming a "long tail" of challenges that it cannot autonomously overcome, preventing it from reaching higher levels of competence.

- **Limitations of Current Guidance Methods:** Existing strategies to address the learning cliff often involve providing models with partial "golden" solutions (prefixes) from a more capable teacher policy. While these methods aim to provide a positive learning signal, they introduce challenges:
 - **Distributional Mismatch:** Mixing teacher-generated prefixes with student-generated continuations can create a discrepancy between the input distributions, necessitating complex algorithmic adjustments to maintain training stability.
 - **Restricted Exploration:** Guiding the model down a predetermined path may stifle its capacity to explore and discover novel, potentially more efficient, reasoning strategies.

To overcome these issues, the authors propose Scaf-GRPO with two specific objectives guiding its in-prompt scaffolding approach:

1. **Maintain Policy Consistency:** The framework aims for the model to process both the problem and the hint under a single, unified policy. This design is intended to avoid the distributional mismatches that arise in prefix-based methods, promoting more stable and coherent learning.
2. **Preserve Exploration Flexibility:** The hints are designed to act as "signposts" rather than "railroads." They provide general guidance without rigidly dictating the solution path, thereby allowing the model to retain its exploratory autonomy and potentially discover unique problem-solving strategies.

In essence, the motivation is to develop a method that provides effective, minimal assistance to LLMs to bridge their capability gaps on challenging problems, thereby reactivating the learning process, while simultaneously avoiding the drawbacks associated with more restrictive guidance paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Scaf-GRPO operates as a progressive training framework built upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, designed to provide targeted guidance to LLMs facing the "learning cliff." The methodology is structured into two main phases:

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO is an on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm that trains LLMs without requiring a separate value function. For a given prompt, the policy generates a group of *N* trajectories. After a verifier assigns a terminal reward to each trajectory, GRPO calculates a normalized advantage for each. The policy is then updated by maximizing a clipped surrogate objective using these advantages. The core limitation addressed by Scaf-GRPO is that if all *N* trajectories receive a zero reward (the learning cliff), the advantage collapses to zero, preventing any policy update.

#### 3.2. The Scaf-GRPO Framework

Scaf-GRPO intervenes in the GRPO training process by strategically augmenting the trajectory group when a learning cliff is detected. This intervention maintains the on-policy nature of GRPO.

**Phase 1: Diagnosing True-Hard Problems (Guidance Exemption Period)**
The framework first includes an initial "guidance exemption period," empirically set to the first 15% of training steps. During this period, the model attempts to solve problems through autonomous on-policy exploration. The purpose is to allow the model to develop foundational reasoning skills and differentiate between "pseudo-hard" problems (solvable with more training) and "true-hard" problems (requiring external guidance). The end of this phase is determined algorithmically by monitoring when the rate of solving zero-reward queries plateaus. Problems consistently failed after this point are classified as "true-hard" and become candidates for guidance.

**Phase 2: Hierarchical Hint-Guided Exploration**
When a "true-hard" problem is identified (i.e., all *N* initial trajectories from the current policy for a query yield zero reward, and the exemption period has passed), Scaf-GRPO activates its guidance mechanism:

1. **Hint Hierarchy:** The framework utilizes a pre-defined, three-tiered hint hierarchy (`H`):
 - `H_knowledge`: Provides abstract concepts or formulas.
 - `H_planning`: Outlines a high-level strategic framework.
 - `H_solution`: Offers a concrete calculation step.
 Each tier is further subdivided into four incremental items, allowing fine-grained guidance.

2. **Progressive Search:** To provide the minimal necessary guidance, Scaf-GRPO executes a deterministic search through this hierarchy. It starts with the most abstract hint (`H_knowledge`) and progresses towards more concrete hints (`H_planning` then `H_solution`). Within each tier, it incrementally provides more detailed items. The search terminates as soon as the model generates a correct solution with the current hint, identifying the minimal effective guidance (`h*`).

3. **In-Prompt Injection:** The selected minimal hint `h*` is injected directly into the user prompt. This "in-prompt scaffolding" approach ensures that the model processes both the problem and the hint under a single, unified policy, which the authors argue avoids the distributional mismatches inherent in prefix-continuation methods.

4. **On-Policy Batch Augmentation:** The successful trajectory (`o*_h`) generated by the policy conditioned on the hint-augmented prompt (`q ⊕ h*`) replaces one randomly chosen failed trajectory from the original group of *N* trajectories. This creates an augmented group (`G_final`).

5. **Unified Loss Function:** The standard GRPO clipped surrogate objective is then applied to this `G_final`. Critically, the probability ratio for the guided trajectory (`o*_h`) is computed with respect to its specific originating prompt (`q ⊕ h*`). This ensures a non-zero reward variance in `G_final`, restoring a meaningful advantage signal and enabling learning to resume on previously intractable problems, while maintaining the on-policy principle (as `o*_h` is sampled from the *current* policy, albeit with a modified input). If no hint leads to a solution, or if a solution was already found in Phase 1, the batch is not augmented, and standard GRPO proceeds.

#### 3.3. Data Preparation

- **Training Data Source:** Derived from the DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset (40k mathematical problems).
- **Dynamic Filtering Strategy:** Based on 8 initial sampling attempts, problems are filtered: "Too Easy" (solved 8/8) are discarded; "Potentially Solvable" (solved 1-7/8) are 50% subsampled; "Too Hard" (solved 0/8) are fully retained. This curates a challenging yet tractable dataset.
- **Hint Generation:** The three-tiered, structured hints are generated offline using the DeepSeek-R1 model, which is prompted with the problem and its ground-truth solution to decompose the solution into the required hierarchy.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Qwen2.5-Math-7B/1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (LongCoT).
- **Baselines:** Vanilla GRPO, Simple-RL, Oat-Zero, LUFFY (prefix-based).
- **Evaluation:** pass@1 accuracy (greedy decoding) on seven challenging mathematics benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC, Minerva, MATH-500, Olympiad, GaoKao2023en) and the out-of-distribution scientific reasoning benchmark GPQA-Diamond. Robustness is also assessed using avg@16.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various models and benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of Scaf-GRPO in enhancing LLM reasoning abilities, particularly in overcoming the learning cliff.

- **Superiority over Vanilla GRPO:**
 - Scaf-GRPO consistently achieved significant performance improvements over the vanilla GRPO baseline across all tested model architectures (Qwen2.5 series, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B).
 - For the Qwen2.5-Math-7B model, Scaf-GRPO boosted the pass@1 score on the AIME24 benchmark by a relative 44.3% (from 30.0% to 43.3%) and achieved an overall average pass@1 of 50.9% compared to GRPO's 45.2%.
 - Quantitative analysis (Figure 2a) illustrated that Scaf-GRPO enabled continuous learning from "zero-reward" problems where vanilla GRPO stagnated, leading to sustained improvements in validation accuracy (Figure 2b).

- **Outperformance against Other Leading Methods:**
 - On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Scaf-GRPO (average 50.9%) outperformed Simple-RL (42.6%) by a relative 19.5% and Oat-Zero (46.5%) by a relative 9.5%.
 - Crucially, Scaf-GRPO also surpassed LUFFY (46.6%), a prominent prefix-continuation guidance method, by a relative 9.2%, suggesting the in-prompt scaffolding strategy can be more effective.

- **Generalization Across Architectures and Specializations:**
 - **Non-Qwen Models:** Scaf-GRPO demonstrated strong generalization on the Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct model, achieving a relative 10.3% improvement in average performance over vanilla GRPO (28.8% vs 26.1%).
 - **Long-Chain-of-Thought (LongCoT) Models:** Applied to the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B model, Scaf-GRPO yielded a 5.9% relative performance gain over vanilla GRPO (average 53.6% vs 50.6%), indicating its applicability to models requiring extended reasoning.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Guidance Exemption Period:** Eliminating the initial autonomous learning phase ("w/o Phase 1") resulted in a 9.2% relative performance drop on Qwen2.5-Math-7B compared to the full framework, confirming the necessity of allowing initial independent exploration. The method also proved robust to varying exemption durations (10% to 40% of training steps), maintaining high performance.
 - **Progressive & Hierarchical Guidance:** A "Solution-Only" variant, bypassing the abstract hint hierarchy, led to a 4.9% performance degradation. Removing any single hint tier also degraded performance, with the most severe drop (5.7%) when the "Solution" hint was absent, indicating that both abstract and concrete hints play complementary roles.
 - **Incremental Guidance:** Providing entire hint levels at once (non-incremental) reduced performance by 6.3%, validating the efficacy of minimal, incremental intervention.
 - **Hint Quality:** Higher-quality hints, as rated by an LLM-as-a-Judge rubric, correlated with improved downstream model performance, yielding a 4% relative accuracy improvement for the student model.

- **Further Analysis and Robustness:**
 - **Skill Internalization:** The framework fostered "skill graduation," with models transitioning from hint-dependent to autonomous problem-solving (Figure 5). Scaf-GRPO yielded significantly more "graduations" (+137.8% on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B) than vanilla GRPO (Table 4).
 - **Data Filtering:** An optimized data filtering strategy (discarding "Too Easy," subsampling "Potentially Solvable" at 50%) provided a 6.0% gain for Scaf-GRPO on Qwen2.5-Math-7B over training on the original dataset, demonstrating that a challenging curriculum is most effective when paired with a robust learning framework.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** On the GPQA-Diamond benchmark, Scaf-GRPO significantly improved Qwen2.5-Math-7B's performance (15.5% relative gain over vanilla GRPO) and matched LUFFY, demonstrating the acquisition of fundamental, transferable reasoning skills.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Scaf-GRPO achieved optimal performance in approximately 12 hours for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, outperforming vanilla GRPO (13 hours for a lower peak), indicating improved training efficiency despite triggering hint-guided exploration for 17.4% of samples.
 - **Robustness (Avg@16):** Re-evaluation using the avg@16 metric confirmed Scaf-GRPO's consistent and stable performance advantage over baselines across all architectures.
 - **Comparison with DAPO:** Scaf-GRPO outperformed DAPO (which filters zero-gradient samples) across all benchmarks (average 50.9% vs 48.5%), suggesting active intervention with hints is more effective for skill advancement than passive filtering.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Scaf-GRPO, a methodology that addresses a fundamental limitation in reinforcement learning for Large Language Models (LLMs)—the "learning cliff" phenomenon. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Overcoming the Learning Cliff:** By providing a mechanism to extract learning signals from problems previously deemed intractable due to persistent zero rewards, Scaf-GRPO enables LLMs to acquire capabilities for tasks that were consistently beyond their reach. This is a critical step toward advancing autonomous reasoning in LLMs.
- **Novel Guidance Paradigm:** The framework introduces "in-prompt scaffolding" as an alternative to existing prefix-continuation guidance methods. This approach is demonstrated to maintain policy consistency and preserve exploratory autonomy, mitigating issues of distributional mismatch and restricted exploration commonly associated with prior techniques. This offers a new avenue for guiding LLM learning effectively.
- **Enhanced Skill Generalization:** The consistent performance gains observed across diverse model architectures (Qwen, Llama), scales (1.5B to 7B), and specializations (math-tuned, instruction-tuned, LongCoT) indicate that Scaf-GRPO fosters fundamental and transferable reasoning skills, rather than mere pattern memorization. Its strong generalization to out-of-distribution tasks, such as the GPQA-Diamond scientific reasoning benchmark, further supports this.
- **Improved Training Efficiency:** The empirical evidence suggests that Scaf-GRPO can achieve superior performance in less total training time compared to vanilla GRPO. By converting unproductive, zero-gradient samples into valuable learning opportunities, it optimizes the utilization of training data and computational resources.
- **Versatility and Applicability:** Scaf-GRPO's ability to integrate with and enhance already capable models, like DeepScaleR, highlights its versatility as a complementary training paradigm. This suggests it can be broadly applied to improve various LLM reasoning systems.

The framework lays groundwork for future research into more sophisticated and automated scaffolding mechanisms, such as dynamic hint adjustment and automated hint generation. Overall, Scaf-GRPO represents a contribution to the field of LLM training, providing a robust and effective methodology for extending the frontier of autonomous reasoning capabilities in AI.
