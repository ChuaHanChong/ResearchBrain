# Jumping Control for a Quadrupedal Wheeled-Legged Robot via NMPC and DE Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong developed a motion control framework that combines Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) for locomotion and Differential Evolution (DE) for trajectory optimization, enabling a quadrupedal wheeled-legged robot to perform dynamic jumps. The framework was validated through hardware experiments, demonstrating successful forward jumps over a 0.12 m obstacle and vertical jumps reaching approximately 0.5 m.

## Problem

- Achieving dynamic jumping for quadrupedal wheeled-legged robots presents challenges due to the integration of wheels and legs, which introduces additional degrees of freedom.
- There was a lack of hardware demonstrations for model-based planning and control pipelines enabling forward jumping in quadrupedal wheeled-legged robots.
- Effectively leveraging the unique capabilities of wheeled legs, specifically for generating initial velocity, to enhance jumping performance had not been fully explored.

## Method

- A comprehensive motion control framework was developed, integrating Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (NMPC) for generating initial velocities during the rolling locomotion phase.
- Differential Evolution (DE) based trajectory optimization was used to compute optimal jumping trajectories for the take-off, flight, and landing phases.
- A centroidal dynamics model was used for NMPC to accurately represent the robot's significant leg and wheel mass, while a simplified Single Rigid Body Dynamics (SRBD) model was employed for the DE optimization for computational efficiency.

## Results

- Hardware experiments demonstrated the robot performing a forward jump over a **0.12** m obstacle and achieving vertical jumps with a height of approximately **0.5** m.
- During experimental jumps, the wheels reached speeds of approximately 20 rad/s and knee joint torques peaked around 30 Nm, effectively utilizing the robot's hybrid capabilities.
- Simulations revealed the potential for more complex maneuvers like backflips, though these required knee joint torques (57 Nm) exceeding the current hardware's capacity (42 Nm).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating active wheeled legs allows the robot to generate initial velocity through rolling, which significantly aids in achieving greater jump distances compared to purely legged systems.
> - The hybrid control framework, combining NMPC for continuous locomotion and DE for discrete dynamic maneuvers, effectively manages the complex dynamics and additional DoFs of wheeled-legged robots for versatile jumping.
> - Employing different fidelity dynamic models (centroidal for NMPC, SRBD for DE) across the control pipeline strikes a balance between model accuracy for specific tasks and computational tractability for real-time performance.
