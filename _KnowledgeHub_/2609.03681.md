# WISE: World-model-guided Imagination Scheduling for Efficient Post-training of Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WISE is a post-training framework for vision-language-action policies that schedules when world-model imagination is used. A DINOv2-based scheduler fires only at interaction-relevant states, a frozen video world model rolls out bounded multi-view futures for sampled action candidates, and a progress/completion reward model turns their relative scores into GRPO-style updates on real contexts, raising average success to 59.5% while cutting ~80% of world-model calls.

## Problem

- Imitation-based VLA post-training needs large volumes of expensive expert demonstrations, which are hard to collect for fine manipulation and rarely contain failure or recovery cases.
- Reinforcement learning alternatives require extensive real-world exploration, incurring interaction cost and risking collisions or hardware wear during trial-and-error on physical robots.
- Prior world-model methods apply imagination uniformly, ignoring that its value varies by task stage, and long rollouts compound prediction error into unreliable learning signals.

## Method

- **Imagination scheduling**: a view-shared ==DINOv2== encoder plus lightweight head scores interaction relevance from wrist and third-person images, triggering imagination only when the score exceeds threshold κ, trained with weak supervision from execution signals.
- **Bounded counterfactual imagination**: a frozen ==Open-Sora==-derived, action-conditioned multi-view world model trained with ==rectified flow== rolls each of M candidate action chunks forward L chunks, capping accumulated prediction error.
- **Counterfactual evaluation and refinement**: a ==DINOv2== reward model scores progress, confidence and completion; a reliability filter on rank consistency, reward variance and separation gates ==GRPO==-style group-relative updates on real-context actions.

## Results

- On five MimicGen tasks WISE lifts π0 from **49.7%** to **59.5%** average success (**+9.8** points, beating DPO by **5.7** points) and π0.5 from **52.3%** to **60.2%**.
- Scheduling ablation: WISE reaches **68.3%** average with **28** selected states and **2.61** GPU hours, versus **60.4%** at **138** states and **11.45** hours for full imagination.
- Real Galaxea R1 Lite tasks: π0+WISE reaches **77.5%** standard and **68.8%** generalization success (**+17.5**/**+26.3** points); reward ablations fall to **57.3%** using progress or completion alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - More imagination is not better: allocating world-model compute to interaction-critical states beat uniform, random and full imagination on both accuracy and cost.
> - Use imagined rollouts for relative ranking of candidates rather than as synthetic training targets; gradients stay grounded in real observed contexts, limiting model-error leakage.
> - Dense progress signals and terminal completion are complementary; confidence weighting and backward-progress penalties matter, and reliability filters should discard groups whose imagined rankings are unstable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, such as the $\pi_0$ family, integrate visual perception, language understanding, and action generation to facilitate general-purpose robotic control. While pretrained VLA policies demonstrate robust semantic understanding and generalization capabilities, adapting them effectively to specific environments and interaction patterns remains a challenge.

Traditional post-training methodologies for VLA models largely fall into two categories: imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL). Imitation learning approaches, which fine-tune policies using expert demonstrations, face limitations due to the high cost and difficulty of collecting diverse and high-quality robotic data, especially for fine manipulation or handling failure cases. Reinforcement learning offers an alternative by improving policies through trial-and-error environment interaction. However, real-world RL exploration is computationally expensive, incurs substantial interaction costs, and carries risks of collisions, hardware wear, or damage. These limitations highlight a need for more efficient and safer post-training strategies that do not extensively rely on physical world exploration or large expert datasets.

World models present a promising paradigm by enabling agents to predict the consequences of candidate actions in an imagined future, thereby decoupling policy improvement from direct physical interaction. Recent research has begun to explore the use of imagined rollouts for refining robot policies. However, the effective integration of world models into VLA post-training is not solely dependent on the accuracy of future predictions. Challenges remain in determining when imagination should be invoked, how far into the future it can be reliably extended without accumulating excessive prediction errors, and how the outcomes of imagined trajectories should be translated into trustworthy policy supervision. Prior work in world-model-guided robot learning often focuses on the generation and general exploitation of imagined trajectories, sometimes treating imagination as a uniformly applied component.

This work, WISE, addresses these specific challenges by proposing a framework that coordinates the selective application of world-model imagination. It aims to optimize *when* and *how* imagination is used, moving beyond uniform application to focus computational resources on interaction-relevant states and ensuring that imagined outcomes provide reliable signals for policy refinement. By doing so, WISE seeks to enhance the efficiency and reliability of VLA post-training, mitigating the drawbacks of both imitation learning and real-world reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for WISE stems from the observed limitations of current VLA post-training paradigms:
1. **Cost and Risk of Real-World Interaction**: Existing RL-based post-training methods necessitate extensive real-world exploration. This process is expensive due to interaction time, energy consumption, and potential for hardware damage through trial-and-error, particularly in robotic manipulation where collisions can be problematic.
2. **Data Scarcity in Imitation Learning**: Imitation learning requires large volumes of high-quality expert demonstrations, which are costly and difficult to collect for diverse and complex manipulation tasks. This also often limits the policy's ability to recover from failures not represented in the demonstration data.
3. **Inefficient World Model Utilization**: While world models can predict future states, their application in post-training is not uniformly beneficial. The utility of imagination varies significantly across different stages of a manipulation task; for instance, fine-grained interactions or critical operational stages may benefit more from predictive reasoning than coarse motions.
4. **Accumulation of Prediction Errors**: Extending imagined rollouts over long horizons can lead to a compounding of world-model prediction errors. This can degrade the reliability of the learning signals derived from these imagined futures, potentially leading to suboptimal or unstable policy optimization.
5. **Lack of Coordinated Imagination Strategy**: Effective post-training with world models requires a strategy to coordinate *when* imagination is invoked, *how far* it is extended, and *how* its predicted outcomes are reliably converted into policy-learning signals. Simply having an accurate world model is insufficient if its application is uncoordinated or untrustworthy.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the WISE framework are:
1. **Develop a Unified Framework**: To create a comprehensive system that coordinates the various aspects of world-model-guided VLA policy refinement.
2. **Schedule Imagination**: To selectively invoke world-model imagination only at interaction-relevant states, thereby focusing computational resources where predictive reasoning is most beneficial and reducing unnecessary inference.
3. **Perform Bounded Counterfactual Rollouts**: To generate multiple candidate action chunks and evaluate their future consequences through action-conditioned, multi-view world-model rollouts that are bounded in horizon to limit the accumulation of prediction errors.
4. **Evaluate Imagined Trajectories Reliably**: To assess candidate futures using a reward model that provides nuanced feedback through progress and completion signals, and to filter unreliable learning signals.
5. **Refine Policies with Trustworthy Supervision**: To convert the relative outcomes of imagined trajectories into feedback for group-relative policy optimization, ensuring that policy updates are grounded in real interaction contexts and guided by reliable imagined outcomes.
6. **Demonstrate Efficiency and Performance**: To show that WISE can consistently improve VLA policies across diverse manipulation tasks and generalize to real-world scenarios while substantially reducing world-model computation time compared to full imagination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WISE (World-model-guided Imagination Scheduling for Efficient Post-training of Vision-Language-Action Models) is presented as a unified framework for VLA policy refinement, operating through an imagination–evaluation–optimization loop.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to optimize a pretrained VLA policy $\pi_\theta$ to maximize expected task success $y(\tau, \ell)$ given a language instruction $\ell$ and a trajectory $\tau$. A world model $p_\psi$ is utilized to predict counterfactual consequences of actions, $\hat{O}_{t+1:t+H} \sim p_\psi(\cdot | O_t, \ell, a_t)$, without physical execution. The framework addresses when, how far, and how these predictions contribute to policy supervision.

#### 3.2. Bounded Counterfactual Imagination

At selected imagination contexts, the policy $\pi_\theta$ samples $M$ candidate action chunks $a_{t,i}$. A frozen, action-conditioned multi-view world model $p_\psi$, adapted from Open-Sora, predicts future observations. The world model is trained using rectified-flow training on VAE latents of observed and future frames, with supervision on future latents providing conditioning context from perturbed observation latents.
For each candidate action, a bounded closed-loop rollout is performed. Starting from the current real observation $O_t$ and robot state $s_t$, the world model recursively predicts subsequent observations $\hat{O}^{(r+1)}_i$ and the policy samples further actions $\hat{a}^{(r+1)}_i$ for $L$ chunks. This process generates an imagined trajectory $\hat{\tau}_i$ containing $LH$ frames. Bounding the rollout to $L$ chunks aims to limit the accumulation of world-model prediction errors.

#### 3.3. Imagination Scheduling

Recognizing that world-model imagination is computationally expensive and not uniformly useful, WISE employs a scheduler to determine when to invoke imagination.
- **Relevance Score**: A view-shared DINOv2 encoder $E_{\phi_s}$ and a lightweight MLP head $g_\eta$ are used to predict an interaction-relevance score $s_t \in$ from current wrist and third-person observations $O_t = (I^w_t, I^a_t)$.
- **Training**: The scheduler is trained offline with weak interaction-relevance supervision. These labels are automatically derived from robot execution signals, specifically gripper and grasp states, indicating whether the robot is in an active manipulation state. During WISE post-training, only visual observations are required for scheduling.
- **Triggering Mechanism**: Imagination is triggered only if $s_t > \kappa$, where $\kappa$ is a predefined threshold. This selective invocation aims to reduce unnecessary world-model inference while focusing computation on states where counterfactual evaluation is likely to yield useful information.

#### 3.4. Counterfactual Trajectory Evaluation and Policy Refinement

For each imagined trajectory $\hat{\tau}_i$, a separate reward model $f_\omega$ evaluates the predicted multi-view observations.
- **Reward Model Architecture**: Uses a separately trained, view-shared DINOv2 encoder $E_{\phi_r}$ with three independent MLP heads to predict:
 - $p_{i,j}$: Task progress (sigmoid output).
 - $c_{i,j}$: Prediction confidence (sigmoid output).
 - $q_{i,j}$: Task completion (tanh output).
- **Reward Computation**: These signals are combined to calculate a trajectory reward $R_i$. This reward includes terms for progress gain $G_i = \sum_{j=1}^{LH} c_{i,j} [p_{i,j} - p_{i,j-1}]_{\delta_0}$, a backward progress penalty $B_i = \sum_{j=1}^{LH} [p_{i,j-1} - p_{i,j}]_{\delta_0}$, and a terminal completion bonus $T_i = \max_j q_{i,j}$. The final reward is $R_i = G_i - \alpha B_i + \beta T_i$.
- **Reward Model Training**: The reward model is trained offline using recorded task trajectories. Progress supervision uses temporal ordering (later states in successful trajectories get higher scores). Confidence is trained with binary cross-entropy, and completion with signed success/failure supervision.
- **Reliability Filter**: To ensure trustworthy learning signals, WISE retains a group of candidate actions only if:
 - $\Delta R = \max_i \bar{R}_i - \min_i \bar{R}_i \geq 0.02$ (sufficient reward separation).
 - $\rho_{rank} \geq 0.5$ (ranking consistency across repeated imaginations).
 - $\sigma_{WM} \leq 0.2$ (low reward variation across repeated imaginations).
- **Policy Refinement**: For accepted groups, advantages $A_i$ are computed from the average rewards $\bar{R}_i$. The policy is updated along a direction $g_{WISE}$ that is based on the signed advantages and a policy regularization term. Crucially, only the *first* action chunk $a_{t,i}$ from the *real interaction context* is supervised; the imagined rollouts are solely used for counterfactual evaluation and to provide relative feedback. The world model and the main VLA backbone remain frozen, with optimization focused on the action head parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated WISE on five MimicGen manipulation tasks in simulation and four real-world manipulation tasks using two pretrained VLA backbones, $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$.

#### 4.1. Policy Improvement (Simulation)

- **Consistent Gains**: WISE consistently improved performance across both $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$ backbones and on every individual task evaluated.
- **Quantitative Results for $\pi_0$**: For the $\pi_0$ backbone, WISE increased the average success rate from 49.7% to 59.5%, representing a 9.8 percentage point gain. This also significantly outperformed the strongest baseline, $\pi_0$ + DPO, by 5.7 percentage points (59.5% vs. 53.8%).
- **Quantitative Results for $\pi_{0.5}$**: When applied to the stronger $\pi_{0.5}$ backbone, WISE similarly improved the average success rate from 52.3% to 60.2%, an increase of 7.9 percentage points.
- **Task-Specific Performance**: WISE variants achieved the highest success rates across all five simulated tasks (Stack, Coffee, Square, Threading, Mug Cleanup), with particularly strong gains observed in Stack, Square, Threading, and Mug Cleanup. These results suggest that the benefits of WISE are generalizable across different pretrained policies and diverse manipulation challenges.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Reward Model Components**: An ablation study on the reward model's components showed that the full formulation, which incorporates dense progress feedback, terminal completion signals, confidence weighting, and a backward-progress penalty, achieved the highest average success rate of 68.3%. Reduced variants, such as using only progress or completion (both 57.3%), or removing confidence weighting (58.0%) or the backward penalty (59.4%), led to substantial performance degradations. This indicates the complementary nature of progress and completion signals, and the importance of confidence weighting and penalizing regressive actions for reliable trajectory evaluation.
- **Imagination Scheduling Strategies**: Comparison of different imagination allocation strategies demonstrated the efficiency and effectiveness of WISE's scheduling. WISE achieved an average success rate of 68.3% with only 2.61 GPU hours. In contrast, "Full Imagination" (invoking imagination uniformly) resulted in a lower success rate of 60.4% but consumed significantly more GPU time (11.45 hours) and approximately 80% more world-model inference calls. Under comparable computation budgets (around 2.6-2.9 GPU hours), WISE also outperformed "Uniform" (61.1% success) and "Random" (59.1% success) scheduling. These findings confirm that selectively allocating imagination to interaction-relevant states provides a better performance-efficiency trade-off.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

- **Robust Real-World Performance**: WISE was evaluated on a Galaxea R1 Lite robot across four real-world manipulation tasks (Pick-and-Place, Two-Cube Stacking, Open, and Open-and-Close) under both standard and generalization settings.
- **Gains under Standard Setting**: For $\pi_0$, WISE increased the average success rate from 60.0% to 77.5% (+17.5 pp). For $\pi_{0.5}$, it improved from 61.3% to 77.5% (+16.2 pp).
- **Gains under Generalization Setting**: The improvements were even more pronounced under diverse distribution shifts (variations in interaction height, lighting, distractors, and language prompts). For $\pi_0$, the average success rate increased from 42.5% to 68.8% (+26.3 pp). For $\pi_{0.5}$, it rose from 50.0% to 71.3% (+21.3 pp).
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization**: The larger performance gains under distribution shifts suggest that world-model-guided post-training, as implemented in WISE, contributes to enhanced robustness and generalization capabilities beyond the initial training conditions.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Qualitative analysis of behaviors under generalization settings showed that WISE-enhanced policies better adapted to altered interaction geometry, such as height changes in Pick-and-Place, relative object-position changes in Two-Cube Stacking, and improved handle localization and articulated interaction in Open-and-Close tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WISE framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic learning, particularly in the post-training and adaptation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Efficiency in Post-Training**: By introducing an imagination scheduling mechanism, WISE substantially reduces the computational overhead associated with world-model-guided policy refinement. The observed 80% reduction in world-model inference calls and 77% reduction in GPU computation time compared to full imagination makes world-model-based post-training more practical and scalable for real-world robotic applications. This efficiency addresses a key bottleneck in deploying advanced VLA models.

2. **Improved Policy Performance and Robustness**: WISE consistently demonstrates notable improvements in task success rates across a diverse range of simulated and real-world manipulation tasks. The gains are observed for multiple VLA backbones ($\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$), indicating the broad applicability of the framework. Crucially, the substantial performance increases under real-world distribution shifts highlight an improved robustness and generalization capability of the policies. This suggests that WISE-trained policies are better equipped to handle variations in operating environments, which is essential for practical robotic deployment.

3. **Reliable and Coordinated Learning Signals**: The framework's integrated approach to imagination scheduling, bounded rollouts, and multi-faceted trajectory evaluation addresses critical challenges in leveraging world models. By focusing imagination on interaction-relevant states, limiting error accumulation through bounded horizons, and using a comprehensive reward model with confidence weighting and penalty for regression, WISE generates more reliable and informative learning signals. This coordinated strategy prevents the policy from being misled by inaccurate or uninformative imagined futures, a common issue in naive world-model applications.

4. **Reduced Reliance on Costly Data and Exploration**: WISE offers a viable alternative to post-training methods that are heavily reliant on expensive expert demonstrations (imitation learning) or risky and time-consuming real-world exploration (reinforcement learning). By generating counterfactual experiences in an imagined environment, WISE allows for policy improvement with less physical interaction, mitigating hardware wear and safety concerns, thus lowering the barriers to entry for advanced robotic capabilities.

5. **Advancement in General-Purpose Robotics**: The ability to efficiently and reliably adapt pretrained VLA models to specific tasks and environments is a crucial step towards realizing general-purpose robots. WISE contributes to this goal by providing a principled method for fine-tuning these powerful foundation models, enabling them to generalize more effectively to novel situations and operate robustly in complex, dynamic real-world settings.

Future research directions identified by the authors, such as exploring adaptive imagination horizons based on prediction uncertainty and extending WISE to a broader range of embodiments and longer-horizon tasks, indicate pathways for further enhancing the framework's capabilities and scalability. Overall, WISE represents an important step towards making sophisticated VLA models more adaptable, efficient, and reliable for real-world robotic manipulation.
