# The DAWN of World-Action Interactive Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A research team from COWARobot Co. Ltd, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Hohai University introduces World-Action Interactive Models (WAIMs), a class of decision-making models where future world states and actions are inferred through recursive refinement, rather than independently. Their instantiation, DAWN, applies this principle to autonomous driving, achieving an average L2 error of 0.33 m on nuScenes and an 89.1 PDMS on NAVSIM v1, improving over previous methods by better coupling world prediction with action generation.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) often suffer from structural decoupling, treating world prediction and action generation as separate processes (e.g., parallel designs, predict-then-plan pipelines).
- Many WAMs lack a reciprocal relationship where an agent's actions can dynamically influence the predicted future world states, and vice versa.
- Current methods either perform computationally expensive pixel-space world rollouts or eliminate explicit future reasoning at inference, limiting effective decision-making in complex, interactive environments.

## Method

- **DAWN (Denoising Actions and World iNteractive model)** is introduced as an instantiation of ==World-Action Interactive Models (WAIMs)==, which infer future world states and actions as coupled variables, recursively refining each other.
- It employs a ==latent-space architecture== with a compact ==semantic latent representation== of the world, avoiding computationally expensive pixel-level future rendering.
- During inference, DAWN performs a short ==explicit latent rollout== of the future world state, iteratively refining both the world prediction and the agent's action hypothesis over multiple rounds.

## Results

- DAWN achieved an overall PDMS (Planning Driver Model Score) of **89.1** on NAVSIM v1, outperforming perception-free models and demonstrating superior No-at-fault Collisions (NC), Ego Progress (EP), and Time-to-Collision (TTC) scores.
- On nuScenes, DAWN achieved the lowest average L2 error for trajectory prediction (**0.33 m**) across all horizons, reducing it from **0.47 m** by the prior strongest method, WorldRFT.
- The model also demonstrated an improved average collision rate of **0.11%** on nuScenes, indicating enhanced safety and robustness in end-to-end planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Recursively coupling world prediction with action generation, where each informs and refines the other, leads to more coherent and effective future-action pairs for autonomous driving.
> - A compact semantic latent space and a short explicit latent rollout can provide sufficient future context for long-horizon action generation without the computational burden of full pixel-space future rendering.
> - Ablation studies confirm that both the action-to-world (action hypothesis conditioning world rollout) and world-to-action (predicted world conditioning action denoising) dependencies are crucial for optimal performance, supporting the WAIM principle.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions its work within the context of World Action Models (WAMs), which extend the concept of world models (predicting environmental evolution) to incorporate an agent's actions for decision-making. Existing WAMs are identified as primarily falling into three categories: predicting world and action in parallel, sequential predict-then-plan pipelines, or approaches that minimize/eliminate explicit world rollout at inference time. These existing paradigms are characterized by a structural decoupling, where world and action generation do not iteratively or reciprocally influence each other during the inference process. For instance, parallel designs allow correlation but not dynamic mutual shaping, and sequential designs condition action on a static future hypothesis rather than one that co-evolves with the action.

The paper argues that decision-making, particularly in domains like autonomous driving, necessitates a reciprocal relationship: a plausible scene evolution depends on the agent's maneuver, and an effective maneuver depends on how the scene might evolve as a consequence. This bidirectional dependency is crucial for reasoning about action-contingent futures, such as whether a gap in traffic remains feasible or if safety-critical interactions emerge.

To address this perceived limitation, the authors introduce World-Action Interactive Models (WAIMs). WAIMs are defined as a class of WAMs where future world states and actions are inferred as coupled variables, recursively refining each other during generation. This approach aims to bridge the gap between models that are either structurally decoupled or that rely on a binary choice between full, often expensive, pixel-space world rollout and zero-rollout methods. DAWN is presented as a minimal instantiation of this WAIM principle, operating in a compact semantic latent space and employing a short, explicit latent rollout to support long-horizon trajectory generation without the computational burden of full pixel-space future rendering. This work attempts to establish a principled path toward models where world evolution and decision-making remain mutually aligned throughout the inference process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to formalize and instantiate World-Action Interactive Models (WAIMs) to address limitations in existing World Action Models (WAMs). The motivation stems from a specific observation: in complex interactive environments such as autonomous driving, the future evolution of the scene and the agent's actions are inherently reciprocal and interdependent.

The paper details several key motivations:

- **Addressing Decoupled World and Action Generation**: Current WAMs often treat world prediction and action generation as largely separate processes.
 - **Parallel Designs**: In these architectures, world states and actions are predicted in parallel from a shared context. While this allows for correlation, it does not enable iterative refinement where an emerging action hypothesis could alter the predicted world, and vice-versa.
 - **Predict-Then-Plan Pipelines**: These models first forecast a future state (e.g., observations, occupancy, or latent scene states) and then plan actions based on this static prediction. This approach limits the agent's ability to influence the predicted future, as the action is conditioned on a "frozen" future hypothesis.
 - **Zero-Rollout Inference**: Some methods suggest that explicit world rollout is not always necessary at inference time, using world modeling primarily as a training signal. While efficient for simpler domains, this approach is deemed insufficient for complex, interactive scenes where some explicit future evolution is necessary to reason about dynamic agents and obstacles. The paper views this as one extreme of a design spectrum, rather than a universal solution.

- **Formalizing Action-Contingent Reciprocity**: The authors argue that a useful WAM should infer a future that is physically plausible under candidate actions and informative for choosing among them, rather than merely predicting a passive future. This necessitates a model where world and action hypotheses co-evolve, allowing the current world hypothesis to refine the action hypothesis, which in turn feeds back to revise the predicted world evolution.

- **Enabling Coherent Future-Action Pairs**: The goal is to generate a *coherent* future-action pair, where the predicted world evolution and the agent's decision-making remain mutually aligned. This interaction is not merely about bidirectional information flow within the architecture, but an inference process where hypotheses are recursively refined.

- **Achieving Efficiency and Effectiveness**: The motivation for DAWN specifically is to instantiate the WAIM principle in autonomous driving by:
 - Operating in a compact semantic latent space, thereby avoiding computationally expensive pixel-level future rendering.
 - Utilizing a *short explicit latent rollout* rather than eliminating test-time world evolution or rolling out the full future in pixel space. This design choice aims to provide sufficient future context for long-horizon action generation in complex scenes, balancing predictive capability with inference-time efficiency.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop models that more accurately reflect the interactive and reciprocal nature of decision-making in dynamic environments, leading to safer and more actionable autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces World-Action Interactive Models (WAIMs) and instantiates this concept in autonomous driving with DAWN (Denoising Actions and World iNteractive model).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The paper defines a WAIM as a specialized WAM where future world states ($v_{1:T}$) and actions ($a_{1:H}$) are inferred as coupled variables, rather than generated independently. Formally, a WAIM seeks a self-consistent pair $(\hat{v}_{1:T}, \hat{a}_{1:H})$ such that $\hat{v}_{1:T} = F_{\theta}(o, l, \hat{a}_{1:H})$ and $\hat{a}_{1:H} = G_{\phi}(o, l, \hat{v}_{1:T})$, where $o$ is the observation, $l$ is the task instruction, $F_{\theta}$ is a world prediction function, and $G_{\phi}$ is an action generation function. This self-consistency is achieved through iterative interaction: $(v^{(k+1)}_{1:T}, a^{(k+1)}_{1:H}) = I_{\Theta}(v^{(k)}_{1:T}, a^{(k)}_{1:H}; o, l)$.

#### 3.2. DAWN Architecture

DAWN implements WAIM through a latent-space architecture comprising:
- **Student Vision-Encoder ($E_{stu}$)**: Extracts dense visual tokens ($u$) from the current observation ($o$). It uses V-JEPA 2 Large as its vision backbone.
- **Teacher Vision-Encoder ($E_{tea}$)**: Used during training to process future observations ($o^+$) and update via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) from the student.
- **Auto Encoder Resampler ($R_{stu}$, $R_{tea}$)**: A learned bottleneck autoencoder that compresses dense encoder tokens ($u$) into compact latent world representations ($z$) to reduce computational cost during rollout. The teacher resampler ($R_{tea}$) processes future observations to produce target future latents ($z_{target}$) for supervision.
- **World Predictor ($P_{\theta}$)**: A causal Transformer that predicts future latent world tokens ($z_{future}$) based on the current latent context ($z$), condition tokens ($c$), and the current action hypothesis ($a_{1:H}$). This component enables the short explicit latent rollout.
- **World-Conditioned Action Denoiser ($G_{\phi}$)**: Implemented as a Denoising Diffusion Transformer (DiT). It denoises action tokens ($a_{1:H}$) conditioned on the latent context ($z$), predicted future world ($z_{future}$), and additional condition tokens ($c$) (ego-state, high-level action, route tokens). It receives role-specific queries ($q_{prop}$ for initial proposal, $q_{ref}^{(r)}$ for refinement) to distinguish its function.
- **Action Head ($H_{act}$)**: A lightweight module that decodes the denoised action states into the final trajectory prediction ($\hat{\tau}$).

#### 3.3. Training Stages

DAWN employs a four-stage training process:
1. **Vision Pretraining**: The Student Vision-Encoder is pretrained on large-scale driving video datasets (OpenScene, DrivingDojo, CoVLA) at 256x512 resolution and 2 Hz frame rate, providing a visual prior. The Teacher Vision-Encoder is updated via EMA.
2. **Auto-Encoder Resampler Training**: The Resampler is trained on the same pretraining corpora to compress dense encoder features into compact latent world tokens, aiming to preserve information for future prediction and action generation. An auxiliary diffusion planner head is used to encourage action-relevant compression.
3. **World Predictor Training**: The World Predictor is attached and trained on downstream task datasets (nuScenes, NAVSIM). It learns to roll out task-relevant future latent world states from the compact latent context.
4. **Joint World-Action Training**: The World Predictor is initialized from Stage 3, and the World-Conditioned Action Denoiser and Action Head are attached. Both world and action branches are jointly optimized on target datasets. The Action Denoiser is trained in two roles (initial proposal and interactive refinement) with shared weights, specified by different query and source embeddings. This stage aligns future world rollout and action generation through recursive interaction.

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, the teacher branch is removed.
1. **Latent Context Extraction**: The current observation ($o$) is encoded into a compact latent context ($z = R_{stu}(E_{stu}(o))$) along with non-visual condition tokens ($c$).
2. **Initial Action Hypothesis**: The World-Conditioned Action Denoiser generates an initial action hypothesis ($a_{1:H}^{(0)}$) directly from the resampler latent and condition tokens.
3. **Recursive Refinement**: DAWN then performs $K$ interactive rounds. In each round ($k$):
 - A short latent future ($z_{future}^{(k+1)}$) is rolled out by the World Predictor, conditioned on the current latent context, non-visual conditions, and the action hypothesis from the previous round ($a_{1:H}^{(k)}$).
 - The action hypothesis is refined ($a_{1:H}^{(k+1)}$) by the Action Denoiser, conditioned on the predicted latent future ($z_{future}^{(k+1)}$), latent context, and non-visual conditions.
4. **Trajectory Decoding**: After $K$ refinement steps, the Action Head decodes the final action state ($a_{1:H}^{(K)}$) into the predicted trajectory ($\hat{\tau}$).
This design allows for both planning from scratch and interactive trajectory refinement, supporting mutually aligned world and action generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DAWN was conducted on multiple autonomous driving benchmarks, including NAVSIM v1, NAVSIM v2 (appendix), and nuScenes, with performance compared against various perception-based and perception-free methods.

#### 4.1. Performance on NAVSIM v1

- DAWN achieved an overall PDMS (Planning Driver Model Score) of 89.1, which was reported as the best among perception-free models and competitive with perception-based methods.
- It obtained the best No-at-fault Collisions (NC) score, Ego Progress (EP), and Time-to-Collision (TTC) score, suggesting the generation of safe, progressive, and smooth trajectories.
- A higher-resolution input (512x256) version of DAWN improved PDMS from 87.9 (DAWN* at 256x256) to 89.1, indicating the benefit of richer visual inputs.

#### 4.2. Performance on nuScenes

- DAWN demonstrated strong performance in end-to-end planning, achieving low L2 errors for trajectory prediction and low collision rates.
- **Trajectory Accuracy (L2 error)**: DAWN achieved the lowest L2 error at all horizons (1s, 2s, 3s), with an average L2 error of 0.33 m. This represents a reduction from 0.47 m by the prior strongest method, WorldRFT. Gains were more pronounced at mid- (0.31 m at 2s) and long-horizon (0.52 m at 3s) predictions.
- **Collision Rate**: DAWN also achieved the best average collision rate (0.11%), with leading or tied-leading results across all evaluated horizons, indicating improved safety.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Contribution of Key Components**:
 - Adding the Auto-Encoder Resampler alone yielded minimal change in PDMS (from 82.9 to 82.8).
 - Introducing the World Predictor led to a noticeable gain, increasing PDMS to 85.2, suggesting that explicit latent future rollout provides useful planning context.
 - Enabling interactive world-action updates further improved PDMS from 85.2 to 87.9, supporting the hypothesis that recursive refinement between world and action hypotheses contributes to performance.

- **Number of Interactive Rounds**: Performance steadily improved as the number of interactive rounds increased from 1 to 4, with PDMS rising from 85.2 to 87.9. Metrics like Ego Progress (EP) and Time-to-Collision (TTC) also improved. Performance saturated and slightly decreased after 4 rounds, indicating an optimal trade-off at 4 rounds.

- **Number of Resampler Tokens**: Increasing the number of output tokens in the Auto-Encoder Resampler from 16 to 64 improved PDMS from 82.8 to 83.2. However, this also increased inference latency more than threefold (from 331.3 ms to 963.6 ms), suggesting a trade-off between latent capacity, planning-relevant information preservation, and computational efficiency.

- **World-Action Coupling**: Ablations demonstrated that removing either the World→Action dependency (predicted world conditioning action denoising) or the Action→World dependency (action hypothesis conditioning world rollout) degraded performance. Removing World→Action reduced PDMS from 87.9 to 81.6, and removing Action→World reduced it to 84.9. This supports the concept that mutual constraint between world evolution and action generation is beneficial.

- **World Rollout Horizon**: Zero rollout performed worse (PDMS of 82.8) than short latent rollouts. Most of the performance gain was observed with a 2-3 second latent rollout (PDMS ~87.3-87.5), approaching the full 4-second rollout result (PDMS of 87.9). This suggests that a full future simulator is not required; a compact, action-relevant dynamic hypothesis is sufficient. The "w/o Int." column showed that rollout alone was less effective than when combined with interactive action refinement.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Visualizations in various urban scenarios (intersections, narrow streets, curved junctions) indicated that DAWN generated trajectories that were consistent with human driving behavior and local road topology, maintaining feasibility and safety in constrained environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces World-Action Interactive Models (WAIMs) as a conceptual framework, proposing that a reciprocal and iterative refinement between future world states and actions is crucial for effective decision-making in complex, interactive environments. The instantiation of WAIM through DAWN demonstrates a potential advancement in the field of autonomous driving and embodied AI.

The significance of DAWN lies in its reported performance improvements across key autonomous driving benchmarks. The quantitative results on NAVSIM v1 and nuScenes suggest that DAWN can generate trajectories with improved accuracy (lower L2 error on nuScenes) and enhanced safety (better collision rates on nuScenes, higher TTC and NC on NAVSIM v1). These results indicate that recursively coupling world prediction with action generation can lead to more precise and safety-aware planning.

The methodology's emphasis on operating in a compact semantic latent space and employing a short explicit latent rollout addresses the computational challenges associated with full pixel-space future prediction while still retaining the benefits of explicit future reasoning. This design aims to offer a practical balance between predictive power and inference-time efficiency, which is a relevant consideration for real-world deployment of autonomous systems. The ablation studies further reinforce that the interactive coupling, rather than merely latent representations or passive world prediction, is a contributing factor to the observed performance gains.

Potential impacts include:
- **Improved Safety and Reliability**: By enabling actions to influence predicted futures and vice-versa, WAIMs could lead to more robust planning, anticipating and mitigating potential safety-critical interactions more effectively. This could be particularly impactful in multi-agent scenarios where agent actions significantly alter environmental dynamics.
- **Enhanced Actionability of World Models**: The framework suggests a path toward truly actionable world models, where the predicted future is not merely an observation but an active component that adapts to the agent's intent and consequences.
- **Efficiency in Complex Environments**: The use of a compact latent space and short rollout horizon offers a method to incorporate dynamic future reasoning without incurring the high computational cost of long-horizon or pixel-level predictions, potentially making such models more viable for real-time applications.

However, the paper also acknowledges limitations. The WAIM formulation does not currently provide formal convergence or safety guarantees. DAWN's short latent rollout, while efficient, may be insufficient for scenarios requiring very long-range anticipation or highly extended multi-agent interaction. Furthermore, the learned latent representations are less interpretable than explicit scene-level predictions, potentially complicating diagnosis of rare safety-critical failures. The data-driven nature of the approach means performance is dependent on the coverage and quality of training data, and the multi-stage training process remains computationally intensive. Therefore, while this work represents a research step toward more actionable world models, it is not presented as a solution for immediate unrestricted real-world deployment.
