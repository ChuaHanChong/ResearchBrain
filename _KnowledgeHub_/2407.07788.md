# BiGym: A Demo-Driven Mobile Bi-Manual Manipulation Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> The Dyson Robot Learning Lab introduced BiGym, a demo-driven mobile bi-manual manipulation benchmark comprising 40 household tasks for a humanoid robot. This benchmark provides 50 human-collected, multi-modal demonstrations per task and revealed that state-of-the-art imitation learning and demo-driven reinforcement learning algorithms largely struggle with its long-horizon and complex object interaction challenges.

## Problem

- Existing robotics benchmarks often use dense, shaped rewards and are not well-suited for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- Demonstration-driven benchmarks frequently rely on unnatural, motion planner-generated trajectories, which lack the diversity and noise of human data.
- Current benchmarks often focus on fixed single-arm manipulation or locomotion, overlooking the complexities of mobile bi-manual control in household settings.

## Method

![[2407.07788-method.png]]
*BiGym features 40 diverse mobile bi-manual manipulation tasks ranging from simple reaching to complex dishwasher loading and sandwich preparation.*

- BiGym provides 40 diverse mobile bi-manual manipulation tasks in home environments using a Unitree H1 humanoid robot simulated in MuJoCo.
- The benchmark features 50 human-collected, multi-modal demonstrations per task, obtained via VR tele-operation, offering more realistic data than planner-generated alternatives.
- It supports flexible action modes (whole-body or bi-manual) and employs sparse rewards to reflect real-world robotic challenges.

## Results

- State-of-the-art imitation learning algorithms like ACT and Diffusion Policy achieved up to 100% success on simpler BiGym tasks such as `dishwasher close` and `drawer top close`.
- Both IL and demo-driven RL algorithms demonstrated 0% success rates or very low single-digit performance on complex multi-object manipulation tasks like `stack blocks` and long-horizon sequences such as `dishwasher unload plates long`.
- Demo-driven reinforcement learning algorithms generally performed poorly across the benchmark, achieving non-zero success rates only on the simplest reaching and closing tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BiGym tasks are highly challenging for current state-of-the-art imitation learning and demo-driven reinforcement learning algorithms.
> - While methods like Action Chunking Transformers and Diffusion Policies show some success on simpler single-step tasks, they, along with all demo-driven RL methods, struggle significantly with long-horizon, multi-object, and mobile manipulation tasks.
> - The poor performance of benchmarked algorithms suggests a need for more sophisticated learning architectures capable of handling partial observability, complex multi-modal demonstrations, and robust whole-body control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Machine learning benchmarks play a fundamental role in evaluating algorithmic progress across various domains, exemplified by ImageNet for computer vision, KITTI for autonomous driving, and SQuAD for natural language processing. In robotics, benchmarks such as OpenAI Gym, DeepMind Control Suite, and MetaWorld have historically facilitated the development of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, primarily focusing on tasks with dense, shaped reward functions.

However, the authors identify limitations in these existing robotics benchmarks, particularly their applicability to long-horizon manipulation tasks where designing accurate and non-exploitable reward functions is challenging. This challenge has contributed to the increasing popularity of demonstration-driven methods in robot learning, encompassing both imitation learning (IL) and demo-driven RL.

While benchmarks like RLBench were developed to support demonstration-driven research by providing expert demonstrations for single-arm fixed manipulation tasks, they often rely on motion planners to generate trajectories. Such planner-generated demonstrations can be unnatural, exhibiting narrow trajectory distributions or artificial movements compared to human-collected data, which can be noisy and multi-modal. Furthermore, the research community has observed a plateau in performance on many RLBench tasks, indicating a need for new, more challenging environments.

Concurrent efforts like RoboCasa offer realistic environments with human demonstrations but are limited to single-arm tasks. Other benchmarks for humanoid robots, such as LocoMujoco, focus primarily on locomotion, while HumanoidBench, though including manipulation, primarily benchmarks RL algorithms with task-specific shaped rewards.

BiGym positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified gaps. It introduces a benchmark specifically designed for mobile bi-manual manipulation with a humanoid embodiment, featuring a diverse set of tasks that are challenging for current state-of-the-art algorithms. Critically, BiGym provides human-collected, multi-modal demonstrations for all tasks, aiming to offer a more realistic data distribution than planner-generated alternatives. By focusing on sparse rewards and offering flexible action modes (whole-body vs. bi-manual), BiGym seeks to facilitate research into robust learning agents capable of operating in complex, real-world home environments, thereby pushing the boundaries beyond fixed, single-arm, or solely locomotion-focused robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce BiGym, a new benchmark and learning environment specifically tailored for demo-driven mobile bi-manual robotic manipulation. This objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from identified limitations in the current robot learning research landscape:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Traditional benchmarks often focus on pure reinforcement learning with dense, shaped rewards, which are difficult to define accurately for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks. When reward functions are misspecified or overly dense, agents may learn behaviors that exploit the reward structure rather than achieving the desired task outcome robustly. BiGym aims to provide a platform with sparse rewards, reflecting real-world scenarios where continuous, precise feedback is often unavailable.

2. **Enhancing Realism of Demonstrations:** Many existing demo-driven benchmarks, such as RLBench, generate expert demonstrations using motion planners. While efficient for data generation, these synthetic trajectories often lack the natural noise, variability, and multi-modality inherent in human movements. This can lead to algorithms that perform well in simulation but struggle when exposed to real-world robot data. BiGym is motivated by the need for more realistic, human-collected demonstrations that better reflect the diversity and noisiness of real robot trajectories, thus fostering the development of more robust and generalizable policies.

3. **Introducing New and Challenging Task Domains:** The robotic manipulation community has observed performance plateaus on many established single-arm manipulation tasks. There is a recognized need for new, more complex challenges that push the capabilities of current algorithms. BiGym addresses this by focusing on mobile bi-manual manipulation with a humanoid embodiment in home environments. This domain introduces significant complexities, including coordinating two arms, integrating locomotion with manipulation, and interacting with articulated objects in cluttered settings.

4. **Enabling Research in Mobile Bi-Manual Manipulation:** Mobile bi-manual manipulation presents a substantially more complex task space compared to fixed single-arm settings. This complexity arises from the high degrees of freedom, the potential for diverse solution paths (multi-modality), and the partial observability of the environment. BiGym explicitly aims to provide tasks that necessitate advanced capabilities in object recognition, precise manipulation, locomotion, and potentially task-level planning, which are critical for deploying robots in unstructured human environments.

5. **Supporting Diverse Algorithmic Approaches:** The benchmark is designed to evaluate both imitation learning (IL) and demo-driven reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms. By providing a consistent set of 40 tasks with sparse rewards and a fixed number of human demonstrations (50 per task), BiGym offers a standardized environment for comparing and advancing different learning paradigms.

6. **Investigating Whole-Body vs. Bi-Manual Control:** Roboticists are still exploring optimal action modes for complex embodiments. BiGym motivates research into this area by offering flexible control configurations, allowing users to switch between a 'whole-body mode' that considers joint locomotion and manipulation, and a 'bi-manual mode' that simplifies locomotion with predefined controllers to focus specifically on upper-body mobile manipulation skills. This separation aims to facilitate targeted research on different aspects of humanoid control.

In summary, BiGym's objectives are to fill critical gaps in robot learning benchmarks by providing a challenging, realistic, and diverse set of tasks for mobile bi-manual manipulation, supported by high-fidelity human-collected demonstrations, to accelerate the development of more capable and adaptable robotic agents for real-world applications, particularly in household assistance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BiGym is constructed as a demo-driven mobile bi-manual manipulation benchmark comprising 40 diverse tasks set in home environments. The methodology involves several key components: the simulation platform, the humanoid embodiment, task design, observation and action spaces, and a human demonstration dataset collection and management system.

**Simulation Platform:**
The benchmark is built on the MuJoCo physics engine, utilizing a custom object-oriented API based on `dm_control`. The simulation environments are designed to be realistic, incorporating 46 high-quality assets, including rigid objects and articulated models like a dishwasher and customizable kitchen modules. These models feature decimated meshes for performance, separated moving parts for articulated objects, and convex collision meshes. The API follows standard Gymnasium APIs, facilitating integration for training IL and RL agents. The simulation performance ranges from approximately 400 to 1400 FPS, depending on the number of cameras and action mode used.

**Humanoid Embodiment:**
The chosen robot is the Unitree H1 humanoid, augmented with two Robotiq 2F-85 grippers, each attached with an actuated wrist joint. This provides a platform for bi-manual manipulation.

**Observation Spaces:**
The robot is equipped with three RGB-D cameras: one on the forehead, one on the left wrist, and one on the right wrist. These cameras provide RGB and depth images, supporting various vision-based algorithms. The default image resolution is 84x84. In addition to visual data, proprioceptive states (`s_proprio`) are provided. In 'whole-body mode', `s_proprio` includes joint angle positions and velocities for all 37 robot joints, plus 2 gripper opening amounts (total 76 dimensions). In 'bi-manual mode', where the lower body is controlled by a floating base, `s_proprio` simplifies to 29 joint positions and velocities, 4 dimensions for the floating base (x, y, z, θ), and 2 gripper opening amounts (total 64 dimensions).

**Action Modes:**
BiGym offers flexible action modes to cater to different research focuses:
- **Whole-Body Action Mode:** Allows full control over all humanoid joints (23 dimensions: 10 for arms, 10 for legs, 1 for torso, 2 for grippers). This mode facilitates research into joint locomotion and manipulation.
- **Bi-Manual Action Mode:** Simplifies locomotion by controlling the lower body as an omni-directional floating base via classic controllers. The action space focuses on upper-body control (16 dimensions: 10 for arms, 4 for floating base delta actions, 2 for grippers). This mode is intended for researchers primarily interested in upper-body manipulation. Both modes support either delta or absolute actions.

**Task Design and Reward Functions:**
BiGym includes 40 distinct mobile bi-manual manipulation tasks categorized into:
- **Reach Target Tasks:** Three variants testing single-arm, multi-modal (either arm), and dual-arm reaching.
- **Table-Top Manipulation Tasks:** Involving interactions with rigid objects like stacking blocks, moving plates, and flipping objects.
- **Dishwasher Tasks:** A series of tasks requiring interaction with an articulated dishwasher, ranging from opening/closing doors and trays to loading/unloading various kitchenware, including long-horizon multi-step tasks.
- **Kitchen Counter Tasks:** Involving complex interactions with articulated kitchen elements like drawers and wall cupboards, and manipulating items like boxes, saucepans, and sandwiches.
All tasks are formulated with sparse rewards, where a reward of 1 is given upon successful completion of the task, and 0 otherwise. Success detectors are defined based on geometric conditions (e.g., distance to target, collision states, object orientation, joint angles).

**Human Demonstration Datasets:**
A core aspect of BiGym is the provision of human-collected demonstrations for all 40 tasks.
- **Collection Pipeline:** Demonstrations are collected by tele-operating the H1 robot in simulation using VR devices (Valve Index headset and controllers). The VR headset controls the robot's body position and orientation, while the controllers operate the arms via inverse kinematics and gripper reorientation.
- **Down-Sampling:** Demonstrations are recorded at the physics calculation frequency (500 Hz) and can be down-sampled to user-specified frequencies (20-500 Hz) to allow flexibility in action frequency.
- **Management System:** Demonstrations are stored in a lightweight format (control signals only) to conserve storage. A tool allows users to pull these demonstrations and replay them to generate full demonstrations with user-specified observations (RGB, depth images), which are then cached locally.
- **Tools:** BiGym provides a "demo player" for managing (downloading, deleting, verifying, replaying, converting, re-recording) demonstrations and a "demo recorder" for streamlining the VR-based data collection process. The demonstrations are designed to be noisy and multi-modal, reflecting real-world variability, with 50 demonstrations provided for each task.

**Benchmarking Validation:**
To validate BiGym's usability and establish baseline performance, the authors benchmarked seven state-of-the-art algorithms:
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** Behaviour Cloning (BC), Action Chunking Transformers (ACT), and Diffusion Policies.
- **Demo-Driven Reinforcement Learning (RL):** DrQV2, Advantage Weighted Actor-Critic (AWAC), Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), and Coarse-to-fine Deep Q-Network (CQN).
The evaluation involved running algorithms with a common set of hyperparameters for 150K (IL) or 100K (RL) training steps, using a frame stack of 4, Adam optimizer, and a batch size of 256. For RL algorithms, a 50% demonstration replay buffer ratio was used. Performance was reported as success rates averaged over 50 episodes and the last three checkpoints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of state-of-the-art IL and demo-driven RL algorithms on the 40 BiGym tasks yielded several findings regarding the benchmark's difficulty and the current capabilities of these methods:

**Overall Task Difficulty:**
- BiGym tasks proved to be challenging for the evaluated algorithms, with many tasks showing low or zero success rates across the board. This indicates that the benchmark effectively identifies current limitations in robot learning for mobile bi-manual manipulation.

**Performance of Imitation Learning (IL) Algorithms:**
- **Superiority of ACT and Diffusion Policy:** Action Chunking Transformers (ACT) and Diffusion Policy generally achieved higher success rates compared to Behaviour Cloning (BC) and all demo-driven RL baselines on a majority of tasks. For instance, ACT achieved 100% success on tasks like `reach target single`, `dishwasher close`, `dishwasher open trays`, `drawer top close`, `drawers open all`, `drawers close all`, and `wall cupboard close`. Diffusion Policy also achieved 100% on `drawer top close` and `wall cupboard open`.
- **Challenges Remain for IL:** Despite their relative strength, even ACT and Diffusion Policy struggled significantly with several complex tasks. Tasks requiring high-precision multi-object manipulation (e.g., `stack blocks`, `move two plates`) and long-horizon tasks (e.g., `dishwasher unload plates long`, `dishwasher unload cups long`, `store groceries lower/upper`) consistently resulted in 0% success rates or very low single-digit percentages for all IL algorithms. This suggests that current IL approaches may lack robustness for complex object interactions and hierarchical planning.
- **Impact of Policy Representation:** The performance difference between BC and ACT/Diffusion Policy suggests that more powerful policy classes, such as those based on generative representation learning (CVAE for ACT, Diffusion models for Diffusion Policy) combined with expressive architectures (transformers, UNets), are more capable of handling the complex, multi-modal, and noisy human demonstrations provided in BiGym compared to simpler CNN+MLP architectures used by BC.

**Performance of Demo-Driven Reinforcement Learning (RL) Algorithms:**
- **Limited Success:** Demo-driven RL algorithms (DrQV2, AWAC, IQL, CQN) generally performed poorly, achieving non-zero success rates predominantly on simpler tasks with minimal object interaction, such as `reach target single`, `reach target multi modal`, `drawer top close`, `drawers close all`, and `wall cupboard close`.
- **Struggles with Complexity and Sparse Rewards:** For most BiGym tasks, particularly those involving complex manipulations, long horizons, or mobile base navigation with objects, the RL algorithms failed to achieve any success. For instance, CQN, which has shown strong performance in fixed single-arm demo-driven RL setups, struggled to solve most BiGym tasks.
- **Hypothesized Reasons for RL Failure:** The authors hypothesize that the combination of a mobile base making meaningful state exploration difficult (e.g., erroneous base actions causing loss of object view) and the challenge of learning effective value functions in long-horizon, sparse-reward settings contributes to the poor performance of RL agents.

**Specific Challenges Identified:**
- **Mobile Manipulation of Articulated/Rigid Objects:** Tasks demanding precise interaction with articulated or rigid-body objects while the mobile base is in motion proved highly challenging. Examples include `move two plates`, `cupboards open all`, and `stack blocks`. The difficulty stems from accurate grasping during movement and effective belief estimation in partially observable environments.
- **Long Task-Horizon:** Tasks composed of multiple sub-tasks requiring sequential planning and execution (e.g., `dishwasher unload cups long`, `put cups`) were beyond the capabilities of all benchmarked model-free agents, which lack inherent task-level reasoning mechanisms.
- **Complex Policy Space and Multi-Modal Demonstrations:** The multi-modal nature of human demonstrations and the complex state-action spaces in BiGym tasks appear to necessitate more sophisticated agent architectures beyond standard CNN-MLP models.

In summary, BiGym has established itself as a challenging benchmark. While advanced IL methods show some promise on single-step or shorter-horizon tasks, both IL and demo-driven RL algorithms currently struggle with the intricacies of mobile bi-manual manipulation, particularly for tasks involving precise multi-object interactions, long horizons, and navigation in cluttered home environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BiGym introduces a significant contribution to the field of robot learning through its novel focus and rigorous design, with several potential impacts:

1. **Advancing Mobile Bi-Manual Manipulation:** By specifically targeting mobile bi-manual manipulation with a humanoid embodiment in home environments, BiGym addresses a frontier area in robotics that is crucial for developing general-purpose domestic robots. It provides a standardized platform to accelerate research beyond the more constrained single-arm, fixed-base manipulation scenarios that have largely dominated prior benchmarks.

2. **Promoting Realistic Algorithm Development:** The use of human-collected, noisy, and multi-modal demonstrations is a key feature. This approach moves beyond synthetic, planner-generated demonstrations, offering a more realistic representation of real-world robot trajectory data. Consequently, algorithms developed and evaluated on BiGym are likely to be more robust and transferable to physical robot systems operating in unstructured environments, reducing the sim-to-real gap.

3. **Driving Innovation in Learning Architectures:** The observed performance gaps of current state-of-the-art IL and demo-driven RL algorithms on BiGym's challenging tasks highlight the need for improved learning architectures. Specifically, the difficulties with partial observability, complex multi-modal task spaces, and long horizons with sparse rewards are expected to motivate research into:
 - **Better policy representations:** For effectively approximating multi-modal and noisy human demonstrations.
 - **Belief estimation mechanisms:** For handling partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) in mobile manipulation contexts.
 - **Inter-arm collaboration modes:** To exploit the full potential of bi-manual systems on mobile platforms.
 - **Whole-body motion planning:** To integrate locomotion and manipulation more effectively and efficiently.
 - **Hierarchical planning methods:** For addressing the long-horizon tasks that current model-free agents struggle with.

4. **Standardized Evaluation and Comparison:** BiGym provides a consistent and open-source environment (code available on the project website) for researchers to develop, benchmark, and compare new algorithms. This standardization is critical for transparently tracking progress, identifying leading approaches, and understanding the strengths and weaknesses of different methodologies in a complex domain.

5. **Relevance to Future Home Assistance Robotics:** The choice of household tasks and a humanoid embodiment directly aligns with the long-term goal of developing intelligent robots for home assistance. By simulating scenarios like kitchen cleaning, object storage, and meal preparation, BiGym contributes directly to the research required for robots to safely and effectively interact within human living spaces.

In conclusion, BiGym is positioned to be a foundational benchmark that can significantly influence the trajectory of robot learning research by presenting a comprehensive and challenging testbed for the next generation of mobile bi-manual manipulation algorithms, ultimately paving the way for more capable and adaptable robotic systems in real-world applications.
