# NovaPlan: Zero-Shot Long-Horizon Manipulation via Closed-Loop Video Language Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> NovaPlan presents a hierarchical framework for zero-shot long-horizon robotic manipulation, integrating closed-loop VLM planning and video generation with a hybrid object/hand flow for robust execution. This enables autonomous multi-step task solving and error recovery, demonstrating improved success rates on tasks like four-layer block stacking and achieving 100% success in hidden object search.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation struggled with zero-shot generalization for novel tasks, often requiring extensive task-specific programming or costly data collection for imitation learning.
- Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based planners often lack the fine-grained 3D spatial awareness and dynamic reasoning necessary for precise physical interaction.
- Existing video-driven planning methods are often open-loop, brittle to real-world inaccuracies, suffer from an embodiment gap, and show temporal inconsistencies over long durations, limiting long-horizon task capabilities.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical framework== is employed, combining a high-level ==closed-loop VLM-based planner== and a low-level execution planner.
- The high-level planner utilizes a ==generate-then-verify tree search== for task decomposition, video rollout, validation, and a ==closed-loop verify-and-recover mechanism== for autonomous error correction and re-planning.
- The low-level planner translates video plans into robot actions using a ==hybrid flow mechanism== that dynamically switches between object-centric 6-DoF trajectory extraction and human hand-pose-guided execution, especially for robust action generation under occlusion or for non-prehensile recovery.

## Results

- Achieved a **70%** success rate on four-layer block stacking, significantly outperforming NovaFlow (**30%** for 4th block) and π0.5 (fails beyond 2 layers), demonstrating robust long-horizon task performance.
- Consistently obtained higher success rates on short-horizon manipulation tasks (e.g., **90%** for Block Insertion vs. **80%** for NovaFlow), validating the efficacy of the hybrid flow mechanism in improving execution stability.
- Successfully completed complex multi-object multi-stage assembly sequences on the Functional Manipulation Benchmark (FMB) and demonstrated robust non-prehensile error recovery (e.g., finger poking) when objects became stuck, showcasing zero-shot capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating high-level semantic reasoning from VLMs with low-level dynamic scene understanding from video generation models within a closed-loop framework is crucial for robust long-horizon robotic tasks.
> - Dynamically switching between object-centric and human-hand-centric flow extraction (hybrid flow) significantly improves execution stability and robustness to occlusion and noisy tracking during robot manipulation.
> - Closed-loop verification, failure detection, and autonomous error recovery mechanisms (including non-prehensile actions like poking) are essential for achieving reliability and reducing human intervention in real-world, multi-step robotic operations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

NovaPlan addresses critical challenges at the intersection of zero-shot robotic manipulation, vision-language models (VLMs), and video generation. The broader research landscape for robotic manipulation is characterized by a push towards more generalist robots capable of performing novel tasks without extensive task-specific training.

Traditionally, robotic manipulation has relied heavily on either explicit programming for specific tasks or extensive data collection for imitation learning. However, these approaches suffer from poor generalization (difficulty with zero-shot generalization) and high data acquisition costs, respectively. The advent of large language models (LLMs) and VLMs has spurred interest in leveraging their high-level semantic reasoning capabilities for robotic planning. Methods like VLM-based planners (e.g., MOKA) can decompose tasks into sub-goals but often lack the fine-grained 3D spatial awareness and dynamic reasoning crucial for precise physical interaction. Conversely, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., π0.5) learn direct control policies from vast datasets of real-world robot trajectories but are limited by the scalability and embodiment gap associated with collecting such data.

A parallel trend involves using video generation models as "world simulators" to predict future states and generate plans. These models can synthesize physically plausible interactions, offering a rich representation for dynamic reasoning. However, existing video-driven planning methods face several limitations:
1. **Embodiment Gap:** Directly translating synthesized video motions into robot actions is challenging due to structural mismatches between generated motion and robot morphology.
2. **Temporal Inconsistencies:** Video models can suffer from hallucinations and temporal inconsistencies, especially over long durations.
3. **Brittleness:** Real-world execution is prone to failures due to perception inaccuracies (occlusion, depth errors) or geometric warping in generated videos.
4. **Open-Loop Nature:** Most video-based methods operate open-loop, meaning they lack mechanisms for monitoring execution or recovering from failures, severely limiting their applicability to long-horizon tasks. NovaFlow, a predecessor mentioned in this paper, represents an advancement in video-based zero-shot manipulation but still struggles with robustness under occlusion and lacks closed-loop recovery.

NovaPlan positions itself as a unifying framework that bridges these gaps. It integrates the high-level semantic reasoning of VLMs for task decomposition and error recovery with the dynamic scene understanding offered by video generation models for low-level action extraction. Crucially, it introduces a closed-loop verification and recovery mechanism, a hybrid object/hand flow extraction strategy, and geometric calibration, addressing the brittleness and open-loop limitations of prior work. By doing so, NovaPlan aims to advance zero-shot long-horizon manipulation, a critical step towards more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of NovaPlan is to enable **zero-shot long-horizon robotic manipulation** in real-world environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Unify High-Level Reasoning with Low-Level Interaction:** The authors seek to integrate the abstract semantic understanding of vision-language models (VLMs) with the precise physical interaction capabilities required for robotic tasks. This addresses the common disconnect where VLMs excel at high-level planning but struggle with grounded execution, and vice-versa for low-level control.
2. **Achieve Robustness for Long-Horizon Tasks:** Complex, multi-step tasks are inherently prone to accumulation of small errors. NovaPlan aims to provide a robust framework that can manage these challenges, specifically by enabling robots to "imagine" and execute complex manipulation plans over extended durations without prior demonstrations.
3. **Enable Closed-Loop Reasoning and Error Recovery:** A major limitation of previous video-driven and VLM-based planning methods is their open-loop nature. NovaPlan aims to introduce a closed-loop feedback mechanism that allows the robot to monitor its execution, detect failures, and autonomously recover through re-planning, thereby enhancing reliability in real-world deployment.
4. **Improve Low-Level Action Extraction Stability:** Existing methods that extract robot actions directly from videos often suffer from issues like the embodiment gap, perception inaccuracies, and tracking drift. NovaPlan is motivated to create a more stable and geometrically grounded method for extracting executable robot trajectories from generated videos, even under challenging conditions like occlusion.
5. **Leverage Human Kinematic Priors:** Recognizing the limitations of purely object-centric tracking, the paper aims to explore the utility of human hand motion as a robust kinematic prior to guide robot actions, particularly in scenarios where the target object is occluded or difficult to track.
6. **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Generalization:** A core motivation is to achieve complex manipulation without any task-specific demonstrations or training, pushing the boundaries of generalizable robotics. This includes exhibiting dexterous error recovery behaviors like non-prehensile poking.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NovaPlan is a hierarchical framework that unifies closed-loop VLM and video planning with geometrically grounded robot execution. Its methodology is divided into a high-level closed-loop video language planning component and a low-level execution planner.

#### 3.1. Closed-Loop Video Language Planning

This high-level component functions as a `generate-then-verify tree search` and orchestrates the overall task. It ensures physical feasibility and semantic consistency through a series of iterative steps:

1. **Task Decomposition:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-5.2, analyzes the high-level task goal, current visual observation, and action history. It proposes multiple language-based sub-goal candidates. The VLM autonomously selects the planning horizon (greedy for independent steps, strategic for coupled tasks) based on task understanding and geometric dependencies, using structured prompts to enforce physical constraints and ordering rules.
2. **Video Rollout:** For each sub-goal candidate, a video generation model (Wan2.2 and Veo 3.1) synthesizes multiple candidate video plans (visual rollouts) simulating potential physical outcomes.
3. **Validation and Selection:** The VLM acts as a critic, evaluating these rollouts based on four key metrics:
 - **Target:** Verifies the correct object is manipulated.
 - **Physics:** Assesses physical plausibility (e.g., no teleportation, deformation).
 - **Motion:** Confirms motion direction/magnitude matches the language command using 2D object flow visualization.
 - **Result:** Ensures the final state aligns with the desired sub-task outcome.
 Candidates are ranked based on a summed score, and the top-ranked ones are selected, allowing for a pruned search space in strategic mode.
4. **Execution Re-grounding:** Before each execution step in the real world, a new video reference is generated based on the updated real-world observation and the selected language action. This ensures the visual plan is geometrically grounded in the current environment.
5. **Verify and Recover:** After each robot action, the VLM critic closes the loop. It compares the real start state, current real state, and the target state (last frame of the generated video plan). If a failure is detected (e.g., "grasp slip"), the VLM analyzes the failure mode and synthesizes a corrective action. This recovery generates a local repair command (e.g., "nudge the object") to transition the scene back to the original target state, allowing the high-level plan to resume. This mechanism allows "improvisation" for runtime failures.

#### 3.2. Low-Level Planner

The low-level planner is responsible for translating the selected video plan into executable robot actions, using a hybrid flow mechanism.

1. **Object Flow:** This method extracts the 3D trajectory (6-DoF motion) of the target object from the generated video.
 - **3D Geometry Reconstruction:** It uses MoGe2 for metric depth prediction, refined by Consistent Video Depth (CVD) to ensure temporal coherence and eliminate flickering. RANSAC-based affine calibration aligns generated depth with real-world metric scale.
 - **Keypoint Tracking:** Per-frame object masks are obtained, and K keypoints on the target object are sampled and tracked in 3D using TAPIP3D.
 - **6-DoF Motion Recovery:** The Kabsch algorithm computes rigid transformations (rotation and translation) between adjacent frames from the 3D keypoint flow.
2. **Hand Flow:** When object tracking becomes unreliable (e.g., heavy occlusion, large rotations), NovaPlan dynamically switches to hand flow. The switching logic uses a maximum allowable rotation magnitude (θ_max = 45°) for object flow; if exceeded, hand flow is activated.
 - **Hand Pose Estimation:** HaMeR is used to extract the human hand pose (MANO mesh) trajectory from the generated video.
 - **Geometric Calibration:** A dual-anchor calibration routine grounds the raw hand pose estimations, which often suffer from scale inaccuracy and projective drift.
 - **Interaction Interval Detection:** Contact onset (t_start) and end (t_end) are identified by detecting significant motion in the object mask.
 - **Metric Scale Recovery:** At t_start, an isotropic scale factor is estimated by snapping fingertips (selected based on projection into the object mask) to the nearest 3D object point. For non-prehensile actions, explicit contact with the designated finger is enforced.
 - **Projective Drift Compensation:** A drift offset is computed at release (t_end) and linearly ramped across a short window to correct temporal distortions.
 - **SE(3) Trajectory Computation:** Calibrated hand positions are converted into a 6-DoF rigid body trajectory using a palm frame constructed from 3D landmarks.
3. **Flow to Robot Action:**
 - **Object Flow:** A grasp proposal network (GraspGen) selects an optimal grasp pose, establishing a static transformation between the object and end-effector frames. The recovered 6-DoF object motion is then transformed into target robot end-effector poses.
 - **Hand Flow:** The initial contact pose guides the grasp proposal network to establish the grasp. Subsequently, the end-effector directly follows the calibrated 6-DoF hand pose trajectory, leveraging the hand as a kinematic prior.
4. **Non-Prehensile Correction:** For specific error recovery scenarios (e.g., nudging a stuck object), NovaPlan extends its grounding procedure. The VLM annotates a single contact region on the current observation, and a detailed prompt P is generated for the video model. The video model then generates a recovery video conditioned on the annotated start image, prompt P, and target image (last frame), ensuring the robot’s poke fingertip maintains contact even with generated hand shape deviations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NovaPlan demonstrates robust zero-shot performance across a variety of challenging long-horizon manipulation tasks and the Functional Manipulation Benchmark (FMB). The experiments aim to answer three key questions regarding long-horizon robustness, hybrid flow efficacy, and zero-shot capabilities.

#### 4.1. Long-Horizon Task Solving (Q1: Long-Horizon Robustness)

NovaPlan was evaluated on three multi-step tasks with dependencies: Four-layer Block Stacking, Color Sorting, and Hidden Object Search. It was compared against NovaFlow (video-based), π0.5 (VLA model), and MOKA (VLM-based planning), with baselines provided with an "oracle" high-level plan for fair comparison of sub-task execution.

- **Four-layer Block Stacking:** NovaPlan achieved a 70% success rate for stacking all four blocks, significantly outperforming NovaFlow (30% for 4th block) and π0.5 (fails beyond 2 layers), which suffered from object flow instability and limited generalization, respectively. The performance drop for all methods in later stages highlights the increasing precision required.
- **Color Sorting:** NovaPlan performed well but experienced some failures (e.g., 60% final success) primarily due to inaccurate pose extraction from depth estimation errors, particularly for a tight-fitting yellow block. This points to a bottleneck in underlying perception models.
- **Hidden Object Search:** NovaPlan achieved 100% success, matching NovaFlow, and significantly outperforming π0.5 (better at drawer opening than object retrieval) and MOKA (fails to grasp drawer handle). This demonstrates NovaPlan's robust exploratory and conditional planning capabilities.

These results strongly affirm NovaPlan's ability to robustly perform multi-step tasks in a zero-shot setting, primarily due to its closed-loop planning and recovery mechanisms.

#### 4.2. Short-Horizon Task Solving (Q2: Hybrid Flow Efficacy)

To assess the efficacy of its hybrid flow mechanism, NovaPlan was compared directly against NovaFlow on a suite of single-step tasks (Block Insertion, Water Plant, Open Drawer, Open Lid).

- NovaPlan consistently achieved higher success rates across all tasks (e.g., 90% for Block Insertion vs. 80% for NovaFlow, 100% for Open Drawer vs. 90% for NovaFlow).
- The improved stability is attributed to the dynamic switching to hand flow when object flow becomes unreliable due to self-occlusion or noisy tracking.
- Failures still occurred due to poor depth estimation affecting both keypoint tracking and hand scale calibration, indicating that while hybrid flow helps, fundamental perception limitations persist.

This confirms that generated human hands serve as effective references for robots, and incorporating hand flow significantly improves execution stability over purely object-centric methods, particularly in scenarios prone to occlusion.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot FMB Solving (Q3: Zero-Shot Capability)

NovaPlan was deployed on the challenging Functional Manipulation Benchmark (FMB) Multi-Object Multi-Stage Task Assembly 1, including a variant requiring error recovery. Baselines failed to complete even a single step, so only NovaPlan's performance and failure modes were reported.

- **Complex Assembly and Precision:** NovaPlan successfully completed long-horizon assembly sequences requiring millimeter-level precision, demonstrating its capability to handle intricate contact-rich behaviors.
- **Non-Prehensile Error Recovery:** A key finding was NovaPlan's ability to perform non-prehensile error recovery (e.g., finger poking) when objects became stuck. This leverages the hand flow extraction strategy and VLM's ability to generate targeted corrective actions.
- **Challenges and Bottlenecks:**
 - **Video Generation for Irregular Shapes:** Current single-view video generation models (Wan 2.2, Veo 3.1) struggled to produce physically feasible videos for irregularly shaped FMB objects, especially for complex recovery motions. This necessitated re-generation and limited overall success.
 - **Perception Accuracy in Recovery:** In recovery modes where object displacement and hand motion are small, keypoint flow and pose estimates were dominated by noise, and grasp proposal networks performed poorly. The non-prehensile "poke" mode was more robust due to clearer trajectories.
 - **Reorientation Tasks:** The system struggled with tasks requiring object reorientation, indicating a limitation in the current pipeline's ability to handle complex re-grasping or multi-stage reorientation planning.

The FMB experiments showcased NovaPlan's impressive zero-shot capabilities for complex assembly and robust error recovery, pushing the boundaries of what's achievable without prior training. However, they also highlighted critical bottlenecks in current video generation model capabilities and perception robustness for fine-grained contact interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NovaPlan represents a significant step forward in the quest for general-purpose robotic manipulation, particularly in its ability to perform complex, long-horizon tasks in a zero-shot manner. Its primary significance lies in the holistic integration of state-of-the-art vision-language models and video generation models within a robust, closed-loop framework.

The potential impacts of NovaPlan are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Generalist Robots:** By achieving zero-shot long-horizon manipulation with autonomous error recovery, NovaPlan moves closer to the vision of truly generalist robots that can adapt to novel tasks and environments without extensive human intervention or task-specific programming. This could unlock robotics applications in unstructured settings like homes, small businesses, or disaster relief, where pre-programming every scenario is impractical.
2. **Bridging the High-Low Level Gap:** The framework effectively bridges the long-standing gap between high-level semantic reasoning (where VLMs excel) and low-level, geometrically grounded physical interaction. This modular approach allows for leveraging the strengths of different AI modalities.
3. **Enhancing Robustness and Reliability:** The closed-loop verification and recovery mechanism is a crucial contribution. Real-world robotics is inherently prone to errors. NovaPlan's ability to detect failures, diagnose them, and improvise corrective actions (including non-prehensile manipulation) significantly improves the reliability and autonomy of robotic systems, reducing the need for human intervention during task execution.
4. **Leveraging Synthetic Data for Real-World Control:** By extracting actionable flow from generated videos, NovaPlan demonstrates a powerful paradigm for grounding abstract plans into concrete robot actions, reducing the reliance on costly real-world robot demonstrations. The hybrid object/hand flow mechanism provides a robust way to extract these control signals even under challenging visual conditions.
5. **Accelerating Robotic Research:** NovaPlan integrates various cutting-edge foundation models (GPT-5.2, Wan2.2, Veo 3.1, MoGe2, HaMeR, SAM3, TAPIP3D). Its modular architecture suggests that as these underlying foundation models continue to improve, NovaPlan's capabilities will naturally expand, offering a scalable pathway towards more advanced robotic intelligence. This allows robotics researchers to focus on system integration and robustness rather than developing every component from scratch.

Despite its impressive achievements, NovaPlan also highlights current limitations of foundation models, particularly in generating perfectly physically plausible videos for complex or irregular shapes and precise low-level recovery motions. These bottlenecks serve as clear directions for future research in generative AI and geometric reasoning. Overall, NovaPlan represents a compelling demonstration of how intelligent planning, robust execution, and adaptive recovery can be combined to achieve unprecedented levels of autonomy in robotic manipulation.
