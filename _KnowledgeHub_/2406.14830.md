# CLIP-Decoder : ZeroShot Multilabel Classification using Multimodal CLIP Aligned Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLIP-Decoder integrates CLIP-aligned representations into a transformer-decoder architecture, achieving state-of-the-art performance in multi-label zero-shot and generalized zero-shot classification. It improved mean Average Precision by 2.3% and 3.9% on the NUS-WIDE dataset for ZSL and GZSL respectively, compared to previous best methods.

## Problem

- Existing multi-label classification methods struggle to classify images into categories for which no training data has been provided (Zero-Shot Learning, ZSL).
- A significant 'semantic gap' exists between visual features and textual label descriptions, hindering effective generalization to unseen classes.
- Obtaining exhaustive labeled training data for every possible category in real-world multi-label classification is often impractical or impossible.

## Method

- A novel ==CLIP-Decoder== architecture is proposed, combining a ==TResNet image encoder==, a CLIP text encoder, and a ==transformer-decoder head==.
- The method leverages CLIP's multimodal representations by projecting image and text embeddings into a ==shared semantic dimension==, guided by prompt engineering for class labels.
- A ==multi-scale weighted joint loss function== is employed to optimize both classification accuracy and the alignment of vision-language representations.

## Results

- The CLIP-Decoder achieved an mAP of **33.4** in Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) on NUS-WIDE, an absolute increase of **2.3%** over the previous best method (ML-Decoder).
- In Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL), it attained an mAP of **23.8** on NUS-WIDE, marking an absolute increase of **3.9%** compared to the previous state-of-the-art (ML-Decoder).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the use of CLIP embeddings and the combined vision-language alignment loss incrementally contributed to improved mean Average Precision and F1 scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP can effectively bridge the semantic gap in zero-shot multi-label classification by providing robust, aligned multimodal representations.
> - Explicitly optimizing for both classification and multimodal alignment via a weighted joint loss function significantly enhances performance in ZSL and GZSL settings.
> - Integrating CLIP-aligned text embeddings as queries into a transformer-decoder structure (building on ML-Decoder) allows for improved contextual and visual understanding, leading to better generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-label classification is a fundamental computer vision task where an image can be associated with multiple labels simultaneously (e.g., an image containing "sky," "car," and "road"). This task has widespread real-world applications, from image tagging and content moderation to medical diagnosis. Historically, multi-label classification methods have evolved significantly. Early approaches often used global average pooling (GAP) combined with fully connected layers for classification. While simple and effective for many tasks, GAP-based classifiers struggle with varying object scales and are generally unsuitable for zero-shot learning (ZSL) scenarios.

More advanced techniques have leveraged attention mechanisms, graph neural networks, and improved loss functions to better capture label correlations and spatial information. Attention-based heads, in particular, have shown success in identifying objects of different scales by focusing on relevant regions of an image. However, these methods can be computationally intensive and may not directly translate to zero-shot settings.

The concept of **Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL)** is a significant paradigm shift, addressing the challenge of classifying images into categories that were not present during training. This is crucial in scenarios where collecting labeled data for every possible category is impractical or impossible. A more challenging variant is **Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL)**, where the model must classify images into both seen (training) and unseen (test) categories. Traditional ZSL methods often rely on auxiliary semantic information (like word embeddings) to relate unseen classes to seen ones.

A recent breakthrough in multi-label classification, particularly relevant to this work, is the **ML-Decoder**. This method introduced a transformer-decoder structure optimized for multi-label classification, offering a good balance between speed and accuracy. ML-Decoder's design addresses some of the computational costs associated with general attention-based models while improving performance.

The current paper builds directly upon the foundation laid by ML-Decoder by integrating **Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)**. CLIP represents a significant advancement in multimodal learning, trained on vast quantities of image-text pairs from the internet. It learns a shared, high-dimensional embedding space where semantically similar images and text snippets are close to each other. This inherent ability to align visual and textual concepts makes CLIP an exceptionally powerful tool for zero-shot tasks, as it can infer relationships between unseen visual concepts and their textual descriptions without direct visual training examples.

Therefore, this work fits into the research landscape as a novel integration of a strong attention-based classification head (ML-Decoder) with the powerful multimodal alignment capabilities of CLIP. It aims to push the state-of-the-art in the challenging domain of multi-label zero-shot and generalized zero-shot learning by effectively leveraging pre-trained multimodal representations, thereby minimizing the semantic mismatch often encountered in these tasks. This approach represents a logical evolution, moving beyond relying solely on visual features or generic word embeddings to harness the deep semantic understanding provided by foundation models like CLIP.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly improve the performance of multi-label classification in zero-shot learning (ZSL) and generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL) settings. The authors are motivated by the critical need to classify images into multiple categories, especially when some or all of these categories have not been seen during training. This scenario is highly relevant for real-world applications where obtaining exhaustive training data for every possible label is infeasible.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Addressing the limitations of existing multi-label ZSL methods:** While methods like ML-Decoder offer good performance, they primarily focus on optimizing the classification head itself. The authors aim to enhance this by incorporating more robust semantic understanding, particularly for unseen classes.
- **Leveraging multimodal representation learning:** The motivation is to harness the power of pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP to bridge the semantic gap between visual content and textual labels. By aligning image and text representations, the model can better generalize to unseen categories based on their semantic descriptions.
- **Minimizing semantic mismatch:** A core challenge in ZSL is that the model must infer knowledge about unseen classes from their semantic descriptions, which often involves a "semantic gap" between visual features and word embeddings. The authors aim to minimize this mismatch by projecting both image and word embeddings into a shared, aligned dimension.
- **Developing a novel architecture (CLIP-Decoder):** The paper proposes to extend the successful ML-Decoder by integrating CLIP-aligned representations directly into its transformer-decoder structure. This involves a dual-modal approach that effectively fuses visual and textual information.
- **Achieving state-of-the-art performance:** The ultimate goal is to demonstrate significant performance improvements over existing methods on benchmark multi-label ZSL and GZSL datasets.
- **Enhancing contextual and visual understanding:** By combining transformer decoder layers with fused visual and textual embeddings, the aim is to create a model with a richer understanding of both visual content and its associated semantic labels.
- **Establishing connections between modalities through weighted losses:** The authors intend to facilitate synergistic learning between the vision and language components by using a combined loss function that promotes both accurate classification and strong multimodal alignment.

In essence, the research is driven by the desire to push the boundaries of zero-shot multi-label classification by intelligently integrating advanced multimodal pre-training with efficient classification architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named **CLIP-Decoder**, builds upon the ML-Decoder architecture and integrates CLIP's multimodal representation learning capabilities. The overall approach can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview (CLIP-Decoder Design)

The CLIP-Decoder uses a dual-modal approach:
- **Image Encoder:** A TResNet image encoder is used to extract robust image embeddings from the input images. TResNet is known for its high performance and GPU-dedicated architecture.
- **Text Encoder:** The CLIP text encoder is utilized to generate feature representations for class names. This is done by converting class labels into "prompts."
- **Transformer-Decoder Head:** Inspired by ML-Decoder, the core classification head is based on a transformer-decoder structure. This head receives the image embeddings and processes them in conjunction with the text-based queries.
- **Shared Projection Matrix:** For Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL), a shared projection matrix is employed within a group fully-connected layer. This matrix facilitates the transfer of semantic information learned from observed (training) classes to unseen (test) classes during inference, enhancing generalization.
- **Cross-Attention Mechanism:** The design integrates cross-attention layers where textual queries interact with visual keys and values, allowing the decoder to selectively focus on relevant image regions guided by the semantic meaning of the labels.

#### 3.2. Pre-processing: Prompt Design

A crucial aspect of leveraging CLIP effectively is the design of appropriate text prompts. The authors transform input labels into descriptive prompts. For instance, if an image contains "sky," "car," and "road," the prompt might be "a photo of sky, car and road" or "a picture of sky, car, and road." This strategy aims to maximize the benefit of CLIP's internet-trained mapping, which is designed to understand natural language descriptions of visual concepts. The paper notes that they test multiple templates and select the best-performing one on the NUS-WIDE dataset. This prompt engineering is vital because the quality of the text embedding from CLIP is highly dependent on how the textual input is framed.

#### 3.3. CLIP-Aligned Representation Learning

The core innovation lies in aligning the image and text representations.
- **Projection to a Shared Dimension:** The image embeddings from TResNet and the text embeddings from the CLIP text encoder are projected onto the same dimension. This ensures that their representations are comparable within a unified semantic space.
- **Semantic Similarity:** The attention mechanism within the CLIP-Decoder relies on the dot-product similarity between query and key vectors (Equation 1). By using NLP word embeddings that preserve dot-product semantic similarity, the model is more likely to associate hidden labels with the most semantically similar keys and values from the decoder.
- **Full-decoding for ZSL:** In the ZSL setting, each label has a corresponding query. These queries are "fixed NLP-based queries," meaning each label is associated with a pre-computed word embedding vector from the CLIP model, rather than being learned during training.

#### 3.4. Multi-Scale Weighted Joint Loss

To optimize the model's performance and ensure effective multimodal alignment, a combined loss function is used:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \alpha \mathcal{L}_{\text{clip}} + \beta \mathcal{L}_c $$
- **$\mathcal{L}_c$ (Classification Loss):** This is a standard cross-entropy classification loss, which aims to correctly classify the labels present in an image.
- **$\mathcal{L}_{\text{clip}}$ (CLIP Alignment Loss):** This loss leverages CLIP's ability to embed both visual and textual information into a shared embedding space. It likely computes the cosine similarity between the aligned image embeddings and the aligned text embeddings, encouraging semantically similar pairs to have high similarity scores and dissimilar pairs to have low scores. The coefficients $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are weights tuned through cross-validation to balance the contributions of classification accuracy and multimodal alignment.

#### 3.5. Training and Evaluation Strategy

- **Dataset:** The NUS-WIDE dataset, a widely used benchmark for multi-label ZSL tasks, is employed.
- **Settings:** Experiments are conducted for both Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) and Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL).
 - **ZSL:** The model is trained entirely on "seen" classes and evaluated on "unseen" classes.
 - **GZSL:** The model is trained on "seen" classes and evaluated on a combined set of both "seen" and "unseen" classes.
- **Metrics:** Performance is evaluated using mean Average Precision (mAP) and F1 score at top-K predictions (specifically, k=3 and k=5).
- **Embeddings:** The authors conduct an ablation study comparing the use of fixed word2vec embeddings versus CLIP-based prompts/embeddings, and also the impact of the joint loss compared to just classification loss.

In essence, the methodology combines a robust backbone and classification head with a powerful multimodal pre-training model, carefully aligning their representations through prompt engineering and a combined loss function to tackle the challenges of zero-shot multi-label classification.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The CLIP-Decoder demonstrates significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods in both zero-shot learning (ZSL) and generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL) multi-label classification tasks. The evaluation was performed on the NUS-WIDE dataset using mean Average Precision (mAP) and F1 scores at k=3 and k=5.

#### 4.1. Ablation Study Results (Table 1)

The ablation study highlights the incremental benefits of the proposed components:
- **Impact of CLIP Embeddings:** Replacing traditional word2vec fixed embeddings with CLIP embeddings (even with only classification loss $\mathcal{L}_c$) resulted in an increase in mAP and F1 scores. This shows the superior semantic understanding provided by CLIP. For example, in ZSL, with $\mathcal{L}_c$ only, CLIP embeddings achieved 33.25 mAP, compared to an un-ticked baseline which would be lower (implied by the text, though not explicitly shown in Table 1 with a separate row).
- **Impact of Joint Loss:** The combined loss function ($\mathcal{L}_c + \mathcal{L}_{\text{clip}}$) further boosted performance. For ZSL, using both $\mathcal{L}_c$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{clip}}$ with CLIP embeddings yielded an mAP of 33.43, F1@3 of 34.80, and F1@5 of 31.10. Similarly, for GZSL, the full CLIP-Decoder with joint loss achieved an mAP of 23.80, F1@3 of 24.87, and F1@5 of 27.54. This indicates that explicitly aligning vision-language representations through the CLIP loss contributes positively to classification performance.
- **Prompt Design:** While specific prompt variations are detailed in the appendix (not provided), the authors state they designed and selected the best-performing prompts, which contributes to the overall effectiveness.

#### 4.2. State-of-the-Art Comparison (Table 2)

The CLIP-Decoder consistently outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods across both ZSL and GZSL settings on the NUS-WIDE dataset.

- **Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL):**
 - The CLIP-Decoder achieved an mAP of **33.4**, which is an absolute increase of **2.3%** compared to the previous best method, ML-Decoder (31.1 mAP).
 - It also showed competitive or superior F1 scores, with 34.8 at k=3 and 31.1 at k=5, slightly outperforming ML-Decoder (34.1 F1@3, 30.8 F1@5).
 - The improvement is even more significant when compared to earlier methods like Attention per Cluster (12.9 mAP), LESA (19.4 mAP), BiAM (26.3 mAP), and SDL (25.9 mAP).

- **Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL):**
 - The CLIP-Decoder achieved an mAP of **23.8**, marking an impressive absolute increase of **3.9%** over the previous state-of-the-art, ML-Decoder (19.9 mAP).
 - The F1 scores also improved, with 24.87 at k=3 and 27.54 at k=5, compared to ML-Decoder's 23.3 F1@3 and 26.1 F1@5.
 - This shows a substantial gain, especially considering the added complexity of classifying both seen and unseen classes simultaneously. The improvement is even more pronounced when compared against methods like Attention per Cluster (2.6 mAP), LESA (5.6 mAP), BiAM (9.3 mAP), and SDL (12.1 mAP).

The results demonstrate that the introduction of CLIP-aligned representation learning on top of ML-Decoder consistently yields significant increases in mAP values for both ZSL and GZSL. The authors attribute this to CLIP's pre-training on large amounts of image-text pairs, enabling it to predict relevant image labels given textual input more effectively. The method successfully removes bias towards seen classes, improving classification for both seen and unseen classes in GZSL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of the CLIP-Decoder represents a notable advancement in the field of multi-label zero-shot classification, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Advancing Zero-Shot Learning Capabilities:** The CLIP-Decoder sets a new state-of-the-art for multi-label ZSL and GZSL. The absolute performance gains of 2.3% in ZSL and 3.9% in GZSL mAP are substantial in a highly competitive research area. This indicates a more robust and effective approach to classifying images into categories for which no direct training data exists.
2. **Highlighting the Power of Multimodal Foundation Models:** This work underscores the critical role that large-scale pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP play in solving challenging computer vision problems. By effectively integrating CLIP's aligned representations, the authors demonstrate how deep semantic understanding learned from vast internet data can be transferred to specific downstream tasks, dramatically reducing the reliance on extensive labeled datasets.
3. **Improved Generalization to Unseen Classes:** The core problem in zero-shot learning is generalization. The CLIP-Decoder's ability to minimize semantic mismatch between image and word embeddings, combined with the shared projection matrix for transferring knowledge, leads to better predictions for novel, unseen classes. This is crucial for real-world adaptability.
4. **Practical Applications in Data-Scarce Domains:** The ability to classify multiple labels in a zero-shot manner has immense practical implications, especially in domains where data annotation is expensive, time-consuming, or scarce. Examples include:
 - **Medical Imaging:** Identifying rare disease types or anomalies for which limited training data is available.
 - **Environmental Monitoring:** Detecting new species, pollution types, or geological features without prior examples.
 - **Content Moderation:** Automatically flagging new or evolving categories of inappropriate content.
 - **E-commerce/Image Tagging:** Automatically tagging product images with novel attributes or categories as they emerge.
 - **Robotics:** Enabling robots to recognize and interact with previously unseen objects based on their descriptions.
5. **Blueprint for Future Multimodal Integration:** The CLIP-Decoder provides a strong framework for how to combine powerful, pre-trained multimodal models with specialized classification heads. This "ML-Decoder + CLIP alignment" strategy could serve as a blueprint for developing solutions for other zero-shot tasks beyond multi-label classification, as hinted in the future work.
6. **Enhanced Synergy Between Vision and Language:** The method successfully enhances multimodal representation learning, fostering better synergy between vision and language modalities. This is not just a technical improvement but contributes to a deeper understanding of how to build AI systems that can reason about the world more akin to humans, by linking visual perception with semantic understanding.
7. **Future Research Directions:** The authors explicitly mention extending this approach to other domains, such as zero-shot action recognition and detecting new cancer types in multiomics frameworks. This suggests the proposed methodology is highly transferable and scalable, potentially sparking further research into adapting multimodal zero-shot learning for a wider array of complex problems.

In conclusion, the CLIP-Decoder represents a significant step forward in making AI systems more adaptable and capable of handling novel information, thereby broadening the applicability of AI in diverse and data-constrained environments.
