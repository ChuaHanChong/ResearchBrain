# CLIP-Decoder : ZeroShot Multilabel Classification using Multimodal CLIP Aligned Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLIP-Decoder integrates CLIP-aligned representations into a transformer-decoder architecture, achieving state-of-the-art performance in multi-label zero-shot and generalized zero-shot classification. It improved mean Average Precision by 2.3% and 3.9% on the NUS-WIDE dataset for ZSL and GZSL respectively, compared to previous best methods.

## Problem

- Existing multi-label classification methods struggle to classify images into categories for which no training data has been provided (Zero-Shot Learning, ZSL).
- A significant 'semantic gap' exists between visual features and textual label descriptions, hindering effective generalization to unseen classes.
- Obtaining exhaustive labeled training data for every possible category in real-world multi-label classification is often impractical or impossible.

## Method

- A novel ==CLIP-Decoder== architecture is proposed, combining a ==TResNet image encoder==, a CLIP text encoder, and a ==transformer-decoder head==.
- The method leverages CLIP's multimodal representations by projecting image and text embeddings into a ==shared semantic dimension==, guided by prompt engineering for class labels.
- A ==multi-scale weighted joint loss function== is employed to optimize both classification accuracy and the alignment of vision-language representations.

## Results

- The CLIP-Decoder achieved an mAP of **33.4** in Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) on NUS-WIDE, an absolute increase of **2.3%** over the previous best method (ML-Decoder).
- In Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL), it attained an mAP of **23.8** on NUS-WIDE, marking an absolute increase of **3.9%** compared to the previous state-of-the-art (ML-Decoder).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the use of CLIP embeddings and the combined vision-language alignment loss incrementally contributed to improved mean Average Precision and F1 scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP can effectively bridge the semantic gap in zero-shot multi-label classification by providing robust, aligned multimodal representations.
> - Explicitly optimizing for both classification and multimodal alignment via a weighted joint loss function significantly enhances performance in ZSL and GZSL settings.
> - Integrating CLIP-aligned text embeddings as queries into a transformer-decoder structure (building on ML-Decoder) allows for improved contextual and visual understanding, leading to better generalization.
