# Beyond Pass@1: Self-Play with Variational Problem Synthesis Sustains RLVR

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Self-play with Variational problem Synthesis (SvS), an online strategy for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) that addresses policy entropy collapse and sustains generation diversity. SvS achieves absolute gains of 18.3% and 22.8% in Pass@32 performance on AIME 24 and AIME 25 benchmarks, respectively, demonstrating improved and sustained reasoning capabilities across various LLM sizes and tasks.

## Problem

- Standard Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training in Large Language Models (LLMs) often leads to policy entropy collapse.
- Entropy collapse reduces generation diversity, limiting Pass@k performance and hindering the LLM's capacity for advanced exploration and sustained reasoning.
- Collecting large, diverse, and verifiable problem sets for RLVR is difficult, and existing augmentation often lacks precise reference answers or relies on external models.

## Method

- An online, self-improving strategy called Self-play with ==Variational problem Synthesis== (**SvS**) is proposed to augment training problems and sustain policy entropy.
- The policy model itself synthesizes "variational problems" from the correct solutions of "underperforming problems," ensuring semantic preservation and shared reference answers.
- A sophisticated reward shaping mechanism is implemented for problem synthesis, incentivizing the generation of genuinely challenging yet solvable variational problems and preventing trivialization.

## Results

- SvS substantially outperformed standard RLVR, achieving absolute gains of **18.3%** and **22.8%** in Pass@32 performance on AIME 24 and AIME 25 benchmarks, respectively.
- The strategy consistently maintained policy entropy throughout training, preventing the decline observed in standard RLVR, and also improved Pass@1 (average 32 times improvement).
- SvS demonstrated generalizability across LLMs ranging from **3B** to **32B** parameters and extended effectively to code generation tasks, showing significant performance improvements with fewer training steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sustaining policy entropy and generation diversity is critical for achieving long-term, significant improvements in Pass@k performance, as opposed to solely optimizing Pass@1.
> - LLMs can effectively self-improve by generating their own diverse and verifiable training problems, reducing reliance on external data and annotation.
> - Targeting problems at the frontier of the model's capabilities (underperforming problems) for variational synthesis maximizes learning advantage and exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically focusing on their post-training enhancement for complex reasoning tasks through Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR has emerged as a crucial paradigm for improving LLMs, particularly for tasks where correct solutions can be programmatically verified (e.g., mathematical problems, code generation).

However, the paper identifies a critical limitation of standard RLVR training: while it often improves Pass@1 performance (the accuracy of the first generated solution), it tends to diminish the policy entropy. This "entropy collapse" leads to reduced generation diversity, which consequently limits Pass@k performance (the probability of finding a correct solution within k attempts), effectively hindering the LLM's capacity for advanced exploration and ultimately plateauing its reasoning capabilities beyond that of the base model. Several recent studies (Yue et al., 2025; Cui et al., 2025b; Zhu et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025) have corroborated this trade-off between Pass@1 and generation diversity, highlighting it as a significant challenge for sustainable LLM improvement.

Existing efforts to mitigate entropy collapse or enhance RLVR include techniques like altering KL constraints, dynamic sampling, or incorporating entropy-based terms into the objective function (Yu et al., 2025a; Cheng et al., 2025; An et al., 2025). Some works have also explored data augmentation, but these often rely on external LLMs for rephrasing or lack precise reference answers, which are critical for RLVR.

The proposed Self-play with Variational problem Synthesis (SvS) strategy directly addresses this fundamental issue by systematically analyzing the policy's generation diversity from a data perspective. It introduces an online, self-improving data augmentation mechanism that sustains policy entropy, thereby fostering continuous exploration and significantly enhancing Pass@k performance. By enabling the LLM to synthesize its own diverse, verifiable training problems, SvS offers a novel approach to overcome the limitations of fixed, limited datasets and entropy degradation in RLVR. This differentiates SvS from prior work by providing an end-to-end self-improvement loop that maintains both data diversity and answer verifiability, filling a crucial gap in the sustained scaling of LLM reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the SvS strategy stem directly from the identified limitations of standard RLVR training:

1. **Address Entropy Collapse in RLVR:** The core motivation is to counteract the observed decline in policy entropy during standard RLVR training. This entropy collapse leads to a reduction in generation diversity, causing the policy to produce homogeneous solutions and limiting its ability to explore advanced reasoning trajectories.
2. **Sustain Long-term RLVR Improvements:** The authors aim to enable RLVR training to sustain long-term improvements, particularly in Pass@k performance, which typically represents the upper bound of an LLM's reasoning capability. Standard RLVR often plateaus and fails to significantly improve Pass@k beyond small values of *k*.
3. **Develop an Effective Problem Augmentation Strategy:** A key objective is to propose a problem augmentation strategy that is "simple yet effective," capable of maintaining sustainable data diversity, aligning with the model's evolving capabilities, and crucially, ensuring accurate labeled answers without requiring extensive human annotation or external model intervention. The difficulty in collecting large, diverse, and verifiable problem sets is a significant hurdle in current RLVR research.
4. **Promote Self-Improvement without External Guidance:** The work seeks to establish an online, self-improving training paradigm where the policy model itself generates and solves new variational problems. This self-play mechanism aims to remove the reliance on external data sources or additional distillation processes, making the training more autonomous and scalable.
5. **Expand LLM Reasoning Boundaries:** Ultimately, the overarching goal is to enable LLMs to expand their reasoning boundaries. By maintaining diversity and promoting continuous exploration, SvS aims to foster more general and advanced reasoning strategies, allowing models to tackle more challenging, competition-level problems.

In essence, the motivation is to transform RLVR from a technique that primarily optimizes Pass@1 on existing problem variations to a more robust and sustainable framework that genuinely enhances an LLM's exploratory and generalized reasoning capabilities, as reflected by substantial and sustained improvements in Pass@k.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, **Self-play with Variational problem Synthesis (SvS)**, is an online strategy designed to augment training problems and sustain policy entropy in RLVR. It operates as a self-improving loop, relying exclusively on the policy model itself. The approach comprises three main components within each training iteration:

1. **Original Problem Solving:**
 - The policy model ($\pi_\theta$) is prompted to generate a group of solutions (G responses) for problems sampled from the original training set (D).
 - A correctness reward ($R_c$) is assigned based on whether the extracted final answer matches the ground truth.
 - Crucially, only "underperforming problems" are retained for potential augmentation. These are defined as problems where the group average accuracy falls within a specific range ($racc_l, acc_h s$), ensuring that the augmentation targets problems at the frontier of the model's capabilities (neither too easy nor too hard). Problems with all-correct or all-incorrect solutions are filtered out, as they provide zero advantage signal in GRPO.

2. **Variational Problem Synthesis:**
 - For the identified underperforming problems, their *correct solutions* (not the original problem statements) are used as context.
 - The policy is prompted to synthesize a group of $G_v$ "variational problems" based on these correct solutions.
 - The key innovation here is that these variational problems are designed to preserve the original semantics and, most importantly, *share the same reference answers* as the original problems. This eliminates the need for new answer labeling, a major bottleneck in data augmentation.
 - This process effectively encourages the policy to rephrase descriptions and structures of problems while maintaining their core mathematical or logical content, promoting diverse reasoning.
 - The synthesis process itself is also incorporated into RLVR training, compelling the policy to learn the inverse mapping from a solution to a problem statement, thereby deepening its understanding of problem structure and semantics.

3. **Synthetic Problem Solving:**
 - The policy then attempts to solve these self-synthesized variational problems.
 - For each variational problem, the policy generates G solutions, and their correctness is evaluated against the *original ground-truth answer* (which, by design, is shared with the synthetic problem).
 - Similar to original problem solving, only synthetic problems for which the policy produces a mix of correct and incorrect solutions are retained in the buffer for policy updates.

**Reward Shaping for Problem Synthesis:** A critical aspect of SvS is the **reward shaping** applied during variational problem synthesis.
- Initially, a simple proxy criterion might reward synthesis if the policy could solve the synthetic problem correctly. However, this was found to be exploitable: the policy could embed hints or even directly include the answer in the synthetic problem, making it trivial and failing to encourage advanced reasoning.
- To counteract this, a more sophisticated reward-shaping constraint is introduced: a positive reward ($R_v$) is assigned to a synthetic problem *only if the policy achieves a moderate level of group accuracy* within a defined range ($r\hat{acc}_l, \hat{acc}_h s$). Problems that are too easy (e.g., policy solves all) or unsolvable (e.g., policy solves none) receive a negative reward. This incentivizes the policy to generate genuinely challenging yet solvable variational problems.

**Full Training Data and Policy Update:**
- In each step, the training buffer (B) is populated with three types of prompt-response-reward tuples: from original problem solving, variational problem synthesis, and synthetic problem solving.
- The policy ($\pi_\theta$) is then updated using the GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm (Shao et al., 2024), which computes token advantages in a group-relative manner without an external critic. This update jointly trains the policy on both problem-solving and problem-synthesis tasks, forming a continuous self-improvement loop.

This comprehensive, self-contained methodology allows SvS to dynamically augment training data, maintain diversity, prevent entropy collapse, and push the boundaries of LLM reasoning capabilities without external data labeling or guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various model sizes and benchmarks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of the SvS strategy.

1. **Significant Pass@k and Pass@1 Improvements:**
 - SvS substantially outperforms standard RLVR, particularly on challenging, competition-level benchmarks like AIME 24 and AIME 25.
 - Absolute gains of 18.3% and 22.8% in Pass@32 performance were observed on AIME 24 and AIME 25, respectively, where standard RLVR showed minimal improvement.
 - SvS also achieved significant improvements in Pass@1 (average 32 times) on these benchmarks, with gains of 10.5% and 10.5% on AIME 24 and AIME 25 when trained on the DAPO-17k dataset. This indicates that enhanced exploration from sustained diversity also translates to better single-shot accuracy.
 - Figure 1 clearly illustrates that while standard RLVR plateaus, SvS achieves substantial and sustained improvements in both Pass@1 and Pass@32 over training steps.

2. **Sustained Policy Entropy and Exploration:**
 - A key finding is that SvS successfully maintains policy entropy within a relatively stable range throughout training, unlike standard RLVR which shows a continuous decline (Figure 5).
 - This stable entropy supports sustained exploration, preventing the policy from prematurely converging to memorized solutions and avoiding "training collapse." This directly addresses the core limitation SvS was designed to overcome.
 - The "Takeaways for Problem Diversity in RLVR" section (derived from Figure 2) explicitly states that augmenting and updating problems counteract entropy drop and significantly improve Pass@k.

3. **Generalizability Across Model Sizes and Tasks:**
 - SvS consistently boosted RLVR performance across LLMs ranging from 3B to 32B parameters and various model families (Qwen2.5-Instruct, LLaMA-3.1-Instruct) (Table 2).
 - Average absolute improvements of approximately 3% were observed over baselines on 12 reasoning benchmarks.
 - The strategy also generalized effectively to **code generation tasks**, showing significant improvements in evaluation performance with five times fewer training steps compared to the RLVR baseline, while maintaining stable policy entropy (Figure 8).

4. **Pushing Reasoning Boundaries (Pass@k Scaling):**
 - Experiments scaling Pass@k from 1 to 1024 demonstrated that SvS significantly expands the model's reasoning capabilities, outperforming both standard RLVR and initial models across all *k* values on competition-level AIME and Beyond-AIME benchmarks (Figure 6).
 - For MATH-500, SvS consistently outperformed both RLVR and the initial model, indicating robust generalization and diverse reasoning.

5. **Ablation Studies and Strategy Validation:**
 - Comparison with alternative augmentation strategies confirmed the importance of SvS's design choices (Table 3, Figure 7).
 - Simply extending RLVR training or just enhancing underperforming problems (without variational synthesis) did not achieve the same level of performance, particularly for Pass@k.
 - Augmenting simpler problems in SvS led to faster overfitting and limited exploration, reinforcing the necessity of targeting "underperforming problems" for variational synthesis and maintaining problem diversity.
 - Further analysis showed that problem synthesis training helps mitigate overfitting and enhances the model's generalizability on out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks (Figure 11, Table 4).

6. **Correctness and Difficulty of Synthetic Problems:**
 - Evaluation by state-of-the-art LLMs (Qwen3-235B, OpenAI O3) showed that over 80% of synthesized variational problems were deemed logically correct and solvable, with a strong correlation between the difficulty of original and synthetic problems (Figures 13, 14, 15).
 - The reward shaping mechanism effectively prevents the generation of trivial or overly simplified synthetic problems (Figure 18).

In summary, SvS provides a robust, self-sustaining RLVR framework that overcomes critical limitations of previous approaches, leading to superior and more sustainable performance across a wide range of complex reasoning and code generation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SvS strategy offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of LLM research and development:

1. **Sustainable RLVR Training:** By effectively mitigating policy entropy collapse and ensuring continuous generation diversity, SvS provides a framework for truly *sustainable* reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards. This is crucial for long-term model improvements, especially as LLMs are expected to tackle increasingly complex and novel problems. It moves beyond short-term Pass@1 gains to foster deeper and more robust reasoning capabilities.

2. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities (Beyond Pass@1):** The dramatic improvements in Pass@k performance, particularly at higher *k* values on competition-level benchmarks, demonstrate that SvS successfully expands the reasoning boundaries of LLMs. This indicates that models trained with SvS are not just getting better at providing *one* correct answer, but are learning to explore a broader and more effective range of reasoning paths, which is vital for real-world problem-solving where multiple approaches might be necessary.

3. **Reduced Data Annotation Burden through Self-Play:** The self-play mechanism, where the policy itself synthesizes variational problems with automatically inherited reference answers, significantly reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming human data annotation. This is a game-changer for scaling RLVR to larger and more diverse datasets, especially for domains where high-quality labeled data is scarce.

4. **Improved Generalizability and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains across various model sizes (3B to 32B), diverse reasoning benchmarks, and generalization to distinct tasks like code generation highlight the robustness and broad applicability of SvS. The analysis also suggests that problem synthesis helps prevent overfitting, thereby improving the model's generalization capabilities on out-of-distribution tasks.

5. **A Stepping Stone for Advanced AI Agents:** The ability of an LLM to not only solve problems but also *generate new, challenging, and relevant problems for itself* represents a significant step towards more autonomous and intelligent AI agents. This self-curation of training data could accelerate the development of LLMs that can continuously learn and adapt in complex environments, akin to human-like self-improvement.

6. **Insights into Learning Dynamics:** The systematic analysis of policy entropy trajectories and the impact of data diversity provides valuable insights into the learning dynamics of RLVR. It validates the hypothesis that continuous problem exploration, rather than repeated exploitation of memorized solutions, is key to fostering deeper reasoning.

In summary, SvS represents a powerful paradigm shift in LLM post-training. By addressing the fundamental challenge of entropy collapse and data scarcity through an elegant self-play mechanism, it paves the way for LLMs that are not only more capable in complex reasoning but also more robust, adaptable, and self-sufficient in their learning trajectory. Its impact is likely to be felt across various applications requiring advanced reasoning, from scientific discovery to highly complex engineering tasks.
