# APC-RL: Exceeding Data-Driven Behavior Priors with Adaptive Policy Composition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Örebro University developed Adaptive Policy Composition (APC), a hierarchical reinforcement learning method that robustly utilizes multiple demonstration datasets, even when misaligned or suboptimal. The approach enabled agents to achieve optimal performance in challenging environments, for example, reaching 100% success on misaligned PointMaze tasks and surpassing suboptimal human demonstrations in CarRacing within 30k steps.

## Problem

- Existing demonstration-guided reinforcement learning methods often implicitly assume demonstrations are optimal, comprehensive, and perfectly aligned with the target task.
- This assumption is frequently violated in real-world settings, where demonstrations can be sparse, suboptimal, or misaligned, leading to performance degradation, sample inefficiency, or learning failures.
- Strict adherence to imperfect data can hinder exploration, introduce negative biases, and prevent agents from discovering truly optimal policies.

## Method

- APC proposes a hierarchical framework with a higher-level 'learning-free ==arbitrator==' that selects among multiple lower-level actors based on their estimated value functions.
- The lower-level actors include both 'prior-free' policies learning from scratch and 'prior-based' policies, each pre-trained with a Normalizing Flow on a distinct demonstration dataset.
- A reward-sharing mechanism, leveraging the invertibility of Normalizing Flows, ensures all actors are updated with every executed action, preventing sample inefficiency and allowing continuous learning across all components.

## Results

- APC achieved **100%** success in approximately **0.5M** steps on misaligned PointMaze tasks, significantly outperforming baselines like PARROT and Imitation Learning (IL) which showed minimal success (0-**7%**).
- In the CarRacing environment, APC reached optimal returns (around 900) in about 30k steps using suboptimal human demonstrations, surpassing from-scratch SAC and other demonstration-guided baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the learning-free arbitrator and the reward-sharing mechanism are critical for APC's robust exploration, accelerated learning, and mitigation of primacy bias.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Composing multiple policies, including a prior-free actor and several prior-based actors, allows the agent to robustly leverage imperfect demonstration data while retaining the flexibility to diverge from unhelpful priors.
> - A 'learning-free' arbitrator, which dynamically switches between actors based on their estimated values, effectively mitigates primacy bias and avoids the instability typically associated with training a high-level selector.
> - The reward-sharing trick ensures all lower-level actors continuously receive learning signals, promoting balanced exploration and enabling faster convergence even when one actor initially outperforms others.
