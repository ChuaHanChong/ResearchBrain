# What Makes Value Learning Efficient in Residual Reinforcement Learning?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University and Mila identified and addressed two key bottlenecks in value learning within residual reinforcement learning: the "cold start pathology" and "structural scale mismatch". Their proposed DAWN method, integrating data-anchored warmup and critic normalization, significantly improves sample efficiency and stability for online policy refinement across diverse robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Residual Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, used for online refinement of powerful pretrained policies, suffer from high sample complexity, requiring substantial online interactions.
- Prior research predominantly focused on policy-centric innovations, overlooking potential inefficiencies in the value learning component of actor-critic frameworks.
- The slow convergence of value functions, which provide gradients for policy improvement, acts as a bottleneck for the real-world deployment of residual RL.

## Method

- The **DAWN** (==Data-Anchored Warmup and Normalization==) method was developed, comprising two main interventions based on a systematic analysis of ==value learning== challenges in ==residual RL== (built on ==Soft Actor-Critic==).
- ==Data-Anchored Warmup==: Pre-collecting and seeding the replay buffer with transitions generated solely by the frozen base policy to provide a stable 'value anchor' for the critic.
- ==Critic Normalization==: Applying ==Layer Normalization== to the critic network to restore its sensitivity and ability to discern subtle value differences caused by small residual actions.

## Results

- DAWN consistently achieved superior sample efficiency, converging to high success rates significantly faster (e.g., reaching **90%** success **~6 times quicker** on PegInsertionSide) than state-of-the-art baselines like Policy Decorator.
- The method demonstrated notably lower variance across multiple random seeds, indicating improved training stability, and outperformed baselines across **8 diverse ManiSkill and Adroit tasks**.
- Both data-anchored warmup and critic normalization were found to be essential and complementary components, with ablation studies confirming their individual necessity for the observed efficiency gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'cold start pathology' arises when randomly initialized critics lack grounding in the value landscape around a competent base policy, leading to unstable value estimates; pre-collected base policy data effectively mitigates this.
> - Explicit critic warmup phases are ineffective and can lead to 'entropy dominance,' where the entropy regularization term in SAC biases Q-targets and causes value collapse.
> - A 'structural scale mismatch' between the large base action and small residual action hinders the critic's ability to attribute value changes; normalization helps the critic become sensitive to these subtle differences, which primarily manifest as a mean shift in Q-values, making complex distributional objectives unnecessary.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has undergone a significant transformation with the advent of generative policies pretrained on large-scale datasets, such as Diffusion Policy and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While these policies demonstrate expressiveness, their deployment often faces challenges due to inherent distribution shifts and compounding errors that imitation learning alone cannot resolve. Online reinforcement learning (RL) is increasingly recognized as crucial for achieving reliability in real-world scenarios, particularly in precision-critical applications like contact-rich manipulation.

Residual Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a method for online refinement of these powerful pretrained policies. This paradigm involves freezing a base policy and learning only a bounded correction term. This approach aims to avoid issues like catastrophic forgetting and training instability that can arise from directly modifying pretrained policy parameters. Prior research in residual RL has predominantly focused on policy-centric innovations, including foundational techniques like bounded residuals and progressive exploration, as well as more granular designs such as per-step corrections and latent steering for multimodal policies. These efforts have addressed aspects of designing effective and stable residual policies, thereby enhancing the practical applicability of the paradigm for real-world refinement.

However, existing state-of-the-art residual RL methods, despite policy-centric advancements, still require a substantial number of online interactions (e.g., over a million samples) to achieve proficiency. This sample complexity can be prohibitive for real-world deployment. The current work identifies an overlooked factor contributing to this sample inefficiency: value learning. In off-policy actor-critic methods, which form the basis of residual RL, policy improvement is dependent on gradients from the critic. If value learning is inefficient, policy engineering alone may not compensate for the slow convergence.

This paper positions itself as the first investigation into the fundamental dynamics of value learning in residual RL. It aims to diagnose the specific challenges that hinder efficient value function acquisition in this context, moving beyond policy-focused solutions to address the underlying issues related to value estimation. By focusing on the critic, the research attempts to provide a mechanistic understanding of sample efficiency bottlenecks in residual RL and develop solutions that are both simpler and more broadly applicable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to improve the sample efficiency of residual reinforcement learning (RL) by addressing overlooked challenges in value learning. Current residual RL methods, despite innovations in policy design, often require a high number of online interactions, which is a barrier to real-world deployment. The authors hypothesize that inefficient value learning is a significant contributing factor to this sample complexity, as policy improvement in actor-critic frameworks relies fundamentally on accurate critic gradients.

To address this, the study poses three core research questions:

1. **RQ1: What are the unique and critical challenges for value learning in residual RL?**
 The motivation here is to identify specific issues inherent to the residual RL setup that distinguish value learning in this paradigm from standard RL. The authors posit that the unique structure of residual RL, where a small, learnable correction is added to a frozen, capable base policy, might create distinct difficulties for the critic.

2. **RQ2: What bottlenecks underlie these challenges, and how can they be fundamentally resolved?**
 This objective aims to uncover the root causes of the identified challenges and to develop principled solutions rather than superficial fixes. The authors suggest that understanding the mechanisms behind poor value learning will lead to more effective and generalizable interventions. The paper specifically identifies two bottlenecks:
 - **Cold Start Pathology:** At the beginning of training, the critic is randomly initialized and lacks knowledge of the value landscape around the base policy. Since the full policy (base + residual) operates near a competent base policy from the outset, inaccurate initial value estimates can misguide the residual policy and lead to performance degradation. The motivation is to find a way to quickly and accurately ground the critic's value estimates in the relevant state-action space.
 - **Structural Scale Mismatch:** The residual action's contribution to the overall executed action is significantly smaller than that of the base action, controlled by a small scaling factor (λ). This structural asymmetry makes it difficult for the critic to discern subtle value differences caused by the residual policy, hindering effective credit assignment and learning. The motivation is to restore the critic's sensitivity to these small but critical variations.

3. **RQ3: What gains in sample efficiency can be achieved through principled solutions?**
 The final objective is to evaluate the practical impact of the proposed solutions by quantifying the improvements in sample efficiency. This demonstrates whether addressing the identified bottlenecks fundamentally enhances the learning process and provides a clear advantage over existing methods. The aim is to show that a targeted focus on value learning can yield substantial performance improvements across diverse environments and policy architectures.

By investigating these questions, the authors aim to shift the focus from policy-centric designs to a deeper understanding and optimization of value learning dynamics in residual RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a systematic investigation approach to analyze value learning in residual RL, culminating in the proposal of a method called DAWN (Data-Anchored Warmup and Normalization).

**Minimal Baseline for Investigation:**
To isolate the core challenges of value learning, the authors establish a minimal residual RL setup. They use Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) as the underlying off-policy actor-critic algorithm. The residual scale (λ) is maintained as a task-specific hyperparameter, following values from previous work (Yuan et al., 2025). Importantly, the authors deliberately omit additional stabilization mechanisms, such as progressive exploration, that are present in some state-of-the-art residual RL methods. This is done to prevent these mechanisms from masking the raw dynamics and fundamental challenges of value learning, allowing for a clearer diagnosis. The residual policy is a lightweight MLP, and its final layer is initialized with near-zero weights to ensure initial actions are close to zero and do not disrupt the base policy.

**Dissecting Value Learning Challenges:**

**Challenge I: The Cold Start Pathology**
The critic begins training from a random initialization without knowledge of the value landscape around the base policy. The study investigates two potential remedies:

- **Implicit Warmup via Warmup Data:**
 - **Approach:** Transitions are collected using only the frozen base policy before online training commences. This data is stored in the replay buffer. The quantity of this "warmup data" is varied (0K, 5K, 10K, 20K, 40K transitions) to observe its effect on learning performance.
 - **Verification of Value Anchor Effect:** To quantify the grounding effect, a "Q-value grounding error" is introduced. This metric measures the discrepancy between the critic's value estimates Q(s, a_base) and the true Monte Carlo returns (G_π_base) obtained from base policy trajectories. This is contrasted with TD error to emphasize evaluation against true environmental returns.
 - **Warmup Data Collection Strategies:** Several strategies for collecting warmup data are compared: `Base Policy Only`, `Full Action` (base + random residual), `Gaussian Noise` (to base action), and `Epsilon-Greedy` (mixing base and full policy). This aims to determine if active exploration during warmup is beneficial.

- **Explicit Warmup Phase:**
 - **Approach:** The residual policy is frozen, and only the critic is trained for an initial "warmup phase" (10K gradient steps) using the collected warmup data.
 - **Variants:** Three configurations of explicit warmup are tested: `Explicit Soft Q (Auto α)` (standard SAC critic updates with automatic entropy tuning), `Explicit Soft Q (Fixed α)` (entropy coefficient fixed), and `Explicit Hard Q` (Bellman target without entropy regularization).
 - **Analysis:** The behavior of the entropy coefficient (α) and Q-value estimates during these explicit warmup phases is analyzed to understand potential failure modes, specifically focusing on entropy dominance and value collapse.

**Challenge II: The Structural Scale Mismatch**
The residual action (λ · a_res) is significantly smaller than the base action (a_base), making it difficult for the critic to discern value differences caused by the residual. The study explores two methodological paths:

- **Restoring Sensitivity via Normalization:**
 - **Approach:** Normalization techniques are applied to the critic network to decouple its internal representations from input magnitudes. Two specific methods are investigated:
 - **Layer Normalization (LN):** Normalizes activations across features within each layer.
 - **Hyperspherical Normalization (HN):** Projects input and weights onto a unit hypersphere, making output dependent only on the angle between vectors.
 - **Diagnostic Metrics:** Two metrics are used to analyze the effect of normalization:
 - **Critic Sensitivity:** Measured by the norm of the gradient of Q-value with respect to the residual action (∥∇_a_res Q∥).
 - **Value Difference:** Measured by the absolute difference between Q-values of the full policy and the base policy (|Q_full - Q_base|).
 - **Robustness:** The robustness of LN and HN is assessed across a range of residual scale (λ) values.
 - **Normalization Placement:** An ablation study is conducted to determine if normalization benefits the actor, the critic, or both.

- **Revisiting Distributional Objectives:**
 - **Approach:** The standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective for the critic is compared against three distributional RL methods:
 - **C51 (Categorical Distributional RL):** Models returns as a categorical distribution over fixed atoms.
 - **QR (Quantile Regression):** Learns a set of return quantiles.
 - **TQC (Truncated Quantile Critics):** Extends QR by truncating highest estimates to mitigate overestimation.
 - **Mechanistic Insight:** Action and Q-value distributions are visualized at critical training checkpoints to understand how the residual's effect manifests in value space, determining if a simple mean shift is sufficient or if richer distributional information is required.

**DAWN (Data-Anchored Warmup and Normalization) Method:**
Based on the insights derived from the investigations, DAWN is proposed as a minimal approach comprising two core interventions:
1. **Data-Anchored Warmup:** Collect M transitions using the base policy alone and store them in the replay buffer.
2. **Critic Normalization:** Apply Layer Normalization to the critic network.

**Experimental Validation:**
- **Benchmarks:** ManiSkill (contact-rich manipulation) and Adroit (dexterous hand control).
- **Base Policies:** Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2025) and Behavior Transformer (BeT) (Shafiullah et al., 2022).
- **Baselines:** Policy Decorator (Yuan et al., 2025) and Vanilla Residual RL (Johannink et al., 2019).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success rate versus environment steps.
- **Ablation Studies:** To confirm the necessity of each DAWN component and further investigate design choices (e.g., actor vs. critic normalization).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The investigation systematically identified and addressed two primary bottlenecks in value learning for residual RL, leading to the development of the DAWN method.

**A. Cold Start Pathology**

- **Necessity of Warmup Data as an Anchor:**
 - Experiments demonstrated that pre-collecting transitions solely from the base policy and seeding the replay buffer with this data substantially improved sample efficiency. Without warmup data, challenging tasks often failed to learn, and easier tasks exhibited significant inefficiency.
 - The "Q-value grounding error" analysis confirmed that warmup data acts as a "value anchor." In its absence, critic estimates diverged rapidly, failing to establish stable value estimates. With warmup data, the error dropped quickly and remained low, indicating the critic was grounded to the relevant value landscape.
 - Surprisingly, collecting warmup data with additional exploration (e.g., Gaussian noise, epsilon-greedy, or even trajectory-consistent noise) did not improve performance and often degraded it. This reinforced the finding that grounding the critic to the base policy's value landscape is more critical than diverse state-action coverage during the initial phase.

- **Redundancy of Explicit Warmup Phase:**
 - Explicit critic pre-training, where only the critic is updated for an initial phase, failed to improve sample efficiency and often deteriorated it.
 - This failure was attributed to "entropy dominance." In SAC, the entropy regularization term (α log π) can become excessively large at the start of training. Since the residual policy (π_res) is initialized to output near-zero actions with minimal variance, it is almost deterministic, resulting in a very large |log π|. Even a small entropy coefficient (α) causes the entropy term to dominate the reward, introducing a strong negative bias in the Q-target and leading to value collapse.
 - Explicit Hard Q (without entropy regularization) avoided value collapse but introduced an objective mismatch with subsequent Soft Q learning, preventing any meaningful benefit.
 - Increasing the quantity of warmup data did not resolve the issue, confirming that entropy dominance, rather than data insufficiency, was the fundamental problem.

**B. Structural Scale Mismatch**

- **Restoring Sensitivity via Normalization:**
 - Applying normalization techniques (Layer Normalization (LN) or Hyperspherical Normalization (HN)) to the critic network substantially improved sample efficiency across all tasks. This effect was most pronounced on tasks requiring high precision.
 - Diagnostic metrics revealed the mechanism: normalized critics exhibited significantly higher "critic sensitivity" (∥∇_a_res Q∥), meaning they responded more strongly to changes in the residual action. They also showed a clearer and more consistent "value contribution" from the residual policy (|Q_full - Q_base|).
 - While both LN and HN were effective, LN demonstrated greater robustness across a range of residual scale (λ) values, degrading less at smaller scales compared to HN. LN was adopted as the default for DAWN due to its adaptive statistics and simplicity.
 - Ablation studies confirmed that normalization was beneficial only when applied to the critic, providing no improvement and sometimes degrading performance when applied to the actor. This supported the hypothesis that scale mismatch primarily impacts value learning.

- **Distributional Objectives Offer No Efficiency Gains:**
 - A comparison of the standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective with distributional RL objectives (C51, QR, TQC) showed that MSE achieved competitive or superior early-stage performance. Distributional methods did not provide significant additional efficiency benefits for residual value learning.
 - Mechanistic analysis revealed why MSE was sufficient: despite the residual's small contribution making it nearly indistinguishable from the base action in action space, its effect on Q-values manifested as a clear *mean shift*. Normalization enabled the critic to translate subtle input differences into a distinct value signal primarily characterized by a shift in the mean of the Q-value distribution, rather than a complex change in its shape. Since MSE directly targets mean differences, it was well-suited to capture this signal.

**C. DAWN Performance and Ablations**

- **Overall Performance:** DAWN (Data-Anchored Warmup and Normalization) consistently achieved superior sample efficiency compared to state-of-the-art baselines like Policy Decorator and Vanilla Residual RL. It converged to high success rates significantly faster across 8 diverse tasks from ManiSkill and Adroit benchmarks, using both Diffusion Policy and Behavior Transformer as base policies, and across different observation modalities (state and visual). For instance, on the PegInsertionSide task, DAWN reached 90% success approximately 6 times faster than Policy Decorator.
- **Stability:** DAWN exhibited notably lower variance across multiple random seeds, suggesting improved training stability in addition to efficiency gains.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablations confirmed that both "data-anchored warmup" and "critic normalization" are essential and complementary components of DAWN. Removing either component led to substantial degradation in learning efficiency, as they target distinct bottlenecks.
- **Robustness to λ:** DAWN demonstrated robustness to the choice of residual scale λ, maintaining strong performance across various values without significant sensitivity.
- **Progressive Exploration:** The study found that Policy Decorator's progressive exploration mechanism, designed to stabilize training, became unnecessary when DAWN's efficient value learning mechanisms were in place. DAWN without progressive exploration still outperformed Policy Decorator even with its carefully tuned progressive schedule, highlighting the effectiveness of addressing root causes over symptom-level fixes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes notable contributions to the field of reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of leveraging pretrained policies for robotic control.

**Scientific Significance:**
The primary scientific contribution lies in providing the first systematic investigation into the fundamental challenges of value learning within residual reinforcement learning. By identifying and dissecting "cold start pathology" and "structural scale mismatch" as two critical and previously overlooked bottlenecks, the paper advances the understanding of why residual RL often struggles with sample efficiency. The mechanistic analyses, such as demonstrating the "value anchor" effect of base policy transitions, detailing the "entropy dominance" causing explicit warmup failure, and illustrating how normalization restores critic sensitivity despite input scale mismatch, offer deep insights beyond purely empirical observations. The finding that the residual's effect on Q-values manifests primarily as a mean shift, rendering complex distributional objectives unnecessary, simplifies the conceptual model of value learning in this domain. This work shifts the focus from purely policy-centric innovations to a more holistic understanding of the actor-critic interaction.

**Practical Impact:**
The proposed method, DAWN (Data-Anchored Warmup and Normalization), represents a practical and efficient solution for improving residual RL. Its simplicity, comprising just two interventions (data-anchored warmup and Layer Normalization for the critic), makes it easy to implement and broadly applicable. DAWN demonstrates substantial efficiency gains and improved training stability across diverse benchmarks, base policy architectures (Diffusion Policy, BeT), and observation modalities (state, visual). This allows for much faster online refinement of powerful pretrained policies, which is critical for real-world robotic deployment where online interaction data is costly and time-consuming. By eliminating the need for complex, task-specific protective mechanisms like progressive exploration schedules, DAWN also simplifies the deployment and tuning process.

**Broader Implications:**
The paper exemplifies a research philosophy that prioritizes identifying and addressing root causes of problems over developing complex machinery to mask symptoms. This approach led to a simpler yet more effective solution compared to prior work. The insights derived from this study could potentially inform the design of more efficient fine-tuning strategies for other foundation models in RL, encouraging a deeper look into the role of value functions in adaptive learning processes.

**Future Directions:**
The authors suggest extending this work to real-world robotic systems, where the robustness and efficiency demonstrated in simulation can be validated under real-world complexities. Further application to larger vision-language-action models is also identified as an important future direction. This research is expected to encourage the broader community to give increased consideration to the critical role of value learning when fine-tuning foundation policies.
