# Any2Caption:Interpreting Any Condition to Caption for Controllable Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Any2Caption introduces an "any-condition-to-caption" paradigm, leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to interpret diverse inputs like text, images, depth maps, poses, and camera movements into detailed, structured captions. This approach consistently enhances the controllability and visual quality of videos generated by existing text-to-video models across various conditions.

## Problem

- Text-only prompts are often insufficient to capture the complex, fine-grained user intent required for controllable video generation.
- Current video generation models struggle to accurately interpret and synthesize multiple heterogeneous multimodal inputs (e.g., images, poses, camera motion) simultaneously, limiting fine-grained control.
- The direct integration of diverse multimodal conditions into diffusion model backbones can be inefficient and less interpretable for complex reasoning.

## Method

- **Any2Caption** proposes an MLLM-based framework that decouples the complex task of condition interpretation from video synthesis, acting as a universal translator.
- It processes any combination of input conditions (text, image, video, depth, pose, camera) using specialized encoders and an **MLLM** backbone (**Qwen2-LLM**) to generate a unified, rich, structured textual caption.
- A large-scale instruction-tuning dataset, **Any2CapIns**, and a two-stage progressive mixed training strategy are used to teach the **MLLM** to interpret conditions into specific six-component captions.

## Results

- Any2Caption achieves a Structural Integrity of 91.25% for generated captions and high lexical/semantic matching (e.g., ROUGE-L 48.63, BERTSCORE 91.95), demonstrating accurate condition interpretation.
- Structured captions generated by Any2Caption consistently improve overall video quality and enhance adherence to diverse non-text conditions across various state-of-the-art controllable video generation methods.
- The model shows strong performance in handling multiple combined conditions and generalizes effectively to 'unseen' conditions (e.g., style, segmentation), improving motion smoothness and aesthetic quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the interpretation of diverse input conditions from the video generation process, by translating conditions into rich structured captions via MLLMs, effectively enhances controllability.
> - A structured caption format, comprising six granular components (dense, main object, background, camera, style, action), is crucial for providing disentangled and effective guidance to video generators.
> - Leveraging the robust vision-language comprehension of MLLMs allows for a 'plug-in' module that can seamlessly integrate with any off-the-shelf text-to-video generator without architectural modifications or retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has rapidly advanced, particularly with the advent of Diffusion Transformers (DiT) models, capable of generating increasingly realistic and long-duration videos from text prompts, as exemplified by models like OpenAI's Sora. While these models represent a significant leap, a critical bottleneck remains: accurately interpreting nuanced user intent to produce highly controllable and high-quality video outputs.

This paper addresses this bottleneck by proposing a novel "any-condition-to-caption" paradigm, which positions itself at the intersection of:

- **Text-to-Video (T2V) Generation:** The core capability of generating videos from text. Prior work has shown that detailed text prompts enhance controllability, leading to efforts in "video recaptioning" (e.g., ShareGPT4Video, MiraData, InstanceCap) to create dense, structured captions for training T2V models. Any2Caption builds on this by generating such captions during *inference* rather than just relying on them for *training*.
- **Controllable Video Generation (Multimodal Conditioning):** To achieve more fine-grained control, researchers have moved beyond pure text, incorporating diverse visual conditions like reference images, identities, styles, human poses, or camera movements (e.g., VideoComposer, Ctrl-Adapter, ControlVideo). However, existing methods often struggle to interpret and synthesize multiple heterogeneous input modalities accurately, as current DiT backbones have limited capacity for complex multimodal reasoning. Any2Caption explicitly tackles this limitation by offloading the complex interpretation to a specialized module.
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** MLLMs have demonstrated robust vision-language comprehension and reasoning capabilities (e.g., Qwen2-VL, LLaVA). This paper leverages this strength by using an MLLM as the core interpreter. This is a crucial distinction from prior controllable video generation models that attempt to integrate multimodal encoding directly into the diffusion model's latent space, which can be less efficient and interpretable.

Any2Caption distinguishes itself by **decoupling the complex task of condition interpretation from the subsequent task of video synthesis.** This is a significant shift. Instead of training or fine-tuning a video generation model to understand diverse visual cues directly, Any2Caption acts as an intelligent "translator." It takes any combination of conditions (text, image, video, depth, pose, camera) and translates them into a rich, structured textual caption. This caption then serves as a highly effective, unified control signal for *any* off-the-shelf, state-of-the-art text-to-video generator, eliminating the need for expensive retraining or architectural modifications of the backbone video model. This "plug-in" approach makes it highly versatile and broadly applicable across the evolving landscape of video generation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **address the bottleneck of accurate user intent interpretation within the current video generation community**, thereby enabling **controllable video generation under any condition.**

The motivations for pursuing this objective are rooted in two key observations and related challenges:

1. **Limitations of Text-Only Prompts:** While text-to-video models are powerful, simple or concise user prompts often fail to capture the full complexity of desired video content (objects, actions, attributes, poses, camera movements, style). Manual prompt refinement is tedious, and automatic prompt enrichment can introduce noise or misinterpretations. This gap between user intent and model comprehension leads to suboptimal generation quality and reduced controllability.
2. **Challenges with Multimodal Conditioning in Existing Models:** To achieve fine-grained control, users increasingly provide additional visual conditions alongside text. However, current video generation models (especially DiT backbones) often struggle with simultaneously interpreting rich heterogeneous multimodal inputs and textual prompts. Their internal encoders have limited capacity for reasoning across diverse modalities with intricate requirements (e.g., multiple object identities, complex camera movements), leading to suboptimal generation quality despite the input conditions.

Therefore, the **key idea and motivation** behind Any2Caption is to **decouple the complex and diverse condition interpretation steps from the actual video synthesis step.** This is based on the premise that:
 - State-of-the-art video generation models already excel at producing high-quality videos when provided with sufficiently rich and detailed text captions.
 - Modern Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated robust capabilities in vision-language comprehension and reasoning.

By leveraging MLLMs for interpreting diverse inputs into dense, structured captions, Any2Caption aims to provide backbone video generators with much clearer, disentangled guidance, ultimately enhancing controllability and video quality across a wide range of input conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Any2Caption proposes a novel framework that systematically processes various input conditions to generate highly detailed, structured text captions, which then guide any off-the-shelf video generator.

**Core Idea: Decoupling Condition Interpretation from Video Generation**
The central tenet is to separate the "understanding" phase from the "generation" phase. Instead of forcing video generators to internally process complex multimodal inputs, Any2Caption acts as a universal interpreter that translates these conditions into a unified, rich textual format.

**Any2Caption Architecture:**
The framework is built upon an MLLM (specifically, **Qwen2-LLM** is used as the backbone) and augmented with specialized encoders for different modalities:
- **Image Encoder (F_I) and Video Encoder (F_V):** These leverage a ViT-based visual encoder from Qwen2-VL to process image- and video-based conditions like depth maps, sketches, video frames, or multiple identities.
- **Motion Encoder (F_M):** To understand human pose, extracted human pose trajectories (keypoints over frames) are visualized within video frames and processed by a dedicated motion encoder, which shares architecture and initialization with the vision encoder.
- **Camera Encoder (F_C):** For camera motion control, a specialized camera encoder processes a Plücker embedding sequence, accurately capturing camera pose information and trajectories.
- **LLM Backbone (Qwen2-LLM):** This is the core reasoning component. It integrates the features from all encoders along with the initial short text prompt. Special tokens (`<|motion start|>`, `<|motion end|>`, `<|camera start|>`, `<|camera end|>`) are introduced to delineate motion and camera features, enabling the LLM to differentiate and reason over these diverse inputs. The LLM's role is to synthesize these inputs into a coherent, structured caption.

**Any2CapIns Dataset Construction:**
To facilitate the MLLM's ability to interpret diverse conditions into structured captions, the authors introduce **Any2CapIns**, a large-scale instruction-tuning dataset. The construction involves a three-step pipeline:
1. **Data Collection:** Videos are systematically categorized by conditions:
 - **Spatial-wise:** Depth maps, sketches, video frames (e.g., generated by Depth Anything, SAM2).
 - **Action-wise:** Human pose, motion (e.g., generated by DW-Pose).
 - **Composition-wise:** Scene composition, multiple identities.
 - **Camera-wise:** Camera angles, movement trajectories.
 In total, 337K video instances and 407K conditions are collected.
2. **Structured Video Caption Generation:** The dataset uses a comprehensive structured caption format, inspired by prior work but enhanced with an explicit action component. This structured caption consists of six granular components:
 - Dense caption (overall scene)
 - Main object caption
 - Background caption
 - Camera caption
 - Style caption
 - Action caption
 Each component is generated independently and then integrated.
3. **User-centric Short Prompt Generation:** To simulate realistic user input, concise "short prompts" are generated from the detailed structured captions using GPT-4V. This process considers user prompt characteristics like conciseness, condition-dependent omission (e.g., omitting camera movement if a camera condition is provided), and implicit instruction of target video. Manual verification refines the dataset.

**Training Recipes (Two-Stage Procedure):**
1. **Stage-I: Alignment Learning:** This stage focuses on aligning the motion and camera encoder features with the LLM backbone's word embeddings. The LLM and vision encoders are frozen, while the motion and camera encoders are trained on specific human pose and camera movement description tasks, respectively. This establishes a strong foundation for condition representation.
2. **Stage-II: Condition-Interpreting Learning:** Building on the aligned encoders, the model is fine-tuned on the Any2CapIns dataset. To prevent catastrophic forgetting and enhance generalization, a **progressive mixed training strategy** is employed. Training starts with single conditions, and gradually, additional vision-language instruction datasets (e.g., LLaVA-instruction, Alpaca-52K) are incorporated. A random sentence-level dropout mechanism (with a rate of 0.6) is applied to short captions during training to enhance robustness and prevent overfitting to specific input phrasing.

**Evaluation Suite:**
The paper introduces a comprehensive evaluation suite beyond standard metrics:
- **Lexical Matching:** BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and a custom **Structural Integrity** score (to check the 6-component format).
- **Semantic Matching:** BERTSCORE and CLIP Score (for visual-text consistency).
- **Intent Reasoning Score (IRSCORE):** A novel LLM-based metric that leverages GPT-4V to assess if generated captions accurately capture user intentions across six aspects (subject, background, movement, camera, interaction, style) by constructing and evaluating QA pairs.
- **Video Generation Quality Score:** Standard metrics for smoothness, dynamic degree, aesthetic quality, image integrity, plus specialized metrics for condition adherence (RotErr, TransErr, CamMC for camera; MAE for depth; DINO-I, CLIP-I for identity; Pose Accuracy for human pose).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and versatility of Any2Caption across various aspects:

- **High-Quality Structured Caption Generation (RQ-1):**
 - Any2Caption achieves a **Structural Integrity of 91.25%**, indicating its success in producing the desired six-component caption format.
 - Strong lexical (ROUGE-L: 48.63) and semantic (BERTSCORE: 91.95) matching scores confirm accurate interpretation and detailed captioning.
 - The model shows strongest performance in interpreting camera-related details (e.g., ROUGE-L 96.10, BERTSCORE 99.31 for camera captions).
 - The **IRSCORE** confirms the model reliably incorporates user preferences into structured outputs, showing a strong grasp of user intent.
 - **Visualizations (Fig. 5):** Structured captions generated by Any2Caption, when fed into off-the-shelf T2V models (CogVideoX-2B, HunyuanVideo), enabled generation of videos that accurately align with complex input conditions (e.g., specific object appearances, depth sequences, compositional requirements), even without explicit visual conditioning in the T2V model itself.

- **Enhanced Video Generation Quality with Structured Captions (RQ-2):**
 - Integrating structured captions consistently **improves overall video quality** (smoothness, frame fidelity) and **enhances adherence to specified non-text conditions** across various state-of-the-art controllable video generation methods (e.g., MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, Ctrl-Adapter, ControlVideo, ConceptMaster, FollowYourPose).
 - Improvements were observed in metrics like CLIP-T (semantic consistency), RotErr/TransErr/CamMC (camera accuracy), DINO-I/CLIP-I (identity preservation), MAE (depth consistency), and Pose Acc. (pose alignment). This indicates that the generated captions precisely capture user requirements, leading to more accurate and visually coherent video outputs.

- **Necessity of Structured Caption Design (RQ-3):**
 - A comparison showed that merely concatenating a condition's caption to the short prompt (without a structured approach) could **reduce video smoothness and image quality**, likely due to conflicting or extraneous details introduced from the condition caption.
 - The structured caption method, by accurately identifying and augmenting prompts with *relevant* information, yields significantly more controllable video generation, highlighting the importance of the disentangled and organized caption format.

- **Effectiveness of Training Strategy (RQ-4):**
 - **Two-stage training** (alignment learning followed by instruction tuning) is crucial. Omitting alignment learning significantly degrades both captioning and video generation performance, indicating the importance of proper encoder adaptation.
 - The **dropout mechanism** during training is essential for robustness. While removing dropout improved captioning quality slightly, it did not significantly benefit video generation, suggesting that without dropout, the model might overfit to input captions rather than genuinely understanding underlying intent.

- **Strong Performance on Compositional Conditions (RQ-5):**
 - Any2Caption consistently enhances performance when handling **multiple combined conditions** (e.g., Camera+Identities, Camera+Depth, Identities+Depth, and Camera+Identities+Depth).
 - The model demonstrates a thorough understanding of interactions among various conditions, accurately guiding video production to align with intricate user requirements (e.g., capturing specific hair color, object positions, and compositional needs in Figure 5 examples).

- **Good Generalization Capability (RQ-6):**
 - Any2Caption shows strong generalization to **"unseen" conditions** like style, segmentation, sketch, and masked images (Figure 6).
 - Structured captions generated for these unseen conditions consistently improve existing T2V frameworks in terms of motion smoothness, aesthetic quality, and accurate generation control. This is attributed to the MLLM backbone's strong reasoning capabilities and the progressive mixed training strategy, which leverages diverse vision and text instructions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Any2Caption presents a highly significant advancement in controllable video generation with several potential impacts:

- **Pioneering a New Paradigm:** The "any-condition-to-caption" approach is a novel and intuitive paradigm that effectively bridges the gap between diverse multimodal user intentions and the instructional requirements of advanced video generators. This decoupling simplifies the problem space, making complex control more accessible.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Quality:** By providing meticulously structured and semantically rich captions, Any2Caption significantly improves the controllability and visual quality of generated videos across a wide array of conditions, from simple text prompts to complex combinations of visual cues. This directly translates to more reliable and predictable creative outputs.
- **Versatile "Plug-in" Module:** One of its most impactful aspects is its ability to seamlessly integrate with *any* existing text-to-video generation framework as a plug-in module. This flexibility eliminates the need for expensive retraining or architectural modifications of backbone video models, accelerating the adoption of advanced control capabilities across the rapidly evolving landscape of video generation technologies. It democratizes access to fine-grained control for various generative models.
- **New Benchmark Dataset:** The introduction of **Any2CapIns**, a large-scale, high-quality benchmark dataset for any-condition-to-caption instruction tuning, is a substantial contribution to the research community. This dataset, along with the established evaluation suite, will foster further research and development in multimodal condition interpretation.
- **Leveraging MLLM Strengths:** The work effectively harnesses the powerful vision-language comprehension and reasoning capabilities of MLLMs, showcasing their utility beyond traditional VQA or captioning tasks into complex generative control.

**Potential Impact:**
The immediate impact lies in making video generation tools more intuitive and powerful for creative professionals (filmmakers, animators, designers) and general users alike. It could enable more precise ideation and iteration, allowing users to articulate complex creative visions with greater fidelity. This could democratize high-quality video production, making advanced customization accessible even without deep technical expertise.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Data Diversity and Real-world Gaps:** Annotation tool limitations and scarcity of real-world data might constrain diversity and generalizability.
- **Hallucinations:** MLLMs can still hallucinate, potentially leading to inaccurate captions and degraded video quality. Future work might explore end-to-end approaches to mitigate this.
- **Inference Time:** Additional condition-understanding modules inevitably increase inference time. Future research can focus on more efficient architectures or optimization techniques to balance speed and accuracy.

Despite these, Any2Caption represents a significant step forward in making video generation truly controllable and user-intent-driven, setting a new standard for how diverse creative conditions can be interpreted and translated into compelling video content.
