# CrowdHuman: A Benchmark for Detecting Human in a Crowd

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Megvii Inc. developed CrowdHuman, a large-scale benchmark dataset for human detection in highly crowded and occluded scenes, featuring comprehensive annotations for full bodies, visible parts, and heads. Models pre-trained on CrowdHuman consistently improved performance and established new state-of-the-art results when fine-tuned on existing benchmarks like Caltech and CityPersons.

## Problem

- Existing human detection datasets suffer from low person density and limited instances of severe occlusion, inadequately representing real-world crowded environments.
- High levels of mutual occlusion between individuals in dense crowds pose significant challenges for detectors in delineating distinct person boundaries and for post-processing steps like Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS).
- Annotation protocols in some prior datasets often classify heavily occluded crowd regions as 'ignored,' limiting their utility for training robust models capable of handling complex occlusion.

## Method

- Created **CrowdHuman**, a new dataset featuring 470,000 human instances across 25,000 images, specifically designed with high person density and extensive occlusion.
- Implemented a detailed, ==three-step annotation process== to provide ==full-body==, ==visible-region==, and ==head bounding boxes== for each individual, including inferring occluded body parts for full-body annotations.
- Conducted a thorough statistical analysis demonstrating CrowdHuman's significantly higher person density (22.6 persons per image) and greater incidence of pairwise and high-order overlaps compared to existing benchmarks.

## Results

- State-of-the-art detectors like FPN and RetinaNet showed higher miss rates on CrowdHuman (e.g., FPN achieved **50.42 mMR** for full body detection), quantitatively confirming the dataset's increased difficulty.
- Models pre-trained on CrowdHuman and then fine-tuned on existing datasets consistently achieved performance gains, for example, improving FPN's mMR on COCOPersons from **41.89** to **39.79**.
- The approach established new state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks, including an mMR of **3.46** on Caltech (compared to previous **4.0 mMR**) and **10.67 mMR** on CityPersons (compared to previous **13.2 mMR**) using standard detection frameworks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - CrowdHuman provides a uniquely challenging benchmark that explicitly targets the complexities of human detection in densely crowded and highly occluded scenes, addressing a critical gap in computer vision research.
> - The dataset's rich, multi-level annotations (full-body, visible-region, and head boxes) enable the development and evaluation of more sophisticated, occlusion-aware detection strategies.
> - CrowdHuman serves as an effective pre-training resource, demonstrating that models trained on it learn robust and generalizable features, leading to performance improvements when fine-tuned on other human detection benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human detection is a foundational component within the field of computer vision, serving as a critical prerequisite for a wide array of applications such as autonomous vehicles, intelligent surveillance systems, robotics, and advanced human-machine interaction. Furthermore, it underpins subsequent research areas including multi-object tracking, human pose estimation, and person search. The advent and proliferation of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have significantly advanced the performance of modern human detection systems on various established benchmarks.

Despite the notable progress, a persistent challenge in human detection pertains to the accurate identification of individuals within highly crowded environments where occlusion is prevalent. Existing major human detection datasets, such as Caltech-USA, KITTI, CityPersons, and the "person" subset of MSCOCO, have been instrumental in driving algorithmic improvements. However, these datasets are characterized by a relatively low density of persons per image and limited instances of significant occlusion. For example, the average number of persons per image is reported as 0.32 in Caltech-USA, 4.01 in COCOPersons, and 6.47 in CityPersons. Additionally, the average pairwise overlap between human instances (with an Intersection over Union, IoU, greater than 0.5) in these datasets is notably low, ranging from 0.02 to 0.32.

This under-representation of crowd scenarios in existing benchmarks contributes to difficulties for current detectors. In crowded scenes, high overlaps between individuals can lead to detectors failing to delineate distinct person boundaries, potentially treating a group as a single entity or misplacing bounding boxes. The post-processing step of non-maximum suppression (NMS) also becomes sensitive in such environments; a low NMS threshold can drastically reduce recall, while a high threshold may introduce an increase in false positives. Moreover, annotation protocols in some existing datasets often classify heavily occluded crowd regions as "ignored," effectively excluding them from training and evaluation. The presented work addresses this specific limitation by introducing a new dataset designed to explicitly target and facilitate research on human detection in densely crowded and highly occluded conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of human detection by specifically addressing the challenges posed by highly crowded scenes. The authors identify a significant gap in existing benchmarks, which, despite their contributions to human detection progress, do not adequately represent the complexities of detecting individuals in dense crowds with substantial occlusion.

The detailed motivations for creating the CrowdHuman dataset are as follows:

- **Insufficient Representation of Crowd Scenarios:** Current benchmarks (e.g., Caltech-USA, KITTI, CityPersons, COCOPersons) exhibit low person densities and minimal instances of severe occlusion. This statistical characteristic means that algorithms trained and evaluated on these datasets may not generalize effectively to real-world scenarios where crowds are common. The paper highlights that the average number of persons per image in these datasets is significantly lower than what is encountered in truly crowded environments.
- **Challenges of Occlusion:** High levels of mutual occlusion between individuals in a crowd present a formidable challenge for detectors. When pedestrians heavily overlap, it becomes difficult for algorithms to accurately segment and identify the boundaries of each person. This can lead to bounding boxes encompassing multiple individuals or misattributing parts of one person to another.
- **Limitations of Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS):** The standard NMS post-processing step, crucial for removing redundant detections, proves problematic in crowded settings. A stringent NMS threshold designed to minimize false positives may inadvertently suppress correct detections of highly overlapping individuals, leading to a drop in recall. Conversely, a relaxed threshold to retain more detections can result in an increase in false positives by failing to adequately distinguish between closely clustered detections.
- **Incomplete Annotation in Existing Datasets:** Some current datasets' annotation protocols often categorize heavily occluded or dense crowd regions as "ignored." This practice, while simplifying annotation efforts, means that valuable information regarding challenging instances is not utilized for training or evaluation, thereby limiting the robustness of learned models.

To address these motivations, the key objectives for the CrowdHuman dataset are:

- **To provide a large-scale and diverse dataset:** The dataset aims to be comprehensive, encompassing a wide variety of scenes and person characteristics encountered in real-world crowded environments.
- **To ensure high person density and significant occlusion:** Explicitly designing the dataset to feature a substantially higher average number of persons per image and a greater incidence of pairwise and higher-order overlaps compared to existing benchmarks.
- **To offer rich, multi-level annotations:** Providing not only full-body bounding boxes but also visible-region and head bounding boxes for each individual person, enabling more nuanced evaluation and facilitating the development of occlusion-robust detection strategies.
- **To serve as an effective pre-training resource:** Demonstrating that training models on CrowdHuman can improve generalization capabilities and yield superior performance when fine-tuned on other pedestrian, person, and head detection benchmarks.
- **To establish a new, challenging baseline:** Creating a benchmark that pushes the capabilities of current state-of-the-art human detection algorithms, thereby stimulating further research and development in this domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce the CrowdHuman dataset, which is specifically designed to address human detection in crowded scenes. The methodology encompasses data collection, annotation protocols, statistical analysis, and experimental evaluation using baseline detectors.

**Data Collection:**
Images for the CrowdHuman dataset were sourced from the Google image search engine. The collection process utilized approximately 150 diverse keywords, such as "Pedestrians on the Fifth Avenue," "people crossing the roads," "students playing basketball," and "friends at a party." These keywords were chosen to ensure a wide range of content, covering over 40 different cities globally, various activities (e.g., parties, travel, sports), and multiple viewpoints (e.g., surveillance, horizontal). To maintain a balanced distribution, the number of images crawled per keyword was limited to 500. From an initial pool of approximately 60,000 candidate images, around 25,000 images were selected for the final dataset after filtering out those with a low number of persons or minimal overlaps between individuals. The dataset is divided into 15,000 images for training, 4,370 for validation, and 5,000 for testing.

**Image Annotation:**
The annotation process for individual persons was executed in three distinct steps:
1. **Full Bounding Box:** Annotators were tasked with exhaustively drawing a full bounding box for each individual. Critically, if a person was partially occluded, the annotator was required to infer and complete the invisible parts to delineate the full body. This approach differs from some existing datasets (e.g., CityPersons) that use fixed aspect ratios for bounding box generation. Human-like objects, such as statues, were also annotated but with a specific label indicating they should be ignored during evaluation.
2. **Visible Bounding Box:** After the full bounding boxes were created, cropped regions corresponding to each annotated instance were presented to annotators for drawing a bounding box around the visible portion of the person.
3. **Head Bounding Box:** In a subsequent step, the same cropped regions were used to annotate the head bounding box for each individual.
All annotations underwent a double-checking process by at least one different annotator to ensure quality and consistency.

**Dataset Statistics:**
The CrowdHuman dataset's characteristics were extensively compared with existing benchmarks (Caltech-USA, KITTI, CityPersons, COCOPersons) across several dimensions:
- **Size:** The training and validation subsets of CrowdHuman contain a total of 470,000 human instances. The training subset alone features 339,565 person annotations and 99,227 ignore region annotations, which is more than ten times the number of persons in datasets like CityPersons.
- **Density:** CrowdHuman exhibits an average of 22.6 persons per image, significantly higher than Caltech (0.32), KITTI (0.63), CityPersons (6.47), and COCOPersons (4.01). A substantial proportion of CrowdHuman images contain more than 10, 20, or even 30 persons, indicating a high level of crowdness.
- **Diversity:** The dataset's images are sourced from the internet, leading to a wide range of poses and domains, contrasting with datasets like Caltech, KITTI, and CityPersons, which are primarily recorded from vehicles on streets. CrowdHuman also boasts approximately 33,000 unique persons, indicating higher diversity compared to datasets with less sparsely sampled images.
- **Occlusion:**
 - **Visible Ratio:** The distribution of occlusion levels (defined by the ratio of visible bounding box area to full bounding box area) is more balanced in CrowdHuman, with 29.89% "bare" (occlusion ≤ 30%), 32.13% "partial" (30% < occlusion ≤ 70%), and the remainder "heavy" (occlusion > 70%). This contrasts with CityPersons, which has a higher proportion of low-occlusion instances.
 - **Pairwise Overlap:** CrowdHuman shows an average of 9.02 person pairs per image with an IoU greater than 0.3, and 2.4 pairs with an IoU greater than 0.5. These numbers are substantially higher than those in other datasets (e.g., CityPersons has 0.96 and 0.32 respectively for these thresholds).
 - **High-Order Overlaps:** The dataset also features a notable representation of high-order overlaps (among three human instances), which are rarely found in previous datasets.

**Experimental Evaluation:**
The evaluation involved two state-of-the-art baseline detectors: Faster R-CNN with Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) and RetinaNet, both utilizing a ResNet-50 backbone. FPN represents two-stage detection algorithms, while RetinaNet represents one-stage algorithms.

- **Evaluation Metric:** The primary metric used is the average log miss rate (mMR) over false positives per image (FPPI) ranging from 10^-2 to 10^0, consistent with the Caltech benchmark. Average Precision (AP) and recall are also reported. Ignored regions are excluded from evaluation.
- **Implementation Details:**
 - Anchor aspect ratios were adjusted to {1:1, 1.5:1, 2:1, 2.5:1, 3:1} for full body detection and {1:2, 1:1, 2:1} for visible body and head detection, to better match human body shapes.
 - Input image sizes were standardized: short edge at 800 pixels and long edge not exceeding 1400 pixels for CrowdHuman and MSCOCO. Other datasets had specific resizing protocols (e.g., 2x and 1x for Caltech and CityPersons, 640x480 for Brainwash).
 - Training was conducted on 8 GPUs using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) for 600,000 and 750,000 iterations for FPN and RetinaNet, respectively, with a base learning rate of 0.02 and scheduled decays. No batch normalization layers were fine-tuned, and multi-scale training/testing was not applied.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed findings from experiments conducted on the CrowdHuman dataset itself and cross-dataset evaluations to assess its generalization capabilities.

**Detection Results on CrowdHuman Dataset:**
Experiments were performed for visible body, full body, and head detection tasks using FPN and RetinaNet.
- **Visible Body Detection:** FPN achieved a miss rate (mMR) of 55.94, while RetinaNet showed an mMR of 65.47. FPN demonstrated superior performance over RetinaNet in this task. The relatively high mMR values indicate that CrowdHuman poses a significant challenge for existing state-of-the-art human detection algorithms.
- **Full Body Detection:** For full body detection, FPN recorded an mMR of 50.42, and RetinaNet obtained an mMR of 63.33. FPN again outperformed RetinaNet. The mMR values on CrowdHuman are considerably higher than those reported for FPN on Caltech (10.08 mMR) and CityPersons (14.81 mMR), quantitatively demonstrating the increased difficulty of the CrowdHuman dataset for full body detection.
- **Head Detection:** In head detection, FPN achieved an mMR of 52.06, while RetinaNet registered an mMR of 60.64. FPN maintained its performance advantage over RetinaNet.
Overall, FPN consistently exhibited stronger performance compared to RetinaNet across all three detection tasks on the CrowdHuman dataset.

**Cross-Dataset Evaluation (Generalization Ability):**
To evaluate the generalization ability of the CrowdHuman dataset, models were first trained on CrowdHuman and then fine-tuned on various other human detection benchmarks. FPN was selected as the baseline detector for these experiments due to its stronger performance on CrowdHuman.
- **COCOPersons:** Pre-training on CrowdHuman and then fine-tuning on COCOPersons resulted in an mMR of 39.79, which improved upon training solely on COCOPersons (41.89 mMR).
- **Caltech Dataset:**
 - Training FPN solely on CrowdHuman yielded an mMR of 8.81 on the Caltech dataset, surpassing the performance of models trained on Caltech alone (10.08 mMR) or CityPersons (14.69 mMR).
 - The most notable improvement was observed when pre-training on CrowdHuman and subsequently fine-tuning on Caltech, achieving an mMR of 3.46. This result not only outperformed models trained on individual datasets but also surpassed previously reported state-of-the-art results on Caltech, such as 4.0 mMR by Repulsion Loss and 5.1 mMR by CityPersons⇒Calt.
- **CityPersons Dataset:**
 - Training FPN on CrowdHuman alone produced an mMR of 21.18 on the CityPersons dataset.
 - Pre-training on CrowdHuman followed by fine-tuning on CityPersons resulted in an mMR of 10.67. This significantly improved upon training solely on CityPersons (14.81 mMR) and also outperformed other state-of-the-art methods like Repulsion Loss (13.2 mMR).
- **Brainwash Dataset (Head Detection):** Pre-training on CrowdHuman and fine-tuning on Brainwash improved the head detection performance to an mMR of 17.24, compared to 19.77 mMR when training only on Brainwash. This also represented a substantial improvement over the previously reported results on the Brainwash dataset.

These cross-dataset evaluation results collectively demonstrate that the CrowdHuman dataset can serve as an effective and powerful pre-training resource. Training on CrowdHuman before fine-tuning on target datasets consistently led to performance gains and, in several instances, established new state-of-the-art results on existing benchmarks for pedestrian, person, and head detection, utilizing standard general object detection frameworks without architectural modifications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the CrowdHuman dataset presents several points of significance and holds potential for notable impact within the computer vision research community.

Firstly, the dataset addresses a critical void in existing human detection benchmarks. By explicitly focusing on highly crowded and occluded environments, CrowdHuman provides a dedicated resource for evaluating and developing algorithms that can handle these complex real-world scenarios, which are under-represented in previous datasets. This directly confronts the limitations of current detectors when faced with dense populations and significant inter-person occlusion.

Secondly, the rich annotation scheme of CrowdHuman, which includes full-body, visible-region, and head bounding boxes for each individual, offers a granular level of detail previously unavailable in a single large-scale dataset. This comprehensive annotation set can foster the development of more sophisticated visual algorithms. For instance, the availability of visible-region boxes can aid in designing occlusion-aware detectors, while head boxes might facilitate robust detection and tracking in situations where full bodies are heavily obscured or where a compact representation is preferred for counting or behavioral analysis.

Thirdly, the demonstrated cross-dataset generalization ability of CrowdHuman establishes its utility as a powerful pre-training dataset. The experimental results show that models trained on CrowdHuman and then fine-tuned on other benchmarks (Caltech, CityPersons, COCOPersons, Brainwash) achieve improved or state-of-the-art performance. This suggests that CrowdHuman effectively captures diverse human appearances, poses, and occlusion patterns, enabling detectors to learn more robust and generalizable features. This capability can accelerate research by providing a strong initial training phase for various human-centric computer vision tasks without requiring complex architectural modifications.

Finally, CrowdHuman establishes a new, challenging baseline for human detection research. The consistently higher miss rates observed on CrowdHuman compared to other datasets indicate that it pushes the limits of current state-of-the-art detectors. By setting a higher standard, the dataset can stimulate further innovation in algorithm design, particularly in areas like advanced occlusion handling, improved NMS strategies for dense scenes, and multi-scale feature learning for varied human scales within a crowd. This work is anticipated to promote future research directions aimed at building more resilient and accurate human detection systems for real-world applications.
