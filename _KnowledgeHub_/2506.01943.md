# Learning Video Generation for Robotic Manipulation with Collaborative Trajectory Control

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboMaster generates robot manipulation videos via a collaborative trajectory that decomposes the interaction into pre-interaction, interaction, and post-interaction phases, driven by robotic arm and manipulated object respectively, with appearance-and-shape-aware object embeddings. Achieves state-of-the-art trajectory-controlled video generation on Bridge, RLBench, and SIMPLER.

## Problem

- Prior trajectory-controlled video generation methods drive each object with an independent, isolated trajectory, ignoring interaction between robot arm and manipulated object.
- Independently modeled object motions cause entangled features in overlapping regions during interaction, degrading visual fidelity and object identity.
- Point-based object representations used by prior methods (e.g. Tora, MotionCtrl) are shape-ambiguous, giving imprecise trajectory control and unreliable identity consistency.

## Method

- ==Collaborative trajectory==: decomposes interaction into pre-interaction, interaction, and post-interaction sub-phases, each driven by the dominant subject (robot arm before/after, manipulated object during), unifying joint motion into one trajectory instead of separate per-object ones.
- ==Coupled appearance-and-shape subject embedding==: pools masked ==VAE== latent features per object then expands them into a circular volume centered on the trajectory point with radius proportional to mask area, preserving identity across frames.
- ==Motion injector with causal representation==: patchifies and convolves the collaborative trajectory latent, injects it into ==DiT== blocks via ==zero-initialized conv layers==, and propagates prior-frame object features causally into interaction/post-interaction phases for smooth transitions.

## Results

- On Bridge (**214** test samples), RoboMaster beats Tora, DragAnything, MotionCtrl, IRASim, TesserAct on FVD (**147.31** vs **152.28** best baseline), PSNR (**21.55**), SSIM (**0.803**), and trajectory error, and wins **45.16%** user preference versus **17.74%** for Tora.
- On RLBench and SIMPLER action-planning benchmarks (**100** trials/task), RoboMaster-generated demonstrations plus an inverse dynamics model outperform Tora on **8 of 10** tasks and beat OpenVLA/TesserAct baselines on most tasks.
- Ablations show removing causal embedding or using point-based instead of mask-based representation raises trajectory error substantially (**24.16** to **31.41** for points), and cross-attention injection degrades FVD/PSNR versus the proposed conv-based injector.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing an interaction into phases dominated by a single active subject at a time avoids the feature-entanglement problem that arises when multiple independently-moving objects overlap in frame.
> - Mask-based, shape-and-appearance-aware object representations generalize better than point-based ones for maintaining object identity under manipulation, and stay robust even with sparse or imprecise user-drawn masks.
> - Higher-fidelity interaction video generation directly improves downstream action-label extraction, showing video-generation quality bottlenecks robotic policy learning more than the choice of inverse dynamics model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of Embodied AI, specifically focusing on robotic manipulation and learning. A significant challenge in this domain is data scarcity, as collecting large-scale, high-quality data using real robots is resource-intensive and requires extensive human supervision. Video generation has emerged as a potential solution to mitigate this bottleneck by synthesizing realistic environments and robot decision-making data.

Existing approaches to video generation for robotics have explored various conditioning inputs, including text instructions, sketches, and trajectories. Trajectory-conditioned generation is particularly relevant for fine-grained control over robot planning, as it allows for structured motions of both the robotic arm and the manipulated object. However, prior methods, such as Tora and DragAnything, primarily focus on driving individual object motions with separate trajectories. This approach leads to limitations, particularly in multi-object interaction scenarios, where feature entanglement in overlapping regions can degrade visual fidelity and lead to physically implausible interactions. The paper identifies that if synthesized videos fail to accurately capture interaction phases, subsequent inverse dynamics models may extract unreliable actions, thereby limiting the effectiveness of learned robotic policies.

This research aims to address these identified limitations by introducing a framework that explicitly models inter-object dynamics within a unified, collaborative trajectory representation, thereby enhancing visual quality and interaction fidelity in generated robotic manipulation videos. It contributes to the development of high-quality synthetic data for scalable robot learning and decision-making.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a video generation framework capable of synthesizing realistic robotic manipulation videos with fine-grained and user-friendly control, specifically addressing the challenges of multi-object interaction.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and limitations in the current state of video generation for robotic manipulation:

- **Data Scarcity in Robot Learning:** Embodied AI and scalable robot learning are constrained by the difficulty and cost of acquiring large, diverse datasets from real-world robot demonstrations. Video generation offers a cost-effective alternative to simulate realistic data for policy learning.
- **Limitations of Existing Trajectory-Controlled Video Generation:** Current trajectory-conditioned video generation methods for robotics primarily model individual object motions. When applied to complex manipulation tasks involving interactions between a robotic arm and an object (e.g., picking up, moving), these methods often exhibit:
 - **Feature Entanglement:** In overlapping regions where the robotic arm and the manipulated object interact, features become entangled. This leads to visual artifacts, degraded fidelity, and a failure to capture physically plausible interactions. For instance, previous models might generate a robotic arm moving through an object without proper physical interaction, or the object's appearance might distort.
 - **Imprecise Interaction Modeling:** The independent modeling of trajectories for multiple objects fails to account for the coupled dynamics during interaction. This results in videos that do not accurately represent the physical responses of objects to manipulation.
- **Challenges in Trajectory Specification:** Users find it difficult to precisely define separate trajectories for both the robotic arm and the manipulated object, especially concerning the temporal boundaries and relative spatial positioning during interaction. Current methods may require complex and full-length trajectory inputs, increasing annotation burden and reducing user interactivity.
- **Semantic Consistency:** Maintaining the appearance and shape consistency of the manipulated object throughout a video sequence, especially during interactions, is a recognized challenge.

To overcome these issues, the research proposes a novel framework, RoboMaster, motivated by the need for:
1. **Unified Interaction Dynamics:** A mechanism to model the joint motions of the robotic arm and the manipulated object during interaction as a single, cohesive process.
2. **Temporal Decomposition:** A strategy to decompose the manipulation process into distinct phases (pre-interaction, interaction, post-interaction) to manage the complexity of multi-object dynamics.
3. **Object Awareness:** Robust object representations that preserve appearance and shape consistency across frames.
4. **Enhanced User-Friendliness:** Simplified user annotation and input processes for trajectories and object masks, making the system more accessible for practical application and iterative refinement.

By addressing these points, the authors aim to produce higher-quality, more reliable video demonstrations that can be effectively used for downstream robot action planning, thus improving the overall efficacy of learned robotic policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, RoboMaster, addresses fine-grained, user-friendly control in image-to-video generation for robotic manipulation through a collaborative trajectory mechanism. The core methodology involves.

### 4. Task Formulation and Preliminary

The system generates a manipulation video given an initial image, a text prompt, binary object masks for the dominant (robotic arm) and submissive (manipulated) subjects, and a collaborative trajectory. The collaborative trajectory `C` is explicitly structured into three temporal phases: pre-interaction (`C1`), interaction (`C2`), and post-interaction (`C3`). The generative model `fθ(·)` operates on these multimodal inputs to synthesize a video `X`. The model builds upon video diffusion transformers (DiTs) and is trained to estimate noise in a diffusion process, conditioned on compressed video data and the collaborative trajectory.

### 5. Subject Representation via Coupled Appearance and Shape Embedding

To ensure identity consistency of subjects throughout the video, RoboMaster employs a specific subject representation:
- **Latent Feature Extraction:** The initial input image is encoded into latent features `z` using a 3D VAE encoder. Object masks (`Md` for dominant, `Ms` for submissive) are downsampled to match `z`'s spatial resolution.
- **Appearance-aware Embedding:** Latent subject features (`~vd`, `~vs`) are extracted by applying these masks to `z` and performing an average pooling operation over the valid pixels. This captures the appearance information.
- **Shape-aware Embedding:** For each timestep in the latent video length, subjects are represented as circular volumes (`vd`, `vs`). These volumes are centered at the trajectory point (`x`, `y`) and confined to the corresponding valid mask region. The radius `r` of the circle is proportional to the mask area. This representation integrates both object appearance and spatial shape, aiming to improve training convergence and identity consistency across frames.

### 6. Collaborative Trajectory Representation

This is the central innovation addressing limitations of decentralized trajectory control. Instead of separate trajectories for each object, RoboMaster learns a unified distribution `pθ(x | I, c, vd, vs, C)` with a temporally decomposed collaborative trajectory:
- **Pre- and Post-Interaction Phases:** During `C1` and `C3`, the dominant subject (robotic arm) is considered the sole moving agent, while the submissive object is static or exhibits minor inertia-induced motion. Therefore, the robotic arm's trajectory and its circular volume `vd` guide the generation (`pθ(x1 | I, c, vd, C1)` and `pθ(x3 | I, c, vd, C3)`).
- **Interaction Phase:** During `C2`, both the dominant and submissive subjects are actively involved. The submissive object's trajectory `C2` and its circular feature `vs` are used to model the conditional distribution (`pθ(x2 | I, c, vs, C2)`). The rationale is that the submissive object's motion implicitly guides the dominant subject due to constrained relative dynamics, and the temporal variation in feature representation (`vd` → `vs` → `vd`) provides cues for behavioral changes.
- **Causal Representation:** Latent feature maps from previous frames are propagated to subsequent ones, and the current object feature (either `vd` or `vs`) is overwritten onto it. This enhances smoother transitions and ensures a causal flow of information, updating the distributions for interaction and post-interaction phases to incorporate prior information (`pθ(x2 | I, c, vd, vs, C1, C2)` and `pθ(x3 | I, c, vd, vs, C1, C2, C3)`).

### 7. Motion Injection Module

The collaborative trajectory latent `V` (which associates `vd`, `vs` with the latent frame length) is processed to guide the video generation:
- It is patchified and encoded sequentially by a zero-initialized 2D spatial convolutional layer and a zero-initialized 1D temporal convolutional layer, producing a compact representation `~V`.
- This `~V` is then combined with the output hidden state `h` from the previous DiT block using an additive merging mechanism: `h = h + norm(~V) + ~V`. This injects the learned motion patterns into the diffusion transformer blocks.
- The overall parameters, including DiT blocks and the motion injector, are optimized using a mean squared error loss function between the predicted noise and the actual noise.

### 8. User Interaction and Data Curation

The design facilitates user interaction:
- **Object Extraction Robustness:** Mask-based representation allows flexible object specification using a simple brush tool, demonstrating robustness even with imprecise input masks.
- **Flexible Trajectory Input:** Users define decomposed sub-trajectories within a single motion path, simplifying input and enhancing adaptability for iterative refinement.
- **Dataset Curation:** A high-quality 21k video–trajectory dataset was curated from the Bridge dataset (Walke et al., 2023). This involved an automatic annotation pipeline using CoTracker3 for per-pixel tracking, Grounded SAM for object detection and mask generation based on prompts, and a motion threshold (`τ`) to identify interaction phase transitions. A human-in-the-loop process filtered and re-annotated samples. Additional data was collected from RLBench and SIMPLER benchmarks.
- **Gradio Demo:** An interactive Gradio demo allows users to provide text prompts, brush-based object masks, interaction time periods, and key points for collaborative trajectories, which are then interpolated.

### 9. Implementation Details

The conditional video diffusion model is implemented based on the pre-trained CogVideoX-5B architecture. Experiments were conducted on the Bridge dataset at 480 × 640 resolution with 37 frames. The model was trained using AdamW on 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs for 30,000 steps, with specific learning rates for DiT blocks and the motion injector, and a CFG scale of 6.0 during inference (50 DDIM steps).

### 10. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Bridge dataset, RLBench, and SIMPLER benchmarks demonstrate that RoboMaster establishes new state-of-the-art performance in trajectory-controlled video generation for robotic manipulation across multiple evaluation criteria.

### 11. Quantitative Comparisons (Bridge Dataset)

RoboMaster consistently outperforms baseline methods (TesserAct, IRASim, MotionCtrl, DragAnything, Tora) in terms of video quality and trajectory accuracy (Table 2):
- **Video Quality:** Achieves the lowest Frechét Video Distance (FVD) of 147.31 (lower is better), highest PSNR of 21.55 (higher is better), and highest SSIM of 0.803 (higher is better).
- **Trajectory Accuracy:** Demonstrates the lowest Trajectory Error for both the robot arm (16.47) and the manipulated object (24.16) (lower is better), indicating more precise adherence to the specified trajectories.
- **User Study:** Secured the highest user preference rate at 45.16%, significantly higher than the next best (Tora at 17.74%), suggesting superior perceived visual and interaction quality.

### 12. Qualitative Comparisons

- **Visual Consistency and Interaction Fidelity:** RoboMaster exhibits improved visual consistency of manipulated subjects and better handles feature entanglement in interaction regions compared to baselines (Figure 5). Examples show that baselines often suffer from appearance distortion, erroneous shape deformation, or missing objects during interaction (e.g., missing apple in Tora), which RoboMaster mitigates.
- **In-the-Wild Generalization:** RoboMaster demonstrates enhanced robustness on in-the-wild image collections, outperforming baselines in generalizing to new scenarios (Figure 6).

### 13. Embodied Action Planning for Robotic Policy

RoboMaster's generated videos serve as effective demonstrations for robotic action planning. A three-stage pipeline was used: (1) video generation, (2) action label extraction via an inverse dynamics model, and (3) robotic planning simulation.
- **Benchmark Performance:** RoboMaster consistently improves embodied action planning over existing baselines on five RLBench tasks and four SIMPLER tasks (Table 4). It outperforms Tora on 8 out of 10 tasks, achieving the highest success rates in tasks such as "pick up cup" (0.83), "put plate" (0.85), "close box" (0.79), "pick coke can" (0.91), "close drawer" (0.63), and "pick object" (0.81).
- **Action Quality Validation:** Actions predicted by the inverse dynamics model from RoboMaster-generated videos produce comparable video quality when fed into an action-conditioned video generation model (Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B/robot/action-cond), validating the high performance of the extracted actions (Table 3).

### 14. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies validate the effectiveness of RoboMaster's key components (Tables 5, 6, 7, 8):
- **Subject Representation:**
 - Removing causal embedding results in degraded visual quality and trajectory accuracy (e.g., object misplacement), supporting the need for conditioning causal visual latents.
 - Replacing mask-based representation with point-based representation (like Tora) significantly increases subject trajectory error, demonstrating the superiority of mask-based object representation.
 - Mask-based approach shows greater robustness to input sparsity, retaining over 97% PSNR performance even with 70% sparsity.
- **Trajectory Injection:**
 - Using separate trajectories instead of collaborative ones leads to feature fusion issues and reduced visual quality and trajectory accuracy (e.g., object distortion), confirming the effectiveness of the decomposed collaborative trajectory design.
 - Alternative injection mechanisms like cross-attention-based injection resulted in degradation, supporting the simplicity and effectiveness of the chosen motion injector.
 - The model exhibits robustness to trajectory perturbations, maintaining over 97% PSNR with 15% deviation in sampled points.
- **Imperfect Prompts:** RoboMaster demonstrates strong robustness to prompt inaccuracies, retaining over 96% of its full-prompt performance even when 40% of the subject prompts are replaced with imperfect descriptions.

### 15. VBench Comparison

On the VBench metrics, RoboMaster shows competitive or superior performance across aesthetic quality, imaging quality, temporal flickering, motion smoothness, subject consistency, and background consistency (Table R12), reinforcing its high video generation quality.

### 16. Long Video Generation and Diverse Scenarios

RoboMaster supports auto-regressive generation of longer, coherent videos using multiple ordered prompts (Figure S12) and generalizes to diverse out-of-domain objects and manipulation skills (Figures S10, S11), indicating its adaptability.

### 17. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboMaster represents a significant advancement in trajectory-controlled video generation for robotic manipulation. Its primary significance lies in its ability to address critical limitations of previous methods, particularly concerning multi-object interaction fidelity and user control.

The introduction of a **collaborative trajectory framework**, which decomposes the manipulation process into pre-interaction, interaction, and post-interaction phases and assigns dominant agents for each, is a core contribution. This design effectively mitigates the issue of feature entanglement in overlapping regions, a common problem in prior work that led to degraded visual quality and physically implausible interactions. By explicitly modeling inter-object dynamics within a unified yet temporally segmented representation, RoboMaster produces more realistic and coherent manipulation videos.

Coupled with a **mask-based object encoding** that incorporates both appearance and shape awareness, the framework enhances subject semantic consistency across video frames. This object representation not only contributes to higher visual fidelity but also facilitates more intuitive user interaction. The system allows for simplified annotation through brush tools and partial trajectory specification, making the generation process more accessible for users and adaptable for iterative refinement.

The demonstrated **state-of-the-art performance** in both visual quality metrics (FVD, PSNR, SSIM, VBench) and trajectory accuracy signifies a notable improvement over existing trajectory-conditioned video generation methods. More importantly, its strong performance in **embodied action planning benchmarks** (RLBench and SIMPLER) indicates a direct and positive impact on robot learning. By generating higher-quality and more reliable interaction videos, RoboMaster enables inverse dynamics models to extract more accurate executable action labels, thereby leading to more effective learned robotic policies and increased task success rates in simulation. This contributes to alleviating the data scarcity bottleneck in scalable robot learning.

The framework's robustness to imprecise user inputs (e.g., sparse masks, trajectory perturbations, imperfect prompts) further enhances its practical applicability in real-world scenarios where perfect annotations are often unavailable. Its capacity for generating long videos and generalizing to diverse out-of-domain objects and manipulation skills suggests a step towards more versatile and general-purpose robotic simulation tools.

**Potential impact includes:**
- **Accelerated Robot Learning:** Providing a reliable source of synthetic, high-fidelity demonstration data to train and evaluate robotic policies, thereby reducing the reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world data collection.
- **Improved Simulation Environments:** Enabling the creation of more realistic and controllable virtual environments for robot training and task development.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** Offering intuitive tools for users to specify and visualize complex manipulation tasks, potentially aiding in robot programming and debugging.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The collaborative trajectory and mask-based object embedding mechanisms offer foundational insights for developing more advanced video generation models capable of complex physical interaction.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to future research directions:
1. **Out-of-Domain Generalization:** While robust in some aspects, RoboMaster may still produce incomplete or distorted objects when applied to highly out-of-domain inputs. Expanding training data with greater diversity in object categories, semantics, and geometries could mitigate this.
2. **2D Pixel Space Operation:** The current framework operates purely in 2D pixel space. Integrating depth cues (e.g., via 3D-aware latent representations or depth estimation) could enable more accurate 3D control and physically accurate interactions.
3. **Generalization to Varied Robotic Embodiments:** The model currently adapts to specific robot morphologies through post-training on limited datasets. Generalizing to novel, unseen robot configurations remains a challenge, requiring broader training data or lightweight adaptation techniques like LoRA.

These future directions suggest a path toward even more capable and generalizable video generation models for the advancement of embodied AI.
