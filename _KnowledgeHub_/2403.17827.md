# DiffH2O: Diffusion-Based Synthesis of Hand-Object Interactions from Textual Descriptions

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffH2O is a diffusion framework that synthesizes dexterous two-hand-object motion from text and object geometry. It splits generation into a grasping and an interaction diffusion stage, links them by subsequence imputing, adds inference-time grasp guidance toward a reference grasp, and uses an object-canonicalized MANO representation with hand-object signed distances. It generalizes to unseen objects and beats IMoS on GRAB physics, diversity, and perceptual studies.

## Problem

- Hand-object interaction datasets are roughly ten times smaller than human motion datasets, so single-stage generative models overfit training objects and fail on unseen shapes.
- IMoS assumes the object is already in hand, covers only training objects, and post-optimizes object pose heuristically, producing interpenetration and implausible contact during fine-grained manipulation.
- Human-motion diffusion models ignore objects or emit coarse large-object motion without fingers; GRAB supplies categorical action labels only, blocking fine-grained textual control.

## Method

- ==Temporal two-stage diffusion==: a grasping model (noise-prediction, ==classifier-free guidance==) for hands approaching a static object, an interaction model (clean-sample prediction) for hand plus object manipulation, boundary set by velocity and contact heuristics.
- ==Grasp guidance== plus ==subsequence imputing==: a classifier-guidance gradient pulls the final grasp frame toward a reference pose from a pose estimator or grasp synthesizer, and the grasp output is masked into interaction denoising.
- Canonicalized pose representation (**MANO** 24-component PCA, 6D rotations, hands relative to the initial-frame normalized object position, 21 joint-object signed distances) plus newly annotated three-stage detailed GRAB text descriptions.

## Results

- Unseen-object split, interaction stage: the UNet variant reaches **0.134 m** sample diversity and **0.837** action accuracy versus IMoS **0.002** and **0.581**, with lower interpenetration volume (**9.03** vs **10.38** cubic centimeters).
- Full-sequence generation beats HOI-adapted MDM and GMD: grasp error **0.12 m** vs **0.38** and **0.30**, handedness accuracy **0.87** vs **0.45** and **0.58**, interpenetration **7.40** vs **8.98** and **7.47** cubic centimeters.
- Ablation: dropping subsequence imputing spikes transition wrist velocity to **5.37 m/s** versus **0.23**; detailed text lifts handedness control to **86.5%** from **59.3%** and action accuracy to **0.887** from **0.516**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting a task at a natural physical boundary lets each sub-model reuse all data regardless of downstream intent, an effective counter to small interaction datasets.
> - Guidance sharpens goal adherence but creates discontinuities at stage seams; imputing a subsequence rather than one frame trades slight accuracy for much smoother transitions.
> - Anchoring poses to a single canonical frame instead of per-frame object-relative coordinates generalizes better to unseen geometry; annotation richness, not model capacity alone, determines controllability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modeling and understanding hand-object interactions (HOI) is a research area crucial for enabling machines to interact with and assist humans, with applications in synthetic data generation, robot training, and virtual reality. Generating realistic 3D HOI sequences presents several challenges: the motions must be geometrically plausible (minimal interpenetration, stable grasp), semantically plausible (respecting object affordances), and temporally plausible (synchronized hand-object dynamics). A significant limiting factor is the scarcity of high-quality HOI datasets, which are substantially smaller than human motion or image datasets.

Previous research has explored human motion generation using diffusion models, but these often either neglect objects entirely or focus on coarse object motions without detailed finger predictions. Methods like IMoS (Ghosh et al. 2023) generate HOI with smaller objects by separating body motion generation from object pose optimization, but typically assume the object is already in-hand, are limited to objects seen during training, and can produce visual artifacts. Other data-driven approaches for HOI often rely on predefined hand-object trajectories, focus on single-hand interactions, omit semantic action information, or exhibit limited generalization to unseen objects. While some recent concurrent works have applied diffusion models to human-object interactions, they tend to focus on full-body motions, often overlooking intricate hand-object details. Another concurrent work, MACS (Shimada et al. 2023), explores mass-conditioned HOI synthesis for a single object, differing from DiffH2O's focus on generalization to unseen shapes and text-conditioned interaction synthesis.

This work addresses the existing gaps by introducing a diffusion-based framework specifically designed for generating natural, fine-grained hand-object interactions that generalize to unseen objects and can be controlled through detailed textual descriptions. It aims to overcome limitations related to data scarcity and generalization by proposing a structured approach to motion synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel diffusion-based framework, named DiffH2O, capable of synthesizing realistic and dexterous 3D hand-object interactions from natural language descriptions.

The specific motivations and objectives include:
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects**: To enable the model to generate plausible hand-object interactions for objects it has not encountered during training, addressing a common limitation in previous HOI synthesis methods.
- **Fine-Grained Textual Control**: To provide users with precise control over the generated hand-object motions through detailed textual descriptions, moving beyond simple categorical action labels. This is motivated by the observation that existing HOI datasets often lack such fine-grained annotations.
- **Mitigating Data Scarcity**: To counteract the limited scale of existing HOI datasets by leveraging a two-stage generation process that decouples grasping from interaction, thereby improving the efficiency of learning from available data and enhancing generalization to diverse textual inputs.
- **Enhancing Realism and Plausibility**: To generate motions that are geometrically, semantically, and temporally realistic, minimizing physical artifacts such as hand-object interpenetration and ensuring natural dynamics and object affordance respect.
- **Improving Controllability**: To offer additional control mechanisms, such as grasp guidance, allowing the model to direct the synthesis towards a specific target grasp pose, which can be derived from images or pre-computed grasp synthesis methods.
- **Supporting Downstream Applications**: To create a framework that can contribute to synthetic data generation for training robots in complex manipulation tasks, or for enhancing the immersiveness and realism of virtual manipulation experiences in applications like virtual reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffH2O is a diffusion-based framework designed for synthesizing dexterous hand-object interactions. Its methodology involves a temporal two-stage diffusion process, a canonicalized hand-object representation, grasp guidance, and a newly compiled dataset of detailed textual descriptions.

#### 3.1. Canonicalized Hand-Object Representation

The model represents a sequence of hand and object poses, x = (O, H_l, H_r), where O denotes object poses and H_l, H_r represent left and right hand poses, respectively.
- **Object Poses (O)**: Consist of a sequence of 3D object locations (τ_o ∈ R^3) and 3D object orientations (φ_o ∈ R^6, using 6D representation).
- **Hand Poses (H_j)**: Based on the parametric MANO hand model. They include global 3D positions (τ ∈ R^3), global 3D orientations (φ ∈ R^6), and local hand poses in MANO PCA space (θ ∈ R^24, using the first 24 components).
- **Hand-Object Coupling**: A key aspect is the inclusion of signed distances (x_sd ∈ R^(21x3)) between each hand joint and its closest point on the object mesh, which helps the model reason about local object shape and reduce physical artifacts.
- **Canonicalization**: Hand positions are represented relative to the normalized object position at the initial frame of the sequence. This approach, contrasting with per-frame object-relative representations used in prior work, is hypothesized to improve generalization to unseen object geometries by being less sensitive to specific training object poses and more correlated with global interaction motions.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Hand-Object Generation

The synthesis process is temporally decoupled into two distinct stages: grasping and interaction. This segmentation is motivated by the observation that hand-object interactions typically involve an initial phase of approaching and grasping a static object, followed by an action-driven manipulation phase. This decoupling aims to improve generalization and mitigate data scarcity.

- **Grasping Stage**:
 - **Focus**: Models the motion of one or two hands approaching a static object from a rest pose until a stable grasp is established. Object motion is not predicted during this phase.
 - **Training Data**: Derived from larger action sequences using heuristics: a frame is marked as the grasp boundary if the object's lateral/vertical velocity exceeds 0.01m/s and at least 7 hand vertices are in contact with the object. Sequences are length-normalized.
 - **Text Conditioning**: Uses generic text descriptions (e.g., "The person grasps the <object>.").
 - **Diffusion Model**: Employs an ε_θ diffusion model (predicts noise) with classifier-free guidance, which is suitable for inference-time guidance like grasp guidance.
- **Interaction Stage**:
 - **Focus**: Models the manipulation and interaction of both hands and the object after the grasp has been established, based on an action defined by a textual prompt.
 - **Training Data**: Uses the same sequence boundary as determined for the grasping stage.
 - **Diffusion Model**: Employs an x_0 diffusion model (directly predicts the denoised output) with classifier-free guidance. This choice is reported to yield less noisy, higher-quality motion for the interaction phase, where inference-time grasp guidance is not primarily applied.
- **Grasp to Interaction Stage Transition (Subsequence Imputing)**:
 - To ensure a smooth transition between the outputs of the grasping and interaction stages, a technique called subsequence imputing is utilized.
 - The start of the interaction sequence is imputed from the output of the grasping model. This adjusts the interaction generation process based on the observed grasping motion.
 - At each denoising step, the imputed denoised output (˜x_i_0,θ) is calculated as a weighted sum of the interaction model's prediction (x_i_0,θ) and a projected, resized version of the grasping model's output (P_i_g x_g_0), using an imputation region mask (M_i_g).

#### 3.3. Grasp Guidance

To enhance controllability and generalization, grasp guidance can be optionally applied during inference for the grasping stage.
- **Mechanism**: A single target grasp frame (ĥ_g_0), representing a desired hand pose at the end of the grasping stage, is leveraged. This prior can be obtained from external sources like image-based pose estimators or grasp synthesis methods.
- **Guidance Signal**: The diffusion model is guided by minimizing a goal function G(x_g_t) representing the distance between the current denoised hand pose and the target grasp. The gradient of this cost term (∇_x_g_t log p(c|x_g_t) ≈ -∇_x_g_t ||h_g_0,θ(x_g_t) - ĥ_g_0||^2) is used to shift the mean in the denoising process (as per classifier guidance). Autodifferentiation allows computing a dense guidance signal for all frames from a sparse target.

#### 3.4. Detailed Text Descriptions

Recognizing the limitations of existing HOI datasets (e.g., GRAB with only categorical action labels), the authors contribute a set of carefully annotated, detailed textual descriptions for the GRAB dataset.
- **Annotation Process**: Annotators described actions in three stages (pre-action, action, post-action), augmented with hand and positional information.
- **Purpose**: These detailed descriptions enable more precise and fine-grained text-based control over the model's output, enhancing robustness to unseen text prompts.

#### 3.5. Conditional Inputs and Architecture

- **Text Prompts**: Encoded using CLIP embeddings.
- **Object Mesh**: Encoded using Basis Point Set (BPS) representations.
- **Time Step**: Encoded using an MLP.
- **Backbone**: The primary architecture for the diffusion model is a UNet with Adaptive Group Normalization (AdaGN), with experiments also conducted using a Transformer backbone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DiffH2O involved quantitative comparisons with baselines, ablation studies, and a perceptual user study.

#### 4.1. Comparison to State-of-the-Art (Interaction Only)

- **Baseline**: IMoS (Ghosh et al. 2023), tested in an interaction-only setting on both unseen subject and unseen object splits of the GRAB dataset.
- **Metrics**: Physics-based (Interpenetration Volume (IV), Interpenetration Depth (ID), Contact Ratio (CR)) and motion diversity (Sample Diversity (SD), Overall Diversity (OD), Action Recognition Accuracy (AR)).
- **Results**: DiffH2O consistently outperformed IMoS across all metrics. For instance, on the unseen subject split, DiffH2O (UNet backbone) achieved lower IV (6.02 cm³ vs. 7.14 cm³), lower ID (7.92 mm vs. 11.47 mm), higher CR (0.064 vs. 0.05), higher SD (0.109 m vs. 0.002 m), higher OD (0.188 m vs. 0.149 m), and higher AR (0.875 vs. 0.588). This was achieved despite IMoS requiring ground-truth initialization for the first few frames, while DiffH2O generates the entire sequence. Qualitatively, DiffH2O demonstrated smoother handling of fine-grained manipulations, such as object switching between hands, compared to IMoS which often produced artifacts.

#### 4.2. Comparison to Diffusion Baselines (Grasping + Interaction)

- **Baselines**: HOI-adapted versions of MDM (Tevet et al. 2023) and GMD (Karunratanakul et al. 2023).
- **Metrics**: Interpenetration Volume (IV), Grasp Error (GE), Handedness Accuracy (HA), and Wrist Joint Velocities at transition (T_vel).
- **Results**: DiffH2O achieved superior performance: lower IV (7.40 cm³ vs. 8.98 cm³ for MDM and 7.47 cm³ for GMD), lower GE (0.12 m vs. 0.38 m for MDM and 0.30 m for GMD), and higher HA (0.87 vs. 0.45 for MDM and 0.58 for GMD). This confirmed the benefits of DiffH2O's temporal two-stage design with grasp guidance and subsequence imputing for generating full sequences.

#### 4.3. Perceptual User Study

- **Methodology**: 21 participants evaluated side-by-side comparisons of DiffH2O and IMoS generated sequences, judging realism and variety.
- **Results**: 63.1% of participants found DiffH2O's motions more realistic, and 72.9% found them to have more variety. Statistically significant Fleiss’ kappa scores (0.34 for realism, 0.43 for diversity) were reported. The study noted that DiffH2O particularly excelled in realism for "offhand" actions (transferring objects between hands) where IMoS exhibited artifacts.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Component Contributions**:
 - A base model (single-stage, no guidance) showed high grasp error (GE) and low handedness accuracy (HA).
 - Adding the 2-stage design (2-ST) without guidance initially led to a decrease in GE and HA.
 - Incorporating grasp guidance (GG) with 2-ST significantly improved GE and HA but resulted in high wrist velocities (T_vel) at the grasp-interaction transition, indicating sudden hand movements.
 - The final model, integrating subsequence imputing (SI) alongside 2-ST and GG, effectively mitigated the high T_vel, resulting in the lowest interpenetration volume (IV) and a smoother transition, at a minor increase in GE and decrease in HA. This validated the contributions of each component to overall performance and physical plausibility.
- **Pose Representation**:
 - Comparing different hand-object representations, the proposed canonicalized representation (hands relative to initial object frame + SDF) achieved the best overall scores in terms of contact ratio (CR), interpenetration volume (IV), and interpenetration depth (ID). This confirmed that representing hand positions relative to the initial frame improved generalization, and SDF features enhanced accuracy by providing local shape information.

#### 4.5. Generation from Estimated and Pre-Synthesized Poses

- Qualitative demonstrations showed that DiffH2O could utilize target grasp poses extracted from image-based pose estimators (e.g., from HO-3D) or generated by grasp synthesis methods (e.g., GraspXL) as reference grasps for guidance. This functionality enables the generation of diverse interaction sequences from a single grasp reference combined with various textual descriptions.

#### 4.6. Controllability via Text Prompts

- **Methodology**: Compared models trained with "simple" (auto-generated) vs. "detailed" (newly annotated) text descriptions for controlling handedness and action.
- **Results**: Training with detailed text descriptions substantially improved control over handedness, achieving an accuracy of 86.5% compared to 59.3% with simple prompts. Action recognition accuracy also increased from 0.516 (tested with simple prompts) to 0.887 (tested with detailed prompts). This demonstrated that the detailed textual descriptions significantly enhanced the model's controllability and robustness to unseen sentences.

#### 4.7. Generalizability to Object Scales

- The model generalized effectively to unseen objects of various shapes and sizes. While performing well on medium and large objects, challenges were observed with very small objects (e.g., small pyramid, 64 cm³), leading to lower contact ratios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces DiffH2O, a framework that advances the field of 3D human-object interaction synthesis. The proposed temporal two-stage diffusion process, coupled with grasp guidance and subsequence imputing, enables the generation of realistic, fine-grained hand-object motions. A key aspect is its capability to generalize to unseen object shapes and to offer fine-grained control through detailed textual descriptions.

The significance of this work lies in several contributions:
- **Advancement in HOI Synthesis**: DiffH2O is presented as the first method to synthesize hand-object interactions on unseen objects directly from textual descriptions, addressing a notable gap in existing research.
- **Improved Generalization and Realism**: The two-stage diffusion process, coupled with the canonicalized hand-object representation and grasp guidance, improves the model's ability to generalize to novel objects and produce physically plausible motions with reduced artifacts.
- **Enhanced Controllability**: The integration of detailed textual descriptions and grasp guidance provides more precise and intuitive control over the generated motions, which is beneficial for complex animation and simulation tasks.
- **New Dataset Resource**: The contribution of detailed textual descriptions for the GRAB dataset provides a valuable resource for future research in text-conditioned HOI generation, facilitating further development in this area.

The potential impact of DiffH2O is multi-faceted:
- **Synthetic Data Generation**: It offers a tool for generating large-scale, high-quality synthetic hand-object interaction data, which can be used to train robotic systems for dexterous manipulation, overcoming limitations posed by the high cost and complexity of real-world data collection.
- **Virtual Reality and Animation**: The ability to generate realistic and controllable hand-object interactions could significantly enhance the immersiveness and fidelity of virtual reality experiences, games, and computer animation.
- **Human-Robot Interaction**: By allowing robots to train on diverse and realistic human-like interactions, this work could contribute to more natural and effective human-robot collaboration.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations. Explicitly integrating physics into the diffusion process could further reduce physical artifacts. The current inference time is relatively slow, suggesting latent space diffusion as a direction for efficiency improvement. Grasp guidance, while beneficial, requires careful alignment of external grasp references and might face conflicts if textual prompts and grasp signals are inconsistent. Additionally, the model showed limitations in generalizing to object sizes significantly outside the training distribution. The authors also discuss potential ethical implications regarding the generation of realistic 3D human motions, emphasizing transparency and open-sourcing to mitigate misuse.
