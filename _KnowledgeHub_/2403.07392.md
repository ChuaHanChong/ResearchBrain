# ViT-CoMer: Vision Transformer with Convolutional Multi-scale Feature Interaction for Dense Predictions

> [!summary] Summary
> ViT-CoMer introduces a Vision Transformer backbone that integrates convolutional multi-scale feature interaction for dense prediction tasks. It leverages existing ViT pre-trained weights while achieving state-of-the-art performance, with ViT-CoMer-L reaching 64.3% AP on COCO val2017 for object detection when using DINOv2 pre-training.

## Problem

- Plain Vision Transformers (ViTs) lack inner-patch information interaction, leading to a loss of fine-grained local details necessary for dense prediction tasks.
- ViTs typically produce single-scale features, limiting their ability to handle objects at diverse scales and contexts in dense prediction.
- Adapting ViTs for dense prediction often requires costly re-pre-training of modified architectures, hindering research and application development.

## Method

- ==ViT-CoMer== employs a ==two-branch architecture== combining a plain ViT with a CNN stream, facilitating ==bidirectional feature interaction== across multiple stages.
- A ==Multi-Receptive Field Feature Pyramid (MRFP)== module enhances the CNN branch by providing rich multi-scale spatial information and expanded receptive fields.
- A ==CNN-Transformer Bidirectional Fusion Interaction (CTI)== module integrates and unifies features from both branches through multi-scale self-attention and bidirectional injection, without altering the core ViT structure or requiring re-pre-training.

## Results

- Achieved **6**4.3%** AP** on COCO val2017 for object detection with ViT-CoMer-L using DINOv2 pre-training, setting a new record without extra detection-specific training data.
- Outperformed plain ViT-S by **+**5.6%** APb** and **+**3.4%** APm** on COCO val2017, and surpassed many existing vision-specific and adapted backbones of comparable size.
- Demonstrated competitive results on semantic segmentation, with ViT-CoMer-L achieving **55.6%** MS mIoU on ADE20K, a **2.1%** improvement over Swin-L.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional interaction between CNN (local, multi-scale) and Transformer (global context) features effectively combines their strengths, enhancing ViTs with local and multi-scale perception.
> - Directly utilizing powerful, open-source ViT pre-trained weights while only randomly initializing new CNN-based components significantly reduces computational costs and accelerates development.
> - The MRFP module, by generating diverse receptive fields within the CNN stream, is crucial for improving plain ViT's performance in dense prediction tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on ViT-CoMer operates at the intersection of two dominant paradigms in computer vision: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs). CNNs, exemplified by architectures like ResNet and ConvNeXt, have historically excelled in dense prediction tasks (e.g., object detection, segmentation) due to their inherent inductive biases such as locality and hierarchical feature extraction. They naturally capture local continuity and multi-scale information crucial for these tasks.

The advent of Vision Transformers (ViTs), inspired by the success of Transformers in Natural Language Processing (NLP), revolutionized image classification. However, a key challenge for plain ViTs in dense prediction tasks has been their limitations, primarily:
1. **Lack of Inner-Patch Information Interaction:** ViTs process images as sequences of non-overlapping patches, leading to a loss of fine-grained local information within these patches.
2. **Limited Diversity of Feature Scale / Non-Hierarchical Features:** Plain ViTs typically operate on a single-scale feature representation, which is suboptimal for dense prediction tasks that require understanding objects at various scales and contexts.
3. **High Pre-training Costs:** Many effective Transformer-based models require extensive pre-training on massive datasets, and architectural modifications often necessitate retraining from scratch.

The broader research landscape has seen three main approaches to adapt ViTs for dense predictions:
- **Plain Backbones:** These optimize the use of ViT features without altering the core ViT framework (e.g., ViTDet). They can leverage powerful, open-source pre-trained weights but struggle with scale diversity.
- **Vision-Specific Backbones:** These redesign the ViT structure to incorporate CNN-like inductive biases, creating hierarchical and multi-scale features (e.g., Swin Transformer, PVT). While effective, they often require re-pre-training from scratch for each structural modification.
- **Adapted Backbones:** These approaches integrate CNN features by adding auxiliary branches to a plain ViT, aiming to leverage existing ViT pre-trained weights while enhancing feature representation (e.g., ViT-Adapter).

ViT-CoMer positions itself as an **adapted backbone** approach, building on the strengths of this paradigm while addressing its shortcomings. It aims to bridge the gap between ViTs' global context modeling and CNNs' local feature extraction and multi-scale representation, allowing for direct use of existing powerful ViT pre-trained weights. This is a crucial distinction, as it bypasses the massive computational cost of pre-training vision-specific transformers from scratch, accelerating research and application development. The paper's contribution lies in proposing specific, efficient modules for this CNN-Transformer bidirectional fusion, pushing the state-of-the-art within the "adapted backbone" category and demonstrating its competitive performance against "vision-specific backbones."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone, named ViT-CoMer, that is specifically designed for dense prediction tasks. The motivation stems from the inherent limitations of plain ViTs when applied to these tasks, despite their widespread success in image classification.

The key motivations and objectives are:
1. **Address Lack of Inner-Patch Interaction:** Plain ViTs segment images into patches and process them, often losing fine-grained local information within each patch. The authors aim to introduce mechanisms that facilitate better local information interaction.
2. **Overcome Limited Feature Scale Diversity:** Dense prediction tasks require models to recognize objects and segments at various scales. Plain ViTs typically produce single-scale features, which is insufficient. The objective is to inject multi-scale capabilities into the ViT architecture.
3. **Leverage Existing Advanced ViT Pre-training:** Many powerful ViT models have been pre-trained on massive datasets (e.g., ImageNet-22K, DINOv2, BEiT). The authors want to design an architecture that can directly utilize these open-source, pre-trained ViT weights without requiring additional pre-training for the entire model, thereby saving significant computational resources and accelerating deployment.
4. **Facilitate Bidirectional CNN-Transformer Interaction:** To achieve the above objectives, the authors aim to design a mechanism that enables effective and bidirectional interaction between convolutional features (known for local and multi-scale properties) and Transformer features (known for global context).
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ultimate goal is to create a plain, pre-training-free, and feature-enhanced ViT backbone that achieves competitive, if not superior, performance on various dense prediction benchmarks (object detection, instance segmentation, semantic segmentation) compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, including vision-specific and adapted backbones.
6. **Ensure Simplicity and Efficiency:** The proposed modules should be simple and efficient, making ViT-CoMer an attractive and easily integrable backbone for future research and applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViT-CoMer adopts a two-branch architecture that combines a plain Vision Transformer with a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) stream, facilitating bidirectional feature interaction. The architecture consists of three core components:

**a) Overall Architecture:**
The input image is processed simultaneously by two branches:
1. **Plain ViT Branch:** The image is fed into the ViT's patch embedding layer, yielding features at a resolution of 1/16th of the original image size. This branch retains the original ViT structure, allowing it to directly load powerful pre-trained weights.
2. **CNN Branch:** The image passes through a stack of convolutions to generate a feature pyramid {C3, C4, C5} at resolutions of 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 respectively, each with D-dimensional feature maps.

These two branches interact across N stages. At each stage, the CNN features are first enhanced by the **Multi-Receptive Field Feature Pyramid (MRFP) module**, and then bidirectionally interact with the ViT features via the **CNN-Transformer Bidirectional Fusion Interaction (CTI) module**. This process aims to enrich both feature streams with multi-scale, semantically diverse information. Finally, after N stages, features from both branches are added at each scale for the downstream dense prediction tasks.

**b) Multi-Receptive Field Feature Pyramid (MRFP) Module:**
This module is designed to provide the CNN branch with rich multi-scale spatial information and expanded receptive fields. It comprises:
- **Feature Pyramid:** It takes multi-scale CNN features {C3, C4, C5} as input.
- **Multi-Receptive Field Convolutional Layers:** These layers enhance the long-range modeling ability of CNN features.
Specifically, the input multi-scale features are flattened and concatenated. This combined feature then undergoes a linear projection for dimension reduction. Subsequently, the features are divided into multiple groups along the channel dimension. Each group is processed by depth-wise separable convolutions with different kernel sizes (e.g., 3x3, 5x5, etc.), effectively capturing varied receptive fields. The processed features are then concatenated and dimensionally expanded back to their original dimensions via another linear projection. This process, denoted as F = FC(DWConv(FC(C))), ensures that the CNN features themselves are highly expressive and multi-scale before interacting with the ViT branch.

**c) CNN-Transformer Bidirectional Fusion Interaction (CTI) Module:**
The CTI module is crucial for integrating and unifying features from the two distinct architectural modalities (CNN and Transformer) without altering the core ViT structure. This module addresses modality bias (e.g., high/low frequency semantics, global/local information) and enhances both branches.
- **Feature Fusion:** The ViT feature (X) (at 1/16 resolution) is directly added to the 1/16 resolution CNN feature (F4) obtained from MRFP. This creates an aggregated multi-scale feature set {F3, F'4, F5}, where F'4 = F4 + X.
- **Modality Unification via Self-Attention:** To resolve representational biases between CNN and Transformer features, a multi-scale self-attention mechanism (specifically, deformable attention) is applied to the aggregated feature set. This allows for interaction among multi-scale features, capturing richer information than traditional single-scale self-attention. The process is O = FFN(Attention(norm(F'))).
- **Bidirectional Injection:** The fused features (O) are then injected back into both the ViT and CNN branches at each stage:
 - **To ViT Branch:** At the beginning of stage *i*, the fused feature (O2i-1) is injected into the ViT branch (X_i) using a learnable scalar weight (α): ˆX_i = α * O2i-1 + X_i. The α is initialized to zero to minimize the impact of the randomly initialized CNN part on the pre-trained ViT during early training.
 - **To CNN Branch:** At the end of stage *i*, a similar injection occurs for the CNN branch: ˆF_i = O2i + F'_i.
This bidirectional interaction ensures that both architectural strengths are mutually reinforced, enabling ViT to gain local and multi-scale perception, and CNN to benefit from global context. The number of interaction stages (N) is empirically determined (set to 4 for optimal performance).

**Weight Initialization:** The ViT module directly uses existing open-source pre-trained weights, while the CNN-specific modules (MRFP, CTI) are randomly initialized. This "pre-training-free" aspect for the added CNN components is a significant advantage. The learnable alpha parameter helps in smoothly introducing these randomly initialized components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ViT-CoMer demonstrates strong performance across various dense prediction tasks, consistently outperforming existing plain ViT and many adapted/vision-specific backbones, especially when leveraging advanced pre-training.

**a) Object Detection and Instance Segmentation (COCO val2017 with Mask R-CNN):**
- **Superior Performance:** ViT-CoMer consistently outperforms plain ViT (e.g., ViT-S by +5.6% APb and +3.4% APm under 1x schedule) and often surpasses state-of-the-art vision-specific (e.g., InternImage, Swin) and adapted backbones (e.g., ViT-Adapter) of comparable size.
- **Efficiency:** ViT-CoMer-S (50M params) achieves better detection results than ViT-L (338M params), showcasing significant parameter efficiency.
- **Robustness Across Frameworks:** The model maintains its performance advantage across different detection frameworks like Cascade Mask R-CNN, ATSS, and GFL, highlighting its versatility as a backbone.
- **Leveraging Pre-trained Weights:**
 - ViT-CoMer effectively utilizes various pre-trained weights (ImageNet-1K, ImageNet-22K, Multi-Modal, Self-Supervised Learning like DINOv2).
 - With DINOv2 pre-training, ViT-CoMer-L achieves **64.3% AP on COCO val2017**, which is reported as the best record *without* extra detection-specific training data (e.g., Objects365) and outperforms SOTA methods like HTC++ with BEiTv2+O365.
 - Compared to ImageNet-1K, multi-modal pre-training (BEiTv2) yields +1.7% APb and APm gains, while DINOv2 leads to even more substantial gains (+3.0% APb, +2.7% APm over ImageNet-22K for ViT-CoMer-L).

**b) Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K val with UperNet):**
- **Competitive Results:** ViT-CoMer-S achieves 47.7% MS mIoU, outperforming Swin-T (+1.9%) and ViT-Adapter-S (+0.6%). ViT-CoMer-L reports 55.6% MS mIoU, which is 2.1% higher than Swin-L and 1.2% higher than ViT-Adapter-L.
- **Advanced Pre-training Impact:** Using multi-modal pre-training, ViT-CoMer-L achieves 56.8% MS mIoU, outperforming ViT-Adapter-L by 1.4%, further solidifying its ability to leverage external knowledge.
- **State-of-the-Art Comparison:** When integrated into the Mask2Former framework, ViT-CoMer-L (604M params) achieves 62.1% MS mIoU, comparable to or surpassing other SOTA methods with fewer or similar parameters, demonstrating its effectiveness even in highly competitive setups.

**c) Ablation Studies:**
- **MRFP Effectiveness:** The MRFP module alone (providing multi-scale CNN features) significantly improves plain ViT, yielding +1.3% APb and +1.1% APm.
- **CTI Contribution:** The CTI module, particularly with bidirectional injection (to both ViT and CNN branches), provides substantial gains. CTI (to V) adds +1.8% APb and +1.1% APm, and further adding CTI (to C) brings another +2.5% APb and +1.2% APm.
- **Cumulative Impact:** The combination of MRFP and CTI results in a total improvement of +5.6% APb and +3.4% APm over plain ViT-S.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis:**
 - The optimal number of CTI modules (N) for bidirectional fusion interaction is found to be 4.
 - Optimal kernel sizes for DWConv in MRFP are 3x3 and 5x5.

**d) Scalability:**
- The approach is shown to be scalable to hierarchical vision transformers like Swin, improving Swin-T's performance by +2.1% box AP and +1.2% mask AP, although the gains are less dramatic than for plain ViTs due to Swin's already-present inductive biases.

**e) Qualitative Results:**
- Visualization of feature maps shows that ViT-CoMer produces more fine-grained multi-scale features compared to plain ViT, leading to enhanced object localization capabilities for dense prediction tasks. This validates the design philosophy of combining local (high-frequency) and global (low-frequency) information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ViT-CoMer research offers several significant contributions and has considerable potential impact on the field of computer vision:

1. **Bridging the CNN-Transformer Divide Effectively:** ViT-CoMer successfully integrates the complementary strengths of CNNs (local feature extraction, multi-scale hierarchy) and Transformers (global context modeling, long-range dependencies). This fusion addresses the inherent weaknesses of plain ViTs for dense prediction tasks without sacrificing their global reasoning capabilities.

2. **Leveraging Pre-trained Models Efficiently:** A major advantage is its ability to directly utilize powerful, open-source ViT pre-trained weights (e.g., from ImageNet-22K, DINOv2, BEiT) without requiring re-pre-training of the entire backbone. This "pre-training-free" approach for the newly introduced CNN-based components dramatically reduces computational costs and accelerates research and development, making state-of-the-art models more accessible.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Dense Predictions:** ViT-CoMer achieves highly competitive, and often superior, results on major dense prediction benchmarks (COCO for object detection/instance segmentation, ADE20K for semantic segmentation). Its performance, especially when combined with advanced self-supervised pre-training like DINOv2, sets new benchmarks for object detection without relying on extra detection-specific datasets.

4. **Simplicity and Generalizability:** The proposed MRFP and CTI modules are simple and efficient, allowing for easy integration into existing ViT architectures. The demonstrated scalability to hierarchical Transformers like Swin also suggests broad applicability as a plug-and-play enhancement module.

5. **Enhanced Feature Representation:** The qualitative results, showing more fine-grained multi-scale features, highlight that ViT-CoMer genuinely enhances the model's understanding of visual information, which is critical for precise localization and segmentation in dense prediction tasks.

6. **Facilitating Future Research:** By providing a robust, high-performing, and resource-efficient backbone, ViT-CoMer can serve as a new foundation for future research in dense prediction. Researchers can build upon this enhanced architecture to explore more complex models or tasks, further pushing the boundaries of what ViTs can achieve in applied computer vision. The release of the code underscores this potential for broader impact and community adoption.

In conclusion, ViT-CoMer represents a significant step forward in making Vision Transformers more practical and powerful for a wide range of dense prediction applications, particularly by intelligently combining their strengths with those of convolutional networks while minimizing the need for extensive new pre-training.
