# MuseVLA: An Adaptive Multimodal Sensing Vision-Language-Action Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MuseVLA is an adaptive multimodal sensing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that dynamically selects relevant sensor modalities and processes them into unified "grounded sensor images" for robotic manipulation. The model achieves an average success rate of 76.4% across thermal, acoustic, and mmWave-guided tasks, and improves zero-shot generalization by 39% on unseen tasks by leveraging a synthetic data pipeline.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models primarily rely on RGB images, limiting a robot's perception of physical properties not discernible from visible light alone (e.g., temperature, sound).
- Current multisensory VLA models often employ modality-specific architectures, require extensive modality-specific datasets, and use static sensor configurations, leading to inefficiencies and scalability issues.
- Integrating diverse sensors for generalized action generation in complex dexterous manipulation tasks remains challenging, requiring fine-grained spatial understanding and precise control.

## Method

- **MuseVLA** introduces an ==adaptive sensor selection== mechanism where a VLA model generates a task-relevant sensor token and target description, treating sensors as on-demand tools.
- It processes diverse sensor observations into "grounded sensor images," a unified intermediate representation that spatially overlays color-coded sensor heatmaps onto task-relevant regions of RGB images.
- A data synthesis pipeline augments existing RGB video datasets with simulated multisensory information to address data scarcity and facilitate robust training for generalization.

## Results

- MuseVLA achieved an average success rate of **76.4%** across thermal, acoustic, and mmWave-guided manipulation tasks, outperforming RGB-only baselines (**22.2%**) and baselines using raw concatenated sensor inputs (**33.3%**).
- The adaptive sensor selection mechanism reduced inference GPU memory usage from **13.23** GB (for all sensors) to **6.61** GB (for selected sensors), demonstrating improved efficiency.
- Pretraining with the synthesized data significantly boosted zero-shot generalization, improving average success on unseen tasks by **39%** (from **27.1%** to **66.7%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating sensors as on-demand tools with adaptive selection significantly enhances efficiency by processing only task-relevant modalities, reducing computational overhead and memory footprint.
> - Unifying heterogeneous sensor data into "grounded sensor images" allows a single, pre-trained vision encoder to process diverse sensory inputs, simplifying the model architecture and enabling scalable multimodal fusion.
> - Synthesizing multisensory training data from readily available RGB datasets effectively mitigates data scarcity, leading to improved zero-shot generalization capabilities for unseen tasks and objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation research has increasingly focused on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large-scale vision-language pretraining to achieve generalization and instruction following. Most prominent VLA models, such as those discussed by Black et al. (2024, 2025), Kim et al. (2024b), and Mees et al. (2024), primarily rely on RGB images as their sole visual input. This reliance limits a robot's ability to perceive physical properties—such as temperature, sound, or material composition via radar—that are not readily discernible from visible light alone.

Recent efforts have begun to extend VLA models with additional sensing modalities. Examples include the integration of depth information for spatial understanding (Li et al., 2025a; Bhat et al., 2025; Qu et al., 2025), tactile feedback for force-aware control (Bi et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025), and audio for human-robot interaction or sound-based object localization (Zhao et al., 2025; Jones et al., 2025). While these approaches have shown performance gains, the paper identifies several limitations in their applicability and scalability. These include:
1. **Modality-specific architectures**: Many existing multisensory VLA models employ specialized model architectures or encoders for each sensor type, which hinders efficient integration of a diverse and evolving set of sensors.
2. **Data dependency**: They typically require large-scale, modality-specific sensory datasets for training, which are expensive and time-consuming to acquire in real-world robotic setups.
3. **Static sensor usage**: Most models assume a fixed or predetermined sensor configuration, lacking the ability for task-conditioned, adaptive selection of sensory inputs. This can lead to inefficiencies, as not all sensors are relevant for every task, and can introduce unnecessary computational overhead.

This work builds upon the foundation of VLA models and multisensor fusion techniques, which have proven critical in other domains like autonomous driving (Liu et al., 2022; Wolters et al., 2024) and 3D scene understanding (Wang et al., 2024). However, existing fusion methods are predominantly designed for perception tasks rather than for generalized action generation and manipulation. MuseVLA aims to address the aforementioned limitations by introducing an adaptive and scalable multisensor fusion approach within a unified VLA framework, specifically targeting challenging dexterous hand manipulation tasks that demand fine-grained spatial understanding and precise control, an area less explored with diverse multimodal sensing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptive multimodal sensing VLA model, named MuseVLA, that can integrate diverse sensing modalities as on-demand tools for robotic manipulation. The model aims to overcome the limitations of current RGB-only or statically-multisensory VLA approaches.

The motivation for MuseVLA is rooted in observing human perception and interaction with the physical world. Humans naturally employ a rich set of senses (e.g., vision, sound, touch) and, crucially, do not fuse all available senses continuously. Instead, they treat sensory modalities as task-oriented tools, flexibly incorporating diverse inputs and adaptively attending to those most relevant to the task at hand. Furthermore, humans can continuously expand their sensing capabilities by adopting external sensor tools and learning when and how to use them on demand. This ability for adaptive, task-conditioned sensor selection is considered fundamental for efficient action in complex physical environments.

Specifically, the paper seeks to achieve the following detailed objectives:
- **Enable adaptive sensor selection**: To allow the robot to dynamically choose the most relevant sensing modality based on the current task instruction and visual context, mirroring human-like adaptive perception. This contrasts with fixed sensor configurations that process all available sensor data regardless of its utility for a specific task.
- **Develop a unified intermediate representation**: To create a sensor-agnostic representation that can encode heterogeneous sensor measurements into a common format. This is intended to decouple sensor-specific processing from the VLA backbone, enabling efficient integration of diverse and new modalities without requiring specialized encoders or extensive model retraining.
- **Address data scarcity in multisensory robotics**: To mitigate the high cost and time required to collect large-scale multisensory robotic datasets. This involves developing a data synthesis pipeline that can augment existing RGB video datasets with simulated multisensory information.
- **Demonstrate performance on complex manipulation tasks**: To evaluate the proposed MuseVLA model on real-world robotic manipulation tasks that inherently require multimodal sensing inputs, particularly focusing on dexterous hand manipulation, which demands precise control and rich sensory information.
- **Achieve zero-shot generalization**: To enable the model to generalize to unseen sensor-guided tasks and objects, leveraging the data synthesis pipeline to improve robustness and adaptability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MuseVLA introduces an adaptive multimodal sensing paradigm by treating sensors as on-demand tools. The core methodology involves a three-stage process that decouples sensor-specific processing from the VLA backbone.

**Adaptive Sensor Selection Mechanism:**
The task is decomposed as follows:
1. **Sensor Token and Target Description Generation:** Given a language instruction ($l$) and an RGB observation ($o_t$), the VLA model first generates a task-relevant sensor token ($l_s$) (e.g., `<Thermal>`, `<Acoustic>`, `<mmWave>`, or `<None>` for RGB-only) and a sensing target description ($l_d$) (e.g., "bottles" for "Pick up the hot drink"). This is analogous to a tool call with structured arguments.
2. **Grounded Sensor Image Construction:** The selected sensor's observation ($s_{i,t}$) is then processed into a unified intermediate representation called a "grounded sensor image" ($m_{i,t}$). This step involves a sensor grounding function ($G$) that uses $o_t$, $s_{i,t}$, and $l_d$.
3. **Action Generation:** The grounded sensor image ($m_{i,t}$) is appended as input for subsequent action generation. The same VLA model ($\pi$) is reused for both sensor selection and action generation, maintaining an end-to-end architecture.

**Sensor Data Representation: Grounded Sensor Images:**
MuseVLA introduces "grounded sensor images" as the intermediate representation ($m$) to unify diverse sensor data. This representation encodes heterogeneous sensor readings into the RGB image space by spatially overlaying color-coded sensor heatmaps onto task-relevant regions. This approach eliminates the need for specialized encoders for each modality, allowing a pre-trained vision encoder to process all sensing inputs uniformly.

The construction of a grounded sensor image involves:
- **Segmentation:** A pre-trained segmentation module (SAM3) takes the RGB observation ($o_{RGB}$) and the target description ($l_d$) to produce a binary mask ($M$) highlighting the object(s) of interest.
- **Overlay:** The sensor heatmap (e.g., temperature map, sound intensity map, radar reflection map) is then overlaid onto the masked region of the RGB image. The resulting grounded sensor image ($m$) is mathematically represented as $m = M \odot s + (1 − M) \odot o_{RGB}$, where $s$ is the spatially aligned 2D sensor observation.

**MuseVLA Model Architecture:**
- **Backbone:** MuseVLA utilizes PaliGemma-2 as its pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone, leveraging its transferable vision-language understanding and generation capabilities. This VLM consists of a Gemma-2 language model and a SigLIP vision encoder, with a linear projection for alignment.
- **Action Expert:** A diffusion transformer (DiT) model is employed as the action expert.
- **Initialization:** The model is initialized with pretrained weights from VITRA (Li et al., 2025b), which has been trained on large-scale human-hand interaction data.

**Sensor Tokens and Target Description:**
Learnable sensor tokens (`<None>`, `<Thermal>`, `<Acoustic>`, `<mmWave>`) are introduced to represent different sensing modalities, enabling adaptive sensor selection. These tokens, along with the target description ($l_d$), are generated by the VLM based on the task instruction and RGB image, formulated as a language grounding task. The segmentation model, SAM3, operates independently to provide masks, allowing asynchronous updates.

**Sensor-Guided Action Generation:**
After constructing the grounded sensor image, it is encoded using the VLM's vision encoder (due to the unified RGB-space representation). A learnable cognition token is also appended. The model then generates a conditioning feature, derived from the task instruction, RGB observation, generated sensor token, target description, and grounded sensor image, for the diffusion action expert. The action expert uses this feature, robot states, and noisy actions to predict the denoised action sequence for robotic manipulation.

**Data Synthesis Pipeline:**
To address the scarcity of multisensory robot datasets, a data synthesis pipeline is developed. This pipeline generates multisensory training data from existing RGB-only robotic datasets:
1. **Sensor Dictionary:** A dictionary maps each modality to various physical property keywords (e.g., thermal to "hot," "cold").
2. **Keyword Injection:** For an RGB video episode, a modality and keyword are randomly sampled, and the keyword is injected into the task instruction (e.g., "Put the mug in the sink" becomes "Put the hot mug in the sink").
3. **Target Description Generation:** A VLM (GPT-5.2) generates a target description for the modified instruction.
4. **Masking and Overlay:** SAM3 segments the described object, and a color corresponding to the keyword (e.g., yellow for "hot") is overlaid on the masked region, simulating sensor observations. Color mapping is randomized for diversity.
5. **Data Augmentation:** An augmentation strategy is applied where the masked region is cloned to a shifted location with an incorrect sensor color, encouraging the model to rely on actual sensor information rather than spatial priors.
This pipeline was applied to MolmoAct, AgiBotWorld-Alpha, and VITRA datasets, synthesizing 9.6K episodes (1.05M frames) covering over 1000 objects.

**Training with Multisensory Data:**
MuseVLA training comprises two parts: VLM training for adaptive sensor selection and target description generation, and VLA training for sensor-guided action generation. The training leverages a mix of real-world collected multisensory data and the synthesized dataset.
- **VLM Training Loss:** A combined cross-entropy loss ($L_{VLM} = L_{sensor} + L_{target}$) is used for sensor token and target description generation.
- **VLM and VLA Co-training:** The overall training loss combines $L_{VLM}$ with the diffusion Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss for the action expert. This joint optimization ensures both accurate VLM outputs and effective action generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MuseVLA involved a real-world robot setup and a suite of challenging manipulation tasks.

**Experiment Setup:**
- **Robot Hardware:** A table-top environment equipped with a robot arm, a 12-DoF Robotera XHand dexterous hand, and a multi-sensor module.
- **Sensor Suite:** Included an RGB-D camera (Intel RealSense), two thermal cameras (infiRay T2S), an mmWave radar (Calterah 4T4R 60GHz radar), and a microphone array (Sipeed 6+1Mic Array).
- **Sensor Heatmap Construction and Alignment:** Thermal cameras directly output 2D temperature maps. mmWave radar and microphone array convert raw signals into spatially resolved 2D heatmaps using digital beamforming. All heatmaps are mapped to RGB space with sensor-specific colormaps. A one-time offline 2D-to-2D homography estimation aligns sensor heatmaps with the RGB camera's field of view.
- **Task Categories:** Three main categories: thermal-guided pick-and-place (e.g., "Pick up the hot drink"), acoustic-grounded object search (e.g., "Pick up the clothes covering the ringing phone"), and mmWave radar-guided object search (e.g., "Open the occupied box"). The dataset comprised 720 teleoperated demonstration episodes.
- **Baselines:** RGB-only VLA models (VLA-RGB, π₀-RGB, π₀.₅-RGB) and VLA models receiving all raw sensor heatmaps concatenated as input (VLA-Raw, π₀-Raw, π₀.₅-Raw).

**Multisensory Manipulation Task Performance:**
- **Overall Success Rate:** MuseVLA (trained on real-world data) achieved an average success rate of 76.4% across all tasks (Table 1), significantly outperforming RGB-only VLA baselines (e.g., MuseVLA-RGB at 22.2%, π₀-RGB at 20.8%).
- **Comparison to Raw Sensor Inputs:** MuseVLA also substantially outperformed baselines that received raw sensor heatmaps without the grounded sensor image processing or adaptive selection (e.g., MuseVLA-Raw at 33.3%, π₀-Raw at 27.8%). This indicated that raw sensor heatmaps, without proper processing and adaptive selection, were noisy and not effectively utilized for manipulation, even if the sensing stage was successful.
- **Ablation Studies:** Removing the grounded sensor image representation (MuseVLA-RawAdapt) led to a drop in average success rate to 59.7%, although it still significantly outperformed MuseVLA-Raw (33.3%). This confirmed the importance of both the grounded sensor image representation for unified sensor fusion and adaptive sensor selection for efficient multisensory perception. The adaptive selection mechanism (MuseVLA-RawAdapt vs. MuseVLA-Raw) improved performance by reducing noise from irrelevant sensors.
- **Multi-Stage Tasks:** MuseVLA demonstrated the ability to chain heterogeneous sensing and manipulation stages within long-horizon tasks. For a Radar → RGB task, it achieved 66.7% end-to-end success. For a Radar → Thermal task, it achieved 75.0% end-to-end success. These results indicated the model's capability to adaptively dispatch appropriate sensors at different stages of a complex task.

**Adaptive Sensor Selection:**
- **VLM Generation Accuracy:** MuseVLA (pretrained) achieved 100% sensor selection accuracy and 93.5% target description accuracy on training tasks, and 100% sensor selection accuracy and 82.0% target description accuracy on unseen tasks (Table 2). This performance significantly surpassed the base PaliGemma-2 VLM, which showed poor accuracy (e.g., 0% sensor and 9.5% target description accuracy on unseen tasks). Pretraining on synthesized data was observed to boost target description accuracy on unseen tasks from 40.5% to 82.0%.
- **Inference Efficiency:** By adaptively selecting only the task-relevant sensor, MuseVLA reduced inference GPU memory usage from 13.23 GB (for MuseVLA-Raw, processing all sensors) to 6.61 GB. This indicates an efficiency gain that also allows for larger batch sizes during training and, importantly, ensures that memory cost does not scale with the increasing number of available sensors.

**Effectiveness of Pretraining:**
- **Zero-shot Generalization:** Evaluating on unseen tasks (recombining sensing modalities with new manipulation targets or objects), MuseVLA with pretraining on synthesized data showed comparable performance on seen tasks while significantly improving unseen task success by 39% on average (from 27.1% to 66.7%) (Table 3). Gains were observed across all three modalities.
- **Benefits of Synthetic Data:** This performance improvement on zero-shot generalization was attributed to more accurate sensor selection, better target descriptions, and enhanced manipulation actions learned from the diverse set of tasks in the synthesized dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MuseVLA research presents a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical challenges in integrating and utilizing diverse sensing modalities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Scalable and Efficient Sensor Integration:** MuseVLA introduces a paradigm where sensors are treated as on-demand tools. This adaptive selection mechanism allows for conditional and scalable sensor perception within a single model. This design reduces the need for modality-specific encoders and extensive retraining when new sensors are introduced, enhancing the practicality and deployability of robotic systems across various platforms and tasks. The demonstrated reduction in GPU memory usage and independence of memory footprint from the number of sensors highlights its efficiency.
- **Unified Multimodal Representation:** The concept of "grounded sensor images" provides a unified intermediate representation that converts heterogeneous sensor measurements into the RGB image space. This allows a single, pre-trained vision encoder to process diverse sensory inputs, simplifying model architecture and enabling effective fusion of visual and non-visual physical information. This approach is instrumental in making multimodal VLA models more robust and versatile.
- **Mitigation of Data Scarcity:** The proposed data synthesis pipeline offers a solution to the significant challenge of collecting large-scale, expensive multisensory robot datasets. By augmenting existing RGB video datasets with synthesized grounded sensor images, the pipeline enables data-efficient training and facilitates generalization to novel sensor-guided tasks. This capacity to leverage readily available RGB data is crucial for advancing multimodal robotics without prohibitive data collection costs.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** MuseVLA demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks and objects, attributing this capability to the adaptive sensor selection mechanism and the diversity introduced by the synthesized training data. This indicates the potential for more capable general-purpose robotic systems that can operate in complex, unstructured, and novel environments beyond their specific training scenarios.
- **Advancement in Dexterous Manipulation:** By successfully evaluating MuseVLA on challenging dexterous hand manipulation tasks, the research contributes to an area where fine-grained control and understanding of physical properties are essential. The ability to leverage thermal, acoustic, and radar inputs provides robots with a richer perception of their environment, leading to more precise and effective manipulation.
- **Contribution to the Research Community:** The stated intention to open-source the data and code can facilitate further research and development in adaptive multimodal sensing for robotic manipulation, accelerating progress in the field.

While the current work acknowledges limitations such as the modest scale of the real-world dataset and dependence on an external segmentation module, the foundational contributions of adaptive sensor selection, unified representation, and data synthesis establish a scalable pathway towards building more human-like, general-purpose multisensory manipulation capabilities in robotics.
