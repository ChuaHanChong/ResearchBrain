# Habitat 2.0: Training Home Assistants to Rearrange their Habitat

> [!summary] Summary
> Habitat 2.0 provides a high-performance 3D simulation platform, coupled with a dataset of interactive indoor environments and benchmark tasks, to accelerate research in mobile manipulation for embodied AI agents. The platform achieves over 25,000 simulation steps per second, enabling large-scale reinforcement learning for complex object rearrangement problems.

## Problem

- Training and evaluating embodied AI agents for complex mobile manipulation (e.g., object rearrangement) is slow, expensive, and difficult to reproduce in real-world robotics.
- Lack of large-scale, interactive 3D datasets with articulated objects and diverse layouts to enable generalization studies for home assistant robots.
- Existing simulation environments are too slow to support the massive data requirements and iteration cycles for modern deep reinforcement learning approaches on long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- Introduces **Habitat 2.0**, a high-performance 3D simulator designed for speed through ==lazy simulation== principles like ==localized physics==, ==interleaved CPU/GPU execution==, and simplified asset representation.
- Curates **ReplicaCAD**, an artist-authored, annotated, and reconfigurable dataset of house-scale interactive 3D environments with ==articulated objects== (e.g., fridges, drawers) for diverse training scenarios.
- Establishes the **Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB)**, a suite of challenging mobile manipulation tasks (TidyHouse, PrepareGroceries, SetTable) that require navigating and interacting with objects and containers.

## Results

- Habitat 2.0 achieves an unprecedented simulation speed of over **25,000 steps per second** (**850x real-time**), representing approximately a **100x speedup** over typical prior simulators.
- Monolithic reinforcement learning policies performed poorly on the Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB) tasks, while hierarchical TaskPlanning + SkillsRL approaches demonstrated significantly higher success rates for complex rearrangement.
- Generalization studies on the Pick task showed that while monolithic RL generalizes well to unseen layouts, generalization to new objects and receptacles remains challenging, particularly for visual features and confined spaces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Monolithic end-to-end reinforcement learning policies perform poorly on long-horizon, multi-skill mobile manipulation tasks, suggesting the need for architectural inductive biases.
> - Hierarchical approaches, combining symbolic task planning with learned low-level skills, are more effective for complex rearrangement tasks, though they still face 'hand-off problems' between skills.
> - Classical Sense-Plan-Act (SPA) methods, while effective for some skills, prove brittle and struggle with imperfect egocentric perception in complex, cluttered environments or with unknown articulated containers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Habitat 2.0 (H2.0) is a pivotal contribution to the rapidly evolving field of **Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI)**, specifically addressing the challenge of **object rearrangement** for home assistant robots. Embodied AI focuses on creating intelligent agents that can perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world, moving beyond static data analysis to dynamic, interactive scenarios.

Historically, robotics research has faced significant hurdles in training and testing complex intelligent systems in the real world due to high costs, time consumption, and safety concerns. This has led to a growing reliance on **high-fidelity simulation environments** as critical testbeds. H2.0 directly addresses this need by providing an exceptionally fast and interactive 3D simulator.

The paper extensively situates H2.0 within the landscape of existing simulators, such as AI2-THOR, ThreeDWorld, SAPIEN, RLBench, and iGibson. While these platforms offer various capabilities like rigid-body dynamics, articulated interactions, and realistic rendering, H2.0 distinguishes itself primarily by its **unparalleled simulation speed** (exceeding 25,000 steps per second, 850x real-time), which is a 100x speed-up over prior work. This performance is achieved through a "lazy simulator" design philosophy, prioritizing speed over the breadth of physical capabilities (e.g., no deformable bodies, fluids, or advanced material transformations). This focus allows H2.0 to unlock large-scale experimentation that was previously infeasible, significantly reducing training times for deep reinforcement learning agents.

The core task addressed, **rearrangement**, is recognized as a fundamental challenge for embodied AI. It involves tasks like picking up, moving, and placing objects, often interacting with articulated containers (e.g., opening a fridge or drawer). This goes beyond simpler **visual navigation** tasks, which the original Habitat platform primarily focused on. It also extends beyond **tabletop manipulation** benchmarks like RLBench, by incorporating house-scale environments and long-horizon tasks that require both mobile navigation and manipulation.

Furthermore, H2.0 serves as a critical platform for systematically comparing different approaches to embodied control: **end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies** versus **classical Sense-Plan-Act (SPA) pipelines**. This comparison is crucial for understanding the strengths and weaknesses of data-driven learning versus model-based planning in complex, partially observable environments. The findings from these comparisons provide valuable insights into architectural inductive biases necessary for solving long-horizon tasks, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in simulation for real-world robot deployment. By open-sourcing the platform, the authors aim to democratize access to high-performance simulation, reducing reliance on proprietary or less performant tools and fostering community-wide progress.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the Habitat 2.0 project is to **advance the development of embodied intelligent systems, particularly home assistant robots**, capable of performing complex rearrangement tasks in interactive 3D environments. The authors articulate a clear motivation stemming from the inherent challenges of real-world robotics: training and testing robots directly in hardware is slow, prohibitively expensive, and difficult to reproduce consistent experimental results.

To overcome these limitations and accelerate research, the paper identifies and addresses three core components of the embodied AI research stack:

1. **Data:** A primary objective was to curate a rich dataset of **house-scale interactive 3D assets**. This includes environments with articulated objects (e.g., cabinets, drawers, fridges that can open/close) to support complex manipulation tasks. A key motivation here is to enable the study of **generalization** to unseen objects, receptacles, and home layouts, which is crucial for robust real-world deployment. This objective led to the creation of **ReplicaCAD**.

2. **Simulation:** The second objective was to develop the **next generation of high-performance, photo-realistic 3D simulators** capable of supporting rich interactive environments and complex physics-enabled scenarios. The core motivation was to achieve unprecedented simulation speeds to enable large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) experiments that were previously computationally infeasible. The design philosophy of H2.0 explicitly prioritizes **performance (speed)** over the breadth of simulation capabilities, deliberately omitting non-rigid dynamics, physical state transformations, audio, or tactile sensing to achieve its remarkable throughput.

3. **Tasks (Benchmarks):** The final objective was to establish a suite of **challenging and representative benchmark tasks** specifically designed for assistive robots. These benchmarks, collectively known as the **Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB)**, aim to enable reproducible comparisons between different embodied AI approaches and systematically track progress over time. The tasks (TidyHouse, PrepareGroceries, SetTable) are instantiations of the generalized rearrangement problem, requiring a mobile manipulator to pick and place objects, often involving interaction with containers. The choice of a "GeometricGoal" specification for tasks, analogous to PointNav in navigation, is motivated by the desire to create a fundamental testbed for manipulation ideas.

In essence, the motivation is to provide the community with a robust, performant, and open-source platform, along with a challenging dataset and benchmark, to accelerate fundamental research in mobile manipulation and long-horizon task execution for future home assistant robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Habitat 2.0 project makes comprehensive contributions across data, simulation, and benchmark tasks, employing distinct methodologies for each:

#### 3.1. Data: ReplicaCAD Dataset Creation

The authors address the need for interactive 3D environments by introducing **ReplicaCAD**, an artist-authored, annotated, and reconfigurable dataset.
- **Starting Point:** It leverages the highly photo-realistic 3D reconstructions of indoor spaces from the Replica dataset, but critically transforms static scans into interactive environments.
- **Asset Creation:** A team of 3D artists meticulously recreated objects (92 unique objects, 6 rooms) from the "FRL-apartment" spaces. For each object, geometry, textures, material specifications, physical parameters (mass, friction, restitution), collision proxy shapes, and semantic annotations were authored. Crucially, several objects were made **articulated** (e.g., refrigerators, kitchen counters with doors/drawers) using URDF files to describe joint configurations and dynamic properties, enabling realistic physical interaction.
- **Layout Generation:** To test generalization, artists created 5 semantically plausible "macro variations" of scene layouts, with large furniture rearranged. Each macro variation was further perturbed with 20 "micro variations" (repositioning or swapping objects), resulting in 105 unique scene layouts.
- **Procedural Clutter Generation:** To maximize data diversity, a pipeline was developed to procedurally populate annotated supporting surfaces and containment volumes with object instances (from ReplicaCAD or YCB dataset), ensuring physically stable initial arrangements.
- **Integration and Availability:** ReplicaCAD is fully integrated with H2.0, with configuration files for easy import and alteration. It represents over 900 person-hours of professional 3D artist effort and is shared under a Creative Commons license (CC-BY-NC).

#### 3.2. Simulation: Habitat 2.0 (H2.0) Design

H2.0 is designed with a core philosophy: **"speed is more important than the breadth of capabilities."** This led to a "lazy simulator" approach with three key ideas:
- **Localized Physics and Rendering:** Instead of simulating everything constantly, H2.0 optimizes by:
 - Assuming the robot is the primary force applier, not recomputing physics for distant objects.
 - Using a navigation mesh for kinematic robot base movement, avoiding expensive wheel-ground contact simulation.
 - Leveraging physics 'sleeping' states to optimize rendering by caching transformations and frustum-culling.
 - Treating constrained object-parts (e.g., cabinet walls) as static.
- **Interleaved Physics and Rendering:** To maximize CPU/GPU utilization (as they are often idle waiting for each other), H2.0 breaks the sequential dependency of simulation steps. The agent policy is changed to $\pi(a_t | o_{t-1})$ (current action from previous observation), allowing physics ($s_t \to s_{t+1}$) on CPU and rendering ($s_t \to o_t$) on GPU to run in parallel. This novel approach significantly increases throughput and offers benefits for sim2real transfer due to inherent sensor latency.
- **Simplify and Reuse:**
 - GPU texture compression (Basis format) reduces memory footprint (4-6x), allowing more concurrent simulations per GPU.
 - Caching object assets in memory reduces scene reset times by pre-fetching.
 - Simplified convex decompositions of objects are used for fast collision calculations, separate from high-quality visual assets.
- **Underlying Implementation:** H2.0 involves a deep, low-level C++ integration of the Magnum rendering engine and the Bullet physics engine, enabling fine-grained control and task-aware optimizations.
- **Benchmarking:** Performance is rigorously benchmarked on Fetch robot interactions in ReplicaCAD scenes, measuring simulation steps per second (SPS) in both idle and interact scenarios, demonstrating 100x speedups.
- **Motion Planning Integration:** H2.0 integrates with the Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL) to facilitate comparisons with classical Sense-Plan-Act (SPA) approaches.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Tasks: Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB)

HAB is a suite of tasks designed to test mobile manipulation capabilities of assistive robots.
- **Task Definition:** Three main task families, increasing in complexity:
 - **TidyHouse:** Move 5 objects from random locations to their designated spots, no container interaction.
 - **PrepareGroceries:** Move 2 objects from fridge to counters, and 1 object back into fridge. Fridge starts open.
 - **SetTable:** Get a bowl from a drawer, a fruit from the fridge, place fruit in bowl on table. Requires opening/closing both fridge and drawer.
- **Task Specification:** Tasks are communicated via "GeometricGoal" – initial and desired 3D center-of-mass positions of target objects.
- **Robot Embodiment:** A Fetch mobile manipulator with a 7-DoF arm and parallel-jaw gripper.
- **Sensing:** Head- and arm-mounted RGB-D cameras (128x128), proprioceptive joint-position sensors, and egomotion sensors. No privileged state information.
- **Action Space:** Continuous end-effector control for the arm, velocity control for the base. Abstracted grasping (object snaps when end-effector is near).
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Success if all target objects are within 15cm of desired positions; failure if accumulated contact force exceeds a threshold. Generalization to unseen layouts and object configurations is emphasized.

#### 3.4. Agent Approaches

The paper compares three distinct approaches:
- **MonolithicRL:** A single, end-to-end 'sensors-to-actions' deep reinforcement learning policy (DD-PPO algorithm). It uses a CNN for visual input, concatenated with proprioceptive sensing and goal coordinates, fed into a recurrent actor-critic network.
- **TaskPlanning+SkillsRL (TP+SRL):** A hierarchical approach that uses a symbolic **STRIPS planner** to break down long-horizon tasks into a sequence of parameterized skills (e.g., Navigate, Pick, Place, Open/Close fridge/drawer). Each skill is then executed by a dedicated, learned RL policy.
- **TaskPlanning+SensePlanAct (TP+SPA) / TP+SPA-Priv:** Similar to TP+SRL in using a STRIPS planner for high-level task breakdown. However, the low-level skills (except Navigate) are implemented via a classical Sense-Plan-Act pipeline. "Sensing" involves constructing a 3D point cloud from depth sensors for collision queries. "Planning" uses Bidirectional RRT in joint configuration space. TP+SPA-Priv uses privileged (ground truth) scene geometry for planning, while TP+SPA relies on egocentric sensor observations, highlighting the impact of imperfect perception.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents rigorous empirical evaluations on both the simplified "Pick" task and the complex "Home Assistant Benchmark" (HAB) tasks, yielding several key findings:

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance Benchmarking

- **Unprecedented Speed:** H2.0 achieves 1,191 SPS (40x real-time) on a single process and an astounding 25,734 SPS (850x real-time) on an 8-GPU node. This represents roughly a 100x speedup compared to typical existing simulators (which achieve 10-400 SPS).
- **Design Efficacy:** Ablation studies confirm that the "lazy simulator" principles – localized physics/rendering, interleaved execution, and simplify-and-reuse – collectively contribute to these significant performance improvements. The deep C++ integration of Bullet and Magnum is highlighted as a major differentiator.
- **Impact:** These speedups dramatically reduce experimentation time (from months to days), enabling large-scale RL training and systematic studies previously impossible.

#### 4.2. Pick Task Generalization Analysis

(A base case for mobile manipulation, involving picking one object from a cluttered receptacle, without base movement).

- **Generalization to Layouts:** MonolithicRL generalizes relatively well from seen to unseen apartment layouts (91.7% to 86.3% success), outperforming SPA (72.7%) and even SPA-Privileged (80.0%) for this specific type of generalization.
- **Generalization to Objects and Receptacles:** Generalization remains challenging for MonolithicRL to new objects (91.7% to 74.7%) due to new visual features and obstacles. Generalization to new receptacles is even poorer (91.7% to 52.7%), with the qualitative analysis suggesting that unseen receptacles (e.g., shelves, armchairs) are inherently more difficult due to confined spaces.
- **Sensor Ablation for MonolithicRL:** Depth sensing with proprioceptive state (D+ps) marginally outperforms RGBD+ps. Surprisingly, **blind policies (proprioceptive-sensing only)** are highly effective (78% success), indicating that agents learn to "feel their way" to the goal, albeit with more collisions and wrong object picks. The combination of arm and head cameras proves most effective.

#### 4.3. Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB) Results

(Evaluating TidyHouse, PrepareGroceries, and SetTable tasks, requiring mobile manipulation and container interaction).

- **Flat vs. Hierarchical Policies:** **MonolithicRL performs "abysmally"** on long-horizon HAB tasks. Despite efforts to craft combined reward functions, a single RL policy struggles to chain diverse skills for complex, multi-step tasks. In contrast, hierarchical approaches (TP+SRL, TP+SPA) show significantly stronger results, indicating that **architectural inductive bias about task structure is crucial** for such problems.
- **Challenges of Hierarchy ("Hand-off Problems"):** While hierarchical methods perform better, a hierarchy with independently trained skills suffers from "hand-off problems." This occurs when a preceding skill does not set up the environment optimally for the succeeding one (e.g., navigating to a suboptimal location for manipulation, only partially opening a drawer).
- **Navigation as a Bottleneck:** Learning an effective navigation policy for manipulation tasks is challenging. The agent must navigate not just to a point, but to an unspecified, non-unique suitable location from which an object can be manipulated, including facing the correct direction. The performance drop between learned navigation and oracle (perfect) navigation highlights this issue.
- **Brittleness of Sense-Plan-Act (SPA):** For simpler skills (e.g., TidyHouse), TP+SPA with oracle navigation can outperform TP+SRL. However, SPA methods are **significantly more brittle** in complex settings involving clutter or tight spaces (e.g., fridge in PrepareGroceries). SPA's reliance on egocentric perception for planning means it can struggle with incomplete information, leading to failures in finding goal configurations or valid plans. TP+SPA fails more often than TP+SRL in PrepareGroceries due to the perception challenge of the cluttered fridge. Crafting SPA for unknown articulated containers (e.g., SetTable) remains an unsolved robotics problem, hence no results are reported for TP+SPA on SetTable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Habitat 2.0 represents a monumental leap forward for research in embodied AI, particularly for tasks requiring mobile manipulation and interaction with environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Large-Scale Embodied AI Research:** The most immediate and profound impact of H2.0 is its **unprecedented simulation speed**. By achieving 100x speedups, the platform transforms the feasible scope of research. Experiments that previously took months can now be completed in days, dramatically accelerating the iteration cycle for developing and evaluating embodied agents. This performance is critical for scaling deep reinforcement learning, which is inherently data-hungry.
2. **Standardized and Challenging Benchmarks:** The introduction of **ReplicaCAD** and the **Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB)** provides the community with a rich, interactive, and reproducible testbed. ReplicaCAD offers diverse and semantically meaningful indoor environments with articulated objects, specifically designed for testing generalization. HAB, with its progression of rearrangement tasks (TidyHouse, PrepareGroceries, SetTable), establishes a clear and challenging benchmark for mobile manipulation, encouraging the development of more capable and robust home assistant robots.
3. **Driving Methodological Insights:** The systematic comparison between end-to-end RL, hierarchical RL, and classical Sense-Plan-Act (SPA) pipelines yields crucial insights into the current state of embodied AI. The finding that flat RL policies struggle "abysmally" on long-horizon tasks strongly suggests the necessity of **architectural inductive biases** or structured approaches for complex multi-skill problems. The identification of "hand-off problems" within hierarchical policies and the "brittleness" of SPA under imperfect perception highlights specific avenues for future research in both learning and planning. These insights can guide the development of hybrid methods that combine the strengths of both paradigms.
4. **Promoting Open Science and Collaboration:** Releasing H2.0 as **free, open-source software** under the MIT license is a major contribution to the academic and industrial research communities. It democratizes access to state-of-the-art simulation capabilities, reducing reliance on proprietary tools and fostering collaborative development. This will likely catalyze a surge of research in mobile manipulation and interactive embodied AI.
5. **Path Towards Real-World Home Assistants:** While currently focused on simulation, H2.0's advancements bring the scientific community closer to developing practical home assistant robots. The tasks modeled in HAB directly reflect real-world challenges faced by assistive robots. The deliberate focus on "gross motor control" (base and arm) acknowledges the foundational nature of these skills, laying groundwork for future integration of fine motor control (grasping) as those sub-problems mature.
6. **Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:** The paper responsibly highlights limitations, such as ReplicaCAD being modeled on US apartments (implying potential cultural bias in future agents) and the current bottlenecks in the simulation-training loop beyond physics/rendering. This transparency provides clear avenues for future work, including incorporating more complex physics (deformables, fluids), refining fine motor control, and holistically optimizing the entire RL training pipeline for even greater throughput.

In summary, Habitat 2.0 is not merely a technical update; it's a foundational platform that promises to accelerate fundamental research in embodied AI, setting new standards for simulation performance and benchmark design, and offering critical insights into the architectural requirements for intelligent agents capable of complex, interactive tasks.
