# PhaForce: Phase-Scheduled Visual-Force Policy Learning with Slow Planning and Fast Correction for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhaForce integrates phase-scheduled visual-force policy learning with a slow-fast architecture for contact-rich robotic manipulation, improving average task success rates by 40 percentage points and enhancing robustness in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation tasks requiring contact interactions struggle with effectively utilizing high-frequency force/torque feedback for real-time corrections due to timescale mismatches in generative policies.
- Current force-aware policies lack explicit, phase-dependent mechanisms to schedule when, how much, and where force should be applied across multi-phase tasks.
- Indiscriminate force application can lead to spurious corrections, degrading alignment, or causing unintended behaviors like jamming in contact-rich scenarios.

## Method

- **PhaForce** employs a ==slow-fast architecture== coordinating low-rate, chunk-level planning with high-rate, within-chunk residual correction, guided by a unified ==contact-aware phase schedule==.
- A ==Contact-Aware Phase Predictor (CAP)== forecasts contact probability and task-specific phase distributions, enabling anticipatory and phase-dependent force utilization.
- The ==Slow Diffusion Planner== uses dual-gated fusion with ==Orthogonal Residual Injection (ORI)== to preserve vision semantics, while a ==Fast Residual Corrector== applies phase-routed, physically-prior-supervised adjustments.

## Results

- Achieved an average **86%** success rate across five real-robot contact-rich manipulation tasks, outperforming baseline diffusion policies by **40 percentage points**.
- Demonstrated superior contact quality in wiping tasks, with a wiping score of **0.85** and significantly lower under- and over-pressure ratios, indicating precise normal force regulation.
- Showed strong adaptability to out-of-distribution scenarios, maintaining an **85%** success rate in a raised wiping board task where chunk-level planners failed completely.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly scheduling force feedback based on predicted task phases and contact probability significantly enhances the effectiveness and precision of robotic manipulation in contact-rich tasks.
> - Decoupling planning and correction into slow (generative) and fast (residual) components allows for real-time reaction to high-frequency force transients without compromising long-horizon task understanding.
> - Orthogonal Residual Injection (ORI) is crucial for integrating force information as a refinement to visual planning, preventing the degradation of vision-dominant task semantics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements, particularly with the advent of diffusion-based visuomotor policies and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These approaches have demonstrated capabilities in vision-dominant tasks such as pick-and-place, rearrangement, and folding. However, a substantial subset of real-world robotic tasks involves contact-rich manipulation, where success relies not only on precise geometric alignment inferred from vision but also on dynamic interactions such as friction, jamming, and transient impacts.

Existing force-aware methods typically incorporate 6D force/torque (F/T) sensing by encoding a short history of F/T measurements and fusing this representation with visual features. This fusion is then used within a chunked generative policy. While this approach integrates force information, the authors identify two key limitations in the current research landscape:

1. **Timescale Mismatch of Force Feedback:** F/T signals are inherently high-frequency and valuable for rapid, closed-loop corrections. However, generative policies, especially diffusion models, often operate at a low inference frequency due to computational latency and action chunking. This limits their ability to react promptly to short-horizon interaction transients (e.g., micro-impacts, stick-slip, early jamming), effectively underutilizing the real-time nature of force feedback. Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP) represents a step towards addressing this by proposing a slow–fast execution architecture, but it lacks an explicit mechanism for phase-dependent force utilization.

2. **Lack of Explicit Phase Scheduling:** Contact-rich manipulation tasks are inherently multi-phase, meaning different stages (e.g., planar search, normal insertion, unlocking) require distinct corrective behaviors and prioritize different degrees of freedom for compliance or adjustment. Current force-aware policies often inject force continuously and indiscriminately, lacking an explicit mechanism to schedule *when*, *how much*, and *where* (in which corrective subspace) to apply force across these different task phases. This can lead to spurious corrections in irrelevant dimensions, potentially degrading alignment or inducing unintended behaviors such as jamming. Prior work like FoAR modulates force based on predicted contact probability, and ForceVLA uses a Mixture-of-Experts approach to implicitly specialize across interaction stages, but these approaches often lack an explicit, task-defined probabilistic phase belief mechanism to interpretably schedule force usage.

PhaForce aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a novel framework that integrates a contact-aware phase scheduling mechanism with a slow–fast policy architecture, allowing for coordinated, phase-dependent utilization of force feedback for both long-horizon planning and high-rate residual correction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust and adaptive visuomotor policy capable of effectively performing contact-rich manipulation tasks by strategically leveraging force/torque feedback. To achieve this, the authors identify several specific motivations and goals:

1. **Enable High-Rate Closed-Loop Force Correction:** A central motivation is to overcome the timescale mismatch inherent in many existing policies. The goal is to allow for rapid, within-chunk corrections in response to transient F/T signals, which are crucial for fine-grained contact adjustments, preventing jamming, and recovering from minor misalignments that visual-only or low-frequency policies might miss.

2. **Implement Explicit Phase-Scheduled Force Utilization:** The authors aim to introduce an explicit, task-semantic mechanism to schedule force feedback. This involves dynamically determining *when* (e.g., only during contact), *how much* (e.g., intensity of force injection), and *where* (e.g., which degrees of freedom) force should influence the robot's actions. This is motivated by the observation that different task phases in contact-rich manipulation require distinct and often orthogonal corrective actions.

3. **Preserve Vision-Dominant Semantics in Planning:** While integrating force, the policy should not degrade the long-horizon task semantics primarily derived from visual observations. The motivation is to maintain the strengths of visuomotor policies for overall task understanding and sequence, while carefully injecting force information to refine interaction.

4. **Enhance Adaptability to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios:** A key objective is to demonstrate that a phase-scheduled slow–fast approach, by intelligently utilizing real-time F/T feedback, can lead to increased robustness and adaptability when faced with novel contact conditions or geometric shifts not encountered during training demonstrations.

5. **Unify Planning and Correction:** The work seeks to provide a unified framework that seamlessly coordinates low-frequency, chunk-level generative planning with high-frequency, within-chunk residual correction, both guided by a common contact/phase schedule. This aims to leverage the strengths of both planning horizons for complex manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhaForce is structured as a phase-scheduled slow–fast policy designed to coordinate low-rate chunk-level planning with high-rate residual correction through a unified contact/phase schedule. The architecture comprises three main components: a Contact-Aware Phase Predictor (CAP), a Slow Diffusion Planner, and a Fast Residual Corrector.

**A. Problem Formulation and Preliminaries**
The policy operates with two update rates: a control frequency (`f_c` = 24 Hz) for the robot command and Fast corrector, and an inference frequency (`f_s` = 6 Hz) for the Slow planner. The Slow planner predicts a nominal TCP pose (`T_slow_t`), and the Fast corrector predicts a small delta pose (`ΔT_fast_t`). The final executed pose is a composition of these: `T_t = T_slow_t ◦ ΔT_fast_t`. Observations for the planner (`o_p_t`) include multi-view RGB images, wrench history, and proprioception, while the corrector (`o_c_t`) uses only low-dimensional signals (wrench history, proprioception). A lightweight TCN-style temporal encoder processes the wrench history across all components.

**B. Contact-Aware Phase Predictor (CAP)**
The CAP (`π_CAP`) is a lightweight neural network that runs at `f_c`. It takes the planner observation (`o_p_t`) as input and outputs a continuous contact probability (`p_c_t`) and a soft distribution over `K` task-specific phases (`p_t`). The CAP uses ResNet-18 encoders for visual features, which are fused with force and proprioceptive features via a small MLP, leading to binary contact and categorical phase heads.
Crucially, CAP is trained for *anticipation*. The contact head is supervised using a future-window label indicating contact within `K_f` steps, and the phase head uses a future offset label. This anticipatory prediction allows for proactive scheduling of force usage. The training loss is a multi-task objective combining binary cross-entropy for contact prediction and cross-entropy for phase prediction. All labels are automatically generated from wrench signals and TCP pose.

**C. Slow Diffusion Planner**
The Slow Diffusion Planner (`π_slow`) operates at `f_s` and takes an augmented planner input, which includes the standard observations (`o_p_t`) along with the predicted contact probability (`p_c_t`) and phase belief (`p_t`) from CAP. It outputs an action chunk of horizon `H_a`.
- **Encoders:** Multi-view RGB images are encoded into a visual token (`v_t`), and wrench history into force tokens (`F_t`).
- **Dual-Gated Fusion:** Vision and force are fused using a multi-head cross-attention block, where the visual token queries the force tokens.
 - A **phase-dependent head-wise gate** (`g_head_t`), derived from `p_t` via an MLP and sigmoid, reweights the output of each attention head. This makes the influence of force phase-dependent.
 - A **global injection gate** (`g_c_t = p_c_t`) controls the overall injection strength, suppressing force influence in free-space.
- **Orthogonal Residual Injection (ORI):** To prevent the force signal from degrading vision-dominant semantics, the cross-attention output (`Δ_t`) is injected as a residual (`Δ_⊥_t`) *orthogonal* to the visual token (`v_t`). This maintains the primary visual context while conditioning on force. The final fused token (`v'_t`) then conditions a diffusion action head to generate the action chunk.

**D. Fast Residual Corrector**
The Fast Residual Corrector (`π_fast`) runs at the higher control rate `f_c`. Its input includes the low-dimensional corrector observations (`o_c_t`), a short history of Slow planner actions (`h_slow_t`), and the CAP outputs (`p_c_t`, `p_t`). It predicts a 6D channel-wise residual `c_t`.
- **Phase-Routed Corrective Subspaces:** For each of the `K` task phases, a `phase-specific subspace selector` (`B_k`) is predefined as a diagonal binary channel mask. This mask activates or disables specific degrees of freedom for correction in that phase (e.g., planar movements in a search phase, normal compliance in an insertion phase). The raw `c_t` is then softly routed using the phase belief `p_t` and gated by the contact probability `p_c_t` to produce the final residual twist (`δξ_t`). This mechanism provides smooth interpolation between corrective subspaces and suppresses corrections when not in contact.
- **Physical-Prior Supervision:** The Fast corrector is trained using physically motivated supervision. Instead of explicitly estimating a target pose, the desired pose offset is treated as a residual twist. Phase-wise physically motivated residual targets (`δξ*_(t,k)`) are constructed from wrench signals to capture ideal corrective trends for each phase (e.g., relieving tangential friction, tracking a desired normal force). These phase-wise targets are then averaged using `p_t` and gated by `p_c_t` to form a single target `δξ*_t`. The Fast corrector is trained to regress its output `δξ_t` to `δξ*_t` using an `ℓ_1` loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PhaForce was evaluated across five real-robot contact-rich manipulation tasks using a Flexiv Rizon 4s robotic arm. Each task featured `K` task-defined phases with corresponding corrective subspaces. The tasks included Charger Plug-in, USB Plug-in, Drawer Opening, Wiping (ID - In-Distribution), and Wiping (OOD - Out-of-Distribution with a raised board). Experiments involved 20 evaluation trials per method with randomized initial conditions.

**Overall Performance:**
PhaForce demonstrated consistent improvements, achieving the highest or tied-highest success rate (SR) on all five tasks. Averaged across tasks, PhaForce achieved an 86% SR, representing a +40 percentage point (pp) improvement over the baselines: Diffusion Policy (DP), DP (force-concat), and Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP).

**Detailed Task Results:**
- **Plug-in Tasks (Charger, USB):** These tasks are highly sensitive to pose errors and local contact geometry. Baselines frequently exhibited "stagnation" (stuck at socket entrance), "partial insertion" (not fully seated), or "slip-induced in-hand rotation." PhaForce mitigated these issues by leveraging its phase-aware scheduling to condition search and recovery behaviors, allowing for timely retreat-and-retry. The anticipatory contact/phase predictions from CAP were observed to align well with wrench transients (Figure 5). PhaForce's use of real action space for Slow planning, as opposed to RDP's latent action space, was suggested to improve precision for millimeter-sensitive tasks, reducing partial insertion failures.
- **Drawer Opening:** PhaForce achieved consistent improvements in this task, which involves overcoming stiction and following a constrained sliding motion. The gains were attributed to phase-aware force utilization, which facilitated compliant pulling under friction variations and enabled timely adjustments.
- **Wiping (ID - In-Distribution):** Beyond success rate, contact quality was evaluated using a wiping score, mean contact normal force (`F_n`), and ratios of over-pressure (`F_n` > 25 N) and under-pressure (`F_n` < 2.5 N). PhaForce achieved the best overall wiping outcome (score 0.85), with the lowest under- and over-pressure ratios, effectively regulating normal force. DP, despite a high SR, exhibited unstable contact quality with frequent over-pressure. DP (force-concat) and RDP occasionally failed to grasp the sponge, indicating issues with un-scheduled force use in non-contact phases. PhaForce's benefits were attributed to CAP's phase-aware scheduling, ORI for vision preservation, and the Fast corrector's force regulation.
- **Wiping (OOD - Out-of-Distribution):** In this challenging scenario where the board was raised by 3 cm, chunk-level diffusion planners (DP, DP (force-concat), and PhaForce w/o Fast) failed completely (SR 0%), exhibiting sustained over-pressure. RDP demonstrated resilience (SR 75%), highlighting the advantage of a slow–fast design for contact adaptation. PhaForce further improved the wiping score (0.75) with comparable contact stability (SR 85%), demonstrating that the Fast corrector is critical for compensating OOD height mismatches.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablations on USB Plug-in and Wiping (OOD) validated key components:
- **PhaForce (w/o PB - Phase Belief):** Replacing the phase belief with a uniform prior significantly reduced USB Plug-in SR (85% to 25%). This indicates the importance of explicit phase scheduling for routing corrections and managing transitions.
- **PhaForce (w/o ORI - Orthogonal Residual Injection):** Replacing ORI with direct cross-attention conditioning degraded Wipe-OOD SR (85% to 60%) and wiping score (0.75 to 0.45). This supports ORI's role in preserving vision-dominant semantics while effectively injecting force information.
- **PhaForce (w/o Fast - Residual Corrector):** Removing the Fast corrector led to complete failure in Wipe-OOD (SR 0%). This confirms the indispensability of high-rate residual correction for stabilizing contact and compensating for rapid F/T transients, especially in OOD environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhaForce represents a significant development in the field of contact-rich robotic manipulation, offering a structured and principled approach to integrating multimodal feedback for complex tasks.

The work's primary significance lies in its effective resolution of two previously underexplored challenges: the timescale mismatch between high-frequency force feedback and low-frequency generative planning, and the lack of explicit, phase-dependent force scheduling. By introducing a Contact-Aware Phase Predictor and coordinating a Slow Diffusion Planner with a Fast Residual Corrector, PhaForce enables robots to react to critical force/torque transients in real-time while maintaining long-horizon task semantics. The dual-gated fusion with Orthogonal Residual Injection ensures that force information is leveraged precisely when and where needed, without indiscriminately altering the primary visual understanding of the task.

The empirical results on real-robot tasks demonstrate substantial performance gains over strong baselines, not only in terms of success rates but also in achieving superior contact quality and robustness. The notable success in out-of-distribution scenarios, such as the raised wiping board, highlights PhaForce's potential to adapt to unforeseen environmental variations, a critical capability for deploying robots in unstructured real-world environments. This adaptability reduces the need for extensive re-demonstration or retraining for minor environmental shifts.

The proposed methodology offers a foundation for developing more intelligent and dexterous robotic systems capable of intricate physical interactions. Its impact could extend to various applications requiring fine motor skills and robust contact handling, such as assembly, medical procedures, or service robotics.

Future work outlined by the authors, including exploring reinforcement learning for the Fast corrector and extending PhaForce to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, indicates a pathway towards even more generalized and autonomous manipulation capabilities. This research contributes to advancing the reliability and versatility of robots interacting with their physical environments.
