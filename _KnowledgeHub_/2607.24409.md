# Accuracy potential of visual localization exploiting high-end street-level imagery

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a visual localization pipeline and the FHNW Muttenz dataset to systematically assess accuracy in street environments, demonstrating that visual localization can achieve median pose accuracies of 1-5 cm for translation and 0.05-0.1° for rotation, potentially meeting survey-grade demands.

## Problem

- Existing visual localization methods lack the sub-5 cm accuracy required to complement survey-grade GNSS in urban environments where satellite signals are often degraded.
- Current public datasets for visual localization do not provide sub-centimeter ground-truth 6-DoF poses, large-scale coverage, or multi-epoch imagery for rigorous evaluation.
- Real-world challenges like changes in illumination, dynamic objects, and significant viewpoint variations hinder the consistent achievement of high visual localization accuracy in outdoor settings.

## Method

- A modular visual localization pipeline was developed, processing precisely georeferenced, high-resolution imagery using ==coarse-to-fine candidate selection== and ==on-the-fly local Structure-from-Motion (SfM) reconstruction==.
- The **FHNW Muttenz dataset** was created, featuring a 10 km street network mapped across two epochs with sub-centimeter ground-truth poses for reference imagery and diverse consumer-grade query devices.
- The pipeline includes ==tiled feature extraction== for high-resolution images and evaluates various state-of-the-art ==image retrieval== and ==local feature matching== methods for robust pose estimation.

## Results

- The visual localization pipeline achieved median pose accuracies ranging from **1 to 5 cm** for translation and **0.05 to 0.1°** for rotation across diverse consumer-grade query images.
- Pose pre-filtering consistently improved visual localization accuracy by up to **25.1%** across all tested image retrieval methods, and higher image resolutions (e.g., 2048 pixels) reduced median pose errors by **30-45%**.
- Using the most recent (2025 campaign) reference data significantly improved localization accuracy compared to older data (2023 campaign), but combining both epochs resulted in degraded performance compared to using only the newest data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual localization can achieve median pose accuracies of 1-5 cm for translation and 0.05-0.1° for rotation in complex outdoor environments, demonstrating its capacity for survey-grade precision.
> - Image resolution is crucial for high accuracy, with an observed optimum for various feature representations; doubling resolution can reduce median pose error by 30-45% for most local features.
> - Effective map management is important for long-term localization; simply combining older and newer reference data can degrade accuracy compared to using only the most recent information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the critical need for accurate and reliable 6-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) pose information in applications such as autonomous navigation, surveying, robotics, and augmented/mixed reality. Traditionally, Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) combined with Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) fulfill this role. However, GNSS applicability and accuracy are often limited in outdoor urban environments due to occlusions and multi-path effects. Visual localization (VL) offers a complementary positioning modality, particularly effective where GNSS signals are degraded, as visual features tend to be abundant in such areas.

The field of visual localization has seen extensive development, broadly categorized into 2D image-based and 3D geometry-based methods. 2D methods use image databases for scene representation, relying on image retrieval (IR) and subsequent pose estimation (e.g., relative pose, local Structure-from-Motion (SfM)). 3D methods leverage explicit 3D scene geometry (e.g., SfM point clouds, meshes, neural radiance fields) to establish 2D-3D correspondences for pose estimation. While 2D methods offer scalability, 3D methods generally achieve higher accuracy due to extensive geometric reasoning.

Existing visual localization methods typically define "accurate" performance as translation errors below 25 centimeters and rotation errors below 2 degrees. However, this level of accuracy is insufficient to complement survey-grade GNSS positioning, which requires sub-5 centimeter accuracy. The gap in achieving survey-grade accuracy with visual localization is compounded by various real-world challenges, including changes in illumination, dynamic objects, weather and seasonal variations, occlusions, structural modifications, significant viewpoint differences, and variations in imaging sensor characteristics.

A significant limitation in current research is the lack of publicly available, large-scale outdoor datasets that provide ground-truth poses with sub-centimeter accuracy. Existing datasets often focus on smaller scales, indoor environments, or outdoor scenarios with coarser ground-truth (typically decimeter level), making systematic evaluation against survey-grade demands challenging. Furthermore, many datasets do not offer multi-epoch imagery for long-term localization studies or comprehensive cross-device query capabilities. This paper aims to address these limitations by introducing both a new methodology and a dataset specifically designed for high-accuracy visual localization in demanding outdoor conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the accuracy potential of visual localization in street environments, specifically assessing its capacity to meet survey-grade accuracy demands. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and broken down into specific contributions:

1. **Addressing the Gap in Accuracy Evaluation:** Current visual localization benchmarks and accuracy definitions (e.g., 25 cm translation, 2° rotation) are not stringent enough for survey-grade applications, which require accuracies below 5 cm. There has been no systematic study to determine if visual localization can achieve this level of precision in complex outdoor scenes.
2. **Lack of Suitable Datasets:** A major impediment to such an investigation is the absence of publicly available, large-scale outdoor datasets featuring ground-truth 6-DoF camera poses with sub-centimeter precision. Existing datasets often have coarser ground-truth or are limited in scale, environmental diversity, or sensor types.
3. **Developing a Scalable and Accurate Visual Localization Pipeline:** The authors aimed to develop a visual localization pipeline capable of achieving high accuracy while remaining scalable for large-scale environments. This pipeline was designed to minimize constraints on the scene representation, using precisely georeferenced, high-resolution imagery directly.
4. **Introducing a Novel Dataset:** A key objective was to create and make publicly available a new real-world dataset, the FHNW Muttenz dataset. This dataset is designed to overcome the limitations of existing resources, providing a contiguous 10 km street network mapped across two epochs (1.5 years apart), featuring high-resolution reference and query imagery from diverse camera systems, and critically, validated sub-centimeter ground-truth poses.
5. **Systematic Performance Evaluation:** Using the newly developed pipeline and dataset, the research aims to conduct a systematic evaluation of visual localization accuracy. This includes examining the performance of individual pipeline components, such as image retrieval and feature matching, and their impact on overall localization accuracy. The objective is to quantify the achievable pose accuracies under various conditions and with different state-of-the-art methods.

By addressing these objectives, the authors seek to determine if visual localization can serve as a viable complementary positioning modality to survey-grade GNSS, paving the way for new applications in 3D geospatial data acquisition and automated georeferencing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research comprises two main components: the development of a visual localization pipeline and the construction of a novel dataset with high-precision ground truth.

#### 3.1. Visual Localization Pipeline

The proposed visual localization pipeline is modular and designed for scalability and accuracy, utilizing precisely georeferenced, high-resolution imagery directly as the scene representation. It operates in two stages:

- **Offline Stage (Reference Data Preprocessing):**
 - **Feature Extraction:** Global image descriptors and sparse local features are extracted from all reference images.
 - **Database Storage:** Global descriptors, along with their associated pose information, are stored in a vector database for efficient retrieval. Local features and metadata (image poses, camera intrinsics) are stored in dedicated databases.

- **Online Stage (Query Image Localization):**
 - **Query Feature Extraction:** A global image descriptor and local features are extracted from the query image.
 - **Reference Candidate Selection:** A coarse-to-fine paradigm is employed:
 - **Prior-Guided Pre-filtering:** Given approximate GNSS information for the query image (common in modern devices), a spatial search region (e.g., 100m or 15m square) centered at the query's approximate position is used to filter candidate image descriptors from the vector database. An optional heading filter (e.g., ±90°) can further refine this subset.
 - **k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN) Search:** Within the spatially constrained subset, a kNN search (with k=10) identifies the most visually similar reference images, which are crucial for subsequent local SfM. Various Image Retrieval (IR) methods are tested, including NetVLAD, CosPlace, EigenPlaces, AnyLoc, DINOv2 SALAD, and MegaLoc.
 - **Feature Extraction and Matching:**
 - **Sparse Local Features:** The pipeline utilizes sparse local feature representations (e.g., SuperPoint, DISK, ALIKED, XFeat, RDD, and SIFT as a baseline) to manage computational and storage costs associated with high-resolution imagery.
 - **Tiled Feature Extraction:** For high-resolution images, a tiled feature extraction strategy is applied (e.g., 1024x1024 or 1600x1600 pixel tiles with 50-pixel overlap) to ensure comprehensive feature detection and mitigate border effects. Non-maximum suppression (NMS) removes duplicate features.
 - **2D-2D Matching:** Exhaustive matching is performed between the query image and the retrieved reference candidates. LightGlue (or LighterGlue for XFeat, MNN for SIFT) is used for robust feature matching, with depth confidence and point pruning disabled to maximize accuracy.
 - **Local SfM Model Reconstruction and Pose Estimation:**
 - **3D Point Triangulation:** A locally consistent SfM model is reconstructed on-the-fly by triangulating 3D points from geometrically verified 2D-2D correspondences among the selected reference images. Reference camera poses and intrinsics are held fixed.
 - **2D-3D Correspondence Establishment:** 2D-2D correspondences between the query image and reference images are examined to identify links to the reconstructed 3D points, forming 2D-3D correspondences.
 - **Pose Estimation:** The 6-DoF pose of the query image is estimated using a Perspective-n-Point (PnP) solver within a RANSAC framework (specifically COLMAP’s EPnP with LO-RANSAC), followed by non-linear pose refinement. Camera calibration parameters for the query image can also be estimated if unknown.

#### 3.2. FHNW Muttenz Dataset Construction

The authors developed the FHNW Muttenz dataset to facilitate high-accuracy visual localization research, addressing several limitations of existing datasets.

- **Study Area and Characteristics:** The dataset covers a contiguous 10 km street network in Muttenz, Switzerland, a suburban municipality. The area features diverse environments (historic, commercial, residential, industrial) and heterogeneous road types, designed to represent realistic outdoor localization challenges.
- **Reference Imagery Acquisition:**
 - **Multi-Epoch Data:** The area was mapped twice: Autumn 2023 (sunny, shadows, tinted foliage) and Early Spring 2025 (overcast, diffuse light, defoliated trees). Significant construction was ongoing in both campaigns.
 - **Professional Mobile Mapping Systems (MMS):** High-resolution imagery was acquired using professional vision-based MMS.
 - 2023 Campaign: Three high-resolution stereo systems (front, back left/right) and a medium-resolution multi-head panoramic camera (Ladybug5+).
 - 2025 Campaign: A high-resolution monocular camera (front) and a high-resolution multi-head panoramic camera (Ladybug6).
 - **Image Processing:** All cameras were pre-calibrated, producing distortion-free images (stereo systems also rectified). Panoramic images were remapped to five non-overlapping camera heads.
- **High-Precision Reference Georeferencing (Sub-centimeter):**
 - **Initial Georeferencing:** Image poses were initially computed via integrated georeferencing (tactical-grade IMU, geodetic GNSS) using 32 RTK-GNSS Ground Control Points (GCPs), achieving sub-decimeter accuracy.
 - **Refinement and Co-registration:** To reach sub-centimeter accuracy, a two-step process was applied using Agisoft Metashape:
 1. **Independent Campaign Refinement:** Each campaign's image poses were refined through an image-based georeferencing bundle adjustment, modeling stereo/panorama systems as multi-camera systems with relative orientation constraints.
 2. **Cross-Campaign Co-registration:** 76 RTK-GNSS GCPs and 180 manually measured Tie Points (TPs) observed across images from both campaigns were used in a global bundle adjustment to precisely align the two epochs.
 - **Accuracy Validation:** The co-registration accuracy was validated using 29 independent Tie Points (ITPs), yielding a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 2-6 mm in 3D coordinate differences between the 2023 and 2025 campaigns. Absolute georeferencing accuracy for each campaign was ~2.5 cm RMSE based on GCP residuals.
- **Query Sequences Acquisition:**
 - **Diverse Scenes:** Five distinct small-scale scenes within the study area were selected, representing varying urban characteristics.
 - **Consumer-Grade Devices:** Query images were captured in late winter 2025 (mixed weather, no foliage) using four different consumer-grade camera systems: Canon EOS R (with 24mm and 35mm lenses), GoPro Hero 7, and iPhone 14 Pro, representing different resolutions, fields of view, and camera models (pinhole, fisheye). Sequences were acquired with varying trajectories and triggering patterns (manual, video, distance/angle-based).
- **High-Precision Query Georeferencing (Sub-centimeter):**
 - **SfM-based Model Generation:** A multi-stage SfM procedure (Agisoft Metashape) was used to generate ground-truth poses for query images. Individual camera sequences were initially aligned, then mutually stabilized across cameras using additional tie points and 7-11 RTK-GNSS control points (CPs) per scene.
 - **Co-registration to Reference:** Approximately 20-25 clearly identifiable points (Relative Ground Control Points - RGCPs) observed in the reference images were used in a bundle adjustment to co-register the query photogrammetric model with the highly accurate reference imagery.
 - **Accuracy Validation:** The co-registration accuracy for query images yielded 3-8 mm 3D RMSE based on RGCP residuals and independent Relative Check Points (RCPs). The absolute georeferencing accuracy of the query poses was in the lower centimeter range (1.4-3.0 cm 3D RMSE) when compared to RTK-GNSS CPs. All query images were undistorted using estimated camera calibration parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments systematically evaluated the accuracy potential of visual localization, along with the performance of its components, under various conditions.

#### 4.1. Accuracy Potential of Visual Localization

The research demonstrated that visual localization in large-scale outdoor environments can provide image poses with median accuracies ranging from 1 to 5 cm for translation and 0.05 to 0.1° for rotation. Under optimal conditions, accuracies as low as 1 cm and 0.03° were achieved.

#### 4.2. Investigation of Reference Candidate Selection

- **Pre-filtering Benefits:** Pose pre-filtering (using approximate query position and/or heading) consistently improved visual localization accuracy across all tested image retrieval (IR) methods. The magnitude of improvement varied, with less robust IR methods like NetVLAD showing larger gains (up to 25.1% in the accurate threshold) compared to more robust methods like MegaLoc (around 1.4% gain).
- **Filter Type Effectiveness:** Position-based filtering yielded substantial and consistent improvements, while adding heading-based filtering provided only marginal additional benefits.
- **IR Method Performance:** MegaLoc demonstrated the strongest IR performance, particularly without pre-filtering. With effective pre-filtering, the performance differences between various descriptor representations narrowed to approximately 2% (excluding NetVLAD).
- **Descriptor Compactness:** Compact descriptors from methods like CosPlace and EigenPlaces exhibited only minor performance decreases compared to their longer counterparts, indicating a good trade-off between descriptor size and retrieval accuracy when supported by pre-filtering.

#### 4.3. Effects of High-Resolution Images on Visual Localization

- **Resolution Impact:** Higher image resolutions significantly enhanced pose accuracy, particularly in the most stringent error thresholds (0.01m/0.1° and 0.025m/0.25°). Doubling image resolution from 1024 to 2048 pixels typically reduced the median pose error by 30-45% for most local feature representations.
- **Resolution Optimum:** Most evaluated feature representations showed a resolution-dependent optimum, beyond which further increases in image resolution could lead to a deterioration in pose accuracy. For instance, SIFT, DISK, and RDD achieved optimal performance at 2048 pixels, while ALIKED and XFeat peaked at 4096 pixels. SuperPoint was an exception, benefiting continuously from higher resolutions, albeit with diminishing returns.
- **Feature Performance:** RDD at 2048 pixels achieved the highest overall performance for the two strictest error thresholds among the tested methods.

#### 4.4. Visual Localization Performance with Consumer-Grade Query Images

- **Impact of Reference Data Epoch:**
 - **Older Reference (2023 Campaign):** SuperPoint generally performed best or comparably to RDD and ALIKED in the high-accuracy regime. DISK and XFeat exhibited inferior performance.
 - **Newer Reference (2025 Campaign):** Localization accuracy significantly and consistently improved across all feature representations compared to using the 2023 data. ALIKED showed the highest overall performance, followed by RDD and SuperPoint. The performance gap between these top three and DISK/XFeat was still present but less pronounced.
 - **Combined Reference (2023 & 2025):** Simply combining reference data from both campaigns resulted in degraded pose accuracy compared to using only the most recent (2025) data, but improved accuracy compared to using only the older (2023) data. This indicates that naive accumulation of reference data over time does not necessarily yield the best performance.
- **Camera Field of View:** Images acquired with wider field-of-view cameras (e.g., GoPro with 122.6° horizontal FoV, Canon 24mm with 72.3°) generally yielded more accurate pose estimates compared to narrower FoV cameras (Canon 35mm with 53.3°, iPhone with 57.1°).
- **Overall Accuracies:** The median pose errors across all consumer-grade queries ranged from 1-5 cm for translation and 0.05-0.1° for rotation.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Effect of On-the-Fly Local Reconstruction:** The proposed local SfM pipeline, which triangulates 3D points on-the-fly, was compared to HLoc, a representative global SfM approach relying on a precomputed, globally consistent 3D model. HLoc outperformed the local SfM pipeline by up to 10 percentage points in the high-accuracy regime (<0.05m/0.5°). However, both pipelines achieved comparable localization performance at coarser thresholds (0.2m/2° and beyond). This suggests a trade-off where precomputed global models offer higher initial accuracy at the cost of pre-processing.
- **Memory Consumption and Runtime:** The study provided detailed analyses of memory requirements for global descriptors (e.g., 2KB for CosPlace 512, 192KB for AnyLoc 49152) and local features (e.g., 260KB for 1024 XFeat, 1032KB for 1024 RDD). Runtime analysis for a single query image showed varied performance depending on the chosen methods and settings (e.g., MegaLoc IR + ALIKED local features at 2048 resolution took approximately 3.3 seconds). This information guides the selection of methods based on system constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides several significant contributions and indicates substantial potential impacts on the fields of photogrammetry, robotics, and computer vision.

1. **Validation of Survey-Grade Visual Localization:** The study empirically demonstrates that visual localization can achieve median pose accuracies of 1–5 cm for translation and 0.05–0.1° for rotation in challenging outdoor street-level environments. Under favorable conditions, these accuracies can reach as low as 1 cm and 0.03°. These findings establish that visual localization can, in principle, meet or complement survey-grade GNSS positioning demands, particularly in areas where GNSS is degraded or denied.

2. **Introduction of a Novel Benchmarking Dataset:** The FHNW Muttenz dataset addresses a critical gap in the research landscape. Its large-scale (10 km street network), multi-epoch nature (1.5 years apart), diverse query camera types (consumer-grade devices), and, most importantly, sub-centimeter ground-truth pose accuracy for both reference and query imagery, enables a more detailed and rigorous evaluation of visual localization methods than previously possible. This fine-grained accuracy allows researchers to distinguish performance differences among methods that were considered comparable under coarser error thresholds. The public availability of this dataset is expected to foster future research and development in high-accuracy visual localization.

3. **Enhanced Understanding of Method Performance:** The systematic evaluation revealed that the choice of local feature representation and image resolution significantly influences localization accuracy. For instance, the optimal image resolution for a given feature detector is not universally "full resolution," and performance differences between seemingly comparable feature representations become apparent at sub-centimeter accuracy levels. This granular insight can guide the selection and development of more specialized and robust visual localization algorithms. The observation that simply adding older reference data degrades accuracy compared to using only the most recent data highlights the importance of effective map management strategies for long-term localization.

4. **Enabling Automated Georeferencing and 3D Data Acquisition:** The demonstrated accuracy potential suggests that visual localization can significantly reduce the manual effort currently required for georeferencing.
 - **Mobile Mapping Systems:** Visual localization can facilitate the automatic co-registration of successive mobile mapping campaigns, using previously mapped areas as a reference, thereby streamlining data acquisition and map updating processes.
 - **Consumer Devices for Surveying:** By providing accurate 6-DoF poses directly from imagery, visual localization could enable consumer-grade devices (e.g., smartphones with cameras or LiDAR) to perform survey-grade 3D geospatial data acquisition. This would eliminate the need for additional, specialized GNSS hardware or manual ground control points (GCPs), making professional-level data collection more accessible and user-friendly for a wider range of applications, such as construction monitoring, urban planning, and environmental mapping.

5. **Practical Design Considerations for Scalable Systems:** The detailed analysis of memory consumption and runtime provides practical guidance for designing visual localization systems that need to scale to large geographic areas. This enables researchers and developers to make informed choices about optimal image retrieval methods and local feature representations that balance accuracy with computational and storage constraints.

In conclusion, this work significantly advances the understanding of visual localization's capabilities, particularly its potential to deliver survey-grade accuracy in real-world outdoor environments. By providing a novel dataset and comprehensive evaluations, it lays foundational groundwork for the next generation of visual localization applications, fostering automation and accessibility in geospatial data acquisition and positioning.
