# Search-R1: Training LLMs to Reason and Leverage Search Engines with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel Reinforcement Learning framework trains Large Language Models to autonomously engage in interleaved reasoning and real-time search engine interactions, achieving average relative performance improvements of 24% with Qwen2.5-7B across diverse question-answering datasets.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with complex, multi-step reasoning and accessing up-to-date or external knowledge, leading to factual inaccuracies and hallucinations.
- Current Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approaches treat search as a static frontend, failing to train LLMs to optimally formulate queries or interact dynamically with retrieved information in a multi-turn context.
- Existing tool-use methods, whether prompting-based or fine-tuning based, face issues with generalization, reliance on costly human-annotated data, or the non-differentiability of search operations hindering end-to-end optimization.

## Method

- An RL framework integrates external search engines directly into the LLM's environment, enabling the model to learn to generate search queries and leverage retrieved results dynamically.
- ==Loss masking== is applied to retrieved tokens, preventing policy gradients from optimizing external content and ensuring stable training focused on the LLM's generative decisions.
- A straightforward, ==outcome-based Exact Match (EM) reward== function is used to drive the learning of complex, multi-turn search and reasoning processes.

## Results

- SEARCH-R1 consistently outperformed various RAG and fine-tuning baselines, achieving average relative improvements of **24%** with Qwen2.5-7B and **20%** with Qwen2.5-3B on **seven** diverse question-answering datasets.
- The method demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, with significant performance gains observed on both in-domain and out-of-domain datasets.
- Crucially, retrieved token loss masking was validated as essential for stable training and improved performance (e.g., an average EM of 0.431 vs. 0.343 for Qwen2.5-7B-base with and without masking, respectively).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex multi-turn search and reasoning behaviors can be effectively learned by Large Language Models using Reinforcement Learning with a simple, outcome-based reward function.
> - Applying loss masking to retrieved tokens is crucial for stable RL optimization when integrating non-differentiable search engine calls, preventing unintended optimization of external content.
> - LLMs trained with this framework learn to autonomously generate valid search queries, integrate retrieved information into their reasoning, and even perform self-verification steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) with external knowledge sources, particularly search engines, has become a critical area of research aimed at enhancing LLMs' reasoning capabilities, reducing hallucinations, and providing access to up-to-date information. SEARCH-R1 positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing paradigms and pushing the boundaries of autonomous LLM interaction with search tools through reinforcement learning.

Traditionally, approaches to integrate LLMs with search engines fall into two main categories:
1. **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** This widely adopted method (e.g., Lewis et al., 2020; Gao et al., 2023) involves retrieving relevant passages based on an initial query and concatenating them with the input prompt for the LLM. While effective, RAG often operates in a single-turn fashion, or in multi-turn variants (Trivedi et al., 2022a) that primarily rely on explicit prompting rather than an LLM *learning* to interact optimally with the search engine. This can lead to suboptimal retrieval strategies or the incorporation of irrelevant information.
2. **Tool-Use/Tool-Augmented LLMs:** Here, LLMs are designed to explicitly utilize external tools, including search engines. This can be achieved through:
 - **Prompting-based methods:** Approaches like IRCoT (Trivedi et al., 2022a) and ReAct (Yao et al., 2023) guide LLMs to iteratively reason and call search engines via sophisticated prompts. However, these methods often struggle with generalization, as the LLM's inherent capabilities to use tools may not have been fully optimized during pre-training.
 - **Fine-tuning-based methods:** Toolformer (Schick et al., 2023) exemplifies supervised fine-tuning to enhance tool usage. The major drawback here is the reliance on large-scale, high-quality annotated trajectories, which are expensive and difficult to obtain, coupled with the non-differentiability of search operations that hinders end-to-end gradient-based optimization.

Concurrently, **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLM reasoning** has emerged as a powerful paradigm (e.g., Ouyang et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2025; Hou et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2025). Models like DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) have shown that RL, even with simple outcome-based rewards, can significantly enhance LLMs' logical inference and problem-solving skills, including self-verification and self-correction. However, the application of RL to *search-and-reasoning* scenarios, where the LLM dynamically interacts with external search engines, has remained largely underexplored.

SEARCH-R1 innovates by marrying these two critical areas: RL for advanced reasoning and dynamic interaction with search engines. It extends the RL-for-reasoning paradigm (specifically drawing inspiration from DeepSeek-R1 Zero) to explicitly include the search engine as part of the RL environment. By training LLMs with RL to *autonomously generate search queries* and *leverage retrieved information* during step-by-step reasoning, SEARCH-R1 aims to overcome the "suboptimal interaction" issue of RAG and the "generalization/data scarcity" problems of tool-use methods. It represents a significant step towards creating more intelligent, self-reliant, and up-to-date LLM agents capable of complex information-seeking and problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SEARCH-R1 stems from the inherent limitations of current LLMs when tackling complex reasoning tasks that require up-to-date or external knowledge. While LLMs excel at language understanding and generation, they often struggle with:
- **Complex reasoning:** Despite advancements like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, LLMs can falter on intricate multi-step reasoning problems (Wei et al., 2022).
- **Accessing up-to-date and external information:** LLMs' knowledge is primarily confined to their pre-training data, making them susceptible to outdated facts or inability to answer questions requiring domain-specific external knowledge (Jin et al., 2024; Peng et al., 2023). This also leads to the phenomenon of "hallucinations" (Zhang et al., 2023).

Existing solutions, such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and tool-use approaches, have their own shortcomings:
- **RAG's Suboptimality:** While RAG introduces external knowledge, it often treats retrieval as a "frontend" step rather than an interactive process. The LLM is not *trained* to optimally formulate search queries or discern the relevance of retrieved passages in a multi-turn reasoning context, leading to potentially irrelevant retrievals or insufficient context (Jin et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2023).
- **Tool-Use Challenges:** Prompting-based tool use lacks generalization. Supervised fine-tuning for tool use demands vast amounts of high-quality, human-annotated interaction trajectories, which are labor-intensive and expensive to produce. Furthermore, the non-differentiable nature of search operations makes end-to-end gradient-based optimization difficult.

The key objectives of SEARCH-R1 are to address these challenges by:
1. **Developing a stable RL framework for search-augmented reasoning:** Integrate search engines into the LLM's reinforcement learning environment in a way that ensures stable optimization, particularly when incorporating retrieved context. This involves figuring out how to handle the "non-differentiability" of search within an RL loop.
2. **Enabling multi-turn interleaved reasoning and search:** Empower the LLM to dynamically generate multiple search queries, interpret retrieved results, and integrate them into an iterative reasoning process. The LLM should autonomously decide *when* to search and *what* to search for, rather than being passively fed information or rigidly prompted.
3. **Designing an effective and simple reward function:** Determine if a straightforward, outcome-based reward (e.g., exact match for question answering) is sufficient to guide the LLM to learn complex search and reasoning behaviors, avoiding the complexity and sensitivity of process-based or neural reward models.

By achieving these objectives, SEARCH-R1 aims to train LLMs to become more effective, autonomous, and robust reasoners that can intelligently leverage external search engines, thereby enhancing their performance on knowledge-intensive and complex question-answering tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEARCH-R1 introduces a novel Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework that enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to engage in interleaved reasoning and real-time search engine interactions. The core methodology revolves around formulating the search engine as part of the RL environment and optimizing the LLM's generative policy to make sequential decisions, including when and how to call the search engine.

#### 3.1. RL Objective with Search Engine Integration

The standard RL objective for LLMs is extended to explicitly include an external search engine `R`. The policy LLM `π_θ` generates output sequences `y` conditioned on the input `x` and the search engine `R` (denoted as `π_θ(·|x;R)`). The objective maximizes an outcome-based reward `r_ϕ(x, y)` while regularizing the policy to stay close to a reference policy `π_ref` using KL-divergence.

#### 3.2. Choice of RL Algorithms

SEARCH-R1 is compatible with established policy gradient RL methods, specifically:
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** A popular actor-critic algorithm that optimizes the policy by clipping the policy ratio to prevent large updates, ensuring stable training.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** An alternative that avoids the need for a separate critic model by using the average reward of multiple sampled outputs within a group as a baseline for advantage estimation.

#### 3.3. Loss Masking for Retrieved Tokens (Key Innovation)

A critical technical innovation is the introduction of **loss masking for retrieved tokens**. In SEARCH-R1, the LLM's generated output (`y`) includes both tokens generated by the LLM itself and tokens from retrieved passages (inserted by the search engine). Applying the policy gradient objective uniformly to all tokens could lead to unintended optimization of the *retrieved content*, which is static and external. To counteract this, loss masking ensures that the policy gradient is computed *only over LLM-generated tokens*, excluding retrieved passages. This stabilizes training and focuses optimization on the LLM's decision-making and generation capabilities.

#### 3.4. Generation with Multi-Turn Search Engine Calling

The LLM's interaction with the environment is iterative and multi-turn, guided by special tokens:
- **Search Call:** The LLM generates `<search> query </search>` to explicitly invoke the search engine.
- **Information Insertion:** The system extracts the `query`, performs a search, and inserts the top `k` retrieved results, enclosed within `<information> ... </information>` tokens, into the LLM's context for subsequent generation.
- **Reasoning Steps:** LLM's internal thought processes are encapsulated in `<think> ... </think>` tokens.
- **Final Answer:** The LLM indicates its final answer by generating `<answer> ... </answer>` tokens.
This iterative process continues until a maximum action budget is reached or a final answer is provided. The `Algorithm 1` details this structured rollout process.

#### 3.5. Training Template

A simple, general template guides the initial LLM behavior. It instructs the LLM to `think`, then optionally `search`, and finally `answer`. This structural format avoids content-specific biases, allowing the RL process to learn natural decision-making dynamics.

#### 3.6. Reward Modeling

SEARCH-R1 employs a **straightforward outcome-based reward function**, specifically Exact Match (EM) for factual question-answering tasks. `r_ϕ(x, y) = EM(a_pred, a_gold)` assigns a binary reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) based on the final extracted answer. This minimalistic reward design avoids the complexities and computational overhead of training neural reward models or designing intricate process-based rewards, testing the hypothesis that simple outcomes can drive complex learned behaviors.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

- **LLMs:** Qwen-2.5-3B, Qwen-2.5-7B (Base/Instruct variants), and Qwen-2.5-14B were used.
- **Retrieval:** 2018 Wikipedia dump as knowledge source, E5 as retriever.
- **Datasets:** Evaluated on seven QA datasets (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle), covering general and multi-hop QA.
- **Training:** Merged NQ and HotpotQA training sets.
- **Baselines:** Comprehensive comparison against inference-only (Direct, CoT), RAG-based (RAG, IRCoT, Search-o1), and fine-tuning (SFT, R1, Rejection Sampling) methods. A crucial aspect is fair comparison, using the same retriever, number of retrieved documents, corpus, training data, and pre-trained LLMs across baselines.
- **Hyperparameters:** Detailed PPO and GRPO settings (learning rates, batch sizes, sequence lengths, GAE parameters, KL regularization, etc.) are provided to ensure reproducibility.

This methodology combines established RL techniques with novel adaptations (retrieved token masking, multi-turn interaction protocol) to address the specific challenges of training LLMs to intelligently use external search engines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SEARCH-R1 demonstrates significant improvements in LLM reasoning augmented with search capabilities. The key findings and results are as follows:

#### 4.1. Superior Performance Over Baselines

- **Overall Improvement:** SEARCH-R1 consistently outperforms various RAG and fine-tuning baselines across seven diverse question-answering datasets. It achieves average relative improvements of **24% with Qwen2.5-7B** and **20% with Qwen2.5-3B** under the same experimental settings (e.g., same retriever, training data, pre-trained LLMs).
- **Generalization:** The performance gains hold for both in-domain datasets (NQ, HotpotQA) and out-of-domain datasets (TriviaQA, PopQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle), indicating strong generalization capabilities.
- **Comparison to R1 (RL without Search):** SEARCH-R1 surpasses `R1`, an RL-based training for LLM reasoning *without retrieval*. This confirms that providing access to relevant external knowledge via learned search significantly enhances overall reasoning performance.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness Across Model Types and Scales

- **Base vs. Instruct Models:** SEARCH-R1 is effective for both base and instruction-tuned models. While instruction-tuned LLMs converge faster and start with higher initial performance, RL successfully bridges the performance gap over time, enabling base models to achieve comparable final performance.
- **Model Scaling:** Larger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B vs. 3B, and later confirmed with 14B in Appendix C) show a greater "performance gap" improvement over the second-best baseline (RAG), indicating that larger LLMs are better at learning how to perform and leverage search.

#### 4.3. Insights into RL Optimization and Stability

- **PPO vs. GRPO:**
 - **Convergence Speed:** GRPO generally converges faster than PPO.
 - **Stability:** PPO demonstrates greater training stability, while GRPO can exhibit reward collapse after prolonged training.
 - **Final Performance:** Despite differences in speed and stability, both PPO and GRPO achieve comparable final training rewards and evaluation performance, making PPO the preferable choice due to its stability.
- **Retrieved Token Loss Masking (Crucial for Stability and Performance):** Experiments confirm that applying loss masking to retrieved tokens is vital. Training with masking consistently leads to greater LLM improvements and more stable optimization, significantly outperforming the variant without masking (e.g., an average EM of 0.431 vs. 0.343 for Qwen2.5-7B-base). This validates the importance of focusing policy gradients solely on LLM-generated tokens.

#### 4.4. Learning Dynamics

- **Response Length and Valid Search Study:**
 - **Early Stage:** Initial response length sharply decreases as the LLM prunes excessive filler words, with a slight increase in training reward.
 - **Later Stage:** Both response length and training reward significantly increase. The LLM learns to frequently call the search engine, leading to longer responses (due to inserted passages) and substantial performance improvement as it effectively leverages search results.
 - **Valid Search Calls:** As training progresses, the LLM learns to make more valid and effective search engine calls, correlating with improved performance.

#### 4.5. Impact of Number of Retrieved Passages (Top-k)

- **Trade-off:** An optimal `top-k` value is crucial. `top-k=1` likely suffers from low recall, while `top-k=5` can introduce noise or irrelevant information, degrading performance and potentially discouraging the model from using retrieved content.
- **Optimal `top-k=3`:** The default `top-k=3` was found to yield the best overall performance, balancing recall and precision.

#### 4.6. Case Studies (Appendix I, J)

- **Enhanced Problem Analysis:** SEARCH-R1 enables multi-step reasoning with multi-turn retrieval, leading to more informed and accurate responses compared to RL without search.
- **Self-Verification:** The model often performs additional retrieval steps to self-verify its conclusions, aligning with observations in pure reasoning RL and reinforcing confidence.
- **Adaptive Query Generation:** The LLM learns to write appropriate queries, including for auxiliary information not present in initial searches.
- **Failure Modes:** The model can sometimes fail to decompose complex problems or be misled by insufficient or irrelevant retrieved information, leading to incorrect answers. It also learns when to stop searching if external knowledge is not sufficient.

In essence, SEARCH-R1 demonstrates that by strategically applying reinforcement learning, LLMs can be trained to autonomously and effectively interact with search engines, leading to substantial gains in complex reasoning and knowledge-intensive tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SEARCH-R1 represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their interaction with external knowledge sources. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Bridging the Gap in LLM Capabilities

- **Enhanced Reasoning with Real-time Knowledge:** SEARCH-R1 directly addresses a core limitation of LLMs: their inability to consistently perform complex, multi-step reasoning, especially when up-to-date or external information is required. By autonomously integrating search, LLMs can transcend their parametric memory, reducing hallucinations and providing more accurate, factually grounded responses.
- **Autonomous Tool Use:** The framework moves beyond mere prompting or supervised fine-tuning for tool use. It demonstrates that LLMs can *learn* to decide when to use a search engine, what to query, and how to integrate the results into their reasoning process, making them more intelligent and self-reliant agents.

#### 5.2. Advancing Reinforcement Learning for LLMs

- **RL for Complex Behaviors with Simple Rewards:** The success of SEARCH-R1 with a minimalistic, outcome-based reward function (Exact Match) is highly significant. It indicates that complex, multi-turn reasoning and search behaviors can be effectively learned without the need for intricate process-based rewards or costly human preference data, which is a major bottleneck in RLHF. This opens avenues for more scalable and robust RL training.
- **Stable Integration of Non-differentiable Operations:** The introduction of retrieved token loss masking is a crucial technical contribution. It provides a practical solution for stably integrating non-differentiable operations (like a search engine call) into the gradient-based optimization loop of RL, a challenge frequently encountered when combining neural networks with external tools.

#### 5.3. Practical Implications and Applications

- **Improved Question Answering and Factuality:** For applications like chatbots, virtual assistants, and knowledge retrieval systems, SEARCH-R1 can lead to vastly improved accuracy and factuality, especially for questions requiring current events or domain-specific knowledge.
- **More Dynamic and Adaptive Agents:** LLMs trained with SEARCH-R1 can function as more dynamic agents, capable of adapting their information-seeking strategy based on the problem at hand, rather than relying on a fixed set of retrieved documents or pre-defined tool-use patterns.
- **Foundation for Advanced AI Systems:** This work lays a foundation for building more capable AI systems that can independently research, reason, and generate responses by intelligently querying and synthesizing information from the vastness of the internet.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The paper itself suggests several exciting avenues for future work, indicating the broader impact of this research:
- **Sophisticated Search Strategies:** Exploring more advanced search strategies, such as dynamic adjustment of retrieval parameters (e.g., `top-k` or query reformulation) based on uncertainty or reasoning progress.
- **Diverse Tool Integration:** Expanding the framework to combine search with other tools (e.g., calculators, code interpreters, databases) for even more complex problem-solving.
- **Multimodal Reasoning:** Applying SEARCH-R1 principles to multimodal tasks where reasoning requires integrating information from text, images, video, or other modalities, potentially by searching multimodal databases.
- **Complex Reward Mechanisms:** While simple rewards were effective, investigating if more nuanced process-based rewards could further enhance specific search or reasoning sub-skills without sacrificing stability.

In conclusion, SEARCH-R1 represents a significant step towards enabling LLMs to become truly intelligent agents that can autonomously navigate and leverage the external world of information. By marrying the power of reinforcement learning with dynamic search capabilities, it paves the way for a new generation of LLMs that are more accurate, adaptive, and knowledgeable.
