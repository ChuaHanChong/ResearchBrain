# Mean Flows for One-step Generative Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> MeanFlow, developed by researchers at CMU and MIT, introduces a generative modeling framework based on learning "average velocity" to enable efficient one-step image generation. The model achieves an FID of 3.43 with a single function evaluation on ImageNet 256x256, demonstrating improved performance over prior one-step methods and approaching the quality of multi-step diffusion models.

## Problem

- Existing generative models like diffusion and flow matching require many function evaluations (NFEs) for sampling, leading to slow and computationally expensive inference.
- Prior one-step generative models often rely on heuristic consistency constraints, pre-training/distillation from multi-step models, or complex discretization curricula, limiting their stability and generality.
- There was a need for a principled, standalone one-step generative model that could rival the quality of multi-step models without their computational burden.

## Method

- **MeanFlow** redefines the target for generative modeling from instantaneous velocity to "==average velocity==," which directly represents displacement over a finite time interval.
- It introduces the "MeanFlow Identity," a mathematically derived relationship between average and instantaneous velocities, enabling a principled training objective for a neural network to model average velocity.
- One-step sampling is achieved by directly applying the learned average velocity from noise to data (x = noise - u), requiring only a single function evaluation, and Classifier-Free Guidance is integrated directly into the training target.

## Results

- MeanFlow achieves a 1-NFE FID of 3.43 on ImageNet 256x256, outperforming prior one-step models by a 50-**70%** relative improvement.
- With 2 NFEs, MeanFlow reaches an FID of 2.20, becoming competitive with state-of-the-art multi-step diffusion models that typically require hundreds of NFEs.
- The model is trainable from scratch without distillation or curriculum learning, and its performance scales effectively with model size and training duration; Classifier-Free Guidance is seamlessly integrated, improving generation quality without incurring additional sampling steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling "average velocity" instead of instantaneous velocity provides a principled and effective theoretical foundation for one-step continuous-time generative models, moving beyond heuristic consistency constraints.
> - High-fidelity image generation is achievable with a single function evaluation (1-NFE) when models are trained with a properly defined average velocity objective, significantly closing the quality gap with multi-step models.
> - A direct, self-contained training approach for one-step models, free from distillation, pre-training, or complex curriculum learning, is feasible and leads to strong performance.
