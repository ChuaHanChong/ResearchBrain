# SynLogic: Synthesizing Verifiable Reasoning Data at Scale for Learning Logical Reasoning and Beyond

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel data synthesis framework, SynLogic, generates diverse and verifiable logical reasoning data at scale to improve large language model capabilities. Models trained with SynLogic demonstrate state-of-the-art logical reasoning, outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B by 6 percentage points on the BBEH benchmark, and exhibit strong generalization to mathematical and coding tasks.

## Problem

- A scarcity of diverse, high-quality, and verifiably correct general reasoning data exists for training large language models (LLMs) with Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- Current LLM reasoning advancements, particularly with RL, are concentrated on domain-specific tasks like mathematics and coding due to easily verifiable outcomes.
- Existing logical reasoning benchmarks primarily serve for evaluation and lack the scale or diversity needed for effective RL training.

## Method

- A ==data synthesis== framework, **SynLogic**, was developed to generate large-scale, diverse, and verifiable logical reasoning data across 35 tasks.
- Rule-based logic generators formalize task rules, ensuring correct instance generation and allowing for fine-grained control over difficulty.
- A dedicated, rule-based verifier for each task provides automatic correctness checks, enabling binary reward assignment for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

## Results

- SynLogic-**32B** achieved **25.5%** on BBEH, outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-**32B** by 6 percentage points, and reached **62.2%** on KOR-Bench, a 7-point improvement.
- Models demonstrated strong generalization to mathematical tasks, with SynLogic-**32B** achieving a **4.4x** improvement on AIME 2024 (from **4.5%** to **19.6%**).
- Mixed training with SynLogic data enhanced training efficiency in mathematical and coding domains and improved logical reasoning scores by nearly 10 points on KOR-Bench.
- Training on SynLogic data led to increased Chain-of-Thought lengths (up to ~4000 tokens for **32B** models) and the emergence of self-correction behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Logical reasoning is a fundamental building block for developing comprehensive, general reasoning skills in large language models.
> - Synthetic data can be generated at scale with precise control over difficulty, making it highly suitable for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).
> - Training on diverse logical data can improve training efficiency and cross-domain generalization in LLMs, complementing mathematical and coding reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, with models demonstrating remarkable capabilities across various tasks. A significant frontier in this domain is the enhancement of LLMs' reasoning abilities. Recent breakthroughs, such as OpenAI-o1 (Jaech et al., 2024) and DeepSeek R1 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025), have highlighted the profound potential of post-training methods, particularly Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), in imbuing LLMs with stronger reasoning skills.

However, the broader research landscape reveals a critical imbalance and a significant gap. While many open-source replication efforts inspired by these seminal works have emerged, they have predominantly concentrated on specific, well-defined domains like mathematics and coding. This concentration is largely due to the relative ease of designing straightforward, binary reward rules in these areas, making them amenable to RL training (e.g., Hu et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025; Zeng et al., 2025a,b; Zhang et al., 2025).

Conversely, the development of general reasoning capabilities in LLMs, which are essential for tackling a wider array of complex, real-world problems, remains underexplored. A primary impediment to this advancement is the scarcity of diverse, high-quality, and verifiably correct reasoning data suitable for RL training. Existing logical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., BBH, KOR-Bench, BBEH) are primarily designed for evaluation and often lack corresponding training data or offer limited task variety. Even prior work applying RL to logic tasks (Pan et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2025b) has typically focused on single tasks, thus failing to capture the breadth required for general reasoning.

"SynLogic" directly addresses this critical gap. The authors hypothesize that logical reasoning serves as a fundamental building block for developing comprehensive, general reasoning skills. By focusing on synthesizing diverse and verifiable logical reasoning data at scale, SynLogic positions itself as a pioneering effort to provide the necessary resources and methodology to push LLM reasoning beyond domain-specific mastery towards more generalized intelligence. This work aims to unlock the potential of RLVR for a broader spectrum of reasoning tasks, thereby complementing and expanding upon the current focus on mathematical and coding reasoning. Its open-source nature also contributes to fostering a more inclusive and collaborative research environment in this area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the general reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by addressing the critical gap in the availability of diverse, verifiable training data suitable for Reinforcement Learning (RL). The authors propose and validate a novel approach centered on synthesizing logical reasoning data at scale.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Bridging the General Reasoning Data Gap:** While RL has shown promise in enhancing LLM reasoning, particularly in mathematics and coding due to easily verifiable outcomes, methods and resources for developing *general* reasoning capabilities remain largely unexplored. This deficiency is rooted in the challenge of collecting diverse and verifiably correct reasoning data beyond these narrow domains. SynLogic aims to fill this void by providing a systematic way to generate such data for logical reasoning.

2. **Hypothesis on Logical Reasoning's Centrality:** The core hypothesis underpinning this research is that "logical reasoning is critical for developing general reasoning capabilities, as logic forms a fundamental building block of reasoning." This suggests that mastery of diverse logical tasks could serve as a powerful foundation for broader cognitive functions in LLMs.

3. **Scalability and Controllability of Synthetic Data:** The authors recognize the inherent advantages of synthetic data. Unlike human-curated datasets, synthetic data allows for unlimited generation, enabling large-scale training. Crucially, it also permits fine-grained control over difficulty levels and quantity, which is essential for developing progressive learning curricula and tailoring data to different model capacities. This controllability ensures that models are exposed to a balanced range of challenges, from easy to increasingly complex problems.

4. **Suitability for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** A key requirement for effective RL training is the ability to assign clear, binary rewards (correct/incorrect). The intrinsic nature of many logical tasks allows for straightforward rule-based verification. SynLogic leverages this characteristic by generating examples that can be "verified by simple rules," making them ideally suited for RLVR, where the model receives immediate and unambiguous feedback.

5. **Enhancing Training Efficiency and Generalization:** Beyond improving logical reasoning itself, the researchers are motivated to investigate if training on diverse logical data can offer complementary benefits. Specifically, they aim to explore whether mixing SynLogic data with existing mathematical and coding tasks can improve the training efficiency in these domains and significantly enhance the reasoning generalization of LLMs across different problem types.

In summary, the paper's main objectives are to present **SynLogic**, a data synthesis framework and dataset that: (1) generates diverse logical reasoning data at scale across 35 tasks, (2) offers adjustable difficulty and quantity, (3) ensures verifiability for RL, and (4) demonstrates the effectiveness of RL training on this data for state-of-the-art logical reasoning and improved generalization across other reasoning domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SynLogic project adopts a multi-faceted approach centered on the systematic synthesis of logical reasoning data and its subsequent application in reinforcement learning for LLMs.

#### 3.1. The Data Synthesis Framework (SynLogic)

The core of the methodology is the `SynLogic` data synthesis framework, designed to generate large-scale, diverse, and verifiable logical reasoning data. This framework (illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper) comprises six key components:

1. **Task Selection**: The authors meticulously curated a diverse set of 35 logical reasoning tasks. These tasks were drawn from two main sources: widely recognized puzzle problems from logic communities (e.g., Game of 24, Sudoku, Cryptarithms, Mathador, Minesweeper) and logical tasks found in established LLM evaluation benchmarks (e.g., BBH, BBEH). Importantly, for 33 of these 35 tasks, the authors independently developed their own data generation methods, as most existing benchmarks do not open-source their generation code. Only two tasks (Zebra Puzzle and ARC-AGI) directly adopted existing open-source data.

2. **Parameter Identification**: For each selected task, specific parameters were identified that directly control the problem's difficulty (e.g., grid size in Sudoku, number of missing values in Math Path). This step is crucial for enabling adjustable difficulty in the generated dataset.

3. **Logic Instance Generation**: The task-specific rules were manually formalized and implemented into rule-based logic generators. These generators produce valid instances of each logical problem, ensuring adherence to the task's constraints (e.g., unique digits in Sudoku rows/columns). This rule-based approach facilitates the efficient production of large quantities of data, and all generated instances undergo automated checks for correctness and solvability.

4. **Appropriate Difficulty Control**: A sophisticated dual-bound approach was employed to calibrate difficulty. An "upper bound" on difficulty was set by identifying the hardest parameters for which strong reasoning models (DeepSeek R1, OpenAI-o3-mini) could achieve a `pass@10` greater than zero. Similarly, a "lower bound" was determined by finding the easiest parameters where chat models achieved a pass rate between 0 and 0.5. This ensures the dataset contains a balanced range of samples that are challenging yet learnable. Two versions of the dataset were created: `SynLogic-Hard` (35 tasks, for 32B models) and `SynLogic-Easy` (27 tasks, for 7B models, excluding 8 tasks too difficult for smaller models).

5. **Prompt Formalization**: Abstract logic instances generated by the system were converted into natural language prompts using task-specific templates. This step makes the synthetic data accessible and usable for training and evaluating LLMs.

6. **Verification Suite**: For every task, a dedicated, rule-based verifier was implemented. These verifiers automatically check the correctness of model outputs, serving as the backbone for both training supervision (reward assignment) and automatic evaluation.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Training

The effectiveness of the SynLogic dataset was validated through extensive RL training experiments:

1. **Base Models**: Qwen2.5-7B-Base and Qwen2.5-32B-Base models (Yang et al., 2024) were used as foundation models.

2. **RL Algorithm**: The GRPO algorithm (Shao et al., 2024), adapted with techniques from DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), was employed for training.

3. **Reward Design**: A binary reward function was designed. A reward of `1` was assigned only if the model's response correctly followed the specified format (including `<think>` and `<answer>` tags) AND the final answer was correct according to the task-specific verifier. Otherwise, a reward of `0` was given. This binary design aligns perfectly with the verifiable nature of the synthetic logic tasks.

4. **Training Setup**: Approximately 16k `SynLogic-Easy` and 33k `SynLogic-Hard` instances were used for training. Training involved a prompt batch size of 128, 16 rollouts per prompt, and maximum rollout lengths of up to 28,672 tokens.

#### 3.3. Mixed Training Experiments

To explore broader generalization and training efficiency, the authors conducted mixed training experiments:

1. **Data Mixes**:
 - `Logic+Math`: Approximately 17k `SynLogic-Easy` samples combined with 17k mathematical samples from DAPO.
 - `Logic+Coding`: Approximately 9k `SynLogic-Easy` samples combined with 9k coding samples from platforms like Codeforces.
 - `Zero-Mix-3`: For 32B models, a large-scale mix of 35k mathematical, 9k coding, and 17k `SynLogic` samples.
 - `Zero-Mix-2`: (Ablation baseline) For 32B models, a mix of only mathematical and coding data, without `SynLogic`.

2. **Evaluation Protocol**: Evaluation was comprehensive, covering:
 - **Logical Reasoning**: `SynLogic-Val`, KOR-Bench, BBH, BBEH.
 - **Mathematical Reasoning**: MATH 500, AMC 2023, AIME 2024.
 - **Coding Reasoning**: A custom coding data validation split and LiveCodeBench.
 - **Out-of-domain Generalization**: GPQA-Diamond.
 - All evaluations were conducted in a zero-shot setting, with metrics like `avg@8`, `pass@1`, and `avg@4` depending on the benchmark.

This systematic and rigorous methodology, from data synthesis with precise difficulty control to multi-domain RL training and comprehensive evaluation, underscores the robustness of the SynLogic framework and its findings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using the SynLogic dataset and framework yielded several significant findings, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Substantial Improvements in Logical Reasoning

The RL training on the SynLogic dataset led to marked improvements in logical reasoning across various benchmarks:
- **SynLogic-7B**: Achieved 48.1% on KOR-Bench, a notable 9.5 absolute percentage point improvement over the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct baseline (38.6%).
- **SynLogic-32B**: Surpassed Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct by 7 percentage points on KOR-Bench, reaching 62.2%.
- **State-of-the-Art Open-Source Performance**: Most notably, the SynLogic-32B model outperformed DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B by 6 percentage points on the challenging BBEH benchmark (25.5% vs. 19.2%), establishing SynLogic as the foundation for state-of-the-art logical reasoning among open-source datasets. Substantial gains were also observed on the internal SynLogic validation splits.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Mathematical Domains

Despite being primarily trained on logical reasoning, SynLogic models demonstrated strong generalization to unseen mathematical tasks, suggesting a fundamental connection between logical and mathematical reasoning:
- **SynLogic-7B**: Achieved 10.0% on AIME 2024 (a nearly 10 absolute point increase from Qwen2.5-7B-Base's 0.3%), 71.8% on MATH 500 (7.2 point gain), and 55.0% on AMC 2023 (25 point increase).
- **SynLogic-32B**: Showed even more significant gains, reaching 19.6% on AIME 2024 (a 4.4x improvement over Qwen2.5-32B-Base's 4.5%), 82.0% on MATH 500 (13.4 point gain), and 57.5% on AMC 2023 (12.5 point gain).
These results are particularly compelling as the models approached or even surpassed instruction models on mathematical benchmarks without being explicitly trained on mathematics data.

#### 4.3. Increased Chain-of-Thought Length and Emergence of Reflection

Analysis of the training dynamics revealed a progressive increase in the average response length for both models:
- The 7B model generated responses averaging approximately 2500 tokens.
- The 32B model generated responses averaging around 4000 tokens.
Additionally, an increasing "reflection ratio" was observed throughout training, indicating the emergence of cognitive behaviors such as self-correction and rethinking (e.g., using phrases like "recheck," "rethink," "try again"). This suggests that the synthetic logical reasoning tasks inherently encourage and align with the "long-thinking" paradigm often seen in advanced LLMs.

#### 4.4. Scaling RL Training with Diverse Verifiable Reasoning Data (Mixed Training)

The study on mixing SynLogic data with other reasoning domains yielded crucial insights into training efficiency and generalization:

- **Mixed Training with Math (7B models)**: When SynLogic data was mixed with math data, the model achieved comparable mathematical performance to math-only training, but critically, it consumed *fewer* math training samples to reach that performance. Simultaneously, mixed training significantly improved logical reasoning scores (nearly 10 absolute points higher on KOR-Bench than math-only training). This indicates more efficient optimization through shared abstract reasoning mechanisms.

- **Mixed Training with Code (7B models)**: A similar trend was observed when mixing SynLogic data with coding data. Mixed-trained models achieved higher coding performance while consuming the same volume of coding data as coding-only training. Concurrently, KOR-Bench scores improved by 10 absolute points, reinforcing the complementary benefits of logical reasoning training.

- **32B Zero-RL Training with Diverse Reasoning Data (Zero-Mix-3)**: The large-scale mixed training on the Qwen2.5-32B-Base model, incorporating SynLogic, math, and coding data (Zero-Mix-3), demonstrated superior overall performance compared to a baseline mix without SynLogic (Zero-Mix-2) and strong competitive performance against state-of-the-art models:
 - **BBEH**: Zero-Mix-3 achieved 28.6%, significantly outperforming Zero-Mix-2 (18.5%) by over 10 points and surpassing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (19.2%).
 - **KOR-Bench**: Zero-Mix-3 reached 65.0%, a 6-point improvement over Zero-Mix-2 (58.6%) and nearly matching DeepSeek-R1-Distill (66.6%).
 - **LiveCodeBench**: Zero-Mix-3 matched DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B's performance.
 - **GPQA-Diamond (Out-of-Domain)**: Zero-Mix-3 outperformed DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, indicating significant generalization benefits to general knowledge-based reasoning.

These findings collectively highlight that SynLogic is not only effective for logical reasoning but also serves as a potent component for building more generally capable and efficiently trained LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SynLogic project makes several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact on the advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the broader field of artificial intelligence.

**1. Addressing a Critical Data Gap:** The most immediate and profound impact of SynLogic is its solution to the scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and verifiable training data for general reasoning in LLMs. By providing a scalable data synthesis framework and the resultant SynLogic dataset, the paper fills a crucial void that has historically hindered the development of more generalized reasoning capabilities beyond domain-specific areas like mathematics and coding. This resource democratizes access to the necessary data for researchers to explore and replicate advanced RL-based reasoning techniques.

**2. Advancing General Reasoning Capabilities:** The core hypothesis that logical reasoning is fundamental to general intelligence is strongly supported by the results. The significant improvements in logical reasoning benchmarks and, more importantly, the impressive generalization to unseen mathematical tasks demonstrate that targeted training on diverse logical puzzles directly enhances broader reasoning skills in LLMs. This suggests a pathway for building truly intelligent agents that can transfer learning across different cognitive domains.

**3. Enhancing Reinforcement Learning Efficiency and Effectiveness:** The findings regarding mixed training are particularly impactful. Demonstrating that incorporating SynLogic data can improve training efficiency (achieving similar performance with fewer domain-specific samples) and enhance cross-domain generalization (e.g., math with less math data, coding with less coding data) is a major breakthrough. This implies that logical reasoning acts as a "meta-skill" that facilitates the learning of other reasoning abilities. For large-scale LLM training, where computational resources are a major constraint, such efficiency gains are invaluable.

**4. Pushing State-of-the-Art in Open-Source LLMs:** By achieving state-of-the-art logical reasoning performance among open-source datasets and even surpassing models like DeepSeek-R1-Distill on key benchmarks (e.g., BBEH), SynLogic sets a new benchmark. Its open-source release of both the data synthesis pipeline and the dataset itself encourages rapid iteration and innovation within the research community, accelerating progress in LLM reasoning.

**5. Fostering Deeper Understanding of LLM Cognition:** The observed increases in Chain-of-Thought (COT) length and the emergence of "reflection behaviors" during training offer valuable insights into how LLMs develop more complex reasoning processes. This suggests that synthetic logical tasks inherently encourage the model to engage in more elaborate, iterative problem-solving, which is a hallmark of human-like reasoning. This line of inquiry could lead to better designs for prompts, architectures, and training methodologies that promote deeper cognitive behaviors in LLMs.

**6. Future Directions for AI Research:** SynLogic opens new avenues for exploring curriculum learning, where models are progressively exposed to increasingly difficult logical tasks. It also highlights the potential of synthetic data in other complex reasoning domains where human-annotated data is scarce or challenging to verify. This work could inspire a paradigm shift towards generating high-quality, controllable synthetic data as a primary training resource for advanced AI capabilities.

In conclusion, SynLogic is a pivotal contribution that not only provides a powerful new resource for LLM training but also offers fundamental insights into the nature of reasoning in artificial intelligence, promising to accelerate the development of more intelligent, versatile, and robust LLMs.
