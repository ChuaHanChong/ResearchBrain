# Do MLLMs Really Understand Space? A Mathematical Reasoning Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> Lu et al. introduce MathSpatial, a unified framework for evaluating and improving mathematical spatial reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This framework, comprising a benchmark, training corpus, and structured reasoning traces, reveals a significant gap between human (96.3% accuracy) and top MLLM performance (58.5% for GPT-5) in spatial tasks, while improving fine-tuned open-source models by up to 4.3% accuracy and reducing token usage by 25%.

## Problem

- MLLMs struggle with "mathematical spatial reasoning," showing a significant performance gap compared to human accuracy (MLLMs typically < 60% vs. human > 95%).
- Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks are confounded by visual perception complexities, obscuring the diagnosis of true reasoning failures.
- A lack of large-scale, high-quality training data specific to mathematical spatial reasoning hinders systematic MLLM improvement.
- Current MLLM approaches for spatial problem-solving are often black-box, lacking interpretability and transparent intermediate reasoning steps.

## Method

- Developed ==MathSpatial==, a unified framework comprising ==MathSpatial-Bench== (2K problems) for evaluation, MathSpatial-Corpus (8K problems) for training, and MathSpatial-SRT for structured reasoning.
- Constructed a large-scale dataset using a semi-automated pipeline that curates and verifies mathematical spatial problems from educational sources, ensuring geometric consistency and solution reliability.
- Introduced ==MathSpatial-SRT== (Structured Reasoning Traces) to decompose spatial problem-solving into three atomic, interpretable operations (Correlate, Constrain, Infer) for supervised fine-tuning of MLLMs.

## Results

- Humans achieved **96.3%** average accuracy on MathSpatial-Bench, while the best MLLM (GPT-5) reached only **58.5%**, confirming a significant performance deficit.
- Fine-tuning open-source MLLMs with MathSpatial-Corpus and SRTs improved accuracy by up to **4.3%** (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B to **22.1%**) and reduced token consumption by approximately **25%**.
- MathSpatial-trained models demonstrated consistent accuracy gains (**+**1.3%** to +3.4%**) and reduced token usage (**7.**1%**–21.3%**) when evaluated on external, out-of-distribution spatial benchmarks.
- Error analysis revealed that reasoning gaps (**34.4%**) and geometry violations (**33.0%**) were the primary causes of MLLM failures on mathematical spatial tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A profound gap exists between human and MLLM capabilities in mathematical spatial reasoning, underscoring a fundamental limitation of current MLLMs.
> - Disentangling spatial reasoning from perceptual noise is crucial for accurate diagnosis and targeted improvement of MLLMs' cognitive abilities.
> - Structured reasoning traces provide an effective method for enhancing both the accuracy and interpretability of MLLM spatial problem-solving, leading to more concise and efficient reasoning.
> - MLLM failures predominantly stem from core reasoning gaps and violations of geometric rules, highlighting challenges in multi-step logical deduction and consistency enforcement.
