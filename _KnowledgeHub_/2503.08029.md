# Elastic Motion Policy: An Adaptive Dynamical System for Robust and Efficient One-Shot Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Pennsylvania developed the Elastic Motion Policy (EMP), a one-shot imitation learning framework enabling robots to adapt full-pose motions in real-time to dynamic environments while ensuring stability. A new convex optimization for Lyapunov functions reduced computation time by nearly 50 times, and EMP achieved 8/10 success in out-of-distribution book placing and 9/10 in cube pouring tasks, exceeding baseline performance.

## Problem

- Traditional Behavior Cloning (BC) suffers from poor generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios and lacks formal guarantees of stability, crucial for safety.
- Acquiring large, diverse datasets for dynamic, human-centric environments is often impractical and resource-intensive.
- Prior stable Dynamical System (DS)-based policies lacked real-time adaptability to environmental changes, especially for full-pose (SE(3)) control, and incurred high computational overhead.

## Method

- ==Extends Laplacian== editing to the orientation space by projecting quaternions to a 3D tangent plane, enabling full-pose SE(3) adaptation.
- Introduces a convex optimization formulation for learning the P-matrix of the quadratic Lyapunov function, drastically reducing computation time for policy updates to approximately 30Hz.
- Integrates perception modules for autonomous extraction of object keyposes from visual information, serving as geometric constraints for real-time policy adaptation.

## Results

- The GMM-informed convex P-QLF optimization reduced Lyapunov function parameter computation time by nearly 50-fold compared to the baseline, enabling real-time policy updates.
- EMP achieved higher success rates in out-of-distribution (OOD) real-robot tasks: 8/10 for book placing (vs. 4/10 for baseline) and 9/10 for cube pouring (vs. 4/10 for baseline).
- EMP demonstrated robust generalization in multi-step pick-and-place tasks with a 7/10 OOD success rate, significantly outperforming the baseline's 1/10.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming orientation adaptation into Euclidean space via tangent plane projection allows for effective Laplacian editing, enabling full SE(3) pose morphing.
> - Formulating Lyapunov function learning as a convex optimization problem, particularly with GMM-informed averages, provides a substantial speedup for real-time stability guarantees.
> - Combining stable Dynamical Systems with elastic morphing guided by external perception (object keyposes) enables robust one-shot learning that adapts to scene changes while maintaining stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly focused on deploying autonomous systems in human-centric and dynamic environments such as homes, warehouses, hospitals, and factories. A critical challenge in this context is endowing robots with the ability to perform complex tasks by learning from human demonstrations, a paradigm known as Imitation Learning (IL). Behavior Cloning (BC) is a prevalent IL method due to its intuitive nature, allowing robots to learn directly from expert trajectories.

However, a fundamental limitation of traditional BC is its inherent generalization issue. Policies learned via BC often struggle when faced with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where environmental conditions or task parameters deviate from the training data. The conventional approach to mitigate this is to collect more data, but this is often impractical, especially in dynamic environments, and does not guarantee robust performance or recovery from unexpected physical interactions. Moreover, BC policies typically lack formal guarantees of convergence or stability, which are crucial for safety in human-robot interaction.

Existing research has explored several avenues to enhance the robustness and stability of imitation learning. One approach involves augmenting training datasets, for instance, through methods like DAgger or noise injection. While these methods can improve generalization, they often require an interactive expert during training or are data-inefficient and not suitable for online adaptation in real-time changing environments. Another direction, which this work aligns with, is to impose theoretical constraints on the learned policy. This includes methods that utilize Lyapunov stability theory to ensure policy convergence towards demonstrated behaviors or safe regions, as well as approaches leveraging diffeomorphic mappings to learn stable motions. While these methods demonstrate improved performance, they typically necessitate multiple demonstrations and additional training time.

One-shot imitation learning has emerged as a particularly challenging and relevant area, aiming to learn complex skills from very few (ideally one) demonstrations. Previous attempts in this domain have explored techniques such as self-attention, contrastive learning, invariant region matching, and interaction warping.

This paper positions itself within the stable Dynamical System (DS)-based motion policy paradigm, which inherently offers guaranteed stable and compliant robot behaviors. While prior DS works have demonstrated success in learning fixed, stable policies, they generally lack the ability to adapt to environmental changes without new demonstrations. The authors' prior work, Elastic-DS, began to address this by morphing DS policy parameters based on geometric constraints in Euclidean space, but was computationally intensive and lacked full-pose capabilities.

The present work, Elastic Motion Policy (EMP), extends this lineage by offering a solution that emphasizes data and time efficiency. It aims to develop a stable-guaranteed, full-pose motion policy learned from minimal demonstrations (1-2) that can adapt in real-time to new object configurations without requiring new data. By focusing on computational efficiency, real-time adaptability, and stable, converging behaviors, EMP seeks to bridge the gap between robust, stable control theory-based policies and the practical requirements of one-shot, adaptive imitation learning in complex, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the increasing deployment of robots in human-centric environments, which necessitates robot behaviors that are compliant, reactive, and capable of adapting to unexpected physical interactions and dynamic scene changes. Traditional Behavior Cloning (BC), while intuitive for specifying robot motion, suffers from significant drawbacks in these contexts:

- **Generalization Issues and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Performance:** BC policies often perform poorly when encountering states not present in the training data. This leads to unpredictable and potentially unsafe robot behavior in environments that are inherently variable.
- **Intractability of Data Collection:** Mitigating OOD problems typically involves collecting more data. However, acquiring large, diverse datasets for dynamic, human-centric scenarios is often impractical, time-consuming, and resource-intensive.
- **Lack of Adaptation to Environmental Changes:** Even with extensive data, learned motions can become obsolete if the environment changes significantly. Existing BC methods, including many Dynamical System (DS) based approaches, generally cannot adjust their policies online to new contexts without requiring fresh demonstrations.
- **Absence of Formal Guarantees:** Many BC policies lack formal guarantees of stability, convergence, or success, which are critical for safe deployment and reliable operation in close proximity to humans.
- **Limitations of Prior Work:** While DS-based motion policies offer stability and compliance, previous implementations, including the authors' own Elastic-DS, were limited to Euclidean space and incurred significant computational overhead (minutes for adaptation), hindering real-time application and full-pose (position and orientation) control.

Given these limitations, the central research question addressed by this paper is: **"How do we allow robots to learn stable, compliant, adaptive and reactive behaviors from very few demonstrations?"**

To address this question, the key objectives of the Elastic Motion Policy (EMP) framework are specifically outlined through its contributions:

1. **Extension of Motion Policy Adaptation to Orientation Space:** To enable full-pose adaptation, the work aims to extend the Laplacian editing approach, previously applied only in Euclidean space, to the orientation space. This involves performing Laplacian editing on the quaternion manifold tangent plane, allowing for the elastic transformation of rotational components of a motion policy.
2. **Development of Real-Time Convex Optimization for Full Pose SE(3) LPV-DS Learning:** The objective is to formulate a convex optimization problem for learning the parameters of the full pose SE(3) LPV-DS, specifically for the P-matrix of the quadratic Lyapunov function. This aims to overcome the computational bottleneck of prior non-convex formulations, enabling policy updates at approximately 30Hz, thus facilitating online adaptation even when task-relevant objects are moving.
3. **Design of a Pipeline for Object Keypose Extraction from Visual Information:** To make the adaptation process autonomous and context-aware, an objective is to integrate perception modules (e.g., language models, segmentation, pose estimation) to automatically extract object keyposes from visual data. These keyposes serve as geometric constraints for the EMP adaptation mechanism.
4. **Demonstration of Framework Flexibility and Capabilities:** The research aims to showcase EMP's versatility by demonstrating its ability to:
 - Achieve real-time adaptation of motion policies to dynamic environment changes.
 - Integrate obstacle avoidance capabilities.
 - Learn and execute multi-step tasks from decomposed demonstrations.

Overall, the motivation is to develop a robust, efficient, and adaptive one-shot imitation learning framework that can operate safely and reliably in complex, dynamic, human-centric environments, requiring minimal human intervention and data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Elastic Motion Policy (EMP) framework is designed as a one-shot imitation learning system that enables robots to adapt their behavior in real-time based on environmental changes while maintaining task specifications. It builds upon the Dynamical System (DS) paradigm, where robot motion is governed by first-order differential equations with inherent stability guarantees.

**A. SE(3) Linear Parameter Varying Dynamical System (LPV-DS) Backbone**
EMP leverages the SE(3) LPV-DS framework for learning full end-effector pose motions, approximating R³ × SO(3) space.

1. **LPV-DS Formulation (Translational Motion):** Position trajectories (x, ẋ ∈ R^m^) are encoded as a mixture of continuous linear time-invariant (LTI) systems (Eq. 1). The mixing functions γ_k(x) are derived from Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) parameters (Θ_γ = {π_k, μ_k, Σ_k}), fitted to the reference trajectory. Each LTI system A_k is learned by solving a semi-definite program (SDP) (Eq. 3), minimizing Mean Square Error (MSE) while enforcing Globally Asymptotic Stability (GAS) using a parameterized quadratic Lyapunov function (P-QLF) V(x) = (x - x*)ᵀP(x - x*) (Eq. 2).

2. **Quaternion Dynamical System (Rotational Motion):** Rotational motion is formulated on the tangent plane of the quaternion space. It generates the next desired orientation (q̂_att)des given the current quaternion q and attractor q_att (Eq. 4). The Riemannian logarithmic map quantifies orientation deviation. Mixing functions γ_k(q) are based on GMM parameters (Θ_γ = {π_k, μ̃_k, Σ̃_k}) fitted to the quaternion trajectory projected onto the tangent space. LTI systems A_k are learned via an SDP (Eq. 5), minimizing MSE on the tangent plane while ensuring GAS.

3. **Stable SE3-LPVDS Policy:** The combination of LPV-DS for position and Quaternion-DS for orientation yields a full-pose motion policy (Eq. 6) with stability guarantees. Angular velocity ω is computed via parallel transport and Riemannian exponential map after obtaining the desired quaternion.

**B. SE3 LPV-DS Policy Morphing (Elastic Adaptation)**
The core of EMP's adaptation lies in morphing the learned SE3-LPVDS parameters based on spatial changes of relevant objects.

1. **Euclidean Space Morphing:** This extends the Elastic-DS approach. Geometric constraints are applied to the GMM parameters (Θ_γ). The method defines "joints" (β_i,k,k+1, Eq. 7, 8) as approximate midpoints between neighboring Gaussians. Laplacian editing (Eq. 9) is then used to adjust these joints, preserving local geometric relationships while conforming to homogeneous transformation constraints (O_start, O_end) related to objects at the demonstration endpoints.

2. **Orientation Space Morphing (Novel Contribution):** To adapt orientation, quaternion GMM means (μ̃_k) and covariances (Σ̃_k) are first projected to a 3D Euclidean space. This involves defining a 3D basis (Λ_q_att, Eq. 11) centered at the attractor's null space (Eq. 10) to transform 4D mean vectors into 3D coordinates (μ̂_k, Eq. 12). Similarly, covariances are transformed using bases derived from the null space of each quaternion mean (Λ_μ_k, Eq. 13, 14). Laplacian editing (Eq. 9) is then performed on these 3D representations. Finally, the transformed 3D means and covariances are recovered back to the quaternion space (Eq. 15, 16) to yield the new set of Gaussians ({μ̃*_k, Σ̃*_k}). This allows full-pose trajectory updates as illustrated in Fig. 3.

**C. Real-Time Convex Lyapunov Function Optimization (Novel Contribution)**
To enable real-time adaptation, the paper proposes a convex optimization for estimating the P-matrix of the P-QLF, replacing the previously non-convex formulation.

1. **Convex Formulation:** The objective is to estimate P by minimizing the number of training data points that violate the Lyapunov stability condition (V̇(x) < 0, Eq. 17). This is formulated as a convex optimization problem (Eq. 18) using the ReLU function to penalize violations, with a positive semi-definite constraint (P ⪰ εI).

2. **GMM-Informed Acceleration:** To further enhance speed, the method can use the average position and velocity of each Gaussian (from Θ_γ) instead of individual data points in Eq. 18. This significantly reduces computation, enabling policy updates in real-time (Fig. 4).

**D. EMP Imitation Learning Pipeline**
The overall EMP system operates through a sequential pipeline (Fig. 5):

1. **Data Processing:**
 - **Demonstration Data Collection:** Human demonstrations are collected using a customized UMI gripper, recorded with an external RGBD camera, and AprilTags are used for robust pose tracking of the gripper and a pseudo-robot base. A microcontroller records gripper contact states.
 - **Keypose/Attractor Extraction:** For goal-oriented tasks, a final desired pose (keypose) is extracted. This involves using a language model (e.g., GPT-4o) for semantic labeling of relevant objects from video frames. Grounded SAM then generates object masks, and FoundationPose determines the object's 6D pose (O_obj). The last end-effector pose from the demonstration, expressed in the object's frame, is saved as the keypose (O_key,obj) which serves as a geometric constraint for adaptation.
 - **Multi-Step Trajectory Decomposition:** For long-horizon tasks, the Universal Visual Decomposer (UVD) is used to automatically segment the demonstration video into goal-conditioned subtasks. Each segment is then processed individually to learn its own goal-oriented motion policy.

2. **Runtime Policy Inference:**
 - **Nominal Policy Training:** The nominal SE3-LPVDS policy is trained from the processed single demonstration.
 - **Online Adaptation:** During execution, object poses (O_obj) are continuously tracked. The keypose (O_key,r) is used as a geometric constraint in the Laplacian editing (Eq. 9) to update the EMP policy online, typically at 30Hz.
 - **Control Execution:** The adapted desired linear (ẋ) and angular (ω) velocities are fed to a passive impedance controller to generate joint torques.
 - **Multi-Step Task Execution:** For multi-step tasks, reaching the attractor of the current subtask triggers the activation of the next subtask's policy via a one-hot activation function δ(ξ). Gripper state changes (e.g., grasp, release) occur only upon subtask completion.

**E. Obstacle Avoidance Integration:**
EMP's framework is compatible with existing modulation approaches for obstacle avoidance. By incorporating a modulation matrix M, the learned velocity field (f(x)) can be adjusted to generate a new velocity (ẋ_new = M(x)f(x)) that circumvents obstacles while respecting their boundaries.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the Elastic Motion Policy (EMP) focused on two primary areas: benchmarking the performance of the proposed convex P-QLF formulation and assessing the efficacy of elastic adaptation in real-world robotic tasks.

**A. Convex Lyapunov Function Evaluation**
The proposed convex optimization approach for P-QLF learning was evaluated on the LASA 2D handwriting datasets, comprising 30 motions. The performance was compared against the baseline P-QLF formulation using two metrics: computation time and percentage of Lyapunov stability violations (Eq. 17).

- **Computation Time:** Both variants of the proposed method—"Convex P-QLF" (using individual data points) and "GMM P-QLF" (using average position/velocity per Gaussian)—demonstrated significant reductions in computation time compared to the baseline. For single trajectories, the Convex P-QLF reduced time from 0.332s (baseline) to 0.038s, and the GMM P-QLF further decreased it to 0.007s. When evaluated on all 7 trajectories per motion, the GMM P-QLF achieved a nearly 50-fold speed improvement (0.09s vs. 2.62s for baseline). This demonstrates the practical feasibility of real-time policy updates.
- **Violation Percentage:** The Convex P-QLF variant showed a slight improvement in stability, with a 11.1% violation rate compared to the baseline's 14.0% for single trajectories. The GMM P-QLF, while significantly faster, exhibited a slightly higher violation rate of 15.1% for single trajectories and 15.4% for all trajectories, which is still comparable to the baseline. This indicates that the speedup gained by using GMM-informed averages comes with a minor trade-off in the strict adherence to individual data point stability, yet overall performance remains competitive.

**B. Real Robot Tasks Evaluation**
EMP's elastic adaptation was tested on a Franka Research 3 robot arm for three goal-oriented tasks: Book Placing, Cube Pouring, and a Multi-Step Pick-and-Place task. Each task was learned from a single demonstration, and success rates were measured over 10 runs in both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. The object-centric SE(3)-LPVDS served as the baseline.

1. **Book Placing:**
 - **ID Scenarios:** Both the SE(3)-LPVDS baseline and EMP achieved a 10/10 success rate.
 - **OOD Scenarios:** EMP demonstrated superior robustness with an 8/10 success rate, whereas the baseline's success dropped to 4/10. The paper attributes this to EMP's ability to enforce task constraints through Laplacian editing, ensuring compliance with the updated policy even when bookrack configurations changed randomly. The baseline, relying solely on global transformations, could easily generate motions that violated task constraints (e.g., collisions with the bookrack).

2. **Cube Pouring:**
 - **ID Scenarios:** Both methods achieved a perfect 10/10 success rate.
 - **OOD Scenarios:** EMP maintained high performance with a 9/10 success rate, significantly outperforming the baseline which achieved 4/10. The task involved an arch-shaped trajectory with wrist rotation. The baseline failed when the robot started from an unfamiliar side of the pot, generating undefined or undesirable motions (e.g., large circular motions, moving towards the table). EMP's elastic adaptation preserved the essential characteristics of the demonstration, ensuring valid motion despite varied pot poses. The single failure for EMP was attributed to the adapted path becoming physically infeasible for the robot (e.g., exceeding reachability or joint limits).

3. **Multi-Step Pick-and-Place Task:**
 - This task involved decomposing a long-horizon demonstration into sequential goal-oriented subtasks using UVD.
 - **ID Scenarios:** Baseline achieved 8/10, and EMP achieved 7/10.
 - **OOD Scenarios:** EMP maintained a 7/10 success rate, showing robust generalization. The baseline, however, experienced a drastic drop to 1/10.
 - The overall lower success rates for both methods in this task were attributed to inaccuracies in segmentation (from UVD) and grasping failures. Misplaced keyposes due to early segmentation cuts could lead to the robot converging to incorrect attractors. Despite these challenges, EMP consistently outperformed the baseline in OOD multi-step scenarios.

**C. Case Study: Book Placing with Obstacle Avoidance**
A qualitative case study demonstrated EMP's compatibility with real-time obstacle avoidance. In the Book Placing task, a mustard bottle was introduced as an obstacle. By adopting a modulation approach, EMP successfully generated a new path that circumvented the obstacle (a spherical model of the bottle) while still completing the book placement task (Fig. 9). This illustrates EMP's flexibility for safe execution in dynamic environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Elastic Motion Policy (EMP) framework represents a notable advancement in the field of imitation learning for robotics, particularly in addressing the critical challenges of generalization, adaptability, and safety in dynamic, human-centric environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations of Behavior Cloning:** EMP directly tackles the inherent generalization issues and poor out-of-distribution (OOD) performance of traditional Behavior Cloning (BC) paradigms. By enabling adaptive behavior from a single demonstration, it mitigates the need for extensive data collection, which is often intractable in complex, real-world settings.
- **Real-time Adaptation and Robustness:** The core contribution of EMP lies in its ability to adapt learned motion policies to novel environmental configurations in real-time (approximately 30Hz). This is achieved through novel contributions like extending Laplacian editing to full SE(3) pose (including orientation) and a computationally efficient convex optimization for Lyapunov function parameters. This real-time capability is crucial for robot deployment in dynamic environments where objects or constraints may change during execution.
- **Guaranteed Stability and Compliance:** By building upon the Dynamical System (DS) paradigm and leveraging Lyapunov stability theory, EMP ensures that learned policies maintain global asymptotic stability. This inherent stability provides formal guarantees for safe and predictable robot behavior, which is paramount for physical interaction with humans and operating in uncertain environments. The compliance derived from DS further enhances safety during physical interactions.
- **Data and Time Efficiency:** EMP is designed as a one-shot imitation learning framework, significantly reducing the data requirements typically associated with robust policy learning. Coupled with its real-time adaptation speed, it offers high time efficiency, enabling rapid deployment and re-tasking of robots without lengthy re-training or additional demonstrations.
- **Framework Flexibility and Integration:** The research demonstrates EMP's extensibility and compatibility with other crucial robotics capabilities. Its integration with advanced perception modules (e.g., language models, Grounded SAM, FoundationPose) for semantic keypose extraction, Universal Visual Decomposer (UVD) for multi-step task decomposition, and modulation-based obstacle avoidance showcases its potential as a comprehensive and versatile framework for complex manipulation tasks.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** By providing stable, compliant, and adaptive robot behaviors, EMP contributes to creating robots that are safer, more predictable, and more intuitively usable in environments shared with humans. The ability to adapt to unforeseen physical interactions or scene changes without compromising stability is a key step towards seamless human-robot collaboration.

In summary, EMP offers a principled approach to create adaptive, robust, and efficient robot motion policies from minimal data. Its focus on real-time full-pose adaptation with stability guarantees positions it as a significant step towards deploying more autonomous and versatile robots in dynamic, unstructured, and human-centric environments. The identified limitations, such as reliance on accurate object tracking and the potential for task-space infeasibilities, also suggest clear avenues for future research, which could further enrich the EMP framework and expand its applicability.
