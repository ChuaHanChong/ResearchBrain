# Elastic Motion Policy: An Adaptive Dynamical System for Robust and Efficient One-Shot Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Pennsylvania developed the Elastic Motion Policy (EMP), a one-shot imitation learning framework enabling robots to adapt full-pose motions in real-time to dynamic environments while ensuring stability. A new convex optimization for Lyapunov functions reduced computation time by nearly 50 times, and EMP achieved 8/10 success in out-of-distribution book placing and 9/10 in cube pouring tasks, exceeding baseline performance.

## Problem

- Traditional Behavior Cloning (BC) suffers from poor generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios and lacks formal guarantees of stability, crucial for safety.
- Acquiring large, diverse datasets for dynamic, human-centric environments is often impractical and resource-intensive.
- Prior stable Dynamical System (DS)-based policies lacked real-time adaptability to environmental changes, especially for full-pose (SE(3)) control, and incurred high computational overhead.

## Method

- ==Extends Laplacian== editing to the orientation space by projecting quaternions to a 3D tangent plane, enabling full-pose SE(3) adaptation.
- Introduces a convex optimization formulation for learning the P-matrix of the quadratic Lyapunov function, drastically reducing computation time for policy updates to approximately 30Hz.
- Integrates perception modules for autonomous extraction of object keyposes from visual information, serving as geometric constraints for real-time policy adaptation.

## Results

- The GMM-informed convex P-QLF optimization reduced Lyapunov function parameter computation time by nearly 50-fold compared to the baseline, enabling real-time policy updates.
- EMP achieved higher success rates in out-of-distribution (OOD) real-robot tasks: 8/10 for book placing (vs. 4/10 for baseline) and 9/10 for cube pouring (vs. 4/10 for baseline).
- EMP demonstrated robust generalization in multi-step pick-and-place tasks with a 7/10 OOD success rate, significantly outperforming the baseline's 1/10.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming orientation adaptation into Euclidean space via tangent plane projection allows for effective Laplacian editing, enabling full SE(3) pose morphing.
> - Formulating Lyapunov function learning as a convex optimization problem, particularly with GMM-informed averages, provides a substantial speedup for real-time stability guarantees.
> - Combining stable Dynamical Systems with elastic morphing guided by external perception (object keyposes) enables robust one-shot learning that adapts to scene changes while maintaining stability.
