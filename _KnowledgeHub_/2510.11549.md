# ODI-Bench: Can MLLMs Understand Immersive Omnidirectional Environments?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI Laboratory introduce ODI-BENCH, a rigorous benchmark for evaluating multi-modal large language models' understanding of 360-degree omnidirectional images. They also present Omni-CoT, a training-free framework that substantially enhances MLLMs' capabilities, particularly in spatial reasoning, within these immersive environments.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) demonstrate advanced capabilities in 2D image understanding but their performance in comprehending immersive 360-degree omnidirectional environments remains largely unassessed.
- Existing benchmarks for omnidirectional images (ODIs) suffer from low resolution, limited scene diversity, restricted question domains, and insufficient evaluation of complex spatial reasoning or varied viewpoints.
- Current MLLMs generally fail to effectively process the unique challenges of ODIs, such as inherent distortions from equirectangular projection and the need for higher-level 3D spatial reasoning.

## Method

- The team developed ==ODI-BENCH==, a new benchmark featuring **2,000** high-resolution, real-world omnidirectional images with over **4,000** human-annotated question-answer pairs across 10 fine-grained general and spatial understanding tasks.
- ODI-BENCH incorporates ==dual evaluation settings== (close-ended and open-ended) and questions are designed to mimic immersive first-person perspectives, using a combination of automated pipelines with human refinement and expert manual annotation.
- A training-free framework called ==Omni-CoT== is introduced, which employs viewpoint-guided textual captions, relevant cropped visual cues with refinement, and a final response refinement step to enhance MLLM comprehension of ODIs.

## Results

- State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit unsatisfactory performance on ODI-BENCH, with the top proprietary model achieving only **62.62%** accuracy in close-ended evaluation, reflecting a significant struggle with omnidirectional environments.
- MLLMs demonstrated particularly poor performance on spatial-level understanding tasks, often scoring only marginally above or below the "Blind GPT-4o" baseline for tasks like allocentric view orientation and scene simulation.
- The Omni-CoT framework consistently enhanced MLLM performance across various models, with viewpoint guiding proving to be a critical component for substantial gains, especially in spatial-related tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs primarily perceive omnidirectional images as warped 2D visual inputs rather than integrated 360-degree immersive environments, leading to poor spatial reasoning.
> - Spatial-level understanding tasks (e.g., view orientation, scene simulation) present a significantly greater challenge for MLLMs compared to general-level tasks (e.g., object attributes).
> - Evaluation in both close-ended and open-ended formats is crucial, as multiple-choice options can sometimes provide hints or introduce "option anchoring bias," affecting comprehensive assessment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated advancements in understanding conventional 2D images and videos. However, their capabilities in comprehending omnidirectional images (ODIs), which offer a full 360° × 180° field of view, remain largely unevaluated. ODIs are increasingly utilized in applications such as virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), spatial navigation, and embodied intelligence. Unlike traditional images that present a limited field of view, ODIs capture extensive visual information and necessitate higher-level spatial reasoning to interpret immersive environments.

Existing benchmarks for ODI understanding present several limitations. These include low image resolutions that restrict real-world applicability (e.g., VQA 360°), limited scene diversity often confined to indoor or synthetic environments (e.g., OSR-Bench), constrained question domains due to automated annotation pipelines leading to narrow or simplistic question types (e.g., Dense360-Bench), and viewpoint limitations, primarily focusing on egocentric perspectives while neglecting allocentric viewpoints and simulated user interactions. These deficiencies prevent a comprehensive evaluation of MLLMs' ability to process the unique characteristics of ODIs.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing ODI-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate both general-level and spatial-level understanding in high-resolution, diverse omnidirectional scenes. It also proposes Omni-CoT, a chain-of-thought reasoning framework, to enhance MLLM performance in this domain. This research aims to provide a standardized tool for assessing and improving MLLMs' capabilities in immersive environments, contributing to the development of more sophisticated vision-language-action (VLA) models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

1. **To develop a comprehensive benchmark for omnidirectional image understanding:** The authors aim to create ODI-Bench, a novel benchmark specifically tailored to evaluate MLLMs' ability to comprehend immersive environments captured by ODIs. This involves curating a dataset of high-quality ODIs and developing a diverse set of question-answering (QA) pairs across various tasks.
2. **To systematically evaluate leading MLLMs on ODI understanding:** The research intends to conduct an extensive study benchmarking a range of proprietary and open-source MLLMs on ODI-Bench under both close-ended and open-ended evaluation settings. This evaluation seeks to identify the current capabilities and limitations of these models in processing omnidirectional visual information and performing spatial reasoning within immersive contexts.
3. **To propose an effective method for enhancing MLLMs' ODI comprehension:** Recognizing that current MLLMs may struggle with ODI understanding, the authors aim to introduce a training-free framework, Omni-CoT, which leverages a human-like step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning strategy to improve model performance on omnidirectional images.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several factors:

- **Growing importance of ODIs:** ODIs are increasingly vital in applications such as VR, AR, spatial navigation, and embodied intelligence, necessitating MLLMs capable of understanding these rich, immersive environments.
- **Insufficiency of existing benchmarks:** Current benchmarks for MLLMs primarily focus on 2D images or narrow-field-of-view videos, or are limited in resolution, scene diversity, question complexity, or viewpoint perspectives when it comes to ODIs. This creates a need for a more rigorous and comprehensive evaluation framework.
- **Unique challenges of ODI understanding:** ODIs present distinct challenges due to their full 360° field of view, the massive amount of visual information, projection distortions (e.g., in equirectangular projection, ERP), and the requirement for high-level spatial reasoning from multiple viewpoints (egocentric and allocentric).
- **Bridging the gap towards embodied intelligence:** A robust understanding of immersive environments is crucial for developing embodied AI agents that can perceive, reason, and act effectively in the real world. Evaluating and improving MLLMs' ODI comprehension is a step towards this goal.
- **Identifying MLLM limitations:** The authors are motivated to reveal the specific areas where current MLLMs underperform in ODI understanding, such as spatial reasoning and handling distorted projections, to guide future research and development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces ODI-Bench for MLLM evaluation and Omni-CoT to enhance MLLM performance.

#### 3.1. ODI-Bench Construction

ODI-Bench is designed as a comprehensive benchmark for omnidirectional image understanding, comprising 2,000 high-quality real-world ODIs and over 4,000 manually annotated question-answering (QA) pairs.

- **Image Collection:** Images were primarily web-crawled from Flickr and selected for quality and diversity. Unlike previous benchmarks often restricted to indoor or synthetic scenes, ODI-Bench includes diverse indoor and outdoor environments, ranging from human activities to natural landscapes. The images maintain high resolutions, with a significant portion above 4K, addressing the low-resolution issue of prior benchmarks.
- **Fine-Grained Task Definition:** The benchmark includes 10 distinct tasks categorized into general-level and spatial-level understanding:
 - **General-level ODI understanding (5 tasks):** These tasks evaluate basic comprehension despite the visual information volume and projection distortions.
 - **Object Attribute:** Localizing instances and extracting attributes (color, shape, material, function).
 - **Human Attribute:** Understanding human actions, poses, and emotions.
 - **Counting:** Counting instances within a full immersive view, challenging due to warped viewpoints and dense information.
 - **Existence:** Verifying the presence or absence of objects in complex, high-resolution scenes.
 - **OCR:** Extracting textual information under distorted perspectives and high-resolution conditions, including cross-view scenarios.
 - **Spatial-level ODI understanding (5 tasks):** These tasks assess MLLMs' ability to handle the unique spatial characteristics of ODIs.
 - **Egocentric View Orientation:** Specifying object orientation (front, back, left, right, up, down) from the viewer's perspective.
 - **Allocentric View Orientation:** Determining spatial orientation of instances relative to another person or agent within the image (third-person perspective).
 - **Scene Simulation:** Reasoning about object locations from a designated agent's virtual perspective.
 - **Relative Direction:** Understanding spatial relationships from an egocentric viewpoint, complicated by ERP distortions and occlusions.
 - **ODI Reasoning:** Interpreting ERP-related spatial properties, inferring motion trajectories, spatial relationships, and anticipating actions within the immersive environment.
- **Question-Answering Annotation:**
 - **Immersive Question Design:** Questions use first-person phrasing (e.g., "What is [A] on my right doing?") to align with immersive ODI viewing and evaluate interactive understanding.
 - **Instance-level QA (Object Attribute, Human Attribute):** An automatic pipeline generates QA pairs. ERP images are cubemap-projected into 6 views. GroundedSAM segments instances, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B generates captions. GPT-4o generates QA pairs from captions, which are then manually refined by human experts to ensure unique reference and answer accuracy.
 - **Complex Tasks (Counting, View-Orientation, Relative Direction, etc.):** Manual annotation by three expert participants in VR environments ensures precision. Annotations were cross-checked for accuracy.
 - **Close-ended Option Generation:** GPT-4o generates three distractor options per QA pair, which are then manually assessed for plausibility and to avoid semantic overlap. Options are randomly shuffled to mitigate bias.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Setup

- **Models Benchmarked:** 20 leading MLLMs, including proprietary models (GPT-4o, o3, Gemini-2.0-flash, Qwen-VL-Plus) and open-source models (LLaVA series, idefics3, XComposer2, Deepseek-VL, glm-4v, MiniCPM-V, InternVL series, Intern-s1), were evaluated.
- **Evaluation Settings:** All models were evaluated across all tasks using both close-ended (multiple-choice or yes/no) and open-ended formats for comprehensive assessment.
- **Metrics:** For close-ended evaluation, accuracy was measured. For open-ended evaluation, an LLM-based evaluator (using GPT-4o) was employed to score responses between 0 and 1, with specific criteria for different task types (e.g., partial scores for OCR or directional deviations).

#### 3.3. Omni-CoT Framework

Omni-CoT is a training-free chain-of-thought framework designed to improve MLLMs' ODI comprehension through step-by-step reasoning.

- **Viewpoint-Guided Answering:** Instead of feeding raw multi-view images (which are high-resolution and computationally intensive), the framework generates textual captions for six extracted perspective views (top, bottom, right, left, front, back). These captions, along with orientation information, are used as compact textual prompts to guide the MLLM to understand the immersive environment.
- **Crop Cue Grounding and Refinement:** The MLLM is prompted to identify relevant bounding boxes in the ODI related to the question. Narrow-FoV crops are extracted from these boxes. Spherical parameters (center coordinates, FoV) are computed to represent these crops. A refinement mechanism queries the MLLM again to label the relevance of each cropped image to the question, filtering out irrelevant cues.
- **Response Refinement:** The ODI, viewpoint captions, and relevant crop cues (with orientation information) are fed back to the MLLM. The model is prompted to reconsider its previous answer based on these additional visual and textual cues, leading to a refined response.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on ODI-Bench revealed several findings regarding MLLMs' understanding of omnidirectional environments.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- Current MLLMs demonstrate limitations in comprehending immersive omnidirectional images. The overall scores for even the best-performing models remain modest.
- Proprietary models generally achieved stronger overall performance than open-source models. O3 achieved the highest overall scores in both close-ended (62.62%) and open-ended (49.53%) evaluations.
- Some open-source models, such as Qwen2.5-VL-72B and InternVL3-78B, demonstrated competitive performance, occasionally surpassing GPT-4o in specific metrics.
- The best-performing model (o3) exceeded a "Blind GPT-4o" baseline (without image input) by less than 30% accuracy in the close-ended setting, indicating that MLLMs still struggle to fully leverage the rich visual information in ODIs.

#### 4.2. Task-Wise Performance

- **General-level tasks** (Object Attribute, Human Attribute, Existence, OCR) were generally less challenging than spatial tasks, with models achieving higher accuracies. However, the complexity of immersive environments still increased difficulty compared to 2D image tasks.
- **Spatial-level understanding** proved substantially more challenging for MLLMs. Performance on tasks like Counting showed a significant drop (around 20% compared to attribute recognition tasks).
- Tasks fully reliant on **immersive spatial comprehension**, particularly non-egocentric ones like Allocentric View Orientation and Scene Simulation, posed the greatest challenge. Model performance in these areas was only marginally above (or even below) the Blind GPT-4o baseline, suggesting MLLMs struggle to capture and reason with the complex spatial information in ODIs. This limitation is attributed to their primary training on 2D data, hindering their ability to effectively interpret the warped 2D projections of 3D immersive scenes.

#### 4.3. Closed Versus Open Evaluation

- Model performance varied significantly between close-ended and open-ended QA settings, highlighting the necessity of evaluating both.
- For tasks with unique ground truths like Counting and OCR, performance generally decreased in the open-ended setting, indicating that multiple-choice options can act as a hint for MLLMs.
- An "Option Anchoring Bias" was observed where models sometimes provided correct open-ended answers but selected incorrect options in the close-ended format. This suggests a distinction between generative and discriminative reasoning and implies that predefined options can sometimes introduce interference.
- The performance divergence was more pronounced in the open-ended setting, especially for spatial-level tasks, revealing larger variations in MLLMs' reasoning abilities. Models often failed to naturally reason about immersive ODI scenes in a human-like, perspective-taking manner, instead processing them as warped 2D images, even when prompted for absolute orientations.

#### 4.4. Omni-CoT Performance

- The proposed training-free Omni-CoT framework consistently improved MLLMs' comprehension of ODIs across both proprietary and open-source models.
- **Viewpoint guiding** was identified as a critical component, leading to substantial performance gains, particularly in spatial-related tasks (e.g., significant improvements in Egocentric View Orientation for o3, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-flash, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B).
- The full Omni-CoT pipeline, including **crop cue grounding and refinement**, further enhanced performance beyond viewpoint guiding alone. Ablation studies showed that direct grounding without refinement could degrade performance, while the refinement step successfully filtered irrelevant crops, boosting overall scores.
- Omni-CoT outperformed alternative methods like multi-view image input and video-based input, which did not yield consistent overall gains due to model input constraints and the lack of dedicated reasoning strategies.
- Compared to Zero-shot CoT, Omni-CoT provided substantially greater improvements, especially in spatial-level tasks, indicating that explicit reasoning steps tailored for ODIs are more effective than general chain-of-thought prompting.
- The framework also demonstrated effectiveness in the open-ended evaluation setting, further validating its utility in improving generative reasoning for immersive environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces ODI-Bench, a novel and comprehensive benchmark that rigorously evaluates MLLMs on omnidirectional image understanding. This development is significant for several reasons:

- **Advancing MLLM capabilities in immersive environments:** ODI-Bench provides a standardized and challenging platform to assess MLLMs' ability to perceive and reason within 360° environments. The detailed task definitions and diverse real-world dataset directly address the limitations of previous benchmarks, pushing MLLM development towards more immersive and context-aware perception.
- **Guiding future research:** The benchmark results highlight specific weaknesses of current MLLMs, particularly in spatial reasoning from both egocentric and allocentric perspectives and in interpreting distorted ERP projections. This detailed error analysis can guide researchers in developing targeted architectural improvements, training strategies, and reasoning mechanisms for MLLMs.
- **Practical applications in emerging technologies:** Improved ODI understanding is crucial for the advancement of technologies such as VR/AR applications (for enhanced user interaction and realism), autonomous driving (for comprehensive situational awareness), robotic navigation (for intelligent decision-making in complex spaces), and embodied AI (for agents capable of human-like perception and interaction within 3D environments).
- **Introduction of Omni-CoT:** The proposed Omni-CoT framework offers a practical, training-free approach to enhance MLLMs' performance in ODI understanding. Its effectiveness, particularly in spatial reasoning tasks, demonstrates the value of human-like, step-by-step reasoning guided by viewpoint information and refined visual cues. This method can serve as a baseline or a component in future systems aiming to tackle similar challenges.
- **Resource for the research community:** The commitment to releasing ODI-Bench and the associated code ensures that the research community has access to a valuable tool for evaluation, pre-training, and fine-tuning MLLMs, fostering further innovation in omnidirectional vision and multimodal AI.
- **Ethical considerations:** By sourcing images permissibly and manually refining annotations to exclude sensitive data, the work adheres to ethical guidelines, promoting responsible AI development.

Overall, this research establishes a foundation for systematically evaluating and enhancing MLLMs' capabilities in comprehending immersive omnidirectional environments, which is a critical step towards more robust and context-aware artificial intelligence.
