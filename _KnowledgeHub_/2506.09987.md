# A Shortcut-aware Video-QA Benchmark for Physical Understanding via Minimal Video Pairs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at Meta, Mila, and McGill University introduce the Minimal Video Pairs (MVP) benchmark to rigorously assess genuine physical understanding in video-language models. This new framework reveals that current state-of-the-art models perform near random chance on tasks requiring true physical reasoning, highlighting a significant gap between reported benchmark scores and actual capabilities.

## Problem

- Current video-language models (VideoLLMs) achieve impressive performance on benchmarks, but it is unclear if this reflects genuine physical world understanding or exploitation of statistical shortcuts.
- Existing video benchmarks, such as MVBench, are susceptible to various biases including language-only, video-only, and single-frame shortcuts, leading to 'score inflation' where models appear more capable than they are.
- A lack of rigorous evaluation methods makes it difficult to assess if VideoLLMs truly grasp spatio-temporal relationships, physical laws, and precise interactions.

## Method

- A new benchmark, Minimal Video Pairs (MVP), is introduced, consisting of 27,414 minimal-change pairs where two visually similar videos require contradictory answers to the same question.
- MVP focuses on four categories of physical world understanding: fine-grained human-object interactions, robot-object interactions, intuitive physics, and coarse-grained temporal understanding.
- A sophisticated three-stage automated pipeline constructs MVP by manually filtering datasets, automatically pairing similar videos with contradictory answers, and rigorously filtering out examples solvable from single frames.

## Results

- Humans achieve 92.9% accuracy on the MVP benchmark, while the best-performing VideoLLM (InternVL2.5-8B) reaches only 40.2%, with most models performing near the 25% random chance baseline.
- Models show sub-random performance on intuitive physics and struggle significantly with robot-object interactions, indicating fundamental weaknesses in understanding core physical phenomena and precise manipulations.
- Ablation studies confirm MVP's effectiveness: random pairing reduces accuracy by 18.1%, and single-frame filtering further reduces it by 2.2%, validating the benchmark's design in eliminating shortcuts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing video-language benchmarks suffer from significant shortcuts (e.g., language-only, video-only, single-frame, generic description biases) that allow models to achieve high scores without genuine spatio-temporal or physical reasoning.
> - The 'minimal-change pair' methodology, where subtly different videos require contradictory answers, is effective in exposing a lack of true physical understanding in VideoLLMs by penalizing shortcut-based responses.
> - Current state-of-the-art VideoLLMs demonstrate a substantial gap in understanding intuitive physics and precise robot-object interactions compared to human performance, often performing near random chance on these tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Current video-language models (VideoLLMs) demonstrate impressive performance on existing benchmarks, but a critical question remains: do these models truly understand the physical world, or are they simply exploiting statistical shortcuts in the data? This research, conducted by a collaborative team from FAIR at Meta, Mila, and McGill University, addresses this fundamental concern by introducing the Minimal Video Pairs (MVP) benchmark—a rigorous evaluation framework designed to assess genuine physical understanding in video-language models.

The motivation stems from a well-documented problem in AI evaluation: models often achieve high scores not through genuine understanding, but by learning to exploit biases and shortcuts present in benchmark datasets. This phenomenon, particularly relevant to Moravec's paradox (the observation that high-level reasoning is easier for AI than basic sensorimotor skills), suggests that current video understanding capabilities may be significantly overestimated.

### 2. Problem with Current Video Understanding Benchmarks

The authors begin by conducting a comprehensive analysis of MVBench, a popular video question-answering benchmark, revealing multiple pathways through which models can achieve high performance without genuine understanding:

**Language-Only Shortcuts**: When provided only with questions and answer choices (no video content), a text-only model (Llama3-70B) achieved 38% accuracy compared to a 30% random baseline. On specific tasks like "Action Antonym," this jumped to 78% versus a 50% random baseline, indicating that models can often infer correct answers from textual patterns alone.

**Video-Only Shortcuts**: A VideoLLM given only videos and answer choices (no questions) achieved 50% accuracy, suggesting that answer options are often not sufficiently specific or that visual context alone provides sufficient information to eliminate incorrect choices.

**Single-Frame Bias**: Perhaps most concerning, single-frame models processing only one frame from each video achieved 47-51% accuracy—often matching or exceeding full video models. This reveals that many supposedly "temporal" reasoning tasks can be solved without any temporal understanding.

**Generic Description Shortcuts**: A text-only model provided with simple, high-level video descriptions achieved 46% accuracy, indicating that fine-grained visual understanding is often unnecessary.

These findings demonstrate that existing benchmarks suffer from significant "score inflation," where high performance metrics mask the absence of genuine spatio-temporal and physical reasoning capabilities.

### 3. The Minimal Video Pairs Approach

To address these shortcomings, the researchers develop MVP using a "minimal-change pair" methodology inspired by successful approaches in natural language processing (like the Winograd Schema Challenge) and image-language tasks.

**Core Design Principle**: Each example in MVP consists of two video-question-answer triplets: $(q_1, v_1, [a_1, a_2])$ and $(q_2, v_2, [a_1, a_2])$ where:
- Questions are identical: $q_1 = q_2$
- Videos are visually similar but differ in crucial, subtle ways: $v_1 \sim v_2$
- Answer choices are mutually exclusive and contradictory

**Scoring Mechanism**: Models receive credit only when they correctly answer both examples in a minimal-change pair. This scoring scheme severely penalizes models that provide the same answer for both videos due to shortcuts, as the correct answers are by design contradictory. In this two-option multiple-choice setup, random performance is 25%.

**Question Taxonomy**: The benchmark focuses on four categories of physical world understanding:
1. **Fine-grained human-object interactions**: Subtle actions and manipulations
2. **Fine-grained robot-object interactions**: Precise robotic manipulation tasks
3. **Intuitive physics understanding**: Fundamental physical laws and object behaviors
4. **Coarse-grained temporal understanding**: Event ordering and sequential changes

### 4. Benchmark Construction and Methodology

The construction of MVP involves a sophisticated three-stage automated pipeline processing videos from nine diverse sources, including egocentric videos, robotic interaction datasets, and cognitive science benchmarks.

**Stage 1: Manual Categorical Filtering**
Researchers manually inspect and filter existing video datasets (Perception Test, Language Table, CLEVRER, IntPhys, STAR, Vinoground, Something Something v2) to select subsets suitable for physical understanding assessment. This involves removing ill-defined questions, tasks that don't require temporal reasoning, and examples with known issues.

**Stage 2: Automatic Pairing**
The most technically challenging stage involves automatically identifying minimal-change pairs:
- **Similarity Detection**: Candidate pairs are identified using both symbolic rules (for datasets with rich annotations) and neural similarity measures (cosine similarity of ViCLIP video embeddings)
- **Answer Contradiction Verification**: Crucial filtering ensures that correct answers for video pairs are truly mutually exclusive, using entailment detection and custom textual rules to eliminate cases where answers might be simultaneously true

**Stage 3: Single-Frame Bias Filtering**
To ensure temporal reasoning is genuinely required, the authors employ an ensemble of five state-of-the-art multimodal models to identify examples solvable from single frames:
- Each model attempts to answer questions using only randomly sampled individual frames
- If 4+ models correctly predict answers from single frames for 30% or more of frames in both videos of a pair, that pair is discarded
- This step ensures remaining examples require genuine temporal understanding

The final MVP benchmark contains 54,828 multiple-choice examples forming 27,414 minimal-change pairs, representing the largest shortcut-aware video understanding benchmark to date.

### 5. Experimental Results and Analysis

The evaluation results reveal a stark reality about current VideoLLM capabilities:

**Human vs. Model Performance**: Humans achieve 92.9% accuracy on MVP, demonstrating that the tasks are reasonable and solvable. In sharp contrast, even the best-performing model (InternVL2.5-8B) reaches only 40.2% accuracy, with most models performing near random chance (25%).

**Category-Specific Analysis**:
- **Intuitive Physics**: Consistently the most challenging category, with most models achieving sub-random performance, highlighting fundamental weaknesses in understanding basic physical phenomena
- **Robot-Object Interactions**: Models struggle significantly with precise manipulation understanding, concerning given the increasing use of VLMs for robotics
- **Temporal Reasoning**: Models show some above-random performance, suggesting basic event ordering capabilities
- **Human-Object Interactions**: Variable performance across different subtasks

**Validation Through Ablation Studies**: The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their curation process through systematic ablation:
- Random pairing (vs. minimal-change pairs): 45.4% → 27.3% accuracy (-18.1%)
- Adding single-frame filtering: 27.3% → 25.1% accuracy (-2.2%)

These results confirm that MVP's design principles successfully eliminate shortcuts while maintaining meaningful evaluation criteria.

### 6. Implications and Future Directions

This research carries profound implications for the video understanding community. By exposing the significant gap between claimed capabilities and actual performance when shortcuts are eliminated, MVP serves as a critical diagnostic tool that reshapes our understanding of current AI limitations.

**Immediate Impact**: The benchmark provides concrete evidence that current VideoLLMs lack genuine physical understanding, operating instead through pattern recognition and statistical correlation rather than causal reasoning about the physical world. This finding necessitates a fundamental reassessment of claimed progress in video understanding.

**Research Directions**: The results point toward several crucial areas for future development:
- **World Modeling**: Poor performance on intuitive physics tasks indicates the need for models with robust internal representations of physical laws, causality, and object behavior
- **Architecture Innovation**: Current model architectures may be fundamentally inadequate for capturing complex spatio-temporal relationships
- **Training Paradigms**: New learning objectives and training data curation strategies are needed to move beyond superficial pattern matching

**Broader Significance**: MVP represents more than just another benchmark—it embodies a methodological shift toward more rigorous evaluation practices. The automated pipeline for creating minimal-change pairs demonstrates that such robust evaluation can scale, potentially inspiring similar approaches across other domains.

The work also has immediate relevance for embodied AI and robotics applications, where genuine physical understanding is not just desirable but essential for safe and effective operation in real-world environments.

By establishing MVP as an open resource, the authors enable the broader research community to honestly assess their models against this more demanding standard, potentially accelerating progress toward AI systems that truly understand and reason about the dynamic physical world rather than merely appearing to do so through clever exploitation of dataset biases.
