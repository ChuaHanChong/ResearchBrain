# A Shortcut-aware Video-QA Benchmark for Physical Understanding via Minimal Video Pairs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at Meta, Mila, and McGill University introduce the Minimal Video Pairs (MVP) benchmark to rigorously assess genuine physical understanding in video-language models. This new framework reveals that current state-of-the-art models perform near random chance on tasks requiring true physical reasoning, highlighting a significant gap between reported benchmark scores and actual capabilities.

## Problem

- Current video-language models (VideoLLMs) achieve impressive performance on benchmarks, but it is unclear if this reflects genuine physical world understanding or exploitation of statistical shortcuts.
- Existing video benchmarks, such as MVBench, are susceptible to various biases including language-only, video-only, and single-frame shortcuts, leading to 'score inflation' where models appear more capable than they are.
- A lack of rigorous evaluation methods makes it difficult to assess if VideoLLMs truly grasp spatio-temporal relationships, physical laws, and precise interactions.

## Method

- A new benchmark, Minimal Video Pairs (MVP), is introduced, consisting of 27,414 minimal-change pairs where two visually similar videos require contradictory answers to the same question.
- MVP focuses on four categories of physical world understanding: fine-grained human-object interactions, robot-object interactions, intuitive physics, and coarse-grained temporal understanding.
- A sophisticated three-stage automated pipeline constructs MVP by manually filtering datasets, automatically pairing similar videos with contradictory answers, and rigorously filtering out examples solvable from single frames.

## Results

- Humans achieve 92.9% accuracy on the MVP benchmark, while the best-performing VideoLLM (InternVL2.5-8B) reaches only 40.2%, with most models performing near the 25% random chance baseline.
- Models show sub-random performance on intuitive physics and struggle significantly with robot-object interactions, indicating fundamental weaknesses in understanding core physical phenomena and precise manipulations.
- Ablation studies confirm MVP's effectiveness: random pairing reduces accuracy by 18.1%, and single-frame filtering further reduces it by 2.2%, validating the benchmark's design in eliminating shortcuts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing video-language benchmarks suffer from significant shortcuts (e.g., language-only, video-only, single-frame, generic description biases) that allow models to achieve high scores without genuine spatio-temporal or physical reasoning.
> - The 'minimal-change pair' methodology, where subtly different videos require contradictory answers, is effective in exposing a lack of true physical understanding in VideoLLMs by penalizing shortcut-based responses.
> - Current state-of-the-art VideoLLMs demonstrate a substantial gap in understanding intuitive physics and precise robot-object interactions compared to human performance, often performing near random chance on these tasks.
