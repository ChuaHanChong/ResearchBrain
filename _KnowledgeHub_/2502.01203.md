# KL-Regularized RLHF with Multiple Reference Models: Exact Solutions and Sample Complexity

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the first exact solutions and sample complexity guarantees for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) when incorporating multiple reference models. The multi-reference approach consistently improves performance over single-reference methods, with specific theoretical bounds derived for both Reverse and Forward Kullback-Leibler regularization.

## Problem

- Current Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) frameworks primarily rely on a single reference model, which limits response diversity and can inherit biases.
- The single-reference approach underutilizes the growing ecosystem of diverse pre-trained open-source large language models (LLMs).
- A robust theoretical foundation, including exact solutions and sample complexity guarantees, for integrating multiple reference models into RLHF was previously absent.

## Method

- Formulated multi-reference RLHF problems for both ==Reverse KL== (RKL) and Forward KL (FKL) regularization.
- Derived the first exact closed-form solution for RKL-regularized RLHF with multiple references, introducing a 'generalized escort reference policy' based on a weighted geometric mean.
- Developed an implicit solution for FKL-regularized RLHF with multiple references, utilizing a 'weighted reference policy' based on an arithmetic mean.

## Results

- Multi-reference RLHF significantly outperforms single-reference methods in both online (GRPO on GSM8K) and offline (DPO on UltraFeedback) experiments.
- The proposed exact analytical solution for RKL-regularized RLHF consistently surpasses the performance of an existing lower-bound approximation (MRPO).
- Theoretical analysis established an O(1/n) sample complexity for the sub-optimality gap in RKL-RLHF and O(1/sqrt(n)) for both sub-optimality and optimality gaps in FKL-RLHF. Large-scale DPO experiments showed a multi-reference DPO achieved a **66.1%** win rate, a gain over single-reference DPO baselines (**56.4%** and **59.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The theoretical framework provides precise mechanisms, such as the generalized escort policy for RKL and weighted policy for FKL, for combining diverse reference models.
> - The analysis shows that incorporating multiple references does not degrade the sample complexity convergence rates for RKL-RLHF's sub-optimality gap compared to single-reference methods.
> - This work provides the first theoretical study of sample complexity for FKL-regularized RLHF, quantifying its data efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized Natural Language Processing (NLP), demonstrating unparalleled capabilities in generating human-like text and performing complex linguistic tasks. However, ensuring these powerful models align with human values, preferences, and safety guidelines remains a critical and complex challenge. Unaligned LLMs can produce biased, harmful, or nonsensical outputs, eroding trust and limiting their societal benefit.

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as the dominant paradigm for addressing this alignment problem. RLHF typically involves training a reward model from human preference data and then fine-tuning the LLM policy using reinforcement learning to maximize this learned reward, often with a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularizer to prevent excessive deviation from an initial pre-trained policy (the "reference model").

This paper strategically positions itself at the frontier of RLHF research by addressing a fundamental limitation of most existing RLHF frameworks: their reliance on a *single reference model*. This single-reference approach, while effective, inherently restricts the diversity of linguistic patterns and inductive biases during training. It risks inheriting the limitations or biases of that singular pre-trained source and inefficiently utilizes the growing ecosystem of diverse open-source LLMs, each excelling in different domains or embodying unique stylistic nuances. The practical utility of incorporating multiple reference models has been empirically shown (Le et al., 2024), but a robust theoretical foundation for such an approach has been largely missing.

The broader research landscape for RLHF has seen significant theoretical progress, particularly in understanding the sample complexity of Reverse KL (RKL)-regularized RLHF. Works by Zhao et al. (2024), Song et al. (2024), Chang et al. (2024), and Xiong et al. (2024) have analyzed the theoretical underpinnings and sample complexity of single-reference RKL-RLHF. However, none of these works extended their analysis to the multi-reference scenario. Similarly, while Forward KL (FKL)-regularized approaches, like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Wang et al., 2024a) and its application in alignment from demonstrations (Sun and van der Schaar, 2024), have gained traction, their theoretical properties, especially within an RLHF context and with multiple references, have not been thoroughly investigated.

This work addresses these critical gaps by providing the *first exact solutions* and *sample complexity guarantees* for RLHF under both reverse and forward KL-regularization when *multiple reference models* are incorporated. By doing so, it extends the theoretical understanding of RLHF beyond the single-reference paradigm, offering a more generalized and powerful framework. It bridges the gap between empirical successes of multi-referencing (Le et al., 2024) and rigorous theoretical grounding, laying the groundwork for more advanced and adaptable LLM alignment techniques that can leverage the collective intelligence of diverse foundation models. The paper also contributes to the theoretical understanding of FKL-regularized RLHF, a less explored area compared to RKL, particularly concerning its sample complexity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a critical limitation in current Large Language Model (LLM) alignment techniques: the widespread reliance on a single reference model in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) frameworks. This single-reference approach, while a cornerstone of successful LLM fine-tuning, presents several drawbacks:

- **Limited Diversity and Inductive Biases**: A single reference model restricts the range of linguistic patterns, styles, and "worldviews" the aligned model can learn from, potentially leading to a lack of diversity in generated responses.
- **Risk of Overfitting and Inherited Limitations**: The aligned model may overfit to the specific characteristics, biases, or limitations inherent in the single reference, rather than developing a more robust and generalized understanding of human preferences.
- **Underutilization of Available Models**: The rapidly expanding open-source ecosystem offers a plethora of pre-trained and fine-tuned LLMs, each with unique strengths and specialized knowledge. Relying on just one reference model leaves this wealth of collective intelligence untapped, hindering the potential for more comprehensive and versatile alignment.

The theoretical underpinning for integrating multiple reference models into RLHF, particularly concerning exact solutions and sample complexity, has remained an open problem. This theoretical gap prevents a deep understanding of how such multi-reference approaches perform, their statistical properties, and how efficiently they learn from data.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:

1. **Develop an Exact Solution for Reverse KL-Regularized RLHF with Multiple Reference Models**: The primary objective is to formulate and provide the first precise, closed-form mathematical solution for the RLHF objective function when regularized by the reverse KL divergence and incorporating multiple reference models. This directly addresses a significant theoretical challenge that has historically been approximated or unaddressed.
2. **Provide Rigorous Statistical Analysis and Sample Complexity Guarantees for Multi-Reference RKL-RLHF**: Building on the exact solution, the research aims to establish a comprehensive theoretical framework, including statistical analysis that quantifies how quickly the performance of the aligned model converges to the optimal policy as the amount of preference data increases. This involves deriving upper bounds on sub-optimality and optimality gaps.
3. **Extend Analysis to Forward KL-Regularized RLHF with Multiple Reference Models**: Recognizing the growing interest in FKL-regularization, the paper seeks to extend its theoretical framework to this setting. This includes deriving an implicit solution for the multi-reference FKL-regularized RLHF problem and analyzing its corresponding sample complexity requirements.
4. **Lay the Foundation for Advanced LLM Alignment Techniques**: Ultimately, the work aims to provide theoretically sound principles that enable the development of more sophisticated, adaptable, and robust LLM alignment frameworks. By better leveraging diverse reference models, these frameworks can be better suited to the complexities and challenges of modern AI ecosystems, promoting models that are not only helpful and harmless but also diverse and resilient.

These objectives collectively aim to push the theoretical boundaries of RLHF, enabling more effective and diversified alignment strategies for LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper adopts a rigorous theoretical approach, grounded in mathematical optimization and statistical analysis, complemented by empirical validation. The core methodology revolves around formulating the multi-reference RLHF problem for both Reverse KL (RKL) and Forward KL (FKL) regularization, deriving their exact or implicit solutions, and then providing statistical guarantees, specifically sample complexity bounds.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work begins by establishing a standard RLHF problem setup:
- An LLM is viewed as a conditional policy $\pi(y|x)$ generating responses $y$ for prompts $x$.
- Human preferences are modeled using the Bradley–Terry model, where $p(y \succ y'|x) = \sigma(r_\theta(x,y) - r_\theta(x,y'))$, with $r_\theta(x,y)$ being a reward model parameterized by $\theta$.
- A preference dataset $D = \{(y_w, y_l, x)\}$, where $y_w$ is preferred over $y_l$, is used to train the reward model $r_{\hat{\theta}}$ via Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE).
- The overall objective is to find a policy $\pi$ that maximizes expected reward while being "close" to a reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ via a KL-divergence regularizer.

#### 3.2. Assumptions

For the theoretical analysis, several standard assumptions are made:
- **Bounded Reward (Assumption 4.1)**: True and parameterized reward functions are non-negative and bounded by $R_{\text{max}}$. This is crucial for applying concentration inequalities. The authors discuss potential relaxations for unbounded or sub-Gaussian rewards using monotonic transformations.
- **Finite Class (Assumption 4.2)**: The reward function class is finite, $|R| < \infty$. This is a simplifying assumption, but the authors note it can be relaxed using covering number arguments.
- **Local KL-ball Coverage (Assumptions 4.4 and 4.5)**: Instead of the more stringent global coverage, the paper uses local reverse/forward KL-ball coverage. This means that for policies within a specified KL-divergence ball around the reference policy, the ratio $\pi(y|x)/\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)$ is bounded. This aligns better with RLHF, where policies are explicitly constrained to stay near the reference.

#### 3.3. Multiple Reference Models for Reverse KL-Regularized RLHF

- **Objective**: The paper formulates the multi-reference RKL objective as:
 $\max_{\pi} E_{Y \sim \pi(\cdot|x)}[r(x,Y)] - \frac{1}{\gamma} \sum_{i=1}^K \alpha_i \text{KL}(\pi(\cdot|x) \| \pi_{\text{ref},i}(\cdot|x))$, where $\alpha_i$ are weighting coefficients summing to 1.
- **Exact Solution (Theorem 5.1)**: The key theoretical contribution here is deriving the *first exact closed-form solution* for this objective. The solution is of the form $\pi^{\star}(y|x) \propto \hat{\pi}_{\alpha,\text{ref}}(y|x) \exp(\gamma r(x,y))$, where $\hat{\pi}_{\alpha,\text{ref}}(y|x)$ is introduced as a **generalized escort reference policy**, defined as $\hat{\pi}_{\alpha,\text{ref}}(y|x) = \frac{\prod_{i=1}^K \pi_{\text{ref},i}^{\alpha_i}(y|x)}{F_\alpha(x)}$. This generalizes the single-reference RKL solution by effectively creating a composite reference from the weighted geometric mean of individual reference policies.
- **Sample Complexity Analysis**: Using functional derivatives, convexity of KL-divergence, and the local coverage assumption, the paper derives upper bounds on the sub-optimality gap (difference between optimal reward for true vs. estimated reward model) and the optimality gap (difference in value function). It establishes a sample complexity of $O(1/n)$ for the sub-optimality gap and $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ for the optimality gap.

#### 3.4. Multiple Reference Models for Forward KL-Regularized RLHF

- **Objective**: The multi-reference FKL objective is formulated as:
 $\max_{\pi} E_{Y \sim \pi(\cdot|x)}[r(x,Y)] - \frac{1}{\gamma} \sum_{i=1}^K \beta_i \text{KL}(\pi_{\text{ref},i}(\cdot|x) \| \pi(\cdot|x))$, where $\beta_i$ are weighting coefficients.
- **Implicit Solution (Theorem 6.1)**: For FKL, the paper derives an *implicit solution* of the form $\tilde{\pi}^{\star}(y|x) = \frac{\bar{\pi}_{\beta,\text{ref}}(y|x)}{\gamma(\tilde{Z}(x) - r(x,y))}$, where $\bar{\pi}_{\beta,\text{ref}}(y|x) = \sum_{i=1}^K \beta_i \pi_{\text{ref},i}(y|x)$ is a **weighted reference policy** (an arithmetic mean), and $\tilde{Z}(x)$ is a normalization constant.
- **Sample Complexity Analysis**: Similar theoretical tools are used to analyze FKL-regularized RLHF. The paper derives a sample complexity of $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ for both the sub-optimality and optimality gaps, a first for theoretical FKL-RLHF studies in this context.

#### 3.5. Extension to DPO

The paper briefly discusses how its theoretical results for both RKL and FKL can be extended to the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework, deriving multi-reference DPO loss functions and discussing implicit bounded reward assumptions for DPO's sample complexity.

#### 3.6. Experimental Validation

Two sets of experiments are conducted to support the theoretical findings:
- **Online RL**: Using GRPO (a policy gradient method) on the GSM8K dataset, with a solution verifier as the reward model. This isolates the effect of multiple references and KL regularization from reward model learning.
- **Offline RLHF**: Using DPO on the UltraFeedback dataset. This represents a more standard RLHF setting, where the Qwen 2.5 0.5B model is fine-tuned with the 1.5B model as a second reference, and evaluated using a reward model. A larger-scale experiment with Qwen 2.5 7B and 14B models evaluated by GPT-4o as judge is also performed.
The experiments aim to evaluate the benefits of multiple references and compare the proposed exact analytical solution against single-reference baselines and the MRPO approximation by Le et al. (2024).

Overall, the methodology is characterized by a strong theoretical core, providing novel exact solutions and statistical guarantees, and supported by empirical results that demonstrate the practical advantages of the proposed multi-reference RLHF frameworks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper delivers several significant theoretical and empirical findings that advance the understanding and practical application of RLHF with multiple reference models.

#### 4.1. Exact Solutions for Multi-Reference RLHF

- **Reverse KL-Regularized RLHF**: The paper presents the *first exact closed-form solution* for the reverse KL-regularized RLHF objective with multiple reference models (Theorem 5.1). This solution introduces a novel **generalized escort reference policy** ($\hat{\pi}_{\alpha,\text{ref}}$), which is a normalized geometric mean of the individual reference policies, weighted by coefficients $\alpha_i$. This elegant mathematical formulation shows precisely how multiple diverse reference policies combine to form an effective composite reference for RKL alignment.
- **Forward KL-Regularized RLHF**: For the forward KL-regularized RLHF, the paper derives an *implicit solution* (Theorem 6.1). This solution incorporates a **weighted reference policy** ($\bar{\pi}_{\beta,\text{ref}}$), which is an arithmetic mean of the individual reference policies, weighted by coefficients $\beta_i$. This is the first theoretical characterization of FKL-regularized RLHF in a multi-reference setting.

#### 4.2. Sample Complexity Guarantees

The paper provides rigorous statistical analysis, establishing sample complexity bounds for both multi-reference frameworks:

- **Reverse KL-Regularized RLHF**:
 - **Sub-optimality Gap**: Achieves an $O(1/n)$ sample complexity, where $n$ is the size of the preference dataset (Theorem 5.2). This result matches the best-known bounds for single-reference RKL-RLHF (Zhao et al., 2024), demonstrating that incorporating multiple references does not degrade the convergence rate for this metric.
 - **Optimality Gap**: Demonstrates an $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ sample complexity (Theorem 5.3).
- **Forward KL-Regularized RLHF**:
 - **Sub-optimality Gap**: Achieves an $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ sample complexity (Theorem 6.2).
 - **Optimality Gap**: Also shows an $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ sample complexity when the regularization parameter $\gamma$ is chosen optimally (Theorem 6.3). This represents the *first theoretical study* of sample complexity for FKL-regularized RLHF in the literature.

These sample complexity results provide crucial theoretical guarantees regarding the efficiency of the proposed multi-reference RLHF frameworks, quantifying how quickly the aligned policy approaches optimality with increasing amounts of human feedback.

#### 4.3. Empirical Validation

The experimental results consistently support the theoretical findings and demonstrate the practical benefits of the proposed approach:

- **Superiority of Multiple Reference Models**: Both online (GRPO on GSM8K) and offline (DPO on UltraFeedback) experiments show that using multiple reference models significantly *outperforms* using a single reference model (where $\alpha=0$ in the weighting scheme). This confirms the paper's initial motivation that leveraging diverse models leads to better alignment outcomes.
- **Outperformance of Exact Analytical Solution**: The proposed exact analytical solution for RKL-regularized RLHF consistently *outperforms* the lower-bound approximation (MRPO) introduced by Le et al. (2024). This highlights the value of the rigorous theoretical derivation, leading to more effective practical algorithms.
- **Scalability and Performance Gains**: In the large-scale DPO experiment with Qwen 2.5 7B and 14B models, the multi-reference DPO achieved a 66.1% win rate against the preferred answer, a substantial gain compared to single-reference DPO with the SFT model (56.4%) or the 14B model (59.8%). This demonstrates the method's effectiveness at a larger scale and its ability to significantly enhance LLM alignment performance.
- **Comparison of RKL vs. FKL**: The experiments implicitly show that both RKL and FKL approaches with multiple references yield performance improvements. The theoretical analysis explicitly shows that RKL offers a superior sample complexity for the sub-optimality gap ($O(1/n)$ vs. $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ for FKL), while sharing the same optimality gap complexity.

In summary, the paper successfully provides the theoretical underpinnings for multi-reference RLHF, filling a significant gap in the literature, and empirically validates the enhanced performance and practical utility of its proposed exact solutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant theoretical and practical advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment, with a broad potential impact on the development and deployment of more robust, diverse, and human-aligned AI systems.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Bridging a Critical Theoretical Gap**: The paper provides the *first exact solutions* for KL-regularized RLHF objectives in a multiple reference model setting. This moves beyond approximations (e.g., Le et al., 2024) and significantly deepens the theoretical understanding of how to optimally combine information from diverse foundational models. The introduction of the "generalized escort reference policy" for RKL and the "weighted reference policy" for FKL are key conceptual contributions that precisely define the effective composite reference in multi-model scenarios.
- **Comprehensive Sample Complexity Analysis**: By deriving sample complexity guarantees for both RKL and FKL variants, the paper quantifies the data efficiency of these multi-reference alignment methods. The $O(1/n)$ sub-optimality gap for RKL matches the best single-reference results, affirming its theoretical soundness, while the $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ bounds for FKL offer the first theoretical characterization for this regularization in an RLHF context. This rigor provides a solid foundation for comparing and selecting alignment strategies.
- **Generalized Framework**: The work extends the theoretical bedrock of RLHF, traditionally focused on single reference models, to a more generalized and flexible framework. This theoretical generalization is crucial for advancing the field beyond simplified assumptions.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact and Implications

- **Enhanced LLM Alignment**: The core practical impact is enabling LLMs to achieve superior alignment with human preferences. By leveraging the collective intelligence and diverse characteristics of multiple pre-trained models, the aligned LLMs are expected to produce more truthful, helpful, and harmless outputs, potentially reducing biases inherited from a single source.
- **Effective Utilization of the Open-Source Ecosystem**: The proliferation of open-source LLMs (e.g., Qwen, Mistral) provides a rich, diverse landscape of models with different strengths. This work provides a theoretically sound method to integrate these diverse models into the alignment process, transforming them from individual assets into a collaborative resource. This could lead to more robust, versatile, and adaptable models better suited for a wide range of tasks and user communities.
- **Reduced Overfitting and Increased Diversity**: Relying on multiple reference models can mitigate the risk of overfitting to the idiosyncrasies of a single model and encourage the generation of more diverse and nuanced responses, reflecting a broader spectrum of linguistic patterns and inductive biases.
- **Foundation for Future Alignment Techniques**: The theoretical framework and solutions presented in this paper serve as a foundation for developing even more advanced and adaptable LLM alignment techniques. This includes potential extensions to handle unbounded rewards, explore other f-divergences (e.g., $\chi^2$-divergence), integrate more complex preference models, or combine with doubly robust optimization algorithms to handle model misspecification. The extension to inference-time algorithms also promises more dynamic and efficient alignment.

#### 5.3. Broader Societal Impact

By enabling the development of more robust and aligned LLMs, this research contributes to building safer and more reliable AI systems. Reduced bias, increased helpfulness, and greater adaptability can lead to LLMs that are more trustworthy in critical applications, less prone to generating harmful content, and more beneficial across diverse cultural and societal contexts. The ability to effectively harness collective intelligence from multiple models could accelerate progress in AI development, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and democratized.

In conclusion, this paper provides fundamental theoretical breakthroughs that immediately translate into practical improvements for LLM alignment. Its findings are poised to influence the next generation of RLHF algorithms, fostering the creation of more intelligent, diverse, and ethically aligned language models that better serve humanity.
