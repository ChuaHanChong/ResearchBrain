# SafeFlow: Safe Robot Motion Planning with Flow Matching via Control Barrier Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> SafeFlow is a training-free motion planning framework that adds safety guarantees to flow matching generative planners. It introduces flow matching barrier functions, a prescribed-time barrier condition that admits unsafe initial samples, solved as a closed-form QP guidance term during ODE integration, plus a terminal safety filter. On planar maze navigation and 7-DoF Franka manipulation it reaches 100% safety rate with sub-second inference.

## Problem

- Diffusion planners need many denoising steps, making inference slow and costly, and offer no formal safety guarantee when deployed among unseen obstacles.
- Prior CBF-diffusion planners such as SafeDiffuser and CoBL assume an initially safe sample, an assumption violated because generation starts from an unbounded Gaussian prior.
- Flow-matching robot policies improved efficiency and smoothness but left physical constraints and safety unaddressed; penalty-based training also cannot enforce hard constraints.

## Method

- ==Flow matching barrier functions (FMBF)==: a ==CBF==-style inequality using a ==blow-up class function== that diverges as t approaches 1, driving unsafe initial samples into the safe set by a prescribed time.
- A guidance term added to the learned vector field, obtained from a per-waypoint decoupled ==QP== minimizing its squared norm; ==KKT conditions== give a closed-form solution, so safety needs no retraining.
- ==Composite FMBF== extends the condition to multiple barrier functions with ==slack relaxation== for feasibility, plus a ==terminal safety filter== projecting the ODE endpoint back onto the safe set.

## Results

- Planar maze navigation over 1000 trials: SafeFlow and SafeDiffus both reach **100%** safety, versus **15.51%** for the best baseline TVSD and **10.96%** for vanilla flow matching.
- 7-DoF Franka manipulation over 1000 trials: **100%** safety at **0.754s** inference, against **81.90%** at **4.14s** for classifier guidance and **0%** for vanilla Diffuser and flow matching.
- Safety costs inference time, **0.26s** to **0.38s** on navigation, with KL rising **0.0265** to **0.0291**; still beats A* at **2.847s** and RRT* at **98.62%** and **6.806s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Barrier certificates assuming a safe initial state break for generative models sampling unbounded priors; prescribed-time blow-up terms repair this by entering the safe set before generation ends.
> - The prescribed-time barrier is generator-agnostic and transfers to diffusion backbones; deterministic flow mainly buys smoother trajectories, fewer outliers and faster inference, not the guarantee itself.
> - Low curvature or acceleration smoothness scores can signal degenerate straight-line plans that ignore obstacles, so smoothness metrics must be read alongside safety rate.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot motion planning is undergoing a transformative shift, increasingly leveraging advanced machine learning techniques, particularly deep generative models. Traditionally, motion planning relied on explicit algorithmic searches (e.g., A*, RRT*) that often struggle with high-dimensional spaces or complex, multimodal distributions of feasible trajectories. Recent advances in deep generative modeling, especially **diffusion models**, have shown significant promise in learning these complex distributions from data, enabling robots to generate diverse and context-dependent motions for tasks like navigation, manipulation, and locomotion.

However, despite their success, diffusion models come with critical limitations. Their inference process is typically slow and computationally intensive, involving iterative denoising steps that make real-time deployment challenging. More importantly for safety-critical applications, standard diffusion models generally lack formal guarantees to ensure that generated trajectories adhere to safety constraints (e.g., collision avoidance, joint limits) when faced with new or dynamic environments. While some prior works have attempted to integrate safety into diffusion models (e.g., via model predictive control or Control Barrier Functions – CBFs), these approaches often increase computational complexity, compromise inference quality, or face fundamental theoretical challenges. A significant hurdle for CBF integration with reverse diffusion processes, as highlighted by the authors, is the CBF's reliance on an initially safe state, which is often violated when starting from an unbounded Gaussian distribution in diffusion.

**Flow Matching (FM)** has recently emerged as a compelling alternative to diffusion models. Unlike diffusion, FM defines a deterministic flow field governed by an ordinary differential equation (ODE) that smoothly transforms samples from a simple prior into target data. This deterministic nature offers potential improvements in efficiency and generation smoothness, making it a natural candidate for robotic applications. Prior work in FM for robotics has focused on policy learning, manipulation tasks, and efficiency enhancements. However, these works have largely overlooked the critical aspects of integrating physical properties and, most notably, safety guarantees.

SafeFlow positions itself directly at this intersection, aiming to bridge the gap between the expressive power and efficiency of generative models (specifically Flow Matching) and the imperative for formal safety guarantees in robotic systems. It builds upon the strengths of FM (determinism, efficiency) while fundamentally addressing the safety shortcomings that plague many existing generative planning methods, including both diffusion-based and earlier FM approaches. By proposing Flow Matching Barrier Functions (FMBFs), SafeFlow directly confronts the technical limitations of applying conventional CBFs to generative processes starting from potentially unsafe initial states, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in safe, learning-based motion planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel robot motion planning framework, **SafeFlow**, that can autonomously generate safe and feasible trajectories in dynamic and constrained environments. This overarching goal is underpinned by several specific aims and strong motivations:

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Integrate Generative Modeling with Safety Guarantees:** To combine the strengths of flow matching (efficient, expressive trajectory generation) with formal safety guarantees, ensuring that all generated trajectories remain within predefined safe regions across the entire planning horizon.
2. **Enable Real-time, Training-free Safety Enforcement:** To design a safety mechanism that can be applied in real-time during inference, without requiring extensive retraining of the generative model when new obstacles or constraints are introduced.
3. **Overcome Limitations of Existing Safety-Aware Generative Models:** To address the computational inefficiency of diffusion-based models when incorporating safety, and crucially, to resolve the fundamental incompatibility of traditional Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) with the "initially unsafe sample" problem inherent in generative processes like reverse diffusion or flow matching from a prior distribution.
4. **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** To empirically show that SafeFlow can achieve superior safety, planning performance (smoothness, goal-alignment), and computational efficiency compared to state-of-the-art generative planners, especially those that attempt to integrate safety.

**Motivation:**
- **The Critical Need for Safety in Robotics:** As robots move from controlled laboratory settings to real-world deployment alongside humans and in complex environments, guaranteeing safety becomes paramount. Collisions with obstacles or humans can lead to property damage, injury, and a loss of trust in autonomous systems.
- **Limitations of Current Generative Planners:** While diffusion models and flow matching excel at learning complex, multimodal trajectory distributions from data, they are typically trained offline without explicit safety constraints. When deployed in novel environments with unforeseen obstacles, they inherently lack mechanisms to ensure safety during real-time operation.
- **Computational Bottlenecks of Diffusion Models:** Diffusion models, though powerful, suffer from slow inference speeds due to their iterative, stochastic nature. Integrating safety mechanisms, which often require additional computation or replanning, exacerbates this issue, making them unsuitable for real-time, safety-critical applications.
- **Theoretical Challenges with CBFs in Generative Contexts:** Control Barrier Functions are a robust tool for formal safety verification in control systems, but they traditionally assume that the system starts from an already safe state. This assumption is fundamentally violated in generative models, where initial samples (e.g., from a Gaussian noise distribution) are often unsafe. Previous attempts to combine CBFs with diffusion models have largely overlooked this issue, leading to probabilistic rather than guaranteed safety.
- **Leveraging Flow Matching's Strengths:** Flow matching offers a deterministic and potentially more efficient alternative to diffusion. The motivation is to harness this efficiency and determinism, coupling it with a tailored safety framework, to create a fast, smooth, and provably safe motion planner.

By tackling these challenges, SafeFlow aims to contribute a robust and practical solution for the safe deployment of intelligent robots, moving beyond merely generating diverse motions to generating motions that are certifiably safe.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SafeFlow proposes a novel framework that fundamentally integrates deterministic Flow Matching (FM) for trajectory generation with a specially adapted safety mechanism, the **Flow Matching Barrier Function (FMBF)**. The core methodology involves modifying the flow field during inference to guarantee safety, without requiring model retraining.

**A. Flow Matching Model (Preliminaries):**
The foundation is a standard FM model, where a robot trajectory `T` (a sequence of states `s_k` over a horizon `H`) is generated by solving an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE): `dψ(t, T_0)/dt = v(t, ψ(t, T_0))`. Here, `T_0` is sampled from a simple prior distribution, and `v(t, T_t)` is a time-dependent vector field learned by a neural network `v_θ`. The goal is to transform `T_0` into `T_1` (the desired trajectory) at `t=1`.

**B. Integrating Safety via Flow Matching Barrier Functions (FMBFs):**
The key innovation is the introduction of a regularization term `u_t` into the flow matching ODE, transforming it into a guided flow: `dψ_t(T_0)/dt = v_θ_t(ψ_t(T_0)) + u_t`. This `u_t` term acts as a "safety controller" that steers the trajectory toward safe regions. The challenge lies in ensuring that `u_t` guarantees safety even when `T_t` is initially in an unsafe region.

This is where **Flow Matching Barrier Functions (FMBFs)** come into play, extending the traditional Control Barrier Function (CBF) concept:
- **Prescribed-time Safety:** Unlike standard CBFs that assume initial safety, FMBFs are designed to achieve "prescribed-time safety." This means that even if a trajectory starts from an unsafe state, it will be driven into the safe set (and remain there) within a predefined time horizon (in this case, `t=1`).
- **FMBF Condition (Definition 4):** The condition for an FMBF `h(s)` is `L_v_theta h(E_k T_t) + L_u h(E_k T_t) + φ(t, h(E_k T_t))h(E_k T_t) >= 0`. The crucial difference from standard CBFs is the function `φ(t, h)`.
 - If `h >= 0` (safe state), `φ(t, h) = φ_0` (a positive constant), behaving like a standard CBF.
 - If `h < 0` (unsafe state), `φ(t, h) = φ_1(t)`, which is a "blow-up function" (e.g., `ω/(1-t)^2` or exponential forms). This `φ_1(t)` is monotonically increasing and goes to infinity as `t` approaches `1`, actively pushing unsafe states into the safe set by `t=1`.
- **Theoretical Guarantee (Theorem 1):** The paper formally proves that if `u_t` is chosen to satisfy the FMBF conditions, the guided FM process ensures that all generated trajectories `T_1` will be safe at `t=1`, regardless of the initial (potentially unsafe) `T_0`.
- **Calculating `u_t` via Quadratic Programming (QP):** To minimize the disturbance to the learned flow field while ensuring safety, `u_t` is obtained by solving a QP problem: `min ||u_t||^2` subject to the FMBF constraints for all states `s_k` along the trajectory. This QP is designed to be decoupled for each `u_k`, offering a closed-form solution (Eq. 16), which is computationally efficient.
- **Boundedness (Proposition 2):** The authors prove that `u_t` remains bounded for `t ∈ [0, 1)`, ensuring numerical stability and preventing extreme deviations from the learned distribution.
- **Terminal Safety Filter:** To account for potential numerical errors near `t=1` (where `φ_1(t)` blows up), a final safety filter is applied. This involves a QP (Eq. 17-18) that projects the nearly-final trajectory `T_1-` onto the safe set, guaranteeing absolute safety for `T_1`.

**C. SafeFlow under Composite Constraints (Multiple Obstacles):**
The framework is extended to handle complex safe sets defined by multiple barrier functions `h_j(s)`.
- **Composite FMBF (CFMBF, Definition 6):** Each constraint `h_j(s)` must satisfy its own FMBF condition.
- **Relaxed QP for `u_t`:** For multiple constraints, the QP to find `u_t` becomes `min ||u_t||^2 + sum(δ_k_j^2)` subject to multiple FMBF constraints, where `δ_k_j >= 0` are relaxation terms. These relaxation terms ensure the feasibility of the QP even with conflicting constraints, allowing a solution to always be found (at the cost of possibly violating some constraints minimally, which is then handled by the terminal filter).
- The terminal safety filter (Eq. 32) is crucial in this composite case to ensure all constraints are strictly met at `t=1`.

**D. Implementation Details (Algorithm 1):**
- **Numerical Integration:** Standard ODE solvers like Runge-Kutta (e.g., `dopri5`) are used for integrating the guided flow.
- **Staged Safety Activation:** The CFMBF component is only activated for `t >= t*` (e.g., `t* = 0.5`). This allows the FM model to primarily focus on matching the target data distribution in the early stages, gradually increasing the emphasis on safety as the trajectory approaches its final state.
- **Normalization/Denormalization:** Trajectories and flow fields are converted between normalized latent space (where FM operates) and physical state space (where barrier functions are defined).

In essence, SafeFlow's methodology hinges on intelligently modifying the learned, deterministic flow field in real-time using FMBFs that are specifically designed to handle initial unsafe conditions and drive the system to safety within a prescribed time. This is achieved via efficient QP-based guidance, complemented by a terminal safety filter.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SafeFlow was conducted on a diverse set of tasks: planar robot navigation in a maze and 7-DoF robot manipulation. The performance was assessed using five metrics: Safety Rate, Inference Time, Kullback–Leibler (KL) Divergence (for distribution alignment), Curvature-based Smoothness (CS), and Acceleration-based Smoothness (AS), along with Starting Distance (SD) and End Distance (ED) for manipulation tasks. SafeFlow was compared against various state-of-the-art methods, including diffusion-based safety planners (SafeDiffuser variants, CoB, CoBL), conventional guidance approaches (Classifier Guidance, Truncate), and traditional path planners (A*, MPPI, RRT*).

**A. Superior Safety Performance (Q1 & Q3):**
- **100% Safety Rate:** SafeFlow consistently achieved a perfect 100% safety rate across both planar navigation and 7-DoF manipulation tasks. This is a significant finding, providing both experimental and theoretical validation for its provable safety guarantees.
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** All other generative models incorporating safety constraints (SafeDiffuser variants, CoB, CoBL, CG, TRUNC) performed poorly in terms of safety, often yielding safety rates ranging from 0% to less than 82%. This validates the authors' claim about the limitations of existing approaches in dealing with the initial unsafe sample problem and the probabilistic nature of diffusion models.
- **SafeDiffus (Our contribution):** The authors' own implementation of FMBFs on a diffusion model (SafeDiffus) also achieved 100% safety, strongly supporting the efficacy of the FMBF concept itself, regardless of the underlying generative model.

**B. Efficiency and Real-time Operation (Q2 & Q3):**
- **Competitive Inference Times:** While SafeFlow and SafeDiffus incurred approximately 60% higher inference time compared to their vanilla (unsafe) Flow Matching and Diffusion counterparts due to the safety regularization, they remained highly efficient.
- **Faster than Traditional Planners:** SafeFlow (0.38s for navigation, 0.75s for manipulation) was significantly faster than traditional methods like A* (2.85s) and RRT* (6.81s) for comparable safety rates, highlighting its suitability for real-time applications.
- **Faster than Other Generative Safety Planners:** SafeFlow was also considerably faster than most other safety-constrained generative planners, particularly diffusion-based ones like RoSD (93s), TVSD (82s), ReSD (62s), CoB (4.1s), and CoBL (4.5s) for manipulation.

**C. Smoothness and Goal Alignment:**
- **Maintained Trajectory Quality:** Despite incorporating strong safety guarantees, SafeFlow maintained good trajectory quality. While there was a slight increase in KL divergence (indicating minor deviation from the original data distribution) and CS (curvature-based smoothness) compared to vanilla FM/Diffuser, this is an expected trade-off for ensuring safety.
- **Visually Consistent Trajectories:** Qualitative analysis (Fig. 2 and Fig. 4) clearly demonstrated that SafeFlow generated smooth, consistent, and goal-directed trajectories that effectively avoided obstacles without exhibiting erratic behavior or significant deviations from the learned task distribution. In contrast, many baselines generated unstable, jerky, or collision-prone paths.
- **Lowest Acceleration-based Smoothness for FM/SafeFlow:** For planar navigation, vanilla FM and SafeFlow yielded the lowest AS scores, indicating smoother velocity profiles. For manipulation, SafeFlow remained competitive.

**D. Comparison between SafeFlow and SafeDiffus (Q2):**
- Both SafeFlow and SafeDiffus achieved 100% safety.
- SafeFlow generally demonstrated better performance in terms of inference time and sometimes smoothness metrics compared to SafeDiffus, which is attributed to the inherent deterministic and often smoother nature of flow matching compared to stochastic diffusion processes. This highlights the particular synergy between FMBFs and Flow Matching.

The main findings conclusively demonstrate that SafeFlow successfully achieves its objectives: it provides robust, provable safety guarantees for robot motion planning while maintaining computational efficiency and generating high-quality, smooth trajectories. It significantly outperforms existing generative safety planners and offers a more efficient alternative to traditional path planners for certain tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SafeFlow represents a significant advancement in the field of safe robot motion planning, with profound theoretical and practical implications.

**A. Addressing a Critical Safety Gap:**
SafeFlow fundamentally addresses the long-standing challenge of integrating formal, provable safety guarantees into modern data-driven generative motion planning frameworks. Unlike previous attempts that offered only probabilistic safety or were computationally prohibitive, SafeFlow delivers 100% collision-free trajectories without compromising efficiency. This is crucial for deploying autonomous robots in safety-critical applications where failure is unacceptable.

**B. Theoretical Breakthrough with Flow Matching Barrier Functions (FMBFs):**
The introduction of Flow Matching Barrier Functions (FMBFs) is a key theoretical contribution. By extending traditional Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) to incorporate "prescribed-time safety" through blow-up functions (`φ1(t)`), FMBFs overcome the fundamental limitation of CBFs—the assumption of an initially safe state—which is problematic for generative models starting from random, unbounded distributions. This innovation paves the way for a new class of safety-aware learning algorithms that are applicable to a wider range of generative processes. The proof of boundedness for the guidance term `u_t` further ensures the numerical stability and practical utility of FMBFs.

**C. Enabling Real-time, Training-Free Safety Enforcement:**
A major practical impact of SafeFlow is its ability to enforce safety in real-time during test-time inference, without requiring the generative model to be retrained for every new environment or obstacle configuration. This "plug-and-play" safety module drastically reduces deployment costs and increases adaptability, making complex, AI-driven robotic systems more flexible and scalable in dynamic, unstructured environments. The efficiency gains from Flow Matching combined with the QP-based safety guidance make SafeFlow suitable for online applications.

**D. Superior Performance and Robustness:**
Empirical results consistently demonstrate SafeFlow's superior performance across diverse tasks, not just in achieving perfect safety rates but also in maintaining competitive inference times and generating smooth, goal-aligned trajectories. This robustness across different robot platforms (planar navigator, 7-DoF manipulator) underscores its generalizability and practical utility compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, many of which exhibited unstable or collision-prone behaviors.

**E. Broadening the Scope of Learning-based Robotics:**
SafeFlow's methodology can accelerate the adoption of learning-based control in high-stakes robotic applications, including:
- **Autonomous Navigation:** Enabling safe movement of drones, self-driving cars, and service robots in dynamic human environments.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** Ensuring safety during collaborative tasks where robots operate in close proximity to humans.
- **Industrial Automation:** Providing safe and efficient manipulation in factories, even with variable layouts or human workers.
- **Exploration and Inspection:** Allowing robots to navigate unknown or hazardous environments safely.

**F. Foundation for Future Research:**
The FMBF concept provides a solid foundation for future research in several directions:
- Applying FMBFs to other generative models beyond flow matching and diffusion, or to other learning paradigms (e.g., reinforcement learning).
- Developing more sophisticated methods for handling composite constraints and ensuring QP feasibility with minimal compromise on trajectory quality.
- Exploring theoretical guarantees for robustness to perception noise and model uncertainties when combined with FMBFs.

In conclusion, SafeFlow represents a crucial step toward building truly autonomous and trustworthy robotic systems. By rigorously integrating safety guarantees with efficient generative modeling, it paves the way for safer, more intelligent, and more adaptable robots in real-world applications.
