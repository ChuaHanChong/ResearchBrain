# Physion-Eval: Evaluating Physical Realism in Generated Video via Human Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Physion-Eval introduces a large-scale, human-reasoning-based benchmark to assess physical realism in generated videos, revealing that leading AI models consistently produce content with pervasive physical glitches. The study demonstrates that expert human judgment is currently superior to state-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models in diagnosing these physical inconsistencies.

## Problem

- Existing video generation evaluation methods primarily focus on perceptual quality (e.g., FVD, CLIP-based scores) and lack fine-grained, diagnostic feedback on physical realism.
- Automated metrics and model-based critics for physical realism often correlate weakly with human judgments and struggle to detect subtle violations of dynamics, contact, and causality.
- Many prior evaluations neglect egocentric (first-person) viewpoints, which are crucial for applications like embodied AI and immersive media, focusing predominantly on exocentric views.

## Method

- Curated a diverse dataset of over 2,400 real-world videos (exocentric and egocentric) and generated 12,718 corresponding videos using five state-of-the-art text-and-image-to-video models.
- Developed Physion-Eval, a human-centric benchmark utilizing 90 expert annotators with **STEM** backgrounds to provide temporally grounded, fine-grained diagnoses of physical glitches, categorized into 22 types, with natural-language explanations.
- Evaluated 10 state-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as automated critics for their ability to detect, localize, and explain physical realism failures, comparing their performance against human perception and expert diagnosis.

## Results

- Expert human annotation revealed 83.3% of exocentric and 93.5% of egocentric generated videos contained at least one physical glitch.
- Untrained human viewers achieved Youden's J statistics ranging from 24.9-37.1% (exocentric) and 48.4-61.8% (egocentric) for glitch detection, while the best MLLM critics achieved only 19.1% (exocentric) and 9.8% (egocentric).
- The most common physical failure categories identified were 'temporal coherence breakdown' and 'material/state inconsistency' in exocentric videos, and 'temporal coherence breakdown' and 'object permanence violations' in egocentric videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art video generation models, despite their visual advancements, exhibit pervasive physical realism failures, with 83.3% of exocentric and 93.5% of egocentric generated videos containing at least one identifiable physical glitch.
> - Expert human reasoning is essential for accurately diagnosing subtle physical violations in generated videos, demonstrating a significant gap where untrained human viewers reliably detect issues that MLLM critics largely miss.
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs show significant limitations in complex visual-physical reasoning, often hallucinating explanations, incorrectly localizing glitches, and failing to match human performance in detecting physically inconsistent events.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation models has seen rapid advancements, with systems such as Veo 3.1 and Sora 2 demonstrating increasing capabilities in synthesizing visually coherent scenes. These models are progressively being leveraged not merely for visual synthesis but as "world simulators" across diverse applications, including filmmaking, interactive simulation, and embodied artificial intelligence (AI). A critical prerequisite for these applications is that generated videos not only appear visually plausible but also adhere to the physical laws governing the real world. Objects must persist, forces must produce expected outcomes, and events must unfold with coherent causal structures.

Existing evaluation methodologies for video generation quality typically focus on metrics such as Frechet Video Distance (FVD) for distributional realism, Frechet Video Motion Distance (FVMD) for motion coherence, and CLIP-based similarity for prompt alignment. While some benchmarks like VideoPhy, PhyGenBench, and PhyWorldBench have begun to address physical realism, they largely rely on automated metrics or model-based critics. These automated methods often exhibit weak correlation with human judgments and struggle to detect subtle violations of dynamics, contact, and causality that require precise spatial and temporal reasoning. Furthermore, much of the prior work primarily focuses on exocentric (third-person) viewpoints, leaving egocentric (first-person) scenarios, which are crucial for immersive media and embodied AI systems, largely unexplored.

This research addresses these gaps by introducing Physion-Eval, a large-scale benchmark designed to evaluate perceptual physical realism in generated videos using expert human reasoning. It distinguishes itself by providing fine-grained, temporally localized annotations of physical glitches, accompanied by structured failure categories and natural-language explanations. By incorporating both exocentric and egocentric perspectives and leveraging human diagnostic expertise, Physion-Eval aims to establish a more rigorous standard for evaluating physical realism, moving beyond coarse perceptual quality assessments to provide actionable diagnostic feedback. This work positions itself as a critical step toward developing video generation models that are not only visually compelling but also physically grounded and suitable for demanding simulation and AI applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a detailed and diagnostically rich benchmark for evaluating physical realism in videos generated by advanced AI models. This benchmark, named Physion-Eval, is designed to enable a comprehensive understanding of *when* and *why* generated video dynamics deviate from real-world physical constraints.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations and needs:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Visual Plausibility and Physical Fidelity:** While modern video generation models demonstrate increasing sophistication in visual synthesis, their ability to simulate dynamic physical worlds that adhere to fundamental physical laws is not adequately understood or evaluated. Visual aesthetics alone are insufficient for applications requiring accurate world simulation, such as embodied AI or scientific modeling. The research is motivated by the necessity for generated content to respect principles like object persistence, plausible force responses, and coherent causal structures.

2. **Limitations of Existing Evaluation Metrics:** Current evaluation methods, including automated metrics (e.g., FVD, CLIP-based scores) and broad human preference judgments, are primarily geared towards assessing perceptual quality or general motion continuity. These methods offer limited diagnostic utility; they indicate *that* a video might be unrealistic but provide insufficient insight into the specific physical principles violated, the temporal localization of these failures, or the underlying reasons. This lack of fine-grained feedback impedes targeted improvements in video generation models.

3. **Lack of Fine-Grained Human Reasoning Data:** The paper argues that subtle physical violations, such as incorrect contact dynamics or implausible material behavior, often require sophisticated spatial and temporal reasoning that automated metrics or even untrained human observers might miss or struggle to articulate. There is a clear need for a benchmark that captures expert human reasoning to diagnose these failures precisely.

4. **Inadequate Coverage of Egocentric Perspectives:** Many existing evaluations focus on exocentric viewpoints. However, for applications like embodied AI or virtual reality, egocentric perspectives are crucial, and physical consistency in first-person views presents unique challenges for generation models. The study aims to cover both viewpoints to provide a holistic assessment.

5. **Benchmarking MLLM Critic Capabilities:** With the rise of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), there is an emerging trend to use these models as automated critics for video evaluation. A key motivation is to assess the reliability of these MLLM critics in detecting and explaining physical realism failures, comparing their performance against human perception and expert diagnosis. This comparison helps to reveal the current limitations of automated evaluation in complex reasoning tasks.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to provide a robust tool for diagnosing weaknesses in current video generation models and guiding the development of future models towards greater physical coherence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach involving extensive video curation, two complementary human evaluation studies, and an assessment of Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) critics.

#### 3.1. Video Source Curation

The study curated a dataset of real-world videos to serve as conditioning inputs for generating new videos, ensuring diversity in physical phenomena and viewpoints.

- **Exocentric Videos:** 1,734 videos were sourced from WISA-80K, a dataset comprising 17 fundamental physical phenomena (dynamics, thermodynamics, optics). Manual filtering was applied to remove low-quality samples, including multi-shot clips, temporally reversed content, near-duplicates, videos with overlays, synthetic content, and incorrectly labeled physics categories. The dataset was then balanced to achieve uniform coverage across the physical categories.
- **Egocentric Videos:** 752 short clips (4–9 seconds) were extracted from EPIC-KITCHENS, a large-scale egocentric dataset. Action segments, identified by verb labels, were mapped to five physical categories (Rigid-Body Interaction, Deformation & Fracture, Soft Materials & Mixing, Fluid & Granular Flow, Thermal & Frictional Effects) aligned with the WISA taxonomy. Captions for these clips were generated using Gemini 2.5 Pro and subsequently reviewed by humans for accuracy.
- **Generated Videos:** For each real-world source video, its caption and the first visually non-black frame were used as conditioning inputs for five state-of-the-art text-and-image-to-video (TI2V) models: Sora 2, Veo 3.1 fast, Kling 2.5, Hailuo 2.3, and Wan 2.2. A total of 12,718 generated videos were produced. All videos were standardized to a 16:9 aspect ratio, 720×1280 resolution (center-cropped), and audio was removed to focus on visual cues.

#### 3.2. Human Evaluation of Physical Realism

Two distinct human studies were conducted to capture different aspects of physical realism.

- **Perceptual Detection by Ordinary Viewers:**
 - **Objective:** To assess the detectability of physical glitches by untrained individuals, representing a typical audience.
 - **Setup:** 16 untrained viewers participated. An evaluation set of 1,121 generated videos (pre-screened to contain clear glitches) was selected from 1,500 source videos. For each generation model, viewers evaluated 100 exocentric and 100 egocentric videos from a randomly ordered 1:1 mix of real and generated clips. Viewers judged whether each clip appeared "physically realistic" based solely on visual evidence. To prevent bias, real and generated videos from the same source were never shown to the same viewer.
 - **Metric:** Youden's J statistic (J_h_G = π_h_R - π_h_G) was used to quantify the drop in perceived physical realism from real to generated videos. Higher J values indicate greater perceptual degradation in generated content.
- **Physion-Eval Expert Reasoning Benchmark:**
 - **Objective:** To create a dataset of temporally grounded, taxonomy-based diagnoses of physical realism failures using domain expertise.
 - **Annotators:** 90 expert annotators, all with bachelor's degrees in STEM fields and formal undergraduate physics training, underwent six training sessions. The top 38 performers were promoted to senior experts.
 - **Annotation Protocol:** A three-expert workflow was implemented: two experts independently annotated each video, and a third senior expert adjudicated any disagreements to produce the final annotation. For each identified physical glitch, annotators provided:
 1. **Glitch Presence:** A binary judgment (True/False).
 2. **Temporal Grounding:** Precise timestamp localization (0.1-second precision) of the failure.
 3. **Glitch Classification:** Assignment of a failure category from a predefined taxonomy of 22 fine-grained physical categories (e.g., Object Permanence Violation, Temporal Coherence Breakdown, Material/State Inconsistency, Contact/Interaction Failure, Causal Sequence Violation, Force & Motion Inconsistency, Geometric/Collision Violation, Other Failures).
 4. **Reasoning:** A natural-language explanation of the violated physical behavior.
 5. **Severity Score:** A 1–5 rating reflecting the perceptual severity of the glitch.
 - **Dataset Size:** The benchmark comprises 10,990 expert reasoning traces across the 12,718 generated videos.

#### 3.3. MLLM Critic Evaluation

The study benchmarked 10 state-of-the-art MLLMs (including GPT-5.2, Gemini family models, Claude-4.5 Opus, Qwen-3-VL, and Cosmos Reason 1/2) to evaluate their ability to detect and reason about physical realism failures, using specific prompts for both binary detection and detailed reasoning. Ablation studies in the appendix investigated the impact of temporal sampling rate and explicit "thinking" on MLLM performance, as well as the effects of physical intensity and dynamics on both human and MLLM perception.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings regarding the physical realism of generated videos and the capabilities of both human and MLLM evaluators.

#### 4.1. Pervasive Physical Glitches in Generated Videos

A central finding is the high prevalence of physical realism failures in videos generated by current state-of-the-art models. The expert human annotation revealed that:
- 83.3% of exocentric generated videos
- 93.5% of egocentric generated videos
exhibit at least one human-identifiable physical glitch. This indicates a significant gap between the visual fidelity and the physical coherence of these models' outputs in physics-critical scenarios.

#### 4.2. Discrepancy Between Human Perception and MLLM Critics

- **Untrained Human Sensitivity:** Ordinary untrained human viewers reliably detected physical inconsistencies in generated videos. Their Youden's J statistic ranged from 24.9% to 37.1% for exocentric videos and 48.4% to 61.8% for egocentric videos. The higher detection rate in egocentric videos suggests that first-person views may make physical violations more perceptually salient or that current models struggle more with egocentric dynamics.
- **MLLM Critic Limitations:** In contrast, state-of-the-art MLLM critics performed significantly worse. The best-performing MLLMs achieved J statistics of only 19.1% (exocentric) and 9.8% (egocentric). This indicates that MLLMs frequently classify generated videos as physically realistic even when clear violations are present.
- **Qualitative MLLM Failures:** Qualitative analysis (Figure 5, Appendix Figure 12) demonstrated systematic discrepancies between MLLM and human reasoning. MLLMs often produced hallucinated explanations, attributed failures to non-existent artifacts (e.g., "shadow artifact"), and provided incorrect temporal localization for glitches. They struggled with temporally grounded reasoning and reliable causal attribution, particularly for violations unfolding over time.
- **Limited Impact of Enhancements:** Ablation studies (Appendix Table 3 & 4) showed that increasing temporal sampling rates or enabling explicit "thinking" mechanisms in MLLMs provided minimal or no significant improvement in their ability to detect physical glitches. This suggests a fundamental limitation in their visual encoding or reasoning capabilities for fine-grained physical events.
- **Sensitivity to Physical Dynamics:** Untrained human judgments were more strongly influenced by the dynamics of physical processes than their intensity, with higher J statistics observed in scenarios with higher dynamics (Appendix Figure 7). MLLMs, however, largely failed to detect glitches regardless of the intensity or dynamics, though some modest sensitivity emerged at very high intensity/dynamics levels (Appendix Figure 8).

#### 4.3. Diagnostic Analysis of Video Generation Models

The Physion-Eval benchmark enabled a detailed diagnosis of common failure modes across the evaluated models (Figure 6, Table 2):
- **Dominant Failure Categories:**
 - In the **exocentric** setting, "temporal coherence breakdown" and "material/state inconsistency" were the most common failure modes.
 - In the **egocentric** setting, "temporal coherence breakdown" and "object permanence violations" were predominant.
- **Model-Specific Performance:**
 - Kling 2.5 exhibited the lowest glitch density (1.15 glitches/video) and severity (2.69) in the exocentric setting.
 - Sora 2 showed the lowest glitch density (1.23 glitches/video) and severity (2.81) in the egocentric view, despite its high failure rate.
 - Wan 2.2, an open-source model, surprisingly demonstrated the lowest failure rate (83.5%) in the egocentric setting.
- **Glitch Density and Severity:** Across models, generated videos contained an average of 1.15 to 1.92 glitches per video, indicating that multiple physical inconsistencies often occur within a single short clip. The average severity scores ranged from 2.69 to 3.86 (on a 1-5 scale), suggesting that these failures are often substantial and clearly implausible.
- **Qualitative Failure Patterns:** Models showed greater difficulty in physics-demanding scenarios involving non-trivial interactions, irreversible state changes, or complex system-level transitions (e.g., hydraulic press deformations, post-impact outcomes), suggesting a reliance on surface-level visual correlations rather than compositional physical understanding (Appendix Figure 11).

These findings collectively highlight that while video generation models have achieved considerable visual sophistication, the accurate simulation of real-world physics remains a significant challenge. Human reasoning, especially expert diagnosis, serves as the current gold standard for identifying and explaining these subtle yet critical failures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Physion-Eval and the accompanying findings hold several significant implications for the fields of video generation, AI evaluation, and physical AI development.

1. **Setting a New Evaluation Standard:** Physion-Eval establishes a novel, large-scale, human-centered benchmark for evaluating physical realism in generated videos. It moves beyond traditional automated metrics and coarse human preferences by providing fine-grained, temporally localized annotations, structured failure categories, and natural-language explanations of violated physical principles. This level of detail offers a more diagnostic and actionable evaluation compared to prior approaches.

2. **Quantifying the Physical Realism Gap:** The study quantitatively demonstrates a substantial gap in physical realism, revealing that a high percentage of videos generated by leading models exhibit human-identifiable physical glitches. This highlights a critical limitation of current models, which, despite advancements in visual fidelity, often fail to produce physically coherent dynamics. This insight is crucial for temperating expectations regarding the current capabilities of video generation models as "world simulators."

3. **Revealing MLLM Critic Limitations:** The research uncovers a significant performance disparity between human perception and current MLLM critics in detecting and explaining physical realism failures. MLLMs' tendency to hallucinate explanations and mislocalize glitches indicates that they are not yet reliable surrogates for human judgment in complex physical reasoning tasks. This finding has broad implications for the development of automated AI evaluation systems, suggesting a need for more robust, visually grounded reasoning capabilities in MLLMs.

4. **Guiding Physics-Grounded Video Generation:** The diagnostic nature of Physion-Eval's annotations provides invaluable feedback for model developers. By pinpointing *when* and *why* physical violations occur (e.g., temporal coherence breakdown, object permanence issues, material inconsistencies), the benchmark can directly inform targeted improvements in model architectures, training data, and learning objectives. This could facilitate the development of new models that prioritize physical consistency alongside visual quality.

5. **Enabling Future Research Directions:**
 - **Reasoning-Driven Diagnostics:** The detailed temporal reasoning traces can be used to develop new diagnostic tools for video generation, allowing researchers to precisely identify and rectify failure modes.
 - **Training Physically Grounded Critics:** The expert annotations can serve as a valuable dataset for training new multimodal critics that are specifically designed to detect, localize, and explain physical inconsistencies, potentially bridging the observed gap between human and MLLM evaluation.
 - **Iterative Model Refinement:** The structured failure signals provided by Physion-Eval could enable closed-loop, self-improving video generation systems. Models could iteratively generate, diagnose against the benchmark, and refine their outputs to achieve greater alignment with physical reality, ultimately leading to more robust world models for applications like embodied AI, robotics, and scientific simulation.

Despite acknowledged limitations (focus on single dominant interactions, reliance on visually observable cues, and inherent subjectivity in human judgments), Physion-Eval represents a foundational contribution towards building physically coherent and reliable AI-generated dynamic content.
