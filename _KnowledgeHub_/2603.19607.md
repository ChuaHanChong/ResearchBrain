# Physion-Eval: Evaluating Physical Realism in Generated Video via Human Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Physion-Eval introduces a large-scale, human-reasoning-based benchmark to assess physical realism in generated videos, revealing that leading AI models consistently produce content with pervasive physical glitches. The study demonstrates that expert human judgment is currently superior to state-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models in diagnosing these physical inconsistencies.

## Problem

- Existing video generation evaluation methods primarily focus on perceptual quality (e.g., FVD, CLIP-based scores) and lack fine-grained, diagnostic feedback on physical realism.
- Automated metrics and model-based critics for physical realism often correlate weakly with human judgments and struggle to detect subtle violations of dynamics, contact, and causality.
- Many prior evaluations neglect egocentric (first-person) viewpoints, which are crucial for applications like embodied AI and immersive media, focusing predominantly on exocentric views.

## Method

- Curated a diverse dataset of over 2,400 real-world videos (exocentric and egocentric) and generated 12,718 corresponding videos using five state-of-the-art text-and-image-to-video models.
- Developed Physion-Eval, a human-centric benchmark utilizing 90 expert annotators with **STEM** backgrounds to provide temporally grounded, fine-grained diagnoses of physical glitches, categorized into 22 types, with natural-language explanations.
- Evaluated 10 state-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as automated critics for their ability to detect, localize, and explain physical realism failures, comparing their performance against human perception and expert diagnosis.

## Results

- Expert human annotation revealed 83.3% of exocentric and 93.5% of egocentric generated videos contained at least one physical glitch.
- Untrained human viewers achieved Youden's J statistics ranging from 24.9-37.1% (exocentric) and 48.4-61.8% (egocentric) for glitch detection, while the best MLLM critics achieved only 19.1% (exocentric) and 9.8% (egocentric).
- The most common physical failure categories identified were 'temporal coherence breakdown' and 'material/state inconsistency' in exocentric videos, and 'temporal coherence breakdown' and 'object permanence violations' in egocentric videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art video generation models, despite their visual advancements, exhibit pervasive physical realism failures, with 83.3% of exocentric and 93.5% of egocentric generated videos containing at least one identifiable physical glitch.
> - Expert human reasoning is essential for accurately diagnosing subtle physical violations in generated videos, demonstrating a significant gap where untrained human viewers reliably detect issues that MLLM critics largely miss.
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs show significant limitations in complex visual-physical reasoning, often hallucinating explanations, incorrectly localizing glitches, and failing to match human performance in detecting physically inconsistent events.
