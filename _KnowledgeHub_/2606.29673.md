# Privacy-Preserving Decentralized Cooperative Localization with Range-Only Measurements: A Convex Optimization Based Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> A decentralized cooperative localization framework leverages convex optimization and a bounded noise model to enable multi-robot systems to estimate positions accurately. It maintains privacy by exchanging only abstract dual variables, achieving localization performance comparable to a centralized optimal solution in GPS-denied environments.

## Problem

- Multi-robot systems operating in GPS-denied environments require robust localization without compromising operational privacy, as explicit position sharing creates security vulnerabilities.
- Current privacy-preserving methods face trade-offs: perturbation-based approaches reduce accuracy, while cryptography-based methods incur prohibitive computational overhead.
- Standard localization often assumes specific noise models (e.g., Gaussian), which are frequently insufficient for real-world, non-Gaussian range measurements where noise is better characterized by bounds.

## Method

- Formulate the cooperative localization problem as a ==Semi-Definite Program (SDP)== for each robot, assuming strictly bounded measurement noise instead of probabilistic distributions.
- Utilize novel ==intersection-plane constraints== derived from landmark range measurements to tighten each robot's local feasible region, represented by a ==Maximum-Volume Inscribed Ellipsoid (MVE)==.
- Develop a **decentralized framework** where robots iteratively refine their position estimates by exchanging only ==abstract dual variables==, ensuring privacy without sharing explicit spatial coordinates.

## Results

- The Decentralized Cooperative Localization (DCL) framework achieved localization accuracy comparable to a centralized optimal solution in 3D Monte Carlo simulations.
- DCL significantly reduced residual localization error and increased the probability of errors falling within operational tolerances compared to baselines relying only on spherical or planar bounds.
- The framework demonstrated computational practicality with an average computation time of **0.145 seconds** per robot, suitable for real-time applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing global coupling constraints and allowing robots to exchange only dual variables enables cooperative localization while preserving privacy by design.
> - Modeling measurement uncertainty with strictly bounded noise and solving via convex optimization (SDP) offers robust position estimates, even with complex, non-Gaussian sensor errors.
> - Localizing robots by containing their position within a Maximum-Volume Inscribed Ellipsoid, refined by both spherical and newly introduced intersection-plane constraints from landmarks, improves precision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of multi-robot systems in environments lacking Global Positioning System (GPS) signals, such as underwater, indoor, or subterranean settings, necessitates robust spatial awareness for autonomous operation. Traditional robotic navigation relies on fusing proprioceptive data (e.g., odometry, IMU) with absolute position updates, typically from GPS outdoors. In GPS-denied scenarios, cooperative localization using range-based relative measurements from sensors like acoustic modems or Ultra-Wideband (UWB) radios serves as a vital alternative for generating stable and divergence-free position estimates.

However, a significant challenge in these cooperative networks is the issue of spatial privacy. Conventional cooperative localization architectures often require robots to continuously broadcast their estimated positions, making them vulnerable to information leakage. This susceptibility allows adversaries to track fleet trajectories, posing privacy and security risks for sensitive applications in defense, resource extraction, or emerging Urban Air Mobility (UAM) networks.

Current research on privacy-preserving range-based cooperative localization primarily falls into two categories, each with limitations. Perturbation-based methods, such as geo-indistinguishability or differential privacy, achieve privacy by injecting artificial noise into shared data, which inherently compromises estimation accuracy. In contrast, cryptography-based techniques, like homomorphic encryption or secret sharing, maintain data fidelity but impose substantial computational and communication overheads, rendering them impractical for real-time, resource-constrained robot swarms.

Another limitation in much of the existing localization literature is the reliance on specific probabilistic noise models, commonly assuming zero-mean Gaussian errors. Real-world range measurements, particularly in challenging environments like underwater acoustics, often exhibit non-Gaussian characteristics such as asymmetric, heavy-tailed noise due to multipath fading or non-line-of-sight (NLOS) conditions. In such contexts, bounding the noise is often more feasible than precisely characterizing its statistical distribution.

This work addresses these identified gaps by proposing a novel framework that simultaneously aims to achieve high-accuracy, privacy-preserving cooperative localization. It departs from probabilistic noise models by assuming strictly bounded measurement noise and formulates the localization problem using convex optimization, specifically Semi-Definite Programming (SDP). Unlike traditional SDP approaches that typically require a centralized solver—which introduces scalability bottlenecks and privacy risks—this research develops a decentralized method that avoids the transmission of explicit spatial coordinates, thereby natively preserving privacy without the drawbacks of noise injection or heavy cryptography.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing demand for multi-robot system deployment in GPS-denied and unstructured environments, coupled with the critical need to ensure operational privacy and security.

**Motivation:**
1. **Requirement for Robust Spatial Awareness:** Multi-robot systems operating in challenging domains (e.g., underwater, subterranean, indoor) need accurate and stable self-localization to perform autonomous tasks safely. Traditional methods relying on GPS are not viable, making cooperative localization with range-based sensors essential.
2. **Privacy Vulnerability in Traditional Cooperative Localization:** Standard cooperative approaches necessitate the sharing of explicit spatial coordinates among robots. This data exchange creates a severe security vulnerability, as adversaries can intercept and track robot movements, compromising the privacy of sensitive missions (e.g., military operations, industrial inspections, urban air mobility).
3. **Limitations of Existing Privacy-Preserving Techniques:**
 - **Accuracy Degradation:** Perturbation-based methods (e.g., differential privacy) inject noise to protect privacy, which inherently reduces the accuracy of localization estimates.
 - **Computational and Communication Overhead:** Cryptography-based methods (e.g., homomorphic encryption) preserve data fidelity but introduce prohibitive computational and communication burdens, making them unsuitable for real-time, resource-constrained robot swarms.
4. **Inapplicability of Standard Noise Models:** Many localization algorithms assume specific noise models (e.g., Gaussian errors). However, real-world range measurements often exhibit complex, non-Gaussian noise characteristics (e.g., asymmetric, heavy-tailed due to multipath). It is frequently more practical to define strict bounds on measurement noise than to characterize its precise distribution.
5. **Scalability and Privacy Issues of Centralized SDP:** While Semi-Definite Programming (SDP) offers robust localization with bounded noise, traditional SDP formulations require a central solver that aggregates all robot positions. This centralized architecture creates scalability bottlenecks and inherent privacy risks.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Develop a Natively Privacy-Preserving Decentralized Cooperative Localization (DCL) Framework:** The core objective is to design an approach where robots can cooperate to localize accurately without ever transmitting their explicit spatial coordinates, thus ensuring operational privacy by design.
2. **Overcome Limitations of Existing Privacy Methods:** To achieve privacy without degrading accuracy (as in perturbation methods) or incurring high computational costs (as in cryptographic methods).
3. **Utilize a Bounded Noise Model:** To formulate the localization problem assuming strictly bounded measurement noise, providing robustness against unpredictable anomalies often found in real-world environments.
4. **Employ Convex Optimization for Robustness:** Formulate the localization problem as a Semi-Definite Program (SDP) to compute a Maximum-Volume Inscribed Ellipsoid (MVE) for each robot. The MVE's center provides the position estimate, and its shape matrix quantifies localization uncertainty.
5. **Introduce Novel Constraints for Feasible Region Tightening:** Develop intersection-plane constraints derived from landmark measurements to significantly refine and reduce the individual feasible spatial bounds of each robot.
6. **Securely Incorporate Inter-Robot Measurements:** Decompose the network-coupling constraints from inter-robot range measurements into localized Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs) that can be handled decentralizably.
7. **Achieve Consensus via Dual Variable Exchange:** Enable agents to reach fleet-wide spatial consensus by iteratively exchanging only abstract dual variables (mathematical penalty signals), ensuring that no primal position estimates are shared directly.
8. **Benchmark and Validate Performance:** Conduct extensive simulations to demonstrate that the proposed DCL framework achieves high accuracy, comparable to a centralized optimal solution, while guaranteeing privacy and maintaining scalable, parallelizable computation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology constructs a privacy-preserving decentralized cooperative localization framework using convex optimization. The core idea is to bound the robot's estimated position within a Maximum-Volume Inscribed Ellipsoid (MVE) based on range-only measurements, without explicitly sharing position information.

**Problem Formulation:**
The system comprises `N` robots and `M` static landmarks in a 3D space (`R^3`). `r_i` denotes the unknown position of robot `i`, and `B_k` is the known position of landmark `k`. Each robot `i` has a sensing range, defining sets `N_A_i` (visible landmarks) and `N_R_i` (visible neighboring robots).

1. **Robot-Landmark Measurements:** For a measurement between robot `i` and landmark `j`, the true distance `∥r_i - B_j∥` is assumed to lie within a measured interval `[ρ_ij, ¯ρ_ij]`.
 - Upper bounds `¯ρ_ij` define convex outer-sphere constraints.
 - Lower bounds `ρ_ij` are used to construct planar constraints that tighten the feasible set.
2. **Robot-Robot Measurements:** For a measurement between robots `i` and `ℓ`, the true distance `∥r_i - r_ℓ∥` is bounded by `ρ_iℓ`. Only upper bounds are used to ensure convexity.
3. **Objective:** The goal is to estimate each robot's 3D coordinates `r_c_i` (center of the ellipsoid) and its corresponding shape matrix `P_i`. The ellipsoid `E_i` is defined as `{r ∈ R^3 | r = P_i u + r_c_i, ∥u∥ ≤ 1}`. The volume of the ellipsoid is proportional to `det(P_i)`, so the problem minimizes `−log det(P_i)` for all robots, subject to `E_i` being contained within the feasible regions defined by sensor measurements.

**Construction of the Spatial Feasible Region:**
The feasible region for each robot is constructed in three steps, with all constraints formulated as convex Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs).

1. **Spherical Upper Bounds from Landmark Measurements:**
 - Each `¯ρ_ij` defines an outer-sphere centered at `B_j`. The robot's position must be within the intersection of these spheres.
 - Containment of ellipsoid `E_i` within a sphere `{∥r - B_j∥ ≤ ¯ρ_ij}` is formulated using the S-procedure and Schur complement, resulting in an LMI (Equation 5). This is applied for all `j ∈ N_A_i`.

2. **Feasible Region Tightening via Intersection Planes:**
 - Lower-bound measurements `ρ_ij` define exclusion zones. The exact region combining upper and lower bounds is non-convex.
 - A convex relaxation is achieved using "intersection-plane constraints." For robot `i` measuring to `B_j` (`∥r_i - B_j∥ ≥ ρ_ij`) and `B_k` (`∥r_i - B_k∥ ≤ ¯ρ_ik`), the intersection of their respective spherical boundaries forms a plane.
 - Subtracting the squared distance inequalities `∥r_i - B_j∥^2 ≥ ρ_ij^2` and `∥r_i - B_k∥^2 ≤ ¯ρ_ik^2` yields a linear inequality `n_jk^T r_i ≤ γ_jk` (Equation 13), defining a half-space.
 - A set `P_i` of valid landmark pairs `(j, k)` is defined where intersection is geometrically meaningful.
 - Containment of `E_i` within this half-space (`n_jk^T r_i ≤ γ_jk`) is formulated as an LMI using Schur complement (Equation 18).

3. **Enabling Cooperation via Inter-Robot Coupling:**
 - Inter-robot upper bound `ρ_iℓ ≥ ∥r_c_i - r_c_ℓ∥` provides coupling.
 - This inequality is squared and then reformulated into a standard LMI (Equation 21) using the Schur complement, linking the position variables of robots `i` and `ℓ`.

**Privacy-Preserving Decentralized Cooperative Localization (DCL):**

1. **Centralized Oracle (CO):** As a theoretical optimal benchmark, a centralized SDP is defined. It minimizes the sum of `-log det(P_i)` for all robots, subject to all landmark-based LMIs (5, 18) and all inter-robot coupling LMIs (21) simultaneously. This centralized approach provides the best possible performance but is impractical for privacy and scalability.

2. **Decentralized Cooperative Localization (DCL) Framework:**
 - **Decomposition of Coupling Constraints:** To enable decentralized operation while preserving privacy, the global inter-robot coupling constraint (21) is explicitly decomposed. An auxiliary matrix variable `R_iℓ = R_ℓi ∈ R^(4x4)` is introduced for each edge `(i, ℓ)`. The original coupling LMI is split into two independent local constraints (26, 27), each involving `R_iℓ` with complementary signs based on lexicographical ordering (`i < ℓ` vs `i > ℓ`). This decomposition is sufficient to recover the original constraint when summed, but it restricts the optimization space, making the CO a theoretical lower bound for DCL.
 - **Local SDPs:** Each robot `i` solves its own local SDP, minimizing `-log det(P_i)` subject to its local landmark constraints (5, 18) and its part of the split coupling constraints (26) for all neighbors `ℓ ∈ N_R_i`.
 - **Dual Variable Exchange:** After solving its local SDP, robot `i` does *not* broadcast its position `r_c_i`. Instead, it exchanges only the dual variable matrix `Λ_iℓ` corresponding to its local coupling constraints (31) with its neighbors `ℓ`.
 - **Shared Matrix Update:** Robots then update the shared auxiliary matrices `R_iℓ` using a step size `α` and the difference between their respective dual variables (`Λ_iℓ - Λ_ℓi`) (Equation 32). This ensures consistency of `R_iℓ` across robots.
 - **Privacy Guarantee:** This iterative process achieves consensus on `R_iℓ` and ultimately refines individual position estimates without any robot directly revealing its spatial coordinates, ensuring native privacy.

3. **Theoretical Justification via Subgradient Primal Decomposition:**
 - The framework's convergence and optimality properties are justified through subgradient primal decomposition. Local value functions `ϕ_i(R_i)` are defined for each robot's local SDP, given the fixed edge matrices `R_i`.
 - The network-level optimization is reconstructed as a global master problem `min Σ ϕ_i(R_i)`.
 - The global master problem is a conservative approximation of the Centralized Oracle (CO) because the introduction of `R_iℓ` effectively restricts the feasible space.
 - Under Slater's condition (ensured by bounded noise), strong duality applies, and the optimal local dual multipliers (`Λ_iℓ`, `Λ_ℓi`) serve as subgradients for the local value functions.
 - Summing these local subgradients yields the exact subgradient for the global master problem (`Λ_iℓ - Λ_ℓi`).
 - The DCL primal update rule for `R_iℓ` (Equation 37) is derived directly from performing a subgradient descent step on the master problem, systematically closing the optimality gap with the CO.
 - To prevent infeasibility at startup (especially when `R_iℓ` are initialized to zero), non-negative local slack variables are introduced into the inter-robot range measurements, temporarily relaxing constraints to bootstrap the optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper evaluates the proposed Decentralized Cooperative Localization (DCL) framework through extensive 3D Monte Carlo simulations. The implementations were carried out in Julia, utilizing JuMP for model formulation and MOSEK as the SDP solver. The simulations aimed to demonstrate the accuracy and privacy-preserving capabilities of DCL.

**Simulation Setup:**
- **Network Configuration:** A network of 10 mobile robots and 15 to 20 static landmarks was simulated.
- **Sensor Range:** Each robot was equipped with a sensor having a maximum range of 50 meters (`R_s = 50 m`).
- **Measurement Noise:** Ground-truth Euclidean distances were corrupted with additive noise margins. Specifically, the upper-bound landmark range `¯ρ_ij`, lower-bound landmark range `ρ_ij`, and inter-robot range `ρ_iℓ` were generated with noise margins `σ_o = σ_i = σ_c = 0.2`.
- **Trials:** Performance was evaluated over 100 random trials, with randomized landmark and robot positions. A connectivity constraint ensured each robot connected to at least three neighbors (`|N_R_i| ≥ 3`).
- **Metrics:** Absolute position error (`e_i = ∥r_c_i - r_i*∥^2`) and total computation time were measured.
- **DCL Parameters:** The DCL results were obtained using `K = 5` consensus iterations and a step size `α = 15`. Initialization of shared resource matrices (`R_iℓ`) was set to zero, and non-negative local slack variables were used to ensure local SDP feasibility at startup.

**Baselines for Comparison:**
1. **Spherical Bounds (SB):** This baseline, adapted from existing literature, relies solely on the upper-bound spherical constraints (Equation 5) derived from landmark measurements. Each robot solves a localized SDP minimizing `-log det P_i` subject to these constraints.
2. **Spherical Bounds and Planar Bounds (SB+PB):** This intermediate formulation incorporates both the upper-bound spherical constraints (Equation 5) and the intersection-plane constraints (Equation 18) derived from static landmarks. Each robot solves a localized SDP using these combined constraints.
3. **Centralized Oracle (CO):** This represents the theoretical optimal baseline, where a single solver has access to all network data and simultaneously optimizes all robot positions and uncertainties using all landmark and inter-robot constraints.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Qualitative Accuracy Improvement:**
 - **SB Baseline:** Exhibits pronounced displacement errors in position estimates, indicating significant uncertainty without tighter constraints or cooperation (Fig. 5a).
 - **SB+PB:** Incorporating local intersection-plane constraints substantially reduces these inaccuracies, leading to better constrained position estimates. However, robots with insufficient local landmark measurements still show considerable localization uncertainty (Fig. 5b).
 - **DCL:** By integrating inter-robot coupling constraints, the DCL method effectively compensates for sparse local landmark data. It tightly bounds the true positions, corrects estimates for previously uncertain robots, and achieves accuracy approaching that of the centralized oracle (Fig. 5c).
 - **CO:** Provides the tightest possible bounds and most accurate estimates (Fig. 5d).

2. **Quantitative Error Distribution and CDF:**
 - **Error Reduction:** The error distribution across 100 trials (Fig. 6) quantitatively confirms that SB+PB outperforms SB. Critically, DCL systematically minimizes the residual localization error, showing a distribution significantly closer to that of the CO.
 - **CDF Analysis:** The Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of the positioning error (Fig. 7a) demonstrates DCL's robust effectiveness. It shows a significantly higher probability of the error falling within stringent operational tolerances compared to the baseline methods, closely matching the performance of the Centralized Oracle. This suggests that DCL can achieve near-optimal accuracy while preserving privacy.

3. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - The DCL framework, with `K = 5` consensus iterations and `α = 15`, achieved an average computation time of 0.145 seconds per robot (Fig. 7b). This indicates that the proposed method is computationally practical for real-time applications.
 - The paper notes that increasing iterations improves accuracy but increases latency, while tuning the step size `α` affects convergence speed and stability, highlighting a tunable trade-off between precision and real-time applicability.

In summary, the simulation results validate that the proposed DCL framework significantly improves localization accuracy over non-cooperative and partially constrained decentralized baselines. It achieves performance comparable to a centralized optimal solution while ensuring privacy through the exchange of only abstract dual variables, all within practical computational limits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of multi-robot localization, particularly in addressing critical challenges related to privacy, robustness, and scalability in GPS-denied environments.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Native Privacy Preservation:** The most significant contribution is the development of a cooperative localization framework that intrinsically preserves agent privacy. By exchanging only abstract dual variables—mathematical penalty signals that cannot be inverted to reveal explicit spatial coordinates—the framework bypasses the need for privacy-degrading noise injection (common in perturbation-based methods) or computationally intensive cryptographic protocols. This "privacy-by-design" approach offers a practical and secure solution for sensitive missions.
2. **Robustness to Noise Model Uncertainty:** The departure from specific probabilistic noise models (e.g., Gaussian) in favor of strictly bounded measurement noise enhances the robustness of the localization algorithm. This is particularly valuable in real-world environments where noise characteristics can be complex, asymmetric, or heavy-tailed, such as in underwater acoustic ranging, and are often better characterized by bounds than distributions. The convex optimization formulation provides deterministic, robust feasible sets.
3. **Enhanced Localization Accuracy:**
 - The introduction of novel intersection-plane constraints, derived from combined lower and upper range measurements to landmarks, substantially tightens the feasible region for individual robots. This inherently improves the precision of the estimated position and uncertainty ellipsoids.
 - The cooperative aspect, enabled by securely incorporating inter-robot range measurements, allows robots with sparse landmark visibility to refine their estimates by leveraging relative distance data from better-localized peers, leading to network-wide accuracy improvements comparable to a centralized oracle.
4. **Scalability and Parallelizability:** The decentralized architecture ensures that the computational complexity for each robot's local SDP depends only on its number of neighbors and visible landmarks, not the total fleet size. This characteristic makes the framework highly scalable to large robotic deployments and inherently parallelizable, a critical requirement for real-time operations in complex environments.
5. **Convex Optimization Advantages:** Framing the problem as a Semi-Definite Program (SDP) allows for the computation of Maximum-Volume Inscribed Ellipsoids (MVEs), which simultaneously quantify position and localization uncertainty (analogous to covariance matrices). The convexity guarantees that global optima can be found efficiently.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Enabling Secure Autonomous Operations:** The framework can serve as a robust, globally guaranteed correction anchor, directly substituting GPS in unstructured and fully GPS-denied environments. This capability is crucial for enhancing the security and autonomy of multi-robot systems in critical applications such as:
 - **Defense and Surveillance:** Preventing adversary tracking of sensitive military assets.
 - **Resource Extraction:** Secure and efficient exploration and operation in hazardous, GPS-denied sites.
 - **Urban Air Mobility (UAM):** Ensuring privacy for future drone delivery or passenger transport networks.
 - **Underwater and Subterranean Exploration:** Providing reliable navigation where GPS signals cannot penetrate.
2. **Integration with Existing Navigation Systems:** Future integration of DCL outputs into standard Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) pipelines with high-rate proprioceptive sensors (e.g., odometry, IMUs) promises a comprehensive navigation solution. The DCL consensus, providing accurate absolute position updates, can function as a delayed correction mechanism, resolving vulnerabilities associated with high-speed motion and asynchronous communication by preventing solver divergence from stale dual variables.
3. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for further research in decentralized optimization, privacy-preserving state estimation, and robust localization in challenging real-world scenarios. It opens avenues for exploring adaptive step-size rules, dynamic network topologies, and more complex measurement models within a privacy-preserving convex optimization framework.

In conclusion, this research offers a practical, theoretically justified, and computationally efficient solution to the critical problem of privacy-preserving cooperative localization, with broad implications for the secure and autonomous operation of multi-robot systems in diverse and challenging environments.
