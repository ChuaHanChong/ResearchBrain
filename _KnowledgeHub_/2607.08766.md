# OPSD-V: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Post-Training Few-Step Autoregressive Video Generators

> [!summary] Summary
> An on-policy self-distillation framework, OPSD-V, is introduced to post-train few-step autoregressive video generators, leveraging real long-video data as privileged temporal context. This approach improves long-horizon video quality and motion dynamics for existing models, achieving quantitative gains on VBenchLong without increasing inference costs.

## Problem

- Few-step autoregressive (AR) video generators suffer from long-horizon degradation, exhibiting error accumulation, weakened motion, and visual artifacts over extended rollouts.
- Existing distillation methods for AR video models rely on short-clip teacher distributions, limiting their ability to provide effective supervision for truly long autoregressive trajectories.
- Directly using real long-video data for supervision in a teacher-forcing manner can lead to semantic and temporal mismatch due to divergence from the student's generated trajectory.

## Method

- A cache-aware **on-policy self-distillation** framework is developed, where a student model generates video chunks autoregressively, mirroring inference-time dynamics.
- A teacher model, an ==Exponential Moving Average (EMA)== copy of the student, provides dense ==denoising-level corrective targets== by evaluating on the student's noisy latents while conditioning on a cleaner cache constructed with real long-video chunks.
- A memory-efficient training procedure computes and accumulates gradients chunk-by-chunk along the rollout, ensuring activation memory does not grow with video length.

## Results

- OPSD-V improved both Quality Score (e.g., from **0.8138** to **0.8242** for LongLive) and Dynamic Degree (e.g., from **0.5012** to **0.5890** for LongLive) on VBenchLong for different base models.
- Qualitative results demonstrate reduced blur, artifacts, and semantic drift, with more stable motion dynamics and scene coherence in one-minute generated videos.
- A user study showed strong preference for OPSD-V post-trained models, with **66.0%** overall preference and **57.5%** for motion quality over base models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying dense, denoising-level supervision from a teacher with privileged real-video context along the student's own rollout trajectory is crucial for mitigating long-horizon degradation in AR video generation.
> - Supervising in the native velocity space of the diffusion model is more effective than matching clean-latent (x0) estimates, preserving fine structures and avoiding geometric degradation.
> - The teacher's cache can selectively incorporate real-video history while maintaining the most recent student-generated chunk, balancing error reduction with autoregressive consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced rapid advancements, largely driven by diffusion transformers (DiTs) and similar generative models. Initial progress primarily focused on offline generation of short, high-resolution video clips with strong visual fidelity and temporal coherence. However, there is a growing demand for real-time and interactive video generation, which necessitates models capable of producing content sequentially with low latency.

This demand has led to the development of autoregressive (AR) video models. These models generate frames or chunks incrementally, utilizing transformer Key-Value (KV) caches to efficiently maintain historical context. To further enhance the practicality of AR generation, methods combining causal video modeling with few-step distillation have emerged. Few-step distillation techniques, such as Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), compress computationally intensive multi-step diffusion models into efficient generators that require only a small number of denoising steps. Approaches like CausVid and Self-Forcing exemplify this trend by distilling bidirectional video DiTs into causal AR students and aligning training with inference through self-rollout mechanisms.

Despite these developments, existing few-step AR video generators still exhibit limitations, particularly in long-horizon generation. They often suffer from error accumulation, weakened motion dynamics, and visual degradation over extended rollouts. A fundamental challenge stems from the fact that the teacher distributions used in many DMD-style training paradigms are typically derived from short-clip video data, which limits their ability to provide supervision for truly long autoregressive trajectories. While some methods have extended rollout lengths and incorporated attention-sink mechanisms to mitigate these issues, the underlying supervision remains constrained by the temporal scope and quality of clip-level teachers.

This paper addresses the gap by leveraging the emerging paradigm of on-policy self-distillation (OPSD). OPSD, previously explored in large language models and text-to-image generation (e.g., D-OPSD), involves a model acting as both student and teacher. The student samples from its current policy, while the teacher provides stronger supervision by conditioning on richer in-context information, thereby providing dense supervision along the model's own trajectory. This work extends OPSD to the complex domain of AR video generation, where the on-policy state includes not only the noisy latent and denoising timestep but also the evolving KV cache generated by previous chunks. By introducing real long-video data as privileged temporal context within an OPSD framework, the authors aim to provide a more robust and cleaner target distribution for long-horizon AR video generation, while retaining the efficiency of few-step inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to further reduce long-horizon degradation and enhance motion dynamics in existing few-step autoregressive (AR) video generators, without altering their original low-latency, few-step inference path.

The motivation for OPSD-V arises from several limitations observed in current few-step AR video generation models:

1. **Long-Horizon Degradation:** Few-step AR video generators, often derived through DMD-style distillation, are designed for efficiency. However, during extended autoregressive rollouts, these models frequently experience a decline in visual quality, characterized by error accumulation, weakened motion dynamics, blur, temporal flickering, and semantic drift in later generated chunks.
2. **Limited Teacher Distribution:** The target distributions used in DMD-style training are typically provided by short-clip video teachers. Such teachers cannot directly supervise truly long autoregressive trajectories, thus limiting the effectiveness of training for long-horizon scenarios. While methods exist to extend rollout length during training, their supervision remains constrained by the temporal range and quality of the underlying clip-level teacher.
3. **Side Effects of DMD:** DMD itself can sometimes introduce undesirable side effects such as weakened dynamics or color drift. Furthermore, its supervision is often defined at the chunk or clip distribution level, rather than as dense corrective targets along the entire denoising trajectory, which could be beneficial for fine-tuning.
4. **Inefficiency of Direct Real-Video Use:** Directly incorporating real long-video data into training is challenging. A naive approach of using real-video chunks as direct teacher-forcing targets or reconstruction targets could lead to semantic and temporal mismatch, as the student's rollout trajectory may diverge from the real video.

To address these motivations, the authors pose a central question: "Can real long-video data serve as stronger training supervision while preserving the original few-step AR generation capability?" Their proposed solution, OPSD-V, aims to leverage real long videos not as direct teacher-forcing targets, but as privileged temporal context to construct a cleaner teacher distribution on the student’s own rollout states. This approach is intended to provide dense denoising-level corrective targets that mitigate degradation in the generated KV cache, thereby improving long-horizon stability and motion dynamics without increasing inference costs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OPSD-V introduces a cache-aware on-policy self-distillation framework designed for post-training few-step causal AR video generators. The methodology centers on supervising a student model along its inference-time rollout trajectory using a teacher model that benefits from cleaner, real long-video temporal context.

#### 3.1. Student On-Policy Rollout

The student branch precisely mirrors the inference-time generation process.
- **Initialization:** The process begins by initializing the student's KV cache with a real first chunk (x_data_1) from a long training video. This chunk serves as a shared prefix, anchoring the scene.
- **Autoregressive Generation:** Starting from the second chunk, the student generates each subsequent chunk autoregressively. For each chunk `i`, it starts from Gaussian noise and denoises it over a fixed, small number of steps (K=4 in experiments) using its current policy `f_θ` and the existing student cache `h_s_i`.
- **Velocity Prediction:** At each denoising step `k`, the student predicts a velocity field `v_s_i,k = f_θ(z_s_i,k, t_k, c, h_s_i)`, where `z_s_i,k` is the current noisy latent, `t_k` is the timestep, and `c` is the text condition.
- **Latent Update and Cache Management:** The predicted velocity guides a numerical solver to update the latent state. Crucially, stop-gradients are applied through the solver transition to prevent backpropagation across the entire autoregressive history, improving memory efficiency. After all denoising steps, the clean latent chunk `x_s_i` is obtained and appended to the student's KV cache `h_s_i+1` for subsequent chunks. This ensures that every student prediction is conditioned on self-generated cache states, consistent with deployment.

#### 3.2. AR-Consistent Real-Video Teacher Cache

The teacher branch is designed to provide cleaner, stop-gradient corrective targets.
- **Teacher Parameters:** The teacher parameters `¯θ` are maintained as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) copy of the student's LoRA adapter, while the base video generator remains frozen. The teacher is only active during training.
- **On-Policy State Evaluation:** The teacher is evaluated at the *same* student-visited noisy latents (`z_s_i,k`) and timesteps (`t_k`). This means the student's trajectory dictates where supervision is applied, while the teacher's cache provides the improved context for the corrective direction.
- **Cache Construction:** The novel aspect lies in the teacher's cache `h_t_i`. While the student's cache for chunk `i` comprises `[x_data_1, x_s_2, ..., x_s_i-1]`, the teacher's cache is constructed as `[x_data_1, x_data_2, ..., x_data_i-2, x_s_i-1]`. This means older history in the teacher's cache is replaced by corresponding *real-video chunks* (`x_data_j`), while the *most recent student-generated chunk* (`x_s_i-1`) is retained. This balance aims to reduce accumulated error from generated history while preserving autoregressive continuation from the student’s latest state. Both student and teacher utilize the same attention-sink cache mechanism.

#### 3.3. Dense Denoising-Level Objective

OPSD-V employs a pure velocity matching objective:
- **Loss Function:** `L_OPSD-V = 1/|S| Σ_(i,k)∈S ||v_s_i,k - sg(v_t_i,k)||^2_2`, where `v_s_i,k` is the student's predicted velocity, `v_t_i,k` is the teacher's predicted velocity (with stop-gradient), and `S` is the set of supervised chunk-step pairs.
- **Supervision Schedule:** The loss is applied to all four denoising steps (`k=1,...,K`) for chunks after an initial warm-up period (`i > M`, with M=7 chunks). This warm-up allows the student to enter long-rollout cache states before dense distillation begins, focusing supervision on the regimes where degradation is most pronounced.

#### 3.4. Training Procedure and Memory Efficiency

- **Algorithm 1 (Memory-Efficient Training):** The training procedure optimizes memory usage. Instead of retaining the computation graph for an entire long-video rollout, gradients for each supervised chunk-step pair (`l_i,k`) are computed and accumulated immediately after the student's forward pass. The activation graph is then released. This ensures that activation memory does not grow with rollout length, making training on long videos feasible.
- **Optimizer Step:** The optimizer is stepped only once after the complete rollout for a video, followed by an EMA update to the teacher's LoRA parameters.
- **Inference:** During inference, the teacher branch and real-video cache components are removed; the model operates using its original few-step AR sampling path.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that OPSD-V consistently improves the quality of long-horizon autoregressive video generation across different base models, without increasing inference costs.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Comparison

Visual comparisons (Figures 1 and 4) show that videos generated by OPSD-V post-trained models exhibit enhanced visual quality and motion dynamics over extended rollouts compared to their base counterparts.
- **Reduced Degradation:** Base models often produce plausible early chunks but suffer from degradation such as weakened motion, accumulated blur, local artifacts, and semantic or structural drift in later frames.
- **Improved Coherence:** OPSD-V reduces these common AR degradation artifacts, leading to more temporally stable videos with better-preserved scene structure and motion dynamics throughout one-minute generation sequences. For instance, in LongLive examples, OPSD-V maintained sharper background details and more stable camera evolution. In Self-Forcing examples, it produced more coherent object motion and suppressed late-stage background artifacts.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison

Quantitative evaluation using VBenchLong on a diverse set of 240 prompts (MovieGenBench and MeiBench) confirms the qualitative observations (Table 1).
- **Consistent Improvements:** OPSD-V consistently improved both the **Quality Score** and **Dynamic Degree** for both LongLive and Self-Forcing base models.
 - For LongLive, Quality Score increased from 0.8138 to 0.8242, and Dynamic Degree increased from 0.5012 to 0.5890.
 - For Self-Forcing, Quality Score increased from 0.8259 to 0.8389, and Dynamic Degree increased from 0.6218 to 0.6570.
- **Inference Cost:** These improvements were achieved while maintaining the same model scale, 4-step AR inference path, and attention-sink cache mechanism, implying no increase in computational cost during inference.
- **Semantic Score:** The Semantic Score remained comparable, showing a slight decrease in some cases, which the authors suggest might indicate a mild trade-off between more active motion generation and conservative semantic matching metrics.

#### 4.3. User Study

A user study involving 10 participants comparing 20 paired videos (10 LongLive, 10 Self-Forcing) indicated a strong preference for OPSD-V post-trained models (Figure 5).
- **Overall Preference:** OPSD-V was preferred in 66.0% of overall-preference judgments (82.5% excluding ties).
- **Motion Quality:** Users favored OPSD-V for motion quality in 57.5% of judgments (82.7% excluding ties).
- **Visual Quality:** OPSD-V was also preferred for visual quality in 60.5% of judgments (78.1% excluding ties).
- **Backbone-Specifics:** On Self-Forcing, OPSD-V received strong preference across all three criteria. On LongLive, OPSD-V was favored for overall and motion-quality, with visual quality showing more ties or base-model preferences.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies investigated key design choices:
- **Velocity vs. Clean-Latent Matching:** Distilling in the native velocity space proved more effective than converting predictions to clean-latent (x0) estimates (Figure 6). X0 matching tended to progressively smooth fine structures and introduce geometric degradation, while velocity matching better preserved high-frequency details.
- **Student vs. Teacher Trajectory:** Supervising the student on its *own* denoising trajectory (`z_s_i,k`) was critical. When distillation targets were derived from the teacher's independent trajectory (`z_t_i,k`), a severe off-policy state mismatch occurred. The teacher's rollout remained coherent, but the student's rollout rapidly lost spatial detail and became blurred (Figure 7), indicating a failure to learn how to recover from its own errors. This confirms the necessity of on-policy supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OPSD-V addresses a limitation in autoregressive video generation: the degradation of quality and motion dynamics over long rollouts, particularly when models are trained with short-clip teacher distributions or teacher-forcing approaches that diverge from inference-time dynamics. By introducing on-policy self-distillation with real long-video context, the work offers a method for effectively post-training few-step AR video generators.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Improved Long-Horizon Coherence:** OPSD-V demonstrates the capability to produce longer, more temporally stable, and visually coherent videos, mitigating issues such as error accumulation, weakened motion, and visual artifacts that commonly affect AR video models. This is particularly relevant for applications requiring continuous, extended video generation.
- **Preservation of Efficiency:** A key achievement is that these improvements are obtained without modifying the existing low-latency, few-step inference path of the base AR models. This means the enhanced quality does not incur additional computational cost at inference time, making OPSD-V a practical refinement strategy for real-time and interactive video generation systems.
- **Data-Driven Post-Training Paradigm:** The work establishes a methodology for leveraging real long-video data as a "privileged temporal context" for supervision, rather than relying solely on synthetic teacher distributions or direct reconstruction. This approach could be broadly applicable to other domains where models suffer from exposure bias or long-horizon degradation in autoregressive generation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** OPSD-V provides a baseline and insights for subsequent research on data-driven post-training for causal AR video models. Future work may explore scaling the amount and diversity of long training videos, developing more sophisticated teacher-cache policies (e.g., adaptive context lengths or mixed cache strategies), or integrating stronger context-enhanced teachers. The memory-efficient training procedure also contributes to the feasibility of training on increasingly long video sequences.
- **Broadening AR Video Applications:** By improving the stability and quality of long-horizon AR video, OPSD-V contributes to enabling more robust applications in areas such as streaming avatars, interactive media, world models, and embodied intelligence, where continuous, coherent video output is essential.
