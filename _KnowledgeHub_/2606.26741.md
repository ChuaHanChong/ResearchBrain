# PressMimic: Pressure-Guided Motion Capture and Control for Humanoid Robot Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> Nanjing University and Tsinghua University developed PressMimic, a framework integrating pressure signals into human motion capture and humanoid robot control, achieving physically grounded pose estimation and stable robot execution. The approach yields superior human pose and trajectory estimation (e.g., 33.2 mm MPJPE), and improves robot imitation success rates by up to 3.9 percentage points, enhancing stability in contact-rich tasks.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid motion imitation methods largely neglect physical interactions (like ground reaction forces) during motion capture and control, leading to unstable robot behaviors.
- Vision-based motion capture suffers from ambiguities like depth uncertainty and occlusions, causing artifacts (e.g., foot sliding, floor penetration) in estimated human motions.
- Reinforcement learning for humanoid control often uses reward functions based solely on kinematic similarities, failing to capture the physical grounding of human movement and leading to unstable robot execution.

## Method

- The **PressMimic** framework integrates ==pressure signals== as a unified modality across the entire human motion imitation pipeline, from capture to control.
- A multimodal human motion estimation network, FRAPPE++, fuses RGB video and pressure maps to estimate human 3D pose and global trajectory with explicit grounding constraints.
- A Pressure-Supervised Policy (PSP) integrates plantar pressure as an explicit supervisory signal into a reinforcement learning framework, guiding the robot to reproduce human contact dynamics and weight distribution.

## Results

- FRAPPE++ achieved significantly lower errors in human motion estimation (**33.2** mm MPJPE, **24.7** mm PMPJPE, **45.8** mm WMPJPE) compared to RGB-only baselines, demonstrating improved accuracy and physical plausibility.
- Pressure supervision (PSP) improved robot imitation performance, increasing success rates up to **0.9444** and motion completion to **0.9846** when using FRAPPE++ references, showing better stability and foot contact accuracy.
- The MotionPRO dataset, a large-scale multimodal dataset with synchronized RGB, pressure, and optical motion capture data (729 sequences, 12.4M frames), was created to support physically grounded motion understanding and control research.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pressure signals provide critical cues for human-ground interactions that are difficult to infer from RGB data alone, significantly resolving foot-ground ambiguities and reducing drift in motion estimation.
> - Incorporating pressure-derived supervisory signals in robot control (PSP) enables the robot to learn and reproduce physically consistent contact patterns and weight distribution, leading to more stable locomotion.
> - A two-stage pressure curriculum in PSP, transitioning from human reference to robot-specific optimal contact distribution, helps adapt control for stable robot dynamics while maintaining imitation fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robot motion imitation aims to enable robots to replicate human movements accurately and physically plausibly. This endeavor intersects computer vision (for human motion capture) and robotics (for control). Prior advancements in human motion capture have primarily relied on vision-based methods (e.g., RGB cameras, marker-based optical systems) or wearable sensors (e.g., IMUs, VR trackers). While these methods provide varying levels of precision, vision-based approaches often suffer from inherent ambiguities such as depth uncertainty and occlusions. Critically, most existing motion capture and subsequent robot control pipelines largely disregard the physical interactions between the human body and its environment, particularly ground contact dynamics. This oversight frequently leads to undesirable artifacts in robot execution, including foot sliding, floor penetration, unstable gaits, and in some cases, hardware damage.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has become a dominant paradigm for humanoid control, allowing robots to learn complex skills in physics simulators. However, many RL-based imitation methods construct reward functions solely based on kinematic similarities (joint positions, orientations, end-effector states), without explicitly incorporating dynamic interactions like ground reaction forces. This limitation prevents robots from fully capturing the physical grounding of human motion.

Previous research has explored the use of pressure sensors for human pose estimation, primarily in static or lying scenarios, or by using derived features like the Center of Pressure (CoP) to constrain pose. However, these applications often have limitations in dynamic movements or do not fully integrate pressure throughout a comprehensive motion imitation pipeline.

This work addresses these limitations by integrating pressure signals as a unified modality throughout the entire humanoid motion imitation pipeline, from human motion capture to robot motion control. It aims to bridge the gap between kinematic imitation and physically consistent execution by leveraging pressure to provide explicit contact and support constraints during perception and to serve as a direct supervisory signal for control. By systematically exploring pressure's role in both stages, the paper positions itself as an effort to enhance the physical plausibility and stability of humanoid robot imitation, moving beyond purely kinematic or vision-only approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework, named PressMimic, that integrates pressure signals into both the human motion capture and humanoid robot motion control stages. This integration aims to achieve physically grounded pose estimation and stable robot execution, thereby enhancing the fidelity of humanoid motion imitation.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing humanoid motion imitation pipelines:

1. **Neglect of Physical Interactions:** Current approaches predominantly rely on vision-based motion capture and kinematic imitation. These methods primarily focus on replicating human kinematics (e.g., joint angles and positions) but largely ignore crucial physical interactions, such as contact dynamics and ground reaction forces, between the human body and its environment.
2. **Perceptual Deficiencies Leading to Simulation Artifacts:** The lack of explicit physical grounding in motion capture often results in artifacts. For vision-based systems, depth ambiguity and occlusions are inherent challenges, leading to issues like temporal jitter, global scale inconsistency, foot sliding, and floor penetration in estimated human motions. These inaccuracies in the reference motion subsequently hinder robust robot control.
3. **Unstable Robot Execution:** When robots are driven by kinematically estimated references that lack physical consistency, they frequently exhibit unstable locomotion behaviors. This is particularly evident in contact-rich scenarios, where accurate understanding and reproduction of weight distribution and contact timing are critical. Consequences can include degraded performance, falls, and, in extreme cases, damage to hardware.
4. **Underutilization of Pressure Information:** Pressure signals naturally encode the critical foot-ground interactions and contact dynamics that kinematic-only signals or pure visual data may fail to capture accurately. Prior research has demonstrated pressure's potential to improve motion capture quality by providing explicit grounding constraints. Additionally, pressure offers a direct, interpretable supervisory signal for robot motion control, guiding the reproduction of contact patterns and weight distribution. However, a comprehensive integration of pressure across both perception and control stages has been underexplored.
5. **Need for a Unified Modality:** The researchers are motivated to leverage pressure as a unified modality that can bridge the gap between perception (understanding human physical interaction) and control (enabling robots to physically interact consistently). By integrating pressure throughout the entire pipeline, they aim to ensure that the robot not only mimics the human's visual motion but also reproduces the underlying physical principles governing that motion, leading to more stable and robust imitation.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond purely kinematic imitation towards physically grounded humanoid robot behaviors by systematically incorporating pressure sensing into both the data acquisition and policy learning phases.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PressMimic framework is designed as a unified system that integrates pressure signals into the entire humanoid motion imitation pipeline. It operates through three main modules: human motion estimation, motion retargeting, and robot motion control. Pressure signals are a central component, enhancing both motion capture accuracy and robot control stability.

**A. Pressure-Guided Humanoid Motion Imitation Framework**

The imitation task is formulated as an optimization problem minimizing discrepancies between estimated human states and ground truth, and between retargeted robot states and actual robot execution, subject to robot physical feasibility constraints. Pressure signals are incorporated into the motion estimation stage (providing grounding constraints) and the motion control stage (serving as auxiliary supervision).

**B. Human Motion Estimation by Fusing Pressure and RGB (FRAPPE++)**

FRAPPE++ is a multimodal human motion estimation network designed to fuse RGB video and pressure maps to estimate human 3D pose parameters ($\theta$) and global translation trajectory ($T$).

1. **Pressure-Guided Motion Estimation Pipeline:**
 - **Input:** Synchronized RGB video sequence and 2D pressure distribution sequence.
 - **Visual Encoder:** Processes RGB images to extract frame-wise visual features related to appearance and pose structure.
 - **Sparse Pressure Encoder (SPE):** Addresses the sparsity of pressure maps by converting the 2D distribution into a compact 1D sequence. This is achieved using a Hilbert space-filling curve to serialize valid pressure points, preserving local neighborhood relationships. Each point's spatial coordinates and pressure value are encoded via Fourier sine-cosine embeddings, then projected by an MLP and refined through an attention mechanism to produce a fixed-length pressure feature vector.
 - **Temporal Context Aggregation Module (TCAM):** Models temporal dependencies across motion sequences. It operates at two scales: within each temporal window (fixed length L), Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) with self-attention capture short-duration dependencies. For broader context, neighboring windows (N on each side) are compressed via temporal average pooling into global representations. These compressed representations are then integrated with the current window's features via a cross-attention mechanism, where the current window serves as query and the extended sequence (current + compressed neighbors) provides keys and values. This extends the receptive field and improves temporal coherence.
 - **Fusion Cross-Attention Module (FCAM):** Fuses the processed RGB (from visual encoder + TCAM) and pressure features (from SPE + TCAM). Pressure features serve as the query, actively probing the visual features (keys and values). This design aims to guide visual attention towards human-ground contact regions, enhancing estimation accuracy and physical plausibility. The fused representation is then passed through a feed-forward network and concatenated with the pressure features.
 - **Regressor:** Takes the final fused representation to predict SMPL pose parameters ($\theta$) and global translation ($T$), which are then used to compute 3D joint positions.

2. **Training Objective ($L_H$):** A composite loss function is used to constrain various aspects of the estimated human state:
 - $L_{pose}$: Mean squared error for pose parameters.
 - $L_{3d}$: Mean squared error for 3D joint positions (pelvis-aligned).
 - $L_{2d}$: (Implicitly mentioned as part of general objective)
 - $L_{trans}$: Mean squared error for global translation.
 - $L_{contact}$: Mean squared error for global joints in contact with the ground.

**C. Humanoid Motion Control under Pressure Supervision (PSP)**

PSP integrates plantar pressure as an explicit supervisory signal into a reinforcement learning framework for humanoid motion control.

1. **Pressure Modeling:**
 - Per-foot pressure intensity ($\phi_{lf}, \phi_{rf}$) is extracted by summing pixel values in the raw pressure maps.
 - A scalar pressure offset ($o$) is defined to quantify the bilateral weight distribution: $o = (\phi_{rf} + k \cdot \epsilon) / (\phi_{lf} + \phi_{rf} + \epsilon)$. Here, $k=2$ and $\epsilon$ is a small constant. This formulation provides distinct values for different contact states: (0,1) for double-foot contact, approaching 1 for right-foot only, approaching 0 for left-foot only, and $k=2$ for airborne, unambiguously separating contact states.

2. **Policy Training with Pressure Supervision:**
 - **RL Framework:** Built upon BeyondMimic and trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
 - **Policy Input:** Concatenation of the robot's current state and the robot reference motion (derived from retargeted human pose).
 - **Reward Function:** Comprises standard kinematic imitation terms (joint position, joint angle, end-effector rewards) augmented by a pressure offset reward.
 - **Pressure Offset Reward ($r_p$):** A Gaussian-shaped reward defined as $r_p = \omega \cdot \exp( - |o_R - o_{ref}|^2 / std^2 )$, where $o_R$ is the robot's pressure offset (from simulator ground reaction forces) and $o_{ref}$ is the human demonstrator's reference pressure offset. This dense, smooth reward encourages the robot to match the human's bilateral weight distribution.
 - **Two-Stage Pressure Curriculum:**
 - **Stage 1:** Policy trains using the fixed human reference pressure ($o_{ref}$).
 - **Stage 2:** After a performance threshold is met, the reference pressure dynamically adapts to the robot's optimal contact distribution. At each step, robot pressure offsets ($o_R$) are recorded. At episode end, optimal environments ($E^*$) are identified (top N-th percentile by total reward). An optimal pressure curve ($o^*(t)$) is averaged from $o_R$ in $E^*$. The reference pressure ($o_{ref}(t)$) is then updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA): $o_{ref}(t) \leftarrow (1-\kappa)o_{ref}(t) + \kappa o^*(t)$, where $\kappa$ is a smoothing factor. This curriculum transitions supervision from direct human mimicry to adaptation for stable robot-specific dynamics.

**D. MotionPRO Dataset**

A large-scale multimodal dataset was constructed to support this research:
- **Modalities:** Synchronized RGB video (30 Hz), pressure measurements (100 Hz), and optical motion capture data (120 Hz). All resampled to 30 Hz for consistent training/evaluation.
- **Scale:** 729 motion sequences, 12.4M frames, 70 subjects, 400 motion categories (daily activities, exercises, robot-oriented movements).
- **Human Representation:** SMPL model parameters obtained by fitting optical marker data with Mosh++.
- **Contact Labels:** Per-joint contact labels derived from pressure signals and geometric constraints, indicating ground contact based on pressure sum ($P_j$) and distance to ground ($D_j$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of incorporating pressure signals into both human motion capture and humanoid motion control.

**A. Human Pose and Trajectory Estimation (FRAPPE++)**

1. **Superior Performance over RGB-based Methods:** FRAPPE++ consistently outperformed existing RGB-based motion capture methods (VIBE, CLIFF, SMPLer-X, TRACE, PhysPT, WHAM, GVHMR) across all evaluated metrics for pose estimation and global trajectory recovery on the MotionPRO dataset.
 - **Pose Estimation (Table I):** FRAPPE++ achieved the lowest Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE: 33.2 mm), Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (PMPJPE: 24.7 mm), Per Vertex Error (PVE: 47.5 mm), and Acceleration (Accel: 1.5 m/frame²). This indicates more accurate and smoother pose reconstruction.
 - **Global Trajectory (Table II):** FRAPPE++ demonstrated significantly lower errors in trajectory estimation, including WMPJPE (45.8 mm), WAMPJPE (32.4 mm), and Root Translation Error (RTE: 25.9 mm). It also showed lower Jitter (2.2 dm/s³) and Whole Body Contact Error (WBCE: 98.8 mm), suggesting improved stability and physical plausibility in long-term motion reconstruction and ground contact.
 - The results indicate that pressure signals provide strong cues for human-ground interactions that are difficult to infer from RGB data alone, leading to reduced drift and improved physical consistency.
 - Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 7) visually corroborated the improved accuracy and physical plausibility, particularly in scenarios where other methods produced distorted or wrong poses.

2. **Ablation Study (Table III):**
 - **Impact of Pressure:** Removing the pressure modality resulted in a substantial performance degradation across all metrics (e.g., MPJPE increased from 33.2 to 65.3 mm, GTraj from 61.3 to 209.6 mm). This highlights the critical role of pressure in resolving foot-ground interactions and improving lower-body pose and trajectory accuracy.
 - **Impact of Temporal Context Aggregation Module (TCAM):** Removing TCAM led to a decrease in pose and trajectory accuracy and increased jitter and acceleration errors. This confirms TCAM's importance in modeling temporal dependencies for motion smoothness and consistency.
 - **Impact of Sparse Pressure Encoder (SPE):** Removing SPE negatively affected trajectory-related metrics, indicating its role in learning effective pressure representations for accurate trajectory estimation, despite a minor pose accuracy improvement.

**B. Pressure-Guided Humanoid Motion Imitation (PSP)**

1. **Effect of Reference Motion Quality (Table IV):**
 - The quality of the human motion capture reference directly influenced the robot's imitation performance. High-quality references from Optical MoCap and FRAPPE++ led to high Success Rates (SR > 0.90) and Motion Completion (MC > 0.97).
 - Lower-quality RGB-only references (e.g., WHAM, GVHMR) resulted in significantly reduced SR (as low as 0.0333 for WHAM) and MC, indicating unstable or failing executions.
 - FRAPPE++ generated reference motions achieved performance comparable to, and in some error metrics, slightly better than, Optical MoCap. This was attributed to FRAPPE++'s smoother kinematics, which are potentially more amenable to the robot's dynamic constraints.
 - Qualitative real-world results (Fig. 8) showed that references from GVHMR and WHAM caused twisted postures and instability, while FRAPPE++ enabled stable locomotion and accurate posture.

2. **Effect of Pressure-Supervised Control (Table V):**
 - PSP consistently improved performance over the BeyondMimic baseline when using estimated reference sources (GVHMR and FRAPPE++).
 - With the FRAPPE++ reference, PSP achieved the best overall performance, increasing SR from 0.9056 to 0.9444 and MC from 0.9746 to 0.9846. It also showed minor improvements in Foot Contact Accuracy (FCA) and reduced error metrics (RPE, RVE, WMPJPE, MJAE, PMPJPE). This suggests a beneficial alignment between pressure-informed motion capture (FRAPPE++) and pressure-supervised control (PSP).
 - When using the Optical MoCap reference, PSP showed a slight decrease in SR and MC compared to BeyondMimic, but improved FCA and other error metrics. This was attributed to potential subtle misalignments between the optical and pressure sensing systems in this specific high-fidelity scenario, leading to conflicting supervision signals.
 - PBHC (another baseline control policy) failed to produce stable locomotion regardless of reference quality, highlighting the importance of a capable base policy.
 - Real-world experiments (Fig. 8) further corroborated these findings, demonstrating that FRAPPE++ with PSP produced more natural foot contact dynamics and weight transfer compared to BeyondMimic, which exhibited more conservative foot placement. This validated the sim-to-real transferability of the pressure supervision.

In summary, the research demonstrates that integrating pressure across the entire motion imitation pipeline, from capture to control, significantly improves the accuracy, physical plausibility, and stability of both human motion estimation and humanoid robot execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PressMimic framework contributes to the field of humanoid robotics and human motion analysis in several ways, with notable significance and potential impact:

1. **Bridging the Perception-Control Gap with Physical Grounding:** The work's primary significance lies in explicitly addressing the long-standing challenge of grounding kinematic human motion imitation in physical reality. By systematically integrating pressure as a unified modality from motion capture to control, PressMimic establishes a methodology to bridge the gap between perceived human motion and physically consistent robot execution. This moves beyond purely visual or kinematic imitation, which often leads to unstable or unrealistic robot behaviors.

2. **Enhanced Human Motion Capture Accuracy and Plausibility:** The FRAPPE++ module demonstrates that augmenting traditional RGB-based motion capture with pressure signals leads to substantial improvements. It resolves ambiguities inherent in vision-only methods, particularly for foot-ground interactions, leading to more accurate 3D pose and global trajectory estimation. The resulting estimated motions exhibit higher physical plausibility, with reduced artifacts like foot sliding, lower acceleration, and jitter, which are crucial for downstream robotic applications. This suggests a pathway to more robust and reliable non-intrusive human motion capture systems.

3. **Improved Humanoid Robot Control Stability and Fidelity:** The Pressure-Supervised Policy (PSP) component highlights the effectiveness of incorporating pressure-derived supervisory signals into reinforcement learning for robot control. By explicitly encouraging the robot to reproduce human contact dynamics and weight distribution, PSP leads to more stable locomotion, higher task success rates, and more natural foot contact patterns in robot execution. This advancement can result in more capable and adaptable humanoid robots that can perform complex, contact-rich tasks with greater reliability.

4. **Creation of a Novel Multimodal Dataset (MotionPRO):** The development of MotionPRO is a significant contribution. This large-scale, synchronized dataset, encompassing RGB video, pressure measurements, and optical motion capture data across diverse human activities, provides a valuable resource for the research community. It facilitates further investigation into multimodal fusion for motion understanding, as well as the development and evaluation of physically grounded robot control policies.

5. **Implications for Embodied Intelligence:** This research advances the broader goal of embodied intelligence by demonstrating how rich physical sensing can empower intelligent agents (humanoid robots) to better understand and interact with their environment. By explicitly modeling physical interactions through pressure, the framework moves towards enabling robots to develop a more nuanced "physical intelligence."

**Potential Impact:**
- **Robotics Applications:** The work has direct implications for various robotics applications, including assistive robotics, entertainment robotics, and industrial automation where human-robot collaboration or human-like task execution is desired. More stable and physically plausible robot motions could lead to safer and more effective human-robot interaction.
- **Human Performance Analysis:** The improved accuracy of human motion capture, particularly regarding contact dynamics, could benefit fields like sports science, rehabilitation, and ergonomics, offering more detailed insights into human movement mechanics.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also lays the groundwork for future research. It highlights the potential for exploring richer physical representations (beyond ground interactions to multi-contact scenarios), developing more rigorous whole-body contact dynamics modeling, generalizing beyond instrumented environments, and achieving tighter coupling between perception and control systems.

In conclusion, PressMimic represents a step toward developing humanoid robots that not only look like humans but also move with a deeper understanding and reproduction of physical laws governing human motion, paving the way for more robust and intelligent embodied systems.
