# Adaptive Safety Filtering for Frozen ACC Policies via Conformal Residual Calibration

> [!summary] Summary
> RACF (residual-aware conformal action filtering) is a runtime safety filter for frozen adaptive cruise control policies facing dynamics shift. It calibrates a fixed nominal predictor's one-step residuals with conformal prediction, turns the quantile into a safety margin, then minimally projects policy actions under a finite bank of dynamics models, updating margin and candidates online. Adaptive RACF reaches 94.3% episode safety, 19.9 points above nominal CBF-QP.

## Problem

- Offline RL methods such as IQL, CQL and cross-domain variants handle dynamics shift during training, but deployed frozen controllers still need a separate runtime safety interface.
- Nominal CBF-QP and predictive safety filters enforce model-based constraints, so mismatched mass, road grade, actuator gain or lead-vehicle motion silently invalidates their predictions and causes violations.
- Conformal prediction provides calibrated residual uncertainty, yet prior work rarely connects one-step coverage to constraint satisfaction by the final applied action, including fallback braking steps.

## Method

- ==Conformal residual calibration==: a ==split-conformal quantile== of a fixed nominal predictor's one-step state residuals, from 500 calibration transitions at alpha 0.1, sets the initial operating margin.
- ==Finite-model action projection==: least-squares projection of the frozen policy action onto actions that keep the ==constant-time-headway barrier== above the margin for every predictor in an eight-model mass-grade-gain bank.
- **Adaptive RACF** update: rolling residual windows raise the margin, while candidate-specific residual quantiles prune the bank to the best-fitting models, with recovery resets and bounded maximum-braking fallback.

## Results

- Over 2,400 paired controller-trial units spanning IQL, CQL, BC and IDM policies, **Adaptive RACF** reaches **94.3%** episode safety versus **74.4%** for nominal CBF-QP, cutting projection frequency from **8.11%** to **6.63%**.
- Controlled ablation isolates residual-margin injection at **+4.54 points** safety (1,993 to 2,102 of 2,400), while candidate selection alone adds no binary safety gain (144/180 with or without).
- Robust CBF-QP stays safest (**2,375/2,400**; **170/180** matched), but Adaptive cuts mean amortized rollout time by **21.2%** at **161/180** safe, and beats ACI and CPSF-style baselines by about **5 points**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High one-step conformal coverage does not imply trajectory safety: 97% one-step coverage coexisted with near-zero episode-maximum coverage, because unmodeled lead-vehicle braking dominated residual exceedances.
> - Calibrating a safety margin from observed model residuals is a cheap, retraining-free way to harden frozen learned controllers, and most of the benefit comes from the margin itself.
> - Online adaptation is not automatically better: well-chosen fixed margins stayed competitive, and heuristic rolling updates give up the formal guarantees that adaptive conformal inference would provide.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A frozen adaptive-cruise-control (ACC) policy may behave poorly when the deployed car or lead vehicle differs from what it encountered during training. The motivating shifts include vehicle mass, road grade and actuator gain, as well as lead-vehicle braking and stop–go motion. The authors' approach has three steps. First, measure prediction errors. Second, turn their calibrated size into a safety margin. Third, use that margin to adjust a policy action only as much as needed, while the policy stays frozen. In simulated tests, the approach improved safety over the evaluated nominal filter and projected actions less often. It did not match the robust filter on safety.

(b) In the candidate-set × margin ablation, fixed-margin cells each show 80.0% (144/180) and updated-margin cells each show 89.4% (161/180). This pattern supports a margin effect in this cohort, not an independent candidate-update gain. (c) One-step score coverage is 97.0%, and episode-max coverage falls to 47.5%, 0.0% and 0.0%. The 90% target applies to one-step coverage only.

### 2. How a residual becomes an action

Conformal calibration sets a threshold from held-out prediction errors. Here the score is the norm of the one-step difference between the next state and a fixed nominal predictor's prediction. With 500 calibration transitions and $\alpha=0.1$, the reported threshold is $q_{\alpha}=0.09188$.

At each step, the frozen policy proposes an action. The filter, called RACF, checks predicted safety across active candidate models and chooses the feasible action closest to that proposal. The eight candidates span two masses (1,500/1,800 kg), two grades (0/3°) and two actuator gains (0.7/1.0). The margin supplies extra clearance beyond the predicted constraint.

Adaptive RACF also updates while driving. It updates its margin using a 100-transition window. These empirical updates do not inherit adaptive conformal validity.

Calibration residuals from $f_{0}$ produce $q_{\alpha}$ and an initial margin $m_{0}$. A candidate bank $\Theta$ supplies model constraints. RACF uses the frozen policy's proposal $a^{\pi}_{t}$, the state $s_{t}$, the active candidates and the margin to select the applied action $a_{t}$. The next state $s_{t+1}$ updates the margin and candidates. After infeasibility or a failed check, the filter falls back to bounded maximum braking $a=-1$.

### 3. What the comparisons establish

The registered test covers 2,400 matched controller–trial units. Episode safety means every executed transition has nonnegative headway margin and the episode has no collision. A collision at a gap of 0.5 m or less ends the episode as unsafe.

Under this endpoint, Adaptive RACF reached 94.3% episode safety versus 74.4% for the evaluated nominal CBF-QP. Its projection frequency, meaning how often the filter projects an action, also fell from 8.11% to 6.63%. Robust CBF-QP was safer, at 2,375/2,400 (99.0%) versus Adaptive's 2,262/2,400 (94.3%).

The controlled ablation is the main mechanistic evidence. The authors held the candidate set, objective, solver, bounds and fallback fixed. Under those conditions, adding the residual margin raised safe episodes from 1,993/2,400 to 2,102/2,400, a gain of 4.54 points (95% CI 3.63–5.54). This isolates the observed effect of adding a margin. It does not show that conformal calibration beats every alternative way to choose one.

A separate 180-unit study points the same way. Candidate updating showed no separate binary safety gain in that cohort.

### 4. A one-step condition, not a trip-long certificate

The theorem gives marginal one-step residual coverage, not state-conditional or simultaneous multi-step coverage. Evidence remains limited to simulated ACC, and the paper does not establish real-vehicle safety or a closed-loop certificate.

### 5. What the margin changes

The approach uses observed prediction error to change the safety buffer around a frozen controller's actions. With other components held fixed, adding the margin raised safe episodes in this simulated ACC setting. Robust CBF-QP nonetheless remained safer overall.
