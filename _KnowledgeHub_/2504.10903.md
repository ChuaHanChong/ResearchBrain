# Efficient Reasoning Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore present a comprehensive survey that categorizes and analyzes methods to improve the computational efficiency of large language models for reasoning tasks, focusing on generating shorter reasoning chains, building smaller models, and enabling faster decoding.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) employing "slow-thinking" paradigms for complex reasoning incur substantial computational costs and memory demands.
- These models often "overthink," generating unnecessarily long or redundant reasoning chains, which wastes resources and can accumulate errors.
- The significant size of current reasoning models limits their practical deployment in resource-constrained environments and hinders real-time applications due to high latency.

## Method

- The authors introduce a goal-driven taxonomy, categorizing methods into =="Shorter"== for compressing lengthy reasoning chains.
- Another category, =="Smaller"==, focuses on developing compact language models that retain strong reasoning capabilities.
- The =="Faster"== category investigates strategies for designing efficient decoding mechanisms to accelerate the inference of reasoning models.

## Results

- Methods for "shorter" reasoning successfully reduce Chain-of-Thought length, with **latent reasoning** offering high speedups and prompt engineering providing cost-effective gains.
- Approaches for "smaller" models demonstrate that **distillation** effectively transfers reasoning capabilities, and high-bit quantization reduces memory footprint with minimal performance impact.
- "Faster" decoding is achieved by optimizing Test-Time Scaling strategies and implementing **adaptive compute allocation**, often without requiring additional model training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diverse efficiency approaches, including reinforcement learning, supervised fine-tuning, prompt-driven strategies, and latent reasoning, are used to reduce reasoning chain length.
> - Knowledge distillation is a highly effective method for transferring complex reasoning abilities from large models to smaller ones, enabling more compact solutions.
> - A critical challenge in the field is the need for holistic evaluation metrics that can jointly capture the trade-off between reasoning performance and computational efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed remarkable progress with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly their ability to perform complex reasoning tasks. Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and its extensions such as Tree-of-Thought (ToT) (Yao et al., 2023) and Graph-of-Thoughts (GoT) (Besta et al., 2024) have enabled LLMs to break down intricate problems into intermediate steps, significantly enhancing performance in domains like mathematics, coding, and logical inference. These "reasoning models" or Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) have demonstrated a "slow-thinking" paradigm, where explicit intermediate steps are generated to arrive at a final answer.

However, this paradigm, while powerful, comes with a substantial computational cost. The generation of numerous tokens, often exceeding what is strictly necessary (a phenomenon termed "overthinking"), and the reliance on increasingly larger model sizes (e.g., DeepSeek R1 with 685B parameters) introduce significant overhead in terms of computation, memory, and energy consumption. This inefficiency poses critical challenges for the practical deployment of reasoning models, especially in resource-constrained environments, real-time applications (like embodied AI or autonomous driving), and for broad accessibility due to high operational costs. Furthermore, excessively lengthy reasoning paths can sometimes lead to accumulated errors, reduced accuracy, and even introduce safety risks by creating longer avenues for potential misuse.

This survey directly addresses this critical challenge by synthesizing the burgeoning research efforts focused on improving the *efficiency* of reasoning models. It positions itself as a crucial guide in a landscape where performance gains are often celebrated, but the underlying computational burden is increasingly scrutinized. Unlike other surveys that might focus on the breadth of reasoning *capabilities* or general LLM *efficiency* across all tasks, this work specifically hones in on efficiency *within the context of reasoning*. It acknowledges the significant performance improvements brought by reasoning models but shifts the focus to how these benefits can be realized in a computationally sustainable manner. By categorizing existing approaches through a "goal-driven" taxonomy (shorter, smaller, faster), the survey provides a structured lens to understand the diverse methodological advancements, offering clarity and direction in a fast-paced research domain. It thus serves as a timely and essential resource for researchers and practitioners aiming to bridge the gap between powerful reasoning capabilities and practical, cost-effective deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Efficient Reasoning Models: A Survey" is to provide a comprehensive and structured overview of recent advancements in the field of efficient reasoning for large language models. The authors aim to systematically categorize and analyze the diverse techniques developed to mitigate the computational overhead associated with the "slow-thinking" paradigm characteristic of modern reasoning models.

The motivation behind this survey stems from several critical challenges and emerging needs:

1. **Addressing Computational Overhead:** The remarkable success of reasoning models, particularly those employing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and its variants, relies on generating extended sequences of intermediate tokens. This "slow-thinking" approach, while effective for complex tasks, inevitably leads to substantial computational costs, memory demands, and increased inference latency. The survey highlights the urgent need for acceleration to make these powerful models practically viable.
2. **Mitigating "Overthinking" and Redundancy:** Reasoning models frequently exhibit a tendency to "overthink," generating unnecessarily long or redundant reasoning chains even for relatively simple problems. This not only wastes computational resources but can also negatively impact final accuracy by accumulating errors. The survey is motivated by the need to develop methods that can shorten these paths without compromising performance.
3. **Enabling Resource-Constrained Deployment:** The reliance on very large model sizes for strong reasoning capabilities (e.g., hundreds of billions of parameters) makes deployment challenging for many real-world applications, especially those on edge devices or with limited computational budgets. The survey seeks to consolidate research on building more compact yet powerful reasoning models.
4. **Enhancing Real-time Application Suitability:** For applications requiring real-time decision-making, such as embodied AI, autonomous driving, or interactive agent systems, the latency introduced by lengthy reasoning processes is a significant bottleneck. Accelerating the decoding stage is therefore a key motivation.
5. **Improving Sustainability and Safety:** High computational costs translate to higher energy consumption and carbon emissions, making the pursuit of efficiency crucial for environmental sustainability. Additionally, overly verbose reasoning paths might inadvertently increase safety risks by making it harder to detect and mitigate harmful outputs.

In response to these motivations, the survey sets out to categorize existing works into three distinct, goal-driven directions:
- **Shorter:** Focuses on methods that compress lengthy CoTs into concise yet effective reasoning chains.
- **Smaller:** Explores techniques for developing compact language models that retain strong reasoning capabilities.
- **Faster:** Investigates strategies for designing efficient decoding mechanisms to accelerate the inference of reasoning models.

By systematically mapping these approaches, the authors aim to provide clarity, identify key trends, and lay the groundwork for future research in an increasingly vital sub-field of AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this survey is primarily a comprehensive literature review and a novel categorization framework. The authors systematically collect and analyze a wide array of recent research papers (many published or pre-printed in late 2024 and 2025, indicating a very up-to-date review) pertaining to the efficiency of reasoning models.

The core methodological contribution of the survey is its **goal-driven taxonomy**, which structures the diverse landscape of efficient reasoning methods into three distinct categories based on *how* they achieve efficiency:

1. **Make Long CoT Short (Shorter):** This category focuses on techniques that reduce the length of reasoning chains while preserving or even enhancing performance. The sub-approaches discussed include:
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based methods:** Utilizing length penalties or tailored reward functions to encourage concise reasoning.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-based methods:** Training models on variable-length CoT data, often involving data compression or generation of shorter paths.
 - **Prompt-driven strategies:** Designing specific prompts to guide models towards more efficient reasoning or to route inputs to appropriate models based on complexity.
 - **Latent Reasoning:** Performing reasoning in a compressed, implicit latent space rather than generating explicit token sequences.

2. **Build Small Language Model with Strong Reasoning Ability (Smaller):** This category explores how to imbue compact models with robust reasoning capabilities, thereby inherently improving efficiency due to reduced model size. The sub-approaches include:
 - **Distillation-based methods:** Transferring knowledge and reasoning capabilities from larger, more powerful "teacher" models to smaller "student" models.
 - **Quantization & Pruning:** Applying traditional model compression techniques to reduce model size and computational overhead while retaining reasoning ability.
 - **RL-based methods:** Directly training small language models (SLMs) using reinforcement learning to enhance their reasoning performance.

3. **Let Decoding More Efficient (Faster):** This category focuses on optimizing the inference process to accelerate reasoning. The sub-approaches entail:
 - **Efficiency for Test-Time Scaling (TTS) strategies:** Improving the speed of techniques like Best-of-N sampling and self-consistency, which typically involve generating multiple reasoning paths. This includes efficient sampling and efficient self-consistency.
 - **Other methods:** Including problem decomposition, adaptive output length adjustment, and speculative reasoning.

Beyond this structured categorization, the survey also details:
- **Background Information:** Provides essential context on Chain-of-Thought reasoning, reasoning models, Test-Time Scaling, and model compression techniques.
- **Evaluation and Benchmarks:** Dedicates a significant section to discussing crucial metrics for assessing reasoning efficiency (e.g., model size, FLOPs, latency, token count, composite metrics like ACU and AES) and lists relevant datasets and benchmarks across various reasoning types (math, logical, common sense, algorithmic, planning). This highlights the importance of robust evaluation in the field.
- **Discussions and Future Directions:** Offers critical insights into open challenges, such as the tension between efficiency and safety, the application of efficient reasoning to multimodal LLMs, strategies for breaking memory limitations, improving training efficiency, and opportunities in traditional model compression.
- **Resource Provision:** The accompanying GitHub repository serves as a valuable resource, making the curated collection of papers readily accessible to the research community.

By adopting this systematic, category-based approach, the survey effectively synthesizes a vast amount of recent literature, clarifies the interconnections between different techniques, and provides a clear conceptual map for understanding and advancing efficient reasoning research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, "Efficient Reasoning Models: A Survey" does not present new empirical research findings but rather synthesizes and categorizes the results and trends from existing literature. Its "findings" are therefore the distillation of the current state of the art in efficient reasoning.

The main findings and results are encapsulated within the three overarching categories:

1. **Shorter Reasoning Chains:**
 - **Multi-faceted Approaches:** The survey identifies that reducing CoT length is pursued through diverse strategies: RL with explicit length penalties, SFT on curated variable-length data, prompt engineering, and latent reasoning.
 - **Latent Reasoning's Promise:** Latent reasoning, which performs inference in a hidden, non-linguistic space, shows significant potential for high speedups by drastically reducing token generation. Methods range from distilling explicit CoTs into implicit representations to using auxiliary models for latent token generation.
 - **SFT and RL Trade-offs:** RL-based methods often require extensive parameter updates (Full FT) and specialized reward designs, while SFT-based methods can leverage Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) like LoRA, though the latter might yield less drastic efficiency gains compared to the upper bound of latent reasoning.
 - **Prompt Engineering's Simplicity:** Simple prompt modifications (e.g., "Be concise") can yield efficiency gains without retraining, offering a cost-effective approach. Routing strategies based on complexity or confidence are also effective.

2. **Smaller Language Models with Strong Reasoning:**
 - **Distillation as a Core Strategy:** Knowledge distillation is a dominant and effective method for transferring complex reasoning abilities from large models to smaller ones. Data-focused strategies (e.g., using mixed, negative, or progressively simplified data) and model-focused strategies (e.g., distilling into new architectures like Mamba) are highlighted. Progressive distillation methods tend to show greater performance improvements.
 - **Model Compression Impact:** Quantization (especially high-bit, like 8-bit) can reduce GPU memory by over 75% with minimal impact on reasoning performance, sometimes even outperforming original models. In contrast, aggressive pruning tends to severely degrade reasoning abilities, suggesting that reasoning capabilities are more robust to precision reduction than to structural sparsity.
 - **RL for SLMs:** Reinforcement learning, particularly with algorithms like GRPO and carefully curated datasets, has proven successful in endowing small language models (SLMs) with strong reasoning capabilities comparable to or even surpassing larger, more traditional LLMs on specific benchmarks.

3. **Faster Decoding Strategies:**
 - **Optimizing Test-Time Scaling (TTS):** Many advanced reasoning techniques, such as Best-of-N and self-consistency, involve multiple inference passes, increasing latency. Efforts here focus on early termination of low-quality samples, predictive decoding, and structured sampling (e.g., in Tree/Graph-of-Thoughts) to reduce redundant computation.
 - **Problem Decomposition:** Breaking down complex problems into smaller, manageable sub-problems (e.g., via DAGs or atomic reasoning actions) allows for more efficient processing, reduced context length, and parallel decoding.
 - **Adaptive Compute Allocation:** Methods that dynamically adjust computational resources based on problem difficulty (e.g., routing easy questions to lightweight models, self-verification for simple tasks, tree search for complex ones) are gaining traction.
 - **No-Retraining Benefits:** A significant portion of "faster" methods achieve notable efficiency gains and can improve model performance *without* additional training, making them highly practical for immediate deployment.

**Intersections and Synergies:** The survey explicitly notes that these three categories are not isolated. Distillation, for instance, is not only crucial for building smaller models but also supports latent reasoning (compressing explicit CoTs into implicit paths) and SFT-based length control. Combining SFT and RL for adaptive reasoning is another synergistic approach identified.

**Evaluation Gaps:** A critical finding is the persistent challenge in evaluation. While metrics for performance (accuracy, Pass@k) and efficiency (tokens, latency, model size) are often reported separately, there's a growing need for holistic metrics that can jointly capture the trade-off between performance and efficiency (e.g., ACU, AES, optimal token budget).

Overall, the survey paints a picture of a vibrant and diverse research area where innovation is driven by the practical necessity of making powerful reasoning models accessible and sustainable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The survey "Efficient Reasoning Models" holds significant academic and practical impact by offering a much-needed, structured synthesis of a rapidly evolving and increasingly critical research domain.

**Academic Significance:**

- **Clarifying a Complex Landscape:** The most immediate impact is providing a clear, goal-driven taxonomy ("shorter," "smaller," "faster") that helps organize the vast and often fragmented literature on efficient reasoning. This conceptual framework is invaluable for new researchers entering the field, allowing them to quickly grasp the different approaches and their underlying mechanisms.
- **Identifying Key Trends and Gaps:** By systematically reviewing recent work, the survey highlights successful methodologies (e.g., the effectiveness of distillation for SLM reasoning, the promise of latent reasoning, the utility of prompt-driven efficiency) and points out areas requiring further attention (e.g., the trade-off between efficiency and safety, the need for better holistic evaluation metrics).
- **Stimulating Future Research:** The dedicated "Discussions and Future Directions" section acts as a roadmap, posing critical open questions and suggesting promising avenues for innovation, such as efficient reasoning for multimodal models, breaking memory limitations, improving training efficiency, and re-evaluating traditional compression techniques. This direct guidance is crucial for pushing the boundaries of the field.
- **Resource for the Community:** The accompanying GitHub repository serves as a valuable, dynamic resource for researchers and practitioners, facilitating access to relevant papers and fostering community collaboration.

**Practical Impact:**

- **Enabling Scalable Deployment:** The core motivation of efficiency directly translates into practical benefits. By understanding how to make reasoning models "shorter," "smaller," and "faster," developers can deploy powerful AI reasoning capabilities in a wider array of real-world applications. This includes resource-constrained edge devices, mobile platforms, and large-scale cloud services where cost-effectiveness is paramount.
- **Facilitating Real-time Applications:** For latency-sensitive domains such as embodied AI, autonomous driving, interactive chatbots, and agent systems, the insights from this survey are crucial. Accelerating inference allows these systems to process information and make decisions more swiftly, enhancing responsiveness and user experience.
- **Reducing Operational Costs:** The computational overhead of large reasoning models translates directly into high energy consumption and financial costs for training and inference. Efficient reasoning techniques can significantly lower these operational expenditures, making advanced AI more accessible and economically viable for businesses and research labs.
- **Promoting AI Sustainability:** By addressing energy consumption and carbon emissions (as highlighted in the report), efficient reasoning directly contributes to making AI development and deployment more environmentally responsible. This aligns with broader global efforts towards sustainable technology.
- **Addressing Safety Concerns:** The discussion on the tension between efficiency and safety is a critical contribution. As reasoning models become more powerful, ensuring their safe and ethical deployment is paramount. The survey's insights into how efficiency might impact safety (e.g., shorter paths potentially bypassing guardrails) underscore the need for careful design and evaluation.

In conclusion, "Efficient Reasoning Models: A Survey" is a timely and impactful contribution that not only synthesizes the current state of a rapidly evolving field but also provides critical direction for its future. Its structured approach, comprehensive coverage, and forward-looking discussions make it an essential reference for anyone working on or interested in the practical deployment of intelligent reasoning systems.
