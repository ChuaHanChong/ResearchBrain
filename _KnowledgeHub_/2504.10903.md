# Efficient Reasoning Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore present a comprehensive survey that categorizes and analyzes methods to improve the computational efficiency of large language models for reasoning tasks, focusing on generating shorter reasoning chains, building smaller models, and enabling faster decoding.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) employing "slow-thinking" paradigms for complex reasoning incur substantial computational costs and memory demands.
- These models often "overthink," generating unnecessarily long or redundant reasoning chains, which wastes resources and can accumulate errors.
- The significant size of current reasoning models limits their practical deployment in resource-constrained environments and hinders real-time applications due to high latency.

## Method

- The authors introduce a goal-driven taxonomy, categorizing methods into =="Shorter"== for compressing lengthy reasoning chains.
- Another category, =="Smaller"==, focuses on developing compact language models that retain strong reasoning capabilities.
- The =="Faster"== category investigates strategies for designing efficient decoding mechanisms to accelerate the inference of reasoning models.

## Results

- Methods for "shorter" reasoning successfully reduce Chain-of-Thought length, with **latent reasoning** offering high speedups and prompt engineering providing cost-effective gains.
- Approaches for "smaller" models demonstrate that **distillation** effectively transfers reasoning capabilities, and high-bit quantization reduces memory footprint with minimal performance impact.
- "Faster" decoding is achieved by optimizing Test-Time Scaling strategies and implementing **adaptive compute allocation**, often without requiring additional model training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diverse efficiency approaches, including reinforcement learning, supervised fine-tuning, prompt-driven strategies, and latent reasoning, are used to reduce reasoning chain length.
> - Knowledge distillation is a highly effective method for transferring complex reasoning abilities from large models to smaller ones, enabling more compact solutions.
> - A critical challenge in the field is the need for holistic evaluation metrics that can jointly capture the trade-off between reasoning performance and computational efficiency.
