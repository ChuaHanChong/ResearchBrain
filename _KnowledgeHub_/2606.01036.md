# Position: Good Embodied Reward Models Need Bad Behavior Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Embodied reward models systematically over-reward suboptimal robot behaviors, especially as task complexity increases, due to a lack of negative data in training. Incorporating real robot failure videos with dense reward labels significantly improves model calibration and alignment with human preferences, yielding up to a 10% accuracy increase in distinguishing good from bad robot actions.

## Problem

- Current embodied reward models systematically over-reward suboptimal, unsafe, or low-quality robot behaviors that human evaluators would penalize.
- Model performance degrades with increasing task complexity, indicating a misalignment with human preferences for nuanced execution and safety.
- Embodied datasets typically lack 'bad' robot behavior data (failures, errors), as it is difficult to collect and often filtered out, leading to biased reward models.

## Method

- The study evaluates three state-of-the-art embodied reward models (ReWind, GVL, Dopamine) using a human-centric evaluation protocol on real robot behaviors.
- It demonstrates the impact of intentionally injecting 'bad' robot behavior data, including text descriptions, robot videos, and dense reward labels of failures, into a VLM-based reward model (GVL).
- The approach specifically leverages in-context learning to provide negative examples without retraining, focusing on different granularities of negative data.

## Results

- All evaluated reward models systematically over-rewarded suboptimal behaviors, with Preference Ordering Accuracy dropping from **0.72**-**0.77** for simple tasks to **0.52**-**0.62** for complex tasks like Tool Use.
- Injecting text descriptions combined with actual robot failure videos improved accuracy by approximately **8%** for simpler tasks like Pick/Place and Push/Pull.
- The most substantial gains, an approximate **10%** accuracy improvement for complex tasks such as Tool Use and Pour Liquid, were achieved by providing dense, per-timestep reward labels for negative behavior examples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art embodied reward models exhibit systematic over-optimism, over-rewarding behaviors that humans perceive as unsatisfactory or unsafe.
> - Text-only descriptions of failures are insufficient for improving VLM-based reward models, as they struggle to ground abstract criteria in concrete physical reality.
> - Explicit, visually grounded negative feedback, particularly dense, per-timestep reward labels associated with failure examples, is crucial for effectively calibrating embodied reward models.
