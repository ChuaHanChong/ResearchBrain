# Visual Generation Unlocks Human-Like Reasoning through Multimodal World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and ByteDance Seed introduced a principled framework and the VisWorld-Eval benchmark to systematically study visual world modeling in Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs). Their work demonstrates that visual generation substantially improves reasoning on physical and spatial tasks requiring world simulation and reconstruction, showing increased sample efficiency and higher fidelity in generated intermediate states.

## Problem

- AI systems show a disparity between advanced abstract reasoning and limitations in physical and spatial intelligence, unlike human cognition.
- Existing language-centric AI struggles with physical world tasks due to representational bottlenecks and insufficient prior knowledge.
- The benefits of visual generation in Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) for improving reasoning lacked a systematic, principled investigation, with prior empirical results being inconclusive.

## Method

- Formalized multimodal "world models" for Chain-of-Thought reasoning, defining capabilities like world reconstruction and simulation, and proposing distinct implicit, verbal, and visual modeling formulations.
- Introduced the "visual superiority hypothesis," asserting that visual generation is particularly advantageous for physical-world tasks due to its informativeness and access to complementary prior knowledge.
- Developed **VisWorld-Eval**, a novel benchmark suite with seven tasks specifically designed to isolate and test atomic world-model capabilities in multimodal reasoning.

## Results

- Visual world modeling substantially improved reasoning accuracy and sample efficiency (e.g., **4x** less training data needed) for tasks demanding world simulation and reconstruction, such as Paper Folding and Cube 3-View Projection.
- It demonstrated dramatically better fidelity in generating intermediate states (over 50% structural correctness in Cube 3-View Projection) compared to verbal world modeling, which often yielded near-zero fidelity for structural changes.
- For simpler grid-world tasks like Maze and Sokoban, visual world modeling offered no clear advantage, with implicit world models sometimes achieving the best performance due to emergent internal representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual generation, when acting as a "visual world model," offers a complementary and powerful pathway for reasoning in UMMs, particularly for physical and spatial intelligence tasks.
> - The effectiveness of visual world modeling stems from its richer expressiveness and stronger prior knowledge, which aids in precise grounding and capturing complex spatial transformations.
> - UMMs can develop emergent implicit world models for simpler tasks with straightforward state tracking, explaining why explicit (verbal or visual) generation is not always necessary for performance gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical frontier in artificial intelligence: achieving human-like reasoning, particularly in physical and spatial domains. Recent breakthroughs in AI, especially with large language models (LLMs) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, have enabled expert-level performance in formal and abstract areas such as mathematics and programming. These systems primarily rely on verbal reasoning, leveraging "world models" believed to be embedded within the linguistic representations of LLMs.

However, the authors highlight a significant disparity: current AI systems still lag considerably behind humans in tasks requiring physical and spatial intelligence. This gap is attributed to their predominant reliance on verbal pathways, which may suffer from representational limitations or insufficient prior knowledge for these types of tasks. Human cognition, as supported by dual-coding theory, utilizes complementary verbal and visual imagery representations, with visual imagery playing a crucial role in spatial reasoning and memory.

The emergence of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) capable of both verbal and visual generation presents a new paradigm. Unlike earlier Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that primarily map visual inputs into a linguistic embedding space for verbal reasoning, UMMs offer the potential for explicitly leveraging visual generation to enable "visual world modeling." This capability could bridge the performance gap in physical and spatial domains, moving AI closer to human-like cognitive abilities.

Despite this promise, the research landscape concerning the benefits of visual generation for reasoning in UMMs has been inconclusive, with mixed empirical results and heuristically designed evaluation tasks. Prior work like RealUnify, Uni-MMMU, ROVER, MIRA, and ThinkMorph has explored this area but often without a principled framework or full exploitation of interleaved multimodal reasoning.

This paper positions itself as the "first principled study" to systematically investigate when and how visual generation benefits reasoning from a comprehensive "world-model perspective." It aims to provide theoretical foundations and empirical validation for the role of multimodal world models in enhancing AI's reasoning capabilities, particularly for tasks grounded in the physical world. By doing so, it seeks to clarify the applicability and potential of UMMs to foster more robust and human-like multimodal AI, contributing a foundational understanding to this rapidly evolving field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research stems from the observed disparity between AI's advanced capabilities in abstract reasoning and its limitations in physical and spatial intelligence, contrasting with human cognitive strengths. Humans effortlessly construct and manipulate internal mental models of the world, employing both verbal and vivid visual imagery to reason, plan, and predict outcomes. Current large language models, while impressive, primarily operate within a linguistic framework, which proves inadequate for tasks demanding rich, grounded physical and spatial understanding.

The advent of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) capable of generating both text and images offers a new avenue to approximate human-like multimodal cognition. However, the scientific community lacks a clear understanding of *when* and *how* this visual generation capability in UMMs can effectively enhance reasoning. Previous studies on the topic have yielded inconsistent results, largely due to a lack of principled theoretical grounding and heuristically designed evaluation tasks that do not isolate specific cognitive functions.

Given this context, the paper establishes several key objectives:

1. **To bridge the conceptual gap between human mental models and AI world models:** The authors aim to formally integrate the notion of "world models" into the framework of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in multimodal AI, drawing inspiration from psychology and cognitive science.
2. **To develop a principled theoretical framework:** This involves:
- Formulating multimodal world models that capture core capabilities like "world reconstruction" (inferring complete structures from partial observations) and "world simulation" (predicting future states based on actions).
- Defining distinct reasoning formulations: implicit world modeling (no explicit state tracking), verbal world modeling (textual state descriptions), and visual world modeling (generated image-based state descriptions).
- Proposing and analytically justifying the "visual superiority hypothesis," which posits that visual generation is particularly advantageous for physical-world tasks due to its informativeness and access to complementary prior knowledge.
3. **To empirically validate the theoretical insights:** This objective involves:
- Creating a novel and principled evaluation suite, **VisWorld-Eval**, consisting of tasks specifically designed to isolate and test atomic world-model capabilities in multimodal reasoning.
- Conducting controlled experiments using a state-of-the-art UMM to compare the effectiveness of different world-modeling approaches across these tasks.
- Analyzing the findings to demonstrate when and why visual generation provides tangible benefits, and conversely, when it offers no clear advantage.

Ultimately, the core motivation is to understand how explicit visual generation can facilitate more powerful and human-like multimodal AI that can reason effectively about the physical world, thereby narrowing the performance gap that currently exists between machines and humans in these crucial domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a dual-pronged methodology, combining theoretical formalization with empirical validation through controlled experiments.

**Theoretical Framework:**
The authors begin by formalizing the concept of a "world model" in the context of multimodal reasoning.
1. **World Model Formulation:** A task's underlying world is represented as a Multi-Observable Markov Decision Process (MOMDP). The system constructs an internal approximation ($\hat{s}$) of the hidden true state ($s$) from various observations ($o$). This internal model supports two atomic capabilities:
- **World Reconstruction:** The ability to infer a complete world structure from partial observations and generate novel views. Formally, this is modeled as `p_θ(o_ϕ_n+1 | o_ϕ_1..., o_ϕ_n)`, implying implicit learning of internal representations for view synthesis.
- **World Simulation:** The capacity to predict how the world evolves under actions. Formally, `p_θ(o_t+1 | o_≤t, a_≤t)` signifies predicting future observations based on past observations and actions.
2. **Deliberate Reasoning with World Modeling:** Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is re-conceptualized as an evolving sequence of logical steps (`r_i`) interspersed with generated observations (`o_i`). This framework allows for three instantiations of world modeling:
- **Implicit World Modeling:** `o_i = ∅`, where no explicit observation is generated, relying on latent representations.
- **Verbal World Modeling:** `o_i` are explicit textual descriptions (e.g., coordinates, symbolic matrices).
- **Visual World Modeling:** `o_i` are explicit generated images.
3. **The Visual Superiority Hypothesis:** This central hypothesis posits that visual generation, as a world model, is superior for tasks grounded in the physical world. This superiority is attributed to:
- **Informativeness:** Visuals offer concrete, information-rich, and fine-grained grounding, overcoming the ambiguity and representational bottlenecks often faced by purely verbal descriptions in complex physical scenarios.
- **Prior Knowledge:** Visual world knowledge (e.g., physical interactions, spatial transformations) is inherently complementary to symbolic knowledge, often acquired through perception and embodied experience, which can be effectively learned from large-scale visual pre-training data.
4. **Formal Analysis:** The authors provide theoretical backing for their hypothesis. Theorem 1 decomposes end-to-end answer error into reasoning errors and world-modeling errors, highlighting a trade-off. Theorem 2 quantifies the reduction in reasoning uncertainty provided by explicit world modeling, linking it to the informativeness of observations about underlying states. Corollary 1 explains that in fully observable and deterministic environments, explicit world modeling may offer no additional benefit, which aligns with empirical findings for simpler tasks. An appendix further details generalization bounds for transfer learning under distribution shift, linking it to the concept of modality-specific prior knowledge.

**Empirical Validation:**
To test their theoretical framework, the authors devised a rigorous experimental setup:
1. **VisWorld-Eval Suite:** A new benchmark suite comprising seven tasks was designed. These tasks were specifically chosen to isolate and demand either world simulation or world reconstruction capabilities:
- **World Simulation:** Paper Folding, Multi-hop Manipulation, Ball Tracking, Maze, Sokoban.
- **World Reconstruction:** Cube 3-View Projection, Real-World Spatial Reasoning (from MMSI-Bench).
2. **Model Selection:** BAGEL, a state-of-the-art open-source Unified Multimodal Model (UMM), was chosen as the base model due to its integrated verbal and visual generation capabilities.
3. **Training and Evaluation Protocol:**
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** BAGEL was fine-tuned on task-specific datasets. CoT data was constructed using rule-based templates and refined with advanced LLMs (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro) to generate implicit, verbal, or visual intermediate steps. Loss functions combined cross-entropy (for text) and flow-matching loss (for images).
- **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** After SFT, models underwent RLVR. This step optimized the verbal generation components using GRPO, while regularizing visual generation via KL-divergence to a reference model. This allowed for assessing performance gains from refined reasoning policies.
- **Baselines:** Performance was systematically compared across the three world-modeling formulations (implicit, verbal, visual CoT). An additional comparison with a pure Vision-Language Model (Qwen2.5-VL) was made to ensure that UMMs' verbal reasoning was not compromised.
4. **In-depth Analysis:**
- **Sample Efficiency:** Measured how much training data was required for comparable performance across modalities.
- **Task Difficulties:** Evaluated performance across varying task complexities, including out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **World Model Fidelity:** Quantitatively assessed the accuracy of generated intermediate visual/verbal states against ground truth, focusing on structural correctness.
- **Probing Implicit World Models:** Used MLPs to probe the hidden representations of UMMs to determine if models implicitly learn world states, even when not explicitly generating them.

This comprehensive methodological approach allowed the researchers to not only demonstrate the benefits of visual generation but also to systematically explain *when* and *why* these benefits arise, grounded in a strong theoretical framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations, conducted on the custom-designed VisWorld-Eval suite using a state-of-the-art UMM (BAGEL), yielded several key findings that strongly support the visual superiority hypothesis:

1. **Suboptimal Zero-Shot Performance of VLMs:** Initial zero-shot evaluations of advanced VLMs (e.g., Gemini 3 Flash/Pro, Seed 1.8, GPT 5.1) on VisWorld-Eval tasks revealed generally suboptimal performance, especially on challenging tasks like Paper Folding, Ball Tracking, Cube 3-View Projection, and Real-World Spatial Reasoning. This underscores the limitations of current multimodal AI systems in these domains and the need for improved reasoning strategies.

2. **Visual World Simulation Significantly Boosts Reasoning:** For tasks primarily requiring world simulation (Paper Folding, Multi-hop Manipulation, Ball Tracking), interleaved CoT with visual world modeling substantially outperformed purely verbal CoT.
- **Reasons for Improvement:** The gains are attributed to the richer expressiveness and stronger prior knowledge inherent in the visual modality. Verbal models struggled to precisely ground object coordinates, perform accurate arithmetic, or capture complex spatial transformations and symmetries (e.g., in paper folding), often leading to ambiguity and hallucinations. Visual generation, conversely, provided clearer, more grounded intermediate states.
- **Sample Efficiency:** Visual world modeling demonstrated significantly higher sample efficiency (e.g., 4x less SFT data for comparable performance on Paper Folding), indicating that UMMs possess stronger prior knowledge regarding visual transformations, likely acquired during large-scale pre-training.

3. **Visual World Reconstruction Also Enhances Performance:** Tasks demanding world reconstruction capabilities (Cube 3-View Projection, Real-World Spatial Reasoning) also benefited considerably from visual world modeling.
- **Stronger Prior Knowledge:** Visual generation effectively leveraged prior knowledge of visual object rotation and scene reconstruction. In contrast, verbal descriptions for novel views or complex spatial relationships were prone to hallucinations and less effective.
- **Performance Across Difficulties:** Visual world modeling maintained a consistent advantage over verbal world modeling across varying task difficulties in the Cube 3-View Projection task, even outperforming verbal models by approximately 10% on out-of-distribution cube stack sizes.
- **World Model Fidelity:** Critically, visual world modeling demonstrated dramatically better fidelity in generating intermediate views (exceeding 50% accuracy for structural correctness in Cube 3-View Projection) compared to verbal world modeling, which yielded near-zero fidelity in representing structural changes.

4. **Visual World Modeling is Not Universally Beneficial:** For certain grid-world tasks like Maze and Sokoban, visual world modeling showed no notable advantage. Interestingly, implicit world modeling (without explicit state tracking) sometimes achieved the best performance.
- **Simplicity of State Tracking:** The authors argue that these tasks involve relatively simple state tracking (e.g., maintaining one or two 2D coordinates), which verbal reasoning alone can adequately handle.
- **Emergent Implicit World Models:** Probing the internal representations of the UMM (BAGEL) revealed that even the *pre-trained* model possessed emergent internal representations predictive of maze states. Supervised fine-tuning further enhanced this implicit predictability to near-perfect accuracy, even without explicit coordinate supervision. This explains why explicit (verbal or visual) world modeling offered no substantial benefit for such tasks, as the model could implicitly track the necessary state information.

5. **UMMs Maintain Verbal Reasoning Capabilities:** Comparisons with a pure VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) demonstrated that BAGEL's verbal reasoning capabilities (in implicit and verbal world modeling settings) were comparable, indicating that UMMs do not compromise verbal reasoning in favor of visual generation. This confirms that the observed benefits of visual world modeling are due to its inherent advantages rather than any degradation in verbal components.

6. **RL Enhances All CoTs, but Gap Persists:** While Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) consistently improved performance across all CoT formulations, the performance gap favoring visual world modeling persisted. This suggests that the superiority of visual world modeling is fundamental to the approach itself and not merely a result of insufficient post-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes significant contributions by providing a principled and empirically validated framework for understanding the role of visual generation in multimodal AI reasoning. Its findings have several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging Human Cognition and AI:** By formalizing world models in AI based on human dual-coding theory, the paper offers a robust conceptual bridge between cognitive science and artificial intelligence. This enables a clearer pathway towards developing AI systems that reason more like humans, particularly in domains where human intuition and visual processing excel.

2. **Clarifying the Value of Visual Generation:** For the first time, this work systematically clarifies *when* and *how* visual generation can genuinely enhance reasoning in UMMs, moving beyond the previously inconclusive and heuristic findings in the literature. The "visual superiority hypothesis" is empirically supported for physical-world tasks, guiding future research on where to best apply multimodal generative capabilities.

3. **Advancing Multimodal AI Capabilities:** The research demonstrates that visual generation, acting as a "visual world model," offers a complementary and powerful pathway for reasoning. It highlights its particular value in physical and spatial intelligence, where purely verbal methods struggle with representational bottlenecks and a lack of grounded prior knowledge. This opens new avenues for developing more robust, general-purpose AI that can tackle complex real-world problems.

4. **Introduction of VisWorld-Eval:** The public release of the VisWorld-Eval suite is a significant contribution to the research community. By providing a set of tasks specifically designed to isolate and test atomic world-model capabilities, it offers a standardized and principled benchmark to facilitate future investigations into multimodal reasoning with visual world modeling.

5. **Implications for Embodied AI:** The findings, especially the visual superiority hypothesis, are highly relevant for the burgeoning field of embodied intelligence. Visual world models can enable embodied agents to better understand their environments (e.g., imagining occluded regions, interpreting intentions from an egocentric perspective), support more reliable and natural interactions, and facilitate sophisticated planning by mentally simulating action outcomes. This work advocates for the integration of flexible multimodal world modeling and reasoning within unified models as a core component for future physical and embodied AI, moving beyond loosely coupled modules or single-step reasoning.

6. **Guiding Future Research:** The paper also lays out clear directions for future work, including:
- **Expanding Task Domains:** Applying the world-model perspective to other complex reasoning tasks like visual jigsaw puzzles, STEM reasoning (e.g., mathematical diagram editing), and scientific discovery.
- **Advanced RL Algorithms:** Developing reinforcement learning techniques specifically tailored for interleaved verbal-visual generation to further enhance world-model fidelity and emergent reasoning capabilities.
- **Probing Internal Representations:** Continuing to investigate and compare the internal representations of VLMs and UMMs to better understand how multimodal training shapes the acquisition and organization of world knowledge.

In essence, this research provides foundational theoretical and empirical evidence for the critical role of multimodal world models in the development of more human-like, general-purpose AI, especially in navigating and interacting with the physical world. It shifts the focus towards integrated multimodal reasoning, leveraging the strengths of both verbal and visual modalities.
