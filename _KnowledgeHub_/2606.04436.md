# 3DThinkVLA: Endowing Vision-Language-Action Models with Latent 3D Priors via 3D-Thinking-Guided Co-training

> [!summary] Summary
> 3DThinkVLA equips Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with latent 3D priors through a 3D-thinking-guided co-training framework, allowing them to perform implicit 3D spatial reasoning from 2D images. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance with 98.7% average success on the LIBERO benchmark and robust generalization on LIBERO-Plus and real-world tasks, without requiring external 3D sensors at inference.

## Problem

- VLA models typically rely on 2D visual inputs, leading to a gap between 2D semantic understanding and the 3D spatial reasoning needed for physical interaction.
- Previous methods for injecting 3D information often require specialized external 3D sensors, modify pre-trained VLM backbones, or fail to provide high-level spatial reasoning (e.g., object localization, relative orientation).
- Co-training with 3D data can suffer from a "prompt-induced reasoning gap," where action-prediction prompts bypass learned spatial priors, defaulting to "action shortcuts" instead of leveraging 3D understanding.

## Method

- A co-training framework simultaneously trains a VLA model on action data and real-world 3D reasoning data, disentangling 3D geometry perception and spatial reasoning into distinct ==latent== representations.
- A latent 3D geometry perception module uses a lightweight adapter to align intermediate visual features from the VLM with 3D geometric features from a 3D foundation model in a patch-level manner.
- An online 3D reasoning distillation module transfers high-level spatial thinking from a teacher's explicit 3D reasoning prompts to the student's action prompts via a shared reasoning anchor token in latent space, mitigating the prompt-induced reasoning gap.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on the LIBERO benchmark with an average success rate of **98.7%**, including **100%** success in LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Object suites.
- Demonstrated robust generalization on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, achieving an **81.0%** average zero-shot success rate across various perturbations (e.g., Camera, Robot, Language, Layout).
- Outperformed previous methods in real-world evaluations on a Realman robot, showing robust performance (**88.0%** for height variation, **93.3%** for transparent container placement) on challenging manipulation tasks requiring 3D spatial understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D geometry perception (low-level) and 3D spatial reasoning (high-level) are distinct capabilities that can be effectively disentangled and injected at different feature hierarchies within a VLA model.
> - A shared reasoning anchor token in latent space can effectively distill high-level 3D spatial reasoning from explicit prompts to action-prediction prompts, ensuring spatial understanding is utilized during action execution without explicit Chain-of-Thought text generation.
> - Directly incorporating disentangled geometric and reasoning features into the action-query token (spatially augmented action integration) prevents "action shortcuts" and fully leverages learned spatial knowledge for robust action prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in embodied artificial intelligence, aiming to enable robotic agents to interpret natural language instructions and execute corresponding actions in physical 3D environments. A central challenge in this field is the discrepancy between the 2D visual inputs typically utilized by VLA models and the inherent 3D spatial reasoning required for effective interaction with the real world.

Prior research has approached this challenge through several avenues. One common strategy involves the explicit injection of 3D information, such as point clouds or depth maps, either directly into the action prediction head or by modifying the Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone. While these methods can enhance 3D perception, they often necessitate specialized external 3D sensors or require substantial architectural changes to the VLM, potentially disrupting its pre-trained vision-language alignment. Another class of implicit approaches aims to integrate 3D geometry-aware information by aligning VLM visual features with those from 3D foundation models. However, this direct feature alignment carries the risk of interfering with the VLM’s original semantic understanding and often focuses predominantly on low-level geometry, falling short of providing the high-level spatial reasoning capabilities critical for complex robotic manipulation.

Furthermore, some methods incorporate 3D data during pre-training to imbue models with spatial reasoning. This strategy, however, frequently encounters a domain gap between the 3D pre-training data and downstream VLA tasks, leading to issues like catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning. Co-training on both action data and 3D data has been explored as a mitigation for catastrophic forgetting and to improve performance, but this paper identifies a "prompt-induced reasoning gap." This gap refers to the phenomenon where general 3D visual question answering (VQA) prompts activate spatial reasoning effectively, but specific action-prediction prompts can cause the model to bypass these learned spatial priors, instead defaulting to "action shortcuts" that do not leverage 3D understanding.

3DThinkVLA positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified limitations. It aims to provide VLA models with both low-level 3D geometry perception and high-level 3D spatial reasoning implicitly, using only 2D images at inference time, without requiring external 3D sensors, significant VLM backbone modifications, or explicit chain-of-thought text generation. The work builds upon the understanding that while co-training helps, a more nuanced mechanism is needed to ensure spatial reasoning is effectively utilized during action prediction, rather than being bypassed. It seeks to disentangle and integrate these distinct spatial capabilities at different hierarchical levels of the model, proposing a targeted solution to the prompt-induced reasoning gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to implicitly perform 3D spatial reasoning during action prediction, operating solely on 2D images at deployment. This is achieved by endowing VLA models with latent 3D priors through a novel 3D-thinking-guided co-training framework, named 3DThinkVLA.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations observed in existing VLA models and their training paradigms:

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap:** Current VLA models predominantly rely on 2D image inputs, which creates a fundamental disconnect between the 2D semantic understanding they acquire and the 3D spatial reasoning essential for successful interaction with physical environments. While previous efforts have tried to inject 3D information, they often face practical constraints.
2. **Limitations of Explicit 3D Information Injection:** Methods that explicitly inject 3D data (e.g., point clouds, depth maps) typically require external 3D sensors, adding complexity and cost to robotic systems. Furthermore, integrating such data often necessitates non-trivial modifications to the pre-trained VLM backbone, which can disrupt its original vision-language alignment and lead to semantic misalignment.
3. **Incompleteness of Implicit Geometry Injection:** Existing implicit approaches, which align VLM visual features with 3D foundation models, tend to focus primarily on low-level geometric cues. They frequently fail to provide the high-level 3D spatial reasoning, such as inferring precise object positions, relative orientations, and distances, which is crucial for nuanced robotic manipulation tasks.
4. **Catastrophic Forgetting and Domain Gap:** When models are pre-trained on 3D data and then fine-tuned on VLA tasks, the domain gap between these datasets often results in catastrophic forgetting of the valuable knowledge learned during pre-training. Co-training on both 3D data and VLA data has shown promise in mitigating this, but it revealed a deeper issue.
5. **The Prompt-Induced Reasoning Gap:** A central motivation is the identification and characterization of this specific challenge. Even when co-trained with 3D data, VLA models exhibit a "prompt-induced reasoning gap." While explicit 3D visual question answering (VQA) prompts successfully activate the model’s spatial reasoning capabilities, standard action-prediction prompts can cause the model to bypass these learned spatial priors. Instead, the model defaults to "action shortcuts," leading to suboptimal performance, as it neglects to leverage its spatial understanding for robust manipulation.

To overcome these motivations, the core insight driving 3DThinkVLA is that 3D geometry perception (low-level) and 3D spatial reasoning (high-level) are distinct capabilities that can be disentangled and injected at different feature hierarchies within the VLA model. This allows for a more targeted and effective integration of 3D intelligence without compromising the VLM backbone's integrity or requiring external sensors at inference. The framework aims to preserve the pretrained VLM's semantic alignment and generalization abilities while enhancing its capacity for spatial intelligence in robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

3DThinkVLA operates as a 3D-thinking-guided co-training framework designed to infuse VLA models with latent 3D priors. The approach centers on disentangling 3D geometry perception from spatial reasoning and injecting these capabilities at different feature hierarchies. The model is co-trained on both VLA action data and real-world 3D reasoning data. During training, three tightly coupled components work in concert primarily within the latent space. The base VLA model utilizes Qwen3-VL-2B as the backbone and an OFT-style action head for action prediction.

The framework comprises the following key components:

1. **Latent 3D Geometry Perception Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To equip the VLA model with low-level geometric priors without modifying the pre-trained VLM backbone or requiring external 3D sensors at inference.
 - **Mechanism:** Intermediate visual features are extracted from a specific layer (18th layer) of the vision encoder. These features, denoted as F_v, are then passed through a lightweight **geometry adapter (G)**, which consists of a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and LayerNormalization. The geometry adapter projects F_v into a geometry latent space.
 - **Alignment:** The projected features (F_Geo = G(F_v)) are aligned with 3D geometric features (F_3D) obtained from a 3D foundation model, VGGT, in a patch-level manner. This alignment is achieved using a cosine similarity loss (L_geo = 1 - S(F_3D, F_Geo)), encouraging the adapter to capture geometric cues. At inference, only the lightweight geometry adapter is retained.

2. **Online 3D Reasoning Distillation Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To mitigate the prompt-induced reasoning gap, preserve spatial reasoning patterns, and transfer high-level spatial thinking from explicit teacher reasoning prompts to student action prompts.
 - **Mechanism:** A teacher-student distillation scheme operates entirely in latent space.
 - **Shared Reasoning Anchor Token (τ_R):** A special token, τ_R, is introduced and inserted after the task instruction in both the teacher and student branches. This position allows the anchor to absorb visual observations and task semantics without being heavily influenced by downstream action decoding. During co-training on the 3D VLM stream, τ_R is also emitted as the first output token, strengthening its representation of 3D spatial reasoning and preventing feature collapse.
 - **Teacher Branch:** Processes input with a task instruction (L_task) and an explicit 3D spatial reasoning prompt (L_teacher) to elicit strong spatial reasoning activations. The last hidden state of the teacher's reasoning anchor (H_R_teacher) is gathered, and gradients are stopped through this branch (sg(·)) to maintain its spatial reasoning capability and stabilize distillation.
 - **Student Branch:** Processes input with the same task instruction (L_task) but a standard action-prediction instruction (L_action). The last hidden state of the student’s reasoning anchor (H_R_student) is obtained.
 - **Reasoning Adapter (R):** A lightweight reasoning adapter (MLP with LayerNorm) projects H_R_student into a latent reasoning space.
 - **Distillation:** Token-level distillation is performed by minimizing a cosine similarity loss (L_reasoning = 1 - S(H_R_teacher, R(H_R_student))). This implicitly transfers high-level 3D reasoning priors into the action-prediction pathway without requiring explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) text generation. The teacher and student branches share parameters, and no additional models are needed to provide distillation signals. At inference, only the lightweight reasoning adapter is retained.

3. **Spatially Augmented Action Integration:**
 - **Purpose:** To prevent action shortcuts and fully leverage the learned spatial knowledge by directly incorporating disentangled geometric and reasoning features into action prediction.
 - **Mechanism:** Features from the geometry latent space (from the perception module) and the reasoning latent space (from the distillation module) are first projected into the action latent space using two additional lightweight MLP layers, yielding H_A_geo and H_A_reasoning.
 - **Injection:** These projected spatial features are then injected into the action-query token (τ_A) through element-wise addition: `Action Head(H_A + H_A_geo + H_A_reasoning)`, where H_A is the last hidden state of τ_A. This provides the action head with complementary low-level geometric and high-level reasoning knowledge.
 - **Regularization:** To balance influences and reduce overfitting, H_A_geo and H_A_reasoning are randomly dropped on a subset of training samples.

**Training Objective and Inference Path:**
The model is trained with a co-training strategy. In each training step, two forward passes are performed: one with VLA data and one with 3D VLM data, accumulating gradients before a single backward update.
- **VLA-step objective:** `L_vla = L_action + λ_a L_geo + λ_d L_reasoning`. L_action is the L1 distance between predicted and ground truth action chunks.
- **3D VLM stream objective:** `L_vlm = λ_3D L_CE`. This stream uses data from Spatial Reasoner, including Basic3D-QA, SpatialReasoning-QA, and SpatialReasoning-CoT, enabling explicit 3D perception and relational reasoning. For this stream, the reasoning anchor token is specifically required to be emitted as the first output token, followed by autoregressive 3D-reasoning text generation, to strengthen its representation.
- **Total Loss:** `L_total = L_vla + L_vlm`.
At deployment, the 3D foundation model and the teacher branch are discarded. Only the lightweight geometry and reasoning adapters are retained, allowing the model to perform implicit 3D reasoning purely from 2D images without needing 3D sensors, external foundation models, or explicit text generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that 3DThinkVLA achieves robust performance across multiple robotic manipulation benchmarks and real-world tasks.

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** 3DThinkVLA attained state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance with an average success rate of 98.7%. This included 100% success rates in the LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Object suites, 98.8% in LIBERO-Goal, and 95.8% in LIBERO-Long. The consistent high performance across diverse task suites indicates the model's capability in various manipulation scenarios.

- **LIBERO-Plus Benchmark:** In zero-shot evaluation on LIBERO-Plus, designed to test generalization under various perturbations, 3DThinkVLA achieved the highest average success rate of 81.0%. It demonstrated robustness across dimensions such as Camera (73.8%), Robot (64.5%), Language (78.0%), Light (98.4%), Background (94.8%), Noise (84.7%), and Layout (81.5%). Qualitative analysis highlighted its advantage in tasks involving height variations, where it produced accurate height-aware predictions, unlike baselines that often misestimated object heights.

- **SimplerEnv Benchmark:** The framework's generalization was further validated on SimplerEnv, where it achieved an average success rate of 72.9%, surpassing previous methods. This result across different benchmarks supports the broad applicability of the framework.

- **Real-world Evaluation:** On a Realman robot platform, 3DThinkVLA demonstrated robust and consistent performance across three challenging real-world tasks: generalization under height variation, transparent container placement, and spatial position understanding. It significantly outperformed previous methods lacking explicit 3D thinking, with success rates of 88.0% for Task 1, 93.3% for Task 2, and 61.3% for Task 3, underscoring the practical utility of incorporating 3D spatial reasoning.

- **Ablation Study on Co-training and Adapter Design (Table 4):**
 - Co-training with 3D data (R3) provided a substantial gain (97.9% average success) over a baseline without co-training (R1, 95.8%), and was more effective than co-training with 2D data (R2, 97.4%).
 - Adding the geometry adapter (R4, 98.3% average) further improved performance, particularly on LIBERO-Long (from 93.0% to 95.8%), suggesting benefits for long-horizon tasks.
 - The reasoning adapter (R5, 98.6% average) contributed additional improvement.
 - The reasoning anchor alone (R6, 98.3% average) showed a notable improvement in the Goal suite (98.2% to 99.4%), indicating its role in goal-oriented decision-making.
 - The full model (R7) achieved the best overall performance (98.7% average), confirming the complementary nature of geometry perception and reasoning distillation.

- **Ablation Study on Reasoning Information Fusion (Table 6):** An element-wise additive fusion strategy for reasoning information within the action head demonstrated the highest performance (98.7% average success), outperforming cross-attention fusion (98.0%) and gate fusion (98.4%).

- **Ablation Study on Distillation Signals (Table 7):** Distilling knowledge using the reasoning anchor as an intermediate representation proved more effective, lightweight, and stable than attempting to distill from explicit reasoning text (privileged information), which incurred significant computational overhead and unstable signals.

- **Comparison of Explicit vs. Implicit 3D Geometry (Table 8):** Implicit 3D geometry modeling via the geometry adapter (97.6% average) was more effective than explicit injection of raw geometric features derived from point clouds using encoders like PTv3 (97.3%) or DP3 (96.5%). This suggests that implicitly modeling 3D relationships within the VLM's latent space provides more robust priors.

- **Co-training Gradient Analysis (Figure 5):** Quantitative analysis using Projection-Space Similarity (PSS) showed that 3D co-training led to stable optimization (PSS consistently above 0.4) and a synergistic effect, mitigating catastrophic forgetting. The online 3D reasoning distillation module further accelerated convergence and enhanced PSS during co-training, confirming its role in aligning learning objectives.

- **Preservation of General VLM Capabilities:** The co-training framework demonstrated preservation of the underlying VLM's general capabilities, with the model successfully performing tasks such as code generation, storytelling, and mathematical reasoning, indicating that catastrophic forgetting was mitigated effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The 3DThinkVLA framework presents a notable contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence and VLA models, with several significant implications:

1. **Addressing Core VLA Limitations:** The work directly tackles fundamental limitations of existing VLA models, specifically the critical gap between 2D visual inputs and the inherent need for 3D spatial reasoning in physical environments. It also provides a solution to the "prompt-induced reasoning gap" and mitigates catastrophic forgetting during VLA fine-tuning, which are prevalent issues in the domain.

2. **Implicit 3D Spatial Reasoning from 2D Inputs:** A key impact is enabling VLA models to perform sophisticated 3D spatial reasoning (e.g., object localization, relative orientation, distance calculation) implicitly, using only standard 2D camera feeds at inference time. This eliminates the dependency on costly and complex 3D sensors (like depth cameras or LiDAR) or external 3D foundation models for deployment, making robotic systems more practical, efficient, and robust.

3. **Novel Disentanglement and Integration Strategy:** The methodology introduces a novel approach to disentangle 3D geometry perception from high-level spatial reasoning and integrate these at different feature hierarchies. This targeted injection prevents disruption of the VLM backbone's pre-trained vision-language alignment, a common pitfall in previous implicit 3D integration methods.

4. **Effective Knowledge Transfer through Latent Distillation:** The online 3D reasoning distillation module, utilizing a shared reasoning anchor token and operating entirely in the latent space, is a significant technical advancement. It enables the seamless transfer of high-level spatial thinking from explicit reasoning prompts to action-prediction paths without requiring computationally expensive or potentially unstable explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) text generation during inference. This mechanism contributes to both efficiency and robustness.

5. **State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization:** By achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple challenging and diverse benchmarks—including LIBERO, LIBERO-PLUS (known for its robustness evaluation), SimplerEnv, and real-world manipulation tasks—3DThinkVLA demonstrates the effectiveness, generalization ability, and broad applicability of its framework. Its particular strength in handling tasks with height variations and spatial understanding in the real world indicates a tangible improvement in robotic manipulation capabilities.

6. **Practicality for Real-world Robotics:** The lightweight inference design, which discards auxiliary training modules (3D foundation model, teacher branch) and retains only minimal adapters, makes 3DThinkVLA highly practical for real-world robotic deployment. This efficiency, combined with strong performance and 3D-input-free operation, lowers the barrier for integrating advanced spatial reasoning into robotics.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper's identification and characterization of the prompt-induced reasoning gap, along with its proposed solution, opens new avenues for research into more effective integration of reasoning capabilities into action models. The quantitative analysis of co-training gradients using Projection-Space Similarity also provides a useful diagnostic tool for multi-task learning in VLA contexts. The commitment to making the code public will facilitate further research and development in this area.

In summary, 3DThinkVLA provides a robust and efficient framework that endows VLA models with a nuanced understanding of 3D space, leading to more capable and adaptable embodied AI agents.
