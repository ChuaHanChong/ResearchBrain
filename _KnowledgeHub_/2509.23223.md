# SAC-Loco: Safe and Adjustable Compliant Quadrupedal Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore and Sun Yat-sen University introduced SAC-Loco, a safety-aware framework for quadrupedal robots that integrates adjustable disturbance compliance with robust failure prevention. The system demonstrated higher success rates under external forces up to 600N, maintaining stability during intentional dragging attempts that caused baselines to fail at 120.4N and 193.8N peak forces.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) controllers for quadrupedal robots often prioritize strict task execution (e.g., velocity tracking) and lack adaptable responses to significant or persistent external disturbances.
- Achieving a broad spectrum of compliance behaviors, from resisting small perturbations to yielding to large forces, while ensuring robust safety (preventing falls) under a wide range of external forces, remains a challenge for legged robots.
- Many compliant control methods either struggle with large disturbance magnitudes (e.g., above 50N to 500N) or require explicit force sensing, complicating real-world deployment.

## Method

- **SAC-Loco** employs a safety-aware locomotion control system comprising three learned modules: a compliant policy for adjustable response to external forces, a safe policy for active balance restoration using Corrected Capture Point (CCP) dynamics, and a safety critic for real-time state evaluation.
- A teacher-student ==reinforcement learning== framework distills complex policies, trained with privileged observations in simulation, into student policies that operate solely on proprioceptive sensing for physical deployment.
- The safety critic continuously monitors the robot's state and orchestrates seamless transitions between the compliant policy and the safe policy when the robot approaches unsafe conditions, proactively preventing falls.

## Results

- SAC-Loco achieved consistently higher success rates across external force magnitudes (up to 600N), impact directions, and durations compared to baseline methods, which saw significant performance drops under larger or lateral forces.
- On hardware, the robot demonstrated adjustable compliance, yielding or resisting pulling forces based on a tunable parameter, and successfully maintained locomotion during intentional dragging attempts that caused baselines to fail at average peak forces of **120.4** N and **193.8** N.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the teacher-student framework, CCP dynamics in the safe policy, and the learned safety critic, showing drastic performance degradation when these components were removed or replaced.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating specialized policies for compliant locomotion and robust recovery, coordinated by a learned safety critic, allows for superior performance across a wide range of disturbance magnitudes compared to single-policy or fixed-compliance approaches.
> - The teacher-student distillation framework effectively transfers complex behaviors learned with privileged information in simulation to policies deployable on physical hardware using only proprioceptive sensors, eliminating the need for explicit force sensing.
> - Leveraging Corrected Capture Point (CCP) dynamics in the safe policy significantly enhances a robot's ability to actively restore balance and prevent failure under large, destabilizing forces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Legged robots, particularly quadrupeds, are engineered to emulate the agile and robust locomotion observed in biological counterparts. A core challenge in this field involves enabling robots to exhibit diverse compliance behaviors while maintaining stability when subjected to external forces. Animals demonstrate a versatile capacity to either resist or yield to disturbances, and crucially, to move with strong perturbations to prevent falls. Achieving such adaptable, safe, and compliant behaviors in legged robots is considered essential for their reliable operation in complex, real-world environments.

Current reinforcement learning (RL) controllers have facilitated advanced quadrupedal tasks, including complex terrain traversal, physical interaction, and obstacle avoidance. However, many existing RL-based approaches primarily focus on strict task execution, such as velocity or position tracking, often overlooking the impact of significant or persistent external disturbances. While deep RL inherently confers some robustness, achieving a broad spectrum of responses, ranging from resisting small perturbations to yielding to large forces, requires more advanced strategies.

Prior work has explored both model-based and learning-based methods for disturbance adaptation. Model-based approaches often rely on external force estimation and model predictive control, but typically face limitations in handling large perturbations (e.g., above 50N) and are often restricted to predefined gaits, limiting high-speed locomotion. Learning-based methods have shown greater generalization and robustness. Some methods use reward shaping for transient perturbations, while others propose torque-based policies for fixed compliance. Hierarchical RL has been applied to modulate velocity commands for active compliance, but can suffer from issues related to integrated force estimation and the need for retraining when compliance parameters change. Recent RL frameworks have integrated virtual mass-spring-damper models for adjustable compliance, but their success rates can decrease under very large forces (e.g., exceeding 500N), indicating a limitation in relying on a single policy for both precise tracking and high force tolerance.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing the specific gap of achieving both adjustable compliance and robust safety under a wide range of external force disturbances, including large impulses. It proposes a framework that integrates specialized policies for compliant locomotion and robust recovery, coordinated by a safety critic, moving beyond the limitations of single-policy or fixed-compliance approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a safety-aware compliant locomotion framework for quadruped robots that integrates adjustable disturbance compliance with robust failure prevention. The authors aim to enable quadruped robots to exhibit diverse compliance behaviors while ensuring stability when experiencing external forces, a key capacity often lacking in existing control methods.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:
- **Achieving Adjustable Compliance:** The authors aim to design a control system that allows the robot to tune its response to external forces, enabling it to either resist or yield to disturbances based on a controllable compliance level. This is motivated by the observation that animals exhibit versatile responses to external forces, and such adaptability could expand future applications, including navigating complex environments and engaging in safer human-robot interactions.
- **Ensuring Robust Safety and Failure Prevention:** A significant motivation is to equip the robot with the ability to maintain balance and prevent falls even under strong or prolonged external perturbations. This involves developing mechanisms for rapid recovery from dangerous states. The challenge lies in extending control beyond the limits of compliant behavior, where external forces might destabilize the robot.
- **Operation Without Explicit Force Sensing:** A practical objective is to enable the deployment of compliant policies on physical robots without requiring explicit force sensing. This simplifies hardware requirements and potentially improves robustness against sensor noise or failure.
- **Real-time Safety Monitoring and Policy Coordination:** The researchers aim to develop a mechanism that can continuously assess the robot's safety status and orchestrate a seamless transition between compliant locomotion and recovery behaviors. This is crucial for prompt intervention when the robot approaches unsafe states, rather than reacting only after a failure has occurred.
- **Broadening the Range of Disturbance Tolerance:** The work is motivated by the limitations of existing methods, which often struggle with large or persistent external forces, leading to failures or restricted behavioral diversity. The goal is to develop a system capable of handling a significantly wider range of external force disturbances.

In summary, the motivation stems from the recognized gap in quadrupedal control for robust, adjustable, and safe interaction with dynamic, unpredictable environments, aiming to bring robot locomotion closer to the adaptive capabilities of biological systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, SAC-Loco, is a safety-aware locomotion control system comprising three learned modules: a compliant policy, a safe policy, and a safety critic. These modules are designed to operate in coordination to achieve adjustable compliance and robust safety under external disturbances.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The robot receives a command vector `c = [v'x, v'y, ω'z, k]` specifying linear and angular velocities and a tunable compliance level parameter `k`. Under an external force `F = [Fx, Fy, Fz]`, the objective is to track the command velocity while exhibiting adjustable compliance to `F` (yielding more for larger `k`, resisting more for smaller `k`). When `F` becomes destabilizing, the objective shifts to restoring balance and stabilization. Failure is defined as the robot's base contacting the ground or falling over. The control policies generate target joint positions `q*t` from proprioceptive observations `st = [gt, ωt, qt, q̇t, at-1, ct]`, which are then tracked by a low-level proportional-derivative (PD) controller.

**B. Compliant Policy Training**
To enable adjustable compliance, a velocity modulator calculates a desired velocity `v* = [v*x, v*y]` based on the external force `F` and the compliance parameter `k` (e.g., `v*x = clip(v'x + kFx, -vx,max, vx,max)`). This `v*` represents the velocity the robot *should* track to achieve the desired compliance.

A teacher-student reinforcement learning framework is employed:
1. **Teacher Force Compliant Policy (`π*comply`):** This policy is trained in simulation using additional "privileged observations" `o't = [st, zt, Qt, vt, Ft, τext,t]`, which include direct access to external forces `F` and torques `τext`. Reward functions are designed to track the desired velocity `v*` and include regularization terms for stable posture and smooth joint actions.
2. **Student Compliance Policy (`πcomply`):** The teacher policy is distilled into a student policy `πcomply` that operates solely on observable states `st` (proprioceptive sensing) for physical deployment. The student policy receives a stacked observation history of length 20 (`ot = [st-20..., st]`) to compensate for the lack of privileged information. PPO distillation, including an MSE loss between teacher and student actions (`Ldistillation`) and additional reward terms (`raction`, `rdirection`) that encourage student actions to mimic teacher actions, is used. Asymmetric PPO is adopted where the critic observes privileged information.

**C. Safe Policy Training**
The safe policy's objective is to actively restore balance and prevent failure under large force disturbances. This is grounded in the Corrected Capture Point (CCP) dynamics.
1. **Corrected Capture Point (CCP):** The method calculates the planar position of the support polygon centroid (SPC) `pcent,t`. To restore balance, `pcent,t` needs to be shifted to `pCCP,t` by planar offsets (`δx,t`, `δy,t`) which are derived from the robot's velocity, base height, mass, gravity, and external forces. A yaw orientation offset `δψ` is also computed to align the robot's x-axis with the disturbance force, converting lateral disturbances into longitudinal ones for better stability.
2. **Teacher Safe Policy (`π*safe`):** This policy is trained in two stages using the same privileged observations as `π*comply` (with `ct` replaced by `δx,t`, `δy,t`, `δψ,t`):
 - **Stage I (Pose Tracking):** The robot is trained to track randomly sampled target offsets (`δx`, `δy`, `δψ`) as quickly as possible.
 - **Stage II (Disturbance Recovery):** External forces are applied, and `δx,t`, `δy,t`, `δψ,t` are calculated at each timestep. Reward functions minimize the position tracking error between the SPC and the target CCP, and the yaw orientation offset.
3. **Student Safe Policy (`πsafe`):** The `π*safe` is distilled into `πsafe` using the same distillation method as `πcomply`.

**D. Safety Critic Training**
A safety critic `Vsafe` is trained to evaluate the robot's state in real time to facilitate timely switching between `πcomply` and `πsafe`.
1. **Data Collection:** Rollouts of `πcomply` are collected under disturbances. If a failure occurs, the 150 preceding timesteps of privileged observations `o'unsafe` are stored in an unsafe dataset `Dunsafe`.
2. **`πsafe` Training Initialization:** `o'unsafe` from `Dunsafe` are used as state initializations during Stage II training of `πsafe`, allowing it to practice recovery from dangerous conditions.
3. **Labeling `Dunsafe`:** After `πsafe` is trained, it is executed starting from states in `Dunsafe` to determine if it can successfully recover the robot. A binary label (success/failure) is assigned to each `o'unsafe`.
4. **`Vsafe` Training:** `Vsafe` is trained via supervised learning using gradient descent on the observable states `sunsafe` and their corresponding recovery success/failure labels from `Dunsafe`.
5. **Deployment:** During operation, the output of `Vsafe(st)` (ranging from 0 to 1) is compared to a safety threshold `ϵ`. If `Vsafe(st)` falls below `ϵ`, `πsafe` is activated.

**E. Training Details**
All policies are trained in the Isaac Gym simulator using PPO with 4096 parallel environments. Policy networks are MLPs, and the safety critic uses a GRU followed by an MLP head. Curriculum learning is applied, progressively increasing command velocity ranges and disturbance magnitudes. Domain randomization (robot mass, friction, joint gains, sensor noise, disturbance ranges, impact duration) is used to enhance sim-to-real transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated SAC-Loco through extensive simulations and hardware experiments, comparing its performance against two learning-based baselines: HAC-Loco and FACET.

**A. Metrics and Baselines**
Performance was assessed using:
- **Effective compliance range (∆C):** Quantifies the range of velocity response to external forces.
- **Command velocity tracking error (`e_v`):** Measures the deviation from desired command velocity during force-free periods.
- **Success rate (SR):** Percentage of times the robot maintains locomotion without failure under disturbances.
- **Average power (`P_motor`):** Energy consumed by motors.

**B. Evaluation on Compliance and Safety**
1. **Adjustable Compliance:** SAC-Loco demonstrated adjustable compliance, with the average effective compliance (C) monotonically increasing with the compliance parameter `k` across different disturbance magnitudes. The framework achieved a larger `∆C` (wider range of compliance behaviors) compared to HAC-Loco and FACET.
2. **Safety Performance:**
 - **Disturbance Magnitude, Direction, and Duration:** SAC-Loco consistently exhibited higher and more uniform success rates (SR) across various external force magnitudes (up to 600N), impact directions, and durations. HAC-Loco showed significant SR decrease under lateral forces, and both baselines' SR declined with larger force magnitudes and longer durations. SAC-Loco notably maintained higher SR under both larger forces and longer impact durations.
 - **Overall Performance Comparison (Table IV):** SAC-Loco consistently achieved higher SR than baselines across all tested force ranges. It also demonstrated lower command velocity tracking error (`e_v`), indicating faster recovery from compliance to velocity tracking. Energy consumption (`P_motor`) was generally lower for SAC-Loco compared to baselines, except under very large forces (exceeding 400N).

**C. Effect of Different `ϵ` for Policy Switching**
The safety threshold `ϵ` for activating the safe policy was investigated. For moderate disturbances (<200N), performance was insensitive to `ϵ`, suggesting `π_safe` was rarely activated. For larger forces (>200N), setting `ϵ = 0` (effectively disabling `π_safe`) led to a decrease in SR, confirming the necessity of the safe policy for robust recovery. Increasing `ϵ` improved SR with only marginal increases in compliance deviation (`e*v`) and power consumption, indicating that safety was enhanced without significantly compromising compliance or energy efficiency.

**D. Policy Distillation Performance**
The teacher-student framework was evaluated. The student compliant policy (`π_comply`) successfully achieved different compliance levels under sinusoidal forces, tracking the desired `v*` with small errors relative to the teacher policy (`π*comply`). Simulation results showed the student policy performing slightly below the teacher, confirming that the distillation process effectively preserved compliance and recovery capabilities while enabling deployment with only proprioceptive sensing.

**E. Ablation Studies on SAC-Loco**
Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of key components:
- **"SAC-Loco w/o teacher":** Training policies directly without a teacher resulted in a drastic decrease in SR, significantly higher velocity tracking errors, and increased power usage, underscoring the importance of the teacher-student framework.
- **"π_safe w/o o'unsafe":** Training the safe policy without using unsafe state initializations led to a slight decrease in SR.
- **"π_safe w/o CCP":** Training the safe policy without leveraging Corrected Capture Point (CCP) dynamics resulted in a substantial decrease in SR, highlighting the effectiveness of CCP for robot recovery.
- **"SAC-Loco w/o V_safe":** Replacing the learned safety critic with a rule-based policy switching mechanism led to more failures, demonstrating the superior effectiveness of the learned `V_safe` in identifying dangerous states.

**F. Hardware Deployment**
SAC-Loco was deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, running at 50 Hz.
- **Compliance Demonstration:** The robot successfully exhibited adjustable compliance, resisting or yielding to pulling forces from another robot based on the `k` parameter. It also pulled a 70kg person on an office chair, with the pulling speed varying as `k` was adjusted.
- **Safety Under Large Disturbances:** During intentional dragging attempts designed to induce failure, SAC-Loco experienced no failures, whereas baselines failed at average peak forces of 120.4 N (HAC-Loco) and 193.8 N (FACET). The system demonstrated prompt switching to `π_safe` when `V_safe` output fell below `ϵ`, restoring balance and aligning the robot's body with the force direction (turning to face a front pull, or turning its tail to a rear pull).
- **Pulling Force Capability:** The robot demonstrated forward and backward pulling forces of 10.54 kg and 10.45 kg, respectively, while maintaining stable locomotion, outperforming a reported baseline (FACET).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SAC-Loco framework represents an advancement in quadrupedal robot control by addressing the critical combination of adjustable compliance and robust safety under a wide range of external force disturbances.

**Significance:**
- **Enhanced Adaptability:** The framework provides quadruped robots with the ability to dynamically adjust their compliance levels, enabling them to respond to external forces in a versatile manner, much like biological systems. This is significant for operating in unpredictable, dynamic real-world environments where interactions with external forces are common.
- **Robust Failure Prevention:** By integrating a dedicated safe policy and a learned safety critic, SAC-Loco proactively detects and mitigates potentially dangerous situations. This moves beyond reactive control by enabling pre-emptive recovery from states that might otherwise lead to robot failure.
- **Operational Efficiency:** The teacher-student reinforcement learning approach allows for the development of complex behaviors using privileged information in simulation, which are then distilled into policies deployable on hardware using only proprioceptive sensing. This eliminates the need for explicit force sensors, simplifying hardware and potentially reducing system cost and complexity.
- **Broader Disturbance Tolerance:** The demonstrated performance against significantly larger and more persistent external forces than prior methods indicates a substantial improvement in the robustness of quadrupedal locomotion controllers.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Safer Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability of robots to exhibit controlled compliance and robust safety under external forces is fundamental for safe collaboration with humans. SAC-Loco's approach could contribute to the development of robots that can operate more closely and safely alongside people in shared workspaces.
- **Expanded Application Domains:** Robots equipped with such capabilities could find new applications in tasks requiring physical interaction, such as assistive load carrying, inspection in complex industrial settings, or search and rescue operations where external impacts are frequent. The ability to pull heavy loads, as demonstrated in hardware, also highlights potential for utility applications.
- **Foundational Research:** This work establishes a framework for integrating multiple learned policies and a safety monitoring mechanism, opening avenues for future research into more sophisticated adaptive control strategies, such as autonomous adjustment of compliance levels based on environmental context, or integration into highly dynamic maneuvers like jumping and manipulation.
- **Reduced Development Cycle:** The teacher-student learning paradigm and extensive domain randomization contribute to a more efficient development process for complex robot behaviors, facilitating quicker transfer from simulation to real-world deployment.
