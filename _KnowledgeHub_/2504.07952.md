# Dynamic Cheatsheet: Test-Time Learning with Adaptive Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> Dynamic Cheatsheet (DC) gives a black-box language model a persistent, self-curated text memory that changes at inference time. A curator model stores concise strategies and code snippets, and a variant retrieves similar past examples before answering. No weights change and no labels are needed. Claude 3.5 Sonnet more than doubles its AIME accuracy, and GPT-4o rises from 10% to 99% on Game of 24.

## Problem

- Deployed language models are frozen and treat every query independently, so they re-derive the same insights and repeat the same mistakes with no memory of past successes or failures.
- Fine-tuning or dynamic evaluation needs gradient access and labels, so it cannot be used with black-box APIs. Static retrieval-augmented generation draws on a fixed corpus and does not learn from the model's own experience.
- Naively appending the full interaction history grows the context without limit and adds uncurated noise. That raises inference cost and can hurt accuracy, so selective, evolving retention is needed.

## Method

- **DC-Cu** (cumulative) alternates a ==generator== step that answers using the current memory with a ==curator== step that rewrites the memory. The curator keeps generalizable insights, prunes wrong or superseded entries, and compacts the rest, with no ground-truth labels.
- **DC-RS** (==retrieval and synthesis==) first retrieves the top-3 most similar past query-answer pairs by ==embedding cosine similarity==. The curator refines the memory with them before the generator answers, so the current query also shapes the memory.
- One ==black-box model== plays both generator and curator through ==task-agnostic prompts==. The memory is a compact text ==cheatsheet== of strategies, formulas and reusable Python snippets, and the prompts also encourage code generation and execution.

## Results

- GPT-4o on Game of 24 rose from **10%** to **99%** under DC-RS, against **19%** for the memory-free DC-∅ prompt. Math Equation Balancer went from about **45-50%** to **98-100%** for both GPT-4o and Claude.
- Claude 3.5 Sonnet's AIME 2024 accuracy went from **23.3%** to **50.0%** and AIME 2025 from **6.7%** to **36.7%** under DC-Cu. GPQA-Diamond rose from **59.6%** to **68.7%**, and MMLU-Pro Physics from **74%** to **82%**.
- Full-history appending scored **26.7%** on AIME 2024 for Sonnet versus **50.0%** for DC, and dropped GPT-4o from **20.0%** to **6.7%** versus **40.0%** for DC-RS. Smaller models such as GPT-4o-mini gained little.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Much of the gain comes from discovering a reusable tool, such as a brute-force Python solver, and then recalling it. Persistent memory converts one-time discovery into reusable skill, rather than adding knowledge.
> - Curated memory beats raw history and plain retrieval. Deciding what to keep, refine or discard matters more than how much is stored, and it keeps the context compact.
> - Test-time memory amplifies models that can already produce good solutions. Weak models fill memory with flawed strategies and cannot fix them, so a capability threshold gates the benefit.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research fits into the broader research landscape in several key areas:

- **In-Context Learning and Meta-Learning:** The paper builds upon the concept of in-context learning, where language models adapt to new tasks based on examples provided in the prompt. Dynamic Cheatsheet extends this by creating a persistent memory that evolves over time, effectively enabling meta-learning at test time.
- **Memory-Augmented Language Models:** The paper contributes to the field of memory-augmented neural networks and language models, where external memory components are used to enhance the capabilities of the model. Unlike traditional approaches that use fixed knowledge bases, Dynamic Cheatsheet dynamically curates its memory based on its experiences.
- **Test-Time Adaptation and Online Learning:** This research is relevant to the growing field of test-time adaptation, where models are adapted to new data distributions or tasks during inference. Dynamic Cheatsheet offers a parameter-free approach to test-time adaptation, making it suitable for black-box language model APIs.
- **Tool Usage and Code Generation:** The paper also connects to research on enabling language models to use external tools and generate code. The Dynamic Cheatsheet framework encourages models to learn and reuse code snippets for solving complex problems, highlighting the potential for improved tool usage.
- **Overcoming Limitations of LLMs:** The research addresses a key limitation of current language models: their lack of persistent memory and ability to learn from past experiences. Dynamic Cheatsheet attempts to bridge the gap between isolated inference events and the cumulative, experience-driven learning characteristic of human cognition.

The paper cites a wide range of related work, including:

- **Test-time adaptation:** Studies on parameter-free online test-time adaptation and dynamic evaluation of transformer language models.
- **Memory-augmented models:** Research on memory networks, neural Turing machines, and retrieval-augmented language models.
- **Tool usage:** Studies on language models using tools and code execution.
- **In-context learning:** Work on chain-of-thought prompting and meta-prompting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives and motivation of this research are:

- **To address the limitation of current language models in retaining and reusing insights from previous queries:** Current language models typically operate in a vacuum, processing each input independently without learning from past experiences.
- **To develop a lightweight framework that endows language models with a persistent, evolving memory:** The Dynamic Cheatsheet framework aims to enable language models to store and reuse accumulated strategies, code snippets, and problem-solving insights.
- **To improve the performance of language models on complex reasoning tasks:** The research seeks to demonstrate that test-time learning with adaptive memory can significantly enhance performance across a range of tasks.
- **To enable language models to adapt and improve their problem-solving skills without needing explicit ground-truth labels or human feedback:** The Dynamic Cheatsheet framework is designed to be self-curated and does not require human intervention.
- **To provide a practical approach for continuously refining responses and cutting routine errors:** The research aims to offer a way to improve the reliability and consistency of language model outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology and approach of the research involve:

- **Introducing the Dynamic Cheatsheet (DC) framework:** The framework consists of two core modules: a solution generator and a memory curator.
- **Developing two variants of the DC framework:** DC-Cumulative (DC-Cu) and DC with Retrieval & Synthesis (DC-RS).
 - **DC-Cu:** This variant cumulatively expands and refines the memory items.
 - **DC-RS:** This variant incorporates a retrieval mechanism to retrieve relevant past input-output pairs and refines the memory before generating a response.
- **Evaluating the DC framework on multiple challenging benchmarks:** The benchmarks include AIME math exams, GPQA-Diamond, Game of 24, Math Equation Balancer, and MMLU-Pro Engineering and Physics.
- **Comparing the performance of the DC framework to several baselines:** The baselines include baseline prompting, DC-empty memory, full-history appending, and dynamic retrieval.
- **Using state-of-the-art language models, such as GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet:** The research evaluates the effectiveness of the DC framework across a range of language models.
- **Employing task-specific accuracy metrics:** The accuracy metrics include soft match and functionally correct.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the research are:

- **Dynamic Cheatsheet enables test-time learning and reduces repetitive errors:** The framework allows language models to learn from past experiences and avoid making the same mistakes repeatedly.
- **Dynamic Cheatsheet provides substantial improvements across various challenging reasoning benchmarks:** The framework significantly improves performance on AIME math exams, GPQA-Diamond, Game of 24, and Math Equation Balancer.
- **Dynamic Cheatsheet fosters generalization and provides gains over full-history appending:** The framework selectively stores and refines high-value content, leading to better performance than simply appending the entire conversation history.
- **Dynamic Cheatsheet fosters efficient tool usage/code generation:** The framework encourages language models to generate and reuse code snippets for solving complex problems.
- **Model scale and capacity impact Dynamic Cheatsheet effectiveness:** The framework is most effective when used with large, capable language models.
- **Test-time task similarity and example ordering can amplify Dynamic Cheatsheet's overall impact:** The framework performs better when test examples share structural similarities and are presented in a curriculum-style manner.

Specific performance improvements include:

- Claude 3.5 Sonnet's accuracy on AIME 2024 more than doubled under DC-Cu.
- GPT-4o's success rate on the Game of 24 puzzle increased from about 10% to 99% under DC-RS.
- GPT-4o and Claude reached near-perfect accuracy on Math Equation Balancer with DC.
- Claude achieved a 9% improvement on GPQA-Diamond and an 8% boost on MMLU-Pro Engineering and Physics problems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are:

- **Provides a novel approach to improve the performance of language models:** The Dynamic Cheatsheet framework offers a promising way to enhance the capabilities of language models without requiring fine-tuning or access to model parameters.
- **Addresses a key limitation of current language models:** The framework tackles the lack of persistent memory and ability to learn from past experiences, which is a significant limitation of current language models.
- **Enables language models to adapt and improve their problem-solving skills:** The framework allows language models to continuously refine their responses and cut routine errors, leading to improved reliability and consistency.
- **Has the potential to be applied to a wide range of tasks and domains:** The framework can be used to improve the performance of language models on complex reasoning tasks, such as math problem solving, question answering, and code generation.
- **Offers a practical approach for deploying language models in real-world settings:** The framework is parameter-free and can be used with black-box language model APIs, making it suitable for deployment in practical settings.
- **Contributes to the development of more human-like AI systems:** The framework bridges the gap between isolated inference events and the cumulative, experience-driven learning characteristic of human cognition.
