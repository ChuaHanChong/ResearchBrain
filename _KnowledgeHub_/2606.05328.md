# The Invisible Hand of Physics: When Video Diffusion Models Know More Than They Show

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper demonstrates that modern video diffusion models implicitly encode physical understanding, showing that both physical plausibility and quantitative physical variables are linearly decodable from their internal states. The research introduces a method for inverting the diffusion process to probe internal representations, revealing that physical knowledge emerges during the denoising flow rather than being pre-encoded.

## Problem

- It is unclear whether video diffusion models truly internalize physical laws or simply reproduce statistical patterns from training data.
- Current video diffusion models often struggle to extrapolate basic mechanical principles beyond their training distributions.
- Analyzing internal physical understanding in diffusion models is challenging due to the lack of direct access to latent trajectories corresponding to real videos.

## Method

- Developed an approximate ==reverse sampling== method to invert the diffusion generation process, recovering latent trajectories and intermediate internal states for real videos.
- Employed linear probes to evaluate physical plausibility (binary classification) and quantitative physical variables (regression) from the internal transformer states of various video diffusion models.
- Utilized causal interventions during forward generation to identify which specific transformer blocks influence the physical plausibility of generated videos.

## Results

- Diffusion models achieved an average probe accuracy of **81.27%** on physical plausibility benchmarks (IntPhys and InfLevel), outperforming dedicated representation-learning baselines like V-JEPA 2.
- Linear regressors trained on internal states predicted quantitative physical variables (e.g., initial position and velocity) from a controlled physics dataset with high R2R^2R2 scores (up to **0.99**).
- Causal interventions revealed that the physical plausibility of generated videos is influenced by distributed transformer blocks across network depth, with smaller, more compressed models exhibiting higher probe accuracy for physical concepts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical understanding, including both binary plausibility and quantitative variables, emerges internally within video diffusion models as an unsupervised byproduct of the denoising computation.
> - The physical signal is not inherited from the VAE latents but is constructed by the diffusion model, sustained across most of the reverse trajectory, and most decodable at intermediate network depths.
> - The decodability of physical signals depends on the fidelity of the approximate ODE trajectory, indicating that physics resides in the dynamic flow rather than solely in the reconstructed visual endpoints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has witnessed substantial progress, particularly with the advent of large-scale diffusion models such as OpenAI's Sora, DeepMind's Veo, and NVIDIA's Cosmos. These models are capable of generating visually realistic and temporally coherent video sequences, leading to their consideration as potential candidates for general-purpose world simulators in domains like robotics, planning, and scientific discovery.

Despite the visual fidelity, a fundamental question persists regarding whether these models truly internalize the underlying physical laws of the scenes they generate, or if they merely reproduce statistical patterns observed in their training data. Prior evaluations have indicated that current video diffusion models often fail to extrapolate basic mechanical principles beyond their training distributions, tending to mimic the closest in-distribution examples. Furthermore, these models typically operate within the latent spaces of variational autoencoders (VAEs) that are primarily trained for reconstruction, without explicit optimization for capturing semantic or physical structure. This contrasts with dedicated representation-learning encoders, like V-JEPA, which are specifically designed with self-supervised predictive objectives known to encourage the emergence of physical understanding.

The current research positions itself by investigating whether physical knowledge might emerge *internally* within these generative diffusion models, even when they are not explicitly trained with predictive or physics-aware objectives. Previous work on physical understanding in video models has largely focused on two areas: improving the physical realism of *generated outputs* (e.g., through motion priors or differentiable simulators) or analyzing physical understanding in *self-supervised representation learning* models. This paper addresses a gap by probing the internal representations of standard video diffusion models, which are trained for generation, to determine if they implicitly acquire physical reasoning capabilities as a byproduct of their denoising process. A key challenge addressed by this work is the lack of direct access to the internal latent trajectories corresponding to real videos in diffusion models, which is necessary for such internal probing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain whether modern video generation models, specifically diffusion models, internally encode physical structure. This question arises from the observation that while these models can generate increasingly realistic and temporally coherent videos, it remains uncertain if this visual plausibility stems from an actual understanding of physical laws (e.g., constant acceleration, momentum conservation, impenetrability) or merely from statistical pattern matching of training data.

The motivation behind this investigation is multi-faceted:

1. **Assessing Generative Models as World Simulators**: The growing capabilities of large-scale video diffusion models have led to their proposition as potential general-purpose world simulators. Understanding if these models internalize physical laws is critical for validating this potential, as visual realism alone does not guarantee a true understanding of dynamics.
2. **Addressing Limitations in Extrapolation**: Existing evaluations indicate that video diffusion models often struggle to extrapolate basic mechanical principles beyond the specific distributions seen during training. This suggests a lack of robust physical understanding, motivating a deeper inquiry into their internal representations.
3. **Exploring Implicit Learning in Denoising**: Video diffusion models typically employ variational autoencoders (VAEs) whose latent spaces are optimized for reconstruction, not for capturing semantic or physical structure. Unlike self-supervised representation encoders (e.g., V-JEPA) that are explicitly designed with predictive objectives to foster physical understanding, diffusion models lack such an explicit mechanism. This raises the fundamental question of whether physical knowledge can emerge *implicitly* as a byproduct of the generative denoising computation itself.
4. **Overcoming Probing Challenges**: A significant obstacle to analyzing internal physical understanding in diffusion models is the inability to access latent trajectories corresponding to *real* videos. These trajectories are crucial for testing whether the model's internal states encode physical plausibility against known ground truth.

Therefore, the specific objectives of the research are:
- To develop a method for approximately inverting the diffusion generation process to recover latent trajectories and intermediate internal states for real videos.
- To evaluate whether physical plausibility (binary classification) and quantitative physical variables (regression) are decodable from the internal transformer states of various video diffusion models.
- To compare the performance of these diffusion models in encoding physical information against established representation-learning baselines.
- To localize *where* within the model's architecture (across depth) and *when* along the denoising trajectory this physical signal emerges.
- To identify which components of the model *causally influence* the physical plausibility of generated videos through targeted interventions.
- To investigate whether the fidelity of the reconstructed latent trajectory impacts the decodability of physical information, beyond mere visual fidelity of the endpoints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core challenge addressed by this work is accessing the internal representations of video diffusion models for real videos, as these models do not natively expose such trajectories. The methodology introduces a **reverse sampling** approach, complemented by **probing protocols** and **causal interventions**.

#### 3.1. Reverse Sampling

To obtain internal features of a video diffusion model for a given real video, the authors propose an approximate inversion of the generation process:
1. **Clean Latent Extraction**: For any real video $X$, its clean latent encoding $\mathbf{Z}_1 = \mathcal{E}(X)$ is obtained using the variational autoencoder associated with the video diffusion model.
2. **Backward Integration**: The learned time-dependent velocity field $v_\theta(\mathbf{Z}_t, t)$ of the flow-based generative model is integrated backward from the clean latent $\mathbf{Z}_1$ (at time $t=1$) to a noise sample $\mathbf{Z}_0$ (at time $t=0$). This process recovers the full trajectory $\{\mathbf{Z}_{t_k}\}$ and all intermediate internal states and attention maps traversed.
3. **Explicit Approximation**: While an exact reverse of the Euler update would require an implicit Euler step involving an iterative solver (significantly increasing computational cost), the authors utilize an explicit approximation. They integrate the velocity field backward using an Euler or Heun scheme, evaluating the velocity at the known endpoint $\mathbf{Z}_{t_{k+1}}$ instead of the unknown $\mathbf{Z}_{t_k}$. This method requires only a single network evaluation per step, matching the cost of forward sampling. Error analysis in Appendix A indicates that the accumulated deviation between explicit and exact implicit reverse sampling is linear in the step size $h$, vanishing as $h \to 0$.
4. **Validation**: The fidelity of the recovered trajectories is supported by re-sampling the recovered $\mathbf{Z}_0$ through the forward process, which reconstructs the original video with only minor artifacts.

#### 3.2. Probing Protocol

To quantify the physical information present in the recovered internal representations, linear probes are employed:
1. **Feature Extraction**: Internal activations from each transformer block along the recovered latent trajectory are extracted at every inversion step.
2. **Linear Probe Training**: For each extracted representation (per block and per inversion step), a linear probe (classifier or regressor) is trained.
 - **Classification**: To predict physical plausibility (binary: plausible/implausible) on benchmarks like IntPhys and InfLevel.
 - **Regression**: To predict quantitative physical variables (e.g., initial position $x_0$, initial velocity $v_0$) from a controlled physics dataset.
3. **Evaluation**: Probes are trained on a 60/40 train/test split, and held-out accuracy or $R^2$ scores are reported, averaged across categories or tasks.

#### 3.3. Causal Intervention

To determine which parts of the model are causally responsible for carrying the physical signal, the authors perform causal interventions inspired by methods for localizing functional components in generative models:
1. **Perturbation**: During the *forward generation* process (from noise to clean video), specific transformer blocks are hooked into, and their output activations $\mathbf{h}$ are perturbed. The perturbation is achieved by adding scaled Gaussian noise: $\tilde{\mathbf{h}} = \mathbf{h} + \alpha \cdot \sigma(\mathbf{h}) \odot \epsilon$, where $\sigma(\mathbf{h})$ is the per-token standard deviation and $\alpha$ is the intervention strength. This ensures consistent interpretation of $\alpha$ across different block depths. The intervention is applied at every denoising step.
2. **Probe Surprise Metric**: For each generated video $V_b$ resulting from an intervention, it is first re-inverted using reverse sampling. The internal states are then scored using the pre-trained linear plausibility probes. The "probe surprise" at a step $s$ is defined as $\psi_s(V) = \text{logit}_{\text{implausible}}(s, V) - \text{logit}_{\text{plausible}}(s, V)$.
3. **Surprise Shift Calculation**: The mean probe surprise $\bar{\psi}(V_b)$ for an intervened video is compared to that of a baseline video $V_{\text{base}}$ generated from the same noise latent without intervention. The surprise shift $\Delta_b = \bar{\psi}(V_b) - \bar{\psi}(V_{\text{base}})$ quantifies the causal influence of perturbing block $b$. A positive $\Delta_b$ implies that corrupting block $b$ makes the video appear less physically plausible to the probe.

#### 3.4. Backbones and Benchmarks

- **Video Diffusion Models**: The study utilized three large-scale latent video diffusion models: WAN-1.3B, LTX-2B, and CogVideoX-2B. These models represent distinct architectural families, allowing for an assessment of the generality of the findings.
- **Representation-Learning Baselines**: V-JEPA 2 and VideoMAE-Large were used for comparative evaluation of physical understanding.
- **Benchmarks**:
 - **IntPhys** [Riochet et al., 2018]: Evaluates the distinction between physically plausible and implausible events across categories like object permanence, solidity, and spatiotemporal continuity.
 - **InfLevel** [Weihs et al., 2022]: Features more complex, compositional scenes with multiple interacting objects and longer temporal dependencies, requiring reasoning about identity and interactions.
 - **Controlled Physics Dataset** [Kang et al., 2025]: Generated by a deterministic 2D physics simulator with known parameters (e.g., initial position, velocity), enabling the assessment of quantitative physical variable encoding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the internal encoding of physical information in video diffusion models:

#### 4.1. Physical Understanding Emerges Internally

Physical plausibility was found to be linearly decodable from the internal states of video diffusion models.
- **Probe Accuracy**: On both IntPhys and InfLevel benchmarks, the average probe accuracy on internal states of diffusion models (WAN-1.3B, CogVideoX-2B, LTX-2B) frequently exceeded that of dedicated representation encoders like V-JEPA 2 ViT-L and VideoMAE-Large. WAN-1.3B achieved an average accuracy of 81.27% across all categories, compared to V-JEPA's 71.36%. This was observed for per-video classification, and also in pairwise evaluations where diffusion models consistently outperformed baselines (Figure 2, 3).
- **Origin of Signal**: Crucially, probing the VAE latents directly (before any flow computation) resulted in chance accuracy (48–53%) across all categories for all evaluated VAEs. This indicates that the physical signal is not inherited from a structured input representation but is constructed by the diffusion model itself during the process of transporting the latent through its learned flow. t-SNE projections confirmed that VAE latents of plausible and implausible videos are visually indistinguishable (Figure 13).

#### 4.2. Emergence Zone Across Depth and Time

The physical signal was sustained across most of the reverse trajectory, from near-noise to near-data, with decodability peaking at intermediate network depths.
- **Time (Noise Level)**: For almost every noise level between $t=0.01$ and $t=0.99$, the best-performing block achieved an accuracy above 76%, indicating persistent physical information throughout the denoising process. The signal was weaker in the earliest blocks and nearest to the clean data ($t=0$).
- **Depth (Transformer Block)**: The strongest linear decodability of physical information clustered within the middle third of the network (e.g., blocks 15–25 for WAN-1.3B and CogVideoX-2B, and slightly later for LTX-2B). This pattern is consistent with previous findings for V-JEPA, suggesting that intermediate-depth emergence is a general property of how video models organize physical computation, irrespective of specific training objectives (Figure 4).

#### 4.3. Causal Localization via Block Interventions

Causal interventions revealed that different transformer blocks contribute to physical plausibility in distinct ways across models.
- **Distributed Causal Influence**: Causal sensitivity was distributed across network depth rather than being confined to a single localized region.
- **Model-Specific Patterns**:
 - WAN-1.3B: Strongest sensitivity in early layers (blocks 0–8), with peak shifts around blocks 3–5, but non-negligible influence from mid and later layers.
 - CogVideoX-2B: Sharp dominance in the first few blocks, followed by a drop in intermediate layers and a partial recovery at later depth.
 - LTX-2B: A bimodal pattern with high sensitivity in both early and late layers, while intermediate layers contributed less (Figure 5).
- **Probing vs. Causal Sensitivity**: Layers exhibiting the highest linear decodability (intermediate depth) did not always coincide with those most causally sensitive to perturbation (often earlier layers). This suggests that physical structure is established early in the computation and then propagated through the network, becoming most linearly accessible at intermediate layers.

#### 4.4. Compressed DiT States Yield Stronger Probing Accuracy

An inverse relationship was observed between the hidden dimension of the DiT (Diffusion Transformer) and the best per-video probe accuracy.
- **Dimension-Accuracy Trade-off**: As the DiT hidden dimension increased, the best per-video accuracy decreased monotonically. WAN-1.3B, the smallest and most compressed model, achieved the highest probe accuracy (Figure 7).
- **Hypothesis**: This trend suggests that narrower models, having limited representational capacity, might prioritize encoding coarse spatiotemporal semantic structure essential for coherent denoising (including physical plausibility) over high-frequency texture details. Wider models, with more capacity, may allocate dimensions to texture-level content, thereby diluting the physical signal.

#### 4.5. Beyond Binary Plausibility

The internal states of diffusion models were found to encode quantitative physical variables, not just binary plausibility.
- **Quantitative Parameter Encoding**: Using a controlled 2D physics dataset, linear regressors trained on internal activations could predict the initial position ($x_0$) and initial velocity ($v_0$) of parabolic ball trajectories with high accuracy.
 - All three diffusion models achieved $R^2 \ge 0.99$ for $x_0$.
 - For $v_0$, WAN-1.3B achieved $R^2 = 0.88$, while the larger CogVideoX-2B and LTX-2B reached $R^2 \ge 0.99$. This aligns with the capacity argument, where finer-grained temporal details required for precise velocity estimation might be better preserved in wider models (Figure 6). These physical parameters are not explicitly supervised during diffusion model training.

#### 4.6. Physics Lives in the Flow

The decodability of physical signals was found to depend on the fidelity of the approximate ODE trajectory, not solely on the visual fidelity of the reconstructed video endpoints.
- **Trajectory Fidelity Importance**: Reducing the number of reverse sampling steps from 100 to 20 for WAN-1.3B caused probe accuracy to collapse from 0.82 to 0.57, despite the reconstructed video retaining mild quality loss. Increasing steps to 40 largely recovered accuracy, which then plateaued (Figure 8).
- **Integrator Accuracy**: Using a more accurate Heun solver instead of Euler for 20 steps partially recovered the physical signal, further supporting the link between decodability and trajectory fidelity (Figure 9).
- **Interpretation**: This implies that the physical signal is a property of the exact ODE trajectory in the continuum limit, meaning that a coarse discretization, while preserving visual endpoints, deviates from the true path the model would integrate, thus losing fidelity in the intermediate states and the physical information they carry.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of advanced generative models by demonstrating that video diffusion models, despite being trained primarily for generation rather than explicit prediction, internally encode meaningful physical structure.

**Key Significance:**
- **Emergent Physical Understanding**: The central finding is that physically meaningful representations can emerge as an unsupervised byproduct of the generative denoising process itself. This challenges the notion that explicit predictive or physics-aware objectives are strictly necessary for models to develop an internal understanding of physical laws.
- **Implicit World Modeling**: The results suggest that by optimizing for temporal coherence and visual realism during denoising, diffusion models implicitly learn and internalize aspects of the physical world's dynamics and constraints.
- **Novel Probing Framework**: The introduction of an approximate reverse-sampling method to access internal latent trajectories of real videos provides a valuable tool for analyzing the internal workings and learned representations of diffusion models, which was previously a significant challenge.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Physics-Aware Guidance**: The identification of internal physical signals opens avenues for leveraging these representations to implement physics-aware guidance mechanisms in video generation. This could lead to models capable of generating videos that are not only visually realistic but also consistently physically plausible, potentially overcoming current limitations in extrapolating mechanics.
- **Enhanced World Models**: The findings suggest that diffusion models' internal states could serve as effective latent spaces for more robust and capable world models, particularly in applications requiring an understanding of physical interactions, such as robotics, reinforcement learning, and scientific simulations.
- **Foundation for Future AI Research**: This work contributes to the broader discussion on the interpretability and capabilities of large AI models, indicating that complex forms of understanding can arise from seemingly simpler generative objectives. It lays groundwork for further research into how physical knowledge is represented and processed within deep learning architectures.

**Limitations (as acknowledged by the authors):**
- The reliance on approximate reverse sampling introduces potential trajectory errors, although reconstruction consistency supports its general validity. Fine-grained conclusions might be sensitive to the discretization choices.
- Linear probes demonstrate decodability (i.e., the signal is present and can be read out) but do not definitively prove that the model explicitly represents physical laws in a mechanistic or human-interpretable manner.
- The probe-based metric for "surprise" may inherit biases from the trained classifier.
- The results are largely correlational. While causal interventions provide some evidence, they do not fully establish a mechanistic, human-interpretable encoding of physical laws within the model.
