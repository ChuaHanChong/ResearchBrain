# The Many Faces of On-Policy Distillation: Pitfalls, Mechanisms, and Fixes

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive empirical study elucidates the conditions under which On-Policy Distillation (OPD) and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) succeed or fail for large language models. The research identifies critical failure mechanisms, including gradient bias and teacher state distortion, and introduces practical solutions that enhance training stability and performance.

## Problem

- Prior research presented conflicting empirical outcomes regarding the effectiveness and stability of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) for large language models (LLMs).
- A lack of clear understanding existed regarding the specific conditions under which OP(S)D methods improve LLM capabilities versus when they lead to instability or performance degradation.
- The underlying mechanistic explanations for the observed successes and failures of OP(S)D were not well-understood, making robust application challenging.

## Method

- Conducted a comprehensive empirical investigation of OP(S)D across diverse LLM architectures, tasks (math reasoning, alignment, system prompt internalization), and distillation configurations.
- Identified and mechanistically explained specific failure modes, such as the distortion of teacher state by student prefixes and optimization instability from biased TopK reverse-KL gradients.
- Developed and evaluated practical stabilizers and fixes, including a stop-gradient TopK KL objective, RLVR-adapted teachers, and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-stabilized students.

## Results

- Replacing the exact TopK reverse-KL with a stop-gradient version successfully removed the biased gradient term, preventing model collapse and achieving stable performance.
- An RLVR-adapted smaller teacher model (Qwen3-1.7B) proved more effective for distillation than a larger, unadapted teacher (Qwen3-8B), leading to stronger supervision signals.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of the student on teacher-generated traces stabilized its output space and regularized response length, enabling subsequent OPD to effectively improve accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - OP(S)D's effectiveness is highly dependent on the task structure and the nature of privileged information (PI), performing well for shared latent rules (e.g., alignment) but struggling with instance-specific PI (e.g., math answers).
> - Student-generated prefixes can distort the teacher model's internal state, leading to unreliable supervision and reduced teacher accuracy in generating consistent continuations.
> - Commonly used unnormalized TopK reverse-KL loss functions introduce a problematic bias (+1 term) in their gradients, causing optimization instability and catastrophic model collapse.
