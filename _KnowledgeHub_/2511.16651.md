# InternData-A1: Pioneering High-Fidelity Synthetic Data for Pre-training Generalist Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Laboratory developed InternData-A1, a large-scale, high-fidelity synthetic dataset and generation pipeline for pre-training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. A VLA model trained exclusively on InternData-A1 demonstrated performance matching or exceeding state-of-the-art models trained on real-world datasets, including effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on complex manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- The prohibitive cost and logistical challenges of collecting vast amounts of real-robot data hinder the development of generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Existing synthetic datasets for robot learning lacked the scale, diversity, and fidelity to effectively pre-train VLA models, leaving real-world data as the primary, yet inaccessible, source.
- A critical challenge was determining if high-fidelity synthetic data could genuinely match the pre-training effectiveness of the strongest real-world datasets for robust generalization.

## Method

- A highly autonomous and compositionally designed ==simulation pipeline== was developed to generate **InternData-A1**, a synthetic dataset comprising over 630,000 trajectories and 7,433 hours of diverse robot interaction data.
- The dataset features physically faithful simulation and ==photorealistic rendering==, covering 4 robot embodiments, 18 skills, 70 tasks, and 227 indoor scenes, including rigid, articulated, deformable, and fluid object manipulation.
- The generation pipeline incorporates modular skill composition, decoupled logic for motion planning, and extensive domain randomization to enhance visual and trajectory diversity, improving sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- A VLA model pre-trained solely on InternData-A1 achieved an average **6.2%** higher success rate on real-world regular tasks compared to a model trained on a leading proprietary real-world dataset.
- The system demonstrated impressive zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving an average success rate exceeding **50%** on ten complex tasks without fine-tuning, with a simulated-to-real data efficiency ratio of approximately 8:1 for complex tasks and 1:1 for simpler ones.
- The highly optimized generation pipeline enabled the cost-effective creation of 209.7 hours of robot data per day on 8 RTX 4090 GPUs at a cost below $**0.003** per episode, with a 2-**3x** performance improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, high-fidelity synthetic data can serve as a primary and effective pre-training source for VLA models, comparable to and often surpassing real-world datasets.
> - Trajectory diversity, particularly through multi-skill compositions and articulation tasks, is a more crucial driver for effective VLA pre-training than the sheer volume of simple pick-and-place tasks.
> - Photorealistic rendering and extensive domain randomization in synthetic data significantly reduce the sim-to-real gap, facilitating strong zero-shot transfer even for complex, multi-skill manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "InternData-A1" directly addresses a critical challenge in the development of generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation: the scarcity and cost of large-scale, diverse, and high-fidelity real-world robot data.

**Current Landscape:** Recent advancements in embodied intelligence have demonstrated that large-scale real-robot pre-training is highly effective in endowing VLA models with strong generalization capabilities across various tasks, scenes, and robot embodiments. Noteworthy examples include datasets like the 𝜋-series (e.g., 𝜋0) and models like RT-1 and RT-2, which have shown impressive results by leveraging millions of real-world trajectories. However, collecting such data is inherently resource-intensive, demanding skilled human teleoperators, specialized hardware, and significant financial investment. This makes large-scale real-world data largely inaccessible to most research groups, hindering systematic studies into the data requirements for successful VLA pre-training and limiting the broader community's ability to develop generalist robot policies.

**The Role of Synthetic Data:** Simulation offers a promising alternative, with its rich asset libraries, controllable environments, and potential for automated data generation. Existing simulated datasets, such as ManiSkill2, RLBench, LIBERO, CALVIN, and RoboCasa, have explored various aspects of synthetic data generation. However, these datasets have historically suffered from several limitations: they often cover narrow skill sets (e.g., primarily pick-and-place), focus mainly on rigid objects, still require non-trivial human operation or extensive manual policy design, and have rarely been validated at a scale comparable to leading real-world datasets for comprehensive VLA pre-training. Consequently, a significant open question has remained: can high-fidelity synthetic data, when scaled sufficiently in terms of embodiments, scenes, skills, and physical realism, truly match the pre-training effectiveness of the strongest real-world datasets?

**InternData-A1's Contribution to the Landscape:** InternData-A1 pioneers a paradigm shift by providing the *first evidence* that synthetic data alone can indeed match the performance of the strongest real-world datasets, like the proprietary 𝜋-dataset, in pre-training a VLA model. This work fundamentally validates the substantial value and untapped potential of large-scale, high-fidelity simulation for embodied AI. By demonstrating that a model pre-trained exclusively on InternData-A1 achieves comparable (and often superior) performance to state-of-the-art models trained on real data, the paper directly challenges the long-held assumption that real-world data is indispensable for achieving robust generalization in VLA models. It posits simulation as not just a complementary tool but a potentially equivalent or even superior primary source of pre-training data, especially when considering scalability and accessibility. This positions InternData-A1 as a significant step towards democratizing access to large-scale robotic data and accelerating research in generalist robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind InternData-A1 stems from the prohibitive costs and logistical challenges associated with collecting vast amounts of real-robot data, which is currently a bottleneck for advancing generalist VLA models. The resource-intensive nature of real-world data collection—requiring skilled teleoperation, specialized hardware, and extensive human labor—renders large, diverse real-world datasets largely infeasible for most research groups. This limitation prevents a systematic understanding of the data requirements crucial for effective VLA pre-training.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the InternData-A1 project are:

1. **Prove the Efficacy of Synthetic Data:** To provide the first conclusive evidence that high-fidelity synthetic data, when generated at sufficient scale and diversity, can match the pre-training effectiveness of the strongest real-world datasets for VLA models. This aims to reveal the substantial, previously underestimated, value of large-scale simulation.
2. **Develop a Scalable Data Generation Pipeline:** To design and implement a highly autonomous, fully decoupled, and compositional simulation pipeline capable of generating large volumes of diverse, high-fidelity synthetic manipulation data with minimal manual tuning and low cost.
3. **Construct a Comprehensive Synthetic Dataset:** To create InternData-A1, a physically faithful and photorealistic synthetic dataset comprising over 630k trajectories and 7,433 hours of data. This dataset aims for unparalleled diversity across 4 embodiments, 18 skills, 70 tasks, and 227 scenes, covering rigid, articulated, deformable, and fluid-object manipulation.
4. **Demonstrate Strong VLA Model Performance:** To pre-train a VLA model (specifically, using the 𝜋0 architecture) solely on InternData-A1 and demonstrate that its performance matches or exceeds that of models trained on leading real-world datasets (like the 𝜋-dataset) across a wide range of simulated and real-world tasks, including zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
5. **Promote Open Science:** To open-source the InternData-A1 dataset and its generation pipeline. This aims to broaden access to large-scale robotic data, lower the barrier to scalable data creation, and enable the broader embodied AI research community to systematically study data scaling effects.

In essence, the project seeks to validate simulation as a powerful and accessible alternative for foundational VLA model pre-training, thereby democratizing robot learning research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of InternData-A1 revolves around the creation and utilization of a large-scale, high-fidelity synthetic dataset generated by a sophisticated, autonomous simulation pipeline.

**Dataset Characteristics (InternData-A1):**
- **Scale:** Over 630,000 trajectories, 401.4 million frames, and 7,433 hours of robot interaction data.
- **Diversity:** Encompasses 4 distinct embodiments (AgiBot Genie-1, Franka Emika Panda, AgileX Split Aloha, ARX Lift-2), 18 manipulation skills, 70 diverse tasks (including rigid, articulated, deformable, and fluid-object interactions), and 227 indoor scenes (kitchens, study rooms, dining rooms, living rooms). A significant portion (21.77%) consists of long-horizon tasks involving at least three sequential skills.
- **Fidelity:** Features physically faithful simulation and photorealistic rendering to minimize the sim-to-real gap.

**Data Synthesis Pipeline:** The data is generated through a highly autonomous, fully decoupled, and compositional simulation pipeline, designed for efficiency and scalability. It comprises four main stages:

1. **Environment Construction:**
 - **Asset Retrieval:** Given a task description, the pipeline retrieves task-relevant assets from a comprehensive library:
 - **Robots:** Stable USD-type embodiments with verified contact dynamics.
 - **Scenes:** Room-level environments selected from GRUtopia, annotated with manipulation-area metadata for context-aware task placement.
 - **Objects:** A vast library spanning rigid (3,185 from OmniObject3D, Objaverse), articulated (321 from GRUtopia, GAPartNet, GenSim2, Infinite Mobility, ArtVIP), deformable (20 digitized garments), and fluid categories. Objects are annotated with physical attributes, canonical poses, joint axes, material properties, and automatically generated grasp poses (e.g., via AnyGrasp).
 - **Configuration:** All functional annotations are managed through a unified interface, allowing easy and physically faithful asset configuration.

2. **Skill Composition:**
 - **Modular Skills:** Users compose tasks by selecting atomic skills from a library (e.g., Pick, Push, Place, Handover). Each skill is a modular scripted policy that takes object states, robot states, and user-defined constraints as input, outputting a sequence of target end-effector 6D waypoints.
 - **Decoupled Logic:** Waypoints serve as a unified representation, decoupling high-level skill logic from low-level motion execution.
 - **Compositional Design:** Skills can be chained sequentially or in parallel for bimanual or multi-stage tasks. The system supports complex behaviors, including object-level annotations, domain randomization, and collision-aware motion plans. Minimal manual effort is required, mainly for adjusting spatial ranges.

3. **Domain Randomization:** To enhance visual and trajectory diversity and improve sim-to-real transfer, extensive randomization is applied:
 - **Visual Diversity:** Perturbations to primary and wrist camera views (±5° rotation, ±5 cm translation), random light temperature and intensity (from 174 environment maps), replacement of target objects with others from the same category, and randomization of tabletop/background layouts.
 - **Trajectory Diversity:** Object positions and orientations are sampled within task-specific spatial ranges. For manipulation, additional randomness is introduced in contact regions and grasp pose selection (randomly selected from top 40 high-confidence AnyGrasp candidates).

4. **Trajectory Generation & Storage:**
 - **Motion Planning:** The CuRobo motion planner is used to interpolate dense joint-space actions between the waypoints generated by the skill policies.
 - **Physics Validation:** Actions are validated through physics simulation; only successful trajectories are rendered.
 - **Data Format:** Recorded data includes object metadata, language instructions, multi-view RGB images, camera parameters, robot proprioceptive states, and action control labels, all converted into the standard LeRobot format for VLA pre-training.

**Framework Optimization:** To overcome efficiency bottlenecks in traditional integrated planning/rendering pipelines, a multi-level system optimization was implemented:
- **Stage Decoupling:** Trajectory planning (CPU-bound) and visual rendering (GPU-bound) are separated into independent, pipelined stages.
- **Dynamic Resource Scheduling:** Parallel batch processing and dynamic scheduling maximize resource utilization across heterogeneous workloads.
- **Rendering Efficiency:** A "Stacked Rendering" technique further boosts rendering throughput.
- **Cluster Stability:** Balancer and Supervisor modules manage load distribution and monitor system stability in large-scale cluster deployments.
These optimizations resulted in a 2-3x performance improvement, enabling generation of 209.7 hours of robot data per day on 8 RTX 4090 GPUs at a cost below $0.003 per episode.

**VLA Model Pre-training:** For evaluation, the researchers adopted the 𝜋0 model architecture, which consists of a Paligemma vision-language model and a flow-matching-based action expert. A new 𝜋0 model was pre-trained exclusively on InternData-A1 (from Paligemma weights and a randomly initialized action expert) for 680k iterations and benchmarked against the official 𝜋0 checkpoint (trained on the closed-source 𝜋-dataset).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several groundbreaking findings that significantly advance the understanding of synthetic data's role in embodied AI:

1. **Synthetic Data Matches and Exceeds Real-World Data for VLA Pre-training:**
 - **Comparison with 𝜋-dataset (Official 𝜋0):** A 𝜋0 model pre-trained solely on InternData-A1 achieved performance comparable to the official 𝜋0 model (trained on the proprietary 𝜋-dataset) across diverse real-world scenarios.
 - **Simulation Evaluation:** The InternData-A1-pretrained model *outperformed* the official 𝜋0 by 6% in "Easy" mode and 5% in "Hard" mode across 49 bimanual tasks from RoboTwin 2.0. This robust performance, especially in hard settings, suggests that InternData-A1's extensive domain randomization imparts strong robustness to visual and spatial variations.
 - **Real-world Regular Tasks:** Across five representative real-world tasks (e.g., "Heat Sandwich," "Sort Rubbish," "Pass Bottle") spanning two embodiments (Genie-1, ARX Lift-2), the InternData-A1-pretrained model consistently outperformed the 𝜋-dataset baseline by an average of 6.2%. This indicates that despite simulation-reality dynamics differences, the broad action priors learned from InternData-A1 transfer effectively due to robust task properties and VLA models' abstract learning.
 - **Real-world Dexterous Tasks:** On four challenging long-horizon dexterous tasks (e.g., "Fold Clothes," "Unscrew Cap," "Zip Bag"), including evaluation on a *novel embodiment* (ARX AC One), InternData-A1 achieved performance comparable to the 𝜋-dataset. This highlights the dataset's comprehensive coverage of atomic skills and long-horizon tasks, fostering continuous control abilities transferable to novel scenarios.
 - **Benchmarking against Open-Source Datasets:** InternData-A1-pretrained models consistently outperformed those trained on other prominent open-source datasets, including real-world datasets like OXE and Agibot World, and simulation datasets like RoboCasa. Notably, InternData-A1 showed an average improvement of 57.7% over RoboCasa in real-robot evaluations, attributed to its high photorealism and abundant data.

2. **Impressive Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
 - **High Success Rates:** Ten selected simulated tasks achieved direct sim-to-real transfer with an average success rate exceeding 50% without any real-world fine-tuning.
 - **Data Efficiency:** For regular tasks (e.g., "Sort Rubbish," "Wipe Stain"), as few as 200 simulated episodes from InternData-A1 could match the performance of 200 real-world samples. For more complex tasks (e.g., "Flip Package," "Instructional Pick"), approximately 1,600 simulated episodes were needed to match 200 real samples, implying a simulation-to-real data ratio of around 8:1 (and sometimes approaching 1:1).
 - **Robustness to Discrepancies:** Strong zero-shot sim-to-real performance was achieved even with coarse alignment of backgrounds, lighting, object textures, or table layouts, provided camera views and joint action spaces were closely aligned. This demonstrates that InternData-A1's photorealistic rendering and extensive domain randomization effectively narrow the visual gap.
 - **Complex Task Transfer:** InternData-A1 is the first to demonstrate direct sim-to-real transfer for diverse and complex tasks beyond single pick operations, including multi-skill operations, articulation, bimanual manipulation, and logistics tasks (e.g., "Flip Package"), which previously required purely real data.

3. **Insights from Data Component Ablation:**
 - An ablation study showed that all four main components of InternData-A1 (Pick-and-Place (PnP), Articulation, Base tasks, and Long-horizon tasks) contribute meaningfully to effective pre-training.
 - Removing "Base" or "Long-horizon" tasks led to a more significant performance decline than removing "PnP" tasks, despite PnP dominating the dataset by volume. This suggests that task diversity and multi-skill compositions are more critical for VLA pre-training than merely scaling simple pick-and-place tasks.
 - Excluding "Articulation" tasks also caused a substantial drop, indicating their importance in exposing the VLA model to a more diverse action space influenced by varied joint geometries and arm configurations.
 - These findings collectively suggest that **trajectory diversity** may be the core driver of effective pre-training in VLA models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The InternData-A1 project carries profound significance and potential impact across several dimensions of embodied AI and robotics research:

1. **Validating Simulation as a Primary Data Source:** The most significant finding is the unprecedented demonstration that large-scale, high-fidelity synthetic data can match, and in some aspects even surpass, the pre-training effectiveness of leading real-world datasets for VLA models. This fundamentally changes the perception of synthetic data from a mere supplement to a potentially equivalent or superior primary source for foundational model training. This paradigm shift can unlock new avenues for research and development.

2. **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By open-sourcing InternData-A1 and its highly efficient generation pipeline, the project addresses a critical bottleneck in robot learning: access to large, diverse datasets. This initiative significantly lowers the barrier to entry for academic institutions and smaller research groups, enabling them to conduct research on generalist robot policies without the prohibitive costs and logistical complexities of real-world data collection. This is poised to accelerate innovation across the embodied AI community.

3. **Accelerated Research and Development Cycle:** The scalable and cost-effective data generation pipeline means researchers can rapidly generate new data tailored to specific research questions or emerging robot capabilities. This allows for systematic study of data requirements, rapid iteration on model architectures, and exploration of new task domains much faster than with real-world data, thereby significantly accelerating the overall research and development cycle for generalist robots.

4. **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap:** The robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities demonstrated by InternData-A1 are particularly impactful. Achieving over 50% success rates on complex tasks with minimal real-world alignment suggests that the high-fidelity rendering, coupled with extensive domain randomization, effectively narrows the visual and physical gap between simulation and reality. This could drastically reduce the need for expensive real-world fine-tuning or adaptation, paving the way for more direct deployment of policies learned in simulation.

5. **Foundation for Generalist Robotic Policies:** The unprecedented diversity of InternData-A1—spanning multiple embodiments, 18 skills, 70 tasks (including long-horizon, bimanual, and object interaction types like deformable and fluid)—provides a rich and versatile foundation for training truly generalist VLA models. The ablation studies further emphasize the importance of this diversity, particularly multi-skill and articulation tasks, in fostering transferable action priors and continuous control abilities. This contributes directly to the long-term goal of developing robots capable of performing a wide array of tasks in unstructured environments.

6. **Economic and Environmental Benefits:** Shifting data collection from the real world to simulation offers substantial economic savings by reducing hardware wear-and-tear, operational costs, and energy consumption associated with physical robots. This also minimizes the environmental footprint of large-scale robot learning research.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** While groundbreaking, the paper acknowledges limitations, particularly the challenge of simulating highly dexterous tasks like tying shoelaces or threading a needle due to current physics simulator constraints. Future work will aim to expand the dataset's task diversity and dexterity further, with the ultimate goal of firmly establishing large-scale simulation data as a cornerstone for the advancement of VLA models towards truly human-level manipulation capabilities.
