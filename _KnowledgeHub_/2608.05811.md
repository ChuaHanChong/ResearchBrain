# Energy-Guided Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Energy-Guided Flow Matching" (EG-FM) framework introduces a dynamic, sample-adaptive generative trajectory for pixel-space generative models, replacing the fixed clean-image endpoint with a moving spectral endpoint. This approach consistently enhances generation quality and training efficiency across various model architectures and tasks.

## Problem

- Pixel-space generative models struggle to efficiently learn both global structure and fine-grained details simultaneously in high-dimensional image synthesis.
- Standard flow-matching methods use a static generative trajectory, implicitly requiring models to learn the coarse-to-fine progression without explicit guidance.
- Existing coarse-to-fine generation techniques often rely on uniform temporal schedules, failing to adapt to the varied spectral compositions of different images.

## Method

- **EG-FM** redefines the generative trajectory by introducing a ==moving spectral endpoint== that evolves from a low-pass image variant to the full clean image, using a heat-kernel frequency response.
- An ==energy-guided scheduling== mechanism is implemented to control the progressive release of high-frequency information in a sample-adaptive manner, tailoring it to each image's spectral characteristics.
- A new ==velocity target== is derived that accounts for the dynamic endpoint's motion, integrating with standard flow-matching training with negligible computational overhead during training and none during inference.

## Results

- EG-FM consistently improved FID scores and accelerated convergence across diverse pixel-space backbones (DeCo-XL/16, HyperDiT-H, PixelDiT-XL) on ImageNet 256x256, achieving better fidelity in fewer training epochs (e.g., FID **1.45** at **600 epochs** vs. baseline **1.61** at 320 epochs on PixelDiT-XL).
- The framework demonstrated strong scalability, achieving superior FID (**1.68**) and Inception Scores (**295.5**) for ImageNet 512x512 with reduced fine-tuning effort, and enhanced text-to-image generation performance (GenEval **0.85** vs. **0.78** baseline).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of sample-level adaptive frequency release and specific release-clock curvatures, with the method incurring negligible computational overhead (**<1%** wall-clock time) during training and no additional cost at inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding the generative process through a coarse-to-fine spectral evolution, rather than relying on implicit learning, can simplify the task for pixel-space models.
> - Adapting the frequency release schedule based on an image's unique spectral energy significantly improves generation consistency and effectiveness.
> - Modifying the generative path and target velocity, without altering backbone architecture or data, can yield substantial gains in generative model performance and training efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of pixel-space generative models, including diffusion models and flow matching, has advanced, enabling high-fidelity image synthesis without the information loss associated with latent compression. However, generating high-quality images directly in a high-dimensional pixel space presents challenges. These challenges include the need for models to concurrently learn global structure and fine-grained details, managing complex spatial dependencies, and optimizing computational efficiency.

Existing flow-matching methods typically establish a generative trajectory that connects a noise distribution to a fixed clean-image endpoint. This formulation implicitly learns the spectral evolution of images, which means the model is expected to progress towards the same full-spectrum target image throughout the generation process. This approach does not explicitly model the empirically observed coarse-to-fine progression in image generation, where low-frequency components (global structure) are typically established earlier, and high-frequency components (textures and details) emerge later. This unified modeling of all frequencies may complicate the learning task for models. While latent diffusion models mitigate some of these issues by discarding high-frequency components during VAE compression, pixel-level models still face this challenge.

Prior research has addressed pixel-level generation through various means, including improved network architectures, stronger patchify strategies, and additional training objectives. However, the explicit design of generative trajectories, particularly those that integrate frequency evolution, remains an area with potential for further exploration. Current trajectory-centric methods that attempt coarse-to-fine generation often rely on a temporal schedule shared across all samples, which may not account for the inherent variations in spectral composition between different images. Consequently, a uniform time step may represent different stages of reconstruction progress for diverse samples. This approach places the burden on the generative backbone to infer this ordering implicitly from the training data.

The work presented in "Energy-Guided Flow Matching" positions itself by addressing this gap in generative trajectory design. It proposes an explicit method to integrate frequency evolution into the generative path in a sample-adaptive manner. By doing so, it aims to provide a structured transition from global layout to fine-grained appearance, thereby reducing the implicit learning burden on the backbone and potentially improving efficiency and fidelity without increasing model capacity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and training efficiency of pixel-space generative models, specifically those based on flow matching, by explicitly modeling a coarse-to-fine generative trajectory.

The motivation for this objective stems from several observations and limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Implicit Frequency Modeling in Standard Flow Matching:** Standard flow-matching methods interpolate between Gaussian noise and a fixed clean-image endpoint. This means the model's velocity is consistently directed towards the complete, full-spectrum target image from the beginning to the end of the generation process. This implicitly requires the model to learn to coordinate the recovery of both global structure (low-frequency components) and fine-grained details (high-frequency components) within a single, undifferentiated trajectory.
2. **Natural Coarse-to-Fine Progression:** In visual perception and generation, it is generally understood that overall structure (low-frequency information) is established before intricate textures and fine details (high-frequency information). Forcing a model to learn both simultaneously in a high-dimensional pixel space can increase the complexity of the learning task.
3. **Lack of Sample-Adaptive Trajectory Design:** While some methods attempt coarse-to-fine generation, they often rely on a temporal schedule that is uniform across all samples. This overlooks the fact that different images possess varying spectral compositions and thus may require different rates of frequency information release. A static schedule can lead to inefficiencies or suboptimal generation quality, as the "same" time step might correspond to different reconstruction progress for different images.
4. **Computational Burden on Backbone Networks:** When the generative trajectory does not explicitly guide the frequency evolution, the generative model's backbone must infer this complex ordering solely through optimization over the training data. This can increase the required capacity and training time for the backbone, potentially leading to slower convergence and higher computational costs.
5. **Desire for Explicit Inductive Bias:** The authors hypothesize that an explicit inductive bias, in the form of a dynamic and sample-adaptive generative trajectory, could simplify the learning problem for pixel-space models. Such a trajectory would allow the model to establish global structure before synthesizing high-frequency details, mimicking the natural progression of visual information.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the "Energy-Guided Flow Matching" (EG-FM) framework are:
- To replace the fixed clean-image endpoint in standard flow matching with a "moving spectral endpoint" that evolves smoothly from a low-frequency image variant to the full-spectrum clean image.
- To develop an image-specific energy-guided scheduling mechanism that controls the progressive release of high-frequency signal in the moving endpoint, ensuring coarse-to-fine generation tailored to each image's spectral characteristics.
- To derive a new velocity target that is consistent with this dynamic endpoint, ensuring compatibility with standard flow-matching training while maintaining negligible computational overhead.
- To empirically demonstrate that this approach consistently improves generation quality (measured by FID) and training efficiency (fewer epochs) across various pixel-space backbones, resolutions, and generation tasks (class-conditional and text-to-image).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Energy-Guided Flow Matching (EG-FM) modifies the standard flow-matching framework by introducing a dynamic, sample-adaptive generative trajectory. This is achieved by replacing the fixed clean-image endpoint with a moving spectral endpoint and deriving a corresponding energy-guided velocity target.

#### 3.1. Moving Spectral Endpoint

In standard flow matching, a noisy sample `z_t` is defined as a linear interpolation between Gaussian noise `ϵ` and a fixed clean image `x`: `z_t = t x + (1 - t)ϵ`. EG-FM redefines this by introducing a moving spectral endpoint `y_t(x)`:
`z_t = t y_t(x) + (1 - t)ϵ`
The function `y_t(x)` is designed to evolve from a low-pass version of the image `x` at `t=0` to the full clean image `x` at `t=1`. This design ensures that the generative process gradually releases high-frequency information, progressing from coarse structure to fine details. The boundary conditions of flow matching, `z_0 = ϵ` and `z_1 = x`, are preserved.

The moving endpoint `y_t(x)` is constructed using a heat-kernel frequency response. This response acts as a low-pass filter, controlled by an image-specific monotonic heat-time `h(x, t) ∈`. The heat-kernel frequency response is defined as:
`R(h(x, t), ρ) = exp(-a * h(x, t) * ρ^2)`
where `ρ` is the normalized radial frequency, and `a = (πσ_0)^2` (with `σ_0` being a hyper-parameter controlling the initial low-pass strength).
`y_t(x)` is then obtained by applying the inverse Fourier transform to the product of `R(h(x, t), ρ)` and the Fourier coefficients of `x`, `bx(ρ)`:
`y_t(x) = F^-1(R(h(x, t), ρ) * bx(ρ))`
At `t=0`, `h(x,0)=1`, yielding an initial low-pass image `y_0(x)`. As `t` increases to `1`, `h(x,1)=0`, making `R(0, ρ)=1` and `y_1(x)=x`. The heat-time `h(x, t)` is a monotonically decreasing function of `t`.

#### 3.2. Energy-Guided Heat-Time Scheduling

To make the frequency release sample-adaptive, EG-FM introduces an "energy-guided" mechanism for `h(x, t)`. This accounts for the varied high-frequency content across different images.
The information gap `˜G_x` is defined as the total missing spectral energy required to recover the clean image `x` from its initial low-pass version `y_0(x)`. This is calculated as the squared `ℓ_2` distance in the frequency domain.
`˜G_x = Σ_ρ E(ρ) [1 - R(1, ρ)]^2`
where `E(ρ) = ||bx(ρ)||^2_2` is the spectral energy of `x` at frequency `ρ`.
Similarly, the amount of spectral change already recovered from `y_0(x)` to `y_t(x)` (at a given `h(x,t)`) is defined as `G_x(h(x,t))`.
`G_x(h) = Σ_ρ E(ρ) [R(h, ρ) - R(1, ρ)]^2`
To ensure that the same time step `t` corresponds to a comparable relative frequency release across all images, a global non-decreasing "release clock" `q(t): →` (e.g., smootherstep function) is introduced. The sample-dependent `h(x, t)` is implicitly defined by:
`G_x(h(x, t)) / ˜G_x = q(t)`
This equation ensures that for a given `t`, the fraction of recovered spectral energy is consistent across samples. Since `G_x(h)` is nonincreasing in `h`, `h(x, t)` can be uniquely solved numerically using bisection for each sample `x` and time `t`.

#### 3.3. Energy-Guided Velocity

Because `y_t(x)` is a moving endpoint, the target velocity `v_t` for the neural network must account for its motion. Differentiating `z_t` with respect to `t` yields the exact path tangent:
`v_t = dz_t/dt = y_t(x) - ϵ + t ∂_t y_t(x)`
This velocity consists of two terms:
1. `y_t(x) - ϵ`: The basic transport velocity from the noise `ϵ` to the current moving endpoint `y_t(x)`.
2. `t ∂_t y_t(x)`: The endpoint-motion term, which contributes to the trajectory proportionally to `t`.
The derivative `∂_t y_t(x)` is calculated by applying the chain rule to `y_t(x)`:
`∂_t y_t(x) = F^-1([-aρ^2 R(h(x, t), ρ) ∂_t h(x, t) bx(ρ)])`
And `∂_t h(x, t)` is obtained through implicit differentiation of the energy-matching equation:
`∂_t h(x, t) = (∂_t q(t) ˜G_x) / (∂_h G_x(h(x, t)))`
The model `v_θ(z_t, t)` is then trained to predict this energy-guided velocity target `v_t` using an `L2` loss:
`L_FM = E_x,ϵ,t [ ||v_θ(z_t, t) - v_t||^2_2 ]`
This formulation implies that EG-FM only modifies the target velocity and the path, without requiring changes to the backbone architecture, training data, or the denoising process itself.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The default values used for experiments include `σ_0 = 3.5`, a quintic smootherstep function for `q(t) = 6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3`, and 16 bisection iterations for solving `h(x, t)`. Special handling is implemented for endpoint stability at `t=0` and `t=1` to prevent numerical issues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Energy-Guided Flow Matching (EG-FM) was conducted across various generative tasks and backbones, consistently demonstrating improvements in generation quality and training efficiency.

#### 4.1. Class-Conditional Image Generation (ImageNet 256x256)

EG-FM was applied to three different pixel-space generative backbones: DeCo-XL/16, HyperDiT-H, and PixelDiT-XL.
- **DeCo-XL/16:** EG-FM improved the FID score from 1.69 (baseline, 600 epochs) to 1.63 (EG-FM, 440 epochs), indicating faster convergence and better fidelity.
- **HyperDiT-H:** EG-FM achieved an FID of 1.51 (220 epochs), outperforming the baseline FID of 1.56 (600 epochs).
- **PixelDiT-XL:** EG-FM significantly reduced the FID. For instance, at 80 epochs, FID dropped from 2.36 (baseline) to 1.99 (EG-FM). At 200 epochs, EG-FM reached an FID of 1.55, surpassing the baseline's 1.61 at 320 epochs. Continued training with EG-FM further improved the FID to 1.45 at 600 epochs.
The consistent gains across these architecturally diverse backbones suggest that EG-FM provides a transferable path prior rather than a backbone-specific optimization.

#### 4.2. Class-Conditional Image Generation (ImageNet 512x512)

To assess scalability, EG-FM was tested at a higher resolution (512x512) by fine-tuning models initialized from 256x256 checkpoints for a limited number of additional epochs.
- **PixelDiT-XL + EG-FM:** Achieved an FID of 1.68 and Inception Score of 295.5 after only 240 adaptation epochs, outperforming the standard PixelDiT-XL which reached 1.81 FID and 278.6 IS after 850 epochs.
- **HyperDiT-H + EG-FM:** Attained an FID of 1.58 after 260 adaptation epochs, which was the best result among the compared methods at this resolution.
These results indicate that EG-FM effectively transfers across resolutions, enabling strong high-resolution performance with reduced additional training effort.

#### 4.3. Text-to-Image Generation (512x512)

EG-FM was also evaluated on text-to-image generation to test its transferability beyond class conditioning.
- **EG-FM-T2I:** Utilizing a PixelDiT backbone, EG-FM-T2I achieved a GenEval score of 0.85 (compared to 0.78 for PixelDiT-T2I baseline) and a DPG-Bench score of 83.9 (compared to 83.7 for baseline).
- EG-FM-T2I achieved the best DPG-Bench score among all compared methods and the second-best GenEval score, closely trailing DeCo-XXL/16. This demonstrates its ability to preserve text-conditioned semantics while generating pixel-space details.

#### 4.4. Ablation and Analysis

Ablation studies were conducted using PixelDiT-XL trained for 80 epochs to understand the contribution of different EG-FM components.
- **Initial Endpoint Strength (`σ_0`):** Varying `σ_0` showed a U-shaped FID curve, with `σ_0=3.5` yielding optimal performance (FID 1.99). Values too low (closer to baseline) or too high (excessive filtering) degraded performance, confirming the importance of balancing detail suppression and structural guidance.
- **Adaptive Heat-Time Schedule Granularity:** Performance improved with increasingly fine-grained spectral adaptation: shared linear (FID 2.48) < dataset-level (FID 2.11) < class-level (FID 2.03) < sample-level (FID 1.99). The sample-level adaptation was identified as a key component.
- **Release-Clock Curvature (`q(t)`):** Different release clock functions (linear, smoothstep, smootherstep, sigmoid) were compared. The quintic smootherstep yielded the best FID (1.99), suggesting that the temporal allocation of energy release along the path influences generation quality.
- **Generality Across Model Sizes:** EG-FM consistently outperformed standard FM baselines across PixelDiT-B/16, PixelDiT-L/16, and PixelDiT-XL/16 models, indicating its broad applicability across model capacities.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) Scale:** EG-FM achieved its best FID (1.45) at a CFG scale of 2.55, which is slightly lower than typical settings for standard models.
- **Training and Inference Efficiency:** The additional computation for EG-FM's target construction was found to be negligible, contributing typically less than 1% to per-step wall-clock time for most configurations. At inference, EG-FM evaluates the learned velocity field directly without additional filtering, FFT, or bisection, resulting in no change to inference FLOPs or wall time compared to standard FM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Energy-Guided Flow Matching" (EG-FM) framework presents a notable contribution to the field of pixel-space generative modeling. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Novel Generative Trajectory Design:** EG-FM introduces a new paradigm for designing generative trajectories in flow-matching models. By replacing the static clean-image endpoint with a dynamic, heat-kernel-filtered moving endpoint, it provides an explicit mechanism for guiding the generative process through a coarse-to-fine spectral evolution. This contrasts with previous methods that primarily relied on implicit learning of this progression.

2. **Improved Generation Quality and Efficiency:** The research consistently demonstrates that EG-FM leads to lower FID scores (indicating higher fidelity and diversity) across various pixel-space backbones (DeCo, HyperDiT, PixelDiT), different resolutions (256x256, 512x512), and diverse generation tasks (class-conditional and text-to-image). Furthermore, it achieves these improved metrics with significantly fewer training epochs, leading to greater training efficiency. This suggests that a well-designed generative path can accelerate model convergence and enhance the quality of generated outputs.

3. **Sample-Adaptive Frequency Release:** A core innovation is the energy-guided heat-time scheduling, which enables sample-adaptive frequency release. This mechanism adjusts the rate at which high-frequency components are introduced based on each image's unique spectral energy, addressing a limitation of prior methods that used universal temporal schedules. This adaptability helps in achieving more consistent and effective generation progress across diverse visual content.

4. **Compatibility and Low Overhead:** EG-FM is designed to be compatible with existing flow-matching frameworks without requiring architectural changes to the backbone models or modifications to the training data pipeline. The computational overhead introduced by calculating the dynamic endpoint and velocity target during training is negligible, and there is no additional computational cost during inference. This makes EG-FM a practical and easily adoptable enhancement.

5. **Transferability and Scalability:** The consistent performance gains observed across different backbone architectures, resolutions, and tasks (from ImageNet class-conditional to text-to-image generation) underscore EG-FM's transferability and scalability. This indicates that the proposed dynamic path prior is a generalizable concept, not an optimization heuristic specific to a particular model or setting. It suggests that EG-FM can serve as a robust and general inductive bias for pixel-space generative models.

6. **Potential for Broader Applications:** While the current work focuses on image generation, the concept of endpoint-guided flow matching and explicit coarse-to-fine frequency release could have broader implications. The authors suggest future work involving heterogeneous or temporally extended signals, such as joint text-image modeling, video generation, and embodied decision-making. Developing a unified adaptive path construction across these modalities could be a significant step towards more versatile and efficient generative and control models.

In conclusion, EG-FM offers a principled and effective method for structuring the generative process in pixel-space flow matching. By explicitly guiding the frequency evolution, it addresses key challenges in high-dimensional image synthesis, leading to more efficient training and superior generation quality.
