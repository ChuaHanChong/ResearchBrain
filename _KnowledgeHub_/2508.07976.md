# Beyond Ten Turns: Unlocking Long-Horizon Agentic Search with Large-Scale Asynchronous RL

> [!summary] Summary
> The ASearcher project, from Tsinghua University and Ant Group, introduces an open-source, large-scale asynchronous reinforcement learning framework that enables LLM agents to perform long-horizon web search and exhibit advanced search intelligence. This system sets new state-of-the-art performance for open-source models on challenging benchmarks like GAIA and xBench-DeepSearch, facilitating learning of multi-step strategies spanning over 100 turns.

## Problem

- Existing open-source online RL methods for LLM search agents often enforce strict turn limits (e.g., ≤ 10 turns), preventing the learning of complex, multi-step search strategies essential for real-world problems.
- There is a lack of high-quality, scalable, and challenging training data (question-answer pairs) required for effective RL training to stimulate sophisticated search behaviors in LLM agents.
- Traditional batch-generation RL training systems suffer significant GPU idle time from the highly variable runtimes of long search trajectories.

## Method

- ASearcher implements a ==fully asynchronous RL training paradigm== that decouples trajectory rollouts from model updates, enabling efficient, large-scale training for long-horizon search trajectories without idle time.
- A novel ==LLM-based data synthesis agent== autonomously generates high-quality, challenging, and grounded QA pairs by iteratively injecting new facts and fuzzing details, then applying multi-stage quality verification.
- The agent design utilizes basic search and web browsing tools, with its decision-making and webpage summarization capabilities optimized end-to-end via ==Grouped Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.

## Results

- ASearcher-Web-QwQ (32B) achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source agents on challenging benchmarks, scoring **58.7** Avg@4 on GAIA and **51.1** on xBench-DeepSearch.
- RL training yielded substantial performance gains, with ASearcher-Web-QwQ improving by **+15.0** Avg@4 on GAIA, **+22.4** on xBench-DeepSearch, and **+14.6** on Frames compared to its pre-RL version.
- The framework demonstrates strong generalization across local and web-based settings, with test-time scaling enabling performance competitive with commercial systems like Kimi-Researcher and OpenAI DeepResearch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The asynchronous training paradigm allows agents to learn extreme long-horizon searches, with single trajectories exceeding 100 tool calls and generating over 400k tokens, addressing a critical limitation in prior work.
> - RL training successfully fosters emergent 'Search Intelligence' behaviors in agents, including uncertainty-aware reasoning, precise key information extraction, cross-document inference, and grounded verification.
> - Autonomous data generation through iterative 'injection' and 'fuzzing' effectively creates a diverse and challenging dataset that drives the learning of robust, multi-turn tool-using strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents capable of complex, knowledge-intensive tasks is a rapidly evolving area of AI research. A central theme in this landscape is equipping LLMs with external tools, among which search tools are paramount for accessing vast, up-to-date knowledge.

Initially, research focused on simple tool integration, allowing LLMs to perform basic searches or use calculators. This evolved into more sophisticated "prompt-based LLM agents" that can orchestrate multiple tool calls without explicit training, relying on the LLM's inherent instruction-following capabilities. Examples include early systems like ReAct and later iterations like Search-o1. While effective for basic tasks, these agents often struggle with "Search Intelligence"—the nuanced ability to resolve ambiguous queries, generate precise searches, analyze conflicting results, and conduct thorough, multi-step exploration. Their limitations often stem from the LLM's inherent capabilities, such as accurately extracting information from noisy web pages or verifying conclusions.

To overcome these limitations, researchers have turned to Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) approaches to explicitly incentivize tool-using behaviors. SFT methods involve collecting expert trajectories (sometimes synthesized by larger LLMs) to fine-tune smaller models. RL, on the other hand, allows agents to learn through trial and error, refining their strategies based on environmental feedback. Proprietary agents (e.g., from Moonshot AI, OpenAI) have already demonstrated advanced search behaviors through large-scale RL training, hinting at the potential of this paradigm.

However, the open-source community faces significant hurdles in replicating and advancing these capabilities. Existing open-source online RL methods for search agents have two primary shortcomings:
1. **Limited Search Horizon:** They often impose small turn limits (e.g., ≤ 10 turns per trajectory), which prevents agents from developing complex, multi-step search strategies necessary for challenging real-world problems. Complex queries often require a long sequence of search queries, browsing, and reasoning steps.
2. **Lack of High-Quality, Scalable Training Data:** Effective RL training requires abundant, challenging, and accurately labeled question-answer (QA) pairs. Many existing open-source datasets are either outdated, oversimplified, or too small to stimulate the sophisticated search behaviors observed in proprietary systems.

This paper directly addresses these critical gaps in the open-source landscape. By introducing ASearcher, it aims to democratize access to advanced search agent capabilities through a scalable, efficient, and open-source online RL training pipeline. It builds upon previous RL work for reasoning (e.g., DeepCoder, DeepScaler) by applying similar principles to the more complex domain of web search and multi-tool use. It also contributes to the growing area of synthetic data generation for agents, distinguishing itself by focusing on generating challenging, uncertain, and grounded QAs without relying on complex knowledge graphs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **unlock "Search Intelligence" in open-source LLM-based agents** by overcoming the current limitations in scalability, efficiency, and data quality within existing online Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches. The authors define Search Intelligence as the advanced capability required to resolve ambiguous queries, generate precise searches, analyze and extract key insights from massive data, resolve inconsistencies, and conduct in-depth exploration.

The core motivations for this work stem from two critical obstacles identified in current open-source RL training for search agents:

1. **Enabling Long-Horizon Search:** Existing online RL methods typically enforce strict turn limits (e.g., ≤ 10 search turns per trajectory). This artificially constrains the agent's ability to learn and execute complex, multi-step search strategies, which are essential for resolving challenging real-world queries. The paper aims to develop a system that can train agents to perform significantly longer search trajectories, exceeding 100 turns and generating hundreds of thousands of tokens, without sacrificing training efficiency.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Quality:** RL training for advanced reasoning and tool use demands a vast supply of high-quality, challenging, and diverse question-answer (QA) pairs. Current open-source datasets are often insufficient in terms of scale, relevance, or complexity, failing to adequately stimulate the learning of sophisticated search behaviors. The goal is to develop an autonomous and scalable method for synthesizing such high-quality, challenging QA data.

By addressing these two intertwined challenges, the authors aim to build an open-source, large-scale agentic RL pipeline that allows LLMs to learn expert-level search strategies, thereby closing the performance gap between open-source models and proprietary commercial systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ASearcher introduces a comprehensive open-source project designed for large-scale RL training of search agents, featuring innovations in both training infrastructure and data generation.

#### 3.1. Agent Design

ASearcher employs a straightforward agent design centered around two fundamental tools:
- **Search Engine:** Takes a text query and returns relevant snippets with URLs.
- **Web Browser:** Accepts a URL and retrieves the webpage content.
The agent's decision-making process, including thinking, tool calling, and summarization, is optimized end-to-end via RL.

A crucial component is **Webpage Summarization**, where the agent condenses potentially lengthy web content into compact summaries. This ability is also improved during RL training.

ASearcher is instantiated in two ways:
- **Base LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B/14B):** Uses an append-only prompting style, where the full chronological history (system prompt, LLM responses, search results, summaries) is fed as input.
- **Advanced Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) (e.g., QwQ-32B):** Leverages the LRM's instruction-following capabilities with different prompts for tool selection, summarization, and answering. To manage the typically longer responses of LRMs and maintain input token budget, only summarized thoughts and tool calls are retained in history, and only the most recent 25k characters are provided as context.

#### 3.2. Training Data and Data Synthesis Agent

The training data comprises two main sources:
1. **Filtered Open-Source Data:** Challenging samples are selected from multi-hop QA datasets (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA) and WebWalkerQA. A model-based filtering process ensures retention of questions that are difficult, solvable, and require multi-turn tool use. Questions where the model fails to find a correct answer, achieves high accuracy easily, or requires only a few turns are discarded.
2. **Data Synthesis Agent:** A novel LLM-based agent autonomously generates high-quality, challenging, uncertain, and grounded QA pairs requiring multi-turn tool use. This agent operates iteratively:
 - **Seed QA:** Starts with a simple question-answer pair and tracks supporting facts.
 - **Actions:**
 - **Injection:** Selects an entity, retrieves a related fact (e.g., from Wikipedia), and injects it into the question, increasing complexity.
 - **Fuzzing:** Blurs specific details in the question (e.g., "1934" to "early 1930s"), increasing uncertainty and difficulty.
 - **Multi-stage Quality Verification:**
 - **Basic Quality:** LLM assesses clarity, accuracy, and grounding.
 - **Difficulty Measurement:** An LRM (QwQ-32B) attempts to answer the synthetic question without tools; low accuracy indicates high difficulty.
 - **Answer Uniqueness:** Checks for multiple valid answers resulting from excessive fuzzing.
This process transforms 14k seed QAs into 134k samples, of which 25.6k high-quality, tool-requiring samples are used for training.

#### 3.3. Asynchronous Agentic RL Training

The paper highlights two critical challenges in scaling RL for long-horizon search:
1. **Complex Tasks Require Long Trajectories:** Empirical evidence shows that accuracy improves with more turns, confirming the need for longer search horizons.
2. **High Variance in Trajectory Execution Time:** Long trajectories lead to highly unpredictable and variable runtimes (e.g., dozens more tool calls, two orders of magnitude more tokens), causing significant idle time in traditional batch-generation RL systems.

ASearcher addresses these with a **fully asynchronous training paradigm**:
- **Asynchronous Trajectory Rollouts:** Trajectories are collected in parallel and independently. Each trajectory makes tool-calling and LLM generation requests concurrently, improving efficiency by not waiting for others.
- **Decoupled Rollout and Training:** Trajectory generation and model updates are fully separated. Long trajectories do not block the training process; a training step is initiated as soon as enough trajectories are collected to form a batch, even if some very long trajectories are still ongoing. This minimizes GPU idle time and achieves near-full GPU utilization.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- **MDP Formulation:** Defines the search task as a Markov Decision Process, with states representing history, search results, and webpages; actions being LLM-generated tokens (potentially containing tool calls); and transitions based on tool application. The goal is to maximize cumulative rewards.
- **GRPO Training:** Employs the Grouped Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, a variant of PPO, to optimize the agent's policy. It calculates advantages based on the relative rewards of trajectories within each group.
- **Dynamic Filtering:** During training, queries yielding identical rewards (zero advantages) are dynamically removed to enhance efficiency by focusing on tasks with meaningful learning signals.
- **Reward Function:** A sparse reward is given at trajectory completion. For base LLMs, it's a product of format reward and F1 score. For LRM-based agents, LLM-as-Judge (using Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) is employed for reward computation.
- **Two-Stage Curriculum:** For ASearcher-Web-QwQ, an initial stage uses varying difficulty data, followed by a second stage focusing on questions requiring at least 5 tool calls to activate long-horizon capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates ASearcher's effectiveness across various model scales and evaluation settings, showcasing significant advancements in search intelligence for open-source agents.

#### 4.1. Performance on Standard QA Benchmarks

- **Local Knowledge Base (RAG):** ASearcher-Local (7B/14B) consistently outperforms baselines like Search-R1 and R1-Searcher on multi-hop (2WikiMQA, HotpotQA, Bamboogle, Musique) and single-hop (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA) QA tasks, measured by both F1 and LLM-as-Judge (LasJ) scores. Notably, ASearcher-Local-14B even surpasses the larger 32B Search-R1 baseline.
- **Web-based Search and Browsing:** When evaluated in a realistic web-based setting, ASearcher models, including those trained *only* with local knowledge (zero-shot transfer), show strong generalization. ASearcher-Web-14B achieves an average F1 of 61.5, outperforming SimpleDeepSearcher (a 32B baseline). This indicates that the learned search strategies generalize across different information sources.

#### 4.2. Performance on Challenging Benchmarks

On more demanding benchmarks like GAIA, xBench-DeepSearch, and Frames, which require real-world web interaction and up-to-date information, ASearcher-Web-QwQ (32B) achieves state-of-the-art results among open-source agents:
- **Avg@4 Scores:** ASearcher-Web-QwQ-v2 achieves 58.7 on GAIA and 51.1 on xBench-DeepSearch, surpassing all comparable open-source baselines.
- **Pass@4 Scores:** It also leads in pass rate, with 74.7 on GAIA and 75.0 on xBench-DeepSearch, demonstrating higher reliability in finding correct answers.

#### 4.3. Effect of RL Training

RL training significantly improves agent performance. ASearcher-Web-QwQ gains +15.0 on GAIA, +22.4 on xBench-DeepSearch, and +14.6 on Frames in Avg@4 scores compared to its pre-RL prompt-based version (Search-o1 (QwQ)). Pass@4 improvements are even more pronounced, especially on xBench-DeepSearch (+24.0), highlighting the agent's ability to learn precise and robust search strategies.

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Transfer and Test-Time Scaling

- **External Summary Tool:** Replacing the agent's learned summarization with a more powerful external model (DeepSeek-V3) further boosts performance, demonstrating the modularity and generalizability of the ASearcher design.
- **Test-time Search (K=16):** Running multiple independent search trials (K=16) and aggregating results with DeepSeek-V3 achieves highly competitive performance with commercial systems like Kimi-Researcher, OpenAI DeepResearch, and Claude-4-Sonnet, demonstrating the potential for performance scaling.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics and Emergent Behaviors

- **Long-Horizon Search:** During RL training, ASearcher-Web-QwQ learns to perform extreme long-horizon searches, with tool calls exceeding 100 turns and generating over 400k tokens in individual trajectories, directly addressing the key limitation of previous methods.
- **Tool Utilization:** Both 7B and 14B local models show increased search queries. The 14B model successfully learns webpage browsing, while the 7B model struggles, suggesting that a certain model capacity is needed for complex skills like summarization.
- **Emergent Expert Strategies:** Qualitative analysis (case studies and keyword analysis) reveals that ASearcher-Web-QwQ learns advanced search intelligence behaviors:
 - **Uncertainty-aware reasoning:** Exhaustively listing and examining possibilities.
 - **Precise Key Information Extraction:** Identifying crucial details from noisy web content.
 - **Cross-document Inference:** Connecting information across multiple sources.
 - **Grounded Verification:** Rigorously confirming conclusions with additional tool calls.
- **Reflection Behaviors:** Keyword analysis shows an increase in reflective terms ("alternatively," "however," "wait") and external referencing terms ("doc," "mention") during training, indicating the agent's learning to reflect on its actions and integrate external information more effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ASearcher" project represents a significant contribution to the field of LLM-based agents and reinforcement learning, with several notable implications:

1. **Advancing Open-Source Agentic AI:** By open-sourcing its models, training data, and code, ASearcher democratizes access to state-of-the-art methods for training search agents. This enables researchers and developers in the open-source community to build upon this work, fostering innovation and accelerating progress in agentic AI. It directly challenges the performance lead of proprietary commercial systems, demonstrating that comparable "Search Intelligence" can be achieved with open-source tools and models.

2. **Unlocking Long-Horizon Agentic Capabilities:** The introduction of a scalable, fully asynchronous RL training system is a major technical breakthrough. It effectively addresses the critical limitation of small turn limits in previous online RL methods, allowing agents to learn and execute complex, multi-step strategies spanning over 100 tool calls and generating hundreds of thousands of tokens. This capability is crucial for tackling real-world, complex problems that cannot be solved with short, simple interactions.

3. **Innovating Data Synthesis for RL:** The novel LLM-based data synthesis agent is highly impactful. It provides a scalable and autonomous method for generating high-quality, challenging, and grounded QA pairs. This directly addresses the perennial problem of data scarcity and quality in RL, particularly for complex tasks requiring multi-turn tool use. By generating diverse and difficult questions through "injection" and "fuzzing," the agent stimulates the learning of robust and generalizable search strategies.

4. **Demonstrating Emergent Search Intelligence:** The paper not only achieves quantitative performance improvements but also provides compelling qualitative evidence of emergent "Search Intelligence" behaviors. The agent learns sophisticated strategies like uncertainty-aware reasoning, precise information extraction, cross-document inference, and grounded verification. These capabilities move beyond mere tool-calling, indicating a deeper understanding and strategic planning, which are hallmarks of true agentic behavior.

5. **Setting New Benchmarks for Open-Source Models:** ASearcher-Web-QwQ sets new state-of-the-art benchmarks for open-source 32B agents on challenging datasets like GAIA and xBench-DeepSearch. Its ability to achieve performance competitive with commercial systems through zero-shot transfer and test-time scaling further validates the efficacy of the approach and provides a clear path for future development.

The potential impact of ASearcher extends beyond search agents. The principles of large-scale asynchronous RL training and autonomous data synthesis could be generalized to other complex, real-world LLM agent applications requiring long-horizon planning, multi-tool orchestration, and continuous learning from environments. This work lays a strong foundation for the development of more intelligent, autonomous, and capable open-source AI agents.
