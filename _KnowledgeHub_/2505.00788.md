# SpatialLLM: A Compound 3D-Informed Design towards Spatially-Intelligent Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Johns Hopkins University developed SpatialLLM, a compound 3D-informed design that systematically enhances Large Multimodal Models' 3D spatial reasoning capabilities. SpatialLLM achieved 62.7% accuracy on the new SpatialVQA benchmark, outperforming GPT-4o by 8.7% and the best open-source spatial-centric models by 10.5%.

## Problem

- Existing Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) exhibit limited 3D spatial reasoning capabilities, struggling with understanding real-world 3D relationships like object orientation and precise distances.
- This limitation stems from a scarcity of high-quality 3D training data, particularly for 3D object orientation, and architectural biases in current LMM designs optimized for 2D visual information.
- Prior work on 3D spatial understanding in LMMs predominantly focused on 3D distance relationships, neglecting the critical aspect of 3D object orientation.

## Method

- A ==compound 3D-informed design== curates two novel datasets, ==3D-informed probing data== (human-annotated 3D orientation from ImageNet3D) and ==3D-informed conversation data== for complex spatial relationships, integrated across data, architecture, and training.
- 3D-informed data is strategically injected into both multimodal alignment and visual instruction tuning stages, alongside an upgraded visual encoder (==CLIP+DINOv2==) and LLM backbone (Llama3-8B).
- A new benchmark, **SpatialVQA**, is introduced for comprehensive evaluation of 3D distance, orientation, and complex spatial reasoning.

## Results

- **SpatialLLM** achieved **62.7%** accuracy on the SpatialVQA benchmark, outperforming GPT-4o (**54.0%**) by **8.7%** and the best open-source spatial-centric models like SpatialVLM-13B (**52.2%**) by **10.5%**.
- The model demonstrated superior performance across all SpatialVQA sub-categories, including 3D Distance (**86.3%**), 3D Orientation (**52.9%**), and 3D Spatial Relationship (**50.8%**).
- Ablation studies revealed 3D-informed data contributed a total of **+13.7%** performance gain, with 3D-informed instruction tuning providing **+10.7%** and 3D-informed multimodal alignment with human-annotated data adding a further **+3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A compound design systematically integrating 3D-informed data, architectural enhancements, and refined training strategies is crucial for building truly spatially-intelligent LMMs.
> - High-quality, human-annotated 3D orientation data is particularly vital for early multimodal alignment, and dedicated 3D-informed conversation data for instruction tuning yields the most substantial performance gains.
> - While an improved general-purpose LLM backbone contributes to better spatial reasoning, isolated pre-pretraining of the visual encoder with 3D-informed data can surprisingly degrade overall performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has seen rapid advancements, with models like GPT-4, Gemini, LLaVA, and Qwen-VL demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and generating content across various modalities, particularly text and images. These models excel at tasks such as object identification, descriptive captioning, and engaging in multi-turn conversations about visual scenes.

However, despite these successes, a significant limitation of current LMMs is their underdeveloped ability for **3D spatial reasoning**. While they can describe objects and their 2D arrangements, they struggle with tasks that require a deep comprehension of real-world 3D spatial relationships, such as predicting physical interactions, understanding object orientations, or inferring distances in a 3D context. For instance, determining if a car is approaching an obstacle or if two objects are facing the same direction requires more than just 2D image analysis; it demands an understanding of their true 3D poses and relationships.

This limitation stems from two primary challenges:
1. **Scarcity of high-quality 3D training data:** Most large-scale multimodal datasets are inherently 2D-centric, lacking explicit annotations or ground truth for 3D object properties and relationships.
2. **Bias in current model designs:** Existing LMM architectures and training paradigms are largely optimized for 2D visual data, making it difficult for them to inherently learn and represent 3D spatial information.

Previous research efforts have attempted to address this gap. Some works have leveraged readily available vision foundation models (e.g., SAM for segmentation, DepthAnything for depth estimation) to semi-automatically generate "pseudo-2.5D" datasets. Models like SpatialVLM and SpatialRGPT have used such data to improve depth ordering or 2D spatial relationships. However, a critical shortcoming of these approaches has been their predominant focus on **3D distance relationships**, largely overlooking the crucial aspect of **3D object orientation**. Understanding orientation is vital for many complex spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., whether two cars are facing the same way to avoid a collision). The absence of robust 6D pose estimators or relevant datasets has made this a challenging area.

This paper explicitly positions itself to bridge these gaps. It is the first to systematically investigate the compound impact of 3D-informed data, architecture, and training setups. Crucially, it highlights its pioneering effort in curating **VQA data that incorporates 3D orientation relationships on real images**, a significant advancement over prior work that focused primarily on distance. Furthermore, while previous works might have injected 3D-informed data only at specific training stages (e.g., instruction tuning), this paper advocates for and systematically explores a more holistic approach, integrating 3D awareness across multiple stages of LMM development. By doing so, "SpatialLLM" aims to provide a comprehensive "roadmap" for building truly spatially-intelligent LMMs, pushing the boundaries beyond current capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **systematically enhance the 3D spatial reasoning capabilities of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs)**. The authors aim to move LMMs beyond superficial 2D scene understanding to a more profound, human-like comprehension of the 3D physical world and the complex relationships between objects within it.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Bridging the Human-AI Gap in Spatial Understanding:** Humans intuitively perceive and reason about 3D spatial relationships (e.g., predicting vehicle collisions based on their directions). Current LMMs, despite their advancements, significantly lack this innate capability, limiting their ability to interact meaningfully and safely with the physical world.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** A fundamental challenge for LMMs in 3D reasoning is the severe lack of high-quality 3D spatial relationship data for training. The authors are motivated to develop scalable methods for creating such data.
- **Overcoming Design Biases:** Existing LMM architectures and training processes are primarily biased towards 2D image data. The motivation is to systematically investigate how to modify or augment these designs to effectively incorporate and leverage 3D information.
- **Incorporating 3D Orientation:** Previous works in spatial reasoning for LMMs have largely focused on 3D distance relationships, neglecting the critical role of 3D object orientation. The authors are motivated to explicitly integrate 3D orientation into training data and model design to enable more sophisticated reasoning.
- **Holistic Design Optimization:** Current approaches often apply 3D-informed data only at specific stages of LMM training (e.g., instruction tuning). The authors are motivated to conduct a systematic study across data curation, architectural choices, and different training stages to identify the optimal "recipe" for achieving superior 3D reasoning.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper include:
1. **Developing comprehensive 3D-informed training datasets:** This involves creating two types of data: (a) "3D-informed probing data" focused on fundamental object-level 3D properties (location, orientation) and (b) "3D-informed conversation data" for more complex spatial relationships. A particular focus is on being the first to curate VQA data with 3D orientation relationships on real images.
2. **Investigating the impact of architectural choices:** Exploring how different visual encoders contribute to 3D awareness.
3. **Optimizing training setups:** Determining at which stages (e.g., pre-pretraining, multimodal alignment, instruction tuning) 3D-informed data can be most effectively introduced.
4. **Introducing a novel evaluation benchmark:** Creating `SpatialVQA` to thoroughly assess LMMs' 3D spatial reasoning capabilities across distance, orientation, and complex relationships.
5. **Proposing a "roadmap" for optimal design:** Systematically combining data, architecture, and training insights to guide future LMM development for 3D reasoning.
6. **Developing `SpatialLLM`:** Implementing their proposed compound design to create a state-of-the-art LMM with superior 3D spatial reasoning abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of SpatialLLM is a **compound 3D-informed design**, which systematically investigates and integrates improvements across three dimensions: data, architecture, and training setups. The goal is to holistically enhance LMMs' 3D spatial reasoning.

#### 3.1. Data Curation: 3D-Informed Datasets

Recognizing the scarcity of high-quality 3D spatial data, the authors curate two novel types of 3D-informed training datasets, leveraging both human-annotated and semi-automated methods:

- **3D-Informed Probing Data:** This data focuses on fundamental, object-level 3D properties like depth (distance from viewer), 3D distances between objects, and crucially, 3D object orientation (azimuth and elevation viewpoint).
 - **3DI-Pb-IN166K:** Derived from the human-annotated ImageNet3D dataset, which provides accurate 3D pose annotations. This is a key differentiator, as human-annotated 3D orientation data is rare.
 - **3DI-Pb-OI1M:** Generated from the OpenImages dataset using semi-automatic tools, including depth estimators (e.g., DepthAnythingv2), camera calibration methods (e.g., WildCamera), and a pre-trained 3D pose estimator (trained on ImageNet3D) to estimate 3D orientations for rigid objects. This broadens the scale of data.
 - The purpose of this probing data is to inject fundamental 3D awareness into the model, particularly during the multimodal alignment stage, by enabling the visual tokens to encode rich 3D information.

- **3D-Informed Conversation Data (3DI-Ft1M):** This dataset contains 1 million question-answer pairs focused on high-level, complex 3D spatial relationships, built upon a combination of the fundamental 3D properties.
 - Generated by exploiting visual foundation models (RAM for tagging, SAM for segmentation, DepthAnythingv2 for depth, and the 6D pose estimator for orientation) to extract 3D information from OpenImages.
 - This structured 3D information (oriented 3D bounding boxes) is then fed into a Large Language Model (e.g., GPT-4) to generate diverse, multi-turn conversations and questions about intricate 3D spatial relationships (e.g., "Are these two objects facing the same direction?", "Which object is in front/left/back/right of the other?").
 - This dataset is designed for the instruction tuning stage, to teach LMMs to aggregate 3D-aware visual features and perform complex spatial reasoning.

#### 3.2. Architectural Design Choices

The paper investigates improvements to the standard LMM architecture, which typically consists of a visual encoder, a multimodal connector, and an LLM.

- **Vision Encoder:** The authors explore mixing different visual encoders. Instead of solely relying on CLIP (which is known to have limited 3D awareness), they experiment with integrating self-supervised visual encoders like DINOv2 and MAE, which are known for learning robust visual features. The final `SpatialLLM` utilizes a mixed visual encoder of CLIP and DINOv2.
- **Multimodal Connector:** A standard MLP connector is used to map visual features into the LLM's token space.
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** The backbone LLM is upgraded from Vicuna-v1.5-7B to Llama3-8B, reasoning that a more advanced general-purpose LLM can inherently improve the spatial reasoning by providing stronger general language reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.3. Training Setup and Strategy

The authors propose a refined training methodology, strategically injecting 3D-informed data across different stages of LMM development, in contrast to previous works that might only focus on instruction tuning:

- **Stage 0 (Pre-pretraining of Vision Encoder):** They explore fine-tuning the LoRA layers of the visual encoder (e.g., CLIP) using 3D-informed probing data (3DI-Pb-OI1M) with objectives like CLIP contrastive loss or next token prediction. (As detailed in results, this stage was found to be detrimental to performance for their final model.)
- **Stage 1 (Multimodal Alignment Pretraining):** The model is trained to align visual and language representations. This stage combines standard image-caption pairs (e.g., CC558K) with the novel 3D-informed probing data (3DI-Pb-OI1M or 3DI-Pb-IN166K). The goal is for the visual features to intrinsically learn 3D awareness from the start.
- **Stage 2 (Visual Instruction Tuning):** This final stage refines the model's ability to follow complex multimodal instructions. It combines standard instruction tuning data (e.g., Mix665K) with the curated 3D-informed conversation data (3DI-Ft1M). This step teaches the LMM to perform sophisticated 3D spatial reasoning and generate appropriate responses.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Benchmark: SpatialVQA

To rigorously evaluate 3D spatial reasoning, the authors introduce `SpatialVQA`, a new visual question answering benchmark.
- **Data Source:** Built upon images and 3D bounding box annotations from Omni3D (covering diverse urban and indoor scenes from datasets like nuScenes, KITTI, ARKitScenes, Hypersim, and SUN RGB-D).
- **Question Generation:** Rule-based methods are used to generate 1,323 visual question-answer pairs from the 3D ground truths.
- **Question Types:** SpatialVQA uniquely covers:
 - **Distance Questions:** Estimating and comparing 3D distances (e.g., "Which object is closer to the camera?").
 - **Orientation Questions:** Understanding 3D orientations (e.g., "Are these two objects facing the same direction?").
 - **Complex Spatial Reasoning Questions:** Combining 3D location and orientation (e.g., "On which side of object A is object B?").
- This comprehensive benchmark allows for a granular assessment of LMMs' capabilities in different aspects of 3D spatial understanding, a critical improvement over previous benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their compound 3D-informed design, leading to the superior performance of SpatialLLM.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on SpatialVQA

- SpatialLLM achieved an impressive **62.7% accuracy** on the SpatialVQA benchmark.
- This significantly outperforms top proprietary models: **8.7% higher than GPT-4o** (54.0%) and 13.4% higher than Claude 3.5 Sonnet (49.3%).
- It also surpasses the best open-source spatial-centric models: **10.5% higher than SpatialVLM-13B** (52.2%) and 12.1% higher than LLaVA-NeXT-8B (50.6%).
- SpatialLLM demonstrates superior performance across all sub-categories of SpatialVQA: 3D Distance (86.3%), 3D Orientation (52.9%), and 3D Spatial Relationship (50.8%). Notably, it excels in 3D Orientation, a challenging area often neglected by previous models.
- The authors also state that SpatialLLM preserves general visual reasoning capabilities, indicating that the specialization for 3D reasoning does not come at the cost of broader visual understanding.

#### 4.2. Thorough Empirical Exploration and Design Roadmap

The detailed ablation study reveals the individual contributions of architectural upgrades and 3D-informed data integration:

- **Architectural Improvements (Total +1.3%):**
 - **Mixed Vision Encoder:** Replacing the standard CLIP encoder with a combination of CLIP and DINOv2 yields a modest 0.3% performance gain. This suggests that self-supervised features (from DINOv2) can provide complementary 3D awareness.
 - **Advanced LLM (Llama3-8B):** Upgrading the language backbone from Vicuna-7B to Llama3-8B contributes a 1% gain. This indicates that improved general language reasoning capabilities from a more powerful LLM positively impact complex spatial reasoning.

- **3D-Informed Data Impact (Total +13.7%):** This is the most significant source of improvement.
 - **3D-informed Instruction Tuning (Stage 2):** Incorporating the `3DI-Ft1M` dataset (1 million 3D-informed conversation data) during instruction tuning provides a substantial **+10.7%** performance boost. This highlights the critical importance of training LMMs directly on complex 3D spatial reasoning tasks.
 - **3D-informed Multimodal Alignment (Stage 1):** Integrating 3D-informed probing data during the multimodal alignment stage (before instruction tuning) further enhances performance:
 - Using `3DI-Pb-OI1M` (semi-automated OpenImages data) yields a solid gain.
 - Using `3DI-Pb-IN166K` (human-annotated ImageNet3D data) provides an additional **+3%** improvement over `3DI-Pb-OI1M`. This finding strongly underscores the **critical importance of high-quality, human-annotated 3D orientation data** in the early alignment stages, as it provides more accurate and reliable 3D information.

- **3D-informed Pre-pretraining of Vision Encoder (Stage 0):** Surprisingly, pre-pretraining the vision encoder's LoRA layers with 3D-informed probing data (3DI-Pb-OI1M) *reduced* overall performance. This suggests that fine-tuning a pre-trained visual foundation model specifically for 3D awareness in isolation might compromise its generalizability for downstream multimodal tasks, supporting the practice of keeping visual encoders frozen or locked in many LMM designs.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparison

Qualitative examples illustrate that SpatialLLM can accurately reason about 3D distance and orientation, providing correct answers where GPT-4o struggles or requests more information, demonstrating its superior grounding in 3D space.

In summary, the results provide a clear roadmap: enhance the architecture (especially the LLM), but critically, prioritize the strategic injection of high-quality 3D-informed data, particularly human-annotated orientation data, at both multimodal alignment and instruction tuning stages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "SpatialLLM" paper makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of artificial intelligence:

- **Setting a New Benchmark for 3D Spatial Reasoning:** By introducing the comprehensive SpatialVQA benchmark and achieving state-of-the-art performance with SpatialLLM, the authors establish a new standard for evaluating and advancing 3D spatial reasoning in LMMs. This benchmark addresses the critical need for robust evaluation metrics that go beyond 2D understanding.
- **Providing a Comprehensive Design Roadmap:** The systematic empirical exploration of data types, architectural choices, and training stages culminates in a clear "roadmap" for developing spatially-intelligent LMMs. This provides invaluable insights and guidance for future research, demonstrating that a holistic, compound design approach is more effective than isolated improvements.
- **Highlighting the Crucial Role of Data Quality and Type:** The findings strongly emphasize that not all 3D-informed data is equally effective. The significant performance gains from human-annotated 3D orientation data (ImageNet3D-derived) during multimodal alignment, and dedicated 3D conversation data during instruction tuning, underscore the necessity of high-quality, task-specific datasets. This will encourage the community to invest more in precise 3D data annotation.
- **Advancing LMMs Towards Human-like Perception:** The ability to understand and reason about 3D spatial relationships is fundamental to human cognition and interaction with the physical world. SpatialLLM's advancements bring LMMs closer to this intuitive understanding, making them more "physically intelligent" and capable of grounding objects in real-world 3D space.
- **Enabling Robust Real-World Applications:** Improved 3D spatial reasoning is a critical prerequisite for numerous high-impact applications:
 - **Autonomous Driving and Robotics:** More accurate prediction of potential collisions, better navigation, and safer interaction with dynamic environments.
 - **Augmented and Virtual Reality:** More realistic and interactive virtual objects that respect physical laws and spatial relationships.
 - **Complex Event Understanding:** Better comprehension of actions, interactions, and physical events in videos and real-time scenes.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots that can understand complex spatial commands and execute tasks with a finer grasp of spatial context.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** While the paper mentions "project page is available here" rather than direct code/data links in the abstract, the spirit of open research and the comparison to open-source models suggest that the insights and potentially the models/data will be made accessible. This fosters collaboration and accelerates progress in the research community.

In conclusion, SpatialLLM represents a significant step forward in making Large Multimodal Models truly spatially intelligent. By systematically addressing data limitations and optimizing model design and training, this work not only pushes the boundaries of current LMM capabilities but also provides a foundational roadmap for the next generation of AI systems that can robustly understand and interact with the complex 3D world.
