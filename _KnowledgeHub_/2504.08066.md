# The AI Scientist-v2: Workshop-Level Automated Scientific Discovery via Agentic Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> The AI Scientist-v2 is an end-to-end agentic system that formulates hypotheses, writes and runs experiment code, plots results, and drafts manuscripts without human-authored code templates. An experiment progress manager stages a parallelized agentic tree search over code nodes, and a vision-language model critiques figures and captions. One of three autonomously generated submissions passed peer review at an ICLR workshop.

## Problem

- Its predecessor needed a hand-written code template per topic area, so each new research domain demanded fresh human engineering before any automation could begin.
- That system's experimentation was strictly linear: every refinement built on the immediately preceding run, blocking backtracking, parallel exploration, or systematic hypothesis search.
- Earlier automated-discovery systems did no vision-language checking of figures and were never submitted to real peer review, leaving output quality unmeasured against human standards.

## Method

- An ==experiment progress manager== agent splits work into four staged phases with explicit stopping criteria: preliminary investigation, hyperparameter tuning, research agenda execution, ablation studies, seeding each stage with the best prior node.
- ==Parallelized agentic tree search== expands nodes concurrently, mixing ==debug-node selection== with ==LLM-guided best-first refinement==, plus specialized hyperparameter, ablation, replication, and aggregation nodes that produce seed statistics.
- A ==VLM feedback loop== marks nodes buggy on bad plots during experimentation and, at manuscript reflection, checks figure-caption alignment, legends, and duplication; Hugging Face dataset loading replaces templates.

## Results

- One of three fully autonomous manuscripts averaged **6.33 out of 10** (individual scores 6, 7, 6) among **43** workshop submissions, **top 45%**, clearing the acceptance threshold.
- The other two submissions fell below threshold, and the authors' internal review judged none of the three at main-conference standard, calling the accepted one borderline.
- Code inspection of the accepted paper found roughly **57%** train-test overlap and a questionable **100%** attention-LSTM accuracy that collapsed once task complexity increased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Staged tree search over executable code nodes turns agent experimentation from a linear chain into a searchable space, making debugging and ablation first-class steps.
> - Passing workshop review does not certify rigor: workshop acceptance runs 60-80% versus 20-30% at main tracks, so the bar cleared here is low.
> - Autonomous pipelines still leak silent validity bugs like train-test contamination and miscaptioned figures, so automated science needs data-integrity audits, not just reviewer scores.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Automated Scientific Discovery:** The paper belongs to the emerging field of automated scientific discovery, where AI is used to automate various stages of the scientific process.
- **Addressing Limitations of Previous Work:** The authors explicitly position their work as an improvement upon "The AI Scientist-v1" (Lu et al., 2024), addressing its limitations in terms of reliance on human-authored code templates and shallow experimentation.
- **Inspired by LLM Agent Research:** The methodology leverages recent advancements in language model agents and tree search strategies, drawing inspiration from frameworks like Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2024) and AIDE (Jiang et al., 2025).
- **Relation to Other AI Scientist Frameworks:** This paper acknowledges the existence of AI-researcher from HKU, Intology, and Carl from AutoScienceAI. It attempts to distinguish its approach from other frameworks which incorporate human oversights.
- **Connection to Benchmarking Initiatives:** It alludes to benchmarks like MLEBench (Chan et al., 2025) and METR Research Engineer benchmark.
- **Ethical Considerations and Transparency:** The authors demonstrate awareness of the ethical implications of AI-driven research and emphasize the importance of transparency. This aligns with broader discussions on AI safety and responsible AI development.
- **Positioning in a Rapidly Evolving Field:** The paper highlights the fast-paced nature of the field, referencing several concurrent works (Agent Laboratory, agentRxiv) that explore similar themes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Primary Objective:** To develop and evaluate an end-to-end agentic AI system ("The AI Scientist-v2") capable of autonomously conducting scientific research, including hypothesis formulation, experiment design, data analysis, manuscript writing, and peer review.
- **Addressing Limitations of "The AI Scientist-v1":** The key motivations include:
 - Eliminating reliance on human-authored code templates to improve autonomy and generalizability.
 - Enabling deeper and more systematic exploration of hypotheses through agentic tree search.
 - Improving the quality and clarity of generated figures and manuscripts through VLM feedback.
- **Demonstrating Real-World Applicability:** The authors aim to showcase the potential of AI in scientific research by submitting manuscripts generated by their system to a peer-reviewed workshop.
- **Advancing Scientific Knowledge:** A broader motivation is to accelerate scientific breakthroughs by enabling unprecedented scalability in research productivity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **System Architecture:** "The AI Scientist-v2" consists of several key components:
 - **Idea Generation:** An agent that generates research ideas, conducts literature reviews, and formulates hypotheses. This stage now integrates literature review tools such as Semantic Scholar to improve the quality and novelty of the generated ideas.
 - **Experiment Manager:** An agent that coordinates the scientific experimentation through distinct stages: preliminary investigation, hyperparameter tuning, research agenda execution, and ablation studies.
 - **Agentic Tree Search:** A novel search algorithm that enables deeper and more systematic exploration of hypotheses through parallelized code generation, execution, and debugging. Buggy nodes are debugged while non-buggy nodes are refined to enhance model performance. Hyperparameter nodes, ablation nodes, replication nodes, and aggregation nodes are introduced to tailored experiments and scientific rigors.
 - **Vision-Language Model (VLM) Reviewer:** VLMs provide feedback on generated figures and manuscripts to improve their clarity and accuracy.
 - **Manuscript Writing:** A single-pass generation approach followed by a reflection stage using reasoning models.

- **Evaluation:**
 - Three manuscripts generated by "The AI Scientist-v2" were submitted to a peer-reviewed workshop at ICLR.
 - The primary evaluation metric is the acceptance rate of these manuscripts and the reviewer scores received.
 - Internal evaluations were also conducted to assess the quality and rigor of the generated manuscripts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Successful Peer Review:** One of the three manuscripts submitted to the ICLR workshop was accepted, receiving an average reviewer score of 6.33 (out of 10). This marks the first instance of a fully AI-generated paper successfully navigating a peer review process.
- **Investigated Topic:** The accepted paper investigated whether incorporating an explicit compositional regularization term into neural network training can improve compositional generalization.
- **Challenges Identified:** The study also revealed shortcomings in the accepted paper, including insufficient justification for the regularization method, unclear method description, potential dataset overlap issues, and inaccuracies in figure captions.
- **Limitations Acknowledged:** The authors transparently acknowledge the limitations of "The AI Scientist-v2," including its inability to consistently generate top-tier conference-level publications and the challenges in formulating truly novel hypotheses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Proof of Concept:** The successful peer review of an AI-generated manuscript demonstrates the potential of AI in automating scientific research and contributing to the scientific record.
- **Acceleration of Scientific Discovery:** The authors suggest that further advancements in autonomous scientific discovery technologies could lead to unprecedented scalability in research productivity and accelerate scientific breakthroughs.
- **Ethical Considerations and Open Science:** The authors emphasize the importance of addressing the ethical implications of AI-driven research and advocate for transparency and open collaboration.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** By open-sourcing the code for "The AI Scientist-v2," the authors aim to foster further exploration by the research community and advance the discussion regarding AI's evolving role in science.

In summary, "The AI Scientist-v2" represents a significant step forward in the field of automated scientific discovery. By addressing the limitations of previous work and leveraging recent advancements in AI, the authors have created a system capable of autonomously generating manuscripts that can successfully navigate the peer review process. While challenges remain in achieving consistently high-quality results, this work demonstrates the potential of AI to transform scientific research and accelerate the pace of discovery. This research is an important contribution to the growing body of work on AI-driven scientific automation and raises important ethical considerations for the future of science.
