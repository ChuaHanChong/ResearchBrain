# Global-Local Attention Decomposition for Terrain Encoding in Humanoid Perceptive Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Humanoid Robot (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. developed Global-Local Attention Decomposition (GLAD), a terrain encoder for humanoid robots that explicitly separates global environmental context from local foothold-relevant geometry. This architecture enhances perceptive locomotion on complex terrains like sparse footholds and narrow paths, achieving higher success rates and faster training convergence while demonstrating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in achieving robust and reliable locomotion on complex, unstructured terrains with sparse footholds or constrained navigation paths.
- Conventional terrain encoders often neglect the robot's immediate kinematic state, leading to a dilution of fine-grained geometric cues necessary for precise foothold selection.
- Existing attention-based terrain encoding methods tend to entangle broad terrain awareness with precise local foothold encoding, increasing computational costs and reducing the interpretability of attention mechanisms.

## Method

- Introduced **Global-Local Attention Decomposition (GLAD)**, a ==terrain encoder== that explicitly separates ==global terrain context aggregation== from ==state-conditioned local foothold encoding==.
- Utilizes spatially aligned ==convolutional neural networks (CNNs)== with strided downsampling to extract initial local terrain features from 3D elevation maps.
- Employs a ==global attention branch== to summarize broad environmental context and a ==local attention branch== with ==state-conditioned sparsification== and Multi-Head Attention (MHA) for fine-grained, foothold-relevant feature extraction.

## Results

- GLAD achieved faster training convergence (**1.00 days**) compared to baselines (e.g., AME at **1.36 days**) and demonstrated superior success rates on unseen challenging terrains, achieving **95.90%** on a sinusoidal narrow path.
- Quantitative analysis showed GLAD's attention distribution was better aligned with footholds (smaller D_attn of **0.137m** on continuous terrains) and more concentrated (higher A_peak of **0.447** on discrete terrains) than other methods.
- The GLAD policy demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot across diverse real-world terrains, including gaps wider than training limits, using only onboard LiDAR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decomposing global and local attention mechanisms is effective for preventing the dilution of fine-grained spatial cues, leading to more precise and robust perceptive locomotion on complex terrains.
> - State-conditioned sparsification of local terrain features significantly reduces computational overhead for attention modules while maintaining or improving performance by focusing on relevant regions.
> - A well-structured terrain representation, without relying on handcrafted rewards or terrain-specific curricula, can enable complex emergent navigation behaviors in humanoid robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) has contributed to advancements in humanoid locomotion, enabling "blind" policies to achieve robust walking over various ground conditions. However, the deployment of humanoids on complex terrains, such as those with sparse footholds or constrained pathways, remains a challenge. Such environments necessitate policies that can simultaneously perceive the surrounding geometry, make terrain-aware decisions, and maintain robust control given the robot's inherent stability limitations.

Perceptive locomotion policies typically integrate exteroceptive observations, such as elevation maps or depth images, to address these challenges. These raw observations are often high-dimensional and noisy, which can increase computational cost, reduce robustness, and complicate policy training. Therefore, encoding these observations into compact terrain representations is a common practice before feeding them to the locomotion controller. Traditional terrain encoders frequently treat perception as an isolated geometric compression task, often disregarding the robot's immediate kinematic state. This approach can dilute fine-grained geometric cues, weaken the spatial correspondence between encoded features and reachable foothold regions, and ultimately limit foothold precision in RL-based locomotion.

For sparse-foothold scenarios, imprecise terrain representations often necessitate strategies such as terrain-specific curricula, expert policies, or handcrafted foothold rewards to achieve satisfactory performance. While effective in specific contexts, these methods introduce human priors and can impede generalization across diverse terrain layouts. Attention mechanisms have emerged as a method to fuse proprioception with exteroception, allowing the policy to emphasize locomotion-relevant terrain regions based on the current motion state. Previous attention-based methods, such as Attention-based Map Encoder (AME) and its successor AME-2, have used multi-head attention (MHA) to aggregate local terrain features conditioned on proprioception, aiming for implicit foothold selection and, in AME-2, incorporating additional global context. However, these methods typically entangle multiple perceptual objectives—namely, capturing broad surrounding terrain context and encoding precise foothold-relevant local geometry—within a single attention mechanism. This entanglement can increase computational cost and potentially weaken attention concentration and interpretability. The presented work aims to address this specific limitation by explicitly decomposing these two perceptual roles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance humanoid perceptive locomotion on challenging terrains, particularly those with sparse footholds and environmental constraints. The authors identify a key limitation in existing perceptive policies: the entanglement of two distinct perceptual requirements within conventional terrain encoders. These requirements are:
1. **Broad terrain awareness:** The ability to understand the general context of the surrounding environment.
2. **Precise foothold selection:** The ability to identify and encode fine-grained geometric details relevant for accurate foot placement.

The motivation for explicitly separating these two objectives stems from several observations and challenges:

- **Dilution of Fine-Grained Cues:** When a single encoding mechanism attempts to capture both broad context and precise local details, fine-grained spatial cues necessary for accurate foothold placement can be diluted. This dilution impedes the policy's ability to make precise local adjustments.
- **Weakened Spatial Correspondence:** Conventional, state-unaware encoders may not effectively maintain the spatial link between the encoded features and the robot's reachable foothold regions, leading to less effective foothold precision.
- **Increased Computational Overhead:** Applying dense attention mechanisms, such as MHA, to the entirety of high-dimensional local feature sets for both global and local objectives can be computationally expensive during training.
- **Reduced Interpretability:** When multiple perceptual roles are combined into a single attention mechanism, the resulting attention patterns can be less interpretable, making it difficult to understand which aspects of the terrain the policy is prioritizing.
- **Reliance on Human Priors:** To compensate for imprecise terrain representations, prior learning-based approaches often rely on human-engineered solutions like terrain-specific curricula, expert policies, or handcrafted foothold rewards. These methods can limit generalization across diverse terrain layouts.

To address these issues, the authors propose Global-Local Attention Decomposition (GLAD). The explicit separation of global context aggregation from fine-grained foothold selection is intended to:
- Prevent the dilution of critical local geometric information.
- Reduce the computational cost associated with dense attention over an entire feature set.
- Yield more interpretable and functionally distinct attention behaviors.
- Enable the policy to jointly reason about broad traversability cues and precise support-region geometry from robot-centric elevation maps, leading to more robust and generalized locomotion.
- Support emergent terrain-responsive behaviors, such as autonomous narrow-path following and obstacle avoidance, without requiring explicit navigation planners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Global-Local Attention Decomposition (GLAD), aims to provide a compact and functionally decomposed terrain representation for humanoid perceptive locomotion.

**Problem Formulation:**
The locomotion task is formulated as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP). The robot receives an observation `o_t` consisting of proprioceptive measurements (base angular velocity, gravity direction, joint positions/velocities, previous action) and an exteroceptive elevation map `m_t`. The elevation map `m_t` is a 3D grid `R^(L×W×3)` where each cell stores 3D coordinates in the robot's base frame. The action `a_t` is a 29-dimensional vector representing joint position offsets from a nominal standing configuration. The objective is to maximize the expected discounted return.

**GLAD Terrain Encoding Architecture:**
The GLAD encoder processes the elevation map input to generate a terrain representation, which is then concatenated with proprioceptive data and fed into the policy network.

1. **Spatially Aligned Local Feature Extraction:**
 - A two-layer Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) processes the input elevation map `m_t`. Unlike some prior methods, this CNN directly convolves the 3D coordinates (x, y, z) at each map location, ensuring positional cues are intrinsically embedded in the features.
 - Zero padding is used in both convolutional layers to preserve boundary information and maintain spatial alignment between the features and the physical terrain map.
 - The first convolutional layer performs strided downsampling (kernel size 5, stride `s=2`, 16 output channels). This decouples sensing resolution from encoded feature resolution, reducing the sequence length of local features (from L×W to (L/s)×(W/s)) and thus lowering the computational cost for subsequent attention modules.
 - The second convolutional layer uses a kernel size of 3 and projects the downsampled features into a unified embedding dimension `D=64`.
 - The CNN output is a feature grid `K_grid ∈ R^((L/s)×(W/s)×D)`.

2. **Global-Local Attention Decomposition:**
 This stage consists of three steps, processing the flattened local terrain features `{k_i}` (where `N=(L/s)×(W/s)`).

 - **Global Attention Branch:**
 - A linear layer is applied to each local feature `k_i` to produce a global-attention logit `u_i`.
 - Normalized global-attention weights `α_i` are computed using a softmax function over `u_i`.
 - A surrounding terrain context feature `c ∈ R^D` is obtained by a weighted sum of all local features `k_i` using `α_i`. This branch aims to summarize broad terrain information.

 - **State-Conditioned Local Feature Sparsification:**
 - The global context `c` is concatenated with the `D`-dimensional proprioceptive embedding.
 - This combined vector is projected via a linear layer into a state-conditioned query vector `q ∈ R^D`.
 - For each local feature `k_i`, a fused feature `[q; k_i]` is formed, and a relevance score `s_i` is computed using another linear layer.
 - Based on these scores `s_i`, only the top-K local features are retained, discarding less relevant regions. This implements state-conditioned sparsification, reducing the number of features for detailed processing. K is set to 32 for the experiments.

 - **Local Attention Branch:**
 - Multi-Head Attention (MHA) is applied over the *retained sparse features* (as key and value).
 - The state-conditioned query vector `q` is used as the query input for MHA.
 - This process yields a fine-grained, foothold-relevant feature `f ∈ R^D`.

 - **Final Representation:** The surrounding terrain context `c` and the foothold-relevant feature `f` are concatenated to form the unified terrain representation that is fed into the locomotion policy.

**Training and Experimental Setup:**

- **Policy Optimization:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for training.
- **Network Architecture:** Actor and critic networks share the terrain encoding module and use separate MLPs with hidden dimensions.
- **Elevation Map:** A 1.6 m × 1.0 m robot-centric area with 0.05 m resolution (33×21 grid) is used as input. After CNN downsampling (stride `s=2`), the feature grid is 17×11 with an effective resolution of approximately 0.1 m.
- **Two-Stage Training:**
 - **Stage 1:** Initial training on basic terrains (stairs, random grid, rough terrain, slopes, stepping-stone-everywhere, gaps) with perfect terrain perception to learn fundamental locomotion skills.
 - **Stage 2:** More challenging sparse-foothold courses (parallel-row, single-row, alternating stepping stones, rails, random cylindrical obstacles) are introduced. Observation noise and domain randomization (per-step observation noise, random scan drift, artificial pushes, torso mass/friction randomization) are applied. A progressive terrain curriculum with ten difficulty levels is used in both stages.
- **Reward Function:** Standard locomotion rewards are used, without handcrafted foothold-specific rewards.
- **Baselines and Ablations:**
 - **Baselines:** AME, and AME with global context (AME-GC, similar to AME-2).
 - **Ablations:** GLAD w/o global attention (only sparsification and MHA), GLAD w/o local attention (only attention pooling).
- **Simulation Environment:** NVIDIA Isaac Sim, with 4096 parallel robots over two stages of 10,000 iterations each.
- **Hardware Deployment:** Unitree G1 humanoid robot with Livox Mid-360 LiDAR and IMU. FAST-LIO is used for state estimation and elevation map construction. The system operates at 50 Hz on a laptop with an RTX 5090 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Training Efficiency and Convergence:**
The GLAD model demonstrated superior training efficiency and convergence behavior. It reached higher terrain curriculum levels earlier than both ablation variants (GLAD w/o global attention and GLAD w/o local attention), indicating that the synergistic operation of both attention branches accelerates and stabilizes learning. Computationally, GLAD completed its two-stage training in 1.00 days on an NVIDIA H200 GPU, outperforming AME (1.36 days) and AME with global context (1.50 days). This efficiency gain is partly attributed to the coarse-to-fine design and state-conditioned sparsification, which reduce the computational burden of dense attention. An analysis of the sparsification retention threshold `K` indicated `K=32` as an optimal balance between expressiveness and efficiency.

**Unseen Terrain Performance (Simulation Experiments):**
The proposed GLAD method was evaluated on four unseen, 10-meter long-horizon test terrains: composite stepping stones, random single-row stepping stones, irregular pentagonal pillars, and a sinusoidal narrow path.
- On stepping stone terrains (a and b), GLAD achieved high success rates (95.51% and 96.88%), outperforming baselines and ablations, particularly GLAD w/o global attention (80.08% and 85.94%), which highlighted the necessity of broad terrain context even on structurally similar terrains.
- On terrains requiring precise foothold identification in cluttered environments, such as pentagonal pillars (c), GLAD maintained a success rate above 90% (91.21%). AME with global context performed less effectively (74.61%), suggesting that entangling context and local geometry within a single MHA can dilute precision. GLAD w/o local attention also showed a significant performance drop (73.44%), confirming that global context alone is insufficient for precise foothold resolution.
- The sinusoidal narrow path (d), which demanded anticipatory directional adjustments, was the most challenging. GLAD achieved a 95.90% success rate, substantially outperforming all baselines and ablations (e.g., AME at 25.00%, AME-GC at 42.58%). This result strongly supported the hypothesis that the explicit decomposition allows for both sharp foothold cues (local attention) and contextual forward traversability understanding (global attention).

**Attention–Foothold Alignment and Concentration:**
Quantitative analysis of attention weights at touchdown events measured the alignment of attended features with realized footholds.
- GLAD achieved the smallest attention-weighted distance (`D_attn`) to the foothold on both continuous (0.137 m) and discrete (0.133 m) terrains, indicating that its full local-attention distribution was accurately aligned.
- While the peak localization error (`D_peak`) was comparable between GLAD and AME, GLAD exhibited significantly higher attention concentration (`A_peak`). On discrete terrains, GLAD's `A_peak` was 0.447, compared to AME's 0.077 and AME-GC's 0.037.
- GLAD demonstrated terrain-adaptive concentration, with `A_peak` increasing from continuous to discrete terrains, suggesting the policy adapted its focus based on terrain difficulty. AME-GC's `D_attn` was the largest, and its `A_peak` the smallest, further supporting the claim that injecting global context into a single attention mechanism can dilute attention around precise foothold regions.

**Interpretable Attention Decomposition (Qualitative Visualization):**
Visualizations of the global and local attention weights confirmed a distinct functional separation:
- **Global Attention:** Displayed broadly receptive, terrain-dependent patterns. On flat ground, attention was distributed towards the far end of the map. On stairs, gaps, and obstacles, it highlighted geometric edges with significant height variations. On constrained terrains (stepping stones, narrow paths), it concentrated on nearby forward-traversable regions. This indicated that the global branch effectively captures anticipatory cues for upcoming geometric changes and traversability.
- **Local Attention:** Exhibited highly concentrated spatial patterns, typically peaking at the robot's current or next feasible foothold locations. This localized behavior confirmed that the local branch provided the fine-grained spatial geometry essential for precise foot placement, guided by state-conditioned sparsification.

**Real-world Deployment:**
The policy trained with GLAD achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot using onboard LiDAR-based elevation mapping.
- The robot successfully traversed challenging real-world terrains, including alternating stepping stones (20x25 cm stones, 70 cm spacing), a staircase–gap composite (30 cm tread, 12 cm height, 30 cm gap), a wide gap (70 cm, exceeding training limits), and obstacle-rich terrains (wooden blocks, suitcases).
- The learned policy demonstrated emergent terrain-responsive behaviors, such as autonomously following narrow paths and avoiding obstacles, guided solely by simple velocity commands, without requiring explicit navigation planners.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Global-Local Attention Decomposition (GLAD) as a method for terrain encoding in humanoid perceptive locomotion, addressing a known limitation in current approaches: the entanglement of broad terrain context and precise local foothold selection.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Architectural Innovation:** GLAD explicitly decomposes attention mechanisms into global and local branches. This architectural design provides a principled way to balance the need for comprehensive environmental awareness with the requirement for precise, state-conditioned foothold-level detail.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The superior performance of GLAD on various challenging and unseen terrains, especially sparse footholds, cluttered environments, and narrow paths, demonstrates its effectiveness. The policy's ability to achieve robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical humanoid robot using onboard sensing is a strong indicator of its practical utility and generalization capabilities.
- **Improved Interpretability:** The distinct functional separation observed in the global and local attention patterns offers insights into the policy's decision-making process. The global branch focuses on anticipatory cues and traversability, while the local branch targets precise foothold regions, making the terrain representation more transparent and potentially facilitating future policy debugging or refinement.
- **Reduced Computational Overhead:** By incorporating strided downsampling in the CNN and state-conditioned sparsification for the local attention branch, GLAD reduces the computational cost associated with applying dense attention to the entire feature set, making it more efficient for training and deployment.
- **Emergent Intelligent Behaviors:** The policy's ability to exhibit complex emergent behaviors, such as narrow-path following and obstacle avoidance, without explicit navigation planners, suggests that an effective terrain representation can enable higher-level intelligence from a low-level locomotion controller. This could simplify robotic system architectures by reducing the need for hierarchical planning.

The potential impact of this research is substantial for the field of humanoid robotics. It contributes to developing more robust, autonomous, and adaptable humanoid robots capable of navigating diverse and unstructured environments designed for humans. Future work building on GLAD could explore more efficient real-time mapping pipelines, incorporate temporally extended perception to handle dynamic environments, and develop multi-scale terrain representations to further enhance the robot's environmental understanding and interaction capabilities.
