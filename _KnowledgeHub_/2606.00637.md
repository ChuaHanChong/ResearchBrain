# Global-Local Attention Decomposition for Terrain Encoding in Humanoid Perceptive Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Humanoid Robot (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. developed Global-Local Attention Decomposition (GLAD), a terrain encoder for humanoid robots that explicitly separates global environmental context from local foothold-relevant geometry. This architecture enhances perceptive locomotion on complex terrains like sparse footholds and narrow paths, achieving higher success rates and faster training convergence while demonstrating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in achieving robust and reliable locomotion on complex, unstructured terrains with sparse footholds or constrained navigation paths.
- Conventional terrain encoders often neglect the robot's immediate kinematic state, leading to a dilution of fine-grained geometric cues necessary for precise foothold selection.
- Existing attention-based terrain encoding methods tend to entangle broad terrain awareness with precise local foothold encoding, increasing computational costs and reducing the interpretability of attention mechanisms.

## Method

- Introduced **Global-Local Attention Decomposition (GLAD)**, a ==terrain encoder== that explicitly separates ==global terrain context aggregation== from ==state-conditioned local foothold encoding==.
- Utilizes spatially aligned ==convolutional neural networks (CNNs)== with strided downsampling to extract initial local terrain features from 3D elevation maps.
- Employs a ==global attention branch== to summarize broad environmental context and a ==local attention branch== with ==state-conditioned sparsification== and Multi-Head Attention (MHA) for fine-grained, foothold-relevant feature extraction.

## Results

- GLAD achieved faster training convergence (**1.00 days**) compared to baselines (e.g., AME at **1.36 days**) and demonstrated superior success rates on unseen challenging terrains, achieving **95.90%** on a sinusoidal narrow path.
- Quantitative analysis showed GLAD's attention distribution was better aligned with footholds (smaller D_attn of **0.137m** on continuous terrains) and more concentrated (higher A_peak of **0.447** on discrete terrains) than other methods.
- The GLAD policy demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot across diverse real-world terrains, including gaps wider than training limits, using only onboard LiDAR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decomposing global and local attention mechanisms is effective for preventing the dilution of fine-grained spatial cues, leading to more precise and robust perceptive locomotion on complex terrains.
> - State-conditioned sparsification of local terrain features significantly reduces computational overhead for attention modules while maintaining or improving performance by focusing on relevant regions.
> - A well-structured terrain representation, without relying on handcrafted rewards or terrain-specific curricula, can enable complex emergent navigation behaviors in humanoid robots.
