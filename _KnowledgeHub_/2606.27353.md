# Continual Robot Policy Learning via Variational Neural Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Zurich developed a framework for continual robot policy learning, enabling quadrotors to adapt to hidden and recurring real-world dynamics by recognizing previously encountered conditions. This approach demonstrated a 5-fold faster recovery to recurring wind disturbances and reduced large-disturbance tracking error by 53.3% compared to prior online adaptation methods.

## Problem

- Robots deployed in dynamic real-world environments face unmodeled and non-stationary conditions (e.g., wind, payload, wear) that fixed, pre-trained policies cannot effectively handle.
- Current online adaptation methods often re-adapt or relearn from scratch when dynamic conditions recur or change, leading to inefficient performance recovery.
- Existing latent conditioning approaches infer contexts, but deployed policies are usually fixed and latent spaces are often learned with privileged information in simulation.

## Method

- A framework that continuously alternates between real-world data collection, Variational Neural Dynamics (**VND**) model learning, and policy refinement via differentiable simulation.
- A VND model composed of a recurrent history encoder to infer hidden conditions (latent 'z') from past state-action sequences and a latent-conditioned neural residual model that augments a known physics prior.
- During policy optimization, the latent 'z' is sampled from a prior, conditioning both the learned dynamics and the policy, while in deployment, 'z' is inferred online by the recurrent encoder for real-time adaptation.

## Results

- Quadrotors demonstrated approximately 5-fold faster recovery (1 second vs. 5 seconds) to recurring wind disturbances on hardware by recognizing conditions compared to re-fitting residual models.
- The method reduced large-disturbance hover error by **65.7%** (from 0.105m to 0.036m) and large-disturbance tracking error by **53.3%** (from 0.137m to 0.064m) compared to the strongest online adaptation baseline, LOTF.
- The framework successfully adapted to an out-of-distribution hardware failure (cut propeller), reducing tracking error from 52 cm to 12 cm within a few refinement iterations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured, condition-aware dynamics can be learned from unlabeled real-world state-action trajectories by forcing a single inferred latent variable to explain multiple transitions from a consistent context.
> - Aligning the distribution of inferred latent dynamics with a simple Gaussian prior enables sampling diverse, condition-specific dynamics for robust policy training in differentiable simulation.
> - Integrating a physics prior with a latent-conditioned neural residual model allows for capturing unmodeled, condition-dependent effects efficiently, leveraging known physics while learning deviations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Context

Learning-based control methods have demonstrated considerable success across various robotics applications, including agile quadrotor flight, legged locomotion, and visuomotor manipulation. These control policies are typically developed in simulated environments, transferred to physical hardware, and then deployed with a fixed set of parameters. This "train-once, deploy-forever" approach presupposes that the learning process concludes prior to deployment and that real-world dynamics are static or perfectly modeled.

However, robots operating in dynamic real-world environments frequently encounter conditions that diverge from their initial training. Factors such as varying wind conditions, changes in payload, battery degradation, shifting contact dynamics, and hardware wear introduce unmodeled and often non-stationary effects. Crucially, these variable conditions can be hidden from direct observation and may recur over time. Existing methods often struggle with this problem. Domain randomization, for instance, trains a single policy to be robust across a range of simulated dynamics, but this typically results in a conservative policy that compromises performance across diverse conditions. Other approaches, such as Real2Sim2Real and residual-identification methods, leverage real-world interactions to update simulators or residual dynamics models. Recent advancements in differentiable simulation have enabled rapid fitting of quadrotor residual dynamics within seconds. A limitation of these methods is that adaptation is usually tied to the specific, currently observed condition. When dynamics change, or a previously encountered condition reappears, the robot is typically required to re-adapt or relearn from scratch. This can lead to inefficient adaptation and performance that is bounded by the original simulation's specified dynamics distribution.

A related line of research explores online policy adaptation, including classical adaptive control techniques like L1-MPC, which offer fast disturbance rejection but rely on predefined model structures. Model-free reinforcement learning, while capable of online updates, often demands prohibitively large sample budgets for physical robots. Differentiable simulation-based approaches for online adaptation have shown promise in refining policies and reducing errors, but they generally fit a single residual model to the active condition, necessitating fresh re-adaptation upon condition changes.

Another relevant area is latent conditioning on unobserved environmental variables, where policies are adapted based on interaction history to infer task or dynamics context. Methods like RMA (Rapid Motor Adaptation) and DATT (Deep Adaptive Trajectory Tracking) have popularized the use of history-inferred latent conditioning for scenarios like legged locomotion and quadrotor tracking. While these works demonstrate the effectiveness of inferred context as a policy input under hidden dynamics, the latent spaces are often learned in simulation, sometimes with privileged information, and the deployed policies are typically fixed.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a framework that enables continual policy learning, explicitly addressing the challenge of hidden and recurring dynamics. Unlike prior online adaptation methods, it trains a single condition-aware policy across a distribution of conditions inferred from real flight data. Furthermore, it distinguishes itself from latent conditioning methods by learning the latent dynamics representation directly from real state-action trajectories through residual dynamics prediction, subsequently utilizing this representation to condition both the policy and a learned simulator for ongoing policy optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that robots deployed in real-world environments rarely operate under static or perfectly known dynamics. Environmental factors like wind, changes in payload, battery discharge, varying contact conditions, and hardware wear introduce diverse, often unobservable, and frequently recurring disturbances. Conventional learning-based control policies, typically trained once and deployed with fixed parameters, are not designed to leverage real-world experience for continuous improvement. This limitation prevents robots from enhancing their performance when encountering new or previously seen dynamics.

The inefficiency of one-shot adaptation is particularly evident when a familiar disturbance reappears. In such scenarios, a robot should ideally recognize the condition and reuse learned adaptations rather than initiating a new adaptation process from scratch. Therefore, the research is motivated by the need for a framework that enables robots to identify active dynamics from interaction history, reuse prior knowledge, and continually improve their behavior in the face of these hidden and non-stationary real-world variations.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a continual policy learning framework:** To allow robots to continually improve their policies by learning from real-world experience, specifically addressing hidden and recurring dynamics.
2. **Learn a condition-aware dynamics model:** To construct a model that can capture the varying characteristics of the robot's dynamics under different conditions, utilizing real state-action trajectories.
3. **Integrate physics priors with neural residuals:** To combine an analytical physics model (as a prior) with a neural residual component to account for unmodeled effects, thereby leveraging known physics while learning unknown deviations.
4. **Infer hidden conditions from interaction history:** To employ a recurrent encoder that can estimate the current hidden condition from a short window of recent state-action interactions.
5. **Condition both dynamics and policy:** To use the inferred hidden condition estimate to modulate both the neural residual dynamics model and the robot's control policy.
6. **Enable differentiable policy learning with diverse dynamics:** To facilitate policy optimization through differentiable simulation, where diverse dynamics are generated by sampling from the learned latent dynamics model.
7. **Support online recognition for recurring dynamics:** To ensure that, during real-world deployment, the policy can adapt to recurring dynamics by inferring conditions online and utilizing its learned knowledge, rather than requiring re-fitting of a new residual model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for continual robot policy learning operates as an asynchronous loop, alternating between real-world interaction and differentiable policy improvement. The process begins with a base policy, initially trained in a simplified simulator, which ensures safe operation and the collection of useful trajectories in the real world. These collected real state-action-transition data are stored in a growing buffer. This buffer is then used to train a Variational Neural Dynamics (VND) model. After updating the VND model, the robot's policy is refined through differentiable simulation rollouts.

**Problem Formulation:**
The robot's interaction with the environment is modeled as a partially observed Markov Decision Process (S, A, W, T, r, γ), where W represents the hidden-condition space. The transition kernel T(· | s_t, a_t, w) depends on this unobserved condition w. To address the hidden condition, a history encoder E_φ maps a rolling context window H_t-C:t of recent state-action pairs to a low-dimensional latent variable z_t = E_φ(H_t-C:t). The policy is then conditioned on both the current observation o_t and this inferred latent: a_t = π_θ(o_t, z_t).

**Variational Neural Dynamics (VND) Model:**
The VND model is designed to ensure that the latent code z_t is inferable from real-world interaction during deployment and also sampleable for policy learning in simulation. It comprises two main components:
1. **Recurrent History Encoder (E_φ):** This GRU-based network takes a sequence of recent state-action pairs as input and outputs the latent code z_t. This design enables the model to infer the active hidden condition from the temporal context, as individual transitions might not provide sufficient evidence.
2. **Latent-conditioned Residual Dynamics Model (D_ψ):** This model augments a known analytical physics prior, f_prior(s_t, a_t), with a neural residual. The predicted next state is given by ŝ_t+1 = f_prior(s_t, a_t) + D_ψ(s_t, a_t, z_t). For quadrotors, f_prior is a rigid-body model (e.g., integrated with RK4), and D_ψ is an MLP with output heads for position, velocity, and orientation (represented as rotation vectors, composed with nominal orientation via the exponential map). This structure allows the model to capture condition-dependent effects (like wind or payload changes) as corrections to the known physics, without requiring a separate model for each environment.

**Learning an Informative Latent Space:**
The encoder E_φ and the residual dynamics model D_ψ are trained jointly without privileged labels for the hidden conditions. The core idea is that a single inferred latent z_t should explain multiple transitions originating from the same trajectory context.
- **Dynamics Loss (L_dyn):** This is a Huber loss applied to the difference between the residual dynamics prediction D_ψ(s_i, a_i, z_k(i)) and the residual target r_i = s_i+1 ⊖ f_prior(s_i, a_i), where z_k(i) is the latent inferred from the context relevant to transition i. Sharing the latent across transitions forces the encoder to capture underlying common dynamics.
- **Auxiliary Reconstruction Loss (L_rec):** An L2 reconstruction loss from the latent code back to the normalized state-action context window (G_η(E_φ(H_n)) − norm(H_n)) is included. This loss helps the latent code retain relevant trajectory information and improves the accuracy of the dynamics model.
- **Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) Loss (L_mmd):** To bridge the gap between encoder-inferred latents (deployment) and sampled latents (policy training), the aggregated distribution of encoded latents, q_φ(z), is aligned with a simple Gaussian sampling prior, p(z) = N(0, I), using MMD. This ensures that samples from the prior produce realistic residual dynamics within the learned space. An annealed schedule for the MMD loss (λ_mmd(e)) is used to maintain stability, allowing the dynamics loss to first separate conditions before enforcing prior alignment.

The full variational neural dynamics objective is L_vnd = L_dyn + λ_rec L_rec + λ_mmd MMD(q_φ, p). During latent model updates, all contexts in the replay buffer are re-encoded to maintain a consistent coordinate system and prevent latent drift.

**Policy Learning with Sampled Latent Dynamics:**
Once the VND model is learned, its parameters (φ, ψ) are frozen. For policy training, at the beginning of each simulated rollout, a latent variable z is sampled from the Gaussian prior p(z) = N(0, I) and kept fixed for the entire episode. This same sampled latent conditions both the policy π_θ(ō_t, z) and the dynamics model (f_prior(s_t, a_t) + D_ψ(s_t, a_t, z)). The policy is then optimized using Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) to maximize the expected discounted sum of rewards E_z~p(z), s0~ρ0 [Σ γ^t r(s_t, π_θ(ō_t, z), s_t+1)]. The Adam optimizer is used for this process.

**Deployment:**
During real-world deployment, the policy interface remains identical, but the source of the latent variable changes. Instead of sampled latents, the online inferred latent z_t = E_φ(H_t-C:t) from the recurrent encoder is fed to the policy. This allows the policy to adapt its behavior to the active, hidden condition in real-time by recognition, leveraging its pre-trained knowledge across diverse conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive empirical validation of the proposed continual learning framework, encompassing both real-world quadrotor experiments and controlled simulation studies across various tasks.

**Real-World Flight Results:**
- **Recovery under recurring wind:** On a real quadrotor performing trajectory tracking under changing wind conditions, the policy demonstrated a recovery time of approximately 1 second when encountering a previously learned wind direction. This recovery is achieved through online inference of the latent condition. In comparison, an online residual adaptation baseline required approximately 5 seconds to re-fit a residual model for the new condition, indicating a ~5-fold improvement in recovery speed by recognition over re-learning.
- **Revisiting previous conditions:** The continual policy learning framework effectively reduced figure-eight tracking error on hardware. Starting from a base policy with an error of about 41 cm, the error decreased to approximately 9 cm after refinement over wall-clock time. This demonstrates that deployment data are converted into persistent policy improvements that hold across subsequent flights and changing disturbance conditions.
- **Adaptation to unmodeled hardware disturbances:** The framework was tested with an out-of-distribution hardware failure: a cut propeller. The method adapted to this genuinely new residual effect, reducing tracking error from 52 cm to 24 cm within the first two refinement iterations, and further improving to 12 cm. This indicates the framework's capacity to incorporate novel dynamics online.

**System Analysis:**
- **Dynamics Learning and Latent Space Analysis:** Analysis of the learned latent space in simulation (Figure 4) revealed that the unsupervised latent embeddings formed distinct clusters corresponding to different wind directions and magnitudes, even though wind labels were not used during training. This indicates that the recurrent encoder effectively discovers and represents structured dynamics. Open-loop rollouts further showed that the latent-conditioned residual model accurately predicts condition-specific motion, matching ground-truth trajectories within 0.1 m.
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art Controllers (Table 1):** The proposed method was benchmarked against fixed sim-trained control, latent adaptation (RMA, DATT), classical adaptive control (L1-MPC), and online residual fitting (LOTF). In small-disturbance hovering, the method matched the best baseline. For large-disturbance hovering, small-disturbance tracking, and large-disturbance tracking, it achieved the lowest errors. Specifically, it reduced large-disturbance hover error by 65.7% (from 0.105 m to 0.036 m) and large-disturbance tracking error by 53.3% (from 0.137 m to 0.064 m) compared to LOTF, the strongest online residual-adaptation baseline. It also maintained stability under conditions where DATT experienced crashes.
- **Ablation Studies (Figure 3):** An evaluation of individual modeling choices confirmed their contribution to performance. The full model yielded the lowest cumulative dynamics prediction error across hovering and tracking tasks. Removing FiLM conditioning, MMD alignment, or the auxiliary reconstruction objective consistently increased errors. Replacing the recurrent encoder with an MLP also degraded performance, particularly in hovering. These results collectively support the importance of residual dynamics, history-based condition inference, and latent-space regularization for the observed performance gains.
- **Comparisons of Learning Pipelines (Figure 5):** Quantitative comparisons across multiple tasks (quadrotor landing, vision-based hovering, trajectory tracking, and contact-rich manipulation) showed that the proposed method consistently approached the performance of a ground-truth oracle. It substantially outperformed simple residual learning, single-condition training, and RMA-style latent adaptation baselines. For instance, in quadrotor trajectory tracking, position error was reduced from 0.311–0.312 m for residual/single-disturbance baselines and 0.215 m for RMA to 0.045 m. Similar trends were observed in manipulation tasks, where the method maintained performance across varying object or payload masses, unlike baselines that degraded under larger hidden dynamics shifts. This suggests that sampling from the learned dynamics distribution provides effective condition variation for policy training, leading to improved generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for continual policy learning that addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: operating under diverse, hidden, and recurring real-world dynamics. By moving beyond the "train-once, deploy-forever" paradigm, the work significantly advances the capability of learning-based controllers to improve continuously from real-world experience.

The key significance of this work lies in several aspects:
1. **Efficient adaptation to recurring conditions:** The framework enables robots to recognize previously encountered hidden dynamics and adapt rapidly by inferring the active condition, rather than undergoing a time-consuming re-adaptation process. This efficiency, demonstrated by a 5x faster recovery time compared to online residual re-fitting, is critical for sustained autonomous operation in dynamic environments.
2. **Integration of physics priors and neural learning:** The combination of an analytical physics prior with a latent-conditioned neural residual model leverages the strengths of both approaches. It ensures that known physical structures are preserved while allowing the neural component to learn complex, unmodeled, and condition-dependent effects, such as wind disturbances, payload changes, or even hardware degradation.
3. **Self-supervised dynamics learning:** The framework learns a structured, condition-aware dynamics representation from real state-action trajectories without requiring explicit labels for the hidden conditions. This reduces reliance on manually specified disturbance models and facilitates discovery of relevant dynamics from interaction data.
4. **Effective differentiable policy optimization:** By aligning the aggregated distribution of inferred latents with a simple sampling prior, the framework creates a sampleable interface for generating diverse, condition-consistent dynamics within a differentiable simulator. This enables effective policy training that generalizes well across varying conditions.
5. **Demonstrated robustness and generalization:** The empirical results, spanning real quadrotor flights and various simulation tasks (aerial and manipulation), indicate superior performance over state-of-the-art online adaptation and latent conditioning methods. The ability to adapt to out-of-distribution hardware shifts, such as a cut propeller, highlights the framework's robustness.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, particularly for the deployment of intelligent robots in unstructured and unpredictable environments:
- **Enhanced autonomy and reliability:** Robots equipped with this framework could operate more robustly and reliably over extended periods in real-world settings where conditions are constantly changing.
- **Reduced human intervention:** The ability of robots to continually improve and adapt may decrease the need for manual retraining or expert intervention, making robotic systems more self-sufficient.
- **Broader applicability of learning-based control:** By mitigating the reality gap and addressing dynamic environments, the framework can facilitate the deployment of advanced learning-based controllers in a wider range of safety-critical or complex applications.
- **Foundation for future research:** The work identifies limitations, such as the reliance on accurate low-dimensional state estimates, suggesting future directions in uncertainty-aware continual learning. This could involve weighting dynamics updates by state-estimation confidence or probabilistically modeling high-uncertainty transitions, further enhancing the robustness of continual learning in perception-limited scenarios.
