# Learning to Get Up Across Morphologies: Zero-Shot Recovery with a Unified Humanoid Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a unified deep reinforcement learning policy for humanoid robots to recover from falls. The policy is trained across multiple robot morphologies without explicit robot-specific data, demonstrating zero-shot transfer to unseen robot designs and enhancing fall recovery capabilities for morphologies that previously struggled.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots require robust fall recovery skills for sustained autonomy in dynamic and unpredictable environments.
- Traditional fall recovery methods, including specialized DRL, are morphology-specific, labor-intensive, and difficult to generalize across different robot designs.
- The high development cost and limited reusability of existing fall recovery solutions hinder the scalability and deployment of diverse humanoid robot platforms.

## Method

- A unified Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policy was developed, trained across a diverse suite of seven humanoid robot morphologies from the RoboCup community.
- A morphology-agnostic control interface, observation space, and reward structure were designed to allow the single policy to learn and adapt to various robot characteristics without explicit morphological identifiers.
- Extensive domain randomization of physical properties and varied episode initialization states were employed during training to ensure robustness and facilitate zero-shot transfer and sim-to-real capabilities.

## Results

- The unified policy demonstrated zero-shot transfer to an unseen robot, achieving a 72% success rate on Wolfgang, while exhibiting varying performance on other held-out morphologies (17-42% success).
- Compared to specialist policies, the shared policy achieved a significant +61% increase in success rate for the NUGUS robot, though it incurred moderate performance deficits (up to -20%) on some other morphologies.
- Morphological scaling analysis showed that zero-shot success improved with an increased number of well-chosen training morphologies (up to 86% on Wolfgang with four robots), but performance could be negatively impacted by including difficult or outlier morphologies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single DRL policy can learn to perform complex fall recovery across diverse humanoid morphologies and exhibit zero-shot transfer to unseen robots, extending generalization beyond simpler locomotion tasks.
> - Shared training can enable fall recovery capabilities for morphologies that are challenging or impossible to train in isolation (e.g., NUGUS), indicating benefits of collective experience and knowledge distillation.
> - Effective zero-shot generalization is critically dependent on the composition of the training set, requiring continuous and overlapping coverage of the morphological space rather than just a collection of disparate robot types.
