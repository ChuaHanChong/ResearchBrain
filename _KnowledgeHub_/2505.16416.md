# Circle-RoPE: Cone-like Decoupled Rotary Positional Embedding for Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Noah's Ark Lab and academic partners developed Circle-RoPE, a cone-like decoupled rotary positional embedding for Large Vision-Language Models that eliminates spurious cross-modal positional biases. The method improved average performance on multimodal benchmarks by +1.73 over baseline Qwen2.5-VL, achieving an average score of 61.13.

## Problem

- Existing RoPE implementations in Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) introduce spurious cross-modal positional biases, enforcing unnatural relative dependencies between text and image tokens.
- Current methods either flatten 2D image data into 1D sequences, losing vital spatial information, or utilize 2D coordinates in ways that still cause cross-modal misalignment.
- A lack of robust positional encoding fully decouples cross-modal dependencies while preserving rich intra-image spatial information, hindering accurate multimodal understanding.

## Method

- Introduces ==Circle-RoPE==, a ==cone-like decoupled rotary positional embedding==, which transforms 2D image token coordinates onto a 3D circular structure oriented perpendicularly to the text token index vector.
- Applies ==Alternating Geometry Encoding (AGE)==, cyclically switching between Circle-RoPE and standard M-RoPE across different Transformer layers to leverage complementary positional information.
- Extends encoding capabilities to multi-image sequences by translating each image's circular encoding center along a fixed global axis, preserving unique positional identifiers.

## Results

- Circle-RoPE achieved an average score of **61.13** on diverse multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MMMU, MathVista, MMStar), representing a **+1.73** improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL baseline.
- The method demonstrated notable performance gains on tasks requiring strong spatial reasoning, including MMStar (+**4.07**), AI2D (+**3.66**), and MathVista (+**1.00**).
- Visual attention map analysis revealed enhanced focus on relevant image regions with fewer irrelevant allocations, indicating improved cross-modal alignment and more precise visual reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Per-Token Distance (PTD) metric effectively quantifies cross-modal positional biases in existing RoPE variants, confirming the presence of biases (e.g., PTD of 2.22 for hard embedding and 0.64 for spatial embedding).
> - A novel geometric projection into a cone-like structure achieves complete cross-modal positional decoupling (PTD=0) while precisely retaining intra-image spatial relationships.
> - Cyclically applying different positional encoding types (Alternating Geometry Encoding) across Transformer layers optimally leverages their complementary strengths, enhancing overall multimodal representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) has been widely adopted in the development of large language models (LLMs) for encoding relative positional information. The expansion of these models into multimodal domains, specifically Vision-Language Models (VLMs), introduces challenges related to representing positional information across heterogeneous modalities. Text is inherently sequential, while visual information possesses a two-dimensional spatial structure with attributes like location and orientation, which differ fundamentally from textual order.

Existing approaches to integrating RoPE into VLMs typically involve one of three strategies:
1. **Hard Embedding:** Image tokens are flattened into a one-dimensional sequence and concatenated with text tokens, followed by the application of standard 1D RoPE (e.g., LLaVA, Emu3, InternLM-VL, DeepSeek-VL2). This method can introduce spurious cross-modal positional bias because relative positions are determined by the concatenation order and index gaps rather than semantic alignment.
2. **Unordered Embedding:** All image patches within an image are assigned a single, shared image token index (e.g., mPLUG-Owl3). This approach mitigates some modality-mixing bias by ensuring all text-to-image relative RoPE distances are identical. However, it sacrifices fine-grained intra-image positional distinctions, which can impede spatial grounding.
3. **Spatial Embedding:** Image tokens are assigned 2D grid indices based on their spatial positions in the original image, preserving intra-image layout (e.g., M-RoPE in Qwen2-VL). While this improves intra-image structure preservation compared to 1D flattening, the cross-modal relative positions remain coupled with text tokens through a shared index space, potentially leading to persistent cross-modal positional biases.

This research positions itself within this landscape by identifying and addressing the limitations of these existing RoPE variants. The authors highlight that current methods either compromise intra-image spatial structure or maintain problematic cross-modal coupling. The work aims to develop a positional encoding scheme that decouples text and image positional information to mitigate spurious biases, while simultaneously preserving detailed spatial relationships within the visual modality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a Rotary Position Embedding scheme for Vision-Language Models that effectively decouples cross-modal positional relationships, thereby eliminating spurious cross-modal relative-position bias, while simultaneously preserving the intra-image spatial structure of visual tokens.

This objective is motivated by several identified shortcomings in existing RoPE applications within VLMs:
- **Spurious Cross-Modal Positional Bias:** Current practices often flatten visual inputs into 1D sequences or use 2D grids, then concatenate them with text tokens. This concatenation embeds both modalities into a shared index space where cross-modal relative positions are determined by arbitrary indexing schemes (e.g., rasterization order) rather than genuine semantic alignment. For instance, a text token intended for a specific object region might be numerically closer in index space to an unrelated image patch, creating misleading positional cues for the model.
- **Inconsistent Multi-Token Distances:** When a single semantic entity in text (e.g., "clock tower") corresponds to multiple image tokens, existing methods can assign varying RoPE distances from the text span to these semantically related image patches. This inconsistency can confuse the model, hindering accurate spatial grounding and multimodal understanding.
- **Compromised Intra-Image Structure:** Approaches that assign a uniform index to all image tokens (unordered embedding) alleviate cross-modal bias but do so at the cost of collapsing all fine-grained intra-image spatial distinctions. This loss of detail is detrimental to tasks requiring precise visual reasoning.
- **Geometric First Principles:** The authors propose to address these issues from a geometric perspective, asking how a textual "observer" should be positioned relative to a visual "canvas" in the embedding space. They argue that an orthogonal relationship is ideal, where text tokens are equidistant to all image tokens. This geometry would ensure that attention is driven by semantic relevance rather than artificial positional proximity.

To quantify and address this problem, the authors introduce the Per-Token Distance (PTD) metric. A core motivation is to achieve PTD = 0, which they formally prove to be a sufficient condition to eliminate the geometric attention bias induced by RoPE. This theoretical grounding underpins the design of their proposed solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Circle Rotary Position Embedding (Circle-RoPE), aims to geometrically decouple image and text positional encodings while maintaining intra-image spatial structure. It comprises two main components: Circular Image Token Index Projection (CIP) and Alternating Geometry Encoding (AGE).

#### 3.1. Per-Token Distance (PTD) Metric

Before detailing the method, the authors formalize the problem of cross-modal positional bias using the Per-Token Distance (PTD) metric. PTD quantifies the degree of cross-modal positional disentanglement by measuring the variance of RoPE-index distances from each text token to the set of image tokens. A smaller PTD indicates greater disentanglement. The authors provide a theoretical connection, demonstrating that if PTD = 0, the geometric attention bias induced by RoPE is eliminated, meaning RoPE contributes a uniform scalar shift across image tokens for a fixed text token, and cannot encode a positional preference.
The PTD values for existing methods are: Hard embedding (2.22), Unordered embedding (0), Spatial embedding (0.64). The goal for Circle-RoPE is to achieve PTD = 0 while preserving intra-image structure.

#### 3.2. Circular Image Token Index Projection (CIP)

CIP is designed to transform 2D image token coordinates into a 3D "cone-like" geometry. This geometry ensures that each text token is equidistant to all image tokens (satisfying PTD = 0) while preserving relative spatial relationships among image tokens. CIP involves three steps:

1. **Coordinate Centralization:**
 - The 2D width-height coordinates `(x_ij, y_ij)` of image tokens are first centered. The geometric center `P_center` of all image token indices is calculated, and this center is subtracted from each coordinate to yield `C' = {(x'_ij, y'_ij)}`. This translation preserves intra-image relative offsets and provides a standardized origin.

2. **Mixed-Angle Annular Mapping:**
 - The centered 2D coordinates `C'` are transformed into polar coordinates and projected onto a 2D annulus. The angular position `θ_mix` for each token is a weighted average of two angles:
 - **Spatial-Origin Angle (θ_SA):** Derived from `atan2(y'_ij, x'_ij)`, normalized to `[0, 2π)`. This angle directly reflects the spatial layout relative to the image center.
 - **Grid-Index Angle (θ_GA):**  Generated by flattening the `H × W` grid into a 1D sequence and assigning uniformly spaced angles `(k/N) × 2π`. This ensures angular separation and regularizes the annular layout, preventing "angular collapse" where tokens on similar rays might have nearly identical SA angles.
 - The final mixed angle is `θ_mix = α · θ_SA + (1 − α) · θ_GA`, where `α ∈` balances spatial structure preservation and positional uniqueness.
 - The radius `R` of the annulus can be fixed or automatically scaled based on the spread of centered coordinates (e.g., `k × max ||(x', y')||_2`).
 - This step produces 2D annular coordinates `(x_circ, y_circ)` which are then expanded to 3D by setting the third coordinate to zero, forming `C_circ = {(x_circ_ij, y_circ_ij, 0)}`.

3. **Target Plane Rotation:**
 - The 2D annulus `C_circ` is rotated into a 3D plane whose normal vector `n` is aligned with the text-index direction `V_text`.
 - An orthonormal basis `{u, v, n}` is constructed, where `n` is the normalized `V_text`. `u` is chosen orthogonal to `n`, and `v = n × u`.
 - Each point `P_circ_ij` on the 2D annulus is then projected onto this target plane using `P_proj_ij = x_circ_ij u + y_circ_ij v`.
 - This rotation ensures that for any fixed text token, the Euclidean distance to all image tokens on the annulus is constant, thus achieving PTD = 0 and eliminating geometry-dependent positional preference in cross-modal attention.

#### 3.3. Alternating Geometry Encoding (AGE)

AGE addresses the complementary nature of different geometric priors. While Circle-RoPE ensures cross-modal disentanglement, it might relax the strict 2D locality of grid-based visual interactions inherent in standard RoPE (M-RoPE).
- **Approach:** AGE implements a layer-wise schedule `s(ℓ)` that alternates between applying Circle-RoPE and M-RoPE across different Transformer layers `ℓ`.
- **Rationale:** This alternation allows Circle-RoPE layers to facilitate unbiased semantic grounding (text-to-image), while M-RoPE layers reinforce structured visual extraction and fine-grained intra-image spatial awareness (image-to-image). AGE is hypothesized to act as a geometric regularizer, preventing overfitting to a single geometric view and fostering specialization across attention heads and layers.

#### 3.4. Encoding Temporal Order in Multi-Image Sequences (Appendix B.7)

For inputs containing multiple images, Circle-RoPE maintains its per-image PTD=0 property. However, the sequential order *between* images is explicitly preserved by translating each image's annular-encoding center along a fixed global axis. This distinguishes within-image patch preference from inter-image sequence order.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Circle-RoPE was conducted on multiple VLM backbones, primarily Qwen2.5-VL and LLaVA, across diverse multimodal benchmarks.

- **Comparison with Other Models and the Baseline (Table 2):**
 - Circle-RoPE achieved an average score of 58.46 across MMMU (val), MMMU-Pro (overall), MathVista (mini), AI2D, and RealWorldQA benchmarks. This represents a +1.00 improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL (SFT) baseline, which scored 57.46.
 - Notable improvements were observed in AI2D (+2.58) and MathVista (mini) (+1.00), suggesting enhanced spatial grounding and visual reasoning capabilities.

- **Ablation on Circular Mapping (CIP) (Table 4):**
 - Ablation studies on the angle mixing parameter `α` and radius `R` for CIP indicated optimal performance with `α = 0.5` and `R = 10`. This configuration balanced the preservation of spatial structure (`θ_SA`) with the enhancement of positional uniqueness (`θ_GA`).

- **PTD = 0 Requires Preserved Image Geometry (Table 5):**
 - A comparison between two PTD=0 strategies—Unordered embedding (collapsing image tokens to a single index) and Circle-RoPE (preserving geometry on an annulus)—demonstrated that merely achieving PTD=0 is insufficient. Unordered embedding resulted in a lower average score (54.67) compared to the M-RoPE baseline (56.76) and Circle-RoPE (57.76). This finding emphasizes that preserving intra-image spatial structure, which Circle-RoPE does through its annular layout, is crucial for effective multimodal reasoning.

- **Ablation on Alternating Geometry Encoding (AGE) (Table 3):**
 - Experiments on different AGE configurations showed that alternating Circle-RoPE and M-RoPE layers (the AGE strategy) yielded the most robust performance (Avg 58.46) across benchmarks. This supports the hypothesis that combining complementary geometric priors—decoupled cross-modal reasoning from Circle-RoPE and grid-based local spatial priors from M-RoPE—leads to more balanced and effective multimodal representations.

- **Diagnostics: Spatial Grounding and Decoupling (TAM benchmark, Table 6):**
 - On the TAM benchmark, Circle-RoPE exhibited a slight improvement in Object-IoU (26.23 vs. 26.15 for baseline), indicating preserved spatial grounding.
 - More significantly, Circle-RoPE achieved a substantial gain in Function-IoU (+3.45; 74.64 vs. 71.19 for baseline), suggesting improved decoupling capability and better suppression of irrelevant visual regions in attention.
 - Attention map visualizations qualitatively supported these findings, illustrating that Circle-RoPE focuses attention more effectively on question-relevant image regions.

- **Generalizability to Other Architectures (Table 7):**
 - An ablation study on the LLaVA-OneVision-Qwen2-0.5B model confirmed the generalizability of Circle-RoPE. It outperformed both the original 1D-RoPE LLaVA baseline (26.95 Avg Score) and an M-RoPE variant (26.93 Avg Score), achieving an average score of 27.36. This occurred without architecture-specific hyperparameter tuning, indicating broad applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the design of positional encodings for Vision-Language Models. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Principled Solution to Cross-Modal Bias:** The work rigorously identifies and quantifies the "spurious cross-modal positional bias" inherent in existing RoPE-based VLM architectures. By introducing the Per-Token Distance (PTD) metric and formally demonstrating that PTD = 0 is a sufficient condition to eliminate geometric attention bias, the paper provides a theoretical foundation for designing truly decoupled multimodal positional embeddings.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Understanding:** Circle-RoPE consistently improves performance on diverse multimodal benchmarks, particularly in tasks requiring precise spatial grounding and visual reasoning. This suggests that by making cross-modal attention more semantically driven and less influenced by artificial positional proximity, VLMs can achieve a deeper and more accurate understanding of visual-textual relationships.
- **Improved Robustness and Interpretability:** The decoupling mechanism implemented by Circle-RoPE can lead to more robust VLMs that are less sensitive to variations in image flattening or concatenation strategies. The diagnostic results, particularly the improved Func-IoU on the TAM benchmark and qualitative attention map visualizations, suggest enhanced interpretability, as attention becomes more focused on truly relevant regions.
- **Practical Applicability:** Circle-RoPE is designed to be integrated into existing RoPE-based VLM architectures with minimal changes to the overall training recipe, primarily involving only the positional encoding module. Its generalizability across different VLM backbones (e.g., Qwen-VL and LLaVA) further underscores its practical utility and potential for widespread adoption.
- **Innovative Architectural Strategy (AGE):** The introduction of Alternating Geometry Encoding (AGE) offers a novel strategy for synergizing different inductive biases. By strategically alternating between a decoupled geometry (Circle-RoPE) and a grid-based geometry (M-RoPE) across layers, AGE provides a mechanism to reconcile conflicting requirements—unbiased cross-modal alignment and fine-grained intra-image locality—without compromising either. This approach could inform future architectural designs for integrating diverse priors in deep learning.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The theoretical framework and the initial success of Circle-RoPE open avenues for future research. This includes extending the method to higher-dimensional inputs like videos, optimizing the AGE schedules for specific tasks, and exploring other geometries that might achieve similar decoupling properties.
