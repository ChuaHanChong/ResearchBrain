# ContactExplorer: Contact Coverage-Guided Exploration for General-Purpose Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ContactExplorer introduces a general method for dexterous manipulation exploration, utilizing a contact coverage-guided intrinsic reward system with dual post-contact novelty and pre-contact reaching signals. The approach, which also incorporates object-state conditioned learning, achieves an average 86% success rate across seven diverse tasks, enhancing sample efficiency and enabling robust sim-to-real transfer to physical hardware.

## Problem

- Existing intrinsic motivation methods (e.g., distance or force-based novelty) are often unstable, noisy, or inefficient for complex, multi-finger dexterous manipulation tasks due to sparse and discontinuous contacts.
- Many dexterous manipulation methods rely on significant human engineering or task-specific prior knowledge (like hand-crafted initializations), limiting their generality and autonomy.
- Reinforcement learning in dexterous manipulation struggles with sparse external rewards and the challenge of discovering effective interaction strategies in high-dimensional action spaces.

## Method

- The method represents objects with ==clustered surface regions== and hands with keypoints to define specific finger-to-region contact patterns.
- A ==learned hashing mechanism== discretizes continuous object states into clusters, allowing exploration statistics to be conditioned on the object's configuration and preventing counter saturation.
- A ==dual exploration reward system== combines a ==sparse post-contact coverage reward== for novel finger-region interactions with a ==dense, continuous energy-based reaching reward== that guides the hand towards under-explored regions before physical contact.

## Results

- ContactExplorer achieved an average success rate of **86%** across seven diverse dexterous manipulation tasks, outperforming existing intrinsic motivation baselines (which ranged **54-58%**) and uniquely solving the 'Constrained Object Retrieval' task with **88%** success where baselines scored **0%**.
- It demonstrated improved sample efficiency, reaching **70%** success with **2 to 3 times** fewer steps than baselines, and consistently exceeding **80%** success within **3-9 million** steps on challenging tasks.
- The approach facilitated robust sim-to-real transfer, with a distilled policy achieving **76.7%** singulation success and **33.3%** grasping success on a real uFactory xArm with a LEAP Hand for 'Cluttered Object Singulation', significantly outperforming a task-reward-only baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incentivizing the discovery of diverse and novel contact patterns, defined by which fingers interact with which object regions, provides a more stable and directly relevant exploration signal for dexterous manipulation.
> - Conditioning exploration statistics on object states via learned clustering ensures that novelty is evaluated in context, preventing saturation and promoting the effective reuse of contact knowledge across different object configurations.
> - Combining a sparse reward for observed novel contacts with a dense, continuous guidance signal towards potentially novel contact regions effectively addresses the inherent sparsity of rewards in complex manipulation tasks, leading to structured and efficient exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes in various domains, including Atari games, navigation, and locomotion. In these areas, exploration is frequently guided by novelty signals derived from states or dynamics. However, dexterous manipulation presents distinct challenges for exploration compared to these domains.

Prior methods for RL exploration in manipulation typically fall into several categories:
- **Distance-based novelty**: Approaches like RND-Dist use hand–object distance as a novelty signal. However, this is considered an indirect proxy for physical interaction and can be inefficient, as moving closer to an object does not guarantee meaningful contact or interaction.
- **Contact-force-based novelty**: Methods such as HaC have attempted to use contact-force prediction error to compute novelty, primarily for parallel grippers. For dexterous manipulation, however, the contacts are often sparse and highly discontinuous, leading to noisy and unstable force-based novelty signals. This makes them less reliable for complex, multi-finger interactions.
- **Task-specific priors**: Many dexterous manipulation methods rely on significant human engineering or prior knowledge to simplify the exploration problem. This includes carefully designed initializations, motion priors, or task-specific heuristics. While effective in specific scenarios, these methods often lack generality, requiring substantial human intervention and limiting their applicability across diverse tasks and objects.

ContactExplorer is positioned as a general exploration method designed to address these limitations. It seeks to provide a robust and efficient exploration signal that is directly relevant to physical hand-object interactions in dexterous manipulation. By focusing on explicit modeling and incentivizing diverse contact patterns, it aims to reduce the dependence on task-specific priors and provide more stable and informative novelty signals than previous contact or distance-based methods. This work contributes to advancing general-purpose dexterous manipulation by enabling RL agents to autonomously discover complex interaction strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general exploration method, named ContactExplorer, for dexterous manipulation tasks that can overcome the challenges posed by sparse rewards and the complexities of hand-object interactions. The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the instability and inefficiency of existing exploration signals**: Existing intrinsic motivation methods often prove inadequate for dexterous manipulation. Force-based novelty signals are prone to noise due to the discontinuous nature of contacts in multi-finger manipulation. Distance-based signals are an indirect measure of interaction and may lead to inefficient exploration of free space rather than purposeful physical engagement. ContactExplorer aims to provide a stable, direct, and efficient signal based on relevant physical interactions.
2. **Reduce reliance on task-specific priors**: A significant limitation of current dexterous manipulation research is the common practice of incorporating task-specific knowledge, such as hand-crafted initializations, motion primitives, or specialized reward shaping. While these priors can accelerate learning for particular tasks, they compromise the generality and autonomy of the RL agent. A key motivation for ContactExplorer is to provide a task-agnostic exploration mechanism that reduces or eliminates the need for such domain-specific human intervention.
3. **Encourage discovery of diverse and novel contact patterns**: Dexterous manipulation fundamentally relies on sophisticated hand-object interactions. The research aims to explicitly incentivize the agent to explore and discover a wide variety of "contact patterns," defined as which fingers contact which regions of the object. This is motivated by the understanding that successful manipulation often requires precise and varied contact configurations, rather than just simple grasping.
4. **Provide continuous exploration guidance in sparse reward environments**: In tasks with sparse external rewards, policies struggle to learn effective behaviors without consistent guidance. While contact-based rewards are relevant, they are inherently sparse (only occurring upon contact). ContactExplorer is motivated to provide a dual approach: a post-contact reward for observed novel interactions and a pre-contact guidance signal that directs the hand towards potentially novel contact regions even before physical contact is established. This ensures more consistent and purposeful exploration throughout the learning process.
5. **Enable state-aware exploration**: The utility of a particular contact pattern can depend on the object's current configuration or the phase of the task. A previously explored contact on one side of an object might still be novel and useful if the object's orientation has changed. The paper aims to condition the exploration statistics on the object's state to prevent the "forgetting" of useful interactions in new contexts, thus promoting effective reuse and continued exploration across varying object states.

By addressing these points, ContactExplorer aims to establish a more principled and effective framework for general-purpose dexterous manipulation within reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ContactExplorer formulates dexterous manipulation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and employs Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for policy learning. Its methodology integrates three core components: object and hand representation, object state clustering via learned hashing, and a structured exploration reward mechanism derived from contact coverage.

**Object and Hand Representations:**
- **Object**: The object's surface is represented by `M` uniformly sampled surface points, each with a position (`p_m`) and normal (`n_m`). These points are grouped into `K` discrete surface regions (`k = ξ(m)`) using K-means clustering initialized by farthest-point sampling, considering both spatial proximity and normal similarity.
- **Hand**: The dexterous hand is represented by `F` fingers. Each finger is abstracted by a set of predefined surface keypoints and their corresponding normals, typically located on the palmar surfaces of the finger links. This sparse representation is considered sufficient for capturing interaction dynamics.

**Object State Clustering via Learned Hashing:**
To enable state-aware exploration and prevent the saturation of global visitation counters, ContactExplorer conditions its exploration statistics on the object's state.
- **Object State Definition**: The object state is defined as `s_obj = [s_cur, s_goal]`, representing the current and goal poses of the object as point clouds.
- **Discretization**: A learned hashing mechanism is used to partition the continuous `s_obj` space into discrete clusters. An autoencoder maps `s_obj` to a binary latent code `z`. This autoencoder is trained with a reconstruction loss and a binarization regularizer to encourage `z` to be near 0 or 1. The binarized `z` is then projected into a compact `H`-bit hash using SimHash, resulting in a discrete state index `s`. This autoencoder is trained jointly with the policy.
- **Purpose**: This approach allows ContactExplorer to maintain independent contact counters for semantically similar object states, ensuring exploration statistics are decoupled across different spatiotemporal contexts.

**Contact Coverage Counter:**
A contact coverage counter `C ∈ R^(S×F×K)` is maintained, where `C_s,f,k` records the frequency of contact between finger `f` and surface region `k` under state cluster `s`.
- **Contact Detection**: Contact is detected at the finger level. For each finger `f`, the closest keypoint `l_f` to any object surface point `m_f` is identified. A finger-level contact (`I_contact(f)`) is registered only if both geometric proximity (distance between `p_l_f` and `p_m_f` below a threshold `δ_dist`) and a sufficient physical contact force (`||F_l_f||^2` above `δ_force`) are met.
- **Counter Update**: When contact is registered, the counter `C_s,f,k_f` (where `k_f = ξ(m_f)`) is incremented by one. Counters are not reset during training, enabling a progressive shift from exploration to exploitation.

**Contact Coverage-Guided Exploration Rewards:**
ContactExplorer utilizes two complementary reward signals derived from the contact coverage statistics:

1. **Contact Coverage Reward (Post-Contact)**:
 - This sparse reward is given only when physical contact occurs, focusing exploration on interaction-relevant events.
 - For each finger `f` in contact at timestep `t`, a contact novelty score `S_contact(t)` is calculated. This score averages the novelty (`g(c) = 1/√c+1`) of the current finger-region contact (`C_s,f,k_f`) across all contacting fingers.
 - A progress-based shaping scheme is applied: `R_contact(t) = α * [S_contact(t) - S_max_contact]^+`. This rewards only improvements over the maximum contact novelty score previously achieved in the current episode, preventing repeated exploitation of already novel behaviors.

2. **Energy-Based Reaching Reward (Pre-Contact)**:
 - This dense, continuous reward guides the policy toward under-explored contact patterns even before contact is established, addressing the sparsity of the contact coverage reward.
 - For each finger `f`, a contact energy `Φ_f` is defined. This energy sums over all object surface points `m`, weighting them by their novelty (`g(C_s,f,ξ(m))`), an exponential spatial decay based on the distance between the finger keypoint `p_l_f` and `p_m`, a directional term (`w_dir_l_f,m`) that prefers physically feasible approach directions (e.g., palm-facing and not back-facing the object), and an occlusion mask (`w_occ_l_f,m`) to ignore unreachable points.
 - The overall energy-based exploration score `S_energy(t)` is the average `Φ_f` across all fingers.
 - Similar to the contact reward, episodic progress-based shaping is applied: `R_energy(t) = β * [S_energy(t) - S_max_energy]^+`, rewarding progress in reaching under-explored regions.

The total reward `R(t)` for policy optimization is the sum of the task-specific reward `R_task(t)` and these two exploration rewards (`R_contact` and `R_energy`). This integrated approach provides both targeted contact-focused exploration and continuous guidance, leading to structured and efficient learning of dexterous manipulation policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates ContactExplorer across a diverse suite of seven dexterous manipulation tasks in simulation and further validates its transferability in real-world environments. The findings are summarized as follows:

#### 4.1. Comparison with Intrinsic Exploration Baselines (Q1)

ContactExplorer was benchmarked against four intrinsic motivation baselines: TR (task reward only), LHCC (learned-hash-code count-based), HaC (haptic prediction error), and RND-Dist (random network distillation to hand–object distance).
- **Overall Performance**: ContactExplorer achieved an average success rate of 86% across all tasks, significantly outperforming baselines which ranged from 54% to 58%. It also demonstrated lower variance in its performance.
- **Hard Exploration Tasks**: Notably, ContactExplorer was the only method to successfully solve the "Constrained Object Retrieval" task, achieving an 88% success rate, whereas all baselines scored 0%. This highlights its effectiveness in scenarios requiring complex, contact-rich interactions.
- **Sample Efficiency**: ContactExplorer exhibited improved sample efficiency. It consistently reached 70% success with 2 to 3 times fewer steps than intrinsic-reward baselines and exceeded 80% success within 3-9 million steps on challenging tasks. Baselines typically plateaued at lower success rates or required substantially more interactions.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Task-Specific Prior Knowledge (Q2)

The necessity of task-specific priors was evaluated on the "Constrained Object Retrieval" task.
- **Result**: The task-reward-only baseline (TR) failed with 0% success. A variant augmented with a carefully designed pre-contact hand initialization (TR-PrePose) achieved a 33% success rate. In contrast, ContactExplorer, without any task-specific initialization, achieved an 88% success rate and a 67% success rate at 40% of training (compared to 25% for TR-PrePose).
- **Conclusion**: These results indicate that ContactExplorer's autonomously discovered contact patterns can effectively replace manually designed priors, leading to superior performance and enhanced learning efficiency.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Q3)

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of ContactExplorer's key design components.
- **Reward Components**: On "Cluttered Object Singulation" and "Constrained Object Retrieval," the full ContactExplorer method (combining post-contact coverage reward `R_contact` and pre-contact energy reward `R_energy`) consistently outperformed variants using only one of the reward terms. This suggests that both signals are complementary and crucial for optimal performance. For instance, on Constrained Object Retrieval, using only `R_energy` or `R_contact` yielded 36% success, while the full method achieved 88%.
- **Object-State Conditioning**: A diagnostic "Box Push" task demonstrated that state-conditioned counters prevent exploration saturation. ContactExplorer achieved 100% success compared to 18% for a variant without object-state conditioning. This highlights the importance of maintaining decoupled exploration statistics across different object configurations.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: Experiments on reward scaling and state-clustering parameters (hash length `H` and regularization weight `λ`) showed that appropriate tuning is essential for balancing exploration strength, state discrimination, and training stability.
- **Keypoint Selection Robustness**: ContactExplorer maintained stable performance with only minor variations even when hand keypoints were perturbed from their default palmar positions to simulate low- or high-level noise. This suggests that the method benefits from the overall structure of contact coverage rather than relying on precise keypoint placement.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments (Q4)

Sim-to-real transfer was validated on a uFactory xArm with a 16-DoF LEAP Hand for "Cluttered Object Singulation" and "In-Hand Reorientation."
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: For "Cluttered Object Singulation," the student policy distilled from ContactExplorer's teacher policy achieved significantly higher singulation success (76.7% vs. 36.7% for TR) and grasping success (33.3% vs. 3.3% for TR) in real-world deployment. Open-loop trajectory replay for "In-Hand Reorientation" demonstrated consistent object pose changes in the real world compared to simulation, indicating effective transfer of learned manipulation behaviors.
- **Conclusion**: ContactExplorer facilitates robust sim-to-real transfer, enabling policies trained in simulation to perform effectively on physical hardware.

#### 4.5. Cross-Embodiment Experiments (Q5)

ContactExplorer was evaluated using an Allegro Hand on "Cluttered Object Singulation" and "Constrained Object Retrieval."
- **Result**: ContactExplorer consistently improved performance over all baselines on both tasks with the Allegro Hand. It achieved substantial gains in success rate for object retrieval and improved learning efficiency.
- **Conclusion**: The method demonstrates robustness to changes in dexterous hand embodiments, suggesting its underlying principles of contact exploration are generalizable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ContactExplorer introduces a general exploration framework that offers several significant contributions to the field of robotic dexterous manipulation.

### 6. Significance

1. **Principled General-Purpose Exploration**: ContactExplorer provides a principled, general, and task-agnostic exploration signal for dexterous manipulation. By explicitly modeling and incentivizing diverse hand-object contact patterns across object regions, it offers a more relevant and stable intrinsic motivation than previous approaches based on indirect state novelty or unstable force signals.
2. **Reduced Reliance on Task-Specific Priors**: The method significantly reduces the need for human-engineered heuristics, carefully designed initializations, or task-specific reward shaping. This autonomy represents a notable advancement towards more generalized and less human-intensive robotic learning.
3. **Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Robustness**: Through extensive quantitative evaluations across a diverse set of challenging manipulation tasks, ContactExplorer consistently demonstrates superior sample efficiency and higher final success rates compared to existing exploration baselines. This robustness is maintained across different objects and even different dexterous hand embodiments.
4. **Structured Exploration Guidance**: The dual reward mechanism, combining sparse post-contact rewards with dense pre-contact guidance, ensures that exploration is both focused on relevant physical interactions and continuously guided, even in free space before contact. This structured approach facilitates the efficient discovery of complex interaction strategies.
5. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The successful deployment of policies trained with ContactExplorer on real-world hardware for tasks like object singulation and reorientation indicates its practical applicability and effectiveness in bridging the simulation-to-reality gap, which is a critical challenge in robotics.

**Potential Impact:**
The development of ContactExplorer has the potential to substantially impact the field of robotic manipulation by:
- **Accelerating Development of Generalist Robots**: By enabling robots to autonomously learn a wider range of dexterous manipulation skills with less human intervention, it could accelerate the development of more versatile and capable robotic systems that can adapt to novel objects and tasks.
- **Broadening Applications of Dexterous Manipulation**: Its generality and robustness could expand the application domains for dexterous robots, including logistics, manufacturing, domestic assistance, and surgical procedures, where complex, contact-rich interactions are common.
- **Informing Future RL Exploration Research**: ContactExplorer's focus on structured physical interaction provides insights for future research into intrinsic motivation, particularly for embodied AI, where environmental interaction is paramount.

**Limitations:**
Despite its contributions, the paper identifies several limitations:
1. **Cyclic Object States**: The method may be less suitable for tasks with cyclic object states where identical object configurations recur, potentially requiring the same contact behaviors. While learned hashing might offer some distinction, it might not fully differentiate such scenarios.
2. **Limited Sensing Modalities**: The current exploration signal primarily relies on geometric contact coverage. Integrating additional sensing modalities, such as force-torque or tactile sensing, could provide richer information about contact quality and interaction dynamics, potentially leading to more sophisticated manipulation strategies.
3. **Simulation-Dominated Evaluation**: While sim-to-real transfer is demonstrated, the majority of the extensive evaluations are conducted in simulation. Accelerating reinforcement learning directly in the real world, rather than relying on simulation and distillation, remains an important direction for future research.

In conclusion, ContactExplorer represents a significant step towards enabling efficient, task-agnostic, and robust learning of general-purpose dexterous manipulation, fostering the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems.
