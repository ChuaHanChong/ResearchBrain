# ContactExplorer: Contact Coverage-Guided Exploration for General-Purpose Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ContactExplorer introduces a general method for dexterous manipulation exploration, utilizing a contact coverage-guided intrinsic reward system with dual post-contact novelty and pre-contact reaching signals. The approach, which also incorporates object-state conditioned learning, achieves an average 86% success rate across seven diverse tasks, enhancing sample efficiency and enabling robust sim-to-real transfer to physical hardware.

## Problem

- Existing intrinsic motivation methods (e.g., distance or force-based novelty) are often unstable, noisy, or inefficient for complex, multi-finger dexterous manipulation tasks due to sparse and discontinuous contacts.
- Many dexterous manipulation methods rely on significant human engineering or task-specific prior knowledge (like hand-crafted initializations), limiting their generality and autonomy.
- Reinforcement learning in dexterous manipulation struggles with sparse external rewards and the challenge of discovering effective interaction strategies in high-dimensional action spaces.

## Method

- The method represents objects with ==clustered surface regions== and hands with keypoints to define specific finger-to-region contact patterns.
- A ==learned hashing mechanism== discretizes continuous object states into clusters, allowing exploration statistics to be conditioned on the object's configuration and preventing counter saturation.
- A ==dual exploration reward system== combines a ==sparse post-contact coverage reward== for novel finger-region interactions with a ==dense, continuous energy-based reaching reward== that guides the hand towards under-explored regions before physical contact.

## Results

- ContactExplorer achieved an average success rate of **86%** across seven diverse dexterous manipulation tasks, outperforming existing intrinsic motivation baselines (which ranged **54-58%**) and uniquely solving the 'Constrained Object Retrieval' task with **88%** success where baselines scored **0%**.
- It demonstrated improved sample efficiency, reaching **70%** success with **2 to 3 times** fewer steps than baselines, and consistently exceeding **80%** success within **3-9 million** steps on challenging tasks.
- The approach facilitated robust sim-to-real transfer, with a distilled policy achieving **76.7%** singulation success and **33.3%** grasping success on a real uFactory xArm with a LEAP Hand for 'Cluttered Object Singulation', significantly outperforming a task-reward-only baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incentivizing the discovery of diverse and novel contact patterns, defined by which fingers interact with which object regions, provides a more stable and directly relevant exploration signal for dexterous manipulation.
> - Conditioning exploration statistics on object states via learned clustering ensures that novelty is evaluated in context, preventing saturation and promoting the effective reuse of contact knowledge across different object configurations.
> - Combining a sparse reward for observed novel contacts with a dense, continuous guidance signal towards potentially novel contact regions effectively addresses the inherent sparsity of rewards in complex manipulation tasks, leading to structured and efficient exploration.
