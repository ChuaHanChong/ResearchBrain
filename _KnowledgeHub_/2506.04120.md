# Splatting Physical Scenes: End-to-End Real-to-Sim from Imperfect Robot Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind researchers developed Splatting Physical Scenes, an end-to-end differentiable framework that reconstructs accurate 3D object geometry and appearance from imperfect robot data by jointly optimizing explicit meshes with 3D Gaussians, robot poses, and camera parameters. This system generates simulation-ready assets for robot learning, achieving robust performance in both geometry reconstruction and novel-view synthesis.

## Problem

- Real-world robot data is inherently imperfect, suffering from occlusions, noisy camera pose estimates, and dynamic scene elements, hindering robust scene reconstruction.
- Existing photorealistic rendering techniques (e.g., NeRF, 3DGS) are sensitive to these data imperfections and produce representations not directly compatible with physics simulators, making digital twin creation challenging.
- Current pipelines often separate visual appearance reconstruction from physical geometry extraction, leading to labor-intensive post-processing and limiting automated high-fidelity digital twin generation.

## Method

- **Splatting Physical Scenes** introduces ==SplatMesh==, a hybrid scene representation combining explicit ==triangle meshes== for geometry with ==3D Gaussians== for appearance, where Gaussians are constrained to lie on the mesh surface.
- Develops an end-to-end ==differentiable optimization== pipeline that leverages ==differentiable rendering (3DGS rasterization)== and ==differentiable physics (MuJoCo MJX)== for efficient gradient propagation.
- Jointly optimizes mesh vertices, Gaussian parameters, camera extrinsic parameters, and robot joint angles directly from raw RGB sequences and imperfect robot data.

## Results

- Achieved superior geometry reconstruction on YCB objects, with a Chamfer Distance of **0.073 mm²** in simulation and **3.07-7.35 mm** on real-world ALOHA 2 robot data.
- Demonstrated high-quality novel-view synthesis with PSNR **30.91** (simulation) and **21.62-25.45 dB** (real-world), outperforming NeRFacto and 3DGS on most metrics.
- Successfully recovered and calibrated object geometry, appearance, and camera/robot poses from noisy real-world monocular robot trajectories, significantly reducing geometric errors (e.g., from **18.92 mm** to **7.35 mm** CD) compared to using nominal camera parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining explicit mesh geometry with surface-aligned 3D Gaussians provides a robust representation (SplatMesh) that captures both photorealistic appearance and simulation-ready physical geometry.
> - An end-to-end differentiable framework, integrating visual rendering and physical simulation, enables visual feedback to directly inform and refine physical parameters like robot and camera poses, even from noisy real-world data.
> - Decoupling visual and geometric information, while enforcing surface alignment, offers flexibility in optimization and improves photometric quality by mitigating rendering artifacts such as 'floaters'.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of accurate physical simulations directly from real-world robot interactions is a recognized area of research critical for advancing scalable and affordable robot learning. Bridging the "sim-to-real" gap, where policies learned in simulation must transfer effectively to physical robots, remains a significant challenge, particularly when dealing with imperfect real-world data.

Recent advancements in novel-view synthesis, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have demonstrated capabilities in generating photorealistic representations of scenes. However, their application in dynamic robotic environments is often limited. These methods typically require precise camera poses, which are frequently noisy or unavailable in real robot trajectories, and are sensitive to dynamic scene elements. Furthermore, the volumetric or explicit Gaussian representations produced by these rendering techniques are not directly suitable for physics simulators like MuJoCo, often necessitating extensive manual post-processing or separate geometry estimation pipelines to extract usable simulation assets. This disconnect between visual reconstruction and physical properties hinders the automated creation of high-fidelity digital twins for robot learning and planning.

The existing literature includes efforts to integrate radiance fields with robotics. Some works focus on learning 3DGS representations of robot arms from CAD models or posed photographs to enable rendering articulated robot poses, which can then be used for tasks like trajectory tracking or sim-to-real policy learning. However, these often require separate data collection, pre-existing robot models, or do not offer a unified approach to handle novel object geometry, dynamic scenes, and imperfect robot data simultaneously. Approaches for object-based scene decomposition, utilizing instance segmentation models (e.g., Detic, SAM), enable the fitting of local radiance fields to individual objects but introduce additional processing steps and potential segmentation inaccuracies.

This research addresses the identified gap by proposing a unified framework that overcomes these limitations. It aims to jointly optimize scene appearance, extract object geometry suitable for physics simulation, and calibrate robot and camera parameters directly from noisy, dynamic robot trajectory data. By integrating differentiable rendering with differentiable physics within a single, end-to-end optimization pipeline, the work seeks to eliminate separate processing steps and leverage visual feedback to ground physical reconstruction, thereby advancing the automation of real-to-sim pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to create accurate, physically realistic simulations directly from real-world robot motion, which is crucial for fostering safe, scalable, and affordable robot learning. The authors identify several significant challenges that motivate their proposed framework:

1. **Challenges with Real Robot Data:** Real-world robot data inherently suffers from various imperfections, including occlusions, noisy camera pose estimates (due to factors like timing, backlash, or imperfect encoder calibration), and the presence of dynamic scene elements introduced by robot movement. These issues hinder the robust reconstruction of scenes.
2. **Difficulty in Creating Digital Twins:** Generating geometrically accurate and photorealistic digital twins of novel or unseen objects directly from such imperfect data is exceptionally challenging. Existing photorealistic rendering techniques (e.g., NeRF, 3DGS) are sensitive to these data imperfections and often produce representations that are not readily compatible with physics simulators.
3. **Disconnection Between Visual and Physical Representations:** Current pipelines typically involve separate processes for visual appearance reconstruction and physical geometry extraction. This often leads to labor-intensive post-processing or the use of distinct geometry estimation methods, which breaks the direct link between visual input and the physical behavior required for simulation. This disconnect limits the automated generation of high-fidelity digital twins for critical applications like robot learning and planning.
4. **Need for Joint Optimization:** The authors argue that overcoming these challenges requires a paradigm shift towards a unified, end-to-end differentiable framework. Such a framework should be capable of simultaneously tackling scene appearance reconstruction, explicit object geometry extraction, and robot/camera calibration. This joint approach is posited to allow visual discrepancies to directly inform and refine physical properties, even with imperfect robot platforms.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Develop an End-to-End Real-to-Sim Pipeline:** Construct a fully differentiable framework that can jointly optimize scene appearance, object geometry, robot poses, and camera parameters, directly leveraging raw RGB sequences from robot sensors.
- **Enable Scene Reconstruction from Imperfect Data:** Demonstrate the capability to reconstruct dynamic robot scenes, including novel objects, from noisy monocular trajectories captured by real, low-cost robots, without requiring extensive manual calibration or ideal data conditions.
- **Generate Simulation-Ready Assets:** Produce controllable object meshes that are directly suitable for integration into physics engines, with their reconstruction driven by visual consistency within the end-to-end framework.

The overall goal is to streamline the creation of high-fidelity digital twins that are both visually accurate and physically plausible, thereby enhancing the practicality and robustness of real-to-simulation pipelines for robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named Splatting Physical Scenes, introduces an end-to-end optimization pipeline designed to derive accurate physical simulations directly from imperfect real-world robot data. The core of the approach involves a novel hybrid scene representation and a unified optimization strategy leveraging differentiable rendering and differentiable physics.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The framework operates by collecting real RGB images and robot states from recorded robot trajectories. It then constructs a model of the scene using a novel scene representation that enables end-to-end optimization of all represented elements. This optimization is performed using differentiable physics simulation (MuJoCo MJX) and differentiable rendering, driven by the discrepancy between predicted and observed data.

#### 3.2. Scene Representation: SplatMesh

The central innovation in scene representation is SplatMesh, a hybrid model combining a triangle mesh for explicit geometry with 3D Gaussians for appearance.
- **Geometry:** Scene objects are represented by a triangle mesh, defined by its vertices and faces. The optimization process deforms these vertices while preserving the underlying mesh connectivity, ensuring a consistent topology and controllable complexity. This explicit geometric representation facilitates the incorporation of mesh regularization terms (e.g., Laplacian smoothing) and provides a direct asset for physics simulation.
- **Appearance:** The visual appearance is captured by 3D Gaussians. These Gaussians are constrained to lie on the surface of the mesh faces, and their orientations transform in tandem with the movement of the underlying mesh. Each Gaussian is parameterized by its mean (μ), which is initialized using weighted barycentric coordinates on the mesh faces, its covariance (Σ), view-dependent spherical harmonics coefficients for color, and an opacity term (o).
- **Decoupling:** By decoupling visual information (Gaussians) from geometric information (mesh), the framework gains flexibility to optimize appearance, pose, or shape of scene elements, or to keep them fixed.
- **Surface Alignment (Surfel Constraint):** A critical aspect is the constraint applied to the 3D Gaussians to align with the mesh surface. The Gaussian covariance is constrained to be axis-aligned with the mesh face normal, with the scale along the normal direction clamped to a tiny constant. This encourages Gaussians to represent surface patches rather than volumetric elements, which contributes to higher photometric quality and improved generalization.

#### 3.3. End-to-End Optimization

The framework employs an iterative optimization pipeline that refines the SplatMesh representation by minimizing the photometric error between rendered and ground-truth images. This process simultaneously optimizes both the mesh geometry and the 3D Gaussian appearance.
- **Differentiable Rendering:** The pipeline utilizes the differentiable rasterization from 3D Gaussian Splatting, specifically a custom CUDA kernel, to enable efficient forward and backward passes. This allows gradients from pixel-level losses to propagate back to all parameters of the 3D Gaussians (position, covariance, SH coefficients, opacity) and, crucially, to the underlying mesh vertex positions.
- **Differentiable Physics:** Integration of MuJoCo MJX, a JAX-native version of the MuJoCo physics simulator, allows for gradient computation through the physics state. This enables optimization of kinematic parameters, such as robot joint angles and camera poses, based on visual feedback.
- **Optimization Objectives:** The total loss function is a weighted sum of several components:
 - **Photometric Losses:** L1 or L2 losses between predicted and ground truth RGB pixel values, supervising the 3D Gaussians.
 - **Geometric Regularization:** Laplacian regularization (L_LL) to promote smooth mesh surfaces and other geometric regularizers (e.g., average edge length loss), or consistency with estimated surface normals from other models.
 - **Object Segmentation:** An L2 silhouette masking loss, comparing the predicted silhouette (obtained from the 3D Gaussian rasterizer adapted for object identity) against a ground truth object mask. To ensure gradient propagation in non-overlapping regions, the binary mask is smoothed using a Euclidean Distance Transform.
- **Parameters Optimized:** The framework jointly refines mesh vertices, Gaussian parameters, camera extrinsic parameters, and robot joint angles.
- **Real-World Context:** The methodology is specifically tested on the low-cost ALOHA 2 bi-manual manipulator. This platform, equipped with four RGB cameras and proprioceptive joint angle sensors, introduces challenges such as noisy camera position estimates and dynamic scenes, which conventional methods like COLMAP often fail to handle effectively. The framework explicitly addresses these constraints by learning from monocular RGB data and leveraging an imperfect initial robot model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed Splatting Physical Scenes framework was evaluated through experiments in both simulated and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (YCB Objects)

Using a synthetic dataset of 64 YCB objects with 50 posed images each, the framework demonstrated capabilities in geometry reconstruction and novel-view synthesis.
- **Geometry Reconstruction:** The SplatMesh-based reconstruction achieved a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.073 mm². Ablation studies showed that removing Laplacian mesh regularization increased geometric error (CD = 0.237 mm²) due to high-frequency artifacts, and without the surface-aligned Gaussians (surfels) constraint, CD increased to 0.122 mm². Direct comparison with NeRFacto's Poisson surface reconstruction was not feasible due to significant artifacts (floaters, background reconstruction) in the meshes recovered by NeRFacto.
- **Novel-View Synthesis:** The method yielded a PSNR of 30.91, SSIM of 0.970, and LPIPS of 0.044 after 15,000 iterations. This performance surpassed NeRFacto (PSNR 30.29, SSIM 0.961, LPIPS 0.057) and 3DGS (PSNR 26.97, SSIM 0.972, LPIPS 0.083) on most metrics. The authors attribute the higher photometric quality to the constraint of Gaussians to desired surfaces, which mitigates "floaters" common in volumetric NeRF models. Ablations confirmed the positive influence of mesh regularization terms (PSNR 30.91 with vs. 30.70 without) and the importance of surfel constraints (PSNR 25.82 without) for preventing unconstrained Gaussian expansion and improving generalization. The method also completed reconstruction within a fixed budget of 15,000 iterations (approximately 3-4 minutes), indicating a streamlined optimization process compared to the iterative heuristic procedures of 3DGS.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments (ALOHA 2 Robot)

The framework was tested on a novel dataset collected with an ALOHA 2 bi-manual manipulator, involving 6 observation trajectories (around 800 frames) of six different YCB objects.
- **Object Recovery:** The framework successfully reconstructed the geometry and appearance of six YCB objects from real robot trajectory data. The optimization involved mesh vertices, Gaussian parameters, and camera extrinsic rotation parameters over 40,000 steps. Mean Chamfer Distance values ranged from 3.07 mm to 7.35 mm for the reconstructed objects. Novel view synthesis on held-out views achieved PSNR values between 21.62 dB and 25.45 dB.
- **Impact of Calibration:** An ablation study, "Proprio-only," where camera extrinsics were frozen at nominal values, resulted in significantly higher geometric errors (CD values up to 18.92 mm) and lower PSNR (up to 20.17 dB). This demonstrates that for low-cost robot platforms, joint optimization and calibration of camera poses are necessary for effective reconstruction.
- **Comparison to TRELLIS:** When compared to TRELLIS, a pre-trained 3D foundation model for single/few-view reconstruction, the proposed method generally achieved superior geometry reconstruction. While TRELLIS sometimes produced high-quality shapes, it exhibited issues on the robot dataset, such as anisotropic scale distortions, spurious geometry (e.g., additional ground planes), or failure to capture the 3D structure of simple shapes. The proposed method also provides metrically accurate posed objects within the robot workspace, unlike TRELLIS which does not predict metric scales or object poses directly.
- **Robot Calibration (Sim-to-Sim):** An additional simulation-to-simulation experiment validated the calibration capability. By adding Gaussian noise to ground truth joint angles and optimizing them visually, the framework consistently reduced the mean tool center point (TCP) positional error across different noise levels (e.g., from an initial 32.6 mm error at 0.03 radians noise to a final 18.5 mm error). This indicates effective refinement of noisy robot and camera poses through visual feedback.

#### 4.3. 3D Asset Generation

The framework extends to generating 3D objects from single images or text prompts. By leveraging CAT3D for multi-view generation from an input, followed by SplatMesh optimization and inverse rendering to bake texture maps onto the optimized mesh, the pipeline produced standard textured meshes compatible with physics simulators like MuJoCo.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses several long-standing challenges in robot learning and simulation, holding the potential for significant impact across various domains in robotics.

1. **Bridging the Real-to-Sim Gap:** The primary significance lies in providing an end-to-end, unified solution for bridging the visual and physical real-to-sim gap. By jointly optimizing scene appearance, explicit object geometry, and robot/camera calibration within a single differentiable framework, the work enables the automated creation of digital twins that are both visually photorealistic and physically accurate. This is crucial for improving the fidelity and relevance of simulated environments for robot training and evaluation.

2. **Robustness to Imperfect Data:** A key impact is the demonstrated ability to reconstruct dynamic robot scenes and novel objects from noisy, imperfect data collected by low-cost robot platforms (e.g., ALOHA 2). This robustness reduces the stringent data collection requirements typically associated with high-fidelity reconstruction methods, making advanced robot learning techniques more accessible and practical for real-world deployments where perfect sensor data or extensive calibration is often unavailable.

3. **Automated Digital Twin Generation:** The framework automates the generation of simulation-ready assets, including object meshes, directly from raw robot interaction data. This eliminates the need for laborious manual annotation, CAD modeling, or separate geometry extraction pipelines, which are typically time-consuming and expensive. Such automation is vital for scaling up the creation of diverse and realistic simulation environments, accelerating the development cycles for new robot skills and applications.

4. **Enhanced Robot Learning and Planning:** By providing accurate simulations derived from real-world interactions, the framework can facilitate more effective robot learning. Policies trained in these higher-fidelity digital twins are likely to exhibit better sim-to-real transfer capabilities, leading to more robust and generalizable robot behaviors. Moreover, accurate scene models can enhance robot planning algorithms by providing precise information about object geometries, poses, and physical properties within the workspace.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The introduction of SplatMesh, a hybrid scene representation combining 3D Gaussian Splatting with explicit meshes, coupled with a fully differentiable pipeline, opens new avenues for research. This includes exploring more sophisticated model identification and scene estimation problems, integrating uncertainty-aware inference methods, enabling topology changes in mesh reconstruction, and extending the framework to handle deformable objects in physics simulators beyond current rigid-body limitations. The method's ability to leverage existing, imperfect robot models and refine them with real-world data is a powerful concept for ongoing development in robotics.

In summary, this work contributes to making high-fidelity robot simulation more practical, accessible, and scalable by providing a robust, end-to-end methodology for deriving digital twins directly from real-world robot data, even under challenging conditions.
