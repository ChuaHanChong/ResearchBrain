# ValueFormer: A Causal Transformer Value Function with Stage-Aware Labels for Semi-Autonomous Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ValueFormer presents a causal transformer value function that generates real-time, per-frame progress and mistake detection signals for semi-autonomous Vision-Language-Action policies. Employing a unique "stage-aware, success-then-decay" labeling scheme, the system demonstrated a validation MAE of 0.015 and enhanced task completion from 70% to 85% in on-robot evaluations.

## Problem

- Behavior cloning-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies fail silently without an intrinsic notion of task progress or real-time failure detection, hindering timely operator intervention.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) is often impractical for training value functions in real-robot settings due to high costs of experience and difficulty simulating complex interactions with deformable objects.
- Existing value estimation methods use sparse terminal labels or coarse/computationally expensive per-frame signals that are often incorrectly shaped, leading to miscalibrated predictions and noisy value curves.

## Method

- Developed **ValueFormer**, a compact ==causal transformer== (3.5M parameters) with a ==dual-head architecture== to output both a smooth Monte Carlo value (V_mc) for progress and a sharp binary value (V_bin) for mistake detection.
- Introduced a ==stage-aware, success-then-decay labeling scheme== for V_mc to provide dense, continuous, and semantically shaped per-frame progress signals that assign partial credit to successful prefixes of failed episodes.
- Leveraged explicit mistake intervals from manual annotations and ==human-in-the-loop intervention logs== to supervise V_bin, enabling scalable and accurate online mistake detection.

## Results

- ValueFormer achieved a validation Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of **0.015** and Mean Squared Error (MSE) of **3.0 × 10⁻⁴**, demonstrating a clear separation (ΔV of **0.537**) between successful and failed trajectories.
- On-robot A/B tests using critic-derived policy training weights increased task completion rates from **70%** to **85%** and entirely eliminated specific failure modes, such as the "repeat-pick" behavior.
- Inference optimizations, including batching and bf16 autocast, reduced the per-tick DINOv3 encoding cost by **3-5x** (from 266 ms to **52 ms**), allowing ValueFormer to run at 2 Hz without impacting policy performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The shape of per-frame labels is crucial for value function learning, with the "stage-aware, success-then-decay" scheme effectively avoiding miscalibration on successful segments of failed trajectories.
> - Integrating human-in-the-loop intervention data significantly improves mistake detection, increasing average precision from 0.38 to 0.82 and detection before operator takeover from 35% to 95%.
> - A compact causal transformer with a dual-head output can efficiently provide both continuous progress tracking and sharp mistake detection in real-time without significant computational overhead, enabling semi-autonomous operation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the rapidly advancing field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for robotics, particularly for complex, unstructured environments like restaurant settings. Modern VLA models, such as those from the π₀ family, OpenVLA, RDT-1B, and GigaBrain, have significantly streamlined robot control from demonstrations. However, a notable limitation of these behavior cloning-based policies is their inability to self-monitor progress or detect failures in real-time, often failing silently.

Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) would naturally provide a value function to track progress, but it is often impractical for real-world robotic tasks involving costly experience and deformable objects (e.g., food), which are difficult to simulate faithfully. Prior attempts to address this in behavior cloning settings often rely on sparse terminal success/failure labels or produce per-frame signals that are either too coarse, incorrectly shaped, or computationally expensive for real-time deployment.

ValueFormer contributes to this landscape by introducing a method to generate a dense, continuous, and semantically shaped per-frame value signal, alongside a sharp binary failure detection signal, for semi-autonomous VLA systems. It distinguishes itself from prior work in value/critic learning for behavior cloning by explicitly modeling failure timing and partial credit through "stage-aware" labels, and by being policy-agnostic, meaning it can operate alongside existing frozen VLA policies without retraining them. Compared to existing failure detection systems, ValueFormer provides a continuous value rather than just binary flags, allowing for the detection of progressive degradation or recovery. It also offers a numerical progress signal that is often absent in higher-level hierarchical or semi-autonomous control systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust, lightweight model capable of providing a real-time, per-frame progress signal for VLA policies, enabling semi-autonomous operation and timely operator intervention. This objective is motivated by several critical challenges inherent in deploying current VLA systems:

1. **Silent Failures in Behavior Cloning**: VLA policies trained via behavior cloning often lack an intrinsic notion of task progress. A robot encountering an issue, such as a missed grasp or a stalled action, may produce action streams indistinguishable from successful progress, leading to silent failures that are only detected post-hoc at the episode's conclusion. This absence of a "calibrated notion of progress" makes it difficult for operators overseeing multiple robots to determine when intervention is needed.
2. **Impracticality of RL for Value Function Learning**: While reinforcement learning naturally yields a value function, its direct application for training VLA policies in real-robot settings is often impractical due to high costs of real-robot experience, potential safety concerns, and the difficulty of accurately simulating complex physical interactions with deformable objects (e.g., food items in a sandwich assembly task).
3. **Limitations of Sparse or Mis-shaped Labels**:
 - **Sparsity**: The simplest feedback—a single terminal success/failure bit at the end of an episode—is too sparse to indicate *when* a rollout went wrong. This "reward-sparsity problem" prevents timely intervention or precise advantage estimation.
 - **Incorrect Label Shape**: Even dense, per-frame labels can be misleading if incorrectly shaped. A common naive approach of assigning zero value to all frames of a failed episode, regardless of partial progress, can incorrectly teach the model that early, successful observations are indicative of failure. This leads to miscalibrated predictions and noisy value curves in deployment.
4. **Absence of Fine-grained Failure Timing**: Existing rollout logs typically provide only episode-level success/failure flags and general human comments, insufficient for localizing failure points or segments in time, which is crucial for generating dense, meaningful supervision.
5. **Computational Overhead for Real-time Monitoring**: Any supplementary monitoring system must be computationally efficient to run alongside a VLA policy on shared hardware without degrading policy performance.

To address these challenges, the authors propose ValueFormer, aiming to provide a compact, policy-agnostic, causal transformer that, in a single forward pass, emits:
- A smooth Monte Carlo value (V_mc) for progress tracking and advantage estimation, designed to be dense, continuous, and "stage-aware."
- A sharp binary value (V_bin) for online mistake detection, using segment-based mistake intervals for supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ValueFormer's methodology encompasses three core aspects: sophisticated label generation, a compact causal transformer architecture, and a dual-head training objective.

**A. Stage-Aware, Success-then-Decay Labels**

The central contribution lies in the design of the per-frame scalar labels `v_k` ∈.

1. **Successful Episodes**: For successful episodes, labels follow a standard Monte Carlo (MC) return curve: `v_succ_k = γ^(N-1-k)`, where `N` is the number of sampled frames and `γ` (set to 0.99) is the discount factor. This ensures `v_N-1 = 1` at task completion and a monotonically rising value throughout. The exponential form is chosen over a linear one because it provides a non-constant advantage signal (`A_t = V_mc(t+H_A) - V_mc(t)`), which is more informative for distinguishing progress than a constant signal.
2. **Failed Episodes (v_mc)**: For failed episodes, a "stage-aware, success-then-decay" scheme is introduced. If `s_fail` stages were completed before failure (at frame `k_fail`), the label `v_fail_k` is defined as:
 - `v_succ_k` for `k ≤ k_fail`: The pre-failure prefix mirrors the success curve, acknowledging that observations up to the failure point are identical to those of a successful rollout. This avoids the "flat-zero" pathology where good early observations are incorrectly labeled as failures.
 - `v_succ_k_fail * γ^(k-k_fail)` for `k > k_fail`: After `k_fail`, the value decays smoothly with the same discount `γ`. This provides a continuous, differentiable target, avoids a sharp discontinuity ("cliff"), and implicitly assigns partial credit based on how many stages were completed.
3. **Sharp Binary Target (v_bin)**: A second, sharp per-frame target `v_bin` ∈ {0, 1} is generated from explicit mistake intervals `(t_start, t_end)`. `v_bin_k = 0` if `t_k` falls within any annotated mistake interval, and `1` otherwise. This allows supervision for transient mistakes that the policy might recover from (which `v_mc` handles via dips and recoveries) and provides an immediate, actionable signal for online mistake detection.

**B. Failure Timing and Segment-Based Annotation**

The labeling process relies on annotations beyond simple episode outcomes:
- **Manual Annotation**: Failed rollouts are manually annotated with a single failure time (`t_fail`) for the `v_mc` labels (parsed into `s_fail` stages) and with multiple `(t_start, t_end)` mistake segments for `v_bin` labels. This captures both terminally failed episodes and successful episodes containing recovered mistakes.
- **Intervention-Derived Segments**: To scale annotation, the system can leverage human-in-the-loop (HIL) teleoperation logs. When an operator intervenes, the `(t_start, t_end)` of the intervention segment is used as a mistake interval. Adjustments are made for operator reaction lag (shifting `v_bin` target earlier) and to down-weight `v_mc` labels during human control. These intervention labels augment, rather than replace, manual annotations.

**C. Architecture**

ValueFormer is a compact model with three main parts:
1. **Visual Frontend**: A shared, frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone processes six views per frame: three full camera images (top, left wrist, right wrist) and three corresponding Region-of-Interest (ROI) crops (top-gripper, left-wrist gripper, right-wrist gripper). Only the CLS token from each view is used, concatenated to a 6144-dimensional embedding per frame. Freezing the DINOv3 backbone ensures robust transferability of features and simplifies dataset processing.
2. **Transformer Body**: Frame embeddings (`z_t`), 14-dimensional joint state (`q_t`), and 2-dimensional time features (`φ_t`) are projected to `d_model` (256 dimensions) and combined by element-wise summation to balance modality contributions. A learnable 16-position embedding is added. A causal TransformerEncoder (2 layers, 4 heads, GELU, pre-norm, 8-second context window / 16 samples at 2 Hz) processes this sequence. The causal mask ensures predictions depend only on past and present frames, making it suitable for online inference.
3. **Output Heads**: Two MLP heads branch off the last-token representation:
 - **V_mc Head**: A 3-layer MLP followed by a sigmoid activation, providing the smooth progress value.
 - **V_bin Head**: A separate 2-layer MLP, also sigmoid-activated, providing the sharp binary mistake detection probability.

The model has approximately 3.5 million trainable parameters, making it significantly smaller than the VLA policies it monitors.

**D. Training Objective**

Both heads are trained jointly using binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss:
- `L_mc`: BCE loss for the smooth `v_mc` target, with optional weighting for flat-zero frames. BCE is preferred over MSE to avoid saturation near {0, 1} endpoints.
- `L_bin`: Weighted BCE loss for the sharp `v_bin` target, using a batch-wise positive weight (`α+`) to balance the class imbalance (typically 95% good states / 5% mistake states).
The total loss is `L = L_mc + β L_bin`, with `β = 1.0`. Optimization uses AdamW with a linear warm-up and cosine annealing learning rate schedule, a batch size of 256, and a balanced sampler over data source groups (expert-success, rollout-success, rollout-fail) to ensure failure data is sufficiently represented.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Quantitative Performance**
ValueFormer achieves a validation Mean Squared Error (MSE) of approximately 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ and a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.015. On held-out data, it shows a clear separation between successful and failed trajectories, with a mean predicted V of 0.537 on successes and 0.000 on failures, resulting in a separation (ΔV) of 0.537. Training completes in under three minutes on a single GPU.

**B. Qualitative Rollout Signatures**
The model robustly reproduces four canonical rollout signatures on unseen episodes:
1. **Clean Success**: Predicted `V(s)` closely tracks the smooth, rising MC label.
2. **Success-with-Retry**: `V(s)` dips during failed grasp attempts and recovers as the stage completes, providing early warning of transient issues.
3. **Early Collapse**: `V(s)` rises through initial successful stages, then peaks and decays as the policy makes a critical mistake.
4. **Stuck-Scratching**: `V(s)` remains low throughout the episode when the policy consistently fails to make progress.
Visual analysis indicates a lag of 5-10 seconds between the true failure event and its full reflection in `V(s)` for the 16-second causal window, reduced to roughly half for the 8-second window.

**C. Labeling-Scheme Ablation**
A controlled ablation comparing five different fail-episode label shapes demonstrated the critical impact of the chosen `MC-smooth` (stage-aware, success-then-decay) scheme. `MC-smooth` significantly outperformed alternatives in terms of validation BCE loss (0.00051 vs. 0.00178-0.00202) and rollout MAE (0.024 vs. 0.047-0.058).
While some alternative schemes (e.g., "outcome-scaled") showed higher `∆V` (separation), this was attributed to label artifacts such as "outcome leakage" (where the t=0 label depended on the final outcome, not learnable from observation) or hard discontinuities ("cliff") that caused high-frequency oscillations in predictions. The `MC-smooth` scheme was the only one to avoid outcome leakage at t=0, hard discontinuities at the failure point, and instead provide smoothly decaying partial credit after failure, aligning with physical intuition.

**D. Supervision from Human Interventions**
Incorporating intervention-derived labels from a Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) dataset alongside manual annotations substantially improved failure detection. On a held-out HIL set, average precision for mistake detection increased from 0.38 (manual only) to 0.82 (manual + intervention), and the percentage of mistakes detected before operator takeover rose from 35% to 95%, at a matched 5% false-positive rate. This gain was robust across various hyperparameter settings.

**E. Critic-Derived Policy-Training Weights**
The paper evaluates two critic-derived per-frame policy training weights (`vf-mask` and `vf-awr`) against a flag-only intervention-weighted regression (IWR) control in an on-robot A/B test for sandwich assembly. Both critic variants were applied to the same training data and retained the same high weight for human takeover frames.
- **Completion Rate**: Both `vf-mask` and `vf-awr` increased task completion from 70% (IWR control) to 85% (17/20 sandwiches), though this gain was within the statistical noise at n=20.
- **Failure Modes**: Catastrophic cells (near-zero subtask score) decreased from 7 to 2, and a specific "repeat-pick" failure mode was entirely eliminated (4 to 0 instances) under both critic-derived weights. This indicates improved subtask quality and robustness against behavioral failures.
- **Subtask Quality**: `vf-mask` particularly improved performance on harder subtasks like tomato and lettuce (from ~0.82 to ~0.95-0.97 mean score), while `vf-awr` achieved the highest overall mean subtask score (0.96) and demonstrated cleaner execution (92% perfect cells).

**F. Online Serving Cost and Inference Optimization**
Running ValueFormer live at 2 Hz alongside the VLA policy on a single GPU initially caused the policy's tail latency to exceed its 100 ms prefetch deadline, leading to visible hitches. The cost was dominated by sequential DINOv3 encoding of six views. Optimizations, including batching the six views into a single forward pass, keeping embeddings on-GPU, and using bf16 autocast, reduced the per-tick encoding cost 3-5× (from 266 ms to 52 ms). This optimization allows the critic to run at 2 Hz without significantly impacting policy performance. The bf16 encoder was validated to maintain detection performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work offers several significant contributions and implications for the development and deployment of semi-autonomous robotic systems:

1. **Enabling Semi-Autonomous VLA Policies**: ValueFormer provides a crucial missing component for VLA policies: a calibrated, real-time notion of task progress and failure. This capability transforms behavior-cloned VLA systems from "silent failures" into semi-autonomous agents that can communicate their state, enabling human operators to oversee multiple robots and intervene proactively.
2. **Addressing the Labeling Challenge in Robotics**: The paper highlights that for dense supervision, the *shape* of the label is more critical than the architecture. The "stage-aware, success-then-decay" labeling scheme for `V_mc` is a key innovation, providing a principled way to assign partial credit and avoid miscalibrating models on the successful prefixes of failed episodes. This approach is likely generalizable to other multi-stage long-horizon tasks.
3. **Dual-Head for Comprehensive Monitoring**: The dual-head architecture, providing both a smooth `V_mc` for progress tracking and a sharp `V_bin` for immediate mistake detection from a single forward pass, is a practical design pattern. This allows for complementary insights: `V_mc` captures gradual trends and recoveries, while `V_bin` provides actionable abort signals, optimizing for different operational needs without redundancy or significant additional computational cost.
4. **Practical Deployment and Efficiency**: ValueFormer's compact architecture (3.5M parameters) and the demonstrated inference optimizations (3-5x cost reduction to 52 ms per tick) ensure that it can run efficiently and in real-time on shared GPU hardware alongside a VLA policy. This addresses a critical barrier to deploying monitoring systems in resource-constrained robotic environments.
5. **Direct Policy Improvement**: The on-robot A/B test demonstrates that critic-derived per-frame training weights can directly improve the performance of the underlying VLA policy. The elimination of "repeat-pick" failure modes and the general improvement in subtask quality suggest that this approach can refine policy behavior by down-weighting problematic autonomous frames during training, thereby addressing specific behavioral deficiencies inherited from demonstration data. This represents a tangible step towards leveraging value functions for iterative policy refinement in behavior cloning settings.
6. **Scalable Annotation via Intervention Data**: The effective integration of human-in-the-loop intervention flags for generating `V_bin` labels provides a scalable and cost-effective method for acquiring dense supervision, crucial for fleet-scale operations where manual annotation is impractical.

In summary, ValueFormer offers a robust, efficient, and well-designed solution for real-time robotic self-assessment. Its technical innovations in labeling and dual-head design, combined with demonstrated improvements in both monitoring capabilities and policy performance, advance the state of the art in semi-autonomous VLA systems and lay foundational work for more intelligent and reliable robot deployments.
