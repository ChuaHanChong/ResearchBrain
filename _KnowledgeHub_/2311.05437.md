# LLaVA-Plus: Learning to Use Tools for Creating Multimodal Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaVA-Plus, developed by researchers from Tsinghua University and Microsoft Research, introduces an approach that trains a Large Multimodal Model (LMM) to intelligently leverage external vision and vision-language tools from a skill repository. It establishes new state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like VisiT-Bench and LLaVA-Bench (Tools), demonstrating improved visual understanding, content generation, and interactive capabilities.

## Problem

- End-to-end Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle to seamlessly integrate a wide range of specialized vision and vision-language skills like precise segmentation or advanced image generation.
- Existing tool-chaining methods, while flexible, heavily rely on brittle prompt engineering for tool selection and composition, lacking robustness and adaptability in dynamic real-world scenarios.
- Prior tool-augmented LLMs often lose active visual grounding during the interaction, only engaging the image when a specific multimodal tool is activated, which limits their planning and reasoning.

## Method

- Extends existing LMMs (like LLaVA) by training them with ==visual instruction tuning== to act as planners that leverage a diverse ==skill repository== of specialized vision models.
- Implements a ==four-step dialogue flow== (Human Input, LMM Planning, Tool Execution, LMM Aggregation & Response) where the LMM continuously considers the raw image query while planning tool usage.
- Constructs a novel, comprehensive ==multimodal instruction-following dataset== for tool use via a self-instruct method, using GPT-4 to generate diverse human-AI interaction sessions and skill composition scenarios.

## Results

- LLaVA-Plus variants consistently outperform LLaVA on LLaVA-Bench, SEED-Bench (e.g., LLaVA-Plus (All Tools) achieves **83.9** on LLaVA-Bench COCO vs. LLaVA's **81.2**), and MMVet, demonstrating improved existing capabilities, especially in OCR and spatial categories.
- Achieves a new State-of-the-Art Elo rating of **1203** on VisiT-Bench, surpassing LLaVA (**1095**) and other leading methods, showcasing its practical effectiveness in real-world use cases.
- Exhibits emergent capabilities such as precise object detection and contextual reasoning, semantic segmentation with mask-based conditional image generation, and multimodal social media post creation, while also reducing false positives from individual tools like Grounding DINO (from **90** to **20** instances in a test set).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An end-to-end trained Large Multimodal Model (LMM) can effectively learn to select, activate, and compose diverse external tools, offering greater robustness and adaptability than prompt-engineered methods.
> - Maintaining active visual grounding throughout the entire human-AI interaction significantly improves the LMM's ability to understand complex visual instructions and generate coherent, visually grounded responses.
> - A high-quality, large-scale multimodal instruction-following dataset, particularly curated with advanced LLMs like GPT-4, is critical for teaching LMMs to intelligently utilize external tools.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of LLaVA-Plus is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of general-purpose AI assistants, particularly those capable of processing and generating multimodal information. The paper identifies two primary trajectories in this field:

- **End-to-end Training with Large Language Models (LLMs):** This approach involves continually training LLMs on large datasets of image-text and multimodal instruction-following data to enable them to process visual information directly. Examples include proprietary models like Flamingo and multimodal GPT-4, as well as open-source counterparts such as LLaVA and MiniGPT-4. While these models have demonstrated impressive emergent abilities in visual understanding and reasoning, they face challenges in seamlessly incorporating a wide range of specialized skills like image segmentation or generation due to their unified architecture. Expanding their capabilities often requires retraining or significant architectural modifications.
- **Tool Chaining with LLMs:** This paradigm utilizes LLMs (e.g., via frameworks like LangChain) as planners that invoke external, pre-trained vision models (tools) to perform specific sub-tasks. Prominent works in this category include VisProg, ViperGPT, Visual ChatGPT, X-GPT, and MM-REACT. The strength of this approach lies in its flexibility and low development cost for integrating new tools. However, its major limitation is reliance on meticulously crafted prompts, which can be brittle and lack the adaptability and robustness needed for LLMs to accurately select, activate, and compose results from a large, diverse toolset on the fly for real-world tasks.

LLaVA-Plus positions itself as a novel attempt to **combine the strengths of both paradigms**. It leverages an **end-to-end training approach** but for an **LMM (specifically, an extension of LLaVA)** that **learns to use external tools** from a "skill repository." Unlike previous tool-augmented LLMs where visual signals are often only used when multimodal tools are activated, LLaVA-Plus directly grounds and actively engages the raw image query throughout the entire human-AI interaction. This distinct feature allows the LMM to "plan and reason" more effectively by continuously considering visual context, rather than just passing it to tools.

This work addresses a critical gap: enabling LMMs to systematically expand their capabilities by *learning* to use a diverse set of specialized tools, thereby achieving a level of versatility and robustness that pure end-to-end models or prompt-engineered tool-chaining systems struggle to attain. It represents a step towards more comprehensive and adaptable multimodal agents, embodying Marvin Minsky's "Society of Mind" concept where specialized agents (tools) combine to produce higher intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching aspiration driving LLaVA-Plus is the development of **general-purpose AI assistants** that can effectively follow diverse multimodal instructions to complete a wide array of real-world tasks. The authors identify specific motivations and objectives to achieve this:

- **Expand LMM Capabilities Systematically:** Current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), despite their emergent abilities in visual understanding, struggle to seamlessly integrate a broad spectrum of advanced vision and vision-language skills, such as precise image segmentation or sophisticated image generation. LLaVA-Plus aims to overcome this by providing a systematic, end-to-end training approach for LMMs to acquire these capabilities.
- **Overcome Limitations of Tool-Chaining Methods:** While tool-chaining with LLMs offers flexibility, it often relies on prompt engineering, which is neither robust nor adaptable enough for dynamic selection and composition of tools from a large repository in real-world scenarios. LLaVA-Plus seeks to improve this by *training* the LMM to intelligently select and utilize tools.
- **Maintain Visual Grounding Throughout Interaction:** A key motivation is to ensure that the image query remains actively engaged and grounded throughout the entire human-AI interaction session, not just when a tool is invoked. This continuous visual grounding is hypothesized to significantly improve tool use performance and enable new interaction scenarios, differentiating LLaVA-Plus from prior tool-augmented LLMs.
- **Enable Skill Expansion via Instruction Tuning:** The system is designed to be continually improvable. A core objective is to allow for the easy incorporation of new skills or tools through targeted instruction tuning, making the LMM adaptable to evolving requirements and new technological advancements in specialized vision models.
- **Curate a High-Quality Multimodal Tool-Use Dataset:** To train the LMM effectively in tool utilization, a critical objective was to create a novel and comprehensive multimodal instruction-following dataset specifically designed for tool use within human-AI interaction sessions. This dataset leverages advanced LLMs like ChatGPT and GPT-4 for curation, ensuring diversity and relevance to real-world tasks.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The authors explicitly state an objective to open-source their assets, including the generated instruction data, codebase, model checkpoints, and a visual chat demo. This reflects a commitment to reproducibility and fostering further research in the multimodal AI community.

In essence, LLaVA-Plus aims to create a more intelligent, versatile, and extensible multimodal assistant by teaching an LMM not just to understand and generate based on its internal knowledge, but also to intelligently leverage an external "Society of Mind" (skill repository) of specialist tools, all while keeping the visual context central to its reasoning and planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LLaVA-Plus proposes a modularized system architecture where a Large Multimodal Model (LMM) acts as a planner, learning to leverage a wide range of specialized vision and vision-language models from a "skill repository." The core of their methodology involves **visual instruction tuning** on carefully curated multimodal instruction-following data.

#### 3.1. LLaVA-Plus Architecture and Dialogue Flow

The system extends existing LMMs like LLaVA. While traditional LMMs respond directly to user image-text queries, LLaVA-Plus introduces "skill-oriented dialogues" in addition to user-oriented ones. The interaction proceeds in a four-step pipeline (illustrated in Figure 2):
1. **Human Input:** User provides a task instruction (X_q) related to an image (I_q).
2. **LMM Planning (X_skill use):** The LMM analyzes both X_q and I_q, and outputs a structured response (X_skill use) that includes:
 - `Thought`: A reasoning process to determine if and which tool is needed.
 - `Action`: A JSON-formatted list of function calls (API name, API params) to invoke tools from the skill repository. If no tool is needed, this is an empty list.
 - `Value`: A natural language response indicating the LMM's intention, e.g., "I will use [tool] to help to answer the question. Please wait for a moment."
3. **Tool Execution (X_skill result):** The selected tool(s) are executed, and their result (X_skill result) is returned to the LMM. This output is presented as a "Human" turn to the LMM, simulating a user providing tool results.
4. **LMM Aggregation & Response (X_answer):** The LMM aggregates X_skill result with the original X_q and I_q, and generates a final natural language response (X_answer) to the human. This response also follows the `Thought`, `Action`, `Value` format, with `Action` typically being empty and `Value` containing the final answer.

#### 3.2. Skill Repository and Multimodal Tool Use Instruct Data Generation

The success of LLaVA-Plus hinges on its ability to learn to use tools. This is achieved by training on a novel dataset created using a **self-instruct method with GPT-4 as the labeler**. The skill repository covers a wide range of "core" (visual understanding) and "extended" (external knowledge, generation, visual prompts, skill composition) skills (summarized in Table 2).

- **Data Format:** The training data conforms to the unified prediction format (`Human: I_q <\n> X_q <STOP> Assistant: X_skill use <STOP> Human: X_skill result <STOP> Assistant: X_answer <STOP>`). Only the "green sub-sequences" (Assistant turns) are used to compute the loss, teaching the model to predict tool use and answers.
- **Core Skills (Visual Understanding):**
 - **Image-only Skills (e.g., Captioning, Tagging, Semantic Segmentation):** For tools that don't require specific user arguments beyond the image, GPT-4 generates diverse instructions (X_q) and then synthesizes `Thought`, `Value`, and `Action` fields. The `X_skill result` is formatted by presenting tool outputs followed by the initial question. Finally, GPT-4 generates the `X_answer` by considering all previous information and image contexts (ground-truth captions, object coordinates, categories).
 - **Skills with Additional Function Arguments (e.g., Object Detection, Instance Segmentation):** These are more challenging.
 - **Method 1:** X_q is generated with placeholder concepts, which are then replaced by randomly chosen image categories, followed by rewriting via GPT-4.
 - **Method 2 (Self-Instruct):** Manually created "seed samples" (full dialogues) along with image contexts are fed to GPT-4, which then generates new, diverse full dialogues for unseen images.
- **Extended Skills (Brief Overview):**
 - **External Knowledge:** Uses CLIP Retrieval on InfoSeek dataset. LMM queries for external info, retrieved results are then used to formulate final answers.
 - **Generation:** Utilizes Stable Diffusion (SD) for image generation and Instruct-Pix2Pix for image editing. GPT-4 is used to create human-like, sometimes ambiguous, instructions that SD or Instruct-Pix2Pix can then fulfill, with LMM generating detailed SD-favored prompts.
 - **Visual Prompts:** Supports interactive inputs like points, sketches, and boxes. Tools like SAM, Semantic-SAM, and SEEM are used for interactive segmentation, with user visual inputs converted to text for the LMM.
 - **Skill Composition:** Critical for real-world tasks. Data is curated for scenarios requiring multiple skills, often in multi-turn interactions. Examples include combining detection, segmentation, tagging, and captioning; Interactive Segmentation + Inpainting (SAM + SD); Semantic Segmentation + Generation (OpenSeeD + ControlNet); and Image Generation/Editing + Social Media Post (SD/InstructPix2Pix + LMM-generated text).

#### 3.3. Model Training and Serving

- **Training:** LLaVA-Plus is trained by combining the curated tool-use instruction data (Table 2) with the existing LLaVA-158K dataset. The LLaVA-158K data is augmented to fit the unified `Thought-Action-Value` format (Table 8). Two settings are explored:
 - **LLaVA-Plus (All Tools):** All visual understanding tools process the input image, and results are converted to symbolic sequences to enrich image features during training and evaluation.
 - **LLaVA-Plus (Fly):** Tools are invoked selectively based on the instruction to reduce computation cost.
- **Serving:** LLaVA-Plus is served using the FastChat system, comprising web servers, model workers (hosting LMM and tools), and a controller. A 7B LLaVA-Plus model and all tools can run on an 80G GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LLaVA-Plus demonstrates significant improvements over its predecessor LLaVA and establishes new state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks, showcasing enhanced existing capabilities and the emergence of new ones.

#### 4.1. Tool Use Improves Existing Capabilities (Quantitative)

- **LLaVA-Bench & SEED-Bench (Table 4):** LLaVA-Plus variants consistently outperform LLaVA on both benchmarks.
 - On LLaVA-Bench (evaluating visual chat, detailed description, reasoning), LLaVA-Plus (All Tools) achieves an overall score of 83.9 on COCO set and 69.5 on In-the-Wild set, compared to LLaVA's 81.2 and 57.1. LLaVA-Plus (Fly) also shows improvements.
 - On SEED-Bench (evaluating image/instance-level perception and reasoning), LLaVA-Plus (All Tools) achieves 55.01 average accuracy, surpassing LLaVA's 44.45.
 - **Ablations:** Directly adding tool execution results to LLaVA at test time ("LLaVA (Tools in Test)") degraded performance, underscoring the necessity of *learning* to use tools during training. Removing the "thoughts" field also led to performance drops, validating the benefits of chain-of-thought style reasoning. GPT4Tools, which trains an LLM for tool use, performed lower than LLaVA-Plus, indicating the importance of visual instruction tuning within an LMM.

- **MMVet (Table 6):** LLaVA-Plus consistently outperforms LLaVA across various capabilities (Recognition, OCR, Knowledge, Generation, Spatial, Math).
 - LLaVA-Plus-7B (All Tools) achieves 27.5 average score compared to LLaVA-7B's 24.1. The 13B version achieves 35.0.
 - Most significant improvements are observed in OCR and Spatial categories, directly attributable to the integration of specialized tools for these tasks. The paper also highlights LLaVA-Plus's advantage over MM-ReAct, which is a tool-chaining system, by being an LMM that learns tool use.

- **VisiT-Bench (Table 7):** LLaVA-Plus (13B) achieves a new State-of-the-Art (SoTA) Elo rating of 1203, significantly outperforming LLaVA (13B) at 1095 and other leading methods, with a higher win-rate against human-verified references. This benchmark focuses on real-world use cases, validating the practical effectiveness of LLaVA-Plus.

- **LLaVA-Bench (Tools) (Table 5):** A new evaluation set specifically designed to measure novel tool-enabled capabilities (Grounding, Tagging, Caption, OCR).
 - LLaVA-Plus (All Tools) achieves an overall score of 82.3, dramatically outperforming LLaVA (58.7) and "LLaVA (Tools in Test)" (48.5).
 - It also significantly surpasses commercial systems like Bard and Bing Chat, and other tool-chaining methods like MM-REACT and "All Tools + GPT4," particularly in grounding and OCR, demonstrating its superior ability to leverage specific tools.

- **COCO Caption (Table 11):** LLaVA-Plus-7B enhances BLIP2's outputs, leading to richer details and improved CLIP scores, while language-only metrics remain lower, indicating a focus on comprehensive multimodal understanding rather than pure text generation.

- **False Positives of Grounding DINO (Table 12):** LLaVA-Plus can reduce false positives generated by Grounding DINO. For instance, the number of false positive instances dropped from 90 (Grounding DINO) to 20 (LLaVA-Plus-7B), and images with false positives dropped from 41 to 12. This shows the LMM's ability to reason about tool outputs in context and filter irrelevant or incorrect results.

#### 4.2. Visual Examples of New Capabilities (Qualitative)

Figure 3 and extensive examples in the Appendix illustrate LLaVA-Plus's emergent capabilities:
- **Object Detection and Visual Chat (Figure 3, left; Figure 6):** LLaVA-Plus can accurately detect object coordinates (e.g., a frisbee) and use this precise location information to infer its state (e.g., "in the air") and describe the scene's activity, a capability lacking in other commercial and research systems like Bard, Bing Chat, and MM-REACT.
- **Semantic Segmentation and Mask-based Conditional Image Generation (Figure 3, right; Figure 11):** LLaVA-Plus can perform semantic segmentation, understand spatial layouts, and then generate new images with similar layouts but different content (e.g., turning a street scene into "underwater" or "cartoon style"). It intelligently crafts appropriate prompts for conditional image generation models (ControlNet).
- **Multimodal Social Media Post Creation (Figure 3, bottom; Figure 12, 13):** LLaVA-Plus can edit an image (e.g., changing seasons, adding fireworks) and then generate attractive, contextually relevant social media captions, combining visual and textual generation skills.
- **Interactive Visual Prompts (Figure 14, 15):** The system supports user-drawn points or reference regions for interactive and multi-granularity segmentation, providing a more intuitive human-AI interaction for visual tasks.
- **External Knowledge Retrieval (Figure 9):** LLaVA-Plus can retrieve external knowledge and integrate factual information into its responses, enriching descriptions of objects and scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LLaVA-Plus represents a significant advancement in the development of general-purpose multimodal AI assistants, bridging a critical gap between two leading paradigms: end-to-end LMMs and LLM-driven tool chaining. Its key significance and potential impacts are:

- **Novelty in Learning Tool Use:** The primary contribution is the demonstration that an LMM can be *end-to-end trained* to *learn* to select, activate, and compose the results of diverse external tools. This is a crucial distinction from existing tool-chaining methods that rely heavily on prompt engineering for LLMs. By deeply integrating tool-use capabilities into the LMM's learning process, LLaVA-Plus achieves greater robustness and adaptability.
- **Enhanced Versatility and Capabilities:** LLaVA-Plus significantly expands the capabilities of LMMs beyond basic visual understanding and reasoning. By intelligently leveraging specialized tools, it gains new skills in precise object grounding, various forms of segmentation, image generation and editing, external knowledge retrieval, and their complex compositions. This moves multimodal AI closer to human-like versatility, where an agent can perform a wide range of tasks by utilizing specific "skills" (tools).
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** The approach of keeping the raw visual signal engaged throughout the interaction, allowing the LMM to "see" and "reason" directly from the image while planning tool use, leads to a more intuitive and effective human-AI dialogue. This active engagement enhances the LMM's ability to understand complex visual instructions and generate coherent, visually grounded responses.
- **Systematic Extensibility:** The modular architecture and the instruction-tuning pipeline for incorporating new tools mean that LLaVA-Plus can be continually improved and expanded with new functionalities. As new specialized vision models emerge, they can be integrated into the skill repository and the LMM can be fine-tuned to learn their usage, ensuring future-proofing and scalability of capabilities.
- **High-Quality Dataset Contribution:** The creation and public release of a large, high-quality multimodal instruction-following dataset specifically designed for tool use is a major contribution to the research community. This dataset, curated using advanced LLMs like GPT-4, will accelerate further research in training multimodal agents.
- **Open-Source Impact:** The commitment to open-sourcing the models, codebase, and data assets will foster transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative development within the multimodal AI community. This will enable other researchers to build upon LLaVA-Plus, test new ideas, and potentially integrate it into new applications.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated capabilities, particularly in areas like interactive image editing, multimodal content creation (social media posts), and precise visual querying, highlight the strong potential for LLaVA-Plus to be deployed in real-world applications requiring sophisticated multimodal understanding and generation.
- **Addressing Hallucinations and Conflicts:** The finding that LLaVA-Plus can help reduce false positives from individual tools (e.g., Grounding DINO) is significant. This suggests that the LMM's contextual understanding and reasoning can act as a filtering mechanism, improving the overall reliability of tool-augmented systems.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as occasional hallucinations and tool use conflicts, LLaVA-Plus lays a robust foundation for future research in building more reliable, general-purpose multimodal AI agents capable of truly "thinking" with images and "acting" through a diverse set of skills.
