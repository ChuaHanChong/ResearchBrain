# A Kung Fu Athlete Bot That Can Do It All Day: Highly Dynamic, Balance-Challenging Motion Dataset and Autonomous Fall-Resilient Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Beijing Institute of Technology developed KungFuAthlete, a high-dynamic martial arts motion dataset, and an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to perform complex movements and autonomously recover from falls. The integrated policy achieved a 100% success rate in challenging single-leg standing tasks and demonstrated robust performance on a real-world Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing motion tracking datasets primarily focus on low-to-medium dynamic human activities, limiting the exploration of advanced agile humanoid robot behaviors.
- Motion data reconstructed from monocular video often suffers from inaccuracies like root node height drift, local jitter, and error accumulation, which hinder high-precision robot control.
- Current fall recovery methods for humanoid robots typically operate as isolated tasks or segmented policies, lacking a unified strategy for seamless integration with agile motion execution and recovery from arbitrary states.

## Method

![[2602.13656-method.png]]
*Flowchart of training a policy using FastSAC for high-dynamic tracking and autonomous fall recovery without reference frames under low-kinetic-energy sampling.*

- A novel high-dynamic martial arts motion dataset, **KungFuAthlete**, was created from professional athlete videos and meticulously corrected using ==parabolic jump reconstruction== and ==Savitzky–Golay smoothing== to ensure suitability for robot control.
- An ==end-to-end reinforcement learning== paradigm was developed, using **FastSAC**, to train a single policy for jointly performing high-dynamic motion tracking and ==autonomous fall recovery== through a combined reward objective.
- The training incorporated ==low kinetic energy sampling== to prevent repeated unrecoverable failures and utilized ==Gravity-based Randomized State Initialization== (D_GRSI) to generate a diverse set of fall poses for robust recovery learning.

## Results

- The KungFuAthlete dataset demonstrated significantly higher dynamic metrics, with the Jump subset showing average joint velocity of **0.02384** and body angular velocity of **0.18017**, surpassing existing datasets like LAFAN1 and AMASS.
- The proposed unified policy achieved a **100%** success rate (**6/6** trials) in challenging single-leg standing tasks, a stark improvement over a **0%** success rate for a baseline motion-tracking-only model.
- The learned policy successfully recovered from various arbitrary fall poses in simulation and exhibited robust high-dynamic movement and recovery under external disturbances when deployed on a real-world Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified policy, trained to switch between motion tracking and fall recovery objectives, can effectively learn to execute high-dynamic motions while simultaneously developing robust autonomous fall recovery capabilities.
> - Integrating a fall recovery task into the training paradigm implicitly forces the policy to learn more stable and robust balance strategies, even when not actively recovering from a fall.
> - Precise data correction techniques, such as physics-inspired root height adjustments and temporal smoothing, are essential to transform noisy video-based motion capture into high-quality reference data for dynamic humanoid control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses critical limitations in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in the domains of highly dynamic motion tracking and autonomous fall recovery.

**Current State of Humanoid Motion Tracking:** Existing motion tracking technologies and datasets, such as AMASS and LAFAN1, are primarily designed for everyday human activities. While these datasets have enabled significant progress in low-to-medium dynamic behaviors like walking, turning, and sitting, they lack the intensity, complexity, and rapid center-of-mass shifts characteristic of high-dynamic actions (e.g., jumping, acrobatic maneuvers, martial arts). This restricts the exploration of the upper limits of robotic hardware performance and the development of agile humanoid control. Recent attempts to generate more expressive datasets, like those used in ASAP and KungfuBot, often rely on monocular video reconstruction, which, while scalable, introduces issues such as jitter, global pose drift, and error accumulation, making them challenging for high-precision robot control.

**Challenges in Fall Recovery:** Humanoid robots are inherently prone to instability and falls, especially during complex or dynamic movements, or when subjected to external disturbances. Most prior research on fall recovery treats it as an isolated task, focusing on standing up from a fallen posture once the robot has already collapsed. While frameworks like SafeFall and FIRM have explored integrated fall protection and recovery, these often involve switching between segmented policies or rely on limited demonstration data. A unified strategy that seamlessly integrates agile motion execution with robust stabilization and recovery from arbitrary states within a single policy remains a significant gap. The goal of fully autonomous humanoid operation in real-world, high-dynamic scenarios necessitates a single, comprehensive framework for both tracking and recovery.

**Contribution to the Landscape:** This paper directly tackles these two intertwined challenges. By creating KungFuAthlete, a novel dataset derived from professional martial arts training, the authors provide the much-needed high-dynamic, balance-challenging data missing in existing resources. This dataset specifically captures motions with significantly higher joint, linear, and angular velocities than previous datasets, pushing the boundaries for robotic control. Furthermore, the paper introduces a unique training paradigm that enables a single policy to learn both high-dynamic motion tracking and autonomous fall recovery simultaneously. This unified approach, which allows recovery to reference motion without requiring explicit "standing-up" reference actions, represents a substantial advancement over segmented or auxiliary-data-dependent recovery methods. It moves beyond pure motion tracking to recovery-enabled execution, paving the way for more robust and autonomous humanoid performance in complex, real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations of this research are clearly articulated within the paper, stemming from the existing limitations in humanoid robotics.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Construct a High-Dynamic Martial Arts Motion Dataset (KungFuAthlete):** The primary objective is to create a large-scale, high-dynamic human motion dataset specifically tailored for challenging humanoid robot control. This dataset should capture the extreme dynamism, rapid center-of-mass shifts, and complex coordination inherent in professional martial arts movements.
2. **Develop Robust Data Correction Techniques:** To address inherent inaccuracies (e.g., root node height drift, local jitter) in motion data reconstructed from video footage, the authors aim to propose effective algorithms for correcting these issues, ensuring the suitability of the dataset for high-precision humanoid control.
3. **Propose a Unified Training Paradigm for Motion Tracking and Fall Recovery:** A central objective is to develop a novel reinforcement learning framework that allows a single policy to jointly learn high-dynamic motion tracking and autonomous fall recovery. This framework should enable robots to recover from any arbitrary state back to the referenced motion without requiring separate, pre-defined "standing-up" actions.
4. **Enhance Robustness and Autonomy of Humanoid Robots:** Ultimately, the project aims to expand robotic capabilities from mere motion tracking to robust, recovery-enabled execution, thereby promoting more reliable and autonomous humanoid performance in real-world, high-dynamic scenarios, reducing reliance on human intervention.

**Motivation:**
The motivation for this work is driven by several critical needs in humanoid robotics:
- **Exploring Hardware Performance Limits:** Current motion tracking systems can handle routine behaviors, but the full potential and performance limits of humanoid robot hardware remain largely unexplored due to the lack of sufficiently challenging data. High-dynamic motions, like those found in martial arts, represent an extreme test case.
- **Addressing High Failure Rates in Dynamic Motions:** High-dynamic actions naturally carry high failure rates, not just for humans, but especially for robots. When robots fail, they often lose autonomy, suffer equipment damage, and require human intervention, which is incompatible with the vision of autonomous robotic operation.
- **Bridging the Data Gap:** Existing open-source motion datasets are inadequate for training robots to perform highly dynamic actions. There is an urgent need for datasets that feature high dynamics, long sequences, and significant center-of-mass transformations.
- **Need for Integrated Stabilization:** Current fall recovery methods often act in isolation from the primary motion tracking task. There is a strong motivation to develop a unified strategy that can anticipate, mitigate, and recover from failures within a single, coherent framework, promoting seamless and resilient robotic behavior.
- **Enabling Real-World Deployment:** To move humanoid robots beyond controlled laboratory settings into real-world applications (service, manufacturing, etc.), they must possess the ability to perform complex tasks robustly and recover autonomously from unforeseen disturbances.

By tackling these challenges, the authors aim to lay a foundational groundwork for future generations of humanoid robots that are not only agile but also inherently resilient and autonomous.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology encompasses two main pillars: the creation and correction of a novel high-dynamic motion dataset, and the development of an end-to-end reinforcement learning paradigm for motion tracking with autonomous fall recovery.

#### 3.1. KungFuAthlete Dataset: Conversion and Correction of High-Dynamic-Range Video

- **Data Overview:**
 - **Source:** The dataset comprises 197 video clips from the daily training of professional martial artists, specifically Xie Yuanhang.
 - **Processing:** Videos were automatically segmented into 1,726 sub-segments to avoid abrupt transitions. These sub-clips were then processed using GVHMR (Global-View Human Motion Recovery) for initial motion reconstruction, followed by GMR (Global Motion Reorientation) for reorientation.
 - **Content:** The dataset includes 848 samples, with 715 (84%) being routine training. The specialized movements cover various martial arts techniques: Fist (e.g., Long Fist, Tai Chi Fist), Staff, Saber (e.g., Southern Saber, Tai Chi Sword), and Skills (e.g., Backflip, Lotus Swing).
 - **Categorization:** The dataset is divided into two subsets: KungFuAthlete (Ground) for non-jumping actions and KungFuAthlete (Jump) for jumping actions.
 - **Dynamic Metrics:** A statistical comparison (Table 1) shows that KungFuAthlete, particularly the Jump subset, exhibits significantly higher joint velocity, body linear velocity, and angular velocity compared to existing datasets like LAFAN1, PHUMA, and AMASS, underscoring its high dynamic range and complexity.

- **Root Node Height Drift and Local Jitter Correction:** Data extracted from video-based reconstruction commonly suffers from inaccuracies like root node height drift (appearing suspended or penetrating the ground) and local jitter (discontinuities between stitched frames). The paper proposes a two-stage correction method:
 - **Root Node Height Drift Correction with Parabolic Jump Reconstruction:**
 - **Minimum Body Height Constraint:** For ground-contact phases, the algorithm aligns the lowest body part with the ground plane by adjusting the root height, ensuring `z_min(q_t) >= 0`.
 - **Temporal Propagation with Velocity Thresholding:** To maintain temporal smoothness, the corrected root height is propagated from the previous timestep using a first-order forward difference in velocity. A small velocity threshold (`τ`) suppresses spurious vertical jitter.
 - **Jump Phase Handling via Local Extrema:** For jumping motions, continuous ground contact would be incorrect. The algorithm detects local maxima (take-off apex) and minima (landing contact) in the root height trajectory.
 - **Parabolic Reconstruction of Airborne Root Motion:** For detected airborne segments, the root height trajectory is reconstructed using a physics-inspired parabolic model, assuming constant gravitational acceleration and zero initial vertical velocity at take-off. This ensures physically plausible airborne trajectories.
 - **Final Ground Penetration Correction:** A final safeguard ensures strict non-penetration of the ground across the entire sequence.
 - **Post-Processing via Savitzky–Golay Smoothing:** To address local jitter and further improve temporal smoothness while preserving motion structure, a Savitzky–Golay (SG) filter is applied to the corrected pose sequence, independently filtering each component of the root position and joint angles.

#### 3.2. End-to-End Motion Tracking with Autonomous Fall Recovery

The paper proposes a novel training paradigm that unifies high-dynamic motion tracking and fall recovery within a single policy.

- **Objective Function:** A single policy is trained to maximize a combined reward objective (Eq. 14). This objective incorporates a Bernoulli distribution to switch between motion tracking (probability `p`) and fall recovery (probability `1-p`) tasks. The initial robot state `q_0` can be either a reference state for tracking or a fall pose from a specialized dataset (`D_GRSI`) for recovery.
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** FastSAC, an off-policy reinforcement learning method based on the maximum entropy principle, is employed for policy learning. FastSAC is known for its efficient data reuse, parallel simulation capabilities, and adaptive entropy temperature, accelerating convergence and ensuring stability in high-dimensional continuous control.
- **Low Kinetic Energy Sampling:** To overcome the challenge of unrecoverable states during highly dynamic motions (where failures often occur at high-kinetic-energy phases), a low kinetic energy sampling strategy is introduced. Initial states for episodes are sampled from "anchor timesteps" corresponding to local minima of a kinetic-energy proxy. Sampling weights for these anchors are adaptively updated, increasing for anchors leading to failures, preventing repeated initialization into unrecoverable failure loops.
- **Reward Design:**
 - **Physics Stability Reward (`r_mt`) for High-Speed Motion:** This complex reward system (Table 2) combines:
 - Tracking fidelity: Penalties for relative body position, orientation, and angular velocity errors compared to the reference motion.
 - Balance: Center-of-Mass (CoM) error (aligning CoM with the supporting foot) and Feet Proximity.
 - Task-specific penalties for high-dynamics: Relative Root Orientation Penalty (for rapid turns), Feet Slip Penalty (for weight shifts), Knee and Ankle Action Rate Penalties (for smooth transitions), Soft DOF Limit Penalty, and Undesired Contacts Penalty.
 - **Autonomous Fall Recovery Reward (`r_rc`) Design:** This reward (Table 3) focuses on promoting stable standing from arbitrary fallen configurations:
 - Penalties for Relative Shoulder Height deviation (to encourage rising).
 - Penalty for XY Root Movement Before Stand (to encourage rising in place rather than translating).
 - Penalty for Action Rate Before Stand (to promote smooth, gradual recovery motions).
- **Termination Conditions:** To avoid premature termination during recovery tasks, a "bad tracking indicator" accumulates consecutive failure steps. During recovery tasks, a maximum allowed length of consecutive bad tracking steps (`τ_bad`) is permitted before termination, providing a grace period for the robot to recover.
- **Gravity-based Randomized State Initialization (D_GRSI):** To ensure the recovery policy can handle diverse post-fall configurations, a dataset of fall poses is generated. The robot is initialized in zero-torque mode and released under gravity with randomized contact friction, naturally generating a wide range of fallen states. This dataset is further augmented by randomly recombining rotational components (base orientation and joint configuration) to increase coverage and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental section aims to validate the proposed KungFuAthlete dataset and the unified motion tracking and fall recovery framework through simulation and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics

- **Dynamic Range:** Table 1 quantitatively demonstrates that the KungFuAthlete dataset, particularly its "Jump" subset, exhibits significantly higher average joint velocity (0.02384), body linear velocity (0.05297), and body angular velocity (0.18017) compared to conventional datasets like LAFAN1, PHUMA, and AMASS. This confirms the dataset's unique high-dynamic and complex motion characteristics.

#### 4.2. Motion Tracking Performance (Ablation Study on Hybrid Rewards - Q2)

- **Baseline (BeyondMimic):** The BeyondMimic model, without the proposed recovery task, consistently failed (0/6 success rate) during a challenging single-leg standing task, even without external perturbations (Table 5, Figure 7). This highlights the inherent difficulty of dynamic balance tasks for conventional motion tracking systems.
- **Incorporating Fall Recovery Task:** Adding the fall recovery objective to the training paradigm drastically improved robustness. The model with "w/ recover task" achieved a 100% success rate (6/6 trials completed without falling). While it initially showed larger orientation errors (`E_mpboe` 40.39) and slightly reduced smoothness (18.6) compared to BeyondMimic before failure, it demonstrated superior balance maintenance. This suggests that the recovery task forces the policy to learn more robust balance strategies.
- **Adding Feet Slip Penalty:** Further integrating the feet slip penalty (with weight 3 then 5) improved tracking accuracy. A weight of 5 for the feet slip penalty reduced `E_mpboe` to 19.93 and slightly improved smoothness to 18.0, surpassing the recovery-only model in both metrics while maintaining a 100% success rate.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** The paper notes qualitative improvements, such as the robot learning to execute cleaner, single-lift stepping motions without foot dragging when action rate penalties and feet slip penalties were combined. The addition of a center-of-mass (CoM) alignment reward was also crucial for maintaining stability during single-leg support.

#### 4.3. Autonomous Fall Recovery (Q3)

- **Recovery from Arbitrary Poses:** Figure 8 visually demonstrates the robot's ability to recover from various lying positions. This qualitatively supports the claim that the unified policy, trained with gravity-based randomized state initialization (`D_GRSI`) and the dedicated recovery reward, can reliably recover from diverse initial fall postures back to the reference motion.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment (Q4)

- **Deployment on Unitree G1:** The learned policy was successfully deployed on a real-world Unitree G1 humanoid robot. Figure 2 provides visual evidence of the robot's performance, including single-foot support recovery and rapid recovery under external pulling forces on its torso. This demonstrates the sim-to-real transferability and practical applicability of the proposed framework, allowing the robot to perform highly dynamic movements and recover from disturbances in a physical environment.

In summary, the experiments confirm that KungFuAthlete provides a valuable high-dynamic dataset, and the proposed unified RL framework effectively enables humanoid robots to perform complex martial arts movements while autonomously recovering from falls, significantly outperforming prior methods in terms of robustness and success rate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper holds significant implications and potential impact across several dimensions of humanoid robotics and broader AI research.

**Advancing Humanoid Robot Capabilities:**
- **Unlocking High-Dynamic Dexterity:** By providing the KungFuAthlete dataset, this work directly addresses a long-standing bottleneck in humanoid control: the lack of training data for highly dynamic and agile movements. This dataset will enable future research to push the physical limits of humanoid hardware, allowing robots to perform complex maneuvers that were previously unattainable.
- **Robustness in Unstructured Environments:** The ability to autonomously recover from falls and disturbances within a single, unified policy is a critical step toward deploying humanoids in unstructured, real-world environments. No longer limited to controlled laboratory settings, future robots could operate reliably in service, manufacturing, disaster relief, or even entertainment sectors, where unexpected events are common.
- **Reduced Human Intervention:** The autonomous fall recovery mechanism significantly reduces the need for human intervention when a robot encounters instability or falls. This translates to increased operational efficiency, lower maintenance costs, and safer interaction zones between humans and robots.

**Methodological Contributions:**
- **High-Quality Data Generation:** The meticulous process of extracting, correcting, and smoothing motion data from real-world videos (including root node height drift correction and Savitzky-Golay filtering) provides a robust blueprint for future video-to-motion pipelines, addressing common artifacts and enhancing data usability for robotics.
- **Innovative RL Paradigm:** The proposed unified training paradigm, which jointly learns motion tracking and fall recovery, represents a novel approach to complex motor skill acquisition. The "low kinetic energy sampling" and the sophisticated reward design are key innovations that overcome challenges in training policies for highly dynamic and failure-prone tasks. This approach could inspire similar integrated learning strategies for other complex robotic behaviors.
- **Physics-Aware Learning:** The emphasis on physics-based rewards (e.g., CoM alignment, feet slip penalties) and gravity-based randomized state initialization for fall recovery ensures that the learned policies are not only effective but also physically plausible and robust to real-world dynamics, facilitating better sim-to-real transfer.

**Broader AI and Robotics Impact:**
- **Benchmarking for Future Research:** The KungFuAthlete dataset will serve as a valuable benchmark for evaluating new algorithms for humanoid control, motion synthesis, and sim-to-real transfer in highly dynamic contexts. It provides a common ground for researchers to compare and advance their methods.
- **Inspiration for Bio-Inspired Robotics:** Studying and replicating the extreme agility and balance of professional martial artists offers profound insights for bio-inspired robotics, potentially leading to new designs and control strategies that mimic human biomechanics and resilience.
- **Foundation for Advanced Humanoid Applications:** This work lays a strong foundation for a new generation of humanoids capable of complex, versatile, and robust interactions. Imagine robots assisting in physically demanding tasks, performing intricate dance routines, or even participating in sports – capabilities brought closer by this research.

In conclusion, "A KUNG FU ATHLETE BOT THAT CAN DO IT ALL DAY" is a seminal paper that not only introduces a groundbreaking dataset for extreme humanoid motion but also pioneers an end-to-end learning framework that bridges the critical gap between agile execution and autonomous recovery. Its significance lies in enabling more robust, autonomous, and capable humanoid robots, thereby accelerating their integration into diverse real-world applications.
