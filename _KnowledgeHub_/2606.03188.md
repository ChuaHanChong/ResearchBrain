# GeoSem-WAM: Geometry- and Semantic-Aware World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKUST(GZ) developed GeoSem-WAM, a framework that enhances World Action Models for robotic control by incorporating geometry- and semantic-aware predictive supervision during training, achieving an average success rate improvement of 6.6% over baseline models in real-world robot manipulation tasks and reaching 98.55% on the LIBERO benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) primarily rely on RGB-based future prediction, limiting their explicit understanding of environmental structure, spatial relations, and high-level semantics.
- The appearance-based supervision in current WAMs often lacks the geometric reasoning and object-level semantic awareness essential for embodied agents in complex real-world scenarios.
- Latent representations learned by these models are frequently insufficient for robotic tasks requiring a deeper grasp of environmental structure and object-level semantics.

## Method

- **GeoSem-WAM** augments World Action Model (WAM) training by incorporating multi-modal predictive supervision for future RGB, geometry, and semantic information into a unified framework.
- A DPT-style dense prediction head, extended with 3D reassemble and fusion modules, applies geometry and semantic supervision to intermediate video ==latent== tokens.
- The framework maintains efficient inference by utilizing structured predictive supervision only during training, discarding auxiliary branches at test time to directly predict actions from current observations.

## Results

- GeoSem-WAM achieved an average success rate of **98.55%** on the LIBERO benchmark and **92.52%** on the RoboTwin 2.0 dataset, outperforming Fast-WAM baselines.
- In real-world experiments, the framework improved the average success rate from **88.9%** to **95.4%** (+**6.6%**) on a Franka Emika Panda robot across various manipulation tasks, demonstrating enhanced robustness in generalization settings.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both geometry-aware (+**0.61%**) and semantic-aware (+**0.51%**) supervision positively contribute to performance, with their combination yielding the highest improvement (+**1.02%**) on LIBERO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The core value of World Action Models (WAMs) primarily derives from learning robust, dynamics-aware latent representations through predictive supervision during training, rather than explicit test-time future imagination.
> - Augmenting WAM supervision with explicit geometry and semantic prediction compels the model to acquire physically grounded motion and spatial-semantic layouts, enriching its latent representations.
> - This structured multi-modal supervision consistently improves action prediction accuracy, robustness, and scene understanding in embodied agents without compromising efficient inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of embodied intelligence, specifically focusing on World Action Models (WAMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for robotic control. WAMs represent a paradigm distinct from conventional policy learning, which directly maps observations to actions. Instead, WAMs leverage predictive world modeling as an auxiliary objective during training to enhance decision-making by learning future-aware representations of environmental dynamics. This approach has yielded performance across diverse embodied tasks and is foundational to modern robotic policies like RT-1, RT-2, and OpenVLA, and generalist frameworks such as Octo and π0.

A key open question within the WAM literature concerns the precise mechanism behind their effectiveness. Early WAM designs posited that explicit future imagination or the generation of future observations during inference drove their success. However, accumulating evidence suggests that the primary benefit derives from the robust, dynamics-aware latent representations learned through predictive supervision during the training phase, rather than explicit test-time future imagination.

Existing WAMs predominantly rely on RGB-based future prediction. This approach, while effective in capturing short-term visual dynamics, provides limited explicit structural, spatial, or high-level semantic understanding of complex environments. Appearance-based supervision often lacks the geometric reasoning and object-level semantic awareness considered critical for embodied agents operating in real-world scenarios. This limitation can result in latent representations that are insufficient for tasks requiring a deeper understanding of environmental structure and object-level semantics.

This work aims to address this identified gap by enhancing the representational capacity of WAMs through the incorporation of geometry-aware and semantic-aware predictive supervision. It builds upon prior work in VLA policy optimization (e.g., inference acceleration, fine-tuning) and RGB-only WAM designs by introducing structured training supervision. The methodology aligns with trends in imitation learning, which focuses on improving efficiency from demonstration data, and with scaling law research, which guides the development of robotic foundation models by enhancing task-specific structured representation ability through customized multi-modal world prediction supervision without solely relying on expanded model scale or training data volume.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the GeoSem-WAM research are:

1. **Revisiting the Core Value of Predictive World Modeling in WAMs:** The authors aim to provide a clear perspective that the fundamental advantage of WAMs originates from training-phase representation learning, specifically the induction of dynamics-aware latent features via predictive supervision. This contrasts with the earlier assumption that explicit future imagination or rollout during test time is the primary driver of WAM effectiveness. By clarifying this mechanism, the work seeks to guide the design of more efficient and effective WAMs, emphasizing structured learning over computationally intensive test-time generation.

2. **Addressing Limitations of RGB-only Supervision:** Existing WAMs primarily rely on RGB-based future prediction, which provides a limited understanding of environmental structure and object-level semantics. This appearance-based supervision lacks explicit geometric reasoning and high-level semantic awareness necessary for embodied agents to robustly operate in complex, real-world scenarios. The motivation is to overcome this by learning richer, more structured latent representations.

3. **Proposing a Novel Structured World Modeling Framework:** To address the above limitation, the core objective is to propose a framework that augments WAM supervision with multi-modal predictive signals. This involves integrating future RGB, geometry, and semantic prediction into a unified framework. The motivation is that this design will explicitly encourage the model to learn spatial geometry, 3D structural consistency, and object-level semantics, thereby overcoming the limitations of RGB-only supervision in capturing intricate environmental structures.

4. **Maintaining Efficient Test-Time Inference:** A crucial objective is to achieve enhanced representation learning without compromising the efficient inference paradigm of modern WAMs. The authors are motivated to design a system where structured predictive supervision is utilized *only* during training, allowing the model to directly predict actions at inference time without requiring computationally expensive future rollout or iterative video generation. This objective aligns with efforts to accelerate VLA inference and makes the framework practical for real-world robotic deployment.

5. **Enhancing Embodied Decision-Making Performance and Robustness:** Through the integration of geometry and semantic awareness, the research aims to consistently improve action prediction accuracy, robustness, and scene understanding. The motivation is to achieve superior performance in challenging embodied scenarios that involve occlusions, complex object interactions, and varying environmental dynamics, ultimately leading to more capable and adaptable robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoSem-WAM is a structured world modeling framework designed to enhance latent representations in World Action Models (WAMs) through geometric and semantic supervision. The approach focuses on leveraging these auxiliary predictions during training while preserving efficient inference by avoiding explicit future rollouts at test time.

#### 3.1. Overview

The core idea of GeoSem-WAM is to augment standard future pixel prediction with auxiliary geometry and semantic segmentation branches during the training phase. This joint learning process, encompassing video generation, action prediction, and geometric-semantic understanding, is intended to compel the backbone network to acquire physically grounded motion and spatial-semantic layouts. During inference, these auxiliary branches are discarded, and the model processes only the current observation's latent tokens in a single forward pass to directly generate actions, thus avoiding the computational overhead associated with future rollouts or explicit video sequence prediction. Importantly, neither geometry nor semantic annotations are used as model inputs during inference; the model relies solely on raw visual observations.

#### 3.2. Architecture

GeoSem-WAM is built upon the **video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) of Wan2.2-5B** as its world modeling backbone. The pretrained T5 text encoder and video VAE from the same model are reused for encoding task instructions and transforming visual observations into latent video tokens, respectively.
The architecture incorporates:
- An **action expert DiT**, which is similar in architecture but potentially different in size, for generating action chunks.
- **DPT-style geometry prediction and semantic segmentation branches** attached to the backbone.
The overall model employs a **Mixture-of-Transformer (MoT) architecture** featuring shared attention between the video and action branches. The inference stage utilizes only the core DiT backbone and the action expert, as indicated by a dashed box in the architectural diagram.

#### 3.3. Latent World Modeling

Future video dynamics are modeled within the latent space derived from a pretrained VAE.
- Let $z^{gt}_{t:t+K}$ represent the ground-truth future video latents.
- During training, a noise level $\sigma \in$ is sampled, and the target video latents are corrupted with Gaussian noise $\epsilon_z \sim N(0, I)$ to produce $z^{\sigma}_{t:t+K} = (1 - \sigma)z^{gt}_{t:t+K} + \sigma\epsilon_z$.
- The video DiT, denoted as $f^{rgb}_{\theta}$, predicts a flow target $\hat{v}_z$ conditioned on the noisy latents, noise level, and context (current visual observation and language instruction). The true flow target is $v_z = \epsilon_z - z^{gt}_{t:t+K}$.
- The video modeling objective, $L_{rgb}$, is an L2 reconstruction loss: $L_{rgb} = \|\hat{v}_z - v_z\|^2_2$.

#### 3.4. Action Modeling

The action branch is responsible for predicting a future action chunk through a denoising process.
- During training, the ground-truth action sequence $a^{gt}_{t:t+H-1}$ is corrupted with Gaussian noise $\epsilon_a$ to yield $a^{\sigma}_{t:t+H-1} = (1 - \sigma)a^{gt}_{t:t+H-1} + \sigma\epsilon_a$.
- The action DiT, denoted as $f^{act}_{\phi}$, predicts a flow target $\hat{v}_a$ conditioned on the noisy action sequence, noise level, and the latent world representation $z_t$ (from the current observation). The true flow target is $v_a = \epsilon_a - a^{gt}_{t:t+H-1}$.
- The action objective, $L_{act}$, is an L2 reconstruction loss: $L_{act} = \|\hat{v}_a - v_a\|^2_2$.
- At inference time, the action chunk is initialized from Gaussian noise and iteratively denoised, conditioned on $z_t$, without generating future video frames.

#### 3.5. Dense Structured World Supervision

To embed geometric structure and object-level semantics into the learned world representation, dense auxiliary supervision is applied to the video latent tokens.
- A **DPT-style dense prediction head** is employed for the auxiliary branches. This head aggregates intermediate video tokens from multiple Transformer blocks, projecting, fusing, and decoding these multi-level features into dense spatial predictions. This enables the supervision to leverage both low-level spatial details and high-level semantic abstractions.
- The DPT-style head is extended with **3D reassemble and 3D fusion modules** to accommodate video inputs effectively.
- For a future prediction step $\tau \in \{t+1, \dots, t+K\}$, let $z_{\tau}$ be the latent representation.
 - **Geometry Supervision**: A geometry prediction head $H_{geo}$ estimates future geometry information $\hat{o}^{geo}_{\tau}$. This branch is trained with an L1 reconstruction objective: $L_{geo} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{\tau} \|\hat{o}^{geo}_{\tau} - o^{geo}_{\tau}\|_1$.
 - **Semantic Supervision**: A semantic prediction head $H_{sem}$ predicts dense semantic logits $\hat{o}^{sem}_{\tau}$. This branch is optimized using pixel-wise cross-entropy: $L_{sem} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{\tau} CE(\hat{o}^{sem}_{\tau}, o^{sem}_{\tau})$.

#### 3.6. Unified Training Objective

The overall training objective combines all individual losses:
$L = \lambda_{rgb}L_{rgb} + \lambda_{geo}L_{geo} + \lambda_{sem}L_{sem} + \lambda_{act}L_{act}$
where $\lambda_{rgb}$, $\lambda_{geo}$, $\lambda_{sem}$, and $\lambda_{act}$ are balancing coefficients for the respective objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates GeoSem-WAM across various simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks, demonstrating improvements in action prediction, robustness, and scene understanding.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Environments:**
 - **LIBERO:** Includes four task suites (LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Long), each with 500 expert demonstrations across 10 tasks. Evaluates generalization across spatial configurations, object categories, goal specifications, and long-horizon execution.
 - **RoboTwin 2.0:** A real-to-sim benchmark for bimanual robotic manipulation, featuring "clean" (in-domain layouts) and "random" (domain randomization with clutter, textures, illumination, height variations) settings.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Success Rate (SR) for all benchmarks.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **LIBERO Benchmark:**
 - GeoSem-WAM achieved an overall average success rate of **98.55%**.
 - This represents an improvement over the Fast-WAM baseline, which achieved 97.60%, indicating the effectiveness of incorporating explicit geometry and semantic supervision.
 - The model also outperformed various other VLA and WAM methods.

- **RoboTwin 2.0 Dataset:**
 - GeoSem-WAM achieved an average success rate of **92.52%** across 50 tasks in both clean and random settings.
 - This result surpassed the Fast-WAM base model by 0.8% (Fast-WAM: 91.80%) and also outperformed LingBot-VA (92.20%), notably without requiring embodied pre-training.

- **Qualitative Analysis of Latent Representations:**
 - **Semantic Understanding:** Visualizations of intermediate Video DiT token embeddings showed that GeoSem-WAM produced clearer semantic clustering compared to Fast-WAM, suggesting an improved semantic understanding within its latent space.
 - **Geometric Understanding:** A depth probe, trained on frozen backbone intermediate tokens, demonstrated that GeoSem-WAM's latent tokens enabled more accurate depth predictions compared to Fast-WAM, which produced blurrier depth maps. This indicates that the latent representations of GeoSem-WAM are richer in geometry-aware information.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study conducted on the LIBERO benchmark investigated the contribution of each auxiliary supervision branch:
- The RGB-only WAM baseline achieved an average SR of 97.6%.
- Adding only geometry supervision improved the average SR to 98.2% (an increase of +0.61%).
- Adding only semantic supervision resulted in an average SR of 98.1% (an increase of +0.51%).
- Combining both geometry-aware and semantic-aware supervision yielded the highest average SR of **98.6%** (an increase of +1.02%).
These results suggest that both geometric and semantic supervision contribute positively and complementarily to the model's performance, enhancing spatial motion perception and task-level logical reasoning.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments on Franka Emika Panda Robot

Real-world validation was performed on a Franka Emika Panda robot across four manipulation tasks of varying difficulty and two generalization test settings:
- **Core Tasks:** Easy-Pick, Multi-Pick, Multi-Goal, and Pick-Pour.
- **Generalization Tests:** Background generalization (Easy-Pick-B1/B2 with different mat patterns) and height generalization (Easy-Pick-D with an elevated platform).
- **Results:**
 - GeoSem-WAM consistently outperformed the Fast-WAM baseline across all tasks and generalization settings.
 - The overall average success rate improved from 88.9% (Fast-WAM) to **95.4%** (GeoSem-WAM), marking a gain of +6.6%.
 - Significant improvements were observed in generalization tasks: +10% on Easy-Pick-B1, +8% on Easy-Pick-B2, and +12% on Easy-Pick-D, demonstrating enhanced robustness to visual distractions and geometric variations.
 - For multi-object and long-horizon tasks, improvements included +6% on Multi-Pick, +6% on Multi-Goal, and +4% on Pick-Pour, indicating stronger robustness in scenarios requiring complex spatial reasoning and sequential action planning.
 - The single-object Easy-Pick task achieved 100% success rate with both methods, indicating that the enhanced supervision did not degrade basic manipulation capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GeoSem-WAM framework presents a methodological advancement in the field of embodied intelligence, particularly for World Action Models (WAMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Representation Learning:** By incorporating geometry-aware and semantic-aware predictive supervision, GeoSem-WAM enables WAMs to learn more structured and comprehensive latent representations of their environment. This moves beyond the limitations of purely RGB-based prediction, fostering a deeper understanding of spatial geometry, 3D structural consistency, and object-level semantics. The explicit encouragement of these features in the latent space provides a foundation for more nuanced decision-making.

2. **Improved Task Performance and Robustness:** The consistent improvements observed across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin) and real-world robotic manipulation tasks (Franka Emika Panda) demonstrate the practical benefits of the proposed approach. The enhanced robustness to visual distractions, geometric variations, and complex, multi-step scenarios signifies a step towards more reliable and adaptable robotic policies.

3. **Efficient Inference Paradigm:** A critical aspect of GeoSem-WAM is its ability to integrate rich, multi-modal supervision during training while maintaining an efficient inference pipeline. By avoiding explicit future rollout or video generation at test time, the model balances performance gains with practical deployment considerations, addressing a key challenge in scalable robotic systems. This design aligns with ongoing efforts to accelerate VLA inference and makes the framework suitable for real-world applications where latency is a concern.

4. **Clarification of WAM Mechanisms:** The work contributes to the fundamental understanding of why WAMs are effective, emphasizing that their primary benefit stems from the training-phase induction of dynamics-aware latent features through predictive supervision, rather than reliance on explicit future imagination during test time. This clarifies the underlying mechanism and provides guidance for future WAM architecture designs.

5. **Plug-and-Play Modularity:** The design of GeoSem-WAM as a "plug-and-play module" suggests its potential for integration into existing robot manipulation pipelines, offering a way to enhance current WAMs with richer world understanding without requiring a complete architectural overhaul.

Despite its contributions, the paper identifies two limitations that outline avenues for future research:
- **Reliance on Pixel-level Annotations:** The auxiliary DPT head currently depends on pixel-level geometry and semantic annotations, which are scarce in real-world RGB-only datasets and may require noisy pseudo-label generation. Future work will explore leveraging self-supervised features from foundation models (e.g., DINO) to implicitly extract spatial and categorical priors directly from raw RGB videos.
- **Training Complexity and Gradient Conflicts:** Jointly optimizing heterogeneous loss functions increases training complexity and introduces the risk of gradient conflicts. Future efforts aim to integrate gradient deconfliction algorithms, such as gradient surgery, to mitigate task interference.

Overall, GeoSem-WAM contributes to advancing scalable and efficient WAMs by demonstrating that structured geometric and semantic prediction serves as a valuable auxiliary signal for learning richer, more robust representations in embodied intelligence.
