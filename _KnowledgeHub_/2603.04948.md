# $\nabla$-Reasoner: LLM Reasoning via Test-Time Gradient Descent in Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> ∇-Reasoner introduces an iterative generation framework that refines Large Language Model policies during inference by leveraging differentiable optimization over token logits. This method achieves higher accuracy on challenging mathematical reasoning tasks while significantly reducing the number of model calls compared to existing sampling-based approaches.

## Problem

- Existing inference-time LLM reasoning methods rely on zeroth-order search (evaluating reward values) which is sample-inefficient and prone to performance saturation.
- The inherent differentiability of LLMs and reward models is underutilized, as current methods do not exploit gradient information to guide policy refinement.
- A need exists for a more cost-effective approach to enhance LLM reasoning capabilities without requiring extensive re-training.

## Method

- The ∇-Reasoner framework employs ==Differentiable Textual Optimization== (DTO), performing gradient descent on token logits to refine LLM policy during decoding.
- DTO optimizes a loss function combining reward and log-likelihood, using a Gumbel-softmax straight-through estimator to enable gradient propagation through discrete token choices.
- The system integrates DTO into an iterative, token-by-token decoding process, incorporating strategies like gradient caching, trajectory reusing, and confidence- and gradient-guided token selection for efficiency.

## Results

- ∇-Reasoner achieved superior accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, scoring **71.0%** on MATH-500 (Qwen-2.5-**7B**) and **80.4%** (Qwen-2.5-**7B**-Instruct), outperforming all other test-time methods.
- The framework significantly reduced computational cost, cutting model calls by up to **40.2%** compared to sampling-only methods like Self-Consistency and Best-of-N, demonstrating a more efficient performance-cost trade-off.
- Acceleration techniques were highly effective, with gradient caching bypassing over **63.8%** of parallel model calls and token selection avoiding more than **89.2%** of token optimization steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging first-order gradient information from LLMs and reward models at test time offers a more efficient and targeted approach to policy refinement than traditional zeroth-order search methods.
> - The theoretical framework establishes a connection between DTO and KL-regularized reinforcement learning, framing it as 'Deamortized PPO' where test-time gradient descent can achieve optimal policy distributions.
> - Bidirectional gradient propagation within DTO, encompassing prefix, postfix, and reward components, allows for global consistency and effective iterative correction of reasoning steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of ∇-Reasoner directly addresses the contemporary challenge of enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), a critical area of AI research. This work positions itself at the intersection of inference-time computation scaling and differentiable optimization, offering a distinct alternative to prevailing methods.

**Inference-Time Scaling (Zeroth-Order Methods):**
The paper begins by acknowledging the significant improvements in LLM reasoning achieved through scaling model size and training data. However, it highlights a growing trend where scaling *inference-time computation* is proving to be a more cost-effective approach.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting** (Wei et al., 2022b) first demonstrated this by generating intermediate reasoning steps.
- Subsequent methods like **Best-of-N (BoN)** (Stiennon et al., 2020) and **Self-Consistency (SC)** (Wang et al., 2022) scale inference by sampling multiple reasoning paths and selecting the most consistent or highest-reward one.
- More advanced "tree-search" or "planning" approaches, such as **Tree-of-Thought (ToT)** (Yao et al., 2024) and **Reasoning-as-Planning (RAP)** (Hao et al., 2023), cast LLM reasoning as a decision-making problem, using strategic sampling and reward models to explore the sequence space.

The authors categorize these existing methods as **"zeroth-order algorithms"** because they rely solely on evaluating reward *values* from sampled trajectories. This trial-and-error approach, while effective, is often sample-inefficient and struggles with sparse or noisy reward signals, leading to performance saturation.

**Training-Time Alignment:**
Another major paradigm is **training-time alignment**, exemplified by Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and related policy optimization techniques like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) and Group Preference Optimization (GRPO) (Guo et al., 2025). These methods fine-tune LLM parameters based on reward signals, amortizing the optimization cost across many samples during training. While powerful, they require extensive training data and computational resources for fine-tuning.

**Other Related Paradigms:**
- **Inference-time Constrained Decoding:** This area focuses on steering text generation towards specific objectives, often using energy-based models (EBMs) and Langevin dynamics to perform gradient-based optimization in continuous relaxation of the discrete token space (e.g., Qin et al., 2022; Kumar et al., 2021). ∇-Reasoner shares the gradient-based aspect but is specifically tailored for iterative reasoning tasks and aims for greater stability and efficiency.
- **Prompt Optimization:** This involves refining the input prompts to elicit better responses (e.g., Zhou et al., 2022; Pryzant et al., 2023). While also using optimization, these methods operate on the *input* space, whereas ∇-Reasoner focuses on optimizing the *output* reasoning chain directly.
- **Continuous Latent Space Reasoning:** Some emerging work explores iterative refinement within the LLM's *hidden states* or latent representations (e.g., Du et al., 2022; Geiping et al., 2025). ∇-Reasoner distinguishes itself by performing optimization directly in the *output token logit space*, which is a continuous relaxation of the textual output, rather than internal model states.

**∇-Reasoner's Novel Position:**
∇-Reasoner introduces a **"first-order optimization"** paradigm for inference-time scaling. Instead of relying on scalar reward values (zeroth-order), it leverages **gradient information** from both the LLM's likelihood and a reward model to provide directional guidance. This is a significant shift, enabling more efficient and targeted exploration of the reward landscape. The paper theoretically connects its DTO approach to KL-regularized reinforcement learning, framing it as "Deamortized PPO," suggesting that test-time gradient descent in sample space can achieve the same optimal distribution as training-time policy optimization. This bridging of parametric (training) and non-parametric (test-time) inference offers a fresh theoretical perspective.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind ∇-Reasoner are:

1. **Overcoming Inefficiency and Suboptimality of Existing Inference-Time Scaling:** Current inference-time methods for LLM reasoning predominantly rely on discrete search algorithms, extensive sampling, or trial-and-error prompting. These "zeroth-order" approaches are often inefficient, sample-hungry, and prone to performance saturation, especially as reasoning chains become longer and the search space expands.
2. **Leveraging Differentiable Information:** The authors observe that both LLMs and reward models are inherently differentiable. This suggests that gradient signals, which provide rich directional guidance for optimization, are readily available but are currently underutilized by existing methods that only consider scalar reward values. The goal is to exploit this gradient information to guide the search process more effectively.
3. **Refining LLM Policy On-the-Fly:** To improve the quality of generated reasoning steps without explicit retraining, the objective is to develop an iterative generation framework that can "refine the policy on the fly" within the decoding loop. This means dynamically adjusting the LLM's next-token prediction probabilities based on an immediate assessment of the generated sequence's quality.
4. **Achieving Cost-Effective Amplification of LLM Reasoning:** Ultimately, the aim is to create a method that amplifies LLM reasoning capabilities with a superior trade-off between performance and computational cost. By using gradient-based optimization, ∇-Reasoner seeks to be more sample-efficient and leverage parallel compute more effectively than traditional sampling-based methods.
5. **Establishing a New Paradigm:** The paper explicitly seeks to introduce a "paradigm shift from zeroth-order search to first-order optimization at test time," offering a fundamentally different way to approach inference-time LLM reasoning enhancement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

∇-Reasoner is an iterative generation framework that integrates differentiable optimization into the decoding loop to refine the LLM's policy.

#### 3.1. Overview of ∇-Reasoner

The process begins with an initial response generated by the LLM. This response's underlying token logits are then optimized using gradient descent. The updated logits for the first token are used to resample the next token, which is then incorporated into the prefix, and the cycle repeats.

#### 3.2. Differentiable Textual Optimization (DTO)

DTO is the core component. It reformulates the search for high-rewarding responses as a continuous, gradient-based optimization problem over token logits.
- **Objective Function:** The goal is to minimize a loss function `L(y)` that combines a reward term and a log-likelihood regularization term:
 `L(y) := -λr(y|x) - log π_LLM(y|x)`
 Here, `r(y|x)` is the reward for the generated sequence `y` given prompt `x`, `π_LLM(y|x)` is the LLM's log-likelihood for that sequence, and `λ` is a hyperparameter. The reward term guides the sequence towards high-reward regions, while the log-likelihood term ensures fluency and consistency with the base LLM's distribution, preventing "reward hacking."
- **Parameterization for Differentiability:** Since the token space is discrete, DTO uses the pre-softmax logit vectors `z` to parameterize the tokens `y`. To enable gradient propagation through discrete token choices, it employs a **Gumbel-softmax straight-through estimator** during optimization. This allows gradient descent to be performed directly on `z`.
- **Gradient Interpretation:** The gradient `∇_z L` propagates information bidirectionally. It comprises three terms: `δ_prefix` (aligns token with preceding context), `δ_postfix` (propagates information from subsequent tokens for global consistency via attention), and `δ_reward` (transmits sequence-level reward signals back to earlier tokens). This bidirectional flow helps mitigate error accumulation and enables more effective refinement.

#### 3.3. Iterative Decoding with DTO

∇-Reasoner integrates DTO into an iterative, token-by-token decoding process:
1. **Initial Rollout:** Given a prefix `x`, the LLM `π_LLM` generates a full continuation `y^(0)` and its corresponding logits `z^(0)`.
2. **Policy Improvement:** DTO refines `z^(0)` for `T` gradient steps, yielding optimized logits `ẽz`.
3. **Resampling:** The first token of the continuation, `y_1`, is resampled from the updated distribution `softmax(ẽz_1 / τ)`.
4. **Rejection Sampling:** If the resampled token `ẽy_1` differs from the original `y_1`, an additional rollout is performed conditioned on `ẽy_1`. Both the original sequence `y` and the new sequence `ẽy` are evaluated by the reward model. The token (`y_1` or `ẽy_1`) that yields the higher reward is accepted and appended to the prefix `x`. If `ẽy_1` is identical to `y_1`, the original `y_1` is accepted.
5. **Iteration:** The process continues for the next token, with the accepted token incorporated into the prefix.

#### 3.4. Accelerating ∇-Reasoner

To address the computational overhead of iterative optimization and rollouts, ∇-Reasoner incorporates several acceleration strategies:
- **Gradient Caching:** The expensive `∂L/∂y` term (requiring full forward/backward passes through both models) is cached. If the maximal entries in the soft logits `z` (i.e., the chosen tokens `y`) do not change during DTO steps, the cached gradient is reused, significantly reducing redundant computation.
- **Rollout Trajectory Reusing:** Instead of generating a full new rollout for every step, the method reuses `y_≥2` and `z_≥2` from the previously accepted trajectory if the resampled token is rejected. An early stop mechanism also terminates DTO and reverts to standard autoregressive decoding if a maximum number of rollouts (`N_max`) is reached.
- **Confidence- and Gradient-Guided Token Selection:** DTO is selectively applied only to tokens where it is likely to have an impact. This is determined by two criteria: the entropy of the token logits `H(z_1) > ϵ_ent` (low confidence indicates potential for improvement) and the gradient magnitude `∥∇_z1 L∥_2 > ϵ_grad` (sufficient gradient signal for effective update).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

∇-Reasoner demonstrates significant empirical improvements across challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks and exhibits superior computational efficiency compared to strong baselines.

#### 4.1. Performance on Mathematical Reasoning

- **Superior Accuracy:** ∇-Reasoner consistently achieves higher accuracy than all other test-time baselines (Greedy decoding, Self-Consistency (SC), Best-of-N (BoN), Tree-of-Thought (ToT), Reasoning-as-Planning (RAP), TPO).
 - On **Qwen-2.5-7B**, ∇-Reasoner scored 71.0% on MATH-500 and 23.3% on AIME24, outperforming all test-time methods. It even reached performance on par with training-based methods like GRPO (70.8% on MATH-500).
 - With **instruction-tuned models (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct)**, the improvements were even more pronounced. For Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, it achieved 80.4% on MATH-500 and 56.8% on AMC, leading all other approaches. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct also saw 55.8% on MATH-500 and 28.9% on AMC.
- **Qualitative Example:** A simplified example on GSM8K (Appendix D.4) illustrates how ∇-Reasoner iteratively corrects reasoning errors (e.g., changing a multiplication operator to addition, then correcting a subsequent calculation) through DTO-based token revision, leading to the correct answer.

#### 4.2. Computational Cost Efficiency

- **Reduced Model Calls:** ∇-Reasoner achieves its superior performance while significantly reducing the number of model calls. When compared to sampling-only methods like SC and BoN (both set to N=8 samples), ∇-Reasoner reduced model calls by **up to 40.2%** for instruction-tuned models and used about 90% of the calls for base models.
- This efficiency stems from its ability to: (1) start rollouts from the middle of the sequence due to confidence- and gradient-guided selection, and (2) leverage gradient caching and parallel execution of transformers for DTO, which updates multiple tokens within one model call.
- **Wall-Clock Time (Idealized):** Under idealized parallel serving conditions (e.g., vLLM, batching), ∇-Reasoner's total wall-clock time (152.1s for 83 prompts on AMC) remained comparable to BoN (136.1s), despite performing significantly more FLOPs (2.46 × 10^17 vs. 9.54 × 10^15). This indicates high hardware utilization during the DTO phase due to parallel attention execution.

#### 4.3. Test-Time Scaling Law

- The test-time scaling curves (accuracy vs. number of model calls) show that ∇-Reasoner consistently operates on a more efficient frontier. For any given computational budget, ∇-Reasoner achieves higher accuracy compared to BoN and SC across multiple models, demonstrating a superior performance-cost trade-off.

#### 4.4. Algorithm Analysis

- **Reward Model Dependency:** An ablation study showed that ∇-Reasoner's performance is robust to reward model size. Using a smaller Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-4B model instead of the 8B variant resulted in a negligible performance gap (below 1 point) on MATH-500 and AMC, suggesting that smaller, more efficient reward models can be used without significant degradation.
- **Rejection Rate Analysis:** The effectiveness of DTO was confirmed by the significantly lower rejection rate in rejection sampling. While a baseline without DTO (equivalent to BoN) had a theoretical and empirical rejection rate of ~66%, ∇-Reasoner's rejection rate was substantially reduced (e.g., 28.9% for Qwen-2.5-Instruct, 32.8% for Qwen-2.5), indicating that DTO effectively improves the next-token policy to yield higher-reward continuations.
- **Effectiveness of Acceleration Techniques:** The acceleration techniques were shown to dramatically reduce computational costs: gradient caching bypassed >63.8% of parallel model calls, trajectory reusing eliminated >74.1% of autoregressive calls, and token selection avoided >89.2% of token-optimization steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

∇-Reasoner presents a significant advancement in LLM reasoning capabilities, offering both theoretical and practical implications.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Contributions

- **Paradigm Shift:** The work introduces a fundamental shift from "zeroth-order" discrete search to "first-order" gradient-based optimization for inference-time scaling. This provides a more principled and potentially more efficient way to navigate the complex reward landscape of reasoning tasks.
- **Bridging Test-Time and Training-Time Optimization:** The theoretical connection established between DTO and KL-regularized reinforcement learning (specifically, PPO) is profound. By proving that inference-time gradient descent in sample space can generate samples from the optimal distribution achieved by training-time policy optimization ("Deamortized PPO"), the paper unifies two seemingly disparate approaches. This provides a novel mathematical framework for understanding the relationship between parametric (training) and non-parametric (test-time, particle-based) inference, offering new perspectives on how LLMs learn and reason.
- **Bidirectional Gradient Propagation:** The analysis of DTO's gradient components highlights how information propagates bidirectionally along the sequence, enabling global consistency and iterative refinement crucial for complex reasoning, in contrast to purely left-to-right generation.

#### 5.2. Practical Implications

- **Enhanced Reasoning Performance:** ∇-Reasoner significantly boosts LLM accuracy on challenging mathematical reasoning tasks, often outperforming other inference-time methods and matching or nearing the performance of costly training-based approaches. This makes it a highly effective tool for improving LLMs in domains requiring precise logical and mathematical steps.
- **Cost-Effective Scaling:** By leveraging gradients and intelligent acceleration strategies, ∇-Reasoner achieves superior performance with a substantially reduced number of model calls. This "sharper and more efficient scaling paradigm" makes it a more economically viable option for deploying powerful LLM reasoning, especially for organizations with limited training budgets or real-time inference requirements.
- **Adaptive Compute Allocation:** The ability to adaptively allocate compute (e.g., via confidence- and gradient-guided token selection) ensures that optimization efforts are focused where they are most needed, further enhancing efficiency.
- **Generalizability:** The framework, while demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, is conceptually generalizable to other complex reasoning tasks where reward models can provide differentiable feedback.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Dependency on Base and Reward Models:** ∇-Reasoner's performance remains bounded by the capabilities of the underlying base LLM and the quality of the reward model.
- **Shared Vocabulary Requirement:** The method requires the base and reward models to share the same vocabulary to enable end-to-end logit optimization, which might be a constraint for certain model combinations.
- **System Co-design for Efficient Serving:** Integrating ∇-Reasoner into efficient LLM serving pipelines (which are optimized for standard autoregressive decoding) requires more careful system co-design to fully realize the benefits of test-time gradient descent, particularly for real-time applications.
- **Exploration with RL-trained Models:** The authors suggest future work could explore applying ∇-Reasoner to further refine the outputs of LLMs already aligned with RL (e.g., OpenAI's o1 or DeepSeek's R1), which could lead to even greater improvements.

Despite these limitations, ∇-Reasoner represents a compelling new direction for amplifying LLM reasoning, demonstrating that first-order optimization at test time can unlock substantial performance gains with improved efficiency.
