# ChordEdit: One-Step Low-Energy Transport for Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Guangdong University of Technology and collaborators introduced ChordEdit, a training-free and inversion-free framework enabling stable, high-fidelity one-step text-guided image editing on fast text-to-image models. It achieves this by deriving a low-energy "Chord Control Field" from dynamic optimal transport theory, resolving the instability and distortion issues prevalent in naive single-step differential methods, resulting in a runtime of 0.38s for a 2-NFE operation.

## Problem

- Existing one-step text-to-image (T2I) models struggle with stable, high-fidelity text-guided image editing in a single inference step.
- Naive differential editing methods, when applied in one step, generate high-energy, erratic control fields, leading to severe object distortion and loss of consistency in non-edited regions.
- Prior editing methods are either too slow (multi-step, few-step), require extensive training, or rely on computationally expensive per-image inversion procedures, hindering real-time interactive editing.

## Method

- The problem is recast as a dynamic ==optimal transport== (OT) problem, conceptualizing image editing as transporting a source image distribution to a target distribution.
- A "Chord Control Field" (CCF) is derived by minimizing a convex quadratic surrogate function over a short time window, acting as a temporal smoothing operator to produce a stable, low-energy editing field.
- The CCF formulation (utilizing model outputs at two time points) enables stable one-step integration, optionally followed by a single proximal refinement step to amplify target semantics without re-inversion.

## Results

- ChordEdit achieves real-time editing with a runtime of **0.38s** (2-NFE) and low VRAM usage (6988 MiB), significantly outperforming multi-step (e.g., **19x** faster than FlowEdit) and few-step alternatives in efficiency.
- The method demonstrates competitive background preservation (PSNR of 23.89) and strong semantic alignment (CLIP-Edited score of 22.96), with a user study indicating high preference for both aspects.
- Ablation studies confirm the Chord Control Field's stability, showing maintained low energy and high PSNR when integrating in one step, validating its model-agnostic performance across T2I backbones like SD-Turbo and InstaFlow.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive single-step differential editing fails due to the high energy and erratic nature of the control field, leading to severe artifacts and distortions when integrated in a single large step.
> - Framing image editing as a dynamic optimal transport problem allows for the derivation of a theoretically grounded, stable, and low-energy control field through temporal smoothing and L2L_2L2-contraction.
> - The Chord Control Field intrinsically reduces variance and improves numerical stability, making it robust even with a single noise sample and negating the need for costly Monte Carlo averaging.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-guided image editing has seen advancements with the emergence of powerful text-to-image (T2I) models. A significant recent development within this domain is the advent of "one-step" T2I models, such as SD-Turbo, SwiftBrush-v2, and InstaFlow. These models are distilled from larger diffusion models and are designed to synthesize high-quality images with minimal inference steps, offering speed advantages for applications requiring real-time interaction.

However, leveraging these fast one-step models for the nuanced task of text-guided image editing presents specific challenges. Existing image editing methodologies typically fall into several categories, each with limitations regarding real-time, one-step application:
- **Multi-step methods:** These often require iterative inversion of the source image into the model's latent space, followed by multi-step generation guided by the target prompt (e.g., MasaCtrl, Plug-and-Play, FlowEdit). While effective in quality, their iterative nature makes them too slow for real-time interaction, often requiring 30-50 steps.
- **Few-step acceleration methods:** Approaches like TurboEdit and InstantEdit reduce the number of steps (e.g., 4-8 steps) but still fall short of true one-step instant interaction and may retain inversion requirements.
- **Training-free, inversion-free differential methods:** Methods like InfEdit and FlowEdit compute an editing field by differencing conditional drifts. These are generally stable when applied over multiple steps, where iterative averaging smooths out irregularities. However, when these methods are constrained to a single inference step with fast one-step T2I models, they tend to fail. This failure manifests as severe object distortion and a loss of consistency in non-edited regions, attributed to the generation of a high-energy, erratic control field from naive vector arithmetic.
- **Training-based one-step methods:** Approaches such as SwiftEdit achieve one-step performance by training dedicated networks, often for accurate one-step inversion. While fast, this sacrifices model-agnostic flexibility and introduces a training overhead for each new T2I backbone, limiting broad applicability.

ChordEdit positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical gap of achieving high-fidelity, training-free, inversion-free, and model-agnostic one-step editing on fast T2I models. It aims to overcome the instability issues of naive differential methods without requiring additional training or complex inversion procedures, thereby enabling truly real-time interactive editing. The work seeks to provide a theoretically grounded solution to the problem of unstable control fields in single-step generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the rapid image synthesis capabilities of modern one-step text-to-image (T2I) models and their limited utility for text-guided image editing. While models like SD-Turbo and InstaFlow can generate images almost instantly, directly applying existing training-free, inversion-free editing techniques to these models in a single step has proven problematic.

The primary problem identified by the authors is that naive single-step editing, typically performed by differencing the conditional drifts or velocity fields (e.g., $v(\cdot, t, c_{tar}) - v(\cdot, t, c_{src})$), results in a high-energy, erratic, and unstable control field. This instability is exacerbated by the highly non-linear and sensitive mappings within distilled one-step models. When this volatile field is applied in a single, large integration step, it leads to two significant failure modes:
1. **Severe object distortion:** The edited entity within the image becomes warped, losing its recognizable structure or identity.
2. **Critical loss of consistency in non-edited regions:** The background and surrounding elements of the image disintegrate or acquire spurious structures, disrupting the overall photorealism and coherence.

These failures prevent one-step T2I models from fulfilling their potential for real-time interactive editing. The authors are motivated to resolve these issues to enable truly real-time, high-fidelity, and consistent text-guided image editing.

To address these challenges, the key objectives for ChordEdit are:
- **Develop a model-agnostic framework:** The method should operate as a black-box, querying the model's output (e.g., velocity or noise prediction) without requiring modifications to the underlying T2I model architecture. This ensures broad applicability across different fast generative backbones.
- **Ensure training-free operation:** The method should not require any additional training or fine-tuning of networks, which would add computational overhead and compromise flexibility.
- **Implement an inversion-free approach:** The method should avoid computationally expensive per-image inversion processes, which are typical in many multi-step editing pipelines and would hinder real-time performance.
- **Facilitate high-fidelity one-step editing:** The goal is to achieve precise edits that accurately reflect the target text prompt while maintaining visual quality.
- **Derive a stable, low-energy control strategy:** By leveraging dynamic optimal transport theory, the method aims to replace the unstable naive drift difference with a theoretically grounded, smoothed, and variance-reduced editing field. This field should be inherently stable enough to be traversed in a single, large integration step.
- **Preserve non-edited regions:** A critical objective is to ensure that modifications are localized to the intended editing region, preventing unwanted artifacts or disintegration of the background and surrounding structures.
- **Achieve true real-time performance:** By combining one-step operation with computational efficiency, the method aims to provide an interactive editing experience.

By achieving these objectives, ChordEdit aims to establish a new paradigm for real-time generative image editing that is both efficient and robust.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ChordEdit addresses the instability of one-step text-guided image editing by recasting the problem as a dynamic optimal transport (OT) problem and deriving a stabilized control field. The approach is model-agnostic, training-free, and inversion-free.

**Problem Formulation as Optimal Transport:**
The core idea is to conceptualize image editing as transporting a source image distribution (defined by the source text prompt $c_{src}$) to a target image distribution (defined by the target text prompt $c_{tar}$). This transport is driven by an unknown, ideal editing vector field $u_t(x)$ that minimizes kinetic energy, as formulated by the Benamou–Brenier dynamic OT problem. The authors define a transport density $\rho_t(x)$ evolving from $\rho_1 = p_1(x|c_{src})$ to $\rho_0 = p_0(x|c_{tar})$, governed by the continuity equation $\partial_t \rho_t(x) + \nabla_x \cdot (\rho_t(x) u_t(x)) = 0$.

**Observable Model and Proxy Field:**
Practical T2I models do not directly expose the ideal $u_t(x)$. Instead, they provide observable outputs $Q(z, t, c)$ (e.g., noise prediction $\hat{\epsilon}_\theta$, velocity prediction $v_\theta$) at a synthetic noisy state $z$ and time $t$. To bridge this, a time-dependent linear map $B_t$ is introduced to convert the model's output into a unified velocity unit. For instance, for noise-prediction models, $B_t(\hat{\epsilon}) = A_t^{(\epsilon)}\hat{\epsilon}$, where $A_t^{(\epsilon)}$ is a coefficient derived from the noise schedule.
The observable proxy field $R(x_\tau, t)$ is then defined as the expectation of the difference in mapped observable outputs between target and source prompts, conditioned on an anchor image $x_\tau$ (typically $x_{src}$) and a forward noising kernel $K_t$:
$R(x_\tau, t) = E_{z \sim K_t(\cdot|x_\tau)} [B_t (Q(z, t, c_{tar}) - Q(z, t, c_{src}))]$.
This $R(x_\tau, t)$ is treated as a noisy measurement of the true field $u_t(x_\tau)$, corrupted by zero-mean noise $\epsilon_t$.

**Chord Control Field (CCF) Derivation:**
The naive use of $R(x_\tau, t)$ as the control field is unstable due to its high-energy and erratic nature. To stabilize this, ChordEdit seeks a locally smoothed, low-energy estimator $\hat{u}_t$ for $u_t$. This is achieved by minimizing a strictly convex quadratic surrogate function $\Phi_t(u; x_\tau)$ over a short time window $[t-\delta, t]$. This objective balances a recursive energy prior (derived from the estimate at $t-\delta$) with agreement to the new measurements $R(x_\tau, \xi)$ over the window.
Setting the gradient of $\Phi_t$ to zero yields an exact minimizer:
$u_t^\star(x_\tau) = \frac{t}{t+\delta} \hat{u}_{t-\delta}(x_\tau) + \frac{1}{t+\delta} \int_{t-\delta}^t R(x_\tau, \xi) d\xi$.
For practical, one-step implementation, first-order causal approximations are applied: $\hat{u}_{t-\delta}(x_\tau) \approx R(x_\tau, t-\delta)$ and $\int_{t-\delta}^t R(x_\tau, \xi) d\xi \approx \delta R(x_\tau, t)$. This results in the practical Chord Control Field (CCF):
$\hat{u}_t(x_\tau) = \frac{t R(x_\tau, t-\delta) + \delta R(x_\tau, t)}{t+\delta}$.
This formulation acts as a temporal smoothing operator. The authors demonstrate that this averaging provides numerical stability by acting as an $L_2$-contraction, suppressing high-energy spikes. It reduces the $L_\infty$-norms of the field, its time derivative, and its spatial gradient, which directly tightens the consistency proxy for explicit Euler integration. This, in turn, reduces the local truncation error and improves the global error bound for a single, large integration step.

**Proximal Refinement (Optional):**
Following the one-step transport using the Chord Control Field, an optional proximal refinement step is introduced. This step involves a single forward pass with the target prompt $c_{tar}$ at a specific time $t_c$, using the image predicted from the transport step ($x_{pred}$). Its purpose is to amplify target semantics, especially for challenging edits, without requiring re-inversion. This modular design separates structure-preserving transport from semantic enhancement.

**Algorithm Summary (Algorithm 1):**
1. **Input:** Source image $x_{src}$, prompts $c_{src}, c_{tar}$, step time $t$, window $\delta$, step scale $\lambda$, optional proximal refinement time $t_c$.
2. **Initialize:** $x_{in} \leftarrow x_{src}$.
3. **Compute Chord Control Field:** Calculate $\hat{u}$ using the formula $\frac{t R(x_{in}, t-\delta) + \delta R(x_{in}, t)}{t+\delta}$. This involves querying the T2I model's observable outputs $Q$ for both $c_{src}$ and $c_{tar}$ at $t$ and $t-\delta$, mapping them to velocity units via $B_t$, and then averaging.
4. **Transport Step:** Update the image: $x_{pred} \leftarrow x_{in} + \lambda \hat{u}$.
5. **Proximal Refinement (Optional):** Apply $x_{tar} \leftarrow prox(x_{pred}, t_c, c_{tar})$.
6. **Output:** Edited image $x_{tar}$.
The authors note that all intermediate variables for $\hat{u}$ are parallel-computable within one batch, making the transport a 1-NFE (Number of Function Evaluations) operation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating ChordEdit's performance, stability, and efficiency compared to prior methods and against its naive baseline.

**Quantitative Performance:**
The evaluation was conducted on the PIE-bench dataset (512x512 images, 700 samples across 10 editing categories), using metrics for background fidelity (PSNR, MSE, SSIM, LPIPS on non-edited regions) and semantic alignment (CLIP-Whole, CLIP-Edited).
- **Efficiency:** ChordEdit (NFE=2, including optional refinement) achieved a runtime of 0.38s and a VRAM usage of 6988 MiB on SwiftBrush-v2 or SD-Turbo. This is significantly faster than multi-step methods (e.g., 19x faster than FlowEdit, 208x faster than Direct Inversion+MasaCtrl) and few-step methods (at least 3.4x faster than the fastest alternative). It also uses less than half the VRAM of SwiftEdit (15060 MiB) on the same model.
- **Consistency and Semantics:** ChordEdit demonstrates competitive performance in both background preservation and semantic fidelity.
 - For background preservation, ChordEdit (Ours w/o prox, SD-Turbo) achieved a PSNR of 23.89, outperforming most other methods, including multi-step and few-step editors.
 - For semantic alignment, ChordEdit (Ours, SD-Turbo) achieved a CLIP-Edited score of 22.96, competitive with or superior to many other methods.
 - The results show that ChordEdit (w/o prox), operating at 1 NFE, prioritizes background preservation (high PSNR, low MSE/LPIPS), while the full ChordEdit (2 NFE, with refinement) balances this with enhanced semantic alignment (higher CLIP scores).

**Qualitative Results:**
Visual comparisons (Figure 7 and Figure 8) support the quantitative findings.
- **Structure Preservation:** ChordEdit consistently preserves the background and non-edited regions, avoiding the artifacts, distortions, or background breakup observed in the naive simple drifts baseline (Figure 3, Figure 8).
- **Semantic Adherence:** Edited images generated by ChordEdit adhere well to the target text prompts, producing photorealistic and faithful changes. This contrasts with some multi-step methods that introduce artifacts or identity failures, and naive one-step methods that lead to severe object distortion (Figure 3).
- **User Study:** A user study (details in Appendix) indicated that participants overwhelmingly preferred ChordEdit for both editing semantics (42.5% preference) and background preservation (48.3% preference), reinforcing its perceived quality.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Chord Control Field (CCF) Stability:** Ablating the temporal smoothing interval $\delta$ confirmed the hypothesis. When $\delta=0$ (naive baseline), the editing field's energy spikes dramatically as the integration step count $S \to 1$, leading to a collapse in background consistency (PSNR). In contrast, ChordEdit with $\delta > 0$ (e.g., $\delta=0.15$) maintains low energy and high PSNR, demonstrating its inherent stability for single-step integration (Figure 9). The CCF also strictly Pareto-dominates the naive baseline across perceptual-semantic trade-off metrics (Figure 9, bottom; Figure 13).
- **Effect of Noise Samples (n):** Experiments show that increasing the number of Monte Carlo noise samples (n) beyond one yields negligible marginal returns for ChordEdit. The n=1 configuration is already stable and robust, consistently outperforming the naive baseline (even with naive's n=4 samples). This indicates that the geometric control and intrinsic variance reduction of the CCF achieve precision without relying on costly MC averaging (Figure 10, Figure 11, Figure 14).
- **Transport and Refinement Contributions:** Analysis (Table 2) demonstrates that the Chord field (w/o prox, 1 NFE) is primarily responsible for consistency and structure preservation (high PSNR). The optional proximal refinement step (adding 1 NFE) significantly boosts semantic alignment (CLIP-Edited score) at a controlled cost to preservation. This confirms the modularity of the framework.
- **Model Agnosticism:** ChordEdit consistently outperforms its naive baseline across different fast T2I models, including InstaFlow, SwiftBrush-v2, and SD-Turbo (Table 3). This validates the method's claim of being model-agnostic, providing performance gains regardless of the specific underlying T2I backbone.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis:** Detailed analysis of $t$, $\lambda$, and $t_c$ (Figure 15, Figure 18) shows that these parameters offer predictable trade-offs between semantic strength and background preservation, allowing for principled tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ChordEdit contributes to the field of generative image editing by addressing a fundamental limitation in the application of fast, one-step text-to-image (T2I) models for interactive editing.

**Technological Advancement:** The paper introduces a principled approach grounded in dynamic optimal transport theory to stabilize the control field for one-step image editing. By replacing naive, high-energy drift differencing with a temporally smoothed, low-energy "Chord Control Field," it resolves the core instability that causes severe distortions and loss of consistency in prior training-free, inversion-free single-step methods. This represents a significant step towards robust single-step control in highly non-linear generative models.

**Efficiency and Accessibility:** ChordEdit achieves state-of-the-art efficiency, with exceptionally low runtime (0.38s for a 2-NFE operation) and VRAM usage. This makes true real-time, interactive image editing feasible on more accessible hardware, lowering the barrier for creative professionals and general users. Its training-free and inversion-free nature further reduces computational overhead and setup complexity.

**Robustness and Flexibility:** The method's model-agnostic design ensures compatibility with various fast T2I backbones (e.g., SD-Turbo, SwiftBrush-v2, InstaFlow) without requiring model-specific retraining or fine-tuning. Furthermore, its demonstrated robustness to random noise seeds, even with a single noise sample (n=1), underscores its reliability and practicality, avoiding the need for costly Monte Carlo averaging typically required for variance reduction.

**Practical Implications for Creative Workflows:** By enabling rapid, high-fidelity edits that preserve non-edited regions, ChordEdit can significantly enhance creative workflows. Designers, artists, and content creators can use it for rapid prototyping, quick conceptualization, and iterative refinement of images, allowing for immediate visualization of diverse semantic changes. This fosters greater creative exploration and reduces the time and resources traditionally associated with complex image manipulation.

**Theoretical Grounding:** The derivation of the Chord Control Field from optimal transport theory provides a theoretical basis for its stability and energy-minimizing properties. This formal grounding differentiates it from heuristic approaches and offers insights into designing more robust control mechanisms for generative models.

**Societal Considerations:** The authors acknowledge the potential for misuse inherent in high-fidelity generative models. The ability to create realistic and consistent edits in real-time carries risks such as generating deceptive content or facilitating misinformation. Additionally, the method inherits any societal biases present in the underlying pre-trained T2I models, which could be reflected or amplified in the edits. The authors state their intent for the work to be used for creative and assistive applications and emphasize the importance of developing robust detection tools for synthetic media and fostering public media literacy.
