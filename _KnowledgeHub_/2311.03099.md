# Language Models are Super Mario: Absorbing Abilities from Homologous Models as a Free Lunch

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper unveils extreme redundancy in Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) delta parameters of large language models, proposing DARE (Drop And REscale) to eliminate up to 99% of these parameters without retraining. Applying DARE significantly enhances model merging performance, enabling the creation of multi-competent models that can even surpass individual specialized source models.

## Problem

- High computational costs and resource demands for developing and deploying diverse capabilities in large language models (LLMs).
- The implicit redundancy within parameters modified by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- Parameter interference issues encountered when attempting to merge multiple task-specific LLMs into a single, versatile model.

## Method

- Defining ==SFT delta parameters== as the difference between fine-tuned and pre-trained weights, focusing on the specific changes introduced by fine-tuning.
- Introducing ==DARE (Drop And REscale)==, a two-step technique to randomly drop a proportion of delta parameters and then rescale the remaining ones, operating solely on CPUs without requiring retraining.
- Integrating DARE as a ==pre-processing step for model merging== frameworks to mitigate parameter interference and enhance the performance of composite models.

## Results

- DARE effectively removes up to **99%** of SFT delta parameters from large language models with negligible performance degradation, demonstrating extreme redundancy.
- Application of DARE consistently improves various model merging methods, with merged models showing potential to surpass the performance of individual source models (e.g., up to **19.57%** improvement on MBPP over the code-specific model).
- A **7B** parameter merged model, 'supermario v2', created using DARE, achieved the top ranking on the Open LLM Leaderboard for its size class as of January 2024.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) delta parameters exhibit extreme redundancy, allowing for 90-99% removal with minimal performance impact.
> - The rescale operation in DARE is critical for preserving model performance by maintaining original embedding approximations after parameters are dropped.
> - SFT primarily unlocks pre-existing capabilities within LLMs rather than introducing entirely new knowledge, as evidenced by catastrophic performance loss when fine-tuned parameters are removed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses critical challenges and explores novel opportunities within several interconnected research domains concerning large language models (LLMs):

- **Large Language Models (LLMs) and Fine-Tuning:** The proliferation of powerful pre-trained LLMs has made supervised fine-tuning (SFT) the de facto standard for adapting these general models to specific tasks. This work scrutinizes the outcome of SFT, specifically the "delta parameters" – the difference between fine-tuned and pre-trained model weights. It challenges the implicit assumption that all changes introduced by SFT are equally important, instead revealing and exploiting significant redundancy. This contributes to a deeper understanding of how SFT modifies and unlocks capabilities within LLMs.

- **Network Pruning:** Traditionally, network pruning techniques aim to reduce the size and computational cost of neural networks by removing redundant weights, often requiring a retraining or fine-tuning phase to recover performance. This paper introduces a novel pruning-like approach, DARE, that operates specifically on *delta parameters* rather than the entire model weights, and critically, *does so without any retraining or GPUs*. This distinguishes DARE from most existing pruning methods (e.g., magnitude-based pruning) that typically target fine-tuned parameters and often necessitate computationally intensive retraining cycles. By demonstrating high sparsity in delta parameters, the paper suggests that the knowledge imparted by SFT is concentrated in a remarkably small subset of parameter changes.

- **Model Merging:** A rapidly evolving field, model merging seeks to combine multiple task-specific models (often fine-tuned from the same backbone) into a single, versatile model capable of handling diverse tasks. This offers an attractive alternative to multi-task learning, which requires joint training on all tasks and access to original training data. Existing merging techniques, such as Average Merging, Task Arithmetic, Fisher Merging, RegMean, and TIES-Merging, often face challenges related to parameter interference, where changes beneficial for one task might negatively impact another when simply combined. This paper positions DARE as a "versatile plug-in" for these methods, proposing that sparsifying delta parameters *before* merging can significantly mitigate interference and improve the overall performance and capabilities of the composite model. This is a crucial advancement for practical deployment, as it enables the aggregation of specialized LLMs without re-training or sacrificing individual abilities.

In essence, this research bridges the gap between understanding SFT's parameter modifications, efficient model compression, and effective multi-task model creation. It suggests a paradigm shift where fine-tuned capabilities are seen as sparse "add-ons" that can be easily manipulated and combined, rather than dense, indivisible modifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's core objectives and motivations stem from the high computational costs and resource demands associated with developing and deploying large language models, especially when attempting to imbue them with diverse capabilities.

The **primary motivation** is to find a "free lunch" – a highly efficient and low-cost method to enhance and combine the capabilities of LLMs without resorting to expensive retraining or powerful hardware like GPUs. This desire is aptly encapsulated by the "Super Mario" analogy, where the protagonist absorbs new powers effortlessly.

Specifically, the **key objectives** are:

1. **To unveil and quantify the redundancy in Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) delta parameters:** The authors hypothesize that the changes introduced by SFT (delta parameters) are highly redundant. A key objective is to empirically demonstrate this redundancy, showing that a significant portion of these parameters can be eliminated without compromising performance.
2. **To propose an innovative, resource-efficient method for delta parameter reduction:** The paper aims to introduce DARE (Drop And REscale), a simple yet effective technique that can sparsify SFT delta parameters. A critical aspect of this objective is to prove that DARE can achieve high sparsity (e.g., 90% or even 99% elimination) while maintaining the original model's performance, crucially without requiring any retraining or GPUs.
3. **To integrate DARE into existing model merging frameworks to enhance versatility and performance:** The authors aim to demonstrate that by applying DARE *before* merging, the inherent parameter interference issues in combining multiple task-specific LLMs can be mitigated. The goal is to create a single, unified model that effectively retains all the diverse abilities of its source models, potentially even surpassing the performance of individual specialized models, through low-cost parameter fusion.
4. **To provide theoretical and empirical insights into SFT and parameter characteristics:** Beyond practical application, the paper seeks to deepen the understanding of how SFT works, why certain parameter modifications are redundant, and the conditions under which DARE is effective. This includes analyzing the value ranges of delta parameters and distinguishing them from full fine-tuned parameters.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to significantly lower the barrier to creating versatile, high-performing LLMs, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and efficient for researchers and practitioners alike.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around the concept of "delta parameters" and a novel operation called DARE, which is then applied within established model merging frameworks.

- **Defining SFT Delta Parameters:** The foundation of the methodology is the precise definition of delta parameters (δ_t = θ_t_SFT - θ_PRE). This represents the direct change in parameters induced by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for a specific task `t`, starting from a pre-trained backbone θ_PRE. By focusing on these differences, the authors aim to isolate the task-specific modifications.

- **DARE (Drop And REscale) for Delta Parameter Reduction:**
 - **Conceptual Simplicity:** DARE is described as a two-step process: random dropping and rescaling.
 - **Drop:** Given a set of delta parameters δ_t, DARE randomly selects a proportion `p` of these parameters and sets their values to zero. This is a form of unstructured sparsity.
 - **Rescale:** The crucial second step involves rescaling the *remaining* non-zero delta parameters by a factor of 1/(1-p). This ensures that, in expectation, the sum of the modified delta parameters remains the same as the original, thereby preserving the original embedding approximation.
 - **Theoretical Justification:** The paper provides a theoretical analysis focusing on linear transformations, a fundamental operation in neural networks. It demonstrates that by setting the rescaling factor to 1/(1-p), the expectation of the output embedding after DARE (E[ĥ_i]) equals that of the original embedding (E[h_i]), thus preserving the model's functionality.
 - **Distinction from Dropout:** The authors explicitly differentiate DARE from Dropout: DARE permanently modifies *parameters* (specifically delta parameters) for inference without training, while Dropout temporarily zeroes *activations* during training to prevent overfitting.

- **Merging Models with DARE:**
 - **Pre-processing for Merging:** Instead of directly merging the SFT models (θ_t_SFT), the methodology proposes applying DARE to each model's delta parameters (δ_t) first, resulting in sparsified delta parameters (δ̂_t). These sparsified delta parameters are then combined with the original pre-trained backbone (θ_t_DARE = δ̂_t + θ_PRE).
 - **Integration with Existing Methods:** DARE is presented as a plug-and-play module. Once delta parameters are sparsified using DARE, any existing model merging method (e.g., Average Merging, Task Arithmetic, Fisher Merging, RegMean, TIES-Merging) can be applied to fuse these DARE-processed parameters. The paper specifically shows how DARE modifies the Task Arithmetic equation.
 - **Hypothesis for Merging:** The underlying hypothesis is that by eliminating the redundant delta parameters (making them sparser), DARE reduces potential "parameter interference" when combining multiple task-specific models, thereby improving the merged model's ability to retain and blend diverse capabilities.

- **Experimental Design:**
 - **Model Architectures:** Experiments are conducted on both encoder-based LMs (BERT, RoBERTa on GLUE benchmark) and decoder-based LMs (various LLaMA/Llama 2/Code Llama variants like WizardLM, WizardMath, WizardCoder). This broad coverage demonstrates the generality of DARE.
 - **Tasks and Datasets:** A wide range of NLP tasks are used, including instruction-following (AlpacaEval), mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH), code generation (HumanEval, MBPP), and general language understanding (GLUE tasks).
 - **Comparative Analysis:**
 - **DARE vs. SFT:** Performance comparison across varying drop rates (`p`) to show redundancy.
 - **DARE vs. DropOnly:** Emphasizes the crucial role of the rescaling step by comparing DARE with a variant that only drops parameters.
 - **DARE vs. Magnitude-based Pruning (MP):** Compares DARE with a common pruning technique adapted to delta parameters, without retraining.
 - **DARE in Merging Context:** Compares baseline merging methods against their DARE-enhanced counterparts.
 - **Parameter Analysis:** Investigates the value ranges of delta parameters under different fine-tuning conditions (e.g., standard SFT vs. continuous pre-training effects) and the impact of dropping *fine-tuned* parameters versus *delta* parameters.
 - **Real-world Validation:** Submission of merged 7B LMs (supermario v1/v2) to the Open LLM Leaderboard for external validation.
 - **Resource Efficiency:** Emphasizes that all benefits are achieved solely using CPUs and without retraining, highlighting the "free lunch" aspect.

This systematic approach allows the authors to thoroughly explore the properties of SFT delta parameters, validate DARE's effectiveness, and demonstrate its practical utility in model merging scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several significant findings that challenge conventional wisdom and open new avenues for LLM development:

1. **Extreme Redundancy in SFT Delta Parameters:**
 - A striking discovery is that SFT delta parameters exhibit "extreme redundancy" across both encoder- and decoder-based LMs. DARE can effectively eliminate a staggering **90% or even 99%** of these delta parameters with only minimal impact on the model's performance.
 - The tolerance for higher drop rates (`p`) increases with the scale of the LLM; larger models (e.g., WizardMath-70B) can withstand higher sparsity (e.g., 99%) while smaller ones might fail at such extreme rates, indicating a connection to scaling laws.
 - The absolute values of SFT delta parameters are typically very small, often within **0.002**, suggesting that SFT makes subtle, highly efficient modifications to the pre-trained model.

2. **Crucial Role of the Rescale Operation in DARE:**
 - The rescale operation (`1/(1-p)`) in DARE is essential for maintaining performance. Experiments with "DropOnly" (dropping without rescaling) show a sharp decline in performance and significant deviation in embedding similarities, validating DARE's theoretical premise of approximating original embeddings. DARE maintains cosine similarities of embeddings above 0.95 even with 90% parameter removal.

3. **Superiority of DARE over Magnitude-based Pruning (MP) for Delta Parameters:**
 - When adapted to operate on delta parameters without retraining, DARE consistently outperforms Magnitude-based Pruning (MP). This suggests that randomly dropping and rescaling is more effective for delta parameters than simply removing those with the smallest absolute values, which might inadvertently discard important but low-magnitude changes.

4. **DARE Significantly Enhances Model Merging Performance, Even Surpassing Source Models:**
 - DARE consistently improves the performance of various existing model merging methods (Average Merging, Task Arithmetic, Fisher Merging, RegMean, TIES-Merging) for both encoder- and decoder-based LMs.
 - **Novel Discovery:** For decoder-based LMs, the merged models, especially when DARE is applied, show the *potential to surpass the performance of any single source LM*. For instance, Task Arithmetic with DARE improved LM & Math & Code by 3.10% on AlpacaEval over LM, 3.18% on GSM8K over Math, and 19.57% on MBPP over Code. This "synergistic" improvement is a breakthrough in model merging.
 - The improvements are more pronounced in larger decoder-based LMs compared to encoder-based LMs, potentially due to the greater capacity and plasticity of larger models.
 - Practical success: A 7B parameter merged LM (supermario v2), created using DARE, achieved the **top-ranking position on the Open LLM Leaderboard** (as of January 28, 2024), demonstrating real-world applicability and state-of-the-art performance for its size class.

5. **Conditions for DARE's Applicability:**
 - DARE is effective when the absolute values of SFT delta parameters are relatively small (e.g., within 0.002).
 - However, if a model undergoes significant architectural changes or continuous pre-training (e.g., Code Llama fine-tuned from Llama 2 rather than CodeLlama, leading to delta parameters around 0.03), DARE becomes infeasible. This indicates DARE thrives in scenarios where SFT makes incremental, rather than foundational, changes to the pre-trained weights.

6. **SFT Unlocks, Not Introduces, Capabilities:**
 - Crucially, dropping even a small percentage (e.g., 10%) of *fine-tuned parameters* (the sum of pre-trained and delta parameters) leads to a catastrophic performance collapse (approaching zero). This finding strongly supports the notion that SFT primarily **unlocks inherent abilities** within the pre-trained LLM rather than introducing entirely new capabilities from scratch. Decoder-based LMs are found to be more susceptible to fine-tuned parameter removal than encoder-based LMs.

All these findings collectively support the core claim that DARE provides a "free lunch" for acquiring and combining LLM abilities by efficiently exploiting the redundancy in SFT delta parameters.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in this paper have significant theoretical and practical implications for the future of large language models:

- **Theoretical Significance and Deeper Understanding of SFT:**
 - The discovery of "extreme redundancy" in SFT delta parameters fundamentally alters our understanding of how fine-tuning works. It suggests that SFT achieves its remarkable effects by subtly re-tuning or emphasizing existing pathways within the pre-trained model rather than by densely re-writing its entire parameter space.
 - The distinction drawn between delta parameters and fine-tuned parameters, and the catastrophic impact of dropping the latter, reinforces the idea that pre-trained LLMs are rich knowledge bases, and SFT acts as an "unlocking mechanism."
 - This work paves the way for further research into the intrinsic nature of SFT-induced changes, potentially leading to more targeted and efficient fine-tuning methods.

- **Practical Significance and Resource Efficiency:**
 - **Cost Reduction:** The most immediate impact is the dramatic reduction in computational resources required for enhancing and combining LLM capabilities. By enabling the removal of 90-99% of delta parameters and facilitating model merging *without retraining or GPUs*, DARE offers an incredibly cost-effective approach. This democratizes access to advanced LLM customization, as it no longer requires massive compute clusters.
 - **Scalability and Deployment:** For organizations deploying many task-specific LLMs, DARE offers a way to create a single, multi-competent model. This simplifies model management, reduces memory footprint, and streamlines inference, making LLM deployment more scalable and efficient.
 - **Faster Prototyping and Iteration:** Researchers and developers can quickly experiment with combining different task-specific abilities without the time and resource overhead of traditional multi-task learning or full fine-tuning.
 - **Community Adoption:** The rapid integration of DARE into popular open-source toolkits like Hugging Face's PEFT and Arcee-AI's MergeKit, as well as its contribution to hundreds of community-created models on Hugging Face, unequivocally demonstrates its immediate and widespread practical value to the NLP community. The achievement of the top rank on the Open LLM Leaderboard for a 7B merged model is a testament to its real-world efficacy.

- **Advancement in Model Merging:**
 - The novel discovery that DARE-enhanced merged models can *surpass* the performance of individual source models is a breakthrough for the model merging field. This implies a synergistic effect where the combined, sparsified knowledge from multiple tasks can create a more robust and capable model than its components, potentially addressing limitations of prior merging approaches.
 - This could lead to a new paradigm in foundation model adaptation, where specialized models are not ends in themselves but rather building blocks for even more capable, versatile general-purpose models.

- **Future Research Directions:**
 - The paper opens several promising avenues for future research, including a deeper theoretical analysis to infer upper bounds of `p` based on LM capacities, and further investigation into the fundamental differences between fine-tuned and delta parameters.
 - Exploring how DARE could be combined with other parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods (e.g., LoRA) or quantization techniques could lead to even greater efficiency.

In conclusion, "Language Models are Super Mario" provides a compelling argument for the vast untapped potential within LLM parameters. By revealing the extreme redundancy of SFT delta parameters and offering DARE as an elegant, computationally inexpensive solution for their management and fusion, this work offers a significant "free lunch" that will profoundly impact the development, deployment, and understanding of large language models in the coming years.
