# Online Adaptive Policy Selection in Time-Varying Systems: No-Regret via Contractive Perturbations

> [!summary] Summary
> An online adaptive policy selection algorithm, GAPS, is introduced along with a novel contractive perturbation framework for time-varying discrete-time dynamical systems. This method achieves an optimal O(sqrt(T)) adaptive policy regret in convex settings, provides the first local regret bounds for nonconvex settings, and operates with O(log T) computational complexity per step.

## Problem

- Achieving optimal regret bounds for online adaptive policy selection in time-varying systems, where previous methods often yielded suboptimal O(sqrt(T) log T) regret.
- High computational demands and information requirements (e.g., oracle access, re-simulation) of existing online control algorithms.
- Limited applicability of prior theoretical guarantees, primarily restricted to specific policy classes and convex system dynamics.

## Method

- The **GAPS** (==Gradient-based Adaptive Policy Selection==) algorithm, which approximates ideal ==Online Gradient Descent (OGD)== by computing gradients along the actual executed trajectory.
- Efficient gradient estimation by ==truncating historical dependence== to a finite buffer length, avoiding expensive re-simulations and requiring only on-trajectory partial derivatives.
- A general analytical framework based on an ==ε-Time-varying Contractive Perturbation== property, which characterizes how past policy decisions' impact decays over time.

## Results

- GAPS achieved an optimal **O(√T)** adaptive policy regret for systems with convex surrogate costs, resolving the **O(log T)** gap found in prior online control literature.
- The work established the **first local regret bound of O((1+V)^(1/2) √T)** for online policy selection in nonconvex settings, where V is the system's variation intensity.
- Numerical experiments demonstrated GAPS's ability to adapt quickly to sudden environmental changes, outperforming baselines in model predictive control and time-varying nonlinear systems, while operating with **O(log T)** matrix multiplications per step.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "contractive perturbation" property effectively bridges online policy selection to online optimization, enabling the derivation of strong regret guarantees.
> - Computing gradients along the actual system trajectory, rather than through hypothetical re-simulations, provides a computationally efficient path to online adaptation.
> - By bounding approximation errors, GAPS can achieve regret performance comparable to ideal online optimization methods, even in complex time-varying and nonconvex systems.
