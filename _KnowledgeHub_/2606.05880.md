# TAGA: Terrain-aware Active Gaze Learning for Generalizable Agile Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Singapore, Zhejiang University, and South China University of Technology developed TAGA, a framework for agile humanoid locomotion that integrates multimodal perception with an emergent active gaze mechanism. The system enabled a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to traverse a 120 cm gap, representing a 50% increase over previous reported maximums for perceptive humanoids, while maintaining stability across diverse challenging terrains.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion in complex terrains requires balancing wide perceptual coverage for anticipatory planning with precise local geometry understanding for reliable foot placement.
- Existing perceptive methods struggle with computational costs of broad coverage or miss critical local terrain information directly beneath the robot.
- Effectively integrating complementary long-range visual cues with accurate local geometric details has been a challenge, with many approaches loosely combining or relying on single sources.

## Method

- **TAGA** integrates egocentric depth vision (for long-range preview), height scans (for precise local geometry), and proprioception into a unified sensing and control pipeline.
- It introduces an emergent, anticipatory ==active gaze== module that dynamically predicts a task-relevant ==Region of Interest== (ROI) within the local height scan, guiding the policy's attention.
- The system employs a hierarchical neural network architecture with visuomotor fusion, a ==Mixture-of-Experts== action decoder, and is trained using PPO with Adversarial Motion Priors and extensive domain randomization.

## Results

- The TAGA framework achieved a **120 cm** gap traversal distance on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, surpassing previous reported maximums for perceptive humanoids by **50%**.
- It demonstrated robust and generalizable locomotion across diverse challenging terrains, including sparse stepping stones (**70 cm** spacing), narrow beams, stairs, and unstructured outdoor environments, even under perceptual disturbances and external pushes.
- The active gaze module enabled performance comparable to processing a full height scan but with **65.2%** lower training cost, while significantly outperforming fixed-gaze policies on tasks requiring distant foothold planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An anticipatory, task-relevant active gaze mechanism can emerge purely through reinforcement learning, without explicit supervision, dynamically focusing perception to improve information density and decision-making.
> - Multimodal fusion of depth vision and local height scans provides complementary information crucial for both long-range anticipatory planning and precise foothold placement in discontinuous terrains.
> - Adversarial Motion Priors contribute to more natural, coordinated, and dynamically stable locomotion, which enhances robustness to sim-to-real variations and real-world disturbances.
