# Does Spatial Cognition Emerge in Frontier Models?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Apple introduced SPACE, a new benchmark to systematically evaluate spatial cognition in frontier large language and multimodal models. Their findings demonstrate that these models consistently perform significantly below human levels on classic spatial tests, particularly when faced with complex or immersive spatial scenarios.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) show impressive performance in various cognitive tasks, leading to broad characterizations of their general intelligence.
- Spatial cognition, a foundational form of intelligence universally present in biological systems and linked to embodiment, has been comparatively under-evaluated in disembodied frontier AI models.
- A need exists for a rigorous, cognitively-informed benchmark to diagnose the presence and limitations of spatial reasoning in advanced AI models.

## Method

- Developed **SPACE** (==Spatial Perception And Cognition Evaluation==), a novel benchmark built on decades of cognitive science research and classic animal cognition experiments.
- **SPACE** includes two broad categories: Large-Scale Spatial Cognition (e.g., direction/distance estimation, map sketching, navigation) and Small-Scale Spatial Cognition (e.g., ==mental rotation==, ==perspective taking==, maze completion).
- Tasks are presented in diverse modalities, including egocentric images, discrete map images, and discrete map text arrays, to evaluate both multimodal and text-only models.

## Results

- Frontier models performed near chance level on most large-scale spatial cognition tasks, particularly with egocentric (first-person) observations, significantly below human performance (e.g., **80-100%** for humans vs. near chance for models).
- Even with simplified allocentric (map-like) views, models struggled with navigation tasks like route retracing and shortcut discovery, achieving less than **30%** Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).
- While some LLMs showed relative strength in textual Selective Attention (over **95%** accuracy) and visuospatial working memory tasks, multimodal models performed poorly (near chance) on visual mental rotation and perspective taking, confirming a lack of genuine spatial reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier models possess a fundamentally different intelligence profile, excelling in abstract domains while demonstrating severe deficiencies in foundational spatial cognition, a pattern unlike biological intelligence.
> - The SPACE benchmark acts as a crucial diagnostic tool, revealing specific weaknesses in AI models' spatial perception, reasoning, and memory that can guide future AI development.
> - The results challenge assumptions that general intelligence emerges solely from vast datasets, suggesting that embodiment or novel architectural/training paradigms may be critical for robust spatial intelligence.
