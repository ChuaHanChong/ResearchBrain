# OccVLA: Vision-Language-Action Model with Implicit 3D Occupancy Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute, Xi’an Jiaotong University, Fudan University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Tsinghua University developed OCCVLA, a vision-language-action model that integrates implicit 3D occupancy supervision for autonomous driving. The model achieves state-of-the-art trajectory planning (0.28m average L2 distance) and superior 3D Visual Question Answering (59.5% accuracy) on the nuScenes benchmark using only camera inputs.

## Problem

- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for autonomous driving lack robust and fine-grained 3D spatial understanding, which is crucial for safe navigation.
- Integrating 3D information into VLMs often requires expensive explicit 3D sensors (like LiDAR) or extensive, labor-intensive 3D annotations.
- Existing 3D-aware models either suffer from sparse 3D supervision or introduce significant computational overhead during inference.

## Method

- OCCVLA introduces a ==Vision-Language-Occupancy (V-L-O)== backbone that unifies 2D vision, language, and dense 3D occupancy, treating occupancy prediction as an implicit reasoning step.
- A latent occupancy prediction mechanism is used to manage sparse 3D occupancy grids efficiently, predicting in a compact latent space decoded by a pre-trained VQ-VAE.
- Motion planning is decomposed into meta-action prediction (high-level driving intent via Chain-of-Thought) and a lightweight planning head for precise trajectory generation, with occupancy prediction being inactive during inference for efficiency.

## Results

- OCCVLA achieved state-of-the-art performance in end-to-end motion planning on the nuScenes benchmark with an average L2 distance of 0.28m, outperforming models that use LiDAR or explicit 3D annotations.
- The model demonstrated superior 3D understanding in Visual Question Answering, reaching **59.5%** overall accuracy on NuScenes-QA, surpassing larger models and those with explicit 3D inputs.
- Ablation studies confirmed that implicit 3D occupancy supervision critically improves meta-action prediction and overall planning performance, strengthening 3D priors within the VLM's visual features.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense 3D occupancy can serve as an effective implicit supervisory signal for vision-language models, enhancing 3D understanding from 2D visual inputs without requiring explicit 3D data during inference.
> - Decomposing motion planning into high-level meta-actions and precise trajectory generation allows LLMs to leverage semantic reasoning for driving intent while achieving accurate physical outputs.
> - A multi-stage training process, combining pretraining, occupancy-language joint training, and planning head training, effectively integrates diverse learning objectives and improves overall performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving has made significant strides, particularly with the advent of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). These models have demonstrated impressive reasoning capabilities, allowing them to interpret complex driving scenarios and even plan actions. However, a persistent and critical limitation, as identified by the authors, is their lack of robust 3D spatial understanding. This gap is crucial for safe and reliable autonomous navigation, where precise perception of the physical environment in three dimensions is paramount for localization, obstacle avoidance, and decision-making.

The existing research landscape for integrating 3D information into VLMs for autonomous driving can be broadly categorized:
- **2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** These models primarily rely on 2D visual inputs (camera images) and textual supervision. While effective for vision-language reasoning, they struggle with explicit 3D spatial grounding, as their supervision often comes from sparse and weak 3D annotations (e.g., bounding box coordinates described in text) which are expensive to generate at scale. Examples include DriveVLM and EMMA, which attempt to enhance 3D grounding but still face scalability issues due to annotation demands.
- **3D Vision-Language Models (3D-VLMs):** To address the 3D deficit, some approaches incorporate explicit 3D inputs, such as LiDAR point clouds or pre-computed occupancy grids. While these provide richer 3D information, they introduce new challenges. First, there's a scarcity of large-scale 3D vision-language pretraining datasets with detailed spatial captions. Second, processing explicit 3D inputs (like point clouds) can be computationally intensive and may lead to a loss of fine-grained spatial details if not handled carefully, or require additional 3D sensors. OmniDrive exemplifies this approach, using both camera and LiDAR.
- **Occupancy for 3D Perception:** More recently, 3D occupancy representations, which assign semantic labels to dense spatial grids, have gained traction for their ability to encode detailed 3D geometry and semantics. Models like OccWorld, OccLLAMA, and Occ-LLM use occupancy features as inputs to LLMs to enhance 3D understanding and world modeling. However, these still often require an explicit occupancy prediction network or suffer from captions that might omit critical 3D information, creating a potential disconnect between the visual modality and the textual reasoning.

OccVLA positions itself at the intersection of these challenges. It aims to overcome the limitations of 2D-only VLMs by implicitly integrating 3D understanding without requiring explicit 3D sensors or extensive manual 3D annotations. Crucially, it differentiates itself from 3D-VLM and occupancy-as-input models by treating dense 3D occupancy as *both* a predictive output and a dense supervisory signal, learned directly from 2D visual inputs. This approach seeks to unify 2D visual processing with robust 3D spatial reasoning, preserving the generalization capabilities of VLMs while providing fine-grained spatial grounding, and importantly, doing so efficiently by making the occupancy prediction process skip-able during inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the OccVLA framework stem from the recognized shortcomings of current MLLMs in autonomous driving, specifically regarding 3D spatial understanding:

1. **Enhance 3D Spatial Understanding in MLLMs:** The overarching goal is to equip multimodal large language models with a more robust and fine-grained comprehension of the 3D physical world, which is indispensable for safe and effective autonomous navigation. Current MLLMs, primarily trained on 2D vision-language data, lack this crucial capability.

2. **Address Challenges in 3D Representation and Supervision:** The authors identify two core challenges:
 - **Accessible and Effective 3D Representation:** Developing a 3D representation that is both rich in spatial detail and scalable without requiring prohibitively expensive manual 3D annotations. Traditional 3D annotations are sparse and labor-intensive to produce.
 - **Dense 3D Supervision:** Implementing a mechanism for dense 3D supervision to prevent the loss of fine-grained spatial details that often occurs when relying solely on weak, text-based 3D annotations or when compressing complex 3D scenes into sparse textual descriptions.

3. **Integrate 3D Occupancy Implicitly and Efficiently:** OccVLA seeks to integrate 3D occupancy representations into the unified multimodal reasoning process. A key motivation is to treat occupancy prediction as an *implicit* reasoning step and a supervisory signal, rather than an explicit input during inference. This design choice aims to leverage the benefits of 3D occupancy for training while avoiding additional computational overhead during real-time deployment.

4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance in Autonomous Driving Tasks:** The model is motivated to demonstrate superior performance across critical autonomous driving tasks, including end-to-end trajectory planning and 3D Visual Question Answering (VQA), thereby proving its practical utility and competitive advantage.

5. **Develop a Scalable, Interpretable, and Vision-Based Solution:** The authors aim for a solution that:
 - **Scalable:** Leverages auto-annotation pipelines for 3D occupancy, reducing reliance on manual labeling.
 - **Interpretable:** Produces interpretable outputs, such as dense 3D occupancy maps and high-level meta-actions, which are crucial for debugging and trust in autonomous systems.
 - **Vision-Based:** Relies solely on 2D camera images as input, maintaining the generalization capabilities of VLMs and avoiding the need for additional, potentially costly, 3D sensors like LiDAR.

In essence, OccVLA is motivated by the desire to bridge the critical gap between 2D visual understanding and 3D spatial reasoning in autonomous driving MLLMs, doing so in an efficient, scalable, and interpretable manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OccVLA (Occupancy Vision-Language-Action model) is a novel framework designed to unify dense 3D occupancy prediction and language modeling within a shared Vision-Language-Occupancy (V-L-O) backbone. Its core approach lies in treating 3D occupancy as an *implicit* reasoning process that enhances 3D understanding during training but can be skipped during inference without performance degradation.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Architecture

The OccVLA framework consists of two main components:
1. **Vision-Language-Occupancy (V-L-O) Modeling Backbone:** This is the central component where visual, language, and occupancy information interact.
2. **Planning Head:** A lightweight module responsible for translating high-level actions into precise trajectories.

The model operates on a "perceive first, then reason" principle, where it first processes visual input to produce an occupancy prediction before performing next-token prediction, aiming to establish a strong perceptual foundation.

#### 3.2. Occupancy Prediction

- **Occupancy Transformer:** The VLM framework is extended with a dedicated 3D occupancy prediction mechanism. Learnable "occupancy queries" are introduced, which interact with visual features from the VLM through cross-attention. This allows occupancy tokens to extract spatial information from 2D image features. The visual tokens themselves are processed via self-attention.
 - **Efficiency during Inference:** A critical design choice is that text reasoning primarily relies on visual features. The authors empirically found that text tokens accessing occupancy features does not significantly improve quality after convergence. This allows the occupancy prediction process to be *inactive* during inference, introducing no additional computational overhead.
- **Latent Occupancy Prediction:** To manage the sparsity and memory intensity of raw 3D occupancy grids (typically 90% empty space), the model predicts occupancy in a compact latent space. The V-L-O backbone outputs hidden states, which are projected into a lower-dimensional latent representation. This latent representation is then fed into a pre-trained VQ-VAE decoder (initialized from OccWorld) to map it back to the high-resolution 3D occupancy space, followed by a classification head. This strategy makes direct prediction more efficient and less memory-intensive.

#### 3.3. Motion Planning

Motion planning is decomposed into two stages to leverage the strengths of LLMs in semantic reasoning while achieving precise numerical outputs:

- **Meta Action Prediction:**
 - **Definition:** Meta actions are high-level, interpretable representations of the vehicle's short-term driving intent, composed of orthogonal components: (1) *velocity action* (Maintain speed, Accelerate, Decelerate) and (2) *directional action* (Go Straight, Turn Left/Right, Change Lane Left/Right, Stop).
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Supervision:** The VLM receives multi-view images and past meta actions. It's prompted to first generate a natural language scene description, then infer driving intent from historical actions, and finally predict the future meta action in natural language. This encourages explicit reasoning.
 - **Automated Data Construction:** A pipeline generates both meta-action labels and CoT annotations:
 - Velocity labels are threshold-classified from acceleration.
 - Directional labels are derived by projecting future trajectories onto an HD map and classified by GPT-4o, followed by human refinement for quality.
 - CoT annotations (scene descriptions, reasoning steps) are generated by GPT-4o, conditioned on image inputs and ground truth meta actions.

- **Planning Head:** A lightweight MLP takes the VLM-predicted meta action embedding, current velocity, and visual tokens as input. It then generates precise future trajectory coordinates for the next 3 seconds. The model makes planning decisions solely based on scene understanding, without high-level navigation commands.

#### 3.4. Training Stages

OccVLA employs a three-stage training process to balance performance across tasks:

1. **Stage 1: Pretraining in Autonomous Driving Scenarios:** The VLM backbone (Paligemma2-3B-224px) is fine-tuned on a large dataset (e.g., OmniDrive) specific to autonomous driving scenarios. This helps adapt the model from general domains to specific objects and motion patterns relevant to driving and enables long-text reasoning.
2. **Stage 2: Occupancy-Language Joint Training:** The full image-occupancy-text dataset is used. The model is trained with a combined loss:
 - `L_text_AR`: Standard autoregressive language modeling loss.
 - `L_occ_NAR`: Non-autoregressive 3D perception loss (cross-entropy between predicted and ground-truth 3D occupancy labels).
 - `L = L_text_AR + λL_occ_N AR`, where λ balances the focus. Adapters are used to fine-tune transformer blocks while preserving original VLM capabilities. VQ-VAE decoder weights are assigned a learning rate of zero (effectively frozen but allowing gradient flow) to maintain stability.
3. **Stage 3: Planning Head Training:** The planning head is trained separately. It takes meta actions (predicted by the trained VLM), current velocity, VLM visual tokens, and ego trajectories as input. Its output (future trajectory coordinates) is supervised using Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss against ground-truth trajectories.

This multi-stage approach systematically builds the model's capabilities, first for general AD understanding, then for precise 3D spatial awareness, and finally for robust motion planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OccVLA demonstrates significant improvements across multiple critical autonomous driving tasks, establishing state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance.

#### 4.1. Motion Planning

- **State-of-the-Art Trajectory Planning:** On the nuScenes benchmark, OccVLA achieves state-of-the-art performance in end-to-end motion planning, particularly in terms of average L2 distance (0.28m), which represents the Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth trajectories. This metric is crucial for assessing prediction accuracy.
- **Efficiency Compared to Baselines:** OccVLA outperforms or is highly competitive with strong baselines, including:
 - **EMMA (0.32m Avg. L2):** A state-of-the-art method that relies on extensive 3D/BEV coordinate and bounding box annotations, which limits scalability.
 - **OmniDrive (0.33m Avg. L2):** A multimodal model requiring both camera and LiDAR inputs. OccVLA achieves better performance using *only camera input*.
- **Comparison with Occupancy-as-Input Models:** OccVLA (0.28m Avg. L2) also outperforms or matches models that explicitly use pre-computed occupancy as input to the LLM (e.g., OccWorld-O, OccLLAMA-O, Occ-LLM). Notably, Occ-LLM with explicit occupancy input achieves 0.28m, matching OccVLA, which *predicts* occupancy from camera input. This highlights the effectiveness of implicit occupancy supervision.
- **Model Size Advantage:** With only a 3B parameter model, OccVLA achieves superior performance compared to larger models like OccLLaMA (7B), demonstrating better efficiency and potential for practical deployment.

#### 4.2. Visual Question Answering (3D VQA)

- **Superior 3D Understanding:** Evaluated on the NuScenes-QA benchmark, designed for complex 3D reasoning in autonomous driving scenarios, OccVLA exhibits superior 3D understanding capabilities.
- **Outperforms Larger and 3D-Input Models:** OccVLA (overall accuracy 59.5%) significantly outperforms:
 - **LLaVA (7B, camera input, 47.4% acc):** A general-purpose VLM.
 - **LiDAR-LLM (7B, LiDAR input, 48.6% acc):** A model explicitly designed for LiDAR 3D understanding.
 - **OccLLaMA3.1 (8B, occupancy input, 54.5% acc):** A model that uses explicit occupancy as input.
 - **OpenDriveVLA (7B, camera input, 58.2% acc):** Another VLM-based driving model.
- The fact that a 3B-parameter, image-only VLM (OccVLA) surpasses larger models and those leveraging explicit 3D inputs (LiDAR or pre-computed occupancy) underscores the effectiveness of its implicit 3D occupancy supervision in fostering deeper 3D understanding from visual-only cues.

#### 4.3. Occupancy Prediction

- **Competitive Performance:** Despite its LLM-based architecture not being solely optimized for occupancy prediction, OccVLA demonstrates competitive performance in predicting real-time 3D occupancy from multi-view images, achieving approximately 10% in mIoU.
- **Strong Object-Level Understanding:** The model excels at predicting critical elements in autonomous driving scenes (lanes, vehicles, pedestrians) and finer details of nearby objects. While it shows limitations in occluded regions due to the lack of multi-timestamp inputs, its ability to produce high-quality 3D occupancy from current-timestep multi-view images highlights its strong object-level 3D scene understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Occupancy Supervision:** The ablation study confirms the critical role of occupancy supervision. Models trained *without* occupancy supervision (OccVLA ✗) showed approximately 1.5% lower meta-action prediction performance compared to those *with* joint occupancy-vision-language training (OccVLA ✓). This directly supports the hypothesis that occupancy supervision strengthens 3D priors within the VLM's visual features.
- **Ego Trajectory:** While past ego trajectory can potentially introduce priors, OccVLA demonstrates strong planning performance even without this information. When comparing performance *with* ego trajectory (Avg. L2 0.28m) to *without* (Avg. L2 0.48m), the inclusion of ego trajectory significantly improves planning accuracy, while still maintaining competitive advantages over state-of-the-art approaches like VAD under comparable conditions. This indicates that while ego trajectory is beneficial, the core scene understanding from OccVLA is robust.

In summary, OccVLA's main findings highlight its ability to achieve superior performance in complex autonomous driving tasks by cleverly integrating implicit 3D occupancy supervision, all while maintaining efficiency and relying solely on camera inputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OccVLA framework represents a significant advancement in bridging the gap between vision-language reasoning and robust 3D spatial understanding for autonomous driving. Its contributions have several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced 3D Perception for VLMs without Explicit 3D Inputs:** The most significant contribution is demonstrating that fine-grained 3D spatial understanding can be implicitly learned by VLMs directly from 2D visual inputs, supervised by dense 3D occupancy. This overcomes a major limitation of current VLMs, which struggle with 3D grounding, and of 3D-VLMs, which often require expensive 3D sensors or explicit 3D data inputs. By doing so, OccVLA makes advanced 3D perception more accessible and scalable.

2. **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The design choice to allow occupancy prediction to be skipped during inference without performance degradation is a game-changer for real-world deployment. This addresses a critical bottleneck in many 3D-aware models – the increased computational cost. By decoupling training-time 3D supervision from inference-time computational demands, OccVLA offers a highly efficient solution. Furthermore, its reliance on auto-annotated occupancy data significantly reduces the scalability challenges associated with manual 3D labeling.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Key AD Tasks:** Achieving state-of-the-art results in both end-to-end trajectory planning and 3D Visual Question Answering on a challenging benchmark like nuScenes validates the effectiveness and robustness of the OccVLA approach. This level of performance, especially with a comparatively smaller model size (3B parameters) and only camera inputs, positions OccVLA as a leading solution for autonomous driving perception and planning.

4. **Improved Interpretability and Explainability:** The model's ability to decode and produce interpretable 3D occupancy representations and high-level meta-actions is crucial for the safety and reliability of autonomous systems. Engineers and regulators can better understand *why* a vehicle made a particular decision, fostering trust and facilitating debugging in complex edge cases.

5. **Foundation for Future Vision-Centric Autonomous Driving:** By effectively enabling 3D reasoning from purely 2D visual inputs, OccVLA strengthens the case for fully vision-based autonomous driving solutions. This could simplify sensor suites, reduce costs, and improve system generalization by leveraging the vast amount of existing 2D image data. It paves the way for a new generation of autonomous driving systems that combine the semantic reasoning power of LLMs with precise, implicit 3D understanding.

6. **Broader Impact on Multimodal AI:** Beyond autonomous driving, the methodology of using an implicit, dense spatial supervisory signal within a multimodal transformer could have implications for other domains requiring fine-grained spatial understanding from 2D inputs, such as robotics, augmented reality, and complex scene understanding tasks.

In conclusion, OccVLA's novel integration of implicit 3D occupancy supervision offers a scalable, efficient, and highly performant paradigm for end-to-end autonomous driving. Its success in crucial perception and planning tasks, coupled with its vision-only input and interpretable outputs, marks a significant step towards more robust and deployable intelligent driving systems.
