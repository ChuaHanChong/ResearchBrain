# MergeMix: A Unified Augmentation Paradigm for Visual and Multi-Modal Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> MergeMix presents a unified augmentation paradigm that leverages token merging for image mixing, improving performance for both image classification and multi-modal large language model (MLLM) alignment. The approach yields average gains of +1.27% on LLaVA benchmarks and +2.88% on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruction benchmarks, alongside enhancements in efficiency and model calibration.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM alignment paradigms (Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning) face limitations such as poor generalization, high computational cost, training instability, and reliance on extensive human annotations.
- Generating high-quality 'loser' responses for preference optimization in MLLMs often relies on random augmentations, leading to uncontrolled data quality and potential loss of valuable 'hard negatives'.
- Traditional Mixup augmentations in computer vision struggle to achieve optimal efficiency-performance trade-offs and are not easily adapted to generate contextually aligned multi-modal samples for MLLM training.

## Method

- **MergeMix** employs a Vision Transformer (ViT) enhanced with ==Token Merging== (ToMe) technology to generate contextually aligned mixed images and labels by preserving salient features during token reduction.
- The approach integrates this token-merge-based image mixing into distinct loss functions: a Mixup Cross-Entropy for image classification and a novel Mixed SimPO loss for MLLM preference alignment.
- It constructs preference pairs where original images are 'winners' and MergeMix-generated images are 'losers', linking the mixing ratio to a soft preference margin in the Mixed SimPO loss for adaptive optimization.

## Results

- MergeMix achieved up to +**4.79%** Top-1 accuracy gains over other Mixup methods on CIFAR100 and a +**0.27%** gain on ImageNet-**1K** with a DeiT-Small model.
- For MLLM alignment, it delivered an average performance gain of +**1.27%** on LLaVA benchmarks and +**2.88%** on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruction benchmarks compared to baseline models.
- The method improved efficiency by reducing FLOPs (e.g., -0.68G on ImageNet-**1K**) and decreasing Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) by up to **22.8%** for MLLMs, while also demonstrating improved model calibration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token merging provides an efficient mechanism for generating contextually relevant augmented samples, preserving spatial and semantic features more effectively than simple token selection methods.
> - The re-scaling policy for the mixing ratio ensures smoother transitions and more robust augmentations, contributing to enhanced performance in both visual and multi-modal tasks.
> - Linking the mixing ratio to a soft preference margin within a ranking loss enables adaptive optimization, balancing the stability of SFT with the preference-aware nature of RL without incurring high computational overheads.
