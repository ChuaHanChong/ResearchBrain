# MergeMix: A Unified Augmentation Paradigm for Visual and Multi-Modal Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> MergeMix presents a unified augmentation paradigm that leverages token merging for image mixing, improving performance for both image classification and multi-modal large language model (MLLM) alignment. The approach yields average gains of +1.27% on LLaVA benchmarks and +2.88% on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruction benchmarks, alongside enhancements in efficiency and model calibration.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM alignment paradigms (Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning) face limitations such as poor generalization, high computational cost, training instability, and reliance on extensive human annotations.
- Generating high-quality 'loser' responses for preference optimization in MLLMs often relies on random augmentations, leading to uncontrolled data quality and potential loss of valuable 'hard negatives'.
- Traditional Mixup augmentations in computer vision struggle to achieve optimal efficiency-performance trade-offs and are not easily adapted to generate contextually aligned multi-modal samples for MLLM training.

## Method

- **MergeMix** employs a Vision Transformer (ViT) enhanced with ==Token Merging== (ToMe) technology to generate contextually aligned mixed images and labels by preserving salient features during token reduction.
- The approach integrates this token-merge-based image mixing into distinct loss functions: a Mixup Cross-Entropy for image classification and a novel Mixed SimPO loss for MLLM preference alignment.
- It constructs preference pairs where original images are 'winners' and MergeMix-generated images are 'losers', linking the mixing ratio to a soft preference margin in the Mixed SimPO loss for adaptive optimization.

## Results

- MergeMix achieved up to +**4.79%** Top-1 accuracy gains over other Mixup methods on CIFAR100 and a +**0.27%** gain on ImageNet-**1K** with a DeiT-Small model.
- For MLLM alignment, it delivered an average performance gain of +**1.27%** on LLaVA benchmarks and +**2.88%** on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruction benchmarks compared to baseline models.
- The method improved efficiency by reducing FLOPs (e.g., -0.68G on ImageNet-**1K**) and decreasing Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) by up to **22.8%** for MLLMs, while also demonstrating improved model calibration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token merging provides an efficient mechanism for generating contextually relevant augmented samples, preserving spatial and semantic features more effectively than simple token selection methods.
> - The re-scaling policy for the mixing ratio ensures smoother transitions and more robust augmentations, contributing to enhanced performance in both visual and multi-modal tasks.
> - Linking the mixing ratio to a soft preference margin within a ranking loss enables adaptive optimization, balancing the stability of SFT with the preference-aware nature of RL without incurring high computational overheads.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges within the field of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and computer vision, specifically concerning data augmentation and model alignment. MLLMs, which integrate visual and textual information for tasks like visual question answering and multi-modal reasoning, are typically pre-trained on extensive datasets. However, aligning these models with human preferences and specific task requirements in the post-training stage remains a significant area of research.

Currently, two primary paradigms for MLLM alignment are Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based preference optimization, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). SFT is a stable approach but relies on high-quality human annotations and often lacks generalization capabilities across various tasks. RL-based methods are more preference-aware but incur substantial computational overhead and can suffer from training instability. Existing efforts to build preference pairs for optimization often rely on random augmentations, which can lead to uncontrolled data quality for "loser" examples, potentially hindering performance on visual tasks and reducing the effective training data by filtering out "hard negatives."

Simultaneously, data augmentation techniques, particularly Mixup methods, have been widely adopted in image classification to improve model generalization and mitigate overfitting. These methods generate synthetic samples by combining existing data points. Mixup augmentations are broadly categorized into static (relying on priors or randomness) and adaptive (data-dependent, using metrics to guide mixing). While advanced Mixup methods exist, extending them effectively to the multi-modal context of MLLMs, particularly to generate contextually aligned samples that can serve as preference-aware training signals, remains an open challenge.

MergeMix positions itself by attempting to bridge the gap between SFT and RL paradigms for MLLM alignment by leveraging an efficient, data-dependent Mixup augmentation policy based on token merging. This approach aims to address the limitations of both SFT (lack of explicit preference modeling, generalization issues) and RL (computational cost, instability) by introducing a method for generating controlled, high-quality preference pairs through augmentation. It also extends the utility of Mixup augmentations beyond traditional image classification to the more complex visual and multi-modal understanding tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the MergeMix proposal are:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing MLLM Alignment Paradigms:** The authors identify two main MLLM alignment strategies: SFT and RL. SFT requires extensive, high-quality instruction-response annotations and optimizes the likelihood of reference responses without explicitly modeling relative preferences. This can limit its generalization and alignment with nuanced human preferences. RL-based methods, while preference-aware, demand a separate reward model, leading to potential biases, susceptibility to exploitation by reward signals, high computational costs, and training instability. MergeMix aims to find a balance among scalability, efficiency, and alignment generalizations.
- **Improving Preference Pair Generation for MLLMs:** A key challenge in preference optimization is the generation of controlled and high-quality "loser" responses to contrast with preferred "winner" responses. Prior methods often rely on random augmentations (e.g., RandomCrop) to create dispreferred examples. However, these augmentations can be highly random, leading to uncontrolled quality of the "loser" data, which can be detrimental, especially for complex visual question answering tasks. Such methods might also reduce useful training data by discarding "hard negatives." The motivation is to construct preference pairs with greater control and quality.
- **Enhancing Mixup Augmentation Efficiency and Applicability:** Traditional Mixup augmentations, while beneficial for image classification, often face challenges in achieving an optimal trade-off between efficiency and performance, particularly those relying on saliency-based metrics. Furthermore, extending these augmentations from simple image corruptions to data-dependent samples suitable for MLLM training requires novel approaches. The authors revisit Mixup augmentation with the goal of improving its efficiency and extending its applicability to generate contextually aligned mixed images and corresponding labels for MLLMs.
- **Bridging SFT and RL with a Unified Paradigm:** The paper explicitly states the objective of proposing a "unified paradigm that bridges SFT and RL with an efficient Token Merge based Mixup augmentation." This aims to combine the stability and scalability of SFT with the preference-aware nature of RL, thereby offering a novel learning approach for MLLM preference alignment that is both efficient and robust.
- **Leveraging Token Merging for Contextual Mixing:** The motivation includes using token merging techniques to generate mixed samples. This involves capturing similarity information to preserve contextual features and ensuring masks retain useful information, contrasting with greedy TopK selection methods that may lose spatial relationships. The goal is to link the merge ratio and mixing ratio to align mixed images with their labels, enabling precise mixed data generation.
- **Adaptive Preference Optimization:** To enhance the preference-driven paradigm for MLLMs, the authors aim to build preference pairs where augmented samples serve as non-preferred responses ("Loser") and clean samples as preferred responses ("Winner"). The mixing ratio is intended to be used as a soft preference margin to enable adaptive optimization with a mixed SimPO loss, leading to more robust calibration and alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MergeMix is proposed as a unified training paradigm that leverages token merging for image mixing, applicable to both image classification and multi-modal large language model (MLLM) alignment. The methodology involves two main components: image mixing via token merge and a unified augmentation paradigm incorporating this mixing into distinct loss objectives.

#### 3.1. Image Mixing via Token Merge

The core of MergeMix's augmentation strategy relies on a vision Transformer (ViT)-based model `f_θ(·)` enhanced with Token Merging (ToMe) technology.

- **Token Merging (ToMe):** Unlike standard ViT models, `f_θ(·)` iteratively replaces *N* attention layers with `ToMeAttention`. Given an initial sequence of tokens `Z_L` (derived from the input image), `ToMeAttention` processes these tokens to produce a condensed token sequence `Z_K` and an attention map `A_K`. A key parameter, `r`, denotes the number of tokens to be merged, reducing high-similarity semantic tokens while retaining essential features.
- **Source Map Generation:** Token merging generates a `source map S`, which captures the spatial relationships between the original `Z_L` and the merged `Z_K`. This map is crucial because token merging aggregates non-similar tokens into compact representations, preserving local feature structures more effectively than simple greedy TopK selection, which discards low-ranked tokens and loses spatial context.
- **Attention Recovery:** To reconstruct a full-resolution attention map, a recovering function `R_K→L(·, ·)` expands the merged attention map `A_K` back to its original length `A_L`, guided by the `source map S`. This process propagates merged attention over the original token topology, restoring spatial dependencies and contextual continuity, thereby minimizing information loss from hard selection.
- **Mixing Mask Generation:** A binary mask `M` is generated based on the recovered attention map `A_L`. Specifically, `M_i` for each token index `i` is set to 1 if `i` belongs to the `TopK(A_L, p)` selected tokens, and 0 otherwise. The selection number `p` is determined by `⌊λ ∗ L⌋`, where `λ` is the mixing ratio and `L` is the original sequence length.
- **Image Mixing:** The mixed image `ˆx` is then generated using the binary mask `M` and two input images `x_i` and `x_j` via element-wise multiplication: `ˆx = M ⊙ x_i + (1 − M) ⊙ x_j`. This process ensures that `M` retains salient features in the mixed sample.

#### 3.2. A Unified Augmentation Paradigm: From Image Classification to MLLMs

MergeMix integrates the token-merge-based image mixing into distinct loss functions tailored for image classification and MLLM alignment.

- **Re-scaling Policy for Mixing Ratio (ˆλ):** The initial mixing ratio `λ` (sampled from `Beta(α, α)`) is further refined to `ˆλ`. This re-scaling considers both the spatial ratio and the inherent features of the ToMe model. A Gaussian-based sampling `ˆλ ∼ N(μ, σ)` is used, where `μ = K/L` (merged token ratio) and `σ = p / (Σ_L M)` (mixing ratio influenced by mask values). This smooth transition aims to alleviate abrupt changes from linear mapping and generate more robust augmentations. The `ˆλ` is then clipped to.

- **Loss for Image Classification:** For image classification, the total loss `L_Total` combines two components:
 - **Mixup Cross-Entropy Loss (L_MCE):** This is applied to the mixed image `ˆx` and linearly interpolates the ground truth labels `y_i` and `y_j` using the re-scaled mixing ratio `ˆλ`: `L_MCE = ˆλ · L_CE(f_θ(ˆx), y_i) + (1 − ˆλ) · L_CE(f_θ(ˆx), y_j)`.
 - **One-Hot Cross-Entropy Loss (L_CE):** This is applied to the original, unmixed images `x` and their corresponding labels `y`: `L_CE = L_CE(f_θ(x), y)`.
 - The total loss for classification is `L_Total = L_MCE + L_CE`.

- **Aggregating Mixing Ratio within Preference Loss for MLLMs:** For MLLM alignment, MergeMix defines a preference-driven paradigm. The clean, original image `x` is considered the "Winner," generating a preferred response `Y_w`, while the MergeMix-generated `ˆx` is considered the "Loser," generating a dispreferred response `Y_l`.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning Loss (L_SFT):** This standard conditional language modeling loss maximizes the likelihood of ground truth responses `y` given the multi-modal input `(x, q)` (image `x` and question `q`): `L_SFT = −E_(x,y)∼D [ Σ_t=1^|y| log π_θ(y_t | x, y_<t) ]`.
 - **Mixed SimPO Loss (L_MixSimPO):** This loss replaces the ranking component of a standard DPO-like loss. It contrasts the log-likelihoods of the ground-truth response `y` when prompted with the original image `x` versus the mixed image `ˆx`. Crucially, the mixing ratio `ˆλ` is linked to a soft preference margin `(1 − ˆλ)`. A larger `ˆλ` (meaning more of the original image `x_i` is present) leads to a smaller margin `(1 − ˆλ)`, indicating higher similarity between winner and loser and thus harder discrimination. Conversely, a smaller `ˆλ` leads to a larger margin, strengthening constraints for clearer preference distinction. The loss is formulated as: `L_MixSimPO = −E_(x,ˆx,y)∼D [ log σ( (β/|y|) log π_θ(y | x) − (β/|y|) log π_θ(y | ˆx) − (1 − ˆλ)) ]`.
 - The total loss for MLLM alignment is `L_Total = L_SFT + L_MixSimPO`. This objective makes the optimization strictness adaptive to the sample difficulty, derived from the mixing ratio, enabling more robust preference optimization.

The implementation details include specific settings for datasets (CIFAR100, ImageNet-1K, CUB200, FGVC-Aircrafts, Stanford-Cars for classification; VQAv2, GQA, VizWiz, ScienceVQA_I, TextVQA, MME, MMBench, POPE, SEED_I, MMMU, MathVista, etc., for MLLMs), model architectures (DeiT, ViT, Vicuna-v1.5, CLIP), optimizers (AdamW), learning rates, and training epochs, as detailed in the appendices.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental results to evaluate MergeMix across various image classification and MLLM benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Image Classification Performance

- **CIFAR100:** On the CIFAR100 dataset, MergeMix consistently achieved higher Top-1 accuracy compared to other Mixup methods. For instance, on DeiT models trained for 200 epochs, MergeMix yielded gains of +2.15% and +2.51% over TransMix for DeiT-Tiny and DeiT-Small, respectively. On ViT models, gains of +2.87% to +4.79% were observed. When trained for 600 epochs, MergeMix maintained competitive performance, achieving 81.20% with DeiT-Tiny and 81.44% with ViT-Small.
- **Fine-grained Datasets (Stanford-Cars, CUB200, FGVC-Aircrafts):** MergeMix demonstrated competitive performance on fine-grained classification tasks, achieving 89.42% and 92.20% accuracy on the Stanford-Cars dataset with DeiT-Small and ViT-Base models, respectively, outperforming other Mixup methods. Similar trends were observed on CUB200 and FGVC-Aircrafts datasets.
- **ImageNet-1K:** On the large-scale ImageNet-1K dataset, MergeMix achieved a Top-1 accuracy of 80.71% with a DeiT-Small model, which was a +0.27% gain compared to TransMix.
- **Robustness to Occlusion:** Experiments on CIFAR100 and ImageNet-1K with varying occlusion ratios indicated that MergeMix maintained relatively higher accuracy across different occlusion levels, suggesting improved robustness compared to other Mixup variants.
- **Audio Classification:** When applied to audio classification using the HuBERT-Base model on ESC-50 and UrbanSound8K datasets, MergeMix achieved marginal improvements over MixUp and TransMix.

#### 4.2. MLLM Benchmark Performance

- **LLaVA Benchmarks:** Experiments with LLaVA-v1.5-7B on 10 visual question answering and understanding benchmarks showed that MergeMix, when training with full vision tokens, achieved an average gain of +0.83% over the SFT Vision baseline. Even when reducing the vision token count to 288, MergeMix continued to perform well, with an average gain of +0.88%. Overall, MergeMix achieved an average performance of 66.84%, which is a +1.27% improvement over the original LLaVA baseline.
- **Qwen2.5-VL-Instruction Benchmarks:** On the Qwen2.5-VL-Instruction-7B model across 9 VQA and reasoning tasks (including MMStar, MMBench, MMMU, MathVista), MergeMix achieved an average gain of +2.88% over the Qwen2.5-VL-Instruction baseline. For the MathVista reasoning task, MergeMix also showed competitive performance.
- **Relationship between Mixing Ratios and Rewards:** A VLM-as-the-judge evaluation, using frontier MLLMs (e.g., Grok-3, Gemini-2.5) to score mixed pairs generated with different mixing ratios (λ), demonstrated a consistent increase in reward scores with larger mixing ratios. This monotonic correlation indicates that λ serves as a reliable control signal for preference strength, validating its use as a proxy for preference supervision in L_MixSimPO.

#### 4.3. Calibration Results

- **Image Classification:** MergeMix obtained competitive Expected Calibration Error (ECE) values on the CIFAR100 dataset for DeiT-Tiny and ViT-Small models, indicating improved calibration compared to several other ViT-specific Mixup methods.
- **MLLMs:** On short-answer MLLM tasks (POPE, GQA, ScienceVQA_I, SEED_I), training with MergeMix and ranking loss generally resulted in lower ECE compared to the baseline and SFT Vision, especially when unfreezing the vision encoder. This suggests that the approach contributes to better calibrated MLLM responses, which is further maintained under token reduction settings.

#### 4.4. Efficiency

- **Image Classification:** On ImageNet-1K with a DeiT-Small model, MergeMix not only achieved higher accuracy but also demonstrated improved efficiency, reducing FLOPs by -0.68G compared to TransMix while maintaining a high throughput of 1591.66 TP/s.
- **Token Merging Overhead:** Detailed analysis of ViT-L models showed that increasing the merged ratio `r` significantly reduced FLOPs (e.g., from 59.57G to 34.93G at r=0.75) and improved throughput (+63.7%). The overhead introduced by Token Merge itself was minimal (e.g., 0.97 ms at r=0.75).
- **MLLM Inference:** For LLaVA-v1.5-7B, Token Merge improved overall throughput and significantly decreased Time-To-First-Token (TTFT), with optimal trade-offs observed around r=0.5 (TTFT reduced from 86.44 ms to 66.74 ms).

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Token Merge Module and Re-Scaling λ:** For image classification, integrating the Token Merge module yielded performance gains of +0.55% to +1.27% compared to simple TopK sampling, indicating the benefit of smoothing discrete attention scores. The re-scaling mixing ratio further improved performance by +0.56% to +2.23% on CIFAR100.
- **MLLM Training Paradigm:** For LLaVA-v1.5-7B, unfreezing the vision encoder during SFT improved performance compared to freezing. The combination of MergeMix augmentation and the proposed ranking loss further enhanced performance compared to only SFT loss, particularly noticeable in tasks like VizWiz and MMBench.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Analysis of hyperparameters (merged tokens and feature layer for attention scores) indicated that MergeMix performance is sensitive to these settings, demonstrating the need for appropriate configuration.
- **Ranking Loss Variants:** A comparison of different ranking losses demonstrated that L_MixSimPO, with its adaptive margin based on `ˆλ`, achieved higher average gains compared to vanilla SimPO, mDPO, and Re-Align.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MergeMix proposes a unified augmentation paradigm that addresses identified limitations in both visual understanding and multi-modal alignment. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved MLLM Alignment and Generalization:** By bridging the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms through data-dependent augmentation and a preference-aware ranking loss, MergeMix offers a method for MLLMs to achieve stronger alignment with preferences without the high computational cost and instability often associated with RLHF. This could lead to MLLMs that are more responsive to nuanced instructions and demonstrate better performance on complex visual-language tasks.
- **Enhanced Data Augmentation:** The integration of Token Merging with Mixup augmentation provides a more controlled and contextually aware method for generating augmented samples. This approach moves beyond random corruptions to create "loser" examples that are systematically designed based on visual saliency and token similarity, potentially leading to more effective training signals for preference optimization.
- **Efficiency Gains:** The inherent efficiency of Token Merging directly translates to reduced FLOPs and improved inference throughput for both standalone vision models and MLLMs. This efficiency is particularly relevant as model sizes continue to grow, making MergeMix a scalable solution for training and deploying large multi-modal systems.
- **Better Model Calibration:** The experimental results suggest that MergeMix contributes to improved model calibration in both image classification and MLLMs. Well-calibrated models provide more reliable confidence scores, which is crucial for real-world applications requiring trustworthy predictions and reasoning.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a foundation for future research in several directions. These include extending Mixup to the text modality in MLLMs for more fine-grained optimization, developing learnable token-merging policies for further enhancement, and exploring advanced sampling strategies for constructing high-quality preference pairs using image editing techniques.
- **Broader Applicability:** The demonstration of MergeMix's effectiveness across various image classification datasets (small-scale, large-scale, fine-grained) and its transferability to other data modalities like audio classification suggests its potential as a broadly applicable augmentation technique beyond the specific MLLM context.

In summary, MergeMix provides a method for scalable and robust training of multi-modal systems by efficiently generating preference-aware training signals, addressing critical challenges in MLLM alignment, and enhancing data augmentation techniques.
