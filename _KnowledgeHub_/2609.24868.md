# DualWAM: Dual-System World Action Models for Asynchronous Global Planning and Local Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> DualWAM is a dual-system world action model that splits one rectified-flow denoising trajectory by role: a low-frequency System 2 bidirectionally denoises a broad multi-view world-action chunk from high noise to a handoff threshold, while a compact wrist-only System 1 completes low-noise refinement on temporally aligned short windows using fresh wrist views. Running asynchronously, it lifts zero-shot success 4.5 points over the strongest baseline with a 16.6x critical-path speedup.

## Problem

- Future visual prediction in world action models is costly, so prior models amortize inference over long action chunks, sacrificing closed-loop responsiveness to interaction feedback.
- Shortening chunks forces more frequent world-action generation and encourages myopic next-chunk prediction that favors local appearance shortcuts over broader temporal dynamics.
- Prior efficiency fixes reduce, compress, or asynchronously decode predictions but never jointly specialize denoising stage, visual context, and update frequency in one shared generation process.

## Method

- **System 2** performs bidirectional high-noise denoising over a 32-frame multi-view world-action state down to handoff threshold tau, forming a coarse but coherent global plan reused across many local updates.
- Wrist-only **System 1** slices a temporally aligned 8-frame window from that intermediate state and finishes low-noise denoising under ==causal temporal attention==, conditioned on the latest wrist observations.
- Shared-trajectory denoising under one ==rectified-flow== noise coordinate, plus random temporal-shift augmentation of System 1 inputs, asynchronous scheduling, ==CFG parallelism==, ==CUDA graphs== and ==W8A8 quantization==.

## Results

- Zero-shot on 20 unseen tasks: **54%** success on Franka and **45%** on Galbot, beating Cosmos3-Nano-Policy by **5** and **4** points, at **63 ms** versus **1043 ms** critical-path latency.
- Ablation: replacing System 1 with an action-only DiT or Fast-WAM drops success from **54%** to **7%** and **14%** at near-identical latency; asynchronous beats synchronous execution **45%** versus **39%**.
- Role-matched auxiliary data (ego video to System 2, UMI to System 1) raises success **45%** to **59%**; edge-cloud downlink falls **104.6x**, **44.2 MB** to **0.423 MB** per sync.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A diffusion trajectory's noise axis can carry a role split: high noise fixes global structure, low noise absorbs fresh perception, letting one plan serve many control updates.
> - Keeping future visual prediction inside the fast local refiner matters; video co-training alone recovers little, so explicit imagination anchors actions to expected interaction outcomes.
> - Role-specialized observation spaces give free interfaces for heterogeneous data and compact edge-cloud messages, since only coarse latents plus action priors cross the boundary.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot extracting a straw from a cup must hold a clear overall intention while correcting for contact geometry that only a close wrist view can reveal. DualWAM handles that tension by keeping a slow broad plan alive while a fast, wrist-conditioned process updates the next short action window from fresh observations. In the paper's zero-shot real-robot evaluation, this design reaches the highest reported success rates on both robot embodiments tested while placing only 63 ms of inference on the action path.

DualWAM successfully completing the labeled task "Extract the straw from the cup."

### 2. One plan, two timescales

The design divides a single denoising trajectory between two roles. System 2 receives language, robot state, global and external views, and wrist views. It denoises the complete broad state from pure noise down to an intermediate handoff threshold $\tau$, using bidirectional attention across the full horizon. The result is a coarse but coherent joint hypothesis over future global-view latents, wrist-view latents, and aligned actions—not itself a final executable action sequence.

At each local update, temporal alignment selects the wrist latents and actions corresponding to the current execution window. System 1 receives that selected state plus the latest wrist observation and robot state, then completes low-noise denoising only for the short window, using causal temporal attention at current and preceding positions while denoising the window in parallel. As execution advances, new wrist observations trigger new local updates; System 2 refreshes in the background, and expired action windows are skipped so actions remain tied to their actual execution timestamps.

The asynchronous schedule removes System 2 from the per-action critical path, while the shared intermediate state prevents local updates from becoming an unrelated short-horizon policy.

The upper blue panel (System 2) shows high-noise bidirectional generation with global vision and action tokens, a denoising-timestep arrow from pure noise toward the dashed handoff $\tau^*$, and low-frequency updates. Blue arrows pass temporally aligned coarse wrist tokens and coarse action plans into the repeated orange System 1 chunks below, each handling a short wrist-view and action window at high frequency. Language instruction enters both roles.

### 3. Does the split explain the speed and success?

The evaluation covers 20 unseen real-robot tasks—ten on Franka FR3 and ten on Galbot G1—with 100 rollouts per embodiment and no task-specific demonstrations or fine-tuning. Success rate (SR) counts only fully completed tasks; task progress averages milestone-based partial scores; mean completion time is physical execution time and excludes inference latency.

On Franka, DualWAM achieves 54% SR versus 49% for Cosmos3-Nano-Policy, with a critical-path latency of 63 ms versus 1,043 ms—a 16.6× speedup on the action-generation path. On Galbot the pattern holds: 45% versus 41% SR. Successful episodes also complete faster: physical execution time falls from 24.88 to 21.76 seconds on Franka and from 53.11 to 48.67 seconds on Galbot, though this is elapsed execution time, not inference latency.

The ablation table below holds the System 2 planner fixed and varies only the local refiner, isolating what the wrist-based WAM adds. Action-only DiT System 1 and [[2603.16666|Fast-WAM]] System 1 retain roughly the same fast local latency—59 ms and 62 ms—but reach only 7% and 14% SR, respectively. DualWAM's local WAM reaches 54% SR and 81.6 task progress at 63 ms, showing that video-action co-training alone does not reproduce the benefit of explicitly refining future wrist dynamics and actions from the aligned intermediate trajectory. The authors interpret this as local future prediction coupling an action correction to expected interaction outcomes, while noting this is an explanation supported by ablation under this protocol, not an isolated causal identification.

The coupled rows hold System 2 fixed; Action-only and Fast-WAM variants preserve fast local latency but lose success, while DualWAM preserves speed and achieves the highest progress and SR.

A controlled asynchronous-execution comparison on Galbot—models and denoising schedule held fixed—improves task progress from 73.3 to 80.6, SR from 39% to 45%, and critical-path latency from 850 to 63 ms. DualWAM's central contribution is not simply adding a fast policy beside a planner; it coordinates two WAM roles along one denoising trajectory so broad organization and local feedback can coexist.
