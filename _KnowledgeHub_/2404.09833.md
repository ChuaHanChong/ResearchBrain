# Video2Game: Real-time, Interactive, Realistic and Browser-Compatible Environment from a Single Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Video2Game presents a framework that transforms a single real-world video into a real-time, interactive, and browser-compatible 3D environment, integrating a large-scale neural radiance field with mesh baking and physics modeling. The system enables free exploration and physically plausible interactions, achieving over 100 FPS in browser-based environments and outperforming state-of-the-art neural rendering and mesh-based baselines in novel view synthesis and geometry reconstruction.

## Problem

- Creating high-quality, interactive virtual environments is costly, time-consuming, and demands specialized expertise, often requiring extensive manual modeling.
- Existing automated methods like photogrammetry necessitate substantial post-processing for interactive use, while neural radiance fields (NeRFs) primarily focus on visual fidelity, lacking real-time interaction, physical plausibility, and game engine compatibility for large, unbounded scenes.
- Current solutions struggle with efficient real-time performance, defining physical interactions within volumetric representations, and direct integration into conventional game engines.

## Method

- **Video2Game** uses a three-component pipeline that converts a single input video into a functional 3D environment by integrating a large-scale Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) with a mesh module and a physics module.
- An enhanced ==Instant-NGP== model is developed to handle sparsely-captured, large-scale, and unbounded scenes by incorporating a contraction function, predicting semantics and surface normals, and regularizing geometry with 2D monocular depth, normal, and semantic priors.
- The learned ==NeRF== is distilled into a game-engine compatible mesh with a ==neural texture map== for efficient real-time rendering and is decomposed into discrete entities equipped with rigid body physics attributes to enable interactive collision detection and object manipulation.

## Results

- The large-scale **NeRF model** achieved superior novel view synthesis (e.g., higher PSNR, SSIM) and more accurate geometry reconstruction (lower Outlier-%, RMSE, MAE) on datasets like KITTI-360 compared to baselines like Instant-NGP and Nerfacto.
- The baked mesh rendering performed comparably to or better than other mesh-based methods while enabling the highest interactive compatibility, including real-time rendering, rigid-body physics, and scene decomposition.
- The browser-based interactive environment achieved real-time frame rates exceeding **100 FPS** across diverse hardware, demonstrating realistic game features (e.g., object manipulation with rigid-body dynamics) and robot simulations (e.g., pushing/picking objects) with high physical plausibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Monocular 2D priors (depth, normals, semantics) effectively regularize large-scale NeRF training, significantly improving geometry accuracy and novel view synthesis from sparsely captured, unbounded video data.
> - Distilling a complex NeRF into a game-engine compatible mesh with a neural texture allows for a balance between photo-realistic rendering and the efficiency required for real-time interaction and broad compatibility.
> - Decomposing the reconstructed scene into individual, actionable entities with assigned rigid body physics attributes enables robust physical interactions (e.g., collisions, manipulation) within the generated virtual environment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The creation of high-quality and interactive virtual environments, such as those found in video games, virtual reality applications, and autonomous driving simulators, is typically a resource-intensive and complex undertaking. This process often necessitates extensive manual modeling by specialized experts using professional software tools, as exemplified by the significant budgets allocated for asset creation in titles like Grand Theft Auto V or simulators like CARLA.

Alternative approaches have sought to automate environment generation from real-world data. Photogrammetry, for instance, constructs digital replicas from photographs. While useful for creating object assets, this technique generally requires substantial post-processing, including material creation, texturing, and geometry correction, which limits its utility for fully interactive environments.

More recently, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have emerged as a promising method for novel view synthesis, offering efficient, photo-realistic free-viewpoint rendering of scenes. However, standard NeRF implementations present several limitations for generating interactive environments. These include inherent computational costs that hinder real-time interaction, difficulties in defining and simulating physical interactions (such as collisions), and a lack of direct compatibility with conventional game engines. Furthermore, scaling NeRFs to large, unbounded scenes or those captured sparsely remains a challenge.

Research in controllable video generation focuses on manipulating visual models with various control signals. While large foundation models offer text-based content generation, they often lack fine-grained, real-time control. Scene-specific generative models can provide better control over dynamics but are typically 2D or limited to specific scenarios and are not inherently 3D or compatible with game engines for general environments.

Data-driven simulation aims to construct realistic simulation environments by leveraging real-world data. Existing approaches in this area often focus on offline training and evaluation, may require additional data modalities like LiDAR point clouds for geometry, frequently do not model physical interactions, and are resource-intensive.

Video2Game addresses a gap in these existing methods by proposing an automated pipeline that transforms real-world video footage into a real-time, interactive, realistic, and game-engine compatible 3D environment, specifically designed to support physical interactions and free exploration. This work combines advancements in neural rendering with physics modeling and integrates them into existing game engine frameworks to overcome the limitations of prior techniques in creating actionable digital twins.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Video2Game stems from the substantial cost, time, and specialized expertise required for the manual creation of visually compelling and interactive virtual environments. Current manual processes are prohibitive for many applications and developers. Existing automated methods, such as photogrammetry, necessitate considerable post-processing, while state-of-the-art neural rendering techniques like NeRFs primarily focus on visual fidelity and novel view synthesis, often overlooking critical aspects of interactivity, physical plausibility, and real-time game engine compatibility. Specifically, NeRFs struggle with efficiency for real-time applications, defining physical interactions within a volumetric representation, and managing large-scale, unbounded scenes.

Against this background, the key objectives of the Video2Game project are to:

1. **Automate Virtual Environment Creation**: Develop a novel framework that can automatically convert a single video of a real-world scene into a functional, interactive, and realistic virtual environment.
2. **Achieve Real-time Performance**: Ensure that the generated environments can be rendered and interacted with in real-time, enabling seamless user experiences in applications like games and simulators.
3. **Enable Physical Interaction**: Integrate robust physics modeling to allow agents (users or virtual characters) to navigate, collide with, and manipulate objects within the reconstructed scene in a physically plausible manner. This includes supporting rigid-body dynamics for various object types.
4. **Maintain High Visual Realism**: Capture the geometric and visual appearance of the scene with high fidelity, providing photo-realistic rendering from novel viewpoints.
5. **Ensure Game Engine Compatibility**: Produce assets and an environment that are compatible with widely used real-time interaction interfaces and game engines, particularly browser-compatible WebGL-based engines, to facilitate broad accessibility and integration.
6. **Address Large-scale and Unbounded Scenes**: Develop enhancements to neural radiance fields to effectively model and render expansive, unbounded real-world environments from potentially sparsely captured video data.
7. **Decompose and Equip Scene Entities**: Automatically decompose the complex scene into individual, actionable entities, each endowed with specific physical properties (e.g., mass, friction) to enhance interaction realism.

By achieving these objectives, Video2Game aims to democratize the creation of interactive 3D content, significantly reducing development overhead and opening new possibilities for digital game design, robot simulation, and virtual reality applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Video2Game implements a three-component pipeline to transform a single input video into an interactive, realistic, and browser-compatible virtual environment. The core components are a large-scale Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) module, a mesh module for efficient rendering, and a physics module for object interactions.

#### 3.1. Large-scale NeRF

The initial step involves constructing a NeRF model capable of capturing the geometry and visual appearance of potentially large-scale and unbounded scenes. The approach builds upon Instant-NGP, which represents the radiance field using a multi-resolution hash encoding combined with lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to predict color and density.

To extend Instant-NGP's capabilities to sparsely-captured, large-scale, and unbounded environments, several enhancements are introduced:
- **Contraction Function**: The contraction function Ct(x) is employed to map unbounded 3D coordinates into a bounded region, allowing the model to handle diverse scene scales.
- **Additional Predictions**: The NeRF is augmented to predict not only color (c) and density (σ) but also semantic logits (s) and surface normals (n) for each 3D point.
- **2D Prior Guidance**: High-quality geometry is crucial for physical interactions. The NeRF is regularized using 2D priors derived from off-the-shelf monocular estimators:
 - **Depth**: A depth loss (L_depth) minimizes the discrepancy between the NeRF-rendered depth (D_NeRF) and monocular depth predictions (D_mono), with learned scale and shift parameters for alignment.
 - **Surface Normals**: A normal loss (L_normal) encourages consistency between normals derived from NeRF's density gradient (N_density), normals predicted by an MLP header (N_mlp), and monocular normal estimates (N_mono).
 - **Semantics**: A cross-entropy loss (L_semantics) matches volume-rendered semantic maps (S_NeRF) with those from a 2D segmentation model (S_mono), helping to correlate semantics and geometry.
- **Regularization Terms**:
 - **Floater Reduction**: A spatial regularization term (L_sp) penalizes density values of randomly sampled 3D points to reduce artifacts.
 - **Sky Regularization**: For sky pixels (identified by the semantic MLP), a loss (L_sky) encourages their depth to be as large as possible, pushing the sky to infinity.
- **Blocking Strategy**: For extraordinarily large scenes like KITTI-360, a strategy similar to BlockNeRF is adopted, dividing the scene into multiple overlapping blocks, each modeled by a separate Instant-NGP instance to capture fine-grained details.
- **Learning**: The entire system jointly optimizes the hash-grid voxel features (Φ) and MLP weights (θ) by minimizing a total loss (L_NeRF_total) that sums the RGB, normal, semantic, depth, sky, and spatial regularization terms.

#### 3.2. NeRF Baking

To enable real-time rendering efficiency, game engine compatibility, and physical interaction definition, the learned NeRF representation is distilled into a game-engine compatible mesh.
- **Mesh Representation**: The scene is represented as a mesh M = (V, F, T) consisting of vertices, faces, and a UV neural texture map. The texture map stores base color and view-dependent specular features.
- **Mesh Extraction**: Initial mesh topology is generated by applying marching cubes to the NeRF's density field. This mesh undergoes post-processing, including pruning invisible faces, mesh decimation, and edge length regularization. UV coordinates for vertices are computed using xatlas.
- **Rendering**: Differentiable renderers, such as nvdiffrast, are used to render the mesh into RGB images and depth maps. A customized GLSL-based shader computes the final pixel color by combining view-independent base color from the texture map with view-dependent specular features processed by a lightweight MLP.
- **Learning**: The shader MLP and the neural texture map are trained by minimizing the color difference between the rendered mesh and ground truth images, and the geometry difference between the rendered mesh depth and the NeRF-rendered depth.
- **Anti-aliasing**: Random perturbation of the camera's optical center during training is applied to mitigate aliasing artifacts in the learned texture map.

#### 3.3. Representation for Physical Interaction

To enable a full range of physical interactions, the scene is decomposed into discrete, actionable entities, each equipped with specific physical attributes.
- **Scene Decomposition**: Neural fields' compositional nature is exploited. For each voxel, 3D points are sampled to determine their semantic category (from predicted logits or bounding boxes). This guides the individual NeRF meshing of objects, where the density of other areas is set to zero. Intersections between objects are resolved by marching cubes. The neural textures for these individual meshes are initialized from the original scene mesh, with new faces inpainted using nearest neighbor.
- **Physical Parameters Reasoning**: Each decomposed mesh is assigned rigid body physics attributes: collision geometry, mass, and friction. Four types of collision geometry are supported: box, sphere, convex polygon (leveraging V-HACD), and triangle mesh, chosen based on object complexity and task requirements. Mass and friction parameters can be set manually or estimated via large language models (LLMs).
- **Physical Interactions**: The rigid body dynamics allow for navigation with collision checks (preventing agents from passing through geometry), object manipulation (e.g., pushing objects), and pre-computed fracturing effects for dynamic objects.

#### 3.4. Interactive Environment

The final interactive environment is deployed within a real-time, browser-based game engine.
- **Engine Components**: Sketchbook, built on Three.js and leveraging WebGL, manages the underlying game logic and assets. A custom GLSL-based shader is implemented for real-time computation of the MLP-based specular shader. Cannon.js is utilized for physics simulation, supporting rigid body dynamics and more advanced features like fracturing effects.
- **Compatibility**: The automatically generated assets are designed for compatibility and can be integrated into other game engines like Blender and Unreal Engine.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Video2Game's performance was evaluated across various scenes using standard metrics for novel view synthesis and geometry reconstruction, alongside demonstrations of interactive game features and robot simulations.

#### 4.1. Novel View Synthesis and Geometry Reconstruction

- **Datasets**: Experiments were conducted on "Gardenvase" (outdoor, object-centric), KITTI-360 (large-scale, unbounded self-driving scene), and an indoor scene from VR-NeRF.
- **Metrics**: PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS were used for image quality, while RMSE, MAE, and outlier rate (compared to LiDAR point clouds for KITTI-360) assessed geometry reconstruction.
- **NeRF Performance (Table 1)**: The proposed large-scale NeRF model demonstrated superior performance in novel view synthesis compared to state-of-the-art neural volume rendering baselines, including Instant-NGP and Nerfacto. This was particularly evident in the large-scale, unbounded KITTI-360 dataset, where it achieved higher PSNR and SSIM, and lower LPIPS. While 3D Gaussian Splatting showed strong performance on Gardenvase, its quality degraded on the sparse camera settings of KITTI-360.
- **Baked Mesh Performance (Table 1, Figure 5)**: After baking the NeRF into a mesh, the Video2Game system's mesh rendering generally outperformed or achieved comparable results to other mesh-based rendering baselines like MobileNeRF and BakedSDF. Significant improvements were noted in KITTI-360, where other mesh-based methods showed reduced quality or blurriness.
- **Geometry Accuracy (Table 2, Figure 4)**: The proposed NeRF model achieved significantly more accurate depth and surface normal estimations. Quantitative evaluation on KITTI-360 showed lower Outlier-%, RMSE, and MAE compared to all baselines, indicating high-quality geometry reconstruction. Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4) highlighted cleaner depth maps and surface normals with less noise.
- **Interactive Compatibility (Table 1)**: The Video2Game pipeline achieved the highest interactive compatibility among all evaluated methods, supporting real-time rendering, rigid-body physics, and scene decomposition, which existing NeRFs and point-based rendering methods typically lack.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Component Contribution (Table 3)**: An ablation study on KITTI-360 demonstrated the incremental benefits of each proposed enhancement. Starting from a basic Instant-NGP, the addition of regularization terms and monocular cues (depth, normal, semantics) consistently improved both NeRF volume rendering quality and the quality of subsequent mesh rasterization, validating their importance in the pipeline. A slight decline in rendering performance was observed during the NeRF-to-mesh conversion, which is attributed to the inherent differences in representation and the trade-off for real-time interactivity.

#### 4.3. System Performance and Interactive Demonstrations

- **Runtime Analysis (Table 4)**: The interactive environment deployed in a browser-based game engine (Sketchbook with WebGL) achieved real-time frame rates exceeding 100 FPS across various hardware configurations (e.g., Mac M1 Pro, Intel Core i9 + NV 4060, AMD 5950 + NV 3090). CPU and GPU usage remained within practical limits.
- **Game Features (Figure 1)**:
 - **Movement**: Agents could navigate the virtual environments while respecting real-world physics and collision models.
 - **Shooting Game**: In the "Gardenvase" scene, a demonstration involved a player shooting footballs (sphere collider) at a vase (box collider) on a table. The vase's movement and fall were realistically simulated using rigid-body dynamics, which were calculated for both the vase and the background (convex polygon colliders).
 - **Temple-Run like Game**: A game built on the "KITTI Loop" (composed of streets from KITTI-360) allowed agents to collect coins and interact with the environment. Obstructive chairs could be "smashed" through pre-computed fracture animations, and roadside vehicles could be pushed via rigid-body dynamics.
- **Robot Simulation (Figure 7)**: The framework's potential for robot learning was demonstrated using the VR-NeRF dataset. The scene was reconstructed, and simulatable rigid-body objects (e.g., a fruit bowl) were segmented.
 - **Stretch Robot**: Demonstrated pushing the fruit bowl off a table.
 - **Fetch Robot**: Performed pick-and-place actions with the bowl.
 - These simulations utilized PyBullet for physics calculations and URDF files for robot rendering in Three.js, showcasing the ability to generate realistic rehearsal environments for robots.
- **Engine Compatibility (Figure 6)**: The assets generated by Video2Game were successfully integrated and demonstrated in both Blender and Unreal Engine, highlighting the pipeline's compatibility with diverse professional tools.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Video2Game represents a significant advancement in the automated creation of virtual environments from real-world video footage. Its primary significance lies in providing a pipeline that bridges the gap between passive neural rendering and interactive, physically plausible digital worlds, while also ensuring compatibility with widely used game engines.

The potential impacts of this research are multi-faceted:

1. **Democratization of 3D Content Creation**: By automating the complex and costly process of environment modeling, Video2Game lowers the barrier to entry for creating high-quality, interactive 3D content. This could empower individuals and smaller teams without extensive expertise in 3D modeling or game development to transform real-world scenes into playable digital environments.
2. **Accelerated Game Development**: For the gaming industry, the framework could significantly reduce the time and resources required for asset creation and environment design. Developers could rapidly prototype games or build realistic environments directly from captured video, fostering more innovative and diverse game experiences.
3. **Enhanced Robot Simulation and Learning**: The ability to generate realistic, interactive virtual environments with robust physics modeling directly from real-world videos has profound implications for robotics. It provides high-fidelity simulation platforms where robots can be trained and tested in physically accurate digital replicas of real environments, allowing for efficient rehearsal of complex tasks before deployment in the physical world. This could accelerate advancements in robot learning and safe autonomous systems.
4. **Immersive VR/AR Experiences**: The creation of photo-realistic, interactive, and explorable digital twins from video streams could enrich virtual and augmented reality applications. Users could experience digital versions of real places with greater immersion and interactivity.
5. **Cost-Effectiveness in Simulators**: Beyond games and robotics, the approach could be beneficial for developing cost-effective simulators for various domains, such as urban planning, architectural visualization, or autonomous driving, by quickly generating detailed and interactive environments.
6. **Technical Integration and Innovation**: The novel integration of large-scale NeRF modeling with mesh-based rendering, scene decomposition for physics, and deployment in browser-compatible game engines demonstrates a comprehensive solution that pushes the boundaries of real-time neural graphics and interactive digital twin generation.

While the current work demonstrates strong capabilities, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the framework not learning material properties necessary for physics-informed relighting. Addressing this could further enhance realism and expand the utility of these generated environments. Overall, Video2Game lays a foundation for future advancements in automated 3D content creation, promising exciting developments in how digital worlds are conceived, built, and interacted with.
