# Socratic Models: Composing Zero-Shot Multimodal Reasoning with Language

> [!summary] Summary
> Socratic Models propose a modular framework that orchestrates large pretrained models to perform complex multimodal reasoning through language-based interaction, achieving new zero-shot capabilities in areas like egocentric perception and robot planning. The approach sets new zero-shot benchmarks on image captioning and video-to-text retrieval by composing distinct foundation models.

## Problem

- Integrating distinct large pretrained models, each specialized in a single modality, to perform complex multimodal reasoning tasks traditionally requires extensive joint training or fine-tuning.
- The high computational cost, significant data demands, and potential loss of generality associated with fine-tuning large foundation models for new multimodal applications.
- The scarcity of labeled multimodal datasets for emerging applications (e.g., egocentric perception, human-robot interaction) limits the applicability of traditional data-hungry approaches.

## Method

- Introduces ==Socratic Models (SMs)==, a modular framework that composes multiple large pretrained models (LMs, VLMs, ALMs) to perform novel multimodal reasoning tasks zero-shot.
- Utilizes ==language as the universal intermediate representation== for communication, converting information from non-language modalities into text for prompting other models.
- Employs ==multi-model multimodal prompting== and ==closed-loop feedback==, allowing models to 'dialogue' and refine outputs without requiring task-specific training or fine-tuning.

## Results

- Achieved zero-shot state-of-the-art on MS COCO image captioning (CIDEr **44.5**) and MSR-VTT video-to-text retrieval (R@1 **44.7**), outperforming prior zero-shot methods.
- Demonstrated qualitative capabilities in egocentric perception, including summarizing videos into textual event logs, answering open-ended questions, and forecasting future events.
- Enabled novel applications such as multimodal assistive dialogue and language-conditioned robot perception and planning by orchestrating interactions between diverse models and external APIs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Different large pretrained models (LMs, VLMs, ALMs) possess complementary forms of commonsense knowledge that can be synergistically leveraged without architectural unification.
> - Language serves as a universal and flexible intermediate representation, enabling diverse AI modules to communicate and collaborate through prompting.
> - Complex multimodal reasoning tasks can be reframed as language-based reading comprehension problems, allowing LMs to apply their reasoning capabilities to non-textual data converted into linguistic form.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "Socratic Models" emerges from the recent paradigm shift brought about by large pretrained models, often termed "foundation models," such as BERT, GPT-3, and CLIP. These models, trained on vast amounts of data within specific domains (e.g., text, images with captions), have demonstrated impressive zero-shot and few-shot capabilities. However, a key challenge remains in integrating these disparate models, each specialized in its modality, to achieve complex multimodal reasoning without extensive additional training.

Traditionally, multimodal AI research has pursued two primary avenues:
- **Joint Multimodal Training:** This involves curating large multimodal datasets and training (or fine-tuning) unified models on specific target tasks. While effective for data-rich domains, this approach can be computationally expensive, data-hungry for new tasks, and may lead to a loss of the original models' generality or robustness to distribution shifts. Examples include VLMs like CLIP combined with LMs for image captioning (e.g., ClipCap) or video understanding.
- **Multimodal Probabilistic Inference:** Less common, this Bayesian-inspired approach treats one model as a prior and another as evidence to perform joint inference.

Socratic Models propose a distinct **third paradigm**: **composition via language-based prompting**. Instead of architectural unification or extensive fine-tuning, SMs leverage the *zero-shot* capabilities of existing foundation models by orchestrating a "discussion" or "dialogue" between them through natural language. This approach builds upon the success of zero-shot/few-shot prompting in Large Language Models (LMs), exemplified by GPT-3 and Chain-of-Thought prompting, extending it to the multimodal domain.

The paper argues that different foundation models store complementary forms of commonsense knowledge – VLMs excel at grounding text to visual content, while LMs possess broad linguistic reasoning abilities. SMs exploit this diversity, viewing language as the universal intermediate representation for information exchange. This positions Socratic Models as an agile, low-resource alternative for deploying multimodal AI applications, particularly for tasks where fine-tuning data is scarce or expensive to acquire (e.g., AR, robotics, egocentric perception). It represents a shift from data-centric model training to a systems-level approach of intelligent model orchestration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and demonstrate **Socratic Models (SMs)**, a modular framework that enables the composition of multiple large pretrained models (foundation models) to perform novel multimodal reasoning tasks **zero-shot**, i.e., without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several limitations observed in current multimodal AI development:
- **Distinct Capabilities and Knowledge**: Large pretrained models are specialized, holding different forms of commonsense knowledge depending on their training data domain (e.g., text-only LMs versus image-caption VLMs). This diversity, while powerful, often leads to silos of knowledge. The authors hypothesize that this diversity is "symbiotic" and can be synergistically leveraged.
- **High Cost of Fine-tuning**: Fine-tuning large (100B+ parameter) foundation models is computationally expensive, requires significant data collection for new multimodal applications, and can lead to a loss of generality or robustness.
- **Data Scarcity for New Applications**: Many emerging multimodal applications (e.g., egocentric perception, human-robot interaction) lack the abundant, labeled datasets necessary for traditional joint training or fine-tuning approaches.
- **"Stale" Knowledge**: Foundation models' knowledge can become outdated due to training latencies, and they may lack access to dynamic online information or proprietary databases.

By enabling models to "talk to each other" through language, SMs aim to overcome these limitations, rapidly capturing new multimodal functionalities and addressing traditionally challenging problems by reframing them (e.g., video understanding as reading comprehension). The overarching goal is to explore new perspectives on building AI systems that embrace the heterogeneity of pretrained models through structured, language-based Socratic dialogue.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of Socratic Models is a **modular framework** where new multimodal tasks are formulated as a language-based exchange between pretrained models and other external modules (e.g., APIs for web search or robot actions). This exchange happens via **multimodal-informed prompting**, without any task-specific training or fine-tuning.

The methodology can be broken down as follows:

- **Modular Composition**: An SM system is conceptualized as a computational graph where nodes represent individual modules (pretrained foundation models like VLMs, LMs, ALMs, or external APIs), and edges signify communication through language. Each module transforms its input into a language-based output that a connected module can use for further inference.
- **Language as Intermediate Representation**: Language serves as the universal interface for communication. Information from non-language domains (vision, audio, robot actions) is converted into text descriptions, which are then integrated into language prompts for LMs.
- **Multi-model Multimodal Prompting**: This is the key enabling component. It involves variable substitution of language-described entities from other modalities into a language prompt. For instance, a VLM might detect visual entities (objects, places), and these linguistic descriptions are then inserted into an LM's prompt for reasoning.
 - **Closed-Loop Feedback**: Interactions can involve multiple back-and-forth exchanges, even calling the same model multiple times, forming a "closed-loop" feedback system to refine predictions.
 - **Pre- and Post-processing**: While no training is required, simple pre- and post-processing steps are often used to make models compatible with language-based interaction. For example, a vision-text similarity VLM like CLIP, which doesn't inherently produce text, can be made to perform zero-shot detection from a library of class names and return top-k categories in textual form.
- **Language-Based World-State History for Video Understanding**: For tasks like egocentric perception, the system processes video by extracting "key moments." These moments are then summarized into natural language captions using SMs (VLM-LM interaction). These individual summaries, along with timestamps, are concatenated to form a "language-based world-state history" (an event log or short story). This textual history serves as context for a Large Language Model to perform various reasoning tasks (e.g., Q&A, summarization, forecasting), effectively reframing video understanding as a reading comprehension problem. Sparsification and condensation techniques (e.g., uniform sampling, video search based on query) are used to manage the size of this history.
- **Integration with External APIs**: SMs can interface with external tools like web search or robot policies, treating them as additional "participants" in the Socratic dialogue. For instance, an LM might generate a search query, which an external web search API executes, and the results are then fed back into the LM for further reasoning.

The methodology emphasizes **minimal engineering** and **creative programming** of prompts and interaction scripts, foregoing the complexity and resource demands of large-scale dataset collection and model fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Socratic Models demonstrate competitive quantitative performance on standard benchmarks and enable novel qualitative applications across various multimodal domains.

**Quantitative Performance:**

- **Image Captioning (MS COCO)**: SMs significantly outperform zero-shot state-of-the-art methods like ZeroCap (CIDEr score 18.0 → 44.5). While not matching fine-tuned methods (like ClipCap), SMs produce verbose and descriptive captions. When few-shot prompted with 3 examples, SMs achieve a CIDEr of 76.3, surpassing MAGIC (which fine-tunes the text generator on unpaired captions).
- **Contextual Image Captioning and Description (Concadia)**: SMs demonstrate strong zero-shot performance, outperforming prior task-specific methods that fine-tune on the dataset. SMs improve CIDEr from 11.3 to 38.9 for caption generation and from 17.4 to 22.6 for description generation. This highlights their ability to leverage article text context effectively.
- **Video-to-Text Retrieval (MSR-VTT)**: SMs set a new zero-shot state-of-the-art, improving R@1 from 40.3 (CLIP-only baseline) to 44.7 on the full test set and from 58.0 to 60.7 on the 1k-A subset. The improvement is attributed to incorporating language-based multimodal reasoning via audio-to-text transcripts (ALM + LM), which substantially increases performance for videos with sufficiently long transcripts (R@1 from 41.5 to 54.9 on this subset).

**Qualitative Applications and New Capabilities:**

- **Egocentric Perception**:
 - **Summarization**: SMs can augment human memory by summarizing first-person video content into textual event logs.
 - **Open-ended Q&A**: By transforming video understanding into a "reading comprehension" problem via language-based world-state history, SMs answer free-form questions about egocentric video, including contextual, temporal, and even cause-and-effect questions (e.g., "why did I go to the front porch today?" -> "to receive a package.").
 - **Forecasting**: SMs can generate plausible future events based on the language-based world-state history, moving beyond binary event classification.
- **Multimodal Assistive Dialogue**: SMs can guide users through tasks like cooking recipes. An LM processes user dialogue, providing instructions, ingredient replacements (using LM priors), and triggering external APIs for visual references (e.g., web search for recipe videos, VLM for frame retrieval based on description). The system can dynamically incorporate external information.
- **Robot Perception and Planning**: SMs enable language-conditioned robot tasks. A VLM describes objects in the scene; this description is fed to an LM for multi-step planning (generating pseudocode or natural language instructions), which then interfaces with a language-conditioned robot policy (e.g., for pick-and-place). This expands robot capabilities beyond pre-trained primitives and enables human-robot dialogue.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Socratic Models represent a significant conceptual shift in building AI systems, moving from monolithic, task-specific training to a modular, compositional approach driven by language-based communication between diverse foundation models.

**Significance:**

- **Zero-Shot Multimodal Capabilities**: The most impactful aspect is the ability to achieve new, complex multimodal functionalities without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning of the underlying models. This drastically reduces the computational cost and data requirements for deploying AI in new domains.
- **Leveraging Existing Knowledge**: SMs effectively tap into the vast and complementary commonsense knowledge already embedded within different foundation models (LMs, VLMs, ALMs) without forcing them into a unified architecture or data distribution.
- **Interpretability**: By using language as the intermediate representation, the interactions between models become more transparent and interpretable, offering a "window" into the system's reasoning process even for non-experts. This contrasts with opaque end-to-end deep learning models.
- **Agility and Resource Efficiency**: The framework allows for rapid prototyping and deployment of new applications with minimal engineering and computational resources, making advanced multimodal AI more accessible for data-scarce domains or smaller research groups.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity**: SMs provide a strong alternative for applications where obtaining abundant labeled multimodal training data is challenging or prohibitively expensive (e.g., egocentric vision, specific robotics tasks).
- **Maintaining Generality and Robustness**: Unlike fine-tuning, which can sometimes lead to a loss of the pretrained models' generality and robustness to distribution shifts, SMs preserve these inherent qualities by keeping the foundation models frozen.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Democratization of Multimodal AI**: By reducing the barrier to entry (no massive datasets, no fine-tuning), SMs could accelerate the development and adoption of multimodal AI across various fields, enabling smaller teams to build sophisticated systems.
- **Enhanced Human-AI Interaction**: Applications like assistive dialogue highlight the potential for more natural and helpful interactions between humans and AI systems, where AI can reason across modalities and integrate external information dynamically.
- **Advanced Robotics and AR**: The demonstrated capabilities in egocentric perception and robot planning suggest a future where robots can understand and act on complex human instructions grounded in real-world sensory input, fostering more intuitive human-robot collaboration.
- **New Research Avenues**: SMs open up new research directions, such as meta-learning the Socratic interactions themselves (i.e., learning how to best prompt and orchestrate models) and exploring inter-module communication beyond language (e.g., passing images or embeddings).
- **Sustainable AI Development**: By optimizing the use of existing pretrained models and minimizing additional training, SMs align with efforts to reduce the significant energy and resource consumption associated with large-scale AI development.

**Limitations and Broader Impacts (as acknowledged by authors):**

- **Inherited Unreliability and Biases**: SMs inherit the unreliability and biases present in the foundation models they compose, which are often trained on Internet-scale data containing societal biases. This necessitates careful monitoring and verification in downstream applications.
- **Overconfidence**: LMs can be overconfident even when providing incorrect information, which is a general challenge in their deployment and applies to SMs.
- **Limited Scope of Sub-Modules**: The quality and richness of reasoning are constrained by the capabilities of the individual foundation models and the chosen key frame sampling strategies (e.g., for world-state history).
- **Unintended End Use**: The flexibility and ease of use of SMs increase the potential for unintended or malicious applications, requiring ongoing ethical oversight.

In conclusion, Socratic Models propose a powerful, flexible, and resource-efficient paradigm for building advanced multimodal AI systems by orchestrating language-based "conversations" between specialized foundation models. This work sets a new direction for exploring complex AI capabilities while addressing some of the critical challenges associated with large-scale model development and deployment.
