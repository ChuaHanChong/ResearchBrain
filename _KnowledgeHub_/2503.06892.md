# SafePlan: Leveraging Formal Logic and Chain-of-Thought Reasoning for Enhanced Safety in LLM-based Robotic Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> SafePlan introduces a multi-component framework that integrates formal logic and Chain-of-Thought reasoning to enhance the safety of LLM-based robotic task planning. The framework achieved 88.4% accuracy in identifying and rejecting unsafe task prompts and reduced robot crash rates to 0% in simulated execution scenarios, where baseline models experienced 100% crash rates.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) used in robotics often lack inherent reasoning about physical safety, ethical implications, and contextual risks, leading to potentially unsafe or impractical task plans.
- Current safety mechanisms for LLM-based robotics are primarily designed for linguistic contexts, failing to adequately address the unique safety considerations of physical robot interaction and execution.
- LLM-robot systems are vulnerable to accepting dangerous or unethical instructions and adversarial attacks, without robust rejection mechanisms at early stages of task planning.

## Method

- **SafePlan** proposes a multi-component framework integrating formal logic and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for hierarchical safety verification across the entire planning pipeline.
- It includes a ==Prompt Sanity Check CoT Reasoner== with three progressive layers (Societal, Organizational, Individual Alignment) that use formal logic to proactively filter out unsafe natural language commands.
- An Invariant CoT Reasoner generates and verifies preconditions, postconditions, and invariants for robot tasks, guiding LLMs in generating safer code and validating it before execution.

## Results

- SafePlan achieved an overall accuracy of **88.4%** in prompt safety classification using Gemini **1.5** Pro, rejecting **92.9%** of unsafe tasks compared to baseline models which accepted a high percentage (**61.3**-**85.8%**).
- The framework significantly improved the ethical-to-unethical acceptance ratio, reaching up to **10.0** for Gemini **1.5** Pro, indicating a much higher preference for safe tasks over unsafe ones.
- In AI2-THOR simulations, SafePlan reduced robot crash rates to **0%** for error-prone tasks across different categories, while baseline approaches exhibited crash rates up to **100%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating formal logic with Chain-of-Thought reasoning provides a structured and systematic approach for LLMs to reason about safety in robotic task planning.
> - A hierarchical, multi-layered verification process, starting from initial prompt interpretation through to code execution, is crucial for comprehensive safety assurance in physical robot systems.
> - Proactive rejection of unsafe or malicious commands at the initial prompt stage is highly effective in preventing downstream errors and enhancing overall system trustworthiness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotics has emerged as a transformative trend, promising to enhance robot adaptability, natural language interaction, and complex task planning. Researchers are increasingly leveraging LLMs for tasks such as receiving human commands, generating intricate task plans, forming multi-robot team coalitions, and allocating tasks efficiently among human and robot agents. This paradigm shift enables robots to interpret high-level natural language instructions and translate them into executable actions, significantly lowering the barrier for human-robot collaboration.

However, this rapid adoption has exposed a critical gap: the inherent safety limitations of LLMs when operating in the physical world. Traditional LLM safety mechanisms are primarily designed for question-answering contexts and fall short in addressing the unique safety considerations of physical robotic systems. Prior research has highlighted several vulnerabilities:
- **Insufficient Physical World Reasoning:** LLMs lack a strong understanding of physical context and scene representation, making them prone to generating plans that are physically unsafe or impractical.
- **Vulnerability to Adversarial Attacks:** LLM/VLM-robot systems can be susceptible to prompt or perception manipulation, degrading performance and potentially leading to unsafe outcomes (Wu et al.).
- **Failure to Reject Dangerous Instructions:** LLMs often fail to reject dangerous, unlawful, or unethical instructions, particularly in open-vocabulary settings, risking discrimination, violence, or other harmful actions (Azeem et al., Hundt et al.).
- **Focus on Capability over Safety:** Much of the contemporary LLM-based robotics research prioritizes expanding capabilities in task planning and allocation (e.g., Singh et al., Kannan et al., Mandi et al., Obata et al.), without adequately addressing the safety profiles of the generated plans or code.

While some existing works have attempted to enhance safety, they often address later stages of the pipeline or rely on resource-intensive methods:
- Wu et al. introduced SELP to improve task planning safety but implicitly assumes the initial task prompts are safe.
- Yang et al. developed a queryable safety constraint module based on Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) to enforce compliance during task planning and execution, primarily focusing on post-prompt processing.
- Althobaiti et al. proposed a safety layer to verify LLM-generated code before execution, but their approach relied on costly fine-tuning and curated datasets.

SafePlan directly addresses these shortcomings by introducing a multi-component framework that proactively assesses and enforces safety *throughout the entire pipeline*, from initial prompt interpretation to code generation and verification. By integrating formal logic and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, SafePlan aims to bridge the gap between LLMs' linguistic capabilities and the stringent safety requirements of real-world robotic deployment. It distinguishes itself by emphasizing early interception of unsafe commands and comprehensive logical validation, thereby enhancing the trustworthiness and reliability of LLM-driven robots operating in human environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind SafePlan stems from the observed safety deficiencies of Large Language Models when employed in robotics. While LLMs offer unprecedented capabilities for flexible task specification and planning, their lack of inherent reasoning about physical safety, ethical implications, and contextual risks poses significant challenges for deployment in human-centric environments. The authors highlight the dangers of a robot being commanded to perform a seemingly innocuous task like "Place this basketball on grandma’s seat" without understanding the potential harm, or a task like "Go to Room 447 and place this bottle of dial on top of the medication drawer for the patient Paul" without considering critical preconditions, postconditions, and invariants.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of SafePlan are to:

- **Enhance System Safety Profile:** Develop a robust framework to investigate and significantly improve the safety profile of LLM-based robotics systems.
- **Reject Unsafe Prompts Proactively:** Implement mechanisms to reject malicious or unsafe natural language task prompts at the earliest stage, before any task decomposition or execution planning occurs.
- **Ensure Logical Consistency of Task Plans:** Guarantee that task plans and allocations generated by LLMs adhere to critical safety requirements by examining, refining, or rejecting outputs that could lead to harmful outcomes.
- **Integrate Formal Logic with Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** Utilize formal logic, combined with invariant, precondition, and postcondition formulations, within a chain-of-thought reasoning framework to guide LLMs towards safer plan generation and verification.
- **Develop a Multi-Component Safety Framework:** Introduce SafePlan as a comprehensive, multi-component framework featuring distinct reasoners for prompt sanity checking and invariant-based verification.
- **Contribute a Safety Benchmark:** Provide a novel, expert-curated benchmark of task prompts and scene descriptions to rigorously evaluate the safety performance of LLM-based robotic systems.
- **Validate in Simulation:** Empirically demonstrate the efficacy of the SafePlan framework in a realistic simulated robotics environment (AI2-THOR) against error-prone tasks.

Fundamentally, the research aims to answer the question: "How can we enhance the safety guarantees of code produced by LLM-based robotics systems by employing a logic-enhanced LLM as a reasoner?"

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SafePlan is designed as a multi-component framework that systematically enhances robotic safety by integrating formal logic and chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning across multiple stages of an LLM-based robotic task planning pipeline. The approach is characterized by its hierarchical verification structure, starting from the initial natural language prompt and extending to the generated robot code.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The problem is formulated as a multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) where a human agent provides a natural language prompt to a team of robots. The system seeks an allocation function, `π: T → R ∪ {∅}`, which assigns each task `t_i` to either a robot `r_j` or rejects it (`∅`) if it's unsafe. The core innovation is a four-level verification process:
1. **Prompt Sanity Check:** Determines if a prompt is safe enough for further processing.
2. **Invariant COT Reasoner (Generation):** For safe prompts, generates predictions (preconditions, postconditions, invariants).
3. **Code Generation:** Uses these predictions as few-shot examples to guide a second LLM in generating task code.
4. **Invariant COT Reasoner (Verification):** Verifies the generated code against the initial predictions using a third LLM.

**B. Prompt Sanity Checking COT Reasoner**
This component acts as the first line of defense, parsing natural language prompts into essential elements (actions `a`, entities `e`, resources `r`, location `l`). It employs three progressive reasoning layers, each outputting a deontic label (Forbidden `F` or Permissible `P`):

1. **Societal Alignment Layer (Layer_S):** Evaluates prompts against fundamental societal and legal safety requirements. This layer formalizes checks for:
 - Actions forbidden by law (`LawForbids(a, e, l)`).
 - Actions involving protected categories (`ProtectedCategory(e, r) ∧ Involves(a, r)`).
 - Violations of societal values (`SocValViolated(a, e, r, l, v)`).
 - Involvement of illegal or restricted resources (`IllegalOrRestricted(r) ∧ Involves(a, r)`).
 - Actions required by law are treated as permissible (`LawRequires(a, e, l)` → P).
 - If not forbidden, it defaults to permissible.

2. **Organizational Alignment Layer (Layer_O):** Builds upon societal constraints, evaluating prompts against specific institutional policies, role-based constraints, and group protocols. This layer formalizes checks for:
 - Actions forbidden by organizational policy or group norms (`OrgPolicyForbids(a, l) ∨ GroupProhibits(a, l)`).
 - Actions infringing on roles (`InfringesRole(a, e, l)`).
 - Violations of organizational values (`OrgValueViolated(a, e, r, l, v)`).
 - Use of unacceptable resources (`ResourceUnacceptable(r, l) ∧ Involves(a, r)`).
 - Actions required by organizational policy are treated as permissible.
 - If not forbidden, it defaults to permissible.

3. **Individual Alignment Layer (Layer_I):** Provides the finest granularity of safety reasoning, examining commands at the personal level to prevent harm to specific individuals. This layer formalizes checks for:
 - Direct harm to an individual (`HarmTo(a, e)`).
 - Individual incapability (`IndividualCannotDo(a, e)`).
 - Violations of personal values (`PersonalValueViolated(a, e, r, l, v)`).
 - Involvement of resources unsafe due to allergies or other individual vulnerabilities (`AllergyOrUnsafe(r, e) ∧ Involves(a, r)`).
 - Actions required by personal ethics (e.g., for a vulnerable person) are treated as permissible.
 - If not forbidden, it defaults to permissible.

This entire formal logic framework is operationalized as a structured system prompt, guiding LLMs through a hierarchical CoT process, ensuring systematic safety evaluation.

**C. Invariant COT Reasoner**
For prompts that pass the sanity check, the Invariant COT Reasoner employs concepts from Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) to formalize safety properties. It systematically analyzes prompts to produce:
- **Invariants:** Conditions that must hold true throughout task execution.
- **Preconditions:** Requirements that must be met *before* an action can be performed.
- **Postconditions:** Expected outcomes *after* an action is completed.

The process involves:
1. **Parsing:** Extracting the Goal (G) and Initial Conditions (I) from the prompt.
2. **Entity Identification:** Using atomic predicates (e.g., `At(R, l)`, `Holding(R, o)`) to represent the world state.
3. **Global Invariants:** Defining constraints like object integrity (no object lost/duplicated) and static object conditions (e.g., `GarbageCan` remains fixed).
4. **Action Specification:** Enumerating candidate actions with explicit preconditions and postconditions. For example, `PickupObject(R, o)` has preconditions ensuring the robot is at the object, not already holding it, the object is in the list, and the robot has the skill.
5. **Synonym Handling:** Normalizing object names using a `Canonical(o)` mapping to ensure consistency.
6. **Skill Verification:** Ensuring the robot possesses the necessary skills (`HasSkill(R, Skill)`).
7. **Structured Output:** Generating a structured list of goals, initial conditions, invariants, and action sequences with their logical specifications. This output, along with the task prompt, environmental objects, and robot skills, serves as a few-shot prompt for a *second LLM* tasked with generating the robot code. This prompt instructs the LLM to reject the task if any of these conditions are violated.
8. **Verification of Code:** A *third LLM*, also leveraging the Invariant COT Reasoner, verifies the generated code against these predefined specifications to ensure adherence to safety requirements before deployment.

**D. Experimental Validation**

1. **SafePlan Benchmark:**
 - A custom synthetic benchmark of 621 expert-curated robot tasks (127 safe, 494 unsafe).
 - Spans three domains (assistive, navigation, manipulation) and three complexity levels (simple, medium, complex).
 - Evaluated OpenAI GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0-flash, and Gemini 1.5-Pro: first with the Prompt Sanity Check COT Reasoner, then with a standard natural language system prompt (baseline).
 - Metrics: Acceptance rates (safe/unsafe), confusion matrix (TP, TN, FP, FN, accuracy, precision, recall, F1), ethical-to-unethical acceptance ratio, and McNemar’s test for statistical significance.

2. **Simulation Experiment (AI2-THOR):**
 - Evaluated the full SafePlan framework, including Invariant COT Reasoners, in the AI2-THOR simulation environment.
 - Used 18 error-prone tasks categorized into Scene Reasoning, Skill Reasoning, and Language Ambiguity.
 - **Pipeline Comparison:**
 - **Baseline:** LLM-generated code executed directly.
 - **SafePlan:** LLM-generated code verified up to three times; if faults are detected, code is blocked; otherwise, executed.
 - **Metrics:** Executed Percentage (E%), Fault Detected Percentage (FD%), Fault Detection minus False Positive Percentage (FD-FP%), Crash Rate Percentage (CR%), and a combined Success Rate (SR%) defined as `E% × (1 − CR_fraction) + F`, where `F` is the effective fault detection rate. This SR% accounts for both successful execution and effective fault interception.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SafePlan demonstrated significant improvements in safety for LLM-based robotic task planning, both in prompt discrimination and in simulated task execution.

**A. Benchmark Results (Prompt Sanity Check COT Reasoner)**

- **Overall Performance:** Models augmented with SafePlan consistently outperformed baselines in discriminating between safe and unsafe prompts. Gemini 1.5 Pro with SafePlan achieved the highest accuracy (88.4%), while accepting 70.9% of safe tasks and rejecting an impressive 92.9% of unsafe tasks. In contrast, baseline models, while accepting all safe tasks (near-perfect recall), failed to reject a large percentage of unsafe tasks (61.3-85.8% acceptance).
- **Ethical-to-Unethical Acceptance Ratio (E/U Ratio):** This metric clearly highlighted SafePlan's impact. Gemini 1.5 Pro with SafePlan achieved an E/U Ratio of 10.0, meaning it accepted safe tasks at 10 times the rate of unethical ones. Without SafePlan, the ratio for the same model was only 1.63. Similar improvements were observed for GPT4o (9.79 vs. 1.35) and Gemini Flash 2.0 (7.47 vs. 1.17).
- **Task Domain Analysis:** SafePlan maintained robust performance across different task domains (navigation, manipulation, assistive). For manipulation tasks, which formed the majority of the benchmark, Gemini 1.5 Pro with SafePlan achieved 91.7% accuracy with only a 4.7% unsafe task acceptance rate. Assistive tasks, potentially due to their nuanced ethical considerations, showed slightly higher unsafe acceptance rates (28.9% for Gemini Pro 1.5 with SafePlan), though still significantly better than baselines.
- **Impact of Task Complexity:** SafePlan's effectiveness scaled well with task complexity. For complex tasks, Gemini Flash 2.0 with SafePlan achieved an exceptional E/U ratio of 43.2, indicating that the formal logic approach provides particular advantages for intricate scenarios where simpler rules might fail.
- **Statistical Significance:** McNemar’s test confirmed the statistical significance of SafePlan’s improvements for GPT4o (χ²=14.78, p=0.0001) and Gemini Flash 2.0 (χ²=34.83, p<0.0001), validating the framework's impact.

**B. Experimental Simulation Results (Invariant COT Reasoner on AI2-THOR)**

- **Overall Success Rate (SR%):** The Invariant COT Reasoner component of SafePlan dramatically improved the overall success rate of task allocation and execution. Baseline GPT4o achieved only a 22% SR, whereas SafePlan implementations yielded significantly higher rates: 84% (Gemini Flash 2.0), 95% (Gemini 1.5 Pro), and 95% (GPT4o).
- **Scene Reasoning Tasks:** The baseline model executed all tasks (100% E%) but crashed on every single one (100% CR%). SafePlan models, in contrast, intercepted most erroneous tasks before execution (0-16% E%) and achieved a 0% crash rate for any tasks that did proceed to execution, demonstrating high fault detection (84-100% F%).
- **Skill Reasoning Tasks:** A similar pattern emerged here. The baseline exhibited a 100% crash rate. SafePlan significantly reduced the execution of problematic tasks (16-50% E%) and maintained strong fault detection (50-84%), with GPT4o with SafePlan achieving a 0% crash rate even for executed tasks.
- **Language Ambiguity Tasks:** While the baseline performed somewhat better (66% SR%), it still executed all tasks. SafePlan models maintained high success rates (84-100%) and, importantly, reduced crash rates to 0% despite higher execution rates (84-100% E%).

These results unequivocally demonstrate that SafePlan effectively enhances safety in LLM-based robotic systems by either preventing the execution of likely-to-fail tasks (high F%) or ensuring that tasks that are executed complete successfully (low CR%). This validates the effectiveness of combining formal logic with chain-of-thought reasoning for a comprehensive safety assurance in robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SafePlan framework represents a significant advancement in the field of LLM-based robotics, addressing one of its most critical and underexplored challenges: safety. The findings underscore its substantial significance and potential impact:

- **Bridging the Safety Gap in LLM Robotics:** SafePlan directly tackles the fundamental problem that LLMs, while powerful linguistic tools, lack inherent physical and ethical reasoning capabilities crucial for safe operation in the real world. By integrating formal logic and structured chain-of-thought reasoning, it moves beyond ad-hoc safety measures to provide a systematic and verifiable approach.
- **Proactive Harm Prevention:** Unlike many existing approaches that focus on mitigating errors during or after task execution, SafePlan emphasizes proactive harm prevention. Its Prompt Sanity Check COT Reasoner intercepts unsafe or malicious commands at the initial prompt stage, effectively "pruning out" dangerous tasks before they can even be decomposed into plans. This early intervention is vital for preventing potentially catastrophic outcomes in physical systems.
- **Enhanced Trustworthiness and Reliability:** By drastically reducing the acceptance of harmful prompts (90.5% reduction) and minimizing crash rates in simulated environments, SafePlan significantly enhances the trustworthiness and reliability of LLM-driven robots. This is a prerequisite for widespread public acceptance and deployment of autonomous systems in sensitive domains like healthcare, elder care, and industrial settings.
- **Leveraging Formal Methods for Practical AI Safety:** The strategic use of formal logic, inspired by deontic logic for prompt checking and LTL concepts for invariant reasoning, brings a level of precision and verifiability often lacking in purely data-driven or natural language-based AI safety solutions. This integration demonstrates a promising pathway for combining the flexibility of LLMs with the rigor of formal methods.
- **Robustness Across Diverse Scenarios:** The empirical validation across different task domains (assistive, navigation, manipulation) and complexity levels (simple, medium, complex), as well as various error categories (scene reasoning, skill reasoning, language ambiguity), highlights SafePlan's robustness and broad applicability. This suggests its potential utility in a wide array of robotic applications.
- **Contribution of a Valuable Benchmark:** The creation of an expert-curated benchmark of 621 prompt-scene description pairs for safety evaluation is a notable contribution to the research community. This benchmark provides a standardized tool for future researchers to test and compare the safety performance of novel LLM-robot systems.
- **Foundational for Responsible AI Deployment:** SafePlan contributes significantly to the discourse and practical implementation of responsible AI. As LLMs become more pervasive in critical infrastructure and human-robot interaction, frameworks like SafePlan are essential for ensuring that technological advancement is coupled with robust ethical and safety guardrails.

In conclusion, SafePlan provides a crucial architectural blueprint for building safer and more reliable LLM-based robotic systems. Its multi-layered, logic-driven approach marks an important step towards realizing the full potential of LLM-enabled robotics while mitigating the inherent risks, paving the way for their safer and more ethical integration into society. Future work exploring its implications in dynamic and more complex real-world scenarios will further solidify its impact.
