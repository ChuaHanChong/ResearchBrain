# AgentTuning: Enabling Generalized Agent Abilities for LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentTuning enables open-source large language models with generalized AI agent capabilities, introducing AgentLM models that achieve performance comparable to GPT-3.5-turbo on unseen agent tasks. This approach maintains general linguistic and reasoning abilities through a hybrid instruction-tuning strategy on the novel AgentInstruct dataset.

## Problem

- Open-source LLMs like Llama 2 significantly lag behind commercial models (e.g., GPT-4) in complex agent tasks requiring planning, memory, and tool use.
- Existing approaches to LLM agents are either limited to prompt engineering (not enhancing intrinsic abilities) or task-specific fine-tuning (sacrificing general capabilities).
- The performance gap restricts innovation and broader research within the open-source community for developing advanced AI agent applications.

## Method

- Developed **AgentInstruct**, a lightweight, high-quality ==instruction-tuning dataset== comprising 1,866 ==multi-turn interaction trajectories== generated by GPT-4 across six diverse agent tasks.
- Employed a ==hybrid instruction-tuning== strategy that mixes agent-specific data from AgentInstruct with general-domain instructions to enhance agent capabilities while preserving general LLM skills.
- Fine-tuned Llama 2 chat models (7B, 13B, 70B) into **AgentLM** using the combined dataset, focusing on learning ==chain-of-thought reasoning== and appropriate actions from high-quality filtered trajectories.

## Results

- AgentLM models demonstrate substantial improvements over Llama 2 across all scales on both held-in and held-out agent tasks, with AgentLM-70B achieving a **+176%** improvement on held-out tasks.
- AgentLM-70B achieves performance on unseen agent tasks comparable to GPT-3.5-turbo, and on some tasks, it rivals or surpasses GPT-4.
- Evaluation on standard NLP benchmarks confirms that AgentLM successfully preserves general LLM capabilities (e.g., MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) at levels comparable to the original Llama 2 models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General language understanding and reasoning abilities are foundational for generalized agent intelligence and must be preserved during fine-tuning.
> - Larger base models exhibit greater improvements in generalization to unseen agent tasks when fine-tuned with diverse interaction trajectories.
> - Targeted instruction tuning can 'activate' and 'enable' an LLM's latent potential for planning and reasoning by reducing elementary errors rather than teaching entirely new concepts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical gap in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and AI agents. While open-source LLMs like Llama 2 have achieved impressive general language understanding and generation capabilities, they significantly lag behind commercial models such as ChatGPT and GPT-4 when tasked with acting as autonomous agents in complex, real-world scenarios. This performance disparity, highlighted by recent studies like AgentBench (Liu et al., 2023), hampers further innovation and in-depth research within the open-source community, as many advanced agent applications rely heavily on the robust planning, reasoning, and tool-use abilities currently monopolized by closed models.

The existing research on LLMs as agents has primarily focused on two avenues:
1. **Prompt Engineering and Framework Design:** Many studies propose sophisticated prompting methods (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, ReAct) or build LLM-based frameworks (e.g., AutoGPT, GPT-Engineer) to enable specific agent functionalities for a given LLM. While effective for particular tasks, these approaches do not fundamentally enhance the LLM's intrinsic agent capabilities or generalize well across diverse agent domains.
2. **Task-Specific Fine-tuning:** Some efforts involve fine-tuning LLMs on datasets tailored to very specific tasks. However, this often leads to a trade-off, where specialized performance gains come at the expense of the LLM's broader general abilities and overall generalizability.

AgentTuning distinguishes itself by offering a **general and fundamental solution** to enhance LLMs' agent capabilities *without compromising their general linguistic and reasoning skills*. It represents the "very first attempt to instruction-tune LLMs using interaction trajectories across multiple agent tasks." This approach marks a departure from task-specific fine-tuning or external prompting strategies by integrating agent-centric knowledge directly into the model's weights through a carefully curated instruction-tuning process. By making open-source LLMs competitive in agent tasks, this work democratizes access to advanced AI agent technology, fostering broader research and development in areas like robotics, intelligent automation, and human-computer interaction, which currently depend on expensive and proprietary models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind AgentTuning stems from the observed performance gap between powerful commercial LLMs (like GPT-3.5 and GPT-4) and publicly available open-source LLMs (like Llama 2 and Vicuna) specifically in complex "agent tasks." These tasks require LLMs to act as central controllers, involving iterative planning, memory management, and effective tool utilization in dynamic environments. The current inferiority of open-source models in this domain is seen as a significant barrier to advancing LLM research and fostering community innovation, as it restricts the scope of what can be built and explored with accessible models.

Given this landscape, the key objectives of the AgentTuning project are multi-faceted:

- **Fundamentally Enhance Generalized Agent Abilities:** The core objective is to improve the intrinsic agent capabilities of LLMs, moving beyond specific task adaptations or prompting tricks, to enable them to perform robustly across a wide range of unseen agent tasks.
- **Preserve General LLM Capabilities:** Crucially, this enhancement must not come at the cost of the LLMs' general linguistic, reasoning, and knowledge-based abilities, which are essential for their versatility. The goal is to achieve specialized agent performance while maintaining overall general intelligence.
- **Construct a High-Quality, Lightweight Instruction-Tuning Dataset:** To achieve the above, a dedicated dataset, AgentInstruct, is required. This dataset needs to capture high-quality, multi-turn interaction trajectories involving planning, action, and observation, ideally with reasoning traces (Chain-of-Thought). It must also be lightweight enough to be practical for fine-tuning.
- **Develop a Hybrid Instruction-Tuning Strategy:** A training strategy is needed that effectively blends agent-specific data with general-domain instructions to balance agent capability enhancement with the preservation of general abilities.
- **Open-Source Resources:** To accelerate open research and development, the project aims to open-source both the AgentInstruct dataset and the resulting fine-tuned LLMs (AgentLM series), providing accessible and powerful alternatives to commercial models for agent-related applications.

In essence, the project aims to equip open-source LLMs with the robust, generalized agent intelligence currently associated with state-of-the-art commercial models, thereby broadening the horizons for AI agent research and application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AgentTuning approach is characterized by two core components: the creation of a specialized instruction-tuning dataset called **AgentInstruct**, and a **hybrid instruction-tuning strategy** that leverages this dataset in conjunction with general-domain data.

#### 3.1. Constructing AgentInstruct

AgentInstruct is the first attempt to build a comprehensive dataset of interaction trajectories specifically designed to improve generalized agent abilities. Its construction involves three meticulously designed stages:

1. **Instruction Construction:**
 - **Task Selection:** Six diverse agent tasks are chosen, representing a wide array of real-world scenarios: ALFWorld (household routines), WebShop (online shopping), Mind2Web (website navigation), Knowledge Graph (information retrieval), Operating System (OS interaction), and Database (DB operations). These tasks are drawn largely from the challenging AgentBench benchmark.
 - **Data Sourcing:** For tasks with existing training splits (ALFWorld, WebShop, Mind2Web, Knowledge Graph), these are directly utilized.
 - **Synthetic Instruction Generation:** For tasks lacking training sets (Operating System, Database), two methods are employed:
 - **Task Derivation (for Database):** Instructions and complete trajectories are constructed by adapting existing datasets like BIRD (a SELECT-only database benchmark). This involves generating SQL queries, executing them, and then using GPT-4 to fill in the agent's "thoughts" retrospectively, or by prompting GPT-4 to generate a trajectory and filtering for correctness.
 - **Self-Instruct (for Operating System and other Database operations):** GPT-4 is prompted to generate diverse tasks, along with reference solutions and evaluation scripts. Another GPT-4 instance (the solver) then attempts these tasks, and only correct trajectories are collected. This ensures high-quality, task-agnostic instruction generation.
 - **Data Contamination Control:** A systematic analysis (detailed in Appendix B) was performed to ensure no data leakage or contamination between the training and test sets, especially from the synthetic generation process.

2. **Trajectory Interaction:**
 - **Agent Role:** GPT-4 (gpt-4-0613) is used as the primary agent to generate interaction trajectories for the collected instructions. For Mind2Web, GPT-3.5-turbo (gpt-3.5-turbo-0613) was partially used due to budget constraints.
 - **1-Shot Evaluation:** To guide the models towards the required output format, a single, successful example trajectory from the training set is provided as a 1-shot prompt for each task.
 - **Interaction Loop:** The process involves the model receiving a task, generating a "Thought" (reasoning) and an "Action," and then receiving "Observation" feedback from the environment. This cycle continues until task completion or token limit. A mechanism to detect and handle repetitive failures is also implemented.
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Rationales:** The ReAct (Reasoning and Acting) framework is adopted, requiring the model to output a detailed "thought" before each "action." This enriches the trajectories with explicit reasoning processes, enabling the fine-tuned model to learn not just the actions but the underlying logic.

3. **Trajectory Filtering:**
 - **Quality Control:** To ensure high data quality, trajectories are rigorously filtered based on a "reward" score. For most tasks, only trajectories with a perfect reward (r=1, indicating complete correctness) are retained. For the particularly challenging Mind2Web task, a slightly lower threshold (r ≥ 2/3) is used to ensure sufficient data quantity.
 - **Impact of Filtering:** An ablation study confirms that training on filtered, high-quality trajectories significantly outperforms training on unfiltered data, underscoring the importance of data quality for agent tasks.

The final AgentInstruct dataset comprises 1,866 verified trajectories across the six tasks, totaling 35,341 instructions.

#### 3.2. Instruction Tuning

The second crucial component is the hybrid instruction-tuning strategy:

1. **Base Models:** The Llama 2 chat series (7B, 13B, and 70B variants) are chosen as the foundation models due to their strong instruction-following abilities.
2. **General Domain Instructions:** To preserve general LLM capabilities, the AgentInstruct dataset is mixed with a large, high-quality general-domain dataset. This general dataset is derived from `ShareGPT`, comprising 57,096 conversations with GPT-3.5 and 3,670 with GPT-4, with a 1:4 sampling ratio of GPT-4 to GPT-3.5 data to prioritize quality.
3. **Mixture Training:**
 - A weighted loss function is defined, combining the losses from AgentInstruct and the general dataset with a mixing ratio `η`.
 - Through ablation studies on the 7B model, `η = 0.2` (meaning 20% agent data, 80% general data) is found to be optimal for generalization on held-out tasks.
 - The authors specifically highlight that training *solely* on agent data leads to a decline in generalization performance, emphasizing the critical role of general capabilities for robust agent generalization.
4. **Training Setup:**
 - All data is standardized into a multi-turn chatbot-style format.
 - Loss is computed only on the model's output.
 - Training is conducted using Megatron-LM for efficiency, employing tensor parallelism (for 7B, 13B) and pipeline parallelism (for 70B). Standard optimization techniques (AdamW, cosine learning rate scheduler with warm-up, gradient clipping) are applied, with specific learning rates tailored to model size.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that AgentLM models are not just fine-tuned for specific agent tasks but learn a generalized agent intelligence while retaining their broad utility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate AgentTuning yielded several significant findings:

- **Superior Agent Performance:** AgentLM models demonstrate remarkable improvements over their Llama 2 (chat) counterparts across all scales (7B, 13B, 70B) in both held-in (tasks present in AgentInstruct) and held-out (unseen) agent tasks.
 - For held-in tasks, Llama 2 often performs near zero, indicating a severe lack of agent capabilities, whereas AgentLM achieves substantial scores. For instance, AgentLM-70B achieves an overall score of 2.55 on held-in tasks, a drastic improvement from Llama 2 70B's 0.27.
 - On held-out tasks, AgentLM also shows significant gains, with the 70B model achieving a +176% improvement over Llama 2 70B, demonstrating robust generalization.
- **Competitiveness with Commercial Models:** AgentLM-70B achieves performance on unseen agent tasks that is comparable to GPT-3.5-turbo. On some specific tasks (e.g., ALFWorld, WebShop, WebArena), AgentLM-70B even rivals or surpasses GPT-4's performance, highlighting its effectiveness. The paper states AgentLM-70B is the "first open LLM that matches GPT-3.5-turbo on agent tasks."
- **Preservation of General Abilities:** Crucially, AgentTuning successfully enhances agent capabilities without compromising the LLMs' general performance. Evaluations on standard NLP benchmarks (MMLU for knowledge, GSM8K for math, HumanEval for coding, and MT-Bench for human preference) show that AgentLM maintains performance on par with or very close to the original Llama 2 models. This indicates that the hybrid tuning strategy effectively avoids catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge.
- **Scaling Benefits for Generalization:** Larger AgentLM models (e.g., 70B) exhibit a significantly greater improvement in generalization (176% on held-out tasks) compared to smaller models (7B at 76%). This suggests that larger models, with their inherent stronger generalization capabilities, are better positioned to leverage the AgentInstruct data for broader agent intelligence.
- **Reduced Elementary Errors:** An in-depth error analysis reveals that AgentTuning substantially reduces common, elementary mistakes observed in the base Llama 2 models, such as invalid actions, short-term and long-term repetitions, and outright refusal to answer. This reduction in basic errors suggests that AgentTuning doesn't just teach specific actions but "activates" or "enables" the LLM's underlying potential for planning and reasoning crucial for agent tasks, rather than merely causing it to overfit.
- **Importance of Hybrid Training:** Ablation studies confirm that mixed training with both agent-specific and general-domain instructions is vital for robust generalization. Training solely on agent data improves held-in task performance but severely degrades generalization to unseen agent tasks and overall general abilities. This finding underscores the hypothesis that general language understanding and reasoning are foundational for generalized agent intelligence.
- **Task-Specific Contributions:** While fine-tuning on individual tasks primarily boosts performance on that specific task, some positive transfer effects to other tasks are observed. Mind2Web is noted as an exception with minimal cross-task enhancement, possibly due to its unique single-round interaction format compared to multi-round tasks.

In summary, AgentTuning successfully demonstrates a viable path to equip open-source LLMs with generalized agent capabilities that are competitive with commercial models, all while preserving their foundational general intelligence, largely attributed to its innovative dataset and hybrid training strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AgentTuning research holds significant implications for the future of large language models and the development of AI agents, particularly within the open-source community:

- **Democratization of Advanced AI Agent Capabilities:** By providing a methodology and open-sourcing AgentInstruct and the AgentLM models (7B, 13B, 70B), this work directly challenges the current reliance on closed-source, proprietary LLMs for complex agent tasks. This democratization makes advanced AI agent development more accessible to a wider range of researchers, developers, and smaller organizations, fostering innovation and reducing dependence on commercial APIs.
- **Towards Generalist AI Agents:** The ability of AgentLM to perform well on unseen agent tasks while retaining general language capabilities signifies a crucial step towards building truly generalist AI agents. Unlike prior efforts that focused on narrow task specializations, AgentTuning shows that LLMs can be trained to develop a robust, transferable understanding of agentic behavior. This opens avenues for creating more versatile and adaptive AI systems that can operate across diverse environments.
- **New Paradigm for LLM Alignment:** The success of the hybrid instruction-tuning strategy highlights a powerful paradigm for aligning LLMs with complex behavioral objectives. It demonstrates that integrating high-quality, multi-turn interaction trajectories with general conversational data is highly effective. This approach could inform future research into how LLMs learn intricate reasoning, planning, and tool-use skills, moving beyond simple question-answering or text generation.
- **Understanding LLM Behavior:** The error analysis, showing a reduction in "elementary mistakes" rather than fundamental reasoning flaws, suggests that base LLMs might inherently possess some agent capabilities that are simply unactivated or poorly aligned. AgentTuning provides a method to "unlock" or "enable" these latent abilities through targeted instruction fine-tuning, offering insights into the internal mechanisms of large models and how they process complex instructions.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The open-sourced AgentInstruct dataset serves as a valuable new resource for the research community. It provides a standardized, high-quality benchmark for training and evaluating agent capabilities, encouraging further exploration into data augmentation, more sophisticated training algorithms, and novel agent architectures. Researchers can now build upon this foundation to push the boundaries of LLM agents, perhaps exploring more complex multi-agent scenarios or tasks requiring deeper levels of real-world interaction.
- **Practical Applications:** For industries and applications that require autonomous AI agents (e.g., customer service automation, intelligent robotics, complex system control, interactive data analysis), AgentLM offers a powerful, customizable, and locally deployable alternative to commercial black-box models. This could lead to more robust, efficient, and privacy-preserving agent solutions in various domains.

In conclusion, AgentTuning is a seminal work that not only addresses a critical performance gap but also provides practical tools and fundamental insights, paving the way for a new generation of generalized, open-source AI agents.
