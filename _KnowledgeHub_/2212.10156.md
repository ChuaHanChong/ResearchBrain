# Planning-oriented Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> UniAD unifies detection, tracking, online mapping, motion forecasting, occupancy prediction, and planning into one transformer network connected by task queries, explicitly organizing every preceding module to serve final planning rather than training separately or with generic multi-task heads. On nuScenes it beats prior state-of-the-art across all five tasks and cuts planning L2 error and collision rate versus ST-P3.

## Problem

- Standalone per-task models risk accumulative errors and feature misalignment because each module optimizes an isolated target without awareness of downstream planning needs.
- Generic multi-task learning shares a backbone across tasks but suffers negative transfer, and prior driving MTL work rarely targets planning as the unifying objective.
- Earlier end-to-end and partial-stack approaches either skip perception supervision entirely or omit key modules like joint motion-occupancy prediction, undermining safety and interpretability.

## Method

- **TrackFormer** and **MapFormer** perform joint detection, multi-object tracking, and panoptic map segmentation using ==query embeddings== attending to a shared ==bird's-eye-view feature==, including a dedicated ego-vehicle query.
- **MotionFormer** models agent-agent, agent-map, and agent-goal interactions via ==multi-head attention== and ==deformable attention==, refined with scene- and agent-level anchors and a ==non-linear trajectory smoother==.
- **OccFormer** predicts instance-wise future occupancy through ==pixel-agent cross-attention== with an ==occupancy-guided attention mask==, producing per-agent grids via matrix multiplication without hand-crafted clustering.

## Results

- UniAD improves tracking AMOTA by **6.5** and **14.2** points over MUTR3D and ViP3D respectively, and reduces motion forecasting minADE by **38.3%** and **65.4%** over PnPNet-vision and ViP3D.
- Against a naive multi-task baseline, UniAD lowers minADE by **15.2%**, minFDE by **17.0%**, and improves far-range occupancy IoU and VPQ by roughly **5-6 points** each.
- UniAD cuts average planning L2 error by **51.2%** and collision rate by **56.3%** versus ST-P3, outperforming several LiDAR-based planning methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit task ordering toward a final objective, rather than symmetric multi-task sharing, avoids negative transfer while still letting upstream tasks inform downstream ones.
> - Query-based interfaces generalize better than bounding-box handoffs because they carry a wider receptive field and richer uncertainty, softening compounding errors across stages.
> - Combining motion and occupancy prediction together, rather than either alone, is necessary for safe planning, showing joint prediction modalities are complementary, not redundant.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems have evolved through several paradigms, each attempting to address the inherent complexities of perceiving, predicting, and planning in dynamic environments. UniAD positions itself at the forefront of this evolution, advocating for a "planning-oriented end-to-end" approach.

#### 1.1. Evolution of Autonomous Driving Paradigms

Historically, autonomous driving systems have been characterized by a **modular pipeline**: A sequential execution of distinct tasks—perception (e.g., detection, tracking, mapping), followed by prediction (e.g., motion and occupancy forecast), and finally planning. Industrial solutions, exemplified by Mobileye, Tesla, and Nvidia, often deploy standalone models for each task. While this modularity simplifies the R&D process across different teams, it introduces critical drawbacks:
- **Information loss:** Crucial contextual information can be lost when passed between isolated modules.
- **Error accumulation:** Errors from upstream perception tasks cascade and compound in downstream prediction and planning, potentially leading to unsafe decisions.
- **Feature misalignment:** Independent optimization targets for each module can lead to features that are not optimally aligned for the ultimate goal of driving.

An alternative, more integrated approach is **Multi-Task Learning (MTL)**, where several task-specific heads are plugged into a shared feature extractor. This paradigm is popular in general vision and autonomous driving (e.g., Transfuser, BEVerse). MTL aims to leverage shared feature abstractions and save computational costs, but it can suffer from "negative transfer," where optimizing for one task negatively impacts the performance of another.

The emergence of **end-to-end autonomous driving** seeks to unify all components—from raw sensor data to control signals or planned trajectories—into a single learnable system. Within this paradigm, two main directions have emerged:
- **"Tabula-Rasa" (Direct Planning):** This approach directly predicts the planned trajectory or control signals from sensor inputs, largely without explicit intermediate supervision of perception or prediction (e.g., works like). While promising in closed-loop simulations, these methods often face challenges in safety guarantees, interpretability, and robust generalization to highly dynamic urban scenarios.
- **Component-based End-to-End:** This direction explicitly incorporates certain perception and prediction modules but attempts to jointly optimize them for planning. Examples include PnPNet, ViP3D, ST-P3, MP3, and LAV. While these works offer good insights and impressive performance, UniAD argues that they "more or less fail to consider certain components" or lack a fully coherent "planning-oriented spirit." For instance, some might omit online mapping, or combine prediction tasks sub-optimally.

#### 1.2. UniAD's Position

UniAD positions itself as a pioneering work in the component-based end-to-end paradigm, but with a critical distinction: its explicit and pervasive **"planning-oriented philosophy."** The core argument is that a truly favorable framework must be meticulously designed and optimized *for* the ultimate goal of planning, ensuring that all preceding tasks directly contribute to this objective.

UniAD distinguishes itself by:
- **Comprehensive Task Integration:** Unlike prior works that often integrate only a subset of tasks, UniAD incorporates a full stack of five essential driving tasks within a single network: 3D object detection, multi-object tracking, online mapping, motion forecasting, and occupancy prediction, all feeding into the final planning module. This comprehensive nature aims to provide a richer, more holistic understanding of the driving scene.
- **Unified Query Interfaces:** A key novelty is the "exquisite" query-based design that connects all nodes. These unified query interfaces facilitate communication and feature exchange between tasks, allowing for complementary feature abstractions from a global perspective. This design aims to mitigate compounding errors inherent in modular pipelines and negative transfer seen in naive MTL. By explicitly modeling interactions through queries, UniAD offers a more robust and context-aware representation compared to traditional bounding box or grid-based representations.
- **Target-Driven Optimization:** The entire system is devised to minimize "accumulative error" and "negative transfer" by ensuring that the choice and priority of preceding tasks are always determined in favor of planning. This "target-driven design" is a central theme, aiming to create a robust and safe autonomous driving system for real-world scenarios.

In essence, UniAD proposes a paradigm shift from merely integrating tasks to *coordinating* them with a clear planning objective, bridging the gap between interpretable modular systems and powerful, but opaque, pure end-to-end learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of UniAD stem directly from the identified limitations of existing autonomous driving system designs:

1. **Overcome Accumulative Errors and Deficient Task Coordination in Modular Systems:** Traditional modular pipelines, where perception, prediction, and planning are handled by discrete, independently optimized models, are prone to compounding errors. Mistakes made in an upstream module (e.g., object detection) propagate and amplify downstream, significantly impacting the reliability and safety of the final planning decision. UniAD aims to mitigate this by jointly optimizing these tasks within a single, coherent network, fostering better information flow and coordination.

2. **Address Negative Transfer in Multi-Task Learning (MTL):** While MTL offers benefits like shared feature learning and computational efficiency, simply co-training multiple tasks can lead to "negative transfer," where the learning objective for one task conflicts with or degrades the performance of another. UniAD seeks a more "exquisitely devised" integration that leverages the advantages of multi-task learning without succumbing to these detrimental interactions.

3. **Enhance Safety, Interpretability, and Robustness for End-to-End Driving:** "Tabula-rasa" end-to-end approaches, which directly map sensor inputs to control signals or trajectories, often lack interpretability and safety guarantees crucial for highly dynamic and complex urban environments. UniAD is motivated to provide a reliable and planning-oriented system by explicitly incorporating and supervising key intermediate representations (perception and prediction), thereby offering better transparency and debuggability while maintaining an end-to-end learning framework.

4. **Achieve a Truly Comprehensive and Planning-Oriented System:** The authors argue that previous joint perception-prediction-planning systems often missed critical components or did not fully embody a "planning-oriented spirit." The core motivation for UniAD is to introduce a *comprehensive* framework that integrates *all* five essential driving tasks (3D detection, multi-object tracking, online mapping, motion forecasting, and occupancy prediction) and ensures they are explicitly designed to contribute to the ultimate goal of planning the self-driving car's trajectory. This holistic integration, with planning as the central optimization target, is believed to yield superior performance and robustness.

5. **Leverage Query-Based Design for Unified Task Communication:** A key technical motivation is the introduction of unified "query interfaces." The authors propose that queries, compared to classic bounding box representations, offer a larger receptive field to soften compounding errors and are flexible enough to model a variety of interactions, including relations among multiple agents. This design choice is critical for enabling seamless communication and knowledge exchange between the diverse perception, prediction, and planning modules.

In summary, UniAD's core objective is to redefine the autonomous driving framework by presenting a robust, interpretable, and comprehensive end-to-end system where all components are synergistically optimized towards safe and effective self-driving planning, addressing the shortcomings of current modular, MTL, and partial end-to-end approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniAD proposes a Unified Autonomous Driving framework meticulously designed around a "planning-oriented philosophy," integrating perception, prediction, and planning tasks within a single deep learning network. Its core architectural principle revolves around **transformer decoder-based modules** and **unified query interfaces** for inter-module communication.

#### 3.1. Overview of UniAD's Architecture

The UniAD pipeline initiates with a sequence of multi-camera images fed into a feature extractor. An off-the-shelf BEV encoder (e.g., BEVFormer) transforms these perspective-view features into a unified Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) feature `B`. This `B` serves as the foundational spatial representation for all subsequent tasks. The framework then proceeds through four main transformer decoder-based modules and a final planner:
1. **TrackFormer:** Performs 3D object detection and multi-object tracking.
2. **MapFormer:** Conducts online semantic mapping.
3. **MotionFormer:** Forecasts multi-modal future trajectories for all agents.
4. **OccFormer:** Predicts multi-step future occupancy grids.
5. **Planner:** Generates the ego-vehicle's trajectory.

Crucially, "queries" (`Q`) act as the unifying interfaces, passing abstracted information between these modules to model various interactions of entities in the driving scenario.

#### 3.2. Perception Modules: TrackFormer and MapFormer

- **TrackFormer:** This module performs joint 3D object detection and multi-object tracking without relying on non-differentiable post-processing steps often found in traditional tracking-by-detection methods.
 - **Query Design:** It employs two types of queries:
 - **Detection Queries:** Responsible for identifying newly appearing agents.
 - **Track Queries:** Model agents detected in previous frames, maintaining their identity across time. These queries aggregate temporal information by interacting with their historical counterparts in a self-attention mechanism.
 - **Output:** It outputs `N_a` valid agent features (`Q_A`), representing detected and tracked agents. A unique "ego-vehicle query" is also introduced and explicitly modeled to represent the self-driving car itself for downstream planning.

- **MapFormer:** This module is designed for online semantic mapping, providing a dynamic representation of road elements.
 - **Sparse Representation:** Road elements are sparsely represented as "map queries" (`Q_M`).
 - **Categories:** It segments meaningful road elements into "things" (e.g., lanes, dividers, crossings) and "stuff" (drivable area) for panoptic segmentation in BEV.
 - **Output:** The updated `Q_M` encode location and structural knowledge of the map, which are crucial for the motion forecasting module.

#### 3.3. Prediction Modules: MotionFormer and OccFormer

- **MotionFormer:** This module bridges perception and planning by predicting the future behaviors of all agents.
 - **Scene-Centric Prediction:** It forecasts `K` plausible multimodal future trajectories for all agents in a single forward pass, providing a global understanding of scene evolution.
 - **Inputs:** It takes highly abstract `Q_A` (dynamic agents) and `Q_M` (static map) from TrackFormer and MapFormer, respectively. The ego-vehicle query is also passed through to model its interaction with other agents.
 - **Interaction Modeling:** The module captures three types of interactions: agent-agent (via self-attention among motion queries), agent-map (via cross-attention between motion and map queries), and agent-goal point (via deformable attention focusing on the predicted endpoint, refining the trajectory).
 - **Motion Queries:** These input queries combine `query context` (from the previous layer) and `query position`. The `query position` integrates four-fold positional knowledge: scene-level anchors (global prior), agent-level anchors (local intention), current agent location, and the predicted goal point (for coarse-to-fine refinement).
 - **Non-linear Optimization (Training Only):** To address the uncertainty from upstream perception, a non-linear smoother (based on multiple-shooting) is used during training to adjust target trajectories, making them physically feasible (e.g., via kinematic constraints like jerk, curvature) given imperfect starting points. This ensures more realistic trajectory predictions for supervision without impacting inference speed.

- **OccFormer:** This module predicts future occupancy grid maps, providing a dense, scene-level understanding complementary to sparse motion trajectories.
 - **Agent-Aware Occupancy:** Unlike previous agent-agnostic approaches, OccFormer incorporates both scene-level (BEV feature `B`) and agent-level knowledge (`G_t`, derived from TrackFormer and MotionFormer queries).
 - **Temporal Unrolling:** It consists of sequential blocks, each responsible for predicting occupancy for a specific future timestep.
 - **Pixel-Agent Interaction:** Dense scene features (as queries) interact with instance-level agent features (as keys/values) via cross-attention. An "attention mask," generated from agent-level mask features, aligns pixel-agent correspondence, ensuring each pixel only "looks at" the agent occupying it.
 - **Instance-Level Occupancy:** A key innovation is generating instance-wise occupancy maps `ˆO_t_A` easily through a matrix multiplication between the updated agent-level features and decoded dense scene features, without heavy hand-crafted post-processing. These instance-level maps can then be merged to form a classical whole-scene occupancy map `ˆO_t` for planning and evaluation.

#### 3.4. Planning Module

- **Input:** The expressive ego-vehicle query from MotionFormer and the multi-step future occupancy prediction from OccFormer. It also takes learnable "command embeddings" (e.g., turn left, keep forward) representing high-level navigation signals.
- **Mechanism:** The ego-vehicle query is combined with command embeddings to form a "plan query." This query attends to the dense BEV features `B` to gain scene awareness. An MLP then decodes the processed plan query into initial future waypoints `ˆτ`.
- **Collision Optimization (Inference Only):** To ensure safety, the initially predicted trajectory `ˆτ` is further optimized in inference using Newton's method. This optimization leverages the merged occupancy map `ˆO` from OccFormer to push the trajectory away from predicted occupied regions, minimizing collision risk. The cost function balances adherence to the initial prediction with collision avoidance penalties.

#### 3.5. Learning Strategy

UniAD employs a **two-stage training process** for stability:
1. **Stage 1 (Perception Pre-training):** Perception modules (TrackFormer and MapFormer) are jointly trained for a few epochs (6 in experiments). Pre-trained weights from BEVFormer are loaded. Gradients are typically stopped at the image backbone to reduce memory.
2. **Stage 2 (End-to-End Training):** The entire UniAD model (perception, prediction, and planning) is trained end-to-end for more epochs (20 in experiments). The image backbone and BEV encoder are frozen to manage computational resources.

A **shared matching mechanism** (bipartite matching) is used in both tracking and online mapping, and its results are consistently reused in motion and occupancy tasks. This ensures consistent instance modeling across the end-to-end pipeline. Various task-specific losses (Focal, L1, Dice, GIoU for perception; multi-path for motion; BCE and Dice for occupancy; imitation L2 and collision loss for planning) are combined to optimize the overall system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniAD demonstrates significant advancements across all integrated tasks and validates the effectiveness of its planning-oriented, end-to-end design philosophy through extensive experiments on the challenging nuScenes benchmark.

#### 4.1. Joint System Performance

The core contribution of UniAD is its holistic system design. Comparative ablations against a naive Multi-Task Learning (MTL) baseline (Table 2, ID-0 vs. ID-12) reveal substantial improvements:
- **Prediction:** UniAD significantly outperforms naive MTL, achieving a 15.2% reduction in minADE and a 17.0% reduction in minFDE for motion forecasting, alongside improvements in occupancy prediction (e.g., +4.9% IoU-f.).
- **Planning:** UniAD demonstrates superior planning safety and accuracy, with a 0.15m reduction in average L2 error and a 0.51% reduction in average collision rate compared to naive MTL.
- **Task Coordination:** The results clearly show the benefits of inter-module cooperation:
 - Integrating both motion forecasting and occupancy prediction simultaneously is critical for achieving optimal planning performance (Exp.10-12).
 - Perception modules (tracking and mapping) significantly contribute to improved motion forecasting (e.g., -9.7% minADE with both perception tasks, Exp.6 vs. Exp.4).
 - The proposed unified design avoids the "negative transfer" seen in simpler MTL setups, maintaining strong perceptual performance while achieving state-of-the-art results for downstream tasks.

#### 4.2. Modular Performance

UniAD not only achieves strong system-level performance but also excels in individual task benchmarks against prior state-of-the-art methods:

- **Multi-Object Tracking (Table 3):** UniAD achieves a notable 6.5% higher AMOTA than MUTR3D and a remarkable 14.2% higher AMOTA than ViP3D (both vision-based end-to-end MOT methods). It also records one of the lowest Identity Switches (IDS) scores, indicating robust temporal consistency.
- **Online Mapping (Table 4):** UniAD shows competitive performance against perception-oriented methods, particularly demonstrating strong lane segmentation (e.g., +7.4% IoU compared to BEVFormer), which is vital for agent-road interaction in motion forecasting.
- **Motion Forecasting (Table 5):** UniAD remarkably outperforms previous vision-based end-to-end methods. It reduces minADE by 38.3% compared to PnPNet and an impressive 65.4% compared to ViP3D, showcasing its superior predictive accuracy.
- **Occupancy Prediction (Table 6):** UniAD achieves significant gains in critical nearby areas, with a 4.0% higher IoU-near and 2.0% higher VPQ-near compared to FIERY and BEVerse respectively. This near-field accuracy is crucial for safe local planning.
- **Planning (Table 7):** Benefiting from rich spatial-temporal information, UniAD significantly reduces planning L2 error and collision rates (e.g., 51.2% reduction in average L2 error and 56.3% reduction in average collision rate compared to ST-P3). Crucially, it even outperforms several LiDAR-based planning methods in most metrics, highlighting the power of a vision-centric integrated system.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Insights

Visualizations provide compelling evidence for UniAD's effectiveness:
- **Consistent Predictions:** Fig. 3 shows consistent outputs across motion and occupancy modules, enabling safe ego-vehicle maneuvers, like yielding to a front vehicle.
- **Navigation Command Responsiveness (Fig. 8):** The planner's attention mechanism adapts dynamically to navigation commands, focusing on goal lanes and critical interacting agents.
- **Robustness to Upstream Errors (Fig. 12):** A key qualitative finding is UniAD's ability to "recover" from upstream perception failures. Even when the tracking module occasionally misses or mis-estimates objects (e.g., a fast-moving vehicle or a stationary car), the planner's attention mechanism can still react to their presence, demonstrating a level of robustness and resilience not typically found in rigid modular pipelines. This highlights the synergy of the planning-oriented design.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Detailed ablation studies confirm the necessity and contribution of each proposed component within the modules:
- **MotionFormer (Table 8):** All design choices contribute, with rotated scene-level anchors providing a significant performance boost in motion forecasting, emphasizing the importance of scene-centric modeling. Non-linear optimization also substantially improves performance by accounting for perceptual uncertainty.
- **OccFormer (Table 9):** The occupancy-guided attention mask and reusing mask features for instance-level occupancy are critical for improved prediction, especially in nearby areas.
- **Planner (Table 10):** Attending to BEV features, the inclusion of a collision loss, and the optimization strategy with occupancy collectively reduce the collision rate significantly, underscoring their importance for safety.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniAD represents a significant step forward in autonomous driving research, offering a new perspective on system design that prioritizes safety and robustness through deep integration.

#### 5.1. Key Contributions and Advantages

- **Paradigm Shift to Planning-Oriented Design:** UniAD's most impactful contribution is its explicit adoption and successful demonstration of a "planning-oriented philosophy." By designing and optimizing perception and prediction tasks with the ultimate goal of planning in mind, it effectively addresses the long-standing issues of error accumulation and feature misalignment inherent in traditional modular systems. This holistic approach fosters a truly synergistic learning environment across tasks.
- **Unified and Comprehensive Architecture:** As the first framework to comprehensively integrate such a wide span of essential driving tasks (detection, tracking, mapping, motion forecasting, occupancy prediction, and planning) into a single network, UniAD sets a new benchmark for system complexity and integration. This comprehensive understanding of the driving scene contributes directly to safer and more intelligent decision-making.
- **Innovative Query-Based Interface:** The query-based design is a critical technical innovation that acts as a flexible, unified interface for communication and knowledge transfer between diverse modules. This mechanism not only facilitates rich, global feature abstractions but also helps to soften compounding errors from upstream predictions, enhancing the overall robustness of the system.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** UniAD achieves superior performance across all integrated tasks on a challenging real-world benchmark (nuScenes). Its remarkable improvements in motion forecasting, occupancy prediction, and especially planning accuracy and collision avoidance, even surpassing some LiDAR-based methods, underscore its effectiveness and potential for real-world deployment.
- **Enhanced Safety and Interpretability:** By explicitly modeling and leveraging intermediate representations like instance-level occupancy and motion trajectories, UniAD offers a more interpretable framework compared to "black-box" end-to-end approaches. The planner's attention mechanism also provides insights into its decision-making, focusing on critical agents and navigation cues. The demonstrated ability to recover from perception failures further highlights its robustness and safety potential.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its significant advancements, UniAD acknowledges certain limitations and outlines compelling directions for future research:

- **High Computational Cost:** Integrating such a comprehensive system with multiple tasks demands extensive computational power, particularly when considering temporal history. This poses a significant challenge for lightweight deployment on embedded platforms in autonomous vehicles. Future work will focus on optimizing the system for efficiency and reducing its computational footprint without sacrificing performance.
- **Expansion to More Tasks:** The current framework integrates five core tasks. The authors suggest exploring the incorporation of additional tasks, such as depth estimation or more nuanced behavior prediction, and devising effective methods to seamlessly embed them into the unified architecture. This would further enhance the system's perception and prediction capabilities.
- **Long-Tail Scenarios:** The paper identifies failure cases primarily in challenging "long-tail scenarios," such as interactions with very large trucks/trailers or in dark, low-visibility environments. Addressing these edge cases is crucial for real-world reliability and will require dedicated research into more robust perception and prediction under adverse conditions.
- **Policy-based vs. Trajectory-based Planning:** While UniAD focuses on planning trajectories, future work could explore integrating more complex, policy-based planning strategies, or adapting to different control interfaces beyond just waypoints.

In conclusion, UniAD provides a robust, comprehensive, and empirically validated framework for autonomous driving, setting a new benchmark for integrated system design. Its "planning-oriented philosophy" and unified query-based architecture offer a promising direction for developing safer, more intelligent, and reliable self-driving systems for complex urban environments.
