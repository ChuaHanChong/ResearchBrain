# Matryoshka Language Model Suites

> [!summary] Summary
> Matryoshka Language Model Suites nest sub-models of increasing width and depth into one jointly trained Transformer via a parameter-free norm-rescaled junction, with free online distillation from the largest to all smaller models at every step, and shared KV cache for speculative decoding; a 500M/1.5B/3B suite matches independently trained baselines while cutting training compute 36% and boosting decoding throughput 14-26%.

## Problem

- Standard model suites train each size independently, multiplying total training compute roughly linearly with the number of sizes released.
- Offline or parallel-teacher distillation from a larger to smaller sibling models adds significant storage or compute overhead during training.
- Speculative decoding needs a small, well-aligned draft model, but independently trained drafts show low agreement with the verifier and separate KV caches waste memory, making larger drafts impractical.

## Method

- ==Nested parameter sharing==: sub-model parameters strictly nest (θ₁ ⊂ θ₂ ⊂ ... ⊂ θ_M), each with its own width, depth, and LM head, so one forward pass yields all sub-model logits.
- **Parameter-free inter-model junction** that norm-rescales a smaller sub-model's output before concatenating it with a freshly initialized embedding to bridge to the next sub-model's wider hidden dimension.
- Integrated ==online distillation== loss combining per-sub-model cross-entropy with a stop-gradient cross-entropy term against the largest sub-model's softmax output, free since all logits already exist per pass.

## Results

- 3B Matryoshka suite reaches within **0.5** average accuracy points of a token-matched Vanilla baseline across seven benchmarks while using **36%** less total training compute.
- Speculative decoding with a 500M draft and 3B verifier hits **2,650 tok/s** versus **2,100 tok/s** for the Vanilla pair, a **26%** speedup, while Vanilla barely beats plain autoregressive decoding.
- Ablations show removing norm rescaling costs **+0.2 PPL** and removing distillation costs **+0.15 PPL** average; against MatFormer, Matryoshka-200M reaches **17.92** PPL versus MatFormer-XL's **19.34**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Nesting along depth rather than only FFN width lets KV cache shrink with sub-model size, giving genuinely lighter memory footprints unlike fixed-attention-backbone approaches.
> - Shared weights plus free distillation raise draft-verifier token agreement substantially, which is the real lever that makes large-ratio speculative decoding pairs viable.
> - Joint suite training reframes model-family design as a co-design problem, trading combinatorial size flexibility for near-linear compute savings across the whole suite.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development and deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) often necessitate a spectrum of model sizes, ranging from hundreds of millions to trillions of parameters, to accommodate diverse computational and performance constraints. This demand typically leads to the release of "model suites," where multiple models of varying scales are provided. Conventionally, each model within such a suite is trained independently. This approach incurs significant computational and memory costs, particularly when techniques like knowledge distillation from larger to smaller models are applied, as these usually require storing teacher logits offline or running teachers in parallel, adding further overhead.

The existing landscape includes several strategies to mitigate these costs or enhance inference:
- **Knowledge Distillation:** Techniques like those explored by Peng et al. (2025) and Gu et al. (2025) focus on transferring knowledge from larger "teacher" models to smaller "student" models. However, these often involve substantial compute or storage costs due to the need for teacher logits.
- **Speculative Decoding:** Introduced by Leviathan et al. (2023), this method accelerates autoregressive inference by using a smaller "draft" model to propose tokens, which a larger "verifier" model then quickly checks. While effective, it typically favors very small draft models due to memory overhead (maintaining two models and their KV caches) and the challenge of aligning draft and verifier predictions. Recent advancements like EAGLE (Li et al., 2024a) and MEDUSA (Cai et al., 2024b) seek to further accelerate this process.
- **Early Exiting:** Methods such as BranchyNet (Teerapittayanon et al., 2016) and subsequent works (e.g., Schuster et al., 2022; Elbayad et al., 2020) allow models to make predictions at intermediate layers, saving computation by exiting early if confidence is high. These methods generally operate within a fixed-width architecture, limiting flexibility in balancing accuracy and compute at different exit points. LayerSkip (Elhoushi et al., 2024) combines layer dropout with early-exit inference and self-speculative decoding.
- **Elastic Compression / Supernets:** Approaches like Flextron (Cai et al., 2024a) and LLaMaFlex (Cai et al., 2025) aim to derive a family of smaller models from an already trained LLM, focusing on post-training efficiency rather than from-scratch suite training.
- **Matryoshka LLMs (e.g., MatFormer by Devvrit et al., 2023):** This line of work applies the concept of nesting, often by varying the FFN hidden size, to extract sub-models. While offering FLOPs and parameter savings, previous Matryoshka models typically share a single attention backbone, resulting in an identical KV-cache footprint across all sub-models and often lacking independently deployable checkpoints.

The "Matryoshka Language Model Suites" framework positions itself as an improvement over these conventional and emergent methods by proposing an end-to-end training paradigm for model suites. It addresses the training and inference inefficiencies directly by integrating sub-models into a single architecture, facilitating low-cost online distillation, and intrinsically supporting speculative decoding with reduced memory overhead and improved alignment, while ensuring sub-models are fully functional and independently deployable. This work specifically contrasts with MatFormer by nesting both width and depth, allowing KV-cache footprint to scale with sub-model size, and producing discrete, standalone checkpoints suitable for diverse deployment regimes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The escalating training and inference costs associated with Large Language Models (LLMs) and the necessity to deploy models across a broad range of sizes for varying load and performance requirements serve as the primary motivation for this research. Current practices of developing model suites, which typically involve training each model separately and serving them independently, are identified as computationally and memory-inefficient. Specifically, the paper highlights several challenges:

- **Inefficiency of Separate Training:** Training multiple models for a suite, each with its own parameters, consumes significant computational resources. For instance, creating a suite of {1B, 8B, 30B, 70B} models using independent training would involve training 109B parameters in total, rather than just the 70B parameters of the largest model.
- **Cost of Distillation:** While knowledge distillation from larger to smaller models is a common strategy to improve the performance of smaller models in a suite, it traditionally introduces additional costs, either by requiring the storage of teacher logits offline or by running the teacher model in parallel during student training.
- **Limitations of Speculative Decoding:** Speculative decoding, an inference acceleration technique, benefits from well-aligned draft and verifier models and typically favors small draft models due to memory overheads associated with maintaining separate KV caches for both models. Larger draft models often fail to amortize their costs effectively.

In response to these challenges, the key objectives of the Matryoshka Language Model Suites framework are:

1. **Enhance Training and Inference Efficiency:** Develop a novel training framework that improves the overall efficiency of both training and serving a suite of language models.
2. **Reduce Total Parameter Count:** Decrease the cumulative parameter count required for the entire model suite compared to independently trained baselines by enforcing a nested, shared-parameter architecture.
3. **Enable Low-Cost Online Distillation:** Integrate knowledge distillation as a natural and computationally inexpensive byproduct of the training process, leveraging the simultaneous availability of logits from all sub-models during a single forward pass.
4. **Optimize Speculative Decoding:** Design an architecture that intrinsically supports speculative decoding by ensuring the draft model is nested within the verifier, thereby enabling KV-cache sharing and enhancing cross-model prediction agreement. This aims to make larger, more capable draft models practical for speculative decoding.
5. **Produce Independently Deployable Sub-models:** Ensure that each sub-model, despite being part of a nested architecture, can be detached and served as a standalone language model with its own depth and KV cache footprint, similar to a regular checkpoint.
6. **Validate Performance Parity:** Demonstrate that the Matryoshka suite can achieve performance comparable to independently trained baselines on standard benchmarks and perplexity metrics, despite the reductions in training compute and parameter count.
7. **Provide Architectural Guidance:** Conduct ablations and analyses of key architectural choices (e.g., width, depth allocation, junction mechanism, distillation loss coefficient) to offer insights and recommendations for building effective Matryoshka LM suites.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Matryoshka Language Model Suites involves nesting sub-models of increasing size into a single, jointly trained architecture. This approach deviates from conventional independent training by enforcing a shared parameter structure and integrating mechanisms for efficient training and inference.

**Modeling – Nested Transformer Architecture:**
The framework utilizes a Transformer architecture where sub-models are strictly nested. If a suite comprises *M* models, their parameter sets are ordered such that θ₁ ⊂ θ₂ ⊂ · · · ⊂ θᵣ = θ, where θᵣ represents the parameters of the largest model. Each sub-model T_θ_m is a distinct Transformer stack with its own hidden dimension (width, D_m), number of layers (depth, n_m), and a dedicated LM head (W_m). A key constraint is that hidden dimensions must be strictly increasing: D₁ ≤ D₂ ≤ · · · ≤ Dᵣ. This design contrasts with standard early-exiting methods that often assume a fixed hidden dimension, providing Matryoshka sub-models with architectural flexibility (variable width and depth). Crucially, a single forward pass through the largest architecture (θᵣ) simultaneously produces logits for all *M* sub-models, activating fewer parameters and writing less KV cache than *M* independent passes. Each sub-model is designed to be detachable and deployable independently.

**Inter-model Junction Mechanism:**
A central challenge in nesting models of varying widths is effectively passing the output from a smaller sub-model (o_m_θ(x) ∈ R^(D_m)) to a larger one (operating in R^(D_(m+1)) where D_(m+1) ≥ D_m). The proposed junction mechanism aims to bridge this dimensional mismatch without introducing additional parameters. It addresses a potential magnitude mismatch where Transformer outputs typically have larger norms than input embeddings, which could destabilize training.
The mechanism rescales the output of sub-model *m* (o_m_θ(x)) so its L2 norm matches the norm of a "fresh" input embedding (e_(m+1)_θ(x) ∈ R^(D_(m+1)-D_m)) designed to cover the additional dimensions of sub-model *m+1*. These two components are then concatenated. Formally, ˜o_m_θ(x) = o_m_θ(x) ⋅ (∥e_(m+1)_θ(x)∥₂ / ∥o_m_θ(x)∥₂), and the input to sub-model *m+1* is concat(e_(m+1)_θ(x), ˜o_m_θ(x)). The first D_m channels of the input to T_(m+1)_θ carry information from sub-model *m*, while the remaining channels are newly initialized. Each sub-model also has a dedicated LM head W_m_θ and its own cross-entropy loss (L_m_ce).

**Integrated Distillation:**
The nested architecture naturally facilitates online knowledge distillation. Since a single forward pass through the largest model (θᵣ) yields logits for all sub-models, distillation from the largest model (teacher) to all smaller ones (students) incurs negligible additional computational or storage cost during training. A distillation term, L_M→m_d, is added to each sub-model *m* < *M*. This term is the cross-entropy between the teacher's (largest model's) next-token distribution (with gradients stopped) and the student's next-token distribution. The total loss for each sub-model *m* is a convex combination: L_m = (1 − α_d) L_m_ce + α_d L_M→m_d, where α_d is the distillation coefficient. The overall training objective is the sum of these individual sub-model losses: L = Σ_M_m=1 L_m.

**Speculative Decoding Enhancement:**
At inference time, any pair of nested sub-models (m, m') where m < m' can serve as a draft-verifier pair for speculative decoding. The Matryoshka structure offers two advantages:
1. **KV-Cache Sharing:** The verifier's early layers coincide with the draft model's layers due to parameter nesting (θ_m ⊂ θ_m'). This allows the KV cache computed during drafting to be fully reused by the verifier for these shared layers, eliminating the memory overhead typically associated with maintaining a separate draft model.
2. **Higher Cross-Model Agreement:** The shared-weight structure and online distillation during training result in higher prediction agreement between nested draft and verifier models compared to independently trained suites of similar sizes. This translates to higher token acceptance rates during speculative decoding.
These properties collectively enable the practical use of larger, stronger draft models, which often prove inefficient in traditional speculative decoding setups.

**Experimental Setup:**
The proposed framework was validated by training a 3B-parameter Matryoshka suite consisting of 500M, 1.5B, and 3B sub-models.
- **Data:** 35 billion tokens from the FineWeb-Edu dataset, with sequences packed to 2,048 tokens.
- **Tokenizer:** SmolLM2 tokenizer (49,152 tokens) and RoPE positional embeddings (θ_RoPE = 10⁵).
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with standard parameters, a peak learning rate of 4 × 10⁻⁴, weight decay of 0.01, and gradient clipping at 1.0.
- **Schedule:** A warmup-stable-decay (WSD) schedule with 3,000 cooldown steps over 33,000 total steps.
- **Batch Size:** 512 sequences, using bf16 mixed precision.
- **Distillation Coefficient:** α_d = 0.3, determined through smaller-scale ablations.
- **Baselines:** A "Vanilla" suite of three independently trained Llama-style models (0.50B, 1.51B, 3.19B) using the same tokenizer, data, and hyperparameters. These baselines were evaluated at both token-matched (35B tokens, higher compute) and FLOPs-matched (23B tokens, equivalent compute) levels.
- **Architectural Design:** A specific (n₁, n₂, n₃) = (24, 10, 5) layer allocation for the 500M, 1.5B, and 3B blocks, respectively, was chosen for the 3B Matryoshka suite. This configuration was selected after a ternary sweep over feasible depth triplets for a total of 39 layers, aiming to match both KV cache and per-token FLOPs to the Vanilla 3B baseline. The 500M sub-model in both Matryoshka and Vanilla suites shared the same width and depth for direct comparison.

Ablation studies were also conducted on a smaller 200M proxy suite (50M/100M/200M sub-models) trained on 20B tokens to analyze the impact of the inter-model junction, distillation, and width/depth configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that the Matryoshka Language Model Suites framework yields improvements in efficiency and inference capabilities while maintaining performance parity with independently trained baselines.

### 5. Training Efficiency and Parameter Count

- **Reduced Training Compute:** The Matryoshka suite required 36% less total training compute compared to the Vanilla baseline suite, which was trained for the same number of tokens (35B). This reduction is primarily due to the single end-to-end training run generating all sub-models.
- **Reduced Total Suite Parameters:** The 3B Matryoshka suite, comprising 500M, 1.5B, and 3B sub-models, had a total trainable parameter count of 3.2B, which represents a 38% reduction compared to the 5.2B total parameters of the equivalent Vanilla suite (sum of independently trained models).

### 6. Downstream Performance and Perplexity

- **Benchmark Accuracy:** The Matryoshka suite achieved near-parity with the token-matched Vanilla baseline on a suite of seven multiple-choice benchmarks (ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, LAMBADA, OpenBookQA, PIQA, Winogrande), typically within 0.5 average percentage points at every sub-model size. When compared against a FLOPs-matched Vanilla baseline (trained with equivalent compute, but fewer tokens), Matryoshka models outperformed at every size, with improvements ranging from +0.4 to +1.9 points.
- **Out-of-Domain Perplexity:** On average out-of-distribution (OOD) byte perplexity across five corpora (WikiText-103, C4, PG-19, arXiv, PubMed), the Matryoshka suite performed comparably to the token-matched Vanilla baseline. It tied at the 500M scale and slightly outperformed at the 1.5B and 3B scales. The largest single-corpus advantage for Matryoshka was observed on arXiv.
- **Training Dynamics:** Validation perplexity of Matryoshka sub-models remained on par with their Vanilla counterparts throughout training, indicating that the parameter sharing and junction mechanism did not negatively impact language modeling quality.

### 7. Speculative Decoding Throughput

- **Increased Throughput:** Using a 500M sub-model as the draft and a 3B sub-model as the verifier, the Matryoshka pair demonstrated a 14–26% improvement in speculative decoding throughput compared to the Vanilla pair. For example, at a draft length of 6, Matryoshka achieved 2,650 tokens/s in greedy mode versus 2,100 for Vanilla (26% speedup). This gain was preserved under nucleus sampling.
- **Higher Accepted Length:** Matryoshka pairs exhibited higher accepted token lengths across most draft lengths, particularly in nucleus sampling (e.g., +5% on average), directly attributed to improved cross-model prediction agreement.
- **Memory Efficiency:** The nested structure enabled shared KV cache between the draft and verifier models, eliminating the memory overhead of maintaining a separate draft model. This allowed Matryoshka to fit a larger batch size (102 vs. 64 for Vanilla) within the same 80GB VRAM on an NVIDIA A100 GPU.
- **Practicality of Larger Drafts:** While standard speculative decoding often struggles to amortize the cost of larger draft models (e.g., a 1:6 size ratio like 500M/3B), the Matryoshka framework made this configuration practical, turning what was a throughput degradation for Vanilla into a 20–40% speedup over its own standard autoregressive decoding.

### 8. Ablation Studies (200M Proxy Suite)

- **Inter-model Junction:** The proposed junction mechanism, involving norm-rescaling and concatenation with a fresh embedding, was critical. Removing norm-rescaling resulted in a +0.2 PPL gap, while a "zero junction" (replacing fresh embeddings with zeros) led to a +0.54 PPL gap, indicating the importance of these components for smaller sub-model performance.
- **Distillation Loss:** The distillation term (α_d = 0.3) was found to be crucial. Removing it (α_d = 0) caused a +0.15 PPL average gap, with a more pronounced negative impact on smaller sub-models. Optimizing α_d was shown to minimize perplexity gaps relative to Vanilla baselines.
- **Width/Depth Configurations:** Ablations demonstrated that various depth allocations (e.g., L=39 total layers for the 3B suite) could be configured to match or even surpass the KV cache and FLOPs profiles of Vanilla baselines while maintaining performance. The selected configuration (24, 10, 5 layers for 500M, 1.5B, 3B sub-models, respectively) proved effective.
- **Loss Weighting:** Further analysis suggested that rebalancing the per-sub-model loss weights (currently uniform) could further close residual perplexity gaps to the Vanilla baseline, indicating potential for optimization in future work.

### 9. Comparison with MatFormer

- **KV Cache Scaling:** Matryoshka effectively scales its KV-cache footprint with the sub-model size (e.g., from 280 KB/token for 3B to 96 KB/token for 500M), aligning closely with independently trained models. In contrast, MatFormer's shared attention backbone results in a constant KV-cache footprint across all extracted sub-models, regardless of their parameter count (e.g., 280 KB/token for a 3B universal MatFormer).
- **Extractable Model Floor:** Matryoshka enables the extraction of genuinely smaller sub-models (e.g., down to 500M parameters). MatFormer, due to its architecture, has a higher minimum parameter count for its smallest extractable sub-models (e.g., ~1.05B parameters for a 3B universal model), limiting its flexibility for very small deployments.
- **Quality-Size Trade-off:** At matched perplexity, Matryoshka achieved comparable or better performance with fewer parameters and significantly less KV cache than MatFormer models.

### 10. Significance and Potential Impact

The Matryoshka Language Model Suites framework presents a notable advancement in the efficient development and deployment of LLMs, with several potential impacts:

- **Reduced Development Costs and Time:** By significantly lowering the training compute (36% less) and total parameter count (38% less for the suite) required to produce a family of models, the framework can reduce the financial and temporal resources needed for LLM research and development. This may enable smaller research teams or organizations to develop comprehensive model suites.
- **Enhanced Resource Utilization:** The integrated distillation mechanism and shared KV cache for speculative decoding lead to more efficient use of computational resources during both training and inference. This can translate to lower operational costs for deploying and serving LLMs across various applications.
- **Flexible and Efficient Deployment:** The ability to extract independently deployable sub-models, each with a memory footprint that scales with its size, allows for greater flexibility in matching LLM capabilities to specific hardware constraints and performance requirements. This can optimize the user experience across a range of devices, from edge devices to powerful data centers.
- **Improved Speculative Decoding Performance:** The inherent architectural advantages for speculative decoding, including shared KV cache and higher cross-model agreement, make this acceleration technique more robust and practical, especially for scenarios involving larger draft models. This could lead to faster and more responsive LLM applications.
- **Paradigm Shift in Model Suite Design:** The work promotes a paradigm shift from independently training models to co-designing and jointly pretraining model suites. This integrated approach can foster new research directions in optimizing capacity allocation, loss weighting, and architectural choices within nested structures.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified open questions regarding scaling with more sub-models, optimizing loss weighting, and integrating with post-training stages (e.g., instruction tuning, alignment, reasoning-oriented finetuning) provide a solid basis for future research in LLM efficiency and architecture. The release of trained checkpoints and a HuggingFace-compatible implementation further facilitates this exploration.

Overall, Matryoshka Language Model Suites contribute to making LLM technology more accessible, sustainable, and performant by addressing fundamental inefficiencies in the current model development lifecycle.
