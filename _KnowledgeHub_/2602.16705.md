# HERO: Learning Humanoid End-Effector Control for Visual Whole-Body Open-Vocabulary Object Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign researchers developed HERO, a modular system enabling humanoid robots to perform open-vocabulary object grasping by integrating large vision models for perception with a highly accurate learned end-effector control policy. The system achieved a 90% grasping success rate on daily objects and reduced real-world end-effector tracking error by 5.5 times compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in precisely manipulating novel objects in unstructured, real-world environments using only onboard sensors.
- Current end-to-end learning approaches for humanoid loco-manipulation exhibit limited generalization to new objects and scenes.
- End-effector tracking policies on physical humanoids often lack the necessary precision for reliable object grasping, showing substantial real-world errors.

## Method

- A modular ==loco-manipulation== system, **HERO**, decouples visual scene understanding and grasp planning from low-level robot control.
- **HERO** utilizes a learned, ==residual-aware end-effector tracking policy==, trained in simulation, to generate precise whole-body motor commands.
- Neural models are trained to correct the robot's ==analytical forward kinematics== and ==base odometry==, addressing hardware inaccuracies and improving sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- HERO achieved a **90%** success rate in grasping 10 diverse daily objects and **73.3%** across 10 broader novel real-world scenes, significantly surpassing baseline controllers.
- The system reduced real-world end-effector tracking translation error to **2.44 cm** and orientation error to **8.22 degrees**, demonstrating a **5.5-fold** improvement in translation accuracy over prior methods.
- Integrating learned residual neural forward kinematics and odometry models improved real-world tracking accuracy, reducing translation error from **4.67 cm** to **2.56 cm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular system, integrating advanced vision models with precise low-level control, provides a scalable approach for generalized humanoid object manipulation.
> - Reducing end-effector tracking error by precisely accounting for robot state and environmental changes is critical for robust grasping.
> - Learned residual neural models can effectively mitigate inaccuracies in analytical forward kinematics and odometry, improving sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is rapidly advancing, with recent demonstrations showcasing agile locomotion and complex maneuvers. However, enabling humanoids to perform precise manipulation of novel objects in unstructured, real-world environments remains a significant challenge. Traditional approaches to humanoid loco-manipulation often rely on end-to-end imitation learning or visual reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation. These methods, while effective for specific tasks or object categories, typically suffer from limited data diversity in the real world or difficulties in creating photo- and physically-realistic simulations that can facilitate broad generalization to novel objects and scenes. Furthermore, precise end-effector (EE) control, crucial for object manipulation, has been identified as a bottleneck, with leading existing trackers exhibiting considerable error rates in real-world scenarios.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a modular system design that separates high-level visual scene understanding and grasp planning from low-level robot control. This approach takes inspiration from successful modular systems in tabletop manipulation but extends it to the more complex domain of whole-body humanoid loco-manipulation. The paper focuses on improving the accuracy of humanoid end-effector tracking, which is a foundational component for robust object interaction. By leveraging large vision models (LVMs) for generalized perception and combining classical robotics techniques with machine learning for precise control, this research positions itself as an effort to overcome the generalization and precision challenges inherent in deploying humanoids for diverse, real-world object grasping tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to equip humanoid robots with the fundamental capability to autonomously reach for and pick up novel objects in previously unseen, everyday environments using only onboard sensors. This involves both understanding the target object from visual inputs and accurately controlling the robot's whole body to execute the grasp.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several key challenges and limitations in the current state of humanoid robotics:

- **Generalization to Novel Objects and Environments:** Existing end-to-end learning methods for humanoid loco-manipulation struggle to generalize to novel objects and scenes. This is attributed to the difficulty of collecting sufficiently diverse real-world data or establishing high-fidelity simulations for visual RL. The authors argue that a monolithic end-to-end approach is hard to scale for the variety and complexity of "in-the-wild" objects and settings.
- **Precision in End-Effector Control:** Object manipulation requires precise and goal-directed end-effector placement. Unlike tasks such as backflips where landing safely is the primary concern, grasping demands high accuracy. Current humanoid end-effector tracking policies, such as SONIC, exhibit real-world tracking errors that are often too large (e.g., 13.38 cm) for reliable object grasping. This lack of precision prevents the effective deployment of modular systems for humanoid object manipulation, even if visual generalization is achieved.
- **Leveraging Advancements in Vision and Control:** The authors are motivated to integrate recent advancements in large vision models (LVMs) for generalizable scene understanding (e.g., open-vocabulary object detection and segmentation) with robust, simulation-trained low-level control policies. This modular decomposition aims to harness the strengths of each component: LVMs provide broad visual generalization and open-vocabulary reasoning, while simulated training allows for the development of accurate and robust control policies.
- **Addressing Inaccuracies in Robot Models:** The authors identified that the analytical forward kinematics (FK) and odometry provided by low-cost humanoid robots like the Unitree G1 are often inaccurate. These inaccuracies can lead to a significant sim-to-real gap and hinder precise control. A specific motivation is to mitigate these errors through learned neural models.
- **Enabling Whole-Body Coordination:** Reaching for objects in diverse environments often requires complex whole-body motions, including bending, squatting, and twisting, while maintaining balance. The motivation is to develop a system that can coordinate these motions to achieve reachability across a wide workspace without relying on predefined motions or teleoperation at test time.

In summary, the core motivation is to unlock a more generalized and robust object manipulation capability for humanoids by developing an exceptionally accurate end-effector tracking policy within a modular system that leverages modern computer vision and machine learning techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper centers around a modular loco-manipulation system, with the core technical innovation being HERO (Humanoid End-Effector ContROl), an accurate residual-aware end-effector tracking policy.

#### 3.1. Overall Modular System Architecture

The system operates in a closed-loop fashion for open-vocabulary object grasping:
1. **Open-Vocabulary Query:** A natural language text query specifies the target object.
2. **Open-Vocabulary Perception:**
 - Large Vision Models (LVMs), specifically Grounding DINO 1.5 and SAM-3, are used to segment the object of interest from RGB-D images.
 - The AnyGrasp model then generates parallel jaw grasp proposals on the segmented object.
 - Geometric filters are applied to select suitable grasp candidates, prioritizing those parallel to the table and on the robot's accessible side.
3. **Grasp Pose Retargeting:** The selected parallel-jaw grasp is retargeted to the 6-DoF pose for the Dex-3 dexterous hand on the Unitree G1 humanoid. This involves a 45° rotation around the z-axis for improved contact and force closure, and clipping the end-effector rotation within 70° to avoid unstable Inverse Kinematics (IK) postures.
4. **Whole-Body Reaching & Grasping:** The retargeted hand pose becomes the target for HERO, which acts as a low-level controller to convey the robot arm to the predicted grasp location and execute the grasp.

#### 3.2. HERO: Humanoid End-Effector ContROl Policy

HERO is designed to output motor commands for all 29 Degrees of Freedom (DoFs) of the Unitree G1 humanoid given a desired end-effector pose in the robot frame. It integrates classical robotics with learned components:

- **Upper-Body Reference Trajectory Generation:**
 - Inverse Kinematics (IK) is used to convert the target end-effector pose (T_EE) into a reference base height (h) and upper-body joint goal (q*).
 - A collision-free motion planner (cuRobo) generates reference joint and end-effector trajectories from q*.
- **Whole-body End-Effector Tracking Policy (π_t):**
 - This is a learned policy trained in simulation using PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization).
 - **Inputs:** Current proprioceptive state (joint angles, velocities, angular velocity, projected gravity, roll/pitch), reference base height (h_t), reference upper-body joint angles (q_t), linear and angular velocity locomotion commands (v_t), and a history of proprioception and actions (past 5 timesteps).
 - **Key Input: Residual-Aware End-Effector Tracking Error (ΔE_t):** This represents the pose error between the accurate current end-effector pose (f_EE(x_t)) and the reference end-effector pose (ee_t).
 - **Architecture:** Two three-hidden-layer MLPs share observations but predict actions for the upper and lower body separately, combining into 29-DoF joint commands.
 - **Training:** Conducted in Isaac Gym using AMASS motion sequences (∼8K) and a curated set of everyday reaching targets (∼8K) covering a significant portion of the robot's workspace.
- **Learned Residual Neural Forward Models:** To provide accurate task-space feedback for π_t, two neural models are trained to correct inaccuracies in the robot's analytical kinematics:
 - **Residual Neural Forward Kinematics (η):** Given the current arm and waist state (x_t) and the analytical FK output, η predicts a 6-DoF SE(3) correction. The final accurate EE pose is computed as f_EE(x_t) = FK(x_t) ⊕ η(x_t, FK(x_t)).
 - **Residual Neural Leg Odometry (ξ):** Accounts for base movement during whole-body balancing. Assuming static feet, analytical FK calculates the base pose relative to time step 0. ξ learns a residual correction: f_odometry(y_t, y_0) = O_FK(y_t, y_0) ⊕ ξ(y_t, y_0, O_FK(y_t, y_0)). This corrects the goal position as the robot's base shifts.
 - **Architecture & Training:** Both η and ξ are lightweight 3-layer MLPs, trained on MOCAP data (3 hours) collected by sweeping the EE through the workspace. They use separate heads for translation (R³) and rotation (6D representation) and are optimized with MSE losses.
- **Replanning and Goal Adjustment:** These closed-loop adaptation strategies are crucial for real-world robustness:
 - **Replanning:** To compensate for base-pose changes and sim-to-real drift, the remaining reference trajectory is replanned every 300 steps (6 seconds) using cuRobo, incorporating the current state and the odometry-corrected goal.
 - **Goal Adjustment:** A damped translational update is applied to the commanded grasp target (g) to remove systematic steady-state EE tracking offsets, especially useful for compensating residual sim-to-real bias.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted on an unmodified Unitree G1 humanoid with Dex-3 dexterous hands, an Intel D435i RGB-D camera, proprioception, and a base IMU. MOCAP data was collected using a 13-camera Optitrack system for accuracy evaluation and neural model training. Domain randomization was employed during simulation training to facilitate sim-to-real transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the HERO system in both end-to-end open-vocabulary grasping and isolated end-effector tracking accuracy.

#### 4.1. End-to-End System Testing (Open-Vocabulary Grasping)

- **Performance on 10 Daily Objects:** HERO achieved an average success rate of 90% (27/30 trials for both 0.74m and 0.56m table heights) at grasping 10 diverse daily objects based on language queries. This significantly surpassed baseline methods: a direct PD controller achieved 10% (4/30 and 2/30 for respective heights), and an automated SONIC controller achieved 16.7% (4/30 and 6/30). This indicates that accurate whole-body end-effector tracking is a critical factor for successful visual grasping.
- **Generalization to 10 Broader Novel Scenes:** When tested in diverse real-world environments such as a kitchen, den, classroom, and office, HERO maintained a 73.3% success rate (22/30 trials) for grasping various objects across different table heights (0.43m to 0.92m).
- **Performance in 5 Cluttered Layouts:** In cluttered scenarios featuring distractors, HERO achieved an 80% success rate (12/15 trials), demonstrating its ability to maintain language-sensitive object detection and grasping performance.
- **Additional Qualitative Results (from Appendix):**
 - **Language Sensitivity:** The system demonstrated the ability to differentiate between similar objects (e.g., red apple vs. green apple) based on language queries.
 - **Moving Object Grasping:** HERO successfully grasped moving objects by continuously re-estimating object poses via visual feedback and triggering replanning, adapting its trajectory in a closed-loop manner.
 - **Door Opening:** The modular design of HERO enabled its direct application to other loco-manipulation tasks, such as opening a fridge door, by identifying the handle's grasp pose and executing the reaching motion.

#### 4.2. End-Effector Tracking Accuracy Evaluation and Ablation Study

- **Comparison against State-of-the-Art (Simulation):** In MuJoCo simulations, HERO achieved the lowest end-effector tracking errors: 2.48cm translation and 11.23° orientation error. This outperformed SONIC (4.10cm, 16.65°), AMO (8.29cm, 13.85°), and FALCON (13.57cm, 19.33°).
- **Comparison against State-of-the-Art (Real-world MOCAP):** On physical hardware, HERO maintained high accuracy, achieving 2.44cm translation and 8.22° orientation error. This represents a 5.5× reduction in translation error compared to SONIC (13.38cm, 16.75°) and significantly surpassed the PD controller (12.09cm, 40.82°).
- **Learned Forward Models Ablation (Table 4):** Replacing analytical Forward Kinematics (FK) and odometry with the learned residual neural models significantly improved real-world tracking accuracy. Using both learned EE and base pose estimates reduced translation error from 4.67cm (using analytical FK for both) to 2.56cm, approaching the MOCAP oracle's 2.44cm. This highlights the impact of reducing sim2real bias in state estimation.
- **Closed-Loop Feedback Ablation (Table 5):**
 - **Replanning:** Removing periodic replanning roughly doubled the translation error from 2.44cm to 5.17cm, emphasizing its critical role in compensating for accumulated drift. Error curve analysis confirmed that replanning prevents error plateauing at high levels.
 - **Goal Adjustment:** Removing goal adjustment led to a smaller but consistent degradation in tracking accuracy, indicating its contribution to fine-tuning and steady-state error reduction.
- **FK and Odometry Accuracy (Table 6):** Analytical FK exhibited notable systematic biases: 1.30cm translation and 6.03° rotation error for EE pose, and 1.00cm translation and 0.49° rotation error for base odometry. The learned residual FK model reduced EE translation error to 0.50cm and rotation error to 3.18°, while the learned odometry achieved 0.52cm translation and 0.46° rotation error. These learned models outperformed traditional kinematic calibration in some metrics, suggesting their ability to capture non-geometric effects.
- **Workspace Analysis:** Enabling waist DoFs substantially increased the robot's reachable workspace volume. The combined two-arm workspace grew from 0.248 m³ (arms-only) to 0.523 m³ (arms+waist), demonstrating the importance of whole-body coordination for extended reach.

#### 4.3. Failure Mode Analysis

Common failure modes included objects slipping from the hand, particularly for large or irregularly shaped objects, and objects being knocked over due to suboptimal grasp orientation or hand size contact with unstable items.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HERO framework represents a significant step towards enabling humanoids to perform generalizable and precise loco-manipulation tasks in diverse, unstructured environments. Its primary significance lies in the demonstration that a modular system design, effectively integrating large vision models for perception with a highly accurate learned end-effector control policy, can overcome limitations of monolithic end-to-end approaches.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Enhanced Humanoid Capabilities:** HERO significantly improves the precision of humanoid end-effector control, reducing tracking error by 5.5× compared to prior methods. This increased accuracy is foundational for any manipulation task, allowing humanoids to reliably interact with a wider range of objects in complex scenarios that were previously inaccessible due to insufficient control fidelity.
- **Validation of Modular Design:** The success rates achieved in open-vocabulary grasping across novel objects and scenes underscore the effectiveness of decoupling high-level visual planning from low-level robot execution. This modularity enables the system to leverage the generalization capabilities of pre-trained large vision models and the precision of simulation-trained controllers, offering a scalable paradigm for developing humanoid skills.
- **Addressing Sim2Real Gaps with Learned Models:** The introduction and validation of learned residual neural forward kinematics and base odometry models demonstrate an effective method for correcting inaccuracies in real robot hardware models. This approach mitigates a critical source of the sim2real gap, making policies trained in simulation more directly transferable and robust in the physical world.
- **Enabling Autonomous Operation:** By eliminating the need for large-scale real-world imitation demonstrations or teleoperation at test time, HERO moves closer to truly autonomous humanoid operation. The system's ability to interpret free-form language queries and act upon them in novel environments opens avenues for more intuitive human-robot interaction.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work identifies several areas for future exploration, including:
 - **Active Perception:** Addressing the limited egocentric field of view through active neck control and visual servoing.
 - **Motion Efficiency:** Integrating learned motion priors to generate more efficient and natural whole-body movements.
 - **Dexterity and Robustness:** Exploring better dexterous hands and retargeting strategies to handle a broader range of object geometries and improve robustness against manipulation failures like slipping or knocking over.
 - **Broader Task Generalization:** The successful extension to tasks like door opening suggests the potential for HERO's control framework to be integrated into broader loco-manipulation systems that incorporate complex trajectory generation and task planning.

In essence, HERO contributes to shifting the paradigm for humanoid robot skill acquisition, demonstrating that high-performance, generalized object manipulation can be achieved through a principled modular decomposition and a focus on precise task-space control, laying groundwork for humanoids to function more effectively in human-centric environments.
