# Energy-Based Transfer for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Purdue University, AWS AI, and Carnegie Mellon University developed Energy-Based Transfer Learning (EBTL), a framework enabling teacher policies in reinforcement learning to selectively provide guidance to student policies. EBTL quantifies teacher familiarity with a state using energy scores linked to state visitation density, consistently improving sample efficiency and final policy returns by preventing negative transfer across various tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents often exhibit poor sample efficiency, requiring vast amounts of experience to learn new tasks.
- Existing transfer learning methods in RL are susceptible to 'negative transfer,' where a pre-trained teacher policy provides counterproductive guidance in states unfamiliar to its training distribution.
- Prior transfer approaches lack a principled, introspective mechanism for teachers to assess their competence or familiarity with a student's current state before intervening.

## Method

- The **EBTL** (==Energy-Based Transfer Learning==) framework allows a teacher policy to provide guidance only in states deemed ==in-distribution== based on its training experience.
- Familiarity is quantified using ==energy scores==, which are the negative free energy of a state, shown to be proportional to the logarithm of the teacher's ==state-visitation density==.
- An ==energy-based regularization loss== is added to the teacher's training objective to improve discrimination between in-distribution and out-of-distribution states, further refining guidance selectivity.

## Results

- **EBTL** consistently achieved superior sample efficiency and higher final policy returns compared to all baseline methods across various single-task (Minigrid) and multi-task (Overcooked) environments.
- The teacher policy effectively distinguished between familiar and unfamiliar states using energy scores, resulting in a higher rate of beneficial guidance and a reduced incidence of detrimental advice in tasks involving covariate shift.
- Energy regularization significantly improved EBTL's convergence speed and overall performance, particularly in environments with strong covariate shifts, by enhancing the teacher's ability to differentiate in-distribution from out-of-distribution states.
- EBTL demonstrated robustness to varying degrees of covariate shift, environmental complexity, and hyperparameters, maintaining positive transfer and stable performance in challenging scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The problem of selectively providing teacher guidance in RL can be formally addressed as an out-of-distribution detection task, where guidance is restricted to states within the teacher's data distribution.
> - Energy scores, calculated from a policy's neural network logits, serve as a theoretically grounded proxy for the logarithm of the state-visitation density, allowing objective quantification of teacher familiarity.
> - Incorporating an energy regularization term during teacher training significantly enhances the teacher's capacity to discriminate between familiar (in-distribution) and unfamiliar (out-of-distribution) states, improving transfer performance.
