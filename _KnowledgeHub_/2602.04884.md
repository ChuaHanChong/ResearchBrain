# Reinforced Attention Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL) introduces a post-training framework that optimizes internal attention distributions instead of output token sequences in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This approach leads to consistent performance gains across diverse image and video question answering benchmarks, improving visual grounding and multimodal reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods, effective for LLM reasoning, yield only marginal gains or degradation when directly applied to MLLMs for perception-intensive tasks.
- Standard RLHF optimizes 'what to generate' (output tokens) rather than 'where to attend' (internal attention distribution), which is crucial for MLLMs to allocate information effectively.
- Output-based supervision can lead to issues like 'diversity collapse' or 'reward hacking,' hindering robust cross-modal alignment and genuine multimodal understanding.

## Method

- ==Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL)== is proposed, reformulating the MLLM's internal attention mechanism as a ==latent policy space== to be directly optimized.
- RAL employs an ==advantage-weighted attention divergence loss==, specifically Jensen-Shannon Divergence, to pull current attention policies towards successful patterns and push away from suboptimal ones.
- ==On-Policy Attention Distillation== extends this framework by aligning a student model's attention patterns with a teacher model, thereby transferring structural reasoning and fine-grained perceptual behaviors.

## Results

- RAL consistently outperforms the GRPO baseline and the base Qwen-2.5-VL-7B model across nearly all evaluated image and video VQA benchmarks, including a **+5.8%** improvement on V*Bench and **+3.4%** on NExT-QA.
- On-Policy Attention Distillation further enhances performance compared to standard token-level distillation, showing gains on **7 out of 8** image benchmarks and all video benchmarks (e.g., **+4.4%** on NExTQA).
- Ablation studies demonstrate RAL's robustness to increasing visual information density, with performance margins widening for higher fidelity inputs, and the RAL-zero variant showing strong visual understanding even without explicit textual rationales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing internal attention distributions provides a more potent, stable, and generalizable training signal for MLLMs, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained perception and long-range multimodal reasoning.
> - Directly shaping attention patterns effectively improves visual grounding and cross-modal alignment, addressing limitations of token-level optimization.
> - Attention patterns are a transferable and semantically rich representation of reasoning behavior, enabling more effective knowledge distillation between models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of post-training paradigms for Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly focusing on enhancing their reasoning and grounding capabilities.

**Evolution of LLM Post-Training:**
The paper acknowledges that LLMs have achieved remarkable proficiency in complex domains, largely due to massive unsupervised pre-training followed by various post-training techniques. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its variants, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have become standard for aligning LLMs with human intent and improving reasoning through explicit optimization of output token sequences. These methods primarily optimize "what to generate" by maximizing rewards derived from learned models or programmatic verifiers, particularly for Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which correlates reasoning length with accuracy (test-time scaling).

**Challenges in MLLMs:**
Extending these successful paradigms to MLLMs, however, introduces specific challenges. While CoT has been attempted for MLLMs in tasks like Visual Question Answering (VQA) by incentivizing verbose textual descriptions of visual inputs, the paper identifies a critical limitation: for core perception tasks, extensive textual reasoning yields only marginal gains and can even degrade fundamental perceptual capabilities. This is attributed to the fact that standard RLHF optimizes the *result* (the output token) rather than the *process* of information allocation, which is crucial for MLLMs to identify and attend to task-relevant visual information via their attention mechanisms. Modality bias, visual hallucination, and robust cross-modal grounding remain persistent issues in MLLMs that current token-level RL struggles to fully address.

**Novel Contribution: Shifting the Optimization Target:**
"Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL)" fundamentally shifts the optimization target. Instead of refining the "next-token distribution" (what to generate), RAL proposes to optimize the "internal attention distributions" (where to attend). This paradigm directly addresses the limitations of current MLLM post-training by focusing on the underlying mechanism responsible for cross-modal information integration. It views the attention pattern itself as a policy, directly reinforcing effective information allocation and improved grounding in complex multimodal inputs.

**Connection to Knowledge Distillation:**
The work further integrates with the knowledge distillation (KD) landscape. Traditional KD transfers knowledge by matching softened output distributions or intermediate representations. The paper extends this to "On-Policy Attention Distillation," where the student model explicitly learns the teacher's attention distribution. This is a significant advancement over standard token-level distillation, as it aims to transfer deeper structural reasoning patterns rather than just output logits, mitigating issues like exposure bias.

In essence, RAL represents a significant step towards more effective and robust post-training for MLLMs, moving beyond superficial output-level optimization to directly influence the internal cognitive processes (attention) that drive multimodal understanding. It positions attention policies as a principled and general alternative for multimodal post-training, addressing a core limitation in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL) is driven by a critical observation regarding the limitations of current reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms when applied to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The primary objectives of this research directly stem from these motivations:

**Motivation:**

1. **Inefficiency of Token-level RL for MLLM Perception:** While post-training with RL has demonstrably improved reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) by optimizing output token sequences (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, CoT), extending this directly to MLLMs for perception-intensive tasks has yielded limited success. The authors observe that generating verbose rationales often results in only marginal performance gains and, in some cases, can even degrade the MLLM's fundamental perceptual capabilities. This suggests that simply optimizing "what to generate" is insufficient for complex multimodal understanding.
2. **Mismatched Optimization Target:** The core issue is identified as the fundamental policy objective in MLLM post-training. In MLLMs, visual inputs are encoded and projected into the textual embedding space, requiring the attention mechanism to precisely identify and attend to task-relevant visual information for accurate answers. However, standard RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) or similar token-based RL methods optimize for the *result* (the output token sequence) rather than the *process* of internal information allocation (how attention weights are assigned). This indirect supervision fails to adequately foster robust cross-modal alignment and grounding.
3. **Limitations of Output-based Supervision:** Optimizing solely for output tokens can lead to issues like "diversity collapse," where the model overfits to specific high-reward surface forms, thus weakening generalization and potentially leading to "reward hacking" of linguistic structure without genuine underlying logic. This superficial optimization hinders the model's ability to truly understand and integrate multimodal cues.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Propose Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL):** To introduce a novel policy-gradient framework that fundamentally shifts the optimization objective for MLLM post-training. Instead of optimizing next-token probabilities ("what to generate"), RAL aims to directly optimize *internal attention distributions* ("where to focus").
2. **Promote Effective Information Allocation and Grounding:** By directly optimizing attention patterns, the goal is to encourage the model to effectively allocate information across multimodal inputs and improve its grounding—the ability to connect textual queries with specific, salient visual evidence.
3. **Develop On-Policy Attention Distillation:** To extend the attention-as-policy concept to knowledge distillation, creating a dual-distillation approach that transfers knowledge via both token and attention distribution alignment. This aims to enable student models to inherit fine-grained perceptual and grounding behaviors from a teacher model more effectively.
4. **Empirical Validation:** To rigorously evaluate RAL against existing baselines (like GRPO) across diverse image and video question answering benchmarks, particularly those requiring fine-grained visual understanding and perception, demonstrating consistent performance gains and the robustness of the new paradigm.

In essence, the research aims to rectify a fundamental misalignment in MLLM training by directly shaping the model's internal mechanisms of perception and reasoning, rather than merely refining its superficial outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL) introduces a novel approach to post-training Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by reformulating the reinforcement learning policy to operate directly on the internal attention distributions. This is complemented by an extension to on-policy knowledge distillation.

#### 3.1. Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL)

The core idea of RAL is to treat the MLLM's internal attention mechanism as an alternative, latent policy space. Rather than optimizing the traditional token-level policy (what token to generate next), RAL optimizes the attention policy (where to focus computation).

- **Problem with Traditional RL:** Standard RL objectives for LLMs, like those used in PPO/GRPO, optimize a policy by maximizing expected returns over the output token distribution. While effective, this token-level optimization often leads to "diversity collapse" or "reward hacking," where the model overfits to specific linguistic forms rather than robust underlying reasoning.
- **Aggregated Causal Attention Distribution Policy:**
 - The paper defines the causal Attention Distribution Policy, $p_t^{\theta}$, for each generated token $t$. This distribution represents how the model attends to all preceding tokens (including prompt and previously generated tokens) at position $i$ (where $i < t$).
 - These attention weights, $\alpha_{t,i}$, are extracted from the final Transformer layer and averaged across all attention heads. The policy $p_t^{\theta}(i)$ is then the normalized attention weight to position $i$.
 - This formulation captures how the model integrates both the initial context (multimodal input, instructions) and its own evolving reasoning process.
- **Advantage-Weighted Attention Divergence:**
 - RAL employs a per-token loss function, $L_{AttnRL} = \mathbb{E}_t [ A_t \cdot D(p_t^{\theta} || p_t^{old}) ]$, where $A_t$ is the sequence-level advantage estimate (from GRPO), and $D(\cdot)$ is a symmetric, bounded divergence measure, specifically Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD).
 - The crucial aspect is how JSD behaves with the advantage term:
 - If $A_t > 0$ (high reward), minimizing $L_{AttnRL}$ pulls the current attention policy $p_t^{\theta}$ towards the successful historical policy $p_t^{old}$.
 - If $A_t < 0$ (low reward), the objective "pushes" $p_t^{\theta}$ away from the suboptimal $p_t^{old}$, encouraging exploration of alternative attention patterns.
 - This per-token granularity helps mitigate the "vanishing gradient" problem over long sequences, ensuring all parts of the response receive attention-based supervision.
- **Combined Optimization Objective:** The final training objective integrates the standard token-level policy gradient loss ($L_{RL}$) with the attention regularization term: $L_{total} = L_{RL} + \lambda_{attn} L_{AttnRL}$. The hyperparameter $\lambda_{attn}$ balances these two objectives.
- **Gradient Derivation:** The paper provides a detailed derivation of the gradient of $L_{AttnRL}$ with respect to the attention logits, utilizing the chain rule, JSD's partial derivative, and the softmax Jacobian. This ensures that the model parameters are updated to reinforce or penalize specific internal attention patterns.

#### 3.2. On-Policy Attention Distillation

RAL extends the attention policy framework to a knowledge distillation setting, allowing a student model to inherit the structural reasoning patterns from a more capable teacher.

- **Teacher-Student Alignment:** For each token $t$ in trajectories sampled from the student policy $\pi_{\theta}$, the distillation loss is defined as $L_{AttnDistill} = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_{\theta}} [ \sum_{t=P+1}^T JSD(p_t^{\theta} || p_t^{\phi}) ]$. Here, $p_t^{\phi}$ is the teacher's attention policy.
- **Pure Structural Imitation:** Unlike $L_{AttnRL}$, this objective does not include an advantage term. Its sole purpose is to align the student's attention patterns with the teacher's, providing a dense, structural supervisory signal.
- **Unified Distillation Objective:** The overall objective for on-policy distillation combines the RL loss, generalized knowledge distillation (GKD) on output logits, and the attention alignment term: $L_{total} = L_{RL} + \mu L_{GKD} + \gamma_{attn} L_{AttnDistill}$.
- **Mitigating Exposure Bias:** By sampling trajectories from the student's own policy, this approach helps the student learn "teacher-like" attention patterns even in states it might generate uniquely, thereby addressing exposure bias.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Baselines

- **Model:** Qwen-2.5-VL-7B serves as the foundation MLLM, with Qwen-2.5-VL-32B as the teacher for distillation. The visual encoder and multimodal projector are frozen, with only the language model backbone updated.
- **Training Pipeline:**
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Models are initially fine-tuned on Video-R1-COT-165k, a dataset with video-question pairs and structured Chain-of-Thought reasoning (using `<think>` and `<answer>` tags).
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Training proceeds on a subsample of Video-R1-260k, where the policy generates 8 rollouts per input for advantage estimation within the GRPO framework.
- **Visual Processing:** Specific configurations for image resolution, video frame sampling (1 fps, max 128 frames), and frame resolution are detailed.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key hyperparameters for RL (sampling temperature, KL coefficient, learning rates) and for RAL/Attention Distillation ($\lambda_{attn}$, $\gamma_{attn}$) are specified.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** The primary baseline, which computes relative advantage within a group of sampled responses, avoiding an auxiliary critic model.
 - **Reward System:** A rule-based reward combines accuracy (0.9 weight for exact answer match) and format adherence (0.1 weight for correct `<think>`...`<answer>` structure).
 - **Video-R1-7B:** A model trained with Temporal-GRPO, incorporating a temporal coherence reward, for a fair comparison.
 - **On-Policy Knowledge Distillation:** Standard token-level distillation without attention alignment.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Extensive evaluation across diverse image QA (V*Bench, MMMU Pro, MME, MuirBench, ChartQA, VizWiz, Blink, CVBench) and video QA (LongVideoBench, NExT-QA, Video-MME, Video-MMMU, LVBench, MVBench, TempCompass) benchmarks covering spatial, temporal, reasoning, and perception capabilities.

This comprehensive methodology, combining novel attention-based policy optimization with rigorous experimental validation, aims to establish RAL as a robust alternative for MLLM post-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate that Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL) significantly improves the performance of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) across a wide array of visual question answering benchmarks, outperforming traditional token-level reinforcement learning baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison

- **Consistent Superiority:** RAL consistently surpasses both the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) baseline and the base Qwen-2.5-VL-7B model across nearly all evaluated image and video benchmarks. This suggests that optimizing internal attention distributions provides a more stable, generalizable, and effective learning signal for multimodal reasoning compared to policy gradients focused solely on output tokens.
- **Mitigation of Degradation:** Unlike GRPO, which occasionally leads to performance degradation relative to the base model on certain benchmarks, RAL consistently delivers gains, indicating a more robust training signal that fosters genuine generalization rather than narrow reward-model overfitting.

#### 4.2. Image VQA Benchmarks (Table 3)

- **RAL vs. GRPO:** RAL outperforms GRPO across all eight image benchmarks.
 - Significant improvements are observed on:
 - **V\*Bench (+5.8%):** A fine-grained visual search and spatial reasoning task, highlighting RAL's enhanced ability to ground visual cues.
 - **MME (+94.1):** A benchmark for basic perception and high-level cognition.
 - **ChartQA (+2.8%):** Requiring complex data extraction from charts.
 - **VizWiz (+3.8%):** Focusing on real-world visual grounding.
 - These gains suggest that attention-level supervision directly strengthens visual grounding and compositional reasoning, particularly for tasks demanding detailed perception and understanding of document-based visuals.
- **On-Policy Attention Distillation:** The integration of attention distillation yields superior results over standard knowledge distillation (which only aligns token probabilities) on 7 out of 8 benchmarks.
 - Notable increases on V\*Bench (+3.6%) and MuirBench (+1.8%) underscore the value of transferring "where to attend" for fine-grained object attributes and spatial relationships.

#### 4.3. Video VQA Benchmarks (Table 2)

- **RAL vs. GRPO:** RAL outperforms GRPO on 6 out of 7 long-video datasets.
 - The most pronounced improvements are seen on:
 - **LongVideoBench (+2.2%):** For long-range referring reasoning.
 - **NExTQA (+3.4%):** For causal and temporal action explanation.
 - **MVBench (+1.5%):** For multi-task temporal perception.
 - These results demonstrate RAL's effectiveness in tasks that require robust temporal understanding and multi-hop reasoning over extended video contexts.
- **On-Policy Attention Distillation:** Attention-enhanced alignment improves performance on every benchmark for video VQA, with significant gains on NExTQA (+4.4%) and VideoMME (+2.6%), highlighting its potency for long-context temporal reasoning and precise event localization.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Section 4.5)

- **Robustness to Visual Information Density (Figure 3):**
 - RAL consistently outperforms GRPO across varying video sampling lengths (32, 64, 128 frames on LongVideoBench) and image resolutions (512, 1024, 2048 tokens on V\*Bench).
 - Crucially, the performance margin of RAL over GRPO *widens* as visual information density increases (e.g., from +1.6% at 512 tokens to +6.3% at 2048 tokens on V\*Bench). This indicates superior scalability and efficacy for high-fidelity multimodal understanding, where distinguishing salient information from dense contexts is crucial.
- **Is an Explicit Thinking Process Necessary (RAL-zero)?**
 - The RAL-zero variant, which removes the explicit `<think>` block during both SFT and RL, aims to isolate the impact of attention-level supervision.
 - **Video Benchmarks (Table 2):** RAL-zero surpasses the base model on 5/7 benchmarks and outperforms the full GRPO baseline on 5 benchmarks. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on NExTQA, VideoMME, and LVBench, demonstrating that the attention policy space, when optimized, can unlock significant visual understanding capabilities even without verbalized rationales.
 - **Image Benchmarks (Table 3):** RAL-zero improves upon the base model in 4/8 cases, achieving the highest scores among RL-based methods on MMMU-Pro (complex visual knowledge) and VizWiz (fine-grained object recognition). This confirms that directly optimizing attention mechanisms effectively bridges modalities and induces superior cross-modal reasoning without requiring explicit textual logic.

In conclusion, the main findings strongly validate the central hypothesis of the paper: optimizing internal attention distributions provides a more potent, stable, and generalizable training signal for MLLMs, particularly for tasks demanding fine-grained perception and long-range multimodal reasoning. The success of attention distillation further underscores that attention patterns are a transferable and semantically rich representation of reasoning behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Reinforced Attention Learning (RAL) marks a significant advancement in the post-training methodology for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with profound implications for the development of more capable, robust, and interpretable AI systems.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Paradigm Shift

The most significant contribution of this work is the conceptual shift from optimizing "what to generate" (output tokens) to "where to attend" (internal attention distributions). By treating attention as a direct policy to be reinforced, RAL offers a principled, process-aware alternative to outcome-based RL methods. This addresses a fundamental limitation in current MLLM training, which often struggles with robust visual grounding and cross-modal alignment despite generating verbose textual rationales. This paradigm shift could inspire a new wave of research focusing on internal model mechanisms rather than just external behaviors.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Multimodal Understanding and Grounding

The consistent empirical gains across diverse image and video benchmarks, particularly for perception-intensive and long-context tasks, demonstrate RAL's efficacy in fostering deeper multimodal understanding. By directly supervising the allocation of information, RAL enables MLLMs to more precisely identify and integrate salient visual and textual cues. This leads to improved visual grounding, reducing issues like visual hallucination and modality bias, which are critical challenges in MLLM development. The widening performance gap with increasing visual information density highlights RAL's superior scalability for high-fidelity multimodal inputs.

#### 5.3. Robustness and Generalization

Unlike token-level reinforcement learning, which can sometimes lead to performance degradation or reward hacking, RAL provides more stable and uniform gains. This suggests that optimizing internal attention mechanisms leads to better generalization and a more reliable training signal. The ablation studies, especially with RAL-zero (without explicit thinking processes), further underscore that attention policies contribute intrinsically to visual understanding, demonstrating a more fundamental improvement in reasoning rather than just surface-level adjustments.

#### 5.4. Advanced Knowledge Transfer through Attention Distillation

The introduction of On-Policy Attention Distillation presents a powerful mechanism for knowledge transfer. By explicitly aligning student models with teacher attention distributions, the method facilitates the inheritance of fine-grained perceptual and grounding behaviors. This is a significant step beyond traditional knowledge distillation, which primarily focuses on output logits, and offers a path to more effective model compression and efficient deployment while preserving crucial internal reasoning structures.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The paper explicitly paves the way for future research into fine-grained credit assignment and the optimization of other internal structures within MLLMs. This includes exploring how RAL principles could be applied to MoE (Mixture-of-Experts) routing, cross-modal fusion mechanisms, or other latent representations. Such extensions could further unlock the potential for more robust, adaptable, and genuinely intelligent multimodal AI systems.

In summary, Reinforced Attention Learning provides a novel, effective, and principled framework for MLLM post-training. Its focus on internal attention distributions addresses core challenges in multimodal AI, leading to more accurate, robust, and interpretable models. This work not only delivers immediate performance improvements but also opens up exciting new avenues for foundational research in AI, guiding the development of the next generation of multimodal intelligence.
