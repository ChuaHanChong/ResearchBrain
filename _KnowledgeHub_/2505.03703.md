# Fill the Gap: Quantifying and Reducing the Modality Gap in Image-Text Representation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A model-agnostic framework introduces spectral and optimal transport techniques to reduce the separation between image and text embeddings in vision-language models, achieving improved cross-modal retrieval performance while introducing new metrics to quantify embedding alignment quality across different model architectures.

## Problem

- Vision-language models exhibit a "modality gap" where image and text embeddings remain separated in shared representation space
- Existing solutions are model-specific and lack direct ways to measure embedding alignment quality
- Retrieval results in multimodal scenarios are often biased towards the query's own modality, limiting seamless integration of information across modalities

## Method

- Post-processing techniques using ==spectral embedding== and ==optimal transport== to align embeddings
- New evaluation metrics including ==Image-Text Ratio== and ==distribution-based measures== to quantify alignment
- ==Model-agnostic approach== that works with pre-trained models without architectural changes

## Results

- Significant reduction in modality bias metrics (ITR and TIR values), with lower Text Mean Rank (TMR) and Image Mean Rank (IMR) indicating better cross-modal retrieval
- Improved Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores showing closer embedding distributions
- Maintained semantic relevance between corresponding image-text pairs

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spectral-based methods outperform other approaches for reducing modality bias
> - Simply reducing dimensionality without considering semantic similarity doesn't improve retrieval
> - Post-processing can effectively align embeddings while preserving semantic relationships

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical issue in the field of vision-language models (VLMs): the "modality gap." VLMs aim to create shared representation spaces for images and text, allowing for seamless multimodal information retrieval and other downstream tasks. However, a persistent problem is the separation of image and text embeddings within this shared space, hindering the effective integration of information from different modalities.

The paper builds upon existing work in the area, acknowledging the pioneering role of the CLIP model and subsequent advancements like LLM2CLIP and Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (MM-RAG). It specifically addresses the limitations of current approaches:

- **Lack of Direct Evaluation:** The paper points out that the quality of alignment in VLMs is often indirectly assessed through downstream task performance rather than directly measured and quantified.
- **Model-Specific Solutions:** Existing attempts to mitigate the modality gap, such as fine-tuning CLIP with high temperature or modifying model architectures, are often specific to particular models and require significant architectural changes.
- **Limited Generic Methods:** The paper emphasizes the absence of generic and practical methods for both assessing and reducing the modality gap across various VLM architectures.

This research fills a gap in the literature by proposing novel metrics and techniques that are model-agnostic and readily applicable to existing VLMs. By focusing on post-processing techniques that can be applied to pre-trained models, the authors offer a practical approach to improve multimodal representation learning without requiring extensive retraining or architectural modifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Quantify the Modality Gap:** Develop dedicated metrics to precisely assess the degree of misalignment between image and text embeddings in VLMs.
- **Reduce the Modality Gap:** Propose and evaluate generic and effective techniques to minimize the separation between image and text embeddings.
- **Improve Multimodal Information Retrieval:** Demonstrate the beneficial impact of the proposed methods on downstream tasks, particularly multimodal information retrieval, where the ability to retrieve relevant items regardless of modality is crucial.

The motivation stems from the observed limitations of existing VLMs in fully multimodal scenarios, where the retrieval results are often biased towards the modality of the query. The authors argue that addressing the modality gap is essential for unlocking the full potential of VLMs in applications that require seamless integration of information from different modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes a two-pronged approach:

- **Post-processing Techniques:** The authors introduce two families of techniques designed to alleviate the modality gap as a post-processing step:
 - **Spectral Techniques:** Adapts spectral embedding techniques by constructing a weighted adjacency matrix based on the similarity between image and text embeddings. Eigenvector decomposition of the Laplacian matrix is then used to generate new representations that better align the modalities.
 - **Optimal Transport:** Uses optimal transport to find a transformation that moves the source distribution (image embeddings) to the target distribution (text embeddings) while minimizing a transport cost. Laplacian regularization is employed to enforce constraints on the positions and displacements of source points, ensuring that similar source points have similar transformations.
- **Evaluation Measures:** The authors develop a suite of evaluation measures to quantify the alignment quality:
 - **Distance-based Evaluation Measures:** Calculates the mean of the norms or squared norms of the differences between image and text embeddings. Normalized Euclidean distances and cosine distances are also used to account for differences in scale.
 - **IR-based Heterogeneity Indices:** Introduces measures like Image Text Ratio (ITR), Text Image Ratio (TIR), Text Mean Rank (TMR), and Image Mean Rank (IMR) to assess the modality bias in multimodal information retrieval tasks.
 - **Distribution-based Evaluation Measures:** Employs the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) to evaluate the closeness of the image and text embedding distributions.

The authors conduct extensive experiments on multiple image-text datasets (COCO-PAIRS and CC-PAIRS) and models (CLIP, SigLIP, and LLM2CLIP) to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed techniques and metrics. They compare the performance of the original embeddings with those obtained after applying the spectral and optimal transport methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods in mitigating the modality gap:

- **Reduced Modality Bias:** The spectral and optimal transport techniques significantly reduce the ITR and TIR values, indicating a decrease in the bias towards retrieving items of the same modality as the query.
- **Improved Retrieval Performance:** The TMR and IMR values are substantially lowered after applying the transformation methods, suggesting that relevant items from different modalities are ranked higher in retrieval results.
- **Closer Representations:** The distance-based evaluation measures and the FID scores show a significant reduction in the distance between image and text embeddings after applying the post-processing techniques.
- **Relevance of Alignment:** The analysis of cosine distances between images and their corresponding texts confirms that the transformation methods bring semantically related items closer to each other, rather than simply reducing the overall distance between all embeddings.
- **Importance of Similarity Criteria:** The PCA results demonstrate that applying dimension reduction techniques alone, without considering the semantic similarity between images and texts, does not improve retrieval performance.

The spectral-based method particularly stands out as the most effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has significant implications for the field of vision-language models and multimodal information retrieval:

- **Practical Solutions:** The proposed post-processing techniques offer practical and model-agnostic solutions to address the modality gap, allowing practitioners to improve the performance of existing VLMs without extensive retraining or architectural modifications.
- **Enhanced Understanding:** The dedicated evaluation metrics provide valuable tools for analyzing and understanding the modality gap, enabling researchers to better assess the alignment quality of VLMs.
- **Improved Multimodal Applications:** By mitigating the modality gap, the research contributes to the development of more effective multimodal applications, such as information retrieval systems that can seamlessly integrate information from different modalities, content recommendation systems that can leverage both visual and textual features, and zero-shot classification models that can generalize across modalities.
- **Open Source Contribution:** The authors have made their code available, which enables other researchers to reproduce and extend their work.

The work is particularly relevant to the development of multimodal information retrieval systems, especially in scenarios where the results are expected to include items from various modalities irrespective of the query's modality. The enhanced alignment of image and text representations can lead to more accurate and unbiased retrieval results, improving the user experience and enabling new applications. The work from PEReN also has implications for digital policy by facilitating the creation of more integrated systems.
