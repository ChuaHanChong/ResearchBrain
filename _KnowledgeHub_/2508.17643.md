# SEBVS: Synthetic Event-based Visual Servoing for Robot Navigation and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SEBVS is an open-source ROS2/Gazebo package embedding a fine-tuned v2e emulator that turns RGB camera topics into real-time synthetic event streams, plus a compact ViT-style policy that early-fuses 3-channel RGB with 2-channel ON/OFF event maps into a 5-channel input for behavior-cloned control. Fused policies beat RGB-only and event-only on mobile-robot object tracking and UR5 pre-grasp pose prediction.

## Problem

- Mainstream robotics simulators lack native event-camera sensors: Gazebo relies on an outdated community plugin, Isaac Sim on closed-source extensions, MuJoCo on external ESIM.
- Offline converters generating events from rendered video are cumbersome and computationally costly, ill-suited to interactive closed-loop policy training at scale.
- Prior event-driven robotics work stays in perception pipelines with custom per-application stacks, lacking closed-loop control comparisons against RGB-trained policies across both navigation and manipulation.

## Method

- **SEBVS** ROS2 ==v2e emulator== node subscribes to /camera/image_raw, resizes and greyscales, publishes events on /dvs/events; exposes tunable contrast thresholds, sigma noise, cutoff frequency, downsampling, blur.
- Shared ==ViT-style backbone==: ==early fusion== of RGB and ON/OFF event-count maps into 5 channels, 16x16 ==patch embedding== to 64-dim, 1600 tokens, sinusoidal positions, 4-head ==self-attention== plus 256-unit MLP.
- Two task heads trained by ==behavior cloning==: PolicyHeadNav regressing linear and angular velocity under MSE, PolicyHeadArm regressing 6-DoF pre-grasp pose under Smooth L1, from YOLO+PID and YOLO+MoveIt expert datasets.

## Results

- Navigation over **15** episodes: fused policy reaches **106.7 px** centroid error and **93.3%** success, versus **129.7 px** / **86.7%** for RGB-only and **152.0 px** / **73.3%** for event-only.
- Single-object grasping: fused policy hits **41.1 mm** error, **71.4%** accuracy, **51.7%** success, against RGB-only **48.6 mm** / **43.5%** / **28.4%** and event-only **66.2 mm** / **31.8%** / **26.9%**.
- Multi-object clutter degrades all variants but preserves ordering, fused at **52.6 mm** and **31.8%** success; event-only is fastest at **3.0-3.2 ms** latency versus **7.6-7.8 ms** fused.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Early channel-level fusion of appearance and motion cues before tokenization beats either modality alone, indicating events supplement rather than replace dense frames.
> - Event-only inference buys the lowest latency but loses accuracy, so the speed-versus-precision tradeoff should be chosen per task rather than assumed.
> - Emulated events inside a standard simulator make event-driven policy learning accessible without neuromorphic hardware, though sim-to-real transfer remains unvalidated here.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly deploying autonomous systems in complex, dynamic, and often challenging environments. In such scenarios, traditional frame-based vision systems exhibit limitations, including susceptibility to motion blur, restricted dynamic range, and inherent latency, which can compromise performance in high-speed navigation, precise manipulation, and closed-loop control applications.

Event cameras, or neuromorphic sensors, present an alternative to conventional frame-based cameras. They operate by asynchronously reporting pixel-level brightness changes with microsecond latency, offering high dynamic range and inherent immunity to motion blur. These characteristics position event cameras as suitable candidates for real-time robotic perception, particularly in time-critical tasks under difficult lighting conditions or high-speed motion. Existing research has demonstrated the utility of event cameras in various applications, including traffic monitoring, autonomous driving, pose estimation, and agile robotic tasks.

Despite the advantages of event-based vision, its integration into mainstream robotics research, particularly within simulation environments, has faced challenges. A primary hurdle is the absence of native event-camera models in widely used robotics simulators such as Gazebo, Isaac Sim, and MuJoCo. Researchers typically resort to offline converters like ESIM or v2e to generate synthetic event streams from RGB video, a process that is often time-consuming, cumbersome, and not conducive to interactive policy training. Additionally, the sparse and asynchronous nature of event data does not align directly with traditional perception pipelines, which are designed for dense, synchronous RGB frames, necessitating specialized learning architectures to effectively leverage this data for robot control.

Prior work has addressed some of these gaps. Recurrent vision transformers (RVT) have been introduced for efficient processing of event streams in object detection. Event-camera-driven reinforcement learning controllers have been demonstrated for robot navigation, indicating the feasibility of event-driven policies. However, a significant gap remains: there has been no integrated, simulation-ready, end-to-end framework capable of learning closed-loop event-based robot policies that encompass both perception and control, directly within a robotics simulator. This work aims to bridge this gap by providing such a framework, facilitating scalable and reproducible research in event-driven robotics. By enabling real-time event stream generation in simulation and developing transformer-based policies that operate on these streams, this research contributes to establishing a foundation for the wider adoption and integration of event cameras in robotic policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate an integrated framework, named Synthetic Event-based Visual Servoing (SEBVS), designed for event-driven robotic perception and control within a simulation environment. This framework aims to facilitate the learning of robust robotic policies that leverage the unique benefits of event cameras.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current robotic vision systems and the underdeveloped state of event-based vision in simulation:

- **Limitations of Conventional Vision Systems:** Standard frame-based cameras struggle in demanding robotic environments characterized by rapid changes, occlusions, and extreme illumination conditions. Issues such as motion blur, limited dynamic range, and processing latency hinder their performance in tasks requiring high-speed navigation or precise manipulation. Event cameras offer a promising solution to these limitations dueoviding asynchronous, low-latency, and high dynamic range data.
- **Lack of Event Camera Simulation Support:** Mainstream robotics simulators, including Gazebo, lack native support for event camera models. This forces researchers to rely on external, often offline, tools like ESIM or v2e to generate synthetic event data. This indirect process is cumbersome, time-consuming, and unsuitable for the iterative and interactive demands of robot policy training, thereby impeding the development and evaluation of event-driven approaches.
- **Architectural Mismatch for Event Data:** The sparse and asynchronous nature of event streams requires dedicated learning architectures that can effectively process their rich spatio-temporal information, which is not readily compatible with traditional perception pipelines designed for dense, synchronous RGB frames.
- **Need for an End-to-End Simulation Framework:** Despite progress in event-based perception and control, a comprehensive, simulation-ready, and end-to-end framework for learning closed-loop event-based robot policies that integrate both perception and control, across diverse robotic tasks, has been lacking.

To address these motivations and achieve the overall objective, the authors define several specific contributions:

1. **Develop an Open-Source ROS2/Gazebo Package:** Create a user-friendly, open-source ROS2 package that integrates a real-time v2e emulator backend with Gazebo. This package enables low-latency neuromorphic perception directly within the simulator, removing the need for specialized hardware during development and streamlining the data generation process.
2. **Propose a Transformer-Based Architecture:** Design a transformer-based neural network architecture capable of fusing accumulated event frames with RGB images. This architecture aims to process both modalities effectively, leveraging patch embeddings, positional encoding, and stacked self-attention blocks to generate high-frequency control outputs.
3. **Evaluate on Representative Robotic Tasks:** Assess the effectiveness of the proposed framework and policies through two distinct robotic tasks:
 - **Object Following (Navigation):** A mobile robot tracking a moving object.
 - **Object Detection and Grasping (Manipulation):** A robotic manipulator predicting a pre-grasp pose for an object.
4. **Compare Event-Driven Policies with Baselines:** Conduct a comparative analysis of the event-driven policies against RGB-only baselines under various operating conditions. The evaluation focuses on metrics such as tracking accuracy, task success rate, and closed-loop latency to quantify the competitive advantages of integrating event-based perception.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to highlight the practical benefits of neuromorphic sensing for real-time robotic navigation and manipulation, thereby laying a foundational framework for broader integration of event cameras into simulation-to-real robot policy learning paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SEBVS framework is designed to integrate synthetic event stream generation within a standard robotics simulator and to train transformer-based policies for robotic navigation and manipulation using this data.

#### 3.1. v2e ROS2 Emulator for Gazebo

A lightweight ROS2 package was developed to embed real-time event camera simulation directly into Gazebo. This package integrates `v2ecore’s EventEmulator` by subscribing to standard RGB camera topics (e.g., `/camera/image_raw`). It processes the incoming RGB stream by performing resizing and grayscale conversion, then forwards these images to the `EventEmulator`. The `EventEmulator` generates an event stream, which is then published on a dedicated ROS topic (e.g., `/dvs/events`). These raw events can be subsequently accumulated into event frames for downstream processing by the robot policy.

The package emphasizes ease of integration, requiring minimal configuration and basic ROS knowledge (primarily topic remapping). It operates efficiently on commodity hardware with NVIDIA GPUs and CUDA support. Tunable parameters are provided, including positive and negative contrast thresholds (`pos_thres`, `neg_thres`), a noise-clamp threshold (`sigma_thres`), a high-pass filter cutoff frequency (`cutoff_hz`), an exponential leak rate (`leak_rate_hz`), and an input image downsampling factor (`downsample`), along with an option to enable Gaussian blur. This flexibility allows researchers to customize event generation to match specific simulation or hardware requirements. The package was tested on ROS2 Humble with Gazebo Classic but is designed to be agnostic to specific ROS2 or Gazebo versions due to its topic-based communication.

#### 3.2. Event-Driven Control Policies (ERPs) for Navigation and Manipulation

The research investigates Event-based Robotic Policies (ERPs) for object detection and tracking tasks, which are critical for robotic manipulation and navigation. These ERPs address the limitations of conventional vision systems by jointly processing synchronized RGB frames and asynchronous event streams to generate real-time control actions.

At each time step `t`, the policy receives an observation `o_t` comprising an RGB image `I_t` and an accumulated event frame `E_t`. The event frame `E_t` is discretized into two channels: `E+t` for positive polarity (ON events) and `E-t` for negative polarity (OFF events).

- **Task 1: Differential Drive Tracking (ERPNav):**
 The `ERPNav` policy maps the observation `o_t` to a `geometry_msgs/Twist` command, specifying linear `v_t` and angular `ω_t` velocities. These velocities are designed to reorient a differential-drive robot towards a target object. The policy is trained using behavior cloning, minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between the policy's predicted commands and expert-provided ground-truth commands.
 - **Expert Demonstration Dataset:** A dataset was collected using a four-wheel differential-drive robot in Gazebo. A 640x640 RGB camera, integrated with the v2e emulator, provided both RGB frames and real-time event stacks. The expert policy employed a YOLOv8 object detector combined with a two-axis PID controller. Object centroids detected by YOLOv8 were fed to the PID controller, which generated continuous linear-x and angular-z velocity commands. A custom ROS2 node synchronously logged RGB frames, event frames (ON/OFF histograms), and the corresponding expert velocity actions into HDF5 files. The dataset consists of 70 episodes (approximately 65 minutes of simulation), yielding 32,033 labeled control vectors, balanced to capture diverse behaviors such as smooth tracking and sharp corrective maneuvers.

- **Task 2: UR5 Pre-Grasp Pose Prediction (ERPArm):**
 The `ERPArm` policy predicts a 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) pre-grasp end-effector pose (position and orientation: `x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw`) for a UR5 robotic arm. This prediction is made from a single initial RGB-event observation `o_0`. Training minimizes the MSE between the predicted pose and the ground-truth pre-grasp pose.
 - **Expert Demonstration Dataset:** This dataset was gathered using a UR5 manipulator equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and a wrist-mounted RGB camera, with event generation via the v2e emulator. Expert pre-grasp poses were derived from a YOLO-based detector that published object world coordinates. A `PickPoseRecorder` ROS2 node synchronously logged RGB tensors, two-channel event tensors (ON/OFF event counts), and 6-DoF pre-grasp pose vectors. The dataset comprises 35 episodes (approximately 70 minutes of simulation), each containing 1,000–2,000 synchronized frames. It includes scenarios with both single and multiple objects, with safety protocols implemented to prevent the arm from moving to invalid positions.

#### 3.3. Transformer-based Policy Learning for Event-driven Control

Both `ERPNav` and `ERPArm` policies share a common backbone architecture inspired by the Vision Transformer (ViT), optimized for real-time inference.

- **Input Processing:** At each time step, a three-channel RGB frame is concatenated with its two event-count maps (ON and OFF polarities) to form a five-channel input tensor. This early fusion approach integrates appearance information with microsecond-scale motion cues at the input stage.
- **Tokenization and Embedding:** The five-channel tensor is divided into non-overlapping 16x16 patches. Each patch is flattened and linearly projected to a 64-dimensional embedding, resulting in a sequence of 1,600 patch tokens. A learnable class token is prepended to this sequence, and fixed sinusoidal positional encodings are added to retain spatial information after flattening.
- **Transformer Encoder:** The token sequence is then processed by a lightweight Transformer encoder. This encoder consists of a four-head multi-head self-attention layer followed by a 256-unit feed-forward block. Residual connections, layer normalization, and dropout (0.1) are applied after each sub-layer, aligning with ViT and RVT formulations while maintaining a modest parameter count. The updated class token serves as a global spatio-temporal summary of the fused RGB-event observation.
- **Policy Head:** The class token is finally passed to a task-specific Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) policy head. For `ERPNav`, `PolicyHeadNav` outputs a two-dimensional action `[v_t, ω_t]`. For `ERPArm`, `PolicyHeadArm` outputs a 6-DoF end-effector pose.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup and Training Details

Three policy variants were trained for both tasks: `RGB-Only`, `Event-Only`, and `RGB+Event`. `RGB-Only` variants used only RGB frames, `Event-Only` variants used only event frames, and `RGB+Event` variants used the fused five-channel input. All variants were trained with identical hyperparameters and protocols for consistent evaluation.

- **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer was used.
- **Learning Rate:** For `ERPNav`, a learning rate of 2x10^-4 with L2 weight decay of 1x10^-4. For `ERPArm`, an initial learning rate of 1x10^-4, with a `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler to adaptively reduce it.
- **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) for `ERPNav` (targeting continuous predictions of normalized linear and angular velocities). Smooth L1 (Huber) Loss for `ERPArm` (regressing target pose vectors).
- **Training Schedule:** Both policies were trained for 10 epochs with a batch size of 32, using early stopping based on validation loss (patience of 2 epochs).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the SEBVS framework involved training and testing the `ERPNav` and `ERPArm` policies in simulation, comparing `RGB-Only`, `Event-Only`, and `RGB+Event` input modalities.

#### 4.1. Training and Validation Loss

Analysis of the training and validation loss curves indicated that for both `ERPNav` and `ERPArm` policies, the `RGB+Event` variant consistently achieved the lowest loss values. The `RGB-Only` variant showed the second-best performance, while the `Event-Only` variant exhibited higher loss and slower convergence rates. This trend suggests that the fusion of RGB and event data enhances the learning process and model accuracy across both navigation and manipulation tasks.

#### 4.2. ERPNav Performance for Tracking in Simulation (Mobile Robot)

The `ERPNav` policy's performance in object tracking was evaluated using four metrics over 15 simulation episodes: centroid error (mean ± standard deviation), mean bounding-box width, success rate (object within a 200 px radius of image center), and mean trial duration.

- **RGB+Event Policy:** This variant achieved the lowest mean centroid error (106.7 ± 26.3 px) and the highest success rate (93.3%). Its mean bounding-box width was 196.6 px, indicating a balanced following distance without excessive closeness. These results collectively demonstrate superior tracking stability and accuracy.
- **RGB-Only Policy:** This variant showed a higher mean centroid error (129.7 ± 45.0 px) and a lower success rate (86.7%) compared to the `RGB+Event` policy, indicating less robust tracking.
- **Event-Only Policy:** This variant exhibited the highest mean centroid error (152.0 ± 44.0 px) and the lowest success rate (73.3%). It also recorded the longest mean trial duration (41.6 s), suggesting slower and less efficient tracking performance.

The results indicate that combining RGB and event data significantly improves tracking stability and success rates for mobile robot navigation.

#### 4.3. ERPArm Performance for Pre-Grasp Pose Prediction (Robotic Manipulator)

The `ERPArm` policy was evaluated for 6-DoF pre-grasp pose prediction under both single-object and multi-object scenarios, across 15 episodes for each variant. Metrics included position error (mm), accuracy (predictions within an error threshold), inference latency (ms), and grasp success rate.

- **Single Object Scenario:**
 - **RGB+Event Policy:** Achieved the lowest position error (41.1 ± 9.5 mm), highest accuracy (71.4%), and highest success rate (51.7%). Its inference latency was 7.8 ± 0.6 ms.
 - **Event-Only Policy:** While demonstrating the lowest inference latency (3.2 ± 0.4 ms), it exhibited the highest position error (66.2 ± 16.4 mm), lowest accuracy (31.8%), and lowest success rate (26.9%).
 - **RGB-Only Policy:** Showed intermediate performance, with a position error of 48.6 ± 12.2 mm, accuracy of 43.5%, and success rate of 28.4%.

- **Multi-Object Scenario:**
 - **RGB+Event Policy:** Maintained its superior performance, achieving a position error of 52.6 ± 11.3 mm, accuracy of 58.9%, and success rate of 31.8%. Its inference latency was 7.6 ± 0.5 ms.
 - **Event-Only Policy:** Again recorded the lowest latency (3.0 ± 0.9 ms) but the poorest accuracy (22.4%) and success rate (21.5%).

Across both scenarios, the `RGB+Event` variant consistently outperformed the `RGB-Only` and `Event-Only` models in terms of accuracy and success rate for pre-grasp pose prediction. Although the `Event-Only` variant offered lower latency, this came at the expense of accuracy and robustness, reinforcing the benefit of modality fusion for dynamic grasping tasks requiring precise spatio-temporal cues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SEBVS, an integrated framework for synthetic event-based visual servoing, addressing a notable gap in the simulation and training of event-driven robotic policies. The work holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Advancement in Event-Based Robotics Research:** The introduction of an open-source ROS2/Gazebo package that integrates a real-time v2e emulator is a substantial contribution. It significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers in event-based robotics by enabling seamless, low-latency event stream generation directly within a widely used robotics simulator. This eliminates the cumbersome and time-consuming reliance on offline conversion tools, facilitating more interactive and scalable policy training.
- **Enhanced Robotic Performance:** The study empirically demonstrates that the fusion of high-resolution RGB frames with microsecond-latency event cues leads to improved control accuracy, robustness, and task success. This was consistently observed in both dynamic object tracking (navigation) and precise pre-grasp pose prediction (manipulation). The `RGB+Event` policies consistently outperformed `RGB-Only` and `Event-Only` counterparts, highlighting the synergistic benefits of combining these modalities for perception and decision-making in robotic tasks.
- **Validation of Transformer-Based Architectures for Event Data:** The successful application of a compact transformer-based architecture for processing fused RGB-event inputs validates its efficacy in generating high-frequency control outputs for real-time robotic applications. This approach leverages modern deep learning paradigms, offering a robust foundation for future event-driven policy learning.
- **Feasibility of Simulation-to-Real Transfer:** The methodology validates the practicality of using event emulators within simulators like Gazebo to train event-driven policies, which can then perform low-latency inference without the immediate need for specialized neuromorphic hardware during the development phase. This is a critical step towards bridging the sim-to-real gap, as it enables the development and iterative refinement of policies in a controlled virtual environment before deployment on physical robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Applications:** This work establishes a foundation for the broader integration of event cameras into robotic policy learning, particularly for applications in visually challenging or fast-moving environments where conventional cameras fall short. The authors outline future directions including deployment on real-world robot platforms (requiring domain adaptation strategies like randomization and fine-tuning), extending policies to more complex cluttered scenes and multi-agent systems, and integrating advanced temporal attention mechanisms to better model event dynamics.
- **Promotion of Reproducible Science:** By providing an open-source framework and associated datasets, the research promotes reproducibility and encourages community engagement. This can accelerate innovation and collaboration within the event-based vision and robotics communities, leading to faster development of more sophisticated and robust robotic systems.

In summary, the SEBVS framework and its empirical validation represent a significant step toward making event-based vision a more accessible and effective tool for real-time robotic perception and control, with the potential to impact a wide array of robotic applications.
