# Composition-Grounded Data Synthesis for Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The COGS framework equips multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities in artificial image domains like charts and webpages. It generates diverse, compositional question-answer pairs from a small set of seed questions and fine-tunes MLLMs using process-level reinforcement learning, achieving competitive performance on ChartQAPro (52.02%) and VisualWebBench (88.04%).

## Problem

- Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) often lack advanced reasoning capabilities in specialized artificial image domains such as charts and webpages.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale human-annotated reasoning datasets for these niche visual domains, making traditional MLLM training challenging and costly.
- Existing data synthesis methods for MLLMs often do not explicitly ground reasoning components in visual features or effectively scale for diverse, compositional questions.

## Method

- **COGS** decomposes complex questions from a small seed dataset into ==primitive perception== and reasoning 'factors' using an MLLM.
- It systematically recombines these factors with new unlabeled images to synthesize a large, diverse dataset of novel compositional question-answer pairs, including intermediate subquestions.
- The framework fine-tunes a pretrained MLLM using reinforcement learning (GRPO) with process-level rewards, which leverages the factor decomposition for granular supervision.

## Results

- COGS-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** achieved **52.02%** accuracy on ChartQAPro, outperforming several proprietary models (e.g., GPT-5-nano at **50.74%**) and open-source baselines.
- The framework successfully generalized to webpage GUI understanding, obtaining **88.04%** accuracy on VisualWebBench, making it the top-performing non-proprietary model.
- Ablation studies confirmed that ProcessRM-max consistently outperformed other reward models (StandardRM, ProcessRM-sum) for fine-tuning, and COGS showed larger performance gains on multi-hop reasoning questions and advanced factors like Extrapolation and Calculation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex reasoning questions can be broken down into a finite set of reusable perception and reasoning 'factors,' enabling combinatorial data expansion.
> - Process-level rewards derived from intermediate sub-answers (specifically ProcessRM-max) are more robust and effective for fine-tuning MLLMs in compositional reasoning tasks than sparse final-answer rewards.
> - Mixing datasets at the factor-level, rather than simply combining data, enhances positive transfer and generalizability across diverse domains like charts and webpages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses challenges in equipping multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities, particularly in artificial image domains where large-scale human-annotated reasoning datasets are scarce. MLLMs have demonstrated proficiency across various multimodal tasks, but their reasoning abilities can be limited in specialized domains such as charts, rendered documents, and webpages. These domains are abundant in practice but lack the extensive reasoning-intensive question-answer datasets typically required for training advanced models.

Existing approaches to MLLM development include large-scale pre-training, instruction tuning, and the development of specialist models for specific domains. Some prior work on automatic instruction generation and evolutionary methods focuses on increasing the complexity of synthetic data for text reasoning, often by searching for reasoning trajectories in text space or relying on hand-crafted heuristics. However, these methods typically do not explicitly analyze and augment seed datasets by detecting reasoning components grounded in visual features.

For chart understanding, numerous benchmarks and data synthesis methods exist. While some convert charts into structured intermediates for LLM processing, others train end-to-end vision-language models. Data synthesis often involves template-based generation or in-context examples. Similarly, GUI understanding research has produced benchmarks for page comprehension, reasoning, and grounding.

This work distinguishes itself by proposing a data-efficient framework that leverages the *compositional structure* of reasoning questions. Instead of relying solely on complex text generation or heuristic-driven data expansion, it decomposes questions into primitive perception and reasoning "factors" that are then systematically recombined. This approach aims to unlock scalable synthetic data generation and provide process-level reinforcement learning, offering a mechanism to bootstrap reasoning skills from a small set of seed questions, particularly in visually grounded contexts. It attempts to address the data scarcity problem by expanding both the quantity and diversity of reasoning types beyond the original seeds, aligning with the broader goal of making MLLMs more capable in real-world, reasoning-intensive applications like document agents or digital environment interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to equip MLLMs with advanced reasoning abilities in artificial image domains, such as charts and webpages, using only a small set of seed questions. This goal is driven by the practical challenge that while artificial images are ubiquitous, large-scale human-annotated reasoning datasets for these domains are difficult and costly to collect.

Specifically, the key objectives are:
1. **Discover relevant factors:** From a limited seed question dataset, identify the fundamental perception and reasoning steps (factors) that constitute complex questions in the target domain.
2. **Generate novel, valid questions:** Systematically recombine these discovered factors with new, unlabeled images to synthesize a diverse and large dataset of novel, complex question-answer pairs. Each generated question should also include intermediate subquestions and answers.
3. **Improve MLLM performance:** Utilize these synthetically generated questions to fine-tune a pretrained MLLM, enhancing its ability to answer complex, compositional questions through reinforcement learning. The design also aims to leverage the factor decomposition for fine-grained supervision via process-level rewards.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several considerations:
- **Bridging the data scarcity gap:** Traditional MLLM training relies heavily on vast annotated datasets. For reasoning-intensive tasks in specialized visual domains, such datasets are rare. The framework seeks to overcome this limitation by generating high-quality synthetic data efficiently.
- **Enabling advanced reasoning:** MLLMs often struggle with multi-step, compositional reasoning. By decomposing questions into factors and then recomposing them, the framework explicitly models and trains for these intricate reasoning paths, aiming to foster more robust and generalizable reasoning capabilities.
- **Supporting downstream applications:** Developing MLLMs capable of interpreting and reasoning over artificial image domains is critical for applications like autonomous agents that interact with digital interfaces (e.g., editing documents, navigating webpages).
- **Compositionality as a key insight:** The authors leverage the principle that complex questions, despite surface-level diversity, can often be broken down into a finite set of simpler, reusable perception and reasoning steps. This compositionality allows for combinatorial expansion of the question space.
- **Process-level supervision:** By generating intermediate subquestions and answers alongside the main questions, the framework provides fine-grained supervision signals, which are argued to be more effective for reinforcement learning than solely relying on final answer correctness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, COGS (COmposition-Grounded data Synthesis), operates in three main stages: Seed Data Decomposition, Question Generation via Factor Recomposition, and Reinforcement Learning-Based Fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Seed Data Decomposition

The initial stage involves taking a small seed dataset of human-curated questions in the target domain and decomposing each complex question into its constituent primitive factors. These factors represent elementary perception steps (e.g., identifying a number, localizing an element) and reasoning steps (e.g., calculation, comparison, counting, logical inference).
- **Process:** A large language model (MLLM) is prompted with a natural language description of the decomposition task, in-context examples of question-factor pairs, the target question, and its associated image.
- **Output:** For each question, the MLLM outputs a sequence of factors, each with a category label (e.g., 'Calculation', 'Counting') and a corresponding subquestion describing its role.
- **Factor Pool:** All discovered factors are aggregated into a factor pool, where each factor is represented by its category name and associated exemplar subquestions. This pool forms a compositional representation of the domain's latent question structure.

#### 3.2. Question Generation via Factor Recomposition

In the second stage, the framework leverages the collected factor pool and a collection of unlabeled images to synthesize a large and diverse set of new, compositional question-answer pairs.
- **Process:** An MLLM is prompted with a description of the recomposition task, a single recomposition example, a new unlabeled image, and a randomly sampled subset of factors from the factor pool. Each sampled factor includes its category name and exemplar subquestions from the seed dataset.
- **Output Generation:** The MLLM generates new subquestions based on the sampled factors, grounding them on the new image. These subquestions are then composed into a coherent overall complex question. Critically, the MLLM also generates answers to these subquestions, which are then combined to form the answer to the overall recomposed question.
- **Data Structure:** Each generated data point is a tuple: `⟨Image, Question, Answer, {Factors}, {Sub-Answers}⟩`.
- **Metadata Utilization:** For artificial domains like charts, auxiliary metadata (e.g., underlying data tables) can be used during question generation to improve answer precision. This enhances the quality of synthetic data.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning-Based Fine-tuning

The final stage involves fine-tuning a pretrained MLLM using the generated synthetic data through reinforcement learning (RL), specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). A key aspect is the design of process-level rewards.
- **Problem:** Standard RL for MLLMs often relies on final-answer correctness, which can be sparse for complex reasoning tasks. The factor decompositions provide intermediate signals.
- **Reward Models:** Three reward models are considered:
 - **StandardRM:** Rewards based solely on the correctness of the final answer.
 - **ProcessRM-sum:** Combines final answer correctness with the average accuracy of subquestions, aiming to encourage faithful reasoning at the factor level. Defined as `r(y) = r_final(y) + λ * r_sub(y)`.
 - **ProcessRM-max:** Prioritizes the final answer but provides reward shaping when intermediate reasoning steps (subquestions) are correct. Defined as `r(y) = max(r_final(y), λ * r_sub(y))`.
- **Theoretical Justification:** Proposition 3.1 demonstrates that ProcessRM-max preserves policy orders under noisy sub-reward signals, whereas ProcessRM-sum does not necessarily. This suggests ProcessRM-max is a more robust approach for integrating factor-level supervision.
- **Implementation Details:** GRPO is used for fine-tuning, leveraging the detailed breakdown of questions into factors and sub-answers for richer reward signals. An LLM-based reward model verifies the correctness of intermediate sub-answers to yield a binary score for each factor.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of COGS was conducted across chart reasoning and webpage GUI understanding domains, with a series of experiments and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Chart Understanding (ChartQAPro Dataset)

- **Performance on Unseen Questions:** COGS significantly improved the reasoning capabilities of base MLLMs on unseen questions from the ChartQAPro benchmark. Using Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the base model, COGS achieved an overall accuracy of 52.02%. This compares to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B's 47.36%, demonstrating a notable gain.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Proprietary Models:** COGS-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B surpassed the overall accuracy of several competitive proprietary models like GPT-5-nano (50.74%), GPT-4o-mini (48.32%), Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite (38.72%), and Claude Haiku 3.5 (46.74%).
 - **Open-source MLLMs:** COGS outperformed other open-source models (e.g., InternVL3.5-GPT-OSS at 46.86%, Phi-4-14B at 31.61%).
 - **Chart Specialist Models:** Models specifically designed for charts (ChartLLaMA 17.19%, ChartMoE 27.28%) performed substantially lower than COGS, attributed to architectural constraints and narrow training data distributions that did not fully cover ChartQAPro's diversity.
 - **Prompting Strategies:** Inference-time prompting strategies like Self-Consistency (47.22%) and Tree of Thoughts (44.44%) did not yield performance comparable to COGS, nor did a Decompositional CoT (46.36%). The authors attributed this to error accumulation in inference-time decomposition, which COGS mitigates through training with process rewards.
 - **Other Data Synthesis:** Other data synthesis approaches (ChartQA-Train 46.64%, Chart-R1 47.32%, In-Context Q Example 50.58%) showed minor benefits over the base model, suggesting potential domain gaps or less effective data generation strategies.

#### 4.2. Generalization Over Mixture of Datasets (ChartQAPro + MMC)

- **Positive Transfer:** When trained jointly on ChartQAPro and MMC-Bench datasets, COGS demonstrated positive transfer across domains. Both data-level and factor-level mixtures improved performance on both datasets compared to single-dataset training.
- **Factor-level Mixture Superiority:** The factor-level mixture strategy (merging factors from both seed datasets into a joint pool before recomposition) consistently outperformed the data-level mixture (synthesizing data independently from each seed and then combining). Factor-level mixture achieved 52.33% on ChartQAPro and 87.55% on MMC, surpassing data-level mixture (50.72% on ChartQAPro, 86.99% on MMC). This indicates that factorization provides a common representational ground, enabling more transferable training and addressing challenges of data heterogeneity.

#### 4.3. Webpage GUI Understanding (VisualWebBench Dataset)

- **Generalizability:** The framework successfully extended to the webpage question answering domain. COGS achieved an accuracy of 88.04% on VisualWebBench, making it the top-performing non-proprietary model.
- **Comparison:** This result was higher than the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (85.65%), the specialist UiX-Qwen2 (68.90%), and other data synthesis methods like MultiUI-WQA (86.60%). Proprietary models like GPT-5-nano (89.47%) showed slightly higher performance.

#### 4.4. Additional Analysis (Ablation Studies)

- **Improvement on Multi-hop Reasoning:** COGS demonstrated larger performance gains on questions with more reasoning factors (multi-hop reasoning chains), particularly for factoid, multiple-choice, and fact-checking question types. For hypothetical questions, the trend was less pronounced, possibly due to the dominance of the initial counterfactual reasoning step.
- **Advanced Reasoning Factors:** Significant gains were observed for advanced reasoning factors such as Extrapolation (+7.62%) and Calculation (+3.04%), as well as Count (+4.25%) and Compare (+4.47%). This suggests improved ability to execute operations and select appropriate reasoning steps.
- **Reward Model Effectiveness:** An ablation study on reward models showed that **ProcessRM-max** (52.02% accuracy) consistently outperformed **StandardRM** (50.96%) and **ProcessRM-sum** (50.35%). This result empirically validates the theoretical analysis regarding ProcessRM-max's ability to preserve policy orders under noisy sub-reward signals.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Adding an SFT phase prior to GRPO with ProcessRM-max resulted in lower performance (46.62%), suggesting that while SFT might regulate output format, it did not enhance reasoning ability in this context.
- **Size of Seed Questions:** Performance improved as the size of the seed question set increased, with a 33% seed set yielding substantial benefits. Very small seed sets (e.g., 1%) were less representative and resulted in lower performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a principled and data-efficient approach to enhance the reasoning capabilities of pretrained MLLMs in domains where human-annotated reasoning data is scarce. By exploiting the inherent compositional structure of complex questions, COGS enables the systematic generation of diverse and high-quality synthetic training data.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** COGS provides a scalable solution to the problem of limited annotated reasoning data in specialized visual domains. This can accelerate the development of MLLMs for real-world applications without prohibitive annotation costs.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** The framework demonstrably improves MLLMs' ability to perform multi-step, compositional reasoning, particularly on complex and reasoning-intensive questions. The factor-level process rewards offer a more granular supervision signal, which is crucial for learning intricate reasoning trajectories.
- **Generalizability Across Domains:** The successful application to both chart understanding and webpage GUI understanding indicates the broad applicability of the COGS framework to various artificial image domains, suggesting it is a robust methodology.
- **Improved Data Mixing Strategies:** The finding that factor-level mixtures yield better positive transfer across datasets than simple data-level mixtures offers valuable insights for foundation model training, suggesting a more effective way to leverage heterogeneous data sources.
- **Theoretical and Empirical Validation of Reward Models:** The empirical validation of ProcessRM-max's superiority, supported by theoretical analysis, contributes to the understanding of effective reward function design for reinforcement learning in complex language and vision tasks.

The potential impact of COGS extends to:
- **Development of Smarter AI Agents:** MLLMs trained with COGS could form the basis for more capable AI agents that can accurately interpret and act upon visually rich documents, interactive charts, or complex web interfaces, leading to advancements in areas like autonomous data analysis or web automation.
- **Accessibility and Inclusivity:** Improved MLLM reasoning over artificial images could enhance tools for visually impaired users to understand charts and digital content, or assist users in navigating complex interfaces.
- **Foundation Model Training:** The methodology for synthesizing compositionally rich data and leveraging process rewards could be integrated into the broader pretraining and fine-tuning pipelines of future MLLMs, leading to more robust and generalizable reasoning foundation models.

Future work directions identified by the authors, such as extending COGS to long-context reasoning over visually rich documents and integrating data synthesis into MLLM pretraining or search algorithms, further highlight the potential for continued impact and advancements based on this compositional data synthesis paradigm.
