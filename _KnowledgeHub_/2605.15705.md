# Feedback World Model Enables Precise Guidance of Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MARS Lab, Nanyang Technological University, developed a Feedback World Model that dynamically corrects its predictions using real-time observations and an Action-Aware Guidance strategy to steer diffusion policies in robotics. This approach enhances policy robustness and generalization in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments, reducing latent prediction error by up to 76.4% on real-world tasks and improving success rates to 80% on Peach P&P under OOD conditions.

## Problem

- World models often produce unreliable predictions when robots encounter out-of-distribution (OOD) states, hindering effective policy guidance.
- Current world models operate as static, open-loop predictors during inference, failing to incorporate real-time feedback to correct internal prediction drift and accumulating errors.
- Standard diffusion policy guidance compares predicted future latent states to expert states uniformly, potentially diluting the signal by focusing on both action-controllable and action-irrelevant latent dimensions.

## Method

- Introduces a **Feedback World Model** that uses real-time observations to ==online correct== its internal latent state predictions, mitigating prediction drift without retraining model parameters.
- Proposes an ==Action-Aware Guidance== strategy that estimates the controllability of each latent dimension and weights the guidance energy to prioritize action-responsive and task-relevant latent components.
- Integrates this feedback-corrected, action-aware guidance into the ==diffusion policy's denoising process==, leveraging the guidance energy gradient to steer action generation towards expert-like outcomes.

## Results

- The feedback world model reduced latent prediction Mean Squared Error (MSE) by up to **76.4%** on real-world tasks (Drawer-Open) and **73.3%** on simulated tasks (Robomimic Square) compared to the base world model.
- The combined feedback and action-aware guidance achieved the highest average success rate across simulated OOD tasks (**52%**), a **30%** relative improvement over the base policy (**40%**).
- In real-world OOD scenarios, success rates improved from **40%** to **80%** on Peach P&P and from **20%** to **70%** on Drawer-Open under initial-state distribution shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online correction of world model predictions using real-time feedback is highly effective at maintaining prediction accuracy under OOD conditions, outperforming costly offline data augmentation.
> - Latent representations exhibit significant anisotropy, where a small percentage of dimensions are highly responsive to actions, highlighting the benefit of prioritizing these for policy guidance.
> - The combination of dynamically corrected world model predictions and targeted, action-aware guidance leads to a substantial improvement in robotic policy robustness and generalization in novel environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of robotic learning, specifically focusing on the robustness and generalization of policies, particularly diffusion policies, through the integration of world models. World models, which predict environmental state evolution under actions, are recognized as tools for improving robotic decision-making by grounding policy learning and execution in predicted dynamics. Existing approaches typically incorporate world models either by integrating predicted dynamics into policy training or by using them at inference time for action evaluation or planning.

However, a known challenge is the degradation of world model predictions when robots operate in states outside the training data distribution (out-of-distribution, OOD). This can occur due to variations in initial pose, object configurations, or visual observations during deployment. Conventional solutions often involve scaling up training data or model capacity, which increases training costs and may not be practical in data-limited robotic scenarios.

This paper addresses the limitation that existing world models are largely treated as static, open-loop predictors at inference time. While new observations are incorporated for subsequent predictions, they are not typically used to correct the model's internal prediction state. This can lead to the accumulation of prediction errors, especially in OOD or long-horizon tasks. The research differentiates itself by proposing an online, inference-time correction mechanism that leverages real-time observations to mitigate prediction drift without requiring additional training data or model re-training. It also contributes to the area of inference-time policy steering for diffusion policies by introducing an action-aware guidance strategy that prioritizes action-controllable components in the latent space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and generalization of robotic policies, particularly diffusion policies, when deployed in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments. The authors aim to achieve this by improving the reliability of world model predictions and the efficacy of world-model-based policy guidance during inference.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current approaches:

- **Unreliable World Model Predictions under Distribution Shift:** World models, despite their potential, often produce inaccurate predictions when the robot encounters states not seen during training. These inaccuracies can mislead policy learning and inference-time guidance, thereby compromising policy performance in real-world, variable environments.
- **Static Nature of World Models at Inference Time:** Current systems predominantly use world models as static, pre-trained predictors. Although new observations are processed for subsequent predictions, the internal state of the world model itself is not actively updated or corrected based on the discrepancies between its predictions and actual observed outcomes. This open-loop operation allows prediction errors to accumulate over time.
- **Underutilization of Real-time Feedback:** The act of robot execution naturally provides a valuable signal: after each action, the robot observes the true next state. This observation directly reveals the mismatch between what the world model predicted and what actually occurred. The authors identify this real-time feedback as an underutilized resource that could be leveraged to improve prediction reliability online, without the need for additional data collection or model retraining.
- **Suboptimal Uniform Latent Guidance:** When world models guide diffusion policies, they often compare predicted future latent states to expert latent states uniformly across all dimensions. However, latent representations can encode both action-controllable factors (e.g., end-effector pose) and action-irrelevant factors (e.g., background appearance). Uniform guidance may dilute the signal by focusing on components not directly influenced by the robot's actions, leading to less effective control.

To address these issues, the research proposes a feedback mechanism that continuously corrects world model predictions using online observations and introduces an action-aware guidance strategy that prioritizes task-relevant and controllable latent dimensions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Feedback-Guided Policy, integrates a novel feedback world model with an action-aware guidance strategy into the inference pipeline of diffusion policies.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

The framework operates as an inference-time process. During each denoising step of a diffusion policy, a candidate action sequence is evaluated by a learned world model, which predicts a future latent state. This predicted state is then compared to a set of expert latent states to derive a guidance energy, typically the squared latent distance to the nearest expert state. This energy gradient is used to steer the diffusion policy towards actions with expert-like outcomes. The paper addresses two limitations of this standard approach: prediction drift under distribution shift and the inclusion of action-irrelevant components in guidance.

#### 3.2. Feedback World Model

To counter prediction drift, the paper introduces a feedback world model that uses real observations to correct latent predictions online, without updating the pretrained world model parameters.
- **Latent State Representation:** The observed context $O_t$ (current and historical observations) is encoded into a latent state $z_t = \psi(O_t)$, where $\psi$ is the context encoder.
- **Velocity Formulation:** The learned world model $f_\theta$ predicts the next latent state $\hat{z}_{t+1} = f_\theta(z_t, A_t)$. This is reinterpreted in velocity form as $v_t(A_t) = (\hat{z}_{t+1} - z_t) / \delta t$.
- **Auxiliary Feedback State:** An auxiliary feedback state $\bar{z}_t$ is maintained, initialized with $z_0$. This state tracks the model's internal belief about the system's state after past executed actions.
- **Discrepancy Measurement:** The discrepancy $e_t = z_t - \bar{z}_t$ quantifies the accumulated prediction error up to the current step.
- **Online Correction:** During diffusion guidance, this fixed discrepancy $e_t$ is used to correct the predicted velocity: $\hat{v}_t(A_t) = v_t(A_t) + Le_t$, where $L$ is a positive-definite feedback gain. The corrected prediction $z^\text{fb}_{t+1}(A_t) = z_t + \delta t \cdot \hat{v}_t(A_t)$ replaces the open-loop prediction in the guidance energy.
- **Feedback State Propagation:** After a guided action $A^{(0)}_t$ is executed, the feedback state is propagated using the same corrected velocity: $\bar{z}_{t+1} = \bar{z}_t + \delta t \cdot \hat{v}_t(A^{(0)}_t)$.
- **Residual Update:** Upon observing the true next latent state $z_{t+1}$, a new feedback residual $e_{t+1} = z_{t+1} - \bar{z}_{t+1}$ is formed for the subsequent decision step.
- **Convergence Guarantee:** Under a bounded residual assumption for the learned latent dynamics, the feedback update mechanism admits a theoretical convergence guarantee, ensuring that the difference between the true and feedback states remains bounded and the corrected one-step prediction becomes asymptotically unbiased.

#### 3.3. Action-Aware Guidance

To ensure that guidance focuses on action-relevant state changes, an action-aware energy function is introduced.
- **Controllability Estimation:** The controllability $w_j$ of the $j$-th latent dimension is estimated by its counterfactual variance under the learned world model: $w_j = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^M \text{Var}_{a \sim A^\text{demo}} ([f_\theta(z_i, a)]_j)$. This measures how responsive each latent dimension is to action perturbations.
- **Weight Normalization and Modulation:** The raw weights $w_j$ are normalized and modulated by a strength parameter $\beta$ to produce $w^{(\beta)}_j = 1 + \beta (\bar{w}_j - 1)$, where $\bar{w}_j$ is the normalized weight.
- **Weighted Guidance Energy:** The standard MSE guidance loss is replaced with a controllability-weighted form: $E^\text{ctrl}(A_t) = \sum_{j=1}^D w^{(\beta)}_j (z^\text{fb}_{t+1,j}(A_t) - \hat{z}_{E_{i^*},j})^2$. This ensures that action-sensitive latent dimensions contribute more strongly to denoising guidance.

#### 3.4. Integration with Diffusion Policy

The guidance energy gradient $\nabla_A E^\text{ctrl}$ is integrated into the diffusion policy's denoising process via score guidance: $\tilde{s} = s_\phi - \lambda \nabla_A E^\text{ctrl}$. Guidance is applied only during the final low-noise denoising steps (e.g., $\tau \leq \tau_g$), where actions have more meaningful structure.

#### 3.5. World Model Architecture (Appendix F)

The world model is a lightweight, task-specific latent dynamics model. It predicts the next latent state from the current observation latent, proprioceptive state, and executed action chunk. It employs a visual encoder (shared with the diffusion policy), a small proprioception encoder, an action encoder, and a Transformer predictor. It is trained with a one-step latent prediction objective and does not reconstruct pixels or generate videos. This design aims for efficiency and data economy, being trained per task from expert demonstrations only, without large-scale pretraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted to evaluate two primary questions: the effectiveness of the feedback world model in reducing prediction error in OOD settings, and the combined benefit of feedback and action-aware guidance on diffusion policy success rates under distribution shift.

#### 4.1. Feedback World Model Improves Online Prediction

- **Reduced Latent Prediction Error:** The feedback world model consistently reduced latent prediction Mean Squared Error (MSE) under robot initial-state OOD shifts in both simulated (Robomimic, LIBERO-Plus) and real-world tasks (Peach P&P, Drawer-Open). For instance, it achieved a 73.3% reduction on Robomimic Square and 65.4%/76.4% on the real-world Peach P&P and Drawer-Open tasks, respectively, compared to the base world model. This reduction occurred without additional training data or parameter updates.
- **Improved Trajectory Alignment:** Visualizations of latent trajectories in PCA space (Fig. 5) demonstrated that while the base world model drifted from the ground truth during an episode, the feedback-corrected trajectory remained closely aligned and recovered quickly from initial discrepancies. This indicates that the feedback mechanism maintains the world model on the correct latent manifold during execution.
- **Limited Impact of Rollout-Enhanced Training:** Rollout-enhanced world models, trained with additional policy rollout data, showed limited and inconsistent gains, particularly when test-time OOD states significantly differed from the collected rollouts. This highlights the advantage of online feedback over potentially costly and insufficient offline data augmentation.

#### 4.2. Action-Aware Guidance Improves Controllable Policy Guidance

- **Latent Space Anisotropy:** Analysis of action controllability weights (Fig. 6) revealed a long-tailed distribution, where a small percentage (e.g., 5%) of latent dimensions contributed a disproportionately large share (20–25%) of the total controllability weight. This indicated strong anisotropy in the latent representation, with some dimensions being significantly more responsive to actions than others.
- **Enhanced Policy Success:** Ablation studies on Robomimic tasks (Table 2) showed that adding action-aware weighting to the feedback world model further improved policy success rates compared to using the feedback world model alone. This suggests that accurate predictions are more effectively leveraged when guidance focuses on action-controllable and task-relevant latent dimensions, down-weighting action-irrelevant visual components.

#### 4.3. Combined Guidance Improves OOD Performance Across Benchmarks

- **Superior OOD Success Rates:** The combined approach, integrating both the feedback world model and action-aware guidance, achieved the highest average success rate across simulated OOD tasks (52%), representing a 30% relative improvement over the base policy (40%). It also outperformed standard guidance (43%) and rollout-enhanced guidance (45%).
- **Robustness in Real-World Tasks:** In real-world manipulation tasks (Peach P&P, Drawer-Open), the method demonstrated substantial gains under OOD initial states. Success rates improved from 40% to 80% on Peach P&P and from 20% to 70% on Drawer-Open. Even in-distribution performance saw a slight improvement (e.g., 90% to 95% on Peach P&P).
- **Limitations of Rollout-Based Baselines:** Rollout-based augmentation strategies (Mixed BC, Filtered BC) showed less stable performance. Mixed BC often degraded performance, and rollout-enhanced guidance provided only moderate improvements, suggesting that simply collecting more offline data does not consistently resolve OOD generalization issues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel paradigm for enhancing the reliability of world models and the robustness of robotic policies, particularly diffusion policies, during deployment. The core significance lies in its proposal of a feedback world model that actively uses real-time observations to correct its internal predictive state online. This approach offers several potential impacts:

- **Improved Robustness to Distribution Shift:** By mitigating prediction drift under OOD conditions without requiring additional training data or model retraining, the method significantly enhances the robustness of robotic policies. This is crucial for real-world deployment where perfect alignment with training distributions is rarely achievable.
- **Data and Computation Efficiency:** The feedback mechanism is lightweight, operating online with minimal overhead and no parameter updates to the world model. This contrasts with data-scaling or model-scaling approaches that demand substantial computational resources and data collection efforts, making the proposed method more practical for data-limited robotic settings.
- **Enhanced Policy Guidance:** The introduction of action-aware guidance provides a more refined way to steer diffusion policies. By focusing guidance on latent dimensions that are demonstrably controllable by robot actions, it ensures that the policy optimizes for task-relevant state changes, potentially leading to more efficient learning and execution.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The convergence guarantees provided for the feedback mechanism offer a theoretical basis for its reliability, indicating that the prediction errors remain bounded and the corrected predictions become asymptotically unbiased.
- **Generalizability:** The concepts of online prediction correction and action-aware guidance could potentially be extended to other forms of predictive models and policy learning paradigms, contributing to the broader field of robust control and learning in robotics.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that, similar to other inference-time guidance methods for diffusion policies, their approach introduces higher inference latency compared to an unguided base diffusion policy. This is primarily due to the iterative nature of guided denoising. Future work could explore applying these ideas to more efficient guidance frameworks or investigating their applicability in latency-sensitive robotic tasks. Nonetheless, the core contribution of real-time model correction and refined guidance remains valuable.
