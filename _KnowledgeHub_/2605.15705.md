# Feedback World Model Enables Precise Guidance of Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MARS Lab, Nanyang Technological University, developed a Feedback World Model that dynamically corrects its predictions using real-time observations and an Action-Aware Guidance strategy to steer diffusion policies in robotics. This approach enhances policy robustness and generalization in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments, reducing latent prediction error by up to 76.4% on real-world tasks and improving success rates to 80% on Peach P&P under OOD conditions.

## Problem

- World models often produce unreliable predictions when robots encounter out-of-distribution (OOD) states, hindering effective policy guidance.
- Current world models operate as static, open-loop predictors during inference, failing to incorporate real-time feedback to correct internal prediction drift and accumulating errors.
- Standard diffusion policy guidance compares predicted future latent states to expert states uniformly, potentially diluting the signal by focusing on both action-controllable and action-irrelevant latent dimensions.

## Method

- Introduces a **Feedback World Model** that uses real-time observations to ==online correct== its internal latent state predictions, mitigating prediction drift without retraining model parameters.
- Proposes an ==Action-Aware Guidance== strategy that estimates the controllability of each latent dimension and weights the guidance energy to prioritize action-responsive and task-relevant latent components.
- Integrates this feedback-corrected, action-aware guidance into the ==diffusion policy's denoising process==, leveraging the guidance energy gradient to steer action generation towards expert-like outcomes.

## Results

- The feedback world model reduced latent prediction Mean Squared Error (MSE) by up to **76.4%** on real-world tasks (Drawer-Open) and **73.3%** on simulated tasks (Robomimic Square) compared to the base world model.
- The combined feedback and action-aware guidance achieved the highest average success rate across simulated OOD tasks (**52%**), a **30%** relative improvement over the base policy (**40%**).
- In real-world OOD scenarios, success rates improved from **40%** to **80%** on Peach P&P and from **20%** to **70%** on Drawer-Open under initial-state distribution shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online correction of world model predictions using real-time feedback is highly effective at maintaining prediction accuracy under OOD conditions, outperforming costly offline data augmentation.
> - Latent representations exhibit significant anisotropy, where a small percentage of dimensions are highly responsive to actions, highlighting the benefit of prioritizing these for policy guidance.
> - The combination of dynamically corrected world model predictions and targeted, action-aware guidance leads to a substantial improvement in robotic policy robustness and generalization in novel environments.
