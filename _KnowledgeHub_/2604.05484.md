# CoEnv: Driving Embodied Multi-Agent Collaboration via Compositional Environment

> [!summary] Summary
> The CoEnv framework enables multiple heterogeneous robots to collaboratively execute complex manipulation tasks by integrating real-world observations with a physics simulator into a compositional decision-making environment. It leverages VLM-driven action synthesis and collision-aware sim-to-real transfer to achieve an overall 49% success rate across five challenging real-world multi-agent benchmarks.

## Problem

- Complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks often exceed the capabilities of single-agent robotic systems.
- Existing multi-agent systems struggle with fine-grained spatial coordination, collision avoidance, and temporal reasoning, often relying on isolated viewpoints or high-level textual planning.
- Traditional robot learning through real-world experimentation is prone to damage and resource-intensive, while current simulation methods often lack robust multi-agent dynamics and effective sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- **CoEnv** introduces a ==compositional environment== that unifies real-world scene reconstruction with a physics simulator, creating a shared decision-making space for multi-agent collaboration.
- Employs a ==VLM-based hierarchical planner== to decompose tasks and synthesize actions, utilizing both ==interactive== (closed-loop, adaptive) and ==iterative== (code-based, full-trajectory) execution modes.
- A ==collision-aware sim-to-real transfer== mechanism verifies swept collision volumes for interpolated robot trajectories before real-world execution, enhancing safety and reliability.

## Results

- **CoEnv** achieved an overall task success rate of **49%** across five real-world multi-agent manipulation tasks involving up to three heterogeneous robots, with the "Cube Stacking" task reaching **75%** success.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that adaptive camera control and checkpoint verification are critical, with their removal reducing the average success rate from **50%** to **30%** and **20%** respectively.
- The framework provides a scalable pipeline for generating high-quality multi-agent manipulation data, with the iterative mode exhibiting significantly higher throughput (e.g., **17.5** episodes/session for "Cube Stacking") compared to the interactive mode.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a high-fidelity simulator with real-world perception offers a safe and effective unified decision-making space for complex multi-agent coordination.
> - VLM-driven planning, combined with interactive and iterative execution modes, provides complementary strengths for generating precise and adaptable multi-agent actions.
> - Adaptive camera control for enhanced observability and checkpoint verification for early error detection are crucial for robust performance in long-horizon multi-agent tasks, especially under occlusion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) has seen substantial advancements driven by foundation models, particularly multimodal large language models (VLMs) and vision-language-action (VLA) architectures. While these developments have enabled notable progress in single-agent robotic systems, the execution of complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks frequently necessitates the coordinated efforts of multiple embodied agents. These multi-agent systems leverage complementary capabilities to achieve task completion more efficiently and robustly than individual agents alone.

However, equipping multi-agent systems with generalist robot policies introduces challenges. Existing research in embodied multi-agent systems has often focused on task allocation and high-level decision-making in controlled environments, sometimes relying on textual representations that limit fine-grained spatial reasoning and collision avoidance. While some approaches integrate VLMs for visual feedback, they typically process individual agent viewpoints in isolation or assume homogeneous robot capabilities. Frameworks exploring heterogeneous robots often limit their scope to high-level planning, omitting the execution of low-level control strategies.

Simulation platforms are critical for robotic learning, offering safe exploration of control strategies without hardware damage. Traditional methods often train policies exclusively in virtual settings and use techniques like domain randomization or system identification to bridge the sim-to-real gap. Recent advancements in scene reconstruction have enabled high-fidelity digital replicas of real-world environments, facilitating zero-shot transfer and offline policy verification. However, extending these reconstruction and simulation techniques to multi-agent systems introduces complexities related to intricate workspace dynamics, spatial conflicts, and coordination challenges.

This work positions itself to address these persistent challenges by proposing a unified decision-making space. It aims to integrate real-world observations with a simulated environment to enable collaborative control and precise spatial coordination among multiple embodied agents. Unlike some prior methods that generate policies for isolated execution, this research leverages the reasoning capabilities of VLMs to synthesize and refine multi-agent actions collaboratively, thereby extending existing simulation-based learning and VLM-driven robotic manipulation to solve complex multi-agent coordination problems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable multiple robotic agents to perform complex collaborative manipulation tasks by addressing critical challenges in spatial coordination, temporal reasoning, and shared workspace awareness. The work is inspired by human collaboration, where cognitive planning and physical execution occur separately, allowing for efficient strategy exploration and verification.

The key motivations driving this research include:
- **Addressing limitations of single-agent systems:** While single-agent embodied AI has progressed, complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks often exceed the capabilities of individual robots, necessitating multi-agent collaboration.
- **Overcoming multi-agent coordination challenges:** Equipping multi-agent systems with generalist policies introduces significant complexity, particularly in coordinating actions to avoid spatial conflicts, reasoning about temporal dependencies in sub-task execution, and maintaining awareness of shared workspace dynamics.
- **Mitigating risks and costs of real-world experimentation:** Physical execution in the real world entails risks of costly collisions and resource-intensive testing. The motivation is to leverage simulation environments for safe, cost-effective, and reproducible exploration of strategies.
- **Developing a unified decision-making space:** The authors propose that planning, coordination, and collision reasoning can occur in a shared virtual space, allowing agents to efficiently explore, verify, and iterate on solutions without physical risks. This leads to the concept of a "compositional environment," which synergistically integrates real-world and simulation components.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives for the CoEnv framework are:
- To **leverage simulation** for safe strategy exploration and validation in multi-agent collaboration.
- To **ensure reliable real-world deployment** of the developed collaborative strategies.
- To implement a **real-to-sim scene reconstruction** process that accurately digitizes physical workspaces.
- To develop **VLM-driven action synthesis** methods that support both real-time planning with high-level interfaces and iterative planning with code-based trajectory generation.
- To integrate **validated sim-to-real transfer** mechanisms, including collision detection, for safe deployment of multi-agent plans.
- To **demonstrate the effectiveness** of the framework on challenging multi-arm manipulation benchmarks requiring tight coordination.
- To establish a **methodology for generating high-quality training data** for multi-agent embodied systems, addressing a bottleneck in scaling robot learning in collaborative settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoEnv operates through a three-stage methodology designed to bridge real-world observations with deployable decision-making for multi-agent embodied systems.

#### 3.1. Real-to-Sim Scene Reconstruction

This stage focuses on converting multi-view RGBD observations from the real world into a spatially consistent simulator-ready representation.
- **3D Asset Generation:** Task-specific objects are reconstructed using Meshy Model, a text/image-to-3D generation platform. These generated 3D mesh assets are standardized to match real-world scales, and their physical properties (e.g., mass, friction, collision geometry) are pre-defined to ensure physics consistency in the simulator.
- **Multi-View Object Localization:** For each camera view, Grounded SAM2 detects and segments target objects. GPT-5 disambiguates visually similar objects using contextual cues. FoundationPose then estimates the 6-DoF pose (translation and rotation) of each detected object. To reduce localization error, estimated poses from multiple views are fused, with translation averaged arithmetically and rotation computed via quaternion averaging.
- **Simulation Environment:** The reconstruction pipeline is implemented within ManiSkill, which is built on the SAPIEN physics engine. The simulator's resettable nature facilitates iterative refinement of camera extrinsic calibration by comparing rendered views with real-world captures, thereby enhancing the spatial fidelity of the reconstructed scene.

#### 3.2. Simulation-Conditioned Action Synthesis

This stage generates actions for multiple agents within the simulator, ensuring feasibility and adherence to task constraints.
- **Stage I: Hierarchical Task Planning:** A VLM-based planner (GPT-5) is employed to decompose complex multi-agent manipulation tasks. It takes the task goal and current scene observation to produce a two-level plan: high-level semantic sub-goals (e.g., "pick up the red cube") and corresponding execution plans. Each execution plan specifies the assigned agent, an action primitive (Move, Grasp, Place, Rotate), and target parameters. An adaptive camera control tool allows the VLM to dynamically adjust the simulation viewpoint, rendering observations from multiple camera poses and aggregating visual evidence to reason about occluded regions, thus improving coordination strategies.
- **Stage II: Grounded Execution:** This stage grounds the execution plan into simulator actions using two complementary modes:
 - **Interactive Mode:** Agents execute each plan element sequentially through a closed-loop cycle: execute → observe → verify → adapt. A verification function evaluates the outcome (Success/Fail). Upon failure, the VLM analyzes feedback (e.g., collisions, pose errors) and either re-parameterizes the current element or inserts corrective actions. Checkpoint elements are introduced as non-action verification steps at critical junctures, where a predicate over the current state must be satisfied before execution proceeds.
 - **Iterative Mode:** This mode abstracts the action primitive library into a code interface, using a code agent (Claude Code) to generate a complete Python program for all agents in a single pass. The program is executed in simulation to produce a trajectory. The VLM evaluates the execution and, if unsatisfactory, generates structured feedback (e.g., collision locations, unachieved sub-goals) to refine the code agent in subsequent iterations. This process continues until success or a maximum iteration limit is reached.
- **Validation and Data Collection:** Successfully validated trajectories from either mode are stored in a knowledge base, serving as in-context demonstrations for future planning and as high-quality training data for multi-agent systems.

#### 3.3. Sim-to-Real Transfer with Collision-Aware Execution

This final stage transfers synthesized actions to real-world robots, addressing the sim-to-real gap and ensuring safety.
- **Trajectory Interpolation:** Joint configurations of each agent are recorded at the completion of every primitive action. To generate smooth real-world trajectories, linear interpolation is applied between consecutive recorded configurations, yielding a dense waypoint sequence for the real-world controller.
- **Collision Volume Verification:** Before executing each interpolated action on real robots, a forward kinematics pass computes the swept collision volume for each agent's links along the interpolated path. An action is considered safe only if the collision volumes of all agent pairs remain disjoint. If a violation is detected, the action is discarded, and re-planning is triggered from the current state, preventing physical collisions and allowing for efficient multi-agent operation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated CoEnv on five real-world multi-agent manipulation tasks involving up to three heterogeneous robots.

- **Overall Performance:** CoEnv achieved an overall task success rate of 49% across all tasks, with the interactive mode yielding 50% and the iterative mode 48%. The "Cube Stacking" task recorded the highest success rate at 75% overall, while "Transfer Cylinder" was the most challenging at 25%. Qualitative comparisons demonstrated high visual correspondence between simulation planning and real-world execution.

- **Complementary Strengths of Execution Modes:**
 - The **iterative mode** demonstrated higher efficacy in tasks requiring precise trajectory control and fine-grained alignment, such as "Cube Stacking" (9/10 successful trials compared to 6/10 for interactive) and "Ball Pickup" (6/10 vs. 4/10). This is attributed to its ability to generate comprehensive, coherent programs for full action sequences.
 - The **interactive mode** performed more robustly in tasks requiring complex multi-stage coordination, real-time visual feedback, and adaptive re-planning, such as "Transfer Cylinder" (4/10 vs. 1/10 for iterative) and "Brush Box" (7/10 vs. 5/10).

- **Task Complexity and Agent Coordination:**
 - Tasks with spatially separated subtasks and largely independent arm operations, like "Cube Stacking," showed higher success rates.
 - Tasks requiring tight bimanual coordination and shared spatial constraints, such as the "Transfer Cylinder" handover, proved the most difficult due to minimal tolerance for error.
 - Among three-agent tasks, "Brush Box" achieved 60% success due to its asymmetric but largely decoupled roles. "Place Cucumber" (35%) was harder, necessitating tightly synchronized actions and prone to error propagation.

- **Failure Analysis:** Three recurring failure modes were identified:
 - **Minor sim-to-real offsets:** Small discrepancies in camera calibration or object pose estimation led to millimeter-level spatial errors, causing contact-rich primitives (e.g., grasping, insertion) to fail, particularly in tasks requiring precise bimanual convergence.
 - **Planning loop stagnation:** Both VLM planner (interactive mode) and code agent (iterative mode) occasionally became stuck in repetitive re-planning cycles, making minor adjustments without fundamentally altering failed strategies.
 - **Mode-specific limitations:** Interactive mode suffered from cumulative drift over long action sequences, while iterative mode struggled with reactive tasks requiring dynamic, closed-loop adaptation that is difficult to encode programmatically.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Adaptive Camera Control:** Removing this feature reduced the average task success rate from 50% to 30%, with tasks involving heavy inter-agent occlusion (e.g., "Transfer Cylinder," "Brush Box") experiencing the most significant degradation (dropping to 0/10). This indicates its role in providing necessary observability under occlusion.
 - **Checkpoint Verification:** Ablating this mechanism resulted in a more severe decline, reducing the average success rate from 50% to 20%. Errors introduced early in the plan propagated without detection, leading to compounded failures in long-horizon tasks.
 - The combination of adaptive camera control and checkpoint verification yielded significant improvements, underscoring their indispensability for robust multi-agent performance.

- **Scalable Data Collection:** CoEnv was demonstrated as a scalable pipeline for generating multi-agent manipulation data. The iterative mode showed significantly higher throughput (17.5 and 9.5 episodes per session for "Cube Stacking" and "Brush Box," respectively) compared to the interactive mode (1.5 and 2.5). Simulation-grounded environment resets consumed a relatively low proportion of the total token budget, suggesting efficient resource utilization for productive task reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces the concept of a "compositional environment," which signifies a departure from traditional approaches by unifying real-world perception with physics simulation into a cohesive decision-making space for multi-agent embodied collaboration. This paradigm proposes simulation not merely as a training ground but as an active cognitive medium where heterogeneous robots can collectively plan, verify, and refine their collaborative strategies prior to physical execution.

The significance of CoEnv and its potential impact are multifaceted:
- **Advancement in Multi-Agent Embodied AI:** CoEnv provides a framework that explicitly addresses critical challenges in multi-agent spatial coordination, temporal reasoning, and shared workspace awareness, which are fundamental for complex robotic applications.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Safety:** The integrated mechanisms, including multi-view scene reconstruction, adaptive camera control, VLM-driven hierarchical planning, two complementary execution modes (interactive and iterative), and collision volume verification during sim-to-real transfer, contribute to a more robust and safer system for multi-agent robotic manipulation. This is crucial for deploying robots in environments where errors can be costly or dangerous.
- **Scalable Data Generation:** The framework offers a practical and scalable methodology for generating high-quality multi-agent manipulation data. This addresses a significant bottleneck in the development of generalist multi-agent policies, potentially reducing the reliance on laborious and expensive manual teleoperation and accelerating progress in robot learning.
- **Complementary Planning Paradigms:** The demonstration of the complementary strengths of interactive (adaptive, real-time) and iterative (precise, coherent) planning modes suggests that a hybrid approach could be beneficial for addressing diverse task requirements in complex multi-agent scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** CoEnv opens avenues for future work, including efforts to further close residual sim-to-real gaps through online adaptation, extend capabilities to deformable and articulated objects, and distill the collected demonstrations into more generalizable, end-to-end multi-agent policies. These directions could further enhance the autonomy and adaptability of multi-robot systems across a broader range of real-world tasks.
