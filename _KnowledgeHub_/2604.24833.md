# MotionBricks: Scalable Real-Time Motions with Modular Latent Generative Model and Smart Primitives

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NVIDIA developed MotionBricks, a framework for real-time interactive motion control combining a modular latent generative model with "smart primitives" for intuitive, fine-grained control. It achieves 15,000 FPS with 2ms latency and reports improved motion quality (MMD 0.1056, FID 1.054 on a 350k dataset) and high user satisfaction in animation and robotics applications.

## Problem

- Existing character animation and robotics methods, like animation graphs, face scalability issues and become labor-intensive for large motion repertoires.
- Data-driven generative motion models often trade off quality, speed, and precise control, making them impractical for real-time interactive applications.
- A lack of intuitive, fine-grained control interfaces hinders the integration of generative motion synthesis into production pipelines.

## Method

- A four-stage, ==autoregressive pipeline== featuring a ==multi-headed tokenizer==, a neural backbone with **Root and Pose Modules**, and a decoder.
- **Smart Primitives** provide a unified, ==keyframe-based interface== for high-level behavior design, supporting both controllable locomotion and object interaction.
- The system employs flexible ==in-betweening constraint handling== and a ==disentangled root-pose architecture== to manage diverse motion specifications and ensure robustness.

## Results

- Achieved real-time performance of **15,000 FPS** with **2ms latency** on an RTX 5090, significantly faster than existing generative methods.
- Demonstrated superior motion quality with the lowest **MMD 0.1056** and **FID 1.054** on a 350k motion dataset, along with high human evaluation scores (**86.5% win rate**).
- Exhibited high physical plausibility (low foot skate, penetration) and precision in constraint adherence, successfully deploying for interactive animation in **Unreal Engine 5** and whole-body control on a **Unitree G1** robot with zero-shot generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-head tokenization with separate feature dimensions allows effective scaling to large motion datasets and complex movement representations.
> - Disentangled processing of root trajectory and body pose enhances the model's robustness and control flexibility, supporting diverse motion types and input variations.
> - A unified keyframe interface, implemented through "Smart Primitives," enables zero-shot generalization, simplifying high-level behavior design and reducing authoring burden.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of character animation and robotics has seen advancements in generative motion synthesis, yet real-time interactive motion control in production environments largely relies on traditional techniques. Traditional approaches, such as animation or behavior graphs, facilitate hierarchical state machines and granular control but encounter severe scalability limitations. For instance, complex animation systems in modern video games can involve managing thousands of animations and states, leading to maintenance challenges and restricted accessibility to large, well-resourced studios.

Recent developments in data-driven neural networks, particularly generative models, have enabled breakthroughs in motion synthesis. Two main formulations for interactive runtime motion control have been explored:
1. **Models receiving direct control commands**: These include non-parametric methods like motion matching and deterministic neural networks (e.g., Phase-Functioned Neural Networks). While providing some control, they often require manual tagging or attribute preparation during training, limit scalability due to predefined control types, and can suffer from artifacts such as foot sliding or object penetration.
2. **Text-to-motion models**: These use text prompts for control but generally lack the fine-grained precision needed for industrial applications and often face trade-offs between scalability, quality, and speed.

Diffusion models have demonstrated high quality in motion generation, but their inference speed typically renders them impractical for real-time applications, often requiring seconds or minutes per clip. Token-based generative models aim to address this by discretizing continuous motion into tokens and using transformers for prediction. While showing promise, these methods also contend with quality-speed trade-offs and have not consistently achieved industry-grade interactivity or scalability.

MotionBricks positions itself within this landscape by addressing two identified challenges: real-time scalability and integration into production pipelines. It aims to bridge the gap between generative model research and practical application by developing a framework that combines a scalable, real-time generative backbone with an intuitive, fine-grained control interface. This work seeks to unify low-level motion synthesis with high-level behavior design, applicable to both virtual character animation and physical robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a framework, named MotionBricks, that enables scalable, real-time interactive motion control for both animation and robotics applications, overcoming limitations of existing methods. The motivation stems from two primary challenges identified in bridging generative motion synthesis research with production environments:

1. **Real-time Scalability**: Industry applications require the real-time generation of a vast repertoire of motion skills. Current generative methods often experience significant degradation in quality and scalability when subjected to real-time computational constraints. Traditional animation graphs, while prevalent, become labor-intensive and difficult to maintain as the number of motion states and transitions increases, limiting their adoption to large studios. This underscores a need for more efficient approaches that can manage large motion datasets and generate diverse actions without compromising performance.
2. **Integration and Fine-Grained Control**: Industry applications necessitate fine-grained, multi-modal control, including precise velocity commands, style selection, and exact keyframe specifications. Existing text- or tag-driven generative models typically fall short in providing this level of control. Furthermore, a systematic and intuitive interface for designing and authoring motions with generative models has been largely absent, hindering their practical adoption.

To address these challenges, MotionBricks proposes a two-fold solution:
- **A large-scale modular latent generative backbone**: This component is designed for robust real-time motion generation, capable of modeling a substantial dataset of over 350,000 motion clips with a single, unified model. The modularity aims to enhance model capacity, spatial precision, and overall robustness under runtime conditions.
- **Smart primitives**: These introduce a unified, robust, and intuitive interface for authoring both navigation and object interaction. The design objective for smart primitives is to enable application development in a "plug-and-play" manner, reducing the need for expert animation knowledge and allowing for zero-shot generalization to new tasks without fine-tuning or task-specific tagging.

Ultimately, the motivation is to make high-quality, runtime interactive behavior systems more accessible to a broader community by simplifying the design process, improving scalability, and providing reliable, fine-grained control for complex animation and robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionBricks operates as a four-stage pipeline for real-time motion control: Smart Primitives, Tokenizer, Neural Backbone (Root Module and Pose Module), and Decoder. This framework is designed to function autoregressively, with replanning triggered by changes in control signals or insufficient future motion in the buffer.

#### 3.1. Flexible In-betweening Constraint Handling

The system adopts an in-betweening formulation, where context keyframes represent the character’s recent states, and target keyframes define future goals from smart primitives. Constraints are categorized for local root, global root, and pose, collectively denoted as T = {T_1, T_2, T_3}. The system accommodates variable constraint densities by replacing unprovided constraints with learnable mask embeddings, enabling graceful handling of partial specifications across all modules. This allows for flexible control, from sparse inputs for navigation to dense specifications for object interaction.

#### 3.2. Structured Multi-headed Tokenizer

The core of the generative neural backbone is a structured conditional multi-head tokenizer, designed to map raw motion data into compact, abstract, discrete latent representations.
- **State Representation**: Motion segments of length T (randomly sampled from 12 to 64 frames at 30 FPS) are represented by a tuple (r_g, r_l, p, q, v, c), comprising global root values, local root values, joint positions, joint rotations, joint velocities, and contact labels. Joint rotations and positions are expressed in global coordinates without heading canonicalization to support diverse motions.
- **Encoder**: The encoder processes local pose states (p, q) separately from root information, progressively down-sampling features at rates of 2 and 4 using 1D convolutional networks or transformers. This produces a continuous latent embedding, z_t_e. This root-pose disentanglement is inspired by traditional animation techniques.
- **Quantizer**: The continuous latent embedding z_t_e is quantized into a discrete latent embedding z_t_q. This is performed separately along the feature dimension using K discrete codebooks, forming a multi-head tokenization. This approach encourages the network to learn latent decomposition of human movement in a data-driven manner, rather than relying on manual partitioning.
- **Decoder**: The decoder up-samples the discrete pose tokens z_t_q progressively. It integrates root trajectory conditions and sparse keyframe constraints (joint positions {p̌} and rotations {q̌}) at each up-sampling layer via skip connections, promoting coherent full-body motion. During training, a random number of keyframe constraints are sampled at arbitrary temporal positions. The decoder can also refine the predicted root trajectory. The tokenizer is trained with a standard VQ-VAE quantizer loss, utilizing a running-mean codebook update for stability.

#### 3.3. Neural Backbone

The neural backbone comprises two modules for coarse-to-fine motion generation:
- **Root Module**: This module takes keyframe constraints as input and operates in two steps:
 1. **Hidden State Initialization and Timing Prediction**: A transformer encoder predicts a distribution over in-between frame counts (T_2), at a 4-frame resolution. Inputs include learnable frame-slot embeddings, a global embedding encoding keyframe constraints (f), and a timing embedding (g).
 2. **Conditional Root Trajectory Estimation**: Intermediate frame-slot embeddings are processed by a temporal transformer to predict an initial global root trajectory ({r_g}), conditioned on keyframe and timing constraints. The output global root values are converted to local root values for subsequent modules.
- **Pose Module**: This is a transformer-based module that models the distribution of pose tokens. It conditions on the root trajectories predicted by the root module and specified keyframe constraints. Its input consists of frame-slot embeddings encoding local/global root values, local pose keyframe constraints (p, q), and pose token embeddings (a mix of ground truth and masked tokens during training). It employs a masked token modeling strategy to iteratively predict pose tokens based on confidence.

#### 3.4. Smart Primitives

Smart primitives provide a high-level behavior system that leverages the neural backbone for zero-shot generalization. They communicate with the backbone through a unified keyframe interface, eliminating the need for task-specific supervision during training.
- **Smart Locomotion**: Addresses controllable navigation through:
 - **Critically Damped Spring Model**: Generates an initial estimate of the root trajectory (r_g,1 to r_g,2) by extrapolating current states with user-commanded velocity/heading and smoothing with a damped spring.
 - **Style Control via Keyframe Selection**: Locomotion style is controlled by placing keyframes, sampled from reference clips or authored poses, onto the spring-smoothed trajectory. The generative backbone handles natural motion generation and smooth style transitions, aided by a replanning mechanism.
 - **Neural Root Refinement**: The spring-smoothed root (r_g,2) is fed to the root module (producing r_g,3) and then to the decoder (producing r_g,4) for iterative refinement. This process incorporates natural movement details and safeguards against control dead zones. The decoder's disentangled handling of root and pose enables robust adaptation.
- **Smart Object**: Facilitates interaction with static scenes and dynamic objects by defining:
 - **Intent Keyframes**: Essential poses for an interaction behavior (e.g., contact, exit poses). Keyframes can be sourced from libraries or authored manually. Each keyframe set includes a `drop-frame` attribute (τ) to control strictness (hard constraint vs. soft guidance) and a boolean flag (ω) for rotation around an interaction pivot.
 - **Interaction Binding**: Manages interaction detection (via box collision tracing), object placement (using sockets and ray casting), and keyframe anchoring (using the object's world transform as a pivot). This allows characters to interact with objects from various angles and positions using identical keyframe definitions.

The system is deployed in Unreal Engine 5 using ONNX and TensorRT for models, and on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot via a physical tracking controller and retargeted motion data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the capabilities of MotionBricks across various benchmarks and applications.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

MotionBricks was evaluated against state-of-the-art methods, including deterministic transformers (Cond. In-betweening, Two-stage Trans., Delta-interpolator) and generative models (CondMDI, MMM, Closd-DiP), on four datasets: a proprietary 350k dataset (700 hours), a 70k subset, HumanML3D, and LaFAN1-G1.
- **Speed**: MotionBricks achieved a real-time throughput of 15,000 FPS with 2ms latency on an RTX 5090 GPU, significantly outperforming other generative methods that typically operate at hundreds to thousands of FPS and higher latencies.
- **Distribution Quality**: MotionBricks recorded the lowest Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores on the 350k dataset (MMD: 0.1056, FID: 1.054), indicating that its generated motion distribution closely matches the ground truth. This trend was consistent across all evaluated datasets.
- **Human Evaluation**: In a user study, MotionBricks received the highest win rate (86.5%) and overall quality score (4.06 out of 5) among all compared methods on the 350k dataset.
- **Smoothness**: The method exhibited low Joint Jitter (3.38 m/s²) and Root Jitter (1.82 m/s²), suggesting smooth and natural motion generation.
- **Physical Plausibility**: MotionBricks achieved low Foot Skate (0.003 m/frame), low Penetration (0.008 m), and high Foot Contact Accuracy (92.6%), indicating physically realistic interactions with the ground.
- **Precision**: The system demonstrated high precision in adhering to constraints, with low Context Keyframe (0.052 m), Target Keyframe (0.076 m), Context Root (0.001 m), and Target Root errors (0.023 m), along with a high Reaching Success rate (99.6%).
- **Diversity**: MotionBricks showed competitive or superior diversity metrics compared to baselines, particularly on larger datasets, without a notable trade-off with speed or precision.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Tokenizer Scalability**: Comparison between the proposed multi-head tokenizer and a single-head VQ-VAE baseline showed that MotionBricks’ tokenizer improved performance consistently with increasing codebook size (up to 10^9 tokens), whereas the baseline quickly plateaued. This indicated the multi-head design's effectiveness in utilizing increased model capacity for better FID and controlled keyframe error.
- **Multi-head Tokenization Configuration**: An analysis with fixed total codebook capacity revealed a trade-off: too many tokens per head degraded temporal coherence (NPSS), while too few negatively impacted FID and keyframe precision. Optimal performance was suggested with 128 to 256 tokens per head.
- **Dataset Size Scaling**: Performance of MotionBricks improved as the dataset size increased (from 10% to 100% of the 350k dataset), showing effective scaling. The baseline method struggled on larger datasets due to limited capacity.
- **Root Interpolation Robustness**: The decoder maintained FID quality and prevented foot skating artifacts even when root trajectories were interpolated (overridden), demonstrating its robustness due to disentangled root and pose handling. Providing pose keyframe constraints further improved FID and target reaching success.

#### 4.3. Interactive Animation, Robotics Applications, and Engineering Details

- **Unreal Engine 5 Demo**: MotionBricks was integrated into a UE5 demo, showcasing diverse locomotion styles, acrobatics, and object-scene interactions with a unified model. It utilized a custom "task actor" system for smart locomotion and smart object primitives, leveraging UE5's plugin system, ONNX, and TensorRT for real-time inference and built-in retargeting for character adaptation.
- **Unitree G1 Robot Deployment**: The framework was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for real-world whole-body control. This involved retargeting the proprietary dataset to the G1 skeleton and using a physical tracking controller. Inference latency on the Jetson Orin platform for the robot was 5ms.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization**: All applications and demonstrations were achieved with a fixed, pre-trained neural backbone, requiring no fine-tuning or additional task-specific tagging, validating the framework's zero-shot generalization capabilities.

#### 4.4. Visual Comparisons

Visual comparisons across various in-betweening test cases consistently showed MotionBricks generating physically plausible, natural, and keyframe-adherent motions, unlike baseline methods that exhibited artifacts such as implausible poses or poor constraint adherence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MotionBricks represents a step in bridging the gap between advanced generative motion synthesis research and its practical application in real-time interactive systems for animation and robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Real-Time Scalability for Production Environments**: The framework demonstrates the capability to generate a vast repertoire of high-quality motion skills in real-time (15,000 FPS, 2ms latency), a crucial factor for game development, virtual reality, and robotics. This addresses a major limitation of many existing generative models, which often sacrifice speed for quality, making them unsuitable for interactive runtime applications.
- **Enhanced Accessibility and Reduced Authoring Burden**: By providing a modular latent generative backbone and intuitive "smart primitives," MotionBricks aims to simplify the creation of complex runtime behaviors. This approach reduces the labor-intensiveness and expertise traditionally required for designing animation graphs, potentially making high-quality character control more accessible to a broader range of developers, including smaller studios and individual creators.
- **Fine-Grained and Unified Control Interface**: The smart primitives (smart locomotion and smart object) offer a unified, robust, and intuitive interface that allows for fine-grained, multi-modal control over motion, including velocity commands, style selection, and precise keyframe specifications. This level of control is essential for industrial-grade applications and is often lacking in text- or tag-driven generative models.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization**: A notable impact of MotionBricks is its ability to generalize to diverse downstream tasks in a zero-shot manner, meaning no fine-tuning or task-specific tagging is required. This flexibility simplifies integration into existing pipelines and allows for broader applicability across various scenarios and content types.
- **Cross-Domain Application**: The successful deployment of MotionBricks in both a production-level animation demo (Unreal Engine 5) and on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1) highlights its potential for a unified approach to motion synthesis across virtual characters and physical robots. This could foster advancements in robot locomotion and interaction by leveraging rich animation datasets and techniques.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: MotionBricks consistently surpasses existing state-of-the-art methods across a comprehensive set of quantitative metrics, including distribution quality, smoothness, physical plausibility, and precision, alongside high human evaluation scores. This establishes a new performance benchmark for real-time generative motion.
- **Robustness to Imperfect Inputs**: The modular architecture, particularly the disentangled handling of root and pose in the decoder, demonstrates robustness to variations and imperfections in input, such as interpolated root trajectories. This property is valuable for practical applications where control signals may be dynamic or approximate.

In summary, MotionBricks presents a scalable and practical solution for real-time motion control, addressing key challenges in bridging research with production. Its architectural design, performance metrics, and successful cross-domain demonstrations suggest a substantial impact on the future development of interactive animation and robotics.
