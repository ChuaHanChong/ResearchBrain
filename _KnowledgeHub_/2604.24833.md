# MotionBricks: Scalable Real-Time Motions with Modular Latent Generative Model and Smart Primitives

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NVIDIA developed MotionBricks, a framework for real-time interactive motion control combining a modular latent generative model with "smart primitives" for intuitive, fine-grained control. It achieves 15,000 FPS with 2ms latency and reports improved motion quality (MMD 0.1056, FID 1.054 on a 350k dataset) and high user satisfaction in animation and robotics applications.

## Problem

- Existing character animation and robotics methods, like animation graphs, face scalability issues and become labor-intensive for large motion repertoires.
- Data-driven generative motion models often trade off quality, speed, and precise control, making them impractical for real-time interactive applications.
- A lack of intuitive, fine-grained control interfaces hinders the integration of generative motion synthesis into production pipelines.

## Method

- A four-stage, ==autoregressive pipeline== featuring a ==multi-headed tokenizer==, a neural backbone with **Root and Pose Modules**, and a decoder.
- **Smart Primitives** provide a unified, ==keyframe-based interface== for high-level behavior design, supporting both controllable locomotion and object interaction.
- The system employs flexible ==in-betweening constraint handling== and a ==disentangled root-pose architecture== to manage diverse motion specifications and ensure robustness.

## Results

- Achieved real-time performance of **15,000 FPS** with **2ms latency** on an RTX 5090, significantly faster than existing generative methods.
- Demonstrated superior motion quality with the lowest **MMD 0.1056** and **FID 1.054** on a 350k motion dataset, along with high human evaluation scores (**86.5% win rate**).
- Exhibited high physical plausibility (low foot skate, penetration) and precision in constraint adherence, successfully deploying for interactive animation in **Unreal Engine 5** and whole-body control on a **Unitree G1** robot with zero-shot generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-head tokenization with separate feature dimensions allows effective scaling to large motion datasets and complex movement representations.
> - Disentangled processing of root trajectory and body pose enhances the model's robustness and control flexibility, supporting diverse motion types and input variations.
> - A unified keyframe interface, implemented through "Smart Primitives," enables zero-shot generalization, simplifying high-level behavior design and reducing authoring burden.
