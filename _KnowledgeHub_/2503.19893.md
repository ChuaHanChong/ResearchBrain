# Visuo-Tactile Object Pose Estimation for a Multi-Finger Robot Hand with Low-Resolution In-Hand Tactile Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers develop a visuo-tactile framework that combines low-resolution tactile sensing with visual pose estimation through factor graph optimization, enabling real-time object pose tracking at 13.3 Hz even under high occlusion while using simple binary tactile sensors distributed across a robot hand's interior surface.

## Problem

- Vision-based object pose estimation becomes inaccurate when objects are occluded by robot hands during manipulation
- High-resolution tactile sensors are expensive and impractical for many robotic applications
- Effectively fusing visual, tactile and proprioceptive information in real-time is challenging

## Method

- Factor graph optimization framework that combines visual pose estimates with binary tactile measurements
- Low-resolution tactile sensor pads distributed across robot hand interior
- Geometric constraints derived from hand configuration to prevent physically impossible poses

## Results

- Achieved real-time pose estimation at **13.3** Hz on a custom 15-DoF robot hand with 16 tactile sensor pads
- Significantly improved pose accuracy compared to vision-only approaches under occlusion
- Robust to varying levels of visual noise while maintaining geometric consistency

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simple binary tactile sensors are sufficient when properly integrated with visual information
> - Factor graph formulation enables effective fusion of multiple sensor modalities
> - Geometric constraints from hand configuration help ensure physically plausible pose estimates

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a significant challenge in robotics: **accurate object pose estimation in the presence of occlusion**. While computer vision has made substantial progress in object pose estimation from images, the problem becomes significantly harder when a robot hand grasps an object, partially or fully occluding it from the camera's view.

The paper bridges the gap between vision-based pose estimation and tactile sensing for robotic manipulation. It builds upon existing research in:

- **Vision-based pose estimation:** The authors leverage a state-of-the-art deep learning approach, FoundationPose, for initial visual pose estimation.
- **Tactile-based pose estimation:** The paper acknowledges prior work using tactile sensors for pose estimation, particularly those with high spatial resolution or vision-based tactile sensors like GelSight.
- **Object-pose estimation from vision and proprioception:** The research distinguishes itself from work relying solely on vision and proprioception (e.g., using physics simulation), emphasizing the importance of tactile feedback.
- **Visuo-tactile pose estimation:** By combining vision, tactile, and proprioceptive information, this research adds to the visuo-tactile pose estimation, differentiating itself by employing simple, soft sensor pads that provide only a scalar measurement per link and fuse this information in a factor graph with a state-of-the-art visual object-pose estimator.

Compared to existing approaches, this work makes a unique contribution by:

- **Using low-resolution tactile sensing:** The paper focuses on using binary, low-resolution tactile sensors distributed across the robot hand's interior surface. This is a more practical and cost-effective approach than relying on high-resolution tactile arrays or vision-based tactile sensors.
- **Factor graph optimization:** The authors formulate the visuo-tactile pose estimation problem as a factor graph, allowing them to effectively fuse visual and tactile measurements with geometric constraints.
- **Real-time performance:** The authors demonstrate real-time object pose estimation (approximately 13.3 Hz) on a real robot setup.

This research contributes to the broader field of robotic manipulation by providing a robust and efficient method for object pose estimation in challenging scenarios with occlusion, potentially enabling robots to perform more complex and dexterous tasks such as assembly or in-hand manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **To develop a visuo-tactile object pose estimation method for multi-finger robot hands.**
- **To leverage low-resolution tactile sensing to improve pose estimation accuracy in the presence of occlusion.**
- **To fuse visual, tactile, and proprioceptive information using factor graph optimization.**
- **To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach in both simulation and real-world experiments.**
- **To achieve real-time object pose estimation for practical robotic applications.**

The motivation behind this work stems from the limitations of vision-based pose estimation in robotic manipulation scenarios. When a robot hand grasps an object, occlusion significantly reduces the accuracy of visual pose estimation. The authors argue that incorporating tactile feedback can mitigate this issue by providing complementary information about the object's contact with the hand. The desire for more dexterous robot hands, capable of in-hand manipulation, drives the need for robust pose estimation even with high occlusion. Therefore, the authors seek to develop a practical and efficient approach that combines the strengths of vision and touch to enable more reliable and versatile robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a visuo-tactile object pose estimation method based on factor graph optimization. The approach involves the following key steps:

1. **Visual Pose Estimation:** An initial object pose estimate is obtained from a deep learning-based 3D object pose estimator (FoundationPose).
2. **Tactile Measurement:** Binary contact measurements are obtained from low-resolution tactile sensor pads distributed across the robot hand's interior surface.
3. **Geometric Constraints:** Geometric constraints are derived from the robot hand's joint angles (proprioception) to penalize interpenetrations between the object and the hand.
4. **Factor Graph Formulation:** The visuo-tactile pose estimation problem is formulated as a factor graph, with nodes representing the object pose, visual pose measurement, tactile measurements, and hand configuration. Factors model the probabilistic dependencies between these variables.
 - **Visual Factor:** Compares the visual pose measurement with the current object pose estimate using twist coordinates.
 - **Tactile Factor:** Measures the signed distance of the closest point on each sensor pad to the object surface based on binary contact measurements.
 - **Penetration Factor:** Penalizes interpenetrations between the object and the hand based on the signed distance function (SDF) of the object shape.
5. **Non-Linear Optimization:** The object pose is optimized by minimizing a robust cost function that incorporates visual and tactile residuals, as well as geometric constraints. The optimization is performed using the Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) solver in the Theseus library. A Welsch loss function is used to reduce the influence of outlier measurements.
6. **Implementation:** The approach is implemented and evaluated in both simulation and real-world experiments using a custom-designed four-finger, 15-DoF robot hand (ISyHand v2) equipped with 16 soft tactile sensor pads.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings of the research are:

- **Tactile sensing improves object pose estimation accuracy under high occlusion:** The simulation experiments show that the proposed visuo-tactile approach significantly outperforms purely vision-based object tracking in scenarios with high object occlusion. The inclusion of tactile measurements leads to more accurate and robust pose estimates.
- **Low-resolution tactile sensors are effective:** The use of simple, low-resolution tactile sensor pads is sufficient to improve object pose estimation accuracy. The approach does not require high-resolution tactile arrays or vision-based tactile sensors.
- **Factor graph optimization enables effective sensor fusion:** The factor graph formulation allows for effective fusion of visual and tactile measurements with geometric constraints, leading to more accurate and robust pose estimates.
- **Real-time performance is achievable:** The experiments with the real ISyHand robot hand demonstrate that the proposed approach can achieve real-time object pose estimation (approximately 13.3 Hz), making it suitable for practical robotic applications.
- **Robustness to Visual Noise:** Results show that the approach that includes tactile and penetration information improves accuracy significantly over vision-based estimation at varying levels of noise.
- **Geometric Alignment:** The visuo-tactile approach can correct for offsets in the visual pose estimate and yield a more geometrically plausible pose estimate of the object in the robot hand, improving the model's fit to the physical world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has significant implications for the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Improved Object Manipulation:** The proposed visuo-tactile pose estimation method can enable robots to perform more complex and dexterous object manipulation tasks, such as assembly, in-hand reorientation, and grasping of objects in cluttered environments.
- **Robustness to Occlusion:** The ability to accurately estimate object pose in the presence of occlusion is crucial for robots operating in real-world environments. The research provides a practical solution for addressing this challenge.
- **Cost-Effective Tactile Sensing:** The use of low-resolution tactile sensors makes the approach more accessible and cost-effective, potentially leading to wider adoption of tactile sensing in robotics.
- **Real-Time Performance:** The demonstration of real-time object pose estimation is essential for practical robotic applications, allowing robots to react to changes in the environment and adapt their actions accordingly.
- **Foundation for future research:** The factor graph formulation provides a flexible framework for incorporating other sensory modalities and constraints, potentially leading to further improvements in object pose estimation and robotic manipulation.

The potential impact of this research extends to various applications, including:

- **Industrial automation:** Robots can perform assembly and manufacturing tasks more efficiently and reliably.
- **Service robotics:** Robots can assist humans in household tasks, such as grasping and manipulating objects in the kitchen or living room.
- **Healthcare robotics:** Robots can assist surgeons in performing minimally invasive procedures or provide assistance to patients with limited mobility.
- **Space exploration:** Robots can explore and manipulate objects in unstructured and remote environments.

In summary, this research makes a valuable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by providing a robust, efficient, and practical method for visuo-tactile object pose estimation. The use of low-resolution tactile sensing, factor graph optimization, and real-time performance makes this approach highly promising for enabling more versatile and capable robots in a wide range of applications. The study successfully combines vision, touch, and proprioception, and demonstrates these capabilities in both simulated and real-world conditions.
