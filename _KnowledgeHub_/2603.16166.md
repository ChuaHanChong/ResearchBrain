# SignNav: Leveraging Signage for Semantic Visual Navigation in Large-Scale Indoor Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Macau and University of Michigan developed SignNav, a new embodied navigation task, enabling autonomous agents to interpret dynamic semantic hints from signage for navigation in large-scale indoor environments. Their Spatial-Temporal Aware Transformer (START) model achieved an 80% success rate on unseen environments, surpassing baselines, and was successfully deployed on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Existing embodied navigation paradigms like ObjectNav and VLN are often limited by closed target sets, lack of directional reasoning, or impractical reliance on granular instructions for Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) environments.
- The crucial role of signage in human navigation within LSI environments has been largely underexplored for autonomous agents, with prior research focusing only on isolated perception tasks.
- A lack of suitable datasets tailored for training and evaluating signage-driven navigation agents in complex, large-scale public indoor spaces hindered the development of robust solutions.

## Method

- The research formally defines **SignNav** as a novel embodied navigation task, where agents interpret dynamic visual semantic hints (directional arrows) from signage to navigate to a target location.
- The Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) Dataset was created, comprising 20 high-fidelity 3D scans of public spaces with procedurally injected directional signage and automatically generated navigation episodes.
- A Spatial-Temporal Aware Transformer (START) model was developed as an end-to-end solution, featuring hierarchical processing for spatial grounding of current hints and temporal reasoning over historical states.

## Results

- The START model achieved an **80%** Success Rate (SR) and **0.74** Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (NDTW) on the challenging val-unseen split, outperforming the strongest baseline, ViNT, by **+12%** SR and **+5%** NDTW.
- START demonstrated robust long-horizon navigation, completing all episodes from 40 to 200 meters with a stable absolute trajectory error (RMSE) of approximately **0.25** m for val-seen and **0.27** m for val-unseen scenes.
- The model was successfully deployed on a DeepRobotics Lite2 robot in a real-world office building, confirming its practical applicability for mapless, signage-based navigation in physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The synergy of both spatial-aware and temporal-aware modules within the START model is critical for effective navigation, achieving substantial performance gains by grounding dynamic hints and maintaining long-term memory.
> - While RGB inputs are essential for semantic hint extraction, relying solely on depth observations for core navigation control resulted in superior performance and better generalization, due to depth's robustness to environmental variations.
> - Using Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) during training significantly enhances the agent's generalization capabilities by exposing it to a wider range of interactive data and its own errors, improving success rates by approximately 8%.
