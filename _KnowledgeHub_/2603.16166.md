# SignNav: Leveraging Signage for Semantic Visual Navigation in Large-Scale Indoor Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Macau and University of Michigan developed SignNav, a new embodied navigation task, enabling autonomous agents to interpret dynamic semantic hints from signage for navigation in large-scale indoor environments. Their Spatial-Temporal Aware Transformer (START) model achieved an 80% success rate on unseen environments, surpassing baselines, and was successfully deployed on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Existing embodied navigation paradigms like ObjectNav and VLN are often limited by closed target sets, lack of directional reasoning, or impractical reliance on granular instructions for Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) environments.
- The crucial role of signage in human navigation within LSI environments has been largely underexplored for autonomous agents, with prior research focusing only on isolated perception tasks.
- A lack of suitable datasets tailored for training and evaluating signage-driven navigation agents in complex, large-scale public indoor spaces hindered the development of robust solutions.

## Method

- The research formally defines **SignNav** as a novel embodied navigation task, where agents interpret dynamic visual semantic hints (directional arrows) from signage to navigate to a target location.
- The Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) Dataset was created, comprising 20 high-fidelity 3D scans of public spaces with procedurally injected directional signage and automatically generated navigation episodes.
- A Spatial-Temporal Aware Transformer (START) model was developed as an end-to-end solution, featuring hierarchical processing for spatial grounding of current hints and temporal reasoning over historical states.

## Results

- The START model achieved an **80%** Success Rate (SR) and **0.74** Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (NDTW) on the challenging val-unseen split, outperforming the strongest baseline, ViNT, by **+12%** SR and **+5%** NDTW.
- START demonstrated robust long-horizon navigation, completing all episodes from 40 to 200 meters with a stable absolute trajectory error (RMSE) of approximately **0.25** m for val-seen and **0.27** m for val-unseen scenes.
- The model was successfully deployed on a DeepRobotics Lite2 robot in a real-world office building, confirming its practical applicability for mapless, signage-based navigation in physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The synergy of both spatial-aware and temporal-aware modules within the START model is critical for effective navigation, achieving substantial performance gains by grounding dynamic hints and maintaining long-term memory.
> - While RGB inputs are essential for semantic hint extraction, relying solely on depth observations for core navigation control resulted in superior performance and better generalization, due to depth's robustness to environmental variations.
> - Using Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) during training significantly enhances the agent's generalization capabilities by exposing it to a wider range of interactive data and its own errors, improving success rates by approximately 8%.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied navigation has seen advancements with the development of photo-realistic datasets and simulators, leading to paradigms such as Object Navigation (ObjectNav) and Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN). ObjectNav tasks agents with locating specific object categories, often relying on semantic priors for efficient search. However, it is typically constrained by a finite set of target categories and lacks directional reasoning. While recent zero-shot approaches leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have expanded ObjectNav to open-set targets, these methods are primarily validated in small-scale household settings and do not fully address the complexities of expansive, semantically intricate Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) environments.

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) requires agents to follow granular, step-by-step natural language instructions. Despite significant progress, VLN's reliance on such detailed guidance is often impractical in real-world public spaces where explicit instructions are rarely available.

Signage plays a crucial role in human navigation within LSI environments like airports and hospitals by abstracting spatial information into symbolic representations. However, its integration into embodied navigation for autonomous agents has remained largely underexplored. Prior research primarily focused on isolated perception tasks such as sign detection, semantic parsing, or global localization. While some recent works have explored signage understanding using VLMs or modular rule-based systems for mapless navigation, these approaches often lack closed-loop control or rely on traditional planners rather than end-to-end learning.

This paper addresses a gap in the existing research by formalizing SignNav as a novel embodied navigation task. It specifically aims to enable agents to interpret dynamic semantic hints from signage in LSI environments for end-to-end decision-making. By introducing a dedicated dataset and a unified learning framework, this work distinguishes itself from previous efforts that are either limited in scale, instruction granularity, or rely on modular pipelines. It positions itself as a step towards more human-like navigation capabilities for robots in complex public indoor spaces, where implicit visual cues are paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and address a novel embodied navigation task, termed "SignNav," which requires autonomous agents to interpret semantic hints provided by signage to navigate to destinations within novel Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) environments.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Replicating Human Navigation Capabilities:** Humans routinely leverage semantic hints from signage (e.g., directional arrows, facility names) to navigate complex LSI environments intuitively. Current embodied agents, however, lack this capability. The paper is motivated by the goal of enabling robots to emulate this human-like wayfinding strategy.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Navigation Paradigms:**
 - **ObjectNav:** While effective for locating specific objects, ObjectNav is limited by a closed set of target categories and the assumption that the target is visually recognizable upon discovery. In LSI environments, destinations are often not directly visible, necessitating the interpretation of intermediate semantic hints.
 - **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN):** VLN relies on granular, step-by-step natural language instructions, which are rarely available or practical in public spaces. Signage offers an omnipresent, implicit instruction set that guides human navigation without requiring explicit, detailed commands.
 - **Zero-shot approaches (LLM/VLM-based):** Although promising for robustness and open-set targets, these methods are predominantly validated in smaller, household settings and struggle with the scale and semantic complexity of LSI environments.
- **Formalizing Signage-Driven Navigation:** The paper aims to formally define the SignNav task, providing a structured problem statement for researchers to develop and evaluate agents capable of this behavior. This includes defining the action space, observation space, and policy learning objectives specific to signage-based navigation.
- **Facilitating Research with a Dedicated Dataset:** A significant motivation is the absence of a suitable dataset for training and evaluating signage-driven navigation agents in LSI environments. Existing datasets predominantly feature small-scale household settings. The paper aims to construct the Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) Dataset to bridge this gap, offering diverse and complex public spaces with procedurally injected signage and automatically generated navigation episodes.
- **Addressing Unique Challenges of SignNav:** The practical implementation of SignNav presents specific technical challenges that motivate the proposed methodology:
 - **Dynamic Semantic Grounding:** Unlike tasks with fixed guidance, signage semantics and their spatial configurations continuously change as an agent moves. The agent must dynamically ground these transient visual hints into its egocentric observations to make informed decisions.
 - **Sparse Observability:** Signage is typically sparsely distributed in LSI environments. Agents often navigate through areas where no explicit signage is visible. This necessitates maintaining a temporal memory of previously observed hints to reason effectively and continue navigation when immediate guidance is absent.
- **Developing an End-to-End Solution:** The motivation extends to proposing a unified, end-to-end learning model that directly maps visual observations and semantic hints to physical actions, rather than relying on modular, rule-based systems or traditional planners. This aims for a more robust and generalizable solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology addresses the SignNav task through a three-pronged approach: task definition, dataset creation, and model development.

**A. Task Definition**
The SignNav task is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The agent's objective is to continuously navigate a 3D environment to a semantic target location (e.g., "Boarding Gate 9") by interpreting dynamically detected visual semantic hints (directional arrows) from signage.

- **Action Space:** A discrete action space `a_t ∈ A` comprising four low-level commands: Move Forward (0.25m), Turn Left (15°), Turn Right (15°), and Stop. This aligns with VLN-CE standards.
- **Observation Space:** At each time step `t`, the agent receives `(o_t, h_t, b_t)`.
 - `o_t = {o^r_t, o^d_t}`: Egocentric RGB image and depth image.
 - `h_t`: Visual semantic hint, defined as the local image region (crop) enclosed by the bounding box of a detected directional arrow relevant to the target.
 - `b_t`: Bounding box coordinates `[xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax]` of the detected directional arrow.
- **Policy Learning:** Due to partial observability, the agent's policy `π(a_t | H_t, o_t, h_t, b_t; Θ)` must leverage a history `H_t` of past states (observations, hints, actions) to resolve ambiguities and capture long-range dependencies, predicting a probability distribution over the action space.

**B. LSI-Dataset Creation**
To support the SignNav task, the Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) Dataset was constructed, designed to capture the spatial complexity and scale of public infrastructure, contrasting with existing small-scale household datasets.

- **Scene Collection:** 20 high-fidelity 3D scans of public spaces (hospitals, office complexes) were collected from open-source repositories, featuring average floor areas of 1000 m².
- **Signage Injection:** An automated pipeline utilizing Blender procedurally injects signage into the 3D meshes. Signage assets, containing only directional arrows (no text), are placed at decision points (e.g., intersections, corners) and aligned with potential navigation paths. This design choice isolates spatial reasoning from semantic matching.
- **Episode Generation:**
 - A navigable connectivity graph `G=(V, E)` is built for each scene using Poisson Disk Sampling for waypoint distribution.
 - Start and goal pairs are sampled from `V`, and shortest paths are computed using Dijkstra’s algorithm, followed by spline-based smoothing to simulate realistic movement.
 - An oracle agent executes the planned path, recording `(o_t, h_t, b_t, a^*_t)` at each step `t`.
 - Directional arrows (semantic hints) are automatically annotated by leveraging local trajectory curvature and the agent’s heading relative to injected signage. This pipeline generates a large corpus of diverse trajectories.

**C. Spatial-Temporal Aware Transformer (START) Model**
START is an end-to-end decision-making model with a hierarchical architecture that interprets visual semantic hints through two stages: spatial grounding of current observations and temporal reasoning over historical states.

- **Input Embeddings:**
 - **Observation Embedding:** RGB (`o^r_t`) and depth (`o^d_t`) images are encoded separately by lightweight CNN encoders. The resulting visual features (`v^r_t`, `v^d_t`) are concatenated along the channel dimension and normalized to form `v^o_t`.
 - **Hint Embedding:** The cropped hint image (`h_t`) is processed by a ResNet-50 for visual features (`f^h_t`). Its bounding box coordinates (`b_t`) are normalized and concatenated to form `f^b_t`. These two features are then projected and fused through learnable matrices and Layer Normalization to create `v^h_t`.
- **Spatial-Aware Transformer:**
 - This module grounds semantic hints into current visual observations, capturing the correlation between directional hints and environment geometry.
 - `v^o_t` is reshaped into a sequence of flattened 2D patches. `v^h_t` is used to initialize the [CLS] token, explicitly forcing the self-attention mechanism to weigh visual patches based on their semantic relevance to the hint.
 - The self-attention blocks output a spatial embedding `f^spa_t` (re-weighted patch features) and an updated hint token (aggregating global context relevant to the cue).
- **Temporal-Aware Transformer:**
 - Addresses sparse observability and long-range dependencies by attending to a history of past states.
 - A history buffer `H_k = {(f^spa_{t-k}, a_{t-k})..., (f^spa_{t-1}, a_{t-1})}` stores recent spatial embeddings and executed actions.
 - For each time step `i` in the history, the spatial embedding `f^spa_i` is aggregated into a global vector `e^spa_i` via average pooling, then fused with an action embedding `e^a_i` (from one-hot encoding of `a_i`) to form a compact state representation `s_i`.
 - This sequence of state embeddings (`s_{t-k}..., s_{t-1}`) is fed into a BERT-like architecture with L layers of Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) and Feed-Forward Networks (FFN).
 - The final output vector `f^s_t` (corresponding to the last token) aggregates historical context and hint-grounded spatial features, serving as the agent's current belief state.
- **Action Head:** A fully connected network (FCN) projects `f^s_t` to predict the action probability distribution `p^a_t` over the discrete action space using Softmax.

**D. Training Objective**
The task is framed as a sequential decision-making problem.
- **Loss Function:** A weighted cross-entropy loss function is minimized: `L_loss = - Σ_t w_t a^*_t log(p^a_t)`, where `w_t` is an inflection weight coefficient to handle long sequences of repeated actions, and `a^*_t` is the ground-truth teacher action.
- **Training Strategy:** A two-stage strategy is employed:
 1. Initial policy training using teacher-forcing imitation learning.
 2. Fine-tuning with Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) to mitigate covariate shift. DAgger enables the agent to collect more interactive data by training on its own mistakes, enhancing generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the LSI-Dataset, along with real-world deployments and ablation studies, yielded several findings:

**A. Quantitative Performance on SignNav Task**
- **Superior Performance:** The START model achieved state-of-the-art performance on the SignNav task, significantly outperforming all baselines (VLM-based, Rule-based, ViNT) across all evaluation metrics (SR, NDTW, SDTW) on both val-seen and val-unseen splits.
 - On the challenging `val-unseen` split, START recorded an 80% Success Rate (SR) and 0.74 Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (NDTW), showing absolute gains of +12% in SR and +5% in NDTW over the strongest baseline, ViNT.
- **Baseline Comparison:**
 - **VLM-based method:** Yielded the poorest performance (30% SR, 0.54 NDTW on `val-unseen`), indicating that while VLMs excel at abstract reasoning, they currently struggle with grounding physical interactions for low-level control commands in this context.
 - **Rule-based method:** Showed better performance on the `val-unseen` split than `val-seen` (+16% SR, +8% NDTW), which the authors attributed to potential data distribution biases within the `val-seen` split. This method requires online odometry for mapping and localization.
 - **ViNT:** Although retrained on the LSI-Dataset, ViNT's basic CNN encoders were observed to lack explicit spatial grounding of semantic hints and temporal state dynamics, leading to inferior performance compared to START.

**B. Long-Horizon Navigation Robustness**
- **Stable Trajectory Error:** START demonstrated robust long-horizon navigation capabilities. When tested on episodes ranging from 40 to 200 meters in length across `val-seen` and `val-unseen` scenes, the model successfully completed all episodes. The absolute trajectory error (RMSE) remained stable at approximately 0.25 m for `val-seen` and 0.27 m for `val-unseen`, regardless of navigation distance.
- **Length-Agnostic Performance:** This stability was attributed to SignNav's reliance on localized semantic cues, allowing the Temporal-Aware Transformer to function effectively over extended periods without accumulating localization errors, unlike instruction-based navigation methods.

**C. Real-World Deployment**
- **Practical Efficacy:** The START model was successfully deployed on a DeepRobotics Lite2 robot in an office building. The robot used an Intel RealSense D435i camera for RGB-D observations, DepthAnything-V2 for enhanced depth images, and a locally deployed Qwen3-8B model for semantic hint perception, parsing, and episode termination.
- **Mapless Navigation:** The deployment confirmed the practical applicability of the approach for mapless, signage-based navigation in a physical environment.

**D. Ablation Studies**
- **Spatial and Temporal-Aware Modules:**
 - Removing either the spatial-aware or temporal-aware module from START (without DAgger) led to substantial performance degradation.
 - The `spatial-only` model outperformed the `temporal-only` model on `val-unseen` (+8% SR, +2% NDTW), suggesting better generalization in unseen environments by relaxing input distribution constraints through single-step predictions.
 - However, integrating both modules yielded significant relative gains of +25% SR and +13% NDTW on the `val-unseen` split compared to the better performing `spatial-only` baseline, indicating that the synergy of spatial grounding and temporal reasoning is critical.
- **Impact of DAgger Training:**
 - Training with DAgger significantly enhanced the agent's generalization capability. Compared to teacher-forcing alone, DAgger improved the SR by approximately 8% on both `val-seen` and `val-unseen` splits, attributed to the collection of more interactive data that exposes the agent to the consequences of its own errors.
- **Input Modalities:**
 - The `RGB-only` variant yielded the poorest results, often causing oscillatory behavior.
 - Surprisingly, relying solely on depth observations for navigation (while retaining RGB for hint images) achieved the best overall performance, outperforming the combined `RGB-D` model. This `depth-only` configuration showed relative SR improvements of +4% (`val-seen`) and +5% (`val-unseen`) over `RGB-D`. This was attributed to depth data's inherent robustness against illumination and texture variations, which aids generalization. RGB inputs remained essential for extracting semantic hints and determining episode termination.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces SignNav, a novel and underexplored embodied navigation task, addressing a fundamental gap in how autonomous agents navigate complex Large-Scale Indoor (LSI) environments. The work's significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Formalization of a Novel Navigation Paradigm:** By formally defining SignNav, the paper establishes a new benchmark for embodied AI, moving beyond traditional ObjectNav and VLN tasks that often fall short in LSI contexts due to scale, dynamic cues, or instruction granularity. This provides a clear problem statement and evaluation framework for future research.
- **Creation of a Dedicated LSI-Dataset:** The LSI-Dataset fills a critical void by providing 20 diverse, high-fidelity 3D scans of public indoor spaces with procedurally generated signage and navigation episodes. This dataset is crucial for training and evaluating agents in environments that more closely mirror real-world public infrastructure, enabling more realistic and generalizable research outcomes.
- **Development of an Effective End-to-End Model:** The Spatial-Temporal Aware Transformer (START) model offers a robust and unified solution for the inherent challenges of SignNav, specifically dynamic semantic grounding and sparse observability. Its hierarchical architecture, which explicitly processes spatial visual cues and maintains temporal memory, represents a technical advancement in designing policies for navigating with implicit, evolving guidance.
- **Demonstrated State-of-the-Art Performance:** START's superior performance across multiple metrics, particularly on unseen environments and over long navigation horizons, indicates its efficacy and robustness. The consistent trajectory error over extended distances highlights a key capability for practical applications in large spaces.
- **Validation through Real-World Deployment:** The successful deployment of START on a physical robot in an office building without pre-built maps validates the practical applicability and transferability of the proposed methodology from simulation to the real world. This demonstrates the potential for immediate utility in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Enabling More Capable Embodied Agents:** The ability of an agent to leverage signage for navigation can significantly enhance the autonomy and utility of robots in various public indoor settings, such as:
 - **Assistance for Humans:** Robots assisting visitors in hospitals, airports, museums, or large corporate campuses.
 - **Logistics and Delivery:** Autonomous delivery systems navigating through office buildings or warehouses.
 - **Search and Rescue:** Robots operating in unfamiliar indoor structures.
 - **Accessibility:** Providing navigation assistance for individuals with visual impairments or mobility challenges, especially in novel environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future investigations into more complex signage interpretation (e.g., incorporating textual information, multi-lingual signs), multi-modal reasoning that integrates signs with other environmental cues, and adaptive navigation strategies that can handle occlusions or damaged signage more robustly.
