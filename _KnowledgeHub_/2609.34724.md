# DexWeave: Learning Dexterous Humanoid Loco-Manipulation from Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> DexWeave turns human body-hand-object demonstrations into dexterous humanoid loco-manipulation skills. A two-stage retargeter first solves the body and the hands separately with specialized solvers, then refines the arms, wrists and fingers together as one interaction chain. An anatomy-aware Transformer policy, trained in a single PPO stage, tracks the result and reaches 97.5% success. It also runs on a Unitree G1 with Inspire hands.

## Problem

- Whole-body retargeters and dexterous-hand retargeters optimize separate objectives at separate stages, so wrist placement and finger articulation end up inconsistent across embodiments even when each is locally well retargeted.
- Rescaling human motion to robot proportions moves the wrist relative to nearby objects, which breaks hand-object contact even when the overall body pose is retargeted well.
- Existing loco-manipulation controllers use separate upper- and lower-body agents, or pretrained body and hand priors joined by residual RL, so fine coordination between whole-body motion and finger interaction stays hard.

## Method

- ==Contact-aware body retargeting== blends a morphology-preserving wrist target with a world-aligned scene target, weighted by hand-object proximity, under ==hard foot-support constraints== that stop foot skating.
- ==Retrieval-based hand fitting== warm-starts finger drivers from a ==precomputed codebook==. ==Coupled upper-body refinement== then optimizes arm, wrist and finger joints together, weighting thumb and index fingertips most and loosening the wrist anchor.
- **Anatomy-aware Transformer policy**: eight regional body tokens with ==directed masked attention==, where hands read body context but not the reverse. Object information flows one way into waist, arm and hand tokens only.

## Results

- On fine-grained retargeting, primary-fingertip error drops to **4.34 mm** on GRAB and **7.20 mm** on HUMOTO, against **14.93** and **30.00 mm** for the best OmniRetarget-plus-IK baselines, with near-zero penetration.
- In MuJoCo sim-to-sim, dexterous loco-manipulation reaches **97.5%** success, against **85.0%** for an MLP with a similar parameter count and **57.7%** for InterMimic. It also converges about **2x** faster than the MLP.
- Ablations: coupled refinement cuts primary-fingertip error by **87.9%** on GRAB. Fully dense body attention lowers completion from **100%** to **91.3%** but slightly improves object-pose error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Retarget the whole wrist-to-fingertip interaction chain jointly. Correcting only the wrist or only the fingers pushes the embodiment mismatch into another part of the chain.
> - Anatomical structure can serve as a policy inductive bias. Directed attention and object-free leg pathways give stable single-stage RL without pretraining, distillation or residual refinement.
> - The gains come with trade-offs. Refinement worsens palm-normal alignment, and structured attention does not consistently improve object-pose accuracy, so check each metric separately.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Human motion data is abundant, but a robot cannot simply copy a person's poses. Differences in body and hand morphology can break the relationship between wrist placement, finger shape, and an object. Even a kinematically plausible motion can be hard to execute while the robot is moving and balancing. DexWeave adapts the arm, wrist, and fingers around the same object. It then trains one whole-body controller to execute that reference. The authors' central claim is that transfer improves when retargeting preserves the interaction across the arm, wrist, fingers, and object, and the controller uses the robot's anatomical structure to coordinate those actions. They demonstrate deployment on a physical Unitree G1 with Inspire dexterous hands. That evidence is qualitative rather than a reported hardware success-rate comparison.

Real-world loco-manipulation on a Unitree G1 humanoid, from the authors' project site.

### 2. Keep the interaction together

Retargeting means adapting the demonstrated motion to the robot's body and hand. DexWeave first initializes body and hand motion with specialized solvers. Its body stage blends a morphology-scaled wrist target with a scene-aligned target according to persistent hand–object proximity, and the object trajectory stays fixed. Its hand stage fits finger shape.

DexWeave then jointly refines arms, wrists, and independent hand drivers against fingertip and palm targets. Other body coordinates stay fixed, so the established lower-body support motion is retained.

Inputs feed separate body and dexterous-hand initialization. An upper-body interaction refinement follows, with the remaining body coordinates fixed. Object and robot observations then enter an anatomy-aware masked Transformer and action decoder.

The GRAB benchmark supplies articulated hand–object references. On GRAB, DexWeave's primary-fingertip error is **4.342 mm**. The best listed modular baseline, OmniRetarget + SBR + arm–wrist IK, reaches **14.925 mm**. This metric is the world-frame thumb/index fingertip distance on source-labeled interaction frames, where lower is better. The IK baseline adjusts arm/wrist placement while keeping finger articulation fixed. Across GRAB and HUMOTO, DexWeave also has lower penetration duration and palm error. Its secondary-fingertip error ranks second on GRAB and first on HUMOTO, so the comparison is not an across-the-board win on every hand metric.

A matched-sequence ablation compares decoupled initialization with refined output. In that ablation, adding the coupled refinement stage greatly reduces primary-fingertip error and the frequency of penetration. It also increases palm-normal error on both GRAB and HUMOTO. On HUMOTO, the remaining penetration events have greater conditional maximum depth.

### 3. Give the controller a body map

The actor divides robot observations into eight regions: base, two legs, waist, two arms, and two hands. It uses directed masked attention so information flows along selected anatomical dependencies. Object state conditions the waist, arms, and hands. Direct object observations do not feed into leg actions. The policy outputs 41 normalized joint-position commands, which joint-level PD controllers execute.

In the MuJoCo sim-to-sim loco-manipulation evaluation, DexWeave's success rate is **97.50%**. An object-conditioned MLP of comparable parameter count reaches **85.00%**. Success means an evaluation episode reaches the reference end or time limit without early failure, and the table averages equally over five loco-manipulation references. Both methods share the G1–Inspire embodiment. Within each reference, they use matched observations, actions, rewards, resets, termination rules, domain randomization, and training budget. DexWeave also has lower body-position and object-position errors. The MLP has lower object-rotation error (**6.16°** versus **6.83°**).

### 4. What the results support

DexWeave bridges interaction-consistent retargeting and anatomy-aware policy learning. Its evidence includes better measured fingertip alignment, fewer penetration violations, and higher simulated loco-manipulation success. These results do not show uniformly better geometry on every metric. The real-world demonstration establishes feasibility on the tested platform and tasks, not generalization to arbitrary objects, motions, or humanoids.
