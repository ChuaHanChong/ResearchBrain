# InCoM: Intent-Driven Perception and Structured Coordination for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, developed InCoM, an end-to-end framework for mobile manipulation that integrates intent-driven multi-scale perception and explicitly coordinated whole-body action generation. This system achieved a mean success rate of 83.8% on the simulated ManiSkill-HAB SetTable benchmark and 51.25% in real-world tasks, outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing mobile manipulation methods struggle with the strong coupling between base and arm actions, complicating control optimization and leading to amplified end-effector errors, often due to unidirectional coordination models.
- Current policies do not explicitly account for the dynamic allocation of perceptual attention required for varied task modes, such as global navigation vs. precise local manipulation, resulting in dispersed attention and reduced task success.

## Method

![[2602.23024-method.png]]
*The detailed architecture of the InCoM framework, showing the flow from multi-modal perception to the coordinated action decoder.*

- An ==Intent-Driven Pyramid Perception Module (IDPPM)== adaptively reweights multi-scale perceptual features based on inferred motion intent, dynamically shifting focus between global and local details.
- A ==Dual-stream Affinity Refinement Module (DARM)== robustly aligns 3D point cloud and 2D image features by explicitly decoupling geometric and semantic correspondence with geometry-guided regularization.
- A ==Decoupled Coordinated Flow Matching (DCFM)== decoder generates smooth, stable actions for the mobile base and manipulator with explicit bidirectional coordination, utilizing ==conditional flow matching== for efficiency.

## Results

- **InCoM** achieved a mean success rate of **83.8%** on the ManiSkill-HAB SetTable benchmark, substantially outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like DSPv2 (**50.8%**) and AC-DiT (**55.6%**), and a **51.25%** mean success rate in real-world mobile manipulation tasks, surpassing other pretrained policies.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Intent-Driven Pyramid Perception Module (IDPPM) contributed the largest performance gain (**19.5%** increase), with the Dual-stream Affinity Refinement Module (DARM) and Decoupled Coordinated Flow Matching (DCFM) decoder also significantly enhancing performance.
- The framework demonstrated superior inference efficiency, with a latency of **140 ms** on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, making it suitable for real-time deployment and significantly faster than other diffusion-based methods like DP (**790 ms**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic, intent-driven allocation of multi-scale perceptual features enhances a robot's ability to focus on relevant information for current task stages, from global navigation to precise local manipulation.
> - Explicitly modeling bidirectional coordination and mutual information exchange between a mobile base and manipulator is essential for stable, smooth, and expressive whole-body actions in mobile manipulation.
> - Decoupling geometric and semantic affinities during cross-modal fusion (3D point cloud and 2D images) provides a more robust and accurate environmental understanding compared to entangled approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mobile manipulation is a foundational capability for the development of general-purpose robotic agents operating in complex, real-world environments. The field has evolved from early systems that decomposed tasks into separate navigation and manipulation phases, often leading to motion discontinuities and error accumulation, to more recent end-to-end imitation learning frameworks.

Current end-to-end approaches, which include methods like chunk-wise sequence modeling with Transformers, multi-modal fusion of visual observations for joint action prediction, and generative trajectory modeling using diffusion processes, have improved temporal coherence. However, many of these systems treat the mobile base and manipulator as a single, unified action vector, thereby overlooking or implicitly modeling their intricate mutual dependencies. While advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have enhanced generalization through large-scale pretraining, their primary focus remains on tabletop manipulation, not fully addressing the unique dependencies between movement and manipulation in mobile scenarios. Some efforts, such as the BEHAVIOR Robot Suite and AC-DiT, have introduced hierarchical or conditional decoding strategies to model dependencies between the base and arm. Nevertheless, these often adopt unidirectional conditioning mechanisms, failing to capture the reciprocal and bidirectional coordination inherent in complex whole-body interactions.

Another significant, yet often less prioritized, challenge in mobile manipulation concerns the dynamic allocation of perceptual attention under continuously changing viewpoints. Unlike single-mode tasks, mobile manipulation involves switching between distinct operational modes—like global navigation and precise local manipulation—each demanding different perceptual granularity and focus. Most existing end-to-end policies do not explicitly address this stage-dependent perceptual requirement, resulting in dispersed perceptual attention that can degrade task success rates. While multi-modal and multi-scale visual perception approaches exist, leveraging pretrained 2D visual models and 3D perception methods, adaptive weighting mechanisms typically operate on fixed-scale representations. The potential of multi-scale feature representations, where shallow layers capture local details and deeper layers encode global context, remains largely underexplored in mobile manipulation, despite its success in computer vision tasks like object detection and scene parsing. Furthermore, cross-modal fusion, particularly between 3D point clouds and 2D image features, often entangles geometric and semantic information within a single attention mechanism, which can be less robust than a decoupled approach.

InCoM positions itself within this landscape by directly confronting these two limitations: the strong coupling of base and arm actions and the dynamic allocation of perceptual attention. It aims to develop a unified framework that explicitly models bidirectional coordination and stage-adaptive multi-scale perception to enhance the robustness and effectiveness of mobile manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the capabilities of general-purpose robotic agents by developing a robust and efficient framework for mobile manipulation. This overarching goal is motivated by two key challenges identified in existing approaches:

1. **Challenges with Coordinated Control of Mobile Base and Manipulator**: Mobile manipulation inherently requires the simultaneous and stable control of both the robotic arm and the mobile base. Existing methods often struggle with the strong coupling between base and arm actions, which complicates control optimization. When base and arm actions are decoupled or weakly coordinated, even minor execution errors in the base motion can be significantly amplified at the end-effector, leading to reduced manipulation accuracy or task failure. While some approaches introduce conditional dependencies between components, they often model coordination as a unidirectional dependency. This limitation prevents the system from capturing the bidirectional coupling and mutual compensation that are crucial for efficient and robust task execution in real-world scenarios, such as when the base needs to adjust its position in response to arm movements, or vice versa. The motivation is to design an action generation mechanism that explicitly models and leverages bidirectional information interaction for more stable and coordinated whole-body actions.

2. **Challenges with Dynamic Perceptual Attention under Changing Viewpoints**: Mobile manipulation tasks involve a variety of operational modes, each with distinct perceptual requirements, and the robot's viewpoint dynamically changes throughout task execution. For example, during navigation, the policy requires a broad understanding of the global scene to identify free space and avoid obstacles. In contrast, during precise manipulation (e.g., grasping an object), perceptual attention must shift to fine-grained local details, such as the interaction point between the gripper and the object. Most existing end-to-end policies do not explicitly account for this dynamic allocation of perceptual attention. Consequently, perceptual resources can become dispersed during execution, hindering the model's ability to focus on salient information relevant to the current task stage, ultimately degrading task success rates. The motivation is to develop a perceptual system that can adaptively allocate attention across different scales—from global context to local details—based on the robot's inferred task intent, thereby improving robustness to viewpoint changes and enhancing task-specific perception.

To address these challenges, InCoM proposes:
- An end-to-end framework that jointly models intent-driven perception and coordinated action generation.
- An Intent-Driven Pyramid Perception Module (IDPPM) for adaptively reweighting multi-scale perceptual features based on inferred motion intent.
- A Dual-stream Affinity Refinement Module (DARM) for enhancing multimodal correspondence by explicitly modeling geometric and semantic affinities.
- A Decoupled Coordinated Flow Matching (DCFM) decoder for explicit bidirectional coordination between the mobile base and manipulator, promoting smooth and expressive action generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InCoM frames mobile manipulation as a discrete-time partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), where the robot receives multi-modal observations (sparse 3D point cloud, multi-view RGB images, and proprioceptive state) and generates a coordinated whole-body action sequence for both the mobile base and the manipulator. The framework consists of three core components: an Intent-Driven Pyramid Perception Module (IDPPM), a Dual-stream Affinity Refinement Module (DARM), and a Decoupled Coordinated Flow Matching (DCFM) decoder.

#### 3.1. Intent-Driven Pyramid Perception Module (IDPPM)

IDPPM is designed to address the dynamic perceptual attention problem by adaptively allocating perceptual resources based on task stages.
- **Hierarchical Feature Pyramid**: Features are extracted using a sparse 3D encoder and a pretrained 2D visual encoder (DINOv2). These features are then aligned across three abstraction levels: shallow (S) for high-resolution local details, mid (M) for an intermediate spatial receptive field, and deep (D) for large-scale global scene understanding. This structure supports fine-grained manipulation at shallow levels and base navigation at deep levels.
- **Intent Modulation**: An implicit intent vector (h_t) is inferred by a History Transformer, which processes the historical action sequence and the current global visual feature (classification token from DINOv2). An MLP-based ScaleGater network (ϕ) maps this intent vector to normalized hierarchical weights (w_t = [w_S, w_M, w_D]) using a Softmax function, dynamically adjusting the emphasis on each scale.
- **Auxiliary Kinematic Supervision**: To align perceptual focus with the robot's kinematic state, a kinematics-guided loss regularizes the weight distribution. Activity levels for the mobile base (I_base) and manipulator (I_arm) are quantified by the L2 norms of their historical action increments. A target weight distribution (w*) is defined where deep features are proportional to I_base, shallow features to I_arm, and mid features to the geometric mean of shallow and deep. The KL divergence between the predicted weights (w_t) and the target weights (w*), combined with an entropy regularization term, encourages the model to emphasize global features during rapid base motion and local features during fine manipulation.

#### 3.2. Dual-stream Affinity Refinement Module (DARM)

DARM aims to achieve robust cross-modal alignment between 3D point cloud voxels and 2D image features by decoupling geometric and semantic correspondence.
- **Dual-Stream Affinity Modeling**: DARM computes geometric affinity (A_geo) from positional encodings of 3D voxels and 2D image patches, capturing spatial consistency. Simultaneously, semantic affinity (A_sem) is derived from high-dimensional feature representations, modeling appearance and semantic correspondence. Both affinities are implemented using scaled dot-product attention in separate representation spaces. The resulting affinity maps are then concatenated and adaptively fused via a lightweight 1x1 convolutional module (f_refine) to produce the final cross-modal attention distribution.
- **Geometry-Guided Regularization**: Geometric affinity, being generally more stable, is used to regularize semantic matching. A transport cost matrix (C) is constructed from the normalized geometric affinity, and the Sinkhorn–Knopp algorithm generates a soft correspondence distribution (T*). The semantic attention distribution (P_sem) is encouraged to align with this geometric prior by minimizing the KL divergence D_KL(P_sem || sg(T*)), where the stop-gradient (sg) operator ensures that geometric information acts as a stable prior without being influenced by semantic noise gradients.

#### 3.3. Decoupled Coordinated Flow Matching (DCFM) Decoder

DCFM addresses the challenges of high-dimensional action spaces and cross-part coordination through a decoupled, generative, and explicitly coordinated action decoding framework. It uses conditional flow matching to improve action smoothness and stability while reducing iterative sampling overhead compared to diffusion models.
- **Decoupled Generation Architecture**: The whole-body action space is explicitly factorized into base actions (a_base) and arm actions (a_arm). A direct linear path is constructed between a noise distribution (a_0) and the expert action distribution (a_1). The network (v_θ) is trained to predict the constant flow velocity (u = a_1 - a_0). Separate Transformer decoders are used for the mobile base and manipulator, each minimizing the prediction error against their respective target velocities, conditioned on a multi-modal context embedding (c).
- **Explicit Bidirectional Coordination**: To account for physical coupling, each decoder branch maintains a learnable trajectory summary token ("Trend Token") that aggregates predicted motion trends. At each Transformer layer, the base and manipulator branches exchange this information via cross-attention. A stop-gradient mechanism is applied in the cross-attention to prevent unstable gradient interference during training, while still ensuring bidirectional information flow for coordination.

#### 3.4. Overall Optimization Objective

The total training objective combines the flow matching loss (L_flow), the auxiliary kinematic supervision loss (L_scale), and the geometry-guided attention regularization loss (L_align) with weighting coefficients: L_total = L_flow + λ_scale L_scale + λ_align L_align.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of InCoM involved extensive experiments in simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating its performance advantages over existing methods.

#### 4.1. ManiSkill-HAB Evaluation (Simulation)

InCoM was evaluated on the ManiSkill-HAB benchmark under perception-constrained settings, where privileged simulator information was removed. It was compared against state-of-the-art baselines including DP, ACT, DSPv2, WB-VIMA, AC-DiT, and π_0.
- **SetTable Scenario**: InCoM achieved a mean success rate of 83.8%, substantially outperforming all baselines. For example, it significantly surpassed DSPv2 (50.8%) and AC-DiT (55.6%), the latter of which had access to privileged information. InCoM showed strong performance in precise manipulation tasks like "Pick Apple" (59.4% vs. 33.3% for AC-DiT) and "Pick Bowl" (84.1% vs. 36.0% for AC-DiT).
- **TidyHouse and PrepareGroceries Scenarios**: In multi-task manipulation scenarios, InCoM maintained significantly higher success rates (47.8% and 40.5% mean, respectively) compared to baselines (e.g., DSPv2 at 21.7% and 16.9%). Baselines like DP, ACT, and WB-VIMA exhibited near-zero success rates on "pick-all" tasks, indicating InCoM's stability and robustness with increasing task complexity.
- **Comparison with Pretrained Models**: InCoM also outperformed π_0, a large-scale pretrained policy (83.8% vs. 59.9% mean on SetTable), suggesting that its specialized architecture for coordination and perception is more effective for mobile manipulation than generalist pretrained models.
- **Qualitative Analysis**: Comparative visualizations of execution trajectories revealed that InCoM generated more effective manipulator paths under occlusion (e.g., "Pick Apple" from fridge) and more continuous, coordinated base-arm movements (e.g., "Open Drawer") than ACT and π_0, which struggled with precise localization or stable coordination.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A comprehensive ablation study on the SetTable scenario assessed the contribution of each InCoM component.
- **Intent-Driven Pyramid Perception Module (IDPPM)**: Removing IDPPM, by replacing it with a single-scale perceptual encoder, led to the largest performance drop (19.5% decrease), underscoring the critical role of multi-scale perception and adaptive attention. Replacing adaptive scale weighting with fixed fusion caused a 5.6% decrease, indicating that dynamic reweighting optimizes feature utilization.
- **Dual-stream Affinity Refinement Module (DARM)**: Substituting DARM with the Q-Former module from DSPv2 resulted in a 6.2% performance drop, validating the benefits of explicitly separating and refining geometric and semantic affinities for robust cross-modal alignment.
- **Decoupled Coordinated Flow Matching (DCFM)**: Replacing DCFM with a dense action head from DSPv2 degraded performance by 7.9%, confirming the importance of modeling coordinated whole-body action generation via conditional flow matching.
- **Additional Ablations on PrepareGroceries**: Further experiments confirmed that intent modulation in IDPPM (6.4% drop without it), bidirectional interaction in DCFM (4.4% drop without it), the stop-gradient mechanism (3.3% drop), geometric regularization in DARM (2.4% drop), and the Trend Token in DCFM (5.8% drop) are all beneficial for overall performance and stability.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

InCoM was validated on a Cobot-Magic robot platform across four real-world mobile manipulation tasks: Throw Rubbish, Close Drawer, Pick Banana, and Move Block.
- **Performance**: InCoM consistently outperformed baselines ACT and π_0.5 (another pretrained model), achieving a mean success rate of 51.25%, compared to 32.5% for π_0.5 and 10% for ACT. This demonstrated the framework's effectiveness in practical scenarios.

#### 4.4. Inference Efficiency

- **Latency**: InCoM achieved significantly faster inference (140 ms on NVIDIA A100 GPU) compared to other diffusion-based methods like DP (790 ms) and WB-VIMA (370 ms). On an RTX 4090 GPU, InCoM's latency was approximately 60 ms, enabling real-time deployment.
- **Memory**: GPU memory usage was moderately higher (5.4 GB on A100) due to multi-scale perception but remained within practical limits.

#### 4.5. Visualization of Feature Receptive Field

Analysis of patch-to-patch attention across network depths in the image encoder (DINOv2 backbone) revealed a clear hierarchical spatial modeling. Shallow layers exhibited strong locality, focusing on fine-grained details. Mid layers expanded to broader contextual regions, and deep layers integrated global scene information, forming a multi-scale perceptual hierarchy. This confirmed that the encoder provides complementary representations essential for intent-driven modulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The InCoM framework contributes to the field of mobile manipulation by addressing two central challenges: the effective allocation of perceptual attention under dynamic viewpoints and the robust coordination of whole-body actions. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Advancement in Mobile Manipulation Capability**: InCoM provides a unified, end-to-end framework that integrates perception and control, demonstrating substantial improvements in task success rates across diverse simulated and real-world mobile manipulation scenarios. This positions it as an effective approach for complex tasks beyond the capabilities of many existing methods.
- **Enhanced Perception under Dynamic Conditions**: The Intent-Driven Pyramid Perception Module (IDPPM) introduces a mechanism for adaptively allocating multi-scale perceptual resources based on inferred motion intent. This enables the robotic policy to dynamically adjust its focus between global scene understanding (for navigation) and local fine-grained details (for manipulation), which is crucial for operating in dynamic and unstructured environments. This approach could lead to more robust and context-aware robotic perception systems.
- **Improved Whole-Body Coordination**: The Decoupled Coordinated Flow Matching (DCFM) decoder explicitly models bidirectional interactions between the mobile base and the manipulator. This structured coordination alleviates the difficulties associated with strongly coupled control optimization and prevents error amplification, resulting in smoother, more stable, and expressive whole-body actions. This mechanism could be generalized to other multi-limbed or multi-agent robotic systems requiring tight coordination.
- **Robust Cross-Modal Fusion**: The Dual-stream Affinity Refinement Module (DARM) offers a more robust method for fusing 3D point cloud and 2D image data by explicitly separating and refining geometric and semantic affinities. This approach mitigates issues faced by methods that entangle these different types of information, leading to more reliable and accurate environmental understanding.
- **Practical Applicability and Efficiency**: The use of conditional flow matching in DCFM reduces inference latency compared to traditional diffusion models, making InCoM suitable for real-time deployment on physical robots. The demonstration of superior performance in real-world experiments, without reliance on privileged information, highlights its practical potential for industrial and service robotics applications.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Robotics**: By concurrently addressing critical perceptual and control challenges, InCoM contributes towards the development of more autonomous and capable robotic agents that can perform complex tasks in human-centric environments, ultimately moving closer to the goal of general-purpose robotics.

While the current framework is trained on task-specific data and its intent inference module could be further enriched for complex task dynamics, InCoM establishes a strong foundation, with future work potentially focusing on integrating larger-scale pretrained representations, advanced reasoning mechanisms, and extending to long-horizon tasks.
